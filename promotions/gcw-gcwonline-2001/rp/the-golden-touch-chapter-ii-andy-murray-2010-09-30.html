The Golden Touch, Chapter II: Andy Murray

30 September 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/30/10 They say the best way to really get to know a person is over a drink, particularly in the world of business. Because, let's face it, everybody enjoys an ice cold beer after a stressful day at work and if they don't, they're probably hiding something. Alcohol is notorious for bringing out a person's true character, whether that be good or bad. But, more importantly, it enables us to get comfortable in otherwise uncomfortable situations. And Steven Caldera knew that, when he arranged for an open bar event after signing six new superstars in a single night. To this point, it was uncharacteristic of Caldera to take a chance on so many unknown superstars, particularly all in one sitting. He was always noted for his carefully-thought, finely structured business algorithm of little risk-high reward. But we were about to enter the year 2008 and times were-a-changing. The purpose of the cocktail hour was to acquaint the new signees to the rest of their colleagues; be it referees, ring announcers, or wrestlers. In a perfect world, Caldera would expose his rookies to some of the top-tier superstars to help develop chemistry. But this is anything but a perfect world. The only people that actually showed up (that weren't just signed earlier in the night) were those who really had nothing else going for them. They were pretty much there just to try and make the rookies feel lowly and to boost their own self esteem. The only problem, though, was that they weren't dealing with any ordinary class of rooks. "Would you look at that glaikit loon," said a man with a thick Scottish accent. "He's marching around like he's a hardass yet he resembles a popsicle stick!" I couldn't help but crack a smile mid-swig of my beer. Part of me was laughing at the fact that the Scot was absolutely right. A fellow by the name of Dorian Ryan was strutting around the room still wearing his sunglasses like an absolute idiot. Another part of me found it hilarious that the Scot was insulting Dorian with an accent. For some reason, everything always sounds funnier when people say it with an accent. And of course, the ringer, "What the hell is a glaikit?!" That was my favorite part about the Europeans. They'd literally just mutter out some gibberish and then give it a reasonable definition. "Ah, you Americans and your simplistic insults! It's like foolish - that foolish loon. Get it now?" Taking another sip of my beer, I nodded my head. "You British have a way with words," I replied. "Whoa! Confusing me with a Brit is probably the highest form of an insult. I'm Scottish, bubbles. The Scottish King of Cool, as a matter of fact." I dipped my head, acknowledging the mistake, "Well King, it's a pleasure to meet you. I'm the Torn Soul. But you can call me Bagwell." "Torn soul? That sounds like some sort of bizarre American sex fetish," he joked. "Name's Andy. But friends call me Murr." We tapped our glasses together with a friendly cheers. My eyes continued to wander the room, intent on complimenting Andy's humor with a bit of my own. They locked in on a beast of a man whose body was entrenched with tattoos. "What about that guy over there," I said. "A few too many needles in his ass, you think?" I motioned over to a nearly three hundred pound monster by the name of Brad Jackson. Jackson hardly had tenure in GCW but was still making quite the impact at the time. Unfortunately for me, he also had excellent hearing. Jackson approached me and wrapped his massive hand around my shoulder. "What about needles," he asked...clearly rhetorically. I couldn't help but sigh to myself. This guy was damn near double the size of me and I was about to get in a fight with him. A career over before it truly began. "Get yer paws off him," Andy exclaimed. The Scottish King of Cool stood from his chair, showing off his six foot seven stature. Jackson gleamed up at Murray as if he was ready to snap back. But Murray beat him to the punch, "you hard of hearin' or something?" I shrugged my shoulder and Jackson's hand fell back to his side. He took a few steps away, holding eye contact with Murray. "You boys better watch what you're getting yourselves into," he said sternly before back stepping into another guy. "Whoa," the guy shouted as he watched both of his beer get blown up all over himself. "Come 'on, Jax! I'm Tyson DeBough - I'm the Television Champion. Look where you're fuckin' going!" Andy and I broke out into laughter. "You may want to take your own advice, MacBubba!" And with that, we shared another toast. * * * * * When you start a new job, the last thing you plan on is sucking at it. But that's exactly what I was doing - I was sucking. When I signed my name on the dotted line, I expected to walk in the doors waging war. After all, the company was featuring a chick as their champion. Better yet, another chick was the number one contender. Now I'm no Ric Flair, so I can't say I've been around the business for a century. But I have been around the business long enough to know that whenever two women are in the ring together and there's a belt on the line, it's a woman's title. Not the GCW World Heavyweight Championship. But...I guess I had slipped into the twilight zone. Not only was the company overrun by estrogen, but I'd went 0-2 in my inaugural matches. And to ice the cake, I came up short to an asshole from across the pond both times. The match with LeStrange was disheartening, but I was at least able to attribute it to ring rust. It'd been almost five years since I'd been in the ring last and so my endurance wasn't necessarily where it should've been. But it was the loss to Andy Murray that really got under my skin. Thinking about it, it probably shouldn't have bothered me much. Andy seemed like a pretty cool guy and we shared quite a few laughs a few weeks prior during our first cocktail hour. At the same token, that's the part that bothered me the most. The only positive that came from my loss at Worldwide Episode 82 was that it gave me an excuse to hit the local bar earlier than I would've had I won. Although everyone in the locker room claimed that Steven Caldera was truly an arrogant prick, he was certainly earning points in my book. In my three weeks with the company, he sponsored three after-event cocktail hours out of his own pocket. Heaven knows I wasn't going to turn down a free beer or a handful of Teater-Tots. Since it was still early in the night, there weren't a ton of guys in the bar when I walked in. Matter of fact, the only other actual wrestlers that were in there was some guy named Kiljadin and another guy wearing a bandana. I think he called himself Rollins, or something. After a quick scouting of the place, I strategically sat myself at the bar within a seat of a pretty brunette. When in Rome, right? "You realize that the entire bar is empty, right?" Within about fifteen seconds of sitting down, she looked my way and called me out. She came off with a bitchy attitude and that'd normally be enough for me to tell the broad to fuck off - especially after the night I'd had. But then I thought to myself, what the hell is a cute, young girl doing sitting in an empty bar, alone? And she was drinking, nonetheless. "Yeah, but this seat just seemed to call my name," I said sarcastically. She didn't smile. "Hmph," she mumbled under her breath. "You sure it wasn't that one over there," as she pointed to the opposite end of the bar. "Perhaps," I entertained. "But if I sat over there, I wouldn't be able to heckle you. So what do you say...another drink?" She rolled her pretty eyes up and down my body to decide if I was harmless. "A Grey Goose and tonic," she shouted to the bartender. When he looked at me, I kept my mouth shut and just raised two fingers. Either he thought I was a hippie or he was getting me the same drink. "So what's a pretty girl like yourself sitting all alone here?" "I'm waiting for my hus-uh...friend," she stuttered. "Some friend of yours to keep you waiting all the time, huh?" She smiled, "Yeah well he's still working, I guess. He'll be here eventually." The pronoun 'he' led me to believe she was waiting for more than just a friend. From my experiences in the past, it was probably a boyfriend or at the very least a guy she was fucking. But based on the fact she was kicking back Goose and Tonics like they were water, I decided she was fair game. Afterall, he was probably dicking her around anyway. "I'm sure he will," I joked. "Fashionably late seems to be the cool thing to do these days, huh?" "At least for him, it is," she added with a bit of disdain in her voice. "You guys are all just a bunch of assholes." I rolled my eyes. The ever-classic chick routine, blaming their woes on the male sex. But, at the very least, I knew immediately that there was trouble in River City - or whatever the hell city she and this guy were in. "Don't go grouping me with these other jerkoffs you've probably spent your life obsessing over." "I don't obsess," she said with an attitude. "I can replace him in a heartbeat. I'm just saying that all guys are assholes. Whether it's him or the next guy - all the same." I couldn't help but to laugh in her face, "You can replace him in a heartbeat yet you're sitting here alone, pouting about how much of a dick he is. Yep - you've really got control of the reigns sweetheart." Before I could finish my sentence, she punched down on the top of my thigh. "Jerk!" she shouted. In case you didn't go to High School, hitting was a good thing. "I knew it," I egged on. "I'm exactly right, aren't I?" "It's more complicated than that," she insisted. "But I wish I could just turn my head and walk away. Try something new...if even only for one night." She stared me in the eyes, giving me the 'I want to have a one night stand' look. Although that could've just been my interpretation of that look. Nevertheless, I slid my hand to the top of her knee with a gentle rub. Staring her back in the eyes, I whispered, "then stop talking about it...and just do it. Forget about this dick." She held the gaze for a few seconds longer before breaking a smile. Turning back towards the bar, she picked up her Goose and Tonic and took a swig. I could already tell I was in for quite the adventurous night. "I didn't catch your name," she mentioned. "Chris. Chris Bagwell," I replied. "And yours?" "Oh me?" She asked as if she was shocked I had even cared. "My name is Jane. Jane Murray." * * * * * My heart was racing; pounding out of my chest. I paced back and forth around my locker room, growing more and more nervous as the seconds ticked by. I was minutes away from finally getting my big break - my chance to burst out into the spotlight. It was an opportunity that doesn't come often. The Rampage Finals. Every piece of promotional material you'd come across labeled that year's Rampage tournament as the most competitive of all time. The wealth of talent that was entered in the tournament was unrivaled by any year previous and would be a challenge to upstage in years to come. But the talent pool was a direct reflection of our hiring class from only a few months earlier. Mark Matthews, Kiljadin Kincaid, Zacharia Taylor, Clyde Fox, Thom LeStrange, Dorian Ryan, and Depraved composed the early rounds of the tournament. But now it was down to two and I had but a handful of minutes until I put everything on the line. KNOCK, KNOCK I scurried to my locker room door the instant I heard the knock. Zacharia Taylor had promised earlier in the night that he, Depraved, Matthews, and Kincaid would stop in before I went out to the ring - just to pep talk. I was only in the company maybe three months and I had already built an army around me; a group that would ensure my quick rise to the top. I swung the door open and exclaimed, "Zacharia! You had me worried." But standing before me wasn't a five-foot-eight, one hundred and ninety pound Zacharia Taylor. Instead, it was a six-foot-seven, two hundred and sixty pound King. "What are you doing here," I asked with commanding diction. "Hello to you, too, Bubbles!" Rather than being blessed with the appearance of a great friend, I was cursed with the company of The Scottish King of Cool, Andy Murray. Or, as some might call him, my opponent for the finals of the 2008 Rampage Tournament. "I just wanted to stop in and offer you a gest’ of respect," Andy said with sincerity. "For whatever reason, we haven't seen eye to eye over the last few months and I attribute that to the stress of the tournament. Hopefully we can put it all behind us when this is over." Andy Murray was a man of class. In our short time in GCW, he grew extremely popular backstage because of his pure, honest demeanor. He carried himself like a true gentleman, despite the situation. But Chris Bagwell - I was a man bottled up with jealousy. Andy and I hit it off great the first time we met and stuck together a lot after the shows. For the first few weeks on the road, we even bunked up together to save some cash. Yet here we were on the cusp of our futures and the only thing on my mind was how I'd be able to outperform him. That was the difference between Andy and I. I wanted to win desperately; I needed to win. But Andy - he couldn't care less. Not saying that he wasn't going out to the ring and giving it all he's got - because he was. But Andy could go home after a big loss, wake up the next morning feeling fine, and still be able to excel through the ranks. It was just his character. "Yeah," I said with a hint of hesitation. "There's just been a lot going on lately. I don't think either of us have been ourselves." He reached his hand out in my direction, "Good luck, mate. We both busted our asses to get here tonight. Let's show 'em a good show." I didn't want to shake it. As a matter of fact, the absolute last thing in the world I wanted to do was shake his hand. "Let's," I replied...and I shook it, anyway. As Andy left my locker room, I couldn't help but have a soft spot. Here was a guy who, despite me being a complete prick, still had the decency to walk up to me before we go and rip each other's heads off to offer his respect. It ate me up to think that I'd been such a duechebag to such a nice guy. What was worse was that I had slept with his wife and he still doesn't know about it ...Yet. * * * * * When Steven Calera resigned from active duty in Global Championship Wrestling, few traditions remained the same. You no longer had to send a half-naked picture of yourself to corporate headquarters once a month for 'security purposes.' You no longer were able to show up damn near a day late to a show and not get penalized for it. You no longer had to go under five miles an hour on all GCW properties. But one thing that did remain the same was the post-show cocktail hours. When Garbage Bag Johnny took over, he brought with him a renewed sense of camaraderie into the arena. He worked diligently to unite all the superstars as one and believed that with high levels of camaraderie, we'd be able to execute better shows and more high-profiled matches. Chemistry was key. So when I returned to GCW after a six month disappearance, there was one thing I knew I could count on. With half the roster turned over, there weren't very many people left that I knew. Of the people I knew, there wasn't a single one of them who cared to talk to me anymore. Truthfully, the only one who even gave a damn that I was back was Clyde - and going out for a drink after a show was 'against the rules.' Or at least, that's what he was fixated on thinking. So as I sat at the bar and stared around at the new blood (and some of the old), I did so with loneliness. But, as Billy Joel put it, it was better than drinking alone. There was nobody next to me I could joke to about Shaman and his new headdress. Nobody I could talk to about how natty Mayhem's dreads were. Nobody to even argue with about Jeff's origins. I dug myself into a grave and, in some act of psycho, thought that everyone would forget about all the self-centered things I've done if I resurfaced. Christ was I mistaken. But even amidst all the bullshit I've pulled, a familiar voice kept ringing in my ears. "Never expected to see you here," he said. As I turned my head, I stared at a man who only two weeks prior ended his term as the longest reigning GCW World Champion of all time. There before me stood Andy Murray, the Scottish King of Cool. I nodded my head, acknowledging his presence. Yet I didn't say a word. I knew he was there to lay into me - to tear me apart for crawling back to the company I thought I'd be able to leave behind. I was already in a bad enough spot, being told by Johnny that I had to work my way from the bottom rung-up. The last thing I wanted to do was listen to Murray equate me to Bishop Steele. "Can't say hello to an old friend, Bubbles?" I tilted my head to the side, doing him the favor of at least giving eye contact, "How's it going." He smirked. He knew I'd seen better days. And, strangely enough, he was okay with that. "I just wanted to say," he began. "It's good to have you back." The King pat me on the shoulder and walked away. And despite me sleeping with his wife; despite me manipulating his son; despite me trying to end his career, Andy Murray still had class. He still had that crisp, honest demeanor about him. When God made Andy Murray, he gave him that golden touch.
