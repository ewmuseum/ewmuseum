1.3 - Undercover

27 December 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/27/10 “Perhaps we need to pay Captain Toms a visit. It appears as though Phillip may be wearing out his use to our cause.” – El Guerra 1 The lighting in the cell was very dim, but that didn’t manage to dull the glistening steel of the straight razor that Alonzo Silva held in his sweaty palms. They always said the first night in the clink was the worst and tonight he agreed with them. He arrived late, later than lights out. But all that meant was that he got a large audience for his walk of shame through cell block A and into his very own private hell. The cop’s words from earlier in the night rang through his head, over and over, like a broken record, they kept repeating. ...”it’s going to disappear. And in the morning, so are you... “Fuck that,” Alonzo said out loud. More to assure himself than anything else, they were words that he needed to say and they were most definitely words that he needed to hear. He kept rolling the blade over and over in his hands, twirling and spinning it. The shining stainless steel dancing a solo ballet in the soft light. His palms were beginning to sweat and his entire body was trembling. He was beginning to wonder what was in store for him the next day. ‘The cop knows people,’ he thought to himself. Clearly this guy was on Los Aztecas payroll, the question was how many inmates of the San Diego County Jail were also on the Los Aztecas payroll, or were members of the outlaw riding club. There were certainly at least half a dozen, Los Aztecas had been gaining steam over the past couple of years, there were bound to be casualties of that, inside the system. He continued staring at the razor, watching the sharp blade cut through the air, as he watched he wondered if it would cut through his skin as easily. He quickly shook the thought from his head, he couldn’t think about that right now. He wouldn’t last another day in this place if he let thoughts like that linger in his mind. He knew people. He knew names. He knew locations. And hell, he knew at least one cop who was dirty. He had plenty of bargaining chips. He knew plenty of secrets. He just had to find the right ears to whisper those secrets into. 2 Miguel was still furious with Captain Toms as he made his way home. The late night traffic in the city of San Diego was relatively light, but Miguel was in no rush. He was too angry, too frustrated. He learned a long time ago what damage he could do to his home life if he went home angry from work. He’d never forget that night a couple of years ago. A case at work had steamed him up something fierce, and the beers with Tommy had done nothing to quell that anger. He remembered when he got home, Michelle had become the focus of that anger. He quit drinking for nearly a year after that, it was only recently that he had begun to indulge his vices once again. Tonight there was no drinking. Tommy had to get home to his family, and honestly Miguel should have as well, but he needed time to kill down. It wasn’t like Captain Toms to take the side of a criminal, especially not over Chase and him. What the fuck did he think was going on with that douche bag? Miguel and Tommy were professionals and Toms knew that. He knew they had followed protocol, he had to have known. So then what was his problem? “FUCK!” Miguel screamed at the steering wheel and slammed his fist against it as well. That’s when he saw something he couldn’t believe going the opposite way on the Martin Luther King Freeway. He spotted the tail car first. If you knew what you were looking for FBI protocol was as easy to spot as the light of day. The jet black Yukon SUV was following the bike, only a few car lengths away. But more interesting than that was who was riding shotgun in the black sport-utility vehicle; FBI Unit Chief Kevin Erwin. “Fucking hell, that must be Rios. What the hell are they up to?” Erazo muttered to himself. He didn’t know what it was exactly that made him decide to follow them. Maybe it was a sense of jealousy, or maybe it was a sense of curiosity. But either way, Miguel had time to kill, and this was sure to provide some entertainment. 3 The day had been uneventful so far, but that hadn’t done anything to calm Alonzo’s nerves. He knew that something was coming, it was like sitting in the desert and watching a storm coming. You never knew how long, you only knew that eventually there would be a storm. He was sitting in the prison yard, watching as the blacks played basketball on the courts, the Aryan Brotherhood congregated near the weight benches, the Asians and Hispanics congregated in smaller groups throughout the yard. He was by himself, there weren’t very many loners in the yard, and they stood out like sore thumbs. In here, everyone knew that you needed friends to get by. And right now, Alonzo Silva had no friends, only enemies. He watched the Hispanics the closest, he figured that they were the biggest threats to his safety. But, that didn’t mean that he wasn’t looking over his shoulders after every breath or the slightest sound. He knew that it was coming, he just didn’t know if it was going to be today, or tomorrow, or the day after. The cop’s words were still echoing around in his brain. The weight of the razor blade was still fresh in his pocket. He wasn’t used to carrying a weapon, until a few hours ago, he never had a reason to. He thought about his life before this shit, about everything that had happened to get him into this mess. He wasn’t a biker, he wasn’t an outlaw. He was a young kid, who liked to drink and party, and get laid. That’s what made Los Aztecas appeal to him. That’s why he reached out to him. He wasn’t even a full-patched member. Hell, he wasn’t even a prospect yet, he was simply someone who knew somebody. But, that was enough. Enough to get him noticed. And at first, when the attention was coming from young girls who liked to open their legs a bit more than was sexually healthy, he didn’t really seem to mind. But now that attention was coming from cops and prisoners and he wasn’t so happy with it. An alarm sounded throughout the prison yard. Apparently, outdoors time was coming to an end. Slowly the prison yard began to come alive, and the prisoners began to assemble in lines. That’s when it went down. Alonzo saw them coming at the last second, but it was already too late. There were only two of them. Hispanic, exactly as he thought. They were covered in tattoos and they were both clearly bikers. Their faces were scarred and weathered. Their hands were rough and gangly. He didn’t even have enough time to pull out the razor blade. It was a prison style shank, a cheap piece of plastic, sharpened over hours in a rotting jail cell. They stabbed him twice and he immediately went down, he screamed and a commotion broke out. There was a mass of bodies as the group began of prisoners began to come together, all of them straining for a look at what happened. That’s when they disappeared. His two attackers simply merging into the sea of bodies. The guards were quick, quicker than he expected. But, they got to him quickly. He was gasping for breath, trying to tell the guards what happened. Trying to relay the message. But he could only manage a few words. After that there was an explosion of light and everything went dark. 4 Erwin was nervous, very nervous. He was shocked when Rios had called him earlier in the night, he had never expected Los Aztecas to reach back out to him so quickly. Everything in their surveillance and intel had told them that they were slow and careful, almost to a fault. So when Rios had called him and told them that only hours after meeting a recruitment officer, he was being called back for a late night meeting, well, something in his stomach told him to be suspicious. Stupidly, he had approved the meeting anyways. Now, as they sat there waiting, he was regretting that decision with every minute that passed. The surveillance vehicle was parked in a nearly empty parking lot across the road. The vehicle probably stood out more than he would have liked, but there weren’t very many other options. All of those involved not having a direct line of sight on Rios, and that wasn’t acceptable. Rios was leaning against his motorcycle, waiting for the contact to show up. He was told to be there at 2:30 in the morning and it was already closing in on 3:00, no one in the entire operation had a good feeling about this one. Somewhere in the distance there was the soft roar of motorcycle engines. And they were beginning to close the distance. “Alright, everyone ready up. Be alert and keep your eyes on our boy,” Erwin said into the communications radio. There was only silence in return, but Erwin knew that every single member of his team had heard the instructions loud and clear. The sound of the engines revving continued to get closer and closer, whatever it was, it was about to go down. Erwin watched through the back of the SUV, using night-vision goggles he stared at the open field behind him. There were five lights, like UFOs drifting across an empty sky, they hovered in the darkness. The lights of the motorcycles showered Agent Rios in light, and Erwin watched as Rios raised his arm to shield his eyes from the brightness. Rios still had his leather riding jacket and his helmet on, covering his face, he was still leaned against his motorcycle. The cyclists kept approaching Rios, but they never slowed down. There was a series of more flashing lights. Hundreds of flashes blasted through the darkness as shots began to rip and echo through the silence of the early morning. Rios’ body shivered and recoiled as his body was pumped full of 9mm bullets. In Kevin Erwin’s world, things were silent then. If only for a moment. His entire world was filled with black silence. And then he was snapped back to reality. The sub-machine guns were silent once again, but the motorcycle engines continued to roar and the headlights raced away into the night. The sound of the bikes was nearly enough to drown out all of the chatter on the walkie-talkies, but Erwin heard the words. “Holy fuck! Rios is down! I repeat, Rios is DOWN!!” Erwin was stunned. It was unlike him to be like this, and it clearly shocked the other two agents in the car who were staring at him, waiting for some kind of command. In those few seconds, Erwin snapped back into the real world. “Follow them! Let’s go!” he said forcefully. And with that the SUV’s tires squealed, and the engine roared to life. The headlights illuminated the empty stockyard across the way, the only thing left in the stockyard was the settling dust, a tipped over Kawasaki and Rios’ lifeless body. Erwin’s mind began to race as they pealed out of the parking lot and in pursuit of the motorcycle assassins. That’s when he noticed one last detail out of place. There was a car racing away from the scene, but it was going in the opposite direction of the motorcycle brigade. Erwin quickly took out a pen and scribbled the license plate down on a piece of paper. He might need it later. 5 Captain Phillip Toms was sitting at home, he was watching Sportscenter on his TV. He was interrupted from the highlights of the Chargers versus Buccaneers game by the ringing of his cell phone. He muted the television and reached for the phone. He didn’t even bother to look at the caller ID, if it was this cell phone ringing, it would only be one person. “Yeah,” he said into the receiver. ”We’ve taken care of one of the rats. I just hope you got through to the other one.” “Yeah, we shouldn’t have to worry about that,” Toms answered. ”I hope not.” With that the line went dead. 6 It had been nearly twelve hours since the attack, and Alonzo had been well-protected. There were very few inmates that were actually given access to the prison’s medical ward. He was in critical condition when he first entered the medical center earlier, but now he was in stable condition. He had never brought up the information he knew. Not about the cops and definitely not about the motorcycle club. He didn’t know if the first attack was a warning or a failed attempt on his life. But, he wasn’t going to leave it to chance. His lips were sealed from here on out. He was lying in his hospital bed. Things were beginning to feel woozy. He looked around, but his eyelids kept feeling heavier and heavier. He tried to fight it off, he shook his head, trying to shake the cobwebs away, but it was no use. Blackness was washing over him. Just before he closed his eyes, he saw someone talking to the guard outside. He couldn’t tell who it was, but he sensed something was wrong. When the stranger turned his face, Alonzo caught a quick glance at his face, and what he saw nearly stopped his heart. There was only one of them this time, but the weathered face, the rough hands, the tattoos, they were too familiar to ever forget. His last moments of consciousness Alonzo Silva watched helplessly in terror as he watched the scene unfold. The prisoner handed the guard something, and the guard only nodded and turned away. “He... Helllppp...” he tried to scream for assistance, but he could barely manage anything audible. The door swung open and Alonzo looked on helplessly at the face of his attacker from earlier in the week. Alonzo tried to fight, he tried to run away but his body was paralyzed, he was helpless. His arms were too heavy to move, his legs felt like lead. He was helpless. “You shouldn’t have sold-out the club ese. Should have did the time like the rest of us.” 7 The office was unusually quiet the next morning, the entire team was still reeling from the death of Agent Rios. He had barely been undercover for a day, and already he was in the city morgue. This was a huge blow to the case. It was also a huge emotional blow to the members of the task-force. All of the members of the FBI said nothing, they simply went about their business as quickly as they could, not wanting to think about the loss of their friend and colleague. The other local officers were drawing their cues from the FBI agents and were saying very little, instead choosing to busy themselves with mindless paperwork. Miguel hadn’t told anyone about what he had seen the night before, but that didn’t make him feel any less awkward about being witness to the death of Agent Rios. Miguel was earlier than usual, Tommy wouldn’t be in for another forty-five minutes, but he had some things to take care of. He took a look into Captain Toms’ office, but he wasn’t in yet this morning either. That seemed strange to Miguel, the Captain was usually an early riser. But this whole week had been weird for him, and he had a feeling that things would only be getting weirder. So it wasn’t surprising to him when he took a seat at his desk only to see a note from FBI Unit Chief Erwin. We need to talk. – Erwin. Miguel took one look at the note and immediately stood up. He thought about leaving the office, about running away. But it was too late. Miguel saw him coming from across the office. Kevin Erwin was walking with a false bravado this morning. On the outside he was clean cut and strong, but on the inside Miguel knew he was shaken, and Miguel knew exactly why. He had witnessed it the night before. “My office now...” Erwin said without even looking down at Miguel, or breaking stride. Miguel didn’t even bother to argue or dispute. He simply stood up and followed as he was told. Miguel had no idea about what it was that Erwin wanted, but he wasn’t exactly sure he wanted any part of it, after what he saw last night. “Shut the door...” Erwin instructed him as they entered his office. Erwin took a seat behind his desk and immediately closed the file folder that was on his desk. He directed his stare immediately at Erazo as he did as he was told and then took a seat. Erwin didn’t even wait for him to settle into his seat before he began. “I know you were there last night,” Erwin said matter-of-factly. “Sir...?” Erazo asked, hoping that it wasn’t what he thought it was. “At the shipping yard outside of the city. I know you were there, I know you saw what happened to Rios,” Erwin continued. Miguel tried to search for words, to offer a response, but instead all he could manage was a nod of acknowledgement. “Rios was a good man, he was young, he didn’t deserve that, but that’s on me...” Erwin began. “I’m sorry sir,” Miguel offered. Erwin held up his hand and shook his head softly, silencing Miguel once again, before he continued. “Don’t. His death is on me, but I’ll deal with that. I saw you pulling away. What I want to know is why you were there,” Erwin pressed. “I saw you on the freeway,” Miguel began before becoming silent again for a second. “I needed to blow off steam, I was pissed about what went down last night. So I followed along, I made you in the passenger seat of the tail vehicle, so I knew what was up.” “Wait, what happened last night?” Erwin asked. “You didn’t hear?” Miguel asked, seeming genuinely surprised. “We brought in a gang-banger, some nothing associate of Los Aztecas. Had a cocaine charge, had him nailed with the dope and everything. The guy was ready to spill the beans, ready to give us some names.” “And? Why the fuck didn’t I hear about this?” Erwin demanded. “We called our Captain, Toms knew all about it. We figured he’d fill you in,” Miguel informed him. “What the fuck is this? You’re under my command Erazo, my fucking command. He may be your Captain, but right now your ass fucking answers to me.” Miguel said nothing, he simply sat in stunned silence. Erwin took that as a sign to continue. “What the fuck happened to him? Where the fuck is he now?” “No idea, Toms ripped us about not having a lawyer there,” Miguel responded. “Who the fuck is us? Chase?” Again Miguel simply nodded in response. The fire in Erwin’s eyes was burning brighter than ever. “And where the fuck is this guy now? I swear if he knew something about Rios,” Erwin was really getting heated now. “He didn’t, he dropped some names, but nothing familiar,” Miguel interrupted him. “But Toms sent him to booking. He’s probably in county if he’s not in our shop right now.” “Fuck, he’s probably already dead,” Erwin deadpanned. “What the hell are you talking about?” Miguel asked. “What the fuck are you trying to get at here?” “There’s a leak somewhere in the shop. And from what you’re telling me, I might have an idea where it was.” Miguel kicked the chair against the window, he had heard enough. “Fuck that,” he screamed. “I don’t know what the fuck you’re trying to get at Erwin, but Toms isn’t dirty. I’ve known him for years, and he’s one of the cleanest we’ve got.” Erwin said nothing. He waited for Erazo to calm himself down. Finally Miguel sat back down into the chair, his heaving chest finally beginning to slow down. The anger slowly escaping from his body. “All I’m saying is Rios was undercover for less than a fucking day and they found him out. There’s a leak in your shop. I’m not saying I know who it is, but there is no other reason that they could have figured it out. Listen Miguel, I don’t care why you were there last night, but I need to know if you saw anything that might help us.” “I doubt I saw anything that you didn’t,” Miguel started, but the look on Erwin’s face told him to continue. “I was parked across the lot, on the other side. I saw five of them roll up, all import bikes, all of their faces hidden under their shields. The bikes were all dressed in black, so were the riders. I saw the shots, and I saw Rios go down. I was already pulling away before you guys were even out of the parking lot.” Erwin didn’t say anything for a long time then. He simply looked forward, a pensive look on his face as he rubbed his hands together. For the first time since he had met him, Erwin actually looked human. Miguel realized that there was nothing further to say and got to his feet. He turned towards the door and reached for the handle. “Erazo,” Erwin called out from behind him. Miguel turned back towards the Unit Chief, his hand still on the door knob. But he said nothing. “I know that I can trust you, I’m going to need you soon. Can I count on you?” Miguel said nothing, he simply nodded his head and walked out the door. He had a lot to think about. 8 El Guerra’s phone was buzzing when he reached over to answer it. He said nothing, simply flipped the phone open. Like the person on the other end of the line, he knew that there could be only one person calling him. ”What the fuck do you think you’re doing killing Silva in the fucking prison hospital?!” “Calm yourself down. You couldn’t take care of our problem, so once again I had to.” ”They’re going to fucking know. First you fucking take out an FBI Agent, gunning him down in cold-blood and then you kill a rat in a fucking prison hospital?! “How I handle my affairs is of no business to you. If you would have kept up your end of our agreement, we wouldn’t be having this conversation. Need I remind you of that?” ”He had a fucking razor, I told him what the deal was. If you would look at your Prospects a little bit closer, instead of trying to recruit a fucking army of idiots, you’d avoid having fucking rats and FB-Fucking-I agents in your fucking ranks!” “Watch your tone with me. I don’t care about your sad stories, or your police ideals. El Diablo wants an army, so it is an army he’ll get. You couldn’t take care of the problems, so I did. Be happy Silva didn’t have anyone listen to his stories of police corruption. Now, if you’ll excuse me, I have business to attend to.” And with that El Guerra snapped the cell phone closed. Knowing the importance of that conversation, he decided not to take a risk. He removed the battery from the cell phone, and removed the SIM card before dropping it to the ground. He raised his leather boot and smashed the cell phone, the fragile plastic piece of technology shattering into hundreds of pieces. He looked to one of his brothers sitting in the chair across the room for him. “Perhaps we need to pay Captain Toms a visit. It appears as though Phillip may be wearing out his use to our cause.” 9 Tommy Chase was already waiting in the car when Miguel walked into the parking lot. He looked tired and had a very serious look on his face. Tommy wondered what could be wrong already. The day hadn’t even started yet. “What’s wrong brother?” Tommy asked as Miguel slipped into the passenger door. Miguel simply shot him a death-stare and maintained his silence. Tommy took that as a hint not to press the issue any further and instead put the car into gear and lurched it out of its parking space. There was an awkward silence between the two friends for a long time, before Miguel finally opened up. “Rios is dead,” he said flatly. “Agent Rios?!” Tommy asked, surprised. “Yeah,” Miguel started. “He was gunned down late last night by Los Aztecas. It’s pretty ugly in there.” “Holy fuck, my god. So what does this mean for us?” Tommy asked. “Same shit, different day, so far. Keep working the case, but it definitely sets their undercover aspirations back,” Miguel answered. Tommy let those words sink in. Rios, dead already. Undercover for barely a day and already discovered. Apparently these weren’t your regular redneck bikers, these guys were smart and they were well-informed. And they sure as hell didn’t like the law. The two partners drove in silence for several minutes, they were driving around aimlessly, but that didn’t seem to matter to either one of them at the moment. Finally, Miguel broke the silence again. “I was there.” “What?” Tommy asked. “I was there Tommy,” Miguel started. “I was blowing off some steam last night, I couldn’t go home right away, I was too pissed off. I spotted Rios and the Unit Chief on the freeway, so I decided to follow them.” “Miguel, why the fuck would you follow them?” Tommy asked. “I don’t know Tommy,” Miguel said, frustrated. He lowered his voice when he continued, speaking in barely a whisper. “Jealousy maybe, curiosity? You know I’ve had my eye on working bigger cases, doing undercover work. I just wanted to see some of it in action, clear my head by watching, maybe learn something, I have no fucking clue to be honest.” “So you fucking saw it? You fucking saw them kill Rios?” Tommy questioned. “Yeah, but that’s not the weirdest part, Erwin saw me,” Miguel answered. “What? Erwin? What the fuck did he say?” “He said he’s going to need my help,” Miguel answered. “Your help? What the fuck does the FBI want your help with?” Tommy continued. “I don’t know. He didn’t say. But that wasn’t all he said,” Miguel began. “He said he think there’s a leak in the department.” “A leak? A fucking leak? Dirty cops? What the fuck is this, a James Patterson novel?! Jesus fucking Christ Miguel, what the hell?” Tommy was nearly yelling now. “That’s not the best part,” Miguel said, staring at his partner. “He said he thinks it might be the Captain.”
