Clinton

20 May 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 05/20/11 I hate my wife’s brother. Hate ain’t really a strong enough word, though. I despise him. I loathe the little fucker. Hell, I simply can’t stand to be in the same hundred miles of the goddamned shithead. You know how sometimes there’s that one turd in the toilet bowl that you keep flushing down, but it just keeps popping it’s head up over and over again? Well, that’s Clinton Thorpe for you. He’s done it all. Stolen my money, my credit cards, even my identity. One time he even claimed he was me to get a car loan on a Saturn. A fucking Saturn. Are you kidding me? I thought we were done with the peckerhead last year, but I found out Lexi’s been secretly giving the punk money from our account. I know she figures I realize I’ve got more money than a Brunei sultan and she sees me piss it away like coke-hungry socialite celebutard, but I keep my eye on things. My uncle Kenji taught me that. Probably the only useful thing he ever gave me. I give her credit. She tried to be stealthy about it in case I ever got curious. Kept it arbitrary. A $3,567.24 withdrawl here, a $2,583.61 charge there. She could just say it was a clothes shopping spree or one of my bar tabs. Taking out large lump sums in even numbers with multiple zeroes, that’s for the assholes that get "Law and Order" episodes written about them. Didn’t hurt she’s still in Milan and I’m stuck in the states, so it’s not like I’m likely to be confronting her anytime soon. Smart girl. That is, until she got sloppy and deposited twenty-five grand into a bank account. I called her up and the chat turned to "umm’s" and "ahh’s", and from there came the shouting right before the full-blown argument. She told me Clinton needed the money, that he was in trouble. But Clinton’s always in trouble, so there went that. She begged me just to let it go, that it was family, and Clinton had no one left to turn to. I told her okay, and that I’d let it go. I only forgot to mention that I was still gonna beat the ever-loving shit out of that punk. ****** Everyone figured Clinton and I would get along swimmingly, and at first, we did. He was the black sheep of the family, something I’m all-too-familiar with. Lexi’s family warned me about him. There’s a reason he wasn’t at the wedding. There’s a reason there’s no family photos with him smiling with the rest of the Thorpe folks. There’s a reason Lexi’s old man’s blood pressure skyrockets at the mere mention of his name. But when he showed up on our doorstep a month after we were married, just looking for a place to crash for a few days, I said okay. We’d drink and play video games and cards and just talk shit. I’m not much in the way of having friends, so it was…nice. When the days turned into two weeks, I was still fine with it. When he stole five hundred bucks out of Lexi’s purse and then lied about it, I let it slide. When he invited his dumbass friends over, my patience started to wane. When he stole the Shelby Cobra from the garage and wrapped it around a telephone pole, and the cops caught him with a package of coke and a half-naked hooker in the passenger’s side, I went and bailed him out of jail. And then I broke his arm. That was just the first of many rides on the Clinton Thorpe Rollercoaster of Pain. The last time I saw the fucker, he tried to take over my house and I threatened to end him if he came anywhere near me or mine. Guess he didn’t get the message. It’s sorta funny when I thought about it. Clinton reminded me of my uncle Terry in a lot of regards. But I still liked Terry. ****** Clinton always claimed that he could disappear off the grid like the snap of a finger, so it came as no surprise that it only took a handful of calls to find out where the bastard was holed up. Hell, all anyone really had to do was look him up on a social networking site. God bless Mark Zuckerberg’s aversion to other people’s personal privacy. It was a short plane ride to Vegas, and on the jet my mind began to wander. I didn’t really care about heading to the east coast right afterward. I didn’t even want to really do any of that anymore. The only thing I was focusing on was finding that rat turd and dangling him over the roof of the tallest building I could find. I figured I’d sort out the rest of it afterward. On the cab ride, it was more of the same. When I pulled up to the address, I admit I was a bit surprised. It was a squalid little apartment complex, the kind of nasty place you’d expect SWAT teams to frequent. The guy wasn’t known for living the bohemian way, but it really didn’t matter. I knew what I was gonna do. I was going to put the fear of hell into his soul, and make sure that he never even spoke to a friend of a friend of a friend of Lexi’s. And if he didn’t listen after that? I’d break his fingers and his jaws. No communication, no nothin’. I’d make sure the bastard couldn’t even jerk it. I strolled up to the door, sweat pouring off my brow with each step. The anticipation to royally fuck him up had my heart pounding out of my chest. I wanted to tear him in half like we were in some ultra-violent video game. No victory. Only suffering. When I arrived at the door, it took me a few moments to knock on the door. Instead, I imagined what it was like inside. He and his buddies, drug paraphernalia and empty pizza boxes scattered about, arguing like fourteen-year-olds on Adderall. A cavalcade of skanks littered within the filth, a group so disgusting that even Bret Michaels’d throw up his stomach full of Disney mom snatch-hairs. Clinton walking around like he’s Hunter S. motherfucking Thompson, bantering about in that white boy ghetto talk bullshit the type of nonsense that no one in the world could possibly give a fuck about. I couldn’t wait to see the look on his smug face anymore. I knocked three times, and it was almost like I expected. There was Clinton, not even looking at me, in a sweat-stained wifebeater, god-knows-what-stained boxers, and a robe stolen straight from the Dude. "Yo, shut yo’ fuckin’ bitch-ass up and don’t be messin’ with my saved game," he barked to one of his fucko friends. When he finally turned and saw me, the reaction on his face nearly made me howl with joy. "Oh shi--" Slam! Front door right in my face. I was thinking of knocking again, but I thought better, and just kicked right through the front door, and whatever locking mechanism his poor door had stood no chance to my superior genetics and steady diet of weight-lifting and booze-drinking. It pays off to be big and strong. Clinton must have tripped over one of the bodies that were lying along the entrance-way, because just as I walked through he was scrambling toward the back of the apartment. Inside was the same collection of assholes I remember from the last time. K-5, his almost stereotypically big black bodyguard, and the one that backed down the last time he got in my face. Scribbles and Bugfuck, the Asian DJ and the Entourage looking guy. I forget which is which. I can’t remember who the rest were. It didn’t matter. What did matter was Clinton was back up and trying to get away. I didn’t really feel like chasing him, though, so I scooped the PS3 off the ground and whipped it into his back. The smash of electronics and his belabored grunt was music to my ears. And as I walked over to Clinton and ripped him up by the back of his neck, none of these pricks said a word. At least someone got the message. Clinton sobbed loudly, but after I screamed for him to shut the fuck up, he did exactly that. I addressed the group. "You losers got a car?" K-5 didn’t waste any time, reaching over to the table and grabbing a set. He tossed them my way. "Black Escalade," he boomed, with a hint of a quiver mixed in. I nodded. It’s not often you see a six-and-a-half foot four-hundred pound man of any kind obey to you. It was kinda nice. "Have a nice night," I said to no one in particular, and led Clinton out of the apartment. Clinton moaned for someone to help him, but no one even batted an eye. Not even that mouthy broad he had said shit, which was probably a first. Not much loyalty amongst trash. Clinton muttered all kinds of crap on the way to the ride, so when we got to the car I slammed his head into the side and that put him right to sleep. I stuffed him in the backseat, and, with no idea where the hell I was going or really what the hell the plan was, decided to take a little drive into the vast unknown. ****** It was at least a few hours of driving in some direction, where other cars were scarce and the endless desert reigned supreme. On the journey, the thought had occurred to me that I might have just completely overreacted and Lexi was eventually going to catch wind of this, if not maybe some sort of authority figures, and I was going to catch endless shit. Maybe I’d get fired from my job. Nah. I figured I wasn’t that lucky. At some point between the sun setting and my head swimming the road had turned to dirt. The bumpy ride seemed to have jarred Clinton from his slumber, which was good. Now was as good a time as any to have a chat with my brother-in-law. I pulled off the assumed path I was on and drove about a quarter-mile through the rugged terrain before coming to one helluva halt that sent Clinton flying, where he met his final destination slamming onto the backseat floor. He howled in pain, and I smiled once more. I leaped out, and my vigor had been renewed. I was gonna read this fucker the riot act. I was gonna make him piss his pants and cry for mommy. I yanked him from the back and brought him to the front of the car, where the only illumination for miles was from the headlights of the SUV and the shimmer of the moonlight. He crashed to the ground and leapt up, and then he spun around to face me. He offered a Clinton-esque greeting. "Yo, what the fuck is a matter witchu?!? Where the fuck is we, huh?!?" "Shut up," I growled. "We’re going to have a talk." He snarled. "Fuck you! I ain’t sayin’ shit ‘bout shit!" And then, something curious happened. Seeing him like this, covered in dirt and bodily fluid, huffing and puffing, the same bullshit boasting he always did, it was…different. I realized this was the first time I had gotten a look at him fully, and he wasn’t the same. He was haggard. He looked worn out. I actually pitied him. "Yo," he screamed. "You ain’t gonna say shit now, huh? What’d you come here to do, yo? Kill me? Bury me like some muthafuckin’ gangsta?" I paused. And I said the first thing that popped into my head. "I love your sister to death. More than I’ve ever loved anyone in the world. So why don’t you?" Confusion hung over him like a dark cloud. "Whatchu talkin’ ‘bout, bitch? I loves my sister, yo!" "Then why are you still treating her like shit," I queried. "Hell, why do you constantly piss all over your family?" He scoffed. "I don’t need them bitches, yo. I gots my shit covered. I’m a playah, a entrah-pah-nooah, yo, and--" I walked up to him mid-sentence and smacked him in the back of the head. He screamed. "Ahh!! ‘The fuck you do that for, yo?" "You’re not a kid like me. You’re a goddamned grown-ass man." I gave him a once-over. "I mean, how old are you, now? Twenty-nine? Thirty?" "Twenty-eight, yo," he quickly responded, like it was some sort of badge of honor. I shook my head and laughed. "You know, I’ve done a lotta stupid shit in my time," I said. "But you, man. Looking at you…" I couldn’t finish. He picked up for me. "I ain’t ‘bout to listen to you all up in my shit, yo!" He scoffed again. He always did that a lot. "You always actin’ like you better than everyone, just ‘cause yo’ moms and daddy left you big bank. My folks ain’t ever gave me shit, yo! Why doncha just give me a couple mil, yo! You don’t need all that money, anyways. But me? I’m a straight visionary, yo, I--" I smacked him in the back of the head again, and he screamed once more. "Yo, stop doin’ that, bitch!" "When you stop saying stupid shit, I will," I retorted. "But you’re still gonna answer my question." I slowed my voice. I raised my tone. I didn’t care about his level of fear. At that point, I really think I just wanted to know why. "Why do you treat them like shit?" He rubbed his face. He turned nervous. He wasn’t wannabe rap star Cali Cock Crusher, jackass extraordinaire. He was just Clinton Thorpe, broken-down kid. "I dunno, man," he admitted. "I was the only boy, yo. Moms and dads loved the three girls. I got the shit, yo. That’s how it goes." And…thud. That was his excuse. So I smacked him in the back of the head once again. "What the fuck, man?!?" He was amped up beyond belief. "Stop doing that, damnit!" "You fucked up your family life over…" I was almost incredulous to admit it. "…your pride?" "So what," he defiantly remarked. "Your stupid-ass sense of male pride at that," I said. "I mean, for chrissake, man, grow your ass up." "You jus’ a punk-ass kid, yo," Clinton barked. "You ain’t know shit ‘bout me." I turned around to leave. I was ready to leave him out here to die, and then probably go home and hang myself, I was so goddamned annoyed. He grabbed my shoulder and spun me ‘round. "I ain’t done, yo," he said, and I shoved him to the ground. "Yes," I said. "We are." "I...I..." He stuttered and muttered and I walked away. Finally, he cried it out. "I’m in trouble, yo!" I don’t know why I indulged him. But I did, and I listened. It was cliched to say the least, but it was Clinton. He was $50,000 in the hole to some dickbag drug dealer who gave Clinton weed to sell. Only Clinton decided it’d be much more fun to smoke it all instead. He got Lexi to front him half, and he figured he’d turn that around at the horse track for the rest, and maybe then some. You can probably figure out how that worked out. Tears streamed down his face as he spilled his guts. Whether they were real or not was up for debate, but, well…like I said. I pitied the peckerhead, and he was family. And as everyone knows, you really can’t ever choose your family. ****** It was a long ride back, and neither of us spoke a word. I’m sure it was torture for him. It was pretty much blissful for me. He slept half the way, which helped some, and by the time we got back into the Vegas city limits it turned to morning. We drove to the bank, and I took out a hundred grand. I figured the extra bit might prevent Clinton from getting killed or some stupid shit like that. Figured, but didn’t hold out hope. I didn’t bother to take him home to clean him up. I wanted him to look like he looked. Grimy. Sullen. Pathetic. I didn’t fuck around with the guy either. Here’s double, I told him, and you can keep the Escalade outside. You and Clinton are quits. I find out something went wrong and Clinton stuck by the agreement, it wouldn’t be pleasant. He said sure, but if Clinton fucked with his shit, he couldn’t make that promise. I agreed. We walked all the way to Clinton’s pad, and in typical Clinton fashion, he didn’t even offer a thanks, just more whines and complaints. I offered a few more slaps to the back of the head. After the fourth, he kept his head down the rest of the trek. I walked with him to the second floor apartment with the door still ajar. I followed him inside and saw that, outside of trash and broken pipes, the whole lot had cleared out. "Some friends," I couldn’t help to remark, and he just shrugged. He cleaned himself up, and I called us a cab to McCarran Airport. And on the ride there, something odd happened. I told him about Kenji. I told him about Terry. I told him just about everything I could think to tell him. And then I told him that he was going to go home to Huntington Beach. He was going to eat shit from his parents and family, and that he was going to straighten his wasted life up and make something of himself. He was going to apologize to Lexi, and pay back the money she gave him, even if it took him the rest of his natural fuckin’ life. And then I told him that if he didn’t, that someday, when me and Lexi started our family and we had a ton of rugrats running around the mansion and everything was truly perfect, I wasn’t going to allow the uncles like I had in their life. I don’t know if he truly understood. I don’t know if it meant anything. But I know I’d never seen Clinton look at anyone like he looked at me that day. I paid for his ticket - coach, of course - and I admit I was surprised that he didn’t look disappointed. I followed him through the obscene joke that is airport security, to his gate. I watched him board the plane. I watched the plane hit the tarmac. I made sure that plane make its ascent. I guess I just wanted to make truly sure. And then I found a bar at ten in the morning and had a shot of whiskey and a beer. Old habits and all that. But as I downed my brew and half-watched the morning edition of SportsCenter on the bar television, my mind wasn’t on making it to the east coast on time. It wasn’t on some stupid World title. It wasn’t even on the amazement of wondering whether or not all this actually happened, or if I really did say what I said to Clinton in that cab ride. I hadn’t seen Lexi in months. And goddamnit, did I miss her so. At that moment, nothing meant more to me than her. And I hadn’t the slightest idea what the hell to do.
