Alone

2 June 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06/02/10 For the longest time the lights had hypnotized him, but not this night. The familiar insect hum of neon penetrated the sounds of tires and feet on wet pavement; but his eyes no longer registered the dingy pastel rainbow cast upon the murk and reflected off the film of oil and fear. The Entertainment District was shrouded, perhaps by the permeating rain, or perhaps by the fog of judgment that lingered like the last shreds of a memory just passing recollection. His mind was shaped by this place; he had been birthed by a mother years before, but was reborn in these alleys, baptized in a river of liquid human vice and nursed on the breasts laid bare for all foolish enough to heed the siren's song. He walked with purpose but without destination; his feet knew where to go and he trusted them. The rain was falling stealthily, dutifully washing the streets of the previous night's sins. The night was hot and the road seemed to sublimate, hissing back toward the sky in a haze that promised to replenish the weeping clouds. The air was dense and he struggled to cut through the gloom, as the endless cycle of cleansing and reputrefying continued. It always rained. There in a window he saw two women, breasts exposed, with lace and leather, gyrating like turbines as if to power hell itself. Two windows down, he saw the same, but there were three. At the terminus of each bar or brothel or den or hovel was an alley, or more accurately a vacant black hole leading to God knows what. He dared not enter for he knew what lurked there; demons like himself, and he was loath to meet such a damnable thing. That darkness once comforted him like a warm blanket pulled over the eyes of a child to keep the monsters out. But the monster has no refuge from himself, and so Kimbusa has spent these years wandering, fleeing, his body unfettered and his mind assiduously consumed in itself, in hopes of outrunning the light of his own reflection. To look into another man's eyes would be to risk catching a glimpse of his own. And so he flees. And yet he found himself on this night back in Babylon, the only civilized place, which is to say built by the hand of man but not constrained by his laws, the only civilized place where Kimbusa could find himself surrounded exclusively by people of the same disposition. Eyes stayed fixed on the ground, voices lowered and collars raised, and should he make contact with another he would find that their eyes reflected nothing whatever. He could not remember how he got there. His last memory was of the stars above him as he rested on a hilltop, fading one by one behind the invisible shroud of a coming storm. Now the rain had come and he found himself transported back to this despondent place. His feet led him to the familiar set of shuttered windows guarding the Golden Dragon. It was a popular Yakuza hangout; the sight of ten fingers with ten tips intact was rare. Stationed at the solid wooden door was a single bouncer, clad in denim and chains, every inch of his skin painted like the canvas of some demented artist bent on recreating a vision of hell. He had a cigarette pressed between his lips, American by the smell of it. He kept his arms folded, his left hand tucked under his arm, though there was little doubt that his small finger was incomplete. The man watched him enter without interest. In a place like the Golden Dragon, even his considerable size was unspectacular and not worthy of notice. It was the average ones, with their neat suits and refined manners, most deserving of awe. For the geeks thrived naturally in this place, but those who seemed normal on the exterior certainly harbored the most sinister intent. Normally he preferred the streets, the alleys, but the Golden Dragon was unique among the clubs of the Entertainment District. It had all the usual features, but was meticulously constructed by some magnificent recluse to refuse access to the human eye. There was hardly any light in the room, yet everything seemed to cast long, deep shadows in a thick smog of cigar smoke, drawing everything in silhouette, permitting escape only to outlines and specters. Voices, full of mirth and suggesting no amount of fear, rose boldly from those hidden faces, disembodied as if uttered by ghouls relieved to be free of the constraints of their earthly shackles. He might have liked the place more had he any interest in camaraderie. Curiously his feet began to lead him to a table. It was away from the others, far enough away from the cacophony of voices and deep enough in the shadows to suggest he had once again found himself on another plane. "Sit down." The voice mutters from the booth, and he can hear the clink of ice floating in bourbon. He sits. "Do you know why you're here?" says the voice. He shakes his head, squinting in the dark and the haze, trying to make out the face of the man across from him. The smog is displaced as the voice exhales deeply. "Neither do I. Yet here we are. What do you make of that, Kimbusa?" He winces at the name. "I am called Kurokame. Kimbusa is dead." "Not what I've heard," says the voice. Another drink. "I've been listening, in this place and many like it. And from what I can tell, Kimbusa is very much alive." "Who are you?" he asked, though his gut already knew. Steven Caldera leaned forward out of the shadow. His face was wan and poorly groomed, and his eyes, normally a brilliant shade of blue, were grey and lifeless. "I might ask the same question of you. Though I believe we'll find the answers to our questions to be the same." "You are not real." He had begun to suspect that he was dreaming, yet in that colorless, featureless place there was no way to tell. "Not in the sense that you mean," said Caldera. He sipped again from a seemingly infinite glass. "But I don't believe reality is relevant to our present conversation." Kurokame shifted in his seat, or as best he could. A wave of laughter rose from across the room from somewhere behind the veil. "Eight years since I left this place and found you, you have followed me. Why have you come?" "You say that as if I have a choice in the matter." "I do not know why I was brought here." "I got here the same way you did." There was a pause. He could feel his chest begin to ache from the smoke. He stifled a cough. "I have no patience for riddles. State your business." Caldera cleared his throat, his attention briefly wandering toward the low hum of voices across the room. "We both hear the voices, Kimbusa. I know you hear them the same way I do. I know how they make you feel." "You know nothing." "I see the fear in your eyes. Every single voice in this room is like a spider crawling up your spine. I see your skin crawl. I see your muscles tense, ready to run away at the first opportunity. And I understand." "You understand nothing." "Neither of us can walk into a room without getting noticed. For you it's a curse, something you've been running from your entire life. For me, it's my goal, something I've been working toward for all of mine. And yet here we are, the two of us, sharing a drink in the Golden Dragon." "I do not drink." "I'm happy to drink your share." The two stared at one another unblinking. Kimbusa grunted and tried to move, but found himself fixed in the seat. For a moment he thought his girth had trapped him behind the table, but in fact he found his feet simply unwilling to depart. "Don't bother leaving. Neither one of us is in control of this conversation. May as well see it through." "State your business." "You wondered why I follow you. I've often wondered why you haunt me, and believe me, you do. The revelation came to me in this place just now." "Yes?" "The pain you feel when you are forced to look into another's eyes, even mine. I've always felt it, Kimbusa. At least, I've been aware of it. I fight it, I ignore it, but it's always there, right there in me. Just like that relentless desire to lead the crowd exists in you, though you are powerless to assuage it." The revelation was passed to him. "We are the same." "There you go. Isn't that something? Different sides to the same coin, as it were. " He raised his glass. "To our very good health." He shook his head. "This place, this is mine. You do not belong here." "And yet here I am. Turns out, this is home to both of us, Kimbusa. Here, among these people, these powerful, terrible people, two unmissable titans seated just out of sight. But while you chose to hide in the darkness of these alleys, I chose to hide in the brightness of the public eye. You are all my fear, all my pain, wrapped up in a form so large that it must cower in the shadows, untended, until the end of time." "Or perhaps you are my courage, my ambition, wrapped up in a form so arrogant and ambitious as to be completely unmistakable. " "I like my way better." They grew quiet. Whether they had actually met, two finite bodies, in some physical place no longer concerned him. Had that place ever truly existed? Had this colorless, lifeless dreamscape every truly existed, or was he simply the excrement of an ego so large that its insecurities were banished to some alternate plane? "No, you're very much real," Caldera said. "Don't get all metaphysical on me." Kimbusa shook his head. "We may have shared a link," he said, "but I reject it." "Ditto." "So what, then?" "I don't know how this split came about. You were clearly conjured to be the incompatible parts of me. But I do know there is only one thing to do about it." "Yes?" Caldera took another drink and stood. He adjusted his tieless collar and buttoned his jacket. "Kimbusa, my friend, one of us must die." A crack of thunder exploded overhead, and his eyes opened to a blurred electric sky. The rain was falling hard and he was soaked to the bone. He had drifted on the hilltop and the storm had consumed him. He rolled to his feet, bewildered, and made for his hut. He entered and a burst of violent wind slammed the door behind him. He looked around. It was his hidden home, very much as he left it, very much tangible. Kurokame arrived at the conclusion that he was very much real. Yet the truth of Caldera's words seemed difficult to deny. Their lives had become entangled because of some diabolical symmetry, and as long as each lived the other could not be satisfied. He felt the water run down his face. Somewhere halfway around the world that same rain had fallen upon Steven Caldera, then risen to the sky and circled the globe to fall on him. No matter how far he fled, no matter how remote his location, he would always be touched by Steven Caldera. Perhaps there would come a day when they would meet again in the flesh. Perhaps they were diametrically opposed, one fated to destroy the other for the sake of preserving his way of life. For now, he helped himself to some cold rice, and with his eyes open, breathed easy at the knowledge that he was alone.
