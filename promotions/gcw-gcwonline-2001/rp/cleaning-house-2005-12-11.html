Cleaning House

11 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12-11-05 Jayce Bradley had parked the white Chrysler Sebring he had rented earlier in the day in front of the house he once considered his home, staring out the window at the weathered white paint that had chipped off over the years since he had last been there. From side to side the roof was steep, with a lower less inclined roof at the front; over the doorway. The trim and window shutters had once been a vibrant royal blue, now weather-beaten and dull like the rest of the place. Time taking it’s toll, is what he thought to himself as he took the time to reminisce about the way it used to look. Jayce grabbed the bag of burgers and fries that laid on the passenger seat, then reached for the large drink cup that had tilted over from the cup holder and spilled out all over the passenger side floor board; soaking the light beige carpet a light shade of red. He stepped out of the vehicle, closing the door behind him and pressing the keyless entry button on the small remote that dangled from the keys. As he made his way up the walkway, Jayce noticed the “For Sale” sign that had been plunged into the uncut grass. Three quarters of a century with one family. Three generations having been born in the home. So much had happened within the walls, moments of bliss as well as moments of distress. Jayce pondered about who would consider buying the place, an old house. If their futures would follow down the same path as his family’s, or travel down a different road of their own. Jayce pulled a set of keys from the pocket of his faded blue denim jeans, fumbling around to find the one that fit the lock on the door. He found it, and with a twist of the key he soon found himself looking over what was left of his childhood. The large mirror; with a crack in the lower right hand corner where a chunk of mirror is missing, that he found himself standing across from, held more than just his own reflection. The reflection held more than just the faded blue denim jeans, black lace-up combat boots, and plain white tee shirt of a child lost. Jayce could see more than just dirty blonde hair, crystalline blue eyes, and white flesh. He saw the reflection of his past, a time when things were much easier. =?=?=?=?= =?=?=?=?= “Come back here you little shit,” was shouted out, as two young boys ran down the hard wood floored hallway. The smaller of the two had something clutched tightly in his palm, as the socks on his feet caused him to slide effortlessly across the glossy wood flooring as he tried to slow down. The sound of glass breaking was enough to cause one of the children to stop shouting, while the contained laughter of the other soon turned to the whimpers of pain. “You alright, Jayce?” Was what the older child asked as he knelt down beside the other, lifting him into his arms to comfort him. That’s when the younger of the two, Jayce Bradley, started to cry uncontrollably. Shards of mirrored glass with a slightly red tint were scattered on the flooring, tinted with the blood of the younger Bradley sibling. “You’re going to be okay, Jayce,” Dominic Bradley reassured, running his hand over the slight gash in Jayce’s forehead. That was the way Dominic had always been. Protective. Jayce considered him more of a father figure than his actual Father. Not that his father was not in his life, but because he worked long hours and was usually gone before he woke up for school and did not return until long after he had been put down for bed. Jason Bradley worked hard to make sure his family was provided for, missing out on the every day activities that other fathers he knew would talk about in a bragging sort of way. Being the sole provider for the family, mainly because of his own selfish pride, he could not afford the luxuries that those other men had. In a way though, he envied them. “Mom,” Dominic yelled out, pulling off the white tee shirt with the G.I. Joe logo that he was wearing and pressing it against the open wound on his brother’s head. With his brother’s assistance, Jayce slowly rose to his feet, still crying like a ten year old girl. Of course, he looked to be about ten as well, so take that as you will. “What did you boys break now,” was what their mother said as she came around the corner from the kitchen. Holding a plate in one hand and a pastel blue towel in the other, her blue eyes filled with panic when she saw her youngest son bleeding from the skull. Porcelain shards scattered across the floor, as Amelia Bradley dropped the plate she held in her hand and ran over to her child. Pulling him away from Dominic, panic overtaking her senses, she screamed. “What did you do to him, you bastard!”” “I didn’t?” Pulling the tee shirt away from Jayce’s head long enough to assess the extent of the injury, she interrupted her eldest son by backhanding him and sending him down to the floor in a heap. Grabbing a set of keys off of a hook next to the door, followed by grabbing the purse that was on the table closest to the same door, she looked back. “That’s nothing compared to the beating your father’s going to give you when he gets home from work.” Jayce glanced back, his vision blurred by the tears that welled up in the corner of his eyes. He saw his brother holding his palm against his cheek, holding back the tears that would soon overpower and make their escape. Jayce managed to mouth the words, “I’m sorry,” as his mother hurried him out the door. =?=?=?=?= =?=?=?=?= Jayce had managed to down his food within minutes, eager to get the house cleaned up, so he could hopefully rid himself of the burden left behind by the recent death of his father. If he was lucky, maybe he could get a small chunk of change to put down on a place of his own. Nothing fancy, just somewhere he could put his feet up on the table and call home. Of course, that is when the reality of his life kicked him square in the nuts and brought him back to reality. For one thing, Jayce was a professional wrestler. He moved around so much, traveling from city to city for each week’s show, that the home would just sit there. Alone. Now what would be the point in paying for something that you probably will not use any time soon. It’s not like Jayce had a family to support. Fumbling through one of the boxes he had brought down from the attic, “The GCW Cruiserweight champion.” The words just seem to flow from his lips for no reason at all, his attention directed on the contents of the box. “Has a nice ring to it if you ask me. You see, I knew that I could prove myself title worthy if I was given a chance, but the higher ups chose to hold me back instead, deciding to go with the blue chipper.” Jayce’s face contorts as he continues, “They decided to see what Varsity could do’Football. Well, it turned out to be as successful as his pro career. Which is also nowhere to be found.” A slight grin on his face, Jayce dusts off an old photo. “But I understand what they were doing. They wanted someone marketable to be their champion, waving the GCW flag with pride and honor whenever he was asked.” Shaking his head, Jayce chuckles softly. “That’s not who I am.” “I’m the son of a bitch that will march into that ring, and pull out all of the stops to get what it is that needs to be done’done. Caldera and his cronies are starting to figure that out, and that could only mean good things from here on out. Because I will have no problem carrying this GCW flag, planting it firmly into the backside of any poor sap that happens to be tossed in my path.” With a grin, Jayce allows the last few words to escape his lips. “Which just so happens to be you, Benjamin.” Jayce shakes his head as the scene slowly fades away, leaving Jayce to continue on alone. Cleaning house if you will.
