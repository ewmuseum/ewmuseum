The New Apartment (late, don't use for judging)

19 November 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/19/11 The apartment is sparse. There are some pictures, a TV and a video game system decorate the living room. The couch looks to be second hand, something you would expect for someone that just got out of college. At one time, this young man foolishly lived the high life in Beverly Hills, thinking that the money that he was bringing in from his former life was enough to sustain a lifestyle. It wasn’t, and now he lives here, in near squalor as he attempts to rebuild the relationships that got him there. He got back with the man that broke him into the business, working out three days a week at his home, and an additional two days at a gym that happened to have a boxing ring set up. He knew that it wasn’t glamorous, he knew that it wasn’t the way many wrestlers learned the ropes, but he was already there once. He had been in a main event with his previous company, held a title. He thought that would bring him prosperity when he came back from his injury. How humbling it was for him when he tried to get back into SCCW to find out that it was a victim of the economic downturn that has affected many businesses. A few contacts that he had made pointed him to Global Championship Wrestling, but there was no way that he was going to get as good of a deal there as he did in SCCW. But for Alexander Searle, he knew that he had to take it. Under contract for two months with the company, he finally worked his way into a new training setup. Still being mentored by Jason Ryan and Michael Durant, he was getting to work with some independent wrestlers to get his timing back. He was working with some of the road agents to find the character he needed to bring into the company. They all took a look at him and said, “Son, there are many in this business that are successful without a gimmick. You should try that.” At least he can afford the roof over his head. He looks out his window, looking at a part of Cleveland that no one ever sees anymore. Funny that he would find himself living in the same city that he would be pulled out of the crowd from. It’s late, but he can’t sleep. He knows that it’s only a matter of time before he has to show what he can do in front of a GCW crowd. Xander takes a look in his refrigerator, seeing a couple of store bought sandwiches and a couple of cans of store brand soda. He pulls one of the cans out and opens it. The taste was slightly bitter, but it was something he was getting used to. On the dinette set that he picked up at the local Salvation Army sits a box that has the markings of Jason Ryan’s “Damage Fight Gear” emblem. Inside were a pair of black and non-descript Vale-Tudo trunks, knee pads and boots. The note that was in the box simply reads, “You want a design on these, you’re going to have to earn them. Until that time, good luck.” Luck, that’s something that he definitely needed. He thinks to himself that it will only take time before he can get out of this place. He chose this place because it was sufficiently enough between the Western Suburbs of Detroit and GCW’s World Headquarters in Columbus that he could get in time at either facility that he trains at. He’s restless most nights. He sees the mementos from his first time in the wrestling ring. The replica SCCW Livewire Title sits in a display case with a picture of him holding the title after Sin on Spike 3. A program from the first event he ever wrestled in. A photo of Jason Ryan, Kirsten Pendleton and himself taken in that old warehouse that he originally trained in. They were memories, nothing more, nothing less. He finishes off his soda, and throws the empty into a trash can. In the back of his mind, he hears the people from home telling him that he was the best basketball player that they had ever seen, and that it was a shame that there wasn’t a Division 1 school willing to take a chance on him. He hears the people from home later telling him that it was a mistake to throw away college to become a pro wrestler. He no longer wanted to listen to them. He made his choice, he was the one that was going to have to live with the consequences. Not them. None of them. He continues to walk around the apartment and finally sits down on his thrift store couch. He opens the laptop that was sitting on the coffee table across from it and starts to read some articles on the web. He even ventures to open the Xander Searle Facebook page. It hadn’t been updated since he went on the shelf with a knee injury. He didn’t want people to know that he went from the Penthouse to the proverbial Outhouse. He wasn’t proud of how far he had fallen. He looked at the messages that were left for him over the months. Friends asking where he’s been. Family wondering how he was doing. The occasional junk message. He looks at the status updates that he hadn’t seen. He realizes how much of his life that he didn’t allow himself to partake because of his personal displeasure for where he was in life. But this was where he was. He continues to read. He sees pictures of his mom and sisters. He thinks to himself that he should try and visit them now that he’s somewhat settled. He sees stories that were posted by the Browns and Indians. He notices the time, it’s late. He tries to sleep again, but is unable. It’s always tough trying getting used to a new place to live. The sounds, smells and sights are different. They seem a little unsettling, but he’s starting to get used to it. Again, unable to sleep, the man that once called himself “Simply Better” gets up and walks over to the laptop. He looks and finds that someone had sent him a message. He opens the message up and is surprised to see who it’s from. He types a response to the message. He gets up and heads back to his bedroom, hoping to get some sleep. It’s three in the afternoon and Xander finds himself in a diner near the Quicken Loans arena. He takes a sip from the water that sits in front of him as he patiently waits. He’s nervous, but he doesn’t show it. He is able to keep his composure as he looks over the menu again. He pulls out his wallet to see how much he has with him, just in case. A couple of bills, but not enough to cover a substantial meal for two. He hopes that he won’t have to cover a second meal. He takes a look at the menu, thinking to himself what he’s going to order to eat. He doesn’t study too hard, as he sees the person that he was supposed to meet enter the diner. She walks over, her flowing blonde locks perfectly styled. The sunglasses that adorns her face aren’t so dark as to keep you from seeing her eyes. She walks over and sits across from Xander. “I’m sorry to keep you waiting.” she says in an accent that could best be described as a mix between Manhattan Socialite and London Royalty. “So, how have you been?” Xander asks. “I’ve been well. How about you?” she responded. “Let’s see, I’m broke. I mean, I can barely afford a hamburger here, I drive a 93 Civic and I’m back wrestling on a shitty probationary contract.” “I’m sorry, you don’t deserve this.” The concern in her voice was genuine. She realizes that she was a part of what turned the happy-go-lucky “Paul Searle” into the one time egomaniacal Xander Searle. But she didn’t see either one of them in front of her. She saw a young man that looked dejected, defeated. “You can come back from this, Paul.” It was the first time in a long time that anyone had called him Paul. It was almost reassuring, but not enough to make him really feel like he belonged in the business. “Thank you.” “Sweetie, let me take care of this for you. You seem like you are having a tough time. The least I can do is help you out.” She pulls a wallet out of her purse and pulls out some cash. “Order what you want.” Another night in the apartment, another sleepless night. He walks around clearing his head. The box that was on his dinette set was replaced with a salt and pepper shaker. The framed title belt was now hung up on his wall, as was the picture of him, Jason and Kirsten. He walks over to his laptop and updates his Facebook profile. Finally, he gets back up and walks over to the refrigerator. The glow from the light brightens his face. “Xander, can you bring me a bottle of water?” Xander smiles as he reaches in and pulls out a couple of bottles of water. “Kirsten, anything else you want me to bring?” “Just you.”
