SCANDAL PART VII: LIFE AND DEATH

25 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 25.01.09 McKail took another generous mouthful of Jack Daniels and gazed out at the breathtaking Arizona sunset, as the cool evening breeze tickled his skin. With a sigh, he climbed over the protective railings of the bridge and perched himself on its very edge, his legs dangling over the side. Glancing downward at the fifty foot drop into the lake below, he smiled a manic smile. Aside from in the ring, he had to admit that this was where he felt most at home. On this age-old bridge, surrounded by the natural beauty afforded him and on a off-beat track with nothing or nobody around for five miles. Perhaps if anyone were to see him and recognise his face, along with the half-emptied bottle of Jack Daniels, they may think that he intended to jump from the damned thing and end his own life. And why wouldn’t they? His wife, Mary, had died some time ago, his time in the ring was numbered, his golden years within the industry behind him, he was on a downward spiral that showed him new lows each and every day and what’s more he’d closed himself off from the world, so that there was no hope of anybody guiding him away from his slump. McKail was ashamed to acknowledge it, but he’d contemplated the notion a number of times in the past. Especially those first few weeks after Mary died and it was she who made sure he never went or would ever go through with it. She made him promise her on his deathbed to live his life when she was gone and to accomplish all of those things he’d never had the strength nor the drive to do before - namely, have his name etched into the wrestling industry’s hall of fame. He’d lived by that promise ever since and thought of it every time the thought of suicide reared it’s ugly head. Perhaps then, it was fate that NC-17 was booked here, in Phoenix. The way McKail’s life was going at the moment, it seemed right about time for another suicide contemplation. The match-fixing scandal had been hanging over his head for the last couple of months had turned him into pretty much the most hated man in wrestling, by both the fans, staff and the other boys in the locker room. On the face of it, it shouldn’t bother him. He doesn’t care what other people think about him or what he does, but this time its different. All of this anger and hatred directed toward him is misplaced, based on a lie that was all too believable. His crime was being an anti-social bastard, framed by those who wanted to make an illegal profit on gambling. Except, these men didn’t like to lose their money and so decided to take matters into their own hands. McKail reckoned they’d probably worked the low-level independent shows for years, but got greedy and set their sights higher. Didn’t take too much thinking power for them to choose McKail. The way he acted on screen and in real life, those bastards figured he wouldn’t mind taking a dive for a healthy payout. They didn’t know him at all and never would. McKail’s attention was diverted from his nostalgic thoughts - and his bottle of Jack Daniels - by the sound of a car pulling up at the far side of the bridge. He sighed. Some do-gooder probably saw his car and thought the worst. Probably take him the better part of fifteen minutes to convince the bastard that he had no intention of jumping. He took a swig of his Jack Daniels and chuckled at the irony of it all. He waited for a while, but nobody came. Then, in the distance, he heard somebody sobbing faintly and upon refocusing his eyes, saw a curly-haired woman climb over the railings of the bridge and onto the ledge not a hundred yards from him. McKail eyed up the woman carefully wondering just what in the hell was going on. It didn’t take him all that long to figure out: she was going to jump. In her emotion-fuelled haze she hadn’t even notice him nor his car. He sighed and shook his head. “Son of a bitch,” he muttered under his breath, rising to his feet. He directed himself towards the woman, walking along the narrow edge of the bridge taking a mouthful of Jack Daniels and glancing downward at the murky depths of the lake below. The woman’s head jerked around upon hearing his approach and backed away a little. The woman wasn’t overly attractive to look at. Her face was plain, a little too round and it didn’t help that her make-up was streaming down her face and she was almost certainly terrified. She clung onto the inner railings of the bridge like a child clinging to its mother. “Wh-wha-what do you want?” She questioned, he voice quivering. “Nothing,” he replied. He made sure to keep a safe distance; last thing he wanted to was to make a sudden movement and the woman to take an unexpected dive into the grim lake below. The woman developed a puzzled expression. “Wh--wha-what are you doing here?” McKail glanced around at the ever-fading sunset in the distance. “Enjoying the view.” He took another mouthful of Jack Daniels and grinned. “And getting drunk.” “Are you going to jump too?” She asked him, eying him warily. He shook his head. “Hell no. Like I said, I’m here for the view and the liquor.” She was quiet for a time, unsure of what to say or do. “Are you going to try and stop me from jumping?” “Do you want me to?” He asked her. She shook her head weakly, her tears returning. “No. I have to do this. My life’s gone to hell and I can’t take it anymore!” “Cry me a river,” McKail muttered, taking another mouthful of his bourbon. “What?” She asked him, a little outraged. “You don’t know me! You have no idea what I’ve been through!” “You’re right,” McKail told her, “I don’t. And I don’t care.” “You don’t care?” He shook his head. “Whatever the hell you’ve been through, it pales in comparison to some.” She shook her head. “Pales in comparison” Fuck you! My marriage is a sham! My husband has taken all of our savings and taken off with the local barmaid! I’m all alone now! What else can I do?” “Get a fuckin” grip of yourself for a start,” McKail told her. “Fuck you!” She screamed. “You don’t know what it’s like to have your world crumble around your ears! You haven’t the first fucking clue!” “The first time’s a kicker ain’t it?” McKail replied. “But if you end it all now, you ain’t gonna be around to realise the second time your life turns to shit it gets easier to cope with.” “What the hell are you talking about?” She questioned. “You think this is a joke?” McKail raised a speculative eyebrow. “You see me laughin?”” “So what’re you saying to me?” She asked him. “Don’t jump this time, because the next time my life gets fucked up I’ll be able to deal with it better?” McKail nodded. “That’s about it, yeah.” “If you’re trying to talk me down, you’re doing a hell of a job it!” “Whoever said I was trying to talking you down?” She shook her head, bemused. “So, what” You’re trying to talk me into it then? What kind of sick bastard are you?” “Most’ll tell ya I’m the sickest kind,” McKail told her, ? but that ain’t anywhere near the truth. At least, that’s what I tell myself.” He took another swig of the Jack Daniels. “And I ain’t tryin” to convince you of doing nothing?, I’m just telling ya how it is.” “And you know this from experience then?” She asked him. “Or is all of this some elaborate lie” Mind games to get me to do what you want to do?” She paused, fire in her eyes. “Well, ain’t playing anymore mind games with you or anyone else! That bastard of husband played them to get me to be the kind of wife he wanted me to be and when he got bored, he just discarded me like a used tissue! I won’t take it anymore! I won’t!” McKail stared at her blankly. “Done?” She didn’t answer him, she was too busy weeping and glaring over the edge at what he fate could possibly be. “Do I look like the type of man who plays mind games with folk?” McKail asked her. “Hell, I’m so inebriated most of the time I barely got a mind to play games with.” “Then why?” She asked. “Why all of this” If you’re trying to stop from killing myself, why not try and drag me from the bridge or go call the cops or something?” “Like I told you before,” McKail responded, “I ain’t tryin” to save ya. I’m just givin? ya the benefit of my experience. If you’re lookin? for someone to tell ya that it’s not worth it and everything’s gonna be okay, then you’re all outta luck. ? She looked at him for a moment in silence and then cast her eyes upon his bottle of Jack Daniels. “Fell like sharing you’re bourbon?” McKail shook his head. “Nope.” He handed her the bottle. “But who the hell am I to turn down a woman’s dying wish?” She unscrewed the cap and took a mouthful, wincing as the bitter liquid raced down her throat. “Man, how can you drink this shit?” McKail took the bottle back from her and guzzled a little more for good measure. “Too easily.” “Well,” she said, “thanks.” With that she turned from him, looking out onto the lake below and the sweet Arizona mountains in the distance. Ever so slowly, she edged forward, allowing gravity to help her on her way. McKail reacted quickly and held her waist before she could fall to her demise. He slammed her backwards against the bridge and cursed under his breath. “I thought you weren’t planning on saving me, hero?” The woman asked, wincing in pain from the sudden impact with bridge’s iron girder exterior. “What changed your mind?” “I thought you’d be smart enough to realise what you were going to do was stupid,” McKail told her. “Guess I should never underestimate the power of idiocy, huh?” The woman shook her head and sighed. “Like I said, mind games. I’m tired of it all.” “So sleep on it and decide whether you want to kill yourself tomorrow,” McKail told her. “I won’t be here to stop you, if you do decide to do it again.” “I won’t have the nerve to try it again,” she replied. “Besides, when I wake up tomorrow, the world be the same shitty place as is today and it was yesterday.” “I don’t doubt it,” McKail agreed, “but in a couple of months your perspective will change some. You’ll start to move on with your life and you life will slowly start to fall back inline. You’ll hate yourself for ever lettin” it get this far, but you’ll be alive. ? The woman nodded a little and McKail wished he could believe his own words, but he didn’t. The only reason he was still alive was because of Mary and he was too damn honourable to break his promise to her. Yeah, right. Honourable. With that, the woman turned to leave and McKail did like wise. All of a sudden, this place didn’t seem all that great anymore. “I’m sorry,” the woman whispered. McKail whirled around. “What?” But it was too late. This time the woman jumped from the edge of the bridge and there wasn’t a damned thing McKail could do about it. McKail glared down at her as she fell gracefully into the distance, wondering whether “the easy way out” was all that easy at all. “Never underestimate the power of idiocy,” he muttered, heading back towards his car to fetch his mobile phone.
