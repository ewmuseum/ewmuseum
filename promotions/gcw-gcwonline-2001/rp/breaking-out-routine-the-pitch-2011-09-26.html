Breaking Out: Routine (The Pitch)

26 September 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/26/11 Part I: Heads/Away The black SUV cut across two lanes of traffic, coming to a halt between two pools of yellow-orange streetlight. The vehicle's driver drummed her hands nervously on the wheel as her eyes slid up to the rearview mirror. She reminded herself to stay calm. They'd be here -- probably were here already, lurking somewhere until they could be sure no one tailed her in. It had been almost two years since Helena Maddox had had the misfortune of being the next detective up on the board when a body was found floating in the hot tub of a model home huddled against a golf course. When she took the case, she thought the only oddities would be the British man they found inside with a bump on his head and no memory of what had happened, or perhaps the victim's odd purple hair. That was before the body was stolen from the morgue, the thieves leaving the elderly coroner in a coma he never recovered from. That was before her prime suspect turned out to be a professional wrestler of all things, a woman with a list of secrets as long as her arm and a circle of friends that could at best be called "unsavory" and at worst were downright sociopathic. Not to mention a live-in girlfriend devoted enough to lie for her who happened to be Maddox's next door neighbor. That was before she got talked into working with said prime suspect, before she'd stood side-by-side with someone she knew to be a killer, just not the one they were looking for. Before a shootout on the freeway left her with a dead killer and a case that still gave her nightmares. That was before someone framed her for bribery and got her drummed out of the force, before she spent six months on the run. Now this was the only job she could find, working as an errand girl for an LA-based private investigation firm, working for a man she didn't even like, doing things she barely understood for causes that were only occasionally ethical. Sometimes, she wished she'd just called in sick that night. Her instructions were simple; they just didn't make any sense. She rented a car and drove to a series of locations, so obtuse she wouldn't have found them without the GPS. She waited there, never more than seven minutes, and she'd been to three before she came here, twenty-one minutes spent on edge with nothing to show for it. She still couldn't shake the feeling that she was being watched, and every movement and every stray sound was just another specter coming to take her away. It wasn't as scary four or five minutes in, however, and Helena had just fired off a bitchy text message to her employer when a shadow split off from the surrounding darkness. Maddox didn't hear it or even see it; one moment she was alone and the next, the rear side passenger door swung open and a lithe brunette in jeans and a light-colored tee all but dove inside. "Drive!" the woman hissed, keeping low across the back bench. Helena was too flustered not to comply, and the SUV lurched away from the curb, jerking out into the street. "Easy..." The passenger sat up, peering over her shoulder to look back down the street. "Just a simple SDR here and we'll be home free." "'SDR'?" Maddox's eyebrows lifted as she strove to get a proper look in the rearview. A set of oncoming headlights illuminated the woman's shirt: it was yellow with a circular, beetle-like symbol across the front (something from Green Lantern, if her memory served her correctly). "Surveillance detection--" The woman sighed, spitting out a set of remarkably realistic fake teeth which came complete with braces. "Just make sure we're not being followed, okay..." She blinked in surprise. "Detective?" Helena narrowed her eyes, and she didn't recognize her guest until the woman pulled off the shaggy brown wig. "Pierce?" Alexandra Pierce plucked a prosthetic mole from her cheek, a wry smile staining her lips. "In the flesh." She glanced down, rubbing her thumb across a tattoo on the inside of her wrist, but the ink didn't smudge. "As it were." She cleared her throat. "Did Jonas pack a bag?" "It's in the back." Helena's hands tightened on the wheel, knuckles whitening. "And before you ask, I didn't look inside." "I wasn't going to." She lifted her eyes to the mirror, catching a glimpse of Pierce's jeans-clad backside (and a bit of hot pink thong) as Alex lunged for a beaten olive gym bag, and she quickly averted her eyes. "Stryker didn't tell me I was playing getaway driver. What did you do?" "Do you really want to know?" The question was accompanied by a brief raise of her brows, but Alex didn't look up. "Probably not." She sighed, eyes falling to the side view mirror. "We're clean. Where are we headed?" "There's an IHOP at Sepulveda and Manchester, northeast of LAX." Pierce unzipped the bag only long enough to tuck away the accoutrements she'd stripped off. "Keep your eyes open, though -- they might be working in shifts." Helena simply nodded, jaw tight, and it was a long time before either of them spoke, but it was Maddox that broke. "You know... they framed me for taking bribes. Got me tossed off the force, three generations of police with their reputations ruined. Because of you." "Who is 'they'? Who framed you?" "I don't know. The people you work for, I'm guessing. I had to be getting too close. That's what happens when people almost figure this shit out, right?" "No." Pierce finally looked up, catching Helena's gaze in the mirror with a dead-eyed glare. "If you'd gotten that close, you'd leave your home some morning and the top of your head would come off, or you'd be on the subway and catch a knife between the third and fourth--" "Stop," Helena growled. "Just stop, okay? I get it. You're a badass and you've killed people and I should be scared of you." "You don't get it. That's what happened to a civilian who crossed the Order. I know because I've been on the other end of it. They don't ruin people's lives for sport -- that turns nuisances into enemies." "Then who does? My bank accounts, the money in my locker, secret stashes buried in my yard..." Pierce was quiet for a long beat, busying her hands with the bag. "I don't know," she said quietly. "I really don't." "You're lying." Maddox touched her foot to the brake pedal as she pulled into the parking lot. "I still have a nose for that shit, you know. You didn't take that with the rest of it." "I didn't take anything from you, Detective." Alex pulled the door open before the car had come to a complete stop. "They took from both of us." She shouldered the bag, turning towards the car. "Get out." Helena's eyebrows lifted. "What? Stryker told me to drive. He didn't say anything else about getting involved in your shit." "We're not in the clear yet. They can track the car." "Who can track the car? Who the fuck are you running from?" "There isn't time to explain. Get out of the car." "No one was following us!" She still climbed out of the car, slamming the door behind her. "You're being ridiculous." Alex wheeled on Maddox, her eyes narrowing. "Keep your fucking voice down," she growled. A single, stabbing stride obliterated the distance between them. "You want to blame me for every single rotten thing that's happened to you since we met? Fine, do that, then. I think it's fucking stupid, but what do I know? But we are not clear, and we are not home free -- not until I say we are, do you understand me? So spare me the petty sniping and your endless arguments, because I promise -- I promise -- that if they catch us, you will look back on the day you got fired as one of the good ones." Helena stared, her nostrils flaring and her fists balled tight. Every fiber of her being screamed for her to fight, and a fresh dump of adrenaline left muscles humming. Perhaps sensing something, Pierce slid one foot back subtly, turning her body to present a smaller target. An eyebrow lifted in challenge. The tension spiked and then fell away. Maddox's sigh whistled through her nose as she forced open her hands. "Fine," she grunted. "But only until this is over. Then I never want to see you again." "I wish I could promise that, but..." Alexandra shrugged one shoulder, summoning up a cheeky grin. "Now laugh like I just said something outrageous, and hug me like we haven't seen each other in years." She opened her mouth to object again, but a single flicker to the redhead's brow stopped her, and she chuffed a laugh that she'd swear sounded fake, shaking her head. What followed was one of the most awkward embraces in Helena's life, made no better when Pierce whispered to loosen up. "I hate you," she murmured. "I really do." "The me you hate no longer exists." "Says the woman on the run from people she can't name." "If I told you who they are, you wouldn't help me." "I'm tempted not to anyway." Maddox sighed. "What now?" "Go inside and get a table. I'll be right behind you." Helena's brows lifted, but after a moment, it was clear Alex didn't intend to explain what she'd be doing, and the former police detective hissed a sigh. "Right. No questions. I forgot already." She turned towards the buildings, jamming her hands into her pockets. Pierce waited until the woman was well gone before she opened the bag again, sliding a shoebox-sized package under the back bench. She moved the remaining two bundles into pastel colored shopping bags and tore open the packaging for a brand new disposable pay-as-you-go phone before she swept towards the restaurant. She was struggling with the old-style T9 keypad as she pushed inside. "Hi, and welcome in to IHOP today," the hostess said. "Will there be just one?' "Hmm?" Alex heard every word, of course, but she still came off as flighty and distracted, just in one sound. "Oh. No. My sister is here already." She turned sideways, lifting her phone as she slid past. The motion bared the intricate tattoo painted on the inside of her wrist for the hostess for just a few seconds before she made it by, and she breezed up to the table, smiling slyly. It wasn't her usual kind of slyness, more the "shopper who got a great deal" kind than the "I have a plan that will ruin your life" kind. Maddox's eyes fell to the two bags, pink and lavender, her eyebrows coming up. "When did you have time to go shopping?" she asked. "Uhh... I don't know. Sometime before now, I guess?" Pierce put enough air behind her words to make her sound several orders of magnitude less intelligent than usual. "Becs, you have got to put it on so I know it fits." Helena tipped the bag towards her warily, and her lips compressed together in a line. "You've got to be fucking--" She caught herself before she launched into a full diatribe. "It's not really my kind of thing, you know?" "Well, no offense, but you might wanna... y'know, freshen up your look some." She sighed, a little bit plaintive and a little bit entitled. "At least try it on," Pierce pleaded, pulling her mass of hair over one shoulder. "If you hate it, I'll take it back, promise." "What, you mean now?" She looked around the restaurant. "Uh, I don't know. We're at fucking IHOP. I'm not about to--" "They have a bathroom, you know." Alex put her knee on the bench, grabbing for Maddox's elbow. "Come on... I'll totally stand guard." Helena relented in the interest of avoiding a scene, sliding out of the bench and up tp her feet. She took up the bag with a sigh. "Let's do this before I change my mind," she said. They walked side-by-side to the restroom, and Maddox held her complaints until they reached to door. "Tell me you're not actually expecting me to wear this thing." "Jonas picked the clothes, not me." "Of course he did." She sighed again. "So I change, and then what? What are we even doing here? If all we need is a different ride..." "We'll get one. First I want to see if it worked." Helena groaned, bumping the restroom door open with her hip. "You are bound and determined not to tell me what the fuck is going on, aren't you?" "The car is bait," was all Pierce would say. It was enough to earn her a nod. "Bait," she said in consideration. "I can get behind bait." * * * * * It was nearly forty minutes before anyone approached the abandoned SUV. A wide, boxy sedan -- the kind that could only have looked more like a cop car if it were painted with flashing lights strapped to the roof -- crept up to the side, stopping at a diagonal a spot or three away. "There." With the prosthetics, Helena could hardly recognize it was Pierce across the table from her, but not even dull, muddy brown contacts could hide the eager gleam to her eyes. "There he is." As Helena turned to steal a glance, a curtain of unfamiliar, dishwater blond hair blocked her view. She huffed an irritated sigh, not her first since they'd returned to the restaurant in whole new roles, hers making her feel more than a little ridiculous and definitely underdressed. She reached a discrete hand down to the bench beside her, tugging ineffectually at her skirt. "Stop doing that," Alex said, not looking down. "Easy for you to say. You're not wearing a belt with delusions of grandeur." She scooted upright, trying to keep the thing from rolling up. "I don't get why we couldn't have waited. I don't get why we came in, changed, and then came back. And I really don't get why I had to take off my fucking underwear." "He knows me," Pierce explained carefully. "You don't think he's going to come here, look around, and not come in, right? Someone had to be here. Someone had to deliver the car so he could find it. That someone couldn't be here when he came in." "We could have staked the place out across the street." "I want to hear what he says. I want to see how much his brow furrows. I want to see him sweat." "So wait, I took off my pa--" She shot a glance around, lowering her voice to a whisper. "I took off my panties because you're pissed?" Alex frowned, but then that was pretty much all she'd been doing. "It has nothing to so do with that at all now. I need to see if this is working." "I know this is going to sound like I'm harping, but... this required me to dress like a streetwalker how?" "You're creating a character, Detective. Not just playing a role but inhabiting a person, and that comes from the skin out. It's why women wear lingerie outside the bedroom." "All I'm saying is... I might've preferred the lingerie." There hadn't been much in Maddox's bag at all, actually -- just the skirt and a lime green halter top a size or two too small, along with a pair of boots and the wig, which she swore made her skin look yellow. While there hadn't been underwear, she had found nearly a fifth of vodka, and even though she knew she was just supposed to use the liquor to make her smell drunk, Helena downed almost a third of the bottle before the other woman had changed. Looking across the table, Maddox was surprised it hadn't taken that long -- and if Pierce was able to pull off that kind of change in an IHOP bathroom, she didn't even want to think about what the Spider could have done from the comfort of her own home. Hell, if she hadn't known what she was looking at, Helena may not have recognized Alex at all. It wasn't only the prosthetics that extended her chin and upturned her nose or the wrinkles meticulously inscribed along her forehead and at the corners of her eyes. It wasn't even the expensive, handwoven wig, blond hair shot through with gray. It was everything. Pierce spoke differently; not just the thin Welsh accent that reminded Maddox of her grandmother, but different cadence, tone, and even word choice. She walked differently, taking smaller, more precise steps. She held herself differently, head higher, nose lifted. Hell, she even sat differently, feet crossed at the ankle and tucked under her. It was almost unsettling how far she'd sunk into the role, but at the same time, it was tremendously helpful to Helena's own play -- she actually felt like she was having a late night breakfast with a disapproving aunt after she'd partied too hard. "Who are you--" Maddox turned in the booth, fidgeting with the cuff of her stiletto-heeled booth to excuse the motion. "Wait, that guy?" The man in question climbed out of his car, approaching the SUV warily. He was a small man with thinning hair and a perpetual frown on his lips, and if he was taller, they'd say he was built like a barrel, but Maddox figured he was probably shorter than she was, so it would have been better to say he was built like a cask. He narrowed dark, deep-set eyes, bypassing the two front doors to try the back. When it popped open, he flicked a suspicious glance over his shoulder, eyeing the restaurant. "The car being unlocked should be a tip-off," Helena said, glancing back to the other woman. Even Pierce's smile had changed. "He thinks he's being clever," she said. Maddox stretched her arms over her head, stealing another glance to the parking lot as the man eased open the car door. "If he knows where the car is, he should wait and grab us on the way out." "Think about it. Why come alone and try to break in the car when no one is around?" "He's doing something he's not supposed to," she deduced. "He wants something in the car, but he doesn't want us to know he's looking. Something in that bag Stryker packed, I'd wager." Pierce might have smiled, but it was so thin, it might as well be invisible. "Very good. He's checking my bonafides -- that I am who I say I am." "Which of course you rarely are. I mean, why tell him who you actually are and just ask for his help, right?" Helena shook her head, trying to mask her interest. "He wouldn't exactly jump at the chance. He needs to believe I can do what I said I can do." "And what did you tell him you could do? Or is that part of the mysterious shit you can't tell me? If they do a forensics sweep on the SUV, they'll find me over it, and I'm in the system. Hair fibers, fingerprints, all of it -- hell, I probably a cigarette in the ashtray. So I'm involved in whatever--" She turned back to face Alex, elbows on the table. "Quit the international woman of mystery act, because I'm not fucking buying it. I'm involved whether you like it or not, so just tell me what's going on. First, you're... you're this hardcore nerdy chick, and now we're watching some guy go through or car and I still don't get to know why?" "Actually, Krista came second." "What?" "Krista. The 'hardcore nerdy chick'. Her name is Krista." "She has a name." "Why wouldn't she?" Pierce seemed honestly confused. "Anyway, she came second. First, I was Tammie -- with an 'ie', not a y -- and I spent almost an hour selling shoes." "I'm confused. You were selling shoes?" "Tammie was. It's simple. Earlier this afternoon, I sold his wife a pair of lovely slingback sandals. This evening, I played Magic with his son. Those pictures he's very looking at now prove that." She snapped her fingers. "The tattoo -- the one you made sure the hostess saw, the flower and vine on the inside of your wrist. Both Krista and Tammie had one?" Alex nodded slightly. "Tammie was on her shoulderblade." "What does it mean? Something bad, obviously." "Membership in the Black Lily Society." "Which is... something bad, obviously." Helena smirked, leaning forward intently. "So he thinks... what? That a group of these Black Lily people got close to his wife and son. Maybe that next time, they'll do something?" Again, the comment earned a nod. "There's a syringe in the box under the photos. It's empty now, but it smells awful, and if he has the stones to have it tested, it will come back with quite a lovely cocktail." "So he thinks there are these crazy undercover poisoner people around, and if he wants them to go away, he's got to... do something, obviously." Pierce tugged her tasteful, royal blue bag onto her lap. At first, Maddox wasn't sure the other woman intended to answer. "I'm looking for a friend of mine," Alex said finally. "That man's employer has him in a deep, dark hole, and he'll be telling us where it is." "So you threatened the man's wife and son?" She shook her head in disgust. "This is how you get people to cooperate with you? Christ." She shrugged diffidently, drawing a thin, golden lighter from her purse. "I haven't threatened anyone, Detective. All I did was sell some shoes and play some cards. Any threat he perceives is something he conjured whole cloth." "In his head?" Maddox demanded. When Pierce didn't answer, instead sliding from the booth, Helena shot to her feet, grabbing Alex's arm. "Is that what you tell yourself in order to get to sleep at night?" she hissed. "You make the mistake of thinking I get to sleep anymore." She yanked her arm free, pushing the trigger on the lighter. "What did you just do? What the fuck did you just do?" Maddox was cut off by the whoompf of the box exploding -- not a lot of boom but a great deal of fire, reaching up to the front seat and clawing at the roof. Even though Alex had to have known it was coming, she still stumbled back in an impressive bout of feigned shock, wide-eyed. "Bloody hell!" she exclaimed, nearly bowling over the hostess. "Someone call the fire department!" The ruckus and the flames drew the bulk of the employees and most of the sparse let night crowd to the front of the place, so they didn't notice Alexandra peel off from the throng. Swift steps carried her through a side door and to the back lot, the keychain she'd lifted from the woman jangling in her hand as she pawed through them. She didn't turn when she heard the door bang open behind her. "That was your big plan?" Helena demanded. "Couple your bullshit threats with arson, reckless endangerment... maybe a little attempted murder?" "I'd quibble with a few of those, but..." She shrugged one shoulder again, the character not quite dissipated. "You blew up a car!" "I didn't blow up anything." Her attention was split, trying to locate a car key in the mess. "It's just on fire." "'Just on fire'?" She stepped forward, eyebrow lifting. "You could have killed someone, and you made me sit there and watch?" "But they weren't, and you did." She sighed sharply, holding the keys up in the pale, orange-yellow light. "Fuck, where is it?" Gravel crunched beneath Maddox's steps, unsteady in unfamiliar boots. "She probably keeps her personal keys separate," she said. "But hey, why don't you just hot-wire it? Add grand theft auto to the list of crimes we've committed tonight?" "Would you rather we wait for the police? Maybe we could explain who we are." "Or here's a funny thought... if somebody's got your friend, maybe they can actually help." "Help?" She laughed bitterly. "They're the ones that have him. Besides, in my experience, cops just get in the way." She turned to push past the other woman. "I don't see you lining up to give your statement; don't worry, I'm sure they'll believe that you were forced to dress like that." "I thought I could--hey!" She grabbed Alex's arm as she passed. "You can't do this. I'm not going to let you do this. We'll go back inside and--" Pierce yanked her arm free again, this time shifting to grab Helena's wrist and wrench her arm painfully behind her in a hammerlock. She used the arm as a lever to bend the former homicide detective over the hood of the nearest car. "You seem patently incapable of seeing me as anything but what I used to be, so let me speak a language you'll understand," she said, stepping in tight to lean over Maddox. Her voice was quiet, lips at the woman's ear. "I am sick of people telling me I have some obligation to help the helpless. My friend sacrificed himself so I could get away after I took a stupid chance for someone I thought deserved it, and he doesn't deserve what's happening to him right now. So I will do whatever I have to to see that he is safe. Do you hear me?" "Yes!" Helena gasped, her breath fogging on the cool metal of the car hood. "All right, okay?" When Alex relented, releasing enough pressure to let Maddox lift her face, something heavy and metallic scraped. "What was that?" Pierce demanded, wrenching the woman up to her feet and forcing her to turn around. "What did you--" She released the arm, one hand frisking up Helena's lime green top, and found a set of car keys tucked into the bust. "What happened to grand theft auto?" "I don't... I don't know why I took them," Helena whispered, having trouble with the words. "They were just lying there on the countertop when I followed you and I... I--" "And took them," Pierce sneered, ripping the keys free. "The third generation cop stole a woman's car keys. I think I underestimated you, Detective." The nickname was delivered with even more vitriol than usual. "You didn't become a cop to stop people like me at all, did you? You did it because you didn't have the balls to be someone like me." Maddox shot up to her feet, as if she were going to object, her fists balled, but all she could manage was a half-hearted mutter. "Maybe you don't know people as well as you think." She let her purse slip down to the crook of her elbow, yanking out a manila envelope. "Here," she said, thrusting the thing into Pierce's chest. "Stryker wanted you to have this, but I was going to spare you, since I figured maybe you weren't actually the monster I thought. Clearly, I was wrong." Her shaky hands tugged self-consciously at her skirt hem again as she teeter-tottered towards the diner. "Go," she said, not looking back. "Take the car. I'll cover as best I can, just leave it where it can be found. And stay the hell out of my life." Alex looked after, considering following until she heard the wail of sirens approaching, and she shoved the envelope in her own bag, hurrying to the car. She sent two texts from two different phones. The first went to someone who thought she was a devil. "Naughty naughty," it read. "You have six hours now." The second went to someone who thought she was an angel. "I love you," said this one. Some days she didn't know which was right. Today was one of those days. ---- Part Two: Tails/Home "Oh, come on! You don't have to--" "I think I do." "You can't..." The automatic door slid open with a shudder and a squeal, as if awakening from a long nap. The little redhead pushed her shopping cart through, dumping her purse on the folded-out child's seat with a long, long-suffering sigh. "I've already told you a hundred times--" "You're going to have to tell me a hundred more." The dark-haired teen trailed her sometimes mother, sometimes sister, sometimes friend. "If you tell Mom what I did with the wig at the mall and Brandon's girlfriend, she's gonna--" Quinn looked around, suddenly afraid they'd be overheard. "--she'll kill me. Or at the very least, she'll want me to move home again." "Is that so bad?" Amy's cart drifted to the right, fighting her as she tried to hang cut through the drugstore to the pharmacy. "It's not like your mom and I that uncool, are we?" "No! No, that's not it at all, Aim. It's just... you guys are almost an hour away from school, and sometimes the condo feels kinda... kinda, I don't know. Not crowded, you know, but awful close." She caught up when Campbell backed out of an overfull aisle to choose a different route, laying her hand on the cart. "Please, Amy. It was a stupid, one-off lapse. I'm totally over it. No need to freak her out, y'know?" "I'd like a house more than you know, but come on, we're not even home half the time," Campbell said with a shrug. "I dunno... I just don't get the whole supervised dorm living thing. Then again, I'm also not a fan of private school in general, but nobody listens to me when it comes to anything even remotely adult, so..." The teen sighed. "I know you don't, but... this has been good for me. And good for Mom, because I'm safe. I'm safe and I have friends my own age, and I don't have to be the third wheel. I mean, you guys can christen every room in the condo if you want..." Amy gives her a flat look, and Quinn raises a hand. "Sorry, sorry. Just... it's not going to help either of us if Mom blows a gasket, you know?" "And that's the only reason I'm not going to say anything." Amy sighed. "But no more hangouts with Kathryn, okay? She... it just weirds me out too much." "She's my godmother." Gregory offered her own shrug. "She's... I mean, she's not a nice person. I know that. But there are a lot of people out there who'd say the same about you and Mom, to be honest, and she's trying. Besides... I mean, no offense or anything but... neither of you are really people who I'd... who I'd go to for advice on being a girly." "I mean it, Quinn." She pushed the cart forward again. "And I'll have you know I'm plenty girly." The girl was maybe six inches taller than Campbell, and she had to lean down to slide her arm across the redhead's shoulders for a brief squeeze. "Sure you are, Aim." She grinned. "Seriously, though, you're a lifesaver." "Yeah, sure." She stopped at the line at the pharmacy. "Just... no more crazy shit, okay?" "Cross my heart." Quinn glanced over her shoulder. "So... aren't you here, like, a lot?" Amy was most conscious of the slight shake to her hands when people pointed things like that out. She stuffed them in her pockets, "Well, yeah. Between the occasional antibiotic, painkillers and the buffet of anxiety meds for my OCD..." She nodded to the pharmacist who'd acknowledged her, even as far back in line as they were, smiling. "You could say I'm a friendly face here." "Is the line always long? Because I feel like I should call to bail on Itze and Margot tonight so I can hang with you guys." "You don't have to--" "I do. Mom's just back from her stupid trip, and you've been all alone for a couple days--" "And you don't trust me not to blurt out your secret the first chance I get." "I didn't--" Quinn sighed, sweeping the hair over her ears. "Amy, I didn't even think that, okay? I just want to hang out with you guys. We'll get something from the Cheesecake Factory, maybe play board games or something." "If you want to stay, stay. Neither of us would mind, and it saves you from being guilted into standing in line with me." Amy chuckled weakly. "Win-win, right?" The girl shrugged a little. "That might have occurred to me, yeah. I should be out front, by the time you're done." She grinned for the pharmacist. "They'll probably have your bag ready before you walk up." Campbell sighed plaintively. "They usually do. I'll text if I can't find you." Quinn shuffled away, hands stuffed in her pockets. It didn't really bother her that she'd massaged the truth with Amy, she realized -- it was that apathy that unnerved her. What kind of person lies to cover up a lie? What kind of person is okay with that? She got to the end of the aisle and glanced back to Amy in line. The little redhead had her iPhone in hand, and she took a surreptitious look around before she dialed. Quinn sighed. She could say that Kathryn "meant well" or that she was helping her goddaughter, but she knew better. Shaw always had an agenda. She didn't do anything out of the goodness of her heart. If Kathryn was helping her, it was because she got something out of it, even if that something was just a favor. She knew she should listen to Amy.
