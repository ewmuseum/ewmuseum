Twist of Fate - Part I

7 January 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: January 7th, 2002 Detroit, Michigan January 7th 2002 2:30 AM Feeling worthless is no vacation on the mind. It can make you lazy, depressed, and ultimately a bummer to be around. Why feel good about being worthless? Why go on, living life with a smile, if you have nothing to smile about? “Sure thing, sug,” she says through her lips, clamping down on a cigarette. “Just give me a second.” The click of her knee-high heels on the concrete sidewalk echoes off the brick building across the street. The redhead, dressed like a whore, passes by similarly tasteless women until she comes to a big, black steel door. Her tiny hand makes a hard, street-tough knock. BANG BANG BANG BANGBANGBANG The window slides open, revealing a pair of smiling eyes. The same she has seen too many times in her life. Taking a brief drag, she looks up. “Ahh! Justice,” the man cries out in a jovial tone. “Busy night! Three times already? You go-“ “Save it for the youngin’s, yeah Franky?” she cuts in, stomping out her cigarette. She slaps her hands on her hips and glares up at the pimp. “This guy, with the mask. I thought you said I could see him? Now he says that you-“ The eyes grow wide with fire, “That motherfucker’s back here?! Yo, Tyrone! Quick get-“ KAAABOOOOOM The steel door comes off its’ hinges, smacking whoever the big feller behind it is clear into the kitchen table – two rooms away. Justice stares on in awe and shock, standing still without making the slightest move. She looks at the giant hole in the building where the massive door used to be. “Holy shit!” she whispers to herself, removing another cigarette from her purse. Her quivering hands make it difficult to light. CACHINK Looking ahead, her eyes lock onto the Zippo before her. She takes the gesture in, lighting her smoke, and then looks to the kind soul. Wearing the mask she knew to be his, the man gives her a nod. “Thanks,” she says softly. “No sweat, kid.” He replaces the lighter in his jeans before turning his attention back to his demolition of the city block. The big man, trapped beneath the giant door and bleeding from his head, looks to the masked man in fear. "Now scram." “Nah, man! Nah! Call some help, bitch! Don't jus' stand there. This ain’t my problem, man,” he starts firing out of his mouth. Shaking his head in a series of negative twists and turns, he pleads with the nearing figure above him. He steps onto the door, pressing down all the weight of the eight-foot steel door. The man’s blue eyes peer down through the dark mask, locking onto the bigger man’s immobile torso. He kneels down and tears the expensive gold chain from his neck. He hold sit in his gloved hands, looking at the diamond-studded cross. Glancing up, he looks to the man’s skin, noticing crosses and religious tattoos covering his body. “Where is your boss?” he says through a patient, mellow raspy voice. Waiting through silence, he says nothing and stares on. The hooker looks on in strange excitement. She feels bad for this but can’t help but feel obligated to watch these scumbags get what’s coming to them. For all the grabs, slaps, and unnecessary harassment they dish out – she wants to make sure they pay. All of them. “He’s upstairs,” she says, fighting all the things inside her screaming not to. The mask turns around and stares. His gaze is that of somebody just interrupted during his torture of another. It is angry, fierce, and nowhere near sympathetic. She twists her foot on the ground nervously. “His name is Coco. He has a gun. A lot of guns.” The man stands upright, displaying his muscular frame and stature. He looks down to the big man who just stares on in pain and blood. He looks to the staircase and then back at the man above him. The two exchange a curious eye and then the healthier makes his way for the staircase. The eyes of the big man widen as he finds all the strength in his body. “COCO! GET OUT! RU-“ THWACKTHWACK The black boot of the man in the mask silences the alarm. He looks up at the woman who is half-way smiling. She looks up, startled, and looks away when she sees him noticing her obvious pleasure. “Thank you, miss. Remember what I said,” he says, looking down at the unconscious thug. “This is no place for an angel.” Turning away, he steps over the door and toward his real objective. “Wait!” she cries. He turns, looking back with a cocked brow (under the mask). “Yes, miss?” What is your name? You know mine is Carmen,” she says, thinking about how he is the only one in the city who knows that she has a real name – other than her landlord and doctor. “What is yours?” He looks to the ground. Thoughts of names and birthdays and real life run through his head. But quickly fade when he smiles at Carmen’s beautiful eyes. “My name is Friar. Years ago I used a real name and had a real life. Now…” he pauses and thinks about Coco, Carmen, and this cocksucker bleeding all over the floor. “I spend my time helping people like you remember yours.” TO BE CONTINUED...
