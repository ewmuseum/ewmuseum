Date: Sept. 19, 2005 =================== A Brother’s Acceptance =================== Backyard Wrestling. For the most part, wrestlers look down on it. They hear about some kid imitating them, putting another kid through a flaming table in the backyard of their parent’s house, and they scoff. It is unbelievable the amount of pain and anguish these kids put themselves through to imagine themselves as some of the top draws in this business today, but it is being done. Johnny Come Lately smashes Anonymous Masked Man Number Two with a chair wrapped in barbed wire, ripping the flesh from his forehead with one miscalculated swipe, and tosses it to the ground to capitalize on the move before the Masked Man has enough time to bleed to death. It’s sad, but it is things like this that are happening all over the United States, and no one has an idea on how to put an end to it. With the amount of injuries and deaths that this brand of wrestling has caused, it has become more of an epidemic ? a plague ? than some sort of entertainment. But what do these kids have to look forward to? It’s not like any kid off the street can just waltz into a small-time independent promotion and ask for a shot to prove themselves. Most of the time, you need to have proof of the amount of training that you have. Most of these kids? No, none of these kids have that sort of training or experience to warrant a response of that magnitude. Most of them cannot even afford to pay for the training, and that is what causes them to entertain other options to quench their thirst for excitement? to fulfill their lifelong dreams. Unfortunately, for a select few, it also leads to them living the rest of their lives in a wheelchair? if they get to live out the rest of their lives at all. Jayce Bradley was one of these kids. Jayce grew up in a nice suburban home. His father had a nine to five that paid him quite generously, his mother was a stay at home mom that sold Mary Kay products to her friends and family to garner a bit of extra income’her way of contributing to the household and getting a few bucks to stash away for a rainy day. Life was good, and the relationship between Jayce and his older brother, Dominic, was one where Jayce looked up to his older brother for guidance. He; like most other kids, wanted to be just like his older brother. Jayce mimicked Dominic in every which way, shape, or form. Jayce idolized his older brother, and Dominic did everything he could to give his younger brother someone to look up to and be proud of. Of course, life is never that perfect. Things changed, and no one took it harder than Jayce. When Dominic started high school, he had the size, strength, and build that would land him a starting spot on the junior varsity squad. He took it, and after the first two weeks of the season; due to an unexplainable injury to a starter on the varsity squad, he was rewarded with a back-up spot on the team’s so far undefeated team. It was nice, but with all the practices and time spent studying film on the following week’s opponents’yeah, Texas takes their football real serious. Well, that did not leave much time left to spend with a little brother that was starting his second year of junior high school. It was something Jayce had no control over, and something he chose not to dwell on. While he still idolized his brother, he was nowhere near his brothers size, and could not play football if his life depended on it. Jayce had to find other ways to occupy his time, and that was about the time when he started watching wrestling a bit more. Jayce and Dominic had spent many a night doing homework in front of the television, watching their favorite National Wrestling Council superstars as they attempted to out-wrestle; and more importantly, out-think their opponent. Jayce’s favorite wrestler was the “Cruiserweight Sensation” Jeremy Thomas, while Dominic preferred to cheer for “The Devil Himself” J.W. Oswald. The two could barely control themselves as they watched the two battle it out for Oswald’s Heavyweight title at HIW’s AB4. Dominic would go on to rub his younger brother’s nose in it for quite some time after, as Thomas failed to beat Oswald for the title. Dominic always liked to believe that the bigger man should always prevail. He believed that cruiserweights had no business wrestling against the bigger, stronger wrestlers? which was why they received their own division in the rankings. It was a sad way of thinking, and Jayce never agreed with him. Jayce always believed that it was the smarter man, the faster, more agile wrestler that could overcome his bigger, slower adversary. That night he was proven wrong, but over time; as Jayce started watching more and more wrestling, Jayce decided that he was going to prove his brother wrong. He was going to prove to his brother that the smaller guys had every bit as much of an advantage as the big burly wrestlers. He was going to become a wrestler and prove it to him, himself. Of course, his parents would not hear of it. With his grades, Jayce had the opportunity to go on to law school or med school. He was on the honor roll, and something like that’in his parent’s eyes’could cause him to lose focus on a future that would be more promising than being on the road for most of the year for little to nothing as far as wages went. Like most kids though, Jayce paid them no mind. Without the money to fund his exploits, Jayce started showing up in random backyards, doing insane stunts to garner a cult following of other kids in the neighborhood. Jayce was in the eighth grade, and in less than a year he would be moving on to high school’to the same high school his older brother was attending. This was something he was keeping secret from his family, and with the amount of fans that were coming along’the secret was bound to be revealed. This was something that Jayce could not have discovered, as Dominic himself had a huge hatred for the people that took part in backyard wrestling. Jayce had to tell his brother, himself. Dominic was not pleased at all when he heard the news. His eyes filled with rage, Dominic sat across from his brother with a disgusted look upon his face. His features filled with disdain, Dominic could barely even speak. “Are you out of your mind,” he asked in a coarse voice. “It’s just something that I had to do,” Jayce responded. His eyes welled up with tears, eager to have his brother’s support in something he knew he was eventually going to have to reveal to his parents as well. “Mom and Dad would not pay for me to have the training, and?” “And what,” Dominic interrupted. “You think that putting your life on the line is somehow going to get someone to notice you” You’re still a kid, Jay. Don’t be so quick to grow up.” With his arms folded across his chest, Dominic could barely even look at his younger brother, instead opting to glare off to the side; towards the kitchen area. “You don’t understand,” Jayce stated, his eyes pleading for acceptance. “I need you to be on my side on this.” “On your side,” Dominic said in a harsh tone, his voice filled with a tinge of anger. “You’re lucky I’m not kicking your ass right now! On your side” I wouldn’t be on your side if you were getting paid for it, Jay. This is just wrong on so many levels, and I don’t even want to hear you crying when dad finds out. You won’t be able to sit down for weeks after he’s through with you.” Jayce stared at his older brother, who would not turn to face his adolescent sibling. “You can’t tell dad,” Jayce whispered. “Why not,” Dominic questioned. Jayce looked toward the floor, his face filled with shame and said, “Because they told me not to even think about it. They made it clear that I could not do this, and I disobeyed them. You can’t tell them. I’ll do anything.” Dominic looked toward his brother, watching as the tears streamed down his face. “Fine, but you have to quit.” “I can’t.” “You will, because if you don’t’I’ll tell dad without hesitating. “But?” “No buts, Jay, drop it or else.” Jayce wiped away a stray tear, his nose starting to run as well. “Fine,” Jayce replied. “I’ll quit.” “That’s what I want to hear,” Dominic said as he stood up and walked over to the other side of the table. Placing an arm around the shoulder of his brother, Dominic pulled him close and said in a whisper. “I love you, bro. I don’t want to see you end up like those little punks that get stuck living their lives in a wheelchair, or sucking their food through a straw.” “I’m sorry, Dee,” Jayce answered. “It’s just that you have things to occupy your time now, and I don’t have anything. This was just my way of proving myself to you.” “Proving yourself to me?” Dominic backed away from his brother, still holding him around the shoulders. “Bro, I’ve always been proud of you. From your grades to the way you carry yourself. You don’t have nothing to prove to me, because I’ll be proud of you no matter what.” “No matter what?” “Yeah, bro,” Dominic answered. “No matter what.” “Thanks, bro,” Jayce said as he lowered his head and stared at the varnished hardwood floor. “Look,” Dominic started, “I got some stuff I need to do right now, but I’ll be back in about half an hour. What do you say we go and grab some tacos from Teka Molino?” “That sounds cool, Dee.” “Alright then, I’ll be back in a bit.” The two embraced, a respect and love that only two brothers as close as these could comprehend. Jayce pulled his brother close, almost hesitant to release him. It’s as if he knew something, but Jayce knew nothing of what was to come. Dominic left, off to help a friend pick something up from one of his cousin’s house. Dominic never came back. ============= A Brother Lost ============= “Get the fuck out of the car!” The voices, muffled behind black bandanas pulled tightly over the bottom half of their faces, screamed out at them from outside of the vehicle. Dominic tried his best to keep calm, lifting his arms up above his head as he slowly exited the fully customized, nineteen ninety two, lime green and silver Honda Civic hatchback. His friend, Edison Golden Bears starting varsity quarterback, had built this show stopper from the ground up. Having a family that was very well off, money was no expense. He would just take the car to a shop and tell them to go wild. It was a nice thing to have money, but this car jacking that was taking place right now was one of the drawbacks to such an extravagant lifestyle. “Hurry the fuck up,” one of the jackers exclaimed. “I don’t want to shoot your bitch ass!” The two friends exited the vehicle, moving aside so the thieves could get in and drive away. The car was fully insured, and it was certainly not worth more than their lives. “Get the fuck out of the way.” The voice sounded familiar to Dominic as he was pushed aside. He glanced back and made the biggest mistake of the night, he spoke. “Duncan?” Dominic paused as the car jacker turned and faced him, the sudden sense of fear running down his spine when he realized that it was a kid he went to junior high school with. “This bitch knows you,” the car jacker on the driver’s side asked his compatriot. “Nah, he don’t know shit.” “But he just said your fucking name!” “He was just guessing until you fucking opened your mouth.” Duncan, the car jacker on the passenger side, shook his head as he turned towards Dominic. Dominic turned to run, but it was too late. Duncan extended his arm out the window, opening fire with his black nine millimeter and catching Dominic four times in the back. Dominic fell face first into the cold, black asphalt of the street, as the Honda Civic sped off into the night. His friend ran over and pulled him over to the curb, pulling his cell phone from his pocket and dialing nine one one. Like most emergencies, the response took longer than expected. Dominic died in his friend’s arms, coughing up blood as he tried to gasp for each and every breath. It would later be revealed that one of the bullets had punctured a lung, filling them with the thick, crimson fluid that gives us life. Unfortunately, it is also what can take it away. ============== Nothing To Lose ============== The blood seeped through the gash’slowly trickling down his cheek and landing on the cold, off-white linoleum floor of the locker room. With the amount of blood lost, the drops have gone on to become puddles, and it does not look as if the bleeding is going to stop anytime soon. Jayce just lays there on the long wooden bench, staring up at the lights and thinking back to everything that had just happened. It was not for long though, as a voice brought him back to his senses. “Damnit kid, what the hell happened to you” That’s a hell of a cut you got there.” Even through the thick sarcasm, the voice appears to be riddled with pain. “You know, I would have never fucking guessed.” The man that has come over to check on Jayce is not a doctor, he looks more like someone that should be on the sidelines of a football game taping up some kid’s high ankle sprain. The older gentleman; somewhere around his late thirties, stands next to Jayce with thread and needle in hand, shaking his head at the sight. Jayce looks over finally, catching a glimpse of the short stocky man for the first time before turning his attention back up at the ceiling. “You sure you want me to do this kid?” The thick southern accent fit the older gentleman’s appearance, as do the blue jeans and white, pocket t-shirt that he wears. “If you ask me, I think you should go on and get checked out at a hospital. They know how to take care of this sort of stuff better than I ca?” Interrupting the man, Jayce turns towards him and barks out a response. “Look, I don’t have any money, I don’t have any insurance, and I sure as hell am not going to go begging for a handout from my parents, so what else is there left for me to do?” “I didn’t think about that,” the man responded. “It’s just that?” “Seriously, how hard can it fucking be,” Jayce asked. “You know what, give me the shit. I’ll do it my damn self.” Jayce starts to sit up, but the man places a hand on his chest, motioning for him to lay back. “I’ll do it,” he said with a bit of bitterness. “You just need to wipe away some of that blood. I can’t see anything, especially the wound.” Jayce grabs a sweat drenched t-shirt, wiping away a spot below his left eye. The gash itself was about an inch long, running right below his eye socket horizontally. With every wipe, the blood continues to ooze out of the laceration. The man leans in, penetrating a section of skin that was not slit. As the needle slides effortlessly through the wound, Jayce winces a bit in pain; causing a bit more blood to leak out. “Son of a bitch,” Jayce exclaimed as the needle passed through his skin like a hot knife through butter. “Look kid,” the man started, “it’s hard enough to do this without you hollering. If you start to move, even more blood is going to come out and make this damn near impossible.” “Yo Jay, you in here,” a voice asked, as the sound of a door opening could be heard. “Over here man,” Jayce responded. In walked an African American teenager about the same age as Jayce. His baggy black jeans, throwback Emmitt Smith Dallas Cowboys football jersey, and brown Lugz boots gave him that thugged out appearance one would tend to expect from a black kid. He actually dressed that way on purpose, he wanted to fit the profile. “Hey man, you okay,” the kid asked with a bit of remorse in his voice. “Yeah, Ty, I’m cool,” Jayce responded, glancing over towards his visitor. “Look,” the man said angrily, “I already told you not to move. It’s bad enough I can barely see through all of this blood, but you moving can make me poke you in the eye with this needle. I doubt you want to be blind in one eye.” “Sorry,” Jayce replied, a bit apologetic. The man went back to stitching Jayce up, as Ty’that’s what Jayce called the black kid’walked over and took a seat on one of the benches; across from the one Jayce is laid out on. “Sorry about the chair shot man,” Ty started. “I didn’t mean to hit you with the corner.” Jayce just waved it off, trying not to move too much. “Look,” Ty continued, “I know this was your last match here and all, but you ain’t even found a place to wrestle for. Why don’t you stick around for a bit, we could really use you. The crowd loves you, Jay.” “It’s the best I could do,” the man said as he lifted his hand away from Jayce’s face. “If I were you, I’d find a way to get it checked out.” “Thanks old man,” Jayce said as he sat up on the bench. “I’ll take care of it.” Jayce dropped his legs over the edge of the bench, using his hands to keep him propped up as the old man walked out of the locker room. “Look, Ty,” Jayce started, “I’d like to help you guys out, but this is my big chance. If I do good in this match, I can probably get signed by an actual company. No offense, but twenty bucks a night isn’t cutting it right now. I need more than that to get by, and this is the only way I know how to get where I want to be.” “I know man,” Ty said as he shook his head. “You’re just one of the best we got, and seeing you go is going to suck.” “Look at it this way,” Jayce said playfully. “At least I won’t be kicking your ass anymore.” The two share a laugh. “Good luck out there, bro,” Ty said as he walked over to the door of the locker room. “This shit ain’t easy.” Jayce just smiled. “Neither am I, bro, neither am I.” Ty stopped at the door, glanced back over his shoulder and asked Jayce, “Where you going anyway?” Jayce glanced towards the door, the left side of his face still a slight crimson red and said, “Someplace called Prime Time Central, they have this big Infinite Gauntlet match going on this week.” “Infinite Gauntlet?” Ty paused, not familiar with the name. “What’s that?” Jayce smiled, the white thread used for stitches also a bright scarlet that stands out on his Caucasian skin tone. “It’s my shot at the big time.”
