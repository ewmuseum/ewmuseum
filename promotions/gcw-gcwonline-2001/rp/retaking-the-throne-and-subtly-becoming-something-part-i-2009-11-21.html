Retaking the Throne and Subtly Becoming Something: Part I

21 November 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 21, 2009 As Kevin looked up from his stool, he didn't know who the man on his shoulder was. His appearance gave him the impression of a hippy but his gut reaction told him that he wasn't as peaceful. His eyes hid behind a pair of sunglasses while his body was wrapped in a pair of old jeans and a leather jacket. Maggie McVay glanced into his glasses as the tears still slowly fell. Once again, he was the last person that she expected to see at that bar. She was first to speak fumbling on words through her drunken stupor. "What are you doing here?" Jay Terror pulled the sunglasses off his face and hung them from his white shirt collar. "I was about to ask you the same question." Kevin, who still wore Jay's hand as a shoulder pad, wasn't quite sure what to do. He'd lost any interest in Maggie with the new addition to the game but didn't think he was free to leave. McVay turned back to the bar to grab her vodka with strawberry trying to ignore Jay. Jay and Kevin finally locked eyes and immediately Kevin could feel his soul beginning to melt. Terror's stare were cold, the kind a mother would petition to keep out of local parks. He hadn't smiled since showing up and if Kevin had to guess he'd gander that it wasn't going to happen. Taking the inaudible advice, he stood giving the stool up. Jay took over the seat and grabbed Maggie's drink from her. "You don't need this right now." "I do too!" Her voice was loud, almost too loud for a bar. Within seconds eyes had focused on the couple. "I paid for it." "Something tells me your tab's still floating around here." There was a sense of agitation living in his voice and both he and Maggie knew it. It carried sharp tendencies and stung her ears as she reached for the glass. Jay denied her wrapping his arm around her shoulder. "Maggie, chill for a second." She shoved him off, "Get off me!" The same eyes that had been attracted a minute ago refused to leave the scene. Jay's arm became tighter holding her steady as he leaned in close to her ear. "Let's get out of here. I want to talk to you." "I'm not going anywhere with you Jay. I don't want to see you ever again." Jay loosened enough for her to breathe steadily. The vultures hung over their conversation with unrelenting eyes shadowing them. Jay turned heads though as he stared back at the mob. Focusing on Maggie again, he couldn't pin point what he had done. In the front of his mind he wanted to believe it was the alcohol talking. Considering her scent, he believed it more and more. Still, he wasn't convinced that this wouldn't come up in a sober conversation. "Maggie, I want to help you. That's all." "You want to help me?" She made a desperate dash for the glass again and Jay blocked her hand with a chop. Grabbing the vodka himself he tipped back the glass guzzling the contents in a single swig. The strawberry hardly helped the taste as he puckered his lips afterwards sliding the glass to the front of the bar. "Bartender, I need anot -" Jay gripped her mouth and called off the bartender. Under normal circumstances he would have investigated the situation between the two but considering how she was before Jay arrived, he felt he could only help her condition. He kept his hand over her mouth. "Maggie, I don't want to cause a scene. Let's go back to my hotel room. We can talk." He slowly let her speak holding his hand inches from her lips. "I'm not going anywhere with you Jay. You'll kill me just like you killed Steve!" The tears came back in flood form reddening her cheeks and dampening her revealing tank top. She placed her head on the countertop using her drink napkin to soak up the river forming on the Mahogany. Jay removed his arm from her shoulder and pulled it to his side. He wanted to light a cigarette with those words but was afraid he'd burn his hand with the heat they packed. Her mind was beyond alcohol and much past reason. Yes, it was absurd to think that he had killed her husband but one had to wonder how the idea would even come into fruition. It had been at least a year since they had last talked let alone seen one another. Jay tried to structure his words into some kind of sentence but he couldn't. Instead he simply watched her cry. He listened to her panting making awful noises against the bar. Finally, he couldn't take it any longer. Grabbing her under the arm, he attempted to pry her out of the puddle before she drowned herself in public. "C'mon Maggie. Let's get out of here." Her head shot off the bar in possessed fashion with eyes wide as biscuits. "I said I'm not going anywhere with you. It's your fault that he's gone. You're the reason that I'm alone. I hate you Jay Terror. Go to hell!" The entire bar was quiet besides the TV mounted in the corner. He didn't much care for the silence though; he was more interested in the way she named him. In all their time of knowing one another, Jay had never been referred to by his ring name around her. She was cordial and hated how he created it. Hearing her voice say Terror was like hearing a cow say meow. Had he bought too heavily into the Jay Terror gimmick once again to the point of erasing his true identity? Was he really at fault for Steve's death? There were too many questions and the bar wasn't the place to answer them. He grabbed her arms again. She struggled as he hoisted her onto his shoulder doing his best to shield her backside from creeping out of the bottom of her skirt. "Let me fuckin' down! Help! Someone!" No one came to her calls though. There was something about the name Jay Terror that shook the bar into complete apathy for her situation. To the onlookers that knew him, it wasn't just a name. Jay Terror was wretchedness personified. As he carried Maggie through the double doors she continued to kick and scream. "Fuck you Jay. Are you going to kill me too?" He ignored the opportunity to explain himself finding the limousine around the block. He opened the door himself and tossed her onto the back seat. She rolled to the floor as tears continued to spill. Jay followed inside and immediately reached for the manual lock making sure it was secured. "You bastard!" Jay ignored her again knowing that the alcohol had consumed her. She was no longer Maggie McVay but rather the alter-ego he and Steve had created for her - McMags. She was instant temper and completely ruthless. "I bet you're happy he's gone." He couldn't hold back on it, "Shut the fuck up Mags. I didn't kill Steve and I'm not happy that he's gone. Not by a long shot." Slurring her 'T', "Bullshitttt!" He found it easier to ignore her. The hotel was only three block away from the bar, a distance that would have taken ages if he would have carried her. She hissed and moaned banging on the glass between them and the driver. He had been warned of what he could expect prior to leaving however and also did his best to ignore her. Maggie was a mess by the time they pulled around front of the Marriott. Her hair was tied in knots and her shirt looked more like a dish rag. The skirt became a belt of sorts revealing too much for Jay to look at. As the driver came around to open the door, Jay grabbed her again fixing her garments. Walking into the lobby was a sport as she found a pair of fresh lungs. "Help me, please. Someone help! He's going to kill me!" Jay shook his head to the desk clerk expressing his annoyance. A family with a son and daughter were checking in at the moment and Jay made no conscious effort to apologize. The elevator seemed to take an extra hour between stops and when it arrived two emptied out. He and Maggie filtered in and they rose to the fifth floor. "Fuck you Jay. God Damnit!" She was getting tired, he could hear it in her voice. The kicking started to slow as well giving the small of his back a slight reprieve. He had taken the largest suite they offered making the hike down the hall an extra few minutes. Fumbling for the card key, Jay nearly dropped Maggie onto the floor. Once in hand, he slid it through and entered heading straight for the bed. She was nearly silent by the time they got to it and he lightly dropped her into the center. As he had expected, her eyes were rolling into the back of her head. He tucked the comforter around her limp body and found his cigarettes. If he ever needed an excuse to reduce stress, this opportunity was it. Lighting the significantly lighter object, he walked to the corner. It would be a night he wouldn't soon forget. Maggie on the other hand though... --- Steven Caldera was more than just a mentor to Jay Terror; he was what he believed a good father was meant to be. The way he spoke to Jay was enlightening - not like a traditional boss at all. In fact, Jay couldn't help but adopt the idea that Caldera was his new father. With the passing of his own noteworthy dad, Caldera filled a void in his heart that Jay had been missing for sometime. He was there no matter how great the odds were stacked against him and always found time to hand-out pointers on how to act and what to do if he ever encountered certain environments. As the two sat across from one another inside Say Sushi off of South Allen Street, Jay knew that he was in store for another famous lecture on culture and life. "You know, this stuff isn't all that bad if you give it a chance," Jay said while chewing. Caldera reminded him to shut his mouth and he obliged finishing the bite. Before Steven Caldera, Terror would have never tried half the foods he had encountered. "I told you. It's one of those things that people seem to be instantly put off about. I don't get how a person can make an honest decision without giving it an actual chance." Caldera was a seasoned veteran it seemed when it came to his meal. He had ordered the soup to start and was casually making his way through it. As their bouncy waiter returned, Jay spoke before he could. "Do you serve liquor here?" Caldera shot him a look but Terror pretended that he missed it. "We don't serve it. You're allowed to bring it though. Would you like another drink?" "I'll take a glass." The waiter left the table mid bow. Caldera continued to stay silent as he watched Jay retrieve a flask from his pant pocket. Dropping it onto the table it was obvious that he had stored a sizeable amount within. "Do you want some?" Steven Caldera was known to wear different faces for his emotions. Each expressed his mood quite clearly and as Jay read the current state he knew that he was in for something. His lips were straight as football laces and his eyes hung low. It was nearly blank and Jay sighed. "I'll take that as a 'no' then?" "It's two o'clock in the afternoon and you just pulled out a flask. You don't find anything wrong with that?" "Why would I?" The waiter returned with the glass and scooped up their sushi order sheets before heading back into the kitchen. Jay watched as Caldera leaned back in his seat. "I don't understand." "Who do you think you are?" "C'mon. I mean, it's just a little drink. Something to get me through the day. You'd understand if you knew what kind of night I had last night." Caldera sat up and leaned forward. "I don't care if you think you're a rock star Jay. Honestly, you can live your life any way you choose during the nights. What I care about is the road you're taking to becoming the greatest superstar GCW has known. How do you expect your liver is going to feel when you step into the ring with Jason O'Neil? Are you going to be able to take that extra punch in the stomach when you're out there?" Jay thought a second before settling on, "I'll feel like him." Caldera passed the answer around taking correct posture again. It was a well crafted argument in Jay's favor but Steven was already expecting it. If anything, he rolled the ball further in his court. "Is that a good thing in your mind?" "No. But we're different. He's a drunk and I'm just drinking some." "You're drinking to get through the day," he began. "Do you think Jason O'Neil wakes up every morning to a glass of orange juice and a wheat bagel? I doubt it. The Jason that I know rolls over and before hitting the alarm clock he grabs a bottle of whiskey. He's not even out of his bed before he begins drinking and do you know why? It's so he can come to terms with the person he is. He's just like you Jay, he's simply using it to get through the day. Take a hard look at Jason O'Neil and tell me that he isn't at a completely different level than the rest of the locker room." The similarities between O'Neil and GCW were thin and thinner when you looked at work ethic and overall stability. He was in a league of his own and was best known for waiting in his locker room for his match to begin. Quick in and quick out the crew had joked from time to time. Hearing himself compared to O'Neil was another first for Jay and already he didn't like the ring it had. "You honestly believe that I'm living his life?" Caldera took another ladle of his soup in. "If I know anyone on the GCW roster it's Jason O'Neil. This is why I've decided on you for this task Jay. You're not just someone on the roster; you're a stand-out figure. The similarities between you and him however are something I hadn't picked up until just now. Jay, I need to ask you to stop drinking like this. I have plans for you lined up and I don't want them to backfire on me because you couldn't outlast O'Neil in a match. You understand?" He understood but in any father son relationship, their was a sense of rebellion trampolining in his head. Obeying Caldera was something he tended to stick to but some things seemed to be more restrictive than others. Nevertheless, he accepted. "I can do that. I know how important this is to you." "Thank you." He finished his soup just in time for the main course to arrive. Four platters filled the center of the table arranged in different colorful designs. Looking at his flask he stuttered a minute before flagging the waiter. "Could I get a water?" "Of course sir." Of Caldera's bag of expressions, he chose the weak smile. Any form of smile on the face of the devil though was a sigh of relief. --- As Jay slid his card through the reader on the door, he walked in only to be welcomed by sunlight and its entourage. The curtains had been fanned completely open increasing the temperature by nearly four degrees. The slam the door made stirred Maggie from the comforter and she sat holding the top of her head. Jay sluggishly made his way to the bed taking a seat on the end. "How're you feeling?" She was pale but sober. "I don't know the last time I woke up like this. How did I get here?" Confirming his original guess Jay smirked slightly. "I picked you up from the bar and brought you back. Looked like you could use a comfortable bed somewhere close." Unaware of the events that unfolded she questioned, "Was I horrible?" "Nah, don't worry about it. You had reasons to be there. How're you holding up?" In reality, she wasn't holding up at all. The loss of her husband felt like someone had dropped a weight inside her rib cage. Everything hurt almost as if her heart was broadcasting her pain as a radio station to the rest of her body. Of all things she had endured in life, this was the hardest. "I don't know. I just don't know anything anymore. I don't understand how a person like that can be there one second and gone the next. It's stupid but the last thing I said to him was turn the lights off. I just want a mulligan or something." "It's not stupid, I understand. I don't want you blaming yourself though. It wasn't anything having to do with you." "It could have been," she interrupted. Thinking back to the day she discovered the gym lights were being left on at night, she knew that she was partially the reason why he left the bed that night. As much as she wanted to deny the fact that she could be the reason, she knew deep down that she was just as big of a culprit as anyone else. "He left that night for me. It was because of what I said." "He left that night because he had a bad feeling. There's no way that you can take the blame for a bad feeling. Trust me, the people who hit him will get exactly what's coming their way." "It's the ones who wanted to buy the gym. It must have been. Koz said he saw a suited man like the one that came into our office." "Who's Koz?" "A wrestler we know. He was always there for us when we needed something. Trained late at night. He was the last guy to talk to Steve on that night." Jay didn't know him personally but a sudden urge to meet him arose. He sidelined it though. "Someone was trying to buy the gym out?" "Yeah, it was something technical. I don't know." Jay knew that she didn't want to linger on Steve for very long considering the way her head had to be pounding. Still, he had a pressing question waiting to be answered from the night before. "Would you blame me at all for what happened?" "Why would I blame you?" "Just a curious question. I know I haven't been around and I didn't know if that had anything to do with it." "Steve would talk about you but I would never blame you. Never." Jay smirked again standing from the bed. He found an old water bottle and his tube of Aspirin. She took three tablets and swallowed down the warm water. She fell onto the bed and took note of the ceiling pattern. It was a simple dot pattern that amused her in the sudden silence. Jay decided to break it. "You think I could meet Koz? He seemed like a person I'd like to talk to." She was still quiet after hearing it hoping he took the ceiling spying as an excuse. "Yeah, sure. I'll give you his number later. Lay with me?" Being beside her reminded him of older days. As he forced his arm under her shoulders he pulled her close. A small tear freely rolled down her cheek and she swatted it away hoping Jay hadn't noticed. He didn't miss it. "It's ok. You don't have to hold it in any longer." "Thanks Jay," she mustered through fresh falls. Hearing his name reminded him that there was someone besides Jay Terror. No matter how he felt about society and those that doubted him, there was a human within. Someone certain people would never see.
