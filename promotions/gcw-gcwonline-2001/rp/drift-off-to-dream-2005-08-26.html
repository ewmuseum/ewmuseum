Drift Off To Dream

26 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/26/2005 As the wind blew, shutters smacked against the wall outside his bedroom window. Awakening from the deep slumber he had enjoyed the night before, the first thing that came to his mind was his lovely wife. His head ached from the sound of metal clanking against the rocky soil and he couldn’t help thinking how much more peaceful his life would be without her. No further would he need lie about how much he liked the many home cooked meals which he forced himself to eat, not to mention the annoying activities he allowed her to partake in. Pulling the covers from his body, from behind them he swung his legs, draping them over the side of the bed. He wiped the sleep from his bloodshot eyes and rubbed his aching stomach, which both were irritating reminders of last night’s partying. He tried to forget the pain; thinking of how much fun it had been, a smile briefly found itself upon his face. Suddenly the joyful smirk was replaced by the lingering effects of a hang over. He slowly rose from the bed and the rolling in his gut continued. He moved forward pausing momentarily along the way, trying not to puke before reaching the rest room. Before he stared face to face at himself in the vanity mirror, he stopped to meet the morning son. Looking out a small octangular window which provided half the illumination to the dimly lit loft, he studied the surroundings below. He starred at her as she dug in what his wife claimed to be a flower garden. He couldn’t help to pray that she would break a nail or maybe worse. Thanks to the plastic handles of her tools, his wish for the worse wasn’t about to be answered at that moment. For all of her horticulture creativeness, the only thing he had to show was a head ache that not even Excedrin could help and a stomach ache from the night before. For now his only hope was that somehow her green thumbs would end up with a large blister along with the rest of her fingers. He found himself wondering how difficult it would be for her to work in the flowers with blood blisters on her hands. At that moment he remember how he once never had to gets his hands dirty. Perhaps it was from the fact he had been privileged and had always been spoiled during his youth. Then again maybe it was the fact he had better things do to then to run his fingers through potting-soil and Miracle-Grow. He always seemed to be in some city, in some gym, on some mat, wiping the floor with another unknown amateur wrestler. Now in his late-twenties, it was still hard for him to adjust to not being able to come and go as he pleased. Often his friends kidded him about how they could never imagine him ever doing anything without conferring with her first. And each time his peers ridiculed him, his anger grew more and more only to be replaced by the hell his parents had put him through. But that was a story that not even he wish to reflect upon at this moment. All he ever asked from his wife was to have a cappuccino, a bagel, at the least maybe some pancakes waiting on him when he awoke from a good night’s sleep. He stole her away from poverty and made her a queen in the mansion they once lived in; having breakfast ready was the least a Cinderella who refused maid service could do for her Prince Charming. Perhaps he was remembering the days gone by of when the maid or butler answered to his ever beckon and call. Reality here lately was a bowl of cornflakes and a cold cup of black coffee. That is if he was lucky. For all the times he cursed the fact he was no longer single, at this moment the way her sweat-drenched t-shirt clung to her bosom quickly made him forget it all. While he disliked the fact she was involved in a variety of rural activities, when she slipped on mini-skirt and they were dancing the night away at some club, she was the belle of the ball and he was the envy of every guy in the room. Wrapping a terrycloth robe around himself, he made his way down the stairs and to the rear French doors standing wide open. He hesitated, trying to decide whether or not he should even question her actions. Usually he came up on the loosing end of the argument. Indeed he was given the right to speak his mind, but yet he never walked away with the satisfaction of winning the debate. He stared intently at her, while she continued to work away in the small backyard garden. “Honey, what the hell are you doing?” he called out to her. “I’m just pruning the tulips,” she answered. ??pruning tulips? What the hell?” he thought to himself. “Nice to see you finally decide to craw out of bed!” she yelled back to her husband. As she removed the yellow gardening gloves and stood up, he studied his wife intently, before turning and making his way back towards the refrigerator. “Why didn’t you hire a gardener to do that” It would be a lot easier and given her more time for other things,” he questioned himself, while grabbing a gallon of orange juice from the refrigerator. She stood in the door frame, unlacing the soil stained white tennis shoes, “what would you have me do instead” Spend all my time playing tennis at the country club? Or much worse spending thousands at Macy’s?” she asked, halfway uncertain whether she actually would rather be doing such. Surprised by the fact his lovely wife was now standing behind him in the kitchen, he laughed, “When in Rome, one must do as Romans.” “Ever hear the saying you can take a gal out of the country. But you can never take the country out of a gal?” she asked scoffing at his suggestion that she become more like the socialite his mother was. Suddenly the sound of an alarm clock rung out? “Golden Boy” Jake Summers lay in his hotel room bed at the Los Angeles Hilton. He rolled over and reached across his body with his left arm. She always preferred to lie on the left side of the bed and she usually got her way. Reaching across himself he felt for her closeness. “Faith!” he yelled out. She was not there. She was never there. He wiped the sleep from his eyes and rolled back to his right and swung his legs over the side of the bed. As he rested his elbows on his knees, burying his face in the palms of his hands, a tear slowly crept down his face falling from his chin. With all the little things about her that irritated him, at this moment the wetness on his face reminded him of how greatly his missed her. Sadly enough the memories they once shared was drifting away day by day.
