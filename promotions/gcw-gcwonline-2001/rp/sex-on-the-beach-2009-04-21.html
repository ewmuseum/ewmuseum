Sex on the Beach

21 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 21, 2009 He was a cigarette deep when she caught his eyes. A dark blue top and jean skirt covered her thin frame showing off her long legs. It wasn't the outfit that caught his attention though, it was the way she flicked her hair away from her sky blue eyes. Eyes he hadn't quite forgotten. As she stood in the center of the floor it was obvious she wasn't there to get a drink; the way she turned gave other hints to her true reason for wandering in. As she spun to face Jay, that reason became painfully obvious. Her steps were short but her purpose was written on her face giving her walk a nasty snarl. Jay simply took another drag of toxins in. "Jackie, you remember?" He nodded casually and pulled the cigarette loose from his teeth. The smoke spilled out of an 'o' in his lips and filtered toward her. "Take a seat." Sitting, "Surprised?" "A bit, yeah." She grinned slightly, enough to show her stainless teeth. "I'm not stalking you you know, just needed to talk to you. About all that and -" He interjected, "You want a drink?" Taken back, "Uh - sure." He stood taking his cigarette along. Did she want a vodka cranberry? Rum and coke maybe? Scotch? Possibly a pina colada? Maybe she was the adventurous type and preferred a fuzzy navel. Perhaps sex on the beach to lighten the mood some. As he approached the bar he placed his elbows on the countertop and leaned over landing nearly face deep in the bartender's cleavage. "Sex on the beach," he decided, "and another whiskey sour." She seemed OK with the gesture considering the twenty balled up in his hand and went to work. Jay on the other hand drew in another dose of nicotine and turned to face Jackie in her seat. She was a dainty girl, the same dainty girl that nearly sent him to a cornstalk death a week prior. Still, there was something odd that he liked about her. The way she spoke her mind and the way she liked to talk. The things she said, the quantity of things she said - it was all different than the usual girl he had known. That, if anything, was enough to give her a minute or two. "Sex on the beach and whiskey sour for you sir," she said happily. Jay turned again and smiled handing off the twenty. "Keep the change." She was gracious and he was on his way. As he approached the table Jackie raised an eyebrow in his direction. "What'd you get me?" "Don't worry, you'll like it." "And if I don't?" "Let me rephrase. Most girls like it." "Don't tell me this is one of those ploys to secretly knock me unconscious so you can sleep with me in your hotel room." "If it was I wouldn't give it to you here. I'd invite you over first." "Really?" He grinned "It'd be kinda' odd to see me carrying a woman out of here placing her in my back seat, yeah?" "Fair enough." She took a sip through the straw and immediately Jay knew he had hit this one hard on the nose. She tried to hold back her teeth. "Ok, this is good." "I've had practice," he said sipping his own drink. "You're not insinuating anything by this, are you?" "That depends. Is it the message you're reading?" "I'm not sure." "Then probably not. Just drink it and let loose." She took another sip through the straw before leaning back against the cushioned seat and crossing her arms. "I'm sorry if I'm bothering you. I just heard that you were most likely here." "Was it the deskman?" "He was helpful, yeah." "You do know that Washington and California aren't really in the exact neighborhood as each other, right?" "Trust me, the trip here gave me enough insight on that. I just needed to talk, you know? About the accident and the consequences and -" "You sure can handle a mouthful." Seemingly slightly offended, "Excuse me?" "Your words," he started before drinking again, "you fit a lot in one breath." "Oh, right. It's a habit of mine." "Of course." He fingered out another cigarette and offered her one too. She declined. "I think that once you start you never stop. Doesn't matter how much you want to, it's just impossible." "Smoking you mean?" He lit the tobacco, "Yeah." "Never tried it. My mom died of lung cancer so I had my reasons I guess." He inhaled and blew the smoke away from her, "Sounds like a good excuse, sure. Shitty way to go out of you ask me." "It catches up to you I guess. The smoking that it. I think she was at two packs a day when we lost her." Jay nodded and sipped again, "I'd say I'm at that mark this week. Just started again after the crash." "I'm really sorry." "It's not you. Work stuff has me going again." "What do you do?" "What did you want to talk about?" She was catching onto his game quickly and thought nothing of his occupation. Instead, she tried to form as few words as possible to explain her situation. "I wanted to talk to you about the accident and all. I mean, you kinda' just up and left without anyone knowing you were going." "Didn't someone handle things?" "He gave us a Caldera Enterprise business card." "Then it's in good hands. Just make the call." "I did." "And?" She leaned forward lifting her drink into her hand, "And they said that things would be taken care of." Jay mimicked her action and took a heavy gulp of his whiskey. "Then what's the matter? It'll be handled fine." "Fine means that when we go to court I'm sitting against you and some guys in suits saying that I'm a mad woman driver. That's not exactly the best thing for me considering my automotive past." "You think I'll oppose you?" "You wouldn't?" He leaned closer, "Jackie. I flew away from the wreck in a helicopter. What makes you think I want to sit in a court room?" She grinned and put her hands on the table. Her nails were a radiant red. "You can have someone else do it. Just like last week. I know someone's going to screw me out of things." "You know, you worry too much." "Of course I'm worried though. You just don't get it." "It's a female thing." "That's what you said about bad drivers." He released another cloud of smoke from his mouth, "And I stand by it." "You're an ass. You know that, right?" "I've been told that, sure. Doesn't make me hate myself though." "You know what, I shouldn't have even tried to fix this. Thanks for the drink." She gathered her handbag and took a last gulp of her drink before standing up. Jay grabbed her arm before she could move away from the table however. "Sit back down. I'll get you another drink." "Why? So you can down-talk me and my driving some more?" "Just sit." She looked into his eyes and reasoned with herself that things would be ok. Placing her handbag back on the seat, she took her place across from him again. "What are you drinking?" "You want it?" "Depends." "It's alcohol. Here." He slid it her way with the napkin underneath. She lifted the glass to her lips and let the whiskey dump down her throat. He watched her gulp it. "You a whiskey girl?" "I can be." "Finish it. It'll give me more time to my cigarettes." "You know you really should try and hold back." "You a doctor?" She turned her head to the side giving him an 'are you serious' look. He nodded and kept the pack in his pocket. "About the case, don't worry about it. I'm not in any rush to stike you down." "You swear?" "I just said it. That's good enough for anyone, trust me." "And what makes you so high and mighty with your word?" "Just something about me I guess. Not sure really. People just tend to hate me for the things I say and in that sense I figure they're jealous because I'm always right. You know?" "Not really." "Let's just say I'm influential and know the right people." She took another swig of whiskey in. "You're cocky is what you are." Jay looked away for a second at the TV. A commercial for Terrific Ben Tea was playing. "You know what, I get that a lot. Fact is, you have to be a little cocky to make it anywhere." "How do you figure?" "What do you do for a living?" "I design clothing actually. Like on that show Project Runway." He laughed a bit, "Really?" "What's wrong with that?" "Nothing's wrong with it. I just kinda' saw you doing that for some reason. Not really sure what it was about you but it seemed right." "Thank you?" "Are you good at it?" "I like to think so." He fixed his seating becoming taller and straighter. "Then you're cocky too." "I don't tell the world I'm amazing though. I keep it to myself in my head where no one else has to hear from me. "That doesn't mean you're not cocky. It just makes you closet cocky. Me on the other hand, I'm open and straight about it." "Sometimes that's obnoxious though." "Am I obnoxious?" A bit confused she shrugged, "I'm not sure." "I'll take it." It was silence that played out next as Jackie swirled the last of Jay's Whiskey sour around before taking it down in one swig. She wiped her mouth with a napkin and looked off toward the TV herself. "You want to get out of here?" "And go where?" "The hotel room." Jokingly, "So you can slip me hard drugs and have me pass out?" "Sound like a good night to you?" "Yeah, how about no." He stood from the booth and helped her out making sure she didn't forget her handbag. "Really. Just somewhere else to be and something else to do." "What's something else?" He led the way to the bar double doors as she followed. "Who knows. You never know what can happen in a hotel room." "Are you hitting on me?" "Just trying to talk to you," he said pushing open the doors. A street came into focus before their eyes holding cars and people of all shapes and types. Jay held the door as she walked past taking the lead toward the hotel. "Do you do this often?" "Do what? Go to my hotel room?" "With girls. Like from the bar." "I've been known to do it, sure. Not my best trait I'd say though." "And what's that?" "I can usually hold a conversation or I can piss people off. I'd vote for one of those personally." She looked back while keeping her steps fluent in front of him. The hotel was only a block away and already they were three quarters of the way there. "You never told me what you do for your job." "I fight." "Like boxing?" "Wrestling actually." She grinned again flashing her pearly whites, "You mean that fake stuff on TV?" "Fake huh?" He pulled up the bottom of his tee-shirt exposing a scar across his stomach. "I didn't paint that on because I liked the way it looked." Jackie cringed for a second, "How'd you do that?" "Things happen out there. People don't realize that I'm more than some guy who can talk into a microphone. I put myself on the line." "I can tell." He lowered his shirt and they both stopped at the corner waiting for the crossing man to turn green. "What's your name in the ring?" "Jay Terror." She laughed briefly, "Terror? Do you wear a mask or something?" "Let's just say I'm not the guy you want to be across the ring from." "You really are cocky." "Again. I know." The man turned green and they walked across the street along with a family of four. At the other side the young boy of the group looked up at Jay. "Dad look, it's Jay Terror." The dad looked Jay's way and then at Jackie. "I recommend getting away from him as fast as possible. The guy's bad news." "And you shouldn't have had dinner tonight guy," came Jay's response. Before the argument could go further the man's wife pulled him away passing on a pair of evil eyes to Jay. Jackie on the other hand looked shocked by the outburst. "Really?" "I told you I was pretty good at pissing people off." "People hate you?" "People hate that they're not me." "Wow. That really takes the cake for cockiest comment of the night." "Where do I accept my award?" She shook her head and looked Jay in the eyes. "I'm sorry but I think I'm going to go back to my hotel room for the night. Not sure I could fit in bed with your ego." "Really?" "Yeah." "Let me get a number then." "Why would you need that?" "For the court case thing. You know, for when that all goes down." "You're going to show up now?" "Did I say I was?" She shrugged, "I don't know anymore." "Just give me your number. Don't worry about the case." He pulled his phone from his pocket and opened it waiting for the number. Instead, she took it from him to do it herself. "Jackie Bates. Just give me a call when things are getting settled." "Of course." He took his phone back and let it slide down into his pocket. "I'll talk to you later then." "Yeah, I guess so." "Sorry about things." "It happens." With that she turned and walked off. Jay found his cigarettes and lit one outside his building. The stairs leading to the main entrance gave him a seat. He watched her walk away in her short jean skirt and wondered what was going through her mind at that point in time. "Fuckin' women. " Then again, he didn't know if it mattered.
