The World on His Shoulders

1 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10.01.2009 “Jacob McKail sucks!” McKail rubbed his eyes and sighed, but made sure to keep on walking. The Bronx in January was bitterly cold and people often thought of inventive ways to warm themselves up. These people wouldn’t think twice about getting into a fistfight with a professional wrestler. “We HATE you McKail!” He ducked into a liquor store and directed the clerk to the larger bottle of Jack Daniels in his shelves. The store clerk eyed his customer warily and then grinned. “Don’t I recognise you, chief?” McKail shook his head and handed over his money. “Doubt it.” The clerk took the money and as he did a wave of recognition washed over his face. McKail sighed, there could be only one response from here. “Jacob McKail, huh?” The clerk asked, rather needlessly. “I don’t think I wanna serve a scumbag like you.” McKail shook his head. “Too late,” he replied, waving the full bottle of liquor in the air. “You’ve got my money and I’ve got the Jack Daneils. Transactions already complete, genius.” “Hey, fuck you buddy!” The store clerk yelled, reaching for a baseball from under his counter. He waved the damned thing in the air and used the tip of it to point square at McKail. “You get the hell out here!” McKail shook his head and took his leave. He was the most hated man in the world right about now, and for what? He hadn’t done anything, but in the world of propaganda and lies that didn’t much matter. He thought he could handle it. He thought he could carry the hatred of every damn person who gave the notion a thought and take it. He thought he was strong enough to not care about what anyone else thought about him, but he was wrong. It was getting to him and he didn’t know whether he could take much more it. He unscrewed the top off the Jack Daniels and started drinking right then and there in the streets. It was the only thing he had left to do.
