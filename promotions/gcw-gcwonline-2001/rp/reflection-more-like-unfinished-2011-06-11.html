Reflection:More Like Unfinished...

11 June 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06/11/11 OOC: I'm really not feeling anything long after my first work week. I started this piece and intended to flesh it out a lot more, but oh well. Sorry to the others for the lack of everything. --- Even while he stood over Rich Rollins with a steel chair in hand, Jay Terror didn’t feel like himself. He was fatigued, empty inside, and slightly sick to his stomach as he watched Rollins plead. Jay wanted no part of the flashing lights or the noisy crowd, but he had no choice. He was a monster at heart, a well-oiled machine meant to make the world stir. Sucking all the air he could into his black lungs, he swung away at the hall of famer’s worn knee making sure that each shot left a lasting impression. He glanced at the spider hoping that she’d intervene, and when she finally did Jay released the air he’d been holding back. He ignored the middle fingers, the redneck remarks, and the vomit sitting on his tongue. The walk up the ramp was long and Jay forced every ounce of his being into making his face show power and greed. He tried to look menacing on the top of the ramp, but it wasn’t easy with the thought of bile breaking the levee he’d created. When he finally reached the backstage area, he found the nearest bathroom. He staggered in, hands on the walls, and crawled the last few inches to the first urinal within reach. His fingernails scraped the sticky tiles as his mouth opened and a stream of bile and filth fell loose. His eyes watered and his abs crunched, but the only thing on his mind was doubt. He had been wondering how long he could keep up the charade before people found out. He wondered how long he would be eligible to work for GCW. On top of it all, he wondered why it mattered. Maggie had said on many occasions that Jay had already made enough money to leave. He’d earned the television title, two world titles, and even a Just Wrestling championship. It was a career to end on, one to be proud of, but Jay couldn’t fathom leaving the game. He loved the feeling of making a crowd roar and the bliss of hearing his name over the loud speaker. With his health on the line though, it all seemed to fall away. Maggie’s opinion sounded right whether his stubborn edge was willing to admit it or not. Jay stretched his legs forward and fell onto his backside. He stared blankly at the porcelain in front of him and wiped his mouth with his index and middle finger. Beneath the smeared mess of vomit in the urinal, Jay could see a feint reflection of himself. His jaw looked thin, his eyes baggy, and his forehead clammy. Jay sighed and leaned until his back rested comfortably on the bathroom floor. His eyes were shut when he heard the door creak open and a voice come inside. “Everything alright in there, Jay?” Terror rubbed his eyes with the palms of his hands before placing them behind his shaved head. “Yeah. Just another minute,” he replied. “Do you need anything?” He had a list of things that he needed, but he refrained from running through it. “Nope,” he said instead. Jay could hear the fans shouting again and he wasn’t sure why. He listened though and tuned out his thoughts for a long moment while trying to believe that he was the cause for the uproar. He soon tried to imagine a world without the gasps and jeers. He tried to find a place where reactions were refined and polite. The longer he pictured it, the more he hated the concept. Although he was risking his well-being as a part of the GCW roster, Jay knew that there were no other options.
