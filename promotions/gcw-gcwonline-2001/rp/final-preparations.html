Date: Today “It is not the mountain we conquer, but ourselves.” ? Sir Edmund Hilary. For the third time in his career, Seymour Almasy stood at the base of Mount Gagazet, the nearly-mythical mountain in Final Fantasy X that posed the final challenge to summoners on the journey to Zanarkand. Or, as it was in the real world, a small mountain peak somewhere in the Green Mountains of Vermont. He was somewhat surprised to realize that he remembered the place very, very well. “So much of my blood has been spilled here,” he whispered, to himself, looking nervously at the mountaintop. “And yet, the first two matches now feel like only previews of what I am in for now. Tsunami Oni kidnapped the love of my life’and Joshua Dunn challenged my spirit, but Rich Rollins challenges everything. He challenges my entire being.” That was the point. Rollins hated Seymour, and Seymour hated Rollins. That was the way it was, and the way it likely would always be. But that hatred was of a largely professional nature. Almasy would never wish death on the Renegade, and he had a feeling that Rollins didn’t want him dead any time soon either. Looking up again, Seymour saw something he didn’t expect. Someone was on Mount Gagazet. She wasn’t, of course, a summoner. She was a skier, descending the mountain at rapid speeds. And behind her came another. And another. And another. The Final Fantasy couldn’t help wincing. After all, Mount Gagazet, a site nearly sacred to him and his past, was now a tourist hotspot. The signs of it were all around, from the skiers to the cars in a nearby parking lot, to the rustic lodge where Seymour was, in fact, staying on this, the night before he and Rollins would do battle. Suddenly, Seymour Almasy felt incredibly sad. He knew that he didn’t really have a right to, after all, this was a mountain. A site that had been rented for every Mount Gagazet Deathmatch. It wasn’t REALLY the Final Fantasy mountain, a fact he’d realized a year and a half ago. But at the same time, he didn’t like that the mountain was being used for play. His blood likely still coated rocks in obscure, craggy regions of the summit. And the memories that the mountain held, of both elation and disappointment It shouldn’t be used for this. It simply shouldn’t. But it was. That was reality, and there was no fighting it. “Ah, Seymour!” The voice was immistakeable. He hadn’t seen the man in a few weeks, but even so, that slightly high-pitched voice couldn’t be forgotten. It was Jonathan Calver, VP of Talent Relations for GCW. “Mr. Calver,” Seymour responded, turning to face the suit-clad executive. “It’s been a while. How are things?” “I can honestly say I’ve been better,” came the response. “I’m not exactly here with good news today.” “Oh?” Seymour sighed. There were many sorts of bad news Jonathan could bring him. Well, no use in postponing it. “What’s wrong,” Seymour asked, clearly worried. “GCW wants you to sign a waiver,” he said, shaking his head. “in case something happens to you on the mountain, they don’t want to be liable.” “I’ve got medical insurance. But that’s still a dick thing to do.” Truth be told, it had the fingerprints of Steven Caldera all over it. The shrewd businessman liked to maximize his investments in talent, and, truth be told, he’d heard rumors that the head of Caldera Enterprises, Inc. wasn’t especially fond of the concept of Mount Gagazet. Not that Seymour could really BLAME Caldera, mind you. “I know,” came the slightly annoyed reply from Calver, “but you know Caldera almost as well as I do.” “So,” Almasy reasoned, “if I don’t sign this piece of paper that you have for me, the match doesn’t happen?” Calver nodded. “Yes, that’s right.” Almasy let his head fall into his hands. Caldera expected him to sign it, as did Mr. Calver. To not sign it would be to cause a major blow to the promotion, as one of the matches NC-17 was being sold on would not occur. At the same time, though, it would make Seymour feel a lot better. He wasn’t just some plaything of Steven Caldera. He was a person. A human being. “He’s got me over a barrel, you know,” Seymour finally said, breaking his silence. “He knows Rollins and I want at one another on this mountain. He’s counting on that to make us sign, isn’t he?” Calver nodded. There was no point in saying anything, really, considering that Seymour had essentially hit the nail on the head. “Heh. Now ain’t that a bitch?” The words were foreign, at least foreign coming from Almasy’s mouth. He was usually so well spoken that it caught Calver off guard to hear him using such base language. “I’m sorry,” Seymour explained. “I’m just on edge. This is a big match, you know.” “Damn right it is,” came Jonathan’s equally informal response. “It’s the reason you came here.” Almasy nodded. Rich Rollins was the primary reason he’d signed with Global. He wanted the chance to go one on one with the Renegade, to settle business from the UWF. To go to war. To take no quarter. To prove himself the best. “You have a pen,” Almasy asked. “Of course,” Calver replied, handing it and the document to Seymour, who immediately signed. “There. All nice and legal now.” Jonathan nodded. “Be careful up there, Seymour. Rollins wants your head on a silver platter.” Seymour laughed then. That was what everyone thought, that Rich wanted to destroy Seymour, and leave him a broken and crippled mass in a snowdrift somewhere. But Seymour knew otherwise. He knew the PTC Global Champion’s real aim. “He doesn’t want to kill me,” Seymour told Jonathan, a slight smile on his face. Jonathan gazed back at him, somewhat thunderstruck. “What DOES he want then?” The ease of the Final Fantasy’s answer was somewhat chilling in its accuracy. “He wants my legacy. And if he beats me in my own match, he’s well on his way.”
