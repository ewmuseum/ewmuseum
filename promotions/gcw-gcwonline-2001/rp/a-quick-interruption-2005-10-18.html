A Quick Interruption.

18 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Oct 18, 2005 So I was sitting at the table the other night, cutting off a fat piece of a freshly grilled, one inch thick, rib eye steak, when my cell phone starts to ring. I sighed out loud, staring at the medium rare chunk of meat on the end of my fork, as I pulled the Motorola Razr Black from it’s clip; which happened to be fastened to the belt around my waist. I flipped it open and glanced over the name on the caller ID. Jayce Bradley. Only my good buddy, Jayce, would have the perfect spot on timing to interrupt me during my dinner. I pressed the talk button and placed the phone against my ear. “Hello?” It did not take long for Jayce to get started, ranting and raving about how Varsity could get a shot at the Tee Vee title before he did, and how his manager was the one who set it all up. I wanted to laugh, but I held back. “He just got lucky, is all,” I told him, my mind on the meal resting on the plate before me. “You don’t want that title anyway. You’ll end up just like Savige. Stuck in mid-card hell.” He laughed when I told him that, and I could just picture him shaking his head from side to side as he tried to regain his composure. The laughter subsided though, and Jayce let me know how he felt. He was a bit upset. He wrestled great against Macavity Frost, surprising quite a few people who thought he never stood a chance. Then he was left off of a card. Now Varsity has a Tee Vee title shot in his debut match. I kind of felt for the guy. Until he started blaming me for his problems. I almost told him to go screw himself, until I realized what he meant. It was my fault. I made him look professional, I made him act a certain way, and I made him cater to what I felt Gee See Double You expected from him. I should have just let him be himself. I should have told him to go off on Frost and make a point out of proving what the rest of the roster should expect from him. I held him back. I needed to make sure things changed. I needed to make sure I did not control every aspect of Jayce’s personality and let him be himself. I knew exactly what to do, and I told him. He loved the idea, and said he would be more than happy to go through with it. He hung up the phone, as did I. Then I glanced over my steak, licking my lips as I anticipated the flavors that were going to fill my mouth in a few seconds. It was going to be great, just like the plan we devised. It was going to work. It had to. My rep’err’his reputation depended on it. What? You didn’t think I was just going to tell you what I told him. Did you? HAHA! You are too much. All of you are just going to have to wait and see, because this has to be a total surprise. Stay tuned, folks, the game’s just about to start. Now if you’ll excuse me, I have more important matters to attend to.
