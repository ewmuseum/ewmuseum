Imprisoned in Paradise: Begin Again

22 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/22 Another WorldWide, another win. Like the prior two opponents, Typhoid Mary had put up a difficult fight, but Seymour Almasy had reigned victorious. Some had considered Almasy done after his last disappearance from PTC. They’d said he didn’t have what it took to compete anymore. The Final Fantasy hoped 3-0 against three of GCW’s best would silence those thoughts. “Enough of wrestling,” he told himself, climbing up a set of stairs. “Today is about a new beginning.” New York City had been Seymour’s home for years. In happier times, he had lived with his former wife in a penthouse apartment, an apartment that she had taken when they split. Try as he might to leave the city, though, Seymour couldn’t bring himself to. And so, he finally had to re-establish his place of residence in the place he loved. Anticipation rose as he finally reached the third floor, fumbling with the key in his pocket. He had seen the empty apartment before, of course, but this would be his first time seeing it as the official owner of the property. The key managed to slide easily into the lock, and Seymour opened the door, taking a look at his new dominion. “How”.empty.” Bare, white walls were the order of the day inside Seymour’s new home. The basics were there, sure, such as cabinets built into the walls, and a stove, but little else. Closing the door behind him, he took a tour of the rooms. Again, the bare necessities were in every room, but little more. There wasn’t even a chair for Seymour to sit in. He shrugged, and simply walked back out to the living room. The lack of chair didn’t really bother him very much, so he sat down on the wooden floor, Indian-style, and began the long process of pondering what to do with the space. Wrestling had been good to Seymour. He could afford to furnish his house well, and in truth, he already had bought much of what was to be brought into the place. But the goal was to create a new beginning, while retaining what he loved about the apartment where he had lived happily for so long. Before he could get too far, though, the seemingly omnipresent sound of a ringing cell phone cut him off. The tinny notes formed a surprisingly decent rendition of “One Winged Angel”, the final boss theme from Final Fantasy VII. Not surprisingly, it was the music Seymour had assigned to calls from Jonathan Calver. For a few seconds he debated even bothering answering the damned thing. The VP of Talent Relations called at least ten times a day. Missing one wouldn’t hurt much. Unfortunately, the ringing continued. The one minute loop of song had already played once, and was beginning to play again. Clearly, Calver was not hanging up. With a familiar sigh of defeat, he answered the phone. “Hello,” he asked, almost disbelieving that Calver had stayed on the phone so ridiculously long waiting for him to pick up. “Sorry to interrupt you,” the exciteable man said, clearly not sorry for the interruption at all, “but I’ve got big news! You’ve got a match this week on WorldWide!” Sitting in his living room, on a bare wooden floor, with his knees slowly starting to cramp, that news barely registered a blip on Seymour’s radar. “Um, Jonathan,” he replied, trying to put it as nicely as he could, “I don’t know if you’ve noticed, but I’ve had a match every week I’ve been here.” “I know, I know,” came the response, “but this is a BIG match!” Again, Seymour was unimpressed. “I faced the reigning US Champion,” Seymour said, firmly, “and beat him. I faced a former three time GCW World Champion, and beat him. I faced the manager of the World Champion, and beat her. So unless you’re telling me Caldera’s giving me LeStatt for the World Title, I fail to see how this is a big match.” At that, the voice on the other end fell silent for a few moments. That was a rarity for Jonathan, Seymour knew. “Well,” he began, a bit less excitedly, “it’s not LeStatt, but it’s close.” “Borealis again, so I can win the US title legitimately?” “No, not exactly?” By this point, Seymour was starting to get very, very annoyed. “Then who am I facing” Tell me, already!” “Trey Tremendous!” Jonathan exclaimed, happily. “Isn’t this great!” Seymour was somewhat less pleased. “Wonderful,” he managed. “I’ve got Rollins and FallOut, and a guy coming off snapping against the World Champion the show before.” “Don’t you realize the opportunity this is, Seymour?” Calver asked. “Beating Tremendous and Rollins back to back would--? “Keep me exactly where I am,” Seymour said, a bit disgustedly. “I beat the US Champion my first week here, and got no title match. Caldera knows my history with winning championships. He doesn’t want me anywhere near a title match.” “But--? “Jonathan, I appreciate the heads up, but I’m in my new apartment, trying to figure out what to do with it. I’ll call you later.” He hung up without waiting for a response. That was quite enough of his professional life, especially on a rare day off. With Jonathan tuned out, he could go back to pondering what he would do with his new home. It was nowhere near as big as the apartment he shared with Laura, and wouldn’t have half of the amenities. That was mainly due to the GCW schedule, which had him on the road more often than any company he’d ever worked for in his life. Like all the other fruits of his career, his schedule meant that he wouldn’t be able to truly enjoy the apartment. “Such,” he told himself, “are the sacrifices of fame.” Almasy snorted, loudly. He didn’t buy that shit. Not for a minute. Wrestling had given him so much, when he thought about it, only to take everything back in return. Titles were temporary. Even the UWF World Title, which he held for nearly six months, was taken from him. Gold came and went. Tournament victories were eternal, true, but new ones arose every year. But what wrestling had stolen from him was irreplaceable. It was all so pointless, really, the titles and the tournaments. The career. All of it. He would have traded it all gladly to have someone care. He’d felt it momentarily, when he’d been speaking with Eva. His total lack of friends in GCW meant that the former Television Champion was a welcome distraction from all of his professional and personal difficulties. That, and she was rather easy on the eyes? Seymour shook his head. While she was certainly pretty, he was NOT going to throw himself on the pile of GCW superstars which seemed to follow her around and try to convince her of their unique positive qualities. He was the Final Fantasy. A good guy. He wasn’t the sort to do such a thing. In truth, his life was pretty much like his apartment. Empty, but with lots of potential. “I’m going to start anew here,” he said, looking around at the plain white walls. “First step, paint the walls a different color. Second step, get everything in here. Third step, get a life. Simple.” It was time for Seymour to move on. This apartment would be the symbol of the new Almasy, an equally dominant figure in the wrestling world as he had always been, and the rebirth of his personal life. He would rise again. He had to. Wallowing in his own self-pity wasn’t helping anymore. But before he rose again, he needed some pizza. After all, one couldn’t drag himself out of the ashes of a failed marriage without some nourishment. He opened his cell phone to order out, only to see the damning words on his screen. “You have 25 missed calls.” All were from the same number. Somehow, Seymour wasn’t hungry anymore.
