The Breakdown of the Bicameral Mind: Chapter 1

25 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 25, 2009 It was like a jungle inside of his mind. It was a place where only the fittest had any chance of survival. It was no longer safe being locked inside that place. He had tried as hard as possible to reclaim himself or at least the small part of him that had been lost, but while inside he found someone else entirely. Someone he had been hiding from himself for much too long. For too long he had become a victim. It was no longer about actions. He only worked with reactions. It is understandable if one looked deep into the abyss that had been formed in his life. It was a bottomless pit where no light was visible, like a black hole. It was not just a place where the lights had been turned off, but somewhere were lights never existed. The blackest place of them all. He had become a victim of circumstance and that had never been in his make-up. His DNA had never allowed him to be just in the sidelines, a benchwarmer in his own life. That was until he met her. Besides her, he had been powerless. She was none other than Death herself. How can you compare yourself with the end of all life, the only person that would be there at the end? She was eternal. He wasn’t. That simple fact was too much for his already fragile brain. Knowing for a fact that his time and deeds were merely fleeting broke him. It broke him into pieces and in ways that he had never thought possible. He was in a dark place. ---- Months ago… It was a dark place. The only light that could be seen inside came from the light post just outside the window as it sneaked through the corners of the black plastic trash bags that covered the glass. The room was damp and seemed abandoned. Trash was strewn all over the place in formless mounds of indescribable crap. Cockroaches and all manner of creepy crawlers had taken over that domain. There was an unmoving mound of cotton and flesh on the bed that seemed to be the only thing resembling a human being. The mound began to slightly move as its motor functions began to work. There were now sounds that could be heard coming from the mound. Guttural noises of pain and lack of actual sleep. After a few minutes of tossing and turning the actual form of a human being was seen as it tried to get up. “Another day…” The raspy voice hardly seemed human. The man went to the mirror and looked at himself. What he saw was hardly surprising. He had gotten used to seeing the shell of his former self. A hollowed game board piece that moved at the will of the gods. He scratched his unkempt beard and ran the shower. As he stepped inside and washed himself in the crimson water, he couldn’t help but remember all the people he had seen die and all the people he thought he had seen die. Some where the same, but others were not. He had called many times now, but no one would come and fix the blood coming out of the shower head. Everyone he called said it was fine and he had to agree. It was perfectly fine blood. When he had finished cleaning himself off, he walked slowly through the kitchen. The carpet made that really annoying squishing sound as he walked through it. He had learned not to bother with the moss that had begun to grow. It was the next obvious step to the water that had found itself inside of the apartment. There had been no rain outside for a few days, but inside it were constant thunderstorms. Partly cloudy was a good day inside that place. As he served himself the usual morning meal of cereal and scotch, he heard something that had not been heard in that place for a long time. There was a knock. He heard it louder every time. Getting used to ignoring the knocking on the door was not the hard part. It was figuring out when it was the delivery that was hard. The knocking was persistent this time. It was no longer just a tap tap tap but it was as if a huge bird was pecking at the door. His movements towards the door were sluggish, but he found his way through the mangroves and to the door. He had always hoped that the rapping on the door would be a talking bird. There was no such luck that day. As he tried to focus on the raven-haired person standing in front of him, all the words that came to his mind where ‘Nevermore’. He couldn’t help but to stifle a chuckle. His body instinctively moved out of the way as the woman walked inside. There was a faint recollection of who she was deep in the recesses of his mind, but he couldn’t quite place her. The last woman he had seen who had jet black hair was Death. He didn’t want her to visit. “You’ve really let yourself go,” the woman said as she picked the less dirty place she could find to sit down. “Yeah… I guess.” He kept scratching his beard and trying to defeat his drunken brain to figure out who this woman was. “It’s been too long since we last saw each other. We had some good times together, you and I. When Burton and Nick told me about your current state, I had to see it with my own eyes,” she sort of shook her head in disappointment and sadness as she said it. “Vero! I think the black hair kind of threw me off. What happened to your beautiful red hair?” The memories were now running around inside his mind. That red hair used to be his only really big weakness when it came to women. His ex-fiancée really knew how to use it to her advantage. “It was time for a change.” Change was a word that Antonio had just forgotten about inside of the wild cocoon he built for himself. The world around him evolved and he stayed the same. He could see as the tail and fin would slowly turn into feet and the gills into lungs. Not in himself though. His inability to crawl out of the primordial soup and join his fellow men had angered him at first but with a lot of alcohol and cheap hookers, it had been forgotten. Their eyes locked for a few awkward minutes. They hadn’t seen each other in a long while. While Antonio had seen Vero once or twice after she had left him standing at the airport, there was still an unhealed wound close to his heart and hidden deep beneath the flesh. “Why did you leave?” Antonio couldn’t help but blurt it out. It was not that he had forgotten her explanation, but that he never thought it was a really valid one and even though seeing her had been sort of sobering, the alcohol in his body still had some power over him. “I told you--” “I know what you told me. Something about your career or whatever. That wasn’t enough. I want to know what was in your head, what made you broke it off. I thought you were happy.” The frustration in his voice was evident. “I don’t want to talk about this.” The movements of his body seemed super human as he stood up and dashed towards her. His hands shot up grabbed her by the throat. The pressure on her throat was almost enough to kill. Antonio’s eyes had turned from expressing simple regret and sadness, to full blown anger. Smoke came out of his nostrils as they flared with seething rage. For the first time in her life, Vero felt scared. Not only because his former lover was choking her to death, but because she knew that he would probably not stop. She saw it in his eyes. As his hands squeezed tighter and tighter, his body received the raindrops as a sudden rain cloud had formed just over their heads. Thunder roared on top of them, deafeningly. Antonio didn’t say anything as he saw Vero’s face start to turn red. He could feel the life slipping out of her body. --- You could feel the life slipping out of her body as your hands squeezed tighter and tighter. The rush of holding someone’s life in your hands overcame you like a certain kind of ecstasy. It was almost better than sex. Almost. It took you a while to realize what was happening. The pleasure of it all had blinded and numbed you to what was happening. There you were, choking the life out of a woman who you had loved at one point in your life. Your fingers began to slacken their hold until they were just touching her skin. Life and color began to return to Vero’s face as she gasped for air. She coughed a couple of times while you still looked at her and softly caressed the skin of her neck. You stared directly at her, or rather through her. Completely oblivious to the tears that had begun to stream down her face, you just stood there. After a short while, your muscles slacked and you seemed to come back to reality. You fell to your knees and looked at your hands. They would forever been stained. “I need help,” you whispered in your only moment of lucidity in weeks. Instead of running away, Vero sat down besides your slumped body and put her hand on your shoulder. This was nothing new to her having been brought up by an abusive father. She had been hurt worse by people she loved. “Don’t worry. We’ll find help.” You didn’t hear anything else that day. You fell asleep in the same place you were and before long; you heard the thunderous crash of the front door as it was kicked open. You looked up startled, but the only thing you saw where three white blurs who picked you up and got you into a straightjacket. You couldn’t help but look at the room you left behind as you were dragged by your arms towards that little padded room. It was a jungle. Not just of trash and broken down stuff or of trees and weeds but of thoughts and memories. With one last look, you left that place behind for the next poor soul who lost his way. What you left was a haven for anyone who wasn’t sure who he was anymore. --- It took me a while to understand who I really was. But I finally got there. For years I had been living in a fantasy that I created for myself. One where the good guys prevail and the bad guys get their eventual comeuppance. It was a dream, a mirage. The real world wasn’t like a comic book from the 1940s. Most of the time, the bad guys not only prevailed but got away with it. And who is to say who the bad guy is? By what standards do we become judge, jury and executioners when we don’t really know what is going on? Is someone bad because he’s German or Iraqi or Japanese? Or because he will do anything to get what he wants? Because he’s rich? What rule do we use? I was brought to this place because they said I was sick. I was seeing things. I was hearing things. That wasn’t the reason. I knew what the real reason for my one way ticket to dragon town was. It was the violence. I had become a danger to society because I could no longer control the parts of my brain that knew when I was hurting someone that didn’t deserve it… or something like that. Psychobabble was never my strong suit. In my line of work though, how is anyone supposed to know the difference between acceptable violence and the unacceptable kind? I’ve asked myself that question many times while I’ve been trapped here. The answer has never really materialized. It is in this ominous place that I finally started to understand the reasons for my being there. I was incomplete. There was a part of me missing that I needed to complete myself again. I still don’t know what that is, but I’m sure it’s out there somewhere waiting for me to find it. Meanwhile, I’m stuck in this place with all the loons and the pompous doctors who pretend to know what is going on inside my mind better than me. The white walls seem to be closing in every time I look at them. Most of the time I don’t know if I’m standing on the ceiling or on the floor or if the floor is the ceiling or the walls are the floors or the floor is the walls. After all that I have seen, I’m not exactly sure of almost everything. I’m not sure if I really choked Vero that day and almost killed her. I’m not sure if I ever went to that parallel universe or that I really met Death. I get so frustrated. There is no way for me to prove it to these smug doctors. Of course they don’t believe me without proof. Who would? Everyone here seems to think they are Jesus Christ, Napoleon Bonaparte or Elvis. Am I just one of the loons, like the rest of them? I don’t know. I don’t know. --- There was no way for him to know for sure. Some philosophers claimed that you can’t believe anything that your senses tell you, and science has found out that the mind under duress will make up things as it goes along. So, if neither the mind nor the eyes can be trusted, what can you really trust? That was his dilemma. He sat in his room and pondered that very question. The memory of what had happened so vividly came to mind as if it had been the day before. The smell of her hair and the feeling of her flesh were two things Antonio could never forget. Antonio remembered as they sat side by side in the subway, going nowhere for hours as they saw the fleeting lives of mortals like him slowly wasting away. Death was used to it. Antonio was not. Antonio couldn’t help but bang his head against the white cushions on the walls of the room they had stuck him in. The little window on the door opened suddenly and the face of an orderly stared at him with pity. “The doctor wants to see you. Behave.” Even though he hadn’t given them any violence or trouble since he had been there, the orderlies knew that Antonio could fly off the handle at any time and they had to be prepared. Antonio slowly stood up and walked through the door and to the right to where he knew the office of his doctor was. “Good afternoon, Antonio. How are you feeling today?” Antonio couldn’t help but roll his eyes at the fakeness of the doctor’s cheery voice. There was nothing very cheery about his situation. Before he could sit down, the doctor motioned him to stop. Antonio looked up questioningly. “Wait, don’t sit down. I have some good news for you. We are going to take away the straightjacket for a little bit. You have been making a lot of progress and I think you should be given a chance.” That was the best piece of news Antonio had heard in a while. “Also, you have a visitor.” The surprise on Antonio’s face was apparent. He wasn’t really sure what to say. Antonio didn’t think that anyone besides Vero knew what had happened. Nobody would come visit him after what he had done. The orderly helped Antonio out of the straightjacket. With his arms outstretched, he sort of felt like some of his humanity had been handed back to him, like a puppy who had done the correct trick for his master. Instead of feeling grateful, that was how he felt. He walked towards the door to follow the orderly towards the visit room. “I think this might do you a lot of good, to see a familiar face,” the doctor told him as Antonio was out of his office. The names of people who might have come to visit were jumping around inside his head. Some of them he didn’t want to see and others he thought might be of some help to his mental state. When he opened the door he was stopped cold. The familiar feeling of the hair on his arms standing on end and the sudden cold spot around the room were unmistakable. The orderly gave Antonio a little nudge and he sat down. Antonio’s arms were strapped to the table. “You know I’m very sorry about everything, right? I never meant for any of this to happen.” Her voice still had the same effect on him. Antonio couldn’t help but feel at ease around her. Comforted. “Is it my time?” Antonio asked. His voice was hoarse and very low. “Not yet. I had to come though when I heard what had happened to you.” All this time she had been giving him her back. She was looking outside through the small window at the garden of the place. She turned around finally. She didn’t look exactly as he remembered her, but he knew it was her, no matter how she looked in the flesh. There was no mistaking her. “I feel responsible. It was too much of a burden, what I put you through.” She said as she looked at him longingly, knowing that as long as it was her job to do what she did, they could never be anything more. “I think I was messed up even before you came into the picture.” Antonio looked at his hands as he said it. He had never felt more powerless. “Look, Antonio… I just came by to say that I’m sorry. I have to get back to work,” she said as she came closer and grabbed his head with her hands. She kissed him and then she was gone. After a few moments, Antonio couldn’t help but smile. “Don’t worry; we will meet again at some point. Of that is the only thing I can be sure of,” Antonio said to himself. Before he could continue thinking about his encounter, the door was opened and the doctor walked in holding a clipboard and shaking his head. “The problem is more serious than what I had imagined.” He began writing something in his clipboard before he continued. “Who were you talking to just now, if I may ask?” “You wouldn’t believe me if I told you.” “Try me.” “I was talking to Death.” The doctor sighed. “Are you sure of that? Because I’ve been watching on the monitors and you were just talking to yourself. There was no one in this room but you, Antonio.” Antonio looked at the doctor, unbelieving. “She was just here. Why are you trying to play tricks with my mind?” He couldn’t help but struggle against the restraints that held him down. “She was here!” The doctor called for the orderly with a wave of his hand. The big burly man in white came inside with a needle in his hand. “She was here! I spoke to her.” Antonio pulled with every ounce of his strength and broke the strap that held his right hand down. He took a swing at the orderly, who stumbled back after the right hand connected to his face. Antonio kept trying to release his left hand but before long, the doctor had picked up the syringe and stabbed Antonio with it. Antonio felt incredibly drowsy before he stumbled back to his chair. He kept muttering that he had seen her and spoken to her all the time in a very soft and weary voice. The drugs were almost instantaneous in taking him down. As he was being dragged from the room Antonio said one last thing. “I spoke to her… I think… did I?”
