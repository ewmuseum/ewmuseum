Tropic of... Portland?

24 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/24/2006 Denise Rottonbottom smiled almost giddily as she stretched out a small finger in front of her. She giggled with delight as she watched a gaily-colored Costa Rica clearwing butterfly flitter around it. The insect seemed to be sniffing or tasting her fingertip to tell if there was food there. Sensing none, the clearwing fluttered on its merry way, toward the next curious tourist. “Sure and “tis a most beautiful little creature,” Katie smiled as she ruffled Denise’s hair. “Of course, I am rather partial to a different species, but this one is very pretty to look at as well.” “Are monarch butterflies considered a tropical breed?” Percival Rottonbottom asked the Oregon Zoo Tour guide. The zoo was hosting a special display of several new butterfly species being introduced into their Winged Wonders exhibit. “No,” the guide said. “They spend the summer in semi-tropical climates, but return northward in the summer to procreate.” Katie slightly tightened her grip on John’s hand to keep the infant from reaching out and accidentally crippling or destroying a delicate Aglaura olivewing butterfly that flitted too close to him. The boy was fifteen months old now, able to walk upright, but still too young to understand the fragility of these beautiful butterflies. “Is there a danger they might land on us on our way out of here?” she inquired. “Oh yes,” the guide answered with a small smile. “In fact, look over there by the exit, and you’ll see the sign warning our visitors to check their clothes and bodies before they leave, for stowaway butterflies. Often times, people don’t know that butterflies have perched on them, because they’re so light and delicate.” “Like some people I know,” Percival teased lightly, pointing out a butterfly that landed right on Katie’s shoulder without her knowing it. Katie melted into giggles, giving her husband a buss on the cheek and then watching the butterfly lightly crawl over to a small flower on her blouse. “Sorry, me little friend, that flower isn’t real,” Katie smiled, very gently blowing on the butterfly to shoo it off toward where it would find real food’the feeding troughs in the center of the exhibit. A tell-tale growling noise brought a perfectly silly expression to Denise’s face. “Aye, Simon, we shall go get lunch now,” she quipped, lightly rubbing her hand on Katie’s stomach. They all laughed’all except John, that is, who was too young to understand. Even the zoo guide laughed, knowing who Katie was and about her legendary appetite. “So where would you all like to go?” Percival asked, taking out a guide to diners and restaurants for the Portland area. “I think I shall like to go Japanese today,” Katie said. “Sure and I have a hankering for some sukiyaki and perhaps a bit of tempura.” The diminutive Irishwoman was known for a taste in foreign foods, though it wasn’t often she felt like “going Japanese,” as she’d just put it. She had a pronounced dislike of their sake, and she found their green tea more tolerable than palatable. Still, Percival and Denise had no problems with it, and so they all shook hands with the zoo guide and headed over to the exit. Before proceeding through, they did the routine check for stowaway butterflies’one was perching on John’s head, and another on Percival’s back. Gently shooing the delicate creatures away, back toward the center of the exhibit, the Rottonbottoms made their way to the main exit and parking area. “So why the interest in Japanese food today?” Percival asked as the hostess sat the four of them at a table. “Sure and Stuart told me that I would be wrestling Keiko Yoshimoto at WorldWide,” Katie answered. “Didn’t the dossier Stuart gave you about Keiko, say that she was from Korea?” Percival raised his eyebrows a bit. Katie nodded. “Aye, but he said the name was Japanese, and that she grew up in Japan--? Denise quickly chimed in. “Then sure and I think I know what type of restaurant we’ll be having dinner in tonight!” Katie laughed appreciatively and kissed her adoptive daughter on the top of the head. Denise flashed a complacent grin and gave a polite wave to the approaching waiter. Lunch was excellent; even better than the pocket guide said was the “norm” for this particular restaurant. The old saying, “One man’s trash is another man’s treasure” aside, the tempura, sukiyaki and tuna rolls were simply exquisite. Katie chose to drink plain ice water with her sizeable meal, including seconds which consisted mainly of beef teriyaki with saut’ed vegetables and bean sprouts. Katie went light on the tofu, finding it acceptable but not one of her favorites. Denise watched her adoptive mother with a bit of an amused grin. Despite having grown up with the whole Mulvane family and watched Katie eat hundreds of times, she always found watching her, an amusing experience. John, meanwhile, just beginning to eat solid food, was fed small pieces of meat and vegetables off of Katie’s plate. He ate heartily, but showed no signs of a Katie-sized appetite of his own... yet. The restaurant having been in close proximity to Montgomery Street, the Rottonbottoms? next destination was an event by the Actors Conservatory, known simply as “Summer on Stage.” Katie having majored in theater arts in college, always held a keen interest in stage acting. The event allowed tourists to take part in stage lessons and a mock performance of a play. Katie played the part of Margaret in a mock performance of “Much Ado about Nothing,” one of the Shakespearean comedies. Her college experience helped greatly with improvisation and voice intonations, and the troupe was given a rousing ovation. Even reading off of scripts held in their hands, the participants in the mock performance brought the stage to life. “Simply wonderful, Katie,” Percival said proudly, giving her a big hug as she stepped down from the stage. “If you hadn’t gone into wrestling, I’m sure you’d have made it big on Broadway.” “Sure and they still remember me to Herald Square,” Katie retorted with a bemused grin, and both she and Percival chuckled. John, however, was less interested in the whole affair, sitting in his child-seat with a bored look. A yawn from the infant brought a responding giggle from his adoptive sister. “Sure and everyone’s a critic!” Denise quipped. The Monarch Butterfly picked her son up and smothered his cheek with a big smooch. “I still love him,” she said. “For him, I’d go up on a stage. For him, I’d lose both me legs. Sure and for the family... I’d fight any challenge. Even battling dragons.” “So you prepare yourself for a Korean/Japanese opponent by having lunch in a Japanese restaurant,” Percival said, “and yet it’s your Celtic roots, with stories of dragon slayers, that gives you the edge you truly need.” Katie grinned. “Some cultures depict dragons as noble creatures,” she said. “But anything that wants a piece of the Monarch Butterfly, is the “bad guy” in me book. Or the “bad girl,” in Keiko’s case.” Denise nodded. “And in me book too.”
