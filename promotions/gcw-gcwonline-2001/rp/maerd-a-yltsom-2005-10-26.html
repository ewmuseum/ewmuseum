Date: October 26th Okay. I grab a can of coke. Let's begin with my downfall. Something monumental. The ingenuous craft of a master story-teller. Details intricately woven together in a seamless concoction of descriptive adjectives over a cup of steaming tea and a mind orgy. Details. The little things I try to remember but forget more often than the bigger things. I keep notes. Small, yellow post-its that announce things I've heard and remembered at least ten times before. The sentences laugh at me, punctuation pointing an accusing finger as I stare and try to surface the memory on why. My life is built around one word questions. Why? What? When? Where? The notes answer, which, by then, the question makes its grand escape from the bowels of my mind. The notes remind me of the questions, and the questions, of the notes. It sounds like something I'd laugh at as a kid. A circle that keeps redrawing its lines, wandering aimlessly across the notebook paper. Big, cartoon eyes staring back at the artist, wondering when something else will happen. I know it's only a matter of time before the notes blatantly mock me. Crude ink informing me that I should, sometime during the course of the day, take a shi*. Under it, a drawn diagram of a portly cartoon character sitting his nude ass on a cold toliet seat, a smile forming across his lips as he defecates. I nod, attempting to try, forgetting when I get there because I became distracted by the sandwich on the table. I eat the sandwich and end up soiling myself. Of course, I'm exaggerating, but it's a possible future considering my condition. ... What was I talking about before? I shake my head, reaching for the post-it note on the table beside me. I'm shirtless, opting for the comforts of the cool air blowing on my scarred chest ... I'll get to that in a minute. Actually, I probably won't. It's on account of my condition. I unfold the post-it note and digest my hand writing. Tell him about your condition. Him? I see myself in the mirror, shirtless and vulnerable in my constant confusion. My palm is sweaty. So is the phone. I'm on the phone. F***ing hell. I turn the post-it note over, eyes washed in a blank yellow. Turn. Tell him about your condition. Turn. Blank yellow. No ... Eraser marks. Where's the pencil? The writing's now illegible. Blurred and obscured by a dry eraser. Turn. I read it again, phone slipping in my damp palm. Another reminder. I listen for breathing. Shallow. How long had I been on the phone? I glance at the clock, not that it tells me anything. Time is just something else I lose track of. Unless its on a post-it note. Anyway, the phone. Who am I talking to? I lean in, hunched over the bed and linen sheets. 'I suppose I should tell you about my condition.' Whoever you happens to be. My voice is hoarse, throat caked with dry spit. I'm thirsty. An unopened can of coke sits beside me. It's still semi-cold when I touch it. When did I get this? Pressing it to my forehead with my free hand, I feel the cold wash over my body. I realize it's hot in here. ... Here. I survey the surroundings. Everything is white. The walls. The ceilings. The white carpet. Curtains. Sheets. The only color in the room almost is me and the post-it notes. Yellow and tan. I scramble in the sheets for something, my mind keeping secrets from me. Key on the bedstand. I pick it up, running my fingers over the etches in the ornamental ring. 411. I peel off the note on the back, my familiar handwriting once again clueing me in. Room 411. Your room. You can laugh at the irony. Which I do. Inwardly. So this my room. I spy my packed bag on the cheap dresser and start to feel more comfortable. Now, what was I doing? ... RIIIIING! The phone. Let's see. I can't, because it's dark. RIIIIING! I hold my hands in front of my face and I still can't see. Pitch black. I kick my feet out, striking solid with a slight bending of my knee. RIIIIING! I'm cramped. Not able to spread out fully because I'm in a box. That's what it feels like. A wooden box. RIIIIING! A coffin? That would make me dead, then? Yet, I can move. I don't remember dying. Not that I would. RIIIIING! I have this condition. I forget things after a bit, provided they happened after the accident. Everything up to that point is crystal clear, while everything afterward ... I'm blind and deaf in a world everyone sees and hears vividly. RIIIIING! 'Let's talk about the accident. Yeah. The thing that made me who I am today. A forgetful, vulnerable f***. It doesn't help matters that I have to learn organization on top of that. I wasn't organized. I didn't have to be. A professional wrestler has one responsibility - wrestling. Which I did. Up to the point of the accident, I was really good at my job.' Reaching my arms up, I feel the wall behind me. Not wood. Hard, molded plastic. Great. At least when I thought it was wood, I had an idea of where I might be. 'Of course, after the accident, nothing was the same. They'd make bookings that I'd forget. Schedule press conferences that I'd show up two days late for because I got my notes mixed up. There was one particularly bad instance ...' I run my free hand up the wall, feeling damp spots. Wet. What the hell? 'I remember the match that night. I was hyped up as it was a few days before the federation's monthly big event. An oversight? I don't know, maybe. It must have been. My head wasn't where it should have been.' Hmm. I'm naked. How I discovered this was when I tried to grab my feet. I'm also soaking in liquid. I hope its water. 'No, nothing was different that night. No stipulations. Just a match. I wasn't really worried, because, like I said, I was exceptionally good at my job. The only time I had ever lost was during a championship match and that was my first big match ... Not really. I lost that one by a narrow margin.' I fumble around a bit and manage to find a door. Glass. It slides easily, which could be good or bad. I manage my footing and climb through the opening, patting my feet lightly on ... carpet. I'm fumbling blind. 'Well, after all that happened. The quick move-what? I can't really remember her name. Mary. I guess I didn't tell you about my condition, then?' Running my palm along the wall, I note its paneling and the narrow confines of the .... Room? Cage? I try to spread my arms out to the side, but am stopped at the elbows. I'm getting cold from being naked and submerged in liquid. My body begins to crawl with gooseflesh. 'No, it's not that I can't remember anything. I can't make new memories. There's no capacity or space for it. The accident, or the purposeful instance without intent to cause long-term bodily harm - the very f***ing DDT that did this, left my mind broken and my memory shattered ... Yeah. Typhoid Mary. Global Championship Wrestling. Why would I know that?' A switch. I flip it on, engulfing the room in a dim, old yellow. A bathroom. There's the white toilet. Half-full tub. Fresh towel beside the tub and smoking, burnt-out candle on the toilet seat. Smells of melon, a sweet aroma that I like. So I was taking a bath. There's a candle. ... I'm on the phone, but I don't know with who. What had we been talking about? How long had we been talking? Did they know about my condition? Wrapping the towel around my waist, I stare at myself in the foggy bathroom mirror. I'm no good on the phone. If I accepted phone calls, they were short and I always had - F***. No post-its. Don't panic. I sit on the toilet seat, mind wandering. 'Who the hell is this?' Click. Connection severed. I throw the phone on the floor. I need a post-it. Don't blank out. Don't blank out. Don't Hmm. Everything seems normal. I'm wrapped in a towel. Judging by the water in the tub, I was taking a bath. Overturned candle. I don't know what that's for. There's still condensation on the mirror, so it couldn't - RIIIIING! Phone. 'Hello?' My head shoots up and I'm staring at the foot of the bed. Sour sleep breath. Bitter taste of post-sleep forming saliva. Sunlight brings in the afternoon, hot pins stabbing my pupils as I glance toward the window. A beeping alarm clock chirps, current time telling me I've overslept by a couple of hours. The beeps are screeches, birds of hell a chorus in my virgin morning ears. Perfect start. Call me Rei. Early to bed, too late to rise. The dream is something of a puzzlement. If it's a vision of the future, forget WorldWide. Typhoid Mary could face herself. Ha. Popular locker room banter says she's quite good at doing things herself. My first smile of the day. I yawn and stretch, kicking back the hotel room comforter and setting my legs free of what has become a scorching prison. I run the back of my hand over my sweat-soaked back. White t-shirt clinging to my skin. If anyone asks, I unknowingly camped under a lawn sprinkler. Wiping my brow, I blink several times as a salty sweat drop slinks in the corner of my eye. I'm in Colorado. Site of GCW's next WorldWide. Hotel accomodations booked by Caldera enterprises. I'm sharing this hotel with several other superstars, yet I haven't seen anyone outside of employees and other tenants. I've got to be GCW's most isolated superstar. Save for Aiden Jackson. Listening to some of his stories, it’s no wonder. They’re emotional free-falls that scream for tears before they release the lever and let the river flow. Secret societies. Shane Carbon. It’s a suspense thriller that reads like a novel and turns the pages for you. But it’s his life. And yet, he’s here now. I can’t say that I envy him. Anyway, that’s another story for another day. I open the blinds. Outside, the day is pretty and warm. Everywhere I look, schools of colors highlight the mid-afternoon. A brazen succulence of form and substance that can be mind numbingly erotic. Other tall buildings play on the horizon, granting the city a flourishing and bustling platter of business life. And somewhere down the road? The arena for WorldWide. Another stepping stone. Devil’s Night is right around the corner and I'm sure to be bombarded with something. I am Rei. Expecting the unexpected to be expectedly unexpected. I suspect. Anyway, the sky is without a blemish, no clouds to filter out the beaming sunlight. Sunshine on my shoulders makes me happy! John Denver was such a hippie. I feel the warmth of the window, my palms absorbing the heat and leaving oily hand prints. My single duffle bag pouts its lips at me, flirtingly reminding me that after two weeks of inactivity, I'm booked against Typhoid Mary. I'm not sure what I'm supposed to be proving, but that dream I had reeks of anxiety. Anticipation in the embodiment of doubt. I've got a busy week indeed. But the room is an average sized lodging, queen sized bed the worshipped center-piece. The overwhelming white playing f***ed up havoc on my vision. White sheets. White curtains. White classical style bedspread. It's supposed to be for peace of mind, but they've never met me. I see the dust particles I'm constantly breathing from the filtered rays invading the room. The corners of the room are dim, thick black carpet struggling against the obnoxious white that is the majority. In one of the far corners, a small plastic trash can holds wrappings and balled-up napkins. The cleaning lady's plunders from previous tenants. It’s a tidy room, dusted and polished nearly to perfection. I’m tired, again. The day greeted me with its warm hello and I acknowledged only by kicking sheets in its face. One of the luxuries of being a professional wrestler is not having to wake up until your alarm clock gets good and pissed. Alarm Clock: Goddamit, you piece of shi*! BEEEEP! You understand. I walk over to my packed bag, nearly tripping over the comforter that dangles all the way to the floor. Unzipping it swiftly, I take out a plain black shirt and a pair of khaki shorts, going for the sporty but somber look. Call me Rei. Setting new trends every day. Be sure to tune in next week when I introduce the sport of repeatedly slamming your head against the hardest surface you can find. It’s sure to be a winner. But I’ve got to soak. Another day. Another bath. Another mind orgy. I’d let you join, but you’re better off just using your imagination. I’m much more interesting that way. An-nyong-hi Ka-ship-sio Yong-o-ro. Goodbye in English.
