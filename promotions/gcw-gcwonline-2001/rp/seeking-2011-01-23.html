Seeking

23 January 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/23/11 "The City of Brotherly Love". Colin O’Ryan had never been sure how Philadelphia had gotten that nickname. He looks out the window to his hotel room, gazing over the city scape and ponders it. He had seen the Flyers, both current and of old play on tv, and they didn’t exactly show love. Perhaps it was an ironic name. Like Little John in Robin Hood. He lights a cigarette and blows smoke, watching as it hits the window. It’s three in the morning and as usual Colin can’t get a wink of sleep. He glances over at the bed, the girl’s body heaves slightly up and down with each breath as she sleeps. Colin doesn’t know her name. Not her real name. She went with some typical call girl name, Candy or something. She had been good company but wasn’t much for conversation. At least when he finally did fall asleep he wouldn’t be alone. Colin inhales another puff and holds it for a bit before releasing the puff of smoke. He finally had the courage to stop looking at the city and instead look at his reflection in the glass. "In order to know where you’re going, you have to know what path you have been on." Somebody had told him that when he was a child. He couldn’t recall who it was, and at the time it meant nothing to him. It was one of those things that adults say without elaborating. They just expect the kid to one day go, "Aha! Now I understand that!" It took years, but in this this case it actually worked. Colin finally understood those words. Flashes of the past years of his life darted into his mind like a cobra. Some of the thoughts were happy ones, the birth of his little sister Alexis for example. Other’s not so much. He never knew his father till he was about eight. The irony now that between his mother and father, it was his heavy drinking father that was the less abusive one was not lost on him. His "bio-mom", as he called her, was named Caley. She had been a fling of his dad’s years ago. At some point he got her pregnant and things went bad. Colin never knew the full story, and he was not sure he ever wanted to know it either. But that was beside the point. Caley birthed him, and took advantage of certain talents he had developed at a young age. In a way however Colin thanked Caley for what she had put him through. It had given him a skill set he would have never developed without her wanting to take advantage of it. The street smarts he had developed at such an early age, the gambling. Yes it was abusive but the older Colin could see how it could all come to his advantage. Colin knew how to survive. He always did. He glances over at the bed before opening the patio door and stepping out into the night air. It’s cold. The smoke from his cigarette mixes with the vapour of his breath as he exhales into the night sky. Colin smiles as the cold air hits his shirtless body. Why wouldn’t it? Not long ago Colin was spending nights in bus terminals, under piles of cardboard, any place he could find some shelter for the night. He hadn’t been homeless by fault; he had been homeless on purpose, his purpose. He didn’t expect anybody to really understand but it was something he had to do. His own rite of passage if you will. The bank statement he pulled out of his pocket was slightly worn, any piece of paper you carry with you for a year straight would be. But it was important to him. What most people didn’t know, and even fewer would expect, was that Colin was actually very wealthy. By the time he was nineteen he had amassed a fortune in the sum of three million dollars. But this was all money that he made himself. He was not fed with a silver spoon, and he didn’t rely on the money. This was the biggest reason that he had cut ties to his bank account for that time period. It’s easy when you have millions of dollars at your disposal. Colin wanted to make sure that he made it the toughest way possible. He wanted to make sure and forced himself to have to survive on his own merits. Colin was rich, but he didn’t brag on it, nor did he want to use it as a crutch like so many had already. It was a convenience, no question, but he wanted to make sure that nobody could say that he rode into GCW on anything except heart and determination. If there was a silver spoon in Colin O’Ryans life, it was one he formed and forged himself. Colin flashes out his cellphone and presses a few buttons, scrolling through contacts and stopping on one. He presses dial and waits as the screen changes to read "Now dialling – Raquel Wednesday." "Hello?" says the sultry, slightly raspy voice on the other end. "Aunt Raquel? It’s Colin." There’s a pause followed by an excited response, "Colin! How’ve you been darling?" "Good, I’m working for a company now." Technically Raquel was not Colin’s aunt; she was Colin’s father’s cousin. But as long as he knew her, she was more like an aunt to him. Raquel was a former wrestler, like his father and the woman he considered his mother. Alongside that Raquel also enjoyed a lifestyle and employment that was considered questionable, if not immoral, by many. She spent most of her time as a professional dominatrix and Madame of a high class escort service that ran out of the Greater Vancouver Regional District. Her passion for being a dominatrix was common knowledge; however Colin was the only one who knew of her escort service. Colin was one of the few, if not the only person in the family that didn’t make judgements about what she did. She was the most open minded person in the family. Colin returned that right back to her. Because of this she was the one person that Colin knew he could truly trust, especially if the chips were down. "I heard," Raquel responded, sounding proud of his accomplishment. Colin looked at his phone for a moment, puzzled. "How did you know?" One of Raquel Wednesday’s trademarks was her laugh. It took many forms. But this one was one of amused knowledge. "I have my sources." "Micah," Colin says under his breath. Raquel laughs again. "How is the dear Snake’s spawn anyways? He still has the eyes of his dad?" Colin couldn’t help but smile a little. He had first met Micah through Raquel. She had taken Micah to a dinner at the house of Gabrielle and her wife, Jenny’s. There Colin first met Micah. Even though Micah didn’t say much that night, there was something deep and curious about him that made Colin sense their paths would cross again. Not long after that and that feeling proved to be right. Micah and he went through hell and back together. It was the hell only Micah’s father’s training could have created. Colin could swear to this day that Stu had wanted to break them both. On the surface it seemed that he wanted to break Colin for being an O’Ryan. Like somehow, this old feud between two bitter middle aged men automatically bridged over to their children. It would have also seemed like Stu pushed Colin in what some might call "tough love". But there was no love there. The real reason that Stu pushed and pushed both Colin and Micah is because they were a reflection to him. Their friendship panged him, because of what it represented; of what they both represented. In Colin, Stu saw his failure to destroy Colin’s dad years ago. Ashley had what Stu wanted more than any damned belt. Ashley had love. He had the love of a woman and the love of a son. Eventually Ashley would squander that away, but he did it to himself; Stu never had the chance to claim that as a personal victory. In Micah, Stu saw what he could have had. Micah had the love of his mother. The love that, despite the fact they had conceived a child, was never and never would be there between Stu and Jenny. As much as he hated his son’s friendship with the fruit of his rival’s loins, the fact Micah had her love made Stu loathe him most of all. In Colin and Micah, Stu had seen what he could never have and never be. He saw his own shortcomings and what he could never have and never be. It was through this that Colin and Micah’s brotherhood was formed. They survived because of this. They would continue to survive because of this. "He’s doing fine Raquel. I suppose you know we are working for the same place?" That same laugh again, "You, Micah, and your sister. Gabrielle told me that her daughter had joined. I also heard that Alex Edwards joined too. It’s good to hear that kick your father delivered to Jillian when she was pregnant didn’t do any long lasting damage." Colin cleared his throat, not sure how to respond to a reminder of one of his dads most infamous moments. He decided a subject change would be best. "Do you still have that envelope?" "Yes I…hang on a moment." Colin couldn’t help but flinch as he heard the loud sound of several smacks, followed by her yelling at somebody to get back on their knees. After a moment she returned to the phone. "I’m sorry hun, I’ve got a client over here. He can’t seem to give me a moment to talk to my favourite nephew. He’s rude." She pulls the phone away and yells at the man "Rude little boy!" Colin says nothing, Raquel giggles. "Don’t worry, he loves it. Now, about this envelope…" His response is cut short by a slender hand reaching round his shoulder as Candy rubs her forehead in the nape of Colin’s neck. "Can’t sleep?" she whispers into his ear. Raquel can’t help the inflection of amusement in her voice, "Sounds like somebody has company," she teases. Candy kisses his cheek and goes to sit on the bed. She pats beside her, inviting him to join her. "I’ll call you back Raquel." Colin hangs up and sits next to the girl. "What are you thinking?" she inquires as she runs a hand through his hair. "You seem so closed off." "Heh, my dear," Colin looks at her, "you only just met me." Colin had been in this situation before. For some reason, every so often, a girl he decided to spend time with wanted to reach deeper into him, to find something. Usually it was an act, something to make the client feel more cared for. But as he looked into the eyes of the amber haired beauty before him he could see the sincerity in her eyes. She actually did care. There was something familiar about her too. There was a comfort he felt with her. Shame, he thought, that in the morning she would be gone. But until that time came, he felt no reason to not confide in her. "Have you ever made a decision to do something with your life so drastic that it did or might have alienated you from people who should care for you?" She couldn’t help but laugh a little bit, "You do realize what line of work I’m in right?" Colin laughs back, mostly at himself, "yeah I just…" "Thought maybe my folks would have been okay with me working for an escort service? They think I’m a physical trainer." "Well, in a way…" She laughs, "Look, I didn’t take this job because of sex, I took it because of moments like this moments of being able to connect with somebody. Even if that is only for a moment, it’s still something special. Some of the people I’ve met have been quite interesting, present company included." Colin couldn’t help but blush a little as he smiled. "You know, I didn’t think you were much for conversation when you got here." "Sometimes a girl likes to know what her company for the night is like. Some men don’t exactly want to pay me to talk to them. Those are clients I tend to make sure aren’t repeat ones." Colin pops a fresh cigarette into his mouth and reaches for her lighter. Candy grabs the cigarette and kisses him tenderly. "What are you searching for?" "You mean here? Now?" "I mean period. What are you seeking?" Colin had never been asked that. His mind searched for a good answer. Something that was meaningful and deep. But he knew he couldn’t say something for the same of simply saying it. He stared blankly into the floor for a moment, the past year flashing back at him. Suddenly it hit him in one simple word. What he was seeking. "Myself." At first she didn’t say anything. She sat there in silence next to him. It wasn’t awkward, it was just silence. Slowly her head moved, resting onto Colin’s shoulder. "Then you need to find yourself." It had never been so obvious to Colin. He knew he did what he did for a reason but he could never pinpoint what it was. Somehow this girl had drawn that reason out of him. Things had never been as clear as they were now. He had made it to GCW, but that was nothing. That was the tip of the iceberg. Colin was destined for more than making it to some company roster. There was so much more for him. And the most important part of all that was finding who he was. He stands and pulls his phone out again, hitting redial. He looks at her, and kisses her forehead. "Thank you." She smiles like an angel while the phone rings. "Hello?" "Raquel? I need you to do me a favour" "Sure, what is it?" "Keep my things safe for a bit longer. I can’t take them yet." "You know how long before I can send this back to you hun?" Colin sighs, wishing he had the answer. But he doesn’t. He couldn’t possibly have a solid timeline for this. "I wish I knew. It won’t be until I find something important." "…okay. When you’re ready, I’ll send it. But Colin, be careful." Colin laughs, "You know me." Raquel sounds amused, "Exactly. That’s what concerns me." He smiles as he hangs up the phone and looks out at the city. He knew he wasn’t going to find who he was in the ring. But he hopes that he will find a clue or two when he is in there.
