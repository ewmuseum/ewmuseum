The Lesson Of Bettie Brown Bear

Date: May 19th...Kane ain't coming?! The writer sat at his desk, the time 10:24 on his computer desk top clock. He stared blankly at the notepad document, seeing words but in reality not feeling confident in their structure. 'I'll have to clean this up later...I just need to get this done.' he said to himself mentally, then languished that the Pistons had looked so horrible in game one of the Eastern Conference Finals. Staring at the computer screen, he felt an uneasy sense, as if this was all coming too easy. He'd lost his way certainly, as rather than sit in contempt of others, he felt a solid confidence in himself. He thought back to his past, thinking about all the times he'd faced opponents less than he. That was not arrogance, as many within a given community would come up to him and state it as such. 'How could you lose to him? Didn't you just beat that main eventer a week before?' Or better yet, 'Damn...you won that upper card title but why the hell did you lose to that guy right before the PPV?' It was inconsistency. It was what separated him from others. Undeniable talent, yet in the end he still had a penchant to take matches off, to take them lightly. In this instance, he had tried so hard to get ready, but his opponent seemed to quake in fear of him. It pissed him off. At first he thought it a compliment. After all, not many could topple icons such as he. But an uneven sense of disgust filled his mouth as he thought it could be a mental trick to throw him off his game. Butter someone up and they won't be so liable to smash your face into paste. But the scheme had worked, even if it wasn't a scheme. In the past, he'd have thrown something up without half a thought, taken him lightly because his opponent had taken himself lightly. It wasn't long ago when he longed for the days of combat, yet as he stood before it, people with half cocked white flags seemed to ruin in his opinion the integrity of the challenge. Belief in self was paramount. If you did not believe in yourself, you were liable to get hurt. So the writer looked over. It was 10:33 now, and his desire to make right his opponent's assessment that he would run him over was extremely high. *** A man was offered a cup of coffee at a local diner. He took a long sip, spitting it out. 'I thought I ordered something different this week!' He exclaimed. The waitress smiled, explaining 'You did sir. This is the same black coffee you ordered a week ago, it just came in a different package.' *** Vampires. Huge mansions. Corporations. And I look at all this and can not figure out a single real tangible reason as to why ANY OF YOU wrestle. NOT A FUCKING ONE OF YOU! Sorry kids, but what I am reading is akin to geocities, almost hilarious in the over-the-topness of the gimmick. But you sell it like nothing else, and people lap it up like fine wine. Why is that? Why don't you go run your own television network and go all the way? Why would vampires fight in the ring? And better yet, why would a vampire get their ass KICKED by a mere mortal. A flesh and blood man. I would like to think I bleed motor oil, but the realist in me says I can't. Especially with how many times I have bled all over a piece of canvas. I fashion myself a badass, but I do not make myself anything more than what I am. I love to fight. I love to wrestle. Hell, I dedicated my life to this game. What purpose do you have? What did you do? Oh, you are little miss brilliant. You were a bright ray of sunshine and had a high GPA and were voted prom queen. Then momma kicked you out of the house and somewhere or another you’re working in corporations dealing with dead drug dealers and wrestling in WRESTLING matches to be a champion with your vampire friends? Again, how much of that is made up? How much of that is just you living your goddamn gimmick? Hell, you did a worse job staying above the lines of clich? than even Shadow did, and THAT is pretty tough to do. You were the person who just fell into this lifestyle. And all of a sudden now you stand booked against me, spitting about your own superiority while in private you yearn for the gratification of others patting you on the back for ARRIVING as a wrestler-- Excuse me...CHAMPION. While Shadow was mentally retarded for believing her tripe Kane-esque existence, you suffer from psychological issues and poor taste in creativity. See...I wrestle. I beat people up. I used to drink booze and I got divorced by my wife. I've fucked up in my past. These are flaws honey...tried and true REAL flaws that REAL people go through everyday. And before someone retorts with a 'Your life is just a glorified gimmick too!' needs to look at fuckers like Lex Luger, or Jake the Snake Roberts, or any number of people who got into the pills and the booze and the women. And even when I was doped out of my mind I never believed in vampires and never mistook my flat mates as being heads of conglomerates. You talk the biggest game in the world, but behind your words is this tired gimmick and an even more tired emoish personality. Come out blaring your screamo death metal about bleeding through your eyes and how bloody mascara ain't sexy. Come out and run that jaw numb with shit talk. Because inside you’re just a little emo punk bitch who has no idea what real problems are. *** The Native American man sat in his comfortable wicker rocking chair, a leather bound book resting firmly in his hands. His face was wrinkled, his age visibly showing. His hands seemed to tremble as he tried to open the book before his audience. That audience was a group of children in Kindergarten, all colored snow white like God had intended. The proud Chief of the local Indian tribe had seen his lands raped and pillaged. He'd seen his people become nothing but fire water guzzling half lings, dead to their own heritage. Americanized. He'd seen the vast farm lands he had when he was but a young lad turn into parking lots reflecting the neon of casinos. He was ageless, and while death seemed to have a stranglehold on him, he still fulfilled the obligation he had made weeks before. Their eyes twinkled as the old tanned prune rocked back and forth, ready to tell them a story. 'Your teacher, Miss Collins has had me in every year for the past fifteen years to read this story.' Miss Collins was an forty two year old woman who wore jeans and a blouse. She looked rather beautiful but at the same time had to deal with the crippling liability of being a Catholic, as well as being a country girl. The Chief had no idea why she would have him in to read the story until he spoke with her. She was the white devil incarnate, holding back her own racism deep within. 'It's called Bettie Brown Bear: How The North Was Won.' Did Miss Collins not understand that he was from Florida? Apparently not, as her smile of ignorance beamed like a million suns. The Chief just shook his head, beyond insulted yet again. 'Bettie Brown Bear was--' 'Where did you come from?' It was the fat kid, picking his nose and eating his boogers that was in the back. The other kids stayed clear of him, as he smelled like puberty was hitting him like a fright train already. 'Now Bobby...that is not polite. You do not understand his culture.' Miss Collins interceded. Bobby threw his fist up his nose once again digging for more sustenance, while the Chief did his best not to pay attention. 'As I was saying...Bettie Brown Bear was a small bear cub. This was in the times before the north of the United States was inhabitable. See, a brown bear is a very powerful animal, and man was not able to combat its size and strength with their own. So the brown bear dominated the north, and those that tried to explore up in that region fell upon hard times.' 'I can't believe that!' Exclaimed the brightest white child of them all. Her skin was pale, almost alien in tone. She wore a beautiful white dress with white dress shoes and had white ribbons doing her hair in pigtails. 'You people were SOOOO uncivilized! We are superior to you in all ways!' That was unsettling to the Chief, who simply sat back and retorted. 'My young lady...it was your people who could not kill the brown bear. Back when this land was pure, Indian warriors slayed without issue these animals, even though a great many still feared these powerful creatures. But then the white man came and perverted the landscape. They made everything the same, instead of allowing the individual to enjoy the fruits of the land in their own way.' The white girl sneered. 'Our way is better!' She roared, practically beating her chest like a savage. The wise Chief just smiled, already knowing he had won. 'My young lady friend...your way is mediocrity. When all is the same, nothing is different.' But the message was lost upon them all, their ignorant smiles showing that they were nothing but soft weaklings, all of which would hold their superiority over those who had laid the ground work for them. The Chief settled back into his story once again. 'Bettie Brown Bear was the smallest bear cub, but at the same time had the biggest mouth of all. She would constantly get her bear brethren into fights in which she would actively do nothing but let the bigger, tougher bears take care of her problems.' He coughed into his hands. He looked into them, seeing some blood, and nodded knowingly. 'The brown bear had a reign in the north, until one day the white man came to the land. They came with guns, explosives, and other steel weapons that would surely aid them in their exploration of the north. So they all separated, looking to claim the land that they believe was theirs.' The little white girl snickered. 'And the white man killed all the brown bears and the story ends in a shopping mall.' Miss Collins sneered at the young woman. 'TINA! You do not understand his culture!' The Chief smirked. 'No...Tina...the white man was separated and therefore they were left to fight the brown bear individually. They were divided, and even though each had vigor they were unfamiliar with the animal. In doing so, they were all slaughtered, their weapons left to rust in the wilderness.' 'THIS STORY IS INACCURATE!' Stood little Billy Hickenbottom. 'It is a well known fact that the white man was the one who made the north inhabitable. It was our cunning...it was our penchant for the dramatic in everyday life that to this day make us superior!' Miss Collins once again sneered and responded. 'BILLY! You do not understand his culture! Besides, this is a story involving imagination and is not to be taken as literal fact.' The Chief continued, undeterred by Billy's disdainful protests of the very style of story he was presenting. 'The white's sent a second wave to take over the north, but this time talked to the local Indians. The Indians spoke the language of the brown bear, but even so, they feared them. All except one. This man was grizzled, and had conquered many a brown bear. And as he led a legion of whites towards Michigan, he came upon Bettie Brown Bear.' Another cough. Blood trickled down his lips at this point, but nobody seemed to care. 'Standing behind Bettie was a legion of brown bears. And Bettie yelled and snapped at the Indian Warrior. While others were scared by Bettie's talk and great sense of self, the Indian Warrior cared not, and slaughtered the entire legion of brown bears with ease. Bettie was flabbergasted, and continued to run north. Bettie was, for the first time in her miserable existence, outed as a coward. The Indian Warrior ran after her, and even in her fear, her hubris got the best of her, causing countless thousands of completely innocent brown bears to be slain.' The Chief reached into his pocket as he continued. 'On the edge, Bettie Brown Bear was finally cornered. The Indian Warrior cackled, knowing that this little bear cub's arrogance was all a front. Bettie Brown Bear cried, knowing her actions had lost the brown bear the north. Quickly, the Indian Warrior slaughtered Bettie Brown Bear, and wore her tongue on a necklace. And that was how the north was won.' The kids did not clap, rather they sat there puzzled. The Chief had to explain, as Miss Collins smirked knowingly. For fifteen years, Miss Collins thought she knew the lesson of the story, but in reality did not. The Chief knew this, as he called upon her five years before to explain it to the children and she stumbled through an ill thought out diatribe about character development. It made the Chief cringe. 'The lesson of the story is akin to a folktale. There is a line between arrogance and confidence. Bettie Brown Bear always got others to do her fights for her, and always was the most vocal when others were around. However, when cornered, Bettie Brown Bear whimpered and cried like the bear cub she was. Her mouth was not only her undoing, but her entire race's undoing as well.' And the children clapped, not necessarily getting it even when it was explained. Instantly, the Chief pulled from his pocket a hatchet, which he used to slaughter the entire classroom full of children, then Miss Collins. Their blood soaked the beautiful white carpet of the floor, and the Chief exhaled, sad that the white man's mediocrity had ruined his fun. They didn't even put up a fight. 'Acting can be so fun.' the Chief laughed as he spit a blood capsule from his mouth. *** Brandon languished looking back at his own life. But when he did, he saw the fruition to issues presented within the context of others. Vivica J. Valentine was nothing more than a Brandon Youngblood growing up. Hell, Brandon was a pretty good student in school, and even wanted to get into doing the stock market thing. He was always good with numbers. But then he was also a very good amateur wrestler and had a mere dream from watching Mike Randalls on CSWA shows in his tightly regulated household to become a professional wrestler. Youngblood used to play upon the importance of his past, even going so far as to turning his own father's death into a selfish item to give him the desire necessary to be a World Champion. He had cheapened his family, he had even cheapened the death of the man which whom his seed came from. It was around that time, through the boredom of needing to find self worth in the suffering of the past, that he grew up. No longer would he bring up his family, no longer was he going to blame other people for his problems or his lack of humanity. Brandon Youngblood had always been hungry for respect. He wanted to be a World Champion and for the world to love him as he held the belt. He wanted universal recognition, and wanted the wrestlers themselves to treat him as an icon. He had gotten into wrestling for the selfish need to have others pat him on the back. He suffered from the psychological disease of attention whoring. And when he was fucking up chances due to his own stupidity against legitimate legends and underselling World Champions he would cry about being outright screwed, how the system was out to get him. And always, he worried deeply how others viewed him. Was he considered to be elite? Was he considered to be the best? In worrying about others, he lost opportunities. He shot himself in the foot and practically black listed himself from every major professional wrestling organization who before wanted a piece of him. He was written about from afar, without a chance to respond. Because surely, Brandon Youngblood would rip into them and blame them for the things he fucked up. The steps to becoming truly great are not world wide acceptance. It isn't a pat on the back or the write up from a fan or writer. It was in belief in the self. If you can accept responsibility for yourself and do not give a damn what others thought, you infinitely became what you sought for so long. Brandon Youngblood had punched out his wife. Brandon Youngblood had fucked things up for himself in PRIME, the Squared Circle, Action!, the fWo, and countless other places. Brandon Youngblood put the pain pills in his mouth and chased them with Everclear. Brandon Youngblood was the one doing somas to make things a little more interesting. Brandon Youngblood was the one who had lost chances to become World Champion in PRIME, fWo, tSC. It was his own arrogance that had cost him against Hoyt Williams, against 'the Wolf' Mike Randalls, and against Angelo Deville. Titans all. Brandon Youngblood had grown up finally. No longer did he care what a website said, or what the world at large thought about him. He didn't care if people looked at him and only saw a wrestler with no real depth. Because when he cared, he became something very bad. Emo. Vivica J. Valentine spoke of such self confidence, yet suffered from the same ailments as many emos? have. The constant fake front put on in front of others. Once you peel away the layers of feces you see that person for what they truly are: a sniffling snot bag who can't accept reality. If someone wants to be a champion, they take it. They don't wait. They don't cry about it. They go and they earn the accolade. It made you a lone wolf, but should you care? You were already selfish enough to waste the time of others to get their reassurances. You want them there to fluff your cape as you blow past them. It was not as if they were anything but sidekicks anyway. Vivica J. Valentine is emo. A girl who even if he laughed at her because of her over the top gimmick had the vital crux of doubt. She thought herself a champion, but in the reality was just another person left out to be walked over. And even if she denied it, even if she screamed at you for being wrong, all you needed to do is reach out. The paper thin veil of confidence would easily tear away. Vivica J. Valentine could blame inexperience. She could blame anything and it wouldn't matter. Brandon Youngblood knew she was easy pickings. Inflated gusto that couldn't react with a hard fall with any form of class. Vivica surely hoped to break out against him because he had soundly defeated her hero. To show the world she belonged. She would just tap out like those before her. After all, how can you compete when you come to a gun fight with a twizzler? *** Monsters do exist Vivica J. Valentine.
