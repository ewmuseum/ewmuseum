Lifesblood for the Downtrodden, Part 4: Bloodlines

Date: Today, yo I Sidney Murray The elder sibling swirled smoky Kentucky bourbon around his cheeks, letting the uncomplicated corn-distilled tang settle in his tastebuds before flushing it down his throat with a hard gulp. It stung the back of his throat and dribbled down to the pit of his stomach, lingering like a sliver of cyanide. Cheap American shite, he pondered as he closely examined the half-full crystal glass in his hand. Nothing comes close to a good old-fashioned single malt. Hunched over the edge of the porch at the back of the house, Sid gazed out across the unending darkness of the sticky Tallahassee evening. He was sweating and had cursed his choice of attire ? a thick hooded sweatshirt and a pair of jeans ? within seconds of stepping outdoors, pining for the air-conditioned climes of Andy’s kitchen. Making the decision to head back inside at the end of his cigarette, he took a long, hard drag on the Marlboro Red and stood upright, yawning. Tired but definitely switched-on. Watching an army of mosquitoes and moths doing their best kamikaze impression against the porch lighting, he couldn’t help but admire the home that his younger brother’s millions had brought him. Andy had done very well for himself, no doubt, and the expansive suburban abode made Sidney’s own home look like an African mud hut. He often pondered how absurd it was that he’d been in the wrestling industry for almost five years longer than his brother, and yet it was Andy who was on the verge of international superstardom. After all, he was the one that had introduced Andy to the sport in the first place, it would be perfectly understandable for him to be more than a little bit bitter that his brother’s career had eclipsed his own. In the space of the past twelve months, Andy, three years his junior, had gone from over the hill has-been to one of the most popular babyfaces in Global Championship Wrestling history. He’d defeated champions, Hall of Famers, and was on the cusp of becoming something truly remarkable. And Sid? For years he’d toiled on the US independent scene, working show after show for little more than scraps and with only the promise of the odd continental tour to break up the monotony of life on the road. He’d never really had that big break and at the age of thirty-four it was beginning to look unlikely that he ever would. Sid knew that he was stuck on the independent wrestling scene until the day he retired. It was something that he’d come to accept over the years. He knew that, technically at least, he was a far better wrestler than his brother, he just lacked that IT factor that mainstream wrestling demands in this day and age. The big promotions didn’t want to see flawless chain wrestling, they wanted entertainment. They wanted fanfare, over-blown ring entrances, ridiculous gimmicks and self-indulgent interviews, and that wasn’t Sid’s bag at all. The bearded Murray slung his head back and swallowed another mouthful of the noxious liquid. Despite the backbreaking schedule, despite the legions of small-minded dipshit internet “smarks” that scrutinised his every move as an indy worker, despite the miniscule paypacket, he was a man at ease with where he was going. He’d grown to love a lifestyle he’d loathed at first, and on the eve of a four-week Japanese tour with his youngest brother, Cayle, harboured no jealousy whatsoever towards the man GCW fans have come to know as The Admiral of Awesome. And why should he? It’s not like Andrew Hugh Murray asked to leave his brothers in his slipstream, career-wise. The fact was that Andy was born to be a star. He had it all; wrestling ability, charisma, star power. Everything the modern wrestling promoter could want from a franchise superstar. It was bound to happen, and Sid could feel nothing but pride for all his little brother had accomplished. “Yo, Chewbacca, what’chu doing out here?” Speak of the devil. A slight smirk spread across the craggy, weathered features of the bearded, shaggy-haired Sid Murray as he turned to face the John Murray’s most successful son. “Just thought you wouldn’t want me stinkin” up all yer fancy furniture now that you’re such a “megastar” and all,” he smiled, jestfully mocking his brother. “How’s it going, Andy?” “Oh, you know. Saving the world, fightin” terrorism, smiting evil-doers. All that good stuff,” Andy said as he casually strolled up, joining sid at the edge of the porch. “What time’s the flight” You guys need a lift to the airport?” Sid chuckled. “S’alright, I’ve got that covered. Just hoping the wee man hurries the fuck up, though. Hell’s he doing in there, anyway?” “Trying to stuff as many of his worldly possessions as possible into one suitcase,” Andy smirked. “Thanks for taking him with you, though. It’ll be good for him, trying to get back into the swing of things.” “He’s doing all right, Cayle,” Sid nodded. “I think wasting another year of his life behind bars toughened the little bastard up a little. Besides, there’s no place like Japan to transform a guy from a minnow to a monster inside that ring. But shit, man, what the fuck are we talking about this crap for” You’re the one with the big day ahead of ya.” Andy watched his brother tip some ash from his cigarette off the porch. “Ahhhh, yeah. That?” his voice trailed off. “Well, I can’t say I’m looking forward to it, Chewie.” “You fuckin” call me “Chewie” one more time and I swear I’m gonna kill you,” Sid deadpanned. “Really, though. Yer doing just fine, bro. Don’t think I’d be able to cope too well with suin” my own wife?” “EX-wife.” Andy corrected his brother, sternly. “I don’t know, though. My head’s telling me to take everything Jane’s got; after all, she did try to completely derail my career and all. But my heart” Shit, man? that’s the mother of my child, know what I mean?” Andy’s words were met with an understanding nod from his brother. “I hear you. You just gotta do what’s right for Andy Murray, I guess. I mean, it ain’t like you need the money or anything” but hey, maybe you could find a good use for it. Like, say, I dunno, helpin? out yer older brother a little, huh?” Sid joked. After a brief spurt of laughter Andy regained his composure. “It’s not about the money anymore, that’s the thing. Honestly, I don’t give a flying fuck how many pennies I can line my pockets with; she just needs to learn a lesson is all.” “I dunno, man,” Sidney began before taking a sip. “You don’t think she’s suffered enough” Bagwell messed her up good, man. She’s just outta hospital too, and it don’t sound like the monetary situation’s too hot either.” “That’s the thing. This is a woman that I loved, a woman with nothing left. She’s got no money, no possessions... and shit, she tried to kill herself last month. If I go ahead with this and she’s left with nothing” well? I don’t really want to think about what she might do.” Again Sid nodded, resting the glass down on the ledge. “But then again, she did try to wreck your life, bro. I mean, that was some seedy shit she sold to the press?” Such was the dilemma on Andy Murray. Due in court tomorrow for a damages hearing against his ex-wife, Jane, the Scot found himself stuck in a mire he couldn’t climb out of. “I dunno. I dunno,” was all he could muster as a response. “Well, it’s your choice, Andy. Your decision, and I ain’t gonna make it for ye, but honestly, just ask yourself; is it worth it” Is it worth suing this woman just to make a point? Is it really worth ripping her life to shreds as she wished to do to yours?” Before Andy really had a chance to ponder his wiser brother’s words he found his train of thought derailed by the abrupt clunk of a bag being dropped on the porch. “Ohh, I geddit. You two old bastards are having a party and I’m not invited, huh?” Pretending to be aggrieved, Cayle Murray folded his arms across his chest and flashed Sid a knowing grin. “You ready to go yet, Princess?” Sidney asked, extinguishing his cigarette on the fence and dropping it between the gap. “Shit yeah! I thought I was waiting for you!” Sid glanced at Andy and rolled his eyes. “All right, we’d best hit the road then?” “Enjoy it, boys,” Andy stuck his oar in. “And Sid, try to make sure this little runt doesn’t get into too much trouble, okay?” “Pffft.” The noise came from Cayle. Sidney nodded. “I gottit, Andy. Take care, big guy, and fuckin” punch that little terror cunt in the chops a couple times for me, huh?” Flashing Andy a wink, Sid trod off with Cayle who only offered his brother a goodbye salute. Soon enough the two had departed the Murray residence and were winging their way towards another great voyage. His brothers gone, Andy turned and looked out across the darkened land ahead of him as his elder sibling had done just a few minutes prior. Noticing the crystal glass with a mouthful of whiskey still sitting in the bottom, Andy grabbed it and quickly knocked it back. He didn’t usual drink alcohol, but tonight was different. Tomorrow he was supposed to be heading to court to drain Jane of resources she didn’t have, and he wasn’t feeling too good about it. It was going to be a long night. II Jane Murray She had accepted her fate. Broken, beaten down and tired of the turmoil that had reduced her life to tatters, Jane Murray had the strength to fight no longer. This was a battle that she could not win; there was no way that a woman on her budget could mount a successful legal defence against a man of Andy’s resources, and the evidence against her was damning. She had resigned herself to defeat. Dejectedly she sat in a lounge devoid of all but a single cheap couch. She could head to bed and at least try to get some shut-eye, but what would be the point? There were far too many butterflies in her stomach, far too many thoughts bouncing around her head for her to relax. It wasn’t exactly a simple process, getting to this point. At first the prospect of being taken to court by Andy filled her with fear, it terrified her. The prospects of being reduced to ruin by the father of her child was something that she could not cope with, and it had driven her to the point where washing down a handful of pills with a bottle of vodka seemed like the only option. What did she even have left to fight for, anyway? Her debts were spiralling and the repo men had been around more than once to seize her trinkets and trophies. She’d lost her job a couple of months back, and her son, the one thing she thought she could rely on above all else, made the decision to go and live with his father after seeing the glorious wreck that his mother had become. There was nothing left. Nothing at all. Jane Murray had faced so much trouble over the past few months that she’d became almost desensitized to it all, and that was how she had come to accept the punishment that was coming to her. After all, what did she have that was worth sticking up for? A handful of material possessions in a skeleton of a home? An ever-increasing mountain of debt that showed no signs of disappearing any time soon? It wasn’t worth it. Not at all. Tomorrow she’d go to court and she’d watch her ex-husband drain what little lifesblood she had left. It was bound to happen eventually, anyway. No need to prolong the inevitable. III Andy Murray He polished off his third glass of whiskey with a sigh as he leaned back in the ludicrously comfortable leather chair and stared straight ahead at a blank television screen. Of all the rooms in the house, this marvellous great room was definitely his favourite. Even in the dim light of a single desk lamp, the room that combined kitchen, lounge and dining room looked simply majestic. And the flatscreen television suspended about the mock fireplace? You wish you could afford a screen that huge. Professional wrestling had brought him this wonderful home as it had brought him so many other material benefits. He lived in one of the most aesthetically pleasing cities in the country and drove around town in automobiles fit for a king. His bank balance had a dizzying amount of zeros latched onto the end and even if he retired there and then he could quite easily live like this for the rest of his life without having to work another day. Yes, professional wrestling had given a lot to him, but it had taken away in equal measure. The small, crumpled photograph in his hand only succeeded in further emphasised this point. If he wasn’t wrestling, Andy had no doubt that he’d still be with Jane. By now they’d probably have a couple of children on the go. They’d have a nice little house in the “burbs with a single car garage and he’d come home from work at 5pm every night to a fantastic home-cooked meal with the family. Simple, uncomplicated bliss. As much as Andy loved the sport of pro-wrestling, it was at times like this that he found himself damning his continued devotion to the industry that had made him a household name. Without it, Chris Bagwell would have never gotten inside the head of his ex-wife. Without it, Andy wouldn’t be heading to court in the morning. “Heyyyyyy, what are you still doing up?” Once again he found his scatterbrained, incoherent thought process disrupted further as Kayla walked into the room, dressed in one of his old AWC t-shirts that was far too big for her. Quickly he stuffed the photograph under his leg, not wanting her to see it. “You’ve gotta get some sleep. Big day tomorrow,” his girlfriend continued, perching down on the couch beside him. “Far too much stuff goin” on inside my head to even consider that about now.” Andy’s voice was cold yet soft. “Figured I’d just stay down here drinking this cheap crap “til I nod off,” he said, only half joking. Kayla was worried. With the fragile mental state that LeStatt Knight had left him in, she was fearing that the burden of what Andy was about to go through would be too much for him to take. And if the Kentucky bourbon was anything to go by, her suspicions were correct. It was only the second time she’d seen Andy willingly consume alcohol since they’d been together. Something was up. “You, uhhhhh” wanna talk about it?” She didn’t sound particularly sure of herself. She knew that, mentally, her partner wasn’t exactly in top condition, but the subject of his ex-wife wasn’t exactly her favourite topic of discussion. “Done enough talkin” to last me a lifetime; in my head, at least,” Andy said, looking at her for the first time since she’d entered the room. He sighed. “I envy Sid, you know. Fair enough, he’s not exactly earning megabucks and he’s not on television every week, but his life’s so simple. He doesn’t have any of this bullshit.” “Andy. Don’t say that.” “It’s true, though. If I was where he is, career-wise, there’s no way in hell I’d have to do this shit tomorrow.” Kayla swallowed. She didn’t know how to retort. In her mind, there was only one thing to be done; Jane needed to pay, dearly. She’d seen what Jane had tried to do to her boyfriend and, having no mental attachment to the woman, found it incredibly hard to harbour any sympathy for her whatsoever. But she couldn’t just come out and say that to Andy, especially not in his current state, could she? Besides, it was his decision, not hers. “Look, I know this must be hard for you, but you’ve got to think about what she was going to do to you. Say if she’d been successful, say that stuff she’d told the press ruined your career. Do you think she’d have a shred of sympathy.” “I know, Kay. I know. It’s just” ah, fuck it.” Andy was lost, well and truly. He leaned forward, unscrewing the bottle cap and pouring himself another glass. Kayla, meanwhile, rose to her feet and softly kissed the Scot on the cheek. “I’m heading to bed, maybe you should do the same. Bet you’ll feel a lot better about this whole thing if you don’t wake up with a raging hangover. Goodnight, Andy.” Gently she patted Andy’s back before sauntering out of the room. Once again Andy leaned back in the chair, holding the glass close to his chest, surprised by how little an effect his girlfriend’s words had had on him. It didn’t take him long to remember the photograph. He slid his hand between leather and denim and pulled it out. Andy and Jane, newborn son Marvin cradled against his father’s barrel chest. Stood before a modest white bungalow with the perfect azure blue sky hanging over them like a guardian angel, they were together. A family. A moment in time, captured forever. A display of unity, an image of a group of people with a seemingly unbreakable bond. Tomorrow, that image would forever be shattered. IV Jane Murray Andy and Jane, newborn son Marvin cradled against his father’s barrel chest. Stood before a modest white bungalow with the perfect azure blue sky hanging over them like a guardian angel, they were together. A family. Jane clutched the very same photograph close to her heart as she sat alone in the twilight, almost weeping. Her fate now lay in the hands of the proud father in the photograph. The gentle giant who’s selfless nature and unrelenting drive to “do the right thing” belied his massive, imposing figure. The handsome, black-haired gentlemen with a smile that could melt the heart of even the world’s coldest souls. The hard-working saint who, in the eyes of the public, could seemingly do no wrong. It was this “saint,” this “gentlemen” who was about to drive her into to dust. She regretted it all, of course. Jane rued the day she’d even considered stepping into the GCW scene to crawl inside her former husband’s head, and she was so angry at herself for letting Chris Bagwell manipulate her psyche that it had driven her to tears on numerous occasions. But at the time it seemed so right. Mad at Andy for the way their marriage had ended and envious of his continued whirlwind success in professional wrestling, the offer to aid Bagwell in his fight against her former husband in GCW was just too good to turn down. Knowing the truth behind Murray’s alleged history of “back injuries,” Jane would twist the truth and leak a story of substance abuse to the press, a story that even GCW “golden boy” Andy Murray could not help but succumb too. That was the plan, at least. She often asked herself how things would be if the plan had actually worked at Andy had been vilified, driven from a sport and profession that he had dedicated his life to. Would things be so different? Sure, she’d have had the satisfaction of seeing her ex-husband’s star fall spectacularly, but would it have brought her fame? Would it have brought her a fortune? Would it have brought her peace and tranquillity? Absolutely not. Only now could she see the selfishness of the endeavour. Chris Bagwell had tricked her into believing a public castration of Andy Murray would be a good idea when he was only out to further his own career, and it made her sick. Jane dropped the photograph, allowing it to glide gracefully down to the carpet. Yes, she regretted her actions deeply and if there was any way she could make things right, she would. But there wasn’t. She knew Andy Murray. She knew he would never relent. Nothing was going to divert her from the uncertain future that awaited her, and so she sat quietly, alone, enjoying the remnants of the last bottle of wine from the fridge. She raised the glass, offering a silent toast. To the glorious past. To the uncertain future. To Andy Murray. To herself. Jane necked the glass, savouring every last drop of a bouquet she wouldn’t be able to afford to drink for God knows how long. V Rueben Dallas It was the morning of his highest-grossing client’s big court case and Rueben Dallas was calm. He knew with the evidence that had been gathered against Jane’s paper-thin defence that Andy’s “victory” in the campaign was now but a formality and that the hearing shouldn’t drag on for too long. And as far as he was concerned, the conclusion couldn’t come soon enough; if he had to spend another hour in the presence of this God-damn prick he swore he was going to wring the infuriatingly well-groomed attorney’s neck. “You feelin” it, big fella, mmm?” an excited Brendan Perry chirped almost in the manner of a sports fan on the morning of a big game. “This bitch is going down, big time.” Watching on with disdain as the parasite adjusted his tie, Rueben found himself having to clench a tight fist in order to suppress his stress and prevent him from hitting the rodent. Rueben Dallas had a hard time liking lawyers in the first place, and this guy’s over-enthusiastic approach to his job certainly wasn’t helping matters. Blonde-hair combed back and clad in an impeccable grey suit individually-trailed to every curve and counter of his body, Brendan Perry was in his element as the two of them stood in the coffee shop around the corner from the courthouse. This was what he lived for; a big case for a big client, and most significant of all, the promise of a big, big bonus should the battle be successful. “Yeah, yeah.” Rueben said, trying his best not to provoke further conversation from Perry as he let a mouthful of plain old black coffee trickle down his gullet. “Sounds like a dead cert, baby. A dead cert. We got enough stuff on Jane to sink a battleship, man. But shit, where’s your boy at” It’s getting late?” Rueben pulled back his sleeve and glanced at the silver Rolex around his wrist. They’d agreed to meet Andy in the shop ten minutes ago and the Scottish King still hadn’t arrived. “He’ll be here. These wrestlers have pretty insane schedules, you know?” “Oh, right, I got’cha. Of course. He’s probably busy hittin” a heavy bag or body slamming some punk-ass wannabe right about now, huh? Am I right?” with the back of his hand Perry slapped Rueben in the chest gently. “Am I right?” Shut. The. Fuck. Up. Rueben’s body language couldn’t have put it anymore bluntly as he flashed Perry a glance that would sink a man of less resolve. Perry took the gaze for what it was worth and slowly the grin across his perfect, Hollywood features began to fizzle away. He raised the cardboard cup to his lips as something caught Rueben’s eye through the shop window. “Wait. There he is!” Dallas breathed a sigh of relief. Finally! Andy was here and they could finalise their last minute plans. Perhaps most importantly, though, he wouldn’t have to spend another moment along with this nauseating little piss-stain of a lawyer. The black Mercedes Benz belonging to the Scot pulled up outside and sure enough the man himself emerged. Clad casually, Andy slammed the door closed behind him and walked towards the store door. “That’s what I’m talking about, baby!” Perry boomed. Andy entered the shop and scanned his eyes around the premises, looking for Rueben and Brendan. It only took a couple of seconds for him to latch onto Perry’s enthusiastic waving and soon enough he’d shambled over towards the duo. “Andy, thank Go-,” Rueben began but was taken aback almost immediately. “Jesus, man. You look like shit.” He was right. Andy Murray was certainly in no shape to be heading into court anytime soon. Dressed in a crumpled black t-shirt and a pair of jeans, it wasn’t until Andy removed his Aviator shades that the true extent of his physical decline became most apparent. Bags hung under his eyes like fruit bats and a one-day beard lined his jawline. For a man who usually looked clean and tidy enough to make Brad Pitt green with envy, Andy look terrible. Nervously, Brendan eyed Andy up. He was disheartened by Andy’s appearance but at least he’d still get paid. “Well, at least you’re here now...” “Yeah, man. I was beginning to get worried for a moment.” “And don’t worry about the suit, Andy. I’m on it!” Almost immediately Perry pulled a ridiculously complicated Blackberry from his pocket, his fingers gliding across the surface instinctively. Andy raised his hand, pushing down on the attorney’s forearm. “That won’t be necessary.” “Huh?” a puzzled Perry quipped, raised his head. Andy offered him no acknowledgement. “I just came to apologise.” “Apologise” What for?” Rueben asked. “Uhhh, yeah man. What are you talking about” We’re nailed-on today, Andy! Ain’t no way Jane’s gonna survive this?” The Scot sighed. “That’s the problem.” Brendan Perry felt something behind his ribcage begin to sink. “I’m sorry, guys. Really?” Rueben and Brendan were gobsmacked; the lawyer moreso than the agent. They could only watch on as Andy turned and walked away from a legal situation that had brought him un almost unbearable amount of internal conflict. “Hey! Andy! Wait!” shrieked Perry, slowly feeling that big fat paycheque slipping further and further away from him. But it was too late. Andy Murray had already gone. “What the fuck is he doing!”!” Perry screamed, directing his frustrations towards the GCW superstar’s agent. “We’re due in the courthouse in a fucking hour! AN HOUR! You’ve gotta go and talk to him, man!” Rueben shook his head. “GOD DAMNIT!” As the dollar sign slowly disappeared into the horizon, Brendan Perry darted from the coffee shop like a lightning bolt but could only watch on in despair as the Mercedes screeched into life and drove off down the straight. Shouting obscenities and cursing his luck, Perry threw his coffee cup down against the asphalt and punched the air. But it was too late. For all the times that he’d sacrificed his personal life to further his career, Andy Murray and finally done something for Andy Murray, and not for the industry. He was closer to his elder brother than he’d thought.
