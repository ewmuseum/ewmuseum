Fine Dining.

18 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 18th, 2005 F*** it all. Modern day transition of a classic phrase. A new age Scrooge with a basketball jersey cutting his “Bah, humbug” remix. That’s my current train of thought. I’m sitting across the table, hands rested casually atop the sequined cloth that hangs down and bunches up in a wad on the floor. My palms sweaty and clammy. It’s an unpalatable mixture of forlornness and below seventy degree temperature. I rake my fingers along the inside of my hand, fidgeting for comfort since the room had been vented in gauche trepidation. The smiling faces around me are expressions of uncertainty. Kid: Mommy, who’s that strange man? Mommy: I don’t know, but if we smile, everything will be okay. I’m the strange man in the trench coat wondering what the hell is wrong with everyone. Call me Rei. Misunderstood social outcast. Anyway, it’s a formal occasion. Dinner served with the ever-flickering light of candles in elegant holders, flames dancing throughout the glamour of the restaurant. It’s a hot spot, bustling with activity on any given day of the week. People scratching the itch to dress up and spend half of a paycheck on lobster dinners. All around me, a motley of wrinkle and static free suits of assorted colors mesh together with evening gowns of varying lengths and proportions. Pinstripe and floral. White suit jackets and risqu? animal print patterns. Donovan’s of La Jolla, as it is named. A five-star, three-fork establishment. I let my gaze roam, surveying the vast dining room with the eagle eyes of a restaurant connoisseur. Dominate, moderate reds flaunt their exotics, flirting with the black velvet carpet that makes my feet feel suspended in an abyss. Ornate and stylish paintings cover much of the walls, M.C. Escher and Salvador Dali outnumbering the Picasso and Van Goghs by a narrow margin. I spot a lone Edward Robert Hughes, a retouched Midsummer Eve painting further illuminated by modern lighting techniques situated above an older couple’s table on the far side of the room. Classical Renaissance shelves hoist different types of plants, all of them a vibrant and shiny plastic green. Beside the shelves throughout the room, the satin curtains are drawn, draping to the floor and cutting the view of San Diego off with abrupt finesse. A collective comfort salivates over our virgin affair. It was already an uncomfortable gathering. The sounds of hushed laughter and whispered promises echo. Stories of the day and recent pop culture news. Endless words and phrases. Streaming lines of text pouring into my ears. It threatens to deafen, but fades out to a ring. Steel on glass. I look up from my thoughts to see Jacob raise his wine glass. Fat penguin. That’s what breaks the ice, and Jacob’s donning the suit. “Alright, everyone. I think we all know why we’re here.” Ha. Another celebration party. Planned, as always, by Jacob. A schemer at heart. He loves to take the initiative in things, which means he’s always trying to stay head and shoulders above the rest. Hair neatly combed, cleanly shaven face. Well-tailored suit and polished shoes. All to feed his ego, which is already bulging through its cage. I often think I should hang a warning sign around his neck. Warning: Don’t feed the ego, lest you suffer dire consequences. He smiles at me as I catch his drifting stare. “It’s a celebration, you could say. Commemorating our friendship and Rei’s arduous journey toward success. Although he hasn’t struck gold, anyone who watches primetime television on Thursday nights can tell you who steals the show.” Most everyone is here, all dressed better than me. Jacob Finnergan and Tobias Rollins. Marty and William. Maria and Scarlett. The noted absence is my missing abstract puzzle piece. Rebecca Hendley. Work in Oregon keeping her bound and tied, holding her free time at gun point. She promised to call and I had promised to answer. Not that my promises meant much to her. Call me Rei. I brake for promises. New GCW bumpersticker. Buy it now, while supplies are endless. Scarlett and Maria are riveting, sparkling dresses twinkling under the dim light. They smile at me, raising their glasses. Matching bracelets clankling lightly against the crystal glass. Tobias has his hair spiked, a post-grunge punk style evident from the black mascara-lined eyes and liquid black nails. His suit is black also, looking like noir detective apparel more than formal attire. He tips his cup against the rest, as Marty and William do the same. Their suits are something out of J.C. Penney’s bargain shopper. A cheap rental. I’m the slutty prom date. “Here, here.” Spoken in unison. I raise my glass out of necessity. Our food rests beneath us, fresh steam billowing in our faces. Combined smells a nauseating mix of seafood, honey sauce and others. Silverware following proper dining etiquette. I want to rearrange it all. I am Rei. Of out order. The waiter stops to spread the napkin across my wrinkled dress pants. A decisive grunt bellows as he bends his knees. “Rei, you’ve got to learn to loosen up, man.” Jacob, again. Spoken between mastication. Several bites of steak forked into his mouth and devoured whole. “You’re getting famous. Way too rich to be bummed out.” He winks at me. Everyone hums in agreement, chewing merrily on their paycheck. I simply take the comment with a small sip of wine, a wave of alcohol rushing my response away. He’s right, however. They’re right. But I see colors that everyone else either doesn’t or cannot. In their eyes, the spark of stardom is the valiant knight crushing the average life with a mighty blow. They see fame. Success. Money. It’s there, but only after you cross a valley of jagged spikes. If you’re Rei Tiu, there’s two closing walls of spinning blades to further hinder you. Group that with the fact that being famous is a terrifying entity, and I’m a lab mouse running a maze without much purpose. Next time someone’s kids say they want to be like Rei, stab out their eyes and buy them a piano. They’d still be better off than me. And I’m staring at my plate. Chicken marinated with a sauce of mushrooms, garlic and marsala wine. Tuscan potatoes. Three pieces of the boneless chicken, smothered in the sauce and a light coating of parmasean cheese. Bell peppers thrown in for the vegetable effect. The plate is huge, a few inches larger than a standard steering wheel, adorned with scenes of nature that look blood spattered from the marsala marinating sauce. Delicious, yet unappealing. I plan on asking for a take-out box. After a while, conversation starts up. I listen to Scarlett talk incessantly about some prick who she claims is her true love. Maria shaking her head as Scarlett can barely contain her excitement. William talking about his band’s newest song. Marty talking about little kids he’s ostracized from the little league baseball team. I chime in eventually, throwing addendums in their conversations. They’re happy to see me let loose. But I’m a tightly coiled spring. Bunched up inside myself. The hour slowly creeps by. Later. The conclusion of the dinner. The night air brushes at my face through the window. San Diego caressing my cheeks with it’s gentle majesty. The lights of the city are bright, fearing the obscurity that the moon brings. Headlights of passing cars. Neon radiance of flashing building signs. It becomes a blur as I squint my eyes, watching the numerous light trails. A painting by the night. I am Rei. Boredom makes for interesting revelations. “So, what’s on your mind?” Jacob. Cue “Drive” by The Cars, just because. He’s driving me back to my place, his Gallant rolling smoothly over San Diego streets. He’s also fishing for conversation. Silence is his devil, sound and ambience, his god. I decide to respond. “Wrong question.” What isn’t on my mind? I shift positions in the passenger’s seat, the seat belt stretching and scraping against my neck. At the restaurant, I hardly spoke. Surrounded by my closest friends, yet distanced because they were strangers tonight. I created an awkward cloud that only dispersed when we got up to leave. Exchanged hugs and smiles. It was a celebration of something I didn’t feel like celebrating. I pat the styrofoam box in my lap, housing my untouched meal for the evening. “Yeah. I tried to get Rebecca to come, but she couldn’t get away.” I know this. It’s a good thing. It seemed a morbid gathering, celebrating my failure to bring back the Cruiserweight prize. Of course, they had the best of intentions. They’re proud of me simply because I’ve gotten back into the swing of things. I tossed out my “Will Work For Scraps” sign and jumped back into the game. I suppose that’s something to be proud of. “You were just quiet back there. Unusually so.” Unusually so. Unusually so? That’s debatable. Maybe I’m beginning to practice being a deaf mute. I often wonder what it’s like being afflicted that way. Then, the realization. The epiphany. The light bulb over my head. I hardly ever speak out loud, anymore. I think instead. Contemplate. I hash out my thoughts on national television. It’s an unspoken voice, you could say. A whisper in the back of your mind that you hear beyond content. The strange noises you think are imagination fragments? That’s me, too. I’m everywhere and nowhere all at once. Reality and purgatory. Take that wisdom with a spot of tea. Anyway, Jacob’s speeding toward my house, angry flames of road rage crackling in his eyes. His Gallant wavering because of reckless driving. My body rocks fiercely from side to side, as he swerves maniacally. The beeping horns and muffled curses as he passes. I catch sight of several raised middle fingers and wave back, causing more horn honking. In the background, Simon & Garfunkel invade my thoughts. The Sound of Silence. Jacob’s sense of humor sometimes has a stroke of genius. And in the naked light I saw Ten thousand people, maybe more. People talking without speaking, People hearing without listening, People writing songs that voices never share And no one dare Disturb the sound of silence. Bitter clash of honking horns and upset drivers flow with the music in sweet irony. I laugh as a smile creeps on Jacob’s boyish face. For now, at least, I’m derailed from disappointment. At least until the next GCW show. Funny that I’m getting tranquil pacification from balanced chaos.
