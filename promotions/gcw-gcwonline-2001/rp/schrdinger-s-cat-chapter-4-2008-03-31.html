Schr’dinger's Cat: Chapter 4

31 March 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 31st 2008 “Drop your gun! Don’t make me riddle you with holes.” Antonio opened his hand and let the handgun fall to the ground harmlessly. He had no idea how the enemy had known of his whereabouts and he was pissed. He turned towards the man with the gun. Antonio remembered him from when he had been to Burton’s headquarters. “So, what happens now?” Antonio asked trying to buy some time and think of something without getting himself killed. “Now I wait for my cue and then you die,” the man said fatally. As much as he tried to find a way out of the mess he had gotten into, there was just no way he could get away from the man with the gun. A sudden burst of gunfire opened some distance away. The man holding the handgun loses his concentration and looks towards where the sound is coming from. When he turns around, Antonio is already running and almost escaped into the inside of the building that stood besides them. “Not today,” the man said to himself as he took careful aim. He had Antonio in his sights so he steadied himself and he controlled his breathing. He gently squeezed the trigger. --- It hadn’t been very long since he had arrived in this strange yet familiar place but he was already feeling homesick. Antonio had spent the last couple of days sitting with Sheila and her people trying to build a plan they could work around. The air was killing him though. He felt it every time he took a breath. Antonio had gone to some of the most polluted cities in his universe but there was something about the air in this dimension that made him feel sick. If there was no other way to know that he didn’t belong in that place, the air was it. Through some trickery and spying they had found out that a convoy of supplies would be coming through the city en route to another settlement. The streets of the municipality of Guaynabo were almost completely deserted except for a few scattered groups and the two main groups, Burton’s and Sheila’s. The most important thing in that dimension was survival. If they had to steal, whatever they had to steal in order to survive, they would. The basic instincts in humankind take over whenever they are in spots like this one. There was a sudden knock on the door and Antonio escaped from his mind for a few minutes. “Come in,” he said as he continued to look down at the map of the route the convoy would be taking. “How are the logistics of the plan going?” Sheila asked as she entered the room. Antonio sighed out of frustration. He was not an expert in making plans like this, but his work with the Russian mob made him the best qualified. It was like that old adage, “In the land of the blind, the one-eyed rules”. “I’m not getting anywhere. I’m not an authority in this kind of stuff,” he answered, the dissatisfaction showing in his voice. Sheila sat down in the chair opposite Antonio and looked at the map intently before calling out to someone. The door immediately opened to show a big muscular man. He was not freakishly muscular, but his physique and his overall look made him look like the typical army. “Anibal will help you. He did two tours of Iraq and has some knowledge of this kind of stuff. Don’t sweat it. You are putting too much on your shoulders. This should be our mission, not yours.” Sheila got up and went to the balcony overlooking the main street of that urbanization. Antonio shook hands with Anibal and moved over to where Sheila stood. Anibal quickly went to work without a word. “Do you think this is going to work” They have a lot of firepower and if I’m right about how things work around here, they will also be trying to steal the supplies? and we also have to deal with the guards of the supplies. It just seems too complicated,” he said as he stared into the horizon and the orange sky. Sheila didn’t say anything for a while as she thought about the consequences of what would eventually happen. She had never asked to be the one in charge, the leader, it just kind of fell on her lap because of her way of being. She was a born leader. The people looked up to her because she knew how to make decisions. “They have the firepower advantage. That is definitely true. That is why we need to be a little bit slyer. We need our plan to go off without a hitch.” But Antonio had more important things going on inside his head. He kept thinking about Justine and his son. He had lived for years knowing that when she died, he would never get to see her again and he would never get to meet his son. Now that he was in this alternate universe, learning that he now had a chance to see the both of them again was just a feeling he could not describe. The news that Justine had betrayed him worried him. He just couldn’t fathom her being responsible of his death. It didn’t seem possible. They had loved each other madly. He did understand that this wasn’t the same place he had lived in all his life and that things were just slightly different in that alternate universe. “How do I see my son” That is the most important thing for me right now. All this planning is delaying my meeting him.” The frustration seemed to seethe from his mouth as if poison. “You will not be able to see him until we get rid of Burton. After that, I promise you that you will see him. Right now, we need to focus on getting the cargo and killing his soldiers.” --- The distant sounds of gunfire made her weary. It appeared that something had gone wrong. She desperately tried her walkie-talkie but there was no answer from the other side, only static. She managed to hold her composure so as not to worry the people behind her. They held to their weapons with a tentativeness that only an untrained person could. If this had been the world they had lived in before they would never find themselves holding guns in their hands. The harshness of their reality told them a different story and they had to follow to where they could survive. That day it was the convoy. Sheila gave up in trying to contact her team and continued on to the position they had set for themselves. If they weren’t at their position, everything would go gravely wrong. She looked seriously at the man behind her and nodded her head. The man acknowledged that he understood and flicked the safety button to off. The people behind him did the same. They silently started walking towards a building right besides the road where the convoy was supposed to pass through. They went inside and took their respective positions and waited. There were more sounds of gunfire as the convoy got even closer to where they would be waiting. Sheila looked at her walkie-talkie with concern. The rumbling sounds of a car could be heard approaching. The only thing that kept her composure was that she knew the gunshots she had been hearing were far away from where Antonio was supposed to be. A pang on guilt hit her all of a sudden. She hated herself for what she had done, but it was necessary. That was when she heard it. The single gunshot came from the exact spot where Antonio was supposed to have been captured. She held a cry inside of her as she readied her gun. The convoy was nearby. “Let’s get ready.” --- Burton had always been one of those people who enjoyed spending the money he had earned. It wasn’t because he was some sort of greedy human being whose only purpose was to buy and buy more and more useless stuff. It was because he knew that keeping money tucked away was a waste. He hadn’t changed since the world had gone to hell. He sat in the leather sofa he had acquired for his abode. The weapons they would use were spread out in the table in front of him. It seemed like they had raided a gun store and it was all inside his warehouse. There was an array of handguns, rifles and grenades as well. Burton knew that the convoy would be prepared for some heavy action with some heavy artillery. “Call everyone in and have them arm themselves to their teeth. I don’t want anything going wrong today,” he ordered the guard standing in front of the doors. The guard promptly left his post and went to complete his orders. “This has to go without a hitch. If we do things right, we will be able to kill two birds with one stone,” he said to himself. Justine appeared from a door on the back of the room holding the hand of a young boy and leading him towards where Burton was seated. She let go of his hand and he ran towards Burton giving him a hug. “Say “good luck” to your father, Milo,” she ordered the kid. Something inside her broke every time she had to keep going on with her calm fa’ade. She knew that keeping Burton happy was the most important thing she could do. The little boy said what his mother had told him to do and he sat down besides him. The kid quickly took notice of the guns on the table and was instantly interested. He reached to touch a handgun but Justine slapped his hand away. “Let him touch it. It’s not loaded. He should start learning how to survive. You never know what might happen to me in one of these runs.” --- Burton slowly and steadily put each bullet into each chamber of his Colt Python. Everyone around him handled their AK-47s and MP-5s with care. The convoy had started to pass and the idea was to divide it into two in order to make it easier to handle. Burton grabbed a grenade and took the slip off and threw it between two cars on the road. The sound of the explosion and the succeeding screams of people trying to organize themselves was the trigger for Burton’s plan to begin. He stood up and motioned for his people to get to action. Instead of joining them, he ran to another building. There, his guard was waiting for him. “So, where is he supposed to come through?” “According to the information we were given, he is supposed to pass right through that small garden over there. That is where he is supposed to see you and then take you out,” the guard said pointing with his gun. “Are you sure this information is accurate?” Burton asked. He had to be sure. “Yes. I don’t think it could get more accurate than what it is. It came right from the top.” Burton smiled. He finally had him. He looked at his guard and gave him a nod. “You know what to do?” “Yes, sir.” Burton left towards the action once again. He knew it was almost time so he positioned himself where Antonio would not be able to see him. After a few moments, he heard the footsteps coming his way. Antonio passed besides him and when he was a little bit away, he came out of his hiding spot. “Drop your gun! Don’t make me riddle you with holes.” Antonio dropped his gun. There was no way of getting out of this one alive. The sound of gunfire distracted the guard and he saw Antonio take off trying to escape. He shot his gun once and hit the target in the arm. The force of the impact sent Antonio down but before the guard could get to the body of Antonio, who was struggling trying to get up, another shot was heard. The guard turned around towards the sound and saw the smoking barrel of a pistol. He touched his chest and saw the blood that had already started to flow and had stained his shirt. Without saying a word, he slumped to the ground. Antonio heard the footsteps coming towards him and he tried to scramble to a defensive position but the pain shooting up his arm was too much. When he turned around, he saw the face of Justine right in front of him. “Come on, I have to get you out of here,” she said as she tried to get him up. Before Antonio fell unconscious, the last thing he saw was the dead body of the guard who had shot him; the last thing he heard were the distant barrage of gunshots; the last thing he thought was that all was not as it seemed.
