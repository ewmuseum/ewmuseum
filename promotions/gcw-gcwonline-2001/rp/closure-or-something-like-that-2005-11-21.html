Closure...Or Something Like That.

21 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 21st 2005 The skyline of Saint Louis, a town of trouble, poverty, and murder is plainly in sight as I stand there on this cold rainy night. The Saint Louis as I knew it was the place that my parents last went because of a cold man. I sipped the warm apple cider; it slid down my throat creating a warm sensation throughout my body. The rain poured around me in a constant beating pattern, the rain caused the temperature drop in the night. Beating, Beating, Beating. I had to find the man, the one who murdered so coldly along with Michael Mason. I sought revenge against the man who destroyed my life, there was a chance that the destruction of his life would come just as it had to Michael Mason. A sudden sound, out crying from my pocket as the vibration from the phone allied with the loud sound of a ring tone. I opened the porch door leaving the wilderness behind, into the comfort of the warm house. Closing the door behind me and draping my coat over the chair in front of me, I answered the phone. Splattering, Splattering, Splattering. “Hello.” I said with the usual deep, dark voice that resonates from my mouth. “Mr. Jackson?” The man said into the phone as he huffed and puff, breathing heavily. “Yes” Who is this?” I said casually as I looked at the phone with an odd expression. The man sounded as though he was heavy set with the way he breathed heavily and he constantly took breaks between his words. “This is Carl Miller; I was the man examining the documents for you and your brother, Jesse.” He said to me heaving as he talked. The man was clearly in a state of distress, I wanted to ask if he was alright but the pending excitement, the nervous sickness in my stomach was what I really wanted to hear about. “What have you found out?” I instantly snapped with my stomach churning, the warm feeling overcoming my body. The sense of urgency in my voice, the faint reminder that closure may come. “Aiden, I don’t want to put you on hold. But I really would like to discuss these matters in person with you and your brother both. I can give you every one of the name’s decrypted within this book. I’d like you and your brother both over for dinner in Long Acre Estates, at the address of 2877 Brook Mead Drive. That is of course, if it is okay with you and your brother both.” He said to me softly, he began to breath heavily but then caught his breath moments later. “That’s fine, we will both be there and we anticipate the answers we’ve been looking for. Thank you for your examinations and your findings. Good-bye Mr. Miller.” I said to him softly, although I knew this should have been the fashion that this is done in. I had to know, I wanted to know at this very moment, the yearning inside of me could not wait another moment. I would have many more sleepless nights due to the tension building. Just as I predicted the nights went sleepless, tossing and turning. Walking through the hallways of my house, I often wept at the fact that I wouldn’t know for several more days passed. The bags under my eyes, falling into the deep slumber finally, guiding myself into the nightmare; an endless circle of nightmares haunted my dreams. The dreams of what I imagined the murderous night to be, the dreams of Georgia Mason haunting me with the reality of it all, and now this nightmare of the day played throughout my brain everyday that the anniversary rolls around. I walked slowly, tears falling; I looked up with the blurriness in my eyes subsiding for a moment, reading the sign. Visitation: Jackson Funeral 12 A.M. to 7 P.M. I would be there every hour on this day, listening to the thoughts of others on my parents, the feeling of emotion was an essence in the air. The hours past, I heard the same thing for nearly 6 hours, “Aiden, boy you’ll grow up to be a fine young man. It is unfortunate what has happened to your parents. Your parents were very close to us and we are here for you to get through this.” Every time I heard this, the anger grew building; I gazed into their eyes, trying to smile softly with tears rolling down my face. “Thank You” I would say with that one passing moment, and I would bow my head once more. The truth is, these people were never here for me when I became a murderer, and these people were never here for me in the hard times of my life. They forgot about the boy named Aiden Jackson, they forgot about all of the Jackson boys who lost their parents. The scenery comes to a blackening as the next phase of the nightmare uproars throughout my mind. The day in which, all is supposed to be left behind, all is supposed to be forgotten, the burial. How could you forget? The impulse running through my body, like OCD a constant knowing that my parents were not ever coming back for me and that the only way to get to them would be through heaven. The day was geared to help find closure, the day was a constant reminder for me, that they were cruelly murdered and that they weren’t coming back’they weren’t coming back. Walking down the long dirt road, tears falling as I sniff to bring keep the fluids within my nose. With every step that I take another tear drop falls in front of me. At this time, I was merely 15 years old, dressed in all black for the occasion. The tie was uncomfortable around my neck, choking me like the moment that had arisen. We drew closer and closer to the holes in the ground. Two holes, dug perfectly into a square perimeter of the ground, I wept at the sight of my parents once more. I had seen them laying there lifeless, no emotion, no words outpouring from their mouths, no thoughts in their mind just the night before. I had honestly known the situation from the start, my parents were dead, but it wasn’t until now that the true reality of their death set-in. Often times I would lie awake in the night, waiting, yearning for my father to walk through the door telling me he had arrived home from the play that night, coming to tucking me into the warm sheets. “Good Night, Dad” I would say, and he would reply with a kiss on the forehead and an “I love you son, good night” It’s the little things like that, the things that I wish my teenage years had been filled with. The memory of Sunday’s watching football with my dad, the talks that I would have with my dad about absolutely nothing, but my father was robbed from me and so were all of those times. I walked closer, standing next to the 5 foot holes in the ground. The words of the pastor were obscured by the thoughts running through my mind. Often times I faded in and out as I was addressed by whispers. Many people cared for my parents; the amount of people who showed up was insurmountable. All of the people stood in a circle as the final prayer was read. It was over. I awoke from what was a nightmare to me, in a cold sweat, my heart racing quickly as I sat up regaining my composure. I looked around, searching for the people who were around me, searching for the pastor who spoke his words, they weren’t there. I searched the room for the clock and found it; the darkness of the room was lit by the luminescent alarm clock. The clock read 5:39 A.M., I pulled the warm sheets back over my body, shivered then closed my eyes in seek of much needed sleep. Although, I feared that the nightmare would follow again, I knew that I was not well running on 2 hours of sleep everyday. I fell into a deep slumber once more. [Fade] The shaking of my body, the sounds around the area, sounds that I couldn’t make out. Finally, I came to the realization that someone was in the room with me, attempting to wake me from the sleep. I sat up quickly as I was startled by the voice; I rubbed my eyes then focused in on the person. My brother Jesse stood there above me uttering words that I still did not pay any attention to. “Aiden, Let’s go it is 5. We have to be there by 6, and the drive isn’t the shortest one in the world lets get moving.” He uttered loudly as I finally tuned into what he was saying to me. I peered down at the clock quickly in disbelief, sure enough though the clock read 5:03 P.M. I quickly jumped from the bed and ran into the shower getting ready as quickly as I could. Walking out the door without a word, my brother and I both in a rush to know the answers. Better yet, not to be late to this occasion. We hoped into the car quickly speeding way in hopes of the answers, in hopes of closure. Arriving to our destination, we peered up at the house; the porch wrapped around the side of the house, the siding was a cream color with nice red shutters on the outside of the windows. The house was fairly large, close to being the same size as the Jackson estate. We walked in silence to the front door where we were greeted by the large man that I had spoken to on the phone. Just as I had predicted, Mr. Miller was a heavy set man, dressed in a pair of khaki pants and a nice blue polo. “Hello boys.” He said to us swiftly as he welcomed us into his home. “Hello.” Jesse and I said in unison as we walked into the house removing our coats. “I’ll take those.” He said reaching his arm out, we handed the coats to him then walked into the living room were he told us to sit. There lying on the table was the Diary of Michael Mason, the tarnished diary was the same as it had looked before. Lying next to it was a sheet full of notes and page numbers; I assumed that this was what Carl planned to talk to us about. Looking back up, Mr. Miller walked into the room and sat in the chair diagonal to us. “Boys, I believe that you are ready for the answers. I will give you the answers under certain circumstances that you must abide by.” He said as he shifted his glasses, and then pushed them up to his face. “What are those circumstances?” I replied. “You must promise to me that none of these men will wind up dead in a hotel room, or anything as to that. I understand that you want to seek justice but Aiden we don’t need you in jail again. I know that X will be able to help you find justice in a more civilized fashion and I hope that is the way that you approach it okay?” He said to us softly, with kindness diffusing from his voice. We both nodded our heads in agreement as he read over his notes carefully and then began to talk about the several men. He noted the names, Julian Maraca, James Peterson, and Mason Keenan. He listed several backgrounds for each of these men, but again although this one hasn’t been used in a long while it’s a quote that you should well remember’Another story for another day. Nearly an hour passed as he presented us with all of the material he had just given to us verbally. The names had been burnt into my mind as I wandered slowly out the door. “You boys aren’t staying for dinner?” He said to us in an inviting manner. We both looked back as we were walking in our daze. We both shook our heads and walked out the door. The door closed softly behind us, as we both slipped on our coats. The cold constricting my lungs as the warmth from the house quickly faded. We got into the car, quickly scrambling for heat in silence. We both glanced up at each other and went to talk but then resorted back to silence. Moments later I broke the silence because I really wanted to know the thoughts running through his mind. “So, what do you think?” I said quickly to Jesse, turning the seat warmers on. “I’m trying to establish a plan of action I guess. I mean ultimately I really just want these guys put away for what they’ve done.” He replied to me. “Do you think the twins care as much as we do about all of this?” I questioned him once more. “Of course they do, their parents were killed too. Aiden, they’ve found their way of moving on I guess and we haven’t. They’re a part of X and you know that, they want justice just as we do. They’ve just know that Mom and Dad aren’t coming back.” He said to me. “But, I really do know that Mom and Dad aren’t coming back.” I said to him wearily. “I know that, but you haven’t found the same closure that they have. They have been able to let go of the fact that Mom and Dad are gone.” He spoke softly as he turned down the heat in the car. “Yeah yeah, I understand. I’m guessing a meeting with X should be suitable correct?” I spoke as softly as him. “Indeed.” As he spoke these words my mind began to wander. My mind raced to the graveyard once more, not the actual funeral this time though. I stood there alone, laying flowers upon the graves of my parents. The feeling of sadness overcame me, as I drifted between reality and this day dream. The tears fell from my face, just as they had when I was fifteen, just as they had every time I thought about the warm memories of my parents. The video tape, of the first time I took steps, my mother cheering me on, my father arms out extended. The first time riding my bike, my dad guiding me down the street then releasing me to fly freely, only with me to fall off once more but I got right back up and he was right there with me. My first wrestling match, although it ended in a loss, my father was there to give me a hug afterwards when the small six year old, fifty-six lb boy was crying at his defeat. A smile grew widely across my face as the memories continuously flowed through my mind. The sun began shining through the clouds of the overcast day. I dropped one last red rose onto my mother’s grave and walked away with the wide smile across my face, remembering them just as they would have wanted. We cannot banish dangers, but we can banish fears. We must not demean life by standing in awe of death. ~David Sarnoff
