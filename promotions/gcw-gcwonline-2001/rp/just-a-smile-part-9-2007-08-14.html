Just a Smile: Part 9

14 August 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 14th, 2007 Every day both her home phone and her cell rang off the hook. By now, the answering machine had to have been close to its capacity, as there were countless messages all with the same theme, concern. "Hey Tessa, it's Viv. I haven’t heard from you in a few days, so I just wanted to check in. Call me when you get this." Another from the nanny, "Hey Tessa, I'm just calling to see if you've had the baby yet. I've been trying to get a hold of you all week, and haven’t heard a word, so I'm guessing you and Scott must be busy. I hope all is well. Call me to let me know what’s going on." Tessa's mother, whom it was obviously disliked Scott as much as me, "Tessa, pick up. Christa called me all upset, but wouldn’t say what’s wrong. I swear if that husband of yours did anything, I'll kill him myself." And then of course, one of Tessa's closest friend in the business, probably the only guy that could talk to Tessa without an evil eye from Scott, Jackson Rust, "Tessa, I haven’t heard from you in days. Please give me a call, I'm concerned about you, hun." Still, she remained planted in her king sized bed when the phone rang yet again. This time it was Scott’s friend Laguna calling for Tessa, “You haven’t picked up your cell phone, you won’t answer this phone, I hope you're just in the hospital with the baby. I'm worried, please call me back." The front door opened, and I ducked for cover behind the nearest stack of boxes. It was only Christa, who'd no doubt just gotten in from soccer practice. Her cleets were still on her feet, and her cheeks were freshly sun burnt. Judging by the intensity of the sigh that she'd let out, she had no doubt heard the end of the message. Her concern for Tessa was slowly starting to turn into anger. "I guess I better go talk to her." Christa sighed, as she tossed the bag containing her soccer equipment to the floor. I peaked around the corner slightly to make sure she wasn’t heading my way. Instead, I saw her climbing the spiral staircase, she was wasting no time. "Tessa!" She called out. By now, she was marching down the hallway, in search of her mother. Granted, it wasn’t like there was much searching to be done. The only time Tessa had ever crawled out from under the leopard print linen covering the bed, was to eat or go to the bathroom, granted at this stage of her pregnancy, which was nearly even half hour. I crept out of my hiding spot and tip toed halfway up the staircase, before stopping to listen to what was being said. "Tessa," She called out again. I heard what I presumed was the master bedrooms door squeak open, and sure enough, there was confirmation that Christa found who she was looking for, "you're still laying down?" I snuck the rest of the way up, and planted myself at the top of the stairs, which was fortunately at just the right position for me to peak into the room to see and hear what was going on without being noticed. Tessa sat up and scooted over to the side of the bed closest to Christa, answering only with a brief, "Yes." Christa let out another frustrated sigh, only this one was much more emphasized. This was not her mother. This wasn’t even her aunt. This was a woman who'd been reduced down to nothing, "You'll need to get up eventually." Again, all that was given was a brief answer, "Tomorrow." Christa, growing tired of being the stand in mother for Tessa, had a lot more on her chest that Tessa would have imagined. Not only was Tessa a total wreck, but Scott was missing in action, and with me lurking around, she seemed to be on alert even in her sleep. Taking her mother’s hands into her own, she looked up with pleading eyes, "We need to get out of here Tessa. That baby is coming soon." Tessa turned her head slightly towards Christa, likely in hopes that Christa would lay off some, "Not yet." Another sigh was dropped, signaling that Christa's temper was about to boil over at any moment, "I cant help you if you go into labor, you know," She paused, took a breath in, then dropped an even bigger bomb shell on Tessa, and myself, "besides, that Rob guy is really starting to creep me out." While I'd love to believe that Tessa was my biggest fan, it was obvious something about me scared her as well, yet as a mother it was her job to try to soothe her children's fears, "I haven’t seen him in a few days." "He's been lurking around the neighborhood all week!" Christa dramatically screamed out. I prayed that it wasn’t all over now. Not when I hadn’t gotten a chance to prove myself. Still Tessa tried to keep a cool demeanor, and clung onto the hope that everything would be fine in the end, "Scott will be back soon. Maybe he's been looking for him. And even if he's not, you know your father won't put up with it when he gets back." Finally, Christa cracked, "He's been gone for three days now! What if he isn’t coming back?" You'd think that by now, Tessa would have thought about that, but when it came to Scott, there was no such thing as giving up hope, "He is Christa, he wouldn't leave me like this." "He's done it before," Christa had been through it all before, it was obvious the one thing she wanted in her life was stability. I could give that to those kids. We'd be a beautiful family, the type you see in the movies or read about in books, with a white picket fence, "I know you love him, but you're only hurting yourself now." Before Tessa could blink, Christa left in a huff. Quickly, I dove into the bathroom across the hall from the stairs, just in time to keep my cover. Christa went right past me without noticing as I did my best to hide behind the door. Listening for another door, I waited until I could tell Christa had gone back into her room before I crept out of the bathroom and started toward Tessa’s bedroom. I could notice the lights to the room were out as I pushed the door open and stepped in. “Scott?” she cried out, sitting up in her bed. The lights from the hallway shined into the room, lighting my face, and let Tessa know that her Knight in Shining Armor had not yet returned for her. “No Tessa, it’s just me, Rob” I answered back, not yet daring to make a move. Tessa seemed to sit in a daze for a moment, trying to figure everything out. “What are you doing here, how did you get in?” “One of the boys let me in” I lied, as I had actually stolen a key on the night Scott was arrested. “I just stopped by to check on you since you hadn’t been answering your calls and I hadn’t seen Scott around the neighborhood.” I tried to lighten the mood for her to make her feel better, “I’ve practically formed my own little neighborhood watch for Scott,” but my laughter didn’t seem to help her any. She couldn’t even crack a fake smile for me. Keeping my moves short and simple, I edged myself to the corner of the bed and took a seat. I didn’t want to make any sudden movements and scare Tessa, but I wanted to try and get in closer to her. Giving her a concern look I told her “You need to eat”, making sure to add, “And you need to stop stressing out about Scott. It can’t be good for the baby’s health and it most certainly isn’t good for your health.” Though Tessa didn’t want to listen, she still lifted her eyes up to at least look at me. I took this as a sign to continue; “now I like Scott, you should know that. I practically consider us friends if it wasn’t for him always trying to roll me down the stairs, but you need a husband who’ll treat you better than what he does. You need a man who’ll be by your side.” Her face turned away, looking at nothing at all but anything other than me. Her face cringed, but somehow I don’t think it was a result of the things I said. “Are you alright?” She never turned to face me, but the look of discomfort stayed. “I’m fine; the baby’s just acting up,” she tried to play it off as it was no big deal, quickly trying to change subjects, “Where are the boys?” I had no idea, truth be told, but I couldn’t tell her that. I had told her that the boys let me in, so I needed to come up with something. “Last I saw them, they were playing in their room.” Tessa looked as if she was about to try and get up to check on them, worrying me that I might get caught in my lie. But she just shifted her position, clearly becoming more and more uncomfortable. “Are you sure you’re alright?” “I’m fine,” she reiterated, doing her best to pretend as if nothing was wrong. But not even the best acting classes would have been able to help her. “I want to stay here until Scott gets back, I won’t want him to worry if he gets home and I’m gone.” It was hard to believe just how much she was willing to take from him. He could seemingly do anything to her and she would still try to stay by his side. It made me infuriated on countless levels. He didn’t do anything to deserve someone like her. It appeared that it will take more time than I initially thought to convince her that she belonged with me. Just as I was about to further try and convince her that she needed me and needed to go to the doctor, a pain shot through her entire body, causing her to scream out loud. “Ok, that’s it; we are taking you to the hospital, now.” I told her, grabbing her arm should she would know that I wasn’t taking no as an answer. Helping her out of the bed, Tessa grabbed a prepacked bag from the closet as we headed out of the room. With each step we took, I could feel her resisting me. None the less I dragged her along. As we reached the door Tessa finally pulled her elbow from my grasp. “Let me just call Scott,” she said, “just so he doesn’t worry.” She tried to get her cell phone out of the bag but I yanked her along, telling her “Don’t worry; I’ll call him from my cell phone. But only once we get you to the hospital, which is the most important thing at the moment.” “But what about the boys” And Christa?” asked Tessa, not wanting to leave until she made sure everyone would be alright. I didn’t have time for this though, as the situation continuously looked worse and worse. Placing my hand on her shoulder, I pointed her out of the door and reassured her “I’ll leave a note for the kids so they know what is going on along with money to order pizza, and then we can call the nanny to check on them.” Tessa seemed pleased enough to not further press the issue. That or the child just fought so much that she didn’t want to press the issue. “Now head down to the car and I’ll call the nanny,” I told her, scooting her out of the condo. I slapped a twenty on the table for the kids to order their food and was just about to pick up the phone when it started to ring. I waited for the answering machine to pick it up, hearing a voice I never expected; “Tessa! Pick up, Tessa! If you are home, pick up the phone. Look, I had to get Markus to bail me out of jail. There is something seriously fucked up in the head with Rob. He set me up for whatever reason. Whatever you do, stay away from him!” Though as much as it panicked me to hear his voice, I couldn’t help but laugh at the situation. Tessa wouldn’t be hearing his warning as I pressed “delete” on the machine. Writing down a quick note for the children, I hurried out of the condo to join Tessa in the car to the hospital. Scott might know to go to the hospital after stopping at the condo, but I planned to stay one step ahead of him each step of the way. As long as I could manage that, I could better Scott, and Tessa would be mine.
