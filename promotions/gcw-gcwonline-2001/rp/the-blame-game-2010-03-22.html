The Blame Game

22 March 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/22/10 “Before you start, don’t!” It would not take a passing glance of a certified psychologist to figure out just what was on the tip of Chris Storm’s tongue as his long time girlfriend Tessa Windsor crawled into the passenger seat of his 2010 Transformers edition Chevy Camaro. Heavily bruised and bandaged, particularly across her forehead, Tessa Windsor looked far more like she had just spent a day in the warzones of Iraq than just an evening in Denver, Colorado. Chris Storm was plenty aware of the beating his significant other had taken at the one hundred and twenty seventh edition of Worldwide, and quite frankly he saw it coming. But it still would not change the fact that the entire situation was completely preventable, particularly on Tessa’s end. He told her to quit the fun and games. He told her to focus. He told her there were far more important matters to handle. And she failed to listen to even just a lick of it. Needless to say, the ride back to Chris’ New York City condo would be a long and treacherous one for Tessa, who dreaded the twenty minute lecture undoubtedly coming her way the moment Chris got an opportunity to open his big mouth. “I already know what you’re going to say.” She reconfirmed, propping her passenger side elbow up against the latch that controlled the power windows and locks. Her chin buried deep into the palm of her hand, muttering the rest of her statement, “I didn’t listen.” Silence met her in return. Something she was not quite expecting, especially when it came to the topic of her making headstrong decisions against her better judgment. “I’m sorry.” She apologized in another attempt to prompt the inevitable chewing out coming her way just to get it over with. Again she was met with silence, silence that completely threw her off her game. “Or we’ll pretend like nothing happened, which is completely fine with me.” The Camaro rolled up to a stop at the light and Chris slammed his fist against the steering wheel, causing Tessa to jump in her seat. “Did you see what they did to the tag belts? After all the fucking work I put into making those things worth something? Fuckin’ sake, I swear.” He cursed, his fist turning white from the pressure. Tessa, on the flip side, was plenty thankful for Mayhem’s actions. Not only did it due away with the past, it managed to redirect Chris’ rage towards a topic that had absolutely nothing to do with her stubborn nature. “Yeah. I guess that had to suck to watch.” She added in, tilting her head slowly in his direction. Maybe she had managed to make it out easy this time. Chris shook his head in disgust and promptly applied the gas after the light finally changed to green. “Always glad to see an ounce of respect from a company with a move like that. Almost makes me glad I’m out of the business.” Turning his head toward her, Chris noticed the relieved expression that washed across her face, and quickly cut back to the chase. “But onto you…” ”Ah shit…” “WHAT THE HELL WERE YOU THINKING!?!” Tessa’s eyes darted in every direction that was not the towards driver’s side of the car. Not that she could blame Chris for his overdramatic nature. She probably would have reacted the very same way if it were him that had gotten beaten within an inch of his life, not just once, but twice within a two week span. Still she danced around the question in any which way she could possibly draft up. “Talking with Shaman? I know he’s a huge man with an anger problem, but he’s plenty harmless Chris! The poor guy just needs some friends.” “He’s about as harmless as Lennie,” Chris snorted, “he’ll crush you accidentally giving you a hug.” He waved the comment off, however, and redirected his ire. “And besides, this isn’t about ‘Chief Wears A Helmet’ and you know it.” “Fine,” Tessa huffed, burying herself as far back into the passenger’s seat as she possibly could, “I was insanely stupid. I got the absolute shit kicked out of me. Good enough for you?” They rolled up to another red light at just the wrong moment for Tessa. “Good enough?” he hollered, turning his full attention to her. “You have a concussion! Who even knows when you’ll be cleared to wrestle again, IF you get cleared to wrestle again.” “I’ll be fine!” Her head quickly turned to face the window on her side of the car, staring out as the car started to move along again. “It does not change the fact that this could have been avoided if you just listened to me.” She refused to turn her head back, even as she swore off the one thing, or more precisely person, he had wanted to her to rid herself of all along, “I’m done with him. I promise.” “Too little way too late there, sweetheart. And look what you walked away with?” “You know for a fact you wouldn’t be saying that if he actually came back to his senses.” “That’d be great if he had any sense to begin with. Thing is, he doesn’t. You don’t remember all the times you wanted to strangle both of us because of our lack of senses? Why the hell did you think that was going to change all of sudden?” Her chin dropped right back down into her palm as she frustratingly sighed in surrender, “Please, just let it go? This whole concussion thing sucks hard enough as is. Yelling certainly isn’t helping it any.” A sigh escaped from his lips as Storm threw on the blinker, easing himself into his turn. “Don’t worry, you’re not going to have to worry about Markus any longer.” Storm’s Camaro pulled into the parking garage that rest under the condominium he lived in, shadows bathing the yellow car with black, “I can promise you that.” If only things were really that easy. “So he’s just going to vanish out of thin air?” “Something like that.” He chimed in while putting his second love to rest, “That and you’re staying home next week.” Her baby blues grew wide as her head jolted in his direction. “Whoa! I’m what?” Like it was nothing, Chris echoed his earlier sentiment as his seatbelt withdrew from around him, “Not going to 128.” “Since when?” “Since right about now.” Before Tessa could blink the driver side door slammed shut, leaving her to ponder where Storm’s proclamation ranked on the truth scale. On a scale from 1 to 10, she placed it right at a 2. After slowly climbing her way out of the car to avoid re-aggravating any one of her dozen or so wounds from NC-17 and WorldWide, Tessa called out to Chris who was halfway to the lobby door, “So what’s really going on next week?” “You’re keeping yourself out of trouble.” “Trouble?” This was of course coming from the guy who gained an extensive fan base and a couple mil with that one word, which Tessa was sure to remind him of, “When’s the last time I got myself suspended or fined?” “August.” Chris ever so suavely chirped in, hoping to at least amuse Tessa. It totally failed. Instead of laughing it off, Tessa clamped down on her bottom lip before shooting back, “That was fired! And that by no means counts.” Naturally, Chris saw it otherwise and refused to turn his head back to face her even as she stood behind him hand on hip as he unlocked the door to the condo, “You stuck your nose where it didn’t belong.” “I helped Vivica!” “And where’s Vivica now? Sure looks to me like she didn’t return the favor.” “Way beside the point.” Chris had been prepared well in advance for that response. He instantly retorted back, “Do you even know what my point is?” She shrugged, “Apparently it’s that I’m a problem.” That thought was put on hold as the two quietly scurried into the condo, slowly shutting the door behind them so that the kids would not wake. As soon as her gym bag hit the carpet beside the door, Tessa made a beeline for the bedroom. At least in there she could rest her head and drown out any other forthcoming lecture with a feathered pillow or two while Chris yapped on. Before Tessa could get there though Chris had laid his hand on her shoulder just as she was about to collapse. Instinctively, Tessa’s head jerked itself back as she pivoted on her toes to see what was needed. Not that Chris had ever done her any sort of harm outside of the ring, but after what Markus had pulled on her back at Worldwide, the last thing she wanted was anymore tricks. Awkward silence fell between the two. Neither dared to look back the others way. “Sorry.” Both exhaled under their breath. Guilt washed over the pair. It was truly a sad affair that both of them lived such guarded lives. Even after eight years of being on and off with each other, trust was still an issue. So much so that it felt like it would never be fully resolved until they both hung up their boots. Neither one felt daring enough to break the awkward silence between them. Thankfully, just in the nick of time, Tessa’s teenage niece turned daughter waltzed past their room, headed for the kitchen. Imagine the pressure fourteen year old Christa felt when both Storm and Windsor’s eyes darted in her direction, looking to strike a conversation. It had happened enough times in the past for Christa to know exactly what she needed to say. “You guys are pathetic.” Any other set of parents would not tolerate such a thing, regardless if it were factual. Tessa and Chris however, let it roll, because it was just that, the absolute truth. They cared just a little too much about the other and it showed most anytime the two had a conflict of interest. Turning her head away from the door, Tessa flopped back first onto the king sized mattress, sympathizing with Christa, “Yes, we are pathetic. But just wait ‘til you get older. You’ll understand completely.” From the kitchen Christa chimed in, “I seriously hope not.” Having witnessed much more than a couple dozen of her adoptive parents meaningless scraps, the one thing that Christa Windsor vowed to never have was a serious relationship. It was far too much work for the often mediocre results that came of it. “This is why you stay away from boys.” Chris added in, never one for heart to heart talks. He far preferred the true to life examples when it came to parenting, particularly when it came to Christa who he had absolutely no clue how to deal with given their lack of any sort of biological link, “Think how miserable you’d be if you had to deal with someone like me every day for the rest of your life.” Like any teenage girl, Christa was none too appreciate of his comments. With a grimace she sneered back, “I’m smarter than that.” “That’s great to hear.” His thumb stuck straight up, hoping to bridge the gap between him and what might as well have been his daughter. To emphasize his lesson in dating he tawdrily added in, “And don’t be friends with them either. Just take a look at your mother right now and you’ll see how that goes.” “It’s your fault too.” Christa apathetically added in as she strolled past Chris back to her room, bottle of water in hand, “You could have been there to stop him.” It certainly caught Chris off guard. Standing speechless in the middle of the hall he poked his head slightly into his own bedroom where Tessa laid sprawled out, head sandwiched between two pillows. Before he could get a word in edgewise Christa’s door shut behind her, leaving the self proclaimed Rock Superstar lingering in the hall by himself. “You could have been there to stop him.” Most of the time, Chris let anything his kids say go. After all, they were just that, kids. But this time the words lingered inside of his head, ringing louder than a church bell on a Sunday morning. “You should have been there to stop him.” Hearing it again did not put him anymore at ease. Yet he could not stop himself from reiterating the thought inside of his mind yet again. “I SHOULD have been there to stop him. His light blue eyes locked on Tessa again as she struggled to get comfortable. He wondered whether her own thoughts were getting the best of her, or if every bump and bruise her body had taken over the past two weeks throbbed to the point where it was impossible to sleep. Or maybe it was both, which ate away at his own mind even more. His eyes peeled away from the situation before his emotions could get the best of him. Becoming soft at a moment like this would not help matter any. If anything, Chris figured it would make it worse. The last thing he wanted was for Tessa to worry about him. After everything that had happened to her these past few weeks, both her body and soul needed some serious rest and relaxation. The only he needed vindication. Before peering Tessa’s way again, Chris promised himself one thing. “Next time I WILL stop him, for her sake alone.”
