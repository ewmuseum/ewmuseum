Reflection and contemplation

19 February 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02/19/10 “Nice place you’ve got here,” Dave sarcastically says to me as he wades through the garbage on my floor. I smirk at him, and then nod my head once as a way of telling him that I don’t blame him for his disgust. It’s true that I’ve let my home become a complete mess for the last month or so, but frankly I can’t summon the motivation to care about my lawyer’s disapproval. A lack of motivation seems to be a common phrase with me these days. Considering that I have a huge match at NC-17 coming up I should be up and about training, preparing, strategizing and all that other silly bullshit. Instead last night I got massively drunk and watched the entire first season of scrubs. Hardly the actions you would expect the United States Champion of GCW to do. Oh I’m sorry; current holder of the United States title belt. Turns out there are not too many people who like calling me the champion. “What’s going on Dan,” Dave demands though the concern is clear in his eyes. “You’ve been acting weird for the last few weeks, and the fact that you lost so quickly to Mayhem proves it. It’s like you don’t even care about your career anymore, but when you hired me you were set to work your way to the very top!” That has gotta be one of the things that I love about Dave. The man hasn’t worked for me for too long, and he didn’t even know me before I hired him. But the second I made him my lawyer/agent my success immediately became a top priority for him. Of course, it helped that he and I became friends very quickly…hell I’ve even babysat for his kids though to this day I have no idea how he talked me into it. “I don’t know,” I answer him as I stare at the remains of takeout that litter the floor. “I guess that I just can’t seem to make myself care about it anymore. Put me in the ring with someone and I’ll do my damndest to kick his ass, but frankly the idea of working for it outside the arena just doesn’t jive with me. Hell, in the match with Mayhem all I could think was that the sooner I got done with this shit the sooner that I could go home and go to bed.” I steal a glance at Dave’s face and have to stifle a chuckle. He’s wearing an expression that a comical mix of disbelief, horror, and panic. I guess I can’t really blame him seeing as how I’m his most valuable client but still, I thought a lawyer could control himself better than that. “B-b-but,” Dave stutters for probably the first time in his life, “why in the hell would you feel like that Dan? You’re one of GCW’s best prospects for future main event status, hell you’re the United States Champion!” “No I’m not!” I snarl violently. Anger that I’ve been holding for weeks inside of me makes itself known, and I jump off the couch I’m sitting on searching for a way to release it. Dave stays silent as I begin to pace around the living room, angrily slapping the walls. “I know you warned me!” I finally burst out unable to stay silent any longer. Dave doesn’t respond, but his right eyebrow lifts clearly telling me to go on.”I know you told me that getting the title from Lantlas the way I did would start a public relations shit storm. But I fucking did what I was supposed to fix it! I gave Axel a title match just a few weeks after I won that fucking title, and I beat him without any cheating at all! But does anybody care?” I stalk over to my computer where a number of emails wait for me. “Great idea for me to get a new account and give it out Dave,” I mutter sarcastically. “What was it that you said? If I answered even one in ten it would make me all that much popular…well let’s see what my loyal fans have to say.” Out of the corner of my eye I can see Dave wince as he sits down. In fairness to him he had originally planned to hire an assistant to answer all the fan mail; some weird idea of how answering the mail would increase the fans’ love of hating me or some shit like that. I thought it was the dumbest thing I ever heard of, but it did give me the chance to read what some people really thought of me. “Well let’s see here. This one says, and I quote, Axel lost his title because of a bullshit fluke. You don’t deserve to wear it you fake.” Dave tries to interrupt but I just drive through his attempts. “You’ve done absolutely nothing with the title Black! That just proves that it should never have come off Axel in the first place!” Another wince, and another ignored attempt to interrupt. “You’re days of ducking Axel are over Black! He’ll finally get a chance to redeem himself? Well I’m going to go ahead and guess that this guy was stoned during World Wide 123. Not that it matters, since when do little things like facts ever get in the way of a chance to make fun of Dan Black?” “OK!” Dave roars unexpectedly. I turn to face him, my face feeling hot and flushed after reading all that out loud. I can see that Dave hasn’t managed stay totally composed, the tips of his ears are flushed red, which I have come to realize are a sign of genuine anger from the man. “Look, I get what you’re saying but who cares? The fact that the people don’t like you is nothing new to you, hell it’s what lets you make so much money in the first place. Caldera loves that fact that people are always willing to tune because they’ll hope you’ll lose. The fact that you win most of the time is your way of saying fuck you!” I shake my head sadly as I realize that, perhaps for the first time, Dave simply doesn’t understand where I’m coming from. The problem is that I’m not sure that I can even explain it. But, if there is anyone who might be able to help me through this emotional shit it’s Dave, so I better give him something to work with. At this point I honestly don’t know if I’m hoping that he’ll be able to talk me out of my depression, or if I plan to convince him that it’s not possible to talk me out of it. “Have you ever heard of a wrestler by the name of Bill Barnstorm?” I ask him and lean back as memories start to come rushing in. Dave looks inquisitive, but his only answer is to nod no. I nod sadly. “Yea I didn’t think so, he retired maybe my second year in the indie circuit. I didn’t know it at the time, but the boss had made it clear to him that if he lost to me he was out of the business. Believe it or not, I was a crowd pleaser back then…and at the end of the match all I could think of was how happy I beat a veteran. But these days…I don’t know. I guess I can’t help but imagine how it all looked through his eyes.” I close my eyes and think back to the day when my ascendancy in the wrestling industry began at the cost of another’s career. Years Ago I don’t deserve this shit. I’ve been wrestling for twenty years now; I’ve held six different championships, destroyed three different factions, and have had some of the greatest stories that a person ever experienced. But I’ve gotten old. It’s gotten harder and harder for me to hang with these newcomers, and now I’m on an unprecedented losing streak. I’ve already been let go by the organization that I poured the last six years of my life into, and now I’m fighting for my life in a fucking indie org. The owner of this shithole should be down on his knees, thanking God, Jesus, Buddha and the local cult that he has me to work for him. Instead, it’s been made quite clear that if I don’t win this next match I’m going to be expected to retire. I pace nervously as I wait for my opponent to enter the ring. Despite the hundreds of matches I’ve had before, I’ve never been as nervous for any of them as I am right now. It’s not a matter of money. Thankfully I’ve enough of a nest egg stored away that I’m more or less set for the rest of my life. But wrestling is the only life I’ve known for so many years. I don’t know how I’m expected to get up in the morning and not know that I’ll be heading into the ring later in the night. Damnit, I have to calm down. I’ve beaten plenty of wrestlers before. This kid isn’t different in anyway. Well….he’s really damn big. Now normally I would deal with someone like him by using my agility and endurance to tire him out. But I’m not as young as I used to be, and that’s been nowhere clearer than in the way I move these days. He stands across from me, his huge bulk making a stark contrast with my lighter build. The bell rings, and without any sort of posturing we both move towards each other. It’s strength versus skill, youth versus experience. And I am shown that all the experience in the world can be overpowered by brute strength. I fight for as long and hard as I can but in the end I find myself pinned right in the middle of the ring. The crowd cheers wildly for the young upstart, and I hear some jeers directed towards me. Oh…please not that. I’ve made a career out of being the bad guy. It wasn’t a role that I enjoyed, but it was a role that I was good at. It meant a long and successful career, but it also meant that I was hated through almost all of it. But I don’t want to be hated now. In my heart I know that I’ll never enter the ring again. Please, I silently plead with the crowd. This once, just this once let me leave the arena with cheers following me. Don’t let my last memory of this industry be the roar of the crowd as it cheers my final loss over some rookie. The crowd ignores me, and I take my leave with my eyes fixed upon the floor. Back in the Present “I don’t know if I could bear to go out like that,” I admit painfully. “Not the fact that he ended up in the indie circuit, but leaving the business you threw your life into with nothing but boos to wave you bye? Fuck, it’s the kind of thing that Chrs Bagwell would love to be able to do to his enemies. I just think it might be better to get out of the whole thing now, before I end up like that. ” Dave is silent as he stares at me. I stare back at him, just daring him to try and give me some shit about how I’m just too talented to go out like that. I’m no more moron, I know that my success is based on my size and strength more than anything. The second that age starts to catch up to me somewhere down the line…well I don’t really wanna think about it. “I need a drink,” Dave says in a resigned tone of voice that I’ve never heard come from him before. I guess he’s finally beginning to accept that my time might be over. Without a word I stand, and we head to the first bar we can find to drown our mutual sorrows in alcohol. The drive is unusually silent. Most times Dave and I will talk about anything that’s on our minds, which usually happens to be girls we want to sleep with, Dave’s unhappiness that he’s only allowed to sleep with his wife, and video games in that order. Instead we’re just sitting quietly, pretending to be immersed in the music of Metallica blasting from the radio. Finally we’ve arrived at the bar. It’s a rundown place that looks like it’s as likely to serve us piss as it is to serve us beer. Sounds perfect to me. The sound of a scuffle reaches our ears as we make our way towards the door. We turn to the source of the sound, and see a fight going on between four men. Well a fight is the kind way to put it. In reality one nerd (There is no other way to describe him. Between his ridiculously skinny frame, the overly large glasses, and he has a pocket protector. And there are actually pens in said pocket.) is getting his ass kicked by three guys who vaguely remind me of the morons on the Jersey Shore. I watch but for once I don’t get involved. Normally I would love to have a valid excuse to give a much needed ass kicking to these idiots, but something is holding me back. Not fear, or disinterest, or even a lack of sympathy for the kid who’s getting his ass handed him. I just have a vague sense that there’s more going on here than I’m seeing. Finally they push the nerd into the ground and begin to move away. But instead of staying down, which is without a doubt the smart thing to do, he rises…and charges at his tormentors. Again they knock him down, and again he stays down only for a second before getting back up and rushing at them again. A second time. A third, fourth, and fifth. A look of panic has begun to spread on the wannabe tough guys’ faces. They’re not stu…err they’re not stupid enough to take this beating to far. But it’s become clear that the their target is not willing to let them simply have their fun and be done with it. The crazy son of a bitch was going to make them pay, and he was more than ready to die to do it. They panic, and begin to run towards their car. With blood running down his face and out of his nose the insane bastard follows them. One of them stops just long enough to deliver another punch to their pursuers face, knocking him down just long enough for them to make their escape. When he gets up the kid doesn’t bitch, or moan, or throw some sort of tantrum. Instead he calmly retrieves his glasses from the ground, and walks off to God knows where. Everyone who was watching the whole incident occurs gives him a wide berth as he passes, a sign of respect that he shed blood to earn. I feel a smile break across my face as I watch the k- as I watch the man depart. I turn to Dave, laughing for the first time in a long time. He stares at me confusedly, and his lack of comprehension only makes me laugh harder. “Don’t you get it Dave,” I finally go down to a slight chuckle. “This whole time, I thought I knew what that day had been like for Bill. But I got it completely, spectacularly, wrong!” Ten Years Ago Inwardly, I smile as the roar of cheers storm the arena because of Black’s victory over me. It doesn’t matter that the crowd is unwilling to give me a proper send-off, because they already legitimized by legacy throughout the entire match. After all, there I was a washed up has been wrestling in an indie org, and they were still determined to see me lose. Every ground shaking storm of boos that they sent my way when it looked like I might pull out a win, all it meant was that even after all this time they still remembered the way I brought their heroes low. Let this kid bask in the glow of winning a match, all that matters is that I went out with people still caring that I lost. That’s a win in my book. Back to the Present Dave looks at me dubiously. “I’m sorry Dan, but that is some of the strangest logic I ever heard. You’re telling me that Bill was glad that he was jeered out of the arena in his very last match?” “That’s not the point Dave,” I answer patiently in a semi-permanent good mood. “Look at that kid, you ask anyone and they’ll say that he got his ass kicked. But he knows that he made those three morons run away from him, fuck what anyone else thinks. So forget what people will think about me in twenty years, what matters is that I’ll be able to look back and say I’m happy with what I did. Right now that means kicking Axel’s ass and forcing him to admit to the world that I’m a better wrestler than he ever will be. “ Dave still looks uncertain, but I know he’ll be content with the knowledge that I’ve gotten my old fire back. Let the whole fucking world say what they want, I’m the rightful GCW United States champion, and I plan to stay that way for a long ass time. “Alright Dan, but I’m still gonna shut down that stupid email account,” Dave declares. “It was a stupid idea on my part and I’m sorry I put you through it.” I nod my agreement, and an idea hits me. I start chuckling again, which puts another puzzled look on Dave’s face. “Tell you what Dave, shut it down if you want. Put hell, they took the time to write, it’s only fair that we send them a response.” I open the bar door as Dave stands behind in an obvious confusion. “Send every last one of em a dreidel.”
