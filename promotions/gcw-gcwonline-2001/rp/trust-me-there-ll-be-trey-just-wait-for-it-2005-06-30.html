Trust me, there'll be Trey. Just wait for it.

30 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 30, 2005 My chin sunk into the loose neck of my t-shirt as we passed through the crowded streets of a town whose name never began to matter. The world was the dying orange of a firecracker and I couldn't separate my life from my lies anymore, lost in cheap tequila and forgetting who were my enemies and who were my friends--and eventually finding a hint of contentment in just accepting that I remembered them, which had to be enough. Human interaction was lost on my primitive brain, so I found myself crawling along with only the vaguest idea of where I was going, of who I was following, of if any of it mattered beyond trying to find a string of thought to pull myself out of the muck with. Faces contorted into cruel mockeries as they pushed past me, attached to wandering streams of flesh that had places to go, people to meet. My stomach twisted at the notion of goals and under the pressure of a Spanish meal that would've fed a sane man for weeks; not to mention that in avoiding the water, I'd been keeping a constant stream of booze running down my throat at all times. It kept me from having to think and forced me down and down until I'd start to drink for the sake of thinking again. It was just the natural effect of Mexico on the dissonant mind--bunched in tight alleyways with each person as desperate as the next, shoulder to shoulder with stucco and buried in a cloud of sand--and no man could fight it in the dingy corner I'd had hunted out for me. I stopped at a stand made of two-by-fours nailed together and draped with dried out leaves from a plant I didn't even believe in, and began singling out amongst the trinkets a roughhewn pipe that looked carved from the thighbone of some beaten animal. The woman behind the stand stared at me with big brown eyes, asking questions that I would've understood years before, and I just nodded along as I picked it up--Trey jerking steadily on my arm: 'Yo, J; hurry up man.' I tried to say 'Hold on,' but I'm not expecting that it came out as anything but an irritated slur, and examined the pipe with more conviction. Stylized skulls, reminiscent of the Dia de los Muertos or worse, were engraved to wrap around the bowl--which itself was dug from the termination of the bone at a joint. It was light in my hands and smooth to the touch like stones in a riverbed. Trey tried to take it from me, to which I jerked away quickly and stammered out something I can't remember. 'I don't wanna lose Sobias, man. We'd never find his ass.' I shook my head and fumbled for my wallet, paying the woman with the first bill I found and following behind Trey in dumb amazement at the pipe I clutched tight to my gut. I smelled everyone else's sweat and ignored how my own radiated in cartoonish waves away from my body. The nape of my neck was drenched, belt wrapped taut around my stomach and rubbing raw, my clothes tight to my skin and I wondered if the heat was the sun's or that of the people that herded this way and that, always body to body, always tight. locked eyes as they came near and saw my desolation in every squinted pair; faking crazy smiles until Trey said something about scaring the natives and wrapped a hand around my bicep... I was being jerked around, ripped against the current of bodies by Trey--still talking about something. 'What?,' or a noise that definitely ended with an interrogative rise in pitch came out of my mouth and Trey turned. He was a hard shadow with the sun against his back, that creeping dread that lingers behind the darkest doors in our mental ghettos. 'Synergy' ran through my head like a curse that just wasn't as catchy as 'croatoa' had been, but at least I understood what it was to see that hell-in-Trey that had pushed him through those early matches. 'Johnny Lexicon. You brought me here so you could deal with that motherfucker, man. Don't forget who he is.' I toyed with the name like a childhood a Transformer, twisting it out of shape endlessly until it finally ended up the same as it started, except endowed with an artificial sense of accomplishment. 'I didn't forget the reason, you son of a bitch.' 'Then quit holdin' us up. I don'even know where Sobias's at now, man. Damn. Just c'mon.' 'Fuck you,' blurted about my animal mind as I pulled my arm back and slipped through a bead curtain into the nearest doorway. Trey came in behind me, looking tired and unamused. 'You ain't gotta make this hard, y'know?' The TV hanging from the ceiling in a far corner had a line of people dancing, headed off by a luchadore with his exposed mouth painted white. Trey said something else, and when I turned back to look a woman in a lavender veil was handing a man and a woman in red pleather cowboy outfits a handful of bills; Christ, I thought, what the hell has this liquor done to me? 'Yo, man, if you wanna chill in here while I look for him, that's straight. You've helped my ass out when I was fucked, so I got no problem with it. That cool man?' I nodded and collapsed in a booth. Trey still hung over me, an uneasy palette of shifting shadows fighting against burst of lights playing out across his wifebeater. 'Aight.' I put the pipe in my lap and let my forehead bang against the table. A faint clapping came from across what I came to understand as a cantina, and I looked back up to see an audience of people in Halloween costumes celebrating a commercial break on the washed-out TV screen. Resting my head on its side with a pool of sweat forming, I saw few people--scattered across the room in random distribution--all sinking themselves headlong into drink. Most were old and wore their years in thick beards and scars that skitched across leathery skin, warnings against whatever kind of life they'd lived. I thought about Trey and Dangerous Games then, finding a certain piece in the abject horror that lay ahead. Cognizance was limited to a small lot of words that I played with as best they could, trying to rationalize how far away I was from what mattered. Anarch stood to lose everything or to walk away with the world, and there weren't enough executive decisions I could hand down to guarantee either. Trey'd been too quiet about it and I'd been too consistently loaded to take stock of what was running through his head that weekend, but I'm not sure he took it as anything but another match and a battle royal--not to take anything away from how much he puts into every match he gets booked for. It's the way he seems to work best, not taking part in idle talk for my ability to turn it into a deeply-ingrained paranoia that could take a person's spinal column and leave them rotting in fear. A good choice, I think. I trusted him pulling through with two victories like I trusted myself sobering up enough to actually impress the woman that walked to my table with a pad in her hand. Long black hair, as jet as a panther, draped over her shoulders and she wore a lime green one-piece with a low hemline that bunched her tanned breasts together and shoved them out for the world to see, and long legs slipped out from the bottom of the flowing cloth. The fabric, sheer and flowing, was held tight to her body with sweat in spots. I could sober up if I tried, yeah, but it wouldn't impress her. Half-way there, that's how I understood Trey's chances. He'd win half of his battles, I figured, even if statistics would leave him at having a better chance of coming out with two wins and no hardcore title. That would feel right. She asked something like 'me pide' and I looked up into her big hazel eyes. 'Mucho rum. Muy caro.' She smiled. 'Es eso todo?' 'Si.' I had six cigarettes left and no desire to ration them, so I struck one and lingered in my corner of the bar and watched as she walked away. Too close to Hemingway or Thompson; I could taste the barrels, feel the shock run through my skull in those last moments. More drink was obviously needed. She came back when the cigarette was half-ash, and sat a bottle of rum and a tall glass of ice in front of me. 'Es eso todo?' again. I smirked, face to the table. 'No habla espanol,' except with the accent. She sat down across from me and poured a glass of rum--I drank it like Caligula. I poured a glass and offered it to her, but she shook her head. I couldn't trust her anymore and finished the glass in silence, staring blankly ahead like the damned when a voice rang out from behind the bar. The girl's eyes twitched up and she said something I've forgotten before she disappeared in time for Trey to find me alone and wholly absorbed in the drink. 'Jordan, shit, man. Get up, I just saw some weird shit.' 'What?' 'Over by this warehouse a few buildings down, I found Sobias--dude was talkin' to this big guy in a mask.' 'And? He probably owes Sobias something.' 'No, asshole. It was your mask, from back when you were the Marauder.' 'You're sure?' 'Skull an' crossbones, rockin' headphones. Yeah, s'your mask.' 'I let that fat bastard in my company and the first thing he does is fucking steal my trademarks.' My hand jerked under the table to the side pocket of my pants and wrapped around the heavy pine box I'd carried along. 'We'll fucking show him what it means to.' 'You trailed off.' 'Fuck it. Lead the way.' I grabbed the bottle of rum and pushed up from my booth, throwing another bill on the table while the raven-haired woman stared at me. Trey was outside waiting when I pushed back out of the curtains and the dying sun caught me unaware--left me half-blind and staggering in a stupid drunk. I drank a shot to escape it and followed Trey as best I could. 'That's the warehouse they were talkin' at.' It looked empty. A beaten gray-and-yellow scooter kicked up dust as it shot past, maybe trying to hammer in how dead the hole I'd wallowed into was. 'C'mon, let's get inside.' It was as good an idea as any I'd gone through with that day, so Trey talked to the men at the doors, eventually handing them money in some bizarre ritual exchange. We were let inside, though they kept a hard enough stare on my shoulders that I wondered how long my presence would last. Another swig from the bottle and a smile; 'Don't worry, I'm a wrestler.' The larger of the two men laughed and stepped up. 'Really?' 'C'mon Jordan.' Trey grabbed my arm again and pulled me inside, instantly defeaned by the roar of the crowd that had disappeared. Throngs of people whipped about, bellowing out in unison a devilish chant as the sound of a body being tossed around echoed above all. We found an aisle--or I followed Trey down one--and followed him again as he muscled through. I clumsily apologized in his wake, waving the rum bottle around and trying feebly to light a cigarette. We came to the metal guard rails as a luchadore began his pre-match routine of flexing for the crowd and springing around on the ropes, waiting for his opponent to be announced. I didn't care about meters and I didn't know kilos, but the name that came next caught me in its mysticism. '--MARAUDER!' The crowd exploded again and I pushed up on the rail to look over the ring and down the short aisle that the wrestlers used. It was hard to make out details, but I knew the walk well-enough that it sent me into a bad headspin of wicked nostalgia. When he finally stepped into the ring, a head taller than the poor sap they'd sent to face him, I'd had enough. Seeing my mask on him sent my stomach retching in despair--I disappeared into the crowd, quickly towards the door, as I heard those tell-tale thumps, the hands slapping, and a tidal wave of humans so rapt in the moment that they'd be ready for sacrifice, all chanting my name. Marauder. Marauder. Marauder. I made it outside and into an alley before the first wave of vomit hit. It slapped against the sand with that disgusting plop that always calls out a second round, and a third. A fourth until I felt human, and the fifth left me hollow and melting in desperation. I pulled the bottle to my lips, drinking to keep it down, and pushed out of the alley, hiding in long shadows cast by the setting sun as I progressed back to the cantina. I stopped in one alley to finish off my assault, then turned and slinked through the beaded curtain with my head low and my ego smashed. The booth was waiting. Calm. Empty.
