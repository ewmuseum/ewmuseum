Do I Know You?

13 November 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/13/09 They told him he wouldn't beat CJ Woods, that his title reign would come to an end at Dangerous Games. The outcome wasn't even up for debate. He would lose, and then he would fade into obscurity, a blip on the radar, a simple footnote in the annals of Global Championship Wrestling Television title history. But he proved them all wrong. A couple of weeks before Dangerous Games, Woods decided to send a message to Cade, and to anyone who was watching. Woods was back, and he was someone that was about to make some noise. After Cade had defeated Lucas Quinton to become the new TV champion, Woods attacked Cade and spoiled any chance for him to celebrate his newly claimed gold. He cheated Cade of his moment in the spotlight, and at Dangerous Games, it was believed he would do so again. This time, however, Cade got his revenge. There would be no sneak attack from Woods, Cade would see him coming from a mile away. And without that advantage, without the benefit of a blindside attack, on that night Cade proved he was the better wrestler, putting an exclamation point on that statement when he nailed Woods with his finisher that he dubs the Act of Contrition. Now was his chance to gloat, to shove it down their throats. The people who doubted him, the people who didn't give him a snowball's chance in hell of walking out of Dangerous Games with the TV title still around his waist. He wanted nothing more than to hold the television title belt to their faces and scream, "How do you like that?". But there was one problem with that. He wasn't sure whether those voices of the naysayers were real, or in his head. Cade sat on a stool at the end of the bar, sipping at a glass of what was now room-temperature beer. His mind was like a hyperactive child, bouncing off the walls, restless, unable to keep one coherent thought for more than a couple of seconds at a time. His emotions, likewise, were on a proverbial rollercoaster ride. One moment he felt elation at his victory at GCW's latest pay-per-view, retaining his television title against a talented opponent. The next moment, embarrassment over his poor showing later on that evening, being the first to be eliminated in the Dangerous Games match. It was as if his mind simply wouldn't allow him a moment of satisfaction. For everything he succeeded at, it reminded him of one of his failures. Well, at least I can never be accused of having delusions of grandeur, he thought with bitter amusement as he sloshed back the last few drops of his flat, foul tasting warm beverage. Getting up shakily from the bar stool, he steadied himself for a moment, then started to make his way to the exit. He'd been at this bar for the past four hours, had drank heavily within the first couple of hours, but then nursed his final beer over the last two. It was long past time for him to head home and crawl into bed. "What's your hurry, handsome?" Cade turned at the sound of the sultry female voice. Sitting alone at a table not too far away from his bar stool was a pretty young woman, with medium length brown hair, a slim, appetizing body, and a playful smile. And the short, tight blue dress she wore made Cade's already-alcohol fogged mind spin even more. She kicked the chair across from her away from the table, and gestured for Cade to join her. "You're not leaving already, are you?" Cade smiled. His cold, lonely bed could wait ... at least for a little while longer. Looks like the rollercoaster's on its way up again. * * * * * * * * * * As soon as he opened his eyes, he instantly regretted it. "Ahh!" Cade gasped at the sudden glare of sunlight spilling in through his bedroom window. He clamped his eyes shut and went to sit upright. And instantly regretted that, too. A wave of dizziness hit him, followed by a blinding pain that seemed to start at the back of his skull, then move to a spot just behind his eyes. He sat as still as a statue for a moment, willing the agonizing pain to go away. After a couple of tense breaths it subsided a bit, replaced by a more tolerable throbbing throughout his skull. He didn't want to tempt fate just yet, so he remained sitting in an upright position, arms behind him to prop himself up. As the pounding in his head lessened to a more annoying than painful level, he could finally clear his mind and think straight. Images of the previous evening returned to him. Wandering the streets until he found an out-of-the-way bar. Drinking alone at the end of the bar, ignoring the bartender's attempts at conversation. Deciding to call it a night and heading for the door. Being intercepted by a beautiful young woman whose name he couldn't remember and then ... He turned to look beside him. The bed was empty, but the pillow was creased and the mattress was indented slightly as if by a body that was much smaller than his own frame. Instinctively he reached over his hand and ran it over the surface of where she'd slept. The sheets were still warm to the touch, so she'd left not too long ago. Cade frowned at that. While he was under no delusions of what happened last night turning into more than a one night stand, he was confused at the woman's actions. Why would she leave without saying a word? She obviously found him attractive. She was the one that beckoned him over. Maybe she was embarrassed by what she'd done. Cade had known a few women that let their wild and nasty side show when they'd been drinking, only to then feel dirty the next day when their sense and reasoning returned to them. Maybe that was what had happened here. He wasn't bothered by her departure, per se. He just hated mysteries that he couldn't solve. Risking an attack of vertigo, Cade stood from the bed, naked apart from a pair of black boxer shorts, and made his way to the bathroom. He intended to get to the bottom of this mysterious woman. But first, he was in desperate need of a hot shower. * * * * * * * * * * "Ye think he's losin' it?" Darius Drannen glanced up from reading the newspaper, and shot his squat friend a curious look. "What are you talking about?" Thibbledorf "Rumple" Stitskin nodded his head in the direction of Cade's bedroom, where their friend had disappeared to, mumbling to himself. "Cade. Don't he seem all outta sorts to ya?" Darius glanced briefly toward the bedroom, then turned to look at Rumple again. He shook his head. "Nah. He just gets like this after his matches. A lot of nervous energy." He shrugged. "It's like he's still running on adrenaline or something. He'll be fine." "Maybe," Rumple didn't sound convinced. He tapped an index finger to his lips, then his eyes widened as a thought struck him. "I think I'll go cheer the lad up." "I'd advise against that," Darius called after him, halting him in his tracks. When Rumple turned and gave him a questioning look, Darius said, "Remember the last time you tried to cheer him up when he wasn't in the mood to be cheered up?" Rumple rubbed the end of his bulbous nose. "Ye make a good point there. Cade can be a little ... violent when somethin'd crawled up his arse." "On second thought, maybe you should go see what's bothering him," Darius offered with a wide grin. "Oh, ha-ha, Darius," Rumple glared back. "Yer so fricken' funny I'd forgotten te laugh." The pitter-patter of footsteps from the direction of the bedroom interrupted their bantering. Both men went silent at the sound of Cade's approach. He wore a frown on his face as he exited his bedroom, and seemed to be surveying the kitchen, searching for something. First he made his way to the kitchen counter and pushed aside the growing stack of mail that sat there. Then he turned his attention to the side table, opening its drawers and rifling around inside. When neither yielded whatever he was looking for, he walked toward the kitchen exit and disappeared inside the living room. Darius and Rumple exchanged looks. "Well that was strange." Rumpled planted his fists on his hips, shooting Darius a mocking smile. "He'll be fine, ye say? Should'a known better'n than to listen to yer boneheaded advice." Darius shot Rumple a dirty look, but didn't bother offering a retort. Instead he put the newspaper down and followed after Cade. When he caught up to Cade, he found his friend rummaging through one of the built-in cabinets under the coffee table. He noticed that the other cabinet sat ajar, and various items were sprawled on the floor in front of it. "What are you looking for?" Darius inquired. "It's gone." "What's gone?" Cade didn't respond right away, instead concentrating on searching the cabinet he'd been looking through when Darius had first entered the room. Then with a sigh, Cade shut the cabinet and sat back on the floor, shaking his head. "The picture, the one with my parents and Father Michea," Cade finally answered. "I can't find it." "Can't find it? Where'd you last see it?" Cade gestured toward his bedroom. "It was sitting on my dresser, propped up against the mirror. But it's not there anymore. I thought maybe I'd put it away with the rest of my pictures," he motioned to the cabinets where, presumably, he kept his photograph albums, "but it's not in here either." "Okay then, when was the last time you remember seeing it?" Cade thought about that for a moment. At first, nothing seemed to come to him. But just as he was about to shake his head in frustration, his eyes widened. "Could it be possible?" Cade mumbled in response, but it appeared as though he was speaking to himself rather than Darius. "But why?" Darius tone was flustered. "Could what be possible? Why what? Cade, you're not making any sense." Cade looked at Darius as if he'd just remembered his friend was there. "The girl I brought home last night. The one I told you about." "You mean Stop-and-Go?" Darius said, referring to their little nickname they'd come up for her after her quick exit before Cade had woken up. "Why would she steal the picture? I mean, it's not even a picture of you." "Your guess is as good as mine," Cade shrugged. "But I've got to find her." "Do you even know how to get in touch with her?" Cade paused. "No. But the bartender might." "Cade ..." Darius began skeptically. Cade interrupted him. "I have to find that picture, Darius. And she's the best lead I've got." Before Darius could offer any more reasons for why this was a longshot, Cade was heading for the front door. Shaking his head, Darius went after him. "Hold up, Cade. I'm coming with you." At the sound of the front door slamming shut, Rumple made his way out of the kitchen, a chicken drumstick in one hand and a dirty napkin in the other. He glanced around the empty apartment, furrowing his brow. "Cade? Darius?" When he received no response he merely shrugged his shoulders and grinned lewdly down at the meaty drumstick in his hand. "Ah well, the more fer me then."
