Same Song, Second Verse

9 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 05/09/06 The melody may have changed, but the song remains the same. It's Shayne Steele in GCW, take two. I took a long hiatus from the wrestling business, and boy...was it fantastic. I even told a good friend of mine that I was walking away for good. And at that time, I probably meant it. This sport has taken my blood, my sweat, and my sanity away from me for 8 long years. I have done everything I wanted to (except win a World Title) and managed to become a rather respected star in this business. I was through. That was until the next call came in. It was from a well known 'garbage' wrestling promoter in Japan. You know, light tubes, C4 and stuff. Not exactly my cup of sake, but what did I have to lose? Boy was THAT the wrong attitude to take. After being offered next to nothing to appear and fight a fellow 'Gaijin', I show up in Osaka, hoping and praying that I would be able to talk this guy into letting me fight an actual WRESTLING match. Of course, he very politely (as is the Japanese way) told me to 'Go forth, be fruitful, and multiply.' Not in those exact words, mind you, but I think you get the picture. Oh no, he wasn't going to let me wrestle. I was going to be in a 'Barbed Wire Osaka Street Fight' the next night. Oh man, I was psyched. Once the bell rang, I knew I was fighting a guy who had very little experience in the ring, when he suplexed me, and dropped me on my damn head. I am sure it looked awesome to the crowd, but it was just a horribly botched suplex. And they cheered. Loudly. And after about 10 minutes of fumbling around, the blood started to flow. I was cut from ear to ear, via a good ol' barbed wire rubdown from my opponent. He grinded my face into that stuff as hard as he could. That son of a bitch was shouting the whole time, 'Welcome to the show, rookie.' ROOKIE? Oh, baby. This guy was dumber that I thought. I snapped. I lost my mind. I went totally Gary Busey on this guy. I threw him back, and next thing you know, he was in a good ol' wrestling hold. The figure four leglock. And I wasn't letting go. The referee looked at me, my opponent screamed, and then POP...goes the knee. He passed out from the pain, and I was run out of the ring by some security folks. 'You will never work in this organization again. You are a disgrace to your profession.' Thank God for small miracles. I left, came back home, and for a few months, did regular tours of Japan for an actual wrestling promotion. I even had a shot at their World Title. But it wasn't enough. Mr. Big is a hot commodity in Japan. So is Jennifer Love Hewitt. I could not be to the Japanese people what David Hasselhoff is to the Germans. As over with them as I was, and wrestling as the top gaijin worker in their company, I just couldn't stomach the thought of being 'big in Japan.' About two months ago, I came home for good. I was looking for work all over the place, but wrestling at Jed's Tire Shop and Mammograms in Dothan, Alabama just didn't seem all that appealing. I had lost name value. I had lost my niche in the industry. I had lost my partner, and with it, my push. GCW gave me the opportunity to work again. And for that, I am grateful. Confucius once said...or, was it Arn Anderson...er...anyways, that 'Adversity introduces a man to himself.' I have seen a lot of things in me that I never knew existed. And as far as that elusive World Title goes, it's only a matter of time. All the hard work I have put in should pay off sooner or later. After all...it is my kismetic destiny.
