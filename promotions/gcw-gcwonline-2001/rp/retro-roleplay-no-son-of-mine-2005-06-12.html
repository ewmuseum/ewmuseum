Retro Roleplay - No Son of Mine

12 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6-12-2005 A kid, maybe in his mid-twenties, walked up to a plush chair and took a seat. He seemed nervous, unsure of the situation. His head looked around the room to get an idea of his surroundings. It was a basic office, a few books on shelves, a fake plant in the corner, and a large oak desk that sat by the far wall. There were no windows in the room, which made the youth uneasy. He jumped when he heard the door close. Turning his head, he saw an older man with gray hair holding a pen and clipboard. The gentleman walked up to the boy and smiled politely at him. He took a seat on the nearby chair of similar design. “Evan,” the man said in a calm and soothing voice, “I want you to tell me what is on your mind.” The man clicked his pen and applied it to the clipboard, ready to take notes. Evan adjusted in his seat and got comfortable. Taking a deep breath, he began to tell his tale. “Well doc, the key to my survival was never in much doubt. The question was how I could keep sane trying to find a way out.” The pen nonchalantly scribbled across the paper as Evan spoke. “Things were never easy for me, peace of mind was hard to find and I needed a place where I could hide.” Evan turned his head toward the doctor, who had his head down into his clipboard. “You know, somewhere I could call mine.” Taking a break from his writings, the doctor nodded “yes, now please, go on.” Evan turned his head and looked forward as he continued. “I didn't think much about it til it started happening all the time. Soon I was living with the fear everyday of what might happen at night.” Memories, painful memories, started to resurface. That was why he had come here though, to try to learn to deal with these memories. “I couldn't stand to hear the crying of my mother and I remember when I swore that, that would be the last they'd see of me.” Evan stopped for a second, seemingly in pain. He clinched his fist, but he continued to speak. “And I never went home again.” The doctor hummed at the last part, writing furiously now on the clipboard. Evan adjusted himself in the chair once more. “They say time is a healer and now my wounds are not the same. I rang the bell with my heart in my mouth. I had to hear what he'd say.” “Your father?” the good doctor asked. Evan just nodded as he recalled the events of that meeting. “He sat me down to talk to me. He looked me straight in the eyes and he said: “You're no son, no son of mine”.” Evan repeated himself, “You're no son, no son of mine.” He had to fight to keep back the tears he felt like shedding. Evan continued to recite what his father had told him on that pale September night. “You walked out, you left us behind and you're no son, no son of mine.” he told me,” Evan had to stop; he needed to take a breath. Once he felt he was better he went on, “oh, his words how they hurt me, I'll never forget it and as the time, it went by, I lived to regret it.” He hung his head in shame, a tear rolled down the side of his face. Quickly he wiped it away before the doctor could see it. His shame grew as he knew his father would just laugh at him if he saw him crying like this. To himself he repeated the words his father said to him, “You're no son, no son of mine.” “But where should I go, and what should I do” he asked the doctor. The doctor had to answer for him, just as Evan knew he would not. His problems were not something a quick answer could fix. In the back of his mind, he could still hear his father’s voice calling out to him: “you're no son, no son of mine”. Those words were like daggers through his heart. “But I came here for help, I came here for you” he thought to himself. His father though had done little to help, if anything it only drove Evan away further. Noticing Evan had gone quietly into thought, the doctor spoke up again. “Evan,” he said, “tell me what happened next.” “Well the years they passed slowly. I thought about him everyday. What would I do, if we passed on the street” Would I keep running away?” Placing his hands together, Evan bridged them over his nose and sulked down into his chair. “In and out of hiding places, soon I'd have to face the facts. We'd have to sit down and talk it over and that would mean going back.” Evan shook his head, his father’s voice went from a quiet whisper to a deafening roar in his head; “you're no son, no son of mine”. The emotions became too much for Evan to bear. He turned away from the doctor, who had his nose in the clipboard, who was still writing away. “I have to go doc,” Evan told him, “They say time is a healer and now my wounds are not the same. I rang the bell with my heart in my mouth. I had to hear what he'd say.” Booming, louder, and louder, Evan thought his father’s voice was going to rip his head to pieces. “He sat me down to talk to me. He looked me straight in the eyes and he said: “You're no son, no son of mine”.” Those seven words kept repeating over and over in his head. As much as he tried to silence it out, it only got louder. Evan jumped to his feet, knocking the chair back over. He grabbed his head with both hands, bent over in pain. “You're no son, no son of mine!” Evan screamed. He shook his head trying to force the voice from his mind. The doctor now stood up and grabbed Evan, in an attempt to calm him down. “You're no son, no son of mine! No! No! No son of mine!” From the lobby the other patients could hear Evan’s cries. They ignored it though; they had their own problems to deal with. Who needed to worry about a repressed son of some former big shot wrestler? --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- There were plenty of reasons people never liked Chris Storm, not in the ring but outside the ring. The main reason, one which most knew, was that Chris never could shake his in ring persona from his real life persona. That is understandable when your whole life became dedicated to wrestling, but that still did not make it acceptable. Chris would come home from a night of wrestling and stayed Chris Storm, never dropping the act and relaxing as Scott Drake. To him, Scott Drake was a complete stranger. One who found herself a victim of this several times was Tessa Windsor. Countless nights Scott had came home and unloaded on her about his recent lost. Poor Tessa, she always had to carry his extra baggage. Scott truly did not deserve someone like her in his life, more so when someone like her who cared about him as much as she did. He was blessed but he never knew it. In a way, they needed each other. Tessa needed someone, anyone, to be a rock for her to cling to when times got rough. She needed a leader who would not think twice about giving orders, even if they were not right. Scott needed someone who would actually listen to his orders. Ying and Yang. One played off the other’s weakness to make their strength. Caught in the middle of all this were the children. The children suffered because of it. Scott came in from another long day at the gym. Since his return to UWF, he had slipped back into his old habit of sending far too long at the gym. Scott was hell bent on getting into tiptop shape. Tessa had also started spending more time away from the home the two shared as she returned to the limelight in GW and PTC. Success had come her way and now she seemed just as driven as Scott had become. “Where is Tess?” Scott asked, looking around for her. From the kitchen came Evan, the six-year-old adopted son of the duo. He had a glass of milk in his hand and seemed laissez-faire about never having two parents home at the same time. “She left out with Dee again; I don’t know where they went though.” Showing surprising calmness about the situation Evan took a sip of his milk, leaving a white mustache over his lip. Scott did not have the same reaction as the six-year-old, “what” Ah, how could she without giving me a call? Damnit I bet Dawn talked her into doing it. She’s been neglecting her responsibilities more and more since she started hanging around her.” The statement dripped with irony, as Scott was not in a position to criticize another person? parenting skills when he himself barely had any. “Can we go throw the baseball outside, please?” Evan asked walking over to the couch where his glove and ball laid. In a world all his own, Scott walked past Evan and flopped down on the couch, sitting on the glove and letting the ball slip between the cushions. “Not right now Evan, I want to relax for a bit.” Granted Scott still felt sore from pushing himself so hard at the gym, it did little to comfort Evan as he walked away to his room. Scott never even bothered to look over his shoulder and watch Evan leave. One was left to wonder if Scott even felt the slightly ping of guilt after what he did, his exterior shell seemed to suggest he did not feel any remorse for his actions. After watching a movie Scott found on the television he heard the front door creep open. Scott looked out the couch to the hallway and saw Tessa casually walking into the kitchen. Scott rolled over and got to his feet, walking into the kitchen. “Well it’s about time someone got home” he remarked to her as she opened the refrigerator for a bottle of water. Tessa closed the refrigerator door and turned to him, spun the cap off the bottle and took a sip. “Yeah,” she answered, “Dee and I went out for a bit; we were doing an autograph signing at a store not far from here.” Her reply showed the lack of understanding for just what bothered Scott, thankfully (or not, depending on how you looked at it) he explained to her his problem. “Do you know how long I have had to stay here to watch the kids” I was supposed to go meet Arizona twenty minutes ago for our usual tactics discussion. Now I am late.” Tessa walked right past him continuing to down her water, “well I’m sorry” she said, sounding like she actually was not, “I told you I would need you to watch the kids today, but as usual you did not pay attention to me.” “When did you tell me?” Scott questioned her, not recalling this piece of information. “Last night!” her voice jumped from normal conversation to a commanding yell, a tone she had been using more often around Scott. Rolling his eyes Scott followed Tessa into the living room. “Well of course I would not have heard you, House was on woman. House! You do not tell me anything during that show; I’m not going to listen to you. I’m in House mode. You don’t bother me when I am in House mode.” “Oh I am so sorry, next time I have important information I’ll just wait for the commercials” Tessa remarked sarcastically. Scott never noticed her sarcasms. “Good, you do that.” For the second time Tessa rolled her eyes. The pint-sized brunette hopped onto the couch and promptly changed the channel from the movie Scott had been watching. She shifted around uncomfortable until she reached under her and pulled out Evan’s baseball glove. “What is this doing here?” she asked looking to Scott. Scott grabbed it from her, tossed it to the chair sitting near the couch, and took a spot next to Tessa. “Ah, Evan wanted to go play catch with me.” Tessa sat up shocked “and you didn’t!”” her tone was a mixture of anger and questioning. “I was tired, I had just gotten in from the gym,” Scott told her, brushing off her anger. “I can’t believe you!” Tessa yelled as she got up from the couch, leaving Scott by himself. This time Scott rolled his eyes as he got off the couch and walked over to Tessa. “What is the big deal” I’ll play catch with him tomorrow.” “No,” Tessa told him as she reminded him, “You have to catch your flight for the UWF show. So unless you plan to play catch on the plane, I can’t see that happening.” Scott slapped his forehead, “ah that is right, I totally forgot. Still Evan and I have plenty of time to play later. The kid didn’t even seem to care really.” Hearing his name mentioned several times, Evan came walking back into the room, unseen by Scott or Tessa. He stood behind the couch, still within ear reach of the two parents. “I really can’t believe you sometimes,” Tessa repeated, still upset about the whole ordeal. Scott turned around and pointed an accusing finger at her. “What about you” When was the last time you played with either of the kids? You’ve been on such a whirlwind of success that you forgot all about them as well.” Tessa seemed taken back by Scott’s attack. It was true; her recent success had caused her to spend less time with the kids than she had in the past. “It’s ok, you don’t have to play with me, I don’t mind.” It was the little voice of Evan that spoke up. Scott and Tessa stopped dead in their tracks. The words tugged at their heartstrings. Tessa kneeled down in front of Evan, “No sweetie, we do want to play with you. We have both just been very busy as of late, that is all.” Standing beside both of them now, Scott placed his hand on Evan’s head. “Yeah, I am really sorry Evan. I promise next time we can go play catch. In fact?” beep’beep’beep Before Scott could finish, his beeper interpreted. Removing his hand from Evan’s head, he reached for his beeper. Tessa gave Scott a disapproving look but Scott decided to ignore it and check the message anyways. “Ah crap” Scott muttered to himself as he put away his beeper. “What?” Tessa asked, knowing his response was a bad thing for them. “Battle Royal” Scott muttered. It was all he really needed to say. Tessa, a fellow wrestler, knew the pains of being in a Battle Royal. It did not help matters that Scott had just recently passed fifth in a PTC Battle Royal. Unfortunately, for him, again there would be no prize for fifth. Scott turned away and grabbed his gym bag he had tossed aside when he entered. Tessa watched him with surprised eyes. “You’re leaving?” Looking over his shoulder Scott replied, “You know my track record for those cluster matches. I can’t just sit around and slack off.” His hand shot to the doorknob, getting ready to make a quick exit. “You have to catch a flight tomorrow to make the show yet you are going to spend your day home at the gym” Is that how little your family means to you?” Tessa was legitimately hurt from Scott’s actions. With their busy schedules, they barely had time together and now when they did, he wanted to spend it at the gym. Scott looked at them than at his gym bag. With the zipper partly pulled back he could see inside the bag at his old WIW t-shirt he always wore when training (it was to remind him of what he knew he could be). He was stuck in a place he did not want to be, having to decide between his career and his family. His fists were clinched in a ball, tightly squeezing the fabric handle of the gym bag. He closed his eyes and looked back at his family. “I’m sorry?” he said, almost whispering it. He turned and walked out that door leaving his family behind. It was not the first time and they all knew it would not be the last. Tessa just hoped it was worth it. To himself, Scott secretly wondered the same thing.
