Things begin to look up

20 October 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/20/08 I desperately hung onto the wrist of the arm that was wrapped around my neck, but for all my strength the action was futile. The vice-like grip around my throat tightened and my ability to breathe was abruptly gone. My tongue came out of my mouth as my lungs began to burn, warning bells began to ring in my mind as the world darkened. Defeated I finally tapped, and my opponent immediately released his rear naked choke hold. I fell back, deeply breathing in every bit of sweet air that I could get into my lungs as I looked up at the ceiling. It had been weeks since I last hired a detective to tail Greg, and find out how he was treating Brian. From the little information that he gave me, things were not looking good as Greg had left Brian’s care entirely over to a hired nurse and that was a hard environment to grow up in. The nurse was apparently loving and caring enough for the time being, but she would probably be fired once Brian could walk and talk. After that he would almost certainly grow up alone. Unfortunately, that was not enough for me to get custody of Brian. As long as Brian was fed and properly cared for the law said that he belonged with his biological father, instead of his mother’s boyfriend. Now I was waiting on a miracle, hoping that the detective I hired would be able to dig up something on Greg that would cause a judge to declare him an unfit parent. But so far, all that Greg seemed to be guilty of was being a workaholic. From what I was told Greg didn’t have a record of any sort, had never been in any sort of trouble at school, college, or with the law. In fact the investigator (whose name as it turns out is Max) doubted that Greg had ever even gotten drunk. To be quite honest I found it rather creepy. Strangely, I found working out my frustrations regarding the whole thing by training in Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu, which was without a doubt one of the hardest martial arts for me to grasp. Hold on, I better back up and explain just how I got into it. *A few weeks ago* Well ever since I learned Greg had taken Brian my life had more or less finished falling apart. I think it would be accurate to say that I had fallen into a deep depression as I spent my days wondering how Brian’s life would turn out in Greg’s hands. I took to wondering the streets the entire day without a purpose or direction. It was on the third day that I finally passed a Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu academy, and walked in solely to get out of the rain that had unexpectedly started. I found myself watching the class comparing it to wrestling. I had never heard of BJJ before, and at the time the practitioners were practicing their takedowns. As a former amateur wrestler and a current professional wrestler I found their takedowns simplistic at best. Their head instructor, a man who went by the title professor Almeida, saw my contemptuous grin. “May I help you sir?” he asked me coolly, as I continued to scan his students. “Nah, I just came to take a look,” I responded arrogantly. “No offense, but there is no way that you guys can beat any decent wrestler. I could take anyone of you down, and from there it would be all over.” He smiled enigmatically and tipped his head. “Is that what you think?” he asked rhetorically. “Tell you what, I’ll spar with you. We’ll start standing up, and you can go ahead and do anything you can think of to either knock me our or make me submit.” I grinned thinking he was joking, but he was already walking to the mat. His students went to the side of room, all eagerly waiting to see the fight. I walked to one end of the mat, entirely sure that the match would be quick and end in my favor. But I’ll admit that I was somewhat anxious when I saw the look of the supreme confidence in the eyes of the students, who seemed sure that they were about to see their professor kick some ass. I ignored the anxious feeling that began to grow in my guy, telling myself that I still gained that feeling when I went to a wrestling match. The fight began as I thought it would. Within seconds I had taken him down, though he managed to wrap his legs around me. I shrugged it off as a desperate attempt to forestall the inevitable and began to push down one of his knees. Before I knew what was happening he switched his hips, and abruptly had me in what I later learned was called a triangle choke. It became hard to breathe, but I still barely able to. I could feel him trying to force me down, and I instinctively fought to stay on my feet, using my free arm to bat away his hand whenever it came close. We stayed that way for about three minutes, with me clearly panicking while he had a cool look of composure on his face. Then his hips abruptly shifted again, and I found my arm being forced back to an unnatural angle. Scared that he meant to break my arm I quickly tapped. Without a word, without looking at the smirking students and ignoring the professor who was coming over, I stood up and stalked out of the building. All night long I told myself that it was a fluke that I would simply pretend it never happened and avoid that building for the rest of my life. The next day I was back, this time actually observing what they taught in the lessons. By the end of the week I had signed up as a student. I am not going to say that Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu was the sole thing that saved me from my depression. But it gave me a focus, something for me to obsess over instead of my situation, and that helped me to no end. Wrestling used to do that for me as well. But after you do something for enough years, it tends to lose that obsessive value. *Present day* The man that had been choking me helped me up and patted me on my shoulder as I tenderly stretched my neck. “Watch my legs next time,” he told me with a smile on his face, “they were crossed, a perfect position for you to get an ankle lock.” “Right Professor Brian,” I said with a nod, and watched as he went to yell at another student for having horrendously bad posture. Deciding that I had taken enough abuse for the day I walked over to the bench, idly flipping open my cell phone. It had become a ritual of mine to check my cell phone on an obsessive basis, each time hoping that Max would give me a call. That ritual had been in vain thus far, so imagine my surprise when I saw that he had sent me a text message. “CALL ME” it said (apparently Max preferred to use capitals instead of exclamation points). I hurried out of the academy and quickly dialed his number. He responded immediately, and lunched into a rant. “I admit I am a ninny,” he said without prelude, “in fact I have barely saved my reputation and self-respect. You should have thought of this to, but it’s even worse considering I am the detective.” “How about you slow down and tell me what you mean,” I responded dryly. “We had been going about this thing the wrong way,” he continued impatiently. “We had been focusing on Greg, trying to find out his past and get some dirt from it. But Greg shouldn’t be our focus, Brian should be.” “Huh,” was my brilliant contribution. He sighed at my denseness. “Think about it would you” The man clearly does not giving a flying fuck about the kid. So why would he take the kid into his home. I dug around, and as it turns out Greg’s wealth is due to get an even bigger boost from inheriting his grandfather’s fortune, as long as Greg has a biological kid at the time of his death.” “So?” I replied bitterly. “It tells us why Greg took the kid, but does not help us get him back.” “On contraire,” Max replied smugly. “I dug around some more. The rumor around Greg’s staff is that your girl had someone on the side when dating him. Normally this wouldn’t mean much, but after hearing that rumor I made a few calls. Greg’s sperm count is very, very low. You wouldn’t believe how much he bribed his doctor to hide that little fact from the inheritance lawyers.” Well now, that was rather interesting. Slowly, a malicious grin began to spread across my face.
