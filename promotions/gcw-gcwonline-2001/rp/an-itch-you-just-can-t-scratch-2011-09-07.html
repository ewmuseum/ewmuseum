An Itch You Just Can't Scratch

7 September 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/07/11 If you were told that you had a week to live, would you climb that mountain you always wanted to or ask out that girl you’ve never really talked to? Would you take a chance on something crazy or even break the rules in every way possible? What if you were told you had a few months? What if you somehow beat the odds and made it over the hurdle? Would you keep living or simply sit back and lose your mind? --- He was smoking cigarettes again, a surefire way to bleed out the last days of his existence. It had become a habit, a mantra that he recited every morning in the mirror before leaving his room. There were the chemo pills and then there was a cigarette to wash it down. Even Maggie seemed tired of of waiting and watched Jay kill himself before noon. It was what he wanted. There was an air of hopelessness in the home. He was a skeleton at 150 pounds and barely had the strength to make it down to the mailbox. In eight months, Jay had seen parts of the world that GCW hadn’t taken him and he’d found ways to make people smile. Even with his good deeds and general living, he still woke up with pain pulsing in his limbs. It was annoying at best and what upset him most was the fact that he couldn’t stop it. There was no end to the pain and there was no immediate answer to his growing weakness. There was time and at the rate that it ticked, Jay Terror was ready to spin the hands forward until he fell off the clock. He thought often about GCW. He recalled the first time that he shook hands with Steven Caldera and James Bryan. He remembered the time that he cheated his way to the title and the moments before he crashed off a forklift at the hands of Jordan White. Then there were fuzzy bits about tag teams and about old faces. “What happened that time?” he’d ask Maggie. She always forced a smile. He absolutely hated the fact that he was so young. Whenever he went to the hospital, he sat in the cafeteria and reminisced with men and women that were double his age. He was a boy compared to the veterans of the disease and they all would tell him how sorry they were. Sorry was getting old. He had become a regular at a local bar and they served him whatever he wanted on most nights. Word spread quickly about who he was and what he had and Jay figured that the world finally felt bad for the villain. He drank more to not think about it. He admired Maggie’s patience though. She was loyal to him and made every effort to take him where he needed to go and bring him what he asked. At the start, she did it because she owed him. After time had passed and she’d seen him degenerate into next to nothing, she did it because she cared for him. After all the men she had chased to replace her late husband, Jay was there the whole time waiting to be discovered. She felt guilty on some days, but realized that it wouldn’t help Jay any if she expressed it. "It sucks waiting," he'd say. "Don't think of it that way," Maggie would reply. He never really got passed that. What worried Maggie most was the time Jay spent thinking about his match with Rich Rollins. In his delusion, Terror made his best effort to end everything in a buried alive match, and she couldn't help but wonder what his intentions were. "You'll kill yourself out there. Everything involved in that match. Everything involved in that man even. You'll kill yourself before falling into the hole," she warned. "It'd be a hell of a way out then." She crossed her fingers that he'd be better by then. It'd be another month or more of rest and treatment and that couldn't do any harm to his chances. She cringed when James Bryan called though. Jay accepted Markus Stone before knowing when it would happen and that alone could cripple him and his reputation. "But you're getting better," she'd tell him. "Then why not test it?" he'd ask. On the morning of the fight, Jay smiled for the first time in months. She watched him admire his yellow teeth through a plume of smoke and she realized that if he was ever going to find peace with himself and the unsettling elephant in the room, he needed to fight. Whether it killed him or not, it would be the one thing that made him happy again.
