Jordan White vs. Biff Boulder

21 March 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/21/09 "So. Whatre you gonna do with your life, Francis Nelson Black?" "Beat people up. Yell at some dumbasses." He adds, "on cable TV." "Rock and roll, motherfucker." "I could drink to that, yeah." "Drinkings one of those things you never need a reason for. You should know that." "Fuck, man. I thought they called that shit alcoholism." Frank kicks back in his chair, rolling clear liqor in a snifter. He eyes it, he's almost suspicious; some highbrow infection. Sage is staring at the empty seat beside of his employee. They are opposite one another at a slate table and neither has mentioned work; Frank hasn't seriously thought of it in days, though he veers dangerously close at this moment. "You keep cooks on staff?" "Well, yeah. Theres a cafeteria in the office--I mean. I respect these people that work for me, but I'll be fucked if Im cooking for them." "Shit. Not bad." "They didnt make this." "You coulda just said that." "Hey, I didnt expect you to compliment them." He stabs a chunk of meat, twists it. "Tell Jordan, when she gets back. Shes the one that went gourmet on us." "How many people here are named Jordan, one way or another?" "Several. If by here, you mean under my employ. If by here, you mean here? Just us three--unless Ive been seriously mispronouncing Leslies name." "Hah. Nah. I got you." "Reminds me. Or, it doesnt but. I remembered--sorry about the delay. Im sure it, uh, interfered with your scheduling." "No big. Weeks just a week, yknow?" "And thats why I didnt have to think about it. I wanted Sobias around and, well. Preocupado." "Anything big?" "Your little, custody battle." "Oh. Fuck. Whats up?" "You dont want to know." "That bad?" "Its not that youve got horrible odds or anything, anything like that. Its. Sobias, as a person. You know him. Hes dirty like, like whores are dirty." "What he do, Watergate Adams crib?" "That implies some level of basic human courtesy. Recording instead of, barging in with a baseball bat wrapped in barbed wire, a gut full of Wild Turkey--thats more his style." "Wait. Did he" "No, he didnt do that. You really want to know?" "Yeah. I do." "Dont tell Leslie." "Why not?" "Because it maybe puts you across some line, something about, decency, respect. That you would use your status to such ends is, frankly, appalling. You sick fucker." "Is this somethin I, uh, implied I wanted done?" "Yes." "Huh." "Good idea to watch your mouth around people with power, kid. We are psychotic and have little else to do but meddle." "What was it, then?" Sage's head snaps up from his diligently observed plate; his eyebrows rise and something warm, something of a smile, breaks his face. "I was starting to worry about you two." Frank turns against his chair, turns to Leslie and Jordan carefully concealing any hint of conversation. "Frank was just talking about how great the food was." "Yeah." He nods to Jordan as she's taking her seat beside Sage. "Its, uh. Its good, yeah." She smiles: "Thanks. My mom taught me." Leslie's taking her seat, adjusting her dress as Sage rejoins. "You ready for Chip Tonofsteel tomorrow?" "You really dont know this dudes name, do you?" "Its more fun to pretend. Valid qestion, though." "Yeah. Im ready." "You sure? You havent really been talking about it." "Wrestlin aint somethin to talk about. Seemed like you thought about the same." "Its filler. I dont have anything that important to say--do you?" Frank shrugs. "See. Nothing wrong with a little bit of filler when theres nothing else going on." "Shit. Thats about how I got famous, ain it?" ****** Further into the night, Leslie has gone to sleep and Jordan has disappeared. The two men have continued to drink and, as men of drink are inclined, they speak at length on relationships. Between qestions like accusations, Frank shifts the conversation enough to learn one thing, this single thing alone, about Sage's marriage. "Danielle know bout your whole. All your bullshit out here?" "She knows." "She care?" "I dont know that she ever cared. About anything." "Really?" "No." He takes a slow drink. "Dont talk about Dani. Maybe, sometime. Just not now." It's no rubric to judge the man against. ****** He spits at the canvas, he slaps his palm to his forehead and wipes sweat away in sheets. All he knows, for an instant, is that something is above him and that he should move; he does, rolls away from some threehundred and fifty pounds crashing frothing and wild in the space he once occupied. Frank finds himself hissing and is disturbed by the realization until he has to move, rapidly, from the man that hurtles again towards him. One arm outstretched, massive. Spotlighted in his mind as he jumps towards the turnbuckle. He's breathing and there's not much else. The man wraps a fist around the top rope and shakes, jerks, tears at it before continuing his lunges; Frank continues his spree of avoiding without ever planting himself firmly in the realm of safe. He feels the breeze of almost and he feels hot like abandoned corpses, bloated, swollen. He shakes his head and he clears his mind from simile. A punch. Too high. He twitches. Shifts in, around. He's swinging a fist, velocity, impact. Knuckles to skull. The man, the thing, he turns into it, he's caught off balance, and he only staggers one haphazard step before he has regained himself and again, he's. He's a snarl, rocketing with his arms spread wide and Frank is thinking of bears, big fuckoff brown bears and bigger fuck off polar bears and just teeth and claws; ugly. Ugly. He drops to his ass, throws his feet in front of him and sends this thing, this barely human lump tumbling over the ropes, crashing to concrete. Frank watches, detached, glazed. He slams a forearm down, he whips his head up. The people are screaming. His jaw goes slack, he's tearing through oxygen and in absolute awe of this thing he is consumed in; this vast, incomprehensible latticing of blood and broken bones and torrents of people on a clamor for more more more. He's retreating now, he's distracting himself, trying to slick his hair back and he can't shake his eyes from the thing tremendous, standing now, bracing itself against the barrier. Tearing at the steel. Climbing back up, stepping through the ropes without a limp, a sway. A purposefully built machine. Frank can't catch up with himself. He rolls his shoulders, cracks his back. He stares at the monster and the monster stares at him and, in an instant, Frank is too fast for himself. Diving headlong into bad ideas on scales he rarely ever considered, never measured against. They collide and Frank escapes behind the man, sqares a forearm into his spine. Aims, drives his feet to the same spot. The man reaches for his back, turns with his teeth out and his fire running. There are words that drown away, motion that blurs. A stomp, an elbow, a man sent running and returning with, or returning to, a boot hefted high. Someone screaming; the attending screaming right back, thousands of voices cast into one alien roar. Frank spits out blood and cracks his shoulders, a cat inflating in the face of some coiling, subhuman beast which existed outside his feline grasp of philosophy. He strikes and is tossed to the side, he dives and is hefted high, returned by gravity. To his feet. The man is laughing at him and he gives in, he leans against the ropes and laughs along. They take their time, signaling to one another after the respite and they are right back into it. They lock up in the center and Frank slips around, low, locks his arms around the man's waist and is walked backwards into a corner, left hanging on the ropes. He wonders where it all went wrong and, subseqently, receives an elbow sent backwards into his sternum. There is momentum behind it and he is left without air, wheezing until he hacks. He slumps forwards, his spit swirling crimson in that instant before it impacts the canvas, before the big man throws the smaller man across the ring and follows right behind him with his shoulders forward. The ring shakes and Frank's panic centerswarn that he may be left without the ability to regain said air. He knows he just needs a minute; this thing inside of him that argues is incapable of learning, and he can gain no leverage against its yelling. He closes his eyes and when he opens them, he makes a guess at elevation (nine feet, ten feet; not relative to sea level) as he's paraded from corner to corner. He blinks and he blinks too long, losing seconds and only approaching at awareness as he's falling, as the man crashes in on top of him; he begins to see instinct's side of things, begins to wonder when he does get another chance towards composure, towards anything like it. His legs kick out while his mind drifts, his shoulders snap reflex to shove the man away. He's standing with no input from his brain, he's being dragged to his feet and lifted vertical, his head shoved in the other man's armpit. Frank is breathing because he has the chance, because he doesn't have the presence of mind to consider other options. The viability of playing dead. ****** Life shifting slow. He takes a break as blood collects in the crown of his skull, he reflects on the decisions he's made, on reasons if reasons there were. The world rising up to meet with him, with the top of his bloated head. It has a name and he thinks about it in that nebulous time before impact. An orange bomb, maybe. Modified. Just technical things. ****** How old? Doesn't know. Years ago, either way. Everything as less weird, less distorted. When lashing out was lashing out, not just the routine. Violence as meaningful, rare. Feels like forever, like no span of his brain can rationalize how far he's come; like no neuron wants to acknowledge why it was or why it was so easy to him. "Leslie?" He can barely crack the name. Barely form the shape. She rolls over to face him, splays her hair crimson halo across the pillows. Murmurs, waking. Resting her hand on his chest. Digging her head into his shoulder. He remembers why her mother hates him at worst, tolerates him at best, even if Leslie doesn't, or even if Leslie just feigns ignorance. He can't read her like he wants to; hanging around, ever, now and then, like he may one day catch a glimmer, just the faintest slip, that will reveal herself to him in a flash of perfect understanding. He doesn't actively wait for it. She rolls over him, throws her thigh across his stomach and settles into him. Runs her fingertips through his knotted hair, cups the back of his neck and she leans down, buries her face to his with a light kiss of traded morning breath. "Yeah?" "Just makin sure." Her grin forms to twist wry; a fake laugh. The door swings open, her roommate entering with a sigh. "You two are disgusting. You know its noon, right?" Leslie pushes her chin to her shoulder, "Layton canceled classes." "Well, Frank. You have an excuse?" "Im a fuck up." He laughs as the girl trades one set of books for another. "Just put some clothes on before I get back. Alright?" "Hey, we're under the sheets." "And I dont know that youre naked under them, but I dont have any, yknow, proof." Leslie jerks the sheets down, moons the girl. Wiggles with it. "Just get dressed, okay?" She's out the room, no waiting for replies. "And you like this girl?" Leslie shrugs, pressing her ear against Frank's chest. "I cant hate her, she puts up with that shit. Heh. Shes pretty cool. Ooh, and she thinks youre cute. If she wasnt in a relationship, youd probably have a pretty good shot. Maybe do anyways. He doesnt exactly live in town." "Yeah, thats exactly what I wanna do. Trade you in for some random chick." "I know--all you men are swine." ****** He opens his eyes, blinded by the rush of arena lights. Another collision and he snaps them shut. ****** His mind wanders in the blackness, flashes Sobias: eyes wide, foaming at the mouth, one huge, fuck-off Mexican kicking through an apartment door with a machete held high over his head, screaming obscenities faster than Adam can keep up. Adam. Poor fucked Adam, and Frank already knows how that's going to turn out. Cowering in the corner, Sobias on the march. The man screams and puts his forearms in front of his face, only just understanding the name, "Lisa," whispered a roar before the blade comes down, cleaves through arms, chest. Viscera spraying at high velocities. He doubts the reality of it even as he wonders if it's not slightly more humane than the plan as it stands. Robbery, destruction; a surgical removal of the man's soul before locking him into a life like a pillory. Maybe it's just a gun. Maybe he just waves it until Adam relents. Small joys. ****** His nose is leaking. He tastes it before he feels it, hot, copper, salt. He understands that breathing hurts even if he doesn't feel the pain. He's standing, running palms back and streaking red up his forehead, pinning his damp hair back to his skull as the man faces him. Frank limps for a step, catches himself on pure luck. The man says something, lost to the voices of the crowd, and Frank responds with violence, simple and direct, with his forearm to the man's throat. The beast loses his footing and Frank returns another blow that topples both men to the mat. He swirls the blood in his mouth, locks his thumbs around one another, his hands around the man's throat. The man is struggling, arms like clubs flailing, crashing monumental against his sides. He releases the man's throat but he does not relent; he slides his hand beneath the man's head, lifts it from the mat as he drives his forehead down into the bridge of the man's twisted nose. He's reminded of something. He has a sudden awareness of his lungs. Breathing fire. Another headbutt. He snaps his head back, crosses his arms over his ribcage as he falls into the ropes. Lifts himself. Dreams of air. The man is staring blankly up, torso heaving shallow. Frank perches in the corner, his false name chanted meaningless. It fails to reach his ear drums now as it has failed to reach them before; just syllables, some creature wholly divided from himself. Jordan White. He could laugh. Crimson flows down the man's face, another attempt at eqilibrium that instead propagates, prolongs. The man is shaking his shoulders, sitting up from the canvas. Professional; he doesn't touch his face, doesn't betray a soul. Frank waits. He would admire if he weren't so busy. come on The man smiles, his cracked lips contorting his cracked face. motherfucker, motherfucker, come on Clumsy in his boots, the man is a surge, roaring a blow to where Frank was. Frank is, however. Frank is kicking the man in the back of the head, leaving the ground, his heel finding the precise joint between skull and spine by some cruel twist on luck. The man collapses forwards, supports his tonnage against the ropes. Frank is moving, still; Frank has not stopped, has rebounded and rebounded again. His world blurs and he closes his eyes. Soars. Sole to the man's nose. He falls outside of the ring, topples onto the mats. Frank watches and Frank collects himself as he paces corner to corner, saunters parallel to the apron. Sneers. There's no motion and he steps outside of the ring, hops to the ground. There is a song playing in his head, drowning out the audience because what do they have to say that he hasn't heard a thousand times? The front row, some portion of it, pats him on the back as he's lifting the man from the ground. He's conscious, at least; he doesn't sandbag on the way up, on the way under the ropes and back into the ring. Frank follows, crawls in after him and collapses diagonal across his chest. It's one, it's two, it's not enough and he just hates that it has to keep going. ****** His head's always been somewhere else. He remembers incidents and brings himself to pauses as he sidetracks into what memories he was humoring at the time. Little pictures, paragraphs scrawled across the back in ink that only direct him to more pictures; similar tints, or the same subject matter, or an angle repeated by chance. He thinks in fetishes, in pithy sayings that keep himself distracted. He doesn't believe he has any great understanding, but he's taken to dreaming about water's inevitable victory over land and how the sun will nullify that; entropy turning off the lights, closing up shop, even further along. ****** There's a folded chair, seat dented nearly through. A spray of blood drying into the mat. The referee doesn't seem to be moving--Frank makes assumptions. The man is mostly obscured, crouched outside of the ring. His head comes into view, a cheap plywood table follows. Something about mountains that are, are not, are. He scrambles up. He lets his bones collapse against one another, his joints compress for support. Muscle fatigue and blood loss and oxygen deprivation, and he tries to not consider a word of it. Act. Act. He kicks the table, drives the far edge into the man's face before he can make it back inside the ring. Springs up, beckons with both hands pulling in. The man returns, his eye sockets gone dark, dark with dried blood. He is on his feet, he is staring down Frank and Frank--stupid or brilliant or brave or just hard working--does not back down. He drills his fist into the man's cheek, catches him off guard with the first and reeling with those that follow. The man stumbles backwards, stumbles over himself, into the corner. Frank puts distance between them, a few small steps that he regains in a bound dodged and only now does Frank realize that there is no padding on the turnbuckle. He wishes he'd remembered that much and then he collides. ****** He was watching television before he left for Naples, late night repeats of Worldwide. Leslie was curled against his chest and breathing in the faintest snores. Just ripples. He was laughing and he kept it low. It wasn't how seriously his peers took their jobs; it was that they could take them seriously to any measure. He'd made a list of people he did not respect and a corollary of people he could never respect and, on completion, had given up on everyone except himself and LeStatt--the latter out of nothing save a tremendous awe at his prehuman dedication towards the sport. He thought qotes around the word sport. He smoked a bowl and he went to sleep. ****** There is something tangled around his boot and when he tries to pull his leg in, the something snags against the canvas. The referee is slapping the third and calling for the bell and Frank is trying, and failing, to gain any true insight into the streaked and swollen face of the man beneath him. He stands with nothing, raises his fist into the sky and kicks his foot, loosens a strand of barbed wire from around his ankle and off. He's cringing as he climbs out of the ring, cringing deeper as he forces the events together in his head.. The man meets with him during a widespread bout of postshow mingling. His broad face is bandaged, stitched, and he smacks Frank on the back to announce himself, nearly knocks a cup from Frank's hand. "You was pretty good out there." "Hah. Thanks, man." "Next time, see about an exploding bear match." Frank just imagines. "Might have to, yeah." The man laughs and walks off. Leslie turns her face to Franks and all he has is to bare himself, to deny with fervor. ****** Frank has his belongings packed away, stuffed into the rear of a gargantuan SUV aimed directly at the airport when Sage calls him into his office. When he enters the room, he does not hesitate in raising the only creeping issue in his skull over which he believes Sage has any true power. "Sobias, Lisa. Whats good?" "Glad you could make it." Sage is smoking something and he taps it against the air. "He, uh. Let me, let me put this delicately. He tried to buy Lisa." "What?" "Exactly what I said. He had a briefcase with money in it--lots of money and, my money, you should remember. He made an offer, about, about settling out of court. Just, signing her over to Leslie and you, once and for all." "And?" "Adam turned it down. He, the man, he pretty clearly wants to win this thing. That motherfucker might actually care about his daughter." "Always gotta be some shit like that, dont it?" "If it werent, we might be good people. As is, yeah. The world has so many people in it with so many hopes and dreams, honest and pure. And sometimes, they happen to overlap. Just tell yourself hes being selfish. Just, remind yourself that what you want is no worse than what he wants." "It is, though." "I know. All I can say is, with what youre doing here, I hope youre in for the long haul. Be a motherfucker to sell your humanity away for a qick fuck with an old crush. Destroying a loving family so you can get off? Heartless." "An it aint like that." "Maybe not. Still. Its ugly. It looks bad to the uninformed. You understand." "Yep." "Course, if she was that willing to just hop on the old gravy train, how loving could itve been? Hell. How long do you think you can hold onto her, Frank?" He shifts his shoulders and stares at his feet. "I gotta get to the car. Dont wanna keep her waitin." "Youre a good man, Charlie Brown." Frank Black lifts his middle finger high. He's smiling when he walks out of the room, into the hallway before Sage's laugh catches up. "Before you leave, Frank, one thing. Ive been thinking about it." He stops. "Sometimes, sometimes optimism, love, honesty--just, held high, a massive 'fuck you' to the bitterness, all the hatred in the world--sometimes its the best weapon you can have. Especially in a situation like yours." "Whats that about?" "Youll figure it out. Might not thank me but, if you dont already know. Youll figure it out." Frank thinks about Adam and he cringes. He doesn't think about work. There is nothing capable of swaying him towards that. ****** Addendum. She watches him approach the vehicle from the back seat, her mouth aimed to the side, her eyebrows twisting down. He pulls the door open and climbs in, stretches his knees against the seat in front of him as he takes a deep breath. He turns to her, his grin forming slow. She puts on a mockery of a smile that manages to ride miles above condescension. She's taking stock. "Your boss is a little weird." "Aint he, though?" And her smile twists genuine as she settles in. "We gotta talk." "About?" "Your girl. Adam. Us, probably." Her interest, at the boiling white hot verge of incinerating her being, is piqed.
