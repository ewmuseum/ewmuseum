Gimme burger Paddy now

6 November 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/6/07 Tyson DeBough v Paddy O’Shea There was a certain, frustratingly intangible echo apparent in the events of Warpath for one Irishman in particular. For a start, there was the forgone conclusion of defeat in his debut that Patrick O’Shea has so unwittingly become accustomed to. Patrick is what they would call, a slow starter. There was also the constant undeserved yet universal scream of adulation for his every action in the ring - he is after all, recognised throughout the wrestling circuit as a happy-go-lucky joker with the crafty streak that creates the coffee stain in his whiter than whites. Repetition’repetition’repetition. The same old shtick. Yet?I wish I cold be the bad guy for once The formula for competition was simply a Chef’s recipe applied with the precision of an engineer. Tape up, stretch out, go out, kick ass (a two large spoonfuls of playing to the crowd). But never forget, in all situations, give everything you’ve got. Because the fans would do it for you. Somewhere in the battle royal that Paddy simply accepted he would lose anyway (as was his tradition), he forgot the formula’somewhere in there somewhere, he decided he’d try something else for a change. One year ago, when his knees were eroding his bones to flour, when his neck felt like one more twist and he’d scuttle off like a wind up soldier, he’d still climb that damn turnbuckle and dive off onto his cranium just to hear that cheer be choked back into a gasp in the mouths of every person present, whether critic or fan. Back then he was no spring chicken but back then there was a vital organ that was grouped in the same sentence as Paddy O’Shea. Now, he’s 37 and still no spring chicken. The desire is still there - he wants to win. Unfortunately, that change at Warpath was when he stopped fighting for the fans and starting fighting solely for himself. It’s been 6 years since Paddy O’Shea last held a singles title. Sure, he’s had his big matches, he was an AWC main eventer, was number one contender to the top gun more times than anyone else. Yet, every time, the title seemed elusive. Too good for midcard, simply inadequate to be a fed’s torchbearer. The thing was, when you were the fans favourite, you simply didn’t care. That is until your joints rust up to the point of incapability. Somewhere in Warpath, Paddy realised’it’s titles that carry your name through time, not what you nearly achieved. I wish I could be the bad guy for once The Model Professional Brian Jenkins had never watched a wrestling show through in his life so the significance of the days events had a significantly smaller effect on him than it may have had on others. All the same though, being paralysed is pretty darn disconcerting. He was hungry. Damn hungry actually so whilst waiting on his ordered meal of three large hamburgers with everything on it, in the family pub of “The Three Flowers” in the desolate outskirts of Ontario, he drank himself a little beer to keep himself occupied. This was usual, Brian liked to drink and his 6’5 muscular frame bade him to drink a lot. He liked beer but whiskey chasers were the shit that kept him up on rooftops, his trousers nowhere to be seen. Sometimes his heavy drinking got him in trouble (for instance, his second wife left him when he beat her to a bloody pulp after a particularly lengthy poker night. He was promptly jailed for six months but then again, the bitch should have made him a God damn sandwich when he asked her so politely) Brian could, in other words handle himself particularly well. Now as he sat here drinking a little beer, his cyanosed eyes heaving from their sockets, his hands clenching and unclenching the splintered table with pulsating regularity, it became painstakingly obvious to him wild horses wouldn’t keep him from his meal’hunger made him restless but not angry. Deprivation on the other hand? A shadow descended on him. The shadow bore food and he managed a smile a maniac makes before devouring your brain with an ice-cream scoop (quite common, let me assure you.) There was a plink-plonk of a piano, being aired through a speaker somewhere in the corner of the room. This was ignored. There was the exaggerated laughter of a man too eager to appear amused in the company of his peers somewhere off to his left. This was grating yet also ignorable. Then there was a second shadow that stank of cheap liquor creeping up behind him. In Brian’s wolfish massacre of his meat, this was simply unnoticeable. That is until the hand fell on his shoulder. Brian suddenly became aware the room was silent, tension in the air so thick it quelled hunger in a way no food could. He turned around and stared into the face of what he believed was a fifty year old hobo. It was a terrible face, seemingly constructed by an alcohol inspired Picasso. The deeply entrenched brow contradicted a crooked yet youthful smile, the unkempt beard a hive for at least two small meals. Then there was the odour - a rank miasma filled his flaring nose, and he inhaled the foul beer tinged aroma to the point he felt light headed. Then there was his eyes. Staring, glaring and worst of all, damned eyes of the stranger bore their gaze deep into his thoughts until they looked upon him from the inside, a pathway to the sterile hell of dreams unleashed and uncontrollable in consciousness or not. There was a groan as the grip tightened. Brian found himself surprised it came form his mouth. This 5’8 wreck of a man had shook him badly with a mere look and it sickened him. Cracked lips writhed and parted revealing badly stained teeth, outlined in diluted blood that was exuding from his deeply decomposed gums. Dead man The same two words bounced from each chamber of his brain. Dead man. Dead man. Dead man. The stare continued. The large man and the small man, one was skating around the rim of madness. Then he spoke. It was a drunken slur that can only be roughly translated into sense: “Can ye spare me a bite there stranger?” A sheepish grin thus followed. “Go fuck yourself.” The reaction was a snap one and full of anxiety and thus perhaps not as intentional as it sounded. A neutral would have compared body weights and scoffed at the drunk’s barefaced cheek. A glance at those stagnant eyes would have reversed it. The grip on the shoulder tightened, the eyes throbbed from both men - one in pain, one in impatient desire. “Give me.” Brian Jenkins wasn’t a man that feared a fight - as a matter of fact he relished the prospect of tearing through a crowd of men, lifting and shunting his ham shaped fists into anything that even appeared facial. Despite this, he couldn’t help but shutter. He had his pride and that was on the line, yes but something told him it wasn’t just that which faced a battering depending on his answer. “No.” In hindsight he would have agreed he had the wrong reply. The following event began with the smile expanding and it concluded with Brian Jenkins being confined to a wheelchair for the rest of his life. Sometimes, off show, he just didn’t want to be the good guy anymore. As Paddy O’Shea sobered up later, he realised what he had done and fled Canada for fear of his good professional name being sullied. He even got away with it and the sweet taste of beer took away his guilt soon enough. Sometime during in his rehabilitation which has thus far stretched a little more than a year, he would explain his actions on that day to his alcoholic addiction, never considering it part of his personality. Never considering, that what Paddy wants Paddy gets. It was after all why such ruthlessness never became present in the wrestling ring. Then again, he never wanted to win so badly as he does against Tyson DeBough. He had a lot of prove to himself. Fuck the fans. ~~ Aye can’t be tha? man anymore ~~ Paddy wants to win? And don’t you dare tell him no. END
