The Enemy of My Enemy is My Friend

21 May 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 05/21/10 "Aaaaaargh!" ~CRASH~ The exclamation, the thrown coffee cup… it was enough to send even the heartiest ferret scurrying for the safety of the nearest laundry basket. Emil and Fernando peered through the bright blue plastic mesh, nestled beneath a small mound of silk and sparkling spandex and watched nervously as their strange, huge mother paced in great agitation. Her long black and blue head fur swung wildly in agitation causing them to fear that some predator must be nearby. But she was a fierce protector and surely they would be fine. Such a display would scare off any danger. They were quiet and still and waited. Soon the males came into view. The smaller one comforted their mother and the larger one seemed tense, perhaps having scared off the danger. The ferrets decided to stay hunkered down a bit longer. The danger was clearly past, but the laundry basket was safe and warm. And now sleep stole over them silently in their hiding place. They curled around each other listening to the voices in the other room as the strange, huge, mostly furless ferrets made noises and communicated with each other. "Is it really necessary to make so much noise?" Frank said, scratching his head blearily. "I was having the best nap. And you should be too." He nodded in Tempest’s direction, to where she stood with her arms folded, leaning awkwardly against the counter with her arms folded. Her eyes were shut and her eyelids fluttered, which seemed to make Troy nervous as he stood rubbing her shoulders gently. "I’ve been down and out long enough," Tempest said, with a relative calm considering the earlier outburst. "Meanwhile my enemies are laughing at me, plotting their next move and… and…" "It’s the drugs," Frank said, waving his hand in front of her face lightly. She turned to look at him, at once bleary and yet nervous. "Paranoid, but unfocused. I read someplace you hated drugs. I guess I can see why now. You ok?" He leaned over and peered into her unfocused eyes. Tempest nodded and leaned on Troy, shifting as if her back could not quite find a comfortable angle. "Yeah. I… they, they’re awful. I know right where I am, but I don’t. And I keep seeing what happened at Shockwave in front of my face. Sons of bitches… I’m not taking any more. Can you make me some more coffee?" "In a paper cup? Yes," said Troy with a little smirk that managed to coax a hint of a smile from her. She stepped away carefully and took the tree limb-like arm of Frank as he helped her over to an oversized armchair. She curled up carefully and flexed her back slowly. "I don’t need drugs anymore anyway," Tempest said softly. "Everything works. People have been trying to put me out of commission for years, but never seem to get the job done." "Fine attempt they made," said Troy, returning with a fresh cup of coffee that he handed to her before sitting opposite her on a hard wooden chair. "I was proud of you." He smiled. It was genuine, if frightened. "You held on through… so much. I may not understand it, but I couldn’t help but admire it, that kind of tenacity. Just…" He stopped and shook his head, at which both Tempest and Frank looked at him. The assault had been a shock to him. But they both knew it was part of the scenery, the very lay of the violent land that was wrestling. "I know it’s hard to watch, sweetie," Tempest said. "But you’ve gotta trust that I can handle this. Now that Christian is out in the open, he’s lost his advantage forever." She took a deep swig of her coffee and shook her head, as if to clear the cobwebs. "And it’s not like you’re the only one who hates his guts. Equinox is back and he’ll be looking to take a chunk out of SurReal in return for that whole eye thing back at NC-17," said Frank, swirling his finger in a circle around his own eye to indicate the injury suffered by Lori Equinox. "Yeah, I just have to make sure I don’t hurt him too much. Not that I’m planning on it." Tempest’s tone was matter of fact as she sipped her coffee noisily, staring over the cup and lost in thought for a moment. "Not the first time I’ve been surrounded by enemies and made strange friends." "And is it all right?" Troy said, concern clouding his expression. "To be there again?" Tempest smiled with some effort through the nerve deadening haze of drugs still swimming slowly through her system, only beginning to retreat from the high tide of caffeine. "I’ve faced worse. These guys are still at a disadvantage because they think they’re the worst I’ve seen. No… they’re not even close." ~ The smell of his cologne was overpowering, but still not enough to cover the stench of his breath as he hissed in her ear. "Move and you die. I don’t care who you pay. This little hole in the wall is nothing, and I can think of better uses for you." She struggled still, almost on instinct, panic prickling beneath her skin. The blade so near her throat was not lost on her, nor the surroundings - nestled behind the laundry, between two buildings and a long alley filled with trash and a small box truck that hadn’t moved in years. But still, she thought, someone must hear, someone must see, someone must be able to stop this. Breath, already coming too quickly, rose in her throat, assumed sound, and escaped in the form of a scream. The man looked around, now almost as frantic as his would be victim. But the alley was still and only invaded by the noise of the traffic on the streets beyond the buildings that surrounded them. Hong Kong moved onward taking no notice of this latest atrocity within its bosom. He pressed the straight razor against her throat and said, "Stop it! I swear I’ll slit you like a fish if you don’t shut u…" At which point her salvation came in the form of a chicken. Flapping wildly, feathers flying, it fell onto his head and she broke free as he struggled with the unruly bird that had come from… where? By the time he looked up it was too late. A tiny sneaker was flying for his face. It was worn by a girl, oddly dressed, with a tangle of jet black hair streaked with blue. That was all he could make out in the blur that kicked him in the face and then, landing lightly, tripped him as he staggered from the impact. He fell; sprawling backwards and her feet tapped a dance on his wrist until his hand released the deadly blade and she scooped it up before he could roll over and grasp it with the other hand. He scrambled to his feet just as his would be victim retreated behind her rescuer. The two women stood a few feet away, the frightened laundress was Chinese like him, small of stature and her delicate features and tearful brown eyes reflected the horror that had nearly befallen her. This new arrival, now wielding his blade with a certain poise that made him nervous was different. She was a bit paler, part white he thought, and those big blue eyes staring at him like some sort of demon from the old people’s tales. "What the hell do you think you’re doing?" he snapped. "I could break you in two, you insolent little…" "You’re funny for a man who isn’t holding his knife anymore." Her smile mocked him and he clearly wasn’t used to it. "Who are you?" She glanced over her shoulder at the woman cowering behind her. "…to be so presumptuous?" The man spat and lurched forward in what might have been a charge, full of rage and pride. But he stopped short of the blade that the woman held out before her, with her head upraised, stopping him, but almost daring him to keep coming. He stepped back, nervous energy seeming to propel him in this stickiest of situations and he paced a short space and yanked up his sleeve, revealing a Chinese character tattooed on his forearm above the wrist. "I am this. That is all you need to know." He glared at both women. "Maybe if you pay me off I’ll go find someone else prettier than you." He sneered, full of the false sense of security that a Triad was wont to imbue, upon flashing its symbol at the women. "You’re a long way from home," said the blue haired woman simply as she narrowed her eyes at him. "Are you a pimp?" She leaned forward and sniffed at him, wrinkling her nose. "You smell like one. Or maybe you’re one of those little rich boys who like to come down here and hang onto the gangs to get laid, get high, and kid yourself that you’re something badder than you were born to be." She smirked as he fumed and raised the edge of her shirt to reveal a single character tattooed onto her right hip, near the point of the hip bone. "Not so in my case." It was not the same character. And seeing it, his expression changed, but he tried to recover himself. "Liu. Hmph, Liu is too soft on his women." "And hard on trespassers. Papa Liu is kind to all those who are loyal to him. He takes care of those who pay him protection. And you are not in your territory. You come into his part of town and attack one of his? You think it will matter that it was a girl? That a girl caught you?" His laugh was harsh and split the calm air. "Girls are of no consequence to matters of importance. You’re just a whore of his and she’s not even that. Anyone can do the damn dry cleaning. In my part of town no one would blink an eye if either of you turned up dead in the gutter!" He glared at them both as if by his mere gaze he could put them both there. "And that is why your part of town is a filthy slum without businesses or happiness or life," snapped the woman with a flick of the blade in his direction. "But your master knows better than to trespass and so should you. Keep your animal ways in your part of town and don’t stray here again or you’ll find yourself dealing with more than Papa’s girls. And for the record..." She smirked in his direction. "I’m a pit fighter. Ask around and you’ll find that Papa Liu’s women in the pits are not all whores, but are all deadly. Now go… while you still can." The man stared from one to the other of the women, his intended victim still shrinking behind her new defender in spite of the confident defense. He slunk away down the alley, never turning his eyes from the woman bearing the blade and she never lowered it until he was out of sight, having moved onto the front street. When at last she was sure, she flipped the blade shut and popped it into the pocket of a ragtag jacket with multicolored patches upon it and turned to offer the laundress a smile. "There. You ok? I’m sorry I didn’t see that happening a little sooner." The young woman looked at her and mopped her eyes, nodding hurriedly. After a moment she managed, "Yes. Yes I’m fine. Thank you. You… well, I didn’t expect that." "Well of course not. Who does? Going about your business and someone just jumps you like that. What’s your name?" Nervously the smaller woman replied, "Zi. I… I meant I didn’t expect anyone to help. My mother pays your Papa Liu, but…" she shook her head, not wanting to look at her rescuer, but seemingly compelled to. "Well, I always thought it was criminal, that we were really just paying not to be harassed by his people. But now… maybe not. I’m sorry. What’s your name?" Her rescuer smiled, apparently not offended and said, "Tempest. It’s all right. I can see how you’d think that. But Papa is far more generous than you might think. And while he may be cruel to some… I can tell you it’s those who deserve it." Zi nodded, eyeing Tempest up and down and said, "I know that… what we pay to a man like Liu doesn’t go to his people, and certainly not his…pit fighters like you. Is there anything I can do for you?" Tempest stood a moment and looked at her curiously. "I um, live in a couple of rooms with four other girls. One of them is pregnant. Do you have any blankets that no one’s picked up for a long time maybe?" Their eyes met and a moment of understanding passed between them. They were women of a culture that barely understood them in a city that barely knew they were alive. And they both stood on the precipice of that city’s dangerous, dark chasm into which lives fell daily. Zi nodded and darted back inside the cleaner’s for a moment before emerging with a bagged blanket, light blue and covered in small ships. "Here. If you come back in a couple of days I will try and gather up a few things for you and your friends, including the pregnant girl. And I will sneak your wash into some of the other loads." She smiled and Tempest smiled back at her gratefully. Such small kindnesses were worth more than any amount of graft in her small world at that time. ~ The world changes and yet it remains much the same. Unexpected enemies and friendships alike cause me to keep a wary eye on those around me. And while I know in the end I must stand alone, I am lucky to have the support I have and to grant what little favor I can to those who might share a common cause with me. Lori Equinox may be my foe, but he is not my enemy. And the enemy of my enemy is friend. Fear not for the one good eye, because it won’t be me that puts it out. I would have you train it on SurReal, and know that you won’t be alone in that. And after we test one another out for this contest, the right to move on and make a name for ourselves, there will still be the ugly business at hand of dealing with the man who has chosen to do us both harm. One thing I can assure, whichever one of us wins, SurReal will be lucky to make it out of Rampage alive.
