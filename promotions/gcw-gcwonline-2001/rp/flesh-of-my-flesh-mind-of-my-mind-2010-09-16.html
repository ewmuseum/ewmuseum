Flesh of my Flesh, Mind of my Mind

16 September 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/16/10 One It began with a code of laws; your pulp manifesto. No face. No voice. No silhouette. Imprisoned within bulked-out leather and peering out through ever changing masks, Rasa could be 6’4, lithe and with the face of a screaming skull one week, then 6’2, barrel-chested and hidden behind an Egyptian death mask the next. No entrance and no theme music. In the ring is where it would end, so in the ring is where you would begin. No legacy. No reputation. No hype. No longer would you have to battle overbearing expectations or coast on past accomplishments. It was meant to purify, not pervert. But Rasa was born from the void you’d sunken in to. It cared little for your human wants and needs… for your sanity. All it cared about was existing; a most basic of animal instincts. So long as you were tied to the mask and suit, it would have a mentally deteriorating vessel to fill with its dark malevolence. Your flesh was owned by Christian Zenith. Your mind was occupied by Rasa. To free yourself, both would have to be broken. Two The coffee was lukewarm and bitter. Promptly it found itself tossed into the gutter, followed by a wad of creamy beige saliva. Under a heavy blanket of swollen cloud, Belial watched you, just as he had every day since he said yes to Christian Zenith. His features were permanently squashed into a snarl, wrinkles carving disgust deep into his expression. A babysitter he was not; his natural calling was a bloodhound. He reveled in the thrill of the hunt, finding little satisfaction in what fate befell the prey at its conclusion. “Now where do you think you’re going?” he snarled to himself. He knew that you knew that he was following in the shadows. You knew that he knew that you knew that Christian Zenith saw you through him. Where you went, he would follow. From eardrum-perforating club to hepatitis-dripping bar to wherever the night pulled you. His orders were simple, their scope narrow; get you to the next WorldWide, conscious and mobile. Thirty seconds after you had disappeared into the warehouse, a limp body was tossed out onto the street, his face swollen dark as the brooding night sky above. Broken ribs acting like a torturous corset, he could barely draw enough air into his lungs to cough up the blood which welled up in his throat. “You take me to all the best places,” Belial scoffed, following you in. Three His pneumatic jab pumped your bottom lip until the swelling burst papery skin. Your tongue lapped at the ferrous crimson syrup; you didn’t find its taste unpleasant. Another jab stung you, his sweaty, dirt-encrusted lead knuckle sparking the exposed nerves in the plump, gaping flesh. Blood had begun to soak into the sawdust, where fight after fight of sloppy, plodding footwork had trodden and stirred it into a fibrous porridge. It squelched under heel and stuck between your toes. This was the one pursuit over which you and Rasa could bond; violence. You were not born into it, nor were you raised on a diet of fists or belts. It wasn’t nature or nurture. You’d grown so tired of hurting all those around you, of falling under their increasingly low expectations. Here, you didn’t care who you hurt. The impatient crowd huddled tighter, closing the holes in the organic wall of fists, beer bottles and recording cell-phones. Their bloodlust unquenched by that which was trickling down your chin, a few jeers punctured the dull blanket of encouragement and amateurish advice which they cast over the fighting pit. “Hit ‘im!” they suggested, as helpful as they were insightful. “Fuckin’ hit the fucker!” His jab tired of grinding your bottom lip and pinged your nose. A sharp shock of pain exploded out across your face and swirled down into your eye sockets. Your nostrils began to weep crimson. Through watery eyes, you flicked your glance into the crowd, where the disapproval was etched deep into Belial’s leathery features. You blew your keeper a bloody-lipped kiss, which almost imploded his face into a black hole of a scowl. Unfortunately for Guy Mrzljak and his metronomic jab, your play time was over. His toothy sneer made it all for easier for you to pin the rodent-like face of Russell Greer onto his sharp features. Guilty conscience, have we? Rasa’s whisper failed at piercing your focus. As if a soft wad of diseased flesh like you could have done anything to stop him. In twenty-three short, painful seconds, your knees chipped away at his cheekbones, flattened his nose, and caved in an eye socket. The blitzkrieg of knees left a weak, mushy defensive line of shattered bone structure through which the infantry of your fists marched on. As Guy crumbled, you scrambled through his desperately flailing limbs and imprisoned him within a mounted triangle. At the eighteen second mark, you commenced bombardment of furious elbows, picking through the crown of his skull until the rusty bell mercifully chimed. Under rich brushstrokes of crimson and the crumbling ruins of Guy’s collapsed features, the face of Russell Greer disappeared. Feel better? Rasa taunted, its voice skipping from ear to ear, pestering you like a hovering insect. Not strong enough to free yourself of the keeper Zenith stuck to you. Haven’t the spine to fight me. Too late to do anything about my dear friend Russell. Guess this was the best you could do. Panting, your body relaxed from his bloodthirsty frenzy and basked in the warm, tingly glow of victory. Every synapse sparked. Your lungs were burning dry, giving you a deep tissue massage as you inflated them. You were smiling; a true, joyous smile. “One more,” you demanded. Stamping a blood-smeared fist onto your cheek, your knuckles painted three crimson stripes across your scar. “I want one more.” Your seed of bravado took but a few seconds to germinate in the crowd. “One more fight!” they echoed, a choir of slurred voices in countless clashing accents. “One more fight!” Guy Mrzljak’s lungs coughed breathlessly as you rose, blood and mucus weighing them down. As he was dragged away by the feet, his stiff, convulsing body cut a deep, bloody slash through the pit’s claggy sawdust. Your eyes gravitated to one man; a gaze of such potency that it peeled the tightly packed crowd from around him. Your index finger, blood dripping from its tip, pointed to Belial. He bared the enamel daggers sheathed behind his thin lips. “Him,” you declared, kicking a clump of sweet jammy sawdust at Belial’s feet. “I want him.” Rasa cackled at the predictability of it all. Four Dr. Werner Sarstedt had made one mistake in his life above all others; he’d said yes. It was an answer that would echo throughout the remainder of his life. A full moon hung low in the starry night sky, peering through the open window and breeze-rippled curtains to where Werner had fallen asleep atop the sheets, fully clothed save for the dress shoes he’d kicked off before collapsing. Stale champagne on his breath, his snoring was interrupted by his body purging the gas from the last two glasses Werner knew he should have refused. His rumpled-up tuxedo jacket restrained him as he tossed and turn, tickled by the chilled breeze. Over the wind’s calming whistle blared the shill ring of the telephone. As Werner came to, he puckered his dry mouth, peeling his tongue off of his palate. His throat burned with claggy, acidic phlegm which he violently coughed and heaved to dislodge. It was an awakening at odds with the calm, glittering night outside his window. After a few seconds of staring at the digital display on his bedside clock, the blurry lights sharpened to show it was 2:31. With his thumb and index finger, Werner crudely massaged his deep-set, olive eyes before reaching for the phone. “What is it?” Werner croaked, any hint of an accent buried under his gravelly voice. Five years ago, Werner Sarstedt had said yes to Christian Zenith; under no duress other than his own reckless ambition. It was an answer that would echo throughout the remainder of his life. He would have to say it again tonight. “Yes, I can be there.” He checked his breath for alcohol; it reeked. Not that it would matter in the makeshift triage stations and operating theatres that he’d become used to making from motel bathrooms or shady club’s back rooms. “Yes, I’ll hurry.” Werner Sarstedt had naively thought that he would be just one cut away from freeing himself, a millimeter further when making an incision. A disguised flick of a scalpel. But it was never Christian Zenith he was called on to treat. As long as there were people who would say yes to him, Christian Zenith would forever avoid becoming a casualty of his own wars. Five Belial was already sweating profusely through his tight, leathery skin as he wrapped shreds of torn shirt around his hairy knuckles. Both rag-wrapped hands then ran through his black, oily hair, slicking it tight to the sides of his head. He wrenched a tight ball of phlegm up from his raspy throat and spat it down between his feet, where yellow, brittle-nailed toes trod it into the sawdust. “Well?” he bit, sharp, uneven teeth gnawing at the heavy, stale air. “If you’re going to fucking do it… then fucking do it, already.” Christian Zenith saw you through Belial’s squinty, shadowed eyes. You had no doubt that he had heard April Greer’s pleading sobs and agonized screams as her husband’s fists had spilled her life out in the blood that poured from mangled flesh and the marrow of shattered bone. You had little doubt that he would have enjoyed the show. For that, you were going to pluck those squinty, shadowed eyes out, wave to Christian Zenith through them… then stamp them into the bloody sawdust. You flew at Belial with a knee, stabbing it into his chest with such ferocity that you could feel his black heart beating against your patella. You followed it up with a short right elbow, trying to chip off the point of his devilish chin. A left elbow next, arced over and down through his leathery cheek with such force that it should have dislodged at least three of those cerated enamel daggers. Each vicious assault failed to draw so much as a wince from your hunched, stocky keeper. As you flung a right, the goblin-like Belial pounced. The very microsecond your body became momentum’s slave, he stole you away, tying your arm up and spinning you sharply around. “You’re not the only one who made a sacrifice at the altar of Zenith,” he toothily chewed at your ear, while Henry Moloch’s final, wheezing pleas - preserved in the dark, bushy hairs - still whispered into his. “Now, let’s bring you to heel shall we, you filthy mongrel.” The point of his elbow stabbed your liver, injecting your nervous system with a potent dose of debilitating agony. Jaw clamped shut, you silently screamed as you hit the bloody, clumped sawdust. Violently shuddering, the shocked gasps of the crowd left you in a vacuum, nothing to focus on other than the maddening pain which locked your other senses down. Hurts like the fuckin’ Dickens, huh? Rasa mocked sympathy. Well, for you. For me, it’s kinda… ticklish. “Now grow up,” Belial spat down onto you. Six The deer twitched at every rustle of leaves in the breeze or grinding of distant tires on the gravel path. Burnt amber eyes peered through the twilight, as nervy as those of the girl whose grave her hooves danced restlessly on. Safe for at least a few seconds, she nuzzled through the plastic wrapping of the flowers which rested against April Greer’s milky tombstone. As gnarled petals showered the grave, so did the simple message which had been left on the card. I’m sorry. Seven You’d died before; it ruined the mystique. There was no bright light to lure you. No pearly gates, no fiery pit of eternal damnation or cryptic purgatory. You died, for one hundred and eleven seconds. One hundred and eleven seconds which removed the fear or death, and the hope of the afterlife. You don’t have the guts, Rasa hissed. You? A spineless, weak bag of decaying flesh and crumbling bone? Don’t embarrass yourself any more tonight. Death. No chill wriggled up your spine at its mention. No shakes ravaged your hand. Still don’t understand, do you? it continued to rasp with an unnatural sense of urgency. When you die… I get this all to myself. Oh, the fun I can have with that mangled face of yours. Maybe I’ll peel the skin off, and tie it in ribbons around you little niece’s hair? It was scared. Rasa had been reduced to a Trojan virus which you had quarantined. You couldn’t hold it off for long, and the effort felt as if two migraines were tearing your brain apart as they waged war on one another. But, for a minute - maybe two - you could expel Rasa from your body; breathe clean air, touch without tingling fingertips. Rasa had been reduced to a Trojan virus which you had quarantined… and would be deleted when you rebooted. Eight Belial had a keen nose for death. It was sense on which you were reliant. As it wafted under the door and down the corridor, his nostrils twitched, bristly black hairs tickled by your last, chemical-laced breath. His orders were simple, their scope narrow; get you to the next WorldWide, conscious and mobile. This week, he would earn his money. This week, he would save the life he was hired to end, until he’d said yes to Christian Zenith. Nine You’d died before; it ruined the mystique. Second time, you thought you’d got it right. “April?” Ten After a lifeless one hundred and seventy three seconds, and the 7 hours of recuperative sleep which followed, the world took its time painting itself across your eyes as they timidly emerged from beneath sluggish lids. The world seemed darker. That, or you’d just returned from somewhere brighter. “… rest… something something something… mental evaluation… something… something…” “… get… something… to compete… something something… care about… something… fucked up head… something something… your job…” Rope bound your wrists to the makeshift gurney. You felt… hollow. “… second time… something something… human being…” “… something something opinion… something… no longer needed…” The voices of Belial and Werner Sarstedt were distant, but you gradually began to tune in. Between their heated words was… silence. Blissful silence. No detuned humming of crackles of static; like using a cell phone beside a cheap speaker. Rope bound your ankles to the makeshift gurney. You felt… free. Reboot complete. Miss me?
