Deceipt Abound: Part 2

12 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/12/05 (But takes place 8/05) August 2005 Tessa Windsor was never one for unexpected visits, especially the ones that were bound to have adverse effects. Guest or hostess, those said visits never seemed to go her ways. It always seemed that each dorm was too chaotic to abide in, rather they be littered with pointless papers or pitiful people, frustrations almost always seemed to be abound. Visiting the Zenith Estate was never any sort of exception to that rule. With six hungry, up and coming stars, an occasional bump in the road was to be expected. After all, the business pretty much defined the popular motto “survival of the fittest”. Given that Tessa was being served a dose of this reality first hand, the locking up of her knees as she stepped out of the tattered taxi that’d brought her as far as the front gate of the Zenith Estate was to be expected. “Just think of the kids when you do this.” It was certainly something that was a lot easier said than done. After all, four months had passed, and never once until the other day had any hints been dropped into her lap that she wasn’t meeting her expectations. And still, from the five others that she’d spent most of her summer with, there wasn’t any word of failed expectations, only a messenger to drop the overwhelmingly bad news to her, at a most inconvenient time. “Nice to see you again, Ms Windsor.” Greeted the always respectable Demitri Miasnikov, who as usual, was clad in black from head to toe. “Same to you.” Tilting her head up with a smile to acknowledge his greeting, she extended her hand out, and shook his hand for what seemed like the final time the two would meet. “Is Christian in?” “Would I be here if he wasn’t?” Drenched in sarcasm, the lead bodyguard of the Hunting Grounds, though more Christian Zenith in particular, answered her question with his own. “You ask such silly questions.” “That’s because I’d expect results just as hilarious.” Cut in Tessa, without as much humor. After all, she didn’t come here for friendship matters, as she had every other time. For now it was strictly business. For now, it was for her and her family. Her real family. Not the family of stars Christian Zenith had lured in with his multimillion dollar business. “Kinda of like the whole “you’re fired” pulled on me, earlier this week.” Politics weren’t up Demitri’s ally. So rather than heckle the beauty before him any further, he extended his arm to the door behind him, and swung the other forward to gesture that she enter and speak to the man herself. “Thank you Demitri.” She made sure to comment before the door closed behind her. After all, he’d done nothing wrong for her to snub him all of a sudden. “You’re welcome Ms Windsor. Good luck.” He managed to get in before the brass hinges swung the front doors to the Zenith estate shut. Along with Demitri, Tessa had also left her confidence on the other side of the stained wood that now long behind her. While her pace began to slow, her heart rate certainly didn’t. “All right, you’re this far in. Might as well continue.” So much easier said then done. Especially at times like this, when out of came the person she most dreaded to see, Christian Zenith. “Ah Ms Windsor, it’s a pleasure to see you here.” Full of cheer that seemed far too excitable for his cold demeanor to be real, Zenith stepped forward with outstretched arms, that Tessa couldn’t avoid. “If that’s the case then maybe you can explain to me all this nonsense I’ve been hearing this week.” Keeping her arms planted to her side, she made it clear that at the moment, his affection wasn’t appreciated. Especially if said rumor was true. “I’d be more than happy to.” His emotions were lacking from head to toe, yet his unexplainable affection for Ms. Windsor, never seemed to fade. Not so much for romantic reasons, but more so as acquaintances in the business. After all, he had invested quite a bit of his money in her supposed talents (in the ring that is). “Vivica, would you mind stepping out for a second?” Referring to his most trustworthy assistant, his girlfriend, Vivica J. Valentine was not afraid to share a scowl with her boyfriend, as she did what she were told to do. While Tessa considered Vivica a friend, it was quite obvious the feelings weren’t mutual. At least, not anymore. “She didn’t need to leave you know.” Remarked Tessa, as she settled into the nearest couch, the next room over. “I thought you’d prefer the privacy.” Even if the tone wasn’t sleazy, Tessa couldn’t help but see his remarks as such. Then again, she’d never really had much luck with the male population. “Is it really that necessary?” With a furrowed brow to do all the questioning, Windsor’s guts once again took a dive. “After all, it’s not like things don’t get around here.” If Tessa thought her words would mean anything to the owner of a multi million dollar business, she really ought have thought again. After all, he was the one that had come to her with nothing more than the purpose of helping her out. “I’m just doing you a favor.” Even a few feet’s distance couldn’t keep Tessa from lashing out in at least a verbal manner “Good, then do me another favor and tell me what this crap is about me losing my job.” Outside the room, Vivica J. Valentine stood there with a smirk, as her ears glued themselves outside the door to the living room, which had suddenly swung shut. This was what she’d been waiting days, if not weeks, for. On the other hand, things weren’t so juicy on the other side of the door. Burying her twitching fingers inside her lap, Tessa inhaled as much air as she could, and prayed that Zenith was somehow blind to her nerves. It’d have to be a miracle at this point for him not to notice how her face mismatched the rest of her summer complexion. “Two shades, three shades, four shades?” Counting certainly wasn’t as calming when you had other things on your mind. Especially when counting all the money she’d wasted just to try and conquer moments like these, or when counting how many shades lighter she’d probably looked by now. Her face had finally reached its boiling point, and out came even more reminders of the tension, sweat beads. They certainly weren’t a woman’s best friend. “Ah yes, that.” Taking in a breath of his own, Zenith seemed to suddenly grow as uncomfortable as Tessa at the moment. “Yes, that.” As firm as she’d wanted to sound, it probably came out as nothing more than a mere whisper. After all, she’d little power to control his decisions, she’d only hoped that maybe he’d take some pity on her like he had in the first place. His slightly callused fingers began to twitch as well, accompanied by a change of pose. At least it served Tessa some relief to think that she wasn’t the only one who’d ultimately end up passing out from the stress of the moment. Then again, his nerves weren’t to serve as comfort. After all, if everything was alright, why would he need to be so nervous? Exhaling, he dug his hands into the gap between the leather cushions beneath him. “I suppose you finally got the call from Bubba.” “Does that mean?” Her heart rate jumped up another five beats per minute. Why was she even asking such a thing? Of course it meant exactly what she’d thought! “I’ll let you do the math?” Even if he had been the one to okay the release of her contract to Global Wrestling, it wasn’t as if he’d wanted to do it. Sometimes though, you got to do thing for the safety of others. More particularly, the three other ladies he had by his side. “But why?” She quietly protested with a pair teary blue eyes, and words that came out as nothing more than a pout. “Because this is a team effort Ms Windsor.” Unfortunately, as just had Tessa had expected, his composure remained in tact with the robotic business man persona he’d always had. “If you can’t make the same sacrifices as everyone else, I can’t have you on my team.” Surprisingly, the door seemed to know just when to open, until Vivica J. Valentine popped out from the other side. Then it wasn’t so much of a surprise. It was more so something that felt like all privacy had been lost. But maybe not hope. “Maybe we can work something out though.” She interrupted the two. While Tessa should’ve been relived, she found herself speechless. Did she really want to be looped back into this crap if there were so many unreasonable expectations to follow. “No.” “What?” Vivica questioned, not so much with authority, but with plenty of confusion. “I said no.” She repeated, much more firmly this time. Slightly confused as to why her supposed friend wouldn’t take her up on a deal that was meant to save her career, she offered up another question. “Why not?” Surprisingly bravery had found it’s way back to the woman known to Zenith as nothing more than Ms Windsor. “Because, I’m tired of this crap.” “Excuse me?” Now, any sort of concern Vivica had, had eroded away faster the erratic blinking her eyes had begun to do. “You heard me Viv.” Assured Tessa, without any sort of eye contract. “I’m sick of being a number. I’m sick of going out there and playing some kind of bitch, just for the sake of a check.” “In that case, forget what I said.” Hissing through her teeth, Vivica backed into the leather couch to keep from tearing Windsor alive just for spitting on everything she’d help Christian create. “Go out there and let the sharks eat you alive. Because that’s exactly what they’re going to do.” Rather than argue back, she decided to pull herself from the grave the Hunting Grounds had dug for her, and head for higher grounds. “Whatever you say Viv.” What else was there for her to say? I hate you? Nah?.certainly not suitable enough for the moment. Sure, at the moment, she’d wished the very man, and woman for that matter before, would fall to his knees, clasping his chest., especially considering she felt like she was about to the very same thing, just for less cynical reasons. But really, what good would shouting at the very man who’d try to turn her career around for the better do? After all, it wasn’t him. It had to be someone else. It just had to be. And nothing could convince her more of that then when he looked back at her while Vivica seethed at his side, his blue eyes drooping in what certainly had to be one of the most sincere apologies she’d ever seen. “I’m sorry. It’s nothing personal, it’s just business.” She’d heard it so many times before, yet it never seemed to lose its sting. Why’d everything always have to be about business? Rather than try and force something out of her throat, which felt as if sealed shut for the moment, she gathered up her composure, and made her way towards the door. “I’m sorry Tess. It’s just business.” Repeated Vivica, in what seemed to be a total fake apology. Maybe it was just her mind convincing her of this though. After all, her and Vivica seemed to get along well enough to where there shouldn’t be any kind of bottled hatred. Once again, Tessa acknowledged the apology with nothing more than silence and a faint dropping of her eyelids to signify she’d at least heard what they’d said. If they were so sorry, why’d they let it come to this? At least there’d be one person who’d understand her. Or at least, she hoped. After all, Dawn McFarlane had been there for her every other time she’d needed her. So trudging down the oversized hallway, towards the room her and Dawn once shared, she went. She knew she’d find Dawn there. It was just a matter if she’d be alone or not. “Swallow your pride for just this once.” After years of taking others abuse, it wasn’t as if the pint sized diva really needed to be coaching herself in such a manner. But after being told you’re not good enough for the job, one can’t help but analyze every single thing they might have done wrong, and criticize it to the highest levels. The same pale blue walls greeted her as she slowly paced into the room, looking for anything she might have left behind. Sadly the walls were about the only thing that’d remained the same. “Just a few weeks and they’ve already moved on?” It was meant to be a thought for just herself, but somehow it escaped from the narrow hole in her throat into the ears of Dawn McFarlane herself. Dawn looked over one pale shoulder, her emerald eyes leaving the window that took up the rooms entire exterior wall. Scanning the teensy tiny terror from head to toe, she forced a pale imitation of a smile. 'Are you surprised? He's efficient with everything, especially his emotions.' Tessa, however, couldn’t be bothered to return even the slightest bit of smile to her closest counterpart. “Should I be?” Though their friendship had always been intimate, it’d also never been tested like this. At that she turned from the window, arms folding against the thin fabric of her sleeping top, still not removed after a restless night. 'Tessa...don't be like this.' “Don’t be upset?” Popping her head out from the walk in closest she’d disappeared into, she questioned Dawn with much more than words on how that could be possible. “How can I possibly be happy to go home and start from scratch all because I won’t make a few supposedly necessary sacrifices?” Dawn moved to the queen sized bed in the middle of the room, crossing one long leg over the other and smoothing out her rumbled boxer shorts while sighing expansively. 'You're taking this too personally Tess, it's just business isn't it? Besides, you've never really seemed like you were happy here...' Tossing the few remaining garments she had beside Dawn on the bed, Tessa finally had to let her frustrations force their way out. “That’s because everything’s about business! It’s never about how we really feel. It’s all about what it’s best for everyone else to feel! You don’t see that, do you?” Biting down on a nasty reply about feelings, Dawn shrugged helplessly, fixing her luminous eyes on Tessa's. 'Why didn't you say something if that's how you felt? It's no secret you've been unhappy...' She found somewhere else to look just then, picking idly at a piece of lint on the bedspread. 'Because I know how things like this end. And never once have they been in my favor. So I stood around and took it’unfortunately I took it too long. And now it's obvious that since I'm not with you because I can't make the same kind of crazy sacrifices, I'm gonna end up against you. Isn't that how this crap always works? ' As if she were some kind of pro on situations like this, she stood there with hands on hips, looking back at Dawn to agree with her. But her rallying call had failed. Dawn refused to meet her significant others gaze, feeling knotting in her stomach the way one hand knotted in the sheets. 'We've all had to make sacrifices Tess...don't you think you're being a little selfish by refusing to do the same?' “Maybe so.” She hesitantly replied as she brushed by Dawn to grab the little she had left here. “But at least I know in the end, I’ll only have my own happiness to worry about.” Marching to the door with plenty of frustration, she turned back and offered the only words she could think to say, “Good luck, I know you’re going to need it.” “No, I’m sure she’ll be just fine. I’ll make sure of it myself.” From behind Tess, came the voice of Lia, Dawn’s other closest friend. The one that chose to stick by her in all this mess. “You do that.” Tessa snarled, before being flooded by the image of the woman behind her. Then it all made sense. Suddenly, she needed not worry about Dawn anymore because she knew exactly why she didn’t fit into the Hunting Ground equation. And that reason stood right behind her. Yes, Shadow. More precisely, the shadow Tessa was forced to stand in right now with her around. The two rarely spoke, and for plenty of reason. Their only true common ground was Dawn, and well, neither really cared to share even that. And now, it seemed as though Dawn had finally decided she needed to side with one. And that one wasn’t Tessa. No wonder Christian seemed reluctant to let her go. No wonder Vivica tried to get her back in. And it was certainly no wonder why Dawn seemed to be so confused as to why Tessa wouldn’t want to be around when no one else seemed to have a problem with her. But Shadow. She’d never said a word to Tessa, and now it all seemed to make sense why. So rather than have petty catfights with someone she once considered close, she left without so much as another word. If Dawn wanted to be like that, she could. Eventually, things would come back and bite her in the ass too. Or so Tessa could hope. After all, she was through playing these games. For now.
