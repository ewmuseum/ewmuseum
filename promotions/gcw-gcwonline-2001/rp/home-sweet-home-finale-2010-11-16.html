Home Sweet Home, Finale

16 November 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/16/10 “Well?” Troy gazed expectantly at Tempest. She stared back, as if dazed, not entirely there. “Please tell me Wei didn’t send you on a wild goose chase.” Troy’s expression hardened. “No! No. It was great. It was my family, just like he said,” Tempest said with a tiny smile that seemed far away. “Well, what’s left of it, of course.” “Well… tell me all about it.” Troy smiled, waiting for the stories to spill forth, as he scooped her bag out of her hand. “You practically bounced in the door, so I know it went well.” Tempest rolled her eyes, still smiling as though she had a secret to tell. “It’s not exactly like that. I mean, I was trying to just see what they were like and …” “Oh come on. You want family more than anyone I know.” He nudged the door shut behind her as a small stampede of animals came hurtling around various corners; two ferrets and one ancient Border collie. “You even put up with my dad’s stories. That had to be the biggest day of your life.” He stopped and watched her expression as she stooped to pet the furry brigade that greeted her with that strange, immutable expression. “What happened?” The silence was a little longer than he would have liked. “Tempest.” He crouched slowly, to the delight of the ferrets who proceeded to romp across his thighs, oblivious to any tension amongst their humans, or perhaps in defiance of it. “Babe, you’re killin’ me here. What happened?” He reached out slowly and squeezed Tempest’s hand. She looked up; seeming almost startled and bit her lip. “It was fine. No, better than that. It’s just a little hard to explain.” ~ “She’ll be fine,” said Peter Tyrell, Tempest’s new found uncle. “Just give her a few minutes. She’s a wee bit old for that kind of shock.” Indeed the old woman with eyes as blue and piercing as Tempest’s own fanned herself with a hanky as other family members flocked around her with concern. “She’s tougher than she looks,” Peter added, eying Tempest with a curious fascination. “So was my dad,” she answered quietly. “I guess that’s appropriate, finding out that it came from someplace.” She turned to Peter and smiled devilishly. “Was he as big a pain in the ass to his mother as I’ve already proven to be?” “Oh yes, very much. But you’ll have to get used to calling her ‘Mum,’ you know?” Peter chuckled. “You are very much the spitting image of old Ken with that little smirk and all full of mischief. And not the least bit shy about putting out an uncomfortable proposal right in the middle of those it would scandalize.” “Did he do that too?” Tempest asked, peering around anxiously, suddenly seeming more uncomfortable with her own behavior. “Oh yes, he delighted in it. Well, not to say he did it on purpose, to make people uncomfortable, so much as to push them out of their comfort zone, to make them think. He didn’t do it if he didn’t think they were smart or could handle it. But…” Peter trailed off with a small sigh. “He did occasionally go too far.” “Hmm, that doesn’t sound at all familiar,” Tempest shook her head, but her wide eyes and the smirk threatening to break out onto her lips said otherwise. Peter smiled back and said, “Oh no, I’m sure it doesn’t.” “Is that why he fell out of favor with the family? He went too far?” Another sigh. Peter glanced at the old woman who was slowly gathering herself and glancing at Tempest occasionally. “You could say that. He was a caring soul and he didn’t much like limits.” Peter shrugged. “Some people become doctors to get rich and have a nice house and a comfy set up, but not Ken. He did it because he thought it was needed. And he was always meaning to go where it was needed and help those who really needed it. He didn’t want to sit around Leigh-on-Sea writing prescriptions for gout.” Tempest chuckled, shaking her head. “He got more than he bargained for. I never knew him to be afraid of anyone, but… sometimes I wished that he had been.” She smiled wanly and turned to look once again at her grandmother. “You’re not the only one.” Peter watched her watching his mother a moment. “Constance Tyrell did not imagine herself losing a son and a granddaughter. She didn’t ever imagine that her family would be split apart the way it was and that Ken would subject himself to he dangers he did. She felt it was irresponsible. And she couldn’t see it as assuming responsibility to those who really needed it.” He shook his head. “It was a conflict that they never resolved.” “She never embraced my family though.” Tempest said, trying to speak calmly and evenly, and not betray any hint of bitterness. “I never knew I had a grandmother. And she never knew my mother or acknowledged her.” Tempest blinked “At least I don’t guess she did. She died when I was so little. But I guess she disapproved. I don’t even know if they were married come to think of it. I didn’t know to ask back then. It wasn’t something that mattered.” “Oh! Oh they were. Yes.” Peter stared at Tempest in apparent shock. “My god, that’s right. You were only a child when he was killed. I did try and keep up correspondence with Ken. I wasn’t ashamed of him. I admit it was hard just because of family tensions and the fact that he was so busy. But I always knew that he was the braver of us.” Peter fidgeted, not unlike Tempest herself when faced with things that made her uncomfortable. “He didn’t like me giving him a hard time about his wife and baby though. So yes, they were married.” Tempest eyed him curiously and asked, “What did you give him a hard time about?” “Well, just that now he had a wife and a baby, he should come home. Or at least he should go some place safer than Hong Kong’s meanest streets. Yes, he was doing important work, but you know, he could go out in the country and treat people just as poor and needy without the crime and the gangs.” He ran a hand through his thinning hair. “He wouldn’t hear of it. His wife came from those streets. He knew that town and its suffering. He was so sure that the friends he had made would b enough to protect him.” “They weren’t,” Tempest said softly. “But he had no idea what was coming.” “Does anyone?” Peter looked up in time to see his mother take a deep breath and motion to him and to Tempest. “Look, go easy on Mum, all right? She has her faults, but she’s felt nothing but guilt since losing Ken, and you too.” “Me?” Tempest’s head turned abruptly toward Peter. “But you didn’t lose me. You just left me there.” Peter shook his head. “Haven’t you been listening? I went to Hong Kong after Ken was killed. I did go looking for you. I’d got frantic after your mother died that he should leave, but it only made him less apt to write back. When he was killed I went there straight away. But I was told you were dead.” “By who?” Tempest stared back, goose bumps suddenly rising on her arms. Peter shrugged. “Dunno. Some short fellow, soft spoken, huge pistol he stuck in my mouth though and convinced me that I had no further business in Hong Kong. Should have known better, I guess.” Now it was his turn to look ashamed, but Tempest patted his arm and said, “No. It’s ok. You didn’t know. And these days I do know what was going on, at least in part. We’ll talk more about this, ok?” She stood up and smoothed her dress as Peter began to lead her toward Constance. “And don’t be silly, of course I’ll go easy on her. All I really wanted was to know my grandmother.” ~ “Wow.” Troy shook his head. “It’s a good thing you didn’t give that poor woman a heart attack.” Tempest nodded. “Yeah, wasn’t going for that. Although I knew it would be a shock. I’m just glad I found them in time to meet her. “All thanks to Wei.” Troy let out a soft whistle. “That guy still creeps me out. I mean great peace offering and all, but… I dunno, just seems like a great way to get your guard down. I mean around him.” Tempest smiled. “Now you sound as paranoid as Miyuki.” “Just because you’re paranoid…” “Yeah, yeah, I know. She’d also hasten to add that the timing is remarkable, seeing as I face off against Zenith’s chief concern, in Vivica Valentine, and we’re still not at all sure that Wei isn’t on his payroll.” “Makes for a hell of a distraction.” Troy scooped up Emil and flipped him over onto his arm, much to the ferret’s squirming delight. “Not that I think it’s such a bad thing. Family keeps you grounded in reality a whole lot better than fighting and being around people who want to break you in half for a tin belt. Just my opinion of course.” He smirked in her direction; his expression making clear the criticism was intended with nothing but concern. She shrugged. “If it was a ploy, they might have thought my family would still reject me. Who knows? But I don’t get that feeling. Wei’s been too careful about so many other things, too well in the know. It seems to me that he’d have a clue about their likely reaction at least.” Troy nodded thoughtfully. “And that makes the case even stronger for him acting alone. Or not acting within Zenith’s orders exactly.” “Unless Zenith thinks that finding my family and discovering that they do love me, even if we have a way to go to understand each other, is going to make me weak.” Tempest scratched Trouble behind the ears and the Border collie lay down on her lap. “Of course, it would be like him to think that. Love is a weakness to him, some to be exploited.” Troy snorted. “Love is where it’s at. Even you know it’s the best weapon you’ve got going for you in the long run.” “What do you mean?” “Well it’s like when you were talking about Rasa and how sad it was that he had no control over his life anymore and was so scared to show his face or be identified in any way. What’d you say about him?” Tempest smiled softly. “I said that he’s as scared of greatness as he is of failure. He’s as scared of cheers or acknowledgement as he is of ridicule. It’s gotta suck to be scared of everything.” “Yeah and her highness is the same way, right?” Troy shrugged. “As great as she really is, it’s never gonna be great enough. And all because she knows nothing of love. Not of herself, not from anyone else.” Tempest smiled at him warmly. “Well that’s very sweet, but what do you suggest I do at Dangerous Games? Hug it out with them?” “I’ve heard worse ideas,” Troy answered with a grin at least as mischievous as hers. “Come on Troy, it’s not a love fest. I have to beat those two and that will require my best technique and more than a little toughness.” Tempest watched him as he silently petted the white ferret. “Violence doesn’t fix anything. Love fixes things. Love is the shit. And that’s all I know for sure. It may not win you a battle, but it wins the war. Because the war isn’t really over until it prevails.” Tempest nodded slowly. “That may be. And I do what I can to that end. But I am a wrestler, a fighter. I’m not there to rescue people to the exclusion of my own success. And meeting my family hasn’t changed that. I mean, why would it?” Troy’s eyes seemed to cloud over as he sat silently across from her on floor. “Family does change everything, Rachel. I know I don’t have to tell you that, having lost some of yours as well.” The two looked at each other and for the first time Tempest realized they had this in common too. “You’re talking about your sister.” Troy nodded. “I lost her just as surely as you lost your father, but with one crucial difference.” “What’s that?” “It was my fault.” Tempest shook her head. “Troy, I’m sure you didn’t…” “No. Just listen.” He stared straight ahead, not focusing on her as he spoke. And the ferrets gathered at Tempest’s feet, watching him curiously. “My sister fell in love with a guy who beat her up. We didn’t know at first. All the family knew was that we didn’t like him. But one day we found out. Well, I did what any good big brother does. I beat the living crap out of him.” Tempest gasped a little as the man she knew to flick bugs out the window rather than crush them admitted to this. “What happened next?” she asked quietly. “She left with him.” Troy sighed, and his jaw twitched as he recalled it. “She ran off with him instead of staying with me, with her family. Somehow he convinced her that I was dangerous despite the fact that he was the one beating her up. We didn’t hear anything for about nine months. Then, we got the call, the one no one wants to get.” Tempest put her hand over her mouth. She knew that Troy’s sister was dead. She just never knew how. “And just like that she was gone. Oh sure, they caught the guy. He’s up in the state pen. He tried to say it was an accident, but that argument didn’t hold up once the coroner figured out he smashed her head into a stove sixteen times. But ya know what?” He peered at Tempest’s saddened face. She merely stared back questioning. “Closure is bullshit. She’s just as gone no matter what sort of justice came after.” Tempest nodded slowly and scooted closer, reaching out and squeezing his hand. “I’m sorry. I didn’t know. That explains a lot.” “I hope so. I hope it makes clear why I know as no one else can that violence really doesn’t solve anything.” Tempest shook her head very slightly and said, “But sweetheart, you didn’t kill her. You’re not responsible for what happened. The man who killed her is. You couldn’t know that kicking his ass would…” “If I hadn’t done it, she would have stayed,” Troy said emphatically. “If I had just been there for her and let her know that she could leave him and come to me, to Mom andDad, at any time, she might have made it. I cut her off from us completely and made her choose. She couldn’t and it got her killed.” Tempest’s mouth moved a little, but she shook her head and embraced Troy. She didn’t need to fight with him. Love, after all, could solve this. ~ How do I tell him that his mistake was that he let the man live the first time around? And how can I possibly criticize him when I’ve made the same mistake myself? Love should solve things. Mercy should triumph over violence. But sometimes it just gets in the way of the things that matter most. ~ My body is a well oiled machine, at least from the outside looking in. My skin glistens even in the low light, through the haze of smoke, bound up only in the briefest of costumes, straps of leather and cloth that cover just enough to be legal. Legal. That’s an alien concept in this place. But this is not a striptease, it’s a fight. And the money laid down here is not merely to get a good look at a lean, young body. It’s to see it in all its glorious motion. Never mind my ribs sticking out. I’m eighteen and I don’t have a future. Some days I even know it. Bets are placed. Pipes are smoked. Deals are struck. Deals of all kinds, from the perfectly legal, to the patently felonious, and everything in between. And some deals having to do with exactly what is in front of the patrons, in the pit before them, which they watch like spectators in a tiny coliseum. If only it had that much charm. It’s just a dirty plank pit filled with straw. And in 1994, before the Chinese resumed what some might call control over Hong Kong, this was one of a dozen such places that served as fight clubs, opium dens and meeting places for those characters less than savory to meet and maybe to watch someone die. Such is the entertainment of monsters. This one is run by my Papa. That’s Papa Liu, the head of my triad, not my father. But he likes people to call him Papa. He considers himself more benevolent dictator than ferocious criminal. Sometimes it’s even true. But he is, nonetheless, a part of this world, bound by its rules and expectations and loathe to give up any of its powers. So as “nice” as he is to me, I am still a pawn. And I’m still here, fighting in this pit almost nightly. Someone even told me once, that this is the pit in which my mother died. I watch my opponent circle. She’s slow and clumsy. This will be an easy evening. I’ll try and roll around with her. The guys love that, bodies sliding around on each other. And it won’t hurt her too much. I think she’s high. They call us opponents and think we’ll kill each other for their table scraps, but most of us know better. Surviving is the key. And good odds week after week are only a small benefit. All it got me was a little more rice. Unfortunately, she doesn’t see it that way. After a few mad lunges and grappling around with her I’m finding it harder to hold onto her. It’s odd because she seems so thin, worse than me, all bones sticking into my sides like little daggers. But she’s stronger than I would have guessed. And someone out there’s getting a good laugh out of it. It’s that table of Ghost Shadows. Papa doesn’t like them. He deals with them because he has to. They’re all over the place, like most vermin. But they have nothing approaching honor. Maybe they’ve placed some money on her and maybe… yes, that’s it. As I break away and throw her headlong into the wooden side of the pit I see it, the glazed look in her eyes. They’ve given her something to make her fight harder. Not opium, something that goes the other way. PCP? Oh god… I get my answer. She barrels into me head first. I hit the back of the pit with a smack that nearly cracks the wood and a cheer goes up. Somebody’s making money now. I’m suddenly less concerned with winning and more concerned with getting out of this in one piece. She grabs me, but in spite of her grip I slide between her legs and smash her head onto the floor. She hasn’t gained coordination from the drug. I roll away and up. That’s when I see it. Somehow she’s gotten hold of a straight razor. I scream at her to drop it. Partly it’s to draw attention to the fact that she has it. This isn’t the way it’s supposed to go down. She lunges and makes a wild slash. All I can do is duck and roll. I steal a look up at Papa. Has he seen? Can’t he…? He’s seen. It just doesn’t matter. I must not have been looking when the deal was struck. I’m on my own. I dodge as best I can while the crowd hoots and finally I land a blow that means something. I smash the hand that holds the blade into the wall with my boot. I don’t get much time to breathe as she swaps hands and we go spinning around the ring again. Once again I beg her to stop this, before one of us gets hurt. Nothing. She’s not hearing, not thinking. And I’m tiring, because she’s so damn high. She just keeps slashing and I keep trying to dodge and defend. I give a good swift kick and spin, but it’s much too much. And time seems to slow down as I realize it and my open side turns toward where she shouldn’t be… but she is, spinning just as wildly, blade in hand. She strikes and the slash ignites a swath of pain, erupting from my skin like fire. Hot blood seeps down my back and side, and all the way down to my belly. I go weak in the knees. I fall. The cheer is deafening. As I hit the dirt she pounces on me with that blade raised, out of the corner of my eye I see Papa. He stirs. He rises very slightly from his seat. One of the Ghost Shadows yells, “She’s the last of them! Finish it!” It’s the wrong thing to say. I summon some primal spark still pushing my heart to beat and ball up a first, before slamming it into the girl’s nose. Blood gushes forth and she drops the razor. I roll her off of me and as she scrabbles for the blade like a lifeline, I shove her back into the wall and smash her right hand over and over into the wood, splintering it as she now screams. I’m still bleeding and dizzy, but when I finally stop, she struggles free and tries to grab the blade with the other hand, now that I’ve wrecked her right one. So I wreck that one too. I stomp down on her left hand with all my might and feel the tiny bones crunch. She screams. I think I do too. Every movement hurts. I see sparkly motes spinning in front of my eyes. Somewhere I hear a voice, now that the crowd is quiet. Is it hers? “Do it,” she says while some bastard child of a beautiful poppy swims through her veins and tells her everything will be all right. It will never be all right again. The blade is in my hand. Behind my eyes I see my father fall, gunshots tearing through his chest. Now I look up, my Papa stands there. “Do it!” he calls. “Finish her. You have proven yourself, my child.” Drunken shouts answer him, but are quickly silenced by a wave of his hand. To survive the thing that should have killed you, will, as they say, make you stronger. It’s the code. I’ve earned it. I come close and she looks at me, calm, at peace with her end. I kneel beside her, with the razor near her throat. “No.” I whisper it and terror fills her eyes anew. I have spared her, but from what? She will go forth into a merciless world unable to use her hands. I gather the last of my fading strength. With a quick spin and leap I hurl the blade in the direction of that man who presumes to call himself my father. ~ What will happen now? Something wonderful? Something terrible? The eyes that stare back at me from the mirror hold no answers, just history, and family, and places that will always be a part of me. It is said that this is the most Dangerous Game. I beg to differ. For I have played the most dangerous and survived. And what I have brought out of those may be more than any number of opponents can handle. Maybe I’ll be merciful this time.
