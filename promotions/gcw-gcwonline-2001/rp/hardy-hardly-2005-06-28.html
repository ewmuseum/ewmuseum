Hardy? Hardly.

28 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6-28-05 Baltimore/Washington D.C. International Airport? Between the sweltering heat and the constant back-and-forth bustle of impatient tourists, the sharp smell of body odor and generic antiseptic cleaning solution was enough to water the eyes of even the most experienced traveler. Wandering from the check points to the gates, families and business moguls alike carried their baggage like sacks full of sand, everyone sweating despite the controlled temperature. There was no question about it; summer was officially here, and nobody seemed very welcome to the fact. They’re all ungrateful anyhow, Rollins thought, once again seated alone in one of the shit-brown plastic bowls that passed as seating. He watched the milling masses scramble by with fastidious interest, simply passing the time until his own Gate 5 began to admit passengers bound for Boston. It had occurred to The Renegade that half of these frantic people were simple tourists, supposedly enjoying their government-protected vacation time, and yet he had witnessed no less than three family arguments and nine cell phone screaming fits. And that was just in the last fifteen minutes. Rollins could relate. He was in a sour mood himself, that much was obvious, but he was just sick of airports in general. “Excuse me, Renegade?” a meek voice chimed in. His annoyance plainly visible, Rollins turned toward his addresser. Standing with a ballpoint pen in one hand and a recent copy of GCW Magazine in the other, a twelve-year-old boy with a bad bowl cut and bunched-up jeans blinked innocently at Rollins. A timid hesitation sat on his dimpled face as he thrust out the magazine, nervously looking down at one untied tennis shoe. “Could you sign this” You’re my favorite,” he babbled, gaining a bit of confidence. A glint lit up his eyes as he added, “Except for Mike Hardy, he’s so cool!” A dull throbbing pain began pounding in Rollins? head. Just as The Renegade started to open his mouth with a reply that he would most likely regret, the kid’s excitement cut him off. “Are you headed to the arena” I am, too! My dad said that if I got good grades he’d take me to a GCW event this summer, and I really wanted to go to Dangerous Games.” He paused, then stated matter-of-factly, “Mike Hardy’s gonna kick your ass.” And so the migraine began. ~<>~<>~<>~ Earlier? With a snap-hiss of his zippo, Rollins lit another cigarette. “You know that shit’ll kill you, right?” Exhaling a smooth cloud through his nostrils, The Renegade painted his face with a scowl. “Didn’t you hear?” he said flatly, “Slow suicide is in this season.” Seated across a green felt card table from one another, Rich “The Renegade” Rollins and the uniformed dealer exchanged surreptitious glances as the dealer tossed down two whole cards for the both of them. The dimly-lit casino offered little distraction, comprised of about a dozen card tables, four roulette wheels and a few rows of slot machines that only a handful of elderly retirees occupied. Business had obviously not been good, as far as Rollins could tell. Cupping his hands over the corners of his cards, Rollins casually took a peek at what he had; the five and ace of clubs peeked back. Pulling his wallet out of his pocket, Rollins dipped his fingers into the fold and produced his bargaining chip. Tossing the plane ticket down on the tabletop, he offered the dealer a slight nod. “One round-trip ticket to Okinawa, Japan,” he intoned, his voice laced with indifference, “valued at about twelve-hundred dollars. Are those stakes you can live with?” He studied the dealer’s face carefully as the stocky man frowned, absently adjusting the green-tinted cap that sat atop a steadily receding hairline. He took another drag on his cigarette, impatiently waiting for the dealer to either tell him to take a hike or deal. Shooting a look up at what Rollins could only guess was a hidden security camera, the dealer shrugged. “I could use a vacation,” he decided, tossing the equivalent value of blue and green chips into the center. Burning the top three, the dealer dealt out three more cards face up on the table, showing a rather interesting flop in what was a rather unusual game of Texas Hold’em. Looking up at both men were the nine of hearts, a seven of clubs and a seven of diamonds. Rollins considered his options, knowing full well that the odds of pulling out a flush were around thirty-four percent, but the stern look in the dealer’s eyes told him that he didn’t have another seven up his sleeve. All he needed was to catch two more clubs and his little experiment would be a success. “Why gamble with a plane ticket, man?” the dealer suddenly asked, burning another card a flopping it down on the table. The king of clubs. One more club to go. “Maybe I just don’t like Japan,” Rollins shot back. The lie was blatantly obvious as his furrowed brow broke for a moment in concentration. “Or maybe it’s none of your business.” Offering an apologetic nod, the dealer tossed down the final card. The queen of clubs. A fluttering excitement raced around in Rollins? stomach. He made the flush. An entire world of importance was resting on this simple game of cards, but the dealer had no clue. No indeed; the dealer just shot The Renegade a smug smile, unknowing of the fact that he had just dealt out the answer to a decision that Rollins had been struggling with all night. It was either a trip to Boston for Dangerous Games and the final confrontation with Mike Hardy, or another disappearing act as he packed his bags for Japan in pursuit of the only woman he had ever loved. The distinction between his professional career and his personal life had blurred to the point that Rollins was unsure of exactly where The Renegade ended and Rich Rollins began. Or maybe it was the other way around. Ultimately, it was irrelevant. “Sorry my friend,” Rollins said, flipping his cards over. “The clubs were good to me today. That’s a flush.” Letting out an impressed whistle, the dealer nodded. “Not bad,” he said, flipping over his own cards. And instantly Rollins? heart melted like an ice cube in the oven. “Full house,” the dealer sighed, showing that he had indeed been holding another seven and a queen. “Sevens over queens. Sorry man, better luck next time. Maybe I’ll bring you back a case of sake, and we’ll call it even.” Rollins could do nothing but accept defeat as the dealer raked in his spoils, slipping the plane ticket into his vest pocket. It seemed that Lady Luck wasn’t ready to give The Renegade the woman of his dreams. Instead, she had handed him Mike Hardy. All things considered, the payoff was almost as good. ~<>~<>~<>~ Back at BWI Airport? The excitable little boy stuck his precious magazine in Rollins? face again, grinning like a Cheshire cat. With his annoyance doing nothing but feeding his headache, The Renegade snatched the magazine and pen from the boy’s grip and flipped it open to a random page. Page 23, the middle section of an article recapping Warpath. Displayed in all his treacherous glory, a ringside picture of Rollins in the midst of planting Hardy with a mid-air Anarchist Elbow covered the top half of an entire page. The memory itself flooded his mind without warning, instantly forcing him to vicariously relive the moments on the page through this one picture. The smell of the hot crowd, the sting of the impact as his elbow connected with Hardy’s throat, the exhilarating sense of accomplishment as Michael Stevens made the cover and scored the win? it was all so suddenly vivid, so abruptly clear in his head. “My name is Andrew,” the child gushed, pointing to a clear place on the opposite page. “Right there.” His focus returning to the here and now, Rollins looked up at the brat. “You’re name’s Andrew, huh?” he asked. The kid nodded vigorously. “That’s my middle name,” he replied thoughtfully, scribbling his signature on the indicated spot. To Andrew, my favorite fan. -The Renegade “Really?” Andrew gasped, his eyes growing wide. “Wait’ll my friends hear this! I was named after The Renegade!” Despite his irritation, Rollins couldn’t help but admit that the kid’s enthusiasm was charming, even if it was a bit misplaced. “Here you go,” Rollins said, handing the magazine back. “I’ll look for you in the crowd, Andrew.” A huge smile exploded across the kid’s face. “Hey! Maybe we’ll sit next to each other on the plane!” he squealed. Damnit. And just when he thought his headache was gone. To be concluded?
