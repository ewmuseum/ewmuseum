Luck: Who Needs It?

20 February 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02/20/10 Ding! Ding! Ding! “And the winner of the match as a result of a pinfall, Ace Action!” The crowd hops to their feet, showering the returning fan favorite with their respect and adoration as Joey Andrews’ voice echoes throughout the small stadium. Having returned to the ring after nearly ten years of absence, the rush of it all feels almost overwhelming. At the same time, it feels like home. As his arm is raised in victory, he gazes down at his fallen opponent, a local jobber trying to make a name for himself at the expense of the wily veteran. “You did good, kid.”, he says to himself, almost as if he were giving himself false reassurance though he was thinking of the young buck he’d left staring up at the lights. He takes a look around the arena, surveying his surroundings as the almost standing room only crowd chants his name. Climbing out onto the apron of the ring between the top and middle ropes, he lifts a hand to wave to the fans as he heads toward the curtains leading to the back. Raising his arms in celebration, a smile curls the corners of his lips as he allows himself to be overtaken by the electricity of the crowd. His heart and soul radiated with warmth, even as chills ran down his spine. He finally knew where he was, where he belonged; the only place that ever truly made any sense to him. Ace was home. ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- Backstage, Ace is greeted by the sound of clapping hands as his younger brother, AXEL comes into view. The exhausted older brother leans against the nearby wall, huffing and puffing in an attempt to steady his breathing. Placing a hand over his chest, he breathes deeply, chest heaving as he struggles to compose himself. “That was a lot harder than I thought it would be. I never imagined I would be this out of shape, even after training for weeks in advance.” “Well, considering the fact that you’ve been completely inactive for almost ten years, big bro, it’s not that hard to believe. It’s like the old saying goes, ‘Rome wasn’t built in a day.’ Besides, whether you’re in tip-top shape or not, you put on one Hell of a match out there.” “Thanks. I suppose you’re right, but I could have done better. I wasn’t at my best, and it’s eating me alive.” AXEL can’t help but pity his older brother, the one who always managed to keep a level head about things. This wasn’t like Ace at all and while AXEL knew it was just nerves, he also knew that the poor fellow needed more than just physical training. Giving his brother a firm slap between the shoulders, AXEL tries to rouse his attention. “Stop moping and be a man, Ace. So what if you botched a few spots or missed a few cues? It happens to everyone. Sure, it may happen to some people more than others, but at least you had the sack to go out there and give it another try after all this time. Your doctor said you’d never walk, much less wrestle again and look at you. You proved him wrong. What have you got to whine and cry about?” A deathly silence falls over the two, neither man looking at the other for a long moment. After a few seconds the silence is shattered by the loud thud of Ace’s fists pounding the wall behind him. His face is contorted with anger, lips curling into a hateful sneer as he shifts his focus to AXEL. “What do you know, anyway? You’ve never had to deal with losing your career and your livelihood all in one, fell swoop. You’ve never had to sit confined to a wheelchair for four years of your life while the doctor’s voice plays over and over in your mind like a broken record. ‘You may never wrestle again.’ Do you have any idea how it feels to hear those words repeating over and over inside your head?” AXEL begins to slowly back away, holding his hands up, palms out at chest level as if waving his older brother off, he tries to diffuse the situation. Having absolutely none of it, Ace furrows his brow and pounds his fist into the wall once again as he nears his younger sibling. “Do you realize what I had to go through just to be able to walk without pain? Have you ever had to live through all the hours of excruciatingly painful physical therapy I had to endure just to be normal again? Who are you to condescend to me about anything?!” “Take it easy, big brother. That isn’t what I meant at all, and you know it. I don’t know why you’re so bent out of shape over this. You have nothing to be ashamed of. If anything, you’re a fuckin’ inspiration to anyone who feels limited by a ‘handicap’.” Regardless of his intentions, all AXEL’s words manage to do is enflame the situation. The vein in Ace’s forehead is throbbing fiercely now, his face as red as a tomato. If it were humanly possible, it’s quite plausible to say that the elder Action would be blowing steam from his ears at that very moment. “Seriously, Ace, I’m sorry. I was trying to motivate you, but it appears I took things a touch too far,” says AXEL as he finds that he’s trapped between a rock and a hard place, with the rock being the wall behind him and the hard place being the angry male stalking toward him. His efforts to calm the nerves of his brother seem to be all but futile, so he does the only logical thing--cover up and hope he doesn’t get clobbered. “It’s…” AXEL tenses as he catches sight of the taller of the two looming over him, and he just knows that it’s about to be lights out for him. Flinching and nearly jumping out of his shoes as Ace lifts his hand, he’s almost certain that hand is about to make contact with his skull. Though his first instinct is to turn away and try to escape down the hallway, he knows that he’s overmatched in strength and if Ace wanted him to stay there, he wasn’t going anywhere. “It’s… fine.” That hand finds its way to AXEL’s shoulder, giving it a pat before the larger of the two steps back to give AXEL some room to breathe. Heaving a heavy sigh of relief, he steps away from the wall and brushes himself off. Looking down toward the front of his jeans, he checks for any wet spots that may or may not have occurred and laughs. “It’s a good thing I didn’t have to piss, man. You’d have made me wet myself.” Ace shakes his head and laughs as he throws his arm around AXEL’s shoulders, guiding him back toward the locker room area. AXEL’s absolutely drenched in a cold, nervous sweat and there were bottles of spring water in the mini-fridge. As they enter the room, Ace motions as if to tell AXEL to make himself at home. Doing just that, the younger Action takes a seat in a chair in the corner of the room. “Want something to drink?” “Sure.” Ace pulls a bottle of water from the mini-fridge and tosses it to AXEL. Without a word, AXEL twists the top off and turns it up to his lips, gulping half the bottle down in one sitting. Placing the water off to the side, he leans back and places his hands behind his head before resting it against the wall. “So now that all of that’s over with, how are you feeling after your big return match? Do you think you’re ready to try and make it back to the big time?” Ace shakes his head, brushing his fingers through his close-cut brown hair. In his mind, his performance wasn’t anywhere near good enough to warrant a return to the bigs. Sighing quietly, Ace plops down on the wooden bench in the middle of the room, leaning over to unlace his boots. “Honestly, I feel great. I don’t remember the last time I felt so alive, and I wouldn’t trade this feeling for anything in the world. Am I ready to come back to the upper echelon of professional wrestling? Not by a long shot. I’ve still got a ton of training to do, and not just as relates to my technique. I’m thirty-two years old, AXEL. I shouldn’t be sucking air like I’m Karl Zarn chasing a Little Debbie truck.” “Well, if that’s how you feel, then that’s fine. I’ll help you train as much as I can. There’s one thing that you need to bear in mind, however. I’m on the road most of the year, and I won’t always be around to keep you motivated. If you want to get back into top physical form, you have to want it; and I mean TRULY want it. I’d watch what I say about Karl, though. He might try to squeeze into the ol’ tie dye, and that’s something no one wants to see.” The two share a good chuckle over the image of their former mentor trying to squeeze his oversized body into that tie dyed t-shirt that looked as if it had seen better days. Shaking his head, Ace calms himself and looks to his younger brother with inquisitive eyes. “Speaking of training, you’ve got a big match coming up in a few days. I’ve been keeping tabs on you, and it looks like you’ve been catching hell from Dan Black and his sleazeball lawyer.” “Yeah, things haven’t exactly been easy or simple for me as of late. I’ve been dealing with him off and on for two years, and it ends now. You say you’ve been keeping tabs on me? Then you’ll know that at NC-17, I’ve got one last chance to reclaim the championship which some say never should have left my waist in the first place.” Ace nods, his facial features holding a look of absolute disgust. Knowing what it’s like to be robbed of something one worked hard to earn, he can sympathize. Taking a sip of his own water, Ace swallows hard, trying to force the lump in his throat down. “You know what? I agree wholeheartedly. You may not be in possession of the championship right now, but you’re its rightful owner. Black didn’t earn the right to hold the United States Championship, you did. He stole it from you in broad daylight and the worst part was that there was nothing anyone could do about it.” “Truth be told, I don’t really care about that. Much like life, championships are fleeting. What bugs me is how he managed to pull the wool over my eyes and get me to sign that stupid contract. I practically signed my life over to him and now that I’ve started standing up for myself, I’m facing a lawsuit that could cost me everything that I own. It’s do or die time for me, big brother.” “Do or die? What do you mean?” “In other words, this is my last chance to right the wrongs that have been done to me. It’s my final opportunity to rid myself of Dan Black once and for all, and to bring prestige and honor back to that belt. I freed it from the clutches of one deadbeat champion in Tyson DeBough, and I can snatch it from the greasy mitts of Dan Black too. If I do, not only do I regain what’s rightfully mine but I’ll also be free of any contractual obligations to Dreidle Boy.” AXEL leans forward in his seat, placing his elbows on his knees and resting his head in his hands. Taking a deep breath, he continues onward. “If on the off chance that I don’t manage to defeat him and end his reign of terror, he’s going to sue me for everything that I’ve got. On top of that, I’ll have to go to the following Worldwide, drop to my knees in the middle of the ring and proclaim him as the greatest wrestler that ever lived.” “It sounds like you’ve got a lot on your plate, little bro. If you need anything, and I mean anything at all, you know you can come to me. I’ll be glad to help you in any way that I can.” AXEL nods, sighing as he stands up and makes his way toward the door. Stopping in his tracks, he glances back over his shoulder at his older brother and forces a smile. “Thanks, Ace. It really means a lot to hear you say that. I think I’m going to go, so that you can finish changing. I need to get started on my training, anyway. I’ve got to be ready for anything, after all.” “Yeah, I know what you mean. Listen, remember what I said. If you need ANYTHING, I’m only a phone call away.” “I know. See you later, big bro.” “Definitely. Good luck, AXEL.” The Angel of Vengeance simply nods as he exits through the doorway, making the short trip down the hallway to the rear exit of the building. Though it’s only a small walk to his car, it felt so much longer than before. “Good luck… Yeah, I’m going to need it.”
