Orchestra of Wolves, Part 7: The King of Nothing

10 September 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10 September ”To give life you must take life, and as our grief falls flat and hollow upon the billion-blooded sea I pass upon serious inward-breaking shoals rimmed with white-legged, white-bellied rotting creatures lengthily dead and rioting against surrounding scenes. Dear child, I only did to you what the sparrow did to you; I am old when it is fashionable to be young; I cry when it is fashionable to laugh. I hated you when it would have taken less courage to love.” - Charles Bukowski, As the Sparrow. * * * * * It was a curious morning in Northern Ohio; a morning of blistering sunlight, blue skies, and wispy white clouds, yet not particularly hot. Salient bursts of wind powered through the air, dishevelling designer haircuts and puffing clouds of dust and sand down the busy street, making the day feel more like the beginning of Autumn than the end of Summer. Across the road a tiny bird fluttered wildly, struggling bravely to keep a steady trajectory against the prevailing turbulence, and a little boy clung tightly to his blue baseball cap so the breeze wouldn't snatch it from his head and send it careering down the street. A wild day, indeed. Hundreds gathered beneath the sky's azure and white quilt. People of all sorts; short people, tall people, thin people, fat people. Young ones, old ones, and some so old they should have better things to do than attend a pro-wrestling autograph signing. They'd began to arrive at 5am, and still the crowd was growing. Brought forth by the promise to "Meet the Stars of GCW!" a few days before the promotion's Warpath pay-per-view, they waited patiently for this faceless chain record store's doors to be flung open, some growing impatient, others just glad to be there, shielded from the sun by the building’s glassy girth. Twenty-three year old Stan Woodrow led the charge. He was the first one there, and his red Thermos flask that had been full of warm coffee a few hours prior had served him well. Since his formative years he’d been a wrestling nut, and the men and woman behind the store’s big revolving doors were his heroes. Stan hadn’t slept the night before and fatigue was desperately trying to dig its claws into him, but his excitement was too great for anything but palpable elation to capture him. A group of high school kids gathered a few yards away. Johnny, Karl, and Louis each held various pay-per-view DVDs and pictures of their favourite grapplers close to their GCW branded tees. They were edgy; having bunked-off school for the day, none of them were able to quell the fear of somehow being found out by a passing teacher or one of their parents. None of them had a clue what they’d say to the multimillionaire superstars when they got inside, but it didn’t matter; they were there, that was the important thing. Roland and Wesley Jarosz were halfway down the line, the only two in the crowd not sharing excited laughter and Mad Hatter grins. They stood awkwardly, sandwiched between groups of excitable fanboys as the Ohioan wind swirled dust harmlessly around their weather-beaten faces. They were the black sheep of the flock, standing out through the lack of wrestling merchandise on their bodies and their gruff, modest exteriors. “Gawdamn, much longer we gotta wait?” said Wesley, growing more impatient with every passing minute. The younger of the brothers, he was a tall, gaunt man of six-two and one-fifty. His face sloped down towards a thin, pointy chin, giving a rodent-like quality that his feeble attempts at growing a patchy beard didn’t exactly satiate. “Shit’s ridiculous; shouldn’t this thing ‘a started a half hour ago?” “Yeah, yeah,” said his elder brother, deflecting words that he must’ve heard at least a dozen times already. Like his sibling, Roland was tall, but he’d filled-out a lot better than his ratty brother. Over a decade’s worth of twelve-hour days on the family farm had buffed his body almost to the level of the wrestler he was there to see, and his XL-sized black-and-white flannel shirt clung to his granite-rough frame like a second skin. “Wouldn’t be so bad if we weren’t s’rounded by all these fuckin’ geeks,” Wes said under his breath, quiet enough so that only Roland would hear him. “Guess that comes wi’ th’ territory ‘a this ‘sport’.” “Just quit yer jabberin’, fool. It ain’t gonna speed this up any,” Roland said as he pushed his side-swiped brown fringe back into place, only for the wind sweep through and immediately displace it again. Roland’s face was a handsome one; battered and weather-beaten his skin may have been, but strong brow hung close to his thin brown eyes and he had a strong, almost angular jawline that could take a punch or two. “B’sides, already drove long enough to get here – what’s a couple of hours standin’ in a queue, huh?” With a resigned huff, Wesley accepted his elder’s words and stuffed his skinny fists into the pocket of his old Wranglers. He was tall enough to see over the heads of most of the crowd, and, for what must’ve been the fifth or sixth time already. The group of six security guards that crowded the door had split, with two of them working their way down the line, conducting “random” checks on anyone that looked like they might want to get their name in the papers by roughing-up a wrestler. “Still think this s’gonna be worth it?” Wes turned to his brother. “I mean, hell we gon’ do with all these people ‘round?” He had some ideas of his own, but it was worth testing Roland’s waters. “Don’t need to do much; we say a few choice words in front ‘a these folks, show what a fuckin’ rat their ‘King’ really is, that’ll be enough.” Wesley raised his brow. “That it?” “Yeah. Shit, ain’t like I can just march in there and plant one on the fuck’s jaw. Naw, gotta be cleverer than that.” Roland paused, watching the two guards in their luminous yellow vests as they worked their way down the deep line. “Man’s an attention-seeker, see. What he does, it’s all ‘bout the public bein’ behind him an’ shit. He’s nothin’ without all these simple fucks screamin’ his name. We turn them on him, even for a second, he falls t’pieces. Simple.” Wesley groaned. “You got no idea how bad I wanna smash that British prick’s face, man.” “The hell I don’t. She’s my sister too, ‘member?” Roland looked into his brother’s cold, blue eyes and scowled. “Nothin’ more I wanna see than him crash an’ burn, but we ain’t animals, man. I know ya wanna do this the old-fashioned way, but we gon’ get arrested if we try anythin’ stupid.” “But shit, you seen what he made her do to herself? Girl’s a wreck; she’s ruined. Ain’t ever seen nobody in that kin’a state b’efore, an’ it’s all ‘cause ‘a him. Words ain’t gon’ be enough t—“ Wes was stopped by a tap on his shoulder. He turned around and his eyes met with those of one of the security guards. “Excuse me, sir.” “Huh?” “Just carrying out a couple of routine security checks. Mind if we pad you down?” Wesley a nervous heat begin to boil between his temples. * * * * * “You’ve beaten Jordan and Clyde. So what? Still a long way to go before you’re getting anywhere near my record.” Vivica J. Valentine smiled as Andy draped the GCW World Heavyweight Championship across his shoulder. “Yeah, well. If a teensy-weensy little woman can hold this thing for almost a year, just think how far a big badass like me’s gonna go.” He punctuated his words with a cheeky wink, much to the chagrin of his diminutive friend. She folded her arms across her ribcage and frowned. “What have I told you about saying that?” “Ach. Chill it, V; I’m just playing with ya.” “Well, maybe that ain’t my kinda play.” Unsure whether he was doing the winding-up or being wound-up himself, Andy gave up. He didn't have any patience for games today, even with the Fearless Phenom. The Scottish King of Cool felt his facial muscles relax as the smile faded from his face. He walked towards one of the room’s porthole windows and glared down at the crowd below as Valentine jawed him with scepticism. “Jeesh. Andy Murray, all outta wisecracks? I never thought I'd see the day.” She stepped forward and playfully jabbed a tiny fist into Andy's meaty bicep. “Oh, I got plenty left, don’t you worry. Just gotta save it for them.” Andy nodded down to the masses as Valentine walked up to the window and pushed up onto her tiptoes to glance over the ledge. “There's quite a lot of them, isn't there?” The Scot turned again, pressing his back against the wall. Cast his gaze around the room and asked himself why this room, of all places, had been deemed a good place for two of the most value commodities in American sports to ready themselves. The top-floor room was small and boxlike, with only two tiny windows to fill it with the sun's rays. Sheets of ancient wallpaper were beginning to peel up front the cracked linoleum floor, much of which was covered by dog-eared boxes of old stock. He must’ve brushed at least a centimetre of dust from the table and chairs when he’d first arrived, too. He thought some of the places he'd been holed-up in during his last Just Wrestling tour were bad, but this was little more than a room the store’s staff left old stock to die. He returned to Valentine's comment and sighed. “Yeah, there is.” The future GCW hall-of-famer stopped peering over the edge, noticing The King's dull, toneless delivery. Dressed in full promotional attire, Valentine flicked a few rogue strands of blonde hair from her eyes and leaned against a crate of old Bon Jovi CDs that nobody wanted to buy (except for Shane). “Everything okay, Murr? You don't seem all there. Please don't tell me that Bagwell's got you--” “Bagwell?!” Andy's head suddenly snapped round. Beneath his mirrored aviators, he cast Vivica a curious gaze. “Come on. You know me better than that.” He watched Valentine slide herself onto the box of obsolete music. “Nah, just some shit I'm dealing with. Don't worry about it.” “'Don't worry about it'? How are we supposed to be a bad-ass, autograph-signing duo if you wont even crack a smile, huh?” Andy flashed her his set of pristine pearly whites. “See? I still got a little Cheshire Cat in me. I'll be fine down there. It's only a couple of hours.” “Good, 'cause when little Jeff Jnr. from Northern Ohio comes up asking you for a photo you can't be gurning like a lion with a sore tooth.” “V, I know. I've done thousands of these bloody things before,” Andy said, his tone lighter than his words. “It just gets a little harder to put on that happy clown mask when the shit starts hitting the fan behind the scenes, you know?” She who makes up the “entine” in “Murrentine” bowed her head; she'd heard what had happened through the locker-room's inevitable Chinese whispers, and Andy knew it too. “I know exactly what you mean.” “Just trying to put a positive spin on it, but it's tough. It’s not exactly the easiest thing in the world to deal with anyway, but when you don’t even know she was pregnant in the first place?”. Andy shook his head. “I dunno, Viv; either the man behind the pearly gate’s really got it in for me, or karma’s gettin’ ‘round to kicking my ass.” “You know that isn’t a healthy way of looking at it.” “Yeah, well. What is? Tell me that, ‘cause I sure as hell don’t know.” Valentine looked at the man; unsure of how to react to someone who'd never confided in her with such openness. “Still, onwards and upwards,” Andy continued, forcing the least convincing smile he'd ever cracked. “Gotta try and brush that aside for a couple of hours at least, 'cause this?” he slapped his hand against one of the belt's gold panels. “This is about the only thing I got left now, and there ain’t no way Chris Bagwell’s taking it from me.” Without warning, the door to their tiny room swooshed open and in stepped a pimply-faced GCW employee. He knew immediately that he'd walked in at a bad time but that didn't sway his momentum one bit. “Uhhh, hey guys,” he said. “Listen; we're gonna have to shut this down. Signing's cancelled.” Relief washed over Andy; meeting fans usually something he enjoyed, but yesterday's meet-and-greet had been more of a chore than a joy, and with a hungry Chris Bagwell looming over him and his home-life lying in tatters, the last thing he needed was another distraction. Vivica J. Valentine didn't take the news so well. “What!? Why?” she said, pushing herself from the box's top. “That's a bit sudden. What happened?” The kid swallowed. “Well, umm. Someone in the queue...” he paused as his unsteady, teenage voice failed him. Took a second to compose himself. “They found a guy with a knife.” * * * * * His was a house of God; a family home of built on strong wooden beams and a sturdy foundation of virtues and vows. Littered with biblical oil paintings and signs of the cross, very much the home of a man who'd poured everything into his faith and family, for better or worse. A lofty farmhouse on the Nebraskan plains, surrounded by infertile farmlands and dying crops. It was the only building in town bigger than the magnificent white chapel at the end of the road, and the only one that Glenn Jarosz saw fit to call him. Now in his late sixties and unable to walk without the aid of his trusty cane, the disease-ravaged Polish-American was still very much the man of the house. Though his movement became more and more restricted every day, he still made sure that he ran a tight ship inside the house he’d been born in. His wife and his eldest son may have left him for the afterlife, but two of his three remaining children still called the place home, and the other had taken temporary residence. They were all under one roof; much as they’d been for generations. Glenn was in the living room with his daughter and the even-dependable tick-tock of his grandfather clock for company. He sat in his armchair; barely able to move a muscle as the world around him passed him by completely. It plagued him, knowing that his body could no longer keep up with his mind. No longer was he fit to man the family farm, and that made it harder and harder for him to justify himself as the head of the house. He was reliant on others to put food on his table, now, and only his lord knew how it infuriated him. Disease and old-age had left him with nothing more than a houseful of memories, and it wasn't enough. His daughter, Kayla, sat in the chair opposite him, dainty legs tucked beneath a body cloaked entirely by an oversized cotton cardigan and a pair of old jeans. She was staring out the window to the gloomy day beyond, her round hazel eyes focusing on nothing at all as she twirled strands of unkempt raven hair between her fingers. “Well I don't know where them boys is it, but they'd best be showin' their faces soon,” old man Jarosz croaked. Each syllable a strain, each word a challenge. “Farm ain't gonna keep itself, an' I don't wanna have t'bring in outside help again.” “It's not like them to just disappear,” Kayla responded, only half-interested. Her mind was elsewhere. “No, no it ain't. Can't just be pickin' an' choosin' days-off in this business – gotta be out there graftin' ever day, else you're gonna fall beyond and you ain't gonna recover b’efore the season’s end.” Kayla had heard the words so many times that she was completely desensitized to them. The ins-and-outs of a farmer's menial tasks interested her in much the same way as an air-conditioning unit would interest an Eskimo. So she didn't respond; just kept looking out that window. “An' you, young lady,” Glenn's voice rose as he addressed his daughter. “You need to pick your head up, start thinkin' forward again. I ain't havin' no more mopin' in my house, ya hear?” She looked over towards him. Olive skin drooped from his jawline and his balding forehead was rife with liver spots and scalp grease. It pained her to see her father age like this, but that's how it goes when you dedicate your life to a career so demanding. “I know,” she replied. “I'm trying.” “Well quit tryin' and start doin'. I know it ain't easy on ya; ya lost yer child, then ya lost that lilly-livered slimeball you thought was gonna marry ya, but things are gonna come back 'round. Things'll improve, you just gotta let 'em.” She had to look down again. Oh, you poor old fool, she thought. If only you knew. Suddenly she noticed him struggle to lift his frail body from the chair. Gripping tightly to the chair's arms, Glenn barely managed to steady himself on wobbly legs as she rushed across the room to aid him. “No, Dad. Sit down.” “I'm fine, I'm fine,” he sat, waving her away as he reached for his walking stick. “This ol' body 'a mine ain't a total write-off yet, ya know.” He smiled as he brought the cane forward with both hands, pressing his entire body weight down on it. “You ain't eaten right in days, Kay,” he said. “Let's fix you a little somethin’.” Glenn began a slow, painful cane-aided plod towards the kitchen. Kayla grimaced. “It's okay, I can do it. You don't have to--” “Ahhh Shaddap an' let yer old pop do somethin' for ya, huh?” She smiled. * * * * * “Fuck, Wesley!” Roland screamed as he thrust his foot against the truck's floor. “Fuck'd do you have do that for?!” “I didn't do anything!” “You brought a fucking knife?! The hell were you thinking?” “I. I--” “Oh shut the fuck up. Jesus.” Roland tightened his white-knuckle grip on the steering wheel and bashed his head against the headrest. He pulled the white pick-up onto a quiet side street and quickly checked his mirrors. “We lost ‘em yet?” Wesley turned around, his movements unhindered by a seatbelt he'd chosen not to buckle. His brother didn't answer him. “Roland.” “Yes, Wesley,” Roland growled through gritted teeth. “They're gone.” The sweating Wesley had a moment's respite. He hadn't thought for a second that the security team would pad him down, and his heart almost hit the floor when they found the silver flip-knife in his pocket. Still, they'd gotten away. They'd piled through the crowd and hopped into their beaten-up old Ford and were long gone before security even had a chance to jump in their van. The Jarosz brothers had too great a head start to be caught, although the police would surely be wise to their stunt by now. “You ain't ever done a dumber thing in all yer life, y'know that?” said Roland, shaking his head and struggling to keep his attention on the road. “Shit, I wasn't gonna use it! Just brought it to scare the fu--” “So what, you were just gonna pull it out in front 'a all those people an' hope nobody noticed?” “I dunno, man. I dunno. I just... brought it.” “Good work, genius.” The elder Jarosz brother exhaled heavily and turned back onto a main road. Traffic was thin around town today, making their quick getaway that little bit easier. But now they were stumped. Their one chance to catch Andy Murray in public had been blown out of the water; now they had no idea where the man who'd crushed their sister lay, and even less an idea of how to get to him. Wesley slumped down in his seat, perspiring from every pore on his body. He raised his forearm to his brow and wiped away some of the sweat as the dreadful through that he may have just have done the dumbest thing ever crept inside his head. “Prick ain't scheduled for any more public appearances 'til after the show, ya know.” “Shit,” Wesley said, pulling himself up. “Hell we gon' do now?” “I don't kn--” Roland stopped himself. Shit, he thought. A silver Mercedes pulled out in front of him, and he recognised the number plate immediately. Roland couldn't believe his luck. “We gon' follow that birdie.” * * * * * “C'mon then, what are ya havin'?” Glenn Jarosz said as he finished the monumental task of grabbing a pan from the cupboard and placing it on the stove. “Ain't no better way to start the day than a big plate 'a greasy meat, if you ask me.” “Whatever's there, Dad. Anything at all.” Barefoot, she padded onto the kitchen tiles and pulled a seat out from the dining table. Holding onto the worktop for balance, her father made his way towards the refrigerator at a glacial pace. Kayla probably wouldn't be able to eat all that he'd cook for her, but the gesture had touched her, especially when she'd seen how much effort it was going to take him. “Come on, Dad. Let me help.” “Now what'd I tell ya?” wheezed Glenn as he knelt down and pulled a couple of packets from the fridge. “I ain't an invalid yet, Kay.” She chuckled. “No, of course not.” That was her father through and through – as tough as old boots, and still too proud to admit that even the simple task of cooking breakfast for his daughter was a bridge too far. Though he was a Christian, he still wouldn't accept help from others unless he deemed it absolutely necessary. Kayla walked across the kitchen and poured herself a glass of half-cold tap water before returning to the table. She sat down and took a drink as Glenn poured vegetable oil into the frying pan and waited for it to heat up. “I meant what I was sayin', Kay,” he said. “You gotta start lookin' up if you don't wanna wind-up in real trouble.” Hands clasped around her glass, she was listening – just didn't want her father to trudge up that subject again. It had been the source of all her recent problems and it was still raked her knowing that she couldn't tell her Father what had happened to the baby, but maybe it'd do her some good to get it out in the open. Confront it head-on. “I ain't tryin' 'a send you int' a panic or nothin', but if ya let stuff like that just stew away inside then ya gonna need to get yerself some help,” Glenn huffed, “and I'll be damned if any offspring of mine's gettin' help from some shrink.” “I won't let it go that far. You're right.” “I know ya won't Kay, but damn; you've barely said a word these past few weeks, and I can't remember the last time I heard ya laugh or saw a smile on yer face.” Now she was beginning to feel awkward. She rubbed her feet together beneath the table as she waited for him to stop talking. Such pep-talks were nothing new, coming from Glenn – but that didn't make them any easier to withstand. “Yer still in yer twenties, still got a lot 'a years ahead 'a ya. Ain't no use wastin' them like this, ya hear?” “Yeah,” she responded unconvincingly. Glenn tossed two strips of bacon into the hot pan, and the oil responded with a satisfying sizzle. “He's just a guy, an' one that wasn't good enough to call you his wife. Remember that.” Kayla swallowed another mouthful of water. Oh, how wrong he was. Andy Murray was more than good enough to be her husband. He was a man without a malicious bone in his body; a kind, caring soul who's wit and charm she'd fallen head over heels for, but now he'd been torn from her life never to return. She thought she was justified in doing what she did, but she saw then, as she sat alone in her father's house without a clue where her life was going, that she was wrong. So her brother had passed away and Andy hadn't been there when he should have. He was in the wrong; he should've cancelled that Just tour to help her through those dark hours, but he didn't. He left her alone to cope without a shoulder to cry on, and it tore her to shreds. But what he'd done could not justify her ultimate sin, no. Kayla knew it was a mistake from the very moment the deed was done, but by then it was too late. Far too late, and when she told him he'd reacted with an anger she'd never seen before. Apoplectic with rage, Andy Murray kicked her to the curb and said “goodbye” one last time. He took their two-year relationship and tore it to pieces in just a few seconds. If only she'd told him she was pregnant. How different things might've been... “It's sickening, if ya ask me. How can he just keep struttin' around on television like nothin's happened, while you're goin' through all this, huh?” Kayla had fallen into a daze, but her father's voice awakened her. “Lookin' at that prick's smug mug you'd never think he'd lost a baby...” Guilt was creeping up on her now. She gingerly raised the glass to her lips and said: “yeah.” “That's somethin' two people shouldn't have t'go through t'gether, let alone one on their own. You have a miscarriage an' he leaves you?! That's a man without morals. That's a shameful, revolting individual. You're better off without him.” Glenn shook his head as he turned the bacon and added a couple of sausages to the deathly breakfast. Kayla's mind was fluttering wildly. Miscarriage. There was no miscarriage. Oh, God. How was she ever going to tell him? She felt tears beginning to build-up in the corners of her eyes and she tried to shut everything out, but her father would not change the subject. “I ain't gonna lie; I ain't ever been in a position like that, so I can only guess how rotten yer feelin', but ya just gotta remind yerself that you've done nothin' wrong here, Kay. Nothin' wrong at all. You lost that baby an' Andy wound-up showin' his true colours while you sat an' suffered. Sometimes the good guys don't finish first – sometimes the innocent get walked all over, an' that's exactly what's happen--” He stopped himself when he heard his daughter sobbing into her sleeves. Turning his head with some strain, Glenn saw his daughter at the table, wiping salty tears from her eyes. “Hey. Hey, now.” Slowly – oh-so slowly - he shuffled towards the table and plopped down on the chair opposite. “Come on, now. I don't wanna hear no more of these tears from you.” He pushed his hands across the table, trying to link fingers with her petite digits. Much to Glenn's surprise, Kayla pulled her hands away. Looked at him. “Dad...” she began, her voice wavering. Trembling. Now was as good a time as any. “Dad, there's something I have to tell you.” “What, darlin'?” he quizzed. “Anything. C'mon.” And she tried to find the courage. This man had been a devout devotee of the Christian faith all his life – he wasn't going to take this one well. But she had to tell him, there was no way around it. It had been eating at her for far too long. He had to know. “Dad, my baby,” she began, but her voice broke up through sobs. “Yeah?” “...my baby...” “Spit it out, Kay.” “My baby didn't die of natural causes.” A sudden, sharp pain coursed through Glenn's body, and then nothing. Just numb. Everything, completely numb. Kayla saw the look on his face and recoiled immediately. “Dad, I'm sorry. I'm so sorry--” she began. “You had an... no, no. That ain't right. Tell me you didn't...” She couldn't manage another “I'm sorry”. “My own daughter. My own flesh an’ blood went an’...” He bowed his head for a moment but soon looked up. The disappointment in his eyes was more than she could take, and she was out of the room quicker than a flash. Glenn couldn't finish his sentence. * * * * * The GCW World Champion leaned back in his plump leather chair, a flick of the ignition killing the engine's gentle purr and Bruce Springsteen's blue-collar drawl. He looked through the tinted windows towards the house he'd rented for the week and cracked a half-smile; the signing session may have ended in farce, but at least now he could spend the rest of the day in peace. Although he would never admit it, Chris Bagwell would prove a formidable challenger. He may have lacked some of LeStatt Knight’s cunning, but the man was on his final tether. He’d fallen short by Andy’s hand so many times before that there was no way he wasn’t going to come out of the starting blocks like a raging bullock. Bagwell was hungry; he’d waited patiently while Andy had ascended beyond him, but now he had his chance, but the worst of it was he had nothing to lose. Another loss to The Scottish King of Cool was hardly going to derail Chris Bagwell’s career – he’d still be able to bullshit his way into another pay-per-view main event – but a loss to his old foe could prove catastrophic to the Admiral of Awesome. Chris Bagwell was a legitimate threat; there was no denying it. Andy would need ever last second of preparation to overcome him again. Andy pulled his massive frame from the vehicle, grabbing his grey holdall and GCW title from the passenger's seat and stepping out into the early afternoon. The wind was strong and brisk, just barely making the sun's unrelenting rays bearable for his pale, Scottish skin. It was nice to have something that reminded him of home. With his belt on his shoulder and his bag in his hand, Andy walked up to the house's front door and slid-in the key. It was a decent little place in a quiet neighbourhood. Two bedrooms, a reasonable living area, and a well-manicured lawn outside. Not the kind of place a man of his wealth and stature would ever choose to live in, but one that was more than apt for a few days' stopover. He pushed the door open and immediately threw his back onto the carpet as the smell of pot pourri drifted up his nostrils. How pleasant, he thought, taking his first step into the air-conditioned confines when the sound of a hoarse engine grumbled close behind him. Too close. Andy quickly turned around and saw the old truck pull up beside his own car. The vehicle with it's rusty hubcaps and flaking paint wasn't instantly recognisable, but the man that first stepped out certainly was. Roland Jarosz threw open the the door and strode towards him like a drill sergeant, his little brother following close behind. “Jesus, Roly. Where the hell did you come from?” The King smiled, trying his best to be affable in what would surely become an awkward social situation, but he could tell from the look on Roland's face that he was not here for niceties. As Wesley violently slammed the pickup's passenger door, Roland walked up the doorstep with a look of pure indignation on his rugged features. “You sonuvabitch you...” barked the younger brother in that shrill voice of his, but Andy could find no time for a verbal retort as Roland's calloused paw forced him inside. Wesley closed the door as Andy found himself being backed into his living room at the behest of two clearly aggrieved Nebraskan farmhands. “You guys mind telling me what's going on here?” Murray said, tightening his facial muscles into a scowl as he dropped the GCW World Heavyweight Championship onto the flower-patterned couch. “Sit down,” said Roland. The command took Andy by surprise. “What?” “I said sit down!” Roland repeated at a higher decibel. Never one for a confrontation out of nothing, Andy raised his hands and slowly lowered himself onto a sofa that was far more comfortable than he could appreciate. Above him, the two men who would've been his brothers-in-law loomed like vultures. The Scot was perplexed – utterly bamboozled. He knew that Kayla's brothers may have had an axe to grind after what had happened, but they were logical men. Surely they would've realised that she'd done him wrong, and not the other way around. “Need to have words, boy. You an' me.” Said Roland, running a tensed hand through his short locks. “Words that couldn't have been exchanged over a telephone line?” “Shut up!” Wesley barked. “Quit tryin' t'be smart, you!” Murray tried his best to soften his heart's pounding rhythm, but the incident had caught him off-guard. The last thing he'd expected was for his ex-girlfriend's siblings to follow him to Northern Ohio and corner him in his temporary accommodation, but there he was. He was bigger than both of them – if he wanted to, he could probably have wiped both of them out with his size and muscle mass, but he knew neither man well enough to trust them not to do something stupid. So he sat. Controlled himself. Let some of that trademark cool take control. “What I gotta say needs t'be done man-to-man. Surely even a rat like you's gotta understand that...” Murray said nothing; only watched as Roland Jarosz clenched a fist and his restless brother buzzed beside him, seemingly willing to let Roland handle things. “What'chu did t'my sister? Ain't no man gettin' away with that, pro-wrestling or not.” “What I did to your sister?” Andy shouted. Outraged, he was unable to help himself. “I loved your sister, Roland. I loved and her and she--” “She did a terrible thing, Andy. Ain't no doubt about it; she done committed the worst sin there is when she took that kid's life outta her womb, but she wouldn't 'a done a damn thing if it weren't for you.” Andy had heard the same thing from Kayla herself. “Don't give me this shit.” “'Shit'?!” Roland roared fiercely. “That all this means to you?! Fuckin' hell. You worthless sack 'a shit. You useless piece 'a crap! Girl's goin' through hell and you ain't even blinkin'.” “She's going through hell? I just lost a kid I didn't even know existed, for fuck's sake.” “Maybe you should'a thought about that before ya fuckin' abandoned her, then!” Wesley shrieked. His face was raw with red rage, and his bloodshot eyes were almost bursting from their sockets. Andy knew he'd have to be careful with this one. “You off gallivantin' roun' the country like Lord Muck's what did this! You did this. You turned my sister inta a fuckin' wreck, man!” “She turned herself into a wreck.” “No!” Wesley said, leaning forward with menace. “YOU did!” Sensing an escalation, Roland Jarosz leaned forward, putting himself between Wesley and Andy and forcing his younger brother to back-off a little. Andy took a moment to ease-up and adjust his posture. No easy way outta this one, he thought. Gonna have to ride it. “Alright, alright,” Andy said calmly. “What do you want from me?” Roland's response was immediate, as if it had been rehearsed a hundred times. “I want you to realise what ya've done t'this woman; I want you t'see the mess you've made of her, an' I want you to feel every little bit of pain she's gone through 'cause 'a you. People when they see you on the TV, they think you're a fuckin' saint, a hero, but you ain't. You a fuckin' abscess. You a ruiner.” Roland leaned in, planting a finger in Andy's chest. He was so close that Andy could smell the stale, dried sweat on his forehead. Murray felt his entire body go tense, not knowing what was going to happen next. Roland's yellow teeth were clenched together and he was snorting like a bull before a red cloth, and fuck knows what his brother was doing. Roland loosened his jaw, prepared himself another verbal barrage, when a quiet, unassuming creek from across the room caught all their attention. Eleven year old Marvin Murray peeked a terrified head through the threshold and mumbled a single word through trembling lips. “Dad?” * * * * * Those minutes spend falling to pieces in her humble bedroom had been murder. She'd told him; the weight was off her back, but something far worse had begun to take hold. She could’ve dealt with his anger; arguments and heated exchanges are a nuisance, yeah, but give it a little while and things usually blow over. But disappointment? That's a far trickier beast to tame. She was curled-up with her bag against the headboard, found herself in a position that had become familiar to her over the weeks that had passed. A shell of a woman; shattered and broken by what has come to pass, and terrified of whatever it is that awaits her next move. A mess; she’d removed the mirror from the opposite wall just so she wouldn’t have to look at herself anymore. Kayla had disappeared so far inside herself that she may never return, and it petrified her. Kayla heard him coming up the stairs long before he reached her room. It was hard not to; the old house's walls were so thin you could hear a mouse scurrying around on the other side of the building, and Glenn was still a heavy man though illness had taken much of his weight. His slow, lopsided steps creaked on the stairs' loose boards and reverberated around the empty hall ominously as he made his painful journey, and as she heard her father turn the doorknob, Kayla felt as if he was the reaper and he'd come to take her away. In a way, she wished he was. Glenn Jarosz in the doorway, his eyes puffy and red. Probably hadn't been crying, but a Christian man learning of his daughter's abortion was a terrible experience for all involved. “Kay,” he said softly. “Kayla, listen to me.” He took two further steps towards the bed. Kayla looked up to see him resting his weary body on his cane. “You're my daughter, an' I love you. There ain't a damn thing in the word anybody can do t'change that, but...” He had to stop himself. Eyes that hadn't seen tears in years were beginning to well-up. Glenn paused. Took a long breath. “But you broke my heart, Kay. You lied t'me 'bout what happened, an' ya disrespected everything I believe in – everything I stand for.” Kayla began to panic. She leaned across the bed and reached out to him, but Glenn swatted her hand away. “No. That's it. No more.” Took a couple of seconds to compose himself. “I want you outta here before the sun goes down. You ain't welcome under my roof no more.” * * * * * “What the fuck...” Wesley said, his voice dissipating completely as Andy Murray's young son came into the room. “Perfect! Absolutely perfect!” Roland barked as his plan fell apart in front of his own eyes. He turned and planted his size thirteen Dr. Marten into a nearby chair. If Andy was thinking fast, he'd have taken the opportunity to get up and subdue Roland Jarosz, but now that son had shown his face there was only one thing occupying his inner-cortex. Worry. Pure, simple worry. “Go back into the bedroom, Marv,” Andy pleaded, standing forward. “You sit back down!” Roland bawled at him. “What's going on, Dad?” “Just go back into the room, son. Let me sort this--” “I SAID 'SIT DOWN!'” Again, Andy raised his hands. He wasn't sitting down – no fucking way. There was too much at stake, now. “S'wrong, little man? Cant stand hearin' yer Daddy get a lickin'?” Wesley said, leaning towards the petrified boy. “You stay away from him!” Andy said, suddenly powering passed Roland and pushing the rat-like Wesley away from his son. “One finger on him and throw your head through that fucking wall!” “Take it easy, Wes!” Roland suddenly called. The last thing he wanted was for a child to get caught up in the mess, even if Marvin was Andy's son. Again Wesley backed away, wordless. His psyche shrouded by too much instability to concoct a verbal retort. Physically, he was a mess of shaggy hair and sweat, a man so distraught by his sister's self-destruction that he no longer knew any reason. Protective of his son, Andy stood in front of Marvin and balled his fists. Neither of these men would stoop so low as to turn on an eleven year old, but Murray wasn't willing to take the risk as he carefully watched a frustrated Roland pace back towards his sibling. “Get the hell outta here,” Andy growled. He'd had enough – now he was ready for war. “Oh we ain't goin' nowhere,” Roland responded. “Not 'til you realise it. Not 'til you feel it!” “W-what they talking about, Dad?” Andy felt a tiny hand clasp round his wirst. “'Bout how your old man's been tryin'a drive my sister t'death, that's what!” Wes snapped. “I ain't the one that checked into the abortion clinic,” Andy replied. “I ain't talkin' 'bout that! Forget about that,” Wesley said, swiping away the clumps of sodden hair that had matted to his head. “I'm talkin' 'bout the scars on her arms. Obviously you ain't seen 'em.” Andy's face contorted with confusion. “The... what?” It caused Wesley's tearducts to swell just thinking about the thin read marks he'd seen on his sister's wrists, so he bit his lips together. Nodded. Saw a little part of Andy Murray's lifeforce die. “Sh-she took a razor to herself.” He sniffled, doing well to stifle his emotions. “And all because you were too concerned with bein' Mr. Wrestling Superstar to care 'bout your fuckin' wife-to-be,” Roland said. Andy felt his heart shudder. He felt like collapsing. Finding out about the child was bad enough but this... this?! “Oh God. Oh my God,” Andy said, eyes wide with disbelief. This is why they'd came for him. This is what they were here for. He barely had time to think. Without warning, Wesley reached into his pocket and thrust a shiny, silver blade dangerously close to Andy's gut. “YOU!” he shrieked. “YOU U--” “Holy shit, Wes!” Roland saw the knife and instinct took over. He grabbed Wesley by the arm and cracked his knee against his fist, breaking the grip and sending the knife tumbling to the floor. Things had spiralled out of control recklessly – his brother had lost all self-control. He was manic. Feral. Wesley looked at Roland, his hand still in his elder brother's grip, and for a moment they were completely still. Until Wesley saw the blade still within reach. He leaned forward, pushed his weight against his brother and reached out of the knife's handle, when a deafening wooden CRASH broke through the air and four or five calls of “FREEZE!” filled the air. Wes turned his head. Saw a stream of armed cops burst through the door. Panicked. As his brother backed off, Wesley, in one final act of desperation, balled a fist and swung it. His knuckles collided with Andy Murray's jaw. It was the last thing Andy felt before the blue-clad officers poured into the house and kicked both brothers to the floor. * * * * * She slung a hastily stuffed bag over her shoulder as she stepped onto the porch with feet that felt like they were made of lead. There would be no more tears; she was done crying, and she was sure her father would to. Closed the door and walked out onto the driveway. She hadn't had a chance to say goodbye to her father, but she didn't need one. Kayla knew that she'd let him down in the worst possible way, and this was her penance. She deserved this entirely, so with a tattered heart and a damaged psyche she accepted her fate. Stopped on the gravel but didn't look back for a second. Town looked small from here; the population was only a couple hundred, but all the buildings were stretched out two-deep across a single stretch of road. Small town; a million miles from the glitz and glamour she'd become accustomed to as the GCW World Champion's life partner. Kayla trudged onto the road and began her journey with no idea where she was going or what was going to happen. She'd been cast adrift, and adrift she would stay until something came to sweep her from the highway. She passed the town church and realised the nearest motel was five miles out of town. Hell of a distance to walk when you're on your own and down on your luck. She sighed. If only she'd told Andy she was pregnant. If only. * * * * * “Are you sure you're okay, sir?” the moustache-wearing police officer asked as he examined Andy's jawline. “That guy hit you pretty hard. Absolutely positive you don't want me to get someone to take a look at it?” “I'm fine, honestly,” Andy said reassuringly. “I get beaten up for a living, remember?” With a nod the officer disappeared back into the flock of lawmen that had descended upon Andy's rented home. Soon the Scot would need to join them and give his statement, but that was the last thing on Andy Murray's mind at that point. Across the room his son was sitting on the couch, shivering like a wet dog. He walked towards Marvin. Sat down and put an arm around him, but he couldn't stop the shaking. “It's alright, son. They're all gone now. We're okay.” In one long, slow movement, Marvin Murray creaked his head round to face his father. “No, we're not,” he said. “We're not okay.” And in that moment, as Andy Murray pulled his son's head towards his shoulder, he felt a tiny candle being snuffed out behind his heart. This wasn’t how it was supposed to be. They should’ve been married by the end of the year, then they might’ve thought about starting a family. He’d come back from those three-day stints on the road to a warm, welcoming household. He’d retire in a couple of years and spend the rest of his days surrounded by friends and family, without a car in the world. Yet now the man who was supposed to have it all realised he’d never felt more alone. It washed over him; an epiphany. For the time he saw the terrible mess that had been his construction. Two men had followed him around the country for the sole purpose of inflicting harm upon him, Kayla had apparently taken to harming herself because of the grief that he'd inadvertently put her through, and now his own son had been put through an ordeal that would surely haunt him for the rest of his days. His personal life had threatened to go up in flames for quite some time, but it was only now that he realised it. Before he could not see the wood for the trees, but now all he can make out are the fragments of collapsed life that may never be repaired. And for what? A shiny strap of gold and leather and a couple of nights every month in front of Steven Caldera’s television cameras. Andy drew a trembling lungful of air into his body and knew then that this mess was far great than anything he and Jane had endured. He was not the King of Cool; he was the King of Nothing, and long would his terrible reign be.
