Retaking the Throne and Subtly Becoming Something: Part III

11 December 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: December 11, 2009 It was a school gym - a small one at that. Walls had hang flags for the trophies they had won in their sixty years of existence. There weren't many of them though. Basketball nets touched the ceiling leaving room for the circle of chairs surrounding the school's Knight. Still, the chairs didn't have occupants yet. Instead, they gathered around a table; it was a refreshment table. Coffee was their savior; Jay could read it in their eyes. Something about hopelessness was the common thread between the individuals. Men and women, they all silently sang the same hymn behind their blue and brown eyes. In the pit of his stomach he thought he felt bad for the lot of people. They conversed like dead batteries passing the time with stories of their dogs and of their children. Most of them had children. The coffee fueled the drivel making their voices echo in his mind as he tried to keep them out. Maggie leaned on him with the weight of her world. It felt like more than just her world. "I don't know about this. It doesn't seem," she paused searching. "Right I guess." "It's not right," he agreed. "But we don't have a choice." "And why not?" "We can't always have choices. Sometimes someone else gets to play god." He didn't know what he was talking about but in his head he thought it would help Maggie. It would be her first interaction with others in a near-month and Jay could feel the uneasiness radiating through his shoulder. "We can leave still. I think I need a cigarette." "You don't smoke." "I want to try." He was unsure, "Let's not Mags. It's not worth it." She was insistent though. "Please Jay. Please." They slinked outside like a pair of fugitives. People noticed, sure, but no one knew who they were. They were new. Jay lit her cigarette and passed it off nearly losing it in the whipping wind. There was snow on the sidewalk and Maggie crushed a block under her boot as she took her first hit. Jay lit his own. Coughing, "How do I do it?" "Takes time. Give it time." She gave it time. The first one lit longer than Jay's due to her small intakes. Her cough slowly faded though and was replaced with a fresh outlook on everything. The nicotine was doing its magic and she swayed with the wind as if she were a part of it. She pretended she could be lifted in a draft. She wanted to be picked off the sidewalk and carried into the sky like dandelion seeds. Where she went she didn't care. Anywhere was better than the high school. She swayed still but she wasn't picked up. Maggie finished her cigarette. "I'll have another." "Later. They started already." "One more. Please." "One more." He didn't have the same sway she did. He was grounded, a brick even. Instead of taking off he took Mother Nature's punches one after the other swallowing heavy doses of tar. The tar healed the wind wounds and he watched Maggie smoke. It was an awkward sight, like a person newly learning how to put their contacts in. She held it funny. She smoked it short. Onlookers probably would mistake them for passing a stick of weed and Jay would be upset that it wasn't. Not that he wanted it but rather that it would be something calmer. It'd be something to cool his nerves and slow his heart when they walked back inside. The cigarette did enough though and after she finished her second he tossed his third to the pavement. They reentered. "Come on in, we have seats," came a calm voice. It belonged to a woman and she was standing on the Knight's head as the circle's guide. Her hands carried Jay and Maggie toward the group until each found a seat among the others. Wandering eyes couldn't help but examine the couple taking mental snapshots in short doses. "We just started." Jay nodded and so did Maggie. They didn't want to interact. They wanted to listen. "Horace, would you like to get us started today?" Horace wasn't old; he was about forty-two by estimate. The stubble on his face looked like a field of ants with flecks of food carried between a few of them. His hair was short and he wore dress slacks with an oversized tee-shirt. As he stood he stole all of the confidence from the world. Horace was a proud man, one that had obviously shown legitimate progress. "Hi everyone, I'm Horace Greenwood." They all knew who he was and as if they shared his confidence, they all smiled. Jay and Maggie simply watched. "I wanted to start with a story tonight. One about Horace Greenwood eleven years ago." The leader of the group took a seat beside Jay and smiled at him. He smiled back breathing Marlboro on her black long-sleeve. "You see, eleven years ago me and my wife lived in a small house in Quakertown. It was a nice place, a little dirty maybe, but it was comfortable living. Maylene, my wife, was at work because well - she worked all the time. Maylene was a nurse. I was a mechanic. Back then, I didn't love cars as much as I do now. Then it was a job to repair them. It was something to pass the time and get a paycheck. That's not why I'm telling this story though." He spoke with esteem in his throat and courage leaking from his pores. His chin was straight and high in a dignified manner. "There had been a point over the weekend where I was home for hours. Maybe twelve hours. She was working at the hospital on a double shift and we were closed on the weekends. I passed the time on Sunday at the bar down the road." Maggie leaned on Jay again. She wrapped her fingers around his bicep and rubbed her scalp into his arm. "I shouldn't have driven on that Sunday but I did drive. I drove straight through the front doors of the hospital taking down seven patients. When I woke up I was looking into Maylene's eyes and I didn't know where I was. The lights were brighter than the bar or even our living room. She didn't look happy either. It took time but I found out exactly where I was. She told me what I had done. She told me that all seven of the people I had hit with my Chevy were injured further and one was in critical condition." Horace shed a tear and wiped it away to maintain the man he had created. He was larger than life hovering above his chair and the circle of people showed it with tears of their own. "That person did not survive the night." Maggie clinched tighter and Jay could feel the moisture dripping from her eyes. His shirt mopped it up. "Maylene left me after I was released from the hospital and the judge decided that I should be locked up for no less than ten years. The family wanted me dead and I'll be honest, I didn't blame them. While I was in jail, I learned things. I took the time to reflect, to read, to write, to observe. I was a caged bird who didn't speak unless asked to. I missed Maylene more than I thought I could miss another person and I still do miss her. I wonder if her new husband is what's best and I hope that her son grows up better than I was." Jay put his arm around Maggie for comfort. She was easily stirred. "I wanted to tell you all this story because I've been sober for eleven years now. I know most of you and I know your stories. I've listened and I understand that you've all come from hard times. I want you to know that there is hope though. I may have had to find my way in a cell but you have an entire world to roam in. You're free to go where you please and make your own decisions. Take control of your life and remain in control because you never know what will happen if you have to rely on that as your way out. Look to us, look to your family, look to anyone. People will lead you." The woman leading the group stood and gave Horace a hug before he sat down. His neighbors shook his hands and he smiled happily. "Thank you Horace, you hit the nail on the head. You may think that you have no one sometimes but always remember that you have all of us here. And now, it seems that you have two new family members." She pointed at Jay and Maggie. Mags lifted her head off of his shoulder and they each looked around the ring. "Would you like to introduce yourselves to the group?" Jay knew he had no say in the matter and looked at Maggie. She was already opening her mouth. "I'm Maggie McVay." "Hi Maggie," answered the woman. The entire situation took the anonymous out of the equation and Jay crossed his arms showing his slight defiance. "How about you tell us a little about yourself and why you're here with us today?" "Well," she started. It seemed she had Horace's confidence and Jay checked the floor to be sure it hadn't been tampered with. It hadn't. "My husband died in a car accident a little over a month ago. I miss him. I drink because I miss him." She fell onto Jay's shoulder again using it as a tissue. She blew her nose into his sweatshirt. "I'm sorry." They sympathized with her. All of them shared her emotions and wore them on their sleeves crying with her. The entire circle flooded the floor. The entire circle, that is, but Jay Terror. He helped her and whispered in her ear. She slowly was able to turn off her tear ducts. The woman in charge broke the silence. "Well Maggie, you've come to the right place. I'm sorry to hear about your husband." She looked at Jay. "Would you like to introduce yourself while she finds herself?" He didn't want to. If he could he'd take Maggie under the knees and throw her onto his shoulder so they could walk away together. They didn't need this. Not these people. "I'm Jay Talley. I don't do much with my life really. I drink because I can. Nothing really else about me." "Because you can?" "That's right. Gets me through things and I like it. I'm not an alcoholic though. Nah." The ring chuckled and their leader went on. "Well hopefully we can help you realize what you're doing in your time here then, yeah?" "What I'm doing?" "Your habit. We'll teach you about it and help you through it. It's been done and can be done again." "Uh huh." He put his arm around Maggie again rubbing her back lightly. More than ever he wanted to leave. The three cigarettes hadn't been enough. Maggie placed her lips on his ear and whispered lightly. "I need a cigarette." Jay nodded. He wasn't proud of the addiction he was feeding but in essence, he thought it would end her other. The trade was worth the consequences in the long run and he was ready to accept responsibility for his actions. A woman stood up now, she was young and thin. Her style was fashionable. If Jay didn't know better he'd guess she was the distraction paid to play off all generations. She'd be the leader's sympathy angle so she could begin her 'it can happen to anyone' game. The thin girl even had her hair whipped haphazardly to play deeper into the character. "My name is Jordan Ashley," she then turned to Jay and Maggie. "Jord-Ash for all intents and purpose. I've been here for only two weeks but I feel like I've already felt a change happening. Like you Jay, I drank because I could. I drank because I thought I had to." Her voice was hoarse and made Jay rethink the original synopsis. "I've learned that I don't have to though. I can swim, and dance, and sing, and eat, and be happy in so many ways other than drinking. It's no longer a matter of drinking myself into happiness, I've been sober and I've been happy. This group can help you Jay." He didn't like how he'd become the center of attention when Maggie was the reason for the meeting. "I believe you," he lied. "Thank you Jord-Ash," said the leader. Even she had been manipulated into using the nickname. "Remember Maggie and Jay, here we are family. We're the people you can always count on no matter what. If everyone agrees, I say we take a quick break and talk to each other about things going on this week." It was unanimous and everyone stood from the circle and marched back to the coffee table. Someone had refilled the rocket ship full of java. Jay and Maggie made a break for the door but their identities had been revealed. They were no longer fugitives on the run. Jord-Ash approached Jay with her maroon scarf and tall black boots. Maggie was taken aside by the leader. "Hi Jay." He acknowledged her but tried to look her off, "Hey Jordan." "Jord-Ash actually. It's Jord-Ash." "Right. Jord-Ash. Hi Jord-Ash." "Hi Jay. I just wanted to see you. We're really alike you know. I mean, really alike. This place helps though. It really does. The stories the people like Horace have they're -" "They're lifting," he was getting good at playing emotions. "Exactly. I'm glad you think so. What do you really do?" "Huh?" "You said you do nothing. What do you do?" "Nothing really. I work for a guy and help him out." "How so?" "I just do that. It's my job." She continued to pry with her petite hands. "It's just a weird job I guess. Maybe you need to find a new job to help with the problem too. I know I had to." "What did you do?" "Bartender." "I like my job. It's a good job. So is a bartender though. I'd do that if I wasn't doing this I think." "I'm back at school now. I'm learning to become a dentist. I'd love to be a dentist." Maggie had broken away from the leader and took a stance at the side of Terror. She smiled at Jordan Ashley with false teeth - a trick learned from Jay himself. "I'm sorry to hear about your husband Maggie. That must be very hard. I was just talking to Jay about how this group will help everything." "Yep. That's what Martha said as well. Just what I'm looking for." "Good." "Let's all circle again. We have more to do." They did have more to do. The leader, labeled as Martha, went on about herself and how she had become the leader. She was a proud mother and spoke of her kids. She said they were the reason for her sobriety. They believed in her. Jay didn't know who believed in him and Maggie. Most of the days he couldn't even believe in Maggie. Did Steven Caldera believe in them? More people spoke about their stories. There was more death, more tears, more smiles, more dogs, more kids, more parents, and more uplifting feelings. In the end, Jay and Maggie were free to leave and they did. They escaped Martha, Jordan Ashley, and even Horace Greenwood. Cigarettes became salvation as they took Terror's limousine. When asked where to go, Jay told him to go three blocks. There was a pub there. They entered and sat at the bar without a thought behind it. Two beers. "You think we should go back?" "I don't know." "Same. Jord-Ash creeped me out. She's too much in one swallow I guess." "You didn't have Martha hugging you and telling you things would be ok. I thought she'd bring Steve back for a second." "That Horace though, man could he command the floor." "He could. Yeah." They finished the two beers and then each had another two. Three more followed that and by six o'clock they called it quits. The limousine came back and they returned to the hotel. The room was a mess from the night before when Maggie shattered a champagne bottle against the television set. They ignored the glass and Jay took to the bathroom. Maggie found the bed. "You think O'Neil would ever be caught dead at a place like that?" "Bryan or Jason?" "Either I guess." "No, neither. You think Jason will be back this week? He did a number on AXEL last week. I mean, you laid it into him but that was pretty nerve-racking." Jay turned the bathroom light off and walked to the bed. "Who knows. He's trying to keep me healthy he said. Something about me making excuses. As if." "Digital Mortality and Tessa aren't exactly going to be easy on you. Damn sure Mayhem will be the same. I don't like your chances in this one." Jay grinned from ear to ear. "Then Maybe it'd be better if Jason came in. Again, who knows. I have footsteps to follow Mags and if they lead me through a mine field then I just got to pay attention to where I step. You know?" "Makes sense." "I'm gonna' smoke. You coming?" Maggie shook him off and pulled the comforter of the bed to her chin. "Not anymore. I'll be ok." "Right." He went through the glass sliding door and looked over the city of Philadelphia. He wanted cinnamon bun air again figuring it'd drown out Jordan Ashley's voice. Terror found his phone and flipped it open. He dialed. "Koz, mind if we meet soon? In a few days maybe?" He paused and smoked. "A week it is then. See you there." The wind cut his chain smoking short and he returned inside with Maggie. He took a spot under the comforter with her and pretended the TV was on. He pretended he was winning and pretended he did it without Jason O'Neil. With all the pretending, it was a miracle that he was who he was and he did what he did.
