Date: 04/15/11 His Eyes We were sitting under an oak tree on a summer day with cigarettes resting warily on loose lips. The field was in full bloom and we watched the bees buzz from daffodils to daisies. On top of the hill, you could see miles worth of growth. It was uncharted land, the last of its kind, and the perfect place to take in the warming hands of a high sun. We had sun glasses on and there was a bandana print blanket under our bottoms. She brought a full basket of food that she insisted she had created, but I knew better. I’d taken hundreds of fresh cookies from her mother’s window sill and her sprinkling of lemon zest was her trademark. “You know you shouldn’t eat desert before bed,” she told me. She was always telling me things though. I talked to a lot of people, but she talked more than most of them combined. She knew the names of weird bugs, all the colors of the rainbow, and how far away we were from Mars. I figured she was born that way. One day I looked around her house and looked for books that would explain all the facts. There was no such book though. “It’s just one cookie. You make the best cookies,” I teased. “My momma makes the best cookies. One day I’ll make the best though.” “Well you should tell your momma that there’s a new sheriff in town.” She laughed and I laughed too. Her body moved up and down when she laughed and her face scrunched up like a pug puppy. I tried to make her laugh often just to see that face. “Do you want bologna or ham?” I shrugged and said, “you pick.” “I asked you first.” “And I asked you second.” “It doesn’t work that way, James.” “It works however we want it to,” I protested. She handed me a sandwich and balled her fist. When she shook the thing, I laughed a little and shook one back. We wrestled sometimes. On the hill, we did just about everything you could do with two people. Sometimes we pretended to be an avalanche in Alaska and we’d roll until we hit each other on the bottom. Another time we pretended that the hill was our ship and the grass was the sea. I’d grab the tree branch like it was a sail and guide us to a hidden treasure far away. No matter what we were doing though, we always made sure we left the hill with a happy ending. It was a sacred place, one where we could come and forget about our chores and forget about school. Leaving on a happy thought kept it special and sealed the magic within the tall oak tree. I grabbed the sandwich from the blanket and unwrapped it. “What kind did you give me?” “You’ll just have to wait and see.” I bit into the bread and first tasted the cheese. It was plain American with a mayonnaise smear on top. Chewing brought out the salty taste of the ham. “You make these too?” “I made everything.” I watched her open her sandwich and bite into the bologna. She seemed happy by the way she shut her eyes when she chewed. There was ecstasy behind shut eyelids, and she had no trouble spelling it out. “Well next time you should make two bologna sandwiches.” “Oh, shush.” “The ham is,” I started. “Good,” she interjected. “I was going to say fantastic.” “I’m sure you were,” she said after another bite. I shook my head and set the sandwich back onto the blanket. I leaned onto my back and placed my hands under my head like a pillow. The tree was all encompassing. It had been growing for years before we were both born and had a full head of leaves. Squirrels usually put on shows in the branches and I looked around for one. Instead, I spotted a bird’s nest cradled in the armpit of two neighboring branches. It was finely crafted and seemed to be heavy. I pointed at it. “Look up there. There’s a bird nest just a little up.” When I looked at Margeret, she didn’t seem to be paying attention. Her eyes were off in the distance. I sat up some to find a better view, and realized that it was impossible to tell what she saw. Something was kicking dirt into the air and creating a cloud barrier around it. It was moving slow, but it was heading directly toward us. Her Eyes For a man who had it all, Jay Terror sure didn’t act like it. There was always something on far in his horizon, even if I couldn’t see it. He preached that if he reached far enough, he could wrap his arm around it and hold it tight. Asking him what it was though wasn’t something that he had an easy answer for. Jay once described it as ultimate power. He said that if he could grab it and hold onto it, he would be able to do whatever he wanted whenever he wanted. The funny part was that he had already tied that thing down to the dock. Nonetheless, it drove him. It gave him reason to move on and I was in no place to stop him from becoming the man he believed he was. With his cigarette withdrawal symptoms flaring at full force though, it seemed that it was up to me to remind him of what he could be. Jay slept often after his title match. Andy Murray had worn him down to the point that he could hardly hold a conversation. Sometimes, he would go for a day and a half before eating and even then, he wasn’t getting the nutrition that a wrestler needed. Instead, he fed on his dreams. I sat by his bed often when he slept. The doctor had mentioned that the first week would be his most draining. He told me to stay by his side when I could in case he needed something. When it came down to it, I owed him more than simply sitting by his side. Jay turned often under the sheets. He was constantly bouncing from dream to dream and often shouted in frustration when he felt invaded. I was sure that he wouldn’t recall the things he said, and found it best to keep them to myself. The times that I did leave his side were justified. With the gym only a few blocks away, I found the time to check up on the management and condition of the building. Even after months away, it still had the same shine and same feeling that it did before Steven’s passing. His picture hung on the wall upon my request and seeing him smiling still gave me hope that Jay would soon do the same. Sometimes I’d simply drive. It was a lonely life for the first few days. Jay was never there to share his opinions of my television choices nor was he there to hold any form of conversation. He simply dig himself deep into the warmest part of the bed in order to escape into his fantasy world. On the drives, I’d explore the city. There were places that I had never seen nested in even the most overrun areas. Tourist traps that I avoid before became interesting suddenly. Most of all was the liberty bell. I drove there on two consecutive days to look at it. To be honest, I’m still not sure why. It sat there. It just sat there. Something about it felt so pathetic and forgotten after the sun went down though. When I returned on the second night, Jay was sitting up watching a reality TV show. Even I didn’t watch that one. “You’re awake,” I said happily. “Are you hungry?” “No. Thirsty though.” “You want water?” “That’ll do.” “Of course,” I replied. As I turned and started out to the kitchen of his Philadelphia condominium, he stopped me with his crusty voice. “Where’d you go?” “Just went downtown. Needed to get a breath of fresh air.” “I woke up and you weren’t here.” “I’m sorry. I’ll try not to do it again.” “No. It’s OK. It’s better that way.” “OK,” I replied. I turned again and placed a glass under the water dispenser. In the time it took to fill his glass, I still couldn’t decide how it was better that way. His Eyes The dust was getting high enough that the wind picked up and sent it sprinkling over our picnic. I tried to cover my sandwich, but ignored it after noticing the ants that had taken a liking to the ham. As the dust cloud moved closer, it was clear that a truck was in the center. It rolled up to the base of our tree and stopped just short of both me and Margeret. I looked at her to see if she was OK, and she was standing. A pair of boots stepped onto our grass and a man’s head soon followed. He was furry along the jaw line and wore a yellow construction helmet. “A little far from home, huh kids?” he asked with a winded breath. “We live just down the way. I’m sure you passed it,” Margeret said. “Brother and sister then?” “No. Just friends.” “Adorable,” he started. “I didn’t mean to interrupt anything here with the picnic and all, but I have some business to attend to. I’ll need you both to clear on out of here.” “What’s your business?” I asked. “The business is with this tree. You see, this tree is the biggest tree in all of the county. I’m fixin’ to build myself a house just off in the distance and I could use all of the wood that this tree has. I’ll need you two to clear away so I can cut her down.” “You can’t cut down the tree,” Margeret protested. “I have paperwork saying that I can.” “This tree’s magic,” she argued. The man huffed and let out a thunderous laugh. “M’am, I don’t want to have to get your daddy involved on the account that you’re trying to save a magic tree.” “It is magic,” I said. “Its roots suck it from the ground and that’s why the tree’s so big.” “The bigger they are,” he said. “There are babies in the tree,” Margeret replied. “Babies? This tree is magic and it reproduces? How haven’t we taken this tree and tested it yet, huh?” “Baby birds,” I spoke up. “There’s a nest full of them and you’ll kill them if you cut down this tree.” “Tell you what. How about I take the birds and give them a nice place at my new home. They can make a nest on my roof when I’m done building if they miss the tree so much.” Margeret walked backwards until her arms felt the tree. She leaned against it and hugged it backwards. “I’m not going to let you take this tree.” I stood finally and joined her side. We looked up to the tall man and tried to make ourselves look tougher than we were. “This is our tree.” “Well we’ll see about that,” he said. He didn’t think twice about loading back into his truck and backing it down the hill. He turned around gracefully and recreated the dust tornado that had carried his car to us. As he drove away, we wondered what he had in mind. I was sure that he was going to get her dad or even both of our dads, and for that reason we thought quickly. “Let’s climb it,” Margeret suggested. “I’ll go first and pull you up.” I took the lead as I usually tried to and placed my boot deep into the bark of the oak tree. I found a branch and shoved my weight into the trunk to grab it around the bicep. We climbed the tree before, but that was to avoid a zombie attack. This time, I felt a true rush and was able to pull myself up into the leaves effortlessly. Margeret followed and lost her footing at first, but I was determined to bring her up. She didn’t weight much and I placed my foot under a branch to hoist her up properly. For a second, we sat there and looked at one another. We weren’t sure what we were doing or how we would scare the man off, but it seemed right at the time. I leaned in and kissed her on the lips and that too felt right. She smiled and then laughed so I could see her cute pug face scrunched up. We both laughed and looked at the baby birds as we waited for the man to return. Her Eyes Jay was already asleep by the time that I could bring him his water. I had heard him coughing on my way back, but he was no match for the sudden fatigue that had been striking his body. I placed the glass of water on his bedside table and turned the television off. Instead of waiting for him to wake, I started back to the kitchen. The phone rang and I picked it up on my way. “Hello.” “Hi. Is Jay there?” “He is. He’s sleeping though. I can take a message though if you’d like.” “Well this is James Bryan. I was just calling to check on Jay and see how he was doing after the big match. I heard that there was a bit of a scuffle backstage when he got back to the locker room.” “He’s fine, just up and down. It’s the cigarettes. Without them, he’s unbalanced.” “Surely. Do you think he’ll be ready to wrestle for the upcoming show?” “Most likely not. It’s too early for him to come back after a title match.” “The show after then. 148?” “It’s too hard to tell sometimes. I’m sure he’ll be ready for then, yeah.” “Well thanks. Have him call me when he wakes up please.” “Certainly.” I turned the phone off and placed it down on the counter. There was a window above the sink and I looked out to see the garden. Not a single flower was alive. Even the bushes looked like they were drooping. I thought of replanting it and letting it grow while Jay slept, but I also hoped that Jay wouldn’t sleep for that long. Instead, I imagined that it was full of life again. I pictured violets, daffodils, and daisies sprouting in each corner as full bouquets. In the center I thought of a bench with vines holding hands with the arms. On it, I could see Jay sitting and staring up at the night sky. He would be waiting for his shooting star most likely with a string in hand so he could take a ride. I know I would follow. He was coughing again and I started back to the room. When inside, Jay was sitting up and drinking the water that I had left. “Was that the phone?” he asked. “It was James Bryan. Just wondering how you were doing.” “You tell him how I was?” “I told him you needed to rest.” “I need more than rest.” “For now you just need rest. We’ll worry about the rest of it later.” He coughed again, this time harder, and buried his face in his pillow. It wrapped around his head and Jay seemed to suffocate for a moment. “I’m sorry about the show. Backstage,” he mumbled into the pillow. Finally pulling his face out, Jay looked at me with somber eyes. “I didn’t mean to get upset. It was just the circumstances. The effort you put into that celebration. The, well. The everything. I just,” he stopped to cough again and this time pounded his fist into the mattress. “God damn cough.” I laughed and felt my face scrunch up some. “Don’t worry about it, Jay. Just try and get some more sleep. You’ll wake up next time and forget you even had that cough.” Jay smiled. “In my dreams.” His Eyes We sat in the tree and tried to decide what the young birds were talking about. We had decided that they were finches by the looks of their body and purple finches by their feathers. They seemed dignified in a way, yet still too young to travel on their own. They seemed to be begging for food and both Margeret and I searched the branches for worms or other things to eat. While my search came up empty, hers resulted in a long caterpillar. “Do they eat these?” she asked me. “I’m not sure. I thought they ate worms and things like worms.” “Is a caterpillar a worm?” “Worms don’t become butterflies so I don’t think so.” “Let’s give it to them. I think they’d eat anything.” I nodded and took the caterpillar from her. I blessed it and thanked it silently for its sacrifice and split it into three pieces for the three baby birds. One by one, I guided the dead creature into their mouths and watched the swallow it down. They continued to beg for more, but we told them that it was all we had to give. Despite the screeching birds, we could hear the tires of a pick-up truck kicking up rocks and grass again. It was getting close. I looked at Margeret. “What do we do?” “I don’t know. Maybe he won’t want the tree anymore.” “And if he does?” “Then we stay up here until he doesn’t.” I heard the engine die and his boots on the grass. He crunched the blades as he walked and this time, I realized that he had a friend in hand. “Where’d you go, kids?” “What are you doing with that?” I asked. “I’m taking my tree.” “Not while we’re up here,” Margeret said. “Girly, you have two choices. First choice is you can come on down and I’ll take this tree. Second choice is you can stay up there and I’ll take you, your friend, the little birds, and this tree. Your choice though.” I looked at her and whispered, “He wouldn’t.” As if he could hear me, he pulled the chain on his chainsaw and it roared at the base of the tree. The birds yelled for help, but their screams were no match for the brutish machine. He looked at us one last time before taking the blade to the tree and cutting into it. “Stop it!” Margeret yelled. He heard and turned it off. “You want to come down now?” “I want to take the birds out.” “Fine,” he replied. “I’ll help you down.” Margeret ignored the help and tapped me. “We have to take them down in the nest. Can you grab it?” “Of course,” I said. As I reached over to see the majestic young finches, I realized that we were heroes for saving such a delicate creature. I felt good saving their lives and wrapped my hands under the nest carefully to remain a hero. Upon touching the bottom though, something strange happened. The birds flapped their boyish wings until they became mighty tools. One by one, the birds hovered out of the nest like a swarm of butterflies and started to move away from the stick and grass home they loved. “They’re flying,” I said. Margeret watched them lift off and she grabbed my hand tightly as if she were keeping them in the air. They dipped some, but ultimately found the strength in their wings to fly high into the sky toward the sun. We looked down to the man below and he hardly noticed the miracle the tree has provided. “Did you see that?” I asked. “See what?” “They flew off. They can fly now.” “Their birds. Of course they can fly.” “They’re just kids. Just young ones.” “Then why don’t you fly and follow them?” For a second, I pondered the request. The tree was an innovator, a healer, and a helper for those that needed it. I was ready to flap my own wings and take flight with the birds, but Margeret held my hand still and refused to let me jump. I looked at the sun and watched our royal birds soar away. We climbed down and let him chop down our tree. It was the only option we had. He was unable to get the roots though and that meant that he was unable to defeat the tree. One day, people would fly again from the tree and they’d vanish into the sun to a new world. Until then, we’d wait be the best that we could at the stump. Her Eyes Jay woke up the following day and I was by his side. There was a magazine across my breasts and when he picked it up, he startled me. “You’re reading about Rich Rollins?” “I was.” “I’ll tell you about Rich Rollins.” Jay took a sip from a newly filled glass of water and sat down on the bed. “You seem better.” “I feel better.” “And your hair. You cut your hair.” “It was time for a change. A new phase of my life.” He paused to admire her smile. “I feel like it’ll help me fly better.” “I’m sure it will.”
