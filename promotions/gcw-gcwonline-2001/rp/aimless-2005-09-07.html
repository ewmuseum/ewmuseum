Date: 9/07/05 So, having sat around and drunk himself into oblivion in his own private corner, heavy-handedly tear jerking ballads screeching out from the jukebox, Trey finally decides to leave the bar and get back to somewhere like home. Except, a home on the road. Somewhat like an apartment, but with smaller payments that occur on a more frequent basis. Christ, the kid's a genius. He smirks in self-satisfaction at having invented hotels, motels, and inns as he passes through quiet streets draped in shadowed trees and hovering auburn street lamps. At a crosswalk, a few voices call his ear and he looks to the right; where the road drops below an overpass and disappears into a murky, suburban kind of darkness not found outside the middle-class of America. He rubs the back of his neck, not that it has anything to do with what he's looking at, and almost calls Jordan to ask how he'd dealt with the wrestling act sober. Of course he realizes his mistake. 'Walk' flashes below a crude caricature of a man, casting Trey in yellow. He looks to and quickly looks away, set to slip under the bridge and, assuming the voices he heard were a pain-based hallucination, take a few quick bumps to ease the pain. If they're not fake, well, he'll deal with that when it comes up. It'll definitely come up, he realizes when he sees a stream of smoke beginning to roll out. The hint of pot in the air leads him on in a walk almost half-way to the classic cartoon float and even closer to the demented stagger of a killer on the loose. He stops and lets his eyes adjust to the darkness. The voices come out again, louder and carrying with them intense laughter. A hacking cough. He steps into the shadows. Silence. Something crashes into the back of his head. Not giving himself the moment to think, he twists and grabs a wrist. A chain grazes his arm as it falls to the ground. The heavy thud of whoever held it follows and Trey quickly pulls a knife from his pocket and flips the blade out. Smiling. 'Bad idea.' The face staring up at him goes pale, wide blue eyes and wild blue hair. Acne. A rat moustache. 'You're a fuckin' kid.' He looks up at a circle of people forming around him, mostly white kids with one black guy and a chick--either Asian or Latino. They, in camouflaged and dirty jeans, ripped t-shirts, baggy jackets, a multitude of piercings that gleam out in the dark, stare down at Trey and their fallen friend. Their hair's all dyed or dreadlocked or stringing out in erratic twists, monstrous silhouettes on a humid night. He stands and helps the kid up. The tallest of the group, a guy with a Rancid--in both logo and condition--hoodie and a week's worth of stubble growing under his chin, takes a long hit from a joint and stares Trey over. He still loses several inches to our loveable protagonist. 'Who are you?' 'Trey Fuckin' Tremendous, kid. You?' 'Jack.' 'What you got to say for your buddy? Jack.' 'We thought it'd work out a little better than it did. What are you doing around here?' 'Was goin' for a walk, 'til you went and fucked it up. Now, just sorta starin' you guys down and wonderin' how many I could take before I got shivved.' 'Not many, probably.' 'Dunno, sorta my career. Do a little better than you'd expect, at least.' 'Weasel,' Jack directs to the blue boy, 'probably oughta apologize to this guy.' He steps forwards, holding his ribs, and extends a hand to Trey. It gets rejected. 'Sorry, man. Just, shit, well, you know how it is.' 'Nah. Not really. What's up with you guys? I mean, look too young to be life-long gutter punks.' 'Well--' 'Don't worry about it.' Jack cuts Weasel off, his voice sharp and quick. 'I know who you are, it's not a life you'd understand.' 'What, you run away and act like the world hates you?' 'Hah, didn't realize you were that big of an ass.' 'Oh yeah, been workin' on it. So, shit, you kids mind if I bust up your party long enough to fall down?' 'Go ahead.' Jack motions and Trey obliges. He collapses on a run of cement that angles up to the highway, dejected and unrepentant. No, fuck that--sixteen syllables too many. He sits. Digging in his pocket, he eventually pulls a small golden canister out, about as wide as a pencil eraser and twice as long. The top pops up and the contents fly into Trey's nostril in one quick jerk of his head. He shakes again; realizes he's being stared at by too many pairs of eyes. 'What?' 'Share and share alike.' 'Nice message. How's that fit in with the chain shit?' Before the next bleating, Trey comes in again. 'Look, fuck that. I don't really give enough of a shit to keep it up, so what if we just chill out for a bit? No reason to hate this quick.' 'I guess.' 'So, what you kids out here for?' 'You said it yourself; we ran away and act indignant about it, fucker.' The exotic-looking girl grabs Jack by the arm, dragging him behind the line they've formed. A few of the less concerned sit down, smoking and circling Trey. 'What's the fame like?' asks a petite blonde with pink streaks. 'Like life, except with less of what you really wanna do. What about you? Life, I mean.' 'It's alright. I mean, I hitched here from Colorado and it's definitely the best place I've found to do this at. Better family than I ever had at home, and the people that live around are nice enough.' 'Why'd you ditch the 'rents?' 'Got tired of being told what to do all the time. We weren't that close anyway, not since mom got remarried.' 'Well, shit. What about you?' He raises a hand to the black guy, half of his head frizzed out into an afro and the other half still tight to his head in rows. 'Parents died and I didn't like living with my aunt. Like Ashleigh said, we're as tight as a group can be. None of us was ever going anywhere, so we all just enjoy the ride.' 'Why you say that? Not goin' anywhere, I mean.' ''Cause I'm honest, shit. No one wants a 9-to-5, but most people won't just say fuck it and do what they really want. I'm the only one here that finished high school, you know, and I really didn't need it. Unless you're going to college, there ain't much difference between being a drop-out and a graduate.' 'No college?' 'If the money was there, yeah, but that's the problem.' The group nods. 'World wouldn't give us what it gave most, so fuck it. You think the scholarships go down to the bottom of the barrel? Nah, some people just get a shitty deal and do their best to play along. That's life.' Trey nods and a joint as wide as his finger comes to him. He hits it, absorbed in thought. 'Why not try?' 'Look, man. The after school special shit's appreciated, but it's not helping anyone. We've got our world and you've got yours, and that's the way things go.' 'Shit, it's all the same world. Difference is how we do it.' 'Might as well be a different world. We live outside society, you know, have our own little culture. It's exactly like any other, just with less houses. We got musicians, artists, writers, dancers, shitheads and geniuses. Just, well, everyone got fucked just as hard as the next. More in common than most blood.' 'Damn. 'Sup with Jack? He the leader, or some shit? An' damn, didn't get your name.' 'Clay. But, damn, Jack a leader? Likes to act it, but only a couple people really listen to him. Boy's got a chip on his shoulder and a trust fund waiting for him if he ever thinks straight.' 'Shit.' 'Yeah, it's shit; don't turn our backs on him for it though. No point in hostility just because his family had a better deal than mine, he still chose the streets over fucking along without much hope for success. I mean, damn man, you heard him. Dude's too big a jackass to make it in that real world shit, so he picked community over that for awhile. Figure he'll get tired of it one of these days, and we won't ever see him again. That's life.' 'Who's this go to?' A redheaded guy leans in and takes the joint from Trey. 'Shit, what's your story man?' 'Not much different th' anyone else's. Split on the parents for a bad deal, guess.' 'An' this is better than what you had?' 'Better than my dad coming back to the crib drunk and pulling a knife on me for the way I dress? Yeah.' Jack's voice comes in over the din of traffic, garbled but angry enough to tell he's arguing. Trey listens and picks out a few words from the muck. 'He always that bitchy?' 'Yeah,' answers Clay. 'Get used to it after awhile, he ain't a bad guy or nothing. Just full of himself, but he's always got a little cash on him to help us out when we need it.' 'That works. Gotta get tired of it after awhile though.' 'Yeah, but that ain't what friends are about. Together through the good and the horrible shit. But, hey man, you mind me asking why you chose your life?' 'Got lucky.' 'How's that? Way I see it, you're a lot less free than any of us. Don't get me wrong or nothing, just seems like you've got shit to live up to that none of us have to worry about. And damn, wrestling? Shit's gotta tear your body up.' 'Does, but it pays the bills. I mean, people know me. I don't really have to worry about gettin' fucked with when I go somewhere, at least.' 'Wouldn't call that lucky.' 'Yeah, if I'da thought about it I probably wouldn't either. But shit, other than the actual job it's an easy life. Not a millionaire or anything, but I can't count all the shit people've given me.' 'So you're living off of handouts too? Damn.' 'Shyeah, you're right. Not much of a wrestler anyway, though. My head's just gettin' less into that shit, y'know? Like, I think I know what a good wrestler is, but I can't pull it off even if I try. More just a fighter, I guess, an' people like me enough that I keep gettin' decent breaks on the way.' 'Whatever it takes to get by, man. Shit, we got pretty easy lives too. Plenty of places to stay, lots of food, lots of clothes, tighter than blood. Just doing it on a smaller level.' 'Makes sense.' Again, the conversation takes a break as Jack's voice raises to a tenor screech, full of whine and delusion. 'Damn. Look, you guys wanna go grab somethin' to eat? I'm fuckin' starved, an' I dunno how much longer I can take douche over there bitchin'.' 'Shit, sounds good. Where to?' 'Ain't really been in town long enough to get favorites. What's good around here?' 'There's a pizza place a few blocks away that's great, big-ass pie with tons of cheese and shit.' 'Fuck yeah, that's a party. But shit, you ain't got a problem with me payin' do you?' The group shakes their heads, Clay shrugs. 'Cool, cool. Just lead the way.'
