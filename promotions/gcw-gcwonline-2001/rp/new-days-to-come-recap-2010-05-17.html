Date: 05/17/10 New Days To Come ---- "Growing up is never easy. You hold on to things that were. You wonder what's to come. But that night, I think we knew it was time to let go of what had been, and look ahead to what would be. Other days. New days. Days to come. The thing is, we didn't have to hate each other for getting older. We just had to forgive ourselves... for growing up." ---- Prologue One of life's greatest clich’s is that you never truly appreciate what you have until it is gone. Although we've all heard this many a times, we still often tend to ignore it and regard it as a distant thought. It is only in the times of grief and sorrow that we call upon this clich? and try to reapply it to our lives. We use it at a comfort and as an escape from heartache and sadness. Yet still the same, it slips away back into distant memory as time goes on. You always too soon forget to appreciate what you have in your life, until it is taken from you again. For some, they merely accept their fate as it is given. They try to explain fate as something that is set in stone. They say there exists a reason beyond what we could ever know. They are ignorant to the fact that they control their fate simply by making decisions. Others see this light and try to squeeze themselves through this loophole. They attack fate. They try to take fate and make it something that will work in their favor. For these people, there is no other option in life than to try and divert from the path they are already on. For if they don't...they'll end up dead. Sooner rather than later. This is a story about changing your fate. It's a story about running away from the tribulations of life and suffering because of it. This is a story about trying to escape from your problems and then deciding you are tired of running. This is a story of passion. This is a story of hatred. This is a story about broken friendships. This is a story of healing bonds. This is a story about life. This is a story about love. This is a story... about fate. ---- Chapter One: His Meeting from the perspective of Chris Bagwell It was almost like they were wearing a concrete coated glove and slapped me across the face with it.
