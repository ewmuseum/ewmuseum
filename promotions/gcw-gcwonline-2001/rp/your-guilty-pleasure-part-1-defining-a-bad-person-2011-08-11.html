Your Guilty Pleasure (Part 1): Defining A Bad Person

11 August 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/11/11 Fist Contact Keith isn’t an athlete or a body builder, and I’m betting he hasn’t been in too many fights in his life. But this is still an angry, full grown man we’re talking about here, and I’m betting in his corporate days he played his fair share or racquetball. So when I see him rearing back, telegraphing his punch for the world to see, I can tell this is going to hurt like a son of a bitch. It takes every ounce of effort to keep my hands down, to accept my fate. If my penance is to give him a free shot, if that is what it takes to start making things right, then I will overcome every synapse that screams, "No!" Two knuckles meet the bridge of my nose. A white flash hits, and the image of Kate is burned on to my retina. She was coming outside to spend some time with the two men in her life, believing they had just made nice. Her past and present seemed to be getting on so well. He and I had finally, after all this time, sat and had a real conversation, and everything looked like it was going to be okay. I’m sure she screamed. The rush of blood to my ears drowned it out, but there’s no doubt in my mind. For once in my life, there was no doubt about anything. The problem with a relationship build upon a lie is what happens when the rug is pulled from under their feet. ---- 2005 Kate was dressed in gray yoga pants and a blue hoodie, her hair pulled back and her high heels exchanged for comfortable slippers. She opened the door right in my face, dropping the garbage bag in her left hand from the startle. One hand shot to her chest, the other to the door to slam it, before she caught her breath and realized I wasn’t a mugger. Then she saw who I was. The breath went right back out. Her jaw agape, it was all she could do to believe it was real. Not that I was doing any better; if I’d had half the courage I brag about, I would’ve knocked on the fucking door about five minutes prior instead of rehearsing a hundred thousand apologies to myself. It had been five years since I’d seen her, and only one thing I could say seemed to make any sense at all. "Hi," cracked the voice of an eighteen year old boy, "Hi Kate." Impossible as it was to tell what she was thinking, I do remember bracing myself to get hit. One shaking hand went to her mouth and the other braced it, and the strongest woman I’d ever known seemed like she was about to break down and collapse. To her credit, the woman has always been made of steel, and a deep swallow that hit her stomach like a rock steadied her shaken mind. One quivering hand reached out to touch me, to see if I was real or a hallucination. About halfway to my chest, her hand turned to a greeting, gracing my shoulder with a touch that I’d memorized and would recognize if I were blind. "John," she almost asked, "I...I can’t believe it’s really you." She looked like she’d matured a great deal, losing none of her beauty but gaining an air, a posture that made her much more of a woman. Whatever had happened over the course of five years must have been incredible to cause only a positive, strengthening change in her. Part of me wondered if I was just imagining it, if absence had made the heart grow fonder. Part of me shuddered to think that maybe that positive change was living five years without me around. "I, um," what exactly do you say when you drop in on someone whose heart you broke? I went the casual route, "...you look good." I’ve always been a jackass. She nodded and smiled, not particularly caring about what I had to say. The girl had always been the smartest person I knew, and she always managed to see right through me. So any attempt at acting casual or macho was going to be completely in vain...but I’d be damned if I didn’t act that way anyway. Because that’s the Johnny she always knew. After letting me squirm like a worm on a hook for a few more moments, Kate invited me in to have a seat. "It must have been a long trip," she said. "It’s so good to see you," she said. She said all the little things you’re supposed to say when you see someone after a very long time, as though acting like this was a high school reunion would ease the tension. We chatted about bullshit and our careers and how little I’d changed. She couldn’t believe I was still my high school weight, and I pointed out that it didn’t come easy. When asked what I did for fun, my answer was ‘burn calories.’ When asked what I’d been eating, I said ‘things that have no taste to them.’ When asked how life had been to me, I could barely tell. Maybe I’d been frozen for five years because I didn’t let life or any of its free radicals, trans fats, or oxidization touch me. I’d worked so hard to stay the same that, sadly, I stayed the same. After ten minutes of bullshit and two straight minutes of silence, she finally asked me. "So why did you leave?" She didn’t seem angry, at least not anymore. She never called or wrote me after I’d gotten up and left, almost treating it as though I died. Perhaps I did. Perhaps that’s why she couldn’t get mad at what I’d done. "Well," would have been the perfect start to telling her the truth, "I guess I was scared." ---- Y2K - The Year I Was Never Scared Laying in bed next to her, the rain on the windows painting waves and lines across her nude body, it was all I could do to hold myself together. Kate and I were intense, had known each other at our most vulnerable and at our least protected. We weren’t each other’s "first" by any means, but she opened up walls in me that I didn’t know existed. The thing is, it didn’t scare me. It amazed me. It filled me with awe. This woman that lay next to me understood me and fit with me and trusted me with all her heart. She accepted, taught, and challenged me at every single turn and never once doubted me. If I told her I would do something, it’s because I knew I would do it. If a thought was going through my head, she was able to read it, analyze it, and articulate it in a way that I never could. This girl wasn’t in love with me. She was me. She was everything decent and good in my life. Kate was perfection, and I was imperfect. That’s why I got up and left. I couldn’t even bear to wake her up. ---- And A Couple Months Before That... "Who’s Sarah?" Fuzzy eyelids and dry mouth. The telltale signs of the real world. I noticed you never have crusty eyes or trouble speaking when you’re dreaming. "...what?" "Who is Sarah?" One eye cracked open and the clock reads way too early. I didn’t know what shit it was about, but I recall being fairly certain Kate was playing some practical joke on me. "The hell are you talking about," I never meant to sound that belligerent, but I’d never been very pleasant upon waking up, "It’s three in the morning." "You were calling out a name. You woke me up calling ‘Sarah,’" there wasn’t an ounce of accusation or jealousy in her voice, "Who is Sarah?" I could feel my veins freeze shut as realization dawned. I’d been calling her name. Just some girl from a couple years before who was head over heels for me, who’d swooned at me at my first wrestling match. Some small company, barely worth a memory in my head, paying an underage kid under the table to wrestle his ass off...and Sarah’s eyes ate me alive. Why she popped in my head that night I’ll never know, but between the flutter of my heart and the moisture in my boxers told me it wasn’t exactly a wholesome dream. Nothing like a sex dream about one girl while sleeping next to another. Kate probably would have understood. You can’t control your nightly dreams, and it wasn’t like I was actually sleeping with another woman. But this was before The Year I Was Never Scared. "Sarah was my mother’s name," was the complete truth, compounded with, "I...was watching her die. Again." The words squeezed out. Kate knew about my mother, how I’d been there when the car crashed. She was the only person that was able to get me to talk about it. And I repaid that gift by using that very tragedy to hide an innocent wet dream. Sometimes the lie is worse than the sin it hides. ---- Sarah and Sarah After being gone for five years, she welcomed me home. Sat me down. Poured the coffee. She let me just sit in silence and apologize by being there. But after killing twenty minutes of our precious lives, she finally had to ask the question that’d been on her mind since she opened the fucking door. "So why do you come here tonight?" "I don't know exactly," just to try to alleviate the guilt of throwing her away, "I just...felt that I had hidden too long." Kate nodded her head thoughtfully. Everything was perfect. And then...something completely different. "Mommy?" a tiny voice called from behind me. Kate’s daughter. Sarah. Named after my mother, who she believed I’d been calling for in my sleep. She believed that because I told her. Because I would never lie to her. Because I am Johnny Borealis pretending to be Johnathan Fitzwillis. And I only want what I can’t have. ---- A Breakup Later "John, this is Keith." "Keith. It’s really great to meet you." Liar. ---- She Felt The Need To Tell Me About an hour before the paperwork hit the desk, she told me how she felt. I’d crushed the girl I left Kate for, and I’d scoured the Earth for evidence against him. Hungover, a throat coated in bile, I sat in front of her radiance as she told me in plain words. "You’re not. A bad. Person." Each word a nail in my hands and feet. Set me up as an example for others to live by. I’d left her twice, each time leaving destruction in my wake. The first time she raised a child for six years by herself, a child I never even knew existed. The second time she found the man I should have been. Both times I found a way to take that from her. Our daughter adores me because I’m never strict, because I let her do what she wants to make up for not being there. Now, just like I’ve stolen Sarah’s affection, I’ve damned Keith’s career. For all I know, those fraudulent accounts were a fraud upon him as well. For all I know, Keith might be the most straight and narrow investment banker in the world. Might have been. Past tense. You’re not a bad person. I replayed it over and over and over until each word means absolutely nothing. Not even coming from Kate, my angel. The words are ash the moment they hit my ears because all the honesty, care, and love that she puts in to them is founded on a bed of lies that I’d been telling her for as far back as I can remember. Seeing Keith's knuckles race toward my head only made me realize one thing. Honesty hurts. And I'm not sure I'm brave enough to keep from hiding from it from here on out.
