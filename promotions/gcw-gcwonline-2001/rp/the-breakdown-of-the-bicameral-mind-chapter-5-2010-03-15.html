The Breakdown of the Bicameral Mind: Chapter 5

15 March 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/15/10 You woke up with a start. The brightness of the room attacked your eyes with unparalleled force. You tried hard to shake it off, but the only way to make it subside was to get under the covers. After a few moments of adjusting your eyes, you were able to look up. The sun stood there in front of you in all its majesty. You looked directly at it and smirked. There was no longer any pain. You got up and stretched. Every muscle felt like it was being stretched for the first time in a long time. After a whole life of being in a static state, your body felt like it had just come out of the box. The smell of new was only good for a little while and you decided to go to the bathroom and take a nice long shower. As you entered that room, you suddenly understood all the power that mirrors held over an individual. Snow White had no chance against the power of the mirror. It stopped you in your tracks. You looked old and faded. Completely contrary to the way that you felt. As you looked at yourself in the mirror, you understood what was happening. What was staring at you in the mirror was not the real thing. It was a shell of your former self. A casing that had protected the person you were born to be for all your life. It was time to get rid of it. The clothes started to fall from your body, and before long, the shower was running and the steam was rising. The mixture of the water in its different stages was soothing; rejuvenating. But you understood that you must shed your old skin, like a snake, in order to be the new man that you felt like. With a growl, you tore at your flesh. Chunks of flesh and blood fell roughly on the shower tiles. Before long, you felt the hot water on your new skin as if feeling it for the first time. The hair on your arms stood on end. You couldn’t help but show your delight. The transformation was finally complete. All the months that had just passed of self torture and isolation were finally over. Mary Shelley would never have been able to imagine a creature such as you had become. Better than a perfect human being; nothing but pure anger and purpose. A perfect example of human evolution at its most advanced. “I’m finally ready.” ----- Her hand came down hard on the counter top. The frustration was audible in her tone of voice. At times it cracked as the sobs and the shouts became one. “Where is he!? Tell me right now, before I go to a higher authority.” She looked into the eyes of the staff members around her and threatened them with her gaze. Most staff members, even the orderlies that had heard the hubbub, shrank back in fear. “He is no longer here, m’am,” a voice said from behind the corner of the hallway. It was an authoritative voice for sure. She guessed it was the doctor in charge of the hospital for mental patients. It took a man with strong convictions to be able to run a place like this. “He left us last week. We tried everything we could to stop him, but he was just too strong for us.” There was a hint of disappointment in his voice as he said it. “How could you have let him leave? He was supposed to get better here. He was supposed to return to normal. He was…” before she could finish her sentence, she dropped to the ground crying. Her tears flooded the hospital’s waiting room. Almost drowning in her tears, she hit herself in the head with her open palm. “It’s my fault... It’s all my fault.” The doctor got down on his knee besides her and gripped her shoulder tight enough to show support. The atmosphere had lost its cutting edge as the woman began to weep. “There, there… it’s not your fault. Whatever Mr. Vargas did has nothing to do with you. He is a deeply troubled man that is sadly away from my influence. If you want, I can find you some clean clothes, some hot coffee and we can go over what happened. Does that sound ok?” She stifled a sob and nodded. They walked slowly towards the doctor’s office. His hand slowly guided her through the maze that was the hospital hallways. When they reached the office, he had her sit down in a leather chair right in front of his desk. The leather creaked when she sat down on it. The sobbing had almost subsided when he sat on his chair and put his psychiatrist face on. “Tell me what happened. The more you tell me, the more I can help Mr. Vargas. Tell me everything.” The woman opened her mouth for a second as she tried to unscramble her brain and give an actual progression of events that made any sense. She closed it back up before taking a very deep breath in order to gain some courage. “My name is Verónica Rodríguez. A couple of years back, Antonio and I were engaged to be married. I had just started working for this dance group and we were set to tour the world. It was an opportunity that I just couldn’t let pass me by. One of those opportunities I would have resented him years from now. So, my work and mixed with his work, which has him also traveling constantly was too much of a strain for both of us,” she said as if finally letting it go after so many years. Verónica couldn’t make eye contact with anything. Her eyes darted around the office never really concentrating on anything. The doctor scribbled on his pad. He understood that something traumatic had happened between Antonio and Verónica and if there was any way to help either of them, he had to know what had happened and start from there. “So, you two broke it up?” She sighed. “I broke it up. It was kind of selfish, I know, but I just had to go out there and live my life.” “What brought you back to his life?” “I went back to Puerto Rico after my tour of the world and decided to see how he was. I still loved him. I never stopped loving him. It was a deep wound that I had left him with, but I had hoped we could at least become friends or something.” She sobbed again. “I never thought I’d see him this low. He had given up on life. He was not in the right mental state.” “Is that when he attacked you?” the doctor asked her matter of factly. She looked at him surprised. Her eyes were wide with fear and regret. For some reason she had blamed herself for the attack. It was a mixture of guilt from leaving him and lament for everything that had happened. “How did you know?” This was the first time during the whole conversation where she felt shy in front of this doctor she didn’t know. “You keep unconsciously touching where your turtleneck covers the bruises. After a while doing this job, Ms. Rodríguez, you learn to read people and their bodies for expressions that they wouldn’t normally talk about,” he explained. “I see some of the most dangerous patients in the United States in this hospital; murderers, serial rapists, everything…” the doctor sighed. “Mr. Vargas is not one of the most dangerous when he got here; he had never killed anyone, but he was on his way. He was becoming desensitized to violence.” “Doctor, what was wrong with him?” “He had started to develop schizophrenia when I first evaluated him. Mr. Vargas created this whole story of a doctor using him for an experiment on creating some sort of super human being. The thing is, this doctor he claimed was doing these experiments on him doesn’t exist. Anywhere. I looked,” the doctor said, showing frustration for the first time during the whole conversation. “Who is this doctor then?” “Himself.” --------- You were looking at the world with new eyes. Everything seemed new. It was like taking everything in for the first time in your life. Life seemed like a grandiose thing. Everything was glossy and everything smelled of new. You couldn’t help but take very deep breaths in order to draw everything inside of you. You were a new man. The perfect man. This lit brightest the farthest and darkest parts of you mind. There were no longer any doubts. The mission had been laid for you. That was when you began to contemplate your future. The park was almost empty as the sun was coming down. All the kids and their parents had left that place and it was silent; it was sleeping. You could hear the park’s light snores if you listened close enough. Everything around you was alive. That gave even more meaning to what you had to do. Dr. Matheson had been destroyed and there was nowhere to go but forward. Revenge was something that you were used to. It was part of your job description. Dr. Andrew Matheson had no idea who he had messed with. That is why you killed him. The doctor had it right when he thought that the world had become stagnant. You knew that he was right. If you had met at some other time and in some other circumstance you probably would have shared common ground, but the good old doctor had abused his power and there was no way that you could have allowed him to go on. Besides, he was like everyone else. Unevolved. Everything had changed. The world was different to you now. There was no going back to the way things were. You need to do your part for humanity’s sake. Breaking people down in order to get them to fulfill their potential. It is not an easy job and you are the only one that can do it. ----- “What can we do to help him, doctor? I would do anything to have the real Antonio back,” she said, almost pleading to him. “This is the problem, Ms. Rodríguez. He thinks he was tortured and made into a machine like human being. He also thinks that he killed this doctor after the experiment was done. The dementia is so deep that I don’t think any pills or normal treatment could work at this point. The schizophrenia is at its highest point and shows no sign of slowing down. There are two options, we let him drift deeper into his paranoia by going to traditional treatments or we shock him out of it,” he explained to her slowly. “You mean like shock therapy or something like that?” she asked, surprised. Her voice trembled a little at the thought of shocks of electricity being pumped into Antonio’s head, possibly crippling him mentally for life. “No, no. It is nothing like that. What I mean is to cause something so shocking that he is forced to come to grips with reality. I would like you to confront himâ€"“ “Wait… I don’t know if I can do something like that…” She felt frightened. It seemed like the walls around her were closing in faster and faster. The thought of actually confronting Antonio, after he had choked her almost to death, was something that she could not have envisioned. “Don’t worry, we would have backup ready for whatever happens. We just need you to talk to him.” Verónica looked skeptical. She didn’t see how putting her life in danger was a good idea. The thought of a demented Antonio attacking her again was too much for her. It took all of her confidence to go to the hospital in the first place. She didn’t know if she had anything left anymore. “I’d have to think about it. Can you give me a few days to reflect on it?” “Sure, but remember, the quicker you do this, the easier it would be on his mind.” Verónica stood up. “Thank you doctorâ€"“ “Matheson. Dr. Andrew Matheson.”
