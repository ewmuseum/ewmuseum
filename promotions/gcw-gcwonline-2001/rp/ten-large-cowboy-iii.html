Date: once upon a time Seeing is believing. I'll believe THAT when I see it. Recently my wife has gone into a coma. AND checked herself out of hospital. I've had a shake down from the scariest man on the planet, trying to get money from me. I've been looking after my children, who miss their mother like a fish misses water. And I've been trying to sustain a wrestling career. Through all of this. With ribs so purple I could be Grimace's stunt double. What goes up must come down, the old saying goes. Should I shut my eyes or keep them open, to watch the asphalt rush up to kiss my lips? Pucker up, sunshine. Pucker up.--- ç --- Ah well. I suppose it had to come to this. Ned Kelly Bushranger, 11th of November, 1880. --- ç --- Wes Noble has thrown a spanner in the machine of my life, turning what once felt like immaculate Swiss cogs, that moved together as one, into a seemingly cheap Chinese knock-off. It wasn't the fact that he'd come at me requesting what it was that Morag owed him. No. It was in the way of portraying himself as the mask for a greater monster. A monster I never knew existed. The idea that there was a greater fiend than that of Wes Noble made my spleen quiver and my blood begin to coagulate, in fear of being spilt. A greater monster than Noble was an unfathomable ideology that neither I, nor mankind, could ever bear to understand. The cab veers into the curb by my apartment block. Shove a twenty at the cab driver, before he’s even stopped. Shove open the door, grab the change from the driver and head toward home, hooking my arm through the strap of my overnight bag. Night has grown a ferocious chill to it, which is unusual for this time of year. The moon seems to hide itself behind the clouds, like a kid watching a horror movie unfurling before their eyes. A chill rises up my spine, standing the hairs on my neck up like soldiers at attention, unsettling me and drawing a sense of urgency in my stride. Up the path and to the caged door, buzz myself into the building and walk through to the mail room. Unlock my mailbox and pluck out the contents. Make my way to the elevators, rifling through the postman's bounty as the first free elevator opens its doors. Step inside to the familiar hum of the building’s cheap European elevator. Thumb at the “1” button and wait for it to swing into action. It clunks and heaves, rising me into the upper echelons of the building. A note, as if plucked from my grasp by invisible fingers, springs free from my mail and gently, like a leaf, twirls and dances through the air as it makes its way to the floor. It captivates me, I cannot help but be hypnotised by its dance. Or was it the message it possessed that had captured my attention? It settles on the cold, steel floor; stark and un-inviting. Immediately, I recognise the writing. By the time I recognise the writing I've read the note. By the time I read the note my blood is chilled. And by the time my blood is chilled my heart sits in my throat, clogging the air I try to breathe. Pay back's a bitch, right? It is Morag's writing. The simplicity of the message was gut-wrenching and frightening all at once. THE KIDS?!? is my first thought, ploughing through to the front of my mind like a bulldozer. The elevator bing'ed at me as the doors open. I’m running before I even realise. Some greater force is pushing me to move quicker. My mind rapidly racing for the safety of my children under the watchful eye of their nanny, Rosie. Get to the door, one hand reaches for the handle and the other forages for keys. But the search is unnecessary. The front door slowly creaks ajar with the slightest of touches. My heart begins to sink as I step into the threshold of my apartment. Lights are out and visibility is tremendously low, yet my mind's eye can already see what my heart tries, fruitlessly, to block out. Swat at the wall, where the light switch is, illuminating the horror before me. Red stains the once familiar world before me. At the end of the hall is Rosie, piled in a heap of tooth, bruise and blood. Pummelled into a bloody pulp, almost beyond recognition. Had it not been for her favourite sundress, the yellow one with the tiny blue polka dots, I might’ve have thought it to be a stranger in my own home. The sundress which is so vibrantly stained with her own blood. “Oh my God… What happened?” I ask, without expecting any answer. Rushing down the hall, sliding across the carpet on my knees to Rosie’s side. Cup the back of her neck in my hand and gently to raise her head from the pool of her blood it lay in. Forcefully, Rosie pushes open her left eye, which has swollen itself shut. A gash trickles blood into her warm-chocolate eye, stinging as it contacts, causing it to well with tears. Her breathing is heavy, almost a gurgle, as if her lungs were filled with blood. “Señor, she come. She come took kids!” she says, hand outstretches and paws at my face, running the back of her pointer-finger down my cheek. As if she were trying to soothe me. “I try to stop her. She hurt me. Bash me with bat!” She bursts into a cough, spraying me with a mist of blood, trying to cover up unsuccessfully. The fit lasts a few moments, her hand clings to my shirt as if drawing strength from me to battle with her cough. “Morag?” I ask, already knowing the answer. “Si, señor. Señorita Morag, señor. She beat me like dog,” she remarks in her broken Mexi-English. My heart skips a beat and begins to drown in the information. Tumble forward onto one knee, clutching my chest. Shaking my head in disbelief. Hearing my own pulse resonate in my ears as the world draws a darkness around me. Rosie’s hand strokes at my cheek, a gesture of kindness and worry. The reasons why Rosie was hired in the first place. “I try and fight, Senor. Bat too hard. Oh, Madre dulce del bebé Jesús! The children! Oooh-ho-ho-ho… the chiiiillllddreeeennnn….” Rosie breaks down into tears upon the realisation that the children are actually gone. The tears roll down her bloodied cheek, leaving trails of clean skin behind them as they carve their way through the horrorific scene. I hush her, with a gentle “shoosh”, pressing her head to my chest, trying to soothe her by stroking her cheek with my hand. Turning the mixture of blood and tears into a paste monster's like Wes Noble would use for war-paint. Physically, I am in the room. Mentally, I am already searching for my children. Shakily, I use the wall to stand, my knees buckling beneath the weight of my situation but I manage to become upright. Stagger down the hall, pressing my hand against it for balance, leaving a Rosie blood palm-print in my wake. Push open Alex and Leslie’s bedroom in hope and desperation. Of course, they are not there. But Morag’s message is. Sprayed on the world in its horrific glory. Red spray-painted letters burn proudly into the white wallpaper, violently stark against the field mice and bunny rabbit motifs. Intentionally, the paint if blood red to get my mind racing onward to the fear my children are in danger. “I warned you.” What was happening to my life? What was happening to me? I can feel the asphalt rushing up to kiss me.--- ç --- I get to the top. I stand on the edge. I look to the sky. And say all my prayers. What could be better than a Swan dive into the asphalt? (hed)PE Swan Dive --- ç --- The smoke filters through my lungs, drawing all the bad stuff into my body that just feels so damn good. I let the smoke linger within for a while before expelling, trying to breathe out all of the evil I was enduring. It wasn’t working. The red and blue flashing lights of the patrol car twirled through the settling fog, mesmerising me as they swirled. Illuminating everything in their path with an almost purple light as it shines through the thickening vapour. Police and paramedics slink around what they’re now calling a “scene”, trying to avoid me as I stand in all of my Rosie-blood soaked glory in the middle of the sidewalk. My mind cannot help but slink into the darkness of what danger my children could possibly lie in. Trying to remain positive, as the detective with the pretty blond hair advised, is almost impossible with my thoughts spiralling into the shadows so easily. “Sir, is your cell phone the only number she’d contact you on?” asks the detective, ever so solemnly, with her best caring voice. Her question almost seems condescending to me, but I shrug at her nonetheless. “I guess so.” Draw on my cigarette, hoping that it could give me some sort of strength through all of this. Alas, it just continues to fill my body with the bad stuff it’s made to produce. A crowd has gathered and I watch their faces. Most of them strangers. Some of them. Some of them point and whisper to one another. Others point at me and whisper to one another. One of them smiles. The face shines through the faceless mass like wolf amongst the lambs. Wes Noble. “MOTHERFUCKERRRRRRRRRRRR!” I scream, dropping my cigarette and sprinting across the path and onto the road, toward the crowd barriers the police have set-up. Police notice my charge; some of them begin to unlatch their weapons in readiness. People in the crowd begin to dissipate, as if they already know who I’m charging at. Noble doesn’t even bat an eyelid. He simply prepares for the collision which is about to take place. I hurdle the crowd barrier and launch myself through the air like a torpedo. Wrap my arms around his torso and drive him straight into the bitumen road, both of our bodies sliding and grating against its roughened surface. Noble is the first to rise to his feet, he whips his mouth on the back of his sleeve, glancing at it fleetingly to check for blood. Adrenalin courses through my veins, giving me a sense of invincibility. Push to my feet to face the demon, ready for his strike. His right fist comes barrelling through the air, poised to strike my eye socket. I duck to the side and grab his arm, midflight, with both hands. I swing his body, launching him off the ground. As I turn one hundred and eighty degrees, I let go and he crashes into the bitumen. His face is torn by the surface of the road, but he still gets up on all fours, laughing as blood begins to trickle from his mouth to the road beneath. He snaps his head up to stare at me. His cheek is split and filled with the debris of the road. Blood runs down his cheek and into the neck of his jacket. It also dribbles down his chin as he begins to laugh. “Ya met the monster, Craig? Have ya seen the monster yet?” Police grab him, hoisting him to his feet and yanking his arms behind his back. More officers grab me, holding me back from charging at him once more. His full belly laugh only does more to fuel the engine of anger deep inside of me. The pistons running hot and ready to tear this monster apart. “Maybe you’ll never know the monster, cowboy.” (Word count: 1,999 according to MS Word)
