Fuck Subtlety. This Is Reality (TV).

11 March 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/11/11 1. - The camera steals your soul. "I don’t really do the whole confession thing." He sighs at me. I suppose it’s easier to get most people to ramble about themselves. "It’s not confession, Johnny, it’s a confessional. You just get to sit there and talk and be honest." "And tell you what’s on my heart and mind and what’s weighing on my soul. The things that bother me, the things that make me tick, my take on a situation the audience just witnessed...sounds like a couple of Commandments and a Hail Mary away from Catholicism." "But there aren’t any rules, Johnny," he tries so hard to be diplomatic. I have empathy. I never said I was easy to work with, "You get to just say whatever’s on your mind." "But they get to see what’s on my mind during the main show." "Yes but here you’re unhindered. There aren’t repercussions-" "Until the show airs. Then I’m fucked." I’m not trying to be a prick, and I definitely understand the concept. Show clips of what’s happening backstage or in the club or wherever, cut to the star of the show giving his feelings on the matter. Only unlike my Johnny’s Final Thought moment in front of the cameras, this has to be me. The real me. The me that I keep saying is somewhere inside but I never have the balls to actually bring him out. "You don’t have to say anything you don’t want to. We don’t pry, we just give you your chance to...elaborate. Without distractions," he pushes without pushing, trying to persuade me so politely. It’s really not easy to constantly put on a show for everybody in your life. Few people let their real selves see the light of day, but even fewer bottle themselves up as tight as I have to. Because the fact is I get addicted very, very easily. In my past I’ve been addicted to painkillers, sex, gambling, sex, booze, diet pills, sex, and Lost. Couldn’t go a week without seeing the next episode of Lost. From there it gets all metaphysical and stupid. Addiction to fame, attention, guilt, adrenaline. Basically, I find a way to make myself need my own bullshit time and time again. The nearest I can guess...I’m a leech. I need to latch on to something to find my purpose, and then I need to drain it dry and move on to something else. So, no, I don’t think getting addicted to honesty is a good idea. "Some people find it cathartic to talk. Even if they’re reluctant at first, it helps them open up. They say it feels good," he hasn’t given up yet. "About how their roommates eat their food without asking. Real deep stuff." The entire experience is custom-designed by high profile TV executives who employ psychiatrists and psychologists and anthropologists and sociologists who want to tap into that part of the brain that doles out the "trust" hormone. The thick black curtains soak up any secret. The camera is my friend that will not give shoddy advice, will not try to relate my problems to their own bullshit, but will only listen. The lighting is dim enough to hide every insecurity and after my producer leaves the silence will pester, poke, and prod me until I fill the void with every dark thought that swirls around in my head. It’s all been scientifically engineered to suck the thoughts out of me and convince me it feels great, like a digital hooker, and get them ready to sell to the world. He sees my face melt a little bit into acceptance. I don’t have to say anything, but I’m willing to let it all tempt me. "Just take a break. You might find it easy to say what you mean," he says as he gets up to leave. ---- ---- 2. - All alone in the middle of a crowded arena. Chris Bagwell and Johnny Borealis had been separated for about half an hour. The blood had been cleaned off the pavement by good little helpers and Phil Atken had been checked out and declared just dandy, or whatever the Glasgow term is, save for the frustration of letting a title belt slip. And Johnny Borealis was looking to leave early, his duty done for the night and his head pounding from his first ever pseudo-hardcore match capped off with a chair shot. His precious "HDTV Title," adorned in blinking Christmas lights and the LCD screen, was packed away in his bag. If the reality TV show cameras did not hover relentlessly around him, anyone would mistake Your Guilty Pleasure for the working class hero that he wasn’t. "‘Night, Marvin. ‘Night, Rich," he bid farewell to some of the ring crew that travelled with the circus he belonged to. They raised their hands and went back to their business, unimpressed by a man that many fans would jump a guardrail to meet. Maybe it’s because they are able to see him up close that they realize he’s flesh and blood just like them. "You," the familiar voice of GCW’s head of security carried through the backstage area, "Come ‘ere real quick before you go anywhere." Shoulders sagged. Johnny Borealis had punched out and Johnathan Fitzwillis was exhausted and sore, but since Toad was blocking the exit it was unavoidable to finish their conversation from earlier in the night. "What, Toad? What do you want?" "No more bullshit backstage with building your own security door like that," Toad stated, running at full steam. He was the last of GCW’s crew to leave, as per job description, and wasn’t about to let Fitzwillis off the hook just because the kid was tired, "Welders with blowtorches, nail guns, and a bunch of bodies I didn’t say could be in the building...I ain’t gonna put up with it." "Fine. Whatever," John tries to walk past, but a meaty palm on his chest stopped him. "I don’t give a shit if the Commish okay’ed your TV show, you ain’t gonna turn my backstage into a joke." John raised his eyebrows and digested the comment for a moment. "And what exactly is it now?" a tiny surge of adrenaline said, "I don’t know what show you’re watching back here, but Joy-Zee Shore Bagwell using my skull as a tee-ball was one of the more normal things that went down tonight. With barbed wire." "I don’t-" Toad started. "Barbed. Wire." Johnny’s exhaustion had been quickly replaced by annoyance. Toad was a hardcore legend, but Johnny Borealis had been gaining a reputation as a formidable warrior himself. In this business, many a confrontation is settled toe-to-toe even if there is no violence. Toad stared down his nose at the smaller man for a few seconds. With tension, he finally nodded and flared the nostrils a bit as he spoke, "Do it again...or do anything like that again...and I’ll make sure you’re locked in something a lot smaller than a dressing room." Johnny’s venomous impersonation of a smirk spoke for him, and Toad looked as though he were waiting for the smaller man to lose his temper. After a moment of staring, missing only a tumbleweed rolling by, the hardcore legend stepped around the TV Champion and continued on with his duties for the night. Johnny Borealis, American Hero, Your Guilty Pleasure, and GCW Superstar quietly walked out the door into the cool night air, letting it shut with a thud as the camera went to black and the crew raced to follow him. ---- ---- Forgive me father, for I have sinned. "Since coming back here, I guess I’ve seen myself as...a bit of a giver. People might hear that and think to themselves about how full of shit I am, but it’s true. Fact is, I give every bit of myself every minute that the cameras are on me. It’s for the fans, sure, but it’s also for everybody here." I hate this. I hate every minute of it because it’s the lie that pretends to be everything I want. All my synapses are firing, trying to tell me that this camera in this dark room is not God. It’s not a listener. It’s not welcoming my deepest thoughts. It’s simply a net for me to trap myself in, one that can and will create needless drama later. If I could resist it, I probably would have by now. "I guess when the cameras are off of me, I really am by myself back there. And it’s scarier than we let on. And I’m not just talking about the fact that there are scary men back there, men like Mayhem and Terror who will cut out your kidneys if it suits them. I mean it’s scary because of the difference between being out there, out in the ring, and being back where nobody remembers you." They want honesty about something? How about I’m honest with this fucking camera and explain how I can’t stop being Johnny Borealis when I want to stop. At what point am I "pretending" to be an unintelligent douche and at what point am I actually that person and pretending to be a real human being? "A few minutes before, you’re out in the ring and the people are going crazy. You go backstage, people give you pats on the back and you get a quick massage and then...what? It’s nothing. It’s death. You know?" The people love Johnny Borealis. I hate him. "It’s death because I leave absolutely every bit of it out there. And the last few people I’ve made connections with have all been injured...every single one of them isn’t around. Maybe I’m a hex, or maybe that’s a message. I tried to befriend Dominic Wall, Tempest, and Tessa Windsor to fill that void and all of them are gone, no different being gone months than being gone years like Jordan White or Rich Rollins. At the heart of it, you are alone. Some choose to ignore it, some lose their minds...I leave it in the ring. That way there’s nothing backstage left to die." ---- ---- 3. - Hint: he won’t want to talk about this either. Montage time. The empty hours of nothingness are erased and the audience is envious of it. Wouldn’t their lives be so much better if they could blip over their eight hour work days, save for a couple of funny moments, by playing some music over it? The envy of "reality" TV. Car, airport, plane, land, airport, car, traffic, caffeine pill, traffic, driveway, and he trudged up the steps and opened the door. "Johnny!" Sarah wrapped her arms around him and she tried to squeeze him dead because of the ecstacy of him being home. His daughter Sarah will do the same thing every time she sees him, though she can’t hug much farther up than his waist. His girlfriend Sarah, though, had a hell of a crush on his aorta. "Honey!...can’t...gonna...pass out-" instinctively he tapped on her shoulder, submitting to her boundless energy. Every time he walks in the door, she greets him like she’s meeting him backstage for the first time. "Sorry, I’m so sorry honey. Let me get that for you-" and before he could object she ripped the suitcase of his hand, threw it inside the door, and welcomed the man of her dreams into the house. The realty show two steps behind, as always, as she noted, "I see they’re still filming you." "Yeah, well, they like to show when I get home from a trip. It must make good TV or something." "Hmph," came the reply from the gorgeous blonde, "Well they’re not staying very long. I’ve got some plans that can’t be shown on basic cable, Johnny-boy." The slightest bit of red flushed his boyish face, though his smile was weak and thin. He placed a hand on each of her arms and looked her in the eye, ready to catch her should he break her heart. "I’m sorry, honey," he said with a look more akin to pity than love, "But my head is splitting and my eyes can’t stay open. I really need a good eight hours or I’m going to collapse." The damage was unmistakable, though Sarah D’Mateo hid it very well. Sadly, the reason she was so good at hiding it was because his life let her down so often. It wasn’t a conscious decision of his, it was the fact that she would allow herself to look this stupid over and over again in front of him. And now in front of a camera. The look in Sarah’s eyes was that of a flagellant sinner, hating herself for thinking only of herself. Yes, she wanted sex. Yes, she wanted him. But he just got off the plane and before that had just been in a fight. Seeing that look of self-loathing broke John’s heart every single time. Sarah was almost more like a dog than his girlfriend, running up so excited to see him and then obedient to the slightest whim of his long day. How much of an asshole was he for turning her into this? "Right," she caught herself looking vulnerable, her smile replaced and her mood artificially brightened, "We have all the time in the world. Well, I mean, before you have to leave again. But that’s not for a while." "Not for a while," he confirmed. "Good," she sounded like his confirmation helped fight back a panic attack, "We have all the time in the world." ---- ---- Back to the therapy session we go. "Sarah..." Where do I start with that name? I don’t even know the God damned universal alignment-shifting, reality fluctuating, mind-blowing reason for that name and why it’s haunting me at every turn in my life. "Sarah loves me." My mother was Sarah. My daughter is Sarah. The girl that would run through traffic to have sex with me is Sarah. Go ahead. Try and tell me that it’s a really weird coincidence. That I get too worked up over it, and that while it’s odd it certainly doesn’t mean that the universe is in some sick way centered around me and a bunch of people named Sarah. "And...Sarah..." Have you ever tried to say something out loud and not been able to? In the car, in your bed, in a soundproof booth in the middle of the woods, you still feel like you are wronging the universe and betraying all that is good if you actually mouth the words. If you say it, it exists. Once it exists it can never un-exist. Your words vibrate the molecules in the air, shatter tiny universes by bouncing sound waves off each other and making tiny supernovas smaller than the eye can see, and the effects are quite devastating every single time. Because every single time you come one step closer to believing what you say. "Sarah loves me...more than I love her." Fffffffffffffffuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuu- "Much more." … It’s done. The hard part is over. The scary thing is how easy it becomes from here on out. "Sarah D’Mateo would do anything for me. She’d do everything for me. If I asked her to cut off her hand, she’d learn how to live with only one. If I hinted that it’d make me happy to date a math genius, I have every confidence that she would bury herself in every textbook and formula until she was teaching classes at MIT. It’s no secret that our whole open relationship thing, the one where we can have sex with other people...that was my idea. And in her head, if it makes me happy to sleep with anybody I want...she’s willing to take that hit and tell herself she’s making me happy." I’ve known this for a long time. I think this is the first chance I’ve ever had to believe it, though. The permanence of the words that have been spoken lend a tangible dimension to them. They are real now. And unlike just about every other word that comes out of my mouth, they are the truth. And while some people believe that words are lost as soon as they’re spoken, I know otherwise. Because my words have been soaked up by the thick black curtains, they’ve reverberated on my own judge and jury eardrums, and they’ve been preserved perfectly on my priest as it records me. "The truth is she makes me happy because she’ll always be there. And that makes me feel like I’m wasting, absolutely ruining something incredible. At least Bagwell is open about what he does. I mean...Sarah just watched me have sex, on national TV, with some chick from Jersey. All just to take a cheap shot at a co-worker. And truth be told, the part that makes me really feel like a piece of shit...I never even thought about her while I was doing it. Not once." ---- ---- 4. - See, his ex named his daughter after his mother because he used to call her name out in his sleep and he told his ex that he was dreaming of his mom who died when he was young BUT he was actually dreaming about this other chick that he wanted because he couldn’t have her and he lied because he didn’t want to piss off his ex because she wasn’t his ex at the time and so he left because he didn’t know he had a daughter and six years later there’s two girls named Sarah that are alive and one mom named Sarah that is dead. Caught up? The camera was sure to zoom in on his face as he hugged his daughter hello. Music cut out, the lens shakes a bit because every little heartbeat from the operator was enough to bounce it as this zoom. This little trick gives a gritty, documentary-type feel to the shot. It’s used by producers and passed down to directors and created by cameramen to bring the show’s larger-than-life character right into your, yes your, living room. It makes the show feel like a God damn home movie, like when you were a kid. John buried his face in his daughters shoulder, trying so desperately hard to hide himself in the last vestige of purity and goodness in this world. His daughter Sarah always received the welcome that his girlfriend Sarah couldn’t be spared. Maybe Johnathan Fitzwillis could have made the woman who devoted herself to him happy, except that he gave every bit of himself to the little girl he created. In the world of Johnny Borealis, there is no medium, and there is no conserving. He gave every bit of love to his daughter and there was none left for the other Sarah in his life. Though the camera was zoomed out, he tried so desperately to whisper something to his daughter. Modern technology, though, beat him yet again as a directional microphone tapped into his secret words. "I’m sorry I wasn’t able to make the school play last week. Your mother told me you were fantastic," the consonants hit hard due to the pseudo-whisper, a raspy ghost-voice hollowed out of all the characteristic Johnny Borealis pitches and flares. "It’s okay, Daddy," she whispered back, for no reason other than the fact that he whispered to her. She was his forgiving angel, holding him more than he held her, and always absolving him of his sins. A shot from another direction showed Kate, his daughter Sarah’s mother, standing a few feet away. Kate never wanted to smile when Johnny hugged Sarah, when he let his walls come crashing down for the ten year old wonder, but a smile always appeared on her face. After precious seconds of embracing, he arose and looked at Kate with an attempt at a boyish smile. She had long since grown immune to his acts and just saw him as John, though to be fair that is what she liked most about him anymore. He still tried to put on a show for her, even though he knew he couldn’t. "Bedtime is nine. She has to be at school at seven-thirty, and she still has to complete her science homework," Kate explained in detail to the boy who was to watch her girl, "And make sure she eats a decent dinner." John eyed her with an innocent smile and puppy dog eyes and playfully remarked, "Right. Because I got these abs from pizza and wings. You think I eat anything bad for me, Kate?" "No, but I also know you have a hard time saying ‘no’ to your daughter," this time Kate looked Sarah in the eyes, "No pizza." The ten year old kicked the with one foot and muttered an, "Okay," as her father told her to go ahead and get in the car. Kate and John’s embodied love bounded into his car and closed the door, and the walls between the two adults came back up. For longer than intended he simply stared at her, remembering a time when the two of them could actually be honest with each other. Those days were dead, ten years gone and cold in the ground, long since looted of any value. There were so many things to say between them that saying absolutely nothing did it the best justice. "No more flat tires, John," she playfully reminded him of last week and his reason for missing Sarah’s school play, "At least not while she’s in the car." "Never," as all he said. She was expecting some sort of joke back, or even if he got defensive. His complete honestly, and his look of staring into the past, were enough to catch Kate off guard. "Right...well...Keith and I have plans. So I’ve got to start getting ready." The fact that John didn’t even flinch when she mentioned her boyfriend’s name was a testament to exactly how lost he was at that moment. He only stared with the slightest shadow of a smile, though a sadness invaded the eyes that she used to treasure so much. It felt almost as if it were the last time he would see her, though she knew that was impossible. What scared her, though, is that she realized he’d looked at her that way every time he’d seen her in the last month or so. Every conversation was John visiting Kate’s grave. "Bye John." "Right." ---- ---- Little bit left in the tank. "Kate is what could have been. My daughter Sarah is what I should have been." No sense beating around the bush any more, is there? The codex to Johnathan Fitzwillis is already inside that little box on a tripod, its lens aimed at everything I’ve ever tried to deny. Might as well go for gusto just like everything else in life. "I don’t think there’s any way I could make that more understandable. Kate gave me everything, and I honestly think I loved her completely. She was The One. And...I wanted to see if she was. I wanted to see if my world would collapse, if the sun would stop rising, and if my life would lose meaning without her. I wanted to verify that fact that all lovers have; that their love is immortal and essential to the very fabric of the universe itself. I wanted to know what the big red button did, and I decided the best way to know was to just fucking push it. I left. The world didn’t end." My electronic absolutionist pays full attention, never blinking or breaking, simply being what every person needs. It’s a conduit for my nephritis to flow out. "Life went on for me and come to find out, life went on for her too. Except she found out she was pregnant and she chose not to let me know. She decided she didn’t need Johnathan Fitzwillis and she raised our daughter for six years without me. And to be honest she probably did a better job than had I been there. When I found out, she had matured past the anger stage. She even got to the point where she offered me something that nobody in this world deserves. I got my second chance, my chance to choke, my reason to bleed. And sure enough I fucked that one up too...so that I could have something I couldn’t have. Let’s just say Kate wasn’t too thrilled when she found out I was having sex with the woman my daughter was named after who wasn’t my mother." I wonder if Kate would’ve been more or less disturbed if she had been my mother. "My daughter has been raised quite well without me. And now between Kate and...and Keith-" Shudder. "She’s got two people that, despite a lack of a quality Y-chromosome, are raising her well. And I think most of the reason I hate that asshole is because every time I look at him I realize that should’ve been me. Everything I’ve had. Everything I’ve ever worked for. Movies, theater, commercials, TV, and the end-all be-all of that wrestling ring, the second chance that I had handed to me to do what I do best...and I should have been a father. That was taken from me because of my own choice and because of hers, but it should have been me." Is that it? Did I get it all out? A quick systems check and we’ll see if there’s anything left. I’m sure I can at least find a racist joke somewhere in my body that I can tell the camera. Or some take on politics. If this is a reality show I probably ought to bitch and moan about my co-workers or something along those lines. If we’re done, I think there’s only one more thing to do. The producers aren’t going to be too happy that I break their confessional camera, but I don’t think they have much of a choice right now do they? They’ll yell at me and dock my pay and I might need to do another episode, and I’ll have to re-do this bullshit. This time, though, with all this off my chest I can fill it with the garbage that people want to watch. I can complain about Bagwell and the HDTV Title. I can rant and rave like I do on WorldWide. The memory card snaps like a twig, and I keep half of it. The data on the camera is erased, but the internet has taught me that nothing ever goes away. A couple dozen heels to the machine put it away, the last witness to Johnathan Fitzwillis. Someone should’ve told them. You can’t just open Pandora’s Box and expect it to close again.
