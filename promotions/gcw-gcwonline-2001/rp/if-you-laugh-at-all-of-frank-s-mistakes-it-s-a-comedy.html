If you laugh at all of Frank's mistakes, it's a comedy.

Date: Before Rampage If you laugh at everything he does right, it's a tragedy. She'd given him directions an hour before; he'd called back when he'd been close and she was waiting out front by the time he showed up. It was nothing, her house. Wide and tall, white, a porch fronted by those old, sqared and tapered southern gothic columns with railing in between. Surrounded by the woods. A yard out front that only grew dirt She was a speck against the house. Dwarfed. Crushed. They were into it before he made it to the porch. "You better have a good reason." "Shouldve asked that when I called." "I hope youre not gonna try to talk me back." "Look, E. Doubt youd wanna come." The corner of her mouth twitched. "Nah, though. Think I already gave keepin you around a shot and lost at that, yknow? Tried to be cool, but, shit. Aint happen. Shit. Lotta people can just, yknow, back out gracefully on that shit." "Not you, though." Frank shrugged and adjusted his skullcap. "Hah. Just cause I aint win, it dont mean that I cant make the other cat pay for it. You, uh. You back with Jimmy?" She took her time, pressed her palms to the vinyl siding and leaned back. "Why would you care?" "So it was about me not bein your boy, huh?" "Everythings not about you, Jordan." "Didnt say it was. Shit. Minute in there, you had me convinced this was about you’and I put some time into figurin your speech out, too, so it wasnt no once over bullshit." "You know, you think a lot." "Never heard that before." That was true. "Well, you do. Compared to how little you end up doing, you think a hell of a lot." Frank Black balled a fist in his pocket. "The fucks that supposed to mean, E?" "God, Jordan. Dont do that, dont act all aggressive. All I meant was that youre not some neanderthal." She softened her shoulders, let them roll down. Her neck took a light angle, green eyes wide in Frank's shadow. Frank, dark against the sunset, kept his back hard and his lips tight. She shook her head and touched her forehead. She glinted into the orange. She looked up. "Actually, you know? Fuck it. Go ahead. Get offended by it. If you wanna be offended that youre less of a dumbfuck dick than most guys Ive known, I wont stop you. I have had a shit day, and I am not going to deal with you turning into a stupid dick too." Her intentions be damned, given. "Why you think I came here?" "Sure wasnt to fuck me." He didn't sneer. "Might not be my type." "Oh. Fuck you, Jordan." She looked up to him, once, with her chin out and her lips between her teeth, before she turned the corner. The door slammed behind. Frank understood that he had said entirely the wrong thing. He didn't care like he hadn't cared when he decided to make the trip, and he started drawing targets on his mental punching bags for all the time spent in transit and away from the world he owed his hours to. He leaned a forearm against a pillar and looked out over the yard. Leaves and red dirt. Around the world in jets to find a dying piece of property across from an orchard of twisted trees. Gravel and sand and barbed wire between. Growing cold in the cut of wind against his red face. His veins pumped gasoline, acetylene, diesel; of course he dreamed of fire. Erika had a rifle ready. Frank watched the sun die and knocked backhand against the door. "I come in?" He shifted his eyes and heard nothing. He knocked again. "No." "So you want me to leave?" She didn't answer. He backed away and sat on the railing, pushed spine against one of the columns. Nothing in him had cooled. Something of his brain was baseline again, some reasoning portion that commented without the conviction to make any difference; it thought he might've scared her, might've run her off in terror at the idea of another psychotic wrestler. He thought about apologizing until he convinced himself that trying to get inside would just scare her more. It was an easy sell. It was dark out. It went black. He lit a joint when it was clear it wasn't getting any brighter. It burned almost even, ran barely before he took to tailoring the area with saliva to cool the smolder. Smoke in his lungs. Smoke in his head. The headlights, just the long glow from beyond the treeline, didn't bother him. He rolled his shoulders and paid his attention to the cherry until its orange glow was superseded; until he was haloed by high beams and fog lamps by a lifted truck shot diagonal across the driveway. He raised his forearm to his brow and puffed his chest out under the shadow. It was Jimmy, then, that broke from the beyond and into the halogen halo, cut himself a silhouette looming across the house as he stomped through the yard. He huffed. He puffed. "Fuck you doing here, asshole?" He bellowed. Frank perked his eyebrows and dropped his arm, fell away from the porch to halve the distance. He didn't have the height or the size, he remembered it and he could tell it from ten yards if he doubted; his gut kicked animal. Like electricity. He pinned the joint between twitching fingers. He flared lips. "God dammit. If you aint gon tell me, then get the fuck out my way." Frank snorted. Fumes. Jimmy laid a hand on Frank's shoulder, gripped and went to walk by. Frank's arm stopped him, palm flat to his rubbery chest, and shoved him back in place. They had a second, qiet, shaded by meat. Just eyes. Red. Jimmy dropped a boot heavy, smacked the joint from Frank's hand. Frank did not look down to see where it fell. Then there was the crack of Frank's right knuckles snapping into place against Jim's cheek. The big man staggered and Frank's world blurred, he drew another fist and followed with a left. His arms went piston, lost on raw power for speed, for rhythm. Jim pushed his heels into the earth and his shoulder to Frank. He shoved. Frank stumbled, lost his balance, dragged a knee and turned. Two fists between his shoulder blades; the aim was off, at least. It didn't comfort him as he dropped shin first to the ground. Jim leaned in over him, wrapped a heavy arm around his neck and laid in on it with a dim grin/grimace. Frank's career was a lie. He might've thought about the ropes, or the pulled punches, or the play brutality they put on. It didn't match the real thing, it couldn't have. He fought elbows backwards into cushioning fat and his world blurred again, into one fading shadow. Stupid. Stupid Frank. Like rage can do anything by itself. Nothing without a gameplan. He didn't fight anymore. A wrist awkward against the ground; how long he been in a choke, anyway? He tensed his legs and tried to keep upright under, 300 pounds? He didn't know. He had one hand in his pocket and heard someone talking, yelling into his ear, breathing hot. Dizzy. Jimmy, he remembered. His hand gripped tight and rose to his chest. Blood vessels burst in a wave of hisses; he pointed his key chain and swung up at Jim's arm, gouged and stabbed and dug until the grip loosened. Then he aimed for the face, caught something that Jim let go screaming. "Fucker. Mothafucker." His fingers were spread across his face, planted into his flesh. Blood pooled in recesses on his arm. Frank reverted. He planted himself sideways and crouched in. His legs tensed. Pressure. It was a superkick. It slammed sloppy into the side of Jim's jaw, heel caught between his molars spreading his mouth wide as it tore. He spiraled down and Frank followed, mounted him with a haze of elbows gone pendulum. Jim threw arms up, caught the blows anywhere but his face until Frank reared back and twisted to pound ribs. Jim covered them and Frank shifted to the head again without missing a beat. Three clean fists before Jim's arms came up. He flailed. Frank caught something bony against his nose in the fray. Turns out, Jim flailed with force, enough to break Frank's rhythm. He wrapped mitts around Frank's trapeziuses and they met halfway again, the crown of Jim's skull to the bridge of Frank's nose. Frank fell to his side, cradled his face and pointed an elbow at Jim. Just distance. Jim stood, circumvented the fight and grabbed Frank's hair to jerk him to his feet. Frank's nose fucking hurt. Just a bag of guts. Fluids that drain, tissues that tear. Jim drilling hooks into his stomach and haymakers through his face made that clear enough. He had exhaled and exhaled and there was nothing left to give. Jim wrapped a hand around his throat and shoved him backwards to the ground. The dog circled. Sqawking. Nipping. Frank admired the stars, whenever he got the chance. He didn't feel the mud caking to him. Didn't feel the blood hot against his lip. He'd taken a few bad hits. He'd given it something; he didn't think it was all he had and he felt’finally’bad for thinking that that might've been everything. He'd tried and he'd failed and it hadn't even mattered. A few bad hits. Just luck, really. He didn't hate Erika, and he didn't even hate Jim. Hell. Whatever. It wasn't up to Frank who Erika was talking to. He didn't have to meter other people's bad ideas. He just. He just really wanted to punch that chubby sonofabitch until his hands hurt and then until his hands broke. All that shadowboxing. All that professionalism, that need to win without injuring; it had just built something in him. He hated the faking. He hated going through the motions without that bloodlust behind them. His muscles had been taunted, his muscle memory tainted; he hated that. He hated himself. Hated the swelling in his head. Hated the ache in his teeth. Hated that he lost his skullcap somewhere in that. Hated the lungs that spasmed to draw anything from the air. Hated the copper taste. Hated the dull, the tight, the emptiness in his gut as he sat up. Hated the resistance in his straightening knees, hated his legs bucking to support him. Hated the breather he took as his spine loosened up. He was ambivalent about the wind against his face on the way to Jimmy, the coiled steel in his forearms, the running. He enjoyed the kidney punch. Jim did not appear to; he collapsed face first on the porch, his shins or his knees cracked into the edges of the steps as he dropped. Frank returned the choke, made a fist around his bicep and locked it in hard to drag the sandbag away from the house. He spun, hefting him into the mud. It was the impacts that he loved. A soccer kick to Jim's side. A stomp to his back. Crouching. Forearms to his shoulders. Standing. A Puma to the face sprawled him; he pushed back up. A shin to the ribs. A stomp. A kick. A smash. Slam. Stomp. Kick. Smash. Crack. Pthap. Crunch. Impact. Impact. Impact. He was stout, Frank would give him that’he stayed up through the most of it, kept rising until the point of Frank's elbow met the side of the neck. Frank sat down with it. Jim rolled to his back, eyes narrow and mouth drawing. "Fuck. You." Frank laughed, propped his elbows on his knees and turned to Jim. "Fuck you. Callin me an asshole an shit." It wasn't that easy to stand up. Frank did it anyway, noticed Erika standing in the door frame and wondered how long she'd been there. He dragassed over, left leg just less agreeable than the right and dog in orbit around his stagger; he supported his weight against a hand on a column. He couldn't read her. Her face was red; but she was pale and her skin always flushed if she'd seen the sun. Her arms were crossed under her chest’wearing his shirt, though. Her back was straight. He hung his head and caught his breath. Adrenaline faded into an afterglow that just couldn't dull pain the same as the real thing. He looked back up and tried to smile. He wasn't sure how many of the muscles agreed with him. "Jesus, Frank. Are you alright?" He jerked his mouth open to the side and feigned a nod. "Is he, alright?" He glanced over his shoulder; back to her with a shrug. She brought him inside to nurse his wounds; Jim was gone before anyone thought to look for him. Frank's night was qiet, onsets and ice packs at a kitchen table piled with mail. Finding Erika took concentration, ears that weren't bubbling pumped magma, eyes that weren't pinching shut in the light. He didn't see much of her until morning. At least she gave him that. Her puppy, however’the highpitched constant kept him from ever getting into his rest. "Jordan, sweetie. Do you want to tell me what the fuck happened last night so I can turn my phone back on and start to deal with it?" He blinked. He was already awake in some technical way, like meditation or an open eye coma. He had heard her, it just took time for her words, her soft voice, to make it through the sludge. "You dont wanna know." "No. I do, because it was in my yard with my brothers drinking buddy who just happens to be friends with most of my friends. So, yes. I want to fucking know." "Aight." He dropped his foot from the chair it had slept on, flexed it against the knee and let the day wash over him. Something was new. He would try blunt honesty. "You fuckin ditch me without talkin about it first, right before I gotta head off to Japan. You dont, your ass dont take the time to like, say youre swingin back by your old crib to see whats good. You just gimme a fuck it an roll. Thats, thats aight. I guess. You werent my girl, so aint like I got much room. Just, after that, you go a week without answerin your shit. I dunno. Maybe its work stress or somethin too, but, yknow. Sometimes you just wanna fuck somethin up, and then I get up with you, get here, an your boy just comes by while youre inside sulkin, an he starts talkin shit too?" "Hold on, hold on. Hold the fuck on, Jordan. Jimmy isnt my boy, and I dont even fucking like him that much, so dont pull that shit." "Fuck ever, girl. You know what I mean." "Jordan." She pulled the chair to her and stepped around it, sat down. Fingers to her brow, eyes tight. Dropped her hand. Looked at Frank. "Okay, Im sorry. Im pissed off at you, very fucking pissed and dont think you can forget because I wont, but could we just, calm down here?" He nodded. "Im sorry about locking you out." "Didnt know you locked it." "Sh. Look. After all the violent idiots Ive dated in my life, I am just a little tired of all that egotripping bullshit." "You knew I wasnt gonna hit you." "How would I know, huh? Maybe youre some weird, passive aggressive freak. I dont know. You wouldnt be the first one to slip by." "That what you think of me?" "No. But you dont want to know what I really think about you." "Like that, huh?" "Yes, it is." She crossed her arms. "I do like you, if that matters and I didnt think you were going to hit me anyways, but, no. Thats not what this is about. You get me all excited about you coming by, like, maybe somethings finally going to happen, then you get into an argument with me and tear up my fucking yard with one of my exs because you wanted to disprove all the nice things I thought about you? And you didnt even wait to see why he was coming by? No. I like you, but there is absolutely no way I am going to keep making the stupid mistakes Ive made. Its, its shit like this thats why Im living in a goddamned shack on a dirt road, Jordan, and Im not gonna let you fuck my life up now that Im starting to get it straightened out." "Fuck." "Yeah. Fuck." Cast glares on linoleum floors smeared with Georgia mud. "I mean. I maybe even think he deserved it, but, like. Jesus. I have to deal with this. You dont. Hes my brothers friend. Hell. Hes my fucking friend. You? You're going to be in Japan in, what, within a day? God damnit." He stretched a hand out in front of his chest, palm down, and bent his fingers to spread the red cracks of his knuckles. He understood complete, without qestion, and unfailing. He'd delayed, wasted time with Ash and lost his chance with Erika’that was that, then. Something about trying to do the right thing, something about trying to be the good guy, something about not trying to hurt anybody and failing tremendously at everything he'd charged himself with’all before he even started to fuck up. Half of his mouth tried to smile. "Can I tell you somethin? Like, before I leave." "Lemme guess, youre gonna tell me how you love me and Im going to tell you to fuck off. Whatever, go ahead." He looked down, almost laughed. "Nah, none of that bullshit. I was just, I was really into you. Feel kinda bad about fucking this up." "Yeah. You should." He lifted his chin; no solace, then. He stood and followed the trails of mud back outside, found his cap in the sun. He picked it up, only kept it with him so she wouldn't have to throw it away herself. She called him an asshole as he drove off. It would've bolstered his masculinity some, like the boost could match the bruises. Back in Japan within the day; he'd packed light anyway, left a room waiting with all of his things. He dreamed of it through customs. Lusted after long white curtains as a desk clerk tried to find his passport picture in the swelling lump of his face. Knees bent to his chest, firing agony on the flight; he needed that allencompassing bed more than he'd needed food or shelter or heat. He didn't sleep again until he found it. He woke up and called Sage before there was time for the opposite to happen. He waited’five rings in. "Fuck me running’Frank Black calls me out of nowhere, just to see whats good? I never expected it." "Yeah. Aint why Im callin." "I knew that. What you got?" He scratched the back of his neck and sat up under the sheets. "Last night, or yesterday, or whatever, I kinda. Well. Somebody got fucked up an I was wonderin if, you know, you" "Could cover your ass?" "Yeah, yeah. That" "How bad?" "He drove off, I mean. Couldnt be that bad." "Tell me you had a good reason, in that case." "Man, he" "Didnt say tell me your reason. Just tell me it was a good one." He thought. "Yeah. Was as good as they get, at least." "Who?" "Jimmy Hall, lives in" "I remember him." "So you got me?" "This time. Yeah." "Word." "This how you prepare for matches these days?" "You know it. Said you trusted me." "Hm. Good point. Hell, used to end up in that sorta shit myself anyways. Youre good. Ive got you covered." "Word up." "That everything, then?" "Yeah. Thats straight." "Let you get back to. Well. Whatever the fuck you do." "Peace." The connection died. Frank fell back into his safety net.
