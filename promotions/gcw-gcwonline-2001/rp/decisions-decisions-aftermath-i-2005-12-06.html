Decisions, Decisions: Aftermath I

6 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Dec. 6 Well, I guess there’s no beating around the bush, is there? I went to Vermont with the intentions of settling a score. Of breaking the impasse of two straight draws. Of beating Richard Rollins on the mountain that I know as well as anyone on this Earth. Of course, that’s not what happened, is it? What happened was that after one of the longest and most bitter wars of my career, I put The Renegade in the Cactuar Crossface in the middle of a wrestling ring one thousand feet above the ground. The move that was heretofore inescapable. I had both of Rollins? arms trapped, and I was pulling back with everything I had. But somehow, he got to his feet. Guess it’s a product of me being so scrawny. He climbed the ropes, with me still in the hold, and he came off that turnbuckle knowing damn well the match was his. Losing is bad enough. After three years in the business, I’ve lost about sixty, maybe seventy times. It happens. It sucks, but it happens. Never, though, have I lost so cruelly. Middle of the fucking ring with the Cactuar Crossface. If you’d have told me that would happen before I stepped foot on the mountain, I would have kissed you. That was the optimal situation, the best possible thing that could happen. That was what was supposed to end Rich Rollins and confirm to the world that I was the best. Of course, we all know what happened from there. Newsflash, world. On November 27th, 2005, Rich Rollins beat the Final Fantasy in the middle of the ring. Where the Hell do I go from here? ------------------------------------------------------------------------ Sighing deeply, Seymour Almasy put the pen down, leaving his thoughts for the evening scrawled on four or five pages of tiny hotel paper. There was a positive in all of this, somewhere. There had to be. It was now December, over a week since the fateful Anarchist’s Elbow that had destroyed Seymour’s GCW singles unbeaten streak. Global Championship Wrestling was still riding the euphoria from the event, by all accounts one of its biggest ever. Michael “Spitfire” Stevens had finally achieved his dream, defeating LeStatt Knight in the middle of the ring to become GCW’s World Champion. Of course, on Gagazet, he hadn’t known this. Both he and Rollins had been airlifted off the mountain and treated for things from the usual cuts and bruises to early-stage frostbite. Hey, it’s Vermont in November, of course it’s really friggin? cold. Kept overnight for precautionary measures, he was back on the road with GCW a few days later. WorldWide in Atlantic City loomed in the near future, but there were more pressing matters on the Final Fantasy’s mind. Namely, a contract. “Yevon, is Caldera serious about this?” From the desk of Steven Caldera himself, the document had been faxed to him by new VP of Talent Relations Leland O’Toole early last night. For a while, Seymour had been concerned about what happened to Jonathan Calver, only to find out that all was well, and Calver had been shifted to an equivalent role elsewhere in the company and gotten a nice raise. After all Calver had done for him, Seymour thought, the poor guy deserved one. At any rate, Seymour had been offered a new contract. And, to put it mildly, this one made Caldera’s last offer seem like Monopoly money. “That is a very large number of zeroes,” was about all that a dumbstruck Almasy could manage. But it was more than that, so much more. It was videogame deals. As per the terms of this contract, Seymour’s face would appear on the cover of the newest game “GCW NC-17”, due out on the X-Box in Q3 2006. It was action figures. The fateful meeting between he and Rollins would be immortalized in the form of a Mount Gagazet Deathmatch playset, complete with plastic mountain, double ring setup, and goats that, when a button was pressed, chanted “HOLY SHIT!” Even after losing that match, he had to admit that the idea of a toy of it was pretty friggin? sweet. Almost more importantly, it was the earnestness of the offer that caught Seymour off guard. While other fedheads had made him offers, it was solely based on the strength of the name that Seymour carried with him. Clearly, he’d been wrong when he’d assumed Caldera was just a hard-ass businessman who didn’t give a shit about his talent. The accompanying two page letter had convinced Seymour of that. It seemed that Caldera had practically poured his heart out on the page. Businessman or not, Caldera gave a damn about GCW. He wanted to see it rise, wanted to see it be the best promotion in the world. And he wanted Seymour to be a part of it. He took a deep breath, almost as if astounded by it all. Without being an arrogant ass, he knew that his presence drew attention. The run he’d had in GCW had helped, at least in some way, with the company’s rise to prominence. For the most part, he was well-liked and respected in the back, a model citizen who didn’t go out and make an ass of himself in bars. Too much, anyway. But at the same time, even with all of that on the table, he felt the compulsion to move on. He had been called a “fedwhore” by many in the business, a fact seemingly backed up by the fact that during his PTC tenure, he’d called the UWF, GCW, GW, LOW, and the DWO home. “Fedwhore” or not, Seymour had a strong wanderlust. Probably due to the fact that he loved RPGs so much, he’d guessed. No hero stayed in one place too long. They went to a town or dungeon, stayed until they’d taken care of business, and then left with sword drawn, ready for the next challenge. But in GCW’he’d failed at his goal. DWO and LOW had closed before he could really go after what he wanted, but GCW stood out as perhaps the only case where, given the proper time and preparation, Seymour Almasy had failed at doing what he wanted. What was left for him in GCW? Sure, he could go after the World Championship, or the US Title, or the TV or Cruiserweight belt if he really wanted, but there was no sense of urgency, no ultimate goal like there had been in his quest to defeat Rollins. If he really wanted, he could have simply refocused his efforts to take down Rollins, but somehow, his sense of honor prevented him from doing so. The Renegade had beaten him, and had many other things on his mind, such as Angelo Deville and defending the PTC Global Title against the Uber Judge. He wanted to get up and go, to find a new quest. But Steven Caldera and GCW had given him millions of reasons to stay. And that wasn’t even counting the seven figure contract. At least he didn’t have to make the decision right away. Caldera had made that much perfectly clear. After all, Seymour was under contract until January 31st, 2006. And there would be plenty to keep Seymour’s mind occupied while he made his decision. Why, this coming week on WorldWide, he had a test that he had been preparing for in the back of his mind. To take on a man who had been as dominant in GCW as he had. A man who had ALSO lost at NC-17. And a man who, by Seymour’s reckoning, he was facing one show too late. Even so, the motivation was still there for Seymour, by all accounts a man who lived to right wrongs and make sure evildoers were put in their place. In a scant few days, Seymour would go one on one with the man who had tried to ruin Eva VanLorne’s life The man who had ruled on high as a dictator until a bloodied challenger had brought him down. Yes, Seymour had much work to be done. Because, whether he would resign or not, he now had a new, short term goal. Defeat LeStatt Knight.
