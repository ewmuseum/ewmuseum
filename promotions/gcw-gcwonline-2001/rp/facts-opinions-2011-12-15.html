Facts & Opinions

15 December 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/15/11 It was like waiting for an exam to be graded. Johnathan Fitzwillis’ foot tapped and tapped as he lay back in his chair, trying to get comfortable. He would let out an audible sigh, he would crack his knuckles, and he would pick up a nearby book and flip through its pages. Anything to telegraph to his lawyer just how incredibly bored he was. With a relaxing, arching stretch, Fitzwillis let out a yawn that was a little too loud and a little too drawn out. One eye popped open to see if his legal attorney had taken notice. Derek Harmon simply continued to the next page of the large stack of papers he’d been examining. With a disgruntled cough, Fitzwillis’ eyes rolled and he sat up straight. "So..." was supposed to be the end of the statement. Derek was supposed to take the cue and snap out of his intent reading. Alas, Johnathan realized he had to continue, "...anything left out?" Harmon licked a finger, turned the page, and held an index finger up that told his client to hold on. Continuing along, Fitzwillis threw his head back in the complete agony that is waiting. "Jesus. Couldn’t you read the thing before I got here?" There was a small clearing of Harmon’s throat, but no other sound to indicate he heard Johnathan. A hand reached out and found a mug of black coffee, still warm enough that he could enjoy the taste, and after sipping it was set back down on its coaster. Still without looking, Derek adjusted its position on the coaster to make sure it was precisely centered. Then the coaster itself was nudged just the tiniest bit to find its proper place on the glass desk. "Alright. Idea," the man known as Johnny Borealis sprung back up and started to stand, "I’ll step outside and make this quick phone call. Let them know I’ll be late to the set. Filming a commercial for TGI Fridays, gotta pretend I can stomach a sizzler. So you just keep...on..." The motion was simple. Derek Harmon’s index finger went up in the air, and very deliberately it turned to point straight down. His eyes did not leave the page, but his message was very clear. Sit your ass down. And with a huff, Fitzwillis obeyed, making sure to lean back and cross his boots on top of Derek Harmon’s desk. For the briefest of heartbeats it seemed Harmon would object, but he simply turned the packet over and made a few marks with his pen. Johnathan crossed his arms and submitted to waiting. To say he didn’t look himself was an understatement. Boots untied, five o’clock shadow at nine in the morning, and dark purple pools under his eyes blemished the once-pristine face of Johnny Borealis. His classic button-up shirt had been replaced by a simple black tee, and his designer cargo pants looked like they hadn’t been laundered since Rampage. Harmon had taken stock of all this when Fitzwillis first strolled in to his office (forty-three minutes late, at that), but tried to think nothing of it. It didn’t matter to him whether his client looked presentable today, so long as he followed his instructions in legal matters. Crossing his last "t," Harmon put the packet of papers down on his desk and folded his hands on top of it. "The police report," a small nod down to the pages he had been reading, "determines that the driver of the pickup truck is likely cleared of legal responsibility. That the tire marks on the road and the force of impact on Mister Jankowski’s body lead them to believe he was travelling at the appropriate speed." "Yes. I know. I told you-" "Furthermore," Harmon pushed past the petulant protest from Fitzwillis, "the statement taken from Miss Katelyn Walsh indicates that you and Mister Jankowski were engaged in a fist fight, and that you threw him in to the road." "Hip toss," he corrected, "It was a hip toss, not a throw. I’ve gone over this-" "So what the court is going to try to determine is why you...tossed...Mister Jankowski into the path of the oncoming pickup truck," there was a very slight hesitation before he continued, "And whether it was intentional." His right foot wiggling back and forth while crossed on the desk, Johnathan Fitzwillis raised his eyebrows and waited to see if his lawyer was going to say anything else. After six or seven seconds of dead silence, he fidgeted his way to better posture. "Christ, Derek, was there any reason I had to sit and watch you read that? I’ve gone over it with you-" "You needed to remain here because I have found it extremely difficult to reach you, John," Derek lectured whilst carefully placing the papers in a binder, and placing the binder in its proper place in his desk, "And I am not certain you understand the weight of the situation. Get your feet off my desk." "Keith’s dead," the words fell like a mess as soon as they hit the air, his boots clunking down to the floor, "Not really that hard to grasp." "He’s deceased. And the authorities intend to hold either him, or you, or the driver of the pickup responsible." This time Fitzwillis had nothing to retort. He glanced about the stark, cold room for something to distract him. Something to talk about or bring up that could derail the topic. "It was an accident," Fitzwillis placed a staccato on each word, "I mean-...what do I have to do to convince you that it was self defense. Reflex." "Oh you don’t have to convince me of anything, John," Derek re-folded his hands on top of his desk and looked his client square in the eye, "I defend you. That is my job. But I need to know all of the facts in order to adequately do my job." "And?" he indicated to the desk, "There’s your facts. You’ve only been pouring over them for a fuckin’ hour." Harmon found his cup of coffee right where he left it and enjoyed a small sip. Looking in the mug, he realized he only had a small amount left and decided to finish it off while it retained some heat. His empty mug was replaced exactly where it belonged as he chewed on his thoughts. "There is a large portion of the story missing. I understand that you and Mister Jankowski were both vying for the affection, if I may use the phrase, of Miss Walsh. And that you two had been...shall we say, competing for some time." Fitzwillis managed an exhausted shrug. "Yeah. He was dating my ex. So?" "Some would call that motivation." "Well you can ask Kate if you want," he still slouched, but his muscles tensed noticeably at her name, "I just got done telling her I’d let them be. You know. Happy and all that." "Mmhmm..." Derek took his pen and scratched that down on a nearby legal pad, to follow up on later, "Then I have to ask, John...how exactly did the fight start that night?" "...what do you mean? He started swinging at me." "Yes but why?" The incredulous look on Johnathan’s face showed his dismay. He searched for the words to hide his hesitation, but in the end he shook it off. The icy stare he threw at his lawyer did not exactly instill trust. "I don’t know. He was drunk, he’d lost his job. He was pissed." More notes and more nodding, that was the response. Without looking John in the eye, Derek was pressed further. "You two didn’t have any...any sort of conflicts that had come to a head?" "No." "You didn’t goad him in any way. Maybe flaunt the loss of his job?" "No. Not at all." Harmon nodded some more, then paused and looked up at his client. "Are you sleeping well?" Eye bloodshot, his face drawn tight, the boyish smile of Johnny Borealis somehow managed to shine through. Tainted though it may be, he was able to summon those pearly whites at a moment’s notice. He reached in to his coat pocket, producing a small bottle. "Yeah," holding up the bottle, he made sure to show the label that said Ambien to Harmon, "Don’t even need a prescription." The look of disapproval told all, and for the first time today Derek let it show that he might actually care for his client’s well-being. After all, dead clientele doesn’t pay the rent on a corner office in the downtown area. "Have you considered seeing someone?" "I do see someone. Usually two or three a day if I can help it." "Alright, listen John," the game had grown old very quickly, "I understand that you’re used to walking all over your agent, but I am not him. And I’m not impressed by this little game you’re playing. But we have got to clear up some facts about this case in front of us because if we don’t, you could easily be looking at jail time." "He threw the first punch!" "I understand that, and I can make a case for self defense if his family presses charges," Derek tried his best to maintain an air of calm, "But if you aren’t telling me everything, then I can’t give you the best defense possible. So I’ll ask you plain and simple; do you know why Keith took that first swing at you?" ---- He’s a little drunk, but nothing too bad. Probably just been keeping a nice buzz all day long, mulling over what he’s going to do with his life since he got shitcanned. One day you’re the hardest worker at the firm, the next you’re out on your ass and you owe a whole lot of people money. And you’re too busy wondering where you went wrong to even consider that your private investigator switched sides. Always count on a mercenary to turn on you. For the first time the two of us are having a conversation like adults, rather than children fighting over a sandbox toy. Two men that spent the better part of four years squabbling over a woman, now our competing territorial pissings were a thing of a past. It’s for the briefest of moments that I think maybe I’m growing up. God, what a great thing this would be had it happened naturally. But instead of maturation or realizing that we’re in this life together, I had to be me. I had to be competitive. I guess we’re both competitive by nature, a businessman and a showman, and with no foundation of friendship we were destined to always be at each other’s throats. Or maybe it was all me. Maybe I just couldn’t let go of the fact that she and I were a thing of the past. Johnny Borealis. Why would I ever grow up? In a whirl I make the decision. Like calling up that girl with the crush or clicking on the handcuffs or jumping out of the plane. The adrenaline rush is unparallelled, and the excitement stirs the pot, and a simple sentence shatters the safety of making peace with an enemy. "Keith...it was me. I hired an investigator...the same one you had tail me," the words are tough to get out, so I just charge through them without further hesitation, "He found the accounts. I exposed them." I’d wronged him. He wronged me first, but I wronged him better. His private eye took pictures of me with an underage girl, and dug up her medical records from an overdose hospital visit...pretty messy stuff for a public figure to be involved in. With some money and some gusto, I hired the same man to find something, anything on little Mister Perfect. He found accounts managed by him that were known to be poisonous, toxic assets passed from the firm to investment partners that were poised to wreck the lives of thousands, all with only his name on them. What I should’ve done, what a man would’ve done, was warn him, call the company out, and help so many people. What a child does is break a toy he can’t play with. His life in shambles...I can’t blame him for wanting to throw that first punch. Nor the ones that followed. ---- "I wish I knew," Fitzwillis did his best to play it cool, "I said hi to him. Then he threw a punch." "I see." "I don’t know. Booze does shitty things to people, right?" it was more of a statement than a question, "I mean maybe he thought I was mocking him or-...I don’t know." Derek Harmon wasn’t sure if John thought he was stupid, or if his client actually thought he was that good of an actor. It remained that there were facts missing from this case, and Harmon intended on finding them out one way or another. "Alright. I think we’ve covered everything we’re going to cover." "Thank fuckin’ God," Fitzwillis popped up again, eager to leave as soon as he could, "Enough of these shenanigans." "Before you go," Harmon piped up just as his client made his way to standing, "...I’d like you to speak with someone. A professional." One raised eyebrow showed his complete disapproval of the idea. "What...kind of professional?" "Someone to...well, maybe to help you get some sleep," Harmon put on his best diplomatic face to ease the message, "So that maybe you can ditch the Ambien." "Seriously? A shrink?" "A counselor. Can you do that for me? Just one...one thing to help me out. Please?" He sighed a heavy sigh and dropped his shoulders a bit in resignation. If he refused, he’d only have to hear it again and again and again...like coming in to go over the events this morning. "Fine. Sounds like a fucking blast."
