Songs of Experience (WW85, vs. Lia Ambrosi)

Date: 2/26 == Backstage, after WorldWide == As Seymour Almasy made his way back behind the curtain at WorldWide, he knew that his career was forever changed. Finally, after a six year career, the Final Fantasy was dead and buried, replaced by a man who once again had the desire to compete and reign triumphant flowing through his blood. He had no desire to stick around. Too many likely angry coworkers. Too many people who, right now, were likely sitting in the locker room and wondering what the Hell had happened. Seymour moved through the backstage passageways quickly, looking to make his way to the loading dock in the back, where his chariot would be awaiting him. Adrenaline thundered through his body as he made his way through the halls. Yevon, but it had been too long since he stood in the middle of a ring before a capacity crowd. More importantly, it had felt better than he'd ever imagined to tell everyone off. To get years of ill will off of his chest. He felt light. Free. Ready to tackle any challenge that GCW could possibly put in front of him. The loading dock was a sight for sore eyes. Parked mere feet from the curb was a long black limousine, poised to whisk Seymour back to the hotel for a good meal and a well-deserved night's sleep. Standing outside of it was perhaps one of the few friends that Seymour Almasy had left. "I'll be damned," he chuckled, as the older man approached. "Never thought I'd see you again, Jon." The former Vice-President of Talent Relations, Jonathan Calver, merely smiled, reaching out his hand for a handshake which was readily accepted. "Never say never in this line of work, Seymour," Calver chided, good-naturedly, before handing over a bottle of water that Seymour gratefully took. "With you back, I'm on Almasy patrol again. Caldera says I'm the only person who can deal with you." "Not too far off from the truth on that one. 'Specially now," Almasy grinned. "So, you got any good news for me before I get to go enjoy an above-average hotel steak and a nice bed?" "Well," the older man began, shaking his head, "I've got good news and bad news." "Love conversations that start like that," Seymour grimaced. "Let's hear it. Better I know about it now than later." "Good news first then: Caldera's got you main eventing WorldWide next week." "So much for getting to go crush the future of GCW," Almasy sighed. "I was rather looking forward to that. Still, a main event's a main event. I suppose the bad news is who I'm facing?" "I don't mean to be a killjoy," Jonathan replied, "but your opponent DOES sort of have your number..." The self-professed Standard Bearer of GCW fell silent for a few moments. That only meant one person. "I'm back a week," he groused, "and he gives me Lia in my first match?" Calver simply nodded. "Really, are you surprised by this?" That was a fair point, Seymour had to admit. This WAS Steven Caldera. A man who thrived on putting together big matches. A man who loved controversy. Loved the spotlight, and loved doing anything he could to stir the pot. This was par for Caldera's course. "No," Seymour said, quietly. "But let's get out of here for now. You and I have quite a bit to discuss. I've had a trip in mind for a while now, and considering the new circumstances, I think it's high time that I make it." "A trip?" Calver's eyebrows arched, as he opened the door for Seymour to get into the limo. Seymour merely grinned and stepped inside, saying no more for the time being. It seemed that his past was going to catch up with him far sooner than even he'd expected. As such, there was only one thing that he could truly do. Confront his past head-on. Look at it dead in the eye and laugh in its face. And that meant that there was only one place he had in mind to go. == The Outskirts of Toronto, Ontario, Canada == The rental car slowly drove its way down the road on the outskirts of Toronto, Ontario, Canada. Behind the wheel sat Jonathan Calver, the once and now current business manager of one Seymour Almasy. And for the life of him, Calver could not understand why he was driving his client through this particular part of the Great White North. “Seymour,” the former GCW Vice President of Talent Relations asked, “why in the name of God did you want me to take you here?” The man in the passenger’s seat merely smiled. With the window down, he could barely hear his driver, but he could hear well enough to understand the other man’s question. It was a good one, as far as questions went. “Because,” the long-haired man replied, grin still dancing about his lips, “one can never truly move on to the future unless one conquers the past. There are only two structures on this Earth that fill me with a sense of dread or discomfort. One of them is the house I shared with Laura on Long Island. And the other?” The smile on Seymour’s face only grew as Calver pulled the vehicle up in front of a mansion. The giant structure seemed abandoned, with its windows boarded up and great lawn ridden with weeds. Indeed, the only sign that it was owned by anyone in particular was a security guard standing out front, by a gate that was padlocked shut. Almasy turned to Calver, and watched the latter man’s face pale. “You may find this hard to believe, Jonathan, but it looked more frightening when it was being lived in. At least now, there aren’t two Hell hounds roaming the grounds.” “It’s’Lord.” Calver found himself lost for words as he looked at the home in which Almasy had lived for months. “Impressive, isn’t it,” Seymour responded, shaking his head. “I lived in the basement. Yevon?” Seymour pulled up the sleeve of his shirt, to show Jonathan a goosebump-flecked arm. “It’s been months and months, and this place still gives me chills.” A wry smile creeped onto the corners of his mouth, and he opened the door, stepping outside into the freezing Ontarian air. “Welcome to the McFarlane Estate, Mr. Calver. I came here in 2006 with the hopes of reinvigorating my career. I left with it on the verge of burial.” Shivering even with several layers of clothing on, Seymour fixed his gaze on the security guard, boots clomping as he walked over to the other man. Jonathan could only look on in confusion as Almasy extended a hand to the guard, shaking the other man’s hand. “Who owns this old place,” Seymour asked, knowing the answer in the pit of his stomach before the reply even came. Calver stumbled his way out of the car over to Seymour, and sharply elbowed his business partner in the ribs. “What the Hell are you doing,” Jonathan hissed, into Seymour’s ear. Almasy’s face remained calm as he heard the security guard’s response. “Miss Dawn McFarlane. The McFarlane Estate has been in her family for years. ? Even though he knew the answer, the sound of the name still sent a shudder through Seymour’s lithe form. Of all the people Almasy had met in the sport over his five year career, none left him quite as conflicted as Dawn did. “Where can I find her,” Seymour asked, forcing himself to be calm. “I have an offer to make her for this place.” Jonathan Calver went several shades of white, but Seymour waved him off with a sweep of his hand. Calver’s mouth opened to protest, but he instead returned to the car to stew in his own juices as Almasy continued conducting business. “She moved out and closed the place about a year ago,” the guard said. “She’s moved out to a small town in British Columbia, but I’ve got no idea where. Your best bet’s probably to go there and ask around, as knowing her, I find it highly unlikely she’s got a listed phone number.” At that, the long-haired man chuckled a bit. Understatement, that last bit. “I will go do that. Thank you.” Nodding, Seymour turned on his heel, returning to the still-pale Jonathan Calver pacing in the freezing cold. “Mr. Calver, please take me to the airport. You are to remain here . Go get a hotel room in Toronto after you drop me off. I will need you here in case my offer is accepted.” Choking slightly, Jonathan nodded his head. “I will do so,” he said weakly, hesitating a bit before continuing on, “but I can’t imagine for the life of me why you would want to buy this place, considering your history with it.” Seymour shrugged his shoulders, looking out over the vast grounds of the estate. “We need a base of operations. Remember, it’s not just the two of us involved. I have a stipend as per my GCW contract to hire staff and other personnel. There will be more of us, and we will all need a place to stay in close proximity. Besides?” Almasy’s face went stoic as he stared at the boarded-up windows of the mansion, remembering all that had occurred there. Things he wanted to remember. Things he didn’t. “I came here to revive my career,” he whispered, echoing his earlier words. “I left here with my career on life support. And I’m returning here to start over again.” With no further comment, Seymour opened the door to the car, returning to the passenger’s seat. Jonathan was slower to re-enter the vehicle, taking a long moment to look at the McFarlane Estate, a shiver going down his spine. As far as he was concerned, there was no possible way that this could end well. == British Columbia, Canada == Surveying himself carefully in the mirror of his small hotel room, Seymour Almasy decided that he was ready. Finding information out about Dawn hadn’t been as difficult as he’d expected. Seemed she was something of a local celebrity. Besides, even if she hadn’t been, most people didn’t forget seeing a tall, attractive blonde with legs that went on for miles. Lucky for him, that. At any rate, Miss McFarlane apparently now played bass and sang for a local underground rock band. That seemed like the best way to approach her. After all, it probably wasn’t the best strategy for a man who had once proclaimed his undying affections for her to go knock on her front door. “Strike probably,” Seymour winced, imagining that scenario ending with a boot planted rather firmly to the side of his head. “Definitely not the best strategy.” So, instead, he would go see her perform. There were enough posters plastered around the town to make finding the particular club very easy. And going to a club, for Seymour anyway, meant only one way to dress. During their period of friendship post marriage, Laura Winters had taken to calling it Seymour’s “I’m going to be a total slut tonight” outfit, and the description wasn’t far from the truth. It all started with the ridiculously tight leather pants that Seymour almost had to shoehorn himself into. The shirt always varied, generally he wore something to make himself as identifiable as possible at less fancy establishments. He considered buying band apparel at the show, but disregarded that thought. After all, he didn’t want to be too blatant of a suck-up. And considering what he wanted, sucking-up was going to be an absolute necessity. So instead, he went with a simple tight, black t-shirt. Neutral. Less likely to give her a reason to be annoyed. As if she wouldn’t be annoyed with his presence to begin with. He spun around in the mirror once, favoring the mirror with a tight grin. Oh yes. Perfect. Minus the fact that his trademark white mane had been shorn short and returned to its natural blonde, It was the look that had caused a thousand fangirls to swoon. The look that had helped charm Lia Ambrosi and Tessa Windsor. And the look that had utterly failed with Dawn. He frowned slightly. Inwardly, that had always bothered him. Really, since graduating from being a shy, nerdish high schooler into Seymour Almasy’the only woman he’d set his sights on that he had been unable to bed was Dawn. Was that actually why he disliked her’or was it Dies Irae? Was it the fact that she seemed to take such joy in throwing herself and Lia in his face? Her reasons for disliking him were seemingly much clearer, involving Seymour’s face-painted chivalric guise of Emoticon, several make-out sessions with the woman of her dreams, and Seymour’s never-ending persistence to woo her. The whole walking out on Dies Irae bit didn’t help either. But time had passed. Enough time for Seymour to consider the crazy idea of asking Dawn to buy the Estate. All of his dealings with McFarlane had been complicated by the “L” word that sat in the corner of all of their conversations like a pink elephant. This was a business deal, no more, no less. At least, that’s what he kept telling himself. There was self-respect here on the line for Seymour too. To prove to himself that he’d truly moved past everything. And that meant not falling to pieces at the sight of her, as he’d spent a year and a half of his life doing. He was a less oblivious soul, nowadays. More aware of the world around him. More aware of the things he said, and he could recognize the arrogance that it took to do what he was planning. To ask the owner of the house he’d walked out on to buy it from her wasn’t something most people would even remotely consider. Seymour Almasy was not “most people”. Never had been. Never would be. He vaguely wondered if she’d hit him. Seymour was Lia’s pet project in Dies Irae’probably the only reason he’d been allowed to move into the Estate to begin with. Lia’s word was gospel for Dawn for a wide variety of reasons. That added a whole new dimension to the problem. He didn’t know if they stayed in touch. If they did’well, his attempted business transaction would be news in the GCW locker room almost immediately. Not that he cared’though he WOULDN’T be telling Dawn of his first match back. That would go poorly. Beyond poorly. He swept over to his bed in a few quick strides, putting on his coat. The club wasn’t even a block away, the rationale behind Seymour’s choice of this particular shithole motel when he could have stayed at much better. He’d have time to rationalize out the rest as he walked. As the door opened, closed, and locked behind him, Seymour allowed himself a soft sigh. He was debuting this week, for Yevon’s sake. After the vitrol he’d thrown in the faces of GCW wrestlers and fans, he had to win. Losing his first match back would make him into a laughingstock. A joke. And yet he wasn’t in a ring training for the contest. He was in British Columbia, about to take in a concert. What the Hell was wrong with him? “Nothing,” he told himself. Because the sad reality was that Seymour’s in-ring skills never had been the problem. Even when he was stinking out rings on a regular basis towards the end of his GCW run, it was never due to a lack of ability. It was due to a lack of will. A lack of spirit. That was why this journey to Canada was more important than any amount of reps in a gym or in-ring practice sessions. It was proof positive that he could look his past in the eye and stand tall and proud. After all, if he couldn’t look Dawn McFarlane in the eye, how would he be able to do it with Lia, Vivica, or any of the myriad others who wanted his blood for past wrongs? How could he stand toe-to-toe with the woman who, as much as he would deny it in front of a crowd, was able to defeat him more times than anyone else on the planet? Even when he’d paid attention to the cheers of the fans who had so rudely betrayed him, Seymour had been a confident, almost over-confident individual. He called himself the God of Tournament . Modesty was never, EVER his strong suit. And that was the attitude he needed back. He’d displayed it in the middle of the ring by pissing in the cornflakes of WorldWide’s third anniversary. But there was a fine line between talking that game and having the confidence to back it up. Once THAT confidence was there, he would be untouchable. Unbeatable. The cold air hit him like a sucker punch as he stepped out of his motel room into the parking lot, reaching into his pocket to pull out the Canadian currency he’d need to pay the cover charge. There were a wide variety of places where being Seymour Almasy got him out of such things, but he imagined that this small town would not be one of them. The parking lot of the club seemed mostly full from a distance. Good sign, that. Meant he could mix in and mingle with the clubgoers until his moment of truth. It was like being a sniper, he figured, his mind going back to the similar words Irvine Kinneas favored in Final Fantasy VIII. You sat there in your hiding spot, waiting for the perfect moment. You had no idea when it would come, or how long you’d have to wait, but when you saw it, you took it. Overeager strides carried him closer to his goal, his heart thumping in his chest. Gods, but he wanted to get this over with. “This is about the Estate,” he reminded himself, approaching the bouncer outside and handing over the cash. A quick glance at the money confirmed its truthfulness, and the burly man waved Almasy inside. “No more and no less.” With no further ado, he was in the darkened club, slipping his way amongst patrons closer to the stage. “No alcohol,” he thought, ruefully. He needed a clear head, at least, for the initial proceedings. The dance floor was jumping, with fans having created the beginnings of a mosh pit, and Seymour instinctively winced. He’d probably get the shit kicked out of him, but such was life. Eyes used to dim environments looked up at the stage, past the lead singer crooning into the microphone, and to the left. Not finding his quarry, he instead scanned to the right, and nodded once, spotting the bass guitar. Of course, the problem was that his eyes then followed the player’s body upward. It was Dawn McFarlane, alright’but far more of Dawn McFarlane than he’d ever seen. The corset she wore seemed like something out of the Lia Ambrosi collection ? it actually might have been, the more he thought about it. She was wearing tight black PVC that was a far cry from anything she’d ever worn in either Global or GCW. His focus disintegrated to ashes in a manner of moments, trying to fight off his body’s automatic reaction to the sight of her. An obscenity tore from his throat, low and guttural, as he realized that he was rapidly losing this particular battle?. ----------------------- It had been a long show and a long night. Right now there was nothing more Dawn wanted then to head home and strip out of her once a week PVC getup, sleep into a bath and get off her feet. Music was a lot like wrestling, she'd decided, an incredible high while in front of the fans but a trial on mind and body that was enough to leave you napping on the floor. The debate, in times gone past, would likely have gone on for ages, with Seymour arguing his righteousness, and Dawn his sins. But tonight, in a bar in British Columbia, the truth would come out. Almasy leant close, to whisper the final three words he would likely ever say to the woman he once loved. With them spoken, Seymour nodded to Dawn, and with a sweep of his coat left the bar. He would be back on a plane to the United States that night, after a phone call to tell Jonathan Calver that the deal wouldn't be going through. Seymour couldn't help but steal one glance at her over his shoulder as he left. He couldn't claim to be happy with how their final meeting had gone. If nothing else, though, he had finally done the one thing that she had asked him for since the very beginning. Been honest with her. For his response to Shadow's criticism, whispered in her ear, was the self-damning phrase... "She was right." ------------------------------- And so we meet again, Shadow. It's been a while, hasn't it? Last time we saw one another, you were flat on your back in the middle of a FUSE ring, and I was PTC Global Champion for a third time. Oh, forgive me, that's not entirely accurate. But you know as well as I do that it's what everyone remembers, don't you? You always wanted to make me out to be the bad guy. Selfish Seymour, always out for himself, unable to prostrate himself for a cause. A bad soldier. Not like Dawn or Vivica. Easy for you to say. Especially when you didn't have to give up a goddamned thing. You led it, after all. The leader makes the rules. The leader does whatever she wants, orders her subordinates around like chess pieces. And you were good at it. Revelled in it. You sold me on Dies Irae as a group of equals. You spoke to me in a hotel lobby and convinced me that I was making the best decision I could possibly make. And then, lo and behold, the group of equals becomes your cult of personality. Vivica was your prized student. Dawn was desperately in love with you. Virgil was your husband. All loyal. All willing to go along with your ego trip. All willing to prostrate themselves before you and let you play the goddess. It's okay though. I understand. You couldn't deal with the fact that you couldn't beat Dawn. And now, you lost your title to Vivica. The student has surpassed the teacher. You're alone now. Alone and on the decline. You may be one and six against me, Lia. But I'm going to add another notch in the column that matters. A win in the main event of WorldWide will prove that when it comes to matches that matter...you simply cannot hack it. You never have. You never will. You've called me selfish. You've called me only out for myself. You've called me a great number of things, Mrs. Ambrosi, and for that, I can only say thank you. After all, Lia, I learned from the best.
