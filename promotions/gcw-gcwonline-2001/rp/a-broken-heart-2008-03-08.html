A Broken Heart

8 March 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Written: March 8th, 2008 "Science may have found a cure for most evils; but it has found no remedy for the worst of them all - the apathy of human beings." Last Time Chris Bagwell's visits with his psychiatrist have spurned him to bring out memories that have since been locked away in the back of his mind, for a long... long time. After reminiscing about his father's lost battle with the evils of cancer, Dr. Erin concluded that she wouldn't see Chris again. Not because she didn't want to see him, but rather because she'd never expected him to return. Chris was cold to emotion and it took her a lot to make him open up. She knew that in doing so, she was going to hit certain nerves that he didn't want touched. But Chris returned for his second session with Dr. Erin. In his second session, he recalled the incidents that occured during his father's funeral. He quickly learned that he'd been dubbed the man of the house and was now responsible for keeping the family together -- as one. Chris recalled a particular moment at his father's funeral during an encounter with his aunt -- his mother's sister. Thinking nothing of it at the time, that moment would soon foreshadow events to transpire. Psychiatrist Office, with Dr. Erin Erin was in the midsts of shuffling through her paperwork when she heard a sudden knock at the door. It was seven thirty in the morning and she'd just gotten into the office. She liked spending about an hour and a half in the mornings to organize her paperwork and really review all of her notes for her subsequent clients that would come later in the day. "Come in," she said with a feeling of precaution in her voice. "Good morning, Doctor. I hope I'm not too early." Chris entered the room, speaking softly. As he poked his head through the door, Erin smiled, relieved to see a familiar face. She'd grown fond of her two previous sessions with Chris. His demeanor pinned him to be a stern, arrogant individual. A hardass. But he really opened up with her. She was certainly glad he felt he could confide in her. "I know its unusual for me to be here this early," Chris reasoned, "but I really wanted to show some incentive with all this." Erin peered through her piles of paperwork, still with a smile on her face. She tried to reply, "it's.." But Chris interrupted, "I'm sorry if you're busy. I can come back later if you want." It was a side of him she didn't even know existed. A gentle side. Nothing of what she'd heard of him. "It's fine," she responded. "It's completely fine. I'm really glad you're taking this seriously. You had me worried." "You said that the last time, too. I just wanted to show you that I want this to work." Erin was extremely glad at Chris' reassurance. She stood from her desk and walked towards her chair. "Let's get started then, shall we?" She invited Chris to take his place on the couch and begin. "If I remember correctly, the last time we met you were just finishing up with your father's funeral proceedings." Chris took a deep breath and nodded his head. "We did." "Would you like to pick up from there?" Perspective Shift. Flashback. It'd been two months since my father passed and I was just starting to get myself back to my feet. It was one of the hardest things I'd ever done before. I was trying to adapt to a life without a man who's been there since the beginning. I'm a realist. I know people die. I just wasn't ready for him to go. And I was finally able to tell myself that it was okay. I remember my phone started to ring. I was relatively obliterated with a couple of my buddies on a gorgeous summer night. The weather was ideal and seemed to promote binge drinking. Then again, it didn't take much to promote binge drinking for a couple guys in their early twenties. Nevertheless, I answered with stagger. My words being slurred. And then in an instant, I sobered up. I got a sudden buzz-kill. It was my mother. "You've got to come home right away," she muttered to me. I could sense in her voice that she'd been fighting off tears. I was beside myself. I couldn't have even imagined what had happened now. It'd been merely two months since my father had passed on. There wasn't a chance that God would let anything else tragic strike my family. He knew I was strong -- he made me that way. But he also knew that I was on the brink of breaking. He wouldn't have made me strong if he were just trying to break me. Would he? I remember bailing out on my friends, being very shady with my exit. I didn't really mention where I was going. What I was doing. Why I was leaving. I just up and left. I stumbled a few blocks home. The alcohol had still been rumbling in my system. As I bursted through the door, my mother was sitting at the kitchen table with her face buried in her hands. She was quiet, but I knew what she was doing. I saw the moisture drizzling down her wrists. I brushed my arm against her back, letting her know I was there to comfort her. It didn't take much for her to open up and let me know what had been bothering her. It was my aunt. She'd fainted at home and was brought to the hospital. What started as lung cancer had now spread tumors throughout her body... And into her brain. My mother had watched her father, her twin sister, her older brother, and her husband all lose battles to cancer. Now she was preparing to watch another sister enter the battlefield poorly armed. She knew what fate was ready to bring. Despite the odds being strongly against her favor, the doctors still said there was hope. Of course there's hope. There's always hope. Hope for a miracle. I convinced my mother to rest and get sleep. We'd be off to see my aunt in the hospital the next day. That night, though... that night I had a bone to pick with God. I remember looking to the sky and cursing him. I'd never been a really religious person, but I always respected my God. I didn't go to church every Sunday, but I still honored him. And this was the way he'd chosen to repay me. At least with the devil, you expect it when he fucks you over. With God, though, you never really are aware of it. You look for the reasons behind God's actions. For some reason, people just can't admit that God fucked them over. It was at this point that I realized religion made our world a bunch of Panglosses. Voltaire was right about religious people when he wrote Candide. Pangloss looked for the good in everything. He looked for the good in death and disease. He looked for the good in poverty and pain. He just couldn't deal with that fact that God was being a prick. I finally realized what Voltaire was trying to tell us with the Pangloss character. He was telling us that faith isn't always the only way to live. God fucked me. He fucked me again. Much like my mother, I knew what fate my aunt would face. There wasn't hope. There was never hope. Never. Perspective Shift. Erin's face was blank. "What ended up happening?" "I'm sure you can guess." Erin nodded her head, understanding. Chris continued, "By the time we'd gotten to the hospital, her throat was extremely sore. It was black and blue from her struggling to breathe. She was miserable and she was ready to go. She kept trying to pull out all the tubes that the hospital had her linked up to. She wanted to die. She never wanted to suffer. That was her one life wish. When she was ready to go, she wanted to go. She wasn't meant to be bed-ridden." Chris shakes his head, letting out a slight laugh. "I really can't help but laugh. My life quickly turned into a soap opera." "What happened after the funeral," Erin asked. "After?" Chris pauses for a second. "He didn't even wait until after." Perspective Shift. Flashback It took only a day for my aunt to join my father in the heavens. Two months and two days -- two deaths. And Bon Jovi tells people you gotta 'keep the faith'? I guess that's easy to say when you're walking on millions. The funeral took place two days later, in Union. I remember waking up that morning, dreading the entire day. My mother was an emotional wreck... again. She'd been hysterically crying since the day she got the phone call. And here I was, having to deal with this all over again. But I was the man of the house. It was my responsibility. So I took charge and I lived up to my responsibility. We drove back to Union, my hometown, and prepared to step foot inside another funeral parlor. When I close my eyes, I can still picture what it looked like pulling into the parking lot of Galante Funeral Home. Before I could even park the car, I was locking eyes with the same distant family members that I'd seen so recently. The types of people you only see at big family reunions every couple years. The ones you see at weddings. And coincidentally, the ones you only see at funerals. As soon as I could open my mother's door, she was embraced by a number of her cousins and friends. I remember just taking a deep breath and inhaling the scent of funeral flowers. I felt like I was beginning to bond with that scent. I'd been experiencing it so frequently. It was to the point where I was about ready to go out to a florist and demand a bottle of whatever their funeral fragrance is. Just so I could make it easier to cope when I smelt the smell. After battling with my senses, I remember peering down the street and seeing a black limousine approaching. It didn't take much to figure out that it was my grandmother. I stood by her door and helped her out of the limo. She wrapped both arms on my shoulder and used me to keep her standing. Her legs were weak. Her heart was fragile. She'd been reciting a number of things in Italian. I really couldn't decipher what they were, but I believe them to be Italian prayers. As we approached the parlor doors, my grandmother continued to ask 'why' and insist that she should have been taken instead. She did not want to bury another one of her children. She wanted all of her children to be there to bury her. But instead, she'd buried three of them. Her heart was so fragile. The funeral director gave us permission to enter and see our beloved Mary Jane Civitano. Our entire family waited for my grandmother to go first, but she refused. She wanted to take a few moments to collect herself, and so she insisted that people enter before her. My brother had taken command over my mother. He was still the emotional type. Nevertheless, he embraced my mother and they began the chain line of sorrowers. I remember hearing my mother's cries when she approached the casket. I wasn't even in the room yet and I could feel her tears on my other shoulder. My grandmother held tightly to my arm and finally gave me the go-ahead. She was ready to see her daughter. She had such a fragile heart. I remember walking into the room with the casket. It felt like she'd been accompanying me down the aisle for my wedding...in a morbid sense. Everyone had cleared the way to allow her priority in seeing her daughter. As we got closer, her grip got tighter. We came to the casket and she dropped to her knees, grasping Mary Jane's hand. She kissed it and held it tightly. She lowered her head and cried. Hysterically. The room subsequently did the same. They cried for Millie, my grandmother. They cried for Mary Jane, my aunt. They cried for themselves. I even think they cried for me. I couldn't cry. I was again apathetic. But this time, I was also the man of the house. I continued to rub her back, encouraging her to let it all out. This would, in fact, be her last opportunity at a goodbye. My grandmother then went limp in my arms. She'd grown weak and fallen back. I tried to help her to her feet so that I could take her to a chair. But that wasn't the help she needed. When I looked at her face, her eyes had been closed. I laid her back and her hands fell upon her chest. The fell upon her heart. Her heart was so fragile. Everyone began to panic. I began to panic. I immediately checked her pulse. It was weak ... faint ... and dying. The funeral director must have called for an ambulance because before I knew it, paramedics were rushing into the room and were strapping her into the backboard. Havoc broke out in the funeral parlor. My family scurried around. I could hear the screaming from all of my other aunts and cousins. My hands started shaking. I looked around for my mother. I remember finding her curled up in a corner, even more hysterical than she was before. She too was shaking. More so than I. The paramedics were loading my grandmother into the ambulance. One approached me and asked me to accompany them as I was with her when she went down. I was worried about leaving my mother alone. A friend of our family assured me he'd take my mother and brother home and make sure they're safe. Where was my brother? I couldn't find him. I was rushed out with the paramedics. My grandmother's life was at stake. It all happened so fast. We rushed to the hospital. The ride was a blur. It still is a blur. I can only recall my grandmother stabilizing a little bit before finally flatlining and joining her daughter and my father. I directed the hospital to contact the same funeral home they'd picked us up from. I had to leave and find my mother before she found out about my grandmother. But I was without a car. It was left at the funeral home. I was stranded. And ahh. The face of a pretty girl. I remember her face so perfectly. She worked at the hospital. She was one of the front desk ladies. She'd just been ending her shift and offered me a ride. Her name... Rebecca. For a moment, I finally felt like something good had struck my life. I hadn't seen such beauty in so long. All I'd seen was death, heartache, and pain. If only we'd had met on different terms. Rebecca brought me to my car. I stood outside the Galante Funeral Home for a short while after Rebecca had left. I remember trying to brainstorm on how I'd tell my mother. I looked at God once more. Fuck you, I thought to myself. Fuck you, I prayed to him. Was this fair? Satan himself hadn't been so cruel. And this was all for the greater good. A tear rolled down my face. I'd practically given up. It hurt so bad. But I had to get home and tell my mother. I was the man of the house. I cannot recall how long it took me to get home that night. It seemed like an eternity. The whole way through, I reminisced of my father. Of my aunt. And now .. of my grandmother. Was I in a storybook? A Hollywood movie? I was waiting to wake up from a nightmare. But my nightmare was reality. I remember my driveway. I remember unlocking my front door. I remember taking a moment to collect myself before I face my mother with the news. I remember opening the door and walking into the house. ... ... ... ... ... ... ... ... ... ... ... ... ... ... ... I remember seeing my mother.. hanging by staircase balcony. I was no longer strong. I felt like a volcano erupted in my eye sockets. Tears rushed down my face. Every single one of them hurt coming out. I grabbed my mother's feet and kissed them. I felt wasted. Far worse than any amount of alcohol could ever make you feel. I was wasted off of emotion. I was drained. I was tired. I was done. I remember falling up the staircase of my mother's house and stumbling into her room. I remember ripping open my father's closet and taking out his nine millimeter pistol that he'd kept ever since retiring from the police force. I remember pointing it to my temple. Perspective Shift. Chris stops speaking. He presses his eyes firmly closed. "I guess you can figure out that I never pulled the trigger." Erin faught back tears. A few managed to escape and roll down the sides of her face. "What stopped you," she asked. Chris shook his head, "I really don't know. I hesitated and the next thing I knew, the police were there." "The police?" "To this day, I don't remember calling them. But I must have. They came with the ambulance and the personel to take my mother down." "What about your brother?" "I don't know. I haven't seen him since that morning, at the funeral." "Do you think..." she spoke slowly and softly. She spoke with worry. "That he's dead?" Chris responded bluntly. "The thought has crossed my mind many a times, but I just have this feeling he's alive. He's somewhere out there. He just needed an escape. We all need an escape from life, sometimes." Erin's tears become a bit more frequent, "I'm so sorry Chris." Erin stands from her chair and sits herself next to Chris, on the couch. She brushes her hand along his thigh. "I'm really glad you've confided in me enough to tell me all of these things." Chris lifts his head and looks Erin in the eyes. "I haven't been able to trust someone in a long time, Erin. I don't know what it is about you -- maybe its the fact that its your job -- but something tells me that you're going to make me better." She sheds a little smile. "Can I ask .. what are you running from?" Chris hesitates. "The life I live." Confused, Erin replies, "But what about it?" Chris turns his head away and looks at the floor again. "I'm constantly on the road, depending on alcohol and pain killers to keep me going. I got myself involved with professional wrestling, but I never took the right approach to it. I got involved because it was an escape from me. I'd never been in one place too long and so I'd never have to fear about getting too close to someone. I'd never have to worry about losing someone else. My best friend became a pirate captain who calls himself Morgan. And I've come to realize that the only way to fix my future .. is to overcome my past." "Is that what brought you here so early this morning?" Chris looks back into Erin's eyes. "Yes," he says softly. "I do have a flight to catch this afternoon, back to Japan for another wrestling event. But I don't want you to think thats the only reason I came so early. I really did have every intention of showing you that I want this to work. I wouldn't have come at all if I didn't care about this." Erin smirks, nodding her head in approval. "I believe you Chris." He returns the smile, "I'm glad." There's a momentary silence between the two. Erin finally says, inquisitively, "Can I ask you something else?" Chris nods, "Sure." Erin says, "You never actually told me what happened to your grandmother in the funeral home?" Chris reminisces again for a brief moment. "They say she died of a massive heart attack. I say she died of a broken heart." To Be Continued...
