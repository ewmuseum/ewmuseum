The Story Of Everything, Chapter Three: Celebrity

Date: It's 2008.07.27 Somewhere My agent at the time, Doctor Wayne Gunn, attorney-at-law, had hooked me up with a per diem gig, but he wasn’t specific with the details. This was some time ago, mind you, and has nothing whatsoever to do with my current occupational situation. I remember the day clearly. It was a Wednesday, or was it a Sunday? Nevertheless, I was still up from the night before, and I was still seeing bubbles everywhere from this chemically sprayed hair I was chewing on. Come to think of it, it was my own hair, and I would’ve chewed myself bald if it wasn’t for that horrible, horrible chiming pulse that shook the bubbles in front of me. My cognizance trickled slowly through the tubes in my brain, and after several pulses, I realized that the noise was coming from my telephone, quite the mysterious character. Finding it would be one endeavor, but remembering how to operate the damn thing ? therein would lay the true challenge. After steadying myself to a sturdy foothold, I bobbled about, wading through the disheveled room with my arms held out at full capacity at either side. Eventually, I guided myself towards the phone, taking into account the increase or decrease in the volume of the ringing as I moved respectively towards or away from it. Once I had cracked this code, I managed to undo the weighty phone from its hinge. But what then? I did not know. “Johnny,” I heard faintly. “There’s a tiny demon in there,” I calculated aloud. “Johnny, is that you?” I froze in terror. “Johnny, put the phone by your ear.” Something inside me warned to run as far away as I could, but the tiny demon had made a convincing argument. I put the phone up to my ear and cupped my hand around the mouthpiece in case anybody was listening. “Johnny, do you know what fucking time it is?” That seemed an odd question for a demon to ask, and the hostile tone of his voice confused me further. Apparently, the question was also rhetorical as the tiny demon continued berating me. With the phone up to my ear, it seemed like the demon was also a bit larger than I had previously expected. “It’s ten-fucking-thirty, Johnny. You were supposed to be there two and a half hours ago.” “Be where ? wait ? who the fuck is this, and why are you so angry?” “It’s Wayne,” he paused, perhaps in disbelief or perhaps he was lying, trying to see if I’d buy it. “Remember that part I had lined up for you?” “I have no idea what you’re talking about. If this is Wayne, I’ll ask you a question only Wayne would know the answer to.” “I’m through playing games, Johnny.” “Who won the 1942 World Series?” “How the fuck should I know” Baseball bores the shit out of me.” He had passed the test. “Alright, I believe you. Get to that address and look for a trailer with a sign that says “Wardrobe.”” “Alright,” I agreed. “What’s next?” “Don’t worry about that now. Just concentrate on getting there,” Wayne barked. “And do it, like, yesterday.” Doctor Wayne hung up his end of the phone. Now it was just a matter of finding that address. It wouldn’t be an easy task with all of the bubbles obstructing my view, but after digging through a pile of unopened envelopes, greasy napkins and empty cigarette boxes, I noticed that I had an address written upside-down on my bare stomach. It was a chance occurrence that I saw this, as I wouldn’t have consciously thought to look at my stomach. “What an unlikely place to write an address!” I laughed aloud. “4463 Bo Fordland Flour St.” Now to provide you with a little history, Bo Fordland was the tycoon behind the Bo Fordland Flour Company. This is Bo Fordland, Jr. who ran the company most recently, not Bo Fordland, Sr. who founded it. The street was named after the latter Fordland after he died in a mysterious flourmill accident. Junior depicted himself as an important man and used his inheritance and the wealth that would continue to be earned from it to sponsor commercials, public events and anything else he could think of to get his name on there. His big, doughy face smiled over traffic on expressway billboards, and he looked like a complete dick. Then, a couple of years ago, a few people had gotten terribly sick off of his flour. Rumor has it that the worst cases were still locked up in asylums. Those were the real flour junkies, the ones who baked breads and pastries. Against massive resistance from the Bo Fordland Flour Company, tests were run on the flour, and it contained traces of human DNA ? hair, blood, bones and the like. I had to get my hands on it to try it out and experience the euphoric effects that consuming the flour would produce. As it turned out, Bo Fordland had been feeding dead hookers through the mills; too many to count. When the tests were in progress, Bo Fordland, Jr. made himself scarce. Some say that he went to Venezuela. Others say he’s hiding somewhere in that flourmill. Nobody knows for certain, though. I had a funny feeling that I was about to find out and get my hands on some of that succulent, hooker-tainted flour. It all added up. Someone had commissioned Doctor Wayne to commission me to retrieve that flour for its illegal distribution, and as long as I pulled it off, I was to be handsomely rewarded with a cut of the stuff. Without further ado, I threw on the first shirt I could find, which would more accurately be described as a bathrobe, and bolted out the door. I took off on foot, the tails of my robe lifted and floated behind me like some sort of cape, like I was some sort of superhero on my way to do some noble thing. Noble? Yes ? but perhaps disguised as to not so easily be seen. I was clandestine, cloaked. I didn’t know what I’d been smoking. That’s right! I was sucking on my sprayed hair. The realization hit me like a sack of bricks, and the tail of my robe came crashing down into the backs of my legs. My feet started pounding against the pavement, and the thunders would ripple through my ears. The ripples reverberated into my brain, setting off many bells inside my ear canal, and whistling through the sutures of my brain like some hellish clarinet solo. The jazzmen were going mad inside. Caution thrown under the carriage of reckless abandon as rubber fingers danced and dashed across the devil’s instruments with no attention paid to tempo or time signature and through indiscriminant scales consisting of all the notes and semitones and then some. I had to stop, and I had to scream. “AHHHHHHH!” I just had to scream. “AHHHHHHH!” I looked around. It had caused no attention. Perhaps I had overestimated my voice. Where was the flourmill? Where was the Bo Fordland Flour Company? It had to be close. I could smell the tainted scent of flour in the air ? almost taste it. A paste formed and foamed in my mouth. I was definitely reaching the Promised Land. I teetered and tottered my way down a few blocks, looking at signs, but none yet seemed the appropriate length. I couldn’t read the street names on account of my vision being so blurred. Another few stumbles down broken arrow alleys, across craggy surfaced roads; the signs were getting shorter, and then a long one. “That’s the ticket,” I decried. And as I turned, I saw it there towering in the horizon like the most dreadful thing you’ve ever seen. It projected moving shadows where the ground had paled from eternal lightlessness. Something told me to turn back ? a windy voice, perhaps that of the flourmill or the spirits of dead hookers inside of it. “Turn back,” it croaked. I should have considered it more thoroughly. I should’ve turned and ran as fast as my bony legs could carry me. But there was my agent, Doctor Wayne Gunn, attorney-at-law to deal with, and he’d be furious. Who knew to whom he had to answer to? It’s the faceless elite that ran the damned show, and if you spit in their general facial void, you’ll draw out the turtle’s head and face the turtle’s wrath. My cape fluttered from a bad breeze coming towards me. I put my head down and chugged forward, and figures started to form from the moving shadows. They were beings, giant bugs perhaps. I couldn’t tell from the distance, and as I got closer, I saw that they were gathered around a trailer. They weren’t bugs either. No, it was much worse. They were zombies. “It’s the hookers!” I shouted, and then I ducked behind a telephone pole beside the cracked street. Some of them turned their ghastly necks towards me, and I started breathing heavily, but they stayed herded around the trailer. What was it that they wanted from that trailer? Brains? Sure, I could go for a few scoops myself, but that wasn’t it. If they wanted brains, they could sense the juicy bulb stewing around my noggin. I had to see for myself what was in that trailer, but I’d have to come up with a plan. “Somehow, I’ll have to lure the zombies away from the trailer.” I surveyed my surroundings, but aside from the trailer, which was canopied away from the sun by the Bo Fordland Flour Company, there was really just the abandoned flourmill itself. “That’s it! The flour mill.” I would make a break for the flourmill and stronghold there. First, I’d gather as much of the Hooker Tainted Flour -- street names include but are not limited to: HTF Taint, Hot Taint, Hooker Taint, Bozo, Bo Flo, BFF, Best Friend, Monthly Flo, White Light District, Baked Crabs and Spread Powder -- and then I’d prepare a fire ? I’d gather all the flammables and soak them in gas, and when I heard the doors breaking, I’d light it up and jump through a window. I surveyed the building for windows that weren’t high enough to kill me, and I found several that seemed to suffice. If I needed to climb to safety through the second floor, I’d still be able to make it down without crippling anything. I’d trained myself for perilous landings in situations like this. I noticed an almost holy glow from within those windows. I felt like it could be the only way to protect myself. I had to make a move. “Hey, zombie scum. I’ve got a brain for you,” and with that I taunted them with a different type of brain, or something that bore a vague resemblance to one. Some of the zombies turned and recoiled, groaning in voices that could pass for human. Was this that far removed from the current state of man? I feared the answer to that question may have been negative, and I ran, huffing and puffing towards the rusty double doors of the Bo Fordland Flour Company. Reaching there was a small victory. I always promised myself I’d quit smoking if I couldn’t outrun a zombie. And what better time than right now to celebrate with a cigarette? I drew one out of my front pocket. “What are you doing, Johnny” They’re gaining on you,” I assumed to myself. “Fuck it.” I lit the cigarette and sought refuge inside the Bo Fordland Flour Company, but when I looked around to find things to barricade the door, I was confused at my surroundings. There were a group of people with cameras and boom microphones, and another set on a dimly lit set. There were others around, milling about like nothing was going on. “What the hell are you all doing?” I shouted. “There are zombies outside. We need to barricade the door.” The room stopped, and all eyes and cameras were on me, but the eyes were disbelieving and not justifiably panicked as I had expected. “What the fuck is wrong with you” We’ve got to get moving.” Beat. “Now!” “Sir, you can’t smoke a cigarette,” an arrogant, balding runt of a man said to me from behind thick paned glasses with a finger pointed snidely upward. “Who the fuck are you?” “I’m the production manager, and I think I’m going to have security escort you out the door.” He unclipped a walkie-talkie from his belt and engaged the speak button. “Security!” “As soon as they open that door, the zombies will all rush in, and you’ll all be dead!” It was no use. Two much more burly men soon grabbed me. They were hairless brutes, a black albino and a regular one, and they dragged me towards the exit. “You’ll all be dead!” I repeated, laughing despite my predictably gruesome demise. Nobody responded, but nobody looked away. The cameras kept rolling. The ensuing footage would tell the story. The doors were opened, and I was tossed outside. The two albino guards didn’t even bat an eye at the sight of all those zombies. What was this; some kind of zombie farm? I burst back towards the door and pulled on the handle. It was locked. I started banging on the square windows adorned on each side. “You can’t just leave me here to die!” Bang, bang, bang. “You can’t do this!” Bang, bang, bang, bang, bang. “You can’t!” Bang, bang, bang, bang. “I swear to God, I’ll fucking haunt each and every one of you.” Bang, bang. "Um, good luck," I offered. "I'm going to, uh, get the fuck out of here.' And with that, I chased after Doctor Wayne, fleeing from this absurd circus before consequence could swarm around me. I had just gotten out of the pen, you see, and I had no intentions of going back. It puzzled me, how so many people sought importance through winning these worthless trinkets. It was bedlam in there. People were prepared to fight each other, and for what? Some cheap prize that probably wasn't made out of real gold anyway? Pride? I don't know. I had more important things on my mind than the Internet and its biased judgment policies. The Internet is imaginary anyway. I had a very real job to do, I assure you, and a dead girlfriend with a dead kid to feed. I was about ready to see them again and the noxious gases were collecting in my tank at home. "Only a few more days," I anticipated as we slammed the doors behind us and dove through the holes where windows used to be in Wayne Gunn's sports car. She was getting on in years, but twenty years ago, she was the hottest ton of metal to ever cruise the freeway that ran alongside the lake. I heard that the girl with the tits from the wheel spinning won the Inny, anyway. Good for her. God knows I didn't give a shit about some meaningless victory. I'll get them next time. I will say this, though. The Inny for Best Video Shot During the Inny Award Show should have been mine, but they credited Shawn Andersen-Anderton and Hank "Trousers" Downing for some mishap that required emergency medical assistance that had occurred shortly after Wayne and I had hightailed it out of the lobby.
