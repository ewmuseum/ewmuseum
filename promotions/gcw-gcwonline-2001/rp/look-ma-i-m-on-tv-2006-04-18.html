Look Ma, I'm on TV!

18 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4-18-06 I did it. I had finally given not only Global Championship Wrestling, but the World, a reason to believe in me. No longer did I have to worry about getting lost in the shuffle, stuck forever in the same meaningless matches against any new face that happened to walk through the doors. I was turning heads left and right, causing whiplash to those that could not believe it was me and took a double take. I was the Television Champion, and as much as Jason Warr needed the title to survive’I needed it to prove my worth. A task I accomplished with flying colors, in a match that could go down as one of the best of the year. Or so I’ve heard. Still, I can’t help but get this strange feeling in the back of my mind that things are not going to get any easier. It’s as if winning the Television title has opened up a slew of doors that people are ready and willing to knock down to come in and snatch it from my grasp. Did I give everything I had to do the same to Jason Warr? Was I just one in a long line of competitors that could have walked away as champion that night? Was I just lucky enough to catch him by surprise? All of these thoughts kept picking away at my mind as I stood there in front of the mirror, my hands firmly grasping the white porcelain sink to the point that my knuckles were turning white from the pressure. With a white towel the motel had had provided me with wrapped around my waist, and water dripping from my sandy blonde hair and trickling down my spine. I couldn’t help but notice how bad I looked. The mirror was slightly fogged over, but I could still see my own reflection staring back at me. I looked tired, with three days worth of stubble overtaking my once smooth as a baby’s bottom chin and cheeks. I could hardly remember the argument I had with my razor, but it must have been a bad one for her to neglect me in such a manner. “You okay in there?” I turned to the door, half wishing she would get the hint that I was done with her and leave. “I’ll be right out.” I forced myself to say, knowing full well that I had to find a way to get rid of her. I couldn’t afford to have any of these groupies thinking she had a future with me. Money hungry whores is all they are anyway, always looking for someone to support them for a little head whenever you’re in town. “Hurry,” I could almost picture her standing there with the sheets wrapped around her chest, her bottom lip protruding just enough to give her that sad puppy dog face. “I’m getting lonely.” Lonely. She could probably walk out of here and find someone else to spend her night with, but she knew where the money was. She knew who I was and where I would be going, women like that had an eye for it. Of course, women like that did not peak my interest. It was too easy, and I loved a challenge. Not to mention someone with a little bit of talent inside the ring. GCW had become overrun with women wrestling in barely anything, and I had been focused on the monitors as soon as I saw them. You’d have to be blind not to notice their firm physiques and tantalizing curves. Still, I knew who they were -- they were part of the roster -- and unlike Seymour Almasy, I knew better than to eat where I shit...or was it shit where I eat? A knock on the door caught my attention again, “Who’s Virgil Ambrosi and Jacob McKail?” I had no idea what she was talking about, but I knew perfectly well who she was asking about. Making my way to the door, I felt the towel slipping down from around my waist. Still, I grabbed the knob and turned it, pulling the door open to find the pretty young blonde standing there with a paper in her hand. And nothing else. As much as I wanted her to leave, finding her like this still managed to arouse me’I am a man after all. “What was that?” She turned the paper so I could read it. “Do you always go through people’s things?” “Some guy brought it up.” “They brought it to the room?” “Yeah,” she scratched her head. “I think he worked here. Said it was important and he was told to bring it right up.” “Yeah, I’m sure he worked here.” She shrugged her shoulders slightly, her firm -- but very fake -- breasts bouncing just enough with the movement of her shoulders that I felt myself becoming fully aroused. She noticed it as well, apparent by the smile that creased her lips as soon as she sneaked a peak. “He didn’t even bother to ask for a tip.” “Did you answer the door like that?” She batted her eyelashes playfully as she nodded, moving closer to me at the same time. “I’m sure he’s taking care of his tip right now then.” She just giggled as she handed me the paper with one hand, grabbing me with the other. “Your hands are a bit cold,” I told her, flinching from her cool grip. “It won’t be cold for long.” She was right. I could hardly keep my attention on the paper in my hand, as I felt the warm inviting moistness of her lips surrounding me. Still, blocking the steady sucking sound from my mind I started to look over the document that I supposedly needed to see immediately. “Oh shit,” I said out loud as I felt the back of her throat for a brief moment. I almost missed the line that was directed toward me. Jayce Bradley versus Jacob McCail versus Virgil Ambrosi I flinched again’she was doing really good. Good enough for me to ball up the paper and toss it aside. I had more important things to deal with at the moment. Like wrapping my fingers around her blonde hair and getting a really good grip. Time to test her gag reflex. =?=?=?=?=?= I couldn’t help but to walk with a little jump in my step. My morning had been spent very wisely, and as much as I hated to admit it’I had promised to see her again before I left Florida. Yeah, it was that damn good. “Hey, aren’t you Jayce Bradley?” The came from behind me, causing me to turn on my heel and nearly stumble as it took me by complete surprise. “It is you!” He was no more than twelve years old, about the same age I was before my life turned to shit, and the smile on his face took me by complete surprise. I wasn’t supposed to have fans. “Yeah, what do you want?” I tried my best to be rude, looking down at him with a bit of a sneer tickling the corners of my lips. “Oh man, my brother Dominic isn’t going to believe this.” The name hit me in the chest like a ton of bricks. Dominic. That was my brother’s name. “He’d probably get more pissed if I bumped into Eva, since he thinks you’re going to be a crap champion.” What the hell’Did he just’He did. “Excuse me?” I stood there in total disbelief. The nerve of this kid to tell me I’m not going to be a good champ, after I’ve been toting that Cruiserweight title for quite some time. “He’s always complaining about guys like you, and he hates those goth rejects from Global.” Okay, maybe the kid had some taste. “That’s kind of messed up, kid. Then again, it’s not like he’s supposed to like me.” The kid just shrugged his shoulders, nonchalantly. “He doesn’t know what he’s talking about. I tell him all the time that next to Rollins, you’re the best guy there.” “Next to Rollins?” I eyed the kid, and let him know that I wasn’t all too thrilled by his choice of words. “You’re not that good.” He might as well had said, “Fuck You!” right to my face. All I could do was nod, because well’I actually agree with the little bastard. “You got a point to all this, or are you just wasting my time?” “Prick.” Yeah, I was not expecting that, and the laugh that resonated in response was sincere. The kid had balls. “I just wanted an autograph, but if you’re too busy holding down the sidewalk I’ll leave you alone.” He turned to walk away, but I grabbed him by the shoulder and stopped him. “You got balls kid, I’ll give you that.” He smiled, and it was at that exact moment that I noticed how much alike the two of us were. Personality wise, it was like looking at myself a few years back when I was the same age. The same love for heels, and everything. It was surreal. “What did you want me to sign?” “That girl’s chest.” He pointed across the street, where a brunette with shoulder length hair had her head tilted to the side as she stared at an outfit in a department store window. I could have written a book report on that chest if I needed to. “I don’t think my mom’ll let me keep her though.” “I like you kid.” And I did. Just not in a Catholic Priest sort of way, that was just sick. “I’ll tell you what I’ll do. There’s a show in Jacksonville on the Twentieth?” I couldn’t believe how excited he got, but as soon as I mentioned the show’his eyes lit up. “All you have to do is go up to the will call window and give them your name. Then, after your brother calls me an asshole for bullshitting you when she tells you there are no tickets, flip him off and tell whoever’s behind the window to give you the tickets under the name J. Bradley.” “That’s so fucking sweet.” “I know.” I couldn’t help but grin at the situation, especially when the kid high-fived me and ran off. It was a strange feeling to have a fan, especially after all the bullshit I’ve done in my life, but I could get used to that feeling. That is, if anyone else actually wants to cheer for me after I beat the hell out of Virgil Ambrosi and Jacob McKail. “Fuck.” I completely forgot about the match I have scheduled in that Total J Cup Tournament’I needed to book a flight to Mexico. Fast.
