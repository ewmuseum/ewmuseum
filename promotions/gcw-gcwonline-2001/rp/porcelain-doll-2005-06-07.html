Porcelain Doll

7 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6-7-05 I had pitched the idea to Mr. Barrata less than a week ago who had initially said no, but with a little persuasion and a folder full of useful information I had convinced Barrata to at least view a pilot episode. In lieu of all the success that reality television had garnered I had decided that I would blatantly rip off Andy Dick’s garbage and provide the masses with garbage of my own. I took it upon myself to hire some apprentices of my own. What’s the catch you ask? Well they aren’t here because they think they’re apprentices. They’re under the impression that I’m training them to wrestle and help get their foot in the door, perhaps a contract with an independent promotion. I didn’t bother to go into much detail on the contract and these buffoons didn’t really care. They were the perfect kind of stupid. I figured it would be a success, and if nothing else I could have a lot of fun humiliating these bastards at their expense and much to my amusement. I laughed to myself. Just as I had humiliated Bryan Marshall. I leaned back in my office chair, perfectly content with myself, envisioning ideas for what would be my new show. It was then that Shelly came wandering in. “Shelly, don’t you know to knock?” I asked pretending to be irritated. “I’m sorry Ryan.” She tried to speak, but I cut her off. “Well,” I paused. “Go back outside and knock this time.” She glared at me with her piercing green eyes from underneath the strands of her dark black hair. It was a mess. I guess that kind of thing happens when you work for me. “You can’t be serious?” she replied appalled. Without hesitation I pointed at the door and shot back. “Do I look like a man that messes around Shelly?” “You’re a real ass Ryan.” I let it slide as she whirled around in her heels and walked out of the office angry and completely flustered. I couldn’t help it. I was trying to think up creative stuff that I could do to my new associates once they all arrived. I snickered as she knocked on the door. “PLEASE COME IN!” I yelled louder than necessary. She came in and rolled her eyes at me. Part of me wanted her to get fed up and quit and go on to better things. But those with a criminal record often don’t have to many options despite qualifications and a sharp brain. Besides, those qualities definitely came in handy from time to time around my office, and I certainly enjoyed matching wits with her. “Mr. Barrata had an associate call. He said if he enjoyed the pilot he would finance half of the show and it would be aired in a favorable time slot for the target age. He urges you to get him the pilot sometime within the next week or so,” Shelly explained. I merely nodded. I already knew all of this and the fact that Mr. Barrata felt it necessary to continue to remind me made me supremely confident that I would be able to take over his business in due time. The show was a cover. It would all unfold, but not yet. “Did you get that promo that Marshall cut the other day. I slid it under the door the other evening after you had already gone,” Shelly stated. “Well if you put it there, I got it wouldn’t you think?” I said. I looked up at her. She had enough. She was pleasantly curvy in her green dress. I suppose that most men would find her fairly attractive, but she wasn’t my type. Smart yes, but a little rough around the edges, after all she’d been to prison. She said nothing, merely frowning back at me as I lounged with my feet up and my tie loosened from my neck. I smiled. “Sorry,” I said. “I got it, thanks.” With that, she was content and left the office shutting the door behind her. I had trained her well I thought to myself with a smile. My match with Bryan Marshall was approaching fairly quickly. The turn around was pretty quick in this fed and I hoped my body would be able to handle it. I might actually have to start working out more frequently. That would definitely deter from my business ventures. I sighed. I leaned forward in the large black office chair and put both elbows on the large mahogany desk and rested my chin in my hands. Bryan Marshall. I was secretly glad he had interfered in my match. I had no desire what so ever to win the television title. For one, the belt just wasn’t prestigious enough for me. At least not through my own jaded eyes. Also, I didn’t want anything I’d have to defend every single week. I really didn’t want to fight every week. It interfered with business and I wasn’t twenty-six years old anymore either. This was the first time I had a chance so sit down and think it all through. I smiled. He really had done me a favor. It was too early to have some little belt like the television title and have all these young up and comers biting at the bit to get at me. I had a whole different agenda. I scoured my office. I looked at the side wall covered in memorabilia and Marshall’s recent promo popped into my head. I had only watched it once, but certain things stood out. He called me the Devil Himself, but that’s not me. I may be scandalous, but I wasn’t JW Oswald, nor was I Angelo Deville. Those were legends like the guys that littered my wall. And the biggest mistake of my professional wrestling career. That had to be when I disguised myself as Don Juan Leisure, the Sultan of Suave, the white guy with an afro. I acted ridiculous? that was definitely the worst mistake of my career. I shuddered at the thought of it. I continued to lose myself in my thoughts when I was started by a knock on my office door. Anticipating Shelly I shouted. “GO AWAY, I’M THINKING.” A deep voice I didn’t anticipate answered. “Stephens, it’s me, Lance!” Ah Lance Nobile. But what the hell was he doing here I asked myself. He usually confined himself to the workout facility and never came to my office. “Come in,” I offered. The barrel-chested man stood before me. He looked somewhat confused. “And to what do I owe this pleasure?” He hesitated. “Some kid showed up at the workout facility looking for you. He said his name was Tyler, Tyler Yates.” I had no discerning idea who the hell Tyler Yates was or why a kid would be looking for me. I just looked back at Lance and shrugged my shoulders. “Lance, you came all the way over here to tell me this. Why didn’t you just kick his punk ass and send him on his way?” I asked quite seriously. “He said he was going to be on your television show boss. It was really weird,” Lance replied. Oh, it kind of made sense. I suppose, but I didn’t know why he had turned up at the workout facility. It was only common knowledge that the place existed around the immediate area and in certain wrestling circles. I hadn’t completely thought through where I was going to host my three apprentices yet. I eyed Lance to seemed rather flustered. “Did you bring him with you?” “No I left him back that the facility. I didn’t want you to kill him if he didn’t belong. What’s this television show?” I waved the question off as if it wasn’t of any importance. “I’ll explain it all later,” I said. “Go get the kid and bring him here to the office. I’ll figure out what to do with him.” Lance nodded and turned to go and then hesitated in the doorway. He looked over his shoulder at me, and I knew what he had on his mind. For whatever reason, I didn’t mind talking to Lance. I think I liked the fact that he lived vicariously through my stories as he was younger and wanted to succeed as I had in wrestling. Like the others though, he wanted to be like the Ryan Stephens of old and he couldn’t understand the transition I had made two years ago. They hadn’t been around the people I had though, they couldn’t know. “Go ahead,” I said before he had a chance to stumble on the words. I started to say something, then stopped. I could tell he wanted to know how I was always a step ahead of him. That was just my disposition I had told him once. “You did pretty well in the debut. Might’ve won had it not been for that Marshall guy,” Lance said. I nodded. I didn’t want to go into detail about why I was glad that I had lost. “He’s so distorted he actually thinks he helped me get as far as he did. He can’t comprehend that I could’ve gotten that far without him, but that it was easier with our little arrangement. And he insists nobody will trust me again, but that’s bullshit too. People these days, with the right persuasion you can get what you want.” I smiled as Lance shuffled his thoughts through his big square head and then proceeded to emit sounds from his square jaw and thin lips. He really was a monstrosity. “He’d rather be lucky than good,” Lance said, laughing. Lance really was an okay chap with a decent head on his shoulders. I could drag him up to the top with me if I so desired, but I really didn’t want to betray Lance and sooner or later it would probably come to that. “Lucky is something that happens from time to time, but if you’re good, then you’re consistent. Good will prevail over luck most of the time. That said if he beats me this Thursday, that will be sheer luck,” I said as I winked at Lance in a very heterosexual manner. “Are you going to flip the proverbial switch?” Lance said. I knew what he was getting it and it frustrated me that it always came back to that. I just shook my head from side to side calmly. “You better run get that kid before something happens to him,” I instructed. Lance knew, it was written on his face. He just nodded and left my office as he had came. I was pleasantly surprised with myself in that I was actually thinking about the match on Thursday. The visions of Marshall in the battle royal played through my head as if I was watching a movie. I evaluated every move, every step’I searched each scenario for weakness. I could dissect virtually anyone within the squared circle and I wasn’t sure, but I kept having an overwhelming feeling that I would actually desire to do against Marshall come Thursday just to prove a point and get him to shut his mouth. He psychotic disposition and style was reckless. It would leave him very vulnerable, of this I was convinced. I just hoped I didn’t take any sick bumps along the way. Which is precisely why another thought wiggled it’s way into my head? I might be totally preoccupied with business ventures and not give two shits about the match’only time would tell’and all Marshall could do was pray for the latter. ******** I was lost in numbers and figures and other such statistics that others would find terribly boring when the phone rang and awoke me to reality. Shelly had gone to lunch and wouldn’t answer and so I looked at the caller ID. I didn’t recognize the number, or the name. It was a woman’s name and I wasn’t in the habit of doing business with women, though I might with Van Lorne It though to myself as I snagged a pad. I would make a note telling Shelly that her friends couldn’t call our office. Paige Miller I scrawled across the stick it pad as the answering machine picked up, and it was then I heard the voice and immediately chills went down my spine and I stopped writing. “I was calling because this name was on my cell phone a few nights ago. I apologize if you were trying to reach me. I have been out of pocket these last few days. If you needed me, or you need your suit dry cleaned call back at your convenience.” Shocked, and overcome with uncertainty I analyzed the message. Did she really think that it was me that had called or did she just say that just in case? It was very strange indeed and she had intrigued me since the moment I saw her that fateful afteroon. Bryan was wrong. I didn’t want my ex-wife. I wanted the little porcelain doll that took my breath away, and that was both disturbing and refreshing at the same time. I knew what I had to do?. In both scenarios.
