Death Is Nothing But A Word

2 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5-2-06 =?=?=?=?=?= April 27, 2006 Mobile, Alabama =?=?=?=?=?= Jayce Bradley felt light-headed as he trudged from the shower, a white towel wrapped around his waist and tied on one end. The events that transpired after his match with Shadow playing over and over in his mind, as if on a loop, and still he could find no reason why he would have been caught so off guard. Taking a seat on one of the benches in the center of the locker room, all he could do was sit there with his head in his hands, as water from his still damp hair began to drip on the cold concrete floor beneath his bare feet. It had happened so fast, yet seemed to linger at the same time. The shock of losing the Cruiserweight title after seeing the man who had called him out the week before making his way to the ring had left him in a daze, completely unaware of the beating that was yet to come. He could hardly remember what happened after McClancy hit his Psychodriver, but he knew that there was more than one person responsible for the countless bruises and welts that now adorned his skin. The entire KnightMare Brigade had made their presence felt, and if Eva Van Lorne, Seymour Almasy, and Tessa Windsor had not interfered on his behalf...Well, it would have been a lot worse. Still, sitting here in an empty locker room with the scent of ammonia tickling the hairs inside his nose, he couldn't help but wonder why they had done it. He was not their friend, and he was sure he had never spoken more than one word to any of them. Yet they had come to his aid. Grabbing a towel from the open duffel bag at his side, Jayce began to pat his hair dry. The door slowly creaking open causing him to spring to his feet as fast he could manage, given the circumstances. 'You didn't look too hot out there tonight, hero.' The rough sarcastic drawl was one he had heard before, and pulling the towel back away from his face revealed his assumption was in fact, true. 'To what do I owe the pleasure, Detective?' Standing there in a gray three piece suit he had probably purchased at Dillard's, Detective Nicholas Pride had a snide grin on his semi-pursed lips as his arms were kept folded across his chest. He carried himself quite well, his broad shoulders parallel to the ground at all times. 'I was in the neighborhood, and decided I'd drop in and check how you've been feeling.' His reply was semi-sarcastic, with a twinge of seriousness in it. Years of questioning suspects had taught him many tricks, and he was now very skilled in getting people to admit to things without realizing it. As you can see, things are going great.' A slight smile had managed to sneak its way onto his lips, as he ran his hand from head to toe as if he was a model on The Price is Right. Being out on the road most of the time, and not having anyone to spend those moments with, becomes taxing over time. Yet, somehow, he had managed to grow accustomed to the lifestyle. Discovering numerous ways to occupy his time, sarcasm had been one of the most entertaining. 'I wonder how you would be feeling if your friends had not come out to help you.' 'Friends?' His face contorted as he asked the question, but Jayce knew exactly who the detective was talking about. 'The people that ran out to help you.' 'Ah...' He nodded his head, as the thought of what could have happened ran through his mind for a brief moment. 'I wouldn't really call them friends.' 'What would you call them then?' A nonchalant shrug of the shoulders, as Jayce shook his head from side to side slowly. 'A couple of people with a common enemy, but nothing more than that. To be honest, I've never even spoken to any of them before.' Pride was a bit shocked at the revelation. Seeing as how he did not keep up with what was going on with the company, it was not like he should have already known that little tidbit of information. 'Well if I was you, I'd be knocking down their door thanking them.' 'I'll keep that in mind.' Removing the towel that was draped over his head, Jayce noticed the detective was attempting to sneak a peak at the contents of his bag. 'So seeing as how Alabama isn't just a walk around the block...' Pride snapped his neck in Jayce's direction. He hoped that his snooping had not been detected, since Jayce had his back turned to him. 'What are you really doing here?' 'I... Well, there was this case from a few years back...' Although he had practiced his excuse countless times on the drive over from the hotel, he still managed to stumble over his words like a bumbling fool. Jayce folded his arms across his chest, nodding along with the prefabricated story. 'The victim's daughter moved to Mobile, and well... I figured I would come down and-' 'No.' Jayce waved him off. 'What are you doing here.' 'I already told you, I-' 'Bullshit.' Pride stood there in shock, his eyes wide with disbelief. 'You still think I had something to do with your daughter's abduction don't you?' Pride began to shake his head from side to side, but Jayce continued nonetheless. 'You think I was somehow connected to that serial killer, Russell Braun, and you just will not let it go.' 'That has nothing to-' 'I'm sick and tired of this, detective.' The expression on Pride's face changed from awestruck to aggravated in a matter of seconds. 'Just let it go already. The bastard is dead and your daughter is safe, that's what's important.' He wanted to yell, tell the little prick that he knew he something was up, but he stood there silent as his eyes burned with a furious rage. He watched as Jayce threw his arms up in frustration and stormed back to the shower room, then he turned his attention back to what he was really here for. The towel. Jayce had lazily tossed it on the bench when he threw his fit, in plain sight. Pride made his way over to the bench, keeping a watchful eye on the hall that lead back into the locker room as he slipped a latex glove over his right hand. Using the gloved hand to lift the towel, he pulled a plastic grocery bag from his jacket pocket with the other. 'I can't let it go...' He stuffed the towel into the bag, then took another glance down the hall. 'Not until I know the truth.' He made his way toward the door, tucking the bag under his jacket as he pulled the door open. 'And another thing, detective.' Jayce took a glance around the room, and noticed the door slowly closing. What he did not notice, was the missing towel. The one he used to dry his hair. =?=?=?=?=?= April 4, 2006 San Antonio, Texas =?=?=?=?=?= 'Hey JJ, wanna come over to my house and play Kingdom Hearts Two?' 'I wish, Max. My mom needs me to mow the lawn... She said she'd give me ten bucks.' JJ and Max were walking down the sidewalk, dressed alike in khaki pants and white button up polo shirts. JJ; the black kid with his shirt tucked out and a black fishnet backpack slung over his shoulder, has a disappointed look on his face as his buddy Max; the white kid carrying a single book under his arm, starts to laugh out loud. 'Ten whole dollars... Man, what are you going to do with all that money?' 'Screw you, punk.' 'Excuse me.' The voice coming from behind the boys caused them to jump a bit, startled. They turned slowly, finding an older man in a pair of gray slacks and a white button up, long-sleeved shirt with the sleeves rolled up to his elbows standing there. Detective Nick Pride. 'Do either of you know where 2115 Indian Path Drive is?' Max looks over at JJ, neither boy sure whether they should answer him or not. 'It's okay, kids.' He pulls his wallet from his back pocket, flashing his badge to put the kids at ease. 'Come on, you can get those things on Ebay now.' Pride manages a slight chuckle, tucking the wallet back into his pocket. 'That's right, but mine is real though.' 'Like you're gonna tell us it's fake.' It's JJ's turn to chime in, pulling the backpack tight against his back as he begins to turn around. 'Look, I just need to find out where the house is.' Max points down the street. 'Go one more block down, then take a left. I think it's the last house at the end of the block.' 'Thanks, kid.' Pride walks back in the opposite direction the kids were heading, toward a white Pontiac Grand Am, as the kids continue on their way. He opens the door and gets in, the radio tuned to some rock station, as Speak by Godsmack begins to play through the speakers as the engine comes to life with the turn of the key. He glances over his shoulder before pulling away from the curb, accelerating slowly at first, then giving it a bit more gas when he knows there is no other traffic. He keeps a watchful eye, making sure to turn at the right corner. That's when he sees the house he is looking for. Nothing fancy, just a plain white house with light blue trim. The grass is well kept, and the black sport utility vehicle in the driveway means someone still lives there. They may actually be home, as well. He pulls to a stop in front of the house, turning the car off before opening the door and stepping out. Thousands of thoughts run through his mind, questions about the hero that saved his daughter life. He was inches away from finding out where the guy came from, who his family was, whether or not they knew who Russell Braun was. He felt as if his breathing had stopped as he knocked twice on the door, mere moments away from answers to questions that had kept him awake at night on more than one occasion. The door opened slightly, a chain restricting how far it could actually move. 'Can I help you?' A short Asian woman peaked through the small crack of the door. 'Yes, I was looking for mister or misses Bradley?' 'They no live here no more.' 'Did they move?' 'They die.' Pride was not expecting that. 'Recently?' 'Few months now. Car crash.' 'I see.' Pride rubbed his chin, he had ran into a dead end. 'I'm sorry to have bothered you.' He did not receive a reply, only a door slammed in his face. Literally as well as figuratively. He turned away from the door, uncertain where he should go from here. That is when he saw the elderly lady rocking back and forth on an old porch swing. He made his way down the cobblestone path toward the street, taking a gander both ways before continuing across. 'Excuse me, do you mind if I ask you a few questions?' He made his way up the concrete path leading to her porch, not even waiting for an answer. 'I guess so.' She looked at him through narrowed eyes. 'You ain't selling nothing, is you?' 'No ma'am. I just had a few questions about the family that used to live across the street... The Bradley's.' 'Nice family. They didn't deserve everything that happened to them.' His heart raced, he was finally going to get some answers. 'What happened?' 'Who are you again?' He pulled the wallet from his pocket, allowing her a few moments to get a good look at his badge before tucking the wallet back in. 'You investigating the accident?' 'Yes ma'am.' She nodded her head, as if she had known it all along. 'I knew something funny had happened.' 'How so?' Pride stood there with baited breath, anxiously awaiting all the woman could reveal. 'Well, they really didn't travel much after their boys died. Seemed kind of funny-' He cut her off. 'Wait, did you say boys?' She nodded. 'Both of them. Really sad.' It was too much at once, so many more questions began to cloud his mind. 'And the third boy, I believe his-' It was her turn to cut him off. 'No, was just the two boys.' Her words had nearly knocked him off of his feet. 'Are you sure about that?' 'I practically raised those two boys. I babysat while Bill and Nancy worked, up until the boys started school. I think I would have known if Nancy had a third child.' 'Look, thanks for your help.' 'My help... You didn't even ask me anything about the night of the accident.' 'Trust me, what you've told me has been more than enough.' With that, Pride sprinted back toward his car, nearly stumbling down the stairs as he lost his footing. 'I didn't even get around to telling you about the gentleman I saw snooping around the garage the night before.' She tried to yell to him, but her voice was not as strong as it once was. He would not have heard her anyway. He was too busy trying to figure out how Jayce Bradley could rescue his daughter, when he is supposed to be dead. =?=?=?=?=?= Present San Antonio, Texas =?=?=?=?=?= Pride hovered over the computer screen, his lips twisted into a sneer as he stared at the various images that flashed on the monitor. 'Anything?' Pride shook his head, never turning his attention to the female voice that called out from behind him. 'Maybe the guy just doesn't have a record.' His eyes closed as he took a deep breath, letting it out with a sigh. 'It's like looking for a needle in a haystack.' 'I need to get a warrant to exhume the body.' The woman walked up behind him, her pant suit black to match her hair. 'Nick, you're getting obsessed with this guy.' 'Obsessed?' He finally turned toward her. 'This guy is living a life that doesn't belong to him.' 'And everything you are doing is inadmissible in court, you know that.' He shook his head, turning his attention back to the monitor. 'I have to do something.' 'You stole his hair, Nick... Don't you think you've done enough?' 'I won't rest until I find out who he is... Who he really is.' 'Even if it costs you your job?' He nodded, then blocked out everything but the monitor in front of him. 'Suit yourself.' He didn't even notice when she left, or at least he didn't make it obvious that he knew.
