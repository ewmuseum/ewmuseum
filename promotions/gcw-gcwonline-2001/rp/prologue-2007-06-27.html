Date: 6/27 Brisbane, Australia. Inside the arena, GCW’s best and brightest are separated into two locker rooms. One is for the men, the other for the women. There is one man who does not dress with his fellow talents. One man who still plots his assault upon the promotion. The man called Tabloid, purveyor of rumor. A man who makes his name on the secrets of others. Tabloid sits in a small rental car, already dressed for the night’s combat. That is not his concern. No, truly, whether he defeats the man nicknamed “Unforgiven” is not his concern. Victory is such a trifling concern. Now, at any rate. One day it will become THE goal of goals. But World Championships cannot be won on skill alone. There are a thousand John Does who enter the wrestling business in twenty years Of these, nine hundred likely never make it off independent shows. Take the one hundred left. Of these, figure’say, fifty become well-known on television. Of that fifty, maybe twenty-five become world champions. Ten of those become hall of fame worthy. And one, maybe two, become truly legendary. Sure, the first nine-hundred are no hopers. But what separates the two true legends from the ninety-eight other talents? Not skill. All one-hundred are at least skilled enough to make it to the bigger promotions. Not size. If size is a promotion’s concern, they have already controlled for it. Not even desire. Even the nine-hundred no-hopers want to succeed. The answer, of course, is connections. Tabloid, as of yet, does not have them. But one cannot push too soon. In the three dimensional chess game that is the backstage environment of a major wrestling promotion, one must keep his ears open and his mouth shut, until he is asked to speak. And then, he must hope that what he says is worth saying. If all goes well, Thomas Dsousa’s name will go down in the annals of GCW, no, wrestling history, as the answer to a trivia question. As the first opponent of Tabloid, Legend of All Time.
