Date: today Man in the mirror, man in the mirror. Talk to me, but don't talk back. Tell me what we both know. Tell me what we both lack. *** Kate and I are doing well. Doing real well. We're trying this one step at a time. Neither of us have come back to someone who's completely crushed our hearts and broken our spirits before, but we're coming back. The euphoria hasn't worn off yet, and I hope it doesn't anytime soon. I hope I never in any way wake up and realize what I've done. I've given in. I've been broken. I've rolled over and given Kate the 3 count without even trying to get up. And the best part is that I couldn't care less or be effected less. Life...is good. At least that's what the front of my mind is saying. That's even what the back of my mind is saying. And then there's that little part that we never like to talk about... 'I love you.' That actually means, 'You make me feel good about myself. 'I hope he's alright.' Translation of that is: I'm going to look on in horror and some sick form of entertainment if he got mutilated. Everything has double meanings. Or triple. Or too many for anyone to count or understand. Coming back together with someone you love is no different. Yes, I am going to love having meaning in my relationship again. I'm going to love being able to talk to someone and have someone there for me all the time. This is going to be fantastic. On the other hand...she's not someone I can be with one day and be tired of the next. She's not something I can use for my good feelings and not care about hers, especially with Kate in particular. She won't let that fly. Ever. I've gained the love of my life. I've lost my freedom to be who I am. Fair trade? And I can still remember what happened when I heard her words. Heard her words about my daughter and hers. 'Her name is Sarah.' My first thought was spoken. 'Sarah was my mother's name.' I don't talk about Sarah Fitzwillis too often. I don't feel that it's anyone else's problem to know why I grieve over her death. That's a story for another day. But what I haven't told anyone is my story about Sarah D'Mateo. And that's the thought that popped in second. The one I kept hidden. 'What's your name?' 'Sarah.' Bright, sunny day at the beach in the fullness of my prime. Lead quarterback in high school. Hockey team captain. Varsity wrestling champ. Prom king. Guiatarist. Sweet car. Varsity jacket. Straight B's. Everything you could ever learn to hate about somebody in high school, but secretly want to be them...that was me. I'm not even kidding. There were times when I would step back from me and realize that I hated what I was. That if I saw myself walking down the street, I'd cast the scornful eye and shoot a look of death at myself. But then again, I was me. I had the liberty of just sitting back and enjoying it. And Sarah was into it. 'You got your tickets, Sarah?' She seemed smart. Too smart. 'To what? The gun show?' A slight blush. The pickup line that I liked the most shot down out of the sky by her calling it out. But that's okay. I am a professional bullshit artist. 'The football game this Saturday. It's against Falconer, those cheap, inbred, backwoods bastards.' 'What are they your arch rivals or something?' 'Well yeah. You should know that.' It occurred to me that maybe she wasn't from Akron... 'I'm from Falconer.' Shit. '...did I say Falconer kids were all those things? You know I was just talking about the football players.' 'My brother plays football.' 'Oh...' Even Johnny Fitzwillis has pants he can't get into and holes he can't dig out of. 'But he is cheap. And might be inbred, for all I know.' Her smile floods me. It's all I can see. Imperfections only made her look more perfect. Kind of like the stars in the sky, she wouldn't have been the same if everything was laid out just the exact way it should be. I suppose it's like a supermodel with a mole, or a singer with an overbite. There's was just something about her smile that seemed...crooked. Just a little. The top lip didn't line up perfectly with the bottom lip, and it made her perfect. Whole. Amazing. I loved her right then and there. 'Well...my name's Johnny. And I'm amazingly flexible. I can put both feet in my mouth at the same time.' That gets a smile from her. Not because it's funny, but because she thinks I'm cute. And I can tell that. It's a gift. We talked for hours, Sarah and I. We talked about where we came from. What we do. Where we want to go. Who we really are. It was one of those things where you just find someone and you at first just want to get into their pants, maybe give them a donkey punch, and move on to sleep with their sister... And then, within mere moments, it turned into one of the most perfect moments of my life. Something I can remember frame by frame, not missing a beat in the slightest. And then...the game came, and she wasn't there. Neither was her brother, the jackass player on the other team. I looked for hours and never saw her again. Funny thing is, I don't even remember if we won that game or lost. All I can know is the the swelling hope that was in my heart to see her again. Even one more time. A few years later, I found out that she was living somewhere in California. Father's job got moved out there, and that meant that their lives were out there. On the other side of the country, on the other side of the world. Story of my life. 'Sarah. That was my mother's name...' Kate hears those words and knows the heartbreak that they carry. But she doesn't know the heartbreak, the can of worms, that gets opened right up because of them. A mother lost. A first love never found. And the fact that within minutes of getting back together, I was already, in my head, leaving Kate again.
