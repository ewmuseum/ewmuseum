Sides Beside Opposite Sides

Date: 5-1-2009 or -2005 Sides Beside Opposite Sides A Garbage Bag Production I was going absolutely fucking vegetables. The walls and the ceiling seemed to be closing in, and nobody else was paying it any attention. There were three of them there, and I can't much recall what they called themselves, but at the time, I assigned them arbitrary distinguishments. I was off in a corner, amazed at how fast I could wiggle my fingers. "Guys, I think we should get out of here." It wasn't the first time I had suggested it, but perhaps if they ignored me long enough, they'd forget I was there, and they'd hear it, and it'd be like some alarm from a subconscious messenger. They ignored me, there, combining with their furnishings as I hummed and mummed to myself on a rectangle-cut sheet of foam, staring past the shadows at the corner, and how the trisection of right angles must be inverted outside of itself into an infinite convexity, seven times its size. Given that a rectangular room has eight corners, and each corner has even more infinite convexities, seven times their sizes in all, I calculated that this room in the center of the universe was but one fifty-seventh of everything. This was troubling news. "Guys, I really think we should get out of here." Let's see. There was the loud one, mustachioed, dressed in some kind of Turkish Cowboy getup. He was a wiry-looking motherfucker, alright. I called this one Slim, even though that wasn't his name, and he wasn't responding to it. Then there was another one who didn't say much. He was a bit darker than Slim, and he was dressed in sweatclothes, head to toe, like some kind of cotton mummy. That's why I decided this guy to be the Colonel. The last one, if I remember right, was just as loud as Slim, but he had a square face. There just wasn't much round about it. His hair was flat enough that you could rest an egg on it. I thought it ironic to call him Harelip, for some reason or other. He didn't seem to care much for that either, but every time I said it, Slim looked like he seemed to think I might be talking to him. It wasn't his name either, though. I think it might've caught him off guard, because even though he'd react, there was kind of a distant glaze over his eyes, the big one being the one that was closer based off of which way he turned back at you. "Guys," I reasoned on, only pausing for emphasis. "I think we should get out of here." This was becoming unreasonable; as unreasonable as was becoming was as becoming was something that could not have been, only something that could have been is where before it was not. It sent my mind aspin, and I became unfathomably dizzy. "Is anyone seeing this? Harelip? Meathook? Toothpicks?" I muttered off whatever seemed like an arbitrary name, hoping someone would respond to something. "We're going to get crushed -- crushed like toothpicks. I can feel it! Seriously! This room is getting smaller." "What's he going off about?" "Don't worry about him. He's tripping." "Should we check on him or something?" "Nah, it happens. He'll be fine." The conversation played out like a transmission, hovering electronically italic, and maybe there was some truth to it, but as entirely truthful as it was, it wasn't the entire truth. It was a component of the truth, and without it, there would be no truth, but without its corresponding parts, would be untrue outside of itself. Truth was all the lies, balled up into one, as lies are only untrue in relation to truths. The previous statement is entirely true. The previous statement is a lie. Two voices argued on inside my head, and I wasn't sure if they were arguing the same point, somewhat adjacent points or completely unassociated points, but they weren't arguing opposite points. The voices grew louder as the room shrank, molding itself like a plaster around me. A quiet voice went off about something in that it went on about nothing before going off about nothing. "Guys, we should seriously consider getting out of here." It got lost in an argument nobody was going to win before the walls collapsed. *** I had managed to squeeze out to the stairwell, and this was back when there was a light in the stairwell, but I had to take a break. It took some crawling to get there, and there was some appeal to staying on the ground for a while. The staircase warbled between slithering walls, and I figured a breather would do me some good anyway before trying to descend the treacherous steps. I wasn't followed, and I figured no one had tried, those poor sons of bitches. I put them to the back of my mind and instead thought of smoking a cigar in as vivid detail as one could assign to the prospect. It was like dreaming of eating an apple in slow motion. These were both semi-paralyzing notions that require too much coordination to be worth the movements produced. "If I walk down those stairs," I whittled to an abruption and back from it. "I can forget all about eating apples and smoking cigars." Of course, walking was an extravagant overestimation; I would have been rendered incapable completely if it hadn't been for the kneading constriction of room against the beckoning of space. I slid down the bending stairs a curly whisp of liquid, fancying the idea of life as a tobbogan, until rendered slopeless before the door. The temptation of opening it was inescapable. It devised its own perpetual captivation. I returned to form from puddling and balanced myself at a slant against the door. I steadied myself before the door wondering what was behind it, and how something could have been behind it if it was also after it. It's one of the many examples of the opposition between time and space. It was forcing me to pick sides. The thing about a door, though, is that there's always a way around it. "Perhaps if I walk out backwards," I shrewdly hypothesized. This turned out to be more of a clumsy ordeal than I had expected, but after bumping off the walls, knocking over a shovel and bumping off the door, I managed to spread my way through with the dullness of an old hash knife, which I had kept on my person in case I needed to tussle with a marauder or shave off some flakes from a brick of old hash. I immediately regretted the decision to walk backwards out the door, because when you walk backwards out a door to the rest of the world, you're walking forward into a backwards world. The lights appeared to blaze rampant, glowing off into a black sky that sailed massively over rows of tiny, flat buildings. People were out jogging, walking dogs, pushing strollers -- one man was walking a cat. I had to sit down. It was too much to take in at once. "It's an all juice diet," voices passed, leaving fragments of conversation in front of me, to fellow passers or through cells, where I sat on an oval of stones surrounding dirt where nothing ever grew. "A block north of North," repeated another from the opposite direction. "I'm looking for a sitter," and I immediately sprang up as to not be detected by a third who walked past. I was more interested with what was going on across the street, but I knew I had to get away from all the pedestrian traffic. I got up and started walking, relearning the process as I did it. As I passed people, they started to stop talking to avoid my delinear path. Some stopped briefly and stared. Others addressed me, but I just kept on, tearing down the walkway in charges and heaving campaigns. The world was a disorienting place, at the time, that looked like it had been built from materials that could be picked away, piece by piece, revealing that behind everything, there was nothing, and everything was just there as a distraction from the fact that it wasn't. *** I had found a tall overpass where trains used to run to walk alongisde, and it got to the point where the bricks dipped low enough to hoist myself up and climb atop. Old tracks were reclaimed by vegetation and freestanding iron beams sat along the sides of the raised abandoned tracks. It would have been enough scrap metal for a handsome wage, but I left it undisturbed. From up there, I could see the world operate without me below. It passed on beneath me as if I wasn't even there as I walked along miles of dead track, wondering how long it went on like this. "Perfect." I assessed. The track looked like it had remained untouched for quite some time. I wouldn't have minded if the rest of life was just walking along this track to see what came of it, even knowing that the end of the line really would really be the end of the line. I tread bravely to stops and juts where the track, I thought, may not continue. There was something primordial about these tracks, as if man had not built them. It was like they had always been there, and there was something wise and mystical about them and how parallel one side was to the other, even when the lines curved, they bent in unison, avoiding the buildings that had been erected on either side, and even swerving to avoid buildings that sprang up in its path. The tracks were very much alive, breathing, gritting teeth like the sound of a ghost train. It made me feel locomotive, chugging and stumbling along them. But as I followed the tracks, my eyes trained on a shining off in the distance. Light refracted off of an empty bottle or a crushed aluminum can. A trail littered a trail, and led eventually to a mannish heap blocking the way, stopping there. I put on the brakes and screeched to a halt before the fetal corpse, which stirred and rose and started mumbling at me. "The trains don't come along here no more!" He roared, a thick scent to his breath. It smelt like it was decaying, and his teeth! They'd all been rounded off from lying among the old ballast. "You'd be wise to climb back down." "What are you doing here?" I felt for the knife in my pocket, gripping its handle but leaving the stout blade sheathed. He danced in a circle, hooting in defense like a wounded beast in an invisible corner. "I was just . . . I don't know. I needed some rest, that's all." "Well, move along. You're blocking my way." I eased my grip, but the handle of the blade steadied loose between my fingers as I tipped it back and forth. "I'm blocking your way? I think you got it the other way around, see. You're blocking my way." Hoarse chirps hiccuped from his old pipes. "You weren't moving. How can you have a way?" I asked the stagnant man as he swayed before me like a slinking spring. I returned my full grip to the hilt of knife. "You want to go through?" he poked, signaling belligerencies. "You're gonna hafta go through me, you hear?" I nodded, sliding the dull knife from my pocket. He took a look at it, mulling over his prospects. I could tell from his eyes that he was renegotiating his position, but his body seemed incapable of vacation. He held steady, apprehensively, but he gulped that down. Nobody would've known. He'd have lied there, dead again for interfering with my course, and when they eventually found him, they wouldn't figure what to make of it. They'd probably make nothing of it. "You gonna stab me or what?" "Yeah, I'm gonna stab you," I said, mustering up the gusto. "You'll see." "Well, go on, then! I ain't got all day!" He made the motions to beckon at me to stab him, but I could tell he was just bluffing. He didn't really want me to do it. To be honest, I didn't really want to do it much either. I had hoped this whole thing would work itself out without incident, but there we went, slowly making a game of chicken out of life as I inched the knife closer to his chest, and he puffed his chest out chickenlike even more in response. When the two collided, we had built up so much suspense that the dull knife just thudded softly off his rib cage, not even making the slightest puncture. We both looked down at it, and he started hooting with laughter. *** The draw of being atop the abandoned tracks eventually faded on me, well before I had been able to walk the line through. I climbed down from the structure where I was, wondering how I had gotten there. It was much colder than I had remembered, and much dimmer, too. Fierce scores raged on in my head as I walked towards where I came, failing to see the brilliance where it had once been. There's a certain man-made vividness to the night that outshines the dull pallor of day, where the light exposes all of the crooks and flaws of structures once bathed in glowing neon disguises. The sun had risen up once again, and as much as I wanted to go to sleep, there were boring things that needed to be done to strike some kind of arbitrary numerical balance. There are two sides, you see, for every one line, and the sides are always the same, no matter how different they may look when you see them one at a time; no matter how hard it'd be to tell one from the other if you saw them both together.
