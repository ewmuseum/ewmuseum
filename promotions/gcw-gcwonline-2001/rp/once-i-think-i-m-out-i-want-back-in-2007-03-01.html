Once I Think I'm Out... I Want Back In

1 March 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/1/07 Davis Branxton feels pretty good today. He’s thirty-six years old and he works full time at Midtown Comics in Manhattan. His favorite comics are Batman and he never understands why people like to read any Hulk books, but they do sell. He makes $11 an hour and has been working at Midtown for the past year and a half. When Davis isn’t at the comic book store he goes to a bar on 4th Street in the lower easy side called KGB Bar pretty often where he’s friendly with the wait staff. One of the bartenders, a solid-looking woman with long red hair named Janine, even flirts with Davis regularly and yesterday the two went out of their first date. Janine is four years younger than Davis and moved to New York to become a director, but lost interest in it very quickly. KGB Bar has three showrooms upstairs that hold independently produced shows almost every night. It’s the sort of building that looks as if it’s been around since Al Capone was running around with the Brooklyn Rippers. A mixture of Jack and coke drowns a few ice cubes with Davis? face hovering above the glass. Janine is bartending and smiles sweetly whenever she wanders by Davis? table. Things aren’t really the way that he thought his life was going to turn out. Comic books? Bars? Red heads? That’s where he’s at now and if you or even John Cusack told Mr. Branxton two, three, or fourteen years ago that this was what he’d be doing he would’ve piledrove you into the sidewalk. Old friends and even old rivals, the few that he’s had of both, would have laughed in your face. It would’ve been a good joke. Now it’s the way things are and it’s not all that bad. He’s content. And that’s fine; things are steady. Davis is a little high and a little tipsy and he’s got a cute girl that likes him and he likes back. He’s in a bar enjoying the company of his self and no one else. He’s got a savings account, a cell phone, an apartment, a job and a bed. A really nice bed with a mattress and a headboard. Davis has three different pairs of sheets and he sleeps with two pillows each night. He reads. He read books. Hunter S. Thompson, Nick Hornby and Chuck Klosterman. The past is the past. A whole lifetime ago he was someone else, but isn’t that statement true for everyone? That’s why you shouldn’t remind people of past lives. “Aren’t you Twizzy?” Davis looks up slowly as if this boy, this child in front of him has the wrong person. “Excuse me?” he asks. The kid is about twenty-one years old. He’s got ruffled black hair and he’s wearing a flannel t-shirt. He’s not an actor. Davis saves his judgment of the kid. “You’re Twizzy. From GCW,” he says. Davis thinks for a minute and furrows his brow. “I was.” This child in front of Davis smiles bright. “Wow,” he utters. “Do you mind?” he asks acknowledging the empty chair across from Davis. Davis nods him into it. “I’m Nathan,” he says with his hand held out. Davis shakes it. “Name’s Davis.” Nathan nods uncaring, “Davis.” He looks down at the Jack and coke then grabs Janine’s attention. “Excuse me. I’ll have a Guinness.” Davis sighs silently. After a minute Janine brings over the beer in a pint glass. She smiles at Davis. “So who’s your friend?” “Nathan.” “I can’t believe you’re here. Right on 4th street,” Nathan says excitedly. “Davis?” Janine asks Nathan. “He’s always here.” Davis fondly watches her away. Nathan sees it, but decides not to mention it. Instead he asks, “So why don’t you wrestle anymore?” “I do,” Davis replies. “Every now and again. I enter a tournament here and there.” He lets out a small laugh. “Usually get knocked out in the first round.” Disappointed: “Really” Ugh, that sucks, man. You were so much fun to watch.” Davis nods a thank you. “So what do you do for a living?” he asks whie taking a drink. “I’m in a band.” “Of course you are.” Nathan kindly laughs. “Yeah. I play bass and we’re mainly a progressive grunge band. Like Radiohead before Kid A.” Davis nods, but he doesn’t really know a lot about Radiohead. “I’ve always been more into Funs N” Roses and Soundgarden.” “Totally.” There’s a long silence. It’s quite obvious to Nathan that Davis isn’t as talkative as he thought he would be. Where were the catchphrases, the rants, the nuttiness? This was a man that would shot at the top of his lungs, “THE POWER OF TWISTODIUM COMPELLS YOU!” Is that what these drugs do to you? They make you complacent and boring in your min-thirties? Shit. “Hey, can I ask you for a favor?” Davis shrugs. “Well,” Nathan begins. “I’ve always been a pretty strong guy. I was just wondering if I could arm wrestle you. I’ll buy you a drink if you win.” “I already got a drink.” “How about we play for your girlfriend’s tip?” “She’s not my girlfriend.” “Oh, well in that case-? No,” Davis interrupts. “We’ll bet that.” Nathan puts his elbow on the table immediately. Davis has just started working out again. He’s a big guy, but he’s got a little bit of a girth to him now. He’s wider than he was even two years ago, but he’s definitely healthy. Besides the pills. Sometimes he thinks they’re poisoning him slowly by keeping him healthy. Davis isn’t even sure he can beat this kid, but why not give Nathan a nice story to tell his friends. Win or lose, the kid’s not being an asshole about anything and that means he’s all right. Davis cracks his knuckles and stretches his arm before slowly gripping Nathan’s arm and locking their wrists together. Davis sits up and says, “We’ll go on three, okay?” Together: “One” two? three.” Nathan is a little stronger than Davis thought, but, surprisingly, so is he. They’re even and shaking for a good ten seconds before Davis realizes that the face he is making must be an intense one. Davis wants to win. He gains an inch on Nathan and his competitive state becomes more focused. His grip becomes harder and he starts pulling Nathan in. Two inches from the table and Nathan is fighting hard, but Davis is having too much fun to end it so quickly. He lets Nathan gain three inches and then starts reeling his arm in slowly. Every second is another ? inch. One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, night, ten and they’re so close to being over. Davis speaks heartily, “You thought you were going to beat me,” and then finishes Nathan off. He’s smiling happily. Nathan smiles back. “Well, I know when it’s time to make my leave. Thanks for chatting.” He pulls out his wallet and slips a bill onto the table. It’s a twenty. “You’re a good guy, Nathan.” The boy’s night has been made and he’s got an excellent story for his bandmates now. As Nathan leaves the bar Davis empties his highball glass. “I think I need to call Steven.”
