Black Wave

12 December 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/12/10 Nothing lasts forever, That's the way it's gotta be, There's a great black wave in the middle of the sea, For me. - Arcade Fire One As the chipped-black nails of Vivica J. Valentine tore into your face, you felt a scream sear your throat, scorching it raw. Skinned alive, the harsh lights above sizzled the sweat which basted your ghostly pale, scar-blighted face and lit your optic nerve like a fuse. The sensory explosion which ensued cast blotchy debris across your vision, while the lungful of fresh air you gulped down fizzed with vitality. The gasps of the crowd swallowed up all sound, save for the intense, static hum which had resonated within you for months. Your tongue tasted sweet and salty; fingertips numb, pulsing. You smelled the burnt-ozone stench of rage which engulfed your blonde assailant. The despising glares from all in the ring bore deep into your eye sockets, forcing Rasa‘s malevolent chill to penetrate deeper into your bones; it clung on with venomous barbs. You felt the pit of your stomach sink, as if you’d swallowed heavy, crude oil. Disbelief evaporating from before the crowd’s eyes, thousands upon thousands of needles pierced your bulked-out leather straight-jacket and punctured your body to its infected core. As Rasa was flushed from your body, its desperate, dark talons tightened, threatening to eviscerate you upon its expulsion. Your pupils and nostrils dilated, throat frothed with effervescent bile, and your eardrums’ membrane stretched to the thickness of a single, fragile cell. Your teeth ached at their roots and temples thundered a fit-inducing techno baseline. As Rasa was purged from your system by the sensory detox, you saw reality ripple, as if it ascended through the surface tension of a murky lake. Rich swathes of colour were painted across those stood before you; Vivica’s shirt now a dense black, not the charcoal gray as you once saw it; Triumph’s hair an autumnal, sandy blonde not a jaundiced grey. The life which had been breathed into the world felt as if it had been siphoned from your withering soul. You felt cold, abandoned, and for the first time since Rasa had insulated your nerves in its sticky black tar… you were scared. Your humanity was slow to reboot, your central nervous system restoring back to its civilised default. Left with only your primal instincts, you retreated under the pressure of the overbearing world until physics was left with but one option. You exploded. Violently and savagely. Two Your name is Karina Marie Wolfenden. This much you know. Only daughter of Lawrence and Cassandra. Brother of Maximus. Aunt and Godmother to Erin May. They are your family and they are real, not just a figment of your imagination. You remember faces, birthdays… but not the last time you spoke to them, or they spoke to you. Sometime, someplace, you got broken… and you won’t let yourself remember why. Three The lights flickered out overhead as you slithered out of your darkened locker room. Every movement sent fierce pains coursing throughout your body; nerves, muscle and bone relaying the aches which reared their dull, agonising head once your adrenaline had boiled dry. Tempest’s feet, Vivica’s fist, Frost’s elbow, Shaman’s head and Andy Murray’s forearm had all signed your skins visitors book with scratches, gashes and deep, mottled bruises. A whisper trickled down the corridor, overlapping strands weaving into white noise. “Hello?” you probed. “Who’s there?” The whispering swallowed your words into the darkness, then intensified, strobing the lights above as a visualisation. As you froze, you could feel the words swirl around your body and trickle into your ear. Wincing, you stared out into the violently bright strobing lights. Your brain tried to convince your eyes that there was a shadowy figure blinking in and out of existence. For eight months, you’d been encased within a dark, toxic cocoon, numb to pain and detached from fear. Vivica J. Valentine had ripped the cocoon from you, and now you were a fragile butterfly, infected to the bone with paralysing pain and trembling in fear. “Viv… is that you?” only angered the stream of whispers, creating a violent torrent of hissing noise. “Johhny? Terror?” Rasa had been torn from your body, but its enemies were never going to be content exacting their vengeance on a ripped mask and a discarded leather suit; not now there was a living, breathing body to punish. The doors at either end of the corridor slammed shut, the draft carrying away the whispers and blowing out the lights. You breathed in, then breathed out. Breathed in, breathed out. A shrill onslaught of distorted screaming flooded the corridor as every light above went supernova, scorching you retinas before showering you with shards of plastic and a fine drizzle of molten tungsten. You dived for the safety of your locker room. “What the fu-…” Four Enveloped by brain tissue, you felt it pulse against your cold, clammy skin. Feeling yourself sinking, you desperately reached out for a frayed neuron, only for the impulses coursing through it to shock you, turning your fingertips to cotton wool. “This isn’t happening,” struggled to convince even yourself as the throbbing tissues shook you deeper. “This isn’t happening…” The whispers returned, plugging your ear with incomprehensible strands of sounds. Dendrites lashed out like jellyfish tentacles, each sting flashing before your eyes. As their attacks intensified, you saw shapes in the flashes, and the static blighting your ear tuned for the briefest of moments. Two dendrites latched onto you, and you saw the ghost of a face remain imprinted on your retina. “Dad..?” Again, the surge of current through your body cleared the signal. You made out faces, times, places. A spider web of nerve endings broke your fall and you completed the circuit; memories slowly pieced themselves back together, frame by frame. Against the tide, your eyelids peeled back, images flooding your eyes. Five You’re not a bad person… at least not deliberately. You found yourself passing through life as much as living in it, always slightly out of sync. Your family had anchored you, but sometime, someplace, you were set adrift into dark, treacherous waters, where you were dragged under by the currents… and you drowned. You tried to remember. Tried to remember where. Tried to remember when. Tried to remember… why? Six Outside the window, a flittering shower of snowflake confetti glistened under the streetlights while churning tyre treads dug a track toward the front door. Your nose pressed up against the window pane, panting, excited breaths misting it in front of your sugar-dilated eyes. As you heard feet crunch the thick, fondant icing of snow which decorated the steps, you scurried down from the windowsill, socks struggling for traction on the varnished wooden floorboards. As your father stepped in from the frigid night, you latched onto his leg, your ear resting against the soft, ice-cold pillow of his snowy coat. “Daddy! Daddy!” you chirped with festive urgency. “Can me and Max open a present now? Can we? Please?! Mommy sai-” “Kari,” he grunted disapprovingly, shaking you from his leg under a deluge of dislodged snow. “Not now, I’ve only just got in. Be a good girl and go see if your mother needs a hand with anything.” All the warmth in the house felt like it escaped into the night as your father closed the door behind him and shooed you away. The notes of “Jingle Bell Rock” began to slow, and the world misted up just like the window pane… save for a small patch through which you saw your father fondly rustle Max’s mop of sandy blonde hair. “Too early…” you muttered. Seven As the world cleared before you, its vibrancy evaporated in the mist, leaving colours bleached and runny. The swirling charcoal clouds outside hung heavy in the sky, their payload denying your dorm room the merest ray of hopeful sunshine. “This isn’t a joke, Karina,” Cassandra Wolfenden’s stern sigh chastised. There were enough packed boxes littered around the room for you to build a wall around yourself, but you knew it would crumble under the weight of your mother’s disapproval. The walls were a bare, washed-out beige, studded with thumb tacks and the torn, worn corners of old posters. It had been stripped of any personality; the college disinfected of your presence. “What was this all about?” your mother interrogated. “You father had to jump through hoops just to get you through the door, and they won’t even have you before your first semester is out? If this was just another petty little act of rebellion then I’m don-” “Mom, I… it’s…” Before the words could arrange themselves on your tongue, a thunderclap blew the windows open. Your stripped-bare room was flooded by the deluge funnelled in through the window, dissolving away the cardboard boxes and carrying your possessions away. “… it isn’t it,” you told yourself as you held your arms open, inviting the tide. As your mother desperately clung on to the closet door handle, you were pulled under water, the ink running from your diary scribbling taunts as it swirled around you. Eight You felt a hand, superheated by vitriol, brand your cheek with a slap. The palm print steamed. Your lungs jettisoned rainwater, washing away your voice as it frothed over your tongue. Desperate, breathless lungs gulped at the heavy, tense air, biting chunks from it. “Never, ever, tell her she can come anywhere near my… our daughter, Max,” Alice Wolfenden barked, her maternal instincts so deep rooted that they bordered on the feral. “How many times does she have to show she can’t be trusted before you realise that she’s a fuck-up? Ten? Twenty? I told you I don’t want her left alone with Erin, and you let… you… what were you thinking? Were you thinking?” Every urge coursing through you screamed in retaliation, but your sodden body was too heavy to lift from the floor. So you lay, every word cutting you deeper like a pendulum was swaying overhead. “Erin is good for her,” your brother appealed, but his words faded with futility once they had left the safety of his tongue. “She’s…” “Our daughter is not a toy you use to domesticate a pet, Max.” Alice’s words whipped your weighted-down body, memories flooding back with every lash of pain. “It’s her or your daughter, Max… and don’t you dare even think for one second about this.” “February 12th, 2010...” you muttered under your breath. “That day, I… I remember.” Nine The weight of your sodden body splintered the floorboards beneath you. As a dull ache cracked across your skull, you lay on the damp floor of your locker room, halogen lights above burning bars across your vision. You remembered. You remembered where. You remembered when. You remembered why. And as a tear tumbled from the corner of your eye, you longed for the warm, numbing comfort of Rasa’s mask.
