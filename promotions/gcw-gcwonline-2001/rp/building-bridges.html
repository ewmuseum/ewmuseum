Date: several weeks ago .:: ::: ::. Outside of Dallas/Ft. Worth Four Winds Academy She had spent the last few hours lost in thought. It was no unusual place for Lia Vlaszy to be, but there were times when the reverie was deeper than others. Today, it was as though she was buried under ten tons of obscuring clouds, keeping her from fully connecting with the outer world around her. She knew that Aine Leanan was aware of this fact, and that Annie was perhaps concerned about her ability to speak cogently with the master of this place, Mr. Ian Childress, if she was so wrapped up in her own mind. But this trip had supposedly been pleasure, not business, no matter how much business it would eventually end up containing. And she had no doubt that at some point, the conversation would turn serious. She glanced to her left side, and received a slightly tense smile from Virgil. It was not so much that he was worried about anything, just aware of their situation as they traveled from the airfield to the main house, despite the fact that they were doing it in style in a limousine. She would have expected such a vehicle from this place’modestly done enough that it was not evidence of poor taste, but rather of cold, hard material plenitude. On her other side was Rune Hvalstr’m. She was finding it exceedingly difficult to not feel badly for Rune of late’she had heard the story several times over, especially when Rune had requested that Virgil retell it to him as he tried in vain to understand what sort of life, exactly, he had fallen into. But there were no answers’there were no answers in knowing that this man, whether he liked the idea or not, would never die. That he would likely never see his ex-wife or daughter again. It was a bitter pill for him to swallow, and probably, on some level, Rune would never forgive Virgil. But right now, he seemed absorbed. Rune was a fairly young man when he had been turned. Now, he would be forever thirty-four. He would always possess that long, slightly unkempt dirty blonde hair, and those ice-blue eyes would never cloud with the cataract of old age. And he might always possess a grudge against Virgil for not letting him die in the middle of a Maryland highway when he felt there was nothing left to live for. Rune had come with them out of necessity. Lia might perhaps have chosen Dawn for the duty, as Dawn might have found it interesting, but it was not yet safe to leave Rune to fend for himself. That was the excuse, at least’Lia was hoping the time away from road trips and travel would do him a bit of good. “Well,” said Annie, softly breaking the spell, “We’re about to the main house, as you can see. This here is the courtyard, with the three paths diverging to the Academy, the cathedral, and the house itself.” “Cathedral?” Rune murmured to himself, “Who the hell has a cathedral at their house?” Virgil shot him a dirty look, letting him know just how rude he thought he was being. Annie simply chuckled. “People who don’na use their house as jus' a house. This is much more of an all purpose facility. Especially since Ian took it over. Which, aside from the Order tooting its own 'orn about how much money it 'as, makes this place big by necessity. Some people in our organization, as ye might imagine, are quite religious. It was founded by people who were kicked ou' o' the churches fer asking t' many bloody questions.” A nervous twitch gripped the pit of Lia’s stomach for a moment. Religion, and in particular religious zealots, had never exactly been her favorite topic. Particularly when it called up reminders of being captured in Iberia at her husband’s home and dragged back to Zdice to be executed for heresy. She shuddered almost imperceptibly. There was a squeeze around her left hand, and as she looked up, she saw the comfort of two blue eyes who appeared to have read the thought that just slid through her mind. Since the revelation of their past, Virgil and Lia had become almost two beings in symbiosis as one. She hadn’t told a soul what they’d remembered. Perhaps it was out of a sense that they might be spoiling it if they told, or perhaps simply because the story would have seemed unbelievable had she told it to anyone except Dawn, who understood such things as a matter of course. The limousine slowed to a halt without so much as a bump. Lia had to give credit to the people who staffed this place: everyone she met thus far had been both respectful and incredibly skilled’though the first was perhaps out of their deference to the wishes of Mr. Childress about the treatment of his guests rather than any personal knowledge of who (or what) they were. Lia, for a moment found herself transfixed by the statue they had just passed, practically pressing her nose to the window glass to get a better look. “Great lord, Annie what is that?” Annie chuckled. “That’s the reaction most people 'ave when they see tha' piece. An angel an' his lover, that’s the best way I cen explain it. If ye know yer lore, you’d understand.” Lia stared at it, awestruck for a moment longer. “Exquisite?” she breathed softly. “Alright, out we go, then,” Annie chirped, slipping out of the car with her usual boundless but graceful energy. Almost before Rune, Virgil and Lia could slide out to join her, she started down the long path that led to the mansion-house in the distance. Though it was nearly a scramble to keep up with her, Lia’s own expectation and’dare she call it excitement’was making her do so. “Annie,” she said, “We’re not overdressed, are we?” Ms. Leanan turned back from the group, giving the three of them a brief once-over. “No,” she returned, “not in the least. Although you may feel comfortable in something a little more” traditional, if you catch my drift.” Lia nodded. “You told me that earlier, I remember” will we have an opportunity to change before we meet them?” Them referring, of course, to both Ian and Fey Childress, the latter of whom Lia had only heard brief references and stories about. 'If I'm t' 'azard a guess, The Mistress, 'er Highness Maeb Fey will be waiting t' meet ye in th' foyer when we go in. But if ye ask, she'll be willing t' provide ye with a place, I'm sure. Ye have something fer yer friend, as well? I'd na' want 'im to be th' only one in a suit.' She had nodded towards Rune, whose attention was briefly caught by the mention. His blue eyes fixed on Annie. “Yes,” Lia returned, “He’s about the same size as Virgil, so until he can collect some things of his own from his home in Norway, he’s provided for.” “Good,” Annie nodded, pleased that at least, Rune would be fitting in. She got a vibe off of the young man that he felt like a sore thumb everywhere he went right now. Best not to exacerbate the problem. A moment later, they had arrived. Annie stepped forward and politely raised the gargoyle-headed iron-ring doorknocker, thumping it against the door three times. For a moment, there was nothing but silence, then the tread of footsteps. The door slid slowly open, like something out of a horror movie. As they stepped inside, however, it was anything but horrific. It was decorated in a fashion that Lia would have termed “tastefully bombastic” if such a dichotomy were possible. Behind the door, she saw the black glove of another servant, and she stepped through second, as Rune and Virgil, both remembering their manners, had already allowed Annie to go in. Virgil brought up the rear, and the door slowly creaked shut, the sound echoing as though they were being locked inside a cavernous vault. Annie leading the way, the little group stepped forward into the middle of the large reception area, where two staircases arced down in horseshoe fashion, centered around a huge tile mosaic. Lia’s eyes could not help but be drawn to its artful renditions of the traditional lore’the fall from Paradise, assembled armies, Judgment Day, all rendered in tiny stones. She found it breathtakingly delightful, and wondered if anyone else did. “Well, what a motley little band we have here,” came a voice from the shadows, “It would seem that our standards have sunk indeed to allow such lowly creatures in such a place as this” And one of them only a whelp.” Lia knew the voice. She knew it all too well from her last encounter with him, and her entire jaw went rigid as she whirled towards the shadows where Korwyn Malik slouched on a mosaic-tiled bench. “Oh shut yer yap. They’re 'ere on The Keeper's personal invitation, an' ye know that,” Annie said. Despite Korwyn being a part of the same order she herself was, she found his arrogance, and his willingness to dwell on old grudges quite irritating at times. Most times. “I know very well on whose invitation they came,” Korwyn snorted boredly, as he stood up to his full height. As he stepped from the shadows, she could see that he was attired in much the same way as Annie was today’older style clothes. He wore a flowing white swordsman’s shirt that contrasted with the swarthy brown of his skin, paired with what seemed to be a pair of brown soft-leather pants, and knee high boots. It was still hard not to notice the air shimmering around his presence, warping slightly as he came forward towards them. Rune, as though sensing an aberration, shifted uncomfortably. “And I see you have not even informed the whelp of his circumstances, tsk” What kind of mother are you?” He hesitated after asking the question, turning his eyes then to Virgil, “If you are even his mother” since I see you are not the only old one here. And who is this? I can practically smell the stench of a thousand years dead on him.” “Korwyn?” Aine started to snap. But before she could finish admonishing him, another voice took over. “Lord Malik! This is na Yer father's temple in Spain. My 'usband is the sovereign on this side o' the water. They come on 'is bidding an' invitation. I won’na 'ave you making this 'ouse or Cathedral feel unwelcome t' our guest!” As though on cue, everyone’s eyes shifted upwards to the woman who was practically gliding down the last few steps to the floor. As she approached the group of visitors with a warm smile, Lia could practically feel ethereality radiating off of her white skin. She, like Korwyn, was dressed in simple, old-style garb’a flowing ivory gown cut to perfectly enhance her feminine form, without overdoing it. Aine nodded towards her. “This is the Colonel’s wife” 'Er Highness Maeb Fey McClancy-Childress, the Mistress of Secrets an' Crown Princess o' Oberon's Court in Tir Na Nog. And Mistress, your guests? Signor Virgil Ambrosi, Baronessa Lia Vlaszy, and Mr. Rune Hvalstr’m.” Fey took each of their hands in turn, receiving deferent greetings from Lia and Virgil, and a somewhat awkward handshake from Rune, who seemed entirely uncertain of how to handle himself in the presence of someone with so many titles. “Welcome to the Academy. Please jus' call me Fey, though.” smiled the radiant red-haired woman who had captivated their attention, “Aine, thank ye fer taking such good care o' our guests. I’ll take over from here. Would ye be a doll an' check with th' kitchen staff t' ensure the dinner is movin' along an' the food will be properly prepared?” Emerald green eyes gleamed in thanks as Aine nodded and silently made her way down the hall, presumably to do whatever else it was she had on the table for the day. Without sound, Korwyn too had slipped away, presumably bored of the pissing contest he had started. “Now,” Fey started, “I can really get down t' welcoming you. Aine does a wonderful job, as I’m sure ye know from being acquainted with 'er like ye are, but she can be a little formal sometimes” an' I have t' warn you? Geofrey doesn’na like all that pomp unless he absolutely has to deal with it. So? be respectful at your own risk. In fact, 'e answers t' the name Ian more readily t' 'is rank 'er titles any day.” There was an amused gleam in her eyes that made Lia feel at ease. Along with that gleam, she caught the sprightly sparkle of light against a velvet choker at Fey’s neck’or more particularly the family crest that was attached to that choker. It wasn’t immediately recognizable. “Is that your family crest?” Lia asked, nodding towards it. “Hm” Fey started, her fingers moving towards the choker, as if she had worn it so long that it had become a part of her. A smile passed over her features, one more knowing than perhaps the occasion called for, “Oh, this. No, it’s Ian’s.” And that was all the explanation, as the Mistress of Secrets turned up the steps. “Now, if ye’d like, we cen all stop standing around 'ere” the study’s much more comfortable.” “Oh,” Lia said, as though jostled out of a memory, “Do you have a place where the three of us could change clothes?” Fey’s brow wrinkled briefly. “Change” But you look fine.” Lia smiled, slightly. “Well, perhaps, but you have to admit we don’t quite fit in” Aine informed me of the general expected dress before we came, so we came prepared. We’d like to respect your Order’s rules.” Fey smiled, though it would seem she felt that the idea was quite odd’one did not, after all, usually impose on one’s guests in such a manner. But, she nodded. “Well, Ian and I certainly won’na prevent ye from doing that. As we come up the stairs 'ere, if ye turn to th' riogh', go down the hall three doors, and then go to the left. There is a bathroom big enough fer at least two t' change in. If some of you are ah” squeamish? there’s a smaller facility across th' 'all and down another door. “Thank you,” Lia said, bowing her head. There was something to be appreciated about a good hostess’it was quite the lost art. .:: ::: ::. Four Winds Academy The Study As they were ushered into the room by Fey, the first thing of Ian that Lia could see was a glimmer on the middle finger of his right hand, draped as it was across the back of the desk chair as he stared out the window into the sunset over his palatial estate. He turned as he heard footsteps behind him, and Lia found herself face-to-face with a lanky, handsome man dressed in attire very similar to his wife’s. She had already been introduced to him, of course, and he stepped forward, bowing his head in greeting as he took her hand. “Good evening, Lia” I’m glad you could make it.” Virgil’s eyes fixed on him somewhat warily, and of course, he took note of that as well as he stepped forward to shake Virgil’s hand. “And Senor Ambrosi” Welcome to the Academy.” With a look of surprise, he turned to Rune and tilted his head slightly, then turned back to Lia. “I ah” don’t mean to be rude, but I thought you would have perhaps brought Dawn?” Lia shrugged slightly. “Dawn is otherwise disposed this week, and didn’t feel like making the trip, I presume, when she had other business. In any case, it was far more pressing to bring Rune with us. He is” still learning.” She didn’t want to belittle him in front of the rather imposing figure that stood before them, but in truth, she and Virgil would have been quite irresponsible to leave Rune on his own. Ian nodded, and stuck his hand out to Rune. “You’re certainly welcome here as well. Ian Childress.” “Rune Hvalstr’m,” he said quietly, “Sorry if I screw something up, I frankly have no clue what you people are doing here, and I feel really stupid wearing these pants.” Despite the quiet and unassuming tone of his voice, Rune’s annoyance with the formalities came through fairly clearly, causing Ian to chuckle, even as Shadow winced slightly. Plainly, whatever they had taught Rune, they hadn’t taught him protocol yet. Ian smiled, and for a moment, behind that smile, Lia could see shades of something else’almost as though Ian Childress was sort of two people in one. But it was an odd perception, one which easily became unsettling, and as such, she tried to push it away as quickly as she could. “This is obviously my study,” he said, gesturing to the walls full of books, “And we’ll wait here until dinner is prepared. We discussed steak, that’s alright, I suppose?” Lia nodded, knowing that Virgil and Rune were both likely making slightly distasteful faces, the former because he wasn’t quite used to the ability to eat again, and the latter because he found the thought of barely-seared meat quite disgusting. “Then, by all means, have a seat.” The trio followed his instructions with seeming deference. It was perhaps strange in Virgil’s and Lia’s case that creatures of such power would follow orders, but in truth, Lia found Ian Childress to be quite commanding a presence, even when he was casually receiving guests. He and Fey took their seats in two side-by-side armchairs with a small table in between; the distance was short enough for them to reach out and take one another’s hands if they felt the need to do so, which they at the moment seemed to. Ian glanced back and forth between his guests, set up on the sofa like a row. “I presume you wonder if there is actually a reason I asked you here, aside from simple hospitality towards a group that is working so closely with the order” you’ll find I’m not nearly so biased as Lord Korwyn when it comes to such things. Lia smiled slightly at the thought. “You could say so” it didn’t have much to do with any research, though, and last I checked, Alexis and Gray are still at a loss for what could have caused the memory loss on a cellular level? Four Winds Academy The Gardens As Lia stepped through the study for a second time, her thoughts were not on the heady, lush smell of orchids that filtered into her nose, but on the scene she had witnessed a few moments ago. Dinner had been a relatively quiet affair, all small talk. But it didn't. 'Here...' He flipped the knife over again, holding it out to her, handle first. 'Take a look.' Shadow took the blade from him and ran her thumb across the blade. It was dull as it could be. 'Now, some hunters. Those who have become creatures of habit, if they had such a weapon, they'd look at that knife and go... well the blade is dull, but the point is still sharp. So I'll just stab everything! It'll stab and stab until it's dead. They'll repeat the stabbing motion over and over again until they have claimed their victim. But, a hunter who does not want to give itself away? would adapt.' He reached out for the blade and Lia gave it back to him. Taking it, he walked over to the edge of the terrace. Methodically he then drew the blade a few times over the bricks that made up the rampart wall. A few times down each side. 'In this case, this instance of adaptation, you look around and find something, a stone or a rock, or a brick... and you then use it for a purpose it might not have been intended for, but one that would get the job done....' He pulled the knife up and then walked over in front of Lia. He then, right in front of her, drew the blade across his arm. A thick river of red formed and then ran down his arm. 'Now, a hunter with this weapon has just added another dimension to their attack. One that could surprise people. Those that knew he was always stabbing away at his enemies would be in for a surprise if, after a few futile stabs, he suddenly brings the knife laterally across their throat, now wouldn't they?' She folded her arms across her chest, her eyes taking on a curious, but not entirely convinced, expression. “What are you suggesting?” Ian dropped the knife back on the wrought-iron table with a clatter. He smirked, an expression that seemed to be usual for him’but, not expecting it, she wasn’t entirely sure what it meant. “You mean to tell me you can’t figure it out on your own” Don’t tell me one of Vasily Zaitsev’s snipers has forgotten all the training she received? and I don’t mean the training with the gun.” She raised both of her eyebrows high on her pale forehead’surprised first at his knowledge of her past, and then by the suggestion. “But” it’s combat SAMBO. Meant for killing opponents, not? wrestling.” Ian shrugged. 'Just like that knife is a tool and already has a blade, you have all the tools you need. You just need to sharpen and adapt them. Even your own friends know what to expect from you now. It's time to mix things up, to stir the pot so to speak, add a few more spices to the same old bland food? what happens when you throw in a SAMBO strike where someone expected flash? The bottom line is, nobody expects you to do this. They expect you to quote unquote “entertain the crowd?” which means they take advantage of less flashy, more effective moves that give them the ability to take you down.” He paused, looking out over his garden like a lion surveying its territory. “I could go into many clich’s, but when it comes down to it... you've seen my work. My best student ever is one of your allies, and dare I say maybe even becoming a friend. Which is a feat for him also. But there are secrets I've not even taught him.' “So” are you making an offer then?” she asked, leaning against the waist-high brick wall that was behind her. “Perhaps,” he said, “An agreement of fair trade, you might call it.” “Trade, huh?” she said, mulling the idea carefully, “Well, put your terms out there.” “I take you on as a student” and that’s not anything on the line of insulting, because I find that I myself learn something new every day. The day we stop learning is the day that we cease maturing, and cease having the ability to overcome our faults. I also know a thing or two about heading a successful group’which you might imagine. Right now, you’ve got clay that could be molded into a powerful force, but up until this point, you’ve been too lenient on them. When nobody expected your attack, that was effective. But GCW is organizing a resistance force, and for that, you need a prepared, well-trained army. And you need the tools to mold them, because if you turn it around now and try to instantly change your tune, you’re going to alienate them. They have to understand that they’re a part of something larger than themselves. I’ll show you how to do that? And the other thing? involves showing you how to use something that you’ve just discovered you have.” She looked up at him sharply. “The mark?” “Mhmm,” he answered, still staring off into the garden, “I noticed you saw my” visitor? in the mirror ball earlier.” “Yeah,” she said, somewhat taken aback that he had noticed at all. “If you saw him that clearly, then it’s beginning to manifest in you. And it’s a power that you need to know how to control.” She nodded. There was, of course, no denying that’long had her kind talked about the Mark of Cain, but she had never heard anyone reference having the ability to manipulate it to your advantage. All this, coming to this conclusion’an alliance with those that were supposed to be her enemies, and one perhaps deeper and more powerful than other alliances that had occurred up to this point. Vampires did not just go out making friends with garou, and angels, and god knows what else? this was precedent-setting.
