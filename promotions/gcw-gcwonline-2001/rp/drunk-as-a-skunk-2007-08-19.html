Drunk as a Skunk

19 August 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8-19-07 Smoke. The room is thick with it, like a dense fog has settled just below the ceiling. There are no flames anywhere, and the sounds of sirens blaring in the distance cannot be heard. No, this smoke has settled here after being exhaled by one of the twenty or so occupants of the local dive bar that we so happen to be in. A few people over by the pool table share a couple of drinks and smoke a few cancer sticks while enjoying a game or two, while a few others lounge around by the outdated pinball machines that hardly get any play anymore. Of all the people in this establishment, only one of them sits alone at the darkest corner of the bar. With his head hunched over, a transparent brown liquid that is probably whiskey in a shot glass in his right hand, the man waves the bartender over. ?Hey,? he slurs. With a glass in one hand, and a bunched up corner of his apron in the other wiping away at the glass, the bartender strolls over. ?Yeah, what ya need?? ?Another damn drink,? came the reply, with not much in the way of body language. ?You still haven't finished-? With a quick motion reminiscent of an Old West Gunslinger in a showdown, the man swallows his drink in one gulp and slams the empty glass back down on the table. ?-that one.? With a shake of his head, the bartender heads over to grab a bottle of Jack. ?You know, ain't no one ever drank away his problems.? ?Yeah, well when I want to get advice from a guy stuck in a small town, spending his years living his life long dream of serving guys booze in dirty glasses,? he lifts his head slightly. ?I'll take that as a sign to just shoot myself.? ?Hey, don't I know you?? ?Does it really matter,? the mysterious man replies with his own question. ?Just do your damn job and serve me my damn drink.? ?You know,? the bartender begins, ?that sign over there says we reserve the right to refuse service to anyone.? ?Yeah, well the sign out front says Happy Times,? snatching the drink from the bartender and slamming down a few bucks on the table, the man replies. ?Guess they're both full of shit.? The bartender lets out a hearty guffaw, ?Yeah, I guess you're right.? He glances around the bar, shaking his head. ?Still, it's always good to put your faith in someone and let it all out. It's bad to keep things bottled up inside, you never know when they'll all come tumbling out.? ?Bravo, Dr. Phil,? the man says with a golf clap added in for emphasis of the sarcasm. ?Maybe I should go lie over there on the pool table and you can help me sort out my fucked up life.? ?I do know you,? the bartender says, a knowing smile on his face. ?You're Jayce Bradley.? ?Congratulations,? Jayce says as he pats his pockets. ?Sorry, but I'm out of cookies.? ?You're a lot nicer on the tee-vee.? ?When I'm on the tee-vee,? Bradley replies with a sneer parting his lips. ?Still hurt from that beating Shaman gave you,? the bartender jabs at the fallen hero with a dull spoon. ?Yeah, guess you could say that,? Jayce answers, before downing his glass of liqour. ?I still remember when-? ?CAN YOU PLEASE LEAVE ME THE HELL ALONE!?? The bartender steps back, a bit surprised at Jayce's sudden tone. ?There a problem here, Sam?? The bartender shakes his head, ?Nah, Ray, just a guy that wants to be left alone.? ?Yeah, Ray,? Jayce butts in, turning in his stool to face the new member of the conversation. ?So how about you butt the hell out.? The man standing there beside Jayce is not a physical specimen by any means. Standing at around six foot four, the guy looked bulky in a loose fitting tee shirt stained with grease and a dirty pair of faded blue jeans ? naturally faded, not that gay designer crap. ?Just making sure everything is cool,? Ray replies. ?We don't like trouble around here.? ?Yeah, then maybe you should put up a sign,? Jayce says with a grin. ?Your buddy over there knows how well those go over, right Sam?? ?You know what,? Ray says, placing his hand on Jayce's shoulder. ?I think you've had enough to drink. How about we get you a room and you sleep it off, pal.? Jayce slaps the man's hand away. ?Easy there,? Ray says as he lifts his hands up, palms facing Jayce. ?Then back the hell off,? Jayce stands up from his chair, swaying back and forth like a blade of grass in the breeze. ?I'm just trying to help you out,? Ray backs off a bit. ?Everyone wants to help me,? the rest of the bar has already turned their attention to the commotion. ?I don't need any damn help. What I need is for people to stop telling me what to do, who to be, and how to act.? Jayce looks around, and notices that no one knows what the hell he is talking about. ?I'm sorry,? he apologizes. ?I must look like an ass right now.? He turns to face the bartender. ?Look, let me make it up to you.? Sam just shakes his head. ?Don't worry about it. We'll just chalk it up to the booze talking.? ?Nah, it's the least I could do.,? Jayce insists. ?GCW's having a show over in Chicago. Why don't you bring your bouncer over here to the show on me, huh.? ?That would be nice,? Sam gets a smile on his face. ?Whaddaya say, Ray?? ?I ain't ever been into wrestling much, Sam.? ?Well, I can use the company on the drive over.? Ray shrugs his shoulders, ?I guess.? ?Perfect,? Jayce says with a huge grin on his face. ?Just say my name at will call and I'll have two tickets waiting for you guys.?
