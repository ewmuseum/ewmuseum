Skeletons in the Closet

1 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/01/2009 Cade Carson slammed the photograph down onto the desk with such force that it sent a violent vibration over the desktop, knocking over the picture frame sitting on it, and sending loose scraps of paper flying. Father Michea stared at the photograph, then lifted his eyes to glance up at Cade in that annoyingly reserved manner of his. "Something troubling you, son?" the priest remarked dryly. "You owe me an explanation," Cade responded through gritted teeth. His breathing was heavy, his shoulders bobbing up and down with each sharp inhale and exhale. "For?" Father Michea said, feigning ignorance. Cade wanted nothing more at that moment than to reach across the table and throttle the older man. But he needed answers, and killing him wouldn't bring him any closer to getting them. Instead, he took slow, deep breaths to slow his heart rate, then pounded an index finger onto the photograph of his parents and Father Michea, which he'd found a week earlier among the priest's possessions in the church basement. "For this." Father Michea studied the photograph, wrinkling his brow as if in concentration, trying to recall something from years ago. The old man was good. He genuinely looked puzzled by the picture, as if he couldn't remember it ever being taken. "Hmm," Father Michea mumbled. He tapped a finger against his lips, still concentrating on the photograph. "This must have been taken, oh I don't know, ten years ago? Maybe fifteen? Strange. I don't remember the couple, though." Cade snorted. "Oh, you don't?" he said sarcastically. Father Michea lifted his eyes from the picture to look up at Cade. "No, I don't." He frowned. "Should I?" The youngster's nostrils flared, and his lips became a thin white line. His fists clenched and unclenched at his sides, and the veins on his neck and the side of his temples looked as though they were about to burst free of his skin. "Those are my parents," Cade said in a low rumble. The priest looked at Cade for a moment as though he hadn't heard him correctly, then glanced down at the photograph. He even bent his neck as if to inspect the picture closely. "Ah! So it is," the priest replied, nodding. "Yes, yes, I remember them now." "I figured you would," Cade said scornfully. "You have to understand, it's been quite a few years," Father Michea explained. "And to be honest, we'd only met once or twice. Quite frankly, I wouldn't have even put the connection together if you hadn't mentioned who they were." Cade shook his head, tiring of the priest's games. "You knew my parents. You knew them, and yet for all these years, you lead me to believe you didn't." He looked imploringly at the priest. "Why?" Father Michea opened his mouth as if to answer, but then decided to keep whatever initial response he had to himself. Instead, he let out a sigh as he sat back in his chair. He studied Cade for a few moments, his eyes roaming over him in a way that made Cade feel uneasy. Finally, he leaned forward and planted his elbows on his desk and said, "It's complicated. But I assure you that my reasons for doing what I did had everything to do with you, and doing what I felt was best for you." Cade frowned. "Oh?" "Sometimes it's easier to not know the entire truth," Father Michea explained, "if that knowledge will bring you nothing but pain and unhappiness." Cade let loose with a sharp, short laugh. "What the hell does that even mean?" "Cade ..." Cade held up his hand. "No more stalling, Father Michea. No more half-truths or stories. I want to know the truth. How well did you know my parents?" The old priest paused for a moment, then simply nodded his head. He gestured to the chair across the desk from him. "Have a seat, then. This might take a while." Cade did as instructed, sitting on the edge of the chair, leaning forward with his elbows resting on his knees. "I'm all ears." ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ "Her name was Cheryl Watson, and she was the most beautiful woman I had ever laid eyes on. More than that, she was a truly wonderful person. Warm, kind, funny, intelligent. From the moment you met her, you just knew she was someone special. "I met her during my freshman year in college, back before I'd decided to dedicate my life to the church, back when I had just as many doubts about the direction I was going in as you likely do. She was also a freshman, and during orientation week, we were assigned one another as 'freshman buddies', a sort of system the university had devised in order for all new students to feel as though they had at least one individual they could turn to, to talk to. "We hit it off almost immediately. I was the more outgoing one, the one who didn't mind drawing attention to himself in a crowd, while she tended to be quiet, reserved, never seeming to want or need to be the center of attention. I think that was the biggest reason we got along so well. She balanced me out, and I her. Apart from when we had class, we were inseparable. "Our friendship blossomed over the course of our first semester in college, and by the end of our first year, our relationship had become more than that of just friends, more intimate. I was in love with her, and she seemed to reciprocate the emotion. And over the course of our four years at college, our feelings for one another deepened. "When we graduated, she quickly found work in her field of expertise, as a registered nurse at a local hospital, but I was at a cross-roads in my life. While she'd known since the first day of college what she'd wanted to dedicate her life to, I had been undecided. And four years of college later -- despite my earning a degree in Business Administration -- I still wasn't sure what I wanted to do with my life. But she stuck by me, she was my rock, while I attempted to find my path. "It was about eight months after graduation that I'd first felt I finally found my calling. It was Christmas time and Cheryl and I attended the midnight mass at the local parish. During the sermon, I happened to look around me, and saw the rapture on the faces of the men and women that sat near me. And it was directed at the priest standing at the front of the church, delivering words with such passion, with such conviction, that it was impossible to not hang on his every word. "It was at that moment that I knew what I wanted to do with my life. To see the profound effect the priest had on those he was speaking to, it made me realize that I wanted to have that kind of effect on people. More than that, I wanted to provide these people with that one beacon of light, one beacon of hope, to those who needed guidance in their life. "When I told Cheryl of my decision, I was afraid of what her reaction would be. I dreaded her response, thinking that she would laugh in my face, think me foolish. Or worse, decide that she wanted nothing more to do with a me. "Instead, her response reaffirmed everything I already thought about her. She simply told me that if that was what I wanted to do with my life, that she would stand behind me, always. Even to this day, I'm not ashamed to admit that her words brought a tear to my eye. "So with that settled, and with her full support, I set up a meeting with the local priest to discuss my desire to enter the priesthood, and what all that would entail. "The meeting was an eye opener, to say the least. Perhaps it was naivete on our part, but during the meeting one item stuck in my mind above all others. It was the fact that, if I were to dedicate myself to this calling, that I would, for all intents and purposes, be walking this path alone. "You see, while rules governing this sort of thing have relaxed slightly in some parts nowadays, priests being married was simply not allowed back then. If I was serious about becoming a priest, I would have to end my relationship with Cheryl. The realization hit me like a punch to the stomach. "I didn't return home to the apartment that Cheryl and I shared right away. Instead I decided that I needed to clear my head, and took a long walk around the neighborhood. My emotions were in turmoil because I had to choose between the love of my life and what I truly felt was my ultimate calling. It was like winning the lottery, but learning that you only had six months to live to enjoy it. "When I finally returned home, I found Cheryl in the bedroom, sitting on the edge of the bed. I walked toward her and decided that the best way to break the news, was to just say it outright, to not prolong the agony. But just as I was about to tell her what the priest had told me, that I had to choose between her and the priesthood, I noticed that she was trembling. Concerned, I put aside my news for the moment and put a comforting arm around her as I asked her what was wrong. "As it turned out, it wouldn't be just her that I'd be leaving if I decided to pursue my calling. She was pregnant, and I was going to be a father." ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ "Cade?" He heard the voice, but was barely cognizant of the fact it was speaking his name. His eyes were transfixed on a painting on the wall. His fingers were balled up into fists. He was standing. He couldn't recall even getting up from his chair. "Cade?" that same voice repeated, more urgently this time. "Talk to me, son." He shook free of his momentary daze. Whirling on the voice -- which belonged to Father Michea -- Cade pointed an accusatory finger at him. "Don't ever call me son!" Father Michea held up his hands in a placating gesture. "Cade, calm down. I know this is a lot to take in, but ..." "You don't know anything!" Cade snarled angrily at him. "You ... You think I believe you?" He shook his head vigorously. "You've lied to me the entire time I've known you. So why should I believe you now?" The priest made his way around the desk, to join Cade by the far wall where a painting of the Virgin Mary hung. "Cade," he began as he placed comforting hand on the distraught youngster's shoulder. Cade promptly shrugged it off and dared him to touch him again with a glare that promised death. "Cade, listen to me. I know I've been less than forthcoming with you about some things ..." "Some things?" Cade interrupted, incredulous. "Okay, a lot of things," Father Michea acknowledged. "But the point is, I'm being honest now. More than that, I need you to give me a chance to explain why I did it." Cade chuckled sarcastically. "Why you did it? You mean, why in the past ten years you've known me, you didn't bother to tell me that I'm your son? I mean, if I'm to believe your story, that's where you were going with it, right?" Father Michea didn't answer him right away. Instead, he studied him in that same way he always seemed to be doing, the kind of scrutiny that always made Cade feel as though he were naked and vulnerable. This time Cade refused to look away, and stared back at him. To Cade's delight, Father Michea looked away first. Incredibly, for the first time since Cade had known the man, the old priest seemed to be at a loss for words. A minor victory, and a rather immature one at that, but Cade would take it. When the priest did return his gaze to Cade's, he looked much more vulnerable than Cade ever remembered him being. His shoulders weren't held quite as squarely, his jaw not quite as set. He fidgeted with his hands, as if he didn't quite know what to do with them. "Yes, Cade, I'm your father," Father Michea finally said. "Your real father. I know I should have told you a long time ago, but I made a promise to your mother a long time ago that I wouldn't. Even after she ... after she and her husband disappeared, I felt I owed it to her to keep my promise." "Why didn't she want me to know the truth?" Cade asked, more confused than angry. Father Michea spread his hands. "She had her reasons," he said. "She didn't go into them with me, though. She just asked me to respect her wishes." He shrugged. "But I can only assume it had something to do with the fact that because of my life I'd chosen, I could never be a real father to you. So it was best that her husband -- who she'd met a year after you were born -- assume that role." "And you were okay with this?" Cade asked. "You didn't mind basically giving up on your own flesh and blood?" Father Michea's tone grew harsh at that. "I never gave up on you, Cade. No, I couldn't be around you as much as I would have liked, but you were never far from my thoughts." His tone softened as he continued. "And I cared deeply for your mother. As much as it pained me to do so, I agreed to her wishes because I ... we thought it was what was best for you." Cade stared down at the carpet, his mind reeling. It was a lot for him to absorb all at once. A part of him wanted to believe Father Michea's story. But another part of him also knew that if Father Michea could be untruthful to him about other things, then it wasn't out of the realm of possibility that this was all part of an elaborate lie as well. The problem with the latter is, why? What would Father Michea gain from claiming to be Cade's biological father? Until he had an answer to that question, Cade decided it was best he not make up his mind about whether or not he believed Father Michea's tale. He got up from his chair and made his way to the priest's office door. "Cade," Father Michea called after him. "Cade, say something." Cade paused at the doorway, but didn't turn around. "I just need to be alone right now." With that, he left.
