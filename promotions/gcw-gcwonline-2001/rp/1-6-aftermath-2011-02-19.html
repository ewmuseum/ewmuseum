1.6 - Aftermath

19 February 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02/19/11 “The job, this life, the danger. Is it worth it you know?” - Miguel Erazo 1 “The city of San Diego is reeling in fear today after a drive by shooting during a police funeral procession. Members of the San Diego Police Department as well as the FBI were holding a funeral for slain FBI Agent Steven Rios. The Agent's death was the hot-topic of local news and media lately, as he was apparently gunned down while undercover by gang violence. It appears as though the violence has not ended as during the ceremony an unidentified number of motorcycles rode by the ceremony and open fired. Police have yet to make a formal announcement, but members of the public said that there were anywhere from three to five assailants, all of whom were using automatic weapons. We hope to have more information as the case develops, but as of right now, police are very tight-lipped about details, but they have said that the will be making a formal announcement soon. For Channel 4 News this is Elizabeth Smith signing off.” Miguel shook his head as he hit the mute button on his television set. The local news channels had been airing this kind of stuff all day, they just kept replaying it over and over. Miguel had been doing his best to avoid watching the news, but he couldn't avoid it forever. It bothered him that he was stuck at home, but FBI Unit Chief Erwin had sent him home and ordered him to stay there. This was an act of violence against the FBI and they would be heading up the investigation, not local Police. So, he sat at home, and he could no longer avoid the television. Angelina had already called him from out of town, where she was visiting some family members, but she hadn't returned home yet. Even though she offered to cut her trip short, Miguel saw no reason at the time to bring her home, he expected to be working this case. Now that he was sitting around on the sidelines, he wished that she was home, wished that he had someone to talk to. He had done his best not to think about the situation, but no matter what it just kept replaying in his mind. It was the cop inside of him, it was always taking over in times like this, he couldn't help but analyze. Miguel kept seeing the look on everyone's faces. The looks of fear in their eyes. The absolute shock and terror in their shrill voices as they screamed for their lives. It was absolute chaos, humans reduced to their lowest forms, absolutely crippled by their fears. It was terrorism, plain and simple. There were no suicide bombs, no chemical attacks, and the death toll was actually surprisingly low for a wild slew of gunfire into a crowd, however that wasn't their plan. The people who were behind that were trying to instill fear. They were trying to scare the police and the FBI, a warning to leave them alone. They were trying to scare the people of San Diego, a warning of the influence they had over the city. Miguel turned his attention back to the television, where they were showing yet another replay of the events from earlier that day. Even with the sound turned off, he could no longer bear to watch. He looked around the room, scanning the area for something to take his mind off of the scene. As horrible as the attack was, Miguel found himself wandering back to something else. Something that took place long before the gunfire. He couldn't get the image of Rios' family out of his mind. Their tears and their pain would always serve as a reminder of what this job could cause a family. Miguel shook the thought from his mind. He couldn't think about that now. Instead, he stood to his feet and headed towards the door. He grabbed his badge and his sidearm from the counter before he grabbed his car keys and headed out the door. 2 “What the fuck is wrong with you? Are you guys fucking insane?!” Captain Phillip Toms screamed. El Guerra did not offer him a reply, instead he offered only a look of disinterest. He turned back towards the television set, the news reporter making a report about the terrorist attack earlier in the day. The corrupt cop had been warned and despite War's best efforts, he had survived the attack. “Are you listening to me?” Toms continued, apparently not recognizing El Guerra's look as a warning to quit these accusations. “You attacked the funeral of an FBI Agent. They're going to be coming after you. If you thought you had it bad, you just made the target ten times larger.” El Guerra craned his neck away from the television set and looked towards the angered Police Captain. He narrowed his eyes as he stared into Toms' face, examining him for a brief second while he contemplated his reply. “Our actions are none of your concern, Captain Toms, of that I assure you,” El Guerra said calmly, while turning back to the television. “Your job is to provide us with information, not tell us how to run our business.” “My job is to keep you stupid fuckers out of jail,” Toms erupted back at the biker. “And with you guys running around playing 'Boyz in tha Hood' it's kind of hard to do my fucking job!” “Lower your voice,” El Guerra said calmly and quietly. His voice nearly a whisper. “What?! Are you fucking listening to me?!” Toms continued to yell. El Guerra quickly reached to the table beside the couch that he was sitting on. In an instant he already had his Desert Eagle trained on the Police Captain and had the safety off. “I said, 'lower your fucking voice,'” War replied. “I told you that our actions are none of your concern and I won't repeat myself again. If you don't like our arrangement, than perhaps we should end it.” He let those last few words linger, letting them hang in the air. The corrupt police captain finally backed down, his chest deflating and his ego shrinking in the face of danger. Toms looked to the floor and was silent for a few moments, apparently he decided to choose his next words wisely. “I want to talk to El Diablo,” Toms said. “I need to speak to him about our arrangement.” “You're not going to speak to El Diablo,” El Guerra deadpanned. Mostly brushing off the cop's demands. Toms stared at the Sergeant at Arms of Los Aztecas. He didn't have a response ready, he had nothing to come back with. He seemed to be searching for words, but the biker already had a reply for him. “I suggest you go back home Captain Toms, work will come early tomorrow morning and it's getting late. Alex will show you out.” 3 “Tommy? It's Miguel,” he said into the phone. The unmarked police sedan rolled through the community's side streets. Miguel followed the low speed limits, carefully piloting the car as he spoke on his cell phone. He waited as Tommy replied on the other end of the line. “Yeah man, I'm doing okay. How's everything at home?” Miguel continued to wind the car through the side streets. He hadn't really had a destination in mind when he left the house, but he just needed to get out. He still couldn't escape the images of earlier that day, so he hoped that maybe Tommy would want to talk about it. If he couldn't escape the images anyways, it was better to deal with him the best way he knew how. “Yeah, Angelina is out of town, visiting family. I'm just out driving around, Erwin ordered me to stay home for the day, but I can't stop thinking about a couple of things.” Miguel waited a few seconds, and laughed when he heard Tommy reply. “Yeah, well you know me too well. I'll be by in a few minutes, I'm already on my way over.” With that Miguel clicked his cell phone shut and tossed it into the cup holder. He quickly flashed his signal and turned the corner, piloting his car towards Tommy's place. 4 The office was dark, the only source of light was a small television set in the corner of the room. Behind the desk FBI Unit Chief Erwin sat in a drunken stupor. There was a nearly empty bottle of cheap whiskey in front of him. There was very little press allowed at the memorial for Agent Rios and even those that were allowed had gotten very little footage filmed. What they had filmed, Erwin was reviewing now. In front of him, other than the nearly empty bottle of whiskey were a bunch of broken leads and dead-ends. He had uncovered a number or partial license plate numbers, but as he tried the combinations of possible plates, it became apparent that the drivers were had applied fake plates. The bikes themselves were very non-descript. Plain black sport motorcycles, no identifying markings. All of the riders, in black leather riding outfits and black helmets. Whoever did this, they were professionals. Not whoever, Erwin thought to himself, Los Aztecas. This was a warning, trying to scare him away. But it wouldn't work, this had just become personal, he would bring them down. “Unfortunately it won't be for this one,” he mumbled to himself. He reached for the bottle and took a long swig, the liquor burning the back of his throat the whole way down. He grabbed the converter and began to rewind the raw video footage, scanning for something, for anything that he might have missed. There had to be something that he missed, these guys couldn't be this lucky. He pressed the Play button on the remote and scoured the video. The opening fifteen seconds were the same, not offering him anything new. His eyelids were heavy and his vision blurry. Exhaustion and cheap whiskey were taking over his body. He closed his eyes for a few moments, trying to regain his train of thought. Next thing he knew, he felt his body sagging and fatigue setting in. He was ripped from sleep's grasp at the sound of his cell phone ringing. His eyes shot open and it took him a moment to refocus. He stared at the vibrating Blackberry Bold in front of him and reached his hand out. The caller ID revealed that the name and number of the caller was private, but that wasn't anything unusual for the FBI. Erwin pressed the green Accept Call button and brought the cell to his ear. “Unit Chief Erwin speaking,” he said into the mouthpiece. The look on his face changed from drunken confusion to anger and intrigue as he listened. “Who the hell is this? How did you get this number?” He heard nothing and took the phone away from his ear, he stared at the screen but the call was ended already. He quickly clicked on his Address Book and searched for the number of the local FBI office. He called the FBI Field Office and was connected almost immediately. “This is FBI Unit Chief Erwin, I need to get a trace on the last call to come to my cellular phone. The call couldn't have been more than fifteen seconds, but I need you to get as close as possible.” 5 The two officers entered the bar and it was relatively empty for a night. It wasn't surprising given the circumstances, every police officer in the San Diego area was at the funeral, and nothing made cops stay home with their families more than seeing one of their own slain in the line of duty. It was true that tomorrow morning the wrath of SDPD would be on the lookout for those responsible, but tonight they were more focused on their children and their wives. But for Erazo and Chase they had things to discuss. They quickly grabbed seats at a booth near the back of the bar. They signalled to the waitress, who smiled at the two of them. She was the regular waitress at the bar, Sandy. A still attractive 40 year old single mother, with a face that was slightly weathered from her age, but a body that was still as attractive as ever. “The regular, boys?” she asked. The two nodded as they slumped themselves into the booth. They were silent at first, both preferring to wait for their drinks before they got down to business. Sandy was quick and only a minute later she was already delivering drinks to the partners. A double shot of Jack Daniels and a pint of draught. She smiled as she placed the glasses on the table. “Thanks Sandy,” Tommy said and reached into his pocket. Sandy waved her hand, signalling that there was no need for payment. “The bars slow tonight boys, no need for that. Been seeing that shit on the TV here all night though, you guys okay?” she asked. “We're fine Sand,” Miguel said quietly. “Can't get away from that shit anywhere though, you think we can change that to the Lakers game?” “Sure thing hon,” Sandy replied, before turning away. Tommy and Miguel both reached for the shot glass at the same time. They clinked their glasses together and quickly tilted the shot towards their mouth and drained the Whiskey in an instant. Miguel sat silently for a few moments after that, his eyes staring at the table. “Alright, you've got me here Miguel, I know that something is bothering you,” Tommy began. “What is it?” Miguel looked up and there was a pained expression in his eyes. He was still silent, still searching for the words, still trying to find the right way to get this off his chest. “You remember our conversation from a few days ago?” Miguel asked. Tommy stared straightforward, his eyes not breaking from his partner. There was a quizzical look in his eyes. “What conversation? Miguel we ride together every day, and we have been for the past few years. We have conversations every fucking day, you're going to need to be a bit more specific than that,” Tommy responded. Miguel said nothing, again for a few seconds he was silent. He looked around the room again searching for the right words. He took a sip of his beer, hoping that the bitter ale might offer the right thing to say. “About the Captain...” Miguel said, his gaze never meeting his partner's. “Oh Jesus fucking Christ, Erazo,” Tommy said, shaking his head as he spoke. “Are you still on about this shit? You know that it's just Erwin grasping for straws. The Captain is solid, he's always been there for us, especially for you, you should know better than to accuse him of something like this.” “I know Tommy, I know,” Miguel began. “But I think I might be able to prove it this time.” Tommy was taking a sip of his beer but instantly froze. He nearly spit the beer all over himself. He put the beer down and stared straight at Miguel. “What the fuck do you mean? This shit isn't funny Erazo, I thought about mentioning something the first time you brought this shit up, but this is serious shit right now,” Tommy said. Miguel could only nod. He took another sip of his beer and stared straight at his partner. Tommy kept going. “Well fuck man, don't leave me sitting here in suspense. What have you got?” Tommy said. “It was in my head at the funeral,” Miguel began. “I was watching Toms, he didn't look confused when the sound of the motorcycles showed up. He was expecting it.” Tommy shook his head, trying his best not to believe his partner's words. He took another long sip of his beer, chugging several mouthfuls down before looking back at Miguel. “How do you know he was expecting it? A look doesn't really mean anything Erazo,” he said. “I know it doesn't, but right after the shots were fired I searched for him. He was standing right across from me at the ceremony, I looked for him for instructions, I wanted to know what to do. But he wasn't there, he had already disappeared,” Erazo answered. “So?” Tommy asked, apparently not convinced. “So? What the fuck do you mean 'so',” Miguel blurted. “Tommy, he was fucking gone. He fucking disappeared. Either the guy is Harry fucking Houdini, or he knew to get out of the way. If he didn't know what was coming, why the fuck would he disappear. You can tell yourself that he was in the bathroom, or he got out of the way quick enough, or maybe he was somewhere else trying to organize the other guys, but you're lying to yourself. It can't be a coincidence.” “Alright, I'll admit that it sounds valid,” Tommy said. “It's weird that he'd just disappear. And now that you mention it, I didn't see him after the shooting either. But, this proves nothing. And Miguel, you cant just throw out accusations like this, it's a fucking career killer whether you're right or wrong.” “I know, I don't know what to do.” “Well you can't go public with this,” Tommy answered. “It has to stay quiet. You need to know for sure, but I'm warning you man. This kind of thing can kill you, and you can't let Toms know that you're thinking something like this.” Miguel said nothing. He took another long drink of beer. Staring at the wooden table a pile of thoughts ran through his mind. “You ever think if it's all worth it?” Miguel asked solemnly. “What the fuck are you talking about now?” Tommy asked with a chuckle. “The job, this life, the danger. Is it worth it you know?” Miguel said. “Erazo, I don't know what's going on in that brain of yours, but you really need to sort this shit out. I've got to get home, but please, for both of our sakes. Keep this shit to yourself and figure this out. But whatever you do, please keep me out of it.” With that Tommy stood and turned away from his friend. He made his way towards the front of the bar and left quickly, leaving Miguel in the booth alone. Left with only his thoughts and the last few sips of beer.
