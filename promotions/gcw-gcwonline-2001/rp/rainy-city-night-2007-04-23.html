Rainy City Night..

23 April 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/23/07 Defeat, it leaves a bitter taste in anyone’s mouth. It’s part of human nature to be proud and want to win no matter what it is you try to do. Losing isn’t enjoyable for anyone at any stage or time of life. The night after a loss it just plays it’s self back in your mind, over and over. If that isn’t enough you can watch the tape of it and see where it all went wrong. Most people tend to dwell on a loss or a defeat, but that’s where most people go wrong. After something like that happens, all you can do is suck up your pride and carry on. It happened and coming back stronger is what they all should look a head to. Minus the seventeen stitches that are in his forehead and the bandage that cover it, he feels fine. Nothing out of the ordinary happened in the ring the other night. It was all business as usual, all of it besides the loss. Knowing what he did wrong he has to look ahead. Wearing a pair of baggy blue jeans, a white t-shirt and a pair of orange Adidas shoes. Walking down main street with his hands in his pocket as the rain comes down on him. The street lights shines off the puddles that have formed on the city streets and sidewalks but it doesn’t bother him. Enjoying the city atmosphere he takes a deep breath and smiles a little as the crisp cold air hits his lungs. Everything was right tonight, or so it seemed. The streets were empty and so were the sidewalks. Everyone was inside trying to avoid the rain and bad weather. Walking alone on the city streets he passes a few places he use to hang out at with his sister. The hotel he use to stay at when he didn’t want be at home, the bennigan's that he ate at all the time. Just another block or so was his old kickboxing academy. Casually walking though the interception he makes it to the other side just as a bus passes, splashing water from a puddle onto his back. Glancing over his shoulder at the bus he shakes his head and keeps walking. With his wife and daughter safely at home he can roam for the evening if he wanted to, but he doesn’t. Needing to clear his head is all this walk in the city was for, it’s where everything he’s known from childhood was or still is. Walking past the twelve story Democrat and Chronicle building he stops in his tracks. Taking his hands out of his pockets he pushes his hair back behind his ears. Now looking up at the top of the building he sees the gargoyle that’s been there since he was a child. Next to it the American flag blows in the wind. Knowing that it’s growing later he starts to walk quickly, crossing main street he walks down the other side of the street. His old kickboxing academy is just two buildings away. Slowing down now he starts to take it all in again. The front of the building hasn’t changed at all, minus the worn down sign and a few bricks missing. Reaching the front door he stands there in the light hanging from the building. Not really sure if he wants to open the door and go in or if he just wants to turn his back like he did before and continue on with his night. Cocking his head to the side he glances at the stormy sky. This use to be his place, where he’d be every day learning new moves and honing in on his skills. Use to is key. He hasn’t been back to this building in almost seven and a half years.. Since the day his sister Diana died. He knows she’s up there watching him right now and that weighs on his mind a little. Traffic passes by him as he stands there thinking. Finally reaching out he pushes the door open and walks inside. The lights are on and a few fighters are still there, working the bags and lifting weights. Taking a deep breath it all returns to him, the musty smell of the brick building that’s damp as well as the sweat on the mats from the fighters. Walking around inside he starts to look at the names on the wall. Focusing more with his eyes as the old lights flicker a bit, looking around he finally sees his name on the wall. The green paint they used to put it on the wall has started to chip and peal away, like everything else on the building time has taken it’s toll. Closing his eyes he starts taking it in more and more. Remembering all the time spent here with his sister, from just training to fighting in matches and tournaments. This was a place that he loved and his sister was always there supporting him when no one else was. It’s something he knows he shouldn’t have given up but he did and that’s all in the past. He’s found something else to replace that and it’s close to his heart. His eyes shoot open as he feels a tap on his shoulder, quickly grabbing the mans arm he turns around and looks at him. Quickly realizing that it’s one of the fighters that was lifting weights he lets go of his arm. Staring at him now he takes a step back. This man is young, with a shaved head wearing nothing but his blue kickboxing trunks. Looking at Kyle now, the man smiles and points to the wall. Man: “I am guessing that’s your name up on the wall?” Hesitant to answer the man he looks back at the wall quickly and then back at the man. Kyle: “Yeah that’s my name up there, why do you ask?” Man: “I’m just wondering why you stopped” Obviously you were a good fighter.” Running his hand across his face now, as if he’s annoyed and angry with the question. Trying to find an answer to it him self, more so then just the death of his sister.. But he can’t. Kyle: “I’m not getting into that anymore.. All I can say is keep your head in the game and push every one of your fights to the limit. Never hold back”. I gotta get out of here.” Turning his back on the fighter just like he turned his back on the sport years ago he heads for the door. Not even bothering to look back he pushes the door open and once again heads out into the stormy night. Breathing deep trying to get the smell of the old building out of his lungs. Starting to walk again he looks around. Cutting down a back ally he takes a shortcut to get onto the bridge that goes over the Genesee river. As he’s walking along the bridge he looks over to his left, a block away is the dinosaur bar-b-que. The best place in the world to get some ribs. Although he’s not hungry right now he can smell it in the air. Seeing the line of Harleys in the parking lot as well as on the street he starts to head that way. As he goes to cross the street he hears the roar of an engine. Looking over his left shoulder he sees a loan headlight speeding his way. Stepping back up on the curb he turns and looks at the bike. The engine starts to get lower and lower and then cuts off. The headlight turns off as the bike comes to a rolling stop a few feet in front of him. Staring at the Harley in front of him he looks at the back of it. The streetlight bounces off the metallic midnight blue, it’s a soft tail. Looking more along the bike he now looks at the man who’s riding it. Recognizing the man right away he takes a step off the curb and toward the bike. Kyle: “Shaman.. what are you doing here?” Shocked to see his former mentor and once allies here in his home town he stairs at Shaman awaiting an answer. Shaman: “I have my reasons..” A bit confused with that answer he knows he lucky to have gotten one at all. As the rain continues to pour down on the two of them he looks over Shaman and once again the dinosaur. Now glancing at it him self Shaman looks back at Kyle. Shaman: “I’ll meet you over there, I have something to inform you of.” On that note Shaman fired up his bike once again and took off. Not sure as to how to take the situation he crosses the street and starts to walk once again, toward the smell of food. A block away he can see Shaman’s bike pulling up and parking inside the gate. Now walking past the Rochester downtown public library he turns the corner. The Rain starts to come down harder now, quickly reaching into his back pocket he pulls out his killer loop sunglasses and puts them on. Now that his vision isn’t affected anymore he looks down the one way street. Seeing nothing coming he jumps off the curb into a puddle that he didn’t see. Running across the street he stops as he jumps onto the curb. Standing there, for just a few seconds it seems like a minute as he looks at the dinosaur. It hasn’t changed at all, the deck still hanging over the river and the outside looking just the same. He can hear the blues music being played inside. Walking though the gate he looks at all of the bikes lined up outside. Looking around at everyone as the heat lamps are on and the food is all around he walks up to the door. Reaching out he pulls the door open to see Shaman again, coming out with two beers. Following him over to a table that’s outside they take a seat. Passing him a beer Shaman sits down and looks at him. No emotion on his face at all, Shaman just stairs at him, almost trying to get him to talk.. Kyle: “So what’s up?” Opening his beer and taking a drink Shaman looks at him once again. Shaman: “Lets cut to the chase, I’m here to find out how everything is.” Pulling the bottle of Labatt blue away from his mouth, Kyle looks like he’s going to spit it out with laughter. Swallowing the swig of beer he wipes his mouth. Kyle: “Everything is fine..” Shaman: “watching you in the ring at high stakes I saw something was wrong.” Kyle: “It was a bad match for me, we all have them. You just have to move on and look ahead.” Drinking his beer once again he looks into Shamans eyes, trying to get a read on him. Shaman: “Something in ring is missing or not clicking. I could tell with ease watching you. How is everything at home?” A waitress comes over and hands them a menu, looking up at her Kyle smiles but waves them away. Kyle: “Those won’t be needed.. I’ll have the full slab of ribs and a pulled pork sandwich, coleslaw, baked beans and fries.” Waitress: “And for you sir?” Shaman looks at her, still showing nothing on his face. Shaman: “The same.” She walks off as Shaman looks back at Kyle who still hasn’t answered his question from before.. Kyle: “Everything at home is good. Faith is enjoying all the time around the house and Skye is doing fine. She’s getting big.” Setting his beer back down on the wood table, Shaman puts his arms up on the table. Shaman: “I’m wondering if you’ll be ready for your match this week, Knight isn’t someone to take lightly.” Kyle: “He should have stayed retired if you ask me. I don’t care what he’s done in the past, none of that matters now. I will push him to his limit in the ring and pass by him like a strike of lightening.” Shaman: “That’s the kind of cockiness that could get you beat in the end. I like it though.” Kyle: “It’s how I feel, he doesn’t hold a candle to me in the ring. He’ll be rusty and off his game and that’s when I’ll take control and hit the KJ for the win.” Shaman: “All I will say is don’t take him lightly.” Kyle: “I won’t.” As they sit there and stair back and fourth with one another a roar of thunder cracks though the sky. Hearing the old door creak open, the waitress comes back with there food. Setting the platters down on the table she looks at them. Waitress: “Anything else for you guys?” Kyle: “Yeah, two more beers and then were all set.” As she walks away to get them more to drink they open up the napkins and start to eat. As the rain continues to fall and the lightening rips though the sky. As he eats Kyle thinks that this couldn’t really be what Shaman showed up here for, there has to be more to it. Something doesn’t seem right.. Digging into his pulled pork sandwich he lets it go for now, wanting to enjoy the food. Knight won’t be a cake walk this week but it will test him none the less.. needing a win and a big showing it must come this week.
