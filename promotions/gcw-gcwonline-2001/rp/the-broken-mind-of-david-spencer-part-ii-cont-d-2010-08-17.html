The Broken Mind of David Spencer, Part II Cont'd

17 August 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/17/10 The Broken Mind of David Spencer Part II: Sanitarium, Let Me In (Continued) CRASH! The sound of a glass smashing up against a wall and scattering across the floor echoes throughout the hotel room. The only light is coming from a dim lamp on a nightstand by the bed, leaving most of the room in darkness. CRASH! CRASH! Two complimentary coolers drain themselves onto the wall, their glass containers shattering, joining the previously thrown glass shards on the ground. Of course, the glass and bottles didn't throw themselves into the wall. The culprit is the man formerly known as Face. The masked man has been steadily declining since bursting out of the gates when he re-entered GCW a few months ago, dropping four matches in a row and getting into a scuffle with GCW newcomer, Dominic Wall. The result is a man more irate and unruly than he's ever been. Unfortunately, the final steps into the abyss David Spencer currently resides in are not exclusive to events in GCW. After going through six months of intensive psychological treatment at a mental institute in his now hometown of Albuquerque, New Mexico, Spencer left with a new sense of how his life should be carried out. He had to attempt to meld with society and its laws and leave his reckless abandonment and intent to injure behind, otherwise he might end up back in that mental hospital or, worse, prison. Spearheading this movement for Spencer is an experimental drug that tones down his emotions and temptation to act on his mind's whims. He had been taking it for the last couple of months in his stay at the mental hospital and the results were promising. His violent outbursts were becoming more and more of a rarity and he began opening up to his psychiatrists and doctors more. This is how Spencer was able to convince his doctors and psychiatrists to allow him to continue wrestling. With a couple of wins in the bank, things seemed to be working out for the man formerly known as Face. The pills were doing the trick: keeping him from irrationally bursting out at his opponents or dragging matches on for no reason other than to torture his fellow wrestlers. But then, the aforementioned losing streak hit him and - more importantly - his confidence in the pills he had been given. "Where's your damn healing magic now, Dr. Lyons!?" screams Spencer, staring up to the ceiling with his back flat on the bed. His voice is even more scratchy and harsh than usual - all signs pointing to him losing it in a screaming match with the walls of his room. "You said these pills would help me..." continues Spencer, breathing slightly heavier than normal, thanks to his near hyperventilation. "...not turn me into a slab of marble to help my opponents find their way up the ladder!" Of course, Dr. Lyons isn't here. Fortunately for David, neither are any other hotel patrons in any of the rooms around or above his. Spencer rolls to his forearms and knees on top of a bed that looks like an exorcism just took place in it. He opens a drawer in his nightstand beside the bed. Looking inside, he lets out a few nasty coughs, seeing all of the pills he has been taking up until a couple of weeks ago. The drawer is emptier than usual, some of his medicine being scattered on hotel floors where he previously dwelled these past few weeks. Spencer clears his throat, sparking yet another hacking fit. He's had a problem with coughing for months, but the problem has been getting progressively worse since going off his medication. Every few hours, David contemplates taking some cough medicine to help. Unfortunately, his irrational thinking has also been on the rise as of late. No... cough medicine is just the first slip down the slippery slope... thinks David, seeing some convoluted path from taking cough medicine to taking his experimental pills, effectively applying his contempt to all forms of medicine in a gross fallacy of judgement. I'll take this coughing over my mental imprisonment... is the conclusion he comes to. I showed weakness by letting Lyons take my freedom away and now I'll just have to face some consequences... it's only natural. Having made his mind up, Spencer slams the drawer shut, nearly falling off the bed in the process. He then rolls over to his back again, staring up at the ceiling once more. Spencer ponders how he got into this situation. Slowly, but surely, he drifts into a memory of how Lyons first brought him along this path... . . . The situation is a strange one for Doctor Jeffery Lyons - one he has not encountered before. On the one hand, he potentially has one of the most interesting subjects he's ever had sitting on a jail cell floor, not more than a five feet away from him. On the other hand, this subject has just diagnosed himself and essentially dismissed Dr. Lyons. How can someone just know he has Antisocial Personality Disorder? is the first question that springs to the psychiatrist's head. He makes a note of Face's self-diagnosis on his clipboard, mentally scratching his chin for a way to approach the new situation at hand. After a few moments of nothing happening, Face looks up from his seated position, noticing Lyons is still standing in his cell. "It is intriguing, isn't it, Dr Lyons?" says Face, turning his gaze to the wall to his right. Nodding his head, Dr. Lyons replies: "It is not something I've encountered before. Most people with the disorder you claim to have are not aware of said disorder." Lyons takes a few steps, pressing the eraser of his pencil up against his chin in thought. "Someone knowing he or she has the disorder would imply that the person is aware of their thoughts being devoid of proper morals," says Dr. Lyons. "That person would then see that any judgement they pass could be erroneous." "Not quite so, Dr. Lyons," says Face in his usual raspy tone. "Error arises in one's definition of the rules. Error is in the eye of the beholder." Face turns to look at the doctor as he continues. "One who is aware of having this disorder is, therefore, simply aware that the majority of the population would see some judgements they pass as erroneous." Dr. Lyons looks down to the prison floor, playing with some crumbs of dirt with his feet for a few moments. He looks up to Face, giving his thoughts. "I suppose you are technically correct, but the fact remains that the majority of society would see those decisions as questionable," exclaims the psychiatrist. "Beliefs held by such a large portion of the society are effectively unwritten laws, though, as far as society is concerned." The masked man wags a scarred finger at that notion. "There are always varying degrees and situations to which an opinion or belief apply, Dr. Lyons," points out Face. "For example: I am here because it is generally frowned upon for one to beat someone to the point where they are unable to get off the ground or threaten to break someone's bones or choke someone unconscious." Shrugging, Face continues. "However, in my profession, those things are not only allowed - they are, in fact, sought after and encouraged. I ask you, Dr. Lyons... Where does that line get drawn?" "It gets drawn when you and others in your profession sign contracts to compete in a sport that allows such things," responds Dr. Lyons, in a quick manner. "Is that so?" Face asks. "What if I belonged to a white supremacist cult and the man I was beating up was black? I would then be part of a sub-society that supports such behaviour." Lyons shakes his head. "You would still be in the minority of society where doing so would be illegal." Face nods. "Alright... What if I was a Nazi in Germany during World War II and the man I was beating up was Jewish? I would then be a part of a nation that supports such behaviour." Lyons begrudgingly nods. "Well, yes... At that point you would be operating within the opinions and laws of a large society. But, you do not live in such a society now." Face suddenly stands up, snapping his fingers together as he points at the doctor. "So, you are saying that beating up and even murdering a Jew would be a perfectly moral thing to do..." Face says as he tilts his head to the side inquisitively. "...as long as the society I'm in supports it and is large enough." "I did not say it was moral," points out the psychiatrist, making sure to stress the fact. "I merely agreed that, within that society, your-" "Views would be supported. Right," says Face, cutting the doctor off. "But, is a moral not just a view of what is perceived to be right and wrong?" conjectures Face, who begins to outstretch an arm and ball his opposite hand into a fist. "Would it not, then, be morally sound to beat..." Face repeatedly and viscously punches the air by his outstretched arm, near the hand. His speech has brief grunts intertwined in it as he continues to speak while punching. "...a Jewish man to a bloody pulp, shatter..." Face stops punching and ‘bends' an invisible arm into an arm wrench along the ground. He then places his foot in the middle, before pulling up on both sides and continuing to speak. "...his arm..." Face walks up to just in front of Dr. Lyons, looking back down to the area where he just broke the invisible man's arm. He then makes a gun shape with one of his hands and ‘aims' it at that location, looking straight at Dr. Lyons as he does. "...and then, in front of his Jewish wife and daughter, shoot five bullets through his skull..." Face motions shooting his ‘gun' five times, then slowly lowers it, only to shrug directly afterwards. "...so long as I did it in Germany during World War II?" Lyons takes a step back, not quite expecting such a vivid description of such a heinous act. He fights back the instinct to raise a worried eyebrow at Face, not allowing for the masked man to get any ‘rise' out of that description. Said masked man is simply staring back at the doctor, his head still tilted in a questioning manner. Dr. Lyons scribbles some things onto the paper on his clipboard before finally giving a response. "No," replies the doctor. "You are saying that the morals of the Nazis are correct when they are most certainly not, as can be seen by the world's reaction to them at the time and the state of the world now." "I'm not saying that today's morals reflect those of the Nazis," says Face. "I was merely pointing out that morals are not set in stone. For instance, the situation of me being superior to someone because she is a woman is a perfectly moral situation in Arab countries under the laws and practices of Islam. I'd have a pack of feminists on me in an instant if I suggested such a thing here." Face stops looking at Dr. Lyons, turning and walking slowly towards the wall he was sitting up against earlier. He clasps his hands together behind his back as he continues. "Anyone can debate about what is truly moral in the world, Dr. Lyons." Face stops a few feet short of the wall. The psychiatrist allows him to continue talking, uninterrupted, as he writes down notes. "It does, however, take a special kind of person to go beyond such restraints..." After writing for a few more moments, Dr. Lyons looks over to Face and poses some final questions. "So, you don't ever question the morals of any of your decisions, then? You don't regret what you did to that man? You don't regret any of the actions that led you to this jail cell?" Face completes his walk to the wall, turns around and sits down again. He looks directly at Dr. Lyons and answers his questions. "The only regret I have, Dr. Lyons, is that I live in a society where I'm forced to live by the moral boundaries of others. If I had it my way, the man I attacked wouldn't have been around to press charges." Dr. Lyons nods to himself about something as he turns around and walks out of the cell. He motions for the guard to close the door and walks away from the cell, giving one final remark as he leaves. "I'll be seeing you later, Face." Face mutters a reply under his breath. "I think you'll see me sooner..."
