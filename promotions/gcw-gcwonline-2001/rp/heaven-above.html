Heaven Above

undated

There are times when we all find ourselves going through the motions with no real reason. Sit here for a while, flip through the channels, and determine that there is absolutely nothing of interest in TV. Turn it off. And I stop. Unfortunately, when it happens in life, it's a different story. Finding meaning isn't something that's as easy as finding what's on TV, even if you're simply searching for the fact that there is nothing on TV...that there is no meaning. There's always that need to prove it to yourself. And, day after day, you eventually just grow so accustomed to the routines that end up wasting away your life that you don't even notice the pain until you try to think about it. But then it's too late. An angelic voice stirs me from my half slumber on the couch. "Daddy? Are you okay?" Sarah is a little over five years old. But she is more mature than most people I've grown to know. She has her mother's intellect. Her mother's wit. Her mother's organization. Her mother's hair. But she's got her father's charm. And her father's incessant need to stir up trouble. "Yeah, hunny...I'm fine. How about you?" Since I've moved back in with Kate, that she has raised a daughter unbeknownst to me, I've been amazed at every corner I've come to. This girl can pick up on the slightest bit of sarcasm, snideness, or any flat-out lie. She knows that I'm not fine. She comes over not to get a hug from me and to sit near me on the couch, but to give me a hug and give me some company. Just like her father, Sarah can't leave well enough alone. "I'm scared, Daddy. You have to go away tomorrow." Kate and I have had this talk. We felt that it wasn't necessary to tell Sarah what I do for a living. There are some minds that just aren't ready to be exposed to the reality of life. The fact that there are places on this earth where two or more people are pit to beat the living hell out of each other for the mere compensation of money and attention. And that people rally on either side of them for entertainment. Yet another one of those things in life that I sometimes just find myself going through the motions with...just showing up and beating the hell out of someone and not knowing the answer to the world's greatest question: why? Anyway, while we didn't feel it was necessary to expose Sarah to what I do, we told her what it was in less detail. We figured if we said that Daddy was a pro wreslter and then followed that by saying that it was like an ongoing contest, she wouldn't pursue it further. She did. "Well, Sarah...I have to go and do what I do for a living. So that I can make good money so that you and me and your mommy can have nice things and so that you can go to school and grow up to be important." "Like you and Mommy?" ...well, like Mommy. Kate's a lawyer. I'm a pro wrestler. Many see us both as the scum of the Earth, but you can guess which field I want my daughter to go into. "Right...like me and Mommy." "But what if you get hurt?" A laugh bubbles up from my chest. A tired laugh that has thought this thought before every match. But I've pushed it back, into the recesses of my mind, and tried my hardest to ignore it. After all, I'm only twenty-five. I'm invincible. I'm unbreakable. I can walk away from anything. "I'm not getting hurt." "But what if the bad people hurt you?" Wow. What if the bad people hurt me? Who are the bad people? Surely she means my opponents. Eva Van Lorne. Seymour Almasy. They're the only ones that I'm going up against, and they're the only ones that I have any beef with. Save Rich Rollins, of course, but I'm confident that he wants to win more than he wants to suckerpunch me. Rollins may be a bastard, but he's not an idiot. He'll take the win. Who are the bad guys that my daughter fears so much? Eva and Seymour? The roaring crowds would disagree with her. The multitude of fans who will be wearing their shirts, shouting their names, holding their signs...those fans would argue the point against my five year old daughter. Eva Van Lorne. The definition of what it is to be above and beyond anyone else on the face of the planet, and yet to be adored by them at the same time. A modern Evita, complete with a small empire which she commands. She is a queen of war, fighting others and waging everything against them in an unending campaign to better herself and her friends. Pointless. Look where that's gotten her. She's gotten under a few too many people's fingernails. Because of that, right now she's probably in a pit in the basement of one of our more demented wrestlers, being told to wear a dress made out of her own hair while Bryan Mayhem, Rich Rollins, Sam Wolack, or some other crazy that Caldera let in off the street whets a knife while looking down on her. Good move, you frigid bitch. Yet another example of what just going through the motions gets you. It makes you blind to the obvious and you don't see what recourses your actions will take. In this case, I'm not the only person in GCW who's sick of Eva. Then there's Seymour Almasy. The man whose father invented the bumper sticker that says, 'My son is an honor student.' He preaches like a fucking minister about what it means to be a wrestler. What it means to be a member of this sport and what we should do and what we shouldn't do. Seymour is the fans' favorite little pet that they can take out from time to time and enjoy and then he just goes back in his cage and awaits his next match. He's a plaything. A person so blind to what the real world is about that once he hits that brick wall that he's going toward, he'll never recover. Seymour has a streak and he has momentum. And the last time we met, he caught me. Never again. I'll put the fear of death back in that boy. But the question remains...are they bad people? "No, hunny...the bad people won't hurt me," a tired, forced, but reassuring smile warms her heart, "After all, your Dad is the strongest man in the world." She squints one eye and furrows her brow, "Are you really?" "Well," time to out-logic a small child, "have you ever met anyone stronger than your father?" She thinks for a moment. Lets out an audible 'hmm' as she mulls over the thought. Looking back at me, a mischievous smirk spreads across her marvelous face. "This girl. In my class. Her name is Jess," she's already laughing, betraying her joke, "And her dad can lift a car in each hand! And juggle them!" "Liar!" I point and scold with a cartoonish hyperbole, "Charlatan!" She laughs. She laughs a beautiful laugh that pierces any dark hatreds of my heart and breaks any clouds in my head. My daughter. The embodiment of the love between John and Kate, more incredible than the two of us put together. With a very wide smile, I pick her up with one arm and hold her upside down. The other arm comes in and the tickling commences, and Sarah's laughs are loud and joyful. The picture perfect perfection of childish innocence. Her laughs cut through the memories, the pain, the confused feelings. Five years of absence and guilt and worry is completely erased. Never let go of this moment. For God's sake, this is happiness. "Who's the strongest man in the world, little girl?" Between laughter, she manages to say, 'You are!' and I lay her back down on the couch and she laughs a bit more. After a moment, she sits up, still with a giant smile on her face, and gives me a hug. She whispers 'Goodnight, Daddy' and, without another word, she climbs the stairs from whence she came. Truly an angel from Heaven. Her voice is medicinal, and her ascent back to her cloud is unmistakable. Stunned, I find myself on the couch, wondering if I should go to bed as well. But there's something about the thought that my little girl is an angel, the upstairs being Heaven, that keeps me cemented in place. Sometimes there have to be sinners...we're a necessity in this world. My place is the pergutory of my position. Just as the couch is my bed, the stance I've chosen is somewhere in between. I don't see eye to eye with the blind men and women like Seymour and Eva. Idealists. Idiots. Children. And because of that, I'm the bad guy? If that is what I am, then that is what I will be, until the winds of change give me what I deserve. The recognition is mine. I will, forever, go through the motions. And let them bring me to whatever damnation they may. This is my choice. This is my rock, being pushed up my hill.
