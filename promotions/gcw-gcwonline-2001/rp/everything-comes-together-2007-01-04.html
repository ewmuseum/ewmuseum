Everything Comes Together

4 January 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/04/07 Trondheim, Norway A small shack several miles from the city proper There was something about home for Rune Hvalstr’m that would never cease to exist. It was permanent, undying, though not quite in the same predicament as himself. Even in this tiny hunting shack that he'd purchased as a young man, he felt like he was where he belonged, not halfway across the world with a former contractor, and undead in addition. He was certain that he had been missed by this point, given that both Virgil Ambrosi and his companion Lia Vlaszy had been keeping a fairly close watch on him, as though expecting him to do something so ultimately foolish that the three of them would be destroyed simultaneously. He understood their desire to be protective. After all, in this new (and unwelcome, in his opinion) life, they were, for all intents and purposes, his parents. It was precisely that which had led him here. Despite all of the problems, all of the trials and tribulations that had accompanied his turning, he had not forgotten that he, too, was a parent. In addition, he had not seen his only daughter in nearly a year. Astara would be growing up soon, and she might not even remember him. She could go for her entire life thinking whatever spineless waste Heidrun married next was her real father... but he would always remember, and from the look of things, he figured he would be remembering this way for a long time indeed. He struck a match, and flicked it into the fireplace. It collided with a spray of natural gas, and burst into flame. Rune had always wondered exactly how the original owner of the place had gotten a gas fireplace installed, but as many times as it had kept him warm during the long winters in Trondheim, he wasn't about to complain. He shoved one of the chairs back, and leaned forward onto the table, letting thought consume him. If this crazy plan of his was to work, he'd have to manage it all very carefully. Nobody could ever see him, especially not Astara. For all Heidrun knew, he'd gone off to some other country and left them to their business. If she laid eyes on him, or if Astara told her anything, as young children are apt to do, the police would be onto him very quickly, and would likely know where to find him, to boot. They knew where the Jaedr's hangar was, and they knew the place he was staying this very moment. He didn't even know where to start, but he knew that it wouldn't do him any good to stay here thinking about it... but sleeping was feeling a little more important at the moment. He knew that the only good chance he'd have to just see Astara would be during the day, and it was getting late. He sighed, and his eyes fell across the table, on a small notebook that he'd left there the last time he'd crashed in the little house, though he remembered that he'd forgotten to write whatever notes he'd been intending to write in it. He reached out and grabbed it, pulling a pen from his pocket. If misery was going to keep him awake, the least he could do was document it. I don't know what day it is. I don't know what month it is. I assume it's still January, because it doesn't feel like it's been that long since I remember watching the ball in New York drop on TV. I can't remember what kind of odd idea led me back to Trondheim, back to the origin point for everything miserable that's ever happened to me. It was here that I got my pilot's license and the Jaedr, it was here that I met Heidrun, it was here that I bought a house for her and Astara to live in. Everything that I can't bear to live around is here, but somehow I can't keep myself from coming back. I don't think anyone knows about me leaving, and that's just as well. The last thing I want is Virgil following me halfway around the world, when he's undoubtedly got better things to do than get in my hair. I'm going to see Astara tomorrow. I'm not sure where, I just want to see her again, I guess for some kind of assurance that she's still doing alright without me. As much as I'd prefer my old life, even with my miserable cow of a wife, there's nothing I can do about it now, save, of course, dying. I don't think I want to do that yet. Everything's been completely horrible lately. It's not anyone's fault but my own, because it comes from the conditions I made for myself before I became the thing I am today. I can't blame it on Virgil or Lia, because neither of them needed to take care of me like they've done, and if I ever get the chance, I'll pay them back for all the things they've taught me, and all the times they've bailed me out of bad situations. Whenever they figure out that I'm gone, they'll probably try to do it again, in fact. This time, though, I don't need help. If I sink myself doing this, it'll be worth it. It brings back the days I remember from a couple of years ago, when Heidrun and I still cared about each other, those days before and just after Astara was born. I remember almost every little thing that she did, and I honestly think that was one of the things that Heidrun started to hate about me. That kid was my life, and I think she started to resent that. I never stopped caring about her, at least, before she started really manifesting that spite. I think that's one of the reasons I'll never really be able to forgive her. Something started buzzing in Rune's duffel bag, startling him out of his writing. “Fear rule my world still Has ruled it, shall rule it Meek is weak? Ill-boding wolfs blood Crowned in woe, death-stained Dread-gard Queen Sear the runes of wrath The rependant writhes in remorse Niflhel - Ware her venom! Rune managed to find his cell phone, after tossing out every possession he'd dragged along with him. How did this thing even get in the bag? A quick glance at the number, and his heart sank. 'Shit.' He flipped the phone open. 'Where the hell are you?' Rune sighed. Not even so much as a hello. This likely meant one of two things. One, Virgil was concerned. Two, and much more likely, Virgil was pissed. He decided that the second was in fact the right choice a couple of seconds later. 'Rune!' 'Sorry, Virgil. I'm a little pre-occupied.' 'With the first question I asked you, I hope.' Rune shook his head. 'I'm in Denmark.' 'Don't bullshit me. I know you're in Norway. You have no reason to be in Denmark. What the hell are you doing there?' Rune found himself getting a little irritated by the tone of Virgil's questions, chiding him as though he was an errant child that had wandered away from home. 'I'm trying to see Astara.' 'That's incredibly foolish, you know.' 'I don't care.' Rune realized that it probably wasn't the best idea to argue with his employer/friend/sire, but there was nothing else to do at this moment. If he couldn't have this one last thing to remember, then there was no point in living. 'If I don't get to see her, I might forget what she looks like.' 'What use is preventing that? You know completely well that you'll never have a normal life with her. Your wife prevented that even before your metamorphosis.' Rune gritted his teeth. 'I'm aware of that. I'm not talking about making some kind of goddamned soap-opera scene here, Virgil, I just want to see my little girl again, so I can remember what she was like before she started growing up without me. It's the last thing from my godforsaken life that I have the chance to do something about, and while I may not be able to get her back or throw Heidrun off a cliff, I'm damn sure not letting anyone stop me from doing this.' Silence hung heavily over the conversation for a moment, and Rune began to wonder if he'd made a mistake. 'Fine. Do what you have to do, but don't get yourself into anything you can't get yourself out of.' 'I'll survive, I think.' 'Next time, the least you could do is let me know before you fly halfway across the world.' 'Alright, dad.' Somehow, that joke sounded incredibly awkward, given the current situation between the two of them. Rune felt fairly squeamish about it, for reasons he wasn't entirely certain of, and on the other end of the line, Virgil was currently grasping more less the same thing. 'Let me know when you get back.' 'Alright.' After that, there was silence. Rune flipped his phone shut, and walked somberly over to his bed, crawling into it with every intention of going to sleep. After a few minutes, he found that he couldn't even shut his eyes. The fire continued with its monotone roar, and he watched the flames dancing. There were too many things on his mind for sleep, regardless of the fact that he was dead tired. Anger, fear, apprehension, jealousy, and confusion were currently engaged in a five-way battle for control, and he knew he wouldn't be able to sleep until one of them won out. He closed his eyes, and relaxed, trying to hum a song that was completely unrelated to what he was feeling right now. The words of 'Fra sj’f’r til passasjer' by Kaizers Orchestra came out somehow, a slow, minor-keyed reminder of everything he wanted to forget. There was something about being in Prague that made him feel fairly insignificant, despite the fact that he'd been around for quite some time himself. It also served to remind him of all the time he'd had to make things in his life right once again. This was a constant theme in his inner monologue, although for the longest time, he'd been searching for anything he could get his hands on that would set him on the proper course. He knew his children were likely dead, and, in fact, the majority of people he had interacted with throughout his life were likewise deceased. There remained, of course, one solitary living relic from his past, and she was currently busy inspecting some artifact or other in a small antique shop in Prague's Jewish Quarter. He'd opted to stay outside, having no particular interest in antiques, and also not being in much of a mood to be around people. It gave him, at the least, time to think. It felt like he'd been doing even more of that than usual, after the fog surrounding the pair's memories had lifted all of a sudden, for reasons he still wasn't entirely certain he fathomed, aside from the whole thing being necessary for their protection. However, of course, that left the revelation that they'd been married at one point dangling over both of their heads. Of course, he was thrilled to have found out, and was still extremely happy about the whole affair, but it left him feeling that something was still left to be said or done. However, not too long ago, he'd realized what that something was, and had planned accordingly. Remembering this for perhaps the thousandth time was interrupted suddenly, as Lia walked out of the store, a time-worn kriss in her hand. She held the blade out to him, and he peered at it curiously. 'Impressive... how old is it?' She smiled rather proudly, turning the blade back down to her side. 'The blade's signed as 1704, and it seems fairly authentic.' She carefully wrapped it in the silken cloth the shop owner had given her, and tucked it under her arm. 'Well... I think I'm satisfied.' Virgil smiled, pleased that she'd found something on their excursion. After all, they'd walked through Prague countless times now. One should at least find something to show for it. 'Back to the hotel, then?' Lia nodded, and the pair began the fairly long trek toward the other side of the Vltava. Judith Bridge loomed ahead, and the sound of the water seemed to grow louder with every step closer. 'I've enjoyed seeing Prague with you once again, amore.' She glanced up at him. 'I have as well... it was a good idea to come here before the show, and enjoy ourselves for a little bit. It's nice to have a break from everyone now and then.' Virgil nodded, finding that it wasn't really possible to make the gesture emphatic enough. He had grown tired of their usual companions and comrades, and had done so quite a while ago in a couple of cases. Granted, the number of said comrades seemed to have dwindled as of late, but Virgil wasn't complaining. What they had lost in mediocrity, they had gained in powerful allies. 'Yes... I do believe I've been growing tired of a specific one in particular.' Lia rolled her eyes. 'Not right now...' He nodded. 'I wasn't planning on wasting our travel time with business... we have enough of that normally.' They slipped through the large iron gate that separated the Jewish Quarter from Judith Bridge, and started to cross. The bridge practically buzzed from the river below, and it was somewhat comforting. Virgil had been here often enough that it felt like a second home, and places like Vysehrad castle and Judith Bridge felt like concentrated versions of that feeling. It seemed like a tradition of theirs to pause in the middle of the bridge, watching the fairly clean water go by. A few minutes of comfortable silence passed them by, and both of them probably could have stood there for hours, just knowing that the other was close by, doing the exact same thing. 'I wasn't sure exactly what would change when I found out the truth about us,' Virgil said, glancing over at her briefly. She turned to face him, looking a little puzzled. 'What do you mean? I don't see what that would change about us.' Virgil leaned forward onto the railing of the bridge, contemplating the idea in his head. 'Really, nothing about how I feel for you changed, of course. Being married that far in the past is almost too old to really mean anything, especially considering how much of that time we've spent apart.' Her face fell slightly as she glanced up at him. 'It still meant something to me... Those are some of the happiest memories I have.' He nodded, turning his head back toward her. 'Don't take me wrong, Lia... I wasn't saying it meant nothing to me. Now that I can remember those days I spent with you, I can be certain that you've always made me feel the way I do for you.' And now, the plan he'd been planning for months began to come into action. He'd been thinking about this so constantly that every word was burning a hole through his vocal cords, ripping and tearing to get out. 'What I mean is, we spent so much time apart that I think it's irredeemably in the past... and I want to renew the vow I made so long ago.' Without further ado, with one fluid motion, he fell to one knee, and produced a small ring from his pocket, holding it out to her. He barely gave her the chance to take on a puzzled and shocked expression. 'Lia... will you marry me once again?' She opened her mouth, but the words simply weren't there for her to say. Happiness surged through her with the speed of a pistol shot, and suddenly she found herself trembling all over, blinking to keep the tears on the rims of her eyes from spilling over onto her cheeks. Mutely, she nodded, a smile breaking over her face that seemed to be the essence of the purest happiness someone like Lia Vlaszy could experience, distilled right there in that moment. Virgil had largely planned out everything up until this point, and as a result, found himself struck mute as well. It was all he could do to take her hand, and slide the ring onto her finger. The sapphire mounted on it caught the moonlight, but Virgil couldn't focus on it. His eyes never left hers as he rose to his feet once again. He felt like there should be something for him to say, but none of the words he could think of in a short time seemed at all appropriate. Instead of fighting for words further, he leaned down and kissed her, and their arms wound around each other. They stood there on Judith Bridge for what seemed like an eternity, only occasionally pausing to look at each other. The night dragged on slowly, but they were in no rush for the morning. --- Outside Barcelona A restored medieval chapel Virgil glanced around the ancient chapel that now sat once again on his ancestral property, re-obtained from a bank after it was seized several years ago. He had gone to great lengths to have this place restored, to the best his memory could create. He felt as though the wait for this day was something that for all purposes should have been eternal, but as Ian Childress stood slightly higher than him at the altar, and Rune Hvalstr’m, Dawn McFarlane, and Alexis Key sat in one of the pews, it was obvious that he'd been wrong. He'd found an appropriate attire for the day, consisting of a light breastplate over a black tunic, slightly different from the first time. The chapel itself, however, was fairly close to its original state. The coat of arms that had once hung on the wall was found nearby, buried under a small pile of debris. It had seen better times, but was more or less intact. He'd had it repainted after finding it, and it now hung proudly behind the altar, where it had once stood hundreds of years ago. As a result of their contact with Lia's sire, Vaclav Ionescu, Virgil had been able to remember that there was a small loose stone covering a small stash, in which he'd hid important documents and items. The majority of them had disintegrated with time, but remarkably, due to its vellum pages and boiled leather binding, the family bible had survived, entombed in the compartment even as the chapel itself crumbled around it. The reconstruction had been fairly simple by modern standards, given that the old method of stone upon stone was not incredibly difficult when compared to complex masonry, drywall, and hardwood. Virgil had been willing to part with whatever sum was necessary, but he was pleasantly surprised when his anachronist contractor had offered to build it at barely over his own costs, for the pleasure of building an antiquated structure. However, given the gravity of the structure, Virgil elected to overpay him significantly. Light poured in through the large windows on the sides of the chapel, lighting the plain but elegant interior well. A large stained-glass window cast a glow over the altar, and nobody inside struggled to see. Time seemed to stand still as Virgil continued to wait, wondering how long he'd been up there. Given that nobody looked bored or confused, he figured he'd just been thinking quickly, or some such thing. He didn't mind the wait, however. It gave him time to think about how everything finally seemed to be coming together for him, and more importantly, for them. He never would have assumed in the past that he'd be standing here again, on ancestral land, re-marrying the same woman after hundreds of years. Everything was as it once was, and that left him with little more to be concerned about. Ian turned his head up toward the door, and Virgil's attention was immediately drawn there as well, as he heard the slight noise of footsteps on the other side. Taking the cue, Rune, Alexis, and Dawn all did likewise, as the doors opened. Imminent death couldn't have pulled Virgil's eyes off of her. Only slightly different than the first time this had happened, Lia stepped through the door, the white roses in her hair distracting him from her only briefly, as he remembered how she'd worn some like them the first time they'd met. He didn't have much more time to take in the rest of her, as their eyes locked from across the chapel as she walked down the aisle between the pews. It registered to him vaguely that someone was sniffling, and he assumed it was Alexis, but couldn't be bothered to check. For a moment, all existence ceased, save the two of them, and right now, he couldn't ask for anything more. --- Before NC-17 There was little left to do, now. The frayed ends of the ropes that had once bound Virgil Ambrosi's complex life together had all been refastened. His desire for personal attainment had been fulfilled, and for the first time, he really understood what self-actualization felt like. For the first time that he could remember, he was content... with his life, anyhow. His profession still left him with several goals to accomplish. There were people who needed to be put down a few rungs, there were threats to Lia, there were threats to him, and he would allow none of them to continue to stand. Now, he was without personal distraction, and would be singly focused on destroying anything placed in front of him. The first thorn in his side would be removed within hours, now. With little left to strive for, his mind was solely occupied with one thing: The suffering of Vivica Valentine.
