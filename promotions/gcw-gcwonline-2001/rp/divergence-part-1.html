Date: The First Day Of The Rest Of His Life (For the record as much as I've worked really hard to keep my storyline for PTC and GCW gel, I'm just not going to be able to do it this time. I have a story I want to tell for GTT5 and I just can't worry about making it fit into what I need to write for GCW, so this is called divergence, because it's where the two plot threads split off. Enjoy.) It’s the moment you first look into the mirror that you realize the change. You hardly recognized the face staring back at you. It seems vaguely familiar like maybe you’ve passed it before the street. Maybe some guy you met at a bar. If you could just stop, and focus your thoughts, take a step back from the situation you’d know him. But try as you might, it’s just not happening. “That guy looks like shit.” You whisper the words to yourself, almost hoping to banish the image from your sight. It doesn’t go away, in fact as you speak his lips move in sync to your words. Is it? Nah, couldn’t be, you’d never let yourself fall that far. Would you? Thinking back, you wonder at the last time you slept, for the matter, the last time you’d showered. Hell, you can barely remember the last drink, and that was just before you’d stumbled into the bathroom. Those dark circles under the eyes, the puffy red nose, couldn’t be. It is, sighing you realize this is what you’ve become. Unshaven, unwashed, hair a mess. God, you’re pathetic, and there’s no one to blame for it, but yourself. this would all be so much easier if you could just pass the buck, swear it was someone else’s fault . Under normal circumstances you’d never end up looking liked this. You couldn’t have become this stranger in the mirror all by yourself. Who else to blame? Knight, Sheena, Lance? God? Anyone of them could be the culprit, why not go for broke and blame them all? At least that’d pass the buck off so you wouldn’t have to deal with it. And that’s really what it had all become about at this point, hadn’t it? Someone else to blame, someone else to claim responsibility. Wouldn’t that just make the world all bright and sparkly again? Someone else to take all the blame. You could just chalk all this up to life experience, a few bad breaks thrown your way by someone else, something else. The truth is you know it’s all your fault. The truth is you know that if you laid off the bottle for a day or two, you’d be in a whole hell of a lot better shape. It’s just so damn hard, that first drink gets you out of bed. The second and third help you walk. Finishing off the bottle gives you the strength to deal with everyone else on the outside. It’s not like they really KNOW what happened. They weren’t there, didn’t see it. Yet someone, you can sense the accusation in there eyes. Somehow they can see through you, see the darkness you’re trying to hide. It’s like the liquor sets you on fire, it burns way that darkness. Keeps you warm by filling up the icy swirling chill of the void in the bit of your stomach. Without the drink you’re not certain you could go out into the light of the day. You’re pretty damn sure you couldn’t face a screaming crowd. They scream that they love you, each holding out their hands, desperate to touch you, desperate for some sort of contact. They call you great, and they believe it. They’re just so damn desperate to touch something they love, to connect to the fame in some small way. Yeah, that’s definitely the worst, those people who don’t seem to know, those people that see the mask you put on. The bottle gives you enough strength to give them the show you know they deserve. And when it’s all over they still call you great, they still think you’re supreme among men. You’re a goddamn superhero and rock star, all rolled into one convenient package. You wouldn’t be able to stand it without having a drink or two. The cheers make you feel even worse than you did before, they love you, even though they shouldn’t. It makes you feel like you should tell them. Scream it out to the world, I’m a murder, take me to jail, please. Still, you know you can’t do that, there are others to think of besides yourself. The guilt you feel is just your cross to bare. For once in your life you’re not going to be a completely selfish asshole. You just need a little help that’s all, the drink makes the cross just a little bit lighter. And really, who’s going to fault you for making things just a little bit easier on yourself? Well, you will for one. Deep down you know you don’t deserve things to be any easier. You should be strong enough to handle the situations you’ve gotten yourself into. Only you’re not, so you use your little liquid crutch, and that just makes you feel even more useless at the end of the day. You look in the mirror tomorrow, and it all starts again. Same as it ever was, same as it’s always going to be until you figure out how the hell you’re going to stop it. The bottles right there, that’s your first thought. It’s so close, a few swallows and you’ll feel better. These thoughts won’t cross your mind so often. You’ll have a little piece of mind, maybe your mind will stop working over time and you can sleep. Maybe you’ll drink enough that you won’t dream and can actually stay asleep. “Easy way out.” You notice the catch in your throat. You hadn’t heard it before, even though you’ve spoken those same words every day for the last month. Something’s different today, maybe you’re finally realizing that stranger in the mirror is you after all. Maybe it’s starting to sink in that when all is said and done, this is your fault. Your condition. You made yourself this way. Slow suicide by inches, subtle death in a shot. Looking into the mirror one last time you see it, just a flash. His face laughing at you. It all makes sense. It’s your fault you took the drink, it’s Lance’s fault he put the needle in her arm, it’s Sheena’s fault she took it. But he started it, he took her, made her want the needle, made him give it to her eased the pain, made you hurt him, made you drink. He won months ago, and now he’s laughing. You exist as his smirking revenge, and it’s his laughing face in the mirror. Grabbing the bottle you want to wash it all away. Lifting it to your lips you see him again. “I win.” The bottle shatters the mirror, the mirror shatters the bottle. Whiskey stings your eyes, blood leaks from the cut on your cheek. Blood, whiskey tears, you make the decision. It’s not about winning a title, it’s not about losing yourself in oblivion. It’s about hurting him, making him pay. In the slivers of glass, in the pieces of mirror. You see his face, one last time. He’s not laughing, he’s scared now. He made a monster. He can’t stop his creation. ~To Be Continued~
