The Ballad of Asgard: Chapter 3

21 October 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 21, 2008 The walls of the hall were draped with intricately woven scenes of battles long fought and the rugs covered the floor with a deep red, as if blood spilled. The hall of Valhalla was majestic. It wasn’t a big as one would have expected, but the artwork, the throne, everything about it exuded magnificence. Out of the few small windows that opened up to the outside you could see the deep green of the plant life that surrounded the hall. It was a wild green. The color you see only in the very few places untouched by human kind. It was the color of the gods. As the two lonely figures walked through the hall towards the throne, one of them couldn’t help but feel insignificant. The grandeur of the place made him feel like he didn’t belong. And he didn’t. He knew it. This hall was for the legends of a forgotten lore, for those who had died bravely and with honor. It was a place of rest for those who had spilled the blood of the conquering enemy or in conquering an enemy. He had not died yet and he had plans to stay not dead for a lot longer. As the two figures came closer to the throne, the sound of a bird flying overhead made Antonio’s head snap upward. The black raven flew gracefully over their heads towards the throne. Once he reached there, the black bird sat softly on a small perch built at the top of the throne’s back. Beside it sat another black raven, looking intently at the two figures who had finally reached the throne. “Welcome to my hall of warriors,” the booming voice resounded over the walls. The old man who sat there looked at the two visitors with an inquisitorial eye. Antonio could see the place where his other eye would be and remembered from his studies in university the story of the sacrifice of that eye for the gift of wisdom. “It has been too long since you have graced us with your presence here in our great hall. If it wasn’t for your work, these halls would be empty and those that die valiantly would be forgotten,” he signaled with his hand and almost as soon as he did one of Odin’s valkyries came out of a well concealed door with a tray. She handed one of the steel mugs to Odin and then gave the other to Death. “It has been long since we drank mead in my hall.” “It has been too long indeed,” she said as she drank the mead that was offered. It would have been an insult to refuse. Not everyone got offered the drink of the gods. She finished drinking the mead and put the mug back on the tray that the valkyrie held. Antonio could still smell the sweetness of the honey in the air. He looked at Dee’s movements and saw the refinement with which she handled everything. He guessed that millennia of practice must have polished her into what she was. The old man drank deeply and quickly his mead and cleaned his long white beard from any residue. He turned his eye from Dee to Antonio, who stood there in solemn silence as the two old acquaintances shared their moment. Antonio saw the wisdom of the ages in the Norse god’s eyes. Even though Dee was older than Odin, she liked to mingle with human kind and she looked like she belonged. Odin had his own little haven where he waited with the strongest warriors for Ragnarok, the end of days, the final battle. He stroked his chin and the beard that covered it with one hand. One of the ravens came down from its perch and rested on Odin’s shoulder. It moved towards his ear, as if speaking to the Old Norse god. He listened intently as the raven whispered memories in his ear. The raven and the god finished their discussion and it went back to its perch atop the throne. “Munin tells me of the helped you have given the world during what might be called a small Ragnarok. Stopping that witch goddess was necessary. I feel like I need to explain this more,” he motioned towards his great hall with his arms. “Come. Follow me into the Hall of Forgotten Gods.” The old man grabbed an elaborately sculptured piece of wood that he used as a walking stick. His long robe made of wolf hide dragged on the floor behind them. For a second, Antonio didn’t know what to do. Dee nudged him and they started to walk a little behind Odin. The Norse god opened a big wooden door and beckoned Antonio and Dee inside. If Antonio thought the throne room inside Valhalla was beautiful and grand, this room was three times greater. It went as far as the eye could see. They stood looking down from a balcony and Antonio still couldn’t see the end of the room. Below the balcony, intermingling as if in a lover’s embrace, plant life and sculptures made their home. “This is our sacred hall. I come here to mourn those gods who for some reason or other have been lost in time and forgotten. Our friend here,” he pointed towards Dee, “she doesn’t have that problem since there will always be belief in death, but gods like me all have a shelf life. It is a waiting game for us. I come here to remind myself that I am not yet forgotten.” “I’m not sure if this is rude or insulting to ask, but how come you are still not forgotten if the Norse religion is not really believed in anymore.” He flinched a little when Odin turned towards him. There was a smile on his face, but it was more a smile of sadness than anything else. “You’d be surprised at how many people still believe in us Norse gods. Even then, there are small things that keep us around here. Even if most people don’t realize it, there are small things that give us power. For example, the day you and English speaking places call Wednesday is named in my honor. Those small things keep us old gods going,” he said as he turned back to the endless gods that had not been able to assert themselves into remembrance. As the two gods and one human looked on in a silence that was deeper than anyone had thought possible, the sound of the door opening behind them startled them. One of the valkyries stood there with the sunlight glinting off of her golden plate mail armor. “Sir, you have another visitor. It seems important.” Odin sighed as if suddenly realizing his position. He gave a small bow in the direction of the sculptures and without another word the air of nobility had returned to the Norse god’s fa’ade. He walked back into the main hall, leaving Antonio and Death alone in that ominous room. “Why are we here exactly” I thought we were going to travel the world,” Antonio said as he kept his eyes on the sculptures in the horizon. It was impossible to try and figure out what these gods represented or who they were, they had been long lost. “You said you wanted to see the world. What better place to go and tell your friends about than Asgard and the hall of Valhalla?” she asked playfully. She saw that it didn’t really amuse Antonio and her smiled faded. There had never been a human who was able to read her as well as Antonio could. It was frightening. The thought that one human could tell what Death was thinking could have some unknown repercussion. “There is something going on. I’m not exactly sure what it is, but I know that it might be big. The fact that a leprechaun was able to keep a whole town hostage and the pieces they were made to dig” it all just seems too strange.” “You are telling me. Do you think Odin know what is going on?” She contemplated the answer to that question. “I don’t think so. If he knew, he would have known why we were here. Hugin and Munin are never wrong.” “Thought and Memory. The knowledge of the world perched on his shoulders. It’s amazing. So, what do we do now” Do we go back? We already know that Odin has no idea what is going on, if anything is,” he said to her. For the first time, he looked at her, really looked at her since they had gotten to that strange place. The sunlight that made its way in from the outside through the myriad of windows around the room lit up her pale white skin. The memories came rushing back in that hall of remembrance. The reasons why they had parted in the first place stared at him stoutly. They were two different people divided by time and by their own future. He had loved Death. He wasn’t sure if it was wise. Antonio knew that one day she would come back for him and it wouldn’t be to travel the world. “We keep on going. There are still a lot of places to see and explore,” she said cheerfully as she grabbed his arm and intertwined it with her own. They looked on in silence for a few more seconds until the door opened behind them. A valkyrie beckoned them to the main hall. When they walked in, they saw a lone figure standing where they had stood only minutes before. The figure wore a pristinely white toga and the petasus that was one of his most important icons. The wings on his hat and sandals seemed to flutter with the small breeze that came into the room. The Greek messenger god Hermes waited until Odin had finished reading the scroll. “It seems there are problems in Mount Olympus. I think this relates to the reason why you two are here. I think it would be a good idea for you two to go to Olympus and resolve this issue. I will convene the Council of the Elders as soon as possible but it won’t get together for some time since it is not easy to get all the elder gods in one place,” Odin said from his throne. He was contemplating the future of his world as he started to piece everything together. Hermes bowed his head towards Death as soon as he saw her; she bowed back as she and Antonio walked towards the messenger god. It was time for her to get to work on something other than death. The prospect exhilarated her. Then again, she had no idea that death would be what she had to investigate. --- “Finally, it is complete! All I need is the blood of the trickster, the blood of Loki, the blood of our brother,” Helbindi smiled with malevolent glee at the thought of killing his brother came across his mind. He looked at the blood on his hands and couldn’t help but smell it. If it wasn’t for the whimpers of his brother in the corner of the room, he would have reveled in that moment. “Why” Why? Why? Brother, why did you have to kill him? He never did anything bad to us. He was just the blacksmith,” Byleist cried out from his haven in pain. Pain for a god he had never met but his brother had just murdered. “He helped us, brother. He helped!” “You still don’t get it, do you” I needed to test the sword. I needed to see if my work of centuries would hold its own against the mighty blacksmith! Hephaestus never had a chance against the mightiest blade this world or any other has ever seen,” exclaimed an obviously excited Helbindi. His brother couldn’t take any more of it though. The first few centuries were bliss for him and his brother. They were the forgotten brothers of Loki, but that didn’t matter, what mattered was that they existed. They would never wield the power of Loki or most of the gods that existed, but they would at least be remembered. One day, Helbindi accidentally cut himself and the wound didn’t immediately heal. It sent him into a rage. He became increasingly malevolent and wicked. For years Byleist had stood by in fear of what his brother would do to him, but he had had it. He couldn’t take it anymore. When he saw the blood and the body of Hephaestus spilled on the ground, something had triggered inside of him. He didn’t know if it was the fear of what his brother might do with the blade, but he stood up behind his brother. Byleist was a good six inches taller than his brother, but it had been a long time since he stood without slouching. He grabbed at the hilt of the sword. The jerk caught Helbindi completely by surprise. “I will not let you use this thing to harm anyone else. I’m done with your hate and the poison that you spew every single day.” Byleist turned around towards the hammer that had been used to build the weapon and grabbed it with his free hand. “Sorry, brother.” He swung the hammer with all of his might but stopped right before he could hit the sword. “No. I’m sorry, brother,” Helbindi said as he turned his hand in a circle and sliced horizontally. The warm mix of blood and internal organs that spilled over his hand was invigorating. For a moment he completely forgot that he had murdered his brother. The thud as the body slumped to the ground didn’t faze Helbindi. He grabbed the sword and stood there, looking at it with hungry eyes. “Finally, there is nothing to hold me back. With the help of my partner, I will be remembered.” --- Since they had gotten to Mount Olympus, Antonio had seen some of the most amazing things he had ever had a chance to see. From satyrs to chimeras and of course, some of the Greek gods he had studied back in college, it was a sight to behold. The sight before him now was a stark contrast to what lay outside the house of the blacksmith. The blacksmith god Haphaestus? body lay almost in two. The wide cut went from the right shoulder to almost the waist. They could see the floor through the hole on the chest. The floor was bathed in the blood of the god and his eyes were full of surprise and what seemed like regret. Hermes stood in silence as Death inspected the body for clues. Antonio tried to look busy but he really felt powerless. He had seen enough crime solving TV series to know what to look for but he really had absolutely no idea what to look for in the murder of a Greek god. “This wound comes from a formidable weapon. As far as I know, no blade can cut through a god like this. Hermes, do you know if Hephaestus was working on some special weapon?” Death asked as she closely inspected the wound. Hermes shook his head. “Not that anyone here knows. He is always working on thunder bolts for Zeus, but other than that, there was no special weapon. Do you think someone might have stolen a sword of his?” “I don’t think he would have created a weapon so powerful for just anyone, so if he was working on a top secret project, it must have been for someone high in the food chain,” she said as she stood up and looked at the body with sadness. She shook her head in a sort of disbelief. “Maybe it was done to prove a point,” Antonio said to no one in particular. “What do you mean?” Hermes asked. “Well, if I were to create a weapon that I thought was as powerful as you guys seem to think this was, who better to test it against than the god of blacksmiths and engineering” Hephaestus would probably be the only one that could have topped that weapon or even make something to defend against him,” Antonio explained. “Bingo.” All the heads turned around at the new voice behind them. The surprise in the faces of Hermes and Death didn’t register in Antonio’s face. This was probably some god, but he didn’t know exactly who. Hermes moved towards the god menacingly. “Now, now” calm down wing-head. I’m not here to cause any trouble,” the figure said as he raised his arms in a mocking surrender. “You don’t cause any trouble, you ARE trouble. You are not supposed to be here.” “I thought that whole racist “Olympus for the Greeks and Asgard for the Nords” was done here by now! You people definitely have to get on with the times. That’s why most of you are irrelevant and this forgotten,” the figure said as he moved closer to Hermes and flicked one of the wings in his hat. Hermes moved to strike the god but Death grabbed the messenger god’s wrist to stop him. “Do you know who did this?” She let go of Hermes? hand as she asked the question. “I might,” he answered walking around Death with a smirk on his face. “Wait a minute. Who is this guy?” Antonio asked aloud. “Who am I” Who am I? Where did you get this clown?” The anger at not being recognized was apparent. “Who are you, mortal plaything of the gods?” “He’s with me,” Death stood in front of the deity defiantly. The deity guffawed. “I am the Trickster God, the Lie-Smith, the Sly God, and the Wizard of Lies, among other more offensive names. I am none other than Loki,” he said with a bow. “Loki, you said you knew who killed Hephaestus. If you are not joking or messing around, tell us your theory,” Death asked him pointedly. “I do know who it was. What is important though is not who did it, but who ordered it done,” he laughed out loud. “If you knew who it was that ordered it done, you would be astounded and perplexed, confused and mystified, bewildered and bef-? “Tell us who it was already!” Hermes couldn’t hold it in any longer. Loki licked his lips and smiled wickedly. “It was none other than the wife of the great Nordic god himself, Odin. The beloved lady herself. Odin is in depression mode and she thinks that by destroying the major players in Ragnarok, she can actually stop the end of times,” Loki explained as he scratched his head trying to put all the pieces together. “She never liked me and Odin would never kill a blood-brother.” “How do we know you are telling the truth?” Antonio echoed the same thought that was passing through the minds of his companions. “You don’t. You will just have to trust me,” he cackled loudly for a few minutes. “Was that too much?”
