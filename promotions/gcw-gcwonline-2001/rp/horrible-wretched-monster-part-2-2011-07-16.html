Horrible, Wretched Monster (Part 2)

16 July 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07/16/11 A horrible, wretched monster was Terrance "Trayvon" Hill, paramour of power and pimp daddy of bitches. He was as vile as Satan, he’d have you believe. "Hell, Satan’s just the next step down," he often told his acquaintances. Horrible, wretched monsters have no friends. Acquaintances were few as well (without power, anyway). Thin, braided rows of corn covered his unnaturally narrow scalp. His skin, though much darker, he would say was a shade of caramel. His limp, though entirely fabricated, he would say was from being shot in the leg a long ass time ago. His hands, though scarred from a kitchen fire as a boy, he would say was from his time Korea. His drive through the lower-income suburbs of Memphis, though requiring a bit of pre-planning, had begun without delay. No man still alive had crossed Trayvon. No man still alive would dare to. The ones who had had met St. Peter. Regardless of whether he may or may not have actually had anything to do with their deaths, the point was they were dead as hell. And that fact alone was good enough to send Trayvon straight to the wall of Egotism. That fact alone was just so good to Tray that he seemed to have forgotten (over time) how he didn’t have much to do with anyone dying. He marched right up to that Wall of Egotism, took one look over it, saw all the beautiful self-important, self-immortalizing perspectives and said, "Yep. That’s what I want right there." Ever since that fateful day he waved guns in motherfuckers’ faces that refused to pay his hoes. He’d even pistol whipped a few sorry sons of bitches, just to remind their white asses he wasn’t fucking around. He was the biggest, richest pimp in the Memphis Area (as far as he knew), and if homeboys think they’re going to get one over on Tray, homeboys better think again. So imagine the disgusting taste it left in his mouth when his main bitch came crawling back, bruised in the face with no money. Imagine how absolutely irate he became when he heard about this motherfucker, Zip Gordon, telling his main bitch, "Sorry, I don’t pay niggers for pussy." It had been a few months since Terrance Hill had fired a gun. He reckoned now was as good of a time as there ever was. Motherfuckers can’t just waltz around, getting nuts and not paying for them, using the word of hatred and not paying for it. In fact, motherfucker’s gonna pay for it with everything he’s got. Motherfucker’s gonna die. He was surprised when he looked up ‘Gordon’ in the phonebook and found only twelve in the Memphis area, none of them named Zip. After a few moments of careful planning, he decided he would ride to every Gordon residence and fuck shit up until he knew for a fact whether or not Zip Gordon was there. Then, if he weren’t, he’d keep fucking shit up until he knew whether or not they were related to Zip. He’d hit ten of the residences with no luck. Two of which no one was there, the other eight had to live through a terrifying ordeal that lasted all of thirty minutes. Tray, waving his gun around. Tray, breaking decorative vases and lamps. Tray, beating the hell out of people. Just two more on the list: Red Gordon of 46 Nightingale Ave, and Rhonda Gordon of 3433 Jasper Drive Apartment C3. Alphabetical order had seemed the most logical approach. Besides, he wasn’t one to break pattern unless absolutely necessary. And the only thing that was absolutely necessary was finding and destroying the man named Zip Gordon. If that meant whooping the ass and taking the names of the innocent, so be it. There was something about the driveway he rolled into with his silver ’89 Cadillac, anyway. Something about 46 Nightingale Avenue that sent shivers down the man’s back. The good kind of shivers, the ones that come from knowing your about to get dirty with a fine-ass woman, the ones that come from the smell of gunpowder, or some good ass bacon an eggs. When Red Gordon, in tighty whities and a wifebeater, burst out of his house and yelled, "Get out of my driveway ya fancy ass nigger! Get out of here before I call Johnny Law! Aint no damn blacky coming in or around this damn property! Least of all your fancy black ass!"… Well, it made Terrance Hill’s day. It really did. ^*^*^*^*^*^ A horrible, wretched monster was Jerry Willborn, lawyered up asshole and Shelby County Kingpin. And, as best as Zip could tell, the man was good for absolutely nothing except ruining your whole god damn life! "I mean, damn!" Zip cried, and got punched in the mouth for it, hard. "Runnin’ your mouth at Jerry aint an enterprise worth investing in, you old dumb ass," taunted Jerry. He was nearing fifty, but a good five years younger than old dumb ass Zip Gordon, and damn better looking— the ugly son of a bitch, couldn’t pay to get laid probably. At least Jerry had a little pepper in the hair, by God! And some thunder still left in the pecker! "Woo," Jerry yelled after thinking to himself for a moment. He always tended to reflect in times of great ass whoopins, "Damn, look at your ugly, beat up ass. Aint no wonder you ordered that HCG." "That’s the whole problem you jerk," spit Zip, who apparently wasn’t afraid of some punches to the face or the fact he was tied to a chair inside of a warehouse. Nor, apparently, was he afraid of the fact that Jerry had about twelve other fellas with him, all acting like dicks by the way (laughing at Jerry’s little comments, assholes), who couldn’t wait to show Jerry how much they appreciated the job by beating the hell out of him. "I didn’t ask for all that shit! I asked for some HGH and you have some guy show up demanding about 2,500 dollars because he loaded me up with god damn HCG and horse tranquilizers and god knows WHATTHEFUCKELSE, which, by the way, what the fuck is HC--" Jerry’s fist slammed so hard into Zip’s face that the twelve dudes around him went, "KAPOW," like a bunch of damn cheerleaders trying to get into their coach’s miniskirt, Zip observed. Then they all giggled like little sissies. "HCG is for your god damn privates, Zip! Your fucking balls! You need that shit you old stupid shit, and I hooked your ass up. And now you don’t wanna pay because I fucking gave you more than you asked for, for nearly free?" He punched Zip in the face again. "KAPOW!" Zip and Jerry both screamed, very coincidentally at the twelve, "SHUT THE FUCK UP, WILL YOU!?" After a few "ooooo youmadehimmad’s…" the cheerleader thugs shut the fuck up. "Now look here Jerry, I think we can reach some sort of compromise here. I mean, damn Jerr! I’m tired of you beating my ass while I sit in this chair. How about we talk it out a minute, see if we can’t some to an agreement?" Jerry cast his own brown eyes on Zips, twiddled his thumbs behind his back (which was coated by an elegant sports coat) for a minute, and sighed. "What do you suggest?" Zip thought a moment, looked at Jerry in the eye like a cold-hearted bastard and said, "What say you and me arm wrestle? You win, you keep whooping my ass for doing you wrong even though you were the one who did ME wrong. I win, you let me go and I get to fuck that hot ass mexican lady you got for a wife." Jerry had to hand it to the stubborn old shit. He had standards, and he stood by them. But those standards wouldn’t matter much longer. "What say you get set on fire?" "YES!" Screamed one of the cheerleaders, a thirty-five year old man dressed like a seventeen year old Pacsun regular, and nearly skipped, merrily to Willborn, who took a gallon-sized can of gas from his hand. After a moment of hesitation, Zip found his resolve and said, "You don’t have the damn guts." "Actually," Jerry grinned and displayed a full set of porcelain veneers. "And you may find this a little funny, I do, but yeah—I lost my matches. Anybody got any matches?" The cheerleaders started feeling themselves up for matchbooks they probably knew they didn’t have, and Zip yawned. "I mean, I have a zippo, but I always like matches, you know, for dramatic effect," he said almost apologetically. "Anyway, yeah, fuck it, I’ll just use the lighter." Zip interjected politely, "Well now wait a minute, if you want some matches for your little queer dramatic effect, I got a book in my shirt pocket there." Willborn feigned being taken aback, condescendingly, "Really? May I use one, kind sir?" "Sure can fag," Zip said assuredly, as though some flamboyant man asked to borrow a quarter in the gas station. Willborn reached in Zip’s pocket and grabbed the book of matches. They were from the eloquent gentleman’s club, "Tom’s Tittybar." After pocketing the matches, Willborn nodded too confidently and said something so polite that it made Zip realize he was in trouble after all. Zip knew that Willborn intended on killing him, otherwise he would’ve earned more punches with the remarks he’d made. But to hear, "Why, thank you. Now, let’s hop to it, shall we?" As Zip was being doused in gasoline, his father’s words came from his own tongue. They tasted bitter. "I guess this is the last check I wrote that my ass would not cash," He said. Jerry lifted an eyebrow, "…What?" ^*^*^*^*^*^ Terrance Hill marched right towards Red Gordon, paying no heed to his warnings that he would hang him and burn him and rape his mama and papa, nor any attention to the rest of his drunken, racist tirade. He walked right up to the son of a bitch and shoved him into his house. "Shut your mouth you fuck," Terrance screamed, livid. And something else, too. Happy? No. Excited. Excited for some action. Excited to once again determine the outcome of future events. Excited to play God. Red Gordon had tumbled backward and into his living room from being shoved, but he came right back with a hard right hand to the jaw. Trayvon looked shocked. Had this asshole really just punched him in the face? "That’s RIGHT," Red confirmed. "I’ll punch your ugly, charcoal face anytime I feel it, y’ni--" Red was slammed so hard in the nose that a red mist burst forth from it, not unlike being sprayed from an aerosol can. Terrance shook his own hand, as though it hurt, and then punched him again in the jaw. The old man dropped like the sack of shit Terrance thought he was. The old man was writhing on the ground, complaining and flinging insults as Terrance folded his fingers behind his back and walked casually around the room. "Let me explain my situation to you, Mr. Gordon." Hill spoke like a college professor standing at a lectern, explanatory but not interested in who or who isn’t paying attention. Not really. "I’ve been looking for a man, who (and I have to be honest here) seems to hold a lot of the same ideals and beliefs towards my people that you do. Which, seeing as how he has your last name, leads me to believe he’s your son or your nephew. As he seems, to me, to be a good twenty five years younger than your sixty-looking year old ass. So, you tell me where Zip Gordon is, and I won’t punish you further for your crude and curlish language. Y’dig?" Red had stopped rolling on the ground somewhere in the middle of that speech and was looking up at the man he disrespected, at the man who was looking for his son. "I aint seen Zip in nearly seven years," old Red said. His frown wasn’t one of sadness, rather shame and embarrassment. "If I were your son," began Trayvon. "I’d stay away from your ass too. But I know you know where he is. I know that you know someone who does." Trayvon removed a butterfly knife from his pocket and slung it around like a pro. Then he reached down and stuck it to the side of Red’s neck, much to his chagrin. He pierced Red’s skin only slightly, enough for him to feel the trickle of blood-flow rolling down his neck. Enough for him to know Terrance meant business. "At least," Trayvon said. "You better know someone who does."
