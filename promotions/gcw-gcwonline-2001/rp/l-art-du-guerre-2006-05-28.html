L'Art du Guerre

28 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 05-28-06 Thornwood Manor, South Carolina- The Library May 28th, 2006 --?If you know the enemy and know yourself, you need not feel the result of a hundred battles.?-- --Sun Tzu, Art of War-- The library at Thornwood Manor was perhaps Virgil's most prized achievement, over the finally-renovated house. It housed thousands of books, of varying rarity, encompassing nearly every conceivable subject, and occupied nearly half of the first floor. Virgil's boots fell lightly on the hardwood floors, and he inhaled the pleasant scent of aged paper, mixed with the heavy scent of expensive wood, from the bookcases. It was truly a masterpiece of a room, he thought to himself, as he crossed through the books of medical science. He was reminded fondly of Lia at the sight of them, but he knew she was occupied elsewhere. The target of his excursion was ensconced in the back corner, where Virgil had ordered a small enclave set up, for extended periods of study. Contentedly perched there was the somewhat odd-to-behold figure of Cyrus, the nearly seven foot Kiasyd bent over a dusty tome of some sort. Virgil had invited him over from Zdice to offer Cyrus the chance to absorb what texts he didn't already possess, and Cyrus had been more than appreciative of the offer. In fact, Virgil hadn't seen him outside of the library for almost a month. He'd begun to wonder whether the glow of the lamps on the desk would tint Cyrus' skin permanently. ?Ah... Senhor Ambrosi. Good evening.? Virgil was slightly taken aback by the awareness Cyrus demonstrated, but took it in stride, smiling graciously at his esteemed colleague and friend. ?Good evening, Cyrus. How go your transcriptions?? Cyrus glanced up from the book he was currently entering into another hand-bound volume, something Virgil found slightly eccentric (but Cyrus' nature made it unsurprising), a pleased look upon his face. ?I come ever closer to the final volume to finish... you have acquired many interesting and difficult to find books. I appreciate your gracious invitation to Thornwood.? He paused for a moment, before turning his coal black eyes upward to Virgil, realizing there was likely some reason for the disturbance. ?Did you need something?? The tone was unperturbed, a rarity in itself. Cyrus rarely took kindly to his work being interrupted, and even more rarely did he offer assistance to any but his closest associates, however implicitly. Virgil nodded. ?Regretfully, yes... I am loathe to impose upon you, but something has been consistently deviling me lately. Of the scholars I've met in my time, your knowledge of history is the most expansive, and I thought it only proper that I come to you with my question.? The delicate line between flattery and respect was one often nearly passed, during discussions between Virgil and Cyrus. Meeting each other over a hundred years ago had left them with a dated sense of reverence, and neither seemed to mind it terribly. ?You overestimate me, senhor, but what is your question?? Virgil ran a finger over his chin, quickly gathering his thoughts into a compact form. ?I have been having dreams lately, Cyrus... I believe they are parts of my memory. The scenes therein are so familiar, somehow, and not in the sense of deja vu. Things are vibrant and real, but their familiarity is what continually strikes me... however, I need to ask, what do you know of my history, my family, anything that could help me sort these into fact or fiction.? Cyrus leaned back from the large book, setting his pen in the crease of the pages, and blinking, rubbing his cheek pensively. ?I do admit, the study of nobility and their actions is not my predominant field...? Virgil nodded slowly. ?Yes... I know it strays from the metaphysical and arcane, but I thought you may remember some off-hand reference from somewhere.? Cyrus squinted, as though attempting to force his mind to mystically conjure some shred of information, if only to prevent Virgil's time from having been wasted. Finally, his eyebrows raised, and he leaned forward. ?Ah... yes, I remember something I read of you, once. I felt it only courteous to become acquainted with some of your history after having met you, but mentions in Czech at the time were very slim... and you know, at that time, how we nations of Europe were so insular... I knew nothing of your native language, save that it was somehow derived from Latin. However, a travelogue mentioned your clan, whose name I honestly have forgotten... you have always referred to yourself as Ambrosi, as long as I have known you.? Virgil nodded once again, this time looking slightly more dejected. However, Cyrus holds up his hand, a solid gesture of comfort to Virgil. ?But... I do recall that your family originated somewhere in the continental east, but had migrated to what would now be Portugal, I believe. You did at one point inherit a castle, which was mentioned as a fair one indeed, but the travelogue I mentioned lamented your absence... I believe it said you were in battle with... the Sarcaens? No, that may not be correct... I do not recall the nation with whom you were at war. I apologize, but I believe that is the extent of my knowledge.? Virgil bowed his head slightly, which Cyrus returned, shrugging regretfully. ?It is more than I had previously... thank you, Cyrus. You have done me a substantial favor, and I am in your debt. However, I think I may go upstairs to think upon what you have told me tonight.? Cyrus nodded, perhaps a little enthusiastically. ?Certainly... I would never complain at assisting you, senhor.? Virgil smiled, ever so slightly, and bowed once again, a little more deeply. ?Dobrou noc.? ?Dobrou noc, senhor.? Virgil turned on his heels, and padded quickly out of the library, no longer desiring to intrude upon his friend. However, his inquiries, aside from being relatively unsuccessful, had left his mind somewhat chaotic, frustrated, and yet mildly curious still... though he was beginning to wonder if he'd ever be able to fight his way through the thick mists surrounding his memories. It seemed more hopeless every time he thought about it. - - - - - - - - - - Miranda de Ebro, Pais Vasco Many years ago --Let your plans be as dark and impenetrable as the night, and when you move, fall down like a thunderbolt-- --Sun Tzu, Art of War-- The daylight had been slowly fading, somewhat early it seemed, something Virgil had noticed during his excursions into the Basque country. The river Ebro rushed noisily next to the raiding party, composed of an even mix of his skilled and not-as-skilled warriors. This would be a simple mission, though perhaps his younger soldiers would learn a few things along the way, even if the final result would likely be nothing spectacular. For some time, now, the citizens in the lands to the north under Virgil's control had been complaining of a small camp of Roma, situated on the river. Rumors had followed the Roma ceaselessly throughout their time spent dwelling in Europe, and this town was no different from the others. As soon as the foreigners were recognized as Roma, accusations of theft, murder, rape, and general misanthropia . Virgil was ambivalent as to the veracity of the claims, but his citizens were regardless more important to him than a few Roma, be they criminals or not. The hooves of the horses were quiet on the soft riverbed, and his soldiers had done very little complaining in regard to the excursion itself. There would be no time for the Roma to prepare for their arrival, and despite generally being somewhat poor, Virgil was entirely conscious that they would arm themselves, given the chance, and he wished to avoid confrontation at all costs. After all, his soldiers had wives at home as he did, and he assumed many of them would have enjoyed returning to them alive. ?Milord, how much farther to the camp?? A young soldier walking next to Virgil asked, carrying his pike on his shoulder. Virgil turned down to him, maintaining the stern look of a leader. ?Not far. A mile, perhaps. Be watchful though, boy, they may spot us beforehand.? The young soldier turned back toward the front of the battalion, following Virgil's instructions well. Virgil overheard some chatter at the front of the pattern, and noticed a dimly-visible plume of smoke rising from a wooded area ahead... obviously the Roma camp they sought. ?Beware! The camp is just ahead! Keep your weapons close, but draw them only if we are attacked!? He needed no affirmation from them, for he knew very well they'd heard every word, and would obey him ceaselessly. The sunset was quickly blotted out by the canopy of trees overhead, as they entered the woods. Branches barely cracked underneath their feet, and Virgil slid down from his horse, leading him forward by the reins. They began to hear the sounds of drums from the camps, and a woman singing in a low alto. Trees cut at their bases for firewood began to appear sporadically, and within minutes, the lights of the campfires could be seen through the trees. Virgil turned to his left, to a dark-skinned man, clad in chainmail, with a large poleaxe strapped across his back. ?Satish... prepare yourself, I will be in need of your skills in their language.? He handed Satish a scroll, and Satish nodded. He had been raised from the age of nine in one of Virgil's villages, after being found orphaned after Virgil's army had interrupted a civil quarrel in a neighboring town. Since the following year, he had served in Virgil's army well, and had saved him the trouble of several conflicts with transient Roma, as he remembered much of their language. ?Yes, milord. I will give them your message.? The merriment of the camp seemed to stop the instant Virgil set foot in the clearing they'd made, and he felt, suddenly, as if the eyes of the entire camp, including ones he couldn't see, were suddenly on him. Satish stepped up to his side, quickly, and the rest of the soldiers began to filter through the trees. The air of suspicion in the camp slowly faded into that of fear, and many furtive glances were exchanged from the men around the campfires, as though mulling the thought of running to their wagons for their weapons. ?Satish...? Satish nodded, and unrolled the scroll, bellowing in a commanding tone. ?Good Roma, you are camped upon the lands of Miranda de Ebro, which is a protectorate of this army you see before you, additionally consisting of many hundreds more! The nearby town has accused you of many crimes, ranging from theft to murder, and it is the order of my lord that you are to disperse immediately! Take your camp to another town, and we may avoid unnecessary conflict! We wish you no harm, but we must consider the protection of our citizens first! We will return in a month's time, and if you are not removed from these lands, we will be forced to take more coercive action!? The last fluid syllable of the Roma tongue fell from Satish's mouth, and he felt the familiar sting of his kind's eyes upon him once again. He had long ago learned to ignore it... for they had left him, and Virgil had taken him in. Tension hung in the air, and for a moment, it seemed as though none of them knew exactly where to proceed... Twang! An arrow whizzed through the air, and Virgil instinctively raised his shield at the sound of the bowstring cracking against the archer's arm. A carved bone arrowhead clanked against his shield, and flew into the woods. He heard the scream of steel as several of his soldiers drew their weapons, but before any heavy-handed action could be taken, a commotion began to surge forth from a few feet back. Virgil turned, and bellowed; ?What goes on in my ranks?!? One of his soldiers called back to him. ?We have caught your attacker, milord!? Virgil's eyes flashed with anger, and his teeth gritted. ?Bring him to me!? The tone of his voice was terrible, and the soldiers amongst them that had seen many campaigns with him knew that his attacker would likely not be treated well. A pair of mail-clad soldiers carried a tall, dark man, clad in the traditional colorful Roma garb, and threw him down at Virgil's feet. He immediately began to scramble upward, but a quick kick from Virgil's boot send him back down, and the man glared up at Virgil defiantly. ?Was it you who dared to attack me upon my own soil?!? The man glared up at him, and spit toward his face. With a loud screech, Virgil's sword was drawn from its scabbard, and leveled at the Roma's chest. However, the fallen man still seemed to have no fear, and screamed something at Virgil. Close enough to the end of his sentence to be punctuation, Virgil turned to Satish, who stammered slightly. ?He said 'only a coward would slay an unarmed man'... and that he 'wishes his arrow had struck your neck'.? ?I cannot let such treachery go unpunished... these are my lands, and have been my family's lands for generations! I will not have Roma filth refusing to respect this!? Before his proclamation could be finished, the man on the ground dove at him, dagger in hand, cutting a fell slash at Virgil's midsection. Virgil feinted to the side, and thrust forward with his sword, the razor-sharp steel piercing him straight through! Women in the camp screamed in chorus, and the men in syncopation shouted furiously at the gathered army. Virgil turned toward them. ?Let your foolish brother's insolence be an example to you all! We will spare you, but will not hesitate to take back what is ours!? His words hung in the air, as the injured man was thrown to the ground inside the camp. The seething fury from the inhabitants could be felt on the backs of every soldier, as they turned to begin the journey homeward. They and Virgil feared no retribution for their injunction, as the forest floor now snapped and crackled loudly under their feet in protest. After all, they could very well have spared none of them. - - - - - - - - - - Thornwood Manor- Third Floor Terrace --?Invincibility lies in the defense; the possibility of victory in the attack.?-- --Sun Tzu, Art of War-- The moon shone brightly through a crystal clear sky, casting a pallid glow over the trees, grass, and the gravel path that led to the entrance of Virgil's most recently acquired residence. Occasionally, a pair of eyes would glint in the dark, before disappearing into the hedges, or thick grass in the meadows. The network of connections he'd formed over his extended life had left him with considerable amounts of disposable income, which he found himself very easily convinced to dispose of. He'd left the plantation house reasonably unchanged, save the interior being selectively renovated and refurnished, and a new coat of paint for the outside, and it had been serving he and his companions rather well throughout GCW's stay in the Southeast. The heavy wooden sun chair, akin to the thick beach chairs sometimes still found, creaked as Virgil leaned back against it, staring up at the sky, his mind lazily identifying constellations. He sighed, and drew a breath, the novelty of which had convinced him to take it up as a hobby of sorts. While it provided him little in the way of actual use, it did tend to make him blend into human society slightly more readily. Truth be told, with the dissolution of the threat of Lilith, there was little that he found himself being concerned with outside of his connection to said human world. Not being involved in international 'business', driving himself to the brink of insanity while searching for his beloved, or other such dramatic pursuits felt indescribably foreign to him, though he didn't exactly find it disagreeable. In fact, he was becoming rather fond of the idea of lacking drama for a few months, maybe, or, at the least, keeping it confined to his current hobby, isolated in its own little petri dish that he could walk away from whenever he pleased. Everyone deserved a break here and there, did they not? ?Concerto for Violoncello in G? began mellowly drifting through the night, and Virgil leaned (rather slowly) toward his appropriately flashy mobile phone, before noting the ring had been assigned to his more important contacts. He flipped the phone open, leaning back and setting his eyes on the night sky again. ?Hello, Rune.? ?God kvell, herr Ambrosi.? The voice coming from the other end of the phone sounded little like the brusque, unshakable pilot that Virgil occasionally employed for legally questionable duties. It sounded as though he'd had another confrontation with his ex-wife, or something of the sort. Virgil's eyebrow raised. He'd spoken to Rune enough times to know that his tone was generally unwavering, cold, and businesslike. At the moment, it was rushed, harried, as though he'd decided to randomly call Virgil after running a few miles. 'To what do I owe the pleasure?' There was a slight pause, the silence heavy, and pregnant with anticipation. 'I need a favor from you... I know you don't owe me anything, but I'm in a sort of bind here.' Virgil's expression remained unperturbed, though his curiosity was piqued. 'I know you're far too skilled a pilot to have problems with the authorities.' Rune scoffed. 'No... police don't check any planes but the obvious. My problem's with a fellow I think you know, Wang Xi Tun. Ring a bell?' That was certainly one of the more potent understatements Virgil had heard, given that nearly every time Virgil and Wang had crossed paths after their contract had been broken, there had nearly been a showdown. 'Why yes... yes, it does. What business would you have with him?' The reply was rather matter-of-fact. 'Well, you haven't needed me for a time, and I have to keep the Jaedr running, and myself too. One of his agents called me, and told me they were desperate for a carrier for some kind of opium they were running. Nothing particularly hard.' Virgil's eyes had been consistently narrowing over the course of the conversation. Somehow, the thought of his pilot working with such filth as Cathayans would have turned his stomach, had he not long ago eliminated the reaction. 'I suppose they've replaced you, then?' He wasn't entirely certain if Rune knew what generally happened to those replaced, but given that he was speaking to Virgil at the moment, he assumed that wasn't the case. 'Not exactly... or I guess not, they haven't told me anything. I've just got Chinese guys taking potshots at me, and I just put two and two together. I kind of assume they're irritated with me.' 'What do you need me to do?' Across the Atlantic and to the north, Rune shook his head stubbornly, though he remembered that Virgil wouldn't be seeing it. 'I don't want you to have to get your hands dirty... I just remember what you used to load my plane up with. Do you have anything I can use to cover my ass until I can get out of Norway?' Virgil's eyes widened slightly, mostly in surprise. 'You're leaving Norway?' 'There's nothing left for me here. Heidrun went through the courts, and somehow, it ended up with me never being able to see Astara again. I have no wife, no daughter, and I'll end up dead if I stay here.' Virgil stood from the chair, pacing the balcony slowly. 'Then what good is it going to do to fight? I can contact someone, and have you out within the hour.' A grunt, of which Virgil guessed was disapproval, came through the other end. 'I'm not leaving the Jaedr here. Come on, just let me have something to keep these assholes off my back until I can leave. I don't care if I don't make it, but if they're coming for me, I want to be ready to give them a good send-off.' The tone of Rune's voice was final, like the short staccato finale of a harpsichord invention. Virgil had little left to interject, as he knew it would do no good, and without his help, Rune would certainly be dead within days. 'Very well... I'll put in the necessary calls now, and someone will get you what you need.' 'Thank you, herr Ambrosi.' Virgil stared out over the foggy landscape, as the humidity began to cool near the ground. His expression was blank, masking his concern. 'That is not a problem... but you cannot be replaced, Rune.' 'Who knows, maybe you won't have to... but I've got to run, I have some repairs to make before I can fly out of here. God natt, herr Ambrosi.' 'Good night, Rune.' As soon as the line dropped out of Virgil's ear, he began rifling through his stored numbers, immediately remembering the proper contact to send Rune's way. However, he wasn't planning on letting his pilot, and, less often admitted, his friend, go to the wolves on his own. The fog began to thicken around the trees, choking out the sight of the estate, but Virgil was currently in no mood to notice much of anything... his calm night seemed a distant memory.
