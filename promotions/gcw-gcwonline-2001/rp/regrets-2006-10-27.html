Regrets

27 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/27/06 “No, for the last time I don’t have anything to say to you people. And tell your bottom feeding, backstabbing, carrion eating friends I don’t have anything to say to them either!” Dawn punctuated the last word by slamming the receiver back into its cradle, emerald eyes narrowed violently at the telephone, as though she were seriously considering the merits of ripping it out of the wall altogether. It had been this way since news of her withdrawal from GTT6, and from PTC competition as a whole, had been made official. Wrestling magazines and online “news” sites had called her at home nearly non-stop, looking for some kind of statement, anything they could quote her on, any kind of bad press’and when she’d fled to the safety of a hotel room near the venue for WorldWide, they’d followed her there too, tipped off by someone in the hotel who was looking to make a quick buck at the expense of her privacy. Turning on her heel she stalked down the hallway of the suite KB: Dies Irae had rented, sock feet hissing into the deep pile carpet with each step. The suite was a veritable beehive of sound, the various members of the suddenly much larger stable scattered throughout the penthouse watching TV, talking amongst themselves or snatching a few minutes of sleep between one task or another. She felt confined, hemmed in, like a wolf trapped in a cage for too long without exercise or air. The penthouse had been her sanctuary from the prying eyes of the wrestling press, a chance to get away from the demanding questions and the constant invasiveness of their interrogation. The feeling had faded, that feeling of safety giving way to an increasing sensation of being trapped, contained within the walls of the penthouse instead of being protected by them. Dawn paused by the doorway to the suite’s living room, her breathing started to come faster, a sense of detached panic rising from her chest towards her throat. She needed to get away for a while, needed to be somewhere by herself and away from the prying phone calls’and the increasingly questioning looks of her stable mates. Trying to seem casual she moved away from the living room towards the sliding glass door to the patio, her pace quickening with each step that drew her nearer. She could practically feel the cool night air on face already, the gusts of wind that were impossible to avoid so high above the city proper. As the sense of claustrophobia rose she yanked the door open and stepped outside, practically slamming it shut behind her and sucking in a deep breath through her nostrils. She was safe out here, not being a large enough media figure to warrant a news helicopter or camera shots from the apartment building across the road. Leaning back against the door she pressed the heels of her palms into her eyes, digging in hard, pushing the pain to clear the fog of panic from her mind. “Get a fuckin” hold of yourself.” She whispered, voice rasping harshly. “It’s just a bunch of no good media hounds, don’t sweat them, don’t let them get under your skin.” It was hollow she knew, a shallow pep talk to try and clear the press from her mind’but the question kept eating at her, dogging her every step. The question that had been asked over the phone, posed as hypothetical discussions on internet smark boards, and reflected in the eyes of friends that were too polite to ask. She had been poised to enter GTT6, her sights set squarely on the newly crowned PTC Global Champion, Seymour Almasy. She had been poised to take vengeance for the woman she loved, to fight Alamasy on the biggest stage of them all with everything he had achieved at risk. And then, with no warning, she had backed out. And the question on everybody’s lips, was “Why?” ****************************************************************************************** September 27th, 10:58pm, McFarlane Estate There was dead silence for a long moment, the only sound coming from the television set across the room. The strobing lights and rapidly changing camera angles caused the light from the TV to flicker oddly, creating an almost ghostly effect, other worldly effect. Much like the other worldly celebration going on in FUSE’s ring, the light seemed too unreal to even consider. The camera zoomed in for a close-up of the new champion, the sweat pouring down his face as he held his prize aloft’and then the entire picture shattered, violently blasted into a hundred pieces as the solid wood stand of a standing lamp pierced through the front of the TV like a javelin, scattering glass all over the living room carpet. With the TV dead, and the lamp the murder weapon, the only light in the living room was moonlight trickling softly in through the window. Her form wreathed in shadow, Dawn’s shoulders heaved up in down as she sucked in great, angry breaths of air. “You’re dead.” She hissed. Skirt swirling around her ankles she stalked from the living room, feet pounding against the hardwood floor into her large kitchen. She snapped on the kitchen light, eyes immediately moving to the island that divided the cooking area of the kitchen from the eating area. A package had lain on the island for the better part of a week, collecting dust and not much else. “Dawn McFarlane, care of PTC.” The package read, with the logo for GTT6 stamped proudly in the upper right corner. Snatching the envelope up she worked her fingernail under the flap, tearing it open and dumping the contents out into the island. Digging through the stack of papers she growled in annoyance, the sound coming from deep in her chest. “Jesus Christ, you’d think I was signing up to fight in a war with all of this legal bullsh’AHA!” Triumphantly she produced the consent sheet, snatching a magnetic pen from the front of the fridge. Bending over the island she hastily sketched her signature here, here and here, adding her initials there, there and there. Grabbing the paper up again she prowled angrily from the kitchen into the main hall, taking the central staircase two steps at a time until she hit the third floor. Knocking the door practically off of its hinges, Dawn stormed into the room that Lia had been using as her office, eyes on the fax machine. Stopping front of it she fed the acceptance form into the machine, punching the PTC number in by memory and then watching in sick satisfaction as the paper slid out of sight. “This time, ? She snarled, “You’ve gone way to far.” ***************************************************************************************** Sighing, Dawn slumped into one of the patio chairs and stared moodily out over the edge of the roof, gusts of wind chasing her black-blonde hair around her head in loose strands. Hugging her arms tight around her torso with her hands under her armpits, she tried in vain to block herself off from the rest of the world by physical means alone. She’d had it, a chance to take the fight to Seymour in his world, to take away everything that was important to him in one match: His title, his chance at winning GTT6 and his chance at ever making their series 2-1 in his favor. It had been in her grasp’and she’d walked away. And now everyone wanted to know why. The wood and plastic of the chair creaked in protest as she leaned forward, elbows on the faded knees of her cargo pants and her face in her hands. Everyone had been asking that question, with their phone calls, with their websites, their news articles and their eyes. Everyone had been asking? “except the one person she would actually answer. She could feel the gulf that had started to widen between herself and Lia once again. It wasn’t spoken, but there was a distance there that was becoming all-too-familiar. She could feel that yawning chasm in the pit of her soul that threatened to pull them apart again, maybe this time for good. “No!” One hand smashed down on the wooden armrest, pain spiking up the base of it and along the bones of her forearm. It felt good, a shot of adrenaline that cut through her moping like a spotlight through fog, searing up her arm and into her brain. “I won’t let that happen.” Dawn launched herself up from the chair, knocking it sprawling to one side in her haste. Slowly, sock feet slapping against the cold concrete of the patio, she walked towards the fenced edge of the roof. The wind picked up as she grew closer, tugging more hair out of her foxtail and whipping it about her face, trying to tug a few strands of the black-gold hair out and carry them away into the night. Heights had never been a fear of hers. Since she was a little girl she had delighted in climbing trees, rocks, cars, houses’anything she could clamor atop of and look out at the rest of the world. Presently she drew herself up to the safety fencing, resting her arms on the top and leaning her body weight against it. “Be just my luck if the safety tests didn’t hold.” She snorted, half chuckling as she looked down at the fencing’and caught sight of movement further down. Just one floor below her a man in pale blue pajama pants moved through and intricate kata, his arms and legs flowing from one movement into the other as he moved slowly from one side of his balcony to the other. Then he crossed back again, moving up to three quarters speed, his limbs starting to blur together in the halogen lighting. She watched him silently for a moment, a slow smile creeping across her face. He’d made two full crosses of the deck before she finally called out to him, cupping one hand to her mouth as she called down. “Tae Kwon Do right” Crane?” He finished his movement before answering, looking up at her and shading his eyes with one hand, an easy smile on his face. “That’s right. I like to start out slow and work up to it, get a better work out that way.” He was broad in the shoulders without being huge, dark hair cropped short atop his head and a trail of fine hair that traveled down his chest and disappeared into his pajama pants. The smile came naturally to his face and added an air of youthful handsomeness to him. “I know what you mean!” She called back, “I prefer to warm up with Yoga first and then start slow and speed things up.” “Yoga” You don’t study the Hatha Yoga by chance do you?” Her grin spread wider, and she cupped both hands to her mouth. “Is there any other kind of Yoga?” Laughing he shook his head, hands on his hips as he looked up. “I suppose that depends on who you ask. Say, you want to meet me in the lounge for a drink?” A pang of uneasiness shot through her and she shook her head slowly from side to side. “I’m already with someone, thanks thought.” “So am I.” He fired back, “My wife is sleeping inside. I’m not asking you out on a date or anything, just a chance to talk as one martial artist to another.” “You’re not press are you?” He clapped one hand to his chest. “That hurts. Do I look like a bottom feeder to you?” “They can be tricky.” She yelled back, smirking. “Fine fine. I give you my word that I’m not with the Press.” Dawn leaned back from the edge, gnawing on her lower lip for a long moment. “I can’t believe I’m doing this’I’ll meet you in ten minutes!” “Great, see you then!” *************************************************************************************** Roughly nine minutes later Dawn had seated herself at the far end of the hotel bar, idly stirring a glass of coke with a straw while she waited. He didn’t keep her waiting long. “Tae Kwon Do right?” Said a familiar voice from behind her. She turned in her stool with a smirk, gesturing to her drink. “I actually call this kata “Girl waiting for a total stranger”.” He laughed, smiling broadly and offered out one hand. “Nathan Cole.” She stood off the stool and took his hand in hers. “Dawn McFarlane.” His hand was warm and calloused, the mountains and crags in his palm paying homage to a life of hard physical work. When he shook he didn’t try to crush or outgrip her, just squeezed it firmly and nodded in satisfaction when she squeezed back. As he released her hand he cocked his head slightly. “McFarlane’that sounds like a name I should know.” Dawn snorted, turning back to her stool and gesturing for him to sit next to her. “Hardly. Unless you make a habit of following wrestling then you’re not likely to have ever heard of me.” Sitting down on the stool next to her he watched her for a moment, like he was putting together some kind of puzzle in his mind. After a moment he slapped the bar with one hand and grinned. “You’re James McFarlane’s kid.” She turned quickly, eyes wide like saucers and her jaw hanging open. Blinking rapidly she recovered some of her dignity and scooped her jaw up off the floor. “You’knew my father?” “Sort of.” He signaled the bartender with one hand, “I did some work for Billy Maclure back when he ran Pacific Rim Wrestling. Never really spoke to your dad at any great length, but I saw him around some times’you look a lot like him. Minus the dye job.” Dawn made a face at the last, but nodded. “Thanks, I appreciate that. What kind of work did you do for PRW?” He grinned and made a gun out of his thumb and forefinger, dropping his thumb like it was a hammer. “I’m a private detective. I could tell you what I was doing for old Billy, but then I’d have to kill you.” Smirking she pulled her drink closer, taking a sip through the straw and shaking her head. “A private eye” What are you doing in Dallas?” “Honeymoon.” He said, practically glowing, “I met the woman of my dreams and she finally agreed to marry me. Dallas isn't exactly Hawaii, but so long as we're together...what does it matter?” The smile slid slowly from Dawn's face as she nodded, taking another sip from her drink. “Yeah...I guess you're right.” “Falstaff please, can not glass.” Then he swiveled in his chair to look at her, “That doesn't sound entirely convinced. Having man trouble?” She couldn't resist the smirk, followed by a sly wink. “Woman trouble.” “A tall glass of water like you” Now that's a damn shame.” He winked in reply, taking his beer from the bartender with a nodded thanks. “What kind of trouble?” She shrugged. “Somebody she trusted took something special and important from her...and betrayed her in about every sense of the word. I had an opportunity to step to, stand up and avenge her in front of everyone, make him look like a fool...and I didn't. Now everyone's getting under my skin about it.” He gave her a measuring look, taking a long pull on his beer. “If anyone did something like that to Lucy, I wouldn't even hesitate. What stopped you?” Shaking her head, Dawn turned back to the bar and hunched over her coke moodily. “You wouldn't believe me if I told you.” “Try me, I've heard stranger I guarantee.” Giving him a measuring look of her own, Dawn nodded slowly. “Okay...I didn't want to give him the satisfaction. Ever since I've known him everything was about him, everything was only worth anything based on what he was getting out of it. Even if I went out there and beat him post to post, embarrassed him in front of millions...he'd have spun it to make himself look like the victim again. He can't do any fucking wrong, oh no, not the great Seymour Almasy. I'm not going to give him the satisfaction of adding another “sensational” match to his record win or lose...if I go after him, it's going to be much more personal.” Nathan was quiet for a little while, thoughtfully sipping his bear and tapping his fingers against the table. “You sound like you're trying to convince someone.” “Yeah.” She snorted. “Me.” He raised an eyebrow. “You don't think you did the right thing?” “I...don't know. I know it's upset her, I can feel it in the way she stands and see it in the way she looks at me...” “Can you make things right?” She sighed. “I'm not sure....no that's not entirely true. I'm going to try my hardest to make it right again...I've got another chance in just a couple of days.” “You've got another chance at him?” “Not exactly. There's this other guy that was trying to crawl all over her a while back, giving her fiance a hard time and always coming onto her...I've got him.” Nodding sagely, Nathan set his empty can down on the counter and nodded. “Sounds to me like you already know what you need to be doing.” “Sort of...I mean, this is like the end of the line you know” Either I take him out and redeem myself...or I might not ever be able to fix things.” Shaking his head, Nathan stood up, stretching out his back. “You make it sound like every thing's already been decided and you can't think like that. Keep in mind she's going to have a say in how all of this goes down too...and if she loves you, I'd bet she'll listen to you.” Dawn snorted. “I've got to talk before she can listen.” “That's the part that you've gotta do. If you can't talk to her...” He shrugged, “She can't do much to help you out. You want my advice” Whup this new guy like he's the guy you really wanted, take out all of the aggression you've got in there...and then go and explain yourself. If you don't...might as well shoot yourself now.” He patted her on the shoulder and walked past, headed for the elevators. She twisted around in her chair to call after him. “Hey, where are you going?” “Back to my wife. You're a great girl Dawn, but I'm on my honey moon and you know what that means.” He turned back around to give her a wink and a thumbs up before disappearing into the elevator. Shaking her head, Dawn paid for her drink and headed for the stairs, preferring the slow burn in her thighs to the lurching hum of the elevator. She jogged up a stair at a time, slowly turning over the things Nathan had said in her mind. Reaching the penthouse floor, her breath coming in pained gasps, she unlocked the door and slipped inside, closing it behind her as quietly as possible. Leaning back against the door she breathed in deeply through her nose, letting it percolate up to her brain. Jonathon Borealis wasn't Seymour Almasy, but as far as Dawn McFarlane was concerned...he was just as bad. She'd blown her chance at Almasy, but maybe, just maybe, she could redeem herself at Worldwide.
