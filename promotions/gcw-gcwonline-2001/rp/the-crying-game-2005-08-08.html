The Crying Game

8 August 2005

Date: 8-8-05 God, not again. What is it about a woman, that innate ability to be so innocent and yet so incredibly cruel, that drives a man to his limit? How can they sway their hips and slash your throat at the same time and do it with such a damnable, almost gleeful delight? When a woman strikes a man they call it self defense, yet when a man strikes a woman its labeled “abuse.” Ironic? the real abuse is how a woman can manipulate that double standard to her favor. Is that how Luna Morbid managed to beat me? Luna Morbid. The woman who destroyed The Renegade in the first round of GTT4. The first woman I ever faced as an enemy across the canvas, and she smeared it with my blood. What was my hesitation then? What’s my hesitation now? Do breasts and a womb really have that much impact on my ability to function successfully between the ropes? Rhetorical questions I have no intention of answering, that’s what it is. Maybe it’s because I already know the answers. There are a lot of women in the world, but they all look like Lacey. I’ve always had the capacity to love. Everyone does, they just grow in love the same way the branches of a tree grow from its foundation - in different directions. But Lacey cut me to my roots, severed my ability to sense the sunlight in my life and move on along a new branch, a new path. For me, I never saw Luna Morbid. I saw only Lacey. And I wanted to kill her. ~<>~<>~<>~ Some private plot of land alongside Interstate 70... Somewhere amidst the incessant twilight conversations of crickets and locusts, Rich “The Renegade” Rollins could almost hear the steady thrum of the power generator he imagined was tucked away next to some tiny cottage, hidden from the world in the middle of the Nebraska forest that surrounded him. His custom-made 1967 Shelby Mustang emitted its own soft vibrations, purring atop a carpet of leaves in the cozy little clearing he had discovered underneath a canopy of lush August foliage. Alone with his thoughts, Rollins dissected the words he had just scrawled on the pages of the little leather-bound journal, leaning his head back against the wheel well of his baby blue behemoth. His mind raced without caution along the superhighway of imagination as a hundred different visualizations of how his actions - both in the ring and otherwise - would affect his immediate future ran laps through his brain. He touched his pencil to the pad again, almost erased the last sentence, but hesitated. And once again he scolded himself for the hesitation. Raw emotion consumed common sense, and he let the passage stand. Pulling himself to his feet, Rollins brushed the dirt and grass off his pants as he tossed the little black book into the open driver’s seat window with a resigned sigh. Somewhere in the distance an owl began hooting in counterpoint to the insect orchestra; the sounds of summer surrounded Rollins, engulfed him within a seductive dance of life without industrialism, without the corrosive tinkering of mankind to break the cycle of nature with its endless string of car horns and construction crews. Standing in the middle of nowhere, absorbing the beauty of an ecosystem so easily overlooked, Rich Rollins felt a kind of satisfaction he hadn’t expected. “Are you gonna stand there and reflect all day, or are we gonna do this?” As soon as the all-too-familiar voice broke into the serene privacy of his personal thoughts, the hairs on the back of Rollins? neck snapped to attention. A murky discomfort settled like a rock in his bowels as his heart began to pound unexpectedly, raging like some muted fear screaming from his subconscious. Clenching his fists into white-knuckled balls, Rich Rollins turned around to face the arrogant smugness that was Dave Olson, a two-time former NCAA champion and fellow survivor of the tragic madhouse that was known as the House of Judgment. The thing was, Dave Olson didn’t exist. He never did, except for in The Renegade’s head. “You aren’t real,” Rollins declared, meeting the hallucination’s mocking sneer with his own steady, quiet anger. “You never were. You-?” “Spare me the sob story and get focused on the here and now,” Olson snapped, bounding off of the vehicle’s hood with an overbearing expansion of his broad shoulders. “You’ve got issues. Who the hell doesn’t” I’m only here because you can’t cope with being yourself? be it Rich Rollins or The Renegade, I don’t give a fuck. The point is, we’ve got work to do my friend.” “There is no we, asshole. And I never said I needed an invisible friend, so please, return to whatever nonexistent void you spawned yourself from before I start getting agitated.” From the veins sticking out of Rollins? neck, it was obvious he was already far beyond that. Olson merely laughed. “Or what” You’ll blink me away? Bash my imaginary head through the windshield? Scold me into oblivion?” His humor instantly vanished, transforming into a barely restrained snarl. “I don’t have the patience to stand here and hold your hand through this shit, so stop whining and start acting like a man. Open the fucking trunk.” Startled, Rollins realized he was holding his keys in the palm of his hand as Dave Olson indicated the Mustang’s trunk. Confusion washed over The Renegade in waves, threatening to drown him completely as he looked from the trunk of his car to the keys, then to the apparition standing between the two. “There are a billion women out there Rich,” Olson crooned sardonically, “and they all look like her? don’t they?” His legs moved as if he were wading through molasses as Rollins staggered to the rear of the idle vehicle. He knew which key to use; it slid into the lock and clicked as he jerked it around, popping the trunk door wide open. There, staring back at The Renegade with mascara-smeared eyes containing pupils the size of saucers, was a woman. Her ankles and wrists were bound tight with plastic garbage ties and the double-thick strip of duct tape wound around her mouth bowled in and out with every hyperventilated breath. Olson was in his ear now, whispering words that froze the very marrow in Rollins? bones: “You can pretend its her. You can pretend she’s anything you want her to be.” And so he did. ~<>~<>~<>~ Earlier, at a gym in Lincoln, Nebraska? “Dammit Donnie, I said spike him, not spank him! If I wanted to see someone flop around like a goddamned fish I’d put my mother in that ring!” Pulling himself vertical, Donnie helped his sparring partner to his feet as he shot his unrelenting task master an exhausted nod, whipping his sweat-soaked hair out of his face and placing the man between his legs for yet another piledriver. Hefting his husky colleague off the canvas, Donnie locked his wrists around his midsection and popped up with a grunt, bringing his opponent down with yet another failed attempt that elicited an exasperated sigh from their master. “I’m sorry children, are we still wiping our asses with both hands” Again!” “Well, you sure didn’t change much,” Rich Rollins heckled from the open frame of a doorway. Folding his arms with a casual air that suggested more than mere confidence, Rollins grinned as his former boss and owner of the Cyber Wrestling Union turned around with a start. “I’ll be damned” Rollins?” Squinting his eyes, Nathan Krotzer rose from his chair at ringside. As Rollins drew away from the doorframe and began walking in his direction, a slow smile spread across the man’s face. “Let me guess, Caldera can’t handle the pressure and sent you to find a real promoter?” The Renegade snorted, extending his hand and enveloping Nathan’s with a wolfish grin. “You wish,” he replied, “From the looks of things, you’ve already got enough on your hands as it is.” The sarcasm wasn’t subtle, and it definitely wasn’t lost on Nathan. Giving the modest training gym a cursory glance, Krotzer offered a miniscule shrug as he gestured around with one arm. “And thus is all that remains of the great Krotzer Dynasty, thanks in no small part to the infinitely deep pockets of Caldera Enterprises. It may not be much, but it’s home.” “Eh, boss” Are we finished?” Donnie called from inside the ring. Nathan spun around without missing a beat. “Finished” Boy, we haven’t even begun. Keep spankin?.” As the two young hopefuls grudgingly went back into their routine, Nathan Krotzer sat himself down at a plain wooden table and motioned for Rollins to do the same. “Can I get you some coffee or something” I think I left I pot going in the office.” “Nah, I’m not much of a fan of yellow teeth,” Rollins said with a smirk, taking his seat. “Then what can I do for you” I’m sure you didn’t drive all the way out here for my health.” A thoughtful consideration nestled itself behind Rollins eyes as he examined the man sitting across from him, the man who had given The Renegade his first big break, the man who had made The Renegade a household name. The man who was in no small way responsible for the existence of GCW, as it was Nathan Krotzer who sold his failing company to Steven Caldera all those years ago. Looking across the table, Rollins began wondering just what exactly he was doing there, himself. “I’m just lookin” for a little advice, I guess,” Rollins finally decided, leaning back in his chair. Krotzer’s eyebrows raised questioningly, urging his former cash cow to continue. So he did. “You never allowed women to wrestle in the CWU, Nathan. Hell, I don’t think we even had a woman on the roster back then, but the principal remains. These days it’s a whole different ball game, and quite frankly, I’m not sure how to handle this situation.” “By “situation”, I assume you’re referring to Eva VanLorne?” Krotzer didn’t miss much, and the surprise on Rollins? face forced a chuckle from the old promoter. “What, you think I don’t know what’s going on” I like to keep tabs on my talent. You never know.” The Renegade nodded once. “Eva’s the first woman to ever win a singles title in GCW. This week I have the chance to take it from her” and Christ, I feel guilty. It almost isn’t fair. If I wanted to, I could have that bitch begging for her pink slip and a green card back to Germany.” “But?”” “She’s a woman.” Rollins stated firmly. “No,” Krotzer replied flatly. “She’s a whore. Slap her around a little and see if she doesn’t like it.” A cold fire lit up behind The Renegade’s icy blues, his tone nothing but stone and steel. “You don’t know her.” “Oh don’t I?” Krotzer scoffed. “I have cable. I watch the damn show. You know what I see every week?” His anger was building, and Rollins couldn’t help but watch it simmer and burn. “I see a gold-grubbing bitch who’ll jump on any gravy train she can lock her lips around just to stay on top of the game” and by “game,” I think we both know what I mean.” Rollins did. “LeStatt.” “Exactly.” Krotzer frowned, but his eyes betrayed his obvious satisfaction. “Don’t be fooled by a pouty face and a curvy figure, Rich. You’re smarter than that.” It was then that everything came together, and something clicked in the dark recesses of The Renegade’s brain. Like a light that had been shrouded by the thick blanket of his own stubborn denial, the curtain dissolved and exposed the personal revelation he had so desperately sought. It wasn’t about the titles. Eva didn’t care if she broke Rollins? spine in her quest for professional acclaim and personal glory?. so why should he? Double standards be damned, this woman was about to discover just how hard the real game was played. “That reminds me,” Nathan said, shooting Rollins a devilish smirk. “You’re still wearing that old red rag, aren’t you” That bandanna of yours has seen better days.” Despite himself, The Renegade couldn’t help but smile. “Yup, she’s still my number one fan,” he quipped. “Thought so.” Excusing himself, Nathan Krotzer began walking over to a row of half-sized lockers against the nearest wall. “The color red is for divas and dickheads, my friend. I think it’s time you retired the old standby.” He popped open a locker and began rummaging through it, pulling out a few pairs of stained boots and some unorganized papers. “Ah, here it is.” His confusion plainly painted across his rocky and unshaven face, Rich Rollins frowned as Krotzer closed the locker and started heading back to the table, a knowing grin touching the corners of his eyes. “I meant to give this to you a long time ago, after you won your second CWU World title. We were gonna turn you heel, remember” Before that whole Iron Man fiasco with Vincent Reed, anyway.” Rollins nodded, his interest piqued. “Red ain’t no color for a true villain. Maybe you can give the old one to Eva” in the meantime, try this on for size.” Krotzer set his tiny bundle down on the tabletop with an exaggerated flourish, obviously pleased with himself as The Renegade began to laugh. There, embroidered with a tiny message on the inside seam, was a jet black bandanna that looked tailored out of fabric much finer than the rags Rollins had worn for years. In small gold letters, the message read simply: For the Iron Man, -Krotzer “Just a reminder of the old days,” Krotzer said with a wink. “And a roundabout way of saying thank you.” Rollins almost laughed. Those were words very seldom directed at him. Before The Renegade could offer his thanks, Nathan Krotzer had already turned his attention back to the two rookies in the ring. ?Fuck, Donnie! Did I tell you to stop? I don’t want to see you kicking back until I see brain matter running down your ass crack, now move!” Rollins would have expressed his gratitude, but he found it was easier to simply slip out the door while his former boss was preoccupied. He was never good at saying good-byes. The way he figured, sometimes it was better to never have to. ~<>~<>~<>~ Back by the highway? ?You can pretend its her. You can pretend she’s anything you want her to be.? Careful not to bang his captive’s head against the trunk door, Rich Rollins grabbed an armful of the woman’s slender figure and slung her over one shoulder, not even bothering to shut the door behind him. She was a prostitute, of that he was certain; the caked-on blue eye shadow and clear stiletto heels were what he noticed first, but the way she had walked told him everything else. She had sauntered up to his car as he was getting ready to leave Lincoln, shaking her ass like it was burning a hole in her pants and The Renegade was the only man in sight with a bucket of water. 'Slap her around a little and see if she doesn’t like it.' Krotzer’s advice. Words he couldn’t erase from memory, not now, no matter how desperately he wanted to. The woman was crying as Rollins set her down on the hood of his car. The not-so-distant sounds of speeding vehicles filtered through the trees above, carried on the wind from the nearby interstate. “She sort of looks like Eva, don’t you think?” Olson offered with a sneer. “Shut up,” Rollins snapped. The woman, under the impression Rollins was addressing her, quickly tried to stifle her tears. Curled up in a fetal ball of complete vulnerability, the mascara-smattered whore looked up at Rollins in despondent hope, like a prayer that she already knew would go unanswered. “I’ll bet she likes it rough,” Olson continued dryly. “Hell, pay her enough and I’m sure she’d-?” “Shut the fuck up!” The Renegade finally snapped, turning on his heels to face his morbid and most unwelcome apparition. “If I wanted to listen to your sadistic bullshit, I’d-?” But no one was there. He was all alone, he and this woman who’s name he never cared to catch. In the back of his mind Rollins swore he could hear Olson’s sarcastic cackle, piercing his thoughts like a white hot spear. Amidst the persuasively lulling mixture of the sounds of mother nature and I-70, it wasn’t until a breathy giggle invaded his momentary frustration that he jerked around in surprise. He almost lost his balance as he looked upon the woman sitting on his car hood, now no longer curled in a self-preserved ball of pity and fear. No; instead of the sobbing prostitute of moments ago, the woman who looked back at him was a face he surely recognized. Eva VanLorne smirked with a coyness that took Rollins completely off guard. “What’s the matter, Herr Renegade?” she purred, her accent almost as seductive as her body language. “I thought they called you the Iron Man, but you’re legs are looking awful wobbly.” She laughed then, and in his mind Rich Rollins could hear Dave Olson jeering right along with her. He fell flat on his ass as she slithered off his car hood, moving with the grace of a dancer as she planted herself right in front of him. “You” no,” he declared, refusing to accept any of it. “This isn’t real, it can’t be real?” “Oh, but I’m just as real as you want me to be.” There was no kindness in that coy smile, and the flirtatious glimmer in her eyes conveyed more bloodlust than passion. Slowly settling herself down on her knees, she began crawling over Rollins like a lioness on the prowl, finally perching herself atop his heaving chest. Leaning in close, Eva began murmuring with a sensual sort of confidence that kept Rollins glued in place, whispering to him and him alone: “Don’t worry sweetie, I only draw blood when I bite.” A wordless cry wrenched itself from Rollins? lungs, hanging in the serene atmosphere like a wailing alarm as Eva VanLorne began to gnaw off the cartilage of his ear. ~<>~<>~<>~ “AH-WHA-WHA-WHA-WHA-W” Jerking awake from the sleepless nightmare, Rich Rollins began panting in dazed confusion as the cheap clock/radio on the nightstand continued to screech. Swatting at the off button, Rollins groaned as he flopped back against the tangled mass of bedding that had twisted around his form, soaked with sweat and the remnants of his morose imagination. Absently he glanced at the clock, not exactly thrilled with what blinked back at him: 5:35am Fuck, was about the only thought that managed to pierce through the grogginess of his nightmare-induced stupor. It wasn’t until he noticed the object sitting next to his alarm clock that a humorless smile spread across his tight lips. It was a black bandanna. For the Iron Man, -Krotzer And just when things were going so well. ~<>~<>~<>~ I had a dream last night. I’m sure I’m not the first man to dream of Eva VanLorne - and I probably won’t be the last - but here’s hoping that it’s a last for me. This woman has my head wrapped so securely around her finger that its amazing I can focus on this writing at all. And she doesn’t even know it. Or then again, maybe she does. The whole world is a crying game, after all. Some cry wolf, some cry foul, and some just cry because they can’t do anything else. Question is, which one am I? We’ll soon find out. ~Fin~
