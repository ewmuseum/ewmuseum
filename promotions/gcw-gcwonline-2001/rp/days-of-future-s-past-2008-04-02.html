Days of Future's Past

2 April 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 2, 2008 Flashes of years past enters Dorian Ryan’s dreams. From the times that he was known as Sam Wolack, as Bishop, as Prime. They say that those that don’t remember the past are doomed to repeat it. For Dorian, he doesn’t have a choice on whether or not he remembers the past. There is no way that he’d be able to forget it. His dreams usually lead him to places that he hasn’t been to in years. The Kawahara Family shrine, the small gymnasiums and judo stadiums in Japan where he really got his education in professional wrestling. The casino Minnesota where he got his true start in the business. They were all parts of his past that have filled his dreams lately. But there is one thing in his dreams that stand out more than any other. That night, before his awakening, where he reached what was thought of as the peak of his career. The night that he won the United States Championship from Eva Van Lorne, the night that he thought that he was on his way to the top of the world. That was the last night that he can remember where he wasn’t looked at like an underdog, and was now looked at as a target. It wasn’t too long after that he suffered the knee injury that caused him to wake up to the world around him. It wasn’t his first injury as a wrestler, but it was the one that took him from being a boy and turning into a man in this business. Dorian wakes up, and he reaches down to his knee. The scar was still there. Complete tear of the tendons that held the patella in place. Six long, hard months away from the ring. Sometimes, it still feels like the stitches are in his knee, causing him to scratch an itch that wasn’t really there. “If you ever get back to where you were as a wrestler, you’ll be lucky.” an old friend had told him as he was rehabbing from the injury. “Surgery like that, I don’t think I ever recovered from it completely.” Dorian was becoming another statistic in the wrestling world. Another young man with shot knees. He was in Tucson rehabbing from the injury when he got the package from FedEx. It was his release from Global Championship Wrestling. They didn’t bother calling, they didn’t bother telling him this face to face. He was hurt, and he was angry. Two months prior, he was their United States Champion. Now, he was tossed aside like an old Wrestling magazine. Dorian worked harder and harder to come back. He wasn’t going to sit idly by as a promoter told him that he wasn’t good enough to wrestle for them. No, he was going to come back fast, and he was going to come back hard. He wasn’t out too long before he found his way to San Jose, to the man that taught him most of what he knew in the wrestling ring. Jason Callion, the man that trained his brother, the man that trained him. At this time, Jason wasn’t doing so much of the training at his school, as it was mainly his two sons that worked the students out. They attempted a wrestling career, but it didn’t go very far. They learned that they would be more effective in the wrestling world doing what their father did. They found that it was their calling as well. Everyday, from the crack of dawn to the edge of night, Dorian worked. First he went slowly over the concerns that his knee might not have been ready. Those concerns didn’t stop him. He went harder, farther, faster. Even with the Callions asking Dorian to slow down, he was undeterred. He took to the skies, he added power, he added precision. It was only a matter of time for Dorian before he made his return to the wrestling ring. His goal wasn’t to return to some independent that could barely afford to put on shows in a Butte, Montana high school gym, but rather back to the company that chewed him up and spit him out. Dorian set his sights on GCW. On the eve of what would turn out to be his return to GCW, he found himself in Japan. He was at Keiko’s grave, asking for strength. He didn’t have a lot of time, as he was going to take a long flight to make it to the arena in time. When he knelt down, he brushed a little debris from her grave marker, and he didn’t see the past, he saw the future. The future wasn’t the name Sam Wolack, which he had gone by during his entire wrestling career, but Dorian Ryan, his birth name. The days before Pay Per Views are always the hardest for Dorian. He is usually trying to loosen up before the beating that he was going to take if he was wrestling. But usually, he was doing it at home. Having a tour of Japan for the last month doesn’t make things easier for him. At least he has a place to stay that’s almost like home. His old in-laws still take care of the man that they look at as more than a gaijin that stole their daughter’s heart and then took her away from them. They look at him truly as a son. Just because Keiko doesn’t walk the earth anymore doesn’t mean that they are going to treat him as anything less than being a son. The trips from Osaka to Tokyo have always been murder for Dorian. He doesn’t look forward to it this time either. His third major event since returning to GCW back in November. This time, he was going for the only title that he ever held. He was looking to become a two-time United States Champion, dethroning his current rival, and former friend, Tessa Windsor. There are many in the industry that doesn’t envy the position that Dorian was in. He asked for this match, wouldn’t give up until he got it. He doesn’t care that the title was on the line, the United States Championship didn’t mean as much to him as it used to. Simply put, all that he cares about was that he comes out and has a good match. All he cares about is putting up the good fight and proving once again that he belongs in the squared circle. He gave up a well paying job to come onto this tour. He gave up on a stable life at home to come back to Japan and wrestle. For Dorian Ryan, there is no going back. His life has changed. He knows that if he doesn’t give his best at Rampage, that he made the wrong choice. He knows that if he doesn’t give the fans their money’s worth when he steps into that ring, that he had made the biggest mistake of his life in the sacrifices that he made when he returned. His match with Tessa Windsor isn’t about the title. It never was. The match that he’s about to have with Tessa Windsor is about his life. That it depends on a good showing. Dorian Ryan can’t afford to go back to where he was just over a year ago when he was wrestling in bingo halls in Minneapolis. He’s bankrupted his future to regain a glimmer of the glory of his past. When he returns to Minnesota after Rampage, he’s going to have to look Andrea in the eye and tell her either that he’s going to retire and that they are going to have to rebuild their lives again, or that he’s going to be putting in a hundred and ten percent into being what he always grew up wanting to be. Either way, he hopes that she can forgive him. Either way, he hopes that the passion to be in the ring is enough to fuel the passion that the two of them once had. A passion that was seemingly destroyed in that fire, seemingly ruined forever. But now a new fire has been lit under Dorian, and he knows that the fire that is burning is going to fuel for him a new passion for wrestling, a new passion for life. This is something that he hopes that he can feed to Andrea as well. Dorian has failures as a wrestler and as a husband. He knows that, but he’s still not going to allow those failures to ruin what he is gunning for. He doesn’t feel sorry for what he’s prepared to do to Tessa. He hasn’t felt sorry for her in months. Instead, Dorian is only looking towards the future. Dorian is only looking to the one day that he feels that his life is complete again. He remembers the past, not because he wants to, but because he has to. Dorian Ryan knows that no matter what, he’s not going to be doomed to repeat it. He will work, and he will fight.
