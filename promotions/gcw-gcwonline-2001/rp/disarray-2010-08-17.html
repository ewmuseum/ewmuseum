Disarray

17 August 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/17/10 The bump of the train crossing over a bridge knocks my tonic water and ice to the ground, splashing the carpet all about. I boarded yesterday and just headed east, with nowhere particular in mind. I grabbed some clothes and threw them in a duffel bag without folding them 'cause I don't know how and left behind my phone and a note for Alexis telling her I'd meet her in Arizona in a few days. I doubt she'll be happy about it, but I need to clear my head. For the past two days, she's been going on and on about this double-title thing. But every time I hear her on the phone, gabbing away to her fucko friends, I hear the same thing over and over again. Her name. It's always, "Oh my god, you won't believe that Tri is gonna face off against..." Her name. "I swear, there's no way he can beat..." Her name. "My hero's gonna be a dual champion! How awesome is..." Her name. Me and Lexi haven't been on the best of terms since coming back from Japan. Things seem fine on the surface, but there's been lingering animosity. She keeps tabs on me like I was a puppy she fished out of the trash. I have to go shopping with her. She poured every bottle of booze she could get her hands on down the drain. Even my 1787 Chateau Lafitteis. I was saving that for our one-year anniversary. I wonder how she even found the damned thing. We argue more. We never really used to argue. At least, not like this. And really, I can only listen to complaining and shit for so long. It happened, it's done with, get over it. Still, it's been bugging me. Making me think on things. Think on us. Everyone has rough patches, I guess. I have no idea where I'm going. I don't even care anymore. ****** The train stops in Kincaid, Kansas. Why anyone would put a stop in this place and actually get off the train is beyond me. But they did, and I do. The town is like a forgotten Norman Rockwell painting at a grandmother's garage sale. I doubt more than five-hundred know the place exists. A freckle on the face of America. I take a stroll through the center of town, and there's nothing. Everyone looks at me funny, but I'm used to that by now. I've seen all of five cars, one of them a police cruiser that slows to a crawl alongside me before driving off to God-knows-where. I'm not sure what my plan was, but I know one thing. I sure am hungry. A half a block and I stumble upon a nondescript diner, and figure I might as well press my luck. I press my way through the door of the greasy spoon, and a small bell rings overhead. The place is desolate outside of an old man that has a coffee cup in front of him and his head huddled within his arms on the countertop. He looks dead. He probably is. From the back of the kitchen, she appears. She can't be more than her mid-twenties, and her blue eyes sparkle. She's a local girl, through and through, and pretty enough, and I wonder where she went wrong to end up stuck in a shithole like this. I spot the name on her tag: Sarah Jones. Why the full name? Why not just the first? "Take a seat wherever, babe," she says with a funky little drawl, breaking me from the thought, and I find a seat near a window that peers out into the town square. I gaze over at the slumped-over old man and wonder if I should check to see if he's alive, but I don't know if these folks skin people alive and dance in their hides under the moonlight. I doubt it highly, but caution is my best bet. The buxom blond brings over a menu and a glass of water. She's polite. A bit too polite. I don't like people that are overtly nice. They usually want something. But she leaves both and walks off with a smile, and I look it over. She comes back after a spell, order pad in hand. "What're you havin', stranger?" "The pancake stack sounds fine," I say. She scribbles on the pad. "With the blueberries or strawberries?" "Both," I reply. Her smile grows. "Whipped cream, too?" I think on it for a second. Only a second. "Why the hell not?" "Thank ya, hon," she declares, and she strolls back into the kitchen. I can see her staring at me the whole time out of the corner of my eye as she files her nails when the hirsute and sweat-stained cook slams on the bell with his meaty hooks. He blurts something out unintelligible, maybe Russian or Ukrainian, and she grabs the plate, swings by my table, and offers an "Enjoy..." before she's off. I scarf down the food, and in the back of my mind hope I'm minding my manners, but at the same time I could give a good goddamn. I'm hungry. When I finish, I slide the plate over, and I look up as she slides into the chair across from me. She cases me like a group of mobsters outside a bank. "So what brings a stranger like you about these parts?" "Just passing through," I say. "Figures," she mutters. Her voice gets high for a second. "Only two types of folk end up here. Folk makin' the mistake of passin' through, and folks that were too damn stupid to get out when they had the chance." "Sorry." She extends her hand. "I'm..." "Sarah Jones," I interrupt. Her head snaps back a bit, before I point to her name-tag and she turns a shade of red. "Damn laminatin' fool." She chuckles, shaking her head about. "That's what happens when you hire someone never made it outta eighth grade." She looks right back at me. "You got a name yourself?" I think about using a fake name. I don't. "Triumph," I say, and as I finish, she howls out with glee. "You're pullin' my chain!" She tries to straighten herself back up. She does a poor job of it. "Ain't no way that's your name." I shrug. "Afraid so." "Well that certainly beats all!" She slaps on the table. There's a slight break, as I check out the joint. "This place is all yours?" She nods. "Going on almost two years now. Was born and raised here." She flips her hair back. "Might as well work here, too." I spot the Ivan in the back, who's now talking to himself. "And him?" "Who, Alexei?" She smirks. "He's been here as long as I can remember." I find myself drawn to her, as she goes all dreamy. "I often imagine he's some ex-KGB gone rogue, hiding out here from his handlers." I'm incredulous and she sees it. She chuckles. "It passes the time, you know. Better than the alternative." After some silence and placating small talk, I take advantage of a lull and fish into my pocket for some cash, and drop it on the table. "It was fun." She nods, and I slide out of the booth and make my way to the door. She hollers from her seat. "Will I see you around, then?" I turn my head slightly. "Suppose so." And the bell rings behind me. ****** "Do you have a room?" The old man in front of me must be pushing ninety by now. His face is pock-marked, his skin is dotted with liver spots and he smells of sulfur. His John Deere trucker hat covers the bulk of his forehead and eyebrows. "Tomb? You talkin' 'bout McMaynerberry Tomb?" He raises his shaking hand and points to nowhere I care to look. "That's over yonder." I shake my head. I raise my voice higher. "No, damnit! I said I want a room!" "Loud boom, you say? But the mill blew up some forty-five years ago!" He 'tsks' about. "Damn shame, it were." I try my best not to lose my patience, but Mr. Burns here is pushing his luck. I swill up the courage to ask one more time when I hear the honk of a car horn. I swing around, and there's Sarah in a beat-all-to-hell red pickup truck befitting of Jed Clampett. She's happy as all get-out. "Hey there, handsome. Needin' a place to stay?" "I'm good," I tell her. "Nonsense," she hollers in that charming little accent of hers. She waves me over. "I know just the place." I turn back to the man, who's already dozing off, and figure I'm not getting anywhere, and say 'what the hell'. I toss my bag in the back of her truck and we drive off down Main street. ****** The truck wheezes up a dirt road to a secluded two-story house, surrounded by evergreen and sparkling lake water. She traipses up the steps and opens the screen door and invites me in. I move slowly. I've never experienced anything like this. Birds fluttering wings and chirping about along a sky so orange that it's almost on fire. No horns, no screams, no cries from the city. Not even a ghetto bird for miles. It's so...simple. I follow the same steps and grip the handle of the screen door and it hisses as it slams behind me. Inside, it smells of fresh apple pie mixed with summer heat. "You can put your stuff wherever," she hollers from another room, and I place it on a bench in the entryway. I stand by the front door. I'm not sure what the hell I'm doing, when she pokes her head in from the living room doorway. "C'mon now, hop to it!" I follow where her head was into the living room, and I see her standing there, hands on hips, as if she's been waiting for me this whole time. She laughs. "You're an odd fellow, you know that?" She crosses the room and plops down on a flower-pattern couch, and slaps the cushion twice. I eye her cautiously, and she sighs. "You sure are shy." I give in, and sit next to her. She keeps staring at me, when her eyes light up like a pinball machine. She jumps off the couch, and walks towards me. "I'll be back in a shake," she whispers into my ear before she leaves the room, sashaying her hips as if she knows I'll be watching. I am. From the couch, I take in the ambiance. The place is quaint, yet I can't help feel like I'm trapped in some family-style sitcom. Two bookshelves filled with unread novels and outdated dictionaries. Banners and memorabilia of a local college plastered about the walls. A television probably made in the USSR. Family photos on the mantle flanked by porcelain trinkets and clown dolls. The everyday ordinary slice of Americana I never got to partake in. When she walks back into the room, her right hand holds two glasses and her left grips around the neck of the Old Number 7. "I'm underage," I say. She pours anyway. She saunters over like a vixen and hands me my glass and slides up next to me on the couch. I take a sip and it's like a snake biting into my throat. She rests her elbow on the couch and her head on her fist, as she stares, longingly. "You're running from something," she says. "We're all running from something," I reply. She goes coy. "Maybe." She purses her lips. "But I'm not the one on the lam." I don't say anything. Neither does she. "Awk-ward si-lence," she finally utters with a laugh, and she rises up and walks over to an old record player. She removes a vinyl from its sleeve and puts it on, and before long, the angelic voice of Nina Simone fills the void, complete with the cracks and pops that the medium provides. She plops back down on the sofa, and I smile. "My old man liked jazz," I say. She takes a sip of the Jack. "Nothing better." She holds up the glass, and we share a toast of sorts, and choke down the drink. She swirls around her glass. "You and him still close?" I pause for dramatic effect. "He's gone." "I'm...so sorry." She reflects for a moment, as if wishing she could go back twenty seconds in time. I take a sip. "Before my time." She remains somber. "I couldn't imagine..." I cut her off. "You learn to manage." She nods her head, and the mood eases just a little. Her eyes wander, and fixate on a KU banner. "My dad, there was nothing he loved more than Kansas basketball." She takes a sip. "Said Paul Pierce was the best player he ever saw play." "One of the greatest moments of his life, he used to say, was seeing Pierce win the NBA Finals MVP along with his championship." She chuckles. "He used to joke the day I was born came a very close second." "And then, he was gone." She chokes up, if only for a moment. "Mom went a month later, and that was that." She reflects again, as she takes another sip. "You just gotta move on, you know?" I nod. "I can relate." She smiles. "I guess you're a bit normal after all." "Normalcy, like most things in life..." I swallow down the drink. "Is overrated." "Oh?" She inches closer, her interest piqued. "And what's underrated?" I smirk. "Blueberry cheesecake. The film 'The Wackness'. Starin' up at the stars on a warm summer night." She looks content. It's pretty on her. "You're cute," she purrs. "That's just the drink talking," I say. Her eyes become near slivers. Her grin grows wider. She pounces, knocking the glass out of my hand. We kiss. It's wet, and passionate, and primal. It's wrong. So I push her off. She's confused. So am I. "I'm sorry," I finally spit out. "It's just..." And then I stop. I can see disappointment in her eyes. I feel like a rotten bastard. "I should go." I rise up She grabs my arm. Her grip is like a vice. "No. Please." She smiles again. "Stay." ****** We spend all hours of the night talking about everything under the sun. She tells me things she's never told anyone. I tell her the same. Things I've been holding in for years. The things of my harshest nightmares. Things not even Lexi knows about me. It's liberating. We talk some more and drink a little bit more and I don't even question why I'm opening up like I am. Maybe it's because she makes me feel comfortable. Maybe it's because I'll probably never see her again after tonight. I don't dwell on it any further, and just enjoy it. It gets late, and she says she's going to bed. I tell her the couch is fine for me. She drags me by the hand upstairs instead. She leads me into her bedroom. It's decorated like she was still ten years old. I don't mind it at all. She lays on her bed. I crawl in behind her. I clutch around her waist. She holds my hands and brings them in tight. I close my eyes, and almost immediately fall asleep. It's the best I've had in a long time. And I don't dream a single dream. In the morning I leave a note on the dresser and catch the train back west. ****** As we flow deeper past the snow-capped Rocky mountains, I try my best not to think on things. I fire up the Zune, put the headphones in, and listen to a mix Lexi made for me. She's always doing that, putting all kinds of random shit together she likes. It starts off with Sigur Ros, of whom I really don't care for, but it fits the mood right now. More follows of shit I do like, like Fat Jon, DJ Shadow, Madlib, and Joanna Newsom. She even tossed on some demo she got from some cats in Chicago. It's alright, I dig it, but I dunno why people send me things like that. As it winds down, the music bleeds into a song of Radiohead's called "Treefingers", and I peer out at the majestic natural rock formations blanketed in white, and I think of leaving again. Of skipping out on everything, just walking out on it all. Maybe I just go. Not return this time. Through Colorado, trekking through the cold virgin snow beneath my feet. Just keep going, up through Wyoming and Montana. To Alberta. To Saskatchewan. Up through the Northwest territories, the Yukon, Alaska. Never look back. Leave it all behind. Then the song passes, and the thought goes with it. ****** I enjoy the music just a little bit longer when I notice some guy I've never seen before sitting right across from me. He's in an all-too-nice black suit and tie and he smiles, and I nod my head and look back out the window. Out of the corner of my eye, though, I see he hasn't stopped staring at me. I turn my head back and pull out the headphones. "I hope I am not interrupting," he says. The guy oozes a confidence that makes me uneasy. And then he says my name with the grin to end all grins, and I lower the hood on my sweatshirt. I don't know him, but I recognize the voice. "You're the prick that sicced your little fucktoy on me." He nods. "We have yet to be formally introduced." He extends his gloved hand. "I am Christian Zenith." I don't return the favor. He brings his hand back, using it to tighten his tie. "I hope the unpleasantness in San Diego caused little inconvenience to you." I don't like this fucker in the bit. I don't feel like playing his games. I nearly bark my words. "How the fuck did you even find me?" He presses his fingers together. "It was anything but easy," he chuckles. "One could say I needed to stalk you to find you." "If you want to fuck me," I tell him, with a smile. "I can save you both the time and awkwardness." I irk him for a split-second, something I only notice because I've been reading this prick the second he sat his overpriced ass across from me. He acts like nothing's wrong. The fucker's good. I'll give him that. "So let's cut the circle jerk short, and how about you get on with what you're gettin' on about," I say without breaking stride. He doesn't break his, either. "I might wonder aloud what you and myself are doing on a train having this very conversation." He pauses, leaning in slightly. "Only for me to ask you why you seem to be..." Another pause, as he leans in even further. "Preoccupied at the moment." I blink twice and rub the undercarriage of my nose. "None of your goddamned business." He leans back, and extends his gloved right hand out, staring at the back of it casually like some teenage girl. "You've been thinking about her, haven't you? Moreso than anyone else." This guy's pissing me off. "Don't act like you know me, you uptight fuck." He grins that damn grin. Without even a sliver of disdain. What is this guy's deal? He stares out the window. "She has an intoxicating quality, you know. Almost like a drug. An addiction you simply cannot let go of." He freezes, trapped in a thought for just a moment. His eyes flutter. "This...obsession of ours..." Her name. "...It scratches away at our very soul. It leaves us barren and unfulfilled. It is a wasteland of sorts, and once you become entrenched, it is very, very difficult to dig your way out." Silence hangs in the air for what feels like an eternity. It's...uncomfortable. I want this over with. "I haven't the slightest idea what you're talking about." His eyes dart back to me, and he laughs. "This game that we all play," he says, "it never ends well." He rises from his seat. He extends his gloved hand once again. My expression tells him all he needs to know, and he lowers it just as fast. "Best of luck, Mr. Frost," he says with a slight nod. And like that, he's gone. I raise the hood back over my head, put the headphones back in, and try to forget any of that ever happened. ****** I'm alone again. I want a drink, and every urge has me finding an attendant and threatening physical violence until he brings a nice casket-aged scotch for me to devour. But I refrain. As much as it pains me, I refrain. It's not the right time. Not yet. All of these things I hate, cluttered in my mind, refusing to go away. I can't focus on anything but being lesser than...her. Is she really better than me? She's a fucking skirt. How can that even enter into my mind? But it does, and it won't go away. Shit, I can't even look Lexi in the eye, knowing she probably doesn't even respect me anymore. I don't want to be a champion. I don't want to be a competitor. I feel like a fucking joke. I just want to escape. Be left alone, forgotten. I won't even wonder what could have been. All this traveling, all this commitment, all this responsibility, I can do without it all. I can't begin to believe that this might end up being my legacy. Is this what I'm going to leave behind? When all is said and done, will I be known for all of this, and only this? I feel lost. I don't belong. None of this is really me. I walk out into the world, and I'm alone. Like I was never fit for this place to begin with. I breathe in deep, and release. I wonder if this is it. I wonder if this is how the world will see me. I wonder if this is how it was meant to be. The train heads into a tunnel. Everything goes black. I wonder. --- (Christian Zenith used with Billy's permission.)
