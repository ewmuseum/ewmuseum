Crescent Hill - I

5 February 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/5/08 The moon reflects off the pond in an eerie way, as fog blankets the moist soil near the shore. The trees sway in the night’s breeze. Twigs snap from their leaders and leaves fall with their petiole while slowly drifting through the air until landing upon the green floor below. The shadows move about, dismembering themselves from original objects to create deformed figures that stretch across the never-ending landscape of Crescent Hill’s Cemetery. A billow of smoke escapes from the man’s half-opened mouth. As he puts the lighter back into his coat pocket, he takes a rather large drag and continues his tour of the cemetery. The moon highlights the scenery, rows upon rows of headstones to match the tall, thick trees above head, placed perfectly into rows as a marked perimeter of the land. A gate was not necessary for many dared not to enter after sunset, for rumors set a terrible legend into stone, dating back to the mid eighteen-hundreds. “Well, are you coming?” The blistering air howls past the words. Two men stand apart, one already within the Cemetery as the other stands with a bit of fear pulling him away from reality. “You know what they say about Crescent Hill,” the distant man responds. He holds a flask in his hand while the other pulls the cigarette from his chapped lips. “Don’t give me that bullshit, Mark. You know as well as I do that those are just Urban Legends.” “No.” A single shake of his head emphasizes the comment. Mark unscrews the cap and tips the flask back. He allows the cold whiskey to run down his throat, and in return it leaves a distinct burning sensation but slowly fades into a bubbly, warm feeling, residing in his chest and stomach. He wipes the wetness from his lips and speaks, “Why’d you even bring me here?” “I know you lost your Mother early on in life,” the man says, taking another hit of his cigarette. Mark slips the flask back into the pocket of his brand new leather jacket. It suits him, but the wind proves to be of a great force, piercing through the leather and blistering his arms and body with flurries of chills. “What’s it to you, Scott,” he asked. The question caused a bit of confusion to run across Scott’s face, as he neared Mark with a few long strides. He stood a good arms length away from Mark. “I know how it feels to lose a mom, too. You’re not the only one who’s been lost at night, in dreams, wondering why it had to happen and even if she was still watching down on me. Sometimes it sure didn’t feel like she was watching but I take the time now to visit her, it helps.” Mark reaches into his pocket. His teeth chatter as he rummages around, the sound of keys jingle as he pulls out the silver platted flask. He unscrews the cap and takes a long swig; a breath of relief escapes his mouth as he consumes the whiskey. He extends his arm, handing Scott the flask which certainly is a signal that he just might be on the same page with this Scott character. Thoughts still plowed through Marks head. Scott was dating Marks ex-wife and something just wasn’t sitting well with Mark. Daniel of course informed Mark of events that possibly were occurring, but it seemed at this point in time the subject wasn’t ready to come up, not just yet. “Damn, this is some strong stuff, Mark.” Mark takes the flask back and tips it back again. “Are you a drinker,” Scott asks while watching Mark down the rest of what’s left. He puts it back into his pocket and looks up at Scott, “Occasionally, times like these.” They both smile. Mark takes the lead, walking up the slick hill and when he reaches the top he stands by a tall Sweetgum tree. “Just a second ago I was pissing my pants about this place and now I’m up here and you’re still standing there, white as a ghost.” “I’m getting another cig, hold up.” Mark waits impatiently, a look of uneasiness still residing upon his face, although he wouldn’t admit to that feeling now. At least not after commenting Scott the way he had. He holds his wrist, looking down at the watch strapped tightly to it. He takes his thumb and rubs it clear across the glass, smearing away any residue that caused an unclear visual of the time. The spark of light almost makes mark jump, as he can clearly see it in the night’s atmosphere, watching closely as Scott puts the cigarette to the flame, lighting it with ease and letting out a heap of smoke in doing so. “Damn, it’s already past midnight.” Scott sprints up the hill with his cigarette hanging loosely from his lips. He takes it from his mouth and bends over for a moment in an attempt to regain some air. “Anyways, I wanted to show you my mother’s grave.” “Why.” It was a harsh question. A question that Scott could easily answer but he instead just looks down at the ground, takes another hit from his cigarette and then flicks it off into the wind. They both watch as the orange ember explodes on impact and then it goes silent again.
