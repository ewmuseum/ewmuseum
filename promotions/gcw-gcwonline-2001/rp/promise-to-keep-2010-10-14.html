Promise to Keep

14 October 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/14/10 The Past He remembered the first time he took the elevator to the upper deck of GCW’s Houston office. He was young then, a firecracker rising the ranks faster than most were accustomed to, but he was humble too. Jamison understood what it meant to be summoned to the upper deck and naturally, he was honored to receive the phone call. As it stood, the only interaction that he and Steven Caldera had made was during the signing of his contract. Few words were spoken beyond notes on Talley’s in-ring footage and typical welcoming words. Still, Daniel Franco was at his side signing on the line next to him ensuring that the character known as Jay Terror would abide by the guidelines listed. One detail that Jay would always remember would be when he lit his first cigarette in the office. There was an awkward silence, a stare accompanied it, and a final word of advice was given from the owner himself. “Quit,” he said. “Quit while you still can. I’d hate to see you not make it anywhere because of a black lung.” As with other advice, Terror brushed it aside and finished the cigarette on his walk back down the hall. That memory rested in New York though, hundreds of miles away from the Texas office. Like the saying, everything was bigger in Texas considering the square footage the Houston office housed. Talley tried not to think of the size inside the elevator though. In his capsule, there was no big and small. There was enough room for him and seven others, none of which were with him on the climb to the top floor. As the numbers ticked past, he could only guess what was ahead of him. He hoped it was a world title shot. There was no way of knowing what the boss had in mind though. From the phone call, all he could gather was that he had a mandatory meeting about GCW business and he wasn’t meant to be late. As it stood, he was a full fifteen minutes early. The elevator stopped on the top and the door opened to show a large waiting room overlooking the Houston skyline. Windows pushed the sun into every corner illuminating the entire floor with a brilliant display. Along the wall stood a line of desks, each with a different person sitting behind them. Stepping out of his stronghold, Terror shoved forward and found a waving hand. It belonged to a woman and he took a seat across from her. A name tag on her chest labelled her as Shannon. Shannon wore a bright pink blouse with a grey dress coat over top. She had glasses and a charming smile much as he imagined she should. “Jay, right?” He fumbled for a second, “Yeah. I’m Jay.” “Good,” she replied. “Mr. Caldera will be happy that you’ve made it. If you’d like to have a seat over there then we’ll bring you back in a few minutes.” Her finger led him to a square of chairs across the way and Jay nodded and followed her guide. On the table were different editions of GCW magazine and Terror took the liberty of opening the most recent one. LeStatt Knight was on the cover with the rest of the Establishment spreading out behind him. In the corner there was a caption asking whether it was the greatest iteration of the group to date. He paged through the magazine looking for mentions of his name. Before long, he found an article on the television title and the names that had earned it during their GCW tenure. In the table at the bottom was his own name with an asterisk beside it denoting that he was the longest reigning champion. Had it not been for his push to the upper division, he believed that the title would likely still be his. Nonetheless, he continued to turn the pages with a sense of achievement in his eyes. There were articles about the big matches to come, the touring schedule, and of course there was the one about The Establishment. He didn’t have the time to read the article though. Shannon was on her feet and whisked Jay past the desk to a hidden hall. It had two corners until finally, the one door in the hall, appeared. The nameplate was gold and looked much like one that the wrestlers had backstage at events. Steven Caldera was behind the door and finally, Jay Terror felt a chill run down his spine. Shannon opened the door, smiled to her boss, and waited for Jay to enter. He took small steps to pass the threshold but finally stood face to face with the boss. Caldera greeted Jay with a smile and asked him to take a seat. Jay did as he was told and heard the door shut behind them. There was no turning back when sitting across form your future. All Jay could do was lean back and listen intently. The Present --- His cough was a hack, a wheeze, and he stifled it with the underside of a bar napkin. He chased his body’s reaction with the one thing that caused it in the first place -- the end of his cigarette. His lips tightened around the filter and pulled the life from the Marlboro stick until the flame burned the face of the filter. Terror smashed the cigarette deep into an overflowing ashtray and lit another. The first drag forced another cough, a longer and harder one than before, and this time he let it freely escape into the air and shower onto the neighboring bar patrons. Some shook it off while others took offense to the smoking fiend. Terror simply hid it under a blanket of Jack Daniels, placing his elbows high on the bar to lower himself. “You sound sick,” said an old man beside him. Jay didn’t bother to look at the man. He was beyond formalities as champion and figured he could avoid a health speech if he was careful enough. “Nah, just the cold. It’ll pass.” “Yeah?” The man had a voice that seemed like it had been around the world a few times. His tone was paced, all-knowing, yet Jay continued to focus his attention on his cigarette. “How long you been smoking those things?” “Years,” he huffed. The short answer wasn’t enough to fend him off. “At the rate you’re smoking them tonight?” “Three packs. Sure.” He laughed a second and finally saw Jay turn his attention. His cough nor smoking was no laughing matter, yet the old man didn’t mind enjoying it. He was well-wrinkled, certainly over the age where bars were fun, and sported a pair of heavy-rimmed glasses. He wore a flannel-patterned shirt along with a pair of old jeans, the ratty kind a carpenter wore in his down-time. “What’s funny about that?” “It’s funny,” he started. “Because you remind me a lot of myself.” Jay took another swig of whiskey to brace himself for the speech. “Go on.” “What do you do to make a living, kid?” “Wrestling. I’m a professional wrestler.” The man shrugged and seemed to think on it for a minute. Jay could see the speech growing with each scrap of kindling he tossed his way. “You know, I was in Mexico once for a business trip. See, I’m one of those heating and ventilation guys and it just so happens that someone down there was having some terrible draft issues. To be fair, most of them have that problem down there anyways. Still, I was asked if I could help this big guy. He found me on the street and someone must have let it slip that I did that sort of thing.” Jay inhaled his cigarette and muffled another cough in the same napkin. The old man just smiled. “You alright?” “What’s this Mexico story have to do with anything?” “Right,” he remembered. “So I get into his place and start to look around at the possible reasons for the draft problem. His doors are awful, the windows are old, and just about everything typical you’d expect is wrong with this guy’s home. As I sat there with my notepad tallying up some totals, I wonder whether or not he could even afford some of the work I have planned for him. Then, that’s when I look at his mantle. Sitting in the center is one of those wrestling masks they rant and rave about down there. It’s a gold and white thing and it’s obviously seen better days. That’s when I started looking harder at the decorations. There’s pictures of the guy in his mask and he’s shaking hands with the who’s who of Mexico. He has pictures of him flying off those ropes and even pictures of him with other masked guys. That’s when I realized that this man must have the money for a house with a better draft.” The old man stopped and Jay flicked his eyebrows. “That’s it?” “Well you’re a wrestler and I installed a better ventilation system in a wrestler’s home once.” “It’s not what I was expecting.” The old man took a sip from his gin and tonic and crossed his arms. “Sometimes, you don’t get what you thought, man. Sometimes you just take what you’re given and roll with it for a while.” “That was an awful story though. It had no point.” “I thought it fit well considering the circumstances. You ever been to Mexico to see them wrestle?” Jay smoked another ring off his cigarette. “Only wrestled there. Never actually watched them do their thing.” The old man nodded kindly, “Well someday, I expect you to get out there and check it out. It’s worth watching if you’re into that sort of thing.” His eyes fell to the cigarette box on the counter and stayed there for a long second. “You want one,” Jay asked. “Me? No,” he replied, looking back at Jay. “No, I gave up smoking a few years ago. I figure I was on the same streak as you with three packs a day and ended up calling it quits.” “That why we’re alike?” “I have that cough too.” The Past --- “It’s been a while since we last met up, Jay. I guess it was when we signed that contract even,” Steven Caldera said as he tapped a pen on the desk. Jay digested the words and actually thought of how to reply. In front of the boss, he lost his hard exterior and found a weaker, more personable figure. “Yeah, I guess so. I guess that means I’m doing pretty well, huh?” Caldera smirked. “You won that television title and held onto it for some time. I know Jayce Bradley was a tough draw for a first match, but good work beating Thunder for the belt. You seem to have that ‘it’ factor that we look for here at GCW, and I’m glad to see it. On top of that, you’re humble and understand that business is business. I didn’t know if you’d be on board with dropping the title to Tyson DeBough, but you proved to me that you were mature enough to move up the ladder. I guess I’m trying to say that I’m impressed with the hard work you’ve been putting in.” With the asterisk from the magazine article still fresh on his mind, Jay’s confidence level increased exponentially. He wasn’t aware of Steven Caldera’s good side, but he knew that he wanted it to continue. “Thank you. I’m just learning as I go and trying to figure out how I fit into this big machine.” “Tell me Jay, where did you see yourself today? Did you expect these things to happen for you like this?” “I guess I expected to be pounded in for a few weeks to make guys like Knight and Thunder look good. Didn’t think that I’d be given a chance this early.” Caldera nodded and took a sip from the coffee cup on his desk. “That’s what I like about GCW. We’ve become one of the leading clubs in the industry because we believe in pushing people to succeed and forcing them to prove themselves. In your case, we decided to see where you could go with the television title and you showed us that you were up to the challenge. Had you failed, perhaps we would have taken it slower for the following weeks. That said, this meeting isn’t about failure or what you’ve done so far. This is about where we’d like to see you headed in the coming weeks.” “Sure,” he said. “Let me know and I’ll do my best to do it.” “That’s what we like to hear.” Steven Caldera shuffled some papers on his desk finding one typed-up sheet of loose leaf. “Lately, we’ve seen you and Jordan White teaming up. You and him are good friends, yeah?” “Great friends,” he replied, guessing that the tag team titles were on their horizon. “Well it’s time to burn the bridge and move out of that show. You see, we’ve been passing names around and trying to decide who would be the best fit for The Establishment. There’s power behind the stable, but it needs a fresh face -- someone that people can hate.” “Hate?” “Naturally. I wanted to talk to you about becoming one of the newest members of The Establishment. You’ve built up such a fan base and people really do love to watch you wrestle. You’re the everyday guy doing your thing to overcome some of the monsters on this roster. When it comes down to it, people will hate to see you selling out and becoming just another monster in order to get ahead. You’ll bring a new sense of disdain to the faction and truly amp up the heat that The Establishment gains whenever the music plays. What we want to know is if you’d be interested in joining the most talked about group in GCW. Would you be willing to give up all the fans and become something more?” Jay was silent for a moment passing around the consequences. He wanted to be great but he wanted to be loved. Deep down, the fans cheering was what drove him to new heights. They were the ones that brought him into the star he was known for. “I mean, it all sounds so different.” “It’s bound to be different,” Caldera reassured. “Changing roles is going to feel awkward at the start and you may not like it. It’s not easy being the guy that kids don’t want to grow up and become. Still, someone has to do it and we think that you’re the right person.” “What if I lose my edge? Maybe I need the fans to do well.” Caldera could see it coming and leaned in. “You’re not the first to have questions like this. If you fail then we’ll find something new for you. The reason why we want you to make this move though is because we believe that you won’t mess up. You’ve proven that you’re strong and able to adapt. You’ve proven that you can do great things with a chance. Now I’m speculating here, but think of this as your jumping of point to reach for the World Title. It’s a base that you can use to gain even more momentum.” Hearing that the World Title could be his was enough to sway his decision. “OK. I’ll try it. I’ll see if I can do it. How’s all of this going to play out?” “You need to break away from Jordan White. Send a statement. We’re going to have you blast him with a steel chair Jay. For this to work, you’ll need to be fully committed and not look back. You and Frank may be friends, but this is your opportunity. Sell it till they can’t stand looking at you.” He shut his eyes and shrugged. “I can do that.” Steven Caldera stood from his desk with a wide Cheshire stretched across his face. Jay followed his lead and reached for the boss’s extended hand. For a moment, Jay could feel his world turning and he felt as if the floor was crumbling. He knew that the Jay Terror that people admired would become a distant memory in due time and that a championship-bound warrior would replace him. On the surface, he loved the thought. Deep down, he couldn’t understand why he had to choose. In the end, there was no turning his back on the boss. Their agreement was formal and Jay left his office as the newest piece of The Establishment’s regime. The Present --- “You have this cough?” The old man nodded and took another sip of his gin and tonic. He was covering his symptoms well. “Had it for the past few months. I’d tell you to follow my lead and quit while you still can, but it sounds like that advice came a bit too late.” Jay inhaled another cloud of smoke. “I’ve heard it before. I need these. Besides, it’s just the cold. I’ve been travelling and wrestling. No time to fix it up properly.” “Doesn’t help that you smoke three packs of those though,” he reminded. “I’m not trying to preach to you and tell you what you should and shouldn’t do, but there comes a time when things are more important than hourly habits. There’s family, friends, and things to live for. Cigarettes may get you through a day but those kind of things will get you through a lifetime.” Jay couldn’t believe the words he was hearing. He’d been given a smoking speech numerous times, each from different people, yet he still hated to hear it. “I don’t have any of that so I think I’ll just keep lighting them up.” “No family?” “Last of them died not too long ago.” “Friends?” “None that care enough to check in on me,” he lied. Caroline came to mind instantly. “What about wrestling? That worth living for?” Jay shook his head, “it’s a job. You get paid to fix air conditioners and I get paid to put on a show. Maybe it was a passion at some point but lately, it’s how I pass the time. It’s all I really know.” The old man used a wry grin as an answer. “I’m sorry to hear it kid. I guess you got all the rights to keep smoking them then. Might as well develop as big of a lump as you can handle in your lungs before it starts to really hurt.” “Cancer?” He put his hand on his chest and nodded. “I’ll be leaving my wife and three grand kids within the next three months. I wish I had a life as free as yours to throw away. It’d make this all so much easier.” Jay leaned back on his bar stool and looked down at his cigarette. There was half left and the cherry slowly slid toward the filter. It had been years since he’d thought of cancer and that was only because his grandfather had lost his own battle to his lungs. He glanced back at the man who was swirling the bottom of his gin around the glass. “Sorry to hear about that,” he said. Jay followed by smoking another few centimeters of the cigarette. “I can’t imagine.” The old man shook his head and tipped the glass backwards. Placing it back on the bar, he stood and extended his hand. “My name’s Earl. Earl Franks. I’ll be around for the next three months so if you feel like talking again, look me up. It was a good conversation.” Jay shook his hand and stood as well. He respected the old man but not enough to call him again. “Yeah. Next time I’ll cover your gin.” Earl halfheartedly smiled and turned away. His pace was slow, as if there was pain in all of his extremities. Before passing through the exit, he pulled his hands to his mouth and coughed into his palms. Jay finished his cigarette as he watched the door shut behind him and turned back to his whiskey and the sweet sound of silence. The Present --- While he’d been to the Houston office numerous times since first joining The Establishment, they had each been through invitation. As Terror rode the familiar elevator to the top floor, he wore the World Heavyweight championship on his shoulder. His posture was firm and he even wore an untucked dress shirt and tie. With a ding, he stepped onto the upper deck of the large waiting area and proceeded past the desks to the solitary hallway in the back. He waved to Janet, the woman who had replaced Shannon after her mid-life crisis, and took note of the pictures on the hallway wall. Steven Caldera shook hands with Jerry Jones, Mark Cuban, President Obama, and there was one with Jay Terror. He liked to think of it as his favorite family portrait he had been a part of. The same golden nameplate, Caldera’s old wrestling plate from his earlier days, decorated the door that Terror gently opened. Sitting behind the desk, knowing nothing of Jay’s appointment, was none other than President Steven Caldera himself. He was crossing the ‘t’ in his signature as Jay took a seat. “Well this sure is a surprise, Jay.” His eyes were focused on the World Championship. It was the face of his company, the one thing that every superstar aspired to hold, and it now rested on his protege’s shoulder. Jay grinned and placed the belt in front of them on the desk. “Just wanted to come by and tell you thank you.” Caldera seemed stunned by the gesture and nodded. “Thank you for what?” “Thank you for,” he stopped due to another cough attack. His wheeze was growing heavier as the days went on. “Sorry. I wanted to thank you for pushing me. You put me in the right position so many times and you’re the reason that I came out on top. We’re both champions with this belt and I couldn’t say enough to let you know how much I appreciate it.” “Well I knew from the day that we signed you up for The Establishment that you’d do great things. It took time and surely there were days where it wasn’t very fun, but you managed to get over that. I may have put you in the right place but you were the one who performed. You beat Tessa Windsor, not me or anyone else backing you.” “I did. And it felt great. There’s just so much more that I want to do now.” “I heard.” “I want that power. I deserve that power, right?” Caldera smirked and crossed his arms. “We’ve had some great champions, Jay. Each of them has brought something new to the table in their time as champion and I’m sure you’ll do the same. While I do believe that Johnny is still the one I want in charge of GCW, I suspect that you will have a greater influence on things as time goes on. That means you have to stay on top though. You can’t slack off and get lazy. You have Tempest this week, yeah?” “I can’t believe I have to defend the title against,” he started before being cut off. “Use it,” Steven Caldera interjected. “Imagine how you’ll look if she beats you in your first defense. You’re meant to be champion and that means proving it to every single person on that roster. Beat her and beat anyone else that’s thrown your way and you’ll surely prove that you’re meant to have power in this company.” “I can do that.” “Good. I don’t see the road clearing anytime soon, especially with Dangerous Games and NC-17 coming around the corner so be on top of your game. Be better than you’ve been and train harder. I understand the mistake you made before High Stakes and I’ve been assured by James Bryan that it won’t happen again. Promise me, Jay. Promise me that you’ll do what’s best for you and come through on being champion.” Jay nods but is stopped again by a heavy cough. He uses his tie to absorb the nasty wheeze. “I promise. No more shortcuts.” “How long has that cough been going on?” “A few weeks. It’s just a passing cold though. Nothing more.” Steven Caldera didn’t seem convinced and shook his head. “I want you to get a doctor to check you out. This is part of that promise to take care of yourself.” “It’s just a cold. I swear.” “Either way,” he declared. “You don’t want a cold going into a World Title match. Find out what medicine you need to take to get over it and let me know. This match is huge, Jay. It’s not worth getting winded and losing your momentum.” “Alright.” Jay and Caldera shared a silent stare before Caldera looked at his wrist watch. “I have a meeting to get to but I’m glad you stopped by. The title looks good on your shoulder.” “Thanks Steve.” “Now get to the doctor’s office. You might still be able to make an appointment for later today.” “I’ll get right on it.” Terror stood and exited the room with the title in tow. He thought of Earl Franks and of his family. He wondered what would come of his grandchildren and what they’d remember of their grandfather. Then he thought of his own grandfather and realized that in all of his memories, he was smoking a cigarette. Jay Terror had choices to make as he rode down the elevator and unfortunately, he wasn’t keen on making the right ones. Deeper than all of his thoughts was the urge to light up another cigarette and he did just that as he made his way to the limousine. The nicotine trickled down his throat and tickled his esophagus bringing forth another violent coughing spasm. Finally entering the back seat, he shut his eyes and leaned his head against the leather. “Where to now, Jay,” asked his driver. Jay sat, took another smoke from his cigarette, and wondered what he was doing to himself. In a moment of clarity, he spoke in a rough tone stating, “to the nearest doctor’s office. I have an exam to schedule.” The limousine moved and Terror continued to finish his cigarette. Whether he believed it or not, it didn’t change his dependence on the nicotine. He’d already finished a pack on the day and wasn’t going to be fooled by the common cold to quit smoking. Securing it all was the fact that Tessa Windsor had fallen to the same sick Terror that would be defending his title against Tempest. Whether she understood it or not, there was no stopping a man with a promise to keep.
