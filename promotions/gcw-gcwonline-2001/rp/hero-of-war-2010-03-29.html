Hero Of War

29 March 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/29/10 "Ra tat tat tat tat tat tat tat!" The firing of imaginary machine guns rattled the ears as the solider ducked for cover. With her back to the sandbags (also known as old wicker laundry baskets that smelled of mildew due to years of non-use in the attic of the garage), she braced for impact as the bomb screeched through the air. "Sheeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeerrrrrooooooooooooowwwwwwwwww BOOM!" With the sound effect she jumped out from behind the sandbags, squinting against the sunlight with assault rifle in arms. Against a horde of unseen enemies she opened fire. Mowing them down one by one, the mind's eye was filled with bloodshed as the Russians had no choice but to retreat. Victory was within her grasp. She would return home a hero, parades and streets would be named in her honor due to the valiant eff... "Look out!" The scream of her companion alerted her just a second too late. The invisible grenade had already been tossed. Staring in shock she watched as her fellow solider dove out of the trees and put his body on the ground in front of her, giving his life to the grenade so that she may live to see the celebration that she so rightfully deserved. Watching Private Patterson give his life so she may live to see another day was endearing in a way, it brought a smile to her face even though it was all for pretend. "Why would you do that for?" She questioned, looking down at the boy who was no longer Private Patterson, but Scott Patterson. The boy from two blocks down the road. Reaching her arm out, she motioned for him to grab it so she could help him up. With a little hesitation he grabbed her hand, turning a bright shade of red as he reached his feet. It didn't take him long to turn away from her, wearing a smile from ear to ear. Sure he had cooties... but she supposed he was an alright guy. Playing war with him sure helped pass the time between getting screamed at by her over-protective mother. "It's all part of a soldier’s duty Private First Class Valentine." He said, finally turning around and making eye contact with his playmate. With dark brown crew cut hair and bright green eyes, little Vivica would never admit that the boy was kind of cute in his own way. She particularly enjoyed his freckles due to the fact that he was the only other non-red headed kid besides herself in her class that had them. Her's were across her nose, but his were right under his eyes. They both were a bit sensitive about the issue. "It isn't a soldier’s duty to be stupid." She answered, "Why kill yourself?" "You have to be willing to give your life for your country." "But not if you don't have to." Vivica responded, "If you ever did that in real life, I'd kill you." "I'd already be dead." "I'd bring you back to life and kill you again." She responded, backed into a corner, "For being so stupid." "I'm not stupid." "All boys are stupid." The words escaped her lips with conviction. "Is that so?" A voice from behind them startled the two of them. Vivica quickly jumped around to see her father's shadow blocking her teal blue eyes from the sunlight. "Hey Mister Valentine." Scott was quick to greet Vivica's father like any young man courting a beautiful young woman would speak to her father. The beautiful young lady on the other hand simply huffed and puffed, knowing full well that her father's presence in the backyard battlefield meant it was time to go home where another war was waiting. A war on chores and homework. The worst kind of war. "At ease solider." Jack Valentine spoke with a smile and Scott answered with a quick salute. He'd been away for nearly a week on business, but that was the life of a high profile lawyer. Vivica would sometimes go months without seeing her father, who probably knew less about his own little girl than most of the parents in the neighborhood. Evidence of that was the doll he clutched in his right hand that was made by a Native American in Arizona. "I come bearing gifts." "A doll?" Vivica's heart dropped as she saw what he had in his hand. She didn't want dolls... she wanted guns, grenades, swords, helmets, and action figures. The son her father would never have, and her mother would never allow her to be. Taking it out of his hands she looking down at it as he rubbed her head, messing up her light blond hair. "Do you like it?" She never much liked disappointing her father, either. "I do!" She forced a big toothy smile, "Thank you daddy." "You're welcome hun, are you ready to go home?" "Do I have to?" She tried to be polite, holding back the whine that she do desperately wanted to let out. "Yes darling, your mother has dinner ready for us and I just got off the plane. I'm tired and I'm starving." Her eyes darted over to Scott Patterson, whose frown told the whole story as he looked down at the dirt. "But dad..." "Let's go." Her mind instantly wanted to know where Private Patterson was to save her when she really needed it. He simply began waving, knowing that it'd only be a few days until he saw her again. That's when the war would continue. It might even be the day when he'd win over her heart. A little boy has to dream, doesn't he? "Bye Scott." With a defeated inflection, Viv waved goodbye and followed her father out of the backyard and towards the Corvette parked in the driveway. The escort was there waiting to take the muddy soldier back to her mother, where a lecture was surely waiting about how dirty she'd become after being told multiple times to stay off the ground. It was pretty hard to be a soldier who couldn't go down in the trenches. As Jack opened the door for his little girl, she stopped before stepping inside. A thought had begun to process in her tiny little head. She sure did have a lot of fun playing with Scott, if there was only a way that it'd never end... "Daddy?" She asked curiously. "Yes honey." "Can I be a soldier like Scott when I grow up?" ******************************************** He said, “Son, have you seen the world? Well, what would you say if I said that you could? Just carry this gun and you’ll even get paid.” I said, “That sounds pretty good.” ******************************************** BOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOMMMMMMMMMM! Jolted back to consciousness, the scene that unfolded in front of her hazy vision seemed almost like a movie. Sirens blared from seemingly every direction as she sat straight up, watching as everyone around her ran around in a chaotic frenzy. The olive green cot she'd fallen asleep on gave her very little give as she tried to push herself up to her feet amidst the calamity. Getting up to a vertical base, she nearly gets trampled as soon as she gets her bearings straight. "Hey, watch where you're going!" Her screams weren't even acknowledged as he ran past. Then ran by another, then another. Trapped in a human game of Frogger, she finally managed to jump out of the way right next to a soldier trying up his boots in a hurry. Looking down she could see his hands shaking and the fear in his eyes. That's when reality began to sink in. Something was going down, and it was serious. "What's going on?" "I.E.D." He explained giving his boots one last jerk. "I.E.D.? What in the hell is tha..." "I'm sorry miss I don't have time." "Do you at least know where Staff Sergea..." She couldn't even get her sentence off before he took off in a mad sprint, taking the time to put on his jacket and his helmet on the way. He seemed to at least be heading in the same direction as the others. In disbelief she looked on as they all trampled towards the same opening like cattle. Moving in that direction seemed to at least be a logical choice. If anything they were all congregating over there and she could get some answers out of Scott. Seeing her break in the traffic, she jumped into the middle of the fray with a dead sprint... trying to keep up with America's Finest. For the first time since she was a little girl, she felt like a soldier. A real one this time, without the water balloon grenades and the old smelly laundry baskets. It certainly got her heart pumping. The fame and fortune faded away if just for a few seconds, she was one of the boys running to go get the job done. "HEY!" With a suddenly jerk she was almost out of the cattle line as fast as she entered. Turning around and looking at the man's uniform it became obvious to her that his rank was Staff Sergeant, but her heart sunk when she looked up at his face. It wasn't the one she was looking for... "You need to get the hell out of here." Staff Sergeant Clemens shouted in her ear, pointing her in the opposite direction, "We're getting all you SCCW people out over that way, it isn't safe for you here." "I'm looking for Staff Sergeant Patterson!" She tried to scream over the sirens, "Have you seen him?" "That's none of your concern, your only concern should be getting the fuck out of here." He replied, "That's what we're going to do." "I need to ta..." "I don't care what you need to do Miss Valentine." He interrupted, "I know you didn't sign up to be a soldier but over here we don't ask you what to do, we tell you what to do. If you don't want to go home in a casket I suggest you go over to the other side of the tent. RIGHT. NOW." Her eyes narrowed as she looked back at him, she had just woke up and wasn't nearly in the mood to deal with being talked to that way.... but it was the life of a soldier she always wanted. Reluctantly after shooting the Staff Sergeant a glare she marched off in the other direction. With her shoulder slumped and her footsteps plodding, she walked against the current... swerving between hurried soldiers making her way towards the "VIP" area. She was almost there when a broadcast over another soldier's radio stopped her dead in her tracks. "Still no word from Patterson's radio, that has to be the patrol that set it off." The words literally took her off of her feet, dropping her to a seated position on an olive green cot much like her own. Dropping her head into her hands she listened in hopes of hearing something positive. His voice more than anything. "Sir we have Patterson's patrol in sight." Thank God... "They got him. It's nasty." "Fuck, we need to get some crews out there. This isn't good." "No..." She said out loud to herself, as a tear began to run down her cheek. Since the explosion that woke her up she had tried her best to deny the obvious, like if she wouldn't ask the question it wouldn't be true. He was too good of a guy, something like this never happened to the good guys. "Anyone but Scott..." ******************************************** Black leather boots, spit-shined so bright They cut off my hair but it looked alright We marched and we sang We all became friends As we learned how to fight A hero of war Yeah that’s what I’ll be And when I come home They’ll be damn proud of me I’ll carry this flag To the grave if I must Because it’s flag that I love And a flag that I trust ******************************************** Four Years Earlier... Just hours before the massive arena was filled from top to bottom with screaming fans. With the silence that no filled the air, it almost seemed like a lifetime away. Kicking back in a folded steel chair, she looked around the stadium in marvel. It wasn't long ago where she'd dream to get tickets to sit exactly where she was at that moment. To be up this close to the ring, she could almost reach out and touch the wrestlers. In her wildest dreams she would be on the other side of that barricade that laid just a few feet in front of her. Every once in a great while, even those wildest of dreams come true. She felt like she'd just been hit by a truck, sure... but how many people could say they were where she was right now? Sitting in an empty 40,000 seat arena watching them tear down the wrestling ring and the MegaTron. The whole thing was still so new and exciting, months into her GCW career. What was even more exciting was the GCW United States Championship laying across her lap. Running her hand across the nameplate that read "VIVICA J. VALENTINE", it sometimes was the only way she'd believe it was real. So often she'd felt like she was in over her head, but obviously something had to be going right. Someone was watching over her, both literally and figuratively. "Vivy?" A not-so-familiar voice echoed through the hollow arena, slightly jolting to someone who may still be on a slight adrenaline high. She uncomfortably looked over her shoulder to see a man in uniform walking her direction. While at first he seemed like a complete stranger, the closer he got the more familiar his face seemed. The high cheekbones covered in freckles... "No one has called me Vivy in years." She responded as she pushed herself up out of her chair with a slight wince, not wanting to show the slightest bit of pain to the adoring fan. Slinging her United States Championship over her shoulder, she couldn't help but shake the feeling that she knew that face. "You don't recognize me, do you?" He responded, but luckily he was in uniform. The eyes of the Black Mariah quickly darted to his chest where the tag read PATTERSON. He could almost see the lightbulb flash over her head. "Of course I do Scott!" "I saw you read my nametag." He responded with a smile, reaching out for a handshake. "Plus that's Private First Class Patterson." While he'd certainly grown since the last time she saw him, the same boyish charm radiated through his smile. Looking up at him was definitely new, now standing at 6' 4" he probably would've been a great candidate for professional wrestling himself. Broad shoulders and in fantastic shape, the young awkward boy had grown into a handsome young man. Her cheeks turned rosy and she really didn't know what to say. Perhaps she'd always had a soft spot for a man in uniform. "For some reason, I don't find it hard to believe that you grew into a soldier." It's all she could think of to say. "I don't find it hard to believe that you make a living kicking boys asses for a living." Scott responded, his eyes wandering around her body. "I know I certainly had my share." "What can I say? I'm a girl with convictions." "You're a girl with a mean right hook." He chuckled as he gazed into her teal blue eyes, "In 6th grade you almost broke my nose when I asked you why you acted like a boy." "I'd do it again." Clinching her fist she playfully braced back to swing, getting a chuckle out of the soldier before sighing. Running her hand through her hair she looked at the ground uncomfortably. Perhaps it was the adrenaline or the fact that she never quite had her way with talking to boys, but it seemed like the right time to just say 'nice to see you' and walk away. "Who would've thought that back when we were little kids playing soldiers we'd stand here tonight, huh?" He mentioned in passing, looking around the empty arena. "Me, just a few weeks away from deployment and you being worshipped by millions. The world famous Vivica J. Valentine once spent the night in my garage, where my mother found her the next morning because she was too stubborn to admit to her mother she was wrong and did, in fact, need a place to live at ten years old." "Deployment?" She asked, "To where?" "Iraq, I'll be there for a year... but I'll come back home after that. Hopefully everything will be taken care of by then." "That's horrible..." Was the only thing she could think of to say, a far cry from the days where she glorified war in the backyards of Highland Park, Illinois. "No, I'm looking forward to it." He cracked a smile, "This is all I've ever wanted to do." "Me too..." Vivica said, looking down at her United States Championship awkwardly. "It's crazy that you're on television and everything. I can't get over seeing you in magazines." Scott said, "Actually I have a really odd request." "What's that?" "Can you sign my ticket stub?" He asked nervously, fumbling through his wallet to find the piece of paper in question. "It's for my mother." "Are you kidding me?" Vivica questioned, "Of course I don't mind, but you have to do me a favor too." "Anything." His response was almost immediate. "Can you sign this?" Vivica asked pulling the only thing she had on her out of her pocket, it was a key card to the Holiday Inn. The card was met with a chuckle by PFC Patterson, "Shut up, it's all I have. You think I bring anything to the arena with me? That'd be insane." "Yeah, yeah..." Scott said holding two sharpies, tossing one to his childhood playmate. She snatched it out of mid-air before exchanging items. As they both signed away, Scott broke the awkward silence. "Your parents still live in the same place?" "Yeah, they love the house. They'll never move." "I'll stop by sometime and get your new address from your mother if that's okay... I'd like to keep in touch with you." He stumbled through his words as he finished signing the hotel keycard. "Yeah, I'd like that." She finished up her autograph with a smile, "Tell your mother I said I'll stop by if I ever get some free time." "She'd like that." Scott replied, exchanging back memorabilia. "I guess you're busy though, you have a plane to catch and another city to see. I suppose you aren't planning on spending a whole lot of time here in Missouri." "Well, my plane isn't until tomorrow afternoon." Vivica responded, "But I do need to get going, I have some stuff I need to do... I guess." "Yeah." He answered, "I guess I'll write you sometime?" "That sounds good." "Right on." They both stood there in silence, Vivica looking around anxiously before taking a few steps past him with a nod. He didn't move an inch and just watched as she passed, a bit flustered and lost for words. Turning around he looked as she jumped the barricade and walked back towards the backstage area. He'd bit his tongue for long enough, he just had to say something... "Vivy!" He yelled, halting her in her tracks. As she turned around he could feel the beads of sweat developing on his brow. The room temperature raised a thousand degrees and the entire world began to spin. "Yeah?" She responded to his silence, puzzled. "Where... are you flying to? After you leave here? Are you going home or is there another show..." He fumbled through his words, doing his best to navigate through his scrambled thoughts. "Just... curious? I guess." "I'm stopping by Chicago to have dinner with Christian." She smiled, "But I'll tell my mother that you said hello." "Yeah..." He forced a smirk, "...sounds good." As she disappeared behind the curtain, she entered the magical world of professional wrestling again and left Highland Park far, far behind. Scott's letter would come, but life on the road wasn't easy. Sometimes she didn't have the time to do laundry, not to mention write Scott back. It went without saying that she had more important things going on in her life as did he. War was a very real and time-consuming venture, both inside and outside of the ring. ******************************************** I kicked in the door I yelled my commands The children, they cried But I got my man We took him away A bag over his face From his family and his friends They took off his clothes They pissed in his hands I told them to stop But then I joined in We beat him with guns And batons not just once But again and again A hero of war Yeah that’s what I’ll be And when I come home They’ll be damn proud of me I’ll carry this flag To the grave if I must Because it’s flag that I love And a flag that I trust ******************************************** There are things that they don't tell you about the trips you're forced to make sometimes that might've been important to know before you packed your clothes. For instance, while visiting the troops in Iraq it's important to wear long sleeves and cover up every inch of your body that you can. Sure it may be hot, but that extra layer of clothing protects your skin from the sun... not to mention from getting sand in places that you didn't even know you had places. The Bulletproof Blonde never got the memo. She soon realized as she stood out in the middle of the scorching desert that she was going to be scrubbing sand out of every little crease of her body for weeks. The sun beat down on her natural blonde hair, hanging down over a pair of black Oakley sunglasses as she glared into the blue sky. Watching anxiously as the helicopter came closer and closer down to Earth. Leave it to SCCW to make this trip as agonizing and uncomfortable as possible, it was a nice gameplan isolating her away from everyone on their roster. They sure knew how to make a girl feel at home. But after the events that had transpired in her own "home federation", she'd grown quite used to the special treatment that often came with being part of the away team. In fact, she'd even began to thrive on it. In her current mindset she was looking forward to the reaction she was sure to receive from the Sin City Faithful. A mix between seething hatred and bitter disappointment across the crowd as her hand was raised in victory. It was an opportunity that she'd missed against Andy Murray just a few weeks before. A chance to prove to the world that she had worth. A chance at vindication. As it turns out, this particular desert was an oasis for missed opportunities. The wind picked up around her as the chopper closed in, closing her jacket she tried to keep the sand out... but by now it was a useless effort. It was a feeling she'd just need to learn to accept in the coming days. With a perfect landing, the side door of the chopper slid open and with a slight nudge the two soldiers who'd stood next to her in complete silence (one who'd never taken his eyes off her butt) directed her towards the inside. In what could be described best as a mix between a jog and a wind sprint, they made their way into the aircraft with her bags. Taking a seat, she buckled up and put on all the necessary equipment that came with riding in a military air vehicle. Still a bit awkward, even after the first trip she didn't quite know how to fasten all the right harnesses. Luckily there were a few good men around to help. What she didn't expect was to see an old face pulling the last strap tight. Backing away he made eye contact but it wasn't with a smile, right now it was all business... but it was nice to hear his voice through the radio. "Miss Valentine is ready." Newly promoted Staff Sergeant Scott Patterson relayed, "Clear for takeoff." As the blades began twirling again, the chopper lifted off the ground just a second before he slammed the side door shut. Turning around he saw her just in time to get a glimpse at her thousandth attempt to brush the sand off of her neck. It was absolutely useless. "You're just wasting your time." He said, "It never comes off." "What are you doing here?" She asked, half shocked and half ecstatic. "A year of duty?" "A year of duty turns into three real quick around here, turns out." He responded, the permanent smile long erased from his lips. Even his face looked different now that she examined. His prominent freckles hidden under a layer of scars and sand. "Wish we could've caught up under better circumstances." "Yeah, me too." She responded, "How's your mother?" "She died last year." His answer was abrupt, "I heard your father sent flowers, tell him I said thank you." "I'm sorry, and I will..." She looked away down towards the desert sands, watching the chopper cast a shadow as it went by. She opened her mouth to break the small silence that came between them, but he took the words right out of her mouth. "You've changed." He said, "What happened to you?" "Reality caught up to me, I suppose." Vivica shrugged off his observation, she'd talked about it enough that she didn't particularly feel like retreading over that territory. "Tell me about it." He looked down at the shadow with her, "I guess not everyone can grow up to be the hero all of the time, but you at least still have one fan." "I'm no hero." Vivica's eyes made contact with his, he looked almost defeated in the way he held himself. "That's your job." "Right." The one word answer was stern and emotionless, but a keen ear would've picked up the hint of sarcasm, "If I could do it all over again..." "I would've listened." Vivica chirped in, "People like to say they understand, they like to act like they know what it's like. They like to think they have all the answers. If they've only seen the things I've seen and have been through the things I've been through they would realize..." "...that no matter what you do you can't undo it." Patterson finished her sentence for her, even if it wasn't exactly what she was going to say, "You can't undo the things you've seen or heard. No one talks about it but it's there, the elephant in the room." A closer look made Vivica realize that it was more than just sand-coated freckles and a few scars. The real scars were mental and emotional. Being in her line of work she'd like to think she knew a thing or two about war, but one look into his eyes made her realize that she was wrong. The boyish charm and everything that made him an alright guy had been stripped away from him. His life had been altered in ways that he would've never believed possible. To see the things he's seen and to do the things he's done... "But I'd do it all over again." He finally spoke after some inner thought, "I'd kill every single one of those fuckers again if I was given a choice." Sometimes you don't always realize what your dreams are made of until you live them. "Would you?" He asked, breaking her away from her own thoughts. "Would you do it all over again, knowing what you know now? Knowing what would happen? What you'd become? How you'd change?" Her thoughts flashed back to the hotel room after her loss at NC-17. The primal screams and the uncontrollable violent urges that went through her body. She felt so helpless and psychotic, like she'd lost complete control of who she'd become. She felt so... alive. "Yeah." She responded with little hesitation, "In a second." "That's what makes a hero." Scott nodded, "At least, that's how I see it." She looked back into his eyes as if just for a second, that boyish optimism fought it's way back through the scar tissue for one last spark. A smile crossed his face for the first time in what seemed to be an eternity. "Thanks." Vivica smirked back too, looking away again down at the desert sands. Little did she realize that would be the last time she'd see his face before he went on patrol that night. He'd never get the chance to tell her how beautiful she was, like he'd wanted to say so many times before. Maybe it was for the best, because just like when she was a little girl... she would've blew him off. Love hasn't been too kind to The Bulletproof Blonde and that'd well documented. What isn't is the fact that in another lifetime, she could've very well fallen for a boy like him. ******************************************** She walked through bullets and haze I asked her to stop I begged her to stay But she pressed on So I lifted my gun And I fired away The shells jumped through the smoke And into the sand That the blood now had soaked She collapsed with a flag in her hand A flag white as snow A hero of war Is that what they see Just medals and scars So damn proud of...
