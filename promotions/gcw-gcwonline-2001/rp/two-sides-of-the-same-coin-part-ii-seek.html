Date: DG RP #2 .:: ::: ::. Sly Fox’s Cabin Moapa Paiute Reservation Valley of Fire, Nevada Brilliant red swelled and blossomed over the edges of the mesas as the sun rose over the Valley of Fire. Lia found herself transfixed by the ever-shifting colors, the contorting shadows that danced into the crevices as the sun widened his eyes on the day. Sly Fox’s cabin was on the edge of the Moapa Paiute Reservation. Lia hadn’t asked him, but she believed she understood why it was here’he was in a position where he was an outsider, even among his own people. Skinwalkers, they called them among his tribe, and it was an ugly name for something that Sly Fox had inherited by blood. Utkena Garou was the term that Blaise had used for his grandfather. A long time ago, someone had told her that the garou were the eternal enemy of her kind. That she would never survive an encounter with a garou, and if a garou were given a choice, he would sooner initiate that encounter than allow her to run away, once he had scented the Kindred on her. Yet here she was, standing in the cabin of a garou, far away from any civilization that might allow her purchase to flee or disappear? and with not one garou present, but two. She felt no fear. Slowly, fear had begun to work itself out of her consciousness. It had started when Lilith had become a part of her life’a being who could smell fear, and exploit it every moment. Lilith had wanted her to fear, and fear she had’for a time. But then, she had realized the weakness that was fear. Some called it rationality, understanding that there were certain things in your life that were very dangerous. But rational or not, it was still a handicap. Without fear, what sort of psychological tactics could someone use against you? There was anger, of course, but anger was even more easily controlled than fear. Master one, and you master the other. These were the lessons that she had of late taken to heart. With so much riding on her own skill, she had to learn to trust herself, and that lesson had been long in coming. “You’re up so early” are you like Coyote, trying to rise with the Sun and go with him into the sky?” She turned suddenly, her long, simple skirt flaring out around her legs as she did so. It was recommended garb’something that was not overly “bourgeois” looking, respectful to the natives who lived here. Sly Fox stood behind her, holding a simple wooden mug in his hand. She presumed from the smell that it was full of coffee, something that the old man seemed to drink a good deal of. His long black hair was tied behind his back with a simple suede cord. She tilted her head at him before answering. “I’m afraid I don’t know the reference?” she trailed off. Sly Fox smiled, a very slight one, and stepped out onto his front porch, sliding easily into his wooden rocking chair. She followed, leaning in the open front doorway of his cabin. “It’s an old legend my people tell. Why are you up so early?” She smiled again at his persistence. “I just like the sunrise. Once I” well, once I was able to watch them again without turning into a hot dog roast, I acquired a habit of watching them regularly. There’s something clean and pleasant about a sunrise.” Sly Fox nodded his head emphatically. It was something she figured he would understand, he and anyone else who had a connection with the land. Most people wouldn’t have considered someone like Lia Vlaszy to be terribly connected to the land herself’not someone who seemed to pride herself on looking urbane and sophisticated at every turn? but the small vial of earth that she always wore very close to her heart told a different story. Prague earth, earth that came from outside Vysehrad Castle, where she had spent so many past nights. Like most others of her kind, reminders of the past were scarcely far from the surface. She had sunk into an easy, peaceful silence with Sly Fox, who finished his last sip of coffee, and then bowed his head. She wondered what he was doing for a moment before it suddenly became clear’praying. She was surprised at herself’had the action really become so foreign that she could not recognize it anymore? Something about the thought made her nearly heartsick. He glanced back up at her out of the corners of his eyes. “Are you prepared for your journey today?” She sighed, much louder than she meant to, and that sound, more than anything, seemed to surprise Sly Fox. He turned his head entirely towards her, eyes taking in the details that seemed to accompany this breathing process. “I don’t know” I’m of two minds about it, at least. I desperately want to learn the answers as to why all this happened, why Virgil and I can suddenly remember our pasts, and especially our past together, but? at the same time, I’m uncertain of what to expect. I don’t know how to get in contact with my sire, I’m worried that I won’t be able to reach out for him, wherever he is?” Sly Fox did not seem sympathetic to her worries. He shook his head, standing up and drilling holes into her with his dark eyes. He reached out to touch her on the shoulder, and was surprised to find her skin still so malleable, even if it was cold. “I hope you don’t consider yourself too old to listen and learn.” “Of course not,” she said, shaking her head insistently. “Good,” Sly Fox went on, “because those who enter a sweat lodge for the first time should always consider themselves students. It’s not an experience for the weak-minded. But in my experience, those who are most successful with their quest are those who go in without anything else weighing them down. Those who go in with the single purpose to seek their answers, instead of worrying about what they may find. You can’t know what you will find, and that’s why you seek.” She nodded’it seemed so simple, but really, she hadn’t thought of it that way. She was thoughtful for a few moments longer before turning to him again as he started back into his house. “What should I do to prepare?” He brushed his hands together, before toweling them off in the sink, having moments ago rinsed out his coffee cup. “There is some sage incense in the bathroom, and some sea salts. A feeling began to grow within her, one of a deep satisfaction, a deep excitement. She wouldn’t have called it a premonition, but she knew somehow that she and Virgil would find what they were looking for once inside the lodge. .:: ::: ::. Inside, Lia could already feel the wet heat on her skin, and the water had not yet been poured over the heated rocks to create the steam yet. It was dark, the warmth threading around her like an enveloping and maternal embrace. “Are you seated?” Sly Fox called down. She did not know what it was doing to her, but it seemed almost as though it was loosing her from her body, wrenching her spirit loose and sending it into the sky to wheel freely, like a tern flittering to the pounding rhythm that was ingrained into her head now, a part of her. The air shimmered with heat. Her eyes had adjusted to the dark but now around her, she saw only glimmering, twinkling starlight. She knew she had not come here alone, but with four other companions, only she could not see any of them. Their presences had become vague dots on a far-away map, cities that she could wonder about and imagine, but could not visit. She was at once rooted to her spot, and soaring high above it all, high above the swooping, dramatic Valley of Fire’s red hills and rainbow sandstone, away to some other place that seemed at once more alien, yet more familiar. The colors were darker here, but the darkness was more vivid. It was as though she was suddenly seeing the darkness for the first time, illuminated in brilliant and rich deep reds, blues, greens and purples. It was not formless grey and black, but a symphony of spectrum, unfolding and widening before her very eyes. And though she knew still that the sweat was beading all over her body, coating her skin in a slick coat, it felt cold here, she could almost sense that the first winter’s snow would be falling soon. The formless distance began to take shape, and within it, she could see dark spires rising up into the night sky. She knew that was her place’that was where she needed to go, and so she closed her eyes and flew herself there on swift raven wings, like the ravens that used to walk these ancient parapets. No one else except Blaise, that is, who hung back for a moment, and stared down at the ring resting in her palm. “I didn’t realize you had a taste for men’s signet rings,” he quipped, raising his eyebrows with interest. “I don’t,” she responded, looking up at him with a completely bewildered face. I’ll let Sly Fox know you enjoyed your trip?” She was caught in the shimmer of the ring’s metal again, a perfect-looking silver despite its age. “Blaise, should I?” She held it up, examining it in the afternoon sunlight. “It’s not gonna burn your fingers off if you wear it,” he said, “Now come on, don’t you want to get cleaned up and unpack all that stuff you’ve just been through” you know, inside?” She took the hint, even as she was sliding her finger into the ring, and walked into the cabin almost sleepwalking. It fit only on her thumb, and she remembered that Vaclav’s hands had been significantly bigger than her own. But as she half-stumbled into the living room of the cabin, she felt that all eyes were on her. And when she looked up, she saw that she was right. “What,” she joked lamely, “did I forget to put my clothes back on?” The silence hung in the air tensely. “Maybe,” Sly Fox said from the corner, in his rocking chair, “you should greet your guest instead of joking with your friends.” There was a scraping of wood on wood as a chair pushed back, and the aforementioned guest stood up. It took a moment for her eyes to adjust from the bright sunlight to the relative dim coolness inside the cabin, but it didn’t take more than a split-second for her to identify who was standing there, in the middle of the living room, against all odds. He stood there, a half-smile tugging at the corners of his expressive lips. Dressed all in black, impeccably dressed, with the air shimmering around him as though it were bending, the light refracting off of him and in another direction. He was the answer to more questions, the object of ever-more frustrating quests’her twin brother, Laszlo Vlaszy.
