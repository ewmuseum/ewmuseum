On a lighter note...

17 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/17/05 The dojo has a certain smell about it that... That is indescribable. One can only say that it smells like training. It smells like making oneself better. It smells like hours and hours one someone's left melted away for some purpose that they at the time cannot understand. All the know is that their muscles, their physical material bodies, hurt and ache because they are being worked in ways that they're not used to. All for what? For betterment on this Earth? Please. If I gave a shit about material goods I'd be a stock broker, not a wrestler with barely a dime to my name. If I gave a shit about the whole healthy body/healthy soul idea, I wouldn't blow that lousy paycheck on beer and gambling. And if I thought that my body and physical well-being were important at all, I wouldn't make that shitty paycheck in a sport where it takes more punishment than pampering. By far. No. The body and the physical self is simply a means to an end. 'Sensai. Why is it that you teach Kempo?' 'Kohai John, you may be, by far, the worst student I've had when it comes to meditation.' We've been sitting in silence, he in full lotus position and myself in half lotus, for a good ten minutes. You really don't know how long ten minutes of silence is until you try it sometime. But apparently it's not long enough. 'I'm sorry, Sensai. But I just-' 'You excel physically, Kohai John. You have discipline that I did not think you would have. And you understand the spirit much better than most of the cromagnan men that inhabit your profession. But you cannot meditate for shit.' 'I just have a lot on my mind.' 'There is an old Japanese proverb. Dumb man asks questions so much he deafens himself to the answers.' '...sorry...' The shaking's what's really been getting to me. And the waking up in the morning throwing up. Since I've put the painkillers down, as was ordered by my doctors, my boss, and now finally Kate, the woman that I love, every day of my fucking life has been the worst hangover ever. And every day is worse than the one before. But I try my hardest not to let it deter my resolve. 'You shake, Kohai John. I have my eyes closed. But I can feel you shaking. I can hear it. I can see it through my eyelids.' 'I know, Sensai.' 'Your body wants the drugs that you cannot have any longer. It fabricates pains that you wish to go away. Your body craves that which is forbidden. That is the eternal struggle, I'm afraid. Your Yin and your Yang are constantly in struggle, and one has been winning inside of you for a long time now. It has set up its house. It has made its equilibrium in your body. But, imbalance is a bad, bad thing. Now that you are trying to balance Yin and Yang, now that the formerly beaten one is emerging yet again, the conflict is at its peak. And it will not settle until balance is achieved. It will not end until they are equal, and you are whole.' Well I wish the two bastards would hurry up, here. Yin and Yang out of balance? That's the God damn story of my life. I'm a good guy in the ring, I'm a fucking wreck outside of it. When my life clears up, my in-ring reputation takes a dive. When I have the power to affect change, I don't have the resolve. When I find the resolve, the power is gone. And when I need to think, I can't. When I want peace of mind, I don't have it. And I'm so fucking sick of it. Well, I'm sick of that and the painkiller withdrawl. 'You never have told me why you are a wrestler, Kohai John. What is it that draws you to a sport where the object is to make others feel pain? Indeed, to feel that pain yourself.' 'Well...I guess since you're always telling my Japanese proverbs, I might as well tell you one of mine. There was once this man and this woman who were married. And they were incredibly happy together. And they lived in a house in the woods, away from anyone that might try to corrupt their happiness. And one day the man stepped outside of his house and he saw a bird flying overhead, and for some reason he was amazed by it. And so he started walking, following the hypnotic movements of the bird. Years passed. The woman never heard anything from her husband, and she mourned losing him, but hated him for leaving her. Until one day he came back to that cabin in the woods. Husband and wife embraced, both being old and no longer beautiful, and she hated him for wasting away her youth on worrying. And she asked him, 'Why didn't you come back sooner?' And he looked at her and said, 'The bird kept moving.'' He understands for a moment. It strikes home for the first time. Yes, I know damn well that I wrestle to chase the bird that I can never get ahold of. That I leave a life of peace and quiet and happiness for a life of adventure and striving that will only leave me old and empty in years to come. But for the first time I realize something I never realized before. Just like the man in the story, I got up and left Kate seven years ago. And I always thought it was just so that I could see what would happen. But I think...I think it was because I had a metaphorical bird flying overhead. Grand Master Chan Fo nods his head and breathes deep. I can almost slowly feel my chi coming into balance just by looking at the peace of mind of this old man. He has found balance and contentment in his life. That's something I will never know. I guess I will always confront the inevitable. I'll always be chasing something that I cannot catch. For, if I could catch it, I would then just move on to something greater. Peace of mind is not a trait among us Fitzwillis' 'I think we have meditated enough for today, Kohai John.' This time it is Chan Fo that interrupts a two minute silence. Everything is a game of chess with him. So my words are chosen carefully. 'You wish to end meditation today?' 'Hmmm...meditation is to find inner peace, that much is true. But it is also to come across revelations. Discover new truths. I feel that you have discovered a new truth today, Kohai John.' What the hell? I never said anything about discovering that truth. What, can this old man read minds or something? 'Yes, I can read minds, actually.' ...bullshit. 'It is not bullshit. It is an art, passed down from ancient Japan.' ... ... ... ... 'You do not believe me. And now you are trying to think of nothing to test my statement.' 'But how do you- How can you- What the fuck are you?' The old man lets out an old Japanese laugh. The prankster strikes again. 'You are so predictable, Kohai John. Not in your actions or in your next decision, but in your thought process. And so gullible as well. In fact, you are the reason I wrote gullible on the ceiling.' I instinctively look up. There's no writing on the ceiling. ... DAMN it! 'Hohoho, Kohai John. You have made Sensai laugh. That can also signify the end of today's lesson. For now, I go. And you go. And you do well in your match tonight. But try as you will not to let what you need to do cloud what you should do.' '...I will try, Sensai.' 'You already sound prepared to fail.' 'I'm sorry, Sensai. I've just been...frustrated...is all.' 'Hmm... If that is your problem, I have another old Asian proverb that may do you well.' Eyebrows perk up. Eager to hear what might help balance my chi, what might help me with this damn addiction. What might help me get through this night. 'Fool climb tree to get cherry. ...wise man spread legs.' He laughs again as he walks off, obviously amused with himself. ... And it took me a minute. But he just taught me yet another lesson. No matter how profound you are, no matter how old or wise you might be, never underestimate one's perversion. Filthy old Japanese bastard. 'I heard that, Kohai!' ... Crazy old man probably can read minds...
