Better Than Perfect

17 July 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 17 2006 “Do you ever wonder what it would be like?” My brother’s eyes remained locked on his needle and thread, barely acknowledging my question with a raised eyebrow. He effortlessly moved though each stroke with seamless perfection and a tenderness in his fingertips that most reserved for a newborn child. With a sigh I took a seat on our porch and returned my gaze to another beautiful Mexican sunset; a momentary reprieve from the throbbing pain coursing through my body after a long day of training with father. “Alex?” I pressed further, wiping the sweat dampened tresses from my face. “Yes, Julio?” he sighed as he bit off a piece of thread from his needle. “Don’t you ever wonder what it would be like, you know, if Papa wasn’t a Luchador?” I turned to my brother looking for the slightest hint of interest in my comments, but could find none. “What if he was a business man” We could have been rich!” “But, we are rich!” He smirked with a sardonic tone in his voice to mask his annoyance. “Take a look around. We aren’t scrounging around to make ends meat, we’re not out slaving away on the fields, we speak English better than most Americans, and we certainly don’t have problems that most people in this village worry themselves to sleep about.” I grimaced at the stringency of his tone as I rubbed an achy shoulder. My gaze floated around to the open fields and farm houses that staggered the horizon. I had never thought of it as anything more than a beautiful view of my homeland. Yet I suppose, to others, still at work on their fields, it could have represented a hole that they were struggling to lift themselves from. Alex threaded another needle as I wrapped my arm around the railing beside me. . “I didn’t really mean for the money. It’s just that” Oh forget it, Alex.” “What’s on your mind kid?” Alex questioned picking up his work and finding a seat next to me. “Papa, pushing you too hard in training?” “The training I can handle,” My eyes wavered in the last light of the day as I held back some frustrated tears. “I’m just tired of everybody else looking at me and immediately seeing a better version of you. When I learn to do something amazing in the ring, it doesn’t even turn a head; it’s expected of me, expected of a De Hoyas. But, when I mess something up and end up on my face, everyone is there, breathing down my throat, scowling and shaking their heads because I am supposed to be better than that” better than perfect! I haven’t even finished training yet and they already criticize every mistake I make!” “So try harder!” “Excuse me?” “Try” Harder!” The sternness in Alex’s voice bordered the line of apathy and anger. His eyes narrowed as if the focus on his sewing had somehow fogged. “If you want to listen to everyone else then fine, let it fuel your self pity until you are crushed by the burden of it all. It’s one thing to be an outsider looking in, but a completely different thing to listen to those kinds of people.” I searched myself for a retort but was speechless. Alex had always been a vent for even the most mundane of my tribulations. Yet, I could see an aura of aggravation in him. His eyes softened as his view trailed to me. “Other people don’t have a clue what it means to be a De Hoyas, Bro. To them it means perfection, being better than the rest, and being a peon to an industry that is starting to have less and less regard for our safety!” He placed a hand on my shoulder reinforcing his comments with a lockjaw-like grip. “When me and Papa look at you, we see the potential to do great things. When others look at you, they see a kid, half their age, and half their experience, able to make miracles happen in that ring!” To Alex, it wasn’t a job; it was a way of life. The lights, the cameras, the fans; it was what he believed we were raised for. While I was still clinging to the lost memory of a childhood I would never get to experience, he was a firm believer that our sacrifices were for the betterment of the sport as a whole. Wrestling was something he took as serious as death. I only wished that I could possess his drive and motivation. “You’re young Julio; much too young to have such worries on your mind. We are part of a dynasty! Being a De Hoyas doesn’t just mean being better than everyone else. We are warriors, of an ancient kind. It means having the heart to continue on when others have thrown in the towel, to press on against all types of adversity, and to strive harder than our opponents for that ever illusive victory.” He said as he broke another piece of thread in his teeth and took my hand in his clutch. ?” Besides Kid, training is over!” His eyes hallowed as he jammed the needle into my palm! ~~~ “ALEX!” I was torn from my daydream, awakened to the prosaic vibrations in my five by five foot section of the train against my gagging breaths. I was still clutching my old acoustic, running a gentle hand over my brother’s picture taped to the back, and greeted to my reflection against the luminous Vancouver backdrop. My glance trailed to the seat across from me, where Brack sat with powdered donut in hand, wide-eyed, and in shock, as he stared at me like he had seen a ghost. Apparently my yelp had interrupted him somewhere in between a chew and a swallow as bits of powdered goodness crumbled from his chin. “Pwabwems?” muffled the stunned Canadian before a disgustingly loud gulp cleared his throat. Brack Conway was as close to a harmless parasite as an individual could get. I met him years ago during a short stint in Canada, at a training camp in Toronto. He had claimed to be something of a legend in those parts and most where either too friendly, or too apathetic to his ramblings to really attempt to disapprove his exasperated assertions of himself. He was a trainer, but you wouldn’t know it to look at him; from the balding gray hairs that littered his head or the tank of a gut he paraded around like a pair of wash board abs. He was a pleasant old man who was, at times, excitable and an amusement to have around. I didn’t have the heart to refuse his company when word had reached his ear that I was leaving back for Mexico to enter the J Cup. After people began to start asking questions I proclaimed him to be my manager; a label Brack took to heart! Even, going as far as to obtain his managing license from a commission in New York! He had promised me everything from riches and fame, to television spots and endorsements; promises that I never took to heart, but Mr. Conway was hell bend on trying to obtain for me through his people! I had often wondered whether ?his people? was referring to Canadians in general or contacts that he claimed to possess through out the world. “Sorry?” I muttered through gritted teeth. “Just a little nervous I guess.” “Shouldn’t be?” he said in between another handful of donuts. “You’re pure gold, kid” Better than perfect? you’re perfection personified!” A downpour began to spatter against my window as I gazed into Vancouver, settled just beyond the horizon. Something about the city just didn’t seem as majestic as I had imagined. Like a vulture it loomed over me. Taunting, poking, and prodding; waiting for me to break and turn around with tail tucked between my legs. But, I had no where to run, no home to go back to, and no fans on the home front waiting to pat me on the back for failure. “Brack, do you think I’m doing the right thing here” Signing a contract outside of Mexico, I mean!” I passed a few fingers over my guitar, letting them casually dance over the chords into something that resembled a tune. “Maybe I was just a little head strong about losing in the J Cup.” “Nonsense, JD!” Brack proclaimed as he paused, staring at his snack in sudden disgust. “It’s something new and exciting. You are going to rock their pants off. You’ll be the best thing to hit GGW since” since? the steel chair!” “It’s GCW, Brack!” “That’s what I said.” “Fine.” “Why?” “Why is it called GCW”!”” “No!” Brack shook his head, wiping the crumbs from his face. “Why here, why now, why GCW?” It was a new beginning, a chance to start something away from the judgmental ogles of my home country, and a place that I could leave my past behind. For years I had battled a seemingly unquenchable fear of failure. The family name and past disgrace had pressed on my shoulders for so long, that even thoughts of a network like PrimeTimeCentral brought a quiver through my hands. Until the Total J Cup made its way to Mexico. A plethora of talent flooded through the TJC, yet one place stuck out in my mind; one brand of competitors made me feel alive in that ring, one company showed me that I wasn’t invincible, one competitor showed me the heart it took to win? even after a horrid defeat. Possibly more so than my own! While most would have run in the opposite direction, I felt compelled to follow. The hint of blood had caught my nostrils; I was hungry for a battle that would tear my mind from the guilt ridden sob story that was my shame and raise my families name from the ashes of nonexistence. Yet, my answer was a bit less complicated. ?” For the competition, Brack!” I set my guitar on its side as I rested my head against the seat. ? No one can lays claims to being the best until they have looked at the best, eye to eye, and struck them down. They tell fairytales about wrestlers like Seymour, Tessa, and Knight.” ??and soon they will tell stories of you JD. You are in Brack country here. Hell I can even pull some strings to get you a decent themed entrance. Nothing but the best for my Superstars! You’re perfect, you’re the Man Without Fear, Baby.” He boasted waving a hand over an imaginary banner before he tipped a hat over his eyes with a yawn. “I don’t want to be perfect, Brack. I’m just trying not to be useless!” I rested my feet against the opposing chairs and finally gave into my fatigue. “But you are perfect, kid” You’re better than perfect... You’ll see? they’ll all see?” my manager sleepily whispered as I slipped into the spaces between consciousness and reality. ~~~ “Pinche Puto.” I shrieked, as Alex stuck me with his needle. I attempted to snatch my hand back, but his grip was tighter than anything I could have overpowered. He simply ignored my yelps, staring with frozen eyes as a trickle of blood ran down my hand. With careful precision, he stuck his own hand and sandwiched it over his work and my palm, collapsing my fingers around it. “This mask is now adorned with the blood of two Luchadors. Within it is the skill inherent in all our all blood. Let it forever represent the fact that, what ever the challenge, what ever the mistake, and whatever the power you possess, you are never alone. Let is represent the extra heart needed to succeed where others have failed before you.” He uttered behind closed eyes. I stopped squirming the moment I realized what he had put into my hand as he added, “Never forget where you came from, Julio! Never forget who you are.” “I’m” I’m done!” I stammered, finally peeling my hand away. “But, Papa said there was still so much for me to learn.” “From the day we step out of the womb until the day we die, we are always learning, Julio. But yes, as of today, you are officially a Luchador.” He nodded as I examined the mask that was inherent of Lucha Libre customs. “L.S.!”!” I questioned examining the letters that adorned the sides of the mask. “What for?” “La Sombra!” “The Shadow?” “We are, all of us, living in the shadow of what our father and grandfathers have plowed the way for in Lucha Libre. As a third generation Luchador, Julio, this burden will be a great weight to carry.” He stood, packing his needle and thread and added, “But, know that you are never fighting alone, you have the strength of three generations behind you.” “But who is going to respect a Luchador with such a horrid name?” “Respect will come with time and patience, Julio. Viva La Lucha Libre, little brother.”
