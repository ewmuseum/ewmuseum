Destroy All Vampires

17 June 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/17/08 “But if we don’t kill him before the big heavyweight boxing fight, everyone in Las Vegas will turn into a vampire,” the buxom blonde said, a forced sense of franticness tingeing her voice as she looked at the grizzled man in the leather trench coat. “I know that, but he is my son,” he replied as he lit a cigarette. “I just wish there was an easier way.” She put her hand on his shoulder. “I know,” she replied. “No one knows how it is to be me,” he shot back, taking a drag from his cig and withdrawing away from her touch. “Remember, I am the only known half-human, half-vampire.” “Then you don’t want anyone else to feel your pain.” He nodded. “Alright then. Let’s go and stop Draculard before he can make his unholy incantation.” The two ran off furiously into the direction of the Las Vegas strip, hurrying to make sure they would be there on time to stop the son of Abraham Van Helsing IV from turning the patrons of... “CUT!” someone screamed from the distance. “God damn it, Mindy, you popped out of your top again.” “Tee-hee, I did, didn’t I?” she replied. Mindy Baggins tucked her left breast back into her magenta-and-black striped tube top, while her co-star facepalmed. Frantically, a balding man carrying a bullhorn rushed onto the set, waving his arms furiously. “That’s the fourth time in two days that’s happened,” he said, exasperation dripping from each word. “Put on a fucking bra already.” “Oh no, I can’t do that,” she replied. “I have to be in character.” “How is it out of character for you to go braless” You’re a fucking hooker! There’s no dress code for a hooker!” The male actor stepped in between the two. “You know what” While you two give me Excedrin headache number forty-eight on this shoot, I’m gonna go to my trailer and take a nap,” he screamed, stomping off the soundstage. “But he doesn’t have a trailer,” Mindy sheepishly muttered. “You catch on quick, don’t you?” the balding man, the film’s director, said sardonically as he walked off the set himself. “Alright everyone, take five!” he shouted, while muttering under his breath “I’m gonna kill them for casting a porn star as the female lead...” His trailer ? a burnt orange 1995 Honda Civic hatchback with cracks in the paint and rusty bumpers ? was his place of solitude, to escape the more troublesome moments on shoots or to sleep off hangovers. More and more lately, he found himself escaping more frequently to his car to get away from the cacophony of ditzy silicone-injected bimbos cast in the dual role as female leads and avenues for gratuitous nudity, or from the self-absorbed actors whose pretentious melodrama belied the grade of movie they were in, or from the overbearing directors who thought they were making GoodFellas instead of Gangster Werewolves 3: The Lupine Yakuza. It used to be maybe once a shoot he’d have to come here, but now, it was grating on him every day at least once a day. People forgetting their lines, special effects that looked cheesy even by b-movie standards, breaks in production because someone on the crew left their position to go sell drugs to the “real” stars. It was getting to be too much. “LESTER!” A stern voice awoke Timothy Barrett Lester from his nap. It was the director. “You done napping yet?” “Yeah,” Tim said groggily, shaking off the sleep from his eyes in the Southern California heat. “Good, because I finally convinced Mindy to put on a fucking strapless bra. She should stop popping out now, and we can finish that scene.” “Yeah, that’d be great...” When Tim finally got home to his Pasadena apartment with a Jack in the Box combo for his dinner, it was 9:15 PM. He slapped the paper bag on his kitchen table and grabbed the bottle of Jack Daniels and a shot glass from his cabinet before sitting down. He pulled a cigarette from his pack of Camels, but before he could light it, his phone started ringing. “That’s odd,” he thought to himself as he got up to look at it. The caller ID read “Caldera Enterprises, Inc.” He picked it up quickly. “Hello.” “Mr. Lester, hello, this is Garrett Michaels from Caldera Enterprises, Incorporated. We spoke before.” “Yeah, yeah, I remember. But why are you calling so late?” “You said that was the best time to reach you.” Tim smiled. “Yeah, it was, wasn’t it?” “Mhm. Anyway, I was just calling to tell you that your tryout for GCW is confirmed for Saturday, May 24th, the date you pegged as your most convenient. Is that still the case?” Tim paused for a second before answering. “Yeah, oh yeah, it is.” “Alright, we’ll see you at 12 noon out at the Galen Center.” “Thanks!” Tim hung up the phone, exhaled a sigh of relief and sat down straighter than usual. After he poured his nightly dinner shot, he raised it in the air and toasted himself. “To starting over.” He knocked the liquor back and closed his eyes as he hunched lower and lower into his seat. The next day, Tim showed up on set with a little more spring to his step than usual. His eyes didn’t show the bloodshot signs of getting blasted the night before on only two hours sleep. The fact that you could see his eyes was amazing; he usually sported his trademark black shades even indoors. “Why you so happy?” a co-star assailed him before even saying “yo.” “What, this is unusual?” Tim replied, words dripping with sarcasm. “Don’t shit me, Tim. One of three things happened last night,” he said. “One, you got some non-co-star trim. Two, the Ravens traded for Peyton Manning, or three... you landed a starring role in a BIG film.” “Y’know what Rick, you’re right,” Tim replied. “One of those three did happen last night.” “Well, I know it ain’t the football thing,” Rick Derkins, the brash young actor who was playing Draculard, “Else I’da heard about it too. And you look too damn scruffy for pussy...” “That just leaves number three, don’t it?” Tim replied. “No way,” said Rick. “Who else is starring in it?” Tim smirked. “Let me put it to you this way. It’s not exactly a motion picture.” “TV” You’re on a TV show?” “I guess you could say that...” It took Rick a moment before it finally hit him. “Oh wait... you’re going back into wrestling! I knew it!” Tim shook his head. “Yeah, real genius you are,” he replied, taking a cigarette from his front pocket and lighting it. “Har har, yeah, spare your sarcasm and tell me, what fed you sign with?” “I ain’t signed with no one yet.” “Oh yeah, then why are you so bubbly.” “Look,” Tim stated, and then paused. “Look, I ain’t no fuckin” starlet. I am NOT bubbly. And for the record, it’s a tryout with GCW.” Rick started to chuckle. “GCW, eh” That should be right up your alley.” “And what the fuck do you mean by that?” “They have all those vampires and shit, man.” Tim shook his head. “Yeah, real funny. For the record, smartass, you and me are more vampire than anyone in that fed, and that’s to say they ain’t no vampires. It’s called a fucking gimmick, you tool, and furthermore...” Before Tim could get the rest of what he was saying out of his mouth, the trailer door flew open and out walked a short, balding man with a full brown beard (though not the same balding man as the film’s director). He walked with somewhat of a limp, hobbling over to where all the cast and crew was preparing to shoot for the day. “Alright, shitheads, listen up. Your Saturdays off, over from here on in,” he shouted to groans from his crew. The director stood forward and moaned, “C’mon Phil, you heartless bastard, my son’s got softball on Saturdays. I told him I’d be there.” “I told him I’d be there,” preened Phil Diamond. “That’s why you’re directing these kinds of shitfests instead of doing A-list movies. Too concerned about softball. That’s why you’re all here. You’re all not all-in for a movie career.” “And what about you, Napoleon?” sneered Tim. “Oh shut up,” replied Phil. “You think you’re so cool, Mr. Cult Hero, well I got news for you. You can sling all the names at me you want, but you still have to understand that I’m the fucking producer on this movie, and that I make sure it makes money. The studio is telling me that in order to make money, we have to film at least six fucking days a week.” “So what, they can release the DVD in time for Flag Day?” asked Tim sarcastically. “Seriously Phil, we’re almost fucking done. Shooting an extra day ain’t gonna make much of a difference.” “Oh really, big shot” I don’t see you having any other projects lined up that you can be arguing the new direction.” “Of course you don’t, Phil. All you do all day is sit in your office and coordinate drug deals while we’re out here on a less than shoestring budget making a movie that, if we’re lucky, would make us four bucks a week in residuals getting played at 3 A.M. on Cinemax every Thursday and Sunday. Well, I ain’t down for that, and I ain’t gonna give up my hangover day just so you can finish up production in enough time to see your shipment of China White come in from Thailand.” “Yeah, real fucking wiseguy you are. What makes you think I won’t can you right now, especially after making those slanderous accusations?” “Because we’re more than three-quarters shot and you can’t change this far into it. Plus, Van Helsing’s Brood is my franchise, and the studio won’t stand for anyone else.” Phil groaned, knowing everything Tim said was true. “I want to see your ass in my trailer now, you son of a bitch. Everyone else, get back to work.” Phil stormed off to his trailer. A smile of satisfaction on his face as he dragged off his cig, Tim stood and waited until the diminutive producer entered his trailer. The director came over to him and tapped him on his shoulder. “Thanks for standing up for me.” Tim turned around and replied, “And what the fuck makes you think I was doing his for you?” The director looked stunned as Tim put his cig out on the back of his left hand and went into the trailer. He entered to find Phil facing his wall, hands on hips, foot tapping. “You rang?” Tim said mockingly. Phil turned around, his face beet red. He went right up to Tim and got into his face... well, into his chest looking up at his face. “Just who in the fuck do you think you are, motherfucker” Showing me up like that in front of everyone out there. What gives you the right?” “Um, the fact that I’m the star and you’re the drug dealer?” Phil turned away in a huff and shuffled towards his desk. He spun around hurriedly and pointed at Tim. “Fuck you, alright” Fuck you. I don’t need your shit. Just why are you doing this to me?” “Doing to you” Really, that’s how you want to play this? Let’s forget that you lowballed me for this movie despite the fact that the studio authorized you to pay me at least thirty percent more, and I went along with it despite the fact that rent went up at my apartment again and gas ain’t getting cheaper, or that you pawned off most of our good safety nets so you could pretend to be a fucking A-lister and get into that party LiLo and her dyke DJ friend were throwing in Vegas. Hey, how about you selling drugs to every stuntman in Hollywood that’ll buy...” “That’s enough.” The veins in Phil’s forehead looked as if they were about to pop. “You shithead, I don’t give you shit for all the shit you pull.” “Shit that I pull?” Tim looked at Phil bewilderedly. “Let’s get it straight, Napoleon. I drink Jack like it was Kool-Aid and smoke three packs a day. I bang anything that moves. Yeah, the fucking Jehovah’s Witnesses that knock on my fucking door every other week might disapprove, but despite all that, I think I lead a pretty fucking normal lifestyle compared to you.” “Fuck you, alright” Fuck you. Why the hell are you so fucking adamant having off Saturday?” “Fuck off, Napoleon. I don’t need to answer to you.” Phil squinted his eyes and scrunched his face. “You motherfucker. I should fire you on the spot.” “I already told you, Napoleon,” Tim interrupted. “If you think the studio’s up your ass about filming now, think about how they’ll be once they find out you fired the lead.” “You fucking asshole...” “Foul mouth on you, eh?” “Is that a fucking joke?” Phil shook his head, then turned around and sat at his desk. He pulled out a bottle of Excedrin from one drawer and a handle of Banker’s Club gin from the other. He gulped two pills and chased it with a deep, long swig of swill. “Look, just tell me why you need off on Saturday.” Tim sneered again. “You really want to know?” Exasperatedly, Phil nodded, closing his eyes and rubbing his temples simultaneously. “I got a tryout with GCW.” Just as soon as Tim uttered the last letter, Phil’s eyes lit up like a Christmas tree. “Wrestling” You’re leaving this shitty industry for that one? You must be a glutton for fucking punishment!” Tim shook his head. “Yeah, because leaving this job, where I’m lucky to make five figures over a fucking four month shoot, for one with a downside guarantee of at least 85 grand a year is punishment.” “Yeah, but you have to go out there every night, and I heard the promoters try to double cross you for your money.” “Hey, dickhead, newsflash. This ain’t the 70s anymore. I’d be working for Steven Caldera. Dude ain’t gonna jip me out of money. I’m not working the indies. I’d be in a steady job for a major fed.” “Be that as it may, you’re still putting your body at risk...” “I do my own stunts, remember” That’s why all you sleazy fucks like me. I save you money out of the budget that you can spend on hookers.” “Fuck you... but remember this. You take a shitpile more risks in that business than you do here. And sooner or later, you’re gonna get hurt real bad, especially now. You, in your early thirties, going up against kids that are doing things that you only dreamed of doing back before you got hurt. And when you do get hurt, you’re gonna take one too many Xanax with your fucking quart of whiskey. Then it’ll be Oxycodone, then heroin. Then, next thing you know, FUCKING BAM. You’re dead in a hotel room because your ticker blew up, just like that motherfucker Rollins.” “Yeah, thanks for the pep talk, Napoleon, but I don’t think I need to talk to you anymore. We got a movie to film, remember?” “Just remember. You ain’t in your twenties. Those punks that are doing all that flippy bullshit are. You’re outclassed, Tim. Outfuckingclassed.” He turned his back to the producer and walked back out to the set. Tim plopped down on his sofa after a hard day on set and turned his television on. Without even hitting the guide button on his remote, the TV was set to X TV, GCW WorldWide, Keegan O’Conner against Zacharia Taylor, just as O’Conner kicked out of Taylor’s Dragon suplex pinning attempt. Tim sat staring at the television as Taylor seamlessly transitioned from a vertical suplex into a Dragon sleeperhold, at O’Conner shaking off punishment that in Tim’s day, would have put an average competitor down and a boxing combo as crisp as the ones Mike Tyson would throw in the first round in the “80s. Realizing his daze, Tim shook his head and hit the DVR button on his remote, saved programs. He cycled through his cache, all wrestling matches. Evolution Pro Wrestling’s return edition of RiOT, Will the Thrill vs. Alex Gray. New Frontier Wrestling Crash .44 Magnum, Impulse vs. Rook Black. TEAM SuperShow VII, Nova vs. Dan Ryan. Ringside Wrestling, Gunther Hunt and Arch Angel from Tradition #3. A wealth of other matches he couldn’t help but watch and look in awe. He went through all of those matches again and again. And every time he watched a high-impact suplex or a twisting backflip splash, Phil Diamond’s voice kept ringing in the back of his mind... “Outfuckingclassed.” For Tim, high noon on a Saturday was usually the time the sun burnt his ass enough to make him crawl out of bed and swing around to Jack in the Box for a burger. This Saturday, however, he was pulling up to USC’s indoor sports arena in his Honda Civic with a cup of coffee, dressed in his ring attire underneath his jeans and t-shirt. A slight nervous twinge tingled his belly, one that differed from the twinge he usually got from coffee or a hangover. He grabbed his bag out of the hatchback and walked into the building, making his way out to the main arena where a ring was already set up with a spry youngster inside. There were three men in suits, all representative of CEI. One stepped forward, hand outstretched. He had curly brown hair and tanned skin. “Hello Tim. I’m Garrett Michaels, we spoke over the phone.” Tim shook his hand. “Good to meet you.” “Likewise. My associates here are also from Caldera Enterprises, Michael Lowen and James Murphy. They’re ancillary talent scouts. You’ll be in the ring with one of our guys from developmental today, and based on what we see from you, we’ll make a decision whether to offer you a contract. Is that alright?” Tim hesitated before he answered, a lump forming in his throat. “Uh yeah, sure. You guys are the boss.” “Good,” Garrett replied. “You can start whenever you want.” At that cue, he unbuckled his belt and was about to drop trough. “Relax everyone, I’ve got my gear on underneath.” He dropped his pants, showing his old tights that still read “Skyhook” across the ass, a relic from days gone by, but his only wrestling-ready attire. He stripped down to his tights and boots, hopped up onto the apron and entered the ring apprehensively, the gravity of the situation weighing on his mind like a fat chick trying to ride a little 99 pound nerd at a college party. He stared across the ring at the gaunt bag of skin and bones with dirty blonde curly locks on top of his head. He ate kids like that for lunch back in the indies, but that was another time, when he was the innovator and they were wet behind the ears. Now, this little kid was a Kimbusa-sized roadblock into getting out of movies. “Mr. Lester.” The silence was broken. Tim looked up at his sparring partner who just called his name. “Mr. Lester, I just wanna say that Men Are from Mars, Zombie Cheerleaders Are from Venus is one of my favorite...” “Kid, I appreciate it, but I ain’t here for pleasantries.” “Sorry.” He sheepishly stepped back before Tim lunged forward to grapple. They tussled for a few moments before the beanpole got the upper hand, twisting Tim’s arm behind his back with relative ease. Tim leaned forward and grabbed the ropes. “Break,” commanded Garrett from the outside. All Tim heard in his mind though wasn’t Garrett. “Outfuckingclassed.” After the break, Tim turned around to find the stick figure hopping around like he was Muhammad Ali floating like a butterfly. Tim lunged at him again, only to have the scrawny kid jump to his right, kick out Tim’s left knee from him, rush to the ropes and drill the side of his head with a springboard dropsault. Tim’s ears rung like the bells at St. Philip the Apostle every Sunday morning. Above the din, he could hear a voice repeating the same word... “Outfuckingclassed.” By the time Tim got up, he saw the lanky lad on the top rope, leaping at him with all the recklessness of a green stuntman. Landing seated on Tim’s shoulders, the kid transitioned from a rana attempt almost seamlessly into a tornado DDT. How the hell did he pull that off before Tim could throw him off? Was he really that out of touch? The word repeated itself loudest again... “Outfuckingclassed.” Tim looked out at the CEI suits, one guy taking notes, the other shaking his head. And Garrett? Watching intently, hoping he didn’t misplace his faith and waste expense money on a plane ticket from Ohio all the way out to SoCal. Before he could catch his breath, Tim felt the skeleton’s bony arms around his throat in a headlock. The word repeated in his head again, but this time, instead of visualizing his shortcomings and Rich Rollins dead in a hotel room, he envisioned the man who said that word, who doubted him. He saw everything, from the sweat beading on his bald head to the hatchet job of a trim he gave his beard to his gimpy ass hobbling across the set. Fuck you, Phil Diamond. Tim found a burst of energy and powered to his feet, driving back and slamming that high school-looking developmental project back-first into the turnbuckle. Fuck you, Rick Derkins. Free of the stranglehold and seeing his opponent dazed, Tim rushed in, kicking the kid in his gut and planting him head-first on the mat with a DDT, the thud echoing through the mostly empty arena. Fuck you, Suzie Baggins. Stomping his aggressions on the poor greenhorn lessened the tension from his muscles. His head didn’t ring anymore. He didn’t hear singular voices or see dead wrestlers. Instead, he heard the roar of the crowd and saw thousands of cheering fans in the Galen Center seats. He lifted his partner to his feet and whipped him off the ropes, sending him to the canvas with a lariat. Fuck you, greedy studios. From there, he climbed the top rope, looking out, basking in the imaginary glow of the roaring masses in his head. He jumped from the turnbuckle, bouncing off the rope, springboarding himself onto the nearly limp body of his corpse-girthed opponent. He broke out his old finishing move, the springboard splash. It felt right. It felt like he had been doing it forever instead of dusting it off for the first time in 10 years. He hooked the leg. Fuck you, everyone... I’m following my dream. “Alright, that’s enough,” Garret commanded. Tim got up, shaking the daze from his head. The crowd was gone. The arena silent. He turned around to find Garrett Michaels, looking him straight in the eye, stern look on his face. He nodded slightly while keeping the air tense for the next few moments. Finally, he broke the tension, and extended his hand. “Welcome to GCW, Tim.” Assist to James von Doom’s write-up of Taylor/O’Conner from WorldWide 90
