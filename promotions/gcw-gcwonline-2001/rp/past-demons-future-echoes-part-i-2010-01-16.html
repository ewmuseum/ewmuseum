Past Demons, Future Echoes [part I]

16 January 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/16/10 "Stand by to receive our transmission" The unmistakable voice of Captain James Tiberius Kirk echoed out from his pocket. Immediately he felt the burning sensation of embarrassment wash over his face, his cheeks quickly turning a shade of crimson as he felt the sting of dagger-like glares pierce him. Looking up bashfully he forced an apologetic smile in the direction of the eyes that met his glance as he reached into the pocket, making a point of ensuring he took this reminder to turn his cellphone onto it’s silent settings. Now taking the time to examine the message he had just received he couldn’t help but feel a little surprised with the contents. It was the general notification sent out by his employers, Global Championship Wrestilng, to all of their active competitors, containing the card for the upcoming edition of their flagship broadcast, Worldwide. That in itself wasn’t really much of a surprise, he had received the same notification every week since he had arrived in the company some eight months previous. What had taken him by surprise was the match in which he had been booked to compete in. He read it again, his mind demanding that he check his eyes had not deceived him. They had not, this week, he would be competing in a one on one match against the current GCW Television Champion, Cheap Labor, with the championship itself on the line. He felt his lip curl into a wry smile, this was not something he had anticipated, nonetheless it was a surprise which was more than welcome. The last few months had been full of surprises that had not been quite so pleasant, but this was an indication that his talent had not gone unnoticed, and for a man trying to rebuild his career, this was encouragement indeed. But at this moment, even that great news was unable to maintain its place at the front of his mind, for he was looking for something, something he had to see, if only to prove to himself that he had not imagined it. He shoved the phone back into his pocket and turned his attention back to the screen in front of him. It displayed the contents of a newspaper, specifically, the Wrestling Observer Newsletter. He quickly flicked through the subsequent pages, his eyes darting back and forth as he quickly scanned the information. Still no sign of the article he had spent the last three and a half hours searching for, but he tightened his resolve, determined to continue until he had found it. Since the 5th of December, one thought had consumed his every waking moment. It had tormented him to the point of obsession, had he simply imagined it? He was beginning to doubt himself now but in the pit of his stomach there was an uneasy feeling, if his mind had fabricated the story to deceive him he was sure that he would not be troubled by this sensation. Donny Diamond. Those two words were the cause of his concern, partly fuelled by the brutal history he shared with him, and, well, as for the other concern, he needed to see it to be sure once more. Donny Diamond was a professional wrestler quite unlike many others. He had been something of a phenomenon in the early half of the last decade, leaving a string of battered opponents in his wake. Two of those opponents meant a very great deal to Seán O'Neill, he felt a shudder pass through him as long forgotten memories battled to the forefront of his mind. His greatest friend and ally, The Real Deal, had been one of the victims of Diamond’s reign of terror. Their viscous rivalry had taken on such intensity that both men became less and less concerned for their physical well-being with every encounter. O’Neill’s concern for his friend had gone unheeded as The Real Deal, blinded by the need to stand victorious over Diamond, took greater and greater risks. The writing was on the wall, the rivalry went too far, in the end it was Diamond who had emerged on top and his pride had cost The Real Deal a price no wrestling match was worth. He had been left crippled, unsure if he would ever be able to stand upright without support ever again. Diamond had crossed the line, he had gone too far. And it fell to O’Neill to show him just how far. And so it was, that on what was scheduled to be the pinnacle of Donny Diamond’s career, the Living Nightmare had to endure a hellish retribution. Diamond had been scheduled to compete against the DWO World Heavyweight Champion, Draven in a Glass Tables match. The wrestling world had tuned in with bated breath to witness this titanic encounter. However, Donny Diamond would not compete for any championship on that occasion, instead of Draven, Diamond found himself caught unawares as SurReal marched down to the ring. They say that vengeance is a dish best served cold, but the warmth of bloodshed served to banish that theory on that horrendous night. SurReal and Diamond beat each other to within an inch of their lives. O’Neill felt utter satisfaction as he recalled how he had ended the match leaving his bloody and battered opponent in need of urgent medical attention, how he had gained some small measure of payback for his friend. It felt good. A distracting thought forced its way into his head, he hadn’t confirmed his availability for Worldwide. As if it really needed confirmed! He was hardly going to turn down an opportunity to take on Cheap Labor, not only is he the Television Champion, but he is also one of the fastest rising stars in Global Championship Wrestling. Nobody in their right mind would turn this opportunity down. That said, he knew that if he didn’t confirm his participation soon, then that opportunity would befall someone else. He couldn’t let that happen. He took his eyes off the monitor in front of him for the first time since he had sat back down, his tired eyes took almost a minute to recover enough to survey the scene. The other library users who had looked so outraged the last time his phone had been used were still there, one of them, a short stumpy man glared up at him, still clearly unwilling to forgive the earlier interruption. Deciding it would be best to find a quiet corner to make his call in he got up, leaving his bag on the chair and a few personal effects around the computer, he hadn’t searched all this time for someone else to come along and lose his place after all! He made the pretence that he was looking for a particular book and shuffled quickly and quietly down one of the library’s maze-like isles. When he felt sure he was out of sight and hearing distance he quickly pulled the phone out of his pocket and held in the key bearing the number four. That particular key was the pre-stored quick-dial to Christian Zenith’s office. Not that Zenith would be taking the call, he never did, O’Neill knew before she answered, that it would be his personal assistant who would be on the other end of the call. "Hello, SurReal here. Just confirming that I got this week’s card and that there is no issues at my end." He listened inattentively as she rattled on through the tedious list of information she had to provide once a wrestler confirmed he would be competing. Truth be told, at this stage he was more concerned that at any moment he would be apprehended by the silent army of librarians! "Sorry, I missed that, where did you say it was? Austin, Texas? Fantastic, thanks again." He quickly slid the phone back in his pocket and with a final glance around to check he had got away with it. Excellent, that was that out of the way, now he could get back to business. Thinking that his less than friendly neighbour might whisper abuse in his ear should he return empty handed he reached for the nearest book, it would do just fine! Sitting back down at his desk he had to restrain a laugh as, unsurprisingly, the short fat little man peered over at him with a judging look on his face. O’Neill, unable to help the smug grin that crept across his face, raised the random book he had brought with him, motioning that he had found what he was looking for. He thought he almost heard a disapproving grunt as he sat back down, but that only served to widen his grin. Shifting his focus back to the particular edition of the Wrestling Observer Newsletter now displayed on the screen he saw that he was into the year 2005. Still no sign of the article, it couldn’t be much further on though he reassured himself as he plunged back into his search. It was not long though, before his thoughts turned to his other friend who had suffered at the hands of Donny Diamond, Kaiowas. Kaiowas. No other man had made such an impact on SurReal’s career. When they had first met, O’Neill was already a very good wrestler, but he lacked the drive and direction to take himself to the top. It had been Kaiowas who instilled these values in him. Kaiowas was not your run of the mill wrestler you see, as a matter of fact, when last O’Neill had seen him, Kaiowas had become the chief of his tribe, the Ktulu. Kaiowas had brought O’Neill into the tribe and set about opening his mind to an entirely different way of living. Granted that the differences in their respective backgrounds and cultures were vast, the two men found that their cultures also had many similarities. It could be said I suppose, that the savage Brazilian ‘cultured’ the Irishman, he made him aware of how to channel his culture and heritage and take strength and encouragement from them. Under Kaiowas’s tutelage, SurReal had been reborn! Kaiowas in turn had benefited from SurReal’s ability in the art of scientific wrestling, and had picked up enough to skills to add to his overwhelming strength and size. Kaiowas was now an incredible force to be reckoned with in the ring, it wasn’t long before he won the DWO World Heavyweight Championship himself. He held the belt for longer than anyone in the company’s history, his reign though was continually under threat from a constant challenger who refused to give up, Donny Diamond. The two men engaged in some of the most memorable encounters in recent memory. It seemed that no matter what Donny Diamond did, he would not be able to defeat the Tribal Warrior, and yet, eventually he found a way, and fulfilled his own destiny, becoming at last the very best at what he did. The fact remained though, that no-one ever held such dominance over Diamond. If anyone knew how to beat Donny Diamond, it was Kaiowas! The thought played over and over in his mind. If anyone knew how to beat Donny Diamond, it was Kaiowas! He tried to shake the thought, for as close as they had been, not even Seán O'Neill knew how to find Kaiowas now. If only he could reach out to his mentor for help, the thought was futile however, he knew that it wasn’t an option, no matter how attractive the thought seemed. His thoughts turned again to his upcoming match with Cheap Labor, he wondered if Kaiowas was here, perhaps he might feel as proud if SurReal was victorious as he himself had been watching Kaiowas all those years before. But, of course, Kaiowas wasn’t here. A depressing realisation occurred to him, he was on his own now. He tried to think of any time in his long career when he had been so utterly isolated. No occasion sprung to mind, he had always had someone he could turn to, but right about now the only person who even feigned support was Lori Equinox, and there would never be a time thought O’Neill that he would ever trust that snake. Equinox had made his life a living hell prior to his arrival in GCW. Somehow, his claims of being a changed man we’re not something he would, nor could, accept. No, when he walked out to try and beat Cheap Labor he would be alone. If he managed to wrestle the Television Championship from Labor’s grasp, he would celebrate alone. It was an empty feeling, but he didn’t have to suffer it long. As he flicked across to the next page on his monitor, he saw it, staring back at him. The article that had haunted him for over a month! It was real! He hadn’t dreamt it. His eyes flicked furiously through the article taking in every single detail. The answer he had found at last, but unfortunately it opened up endless number of new questions in the former’s stead. Quickly finishing the article his eyes darted to the headline once more and he gazed at it with such intensity that a passer-by might have assumed he was in a trance. Over and over he read it. FORMER WORLD CHAMPION COMMITS SUICIDE He lifted his hands to support his head, it felt as though it was going to explode with question after question. But the questions would have to wait, first he needed to fully digest the information in front of him. Donny Diamond was dead...
