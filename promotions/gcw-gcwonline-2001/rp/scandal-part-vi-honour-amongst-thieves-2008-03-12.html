SCANDAL PART VI: HONOUR AMONGST THIEVES

12 March 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/12/2008 INTIMIDATION “You ain’t walking away from this one without giving me a name, McKail,” Detective Faremont announced. Standing, he placed both hands upon the table and learnt forward, desperately attempting to be menacing. He failed miserably. “So who was it?” Jacob McKail glared back at him, unflinching and leant back in his chair. Last time a man intimidated him, the other guy had at least two hundred pounds on Faremont and was about a foot taller. Damn sigh ugly as he recalled too. The good detective continued, oblivious. “Who murdered David Baxter?” McKail shook his head. “I don’t know.” It was a lie and they both knew it. HITTING OU “Look what we got here boys,” the eerily familiar voice of the muscle-bound moron began, “entertainment.” McKail just took another mouthful of his bourbon and went about locating the extra packet of cigarettes he knew he’d bought earlier that morning, despite knowing all too well they were talking about him. Fuck “em, he thought, if they want trouble, they can damn well wait till I’ve had a smoke. “Jacob McKail, GCW superstar and fucking traitor,” the moron continued. “The only man I know, who gives dog-shit a bad name and from my neighbourhood, that’s saying something.” Locating the cigarettes in his jacket pocket, McKail extracted the packet and proceeded to unwrap the damn thing all the while trying to pin the voice to a face and a name, without having to look at the bastard. “Don’t you remember me, boy?” The moron asked. McKail could smell his god-awful a aftershave get stronger and feel his presence get nearer. “We worked together in Chicago about five years back, you know, before you got rich and famous, and before you sold us all down the river.” Tex McGraw was the name and now the face was coming to him too; battle weary and worn before his time with too much beer and not enough sleep. But McKail knew better than anybody that’s what the road can do to a man. Even one so goddamn tough as “Big” Tex McGraw. They were tag partners as McKail recalled, for a brief time at least. Perhaps even friends. That ugly bastard had McKail’s back in and out of the ring more times than he’d care to recall. McKail shook away the thoughts, put the cigarette into his mouth and lit it. All of that shit was in the past now, no point dredging up old memories of how they were buddies back in the day. Times had changed and it’d be easier to get through whatever shit McGraw was going to put him through if he remembered it. “I bet you’re feeling pretty good about yourself right about now, huh?” McGraw continued. “I bet with your pockets lined with all that dirty money you earned throwing those matches the way you did, life’s nice and easy for you, huh?” McKail took a drag of his cigarette and exhaled. McGraw chuckled. “I bet you and Mary are living the high life now, huh?” At the sound of her name, McKail stopped dead. He hadn’t been expecting it and it’d caught him off-guard. He’d clean forgotten McGraw had met Mary a couple of times when they were on the road together. He stayed over on the couch a couple of times and Mary would always cook him eggs just the way he liked them. “Mary’s dead,” McKail muttered, finishing his drink and nodded toward the bartender to order another. But the bartender was frozen and simply blinked at him, pressed back against the wall as far away as possible. For a time, McGraw was silent. The news of Mary’s death had come as something as a shock to him and he was unsure what to do or to say or whether his anger was even still valid. In the end, he decided that it was. “Well, I am sorry to hear that,” McGraw began, “but I reckon if she were alive, she’d be damn well ashamed of you. Don’t ya think?” McKail could contain himself no longer. It was one thing for McGraw to question the morality of his actions, whether they’re accurate or otherwise, but it was something else entirely for a virtual stranger to second-guess whether his wife may or may not approve of him or his way of life. Fuck him! McKail stood immediately, swivelled on the spot and caught McGraw with a fast jab to the jaw, then took a few steps backwards to inspect the damage. McGraw stumbled backwards, gently rubbed his jaw and then grinned at him. It was only then did McKail realise McGraw wasn’t alone. Stood with him were five others and judging by their size, McKail guessed they were in the industry too. “At least you learned how punch,” McGraw noted, with a chuckle. “That’ll help. When I told my friends here that I’d heard you were in town, well, they just insisted on tagging along.” Fuck him! Fuck his friends! Fuck them all! McKail took a final drag of his cigarette and threw it vaguely in their direction, whilst at the same time charging at the bar to his left, leaping upward, planting both feet upon the bar and then pushing himself off and tumbling into at least three of McGraw’s entourage sending them and a few others to the floor hard. By the time McKail got back to his feet again, the majority of the bar had become engulfed in the brawl. DIVIDE AND CONQUER The prison cell opened with an age-old groan and immediately McKail felt all eyes fix on him, as the dutiful police officer unlocked his handcuffs and shoved him inside. McKail ignored the suspicious and angry glances and went about rubbing the sores on his wrists caused by the too tight cuffs. “Thanks for the help, Jacob.” What the fuck...” McKail silently questioned, quickly turning on the spot. It was Detective Faremont. He stood at the other side of the wire mesh wearing a toothy grin. This can’t be good...McKail had wondered why the good detective had allowed to take leave of the interrogation room, without being of much assistance. At the time, he just thought Faremont had had enough of McKail’s infuriating stoicism and decided to move onto a more cooperative target, but it’d become abundantly clear that the good detective was playing another game altogether. “We’ll let you know get those requests organised for you.” Detective Faremont left with a wink and chuckle, whispering something into the supervising officer’s ear as he went. On McKail’s left, McGraw and his cabal of demented wrestlers stood and drew closer and on his right, the other patrons of the bar who’d become embroiled in the brawl did the same. “What did you tell them?” One of the wrestlers demanded. “How did you slime your way out of this one, McKail?” McKail glared at him, but remained silent. He didn’t need to explain himself to any of these people and he had no intention of doing so. Fuck “em... “You must’ve told them something,” another person asked. McKail didn’t recognise the voice. “That Detective seemed awful friendly.” On his right, Dexter Munroe pushed his way to the front of the group and looked him right in the eye. McKail didn’t like it one bit. He felt guilty enough for virtually starting the brawl, but lying to the man’s face about what happened was something else entirely. David Baxter was Dexter’s best friend and he damn well deserved to know what happened, he deserved to know who killed him. Thing was, it wasn’t McKail’s place to say a damned thing; it was the place of those responsible. “You know something don’t you?” Dexter asked. “You saw what happened to Baxter, right?” McKail remained silent for a time, still unable to even look at the man. “Well?” Dexter persevered, clear undertones of anger in his voice now. “Did you?” “Are you people dense?” It was McGraw. Rich, coming from him, McKail idly thought, watching his former friend push to the front of everybody and purposefully position himself between McKail and Dexter. “If the police had made a deal with McKail, you really think that fucker would let us know about it” He’s trying to play us.” The wrestler scratched his head and developed a confused look. “How?” McGraw rolled his eyes. “Think about it, Muldoon. He wants us to tear him apart, turn on each other and give him exactly what he wants.” Muldoon nodded. “Sneaky fucker. But why choose McKail?” “Probably the same reason we chose to go looking for him,” McGraw reasoned. “They reckon he’s up to no good throwing matches and turning an illegal profit and reckon he’s a likely target.” “And why the hell isn’t he just telling us this?” Another of the wrestlers asked. McGraw glanced at McKail for a second and then down at his feet. “Knowing McKail, because he doesn’t feel the need to explain himself to us.” “Is it really such a bad thing?” Dexter asked. Everyone looked at him questioningly; except McKail. “For the truth to come out, I mean” If anybody saw who stabbed Baxter, I sure as hell want to know who!” “I saw who did it,” McGraw announced. McKail glared at him questioningly, unsure of what to make of it. “It happened right in front of me. Some guy pulled a blade and stuck it to him.” McKail shook his head, whilst McGraw could only look at his feet. “Who?” Dexter asked, his voice tinted with a potent combination of urgency and anger. “Was it somebody in here, with us?” A wave of paranoia and curiosity swept over the room, everybody glared at everybody else questioningly, whereas McKail stared only at McGraw who stared right back at him sullenly. McGraw shook his head. “No. He was one of the few that got away before the cops busted in.” “He’s dead,” McKail announced, his eyes still burning a hole in McGraw’s face. Now all eyes attached themselves to McKail, some out of shock he actually spoke, but most more interested in the news. “Baxter died.” “W-w-what did you say?” Dexter asked, his lip quivering. “He’s...dead?” McGraw limply wandered back over to the side of the cell and slumped down on the floor and buried his head in his hands. ACTS OF VIOLENCE McKail’s aerial ability and his natural deviousness only got him so far. Whilst the bar had become a battlefield all around him, one of the wrestlers had managed to tackle him to the ground and then hold him for a few of the others to extract their revenge the only way they knew how - violently. A few body blows later, it was McGraw’s turn and he had something special arranged. He pulled the blade from the inside of his cowboy boot and held there for a few seconds for everybody to see, all the while wearing a sadistic grin. The others looked at each other shocked, some shook their heads whilst others tried to talk him out of it, but McGraw didn’t want to know. “I not gonna kill him,” he told them. “I’m just gonna cut him a little. Make it so he remembers us and more importantly remembers what he’s done.” The others grin and McGraw wastes no time in lunging forward with the knife. But McKail is ready for it. He kicked at the leg of the man holding him to throw him off balance and then dragged him to the ground, and watched as the blade skimmed them both by a gnats wing and planted itself within some other poor soul who just happened to be standing behind them. David Baxter. HONOUR “You should have told them,” McGraw said, taking a seat next to him in the bar. “I wouldn’t have blamed you if you had.” McKail took a generous mouthful of his bourbon and didn’t bother to acknowledge his former friend once he was done. The police had released the majority of them only five minutes ago due to lack of evidence and cooperation, and McKail’s top priority beyond that was to get himself a drink and fast. McGraw eyed him warily. “It was a very...honourable thing to do.” “I didn’t do it for you,” McKail muttered. “But why?” McGraw asked. “You don’t owe me anything. You’ve already turned your back on the wrestling community, so you don’t live by their code of honour anymore...” The truth of the matter was, the wrestling community stuck together no matter what, allies and enemies alike. Sometimes it meant covering for them. Sometimes it meant watching their backs. Sometimes it mean lying for them. It was a cutthroat business and they could only count on each other to survive it. And that’s precisely why they hated McKail. To them, it was a slap in the face, a betrayal of everything they’d held dear. He couldn’t be trusted and they were all silently waiting for him to stab them in the back. McKail didn’t answer him, but didn’t need to. A wave of realisation swept over McGraw’s face and he buried his head in his hands. “You didn’t do it, did you?” He asked. “It’s all bullshit, right” You didn’t throw those matches. You couldn’t have done. If they had proof, you’d be in prison already...or dead.” McGraw chuckled and slapped him on his back. “You had honour all along...” McKail glared at him, his eyes alight with rage. “Don’t speak to me about honour!” McGraw looked at him a little taken aback, but truth be told he knew exactly what was coming. “You killed a man attempting to maim me and for what” Because you believed some bullshit the media spouted? Then you allowed me to lie for you, all the while accusing some mystery man of committing the crime.” McKail finished his drink and stood to leave. “No. You don’t even no what honour is!” With that he stood and made his way to the exit, leaving McGraw sitting at the bar contemplating his future, a lone tear rolling down his check.
