Jumping ahead.

11 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/11/06 After three rounds, Jayce ended up being the first one eliminated in the fourth round of GTT5. After all of his hard work in getting to that point, some damn Mick had sequestered the spot he so rightfully deserved. A shot he had fought tooth and nail with various opponents to acquire, only to watch the drunken bastard piss it all away like a bottle of ale from the previous night’s pleasantries. It was a damn shame, to find out that a man that was considered better than you could not muster up the talent to even show up for the bracket’s final round. Still, at least Rich Rollins was moving on and jamming the GCW name down everyone’s throats along the way. It would have been nice though, to stand across from the Global Champion and take the fight to him instead of rolling over and just letting him steamroll through. His credibility as deserving of the spot in the semifinals will surely be questioned, but it won’t matter. Rollins was certain to become the eventual winner of the fifth Golden Turnbuckle Tournament the moment the referee’s hand slapped the mat for the third and final time, in Rollins? opening round match against Angelo Deville. Anyone that denies that is a complete and utter fool, and not to mention lying his ass off. Of course, while thinking about all of that is fine and dandy, there is a more pressing matter at hand. Especially when someone is expecting an answer to the question he just posed. “It just sort of happened.” With his head down and his gaze focused on how fast he could twiddle his thumbs, Jayce Bradley sat on one side of the large oak table as another man stood on the other end glaring at him. He is a detective we have become quite familiar with in the last few months, as he became not only the lead detective on a case involving the gruesome murder and torture of a cross dressing Latin male, but also the father of the child that the serial killer in question kidnapped in order to remove the detective from the case; when he started to get too close to finding out the truth. His name was Detective Nicholas Pride, and he was a bit upset at the man that sat across from him. “You walked right up to an armed man, and attempted to wrestle the firearm free for no reason at all, or at least not one you can give me.” The twinge in the detective’s voice was drunk with hostility. “Forgive me if I find this a bit difficult to accept, but not many people would take a risk like that. Especially when there are armed officers ready to drop the man at the slightest twitch.” “I just reacted.” Jayce never lifted his head, holding back the emotions that seemed ready to explode from within his chest in a fit of rage. “I saw the guy, I saw the kid, and I saw the gun. I saw an opening and I took it.” “You could have got someone killed,” Pride bellowed. He lifted his head to stare into the eyes of the detective, his eyes red and engorged. “You know, I expected to get at least a thank you from you, considering the other detective that was in here mentioned the kid was your daughter, but it seems to me that you’re more pissed off because I did something that you couldn’t do.” Pride slammed his fist onto the table, silencing the grappler within seconds. “He could have killed her because you wanted to be a hero, and you want me to thank you. Are you so fucking dense that you think I am somehow grateful for you putting my daughter’s life in jeopardy?” His breathing had become erratic as he spit his lethal venom in Jayce’s general direction, holding back the urge to rip his head from his shoulders. “The chief may want to reward you for your heroics, but believe me when I say that I would rather rip through your neck and piss down your throat for what you did.” “Because you couldn’t do it, or because you wouldn’t do it?” Pride dove across the table, clutching his thick hands around Jayce’s neck. The door to the interrogation room flew open, as five officers charged in to gain control over the situation. “This is harassment,” Jayce screamed out, as one of the officers helped him to his feet. “I saved that kid’s life and he acts as if I am the one who couldn’t protect her!” “Get off of me!” Pride pulled himself from the other officer’s grasp, straightening his tie and his shirt before giving Jayce a stare that would put Medusa to shame. “Something isn’t right, and I’m going to find out what it is.” “Come on, Nick,” one of the other officers calmly pleaded as he motioned for Pride to follow him out of the room. Pride followed, but his gaze never left the face of the man who not only saved his daughter, but caused the death of the serial killer who had kidnapped her. “Nick, come on.” As Pride is ushered out of the room, Jayce turns to the officer standing to his left with a smile. “You think I can leave soon, or is there someone else who wants to question me like I’m a damn criminal?” “Your paperwork is nearly done,” the officer sneered, clearly not comfortable with the supposed hero’s tone, “then you can be on your way.” “Thanks for nothing.” Jayce lifted the chair from the floor and took a seat, dusting himself off as the officers exited the room. “I need to get out of here,” he muttered under his breath as the door slowly closed, “before they figure out the truth.”
