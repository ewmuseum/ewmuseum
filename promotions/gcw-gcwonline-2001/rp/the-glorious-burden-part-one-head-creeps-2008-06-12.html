The Glorious Burden, Part One: Head Creeps

12 June 2008

Date: 12th June 2008 The Glorious Burden Part 1: Head Creeps I Robert Boughton Selene Boughton was a saint in the truest sense of the word. It had been almost seven years now since her brother’s unfortunate accident and not once had she shown any qualms about providing him with the constant care and attention that he required. She had dedicated her life to ensuring that her brother’s existence was as secure as possible at the cost of her own freedom, and if it was taking a toll she sure put up one heck of a convincing front. She had every right to feel aggrieved by the whole situation. Her brother’s condition meant that it was no longer feasible for him to take care of himself for anything longer than twenty four hours. Great sacrifices had to be made to her own social life as a result; she hadn’t been on a night out with her friends for longer than she could remember and it had been over five years since her last steady relationship. Every waking second was dedicated to Robert. Everyday she’d wake up before work and make sure that Robert was up too; he didn’t like waking up in the apartment without anyone else there. After working a gruelling twelve hour shift she’d use the money earned on the grindstone to put food on the table, then spend the remains of the day trying to haul her brother from the psychological pit of despair that he found himself in on a daily basis. But she didn’t mind; Robert was her brother, after all. She was all that he had left; their parents had already abandoned them, wanting nothing to do with Rob after his mental decline, and she couldn’t just watch him suffer. So she took him under her wing and brought him into her home. This began a lifetime of servitude. Today was not unlike the others. She arose at precisely 7am and strolled through to the kitchen casually after a quick shower. Immediately she set about pouring two even portions of cereal into a couple of bowls when her brother sauntered groggily through to the main living area clad in little more than a pair of black boxer shorts and a blue dressing crown. The man himself stood at about six feet tall and was of an average physique, his once toned torso ravaged by seven years of sitting around on his ass all day. His shoulder-length brown hair was as ruffled as you’d expect from a guy who had just slipped out of bed, and his chin was lined with a rough layer of stubble. “Mornin” Sis!” he chirped, full of the joys of spring as he poured himself a cup of coffee from the cafetiere that Selene had prepared just a few minutes prior. “Good morning Robert,” she replied, her voice devoid of all emotion; that’s what uttering the exact same line at the exact same time every day for seven years will do to you. “How are you doing today?” “All is good in the hood! Busy day lined up, I gotta catch the train outta town at 9:30! Big OCW show tonight and I sure as hell don’t wanna be late!” he beamed as he took a quick mouthful of caffeine. Selene sighed. She knew exactly what was coming. “Rob, sit down. There’s something I have to tell you,” she said as she lead her confused elder brother from the kitchen and sat him down on the couch. “Look, you won’t be travelling to the show today.” “What the hell are you talking about?” Robert’s face contorted into a grimace. “OCW needs me, sis!” “No they don’t, Robert. You broke your neck, remember” You step between those ropes again and you could be facing paralysis? or worse. Look here?” she reached into the pocket of her suit trousers to pull out a ragged, crumpled sheet of paper which she promptly handed to Rob. Robert Boughton, ladies and gentlemen, suffers from post-traumatic anterograde amnesia. This means that he is simply incapable of picking up new memories thanks to an exceptionally vicious head knock he received during an earlier career as a professional wrestler. For seven years now Mr. Boughton has been living an eternal Groundhog Day. Every day he wakes up knowing nothing more than he knew on the day his burly opponent slammed him head-first against a rock solid set of ring steps. The doctors told him that slowly, his mind may begin to function normally again, but that it might take certain triggers from the past to begin the process. Well, that was seven years ago now, and Robert Boughton still can’t remember what he had for dinner last night, what the last television show he watched was, or, more importantly, the identikit conversation he had with his sister morning after morning, every day, without fail. The note was written in Rob’s own handwriting. Writing things down was one of the only ways he could keep himself in the loop, and thus Selene made him write take note of anything essential to his survival for the sake of her own sanity. My name is Robert Boughton. I used to be a professional wrestler. My career was ended by a botched powerbomb. If I step foot in the ring again, I may end up in a wheelchair. That’s how the note read exactly. Selene kept it with her at all times, ready to produce at the opportune moment. Every time she pulled it out it had the same effect; it sent her brother spiralling into a wretched trough of anguish and despair as every single day he tried to come to terms with the news that he would no longer be able to partake in the only thing that he had ever know, professional wrestling. Seeing the effect that the note had on her brother, she hated doing it to him, but it was essential. Without reminding him of his predicament, lord knows where he’d end up. Selene did here best to pick her brother up from the floor before she headed off to another day at the office, locking the apartment door firmly behind her. Meanwhile, in the apartment, Robert felt his tear ducts beginning to swell. II Andy Murray “Guess you could say everything’s coming up roses then,” said Rueben Dallas before gulping down a mouthful of heinously over-priced Starbuck’s hot chocolate. His bald head glistened in the harsh, Floridian sunshine as he slowly paced his way through a park teeming with life. Even in Tallahassee, where such beautiful days are pretty common, it seems that everyone wants to make the most of the fine weather as the park was a hive of activity. To the right a group of sixteen or so adolescents enjoy a friendly game of football, while young families were gathered by the fountain tucking into the contents of their picnic baskets. It had been a good year for the resourceful sports agent. After taking on several high-profile clients his own stock had gone through the roof and a recent signing-on fee from a recent contract signing meant that he wouldn’t be struggling for cash any time soon. He wore a light, loose-fitting linen shirt, some beige chinos and a pair of sandals so hideous that even your father probably wouldn’t be seen dead in them. “Something like that,” his top earner retorted, gently swirling his cardboard cup of espresso on his right hand. ??Coming up roses? though? C’mon, that’s weak! You can do better than that, Rueb.” Andy Murray shared a laugh with his agent before knocking back a sharp blast of caffeine. In situations like this he always made the conscious decision to dress down for the occasion in a deliberate attempt to avoid the public eye; fonrtunately for him, though, when you’re name is Andy Murray it’s hard not to look like a million dollars. Even dressed in a humble black Lynyrd Skynyrd t-shirt and some blue boot-cut jeans, topped off with a pair of mirrored Aviator shades, the big Scotsman still managed to look like a rockstar. “Man, this coffee tastes like it came from a badger’s ass,” Andy continues, shaking the cup at his African-American agent. “How’s the choc?” “Pretty good, actually. Hot chocolate’s a pretty hard thing to fuck up though, you know?” The Global Championship Wrestling employee chose not to make a verbal response, instead focusing his attention on tossing his half-finished espresso into a nearby garbage can. He paused for a moment to wipe the perspiration from his brow; even after over a decade living in the Deep South he wasn’t fully acclimatised to the weather. “So what’s new with the whole contract situation?” Andy enquired, cracking his knuckles out of habit. “Caldera still dragging his heels?” “I’m working on it,” nodded Dallas. He was squinting in the sunlight, probably ruing his decision not to take any eye protection with him. “The financial package isn’t really what we had hoped for, but I’m sure we can resolve it with minimal fuss. The length’s good though; looks like you’ll be on GCW television for a little while longer than we first thought.” Andy accepted the answer with a nod as a warm sense of satisfaction grew from his belly. If Andrew H. Murray was feeling smug at this point in time he certainly had every right to. It had been a whirlwind seven months for The Scottish King of Cool; he entered GCW in December 2007 following almost a year out of action thanks to a recurring back injury that had already shaved years from his professional career. Since then he has set about making just about every single one of his critics eat their words by burning a hot streak wider than the Grand Canyon through the promotion, to the point where he is on the brink of being considered a genuine main event threat. Andy Murray was beginning to get used to the feeling of being a bona fide global superstar again, and he would relish every last second. Hell of a turnaround for a guy who, sixteen months ago, looked to be at the end of the road following a disastrous spell in the federation formerly known as FUSE. “What can I say man” Guess there’s no other man in the business today that can compete with my overwhelming aura of undeniable awesomeness,” sniggered Murray. “Seriously though, Rueb, thanks.” Andy stopped dead in his tracks, gazing through his glasses to look his agent in the eye. “Once again you’ve pulled me from the doldrums to the brink of something truly phenomenal.” “Awww hell man! Enough already, you’re making me blush,” the modest Mr. Dallas responded, waving away the praise, but he couldn’t prevent the smile that crawled itself across his lips. “I mean it,” Murray replied without a hint of irony. “I’m not too great at the whole “thanking people” thing, but, thanks and stuff? I guess.” The smile on Rueben’s face slowly grew into a giant, shit-eating Cheshire Cat grin. He watched as his client twitched and scratched through his short, black hair nervously. “Pleasure’s all mine, Andy. Really.” For a brief moment there was a silence between the two that was soon shattered by Ruben Dallas? deep, enthusiastic tones. “Oh shit, I nearly forgot?” Suddenly Dallas reached behind him and began to gently pat his hand against the back of his trousers. It didn’t take long from him to find what he was looking for and soon enough he pulls a long, sealed brown envelope from his back pocket. “Got something for you, champ,” he said as he extended the letter in the direction of a curious Andy Murray. Andy pulled in the envelope and set about putting his thumb underneath the flap, fixing to tear it open. “Oh no, don’t open it just yet,” protested Dallas, prompting the Scotsman to cease. “Oh?” was all that Andy could muster. “I wanted you to save that one “til you get home, if you can.” “How come” You firing me or something, you filthy hobo?” Andy suggested in jest. “Call it a reward,” Dallas replied, deflecting the “firing” comment. “You’ve been doing so well for yourself recently; you deserve to have something to show for it. So I pulled a few strings here and there and managed to fix you up good, I’m sure you’ll approve?” “Ohhh, sounds super awesome,” enthused Murray. He still didn’t fully understand why his agent didn’t want to open it there and then, but he accepted his wishes and slid the envelope into the front pocket of his jeans. Andy didn’t get a chance to speak further as his agent pulled a buzzing mobile phone from the recesses of his chinos. “Dallas,” he answered in a way that reminded Andy of The Wire’s Cedric Daniels. “What, that’s today” you’re kidding me?” he sighed. “Alright, okay, I’ll be there in twenty. Bye.” Rueben shook his head with dissatisfaction as he slid the Nokia closed. “Sorry man, I’m going to have to bolt. Duty calls! I’ll catch you in a couple of days” and remember, don’t open that sucker up “til you get home!” The retreat of his agent was so sudden that Andy found himself slightly taken aback by it all. Either way he waved goodbye and watched his agent disappear back up the path towards the car park. His thoughts immediately turned to the mysterious envelope that had just been handed to him. This is some serious cloak and daggers shit, he thought as he examined the manila surface. Perhaps I should open it now? he considered. Nah, fuck it, best leave it for later, I’m sure Ruebs has his reasons? Everything was coming up roses, after all. III Robert Boughton It was quite literally the same shit on a different day for Robert, even though he lacked the mental capacity to realise it. As soon as Selene left and he had gotten over the initial shock at finding out that he would never be able to compete again, a thousand questions began to filter through his mind. When did this happen? How did this happen? Why did this happen? And, perhaps most important of all, who did this to me? Every single day it drove him up the wall. He scanned his mind’s library for answers, but to no avail; the events leading up to and including the accident were a complete blur to him, and he could quite easily pull his hair out trying to recall the events of that fateful Online Championship Wrestling “Massacre” taping back in August 2001. If only he could learn of the futility of straining his wits like this, he’d surely be a much more content individual. By early afternoon he usually had drained every last remaining drop of knowledge from his brain. Today, like any other day, he gave up the fight as a wave of self-pity cascaded over him like a waterfall. As thoughts of his own decline began to fade away (literally), he felt a familiar silvery sensation in his bladder. Realising the need to expulse bodily fluids, he made his way into the bathroom to take a leak. Unfortunately for Rob he failed to notice the small pool of water sitting ominously on the tiles, just begging for someone to step right on it. This is exactly what Robert did, and soon enough he found himself slipping, his body plummeting downwards uncontrollably. His rear end connected with the edge of the bath, sending a pillar of pain from his pelvis right up his spine. “AHHHH!” he screamed in pain as he hobbled up to his feet. “Phew?” he breathed a sigh of relief as he prodded the sore area. “Hurts like a fucker. Sure ain’t broke though?” Rob turned back around; looking to clean up the puddle so hopefully nobody else would slip on it. However, before he could grab a towel, he noticed that the plastic panelling around the bath had come loose as a result of the impact. Sighing, Robert knelt down and grabbed the panel but once again found himself sidetracked. What’s that, he thought as something grey caught his eye. The lighting was too dim for him to make out what it was by sight along, so he stuck his arm through the gap. After a couple of seconds of blind fumbling, Robert finally grabbed hold of it and hauled it through, slapping it down on the tiled floor. He sat cross-legged and gazed at what lay before him inquisitively. It was a pile of publications and periodicals; newspaper cuttings, professional wrestling magazines, and other items of that ilk. “ROBERT “MYZERY” BOUGHTON COMFIRMS RETIREMENT!” The headline on the first article stung him like a wasp, immediately testing his resolve. In a fury he brushed it aside with the back of his hand. Unfrotunately the second article proved to be the sinker? “ROOKIE SPEAKS OF GUILT OVER BOTCHED SPOT!” Robert pulled the cutting in close and carefully inspected the large, full-colour picture breaking up the text. It was of a young, fresh-faced wrestler of no more than 23 years of age. His facial features were naturally handsome and his blue eyes pierced through the page like bolts of lightning, while his in-ring attire adorned the letters “S.K.C.” The doctors told Selene that it may require certain triggers for Robert’s memory to begin its recovery. Well, there was a reason she had hidden these articles underneath the bath? Instantly, Robert recognised the cocky young kid in the picture. It was him; the man who had ended his career. The man who had crushed his dream and ruined his life. The man who had doomed him to a miserable existence with a condition that no human being should ever have to endure. It was Andy Murray. IV Andy Murray “Whoooa, easy there, little dude!” As soon as Andy closed the door behind him he found a small, chocolate brown coloured dashund leaping frantically up his leg. Winston’s tail was wagging at such a rate that Andy thought it might fall off at any second, so he scooped the family pet up and cradled it in his arms. “Ahhh, help me! Someone! Wolf attack!” Andy yelled as the puppy clawed its way up his Skynyrd shirt, lapping gleefully away at his chin with its tiny little tongue. Murray laughed, pulled his shades from his face and gently tickled Winston behind the ears, sending the tiny dog into a state of near-euphoria. The Scotsman walked through the hall of his Floridian home with a spring in his step, clearly enjoying a rare day off from his hectic life as one of the leading lights in one of the country’s largest mainstream wrestling promotions. “Look who’s back,” a feminine voice called out from the kitchen as Andy stepped over the threshold. His brown-eyed girlfriend, Kayla, turned around from the worktop and offered The Most Happening Dude in GCW a smile. Blindly she slapped a slice of bread on top of her sandwich before focusing all of her attentions on the big Scotsman, who knelt down and let Winston run free. Such was the little dog’s excitement at being in the presence of both of his owners that he tried to move across the floor much faster than his tiny paws would allow, causing him to slip and lose his balance. Kayla leaned forward, kissing Andy on the cheek. “Some wolf, eh?” she giggled as Winston struggled to cope with the tiled floor. “You kidding me” He’s straight-up Akela. What’cha doing anyway, Kay?” “Just fixing a little lunch,” she responded, pointing at the ham salad sandwich behind her. “You want some” There’s plenty to spare?” “Nah, I’ll give it a miss just now. Had this RANCID fuckin” espresso with Ruebs, probably not gonna be able to really taste anything for a week,” he exaggerated as a burst of fresh air from the open French doors connected soothingly with his exposed arms. “Suit yourself,” Kayla said as he turned and retrieved her meal. She was certainly dressed for the weather in a white tank-top that hugged her generously-curved upper body like a glove, as well as a light sarong with more colours on it than Andy cared to count. “How did the little meeting go then?” The words seemed to trigger something in Andy’s head. “Oh, right! Of course!” he blurted, immediately remembering the letter in his pocket. He reached down into the depths of his jeans and pulled the warm, brown envelope out, waving it at Kayla. “What’s that?” “I don’t know. Rueben told me to not open it “til I got back, for some reason.” She nodded. “Well, ain’t ya gonna have a look?” By the time Kayla had finished her sentence Andy had already broken the seal. With baited breath he pulled the typed A4 letter from its manila prison, immediately recognising the familiar Primetime Central logo that adorned the paper’s top. “Oh shit?” he said, wide-eyed, as the words began to sink in. “What”! Lemme see!” Kay abandoned her food on the table and almost snatched the letter from Andy’s hands. A broad smile suddenly appeared on Murray’s face and he punched the air triumphantly as his partner began examined the letter closely. Mr. Murray, Thank you for applying to take part in Primetime Central’s upcoming Inaugural Tournament to decide the first ever PTC Elite Champion. It is with great pleasure that we inform you that your application has been successful, and that you will be meeting PRIME Universal Champion Devin Shakur in a first round match-up hosted at Sin City Championship Wrestling in the very near future. Further details will be despatched shortly, until then we wish you luck in your quest for PTC Glory! Sincerely, Argyle R. Gyle. “Ohhhh, so this is a pretty big deal, right?” Kayla raised an eyebrow. While her interest in the sport had increased when she started dating Andy, her knowledge of pro-wrestling was still patchy at best. “That’s putting it mildly. Gee!” Andy could barely contain the excitement and found himself unable to stand still for longer than a couple of seconds. “This is fantastic!” even his ability to accurately put his feelings into words had abandoned him. “Yay!” clapped Kayla, sensing from Andy’s demeanour that something magnificent had just happened. “Oh sweet mother of fuck! This is so badass! I gotta tell Cayle, and Sid! And I gotta thank Rueben!” instinctively Andy reached for his cell phone and began to jab at the keys with fingers of jelly. Fortunately for him he managed to somehow fumble his way down to the number of his elder brother. After a few rings a gruff, Scottish voice picked up the phone. “Yo, Andy, what’s good?” “Sidney, man, I’m about to go stratospheric?” V Robert Boughton He stubbed out what must’ve been his fifth cigarette into the ashtray as he lifted a nervous hand and grabbed a ballpoint pen. With the article from earlier laying before him, Robert scrawled the words “he did it” unevenly across Andy Murray’s torso before folding the sheet up and tucking it away safely in his back pocket. Now he’d never forget. Beside him lay an old pistol that had never been fired before. He’d bought the gun many years ago purely for self-defence reasons, only he’d never really had a good reason to carry it around. Until now. He slid the gun in behind his belt before standing up and throwing a brown coat over his broad shoulders. Indignance filled his head as he glanced one more time at the stack of articles; the smug grin of the Scotsman mocking him from one of the pages. He knew there and then what had to be done. He was going to end Andy Murray. Whatever the cost.
