An Experiment, Part V

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Several Days Before Shockwave An Experiment Part V: A Patriotic Cure “Boredom,” exclaims a rough, crackly voice known to belong to Face. A sudden image of a man slashing another man’s neck with a dagger flashes through Face’s mind. The man’s jugular is sliced open and blood spurts out like a fountain onto the murderer’s face... Face sits in his concrete office in his worn out leather chair, swivelled so as to face the wall directly behind him and his desk. “How I loathe it...” Flick. Fwoosh. Crack. The familiar sounds of his life’s metronome keep the time flowing. Nothing else is heard. “It’s one of the mind’s greatest diseases. It has plagued humans since their dawn and it will continue to do so until our last sunset. A woman stabs a man in his gut. He collapses to the floor and shrieks uncontrollably, attempting ? and failing ? to stop the red flood produced by the wound... There are many so-called cures...” Face lets out a hoarse noise one could call a chuckle. “But even those are just temporary sewing jobs on ripped clothing. They may fix a particular seam, but sooner or later another seam bursts under the pressure...” Flick. Fwoosh. Crack. “Of course, it’s a choice between temporary freedom or none at all ? quite an obvious one, really. And so, it boils down to your drug of choice. Two men struggle in a literal fight for their lives. Already having sustained numerous cuts and bruises, both men fight to drive their dagger through the other in a final blow... Some people choose to play. Some choose to work. Some study. Some let their minds wander and warp with the help of their favourite substances...” Face shakes his head. “I prefer to let a natural cure arise ? one that some would argue is a disease in itself...Curiosity.” Flick. Fwoosh. Crack. “Curiosity is what brought the original experiments to life...Unfortunately, after several months of experimentation...I find myself bored once again.” Face lets out a sigh. A little girl weeps quietly in a room by herself. The crying ends with a swift slice of a knife to her throat... “I could only watch on so many times as people fought for their lives...Sure, I was able to play with their minds and mercilessly demoralize and kill the “winners”...but, as time passed by, it wasn’t enough. There was not enough of a challenge, even after choosing a test subject who recognized me...” Flick. Fwoosh. Crack. “And so...I hope you understand why I couldn’t go through with the experiment tonight, James,” says Face, swivelling his chair back around. “I was just...curious...” Face finally turns his attention to the man he was talking to this whole time. Or, rather, what used to constitute a man. What Face is actually looking at is a man’s bloody shirt nailed to the wall, a rectangle of his jeans nailed on the top left corner of the bloody shirt. On top of the bloody shirt are James’s leg bones, arm bones and ribs arranged in 6 parallel lines held in place by nails underneath them ? the bottom three lines longer than the top three. Glued onto the rectangle of denim are James’s teeth and what appear to be the various bones in his fingers and toes. There are 50 teeth and bones in all. A deep chuckle is now released from behind Face’s mask. He gets up from his chair and steps closer to the wall, admiring his handiwork. Blood trickles from it and down the wall, pooling on the ground, where the rest of James’s severed body lies. “I hope you don’t mind, James. If it’s any consolation, you’re final resting... (Face searches for the words for a few moments, scratching his masked chin) ...formation...is quite patriotic. I don’t imagine that many Americans can say their remains will be arranged into an American flag upon death.” Flick. Fwoosh. Crack. “That’s what Americans like to be...isn’t it” Hmmm? Patriotic ? blindly loyal to their country, like the little sheep they are? It’s really a sad thing when you think about it, isn’t it James?” Face waits for a response from the flag. He doesn’t get one. “Either way, I don’t experience any sort of patriotism. I have ulterior motives, much like I do for my match coming up...” Face suddenly catches himself on something, snapping his fingers with his free hand. “Of course, I forgot to mention that I was a wrestler in GCW...My mistake, James. Well, I happen to be going into a match, whose prize is a so-called American Dream. To me, it doesn’t sound that appealing... But, as I watched you in your deep slumber in a chair I had you tied to earlier, I had a revelation...” Flick. Fwoosh. Crack. Face stoops over to James’s remains, picking up his severed head, the blood from the neck staining his scarred hands. Face tilts his head, examining it, and speaks directly towards it. “If my opponents are going to be fighting for a dream, I might as well assure them a nightmare...”
