Trouble Enough for Two

8 February 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02/08/10 The warm, full sound of Adiemus wafted from the speakers nearby as Tempest’s long black hair swayed in time, dangling freely from her inverted head. Slowly she lifted herself up pivoting at the point where her ankles were caught up in a bar suspended above the wooden floor of the old warehouse loft she called home. A slight tremor of effort shook her taut legs and worked its way up her body, wrapped in soft black stretch knit with a pattern of little green vines working its way up the legs. Her eyes remained closed and her face impassive despite the trembling of her lower body and slowly she reached out over her head, turning her palms upward. Then, in the distance, the sound of metallic thunder not of musical origin began to invade the song, growing louder and nearer. Tempest’s eyes opened with a slight flutter and she said softly, “Frankie… jeez.” Momentarily the thunder came to a crescendo and then a halt, only to be ended with a flurry of pounding upon the door to the loft. Echoes in the high ceilings made it even louder, but it also did the same for her call of, “Come in already!” The door opened and in walked Frank Valleta - friend, protégé, electrician, co-worker (sort of), and at the moment, as bizarre as it seemed for a man who stood six foot six and weighed in at 265, a man who looked like a deer in the headlights. He strode across the loft, work boots slapping out a hurried rhythm as he wound his way around the red sofa that dominated the space and through the work tables, scattered exercise equipment, Shoji screens, and a stray futon or two. “Tempest, have you seen this? What the Hell?” he spluttered, coming to a halt and staring at her horizontal form, looking left to right. “Ok, that is just wrong,” he added, doffing his cap and running a hand through the tangle of dark curls atop his head. “Frank, you’re disturbing my calm,” Tempest said tremulously and pulled her arms back in slowly. “And I’ll have you doing this soon enough.” “I have not begun to disturb your calm,” Frank said, eyes going slightly wider at the prospect of having to duplicate Tempest’s contortions himself. “And as for doing that, well I think that pretty well shoots my calm right to Hell. But anyway, did you see this?” He held up a sheet of paper and then reached out and held it above her face for her to read. “It’s upside down,” Tempest said. “You’re upside down!” Frank said emphatically, flipping the paper around anyway. Tempest read it, now starting to breathe more rapidly with the effort of maintaining her difficult posture. Her eyes skimmed the page and then widened as she squealed, “What?!” She flipped herself up and grabbed hold of the bar to which the box brackets were mounted that held her ankles and slid her feet free of them, then leapt to the floor and landed unsteadily. She snatched the paper from Frank’s hand and read it again with a look of disbelief. “Ok, I’ll see your ‘What the Hell?’ and raise you a Godammit!” Tempest spat. She stood huffing and Frank shook his head in confusion. “What’s going on?” he asked. “Well, isn’t it obvious?” Tempest said, throwing her hands in the air and letting the paper fly. About that time, her ferrets appeared and grabbed it up, before making off with it. “Um…” Frank pointed to the departing m.p. furos, but Tempest waved him off and said, “Pay them no mind. They’ll just be lining a nest with that, which is appropriate. No, it’s obvious that Christian Zenith has decided to get some sort of petty revenge by throwing me into a handicap match and right before my title shot at NC-17. Dammit!” Frank let out a breath slowly and rubbed his hands together, apparently thinking. “Can he do that?” he asked tentatively. Tempest nodded. “Sure he can. He’s the commissioner. He can do whatever he wants. Well, not absolutely anything he wants. I mean, he’s not Caldera. But he can certainly make a match like this one.” “But why?” Frank persisted. “Why just potentially screw up what’s gotta be a good match shaping up for NC-17 by maybe messing up one half of it before the event? That makes no sense.” Tempest looked up at Frank and shifted her weight back and forth from foot to foot uncomfortably as she said, “Well, you saw the show. I um… sort of let slip something I shouldn’t have.” Frank shrugged and shook his head, as he pressed on, “What? That his web site had stuff on it he didn’t know about?” Tempest rolled her eyes. “No Frankie. That stuff wasn’t ON his web site. I found it going where I wasn’t supposed to go in the first place.” “What? Oh no.” Frank said and lowered his face into hands. “No no no no no… I didn’t know all those old stories about you being a hacker were actually true!” Tempest glared at Frank with her hands on her hips. “Now Frank, ‘hacking’ is such an ugly word for what I do.” Frank just gaped at her for a moment before toppling backward onto the nearest futon with a groan. Hacking scares people. Even the most adventurous risk takers in our business don’t like it because they’d at least like to think all that stuff in their computers or those of their big corporations, banks and so on, is safe. Never mind a chair shot from behind, at least no one can read their private thoughts and feelings or get to their finances, addresses, carefully buried family connections, etc. And really that stuff is safer than they might think with me. I’m not some greedy credit card phisher looking to make a buck and even when I do get into someone’s bank account, I take no more than is needed, no more than they can easily stand, and it certainly doesn’t go into my own pocket. It goes where it’s desperately needed. If you didn’t root for Robin Hood, that’s your problem. And the fact that I learn so much that is sensitive is much less of a threat than people fear. But I guess ‘fear‘is the operative word. They don’t know how much I know. And they don’t know that I’m not the type to spill their secrets haphazardly or talk to whatever rag or TV show will pay for the dirtiest gossip. Sure I messed with Jay Terror’s bio page. But if I cracked his emails I would never tell what’s in them. Well, I might whisper it in his ear at a convenient time, but I wouldn’t tell his enemies, even if I counted myself among them. Because that would be mean. And he’s mean, not me. And Christian Zenith? All I found peeking around Zenith Corporation’s restricted files was that he’s a better man than he gets credit for sometimes. Silly me, for trying to let that cat out of the bag. Enter: My next panicked friend… “Tempest!” The shout from the doorway made Tempest jump and Frank half jump, half roll from the futon, but he made it up to a standing position with only minor property damage in the form of a large rice paper lamp toppling to the floor. “Over here Darren!” Tempest called as her manager turned and made his way through the maze inside the loft. “You forget how to knock?” “It was open.” He rounded the last Shoji screen and said, “Tempest have you seen this? Did you…?” “YES!” The reply came from both Tempest and Frank. Darren stopped and looked between the two a moment and said, “Wait a minute, who’s he? And how do you know?” Tempest chuckled and said, “Oh… Darren Stearns, editor of Squared Circle Scene, meet Frankie Valleta, future wrestling superstar and my protégé, and friend of the family so to speak. He got the word before you did. He’s pretty awesome like that.” Frank held his hand out to the baffled editor turned manager who said, “Protégé? Somehow I didn’t picture Tempest training um… someone of your size. I’m guessing you don’t have quite the same style as she does. But wait a second, aren’t you…” He looked to Tempest quizzically, without finished the sentence and she looked back and nodded. “Yes, the guy I wrestled in that independent show.” “And…?” Darren continued, the thoughts seeming to form in his mind slowly. “And the same guy who showed up to work with Troy and me at the theater and accidently told Troy I was a wrestler before I could,” Tempest added, completing the thought for him. Darren nodded with an odd smile and shook Frank’s hand as he said, “Well, welcome aboard Tempest’s weird welcome wagon, where even uprooting her love life and professional plans isn’t enough to get you any real resentment.” Tempest swatted Darren’s arm and scowled up at him as she said, “Darren! C’mon now. It wasn’t Frank’s fault. He’s a great guy and Troy likes him too.” Frank smiled at her and then shrugged in Darren’s direction. “They’re pretty awesome like that. But hey, what are we gonna do about this? She can’t take on both of those guys.” “Hey! I have faced greater odds in my career than this,” Tempest said, turning from both of them and pacing a few steps away. “Ok agreed," said Darren. “But it didn’t do anything good for you. Either one of these guys I’m sure you could handle, but both is not a great scenario. Cheap Labor will be looking to soften you up for NC-17 and this Michaels guy, with his MMA experience has some real potential to hurt you. Now…” Darren’s astute analysis was interrupted by a snicker from Frank as Tempest paced back toward them and she looked up and said, “What’s so funny?” Frank shrugged with a mildly embarrassed smirk and said, “I think the only think Cheap Labor’s trying to soften you up for is a date. Put him in a headlock and he’ll be so dazed you can pin him easy.” Tempest yanked Frank’s cap from where he’d thrown it on a nearby table and proceeded to swat him about the head and shoulders with it as she said, “That is so wrong! He’s just a nice guy who happens to like me. That’s all!” “Ow! Ow! Not the face!” Frank called from beneath his meaty forearms where he cowered from the flurry of blows. Darren laughed and said, “He’s got a point Tempest. And if it works, by all means do it.” Tempest huffed and relinquished Frank’s cap with a final swat in his direction. “Yeah well, I don’t know about that, but he’s not a company guy. He won’t break my neck just because he thinks Christian Zenith might like him to. Michaels…” She shrugged. “I have no idea. But he’s good, so if he has any extra leverage at all, that can’t help.” Darren nodded and scratched his well rounded chin thoughtfully and said, “Yeah, speaking of Christian Zenith, you didn’t really hack into his corporation’s restricted files did you?” Tempest rolled her eyes and said, “I was just telling Frank that hacking such an ugly…” “Oh god, you did!” Darren half groaned as he slapped his forehead. “August, 2001 ‘hacking is such an ugly word for what I do.’ I remember publishing that now. Please tell me that’s all. Tell me that thing with Terror’s bio and the GCW website wasn’t you.” “Well, erm…” Tempest began, shuffling uncomfortably and looking to Frank, who merely grinned back. “Are you kidding that was awesome!” he said and raised his fist to bump with Tempest who grinned back. “If I knew how to do that I’d have done it too.” “Do NOT encourage her!” Darren yelped, running his hands back through his receding hair. “And please please please tell me that LeStatt Knight actually pledged that odd and generous amount of money to your charity campaign. I can’t handle you getting three strikes for felony cybercrime in just a week.” Tempest laughed nervously and twirled a lock of her hair as she said, “Well he did say to bill him, so in a manner of speaking I did. And it’s um… perfectly legit.” She cleared her throat and looked at her feet with great interest. “Good luck proving otherwise in fact.” “Gah!” Darren winced and closed his eyes as he said, “I’ve got to find you a real manager. I’m just not cut out for this. And at this rate F. Lee Bailey would be a good candidate.” Tempest put her hands on her hips and said, “Oh stop having kittens, both of you. I’ll be fine. This is no greater crisis than I usually find myself in. And if either of you expected anything less, you’re both goofballs. I’m Tempest! The party never stops, but neither does the trouble. So put on your running shoes and deal with it!” Darrren sighed and Frank grinned and gave her a thumbs up and said, “See? Now that’s the kind of training I really need.” Tempest shook her head with a tiny grin and added, “Now which one of you two is going to get my damn door fixed since I see it’s still flopping open over there?” She waved in the direction of the door where it stood open across the loft. Both men turned their heads and Frank said, “Oh, I can get somebody from the job site to look at it. Damn, Troy would have it done in the time it took to talk about it. He can fix anything. Too bad he’s not around.” Tempest nodded and stared at the open door as if waiting and then shook her head. “Yeah,” she said quietly. “It is.”
