Date: once upon a time The clich? always goes? you think you know a person. But you don’t. Simple. Effective. Poignant in passing off its message. The message is that you can never assume. There’s another saying about what happens when you do that, too. It makes an "ass" out of "u" and "me". All sayings aside, I’ve been strewn by the wayside by recent events in my life. My two children. My wrestling career. My ex-wife. The endless struggle to push past the other to be the apple of my eye is beginning to grow overwhelmingly intense. I’m not an intense kind of guy. Stress is not something I deal with on a day-to-day basis. I like challenges, but I don’t want wars. Yet here I am. With the candle burning at both ends.--- ? --- In the part of this universe that we know there is great injustice, and often the good suffer, and often the wicked prosper, and one hardly knows which of those is the more annoying. Bertrand Russell British author, mathematician, & philosopher (1872 - 1970) --- ? --- Something inside me is hurting. A constant dull ache resonates through my body. It almost feels like a sharp pain. That sounds like an oxymoron, but that’s how it makes my innards feel. Every now and again, I feel short of breath. The last few weeks have really been catching up with my body. I’m like GCW’s personal punching bag. Don’t know how much more my body can take. Look at the card in my hand, it’s a blank, off-white business card with an address scrawled on it, in blue felt-tipped marker. Check the card against the street light above me. The moon hangs itself in the air precariously, like it were setting the mood. Quick glance to my left then right, I step into the ghetto before me, where every sound makes my colon panic. My heart thumps so loudly you can hear me coming a mile away. Pull the hood of my jumper over my head, to keep my face unseen through the hovel. One hand carries the bag, the other clutches to its lifeline within my hoody pocket. A pair of African-American teens toss a basketball between one another, not watching what they’re doing. Their eyes lock on mine, causing hysteria within me. I might be a professional wrestler, but I’m not bulletproof. People with so much bravado, as if they carry it around like their own special atmosphere, scare the be-jeebus out of me. My footsteps echo through the street, whose silence teases me. Feels like the street can make itself silent, just to help the moon out with setting the disposition. Stupidly, I grin at the teens. One tucks the ball under his arm and stares, his companion looks at the bag in my hand. Find my own gaze trickling down my body toward that bag. My face grows warm with the crimson mask of hiding something. The teen sees it in me and looks at his friend. Fleetingly, he glances at the bag again. They begin to speak without words. Just a series of eye gestures. They will come for my bag. Tonight wasn’t their lucky night. Tonight? I came prepared. My hand rises from my pocket, pointing the lifeline directly at their faces. A .357 Colt Python with a 4" barrel. My fucking saviour. Their hands begin to rise, without any instruction, but from instinct. Sweat beads on my forehead as adrenalin begins to churn my gut. "Now, y’all hold up in here, mista. We ain’t bangers. We jus? lookin? atcha bags’all, aight?" says the one in the LeBron James jersey. Gun bounces across to the other as he speaks. Clearly, he’s the older of the pair. The look on his face tells me that he’s gone through this one million times before. That he’s always cautious, especially of a white man with a revolver. "Yeah, dawg. Jus? coupl’a kids tossin? da pill “bout, homes. Ain’t no need f’y’all to gitcha cannon on us." "Ain’t no need," says LeBron. "Shit, whitebread. Y’all on a tether. Work yo’self up inta a lather, dawg. Jus? keep stridin?. Y’all ain’t somebodeh we’s plannin? on ridin?. I know who dat bag’s fo?," the older remarks, dropping his hands down and popping his collar. "Fucked if I’m gonna pinch a dime from dat muddafucker’s pay day." Ears prick up. Make sure I point the Colt directly into the kid’s face as I step toward him. LeBron watches the gun’s barrel aimed at his friend. He tries to get in the way, only to taste the sharpness of the steel on his eye socket. I feel it cave beneath the butt of my revolver. He turns to jelly, falling to the asphalt in a great heap. Clutching at his sunken cheek bone in agony. Keep coming at the old one, gun never lowering. My hand is not shaking now. It is straight with the pleasurable course of pure adrenalin driving my reactions. I am feeding on my own fear. I grow stronger for it. "Who is he?" splurt my words as I rush forward. The hood of my jumper falls back as I lurch toward him. The kid’s eyes widen as recognition sets in. His accusatory finger rises between us. "Y’all’re CJ Woods! Ya jus? fucken pistol-whipped my friend! Wait “til da papers here “bout dis, dawg! Dis shit’s off da fucken hook!" Shake my head and cock my thumb back. The barrel of the handgun comes to life. The cogs of the weapon move into action and the bullet rolls into the chamber. I press the gun into his cheek, dimpling the flesh beneath its pointy barrel. "You gonna call the papers or be the headline, asshat? What’s his fucken name?" "Wes! Wes Noble!" he cries, now panicked by the white man with the gun in his face. He cowers beneath his forearms as if they are bulletproof. "Noble?" is the question that escapes my lips. The evening seemed full of oxymorons. --- ? --- "Drop the bag," Noble demands. Toss it across the floor to his feet. Lifts it gingerly in one hand, testing its weight. He chuckles to himself. "So? that’s what 10k feels like, huh?" Noble’s eyes peer cold and hard into my soul, which I can feel packing its bags in preparation of fleeing. Fleeing via my colon, in a blaze of brown glory. "Why’s she so sick? What’d you give her, Noble?" I demand. "Twelve inches of hot beef injection." "Don’t be a cunt! TELL ME!" bursts my emotion, plucking the .357 calibres of courage from the pocket dwelling once more. "TELL ME OR I PAINT THE FUCKING WALLS, SO HELP ME!" Noble’s eyes flash red with rage. The piece in my hand seems to have no bearing over Wes Noble. I begin to shake like a dog shitting razor blades as he smiles his shit-eating grin. "Paint “em." "?Sc-“Scu”.?Scuse me?" I stammer. "I said," Noble replies coldly, "paint. them." Cocks his head, as if really investigating the cowardice of my soul. Like a hawk planning its attack on a field mouse. "I don’t think you got the balls," the beast speculates. "Try me, asshole." Step-by-step, Wes comes forward, hand outstretched to remove the gun from my hand. His mere presence and bravado seems to have disabled my central nervous system. I cannot flinch. Nor can I move. Just watch him pluck the gun from my hand. Turns it over, end-for-end, in his hand, looking over the piece and its quality. "You come for answers, cowboy?" Noble questions both rhetorically and proudly. My gaze is too fixated on my gun in his hand to respond to his question. "Ya look at me and see the bad guy, don’cha, cowboy? Look at me and think I’m the monster behind all this, but I’m jus? the fucken mask." Noble explains himself with his puzzle, heading toward his bag of cash. Stands by its side and pockets the Python. "Whaddaya mean mask?" my intrigue asks on my behalf. Noble chuckles and plucks a phone from his pocket, punches some numbers in and waits for it to be answered. "?err, hi. Can ya put me through to Morag Woods? room, darlin??" he asks, winking at me as if he has it all under control. Without waiting for an answer, he outstretches his hand for me to take the cell. I step forward, cautiously, taking it from him, eyes remain locked on his movements the whole time. I recognise the voice on the end of the line as a nurse from my ex-wife’s ward. "I’m sorry, sir. She seems to have checked herself out." Confusion kicks in. "She was in a fucking coma, how does she check herself out?" I ask in desperation. "I’m sorry, sir. I have no idea. She’s checked out of the hospital." My heart skips a beat, snap out of my distant gaze to stare at Noble. He is no longer there.
