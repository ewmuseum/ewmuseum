Date: 06/04/10 “Sir, you’re going to have to check that bag.” His purple shirt clung pathetically to the extra fat on his torso, damp patches extending south beneath the armpits. He wore his credentials like a suit of armour, the American Airlines ID card lent weight by its laminate backing. The queue was long, but now that I was at the front, this no longer concerned me. I looked down at my black holdall with puzzlement. “Sorry, check it for what exactly? I mean, isn’t that what your X-ray machines are for?” “Would you place it on the scales, sir,” the attendant sighed, clearly in a foul mood. It’s hard to pass through LAX and not be in a foul mood, I reasoned. But working here, you’d think he’d be used to it. I hoisted up the bag some difficulty. “There,” I declared, unable to keep the irritation from my voice. “Sir, that’s too heavy to take in the cabin with you. You’re going to have to check it.” “Oh. Check it in.” Now I understood. “Um, I can’t check that bag in. I have to take it in with me. It’s important,” I emphasised, as if nothing else was. I picked at my unshaven chin, awaiting his verdict. “You can’t take that in the cabin, sir,” he repeated wearily. “It’s too heavy.” I blew my cheeks out, looking around me for a suitable example. “Well you can’t take her in the cabin either,” I retorted, gesturing at the doughnut-shaped woman in the adjacent queue. It was her unlucky day. “She’s too heavy.” “Sir, step aside, please,” the man wheezed through his nose, his eyes shut. The fat woman hadn’t heard me, but I didn’t much care either way. “Step aside and let me deal with the next customer in line.” Though I wasn’t moving, I felt myself drifting, the queue bypassing me as one. He was looking right through me. I was the last inhabitant of a ghost town, long declared dead. “Look, this is ridiculous. It can’t be more than ten, maybe twelve kilos. No permissions were sought for the representations of Andy Murray, Garbage Bag Johnny, Clyde Fox and Jay Terror in this roleplay, on account of their scenes taking place in another dimension, and thus requiring no continuity with the GCW universe. The second and fourth scenes (those set in the bar) draw heavily on "Cleaning, Part Two: Another End of the Line at the Other End of the Line", posted for PRIME ReVolution 224 by Garbage Bag Johnny.
