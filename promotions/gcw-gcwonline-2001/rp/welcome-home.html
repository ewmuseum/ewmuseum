Date: Boston The back end of a limo accelerates away, leaving The Marauder to stand there alone. With a big whiff of fresh air he sucks in the scents of America, Boston to be precise, and it was gorgeous morning. Puffy white clouds lazily drift their course across the pristine blue sky, and happy birds chirp their melancholy songs. All of this among the honks and general raucus of the busy metro airport. Cars lined the curb as departing and leaving passengers filed this way and that. 'Well isn't that some shit.' He mutters, the sun glancing off his blue and white mask, his lips twisted sideways in thought he looks first left and then right. With a defeated shrug The Marauder sets about earning his keep in a way that is both fun and easy... 'This shit ain't fun.' Marauder mumbles and advances on the next departing passanger. 'Change. Spare?' She shakes her head nervously and hustles towards her waiting husband. 'Shit ain't easy either.' The Marauder set about earning his keep in a manner most humiliating, people turned off by the large masked freak were quick to deny and even quicker to retreat. But it wasn't long though before Marauder comes across another pan handler, dressed in khakies and a wrinkled blue shirt he scores a giver with many thanks. Their eyes meet, the cut outs casting an ominous shadow over Marauders squinted glare. The fellow holds up two quarters triumphantly and then promptly stashes them in his pocket and attaches himself like a leech to his next target. Marauder nods his head in silent approval of the challenge. His next target found him, nearly walking out of the sliding glass doors and right into him. The Marauder reacted quickly though, with diplomacy and tact he said: 'Change. Spare?' With such elegant grace, but alas it was to no avail. The man apologized and hastened upon his way. 'Hmm.' One large arm raises to support his right elbow, his fingers tapping his chin in thought as he examines his foe, even now advancing upon a middle aged woman with long auburn hair. Marauder paces slowly, keeping a close eye on the pair as the wrinkled shirt man scores himself thirty five, forty five, fifty five cents! An arrogant glance over his shoulder; the younger chap sends Marauder a wink before he pockets his booty. Eyes widened, The Marauder concluded that there must be something devilish in this man's nature, his tongue made of silver and his brain of pure evil. A few quick strides carry them together, wrinkled blue shirt starts and spins about quickly, fear etched into his features. 'Change. Spare?' Pockets devulge themselves of many coins and a single dollar bill, which was still floating towards the ground even as the young man lept the meridian and skipped out of sight. 'Victor. Spoils. It's all the same to me.' He says with a shrug. And spoils indeed, Marauder counted fully nine dollars and fifty two glorious, very American, cents. And with that he kicks up emboldened steps, his chest swelling; crosses the street towards the bus stop. There was enough room for three people to sit on the squat wooden bench, on one side was perched a small blond haired and blue eyed boy, fresh with invigorating youth he could only be of fifteen or sixteen. His hair stood up straight in a long arc across his head, eyebrows, ears, lips, and tongue all contained glittering peices of jewellery. Which of course stands in stark contrast to his pale skin, mascara and black clothes proudly emblazoned with some freakish design far beyond The Marauders desire to decipher. On the other end of the bench is an old woman, black dress, sunday hat, glasses, cane, the whole deal, and her big fat giant purse sits between her and the boy. A defensive maneuver no doubt. Striding up still flush with his previous victory of pocket change, The Marauder examines the pair, his steps slow, neither have moved, and the large bag still consumes the only available seat. Marauder checks the bus listings, steps a little closer, and alas both ignore him. He clears his throat, and finally the elderly woman glances up at him. 'Is that your dollar on the ground?' He queries, pointing to the old woman's right. 'Oh?' She responds, and with the persistent slowness her kind are notorious for, she creaks into a standing position. She shuffles in a full about turn, and sets about searching. 'A dollar you say?' She warbles. Marauder dashes forward, pushes her bag over and plants himself next to the teenager, who by now is looking at him with a disdained sort of apathy, which his kind too are notorious for. Teenagers are a sorry lot. 'Dude... what's with that mask?' The irony settles for a moment. 'Curiosity. Cat.' With a befuddled nod the addle brained, puberty stricken youth looks back to the road and takes a second to decipher the meaning of his new neighbor's reply. 'You a wrestler or something?' He says and looks back with ignorant persistance. The Marauder hesitates a moment and then nods. 'Are you even Mexican?' 'The cat died. Curiosty. Cat. You know?' 'Oh... OH, I get it!' Marauder gives the kid a dumbfouded shake of the head. 'So you like, gonna be at that show tonight? That Dangerous Games?' The Marauder doesn't reply at first - 'Cuz me and my friends are going.' He contiues... The Marauder doesn't - 'Should be a good show.' ... The Marauder doesn't reply at first, instead - 'I'll make an appearance.' Marauder replies. 'Everybody likes Dread, but not me. I'm a Knight fan, since back in Dragon Wrestling Association.' 'You? A Knight fan? Feathers. Flock.' 'Yeah. What's your name anyway?' 'The Marauder.' 'Never heard of you. so is today your debut then? 'You could say that.' 'Classy way to show up though.' 'Beggers. Choosers. When the time comes, I'll play my hand. Aces. Sleeves.' The elderly woman returns to find her seat now occupied by her purse, fortunately she'd by that time forgotten she even had a purse, or that she had a seat. 'Find your dollar?' Marauder asks. 'Oh?' She shuffles to turn about. 'A dollar you say?' With the whine and hiss of air brakes a great metal bus arrives, waits for a moment and then speeds away again, the old woman shuffles back around. 'Oh! My purse!'
