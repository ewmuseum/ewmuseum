Point of View

24 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 24/08/2006 Jacob McKail entered the bar and promptly slumped down into a booth, tiredly. Yesterday, on GCW Worldwide fifty-one, McKail had a pretty gruelling chair match with that damned “mystery man” - they’d unmasked the moron, but McKail didn’t much care about the fool’s identity - and pretty much every part of his body had been hurting ever since. Ah, the life of a professional wrestler. McKail idly glanced around to see where the damned waitress had gotten to - the beer wouldn’t serve itself and right about now a beer was exactly what he needed. The woman was stationed right by some sailors a few booths over and the two seaman were desperately trying to get in her extra large panties like a couple of dogs during mating season. McKail quickly realised it could take some time for the banter to come to an end and so extracted a packet of cigarette’s out of his dark green grunge jacket, flipped open it’s lid and glared at the last remaining cancer stick, his face soon contorting in absent-minded puzzlement. “Goddamn,” he muttered, “I could’ve sworn I had more...” As soon as the words crossed his lips, he remembered precisely where they’d gone. In the locker room just before the chair match, he had two smokes just piss off some damned rookie who thought that he owned the damn joint. He didn’t say much, but he sure as hell made enough damned pointedly loud coughs and when McKail initially made eye contact with the fool he kept on glaring at the large no smoking sign plastered to the back wall, just above the red steel lockers. McKail remembered commenting on how bad the fellows cough was and his face promptly turned to thunder - looking as though McKail had burst into his house on Christmas day and pissed on his kids. McKail chuckled to himself just thinking about it, causing a good number of the fellow bar patrons, to glare at him bizarrely, but McKail didn’t much care about that either. He fished out the last cigarette, delicately placed it in his mouth and lit it, just as the waitress finally waddled over, throwing a final playful wave at the two, obviously desperate, horny sailors. “You can’t light that in here,” the waitress declared, chewing heavily on strawberry chewing gum - the smell of the stuff is unbearably unmistakable. McKail took a drag and then asked, “What?” The waitress rolled her eyes around her head, sighed and clucked. “This is a no smoking establishment, sir.” She wasn’t smiling when she said it, so McKail assumed that she wasn’t joking. More to the pity. “I know,” McKail told her, a wide smile plastered across his face. “That’s why I came in here.” The waitress contorts her face, like McKail knew she would. “I don’t understand.” “I’m sure you don’t,” McKail told her, taking another drag of his cigarette and allowing another mild chuckle. “But then, I don’t really give a fuck.” “This isn’t funny, sir.” The waitress barked, desperately trying to keep her cool. This was beginning to get better than McKail had ever imagined it would. “Not in the slightest.” McKail chuckled again. “That’s odd, I think it’s hilarious.” He took another drag and pointedly blew the smoke at the waitress. She was visibly shaking with anger now and McKail could hardly contain a grin. “I guess that all depends on your point of view.” **** One day a young woman staggers into a police station, her clothes all dirty and torn, her face heavily bruised and her eyes completely awash with tears. The stationed officer quickly springs out from behind his desk to approach her. He’s seen this before. It’s happened many time during his watch and he remembered every single one of them with a strange clarity that he couldn’t, or perhaps wouldn’t, shake. He approached her slowly, tentatively, trying desperately not to scare her - rape victims were fragile at best. She allows him to draw closer and falls into his comforting arms gratefully. She can stop now, she’s safe. Now think to yourself, what kind of image does this conjure in you head? Pity? Anger? Rage? Compassion? What if I were to tell you the woman was a single mother, walking home after a rare night on the town with her friends, while her mother watched her child? What if I told you she’d been followed all night and finally set upon by more than one man? How would you feel then? Any different from before? Angrier maybe? What if what I told you was a lie? What if the woman wasn’t a single mother after all? What if she was, say, a prostitute? How would you feel about it then? Does your opinion change in any way? Do you care any less about her and what’s she’s been through? Maybe not, but consider this: perhaps she hasn’t been raped in any traditional sense at all. Perhaps one of her many clients has treated her particularly rough one day during one of their regular sessions. Perhaps she was even enjoying it, in a purely professional business sense. However, unbeknown to the prostitute, her regular client has made some very bad business decisions as of late and his finances aren’t what they once were - not nearly enough to keep a regular hooker in regular business. To cut a very long story short, her client’s cheque bounces and bounces quite spectacularly. He disappears from public sight and does not even attempt to contact her any more. So, she roughs up her clothes a little, messes up her hair and has her pimp slap her around a little - that was the easiest thing of all to arrange, no doubt - and then stumbled into the police station with a whole sob story as long as your arm ready to spout at the kind police officer. How do you feel about the woman now? Still feel angry for her? Still feel compassion for what she’s been through? It’s doubtful, but there still a few saps out there, no doubt, who still feel for a woman’s plight on the game. Finally, ask yourself this: without the information that I fed you, what would your point of view really be? **** McKail make a point of finishing his cigarette before leaving the bar, flicking ash on the unconscious sailors and slurping down the beer he made the waitress get him before the police showed up - he heard the sirens approach as he rounded the corner of the street and made it to his car. The whole bar drama turned out to be more fun than he ever expected it to be, but his celebrations would end there - and why should they? After all, the whole annoyance of that damned masked man was over now, McKail had wrapped a chair around his head just to make sure of it, and after the match the GCW officials handed him his prize for taking that fool out. Next week, on Worldwide, McKail was placed in a number one contender match against the current GCW Cruiserweight champion Tessa Windsor. Seemed to him as though this was a long time coming and he’d wanted a shot at Windsor for the longest time - ever since leaving the GCW Television championship behind him. But the title scene, and pretty much every aspect of the organisation, has been in a perpetual state of confusion and chaos recently, with these damned factions showing up and vying for dominance. In short, the playing field was no longer level, it was obscured with bullshit and treachery of the highest kind. Hell, that suited McKail just fine. Any fight was a good fight and despite all odds, McKail knew, without a shadow of a doubt, that he would prevail because he was the meanest son of a bitch around and the best that GCW had to offer. But then, that was just one point of view.
