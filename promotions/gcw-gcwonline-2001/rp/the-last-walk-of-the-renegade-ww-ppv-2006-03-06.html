The Last Walk of The Renegade (WW & PPV)

6 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3-6-06 Cracks in a sidewalk. They serve as a constant reminder, a confirmation of a life well served providing the path for men and women to follow. Like the scars on a knife fighter’s arm, the cracks reveal a sense of personality. Proof that it were there, through every rainstorm and winter snow, enduring the unflinching eyes of time only to be blemished by the very people who needed it most. Trampled on and forgotten by the very same people who laid its smooth trail through the vast jungles of suburbia. It was a sad fate to be a sidewalk. Rich Rollins figured they had a lot in common. He watched the cracks pass beneath him with subdued curiosity, wondering whether it was worth jumping the border for Canada or Mexico so he could escape his own fate. As if it were that easy; he could disappear, sure, but the crack he’d undoubtedly leave behind on the concrete of American subconscious was far more noticeable than an imperfection on an inner-city sidewalk. He couldn’t lie to himself no matter hard he tried, and the harder he tried, the more guilty he felt. Suddenly feeling extremely self-conscience, The Renegade pulled the tab on his plain gray jogging hoodie and watched as the world became a small cloth porthole. A few people had recognized him a few blocks back, pointing at him and whispering to the man behind the counter or their father or best friend. “It’s that baby killer,” his mind’s ear could imagine them hissing, eyeing his passing with a sort of disgust usually reserved for rapists and child molesters. “He’s fucked now. I hope they torch him like he torched that poor woman.” The voices began to grow. “He’s going to try and run,” a teenage boy whispered to his friend on the street corner as Rollins jogged by, but the kid didn’t even look at him. As Rollins pulled away he risked a quick glance back at the boy, but the kid didn’t have a friend. He just stood on the corner with a magazine, enjoying the sunny day that Rollins was so desperate to shut out. It was hard to distinguish fact from fiction these days. His trial awaited, a fact that was painfully difficult to ignore during what was unquestionably his biggest week in the world of professional wrestling. Here he was, Rich “The Renegade” Rollins, former multiple-time GCW World Heavyweight Champion and Hall of Famer, reigning PTC Global Champion, and finalist in the biggest inter-promotional tournament in wresting history. In less than a week he was also scheduled to vie for the GCW World title yet again in what was shaping up to the be the most prominent and hotly-contested tournament in GCW history. GTT5 and Rampage, the two stages that Rollins must somehow find a way to share, to dominate, and to do it all before he was sentenced to death by a jury of his peers. Seven days. It was all he had left in the land of freedom. One hundred sixty-eight hours of independence before the state of Illinois threw him a cell and swallowed the key. Ten thousand eighty minutes of hope for a lifetime of dishing out defeat, that’s what fate had in store for Rich Rollins. He had spoken with a few lawyers at Steven Caldera’s request, but he had turned them all away. This was ultimately Rollins? journey to take, not an opportunity for his boss? college buddies to flex their intellectual prowess. They had still helped him negotiate a deal that would allow him to compete through the conclusion of Rampage, but the GTT5 finals were still fair game for the prosecution. As it stood he might not even make it to the finals that he had, after years of grueling work and dedication, finally earned his position in. The thought made The Renegade’s mouth go dry, so he spat an angry glob of sour saliva as he passed a mailman dropping letters into a storefront window box. He didn’t even have the energy to imagine what the man would say, or if he even saw his face at all. It was then that Rich Rollins found himself staring into the stone dead eyes of St. Augustine. He hadn’t been running in any real direction or towards any tangible location, but here he was, standing on the steps to redemption that led to the hallowed arch of St. Augustine’s chapel. The life-size statue looked upon the shadowed face of The Renegade, and those listless eyes of granite rock seared a sudden and frightening path into Rollins? heart. He was not a man of faith, and he never really had been. Looking up those steps, Rich Rollins could hear the unsated rumblings of the spiritual man who dwelled within himself. He left the steps far behind as he passed through that hallowed arch. What the hell else did he have to lose? ~<>~<>~<>~ Meanwhile, down the street? A wayward mass of oiled lettuce fell from Greg’s Subway sandwich as he squinted up the avenue. “Hey, I think I recognize that guy.” Hernandez turned around, craning his neck over their table as he tried to pinpoint his companion’s point of interest. “I don’t see anybody.” “There,” Greg pointed, placing his sandwich back on its wrappings. “That guy headed into St. Augustine’s.” “The one in the hood?” “Yeah, the one in the hood. He ran by a second ago and pulled the hood closed as soon as he made eye contact with me.” Grey’s eyes narrowed as he watched the man disappear into the church as recognition finally dawned. “That’s Rich Rollins, man.” Hernandez frowned at his friend, a decidedly sour expression for a man who possessed three chins. “The cop-killing wrestler” The dude who burned that pregnant lady?” “Yeah, that’s the one,” Greg confirmed with a slow nod of his head. “Looks like he’s trying to enjoy the afternoon incognito.” His eyes narrowed further as one corner of his mouth twitched in deep thought. Hernandez inhaled another sloppy mouthful of meatball submarine before he caught his friend’s expression. “What’s got you so spooked” The guy’s going to prison anyway, what’s it to you?” Greg turned on his Hispanic-American companion with a sympathetic sigh. “You ever hear about Magic Johnson these days?” “Yeah,” Hernandez said around a mouthful of meatballs, “he’s got AIDS still.” “No, he doesn’t.” Greg shook his head fiercely, leaning over the table with a conspiratorial air hanging over his head. “He got cured, man. He was diagnosed in like, what, 1991?” “Okay, so he got cured.” It was obvious Hernandez was more emotionally involved in his lunch than Greg’s conspiracy theory, but he tried to humor his friend anyway. “What’s your point?” “He had money, man. A lot of money. People like him, people like OJ Simpson, people like Rich Rollins, they don’t have to play by the same rules you and I live by. They’ve got the money to do whatever the hell they want, and they’ll buy whoever the hell they need to buy in order to get it.” Hernandez shrugged. “So?” Greg slapped his palms on the table, nearly knocking his sandwich off the edge in the process. ?So?! He’s going to get off with all the force of a fucking slap on the wrist! He’s a Chicago celebrity, a national icon for his business, and his boss is a fucking multimillionaire. You really think that man is going to see jail time for the things he’s done?” “Yes.” It was sharp and pointed, leaving no room for argument. Hernandez returned his friend’s sympathetic look with a shrug. “I have faith in the system, Greg. Just because the man has the means doesn’t mean that everybody else is a complete idiot. He’ll be tried and judged” wait, where the hell are you going? Greg!” But Greg had already hopped the short Subway patio rail and had begun jogging in the direction of St. Augustine’s. Hernandez pulled himself upright, his overweight frame not doing well with the sudden excitement. “Greg! What are you doing?” “My civic duty as a humanitarian,” Greg called back over his shoulder. ~<>~<>~<>~ Inside St. Augustine’s? “Forgive me Father, but I’ve been a bit of a bastard as of late.” There was an awkward silence on the other side of the confession window as the priest cleared his throat at the blatant disregard of etiquette and language. Rollins didn’t mean to be difficult; it was just who he was. “This is the Lord’s house, my son. Please be mindful of yourself and respect His house.” “Sorry Father,” Rollins amended as he unsuccessfully tried to sink back in the rigid booth pew. “I’ve just got a lot of issues, you know” It’s not like everybody else doesn’t, because trust me, I know. But they ain’t dealt with anything like this.” “How long has it been since your last confession?” the priest pried with a calming drone that made Rollins feel strangely secure. After all, Rollins supposed it was his job. “About twenty-seven years, Father.” “How old are you, my son?” “Well, I’ll give you three guess between twenty-six and twenty-eight,” Rollins quipped, then immediately clenched his teeth. His wit was sharpest when he was afraid, and even though he knew he had no reason to be, sitting inside a confession booth for the first time absolutely terrified him. “Yeah, I’m twenty-seven, at least according to my birth certificate.” “Are you of the Catholic faith?” asked the priest, understandably a bit put-off by his confessor’s blatant lack of respect for his authority. The Renegade tried to peer through the gilded window that separated them, but all he could see was a large indistinguishable shadow breathing on the other side. “No,” he finally said, then quickly added, “but I’m startin” to think that maybe I should put some faith in something other than myself for a change.” The shadow bowed forward as Rollins thought he saw the priest pull out his Bible. “You have no strength if you stand alone, my son.” “I’ve always stood alone,” Rollins replied. “And to be honest, it’s done me a lot of good. I’ve made more money than I could ever hope to spend, and I’ve seen more of this world than I ever dreamed I would. I have everything, Father. What did God give me that I couldn’t do for myself?” “My dear lost child,” the priest said with concrete conviction, “he gave you a choice.” Rollins laughed, and it was most definitely not a happy sound. “Is that the best you got, Father” That shit means nothing to me. He gave me a choice to do what? To believe in Him, to bow and scrape and worship His invisible majesty so that I can feel all warm and fuzzy when I turn the lights off at night? Please, spare me the religious bullsh-?” “You came in here to hear me say that God doesn’t love you, is that what you want?” The booth suddenly became so silent that Rollins swore he could hear a church mouse snicker at his expense. “Yeah,” Rollins finally offered, “I was hopin” you could just drop the bomb on me now so I won’t be so surprised later on.” The blurry shadow shook its head sadly. “He is testing you, my son.” “I killed two men.” The anger in The Renegade’s voice took even himself by surprise. “I guess that means I flunked the exam, huh?” The priest didn’t seem remotely surprised by the revelation as he said: “Your fear is your weakness. It is every man’s weakness, and it comes in different packages wrapped in all manner of shiny decorations. One man sees a deck of cards and remembers how he lost his family to a gambling addiction. Another listens to a song on the radio and trembles, re-living the murder of her family as the same song played on the same station all those years ago. Some people see the Devil when they close their eyes at night. I’ve heard it all, my son. There is nothing new on God’s earth, and no new perversion of His will that hasn’t already been carried out in excess all over the world. You need to understand this so that you may come to understand your fear, and destroy it.” The priest’s unasked question burned in Rollins? mind: what was it that The Renegade feared? What drove him to live a life of recluse, trapping him in a dark and bitter world where nothing ever got better, only more and more perverse? Even before he could wrap his mind around the question Rollins already knew the answer, and before he could try and voice it his mouth was already moving. “Have you ever tasted the adrenaline of twenty thousand screaming people all at once, chanting your name so loud that your eardrums just want to fucking explode?” While his voice began to soften, Rich Rollins? emotional roller coaster had only just taken off. “It’s not a drug, Father. It’s not a crutch or a fix or whatever the hell the magazines tell people, but it’s who I am. I’ve walked that aisle for so many years that it’s become a part of my soul, seeing those faces and hearing their chants, feeling and feeding off their every movement almost exactly in the way they feed off of mine. That’s what love is to me, Father. It’s a faceless mob screaming for your blood, screaming for your life, but it’s only because they wish they could live it. “I’m not a good man Father. I never was, and I probably never will be, but damnit I just can’t imagine what my life would be like if I could never walk that aisle again.” Rollins paused, watching as a hundred matches against a hundred opponents unfolded on the stage in his mind. “I’ve put a sour face on a lot of young bucks in my line of work, and I’ll be the first to tell you so. I’m a cocky shitheel who most of my co-workers can’t stand, but I know it’s just because they’re jealous. I’ve been doing this for so long that I’m not sure what to do when the weekend is over.” “What happens then?” asked the priest with a genuinely gentile curiosity. “I’ve got to meet a man tomorrow night, and I’ve got to fight him.” Rollins balled his fists so as not to punch a hole through the confessional door. “If I don’t take this man out, a man who some believe to be a vampire, then it could very well be the last walk of The Renegade.” “A vampire?” the priest asked, incredulous yet somewhat amused. “What is this about?” Rollins chuckled. “It’s his shtick, Father. I’ve faced him before, but not many people think I can pull off a miracle.” “So you’re the one they’ve spoken about on the news,” the nameless priest realized as the shadow nodded reverently. “Your presence here is not a coincidence, my son. This was God’s will, whether you believe in His plan or not. As daunting as your crimes may appear to our mortal brothers and sisters, in His eyes, you’ve done nothing more than what you were meant to do. His forgiveness is limitless, Mr. Rollins. If you leave this place with nothing more than that, you will have taken all you needed to know.” Rollins? head was in his hands as he sat in deep reflection, trying to reconcile his sins before the state of Illinois had their chance to take that choice from him. “You do not find God behind prison walls,” the priest said, practically reading Rich Rollins? mind. “He finds you. Go in peace, my son.” And that was that. Offering a half-hearted thanks to the faceless priest, Rollins tried to collect his nerves as he pushed the confessional door out, flooding the booth with a prismatic rainbow of Jesus? manger-side menagerie captured in sparkling stained glass high above. Loosening his hood, The Renegade finally let his guard down as he exposed his face to the house of Christ. “I knew it was you,” Rollins vaguely heard behind him right before something heavy knocked his head halfway around his shoulders. The sudden impact left him so disoriented that Rollins couldn’t even make out shapes as he felt a man turn him over, hoisting him on one shoulders as the priest’s voice shout out: “This is a house of God! Leave the man be, for none shall pass judgment in His-?” But it all went fuzzy as another blow rendered Rollins unconscious. ~<>~<>~<>~ “Oh dude, what the hell are you doing”!” Hernandez’s face was almost purple as he struggled for breath, having finally caught up to Greg who had taken the incapacitated Rich Rollins around to the back of St. Augustine’s. “You knocked him out” In a church? Are you fucking crazy?!” “He’s a cop-killing baby butcher, man.” Greg was too focused on frisking his target while he still could, procuring Rollins? wallet with a satisfied smile. “We’ve got an opportunity here, don’t you see that?” “No, I see you mugging a man who’d probably snap your neck if he were awake right now,” Hernandez shot back, trying to wedge himself between his friend and The Renegade. “Get off of him!” “W-whugg” s-ssorry?” Rollins mumbled as Hernandez ripped him from Greg’s grasp. As the burley Hispanic placed himself between Rollins and Greg, nobody noticed as The Renegade’s eyes fluttered open. “This isn’t right, and you fucking know it,” Hernandez roared, pushing Greg back with a stiff arm. “Greg, stop it!” “He’s a waste of human breath! He’s fucking soap scum on the toilet bowl of society, and you’re defending him?” Greg was furious, but not so furious as to forget to pocket Rollins? wallet. “I just want a few swings, man. Give me that-?” “You already got that when you blind-sighted him in a fucking church, Greg.” “Cowards always attack from behind,” Rollins suddenly spat as he tore himself away from Hernandez, “and I can always smell the yellow on a man’s spine.” He struck like a raging elephant, shoving Hernandez into the mesh link fence beside them en route to one startled Greg. Hernandez snapped a few of the fence’s bindings as his large girth fell into them, unable to offer his friend assistance as Rich Rollins tackled Greg to the ground, holding the man’s throat in one palm and his wrists above his head with the other. Greg gasped and kicked, but Rollins ended the struggle instantly as he sat his full weight on the man’s chest. His shoulders heaved with fury as The Renegade looked down into the terrified form of Greg, another face in the faceless mob that had come to collect some good “ol fashioned vigilante vengeance. The Renegade was short and to the point. “Do you think you know me?” he asked in a dangerously quiet way, grinding the man’s head into the pavement as Greg failed to come up with an answer. “Do you think you fucking know who I am” You don’t know the first fucking thing about me, bucko.” Rollins released his opponent’s throat, rubbing the goose egg that was starting to grow on the back of his own skull. “You’ve got some nerve walkin” up to an alleged murderer, man. I especially like the part where you knock me around a house of God, only to drag me outside just to pick my pockets. Now tell me, who’s the sinner in this scenario?” “W-what” I’m s-sorry Rollins, I was just-?” ?Who’s the sinner here?” Rollins screamed, causing Greg’s legs to curl up instinctively. The smaller man began to sob. “I’m a sinner,” he cried, shying away from The Renegade’s soulless stare. “Please forgive me Jesus, I’m a sinner?” “I’m not your Jesus, so don’t cry to me,” Rollins snarled as he released Greg’s wrists and stood up, regarding the man disgustedly as he finished his fetal curl. “You’d better pray to somebody who’s listenin”, “cause if I ever see you again?” But he didn’t finish the threat. He simply couldn’t. The man was a blubbering mess and any minute now people on the street were going to notice that something was going on. What was it worth to humiliate the man with threats that he didn’t mean? Was it worth another public backlash, breaking his bail by “assaulting” this poor defenseless man? That’s how the media would see it, Rich Rollins knew full well. It took all he had to turn around, to tighten his hood over his face once more and start heading for the street. Hernandez shook as he walked by, still tangled in the mess of chain link Rollins had sent him through. Looking through the tiny hole that his hoodie allowed, The Renegade offered the man a few words of wisdom before disappearing through the alleys: “Tell your friend that Jesus loves him.” Rollins would later wonder if the Hispanic man ever knew he was being sarcastic, but in the end it didn’t matter. It was just another crack in his sidewalk, threatening to become the pothole that he used to call his life. ~Fin~
