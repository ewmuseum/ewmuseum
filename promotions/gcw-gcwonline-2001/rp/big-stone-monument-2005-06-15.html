Big stone monument

15 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/15/05 'That ain't right, naw; that ain't right.' 'You don't have a body. Where d'you get off trying to make me feel bad?' I shut my conscience up, tired of all the backseat driving I'd gotten from that unlicensed swine. He would do more good if thrown on a fire for warmth than I'd ever gotten out of him; if someone would figure out where in the brain the conscience was located, and then learned a method to remove it without injuring me. Until that scientist exists, I just keep shutting it up on those rare occasions it makes a peep. Not that it's ever been unfounded. Ask Trey--I'm a bad person. Let's focus on him for once, eh? A good place to start would be at where he met me. Years ago, back when I was an active wrestler. You can ask Trey about that one too, I'm better than any of the soulless gimps that've made a name for themselves around here, having done so only in the sheer lack of Jordan they get to enjoy. He came into this place, TSC, when I was one of the only people that mattered. Old friend of Casey's, apparently, and that led him to debut at our side in the worst named stable in the history of wrestling--Primevil. He was a dreadlocked jobber that got the shit kicked out of him a lot, then got fired for not making any impact. Cazart. I helped Primevil beat him in the final message he gave the federation before being fired, 'I fucking suck.' There were no voices of dissent, and the world went on as such. It was easy to forget him, since he still hadn't grown out of being the wacky douchebag that everyone hates. He was like Casey's kid brother, except instead of having a head full of voices, he had a head full of Swiss Cake Roll dreams. That's what I remember most about him, from back then. There didn't seem to be much there other than a junkfood junkie that tried to live like a rockstar, and I wrote him off as such until Casey managed to bring him back. I was in a new place, 3WA, and was already the only clear-cut genius the place had ever seen. Since Casey'd been the one with the bright idea to convince me to join, he figured that bringing Trey in wouldn't be a bad deal. I ran it by Lexicon, and he happened to like the kid when they met. That, of course, lets him take all credit for not only getting Trey famous, but for Trey even existing. Thanks Lexicon, without your self-absorbed bragging, I'd have so much less to laugh about. Another of course, Trey still sucked. He came in with a flourish of success that'd make anyone blush, helped break LeStatt Knight's ankle or something, then dropped onto the backburner in typical Trey fashion. He was over because of who he ran with, but no one seemed to care about him. The carefree rockstar had had some time to mature, and had grown into a decent person. Being a decent person clearly didn't translate well into ring skills, so Casey had a plan. That's where I made the mistake. I didn't hang up on him. It was as simple as Casey could be, which just isn't simple. We were to kidnap Trey, load him up on drugs, then torture him until he became a mindless pitbull. That's when his hair got destroyed and dyed, that's when he got the piercings, that's when he got the tattoos. After a month of controversy and confusion, Trey came back a changed man. Almost. He looked completely different and was definitely wrought with an otherworldly sense of Angst, but he still couldn't wrestle for shit. Casey wrote the experiment off as a failure, and Trey came to terms with what his best friends had done to him. Except, I helped him out. After Casey would have his fun burning Trey's dreads to the scalp or stabbing him with ink or whatever that crazy fucker did, I'd come in and I'd feed him and I'd apply the drugs, and I'd talk to him. With only two people in his life during that time, it was easy for Trey to completely condemn one as evil and to worship the other one as the only good he knew. This is when all the suggestion happened, if anyone was wondering, since he was willing to listen to the good guy. I hid my identity from him the whole time, careful to not plant any bad hoodoo about myself that he could draw back to after I made him a killing machine. That's why he failed when he came back. Casey saw no change because he'd just tortured him, and I just kept quiet and let Casey think the whole thing had fucked up on him. Keeping quiet was easy for a long time, since I was wearing a mask and hiding from the police, but eventually the time had to come for Trey to figure out who was to blame. It was a quick phone call before I finally got arrested, and the only thing anyone learned was that he'd already figured it out. So, why did I bring him into GCW and turn him into the tremendous bastard he's become? The same reason I end up doing anything high-profile: Casey Torpid. He owed me money and didn't look to be ready to pay up, so I put the pressure on him and got Trey signed. It wasn't hard, just had to mutter 'Croatoa' and he'd impress the most cynical of the breed of losers known best as internet smarks. Then Casey quit, and I just let Trey run roughshod through whoever got thrown in front of him. He still hasn't let me down. 'Isn't that right Trey?' 'Huh?' 'Huh?' I looked to him, a little shocked. 'It's an easy question.' 'Shyeah, if I knew what you were talkin' about.' 'I've been quiet for the last five minutes, haven't I?' He nodded to me. 'Fuck.' 'You sure you should be drivin'? Sounds like you're fucked up as hell, man.' 'We're almost there. No harm yet's as good as no harm ever.' 'Where's there?' 'When the gas tank goes empty, that's there.' 'Haha, shit. Really man, where we goin'?' I tapped the fuel gauge and put my eyes back to the sand in front of us. I hated it more than I hated anything else, the endless repetition of golden sand past cactuses past rocks past lizards, and kicked myself for ending up back there. Hazy voices ran in and out of my conscious, bringing bite-sized bits of conversation that I'd put in the back of my head for sanity's sakes. Speed didn't matter, just an ever-growing number that didn't scream anywhere near as loudly as the voices--roars then. No, conscience. Fuck you. Content to accelerate across the dunes, I fumbled with a joint from my pack of Newports, lighting it hasty and uneven. It started an awkward burn, the top left behind the glowing cherry that raced along the bottom, and I laughed at how pointless the whole thing was. Tried to write my headache off to something other than caffeine withdrawal and rubbed my forehead, wary. Trey perked up. He looked confused, worried, or lost. I didn't know. I didn't really care that much, either. 'Where we headed?' I was running on landmarks, trying to remember which hill would send me south, and which rock meant to turn east. They were all vague symbols that had become half-buried in the shifting sands. It was going back home and seeing all the familiar places, razed half to the ground and recognized only for their locations. I put off explaining and almost turned the stereo up--but the music sucked. 'C'mon, J. What's up?' His voice had dropped an octave, like we were about to talk about death and dying and cancer. Let Trey be somber; I was weeks past even caring how things turned out. It was time to just tell my rationality to fuck off and accept that I was about to become a bitter asshole to the world for awhile. It happens, I deal with it. 'Dude.' 'What?' 'Where we goin'?' 'Isn't it obvious?' He looked around for a second. 'Nah. It ain't.' 'Straight into hell, man.' 'Shit.' 'Yeah.' 'Heavy shit.' 'Real heavy.' Smoke clouded my judgment, shattered it into a million pieces that were all determined to make me question the Everything. 'That car, man.' I looked to where Trey gestured. 'Looks familiar.' 'Should. That's hell.' I hit the brakes, digging deep ruts where my tires fought to break the momentum. We were close to the car then; that bright red Ferrari covered with a fine layer of sand and buried half-way up the wheels in it. I shook my head and leaned against it, lighting a Newport. 'That's Johnny's car.' I nodded. 'What's up?' I smirked. 'You know how bad things happen to good people?' 'Shyeah.' 'They happen to bad people, too.' 'Where's Johnny?' 'That's what I wanted to know.' Trey buried his face in his hands and wiped sweat back from his brow. Fresh beads were quick to drop, collecting in his crimson eyebrows. A grimace divided his face. 'Fuck, man.' He paced over to me slowly, not confidently, and leaned over with his hands on the scalding hot hood. I cringed for him. 'Fuck. Jordan.' 'Yeah?' I leaned back to take a deep drag, but got interrupted by hands twisting the bands of my wifebeater in to my neck. My back arched with the contour of the glass behind me. 'Where the fuck is Johnny?' 'You're hysterical.' He roared his question again: 'Where's Johnny?' I still had no answer for him. Somewhere in the sand; my cigarette was burning and waiting to be buried by a sudden burst. 'Calm down.' He jacked me against the side of the car; ripped my feet from the ground and screamed, his face contorted into hard angles and deep wells of shadow. 'What the fuck did you do to Johnny?' 'What he fucking deserved.' His eyes softened from slits to orbs, and his mouth slacked. Sweat ran highways down his face and shined in the glowering sun. His grip went too, and I found the earth as comforting as it'd been when I left it. I straightened out my shirt and found my Newport laying unburied and lit it again as Trey came to terms with what he knew. 'But what, J?' I shrugged. 'I left him out here, after Warpath. He wanted to talk about the good old days, or some other bullshit, and we got really fucked up. I left him, here. He hasn't found his car yet, so maybe he did get what he deserved.' 'Man. You gotta be fuckin' jokin'.' 'No. I'm not.' 'You killed him?' 'He got lost.' 'You fuckin' killed him.' 'I didn't care.' 'You can't just not care about someone's fuckin' life, man. There's a fuckin' difference--this ain't no kiddy bullshit. He's fuckin' dead out there, and it's your fault. And you, you're just smokin' your cigarette, actin' cool as hell, like nothin' fuckin' matters. Fuck, Jordan, you ain't even a fuckin' person.' I leaned my head back and basked in the sun, good arm draped laterally on the roof of the car. 'I didn't bury him.' 'Shit isn't funny.' 'Really? I laughed.' 'Fuck. What the fuck you gonna do about this shit?' 'Let it happen.' 'What if I left you out here, man? How'd you like that?' 'You're not leaving me out here.' 'And why the fuck not? The fuck you gonna do with your busted arm?' 'I'm smart.' 'You ain't pullin' no Professor X shit.' 'I know.' My hand slipped down to the belt loop of my pants, and I tugged the bottom of my shirt up enough to show Trey the grip of the Leinad shotgun-pistol in the waist of my pants. It was light and fairly small, but carried a big kick and an even bigger blast. 'Hah,' laughed Trey. 'So that's how it's gonna be? Gonna pull a fuckin' gun on me, blow my brains out in the desert? Like I was Johnny? Fuck you, man. You ain't shit.' I shook my head. 'It's safety. In case you, you know, planned to leave me out here as some sort of revenge. You're no angel of justice, Trey, so calm down and...' 'And what?' 'I didn't figure it out either. Maybe you should calm down and decide what we do next.' A few minutes passed. Hum the theme from Brazil repeatedly to simulate the passage of time! 'We find Johnny.' 'And--where?' He looked at me like I was the one with the horrible idea. 'Fuck, I dunno. You're the one that ditched him out here, you fuckin' asshole.' 'And if he's still out here,' I took a hard drag from the cigarette and thumped it into the wind, 'then he's dead.' 'Fuck man, fuck. Why'd you do it?' 'Like I said--' 'Why'd he deserve it, man?' I thought. Trey wouldn't care that Johnny wouldn't have me run his old company in peace, or that he wouldn't stop trying to get back at everyone involved in the worst ways possible. He'd think it was too drastic of a measure to just knock ol' Lexi down from his pedestal, even if he deserved it. They weren't the real truth, anyway. They weren't what made it easy, at least. 'Because that son of a bitch broke me.' 'What?' 'He held me to my Anarch contract, when the doctors said I wasn't cleared anymore. Let me wear that stupid fucking mask and go out there, every week, to stumble my way through matches so he wouldn't lose his place in the spotlight. He didn't give a shit, Trey. Why should I?' He turned his back to me and fumbled in his pockets for maybe nothing, maybe something to say. I lit another cigarette and leaned back up against the car, waited. Hell, what was there to say? 'Damn, don't he at least get a tombstone?' I laughed and looked at Trey. 'Got something in mind?' Hours later, we leaned against my Impala in exhaustion and admired the monument; the pyramid of stones hung high over either of our heads and eclipsed Johnny's Ferrari in its long shadow. It was tightly packed, large rocks and small rocks that fit together in the most intricate of ways. Perfect matches. More than Lexicon deserved. I had to piss. Trey waited in the car and I began to fear the only thing that was really left to fear, that it hadn't been enough to get rid of him. That somehow, the bastard was out there and being a major loose end, on top of outliving his own memorial. I needed resolution--to see the deal finalized. That, however, could take some time.
