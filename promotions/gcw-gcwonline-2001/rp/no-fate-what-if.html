No Fate. What If?

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Two weeks ago Before I realise what I’m doing, I’m on the floor beside her. I rest my hands on my knees for a moment, trying to get my breathing under control. I can feel my hands shaking and they’re sticky with blood. I tip back the bottom of her chin, ready to intubate, but my hands fall away and I fall apart. “She’s gone, you did what you could.. she’s gone” I voice says from behind me. An old feminine voice. A voice with cracks I could fall into. I’m panicked but I don’t cry. She can’t die... not like this. I drop my chin to my chest. Suddenly I’m pulled away from the body. I watch as they get her onto a backboard and strap her down. I’m held back in someone’s arms as they load her into the ambulance. While one guy puts in an IV line, another picks up the radio. The words MVP, thirteen-year-old female, severe head injury run through my mind. As they will through my dreams. When the cardiac monitor blanks out, the guy drops the receiver and starts CPR. “What’s her name?” a voice wavers from the ambulance. “Danni-? “Pistone” I snap. “Her name is Danni Pistone” I say, pulling myself away from the paramedic who’s been holding me back. In a flash it seems like I’m left alone, I’m not allowed in the trauma room. I’m left alone with the thought of Danni immobile on the road, a pool of blood seeping towards me as I tried desperately to bring her to life. I sit down, and then stand up. I wait for Cal to get here. What am I going to tell him? Do I tell him that she’s not breathing on her own? Do I tell him that she flatlined? Do I tell him I’m sorry? The ambulance ride here is just a fuzzy memory now. A long two hours later and I’m sat across from Cal, neither of us has touched the hospital food sat in front of us. Cals barely said a word, he can’t cry, he can’t speak about what he’s feeling. It’s the silence that makes me uncomfortable. In the English language there are orphans and there are widows, but no where in the dictionary do you find a word for a parent who had lost a child. Danni never came around, and they said she never would. Her heart continued beating but only with help. If by some miracle she did wake up, they assured Cal that she would never be the same. Danni had left on impact they’d said, or at least what made her Danni was gone. The body Cal was looking over was merely the shell. The car had hit her at such a speed that she didn’t stand a chance. Now as we sat in silence, the police would be out there searching for her killer. A man with no conscience, a man out there somewhere responsible for a hit and run. I watch Cal quietly. Grief is an inquisitive thing. It’s probably hit Cal with as much impact as that car hit his daughter. Grief’s especially hard when it’s unexpected. After my grandfather passed away, I would spend days up in my bedroom, never coming out. I spent days on end with my headphones on, if only so that I couldn’t hear my mother crying. If my mother could cry, someone who was not nearly as human as she should have been, then why couldn’t Cal cry? “Hadn’t you better catch your plane?” Cal asks, his voice flat and empty. “I’ve already missed it” I reply in a quiet voice. As silence falls over us again I can’t help but feel guilty. Like this is partly my fault. You see, when presented with a problem, the first choice you make, the decision you come to in those vital moments, might just be the wrong one. I kick myself. The choice I made was the wrong one. I don’t voice my concern to Cal. Not that he’d want to hear it anyway. Part of the reason I don’t confess my guilt to Cal is because part of me fears he’ll agree. Something inside of me is scared of him pinning the blame on me. So I shoulder this feeling alone. I deal with it during the silence. If I hadn’t have stayed with Katie, if I’d let her sort out her own problems, none of this would have happened. I’d have made it in time to say my goodbye to both Cal and Danni. She’d never have been outside. If I hadn’t listened to Katie, I could have been where I was supposed to be and Danni would not be dead. Sometimes when you make a choice, you will make the wrong one and you’ll end up letting someone down. And it’s always too late. I drop my face into my hands, somewhere between exhaustion and sorrow, guilt stewing deep inside me. Fates a bitch. I wish I could pass along the blame. What if I’d got up and left Katie? What if I’d left her to deal with her own problems? It wouldn’t have been out of character. What if I’d never gone for that coffee? What if I’d only been quicker? What if? I spend the night sat quietly, waiting for Cal to speak. Sometimes it doesn’t matter how hard you try, you end up letting someone down. I’ve seen men cry, I’ve seen men bigger and tougher than Cal break down into tears. But Cal doesn’t shed a single tear. Maybe he doesn’t cry for the same reason I don’t. My father wanted me to believe that men were made for something else, that they weren’t made for crying. I never believed this. Even then I wasn’t my fathers daughter. “Expect the unexpected” 5:49am. I pull my eyes away from the clock and look over at Cal. His face expressionless as he stares back at me. “That’s the last piece of advice I can give you.. expect the unexpected”. I can’t even begin to put my mind to this. “You should get going, I know you needed to be somewhere” he sighs, rubbing a hand across his face. “Really doesn’t seem to matter now” I say, my voice almost a whisper. “Life doesn’t end... not for you” “Maybe not” I say “But I missed my plane... other things can wait” Cal looks down. “I’ll be fine”. I want to apologise and tell him that it’s my fault. Even if it’s just to take the guilt away from him. The house feels empty, now that Cal lives alone. I try not to think about the fact that Danni will never skate across the room again, that she’ll never be at the door with a welcoming smile. “I’m already late.. I’m not scheduled for anything just yet. Funny isn’t it” I’ve only had one match and already I’m down for a two week break. Who said this job wasn’t relaxing?” I offer a small smile. Cal looks up with barely enough energy to even react. “A special show is being held... I’ll do my piece then. Or I’ll catch a plane tomorrow... like you said, life goes on. All over the world bad things are happening, each and every day... ? I pause for a moment, almost lost in thought. “We’re all going to be there.. doing whatever it is we’re supposed to do to make things better. And then we’ll see just how self serving these assholes really are..” “I know you don’t mean that, you’re just angry” Cal says with reason. “I can see that you want to be there, and you should be. You’re a good person, no matter what anyone else says. You’re better than most” I think of all those who would disagree. “I’ve seen how big your heart is... out there in the street. I’ve never seen you turn your back on someone who needed you.. not really. You may not let anyone in, but you don’t turn people away... you’re a good girl” Cal offers a dreary smile. “Yea, well just don’t tell anyone..” I look away, in truth, I wish I had caught the plane. Maybe then things would be different and I wouldn’t be stuck here now. “The world’s never going to give us a break, is it?” “What do you want me to say?” Cal shrugs “I don’t know” I sit forward in my seat. “Lie. Lie to me” He nods. “It gets better, the older you get the easier it gets. In the end, it all turns out nice”. He looks down, leans back into his chair and closes his eyes. “You gotta find your own breaks”. Three Days Later. The morning after I stayed up with Cal, I left him sleeping. Still in the chair, a sad expression on his face, an expression I imagine will be there for years. Part of it will be there forever. Quite by accident I came across a newspaper article, LOST SOUL, ANOTHER GIRL DEAD. It was Katie. After I’d left, she’d gone back to the place she was squatting in, and taken her own life. This had been her plan all along. As she spoke to me that day over coffee, she knew exactly what was going to happen. All she’d wanted to do was talk. For one last time she wanted to find someone to talk to. And I was that person. It didn’t matter what I’d said or done, she had it all planned out. I’d failed again. I was given a choice and it didn’t matter what I’d done, I’d made the wrong choice. And I’d let someone down, again. I don’t have anything to atone for, I have nothing to hide. Now it’s my turn to do something to help. I left Cal a note thanking him and apologising ten times over. I left money in a drawer, behind a picture of Danni. And then I left, got on a plane, determined to do something that matters. I made a choice as I watched Cal sleeping, a choice to make a difference. I think of Danni, a girl I barely knew really. I won’t blame fate, and I try not to blame myself. Now she’ll always be thirteen, always - on elaborate roller skates, dark hair, brown doe eyes, pristine, always with a smile. And right now, I’m finding smiles are hard to come by.
