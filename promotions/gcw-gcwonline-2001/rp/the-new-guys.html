The New Guys

Date: The day of before Worldwide 111 Valentino: But why not? Kids today. If this little 11-year-old nothing were my kid he'd know better than to question adults. But here I am, having to just sit and listen to this kid whine as though they owe it to him to take him along. Valentino: It's gonna be a big show! Why do I have to watch it here like everybody else? You're my brother, you can get me a pass! The kid doesn't need this kind of distraction. In the hours before the show it's all about being focused. You gotta get in the mood, you gotta get in the mode. Putting the game face on and all that. It's bad enough I gotta tell Banning to put the books down and study his opponents; now there's this babysitting crap to deal with. Now Winter... she knows the deal. But then I've been training her from the start. This new guy just gets in the way. Banning: Ey, I ain't gonna get inta this with you, T-No. This ain't gonna be no concert; 's a wrestlin show. Lotta antisocial assholes walkin round and they probly ain't know how ta act round people in a way that don't involve stickin out they chests an talkin down ta folk. Real talk, mos wrestlers got no sense of behavin like a normal gotdamn human bean an I don' wantcha near that. At least Banning had some sense, even if he had no idea what he was talking about. Not that he was wrong, but call me old-fashioned -- rookies have no business criticizing what goes on behind that curtain. It's professional fighting with some of the most lax rules. Let's see ultimate fighters have to deal with the possibility of backstage attacks. I dare Georges St. Pierre to take a chairshot to the face and laugh it off. You gotta be a little crazy, a little on edge, a little misanthropic to be in this sport and business and Banning still doesn't get it. You can't blame them because the people support them. Winter: Valentino, this is still work and we have to be professional. Your brother and I are not going to hang out -- we will be focused on the task at hand, so we will not be watching you. And there will not be anyone else you age present; backstage is not any place for children. See, Cameron knows the deal, but then again I should expect that from her. I've been training and mentoring her for the better part of two years now. Not yet past the rookie phase of her career but damned if she's not got the proper respect for the business. But being second generation that should be par for the course. I've molded that girl into a proper professional wrestler and now all she needs is experience. This Nate Banning kid... she brought him into it, but he's not my responsibility. I get that they've got some connection and I won't argue with it but I'm not about to start coaching every townie with a good punch she happens to pick up like a stray. But it's getting late and I gotta be up early to get these two up early. Banning's gotta get over to set up the ring and I need to run through some more strategy with Winter. Parting thought -- I HATE the kid being called "T-No" instead of Valentino. He's too young to be acting like he needs a street name. And even if he wasn't young, I still hate the T-No thing. Damn ghetto freeloaders; wonder where the hell Cameron picked them up. Look out for more road blogs from yours truly, David Brown, as I get back into the wrestling world more and more lately. ---- Nathan didn't like the diner. The colors were fine, the food was okay -- they managed to have a pretty good bacon cheeseburger. But venues like this were designed to play upon a particular cultural memory that just didn't fit. The Moonlight Diner on the corner of 8th Street and Liberty Avenue had that chrome-and-red-meets-checkerbox 50s motif. Suede pleather seats, male servers looking like the Good Humor Man, female servers dressed in pink and rollerskating around as if in some more mobile answer to Chinese foot-binding. It was cute and maybe white people would get a kick out of it, but a black man from Louisiana had an ENTIRELY different perspective on the 50s and probably didn't want it brought back up again. Cameron, of course, was oblivious to all this. To her, it was a little corny, but maybe nothing more than that- a decent place to do a little carb-loading before a match. She took another bite of her hamburger (plain, with no condiments). It wasn't the best hamburger she'd ever had, but then, food wasn't terribly fun when you were watching every single bite you put in your mouth. "So is your cheeseburger alright?" she asked, turning her hazel eyes to Nathan. She tried to avoid wrinkling her nose as she thought of how much grease was on the thing, but she didn't succeed. "Meat's pretty good. Must've used ground sirloin. I could only get the ground hamburger in big packs at Penn Dutch outside a town. Damn shame you think you gettin all that meat and then the fat cooks out and you ain't left with much." Unlike Cameron, Nathan didn't mind the food. Philadelphia was a good one thousand seventeen miles from his hometown of Lake Providence, but lightyears from the socioeconomic environment from whence he came. To call 'The LP' a ghetto was an insult to ghettos such as those in Philadelphia. Lake Providence had the unfortunate distinction of being dubbed the poorest city in America in TIME Magazine. Everybody in Flint, Michigan could stop their whining and suck it -- their eyes would bleed to witness a town so poor that even Wal-Mart wanted nothing to do with it. FUCKING WAL-MART. Such things were unknown to Cameron, and comments like Nathan's threw her. They made her uncomfortable - how was one supposed to relate to someone who had grown up so differently? "Yeah, I guess Dad must've used something similar. I remember he used to make these big hamburgers kinda like this on the Fourth of July." This Fourth of July would be a sight different. Cameron set down her hamburger and sniffed, looking suddenly out the window. Being completely oblivious to the emotion behind Cameron's brief moment of vulnerability, Nathan's muffled meat-filled mouth loosed a sound akin to, "You smell somethin?" The two hadn't known each other for that long, so there was a lot of learning for them to do. Darren Slade and Lucas Quinton may have been a team thrown together just for the one-shot booking, but they had no more ring experience together than did Nathan or Cameron. Nate would be wrestling his first professional match under the Johnny Fives persona, and he wasn't even that well trained. Cameron did what she could to bring him up to speed, but basically it would be Nathan's hardscrabble lifestyle, his experience fighting on the streets (as cliche as that is for African-American wrestlers these days), and good old spit and bile that were supposed to get him through. "I... no," Cameron said, looking down and picking up her hamburger again. It didn't look very appetizing anymore - and didn't look anything like her father's. She took another bite, chewed for roughly five seconds, and then swallowed. People got choked that way, by not chewing. "So are you ready?" she asked, tilting her head slightly. "Kind of exciting being up here, I've never been before." That was an understatement. A new city, a new situation, and... well, pretty much everything was getting thrown at Cameron for the first time this week. Though she was trying not to show it, her nerves were on a razor's edge. "Probly not, no. But I gotta do it, so I do it. Simple as that." Aside from the fact that Cameron had more training and actual ring experience on the Florida and Georgia indepedent circuits, occasionally venturing as far north as South Carolina on a couple of working vacations, Nathan was treating this merely like another job. In his life he'd worked many different jobs, some ethical, some not-so-ethical (though all legal), but the young man hadn't ever really seen working to be anything other than a survival mechanism. He didn't relish the idea of being a professional combat athlete but the potential earnings couldn't be denied. When one was living out of a van and having to raise a young boy, any get-rich-quick scheme might seem palatable even if it involved risk of bodily harm. The truth was, the prospect of being unprepared something completely alarmed Cameron. Her eyes were wide as she set her sandwich down again. "Well we've gone over a lot of the strategy that we might see in the ring from these guys..." In exhaustive detail. Cameron had a mind for this sort of thing, but she didn't have much of an instinct for knowing when she shouldn't inflict it on other people. Cameron was the strategist and would be the leader of the team. Nathan went by instinct but his was not an instinct to punish but an instinct to survive. To that extent, combined with his training, Nathan Siddeus Banning was going to be rather bare bones in the ring. Especially as he knew that he only had to knock somebody down for 3 seconds. No need to go crazy and end careers when confronting that fact. At that, Cameron had to laugh. She shook her head slightly. "You don't think that maybe there's a little more to it than that?" She had come off sounding much more condescending than she'd meant to. Nate's attention was partially dedicated to a 5'6" Latina woman outside, young and fair skin and with a bust far too out in the open for anybody not to stare. "Nothin I can do about it, right? My job's to go in an hit people more 'n they hit me." An aggravated sigh was Cami's answer to that. "You know..." And then she followed his gaze. "Hey! I mean..." And then she paused, remembering that she really didn't know Nate well enough to chastise him. She put on her best pacifying smile. "I just don't want to screw this up. I mean, they pretty much don't know anything about us, and I really want to make a good first impression." It was the kind of thing only a middle-class suburbanite could really worry earnestly about. "Good first impression? You already had a match." The polyamorous Nathan watched as the latest love of his life hopped into a cab and left him forever. With one form of meat now out of eyesight, Nate returned to his burger. The onions on the side were not touched; he was allergic and didn't appreciate that they were still placed onto the plate. "Besides, an don' take this the wrong way, but America loves hot piece uh ass. Ain't ta say that's all you are, but THAT'S your first impression before you even open your mouth. I mean, they hired me as a favor to you because why, you got sway withem? Life is all about keeping the T&A happy." Cameron's eyes widened and her mouth stood agape for a moment at what he said. She hastily swallowed. Her eyes snapped with indignance. "I'm not letting them make me into some Britney Spears Wrestling Slut Barbie. I don't care what it sells." Cameron was the type of woman who had prided herself on getting by on her skills and not her looks. Or at least, she let herself believe that. At this, she brushed some of her straight black hair out of her eyes. It was cut short around her face in bangs that were at least long enough to swoop over her forehead. Looking at Cameron, there was no way she was going to get away with not being marketed as a pretty face - she had almost elven features: a sharp chin, defined cheekbones, and almond shaped eyes that betrayed her half-Vietnamese ancestry quite clearly. "Yeah. Gotta control the image. S'why I'm doin the Johnny Fives thing. Nobody's gonna hire another poor black kid who don' speak good English." And this was the same young man who wanted to become a teacher. "Gotta change it up jus' enough ta give somethin new. Not ta say that personality ain't valued but if Denzel Washington ain't directing nobody gives a good gotdamn about somebody like me. People pay attention t'ya irregardless long 's you got cleavage and thick thighs. I say, appreciate it a little; gives ya advantage over us menfolk." Cami's expression was very akin to 'pitching a fit' as her mother would have called it. "Appreciate it? Why would I want to play into that system? I want people to recognize me for my talents, not... not... that stuff!" She'd had a number of boyfriends that had told her she came off like quite a prude. The A-1 Teriyaki Steak Sauce (which may as well have been a delicacy as far as Nathan was concerned) spilled onto his Grambling polyester. The yellow number 13 went unscatched, the dark viscuous fluid dripping onto the black primary color of the garment. Using the complementary glass of water, the red-haired Nate used an ice cube to dab the stain with a napkin. "Well you ain't tryin ta use ya dad's name, so talent won't be enough. Gotta have charisma and character, sex appeal, and luck. Not just about wrestling but life. You an I ain't exactly the face of any major networks or fashion catalogs or movies... I'm too black, you're too big for an Asian chick... we just don' fit. First impression? You might be older 'n me but you gotta lot ta learn about people who ain't know you." She wanted to get mad at him. Desperately. But there was a part of her that knew that what he was saying was right, fair or not. She sighed softly. "I think I have a tide pen back at the hotel if you want me to try to get that out." She wasn't the subtlest when it came to attempting to avoid a topic. But the hungry Nate, having had nothing since breakfast 7 hours ago before he had to help set up the ring and stage for Worldwide 111, was already wrist-deep in his pound of sirloin burger again. Chewing but 7 times and swallowing (digestion be damned), he shook his head. "Nah, stains don' show on this, it can wait." Nathan Banning had become an expert of selecting clothing for himself and little Valentino that could go without washing. It was a pain to have to constantly wash clothes at the laundromat on a very limited budget. Cami opened her mouth to say something, but realized she would have sounded exactly like her mother. She wasn't sure if she found the prospect settling or not. "Okay..." She took another bite of her burger, which had suddenly started to taste rather like cardboard. But a carb was a carb, she supposed. "Want to hit the gym for a little while today?" That, at least, was a place where they didn't have to worry too much about the differences between them. "Sure," Nathan looked away from his tag team partner as yet another beautiful woman walked through the door. "I'll bring T-No, he can burn off the cabin fever." She couldn't help smiling at that. She loved kids. "Yeah... hopefully, he won't run the staff ragged, but some of them look like they could use a workout anyway." She had mostly finished her burger now. It was a bit gristly at the end, and a bit dry, as it cooled off. There were times when Cami regretted not ever letting herself indulge, but now was not the time to dwell on it. "We can get out of here whenever you're ready... I guess I'm pretty much done." She had to wonder if he heard her, what with the stunning brunette leaning over the counter facing Nathan, ordering a coke float and a chicken fingers meal.
