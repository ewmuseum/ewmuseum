Moving On: Part II

3 May 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 05/03/11 Aaron stomped his foot on the mat as Karina Wolfenden staggered to her feet across the ring. Aaron slapped the top turnbuckle with intensity as his prey finally turned around to face him. He charged forward and lifted his boot into the pretty face of the United States Champion as she propped herself up in the corner. The crowd groaned at the impact as Fujita freed his foot from the corner and grabbed Karina’s arms in a half Tiger, half Dragon hold. Bryan: Fujita is really rolling now! He’s looking for that half and half suplex! This normally leads right into that QG-Theory piledriver! Aaron lifted Karina up and over, dumping her right on her head and neck. She landed so high that she rolled right to a standing position, out on her feet. Aaron got right back up and took his place behind her. He threw her left arm over his shoulder and lifted her into the air. As he did the building began to creak and whine unexplainably. Aaron looked at the ceiling and saw pieces begin to rip from their frame. Aaron’s face was filled with confusion. His attention was drawn to the ceiling but his wrestling instincts were probing his brain all the while. Aaron dropped his gaze from the ceiling and lifted the United States Champion into the air. Without any hesitation he dropped to the mat and nailed his QG-Theory piledriver. The roof continued to rip apart above their heads until the dark mass above them loomed large. Then suddenly, darkness. Monday – 3 A.M. Aaron startled awake and struggled to catch his breath. Sweat dripped from his brow as he turned to the alarm on the dresser across the room. He reached up and rubbed the sleep from his eyes in an attempt to focus. When he was finally able to read the blue digits he saw that it was still the early morning hours. He sighed loudly and reached for the bottle of water on the nightstand. As he took a drink of the room temperature liquid he thought about the dream that had plagued him over the course of the past few nights. Four nights in a row now Aaron had awoken from the same nightmare. He placed his bottle of water back to the nightstand and lowered himself back to a prone position. He settled back in and began to analyze his thoughts. Soon though the memory faded from his mind and Aaron drifted back to sleep. Monday 12 P.M. “What’s up, Aaron?” Nick asked. Aaron was hoping his office would be empty when he got there, but his fellow Teaching Assistant was there preparing a lecture for later in the day. Aaron dropped his backpack and plopped into his chair. He looked across the room as he reached down to bring his computer to life. “Not much, Nick. Another day.” Nick nodded and remained in a position that faced Aaron as he got his work station set up for the day. Aaron didn’t expect much in the way of students coming in that day and had planned on getting a seven-day forecast done for his personal website. He continued to retrieve supplies from his bag when he realized that Nick was still locked on and expecting more conversation. “How about you?” Aaron asked reluctantly. Nick pushed himself out of his chair and approached Aaron’s desk. He stooped over Aaron’s shoulder and began to inspect the items Aaron had gotten out. Included in that was the form he used to develop his forecasts. Aaron took pride in his mini-innovations and wasn’t ready to make them open-source just yet. He reached up and quickly folded the paper in half to hide the formulas from view. Nick straightened back up and placed an annoyed look on his face for a few seconds. “Just working on some stuff for my class. I’ve got to teach the 105 class about lapse rates; you know the cooling of air as it rises.” Nick said. Aaron laughed on the inside. Of course Aaron knew what a lapse rate was. He did have a degree in Meteorology after all. Aaron brushed the comment off and turned his attention to the computer. He opened up a browser and navigated to his e-mail. Nick continued to look at the monitor as Aaron typed in his log-in. When he came to the password field, Aaron paused a moment for Nick to turn away. When he didn’t Aaron cleared his throat loudly and got the point across. Nick turned and took his seat across the room. Once logged in, Aaron found the usual junk mail that cluttered his inbox. He scanned down a bit further and saw one titled “Weather Central Application.” Aaron’s heart skipped a beat and his stomach turned in knots as he nervously opened the e-mail and began reading it to himself. Dear Applicant, Weather Central would like to take this opportunity to thank you for your interest in employment with one of the fastest growing private forecasting firms in the Midwest. There is no doubt that your qualifications exceeded the expectations set forth for the position of Wind Energy Forecaster. Unfortunately, the staff at Weather Central is unable to offer you employment at this time. We encourage you to check our website often as new job openings are posted frequently. Good luck in your profession. Sincerely, Heather Brinkmann Human Resources Manager The message ripped through Aaron’s stomach like the blades of a wood chipper cutting through thick brush. Aaron groaned aloud as he deleted the e-mail and forcefully dropped his pen to the desk. He placed his face in his hands and took a deep breath before guessing he had drawn Nick’s attention once more. “What’s wrong Aaron?” He asked. Aaron decided not to respond and stood up. He exited the office and went to the restroom to splash some cold water on his face. He wasn’t sure how many more times he could have his dreams shattered. Monday 3:15 P.M. As expected, no students came to Aaron looking for help on the second analysis lab he had assigned. He cut out a minute or two before his office hours ended, trying to beat the afternoon pedestrian traffic as classes got out. He succeeded and approached his apartment after the fifteen minute stroll home. He stopped at the road and checked his mailbox. To his surprise there was little junk mail here and a few letters. He sifted through the junk mail quickly and slid what he didn’t want into the recycle bin nearby before heading up the stairs and into his home. Once inside, Aaron dropped his bag and jacket to the chair just inside the door. He took his mail and fell into a laying position on the couch. Two of the letters were from locations he had applied for Thursday night. WREX in Rockford, Illinois was the first he read. It wasn’t anything more than a computer generated rejection notice. It hurt, but he quickly tossed it aside before he opened the second. It was more of the same here as Weather Plus out of Wichita, Kansas had rejected his application for a forecasting position as well. The pain was eased a bit when he realized it was an actual response and not just a generic notice sent off in mass quantities. He tossed the mail aside and turned his attention to the third letter; it was addressed from, GCW. Aaron, I hope all is going well with you in the recent weeks. I’ll be speaking with you at WorldWide this week, but as a formality I decided to send you a letter as well. As I’m sure you are aware, your initial GCW contract will expire in August of this year. The staff of GCW, Steven Caldera included, have been impressed with your work as of late which has made our decision clear. A new contract will be offered in person at our next event. We can hash out the details there and hopefully both sides will come to an agreement that will keep your character on GCW television for a long time to come. All the best, James Bryan It slowly became evident to Aaron that his career path steadily took him away from meteorology and towards a lifetime on the road. Aaron loved wrestling but never saw it as the business he would have a chance to retire from. He did love the business. But he loved weather even more.
