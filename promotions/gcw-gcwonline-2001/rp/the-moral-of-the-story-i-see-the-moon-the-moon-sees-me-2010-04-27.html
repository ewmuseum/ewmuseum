The Moral of the Story I See the Moon & the Moon Sees Me   * * * * * * * * * * * * * * *   ...the moon sees somebody I'd like to see I'd been trying to call through to the house for well over an hour. First time, Jet answered the phone and as soon as he mentioned the word “Daddy” it was hung up just as quick. The next time I tried to get through it rang out. Same as the third and fourth times. By the fifth time I'd rang through I got a busy signal. Then I got no signal at all.   Jane was keeping me from my boys. I couldn't help but think of them every waking hour, wishing I could hold them in my arms or read them a bed-time story but my foolhardiness prevented me from being able to do that. I could feel the fickle finger of fate looming over me that there was darkness on the horizon. A pitch black night over shadowing me and I hoped for a big, giant moon to light and guide my way.   My way. A concept I'd once knew yet here I was, so far off the path in one aspect of my life and so far up the path in another. It was a topsy-turvy adventure and I wasn't used to it yet. Nor do I think I would ever be.   I sat here sometimes, closing my eyes and wishing myself away to my Buzzard's Bay for just five minutes to play with my boys. Cuddle my boys. See my boys.   Just to see them.   Please. Let me see them.   * * * * * * * * * * * * * * *   ...God bless the moon The roller coaster of my wrestling career zoomed around with complete disregard for my health and well-being and I ought to tell you now, if things weren't going like they were with my wrestling this shit probably would've killed me by now. I'd be that pro-wrestler that junked up and spazzed out on himself, dying in a puddle of what CSI labs would later describe as a combination of urine, liquid heroin and NyQuil. Yet here I was being interviewed in some cafe that poured coffee like they filtered it with a sock. I called prior to making sure they were cool with me wearing a mask. They'd told me they were fine with it as long as I signed some stupid portrait for them to hang on the wall. This joint ain't as Normal as me, I'd written on the glossy.   The kid waving the recording device under my nose was no more than nineteen years-old, but these were the contacts you need to make. These were the things you need to do to get over with your fans. Have time for everyone. Least, that was what Jody Monroe told me. In order to be a success you need to show everybody you've got time for them. Then she reminded me of that song Stan. I reminded her not to remind me of Eminem. Then I thought about punching her in the ear. But I didn't.   The kid probed the cliché, he probed the personal, he probed like the amateur he was and I let him venture where I guided him. I was his Himalayas and his Sherpa all at once when it came to the story of one John Johnson. When we were finished I bought the kid, I think his name was Andy or Arty or something, another coffee and he sipped it like he was from the New York Times, with all the grimace of somebody licking out an asshole. But he was appreciative nonetheless.   “Thanks, John. Really appreciate it,” he said.   “Don't mention it, Andy.”   “Xavier.” I could've sworn I almost heard his heart shattering inside his rib cage.   “Right. Sorry, Xavier.” I was nowhere near getting his name right. “It's my pleasure.”   We sat there awkwardly, his questions had all been gone through. He fiddled with the record and stop buttons on the player all the while gazing like he were staring into space and seeing the stars for the first time.   “I'm glad you're in GCW. I like seein' you on TV,” he finally said.   Nodding, I sipped my coffee one last time before pushing the last half-way in disgust. “I like being on TV.”   “You're gonna be a big star, John.” He nodded in testament to his words, his chin shuffled back and forth with the movement.   “Well, shit, Xavier. I hope so,” I said, smoothing a hand over the head of my mask.   Xavier pounded his chest, about where his heart would be and said the oddest thing I ever thought I'd hear. “God bless GCW.”   “God bless GCW? Really?”   Xavier nodded, beaming proudly like he owned the company. “Uh-huh. God bless 'em.”   “Um... sure. Why not?”   “You joining Rampage, John?” His eyes half-expectant of my answer, excited about perhaps getting a scoop.   “I don't think so. Tournaments aren't my thing. Lethal Lottery kinda gives me some bad memories.”   “Teaming with Harrison?”   I thought back to my night of infidelity with the broody Virginia, going at it like rabbits on Viagra in the pale moon light while my wife was cut from here to there birthing our third son.   “Something like that.” I tossed ten bucks on the table. “Later, kid.”   As I walked off I could sense Xavier standing there, waving with a grin spread from one-side of his chubby head to the other, proud as punch and eager to get home and upload his podcast.   Another satisfied customer.   * * * * * * * * * * * * * * *   ...& God bless me “You need to look after yourself better, John.”   Irene put his hands on his hips and shook his head, staring over clutter which had risen in my sink. None of which was actual dishes but a container for take-out cartons and order-ins since I've moved in. Well, not since I'd moved in, but since I'd filled the bin after I'd moved in. He picked up one carton, then another, glanced around the apartment and having seen nowhere to stuff them dropped them back on the mound he'd taken them from.   “This joint is a fucking mess. Seriously! And look at you...” His right arm rose like he were displaying me as the booby prize to the audience at a game show.   “Look at me? What about me?”   “You're billed at 234, John. Not 240. You're putting on weight.”   “Well, you're ugly.” I clutched at straws. He wasn't ugly, nor was he handsome. The man I called Irene was just regular looking, almost forgettable in features but he had a heart of gold. And that's something no plastic surgeon could fix for somebody. Irene had told me his name once, but it was just as forgettable as his face and he liked being called Irene. For some weird, possibly transgenderous, reason.   “Don't be lame,” Irene spat back, as he folded his arms defensively across his chest.   “I'm not. Your face is lame.” I was seriously clutching at straws. “And ugly.”   “John! What are you doing, man? You're a fucking star yet here you are. Eating yourself into oblivion. Are you trying to be Kimbusa's protégé or something? Coz that's where you're headed.”   “Yeah? Well, you're headed for getting your ass kicked, Irene. That's where YOU'RE headed.”   Ever have those moments where the truth, which apparently will set you free, hurts like ten tonnes of bricks falling from sky onto your pinkie toe? As reason evaded me and I tried to argue with a man who genuinely cared enough about me to still hang out with me, despite my mood and disposition toward him, which seemed to be a constant theme these days in our meetings.   “Don't be a jerk, John. I'm your friend, remember?”   “Friends don't call friends fat, Irene. That's something friends DON'T do.”   “Maybe. Maybe not. Friends ARE s'posed to be honest, John, and if I can be frank... you're starting to stack on some pounds. At least I'm friend enough to be honest.” His hands sat on his hips matter of factly, elbows pointing out at awkward angles condescendingly.   “Well, no. You can't be frank, Irene. You can't. Do you know what this is going to do to my head in its current state? Huh? DO YOU?”   Irene threw his hands in the air out of frustration. “Jesus Christ, John!”   “Don't bring him into this. Don't bring God into this, Irene.”   “Into what?”   “What's he done for me lately?”   “Who?”   “God, Irene. GOD! What's he gone and done for me lately? Y'know... just the other day, when that Indian pointed that gun at my face and threatened to splatter it all over the cereal display I wondered about some things, okay? I wondered.”   Irene's matter-of-fact hip hands dropped by his side as he listened to the crazy man ranting. I knew I was out of my mind but for some reason, I just needed to vent my frustrations, no matter how crazy and inane they sounded. They just needed to come out.   “I wondered if there really was a God, y'know? I mean... what kind'f God lets a guy get drunk and play around with another woman where there's three kids and a wife on the other side of the world? Huh? What kind'f God does that?”   “He gave us the freedom to choose, John. As man we're give-”   “FUCK FREEDOM TO CHOOSE, IRENE! FUCK IT! FUCK IT IN IT'S ASS OR WHATEVER ORIFICE IT DEFECATES WITH, IRENE! FUCK DECISION AND FUCK GOD!”   “You don't mean that, John. You don't mean that at all!”   “Don't I? What would you know about what I mean and what I don't mean, Irene? I crawled out of that 7-Eleven on hands and knees to some hobo laughing his stupid ass off. And you know what he says to me?”   Irene shook his head, to scared to make a peep in the midst of my rant.   “He says God bless. God bless. Some hobo soothsayer blessing me. Calling on his Almighty to watch over me. Do something. What the fuck is blessing? What does it even do? Why do people say it when somebody sneezes?”   “I dunn-”   “The point IS, Irene... the point is that your God has forsaken me, left me high and dry. My kids are 500 fucking miles away from me and people are bashing me with their Bibles... telling me to look for Jesus. Well, I been lookin', son, and I sure as shit haven't seen that motherfucker.”   Irene's bewildered gaze dropped to the toes of his feet, as they circled the linoleum floor awkwardly.   “Where's your fucking God when I need him, Irene? Huh? You tell me that.”   “You gotta have faith, John.”   “Yeah. Faith.” I pffft'ed. “Have faith. I don't want faith, Irene.”   I trudged across the room, kicking a take-out box on my way, staring out at the moon which seemed as big as a pizza-pie in the New York night air. Well, as best I could see it for the smog and smoke in its way. Closed my eyes and let my head fall, defeated.   “I don't want faith, Irene. I want my boys. I just want my boys.”   Heard his footsteps making their way across the lino, then felt his hand across my shoulders and together we both looked out over New York toward the moon. His hand patted my back in consolation and he didn't care for the rudeness of my outburst, nor the ignorance of my beliefs toward his. Irene was just a stand up guy who cared for me like a best-friend could only do. And it was then, standing in that window, staring up at the moon jaded by the human outputs of manufacture, that I realised that Irene WAS in fact my best friend. Standing by my side through all of this, the thick AND the thin. He plucked two cigarettes out of his pocket and handed one across to me after lighting it and we exhaled to the night like two little dragons.   “I know ya want them boys, John. You just gotta be patient. Strong.”   Sighing. Nodding. Exhaling a little plume of exhausted smoke, I knew Irene was right.   “Remember, I'm here for you, man.”   “Great,” I joked.   “You're no Fabio ya'self neither, John. Thank Christ you started wearing that mask.”   “Fuck you.”   “Fuck you, too, John.” He patted my back again. “Fuck you, too.”   * * * * * * * * * * * * * * *   ...& God bless the somebody I'd like to see I stared at the clock, longing it to spin around full face over and over again, so that my life might get along quicker and maybe, just maybe, I could have bypassed the pain which surged through my body every time I thought of my boys. My boys all the way up there in my Buzzard's Bay. 500 miles away.   My subpoena rest on the table, right next to the boxes of Moo Goo Gai Pan and Singapore Noodles it came with, like an uninvited dinner guest waiting for you to tell them to ”dig in”. Except instead of eating me out of house and home it was stealing my appetite. Eating me from the inside out. The more I recognised its presence in my “home”, for lack of a better term, the more unwelcome it became and the more my stomach twisted and turned with the loathe, rage and self-deprecation that bubbled within it.   Today I'd been served.   Earlier, a man knocked on my door, holding two boxes of Chinese take-out up to the peep-hole and I couldn't resist the temptation of the Asian smells wafting beneath my door. He asked if I were Johannus Bjorn Johanssen and I'd told him I was. He told me he had a delivery for me. That everything had been taken care of. I thought he were hinting at the bag of goodies. I grinned beneath my mask and held out my hand, racking my brain to find a name for someone who'd go out of their way to do me a favour like this. I figured Irene, since I'd been so down lately and he'd been bursting to cheer me up (and he knew I was still apprehensive about heading to the store since the Indian tried to perform rhinoplasty on my face with a half-pound of lead and a cannon that could open the broad side of a T-80U battle tank). Held out my hand to the guy and instead of Chinese take-out an envelope was stuffed in the palm of my hand instead. He told me I'd been served. Then he shoved the take-out into my hands and walked away with a bounce in his smug little life-wrecking step. The sweet scent of the Chinese cuisine was quickly overwhelmed by the bitter pungency of self-loathing.   Jane was filing for divorce. Instead of doing this the nice way she'd figured she would have me served. Maybe she thought I'd try to worm my way out of it. Or she wasn't strong enough to go through with asking for one. Lord knows, I would've given it to her.   Either way, here we were. Me and my subpoena. I sat staring at it. Seething silently with a cigarette dangling gingerly from the corner of my mouth, pretending the smoke which danced before my eyes was thanks to the scalding stare I shot the paperwork. Glancing out the window, I stared up at the moon like it were shining in mockery on my lonely studio apartment. Shut my eyes. Plucked the smoke from my mouth and asked the moon to watch over Jane. To shine just a little brighter of her. Shine a little brighter for just a little longer.   Then I imagined Jane looked out the window at the moon of Buzzard's Bay and hoped the same thing for me. I held no grudges on her. She was doing what she had to do to survive. I just hoped that somewhere, deep inside her, in the flowing rivers of hurt and pain, that she still cared about me. Even if it were just for the boy's sakes.   I hoped she cared.
