My Fading Voice Sings Of Love

25 July 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07/25/10 Her rich hazel eyes traced the jagged scar dissecting your right cheek, a cocktail of pity and slight unease mixed by her furrowing features. Her name was Grace Harrison, and she was paid to listen. Your peers lusted after gold, kudos and accolades. You? Right now, you’d just settle for someone to talk to, and a day devoid of pain or loss. "It’s… it’s… it’s like when you’re watching some cheap-as-shit TV, late at night, half-asleep, not-quite-a-half awake." As you spoke, vented, narrow beams of sunlight pricked through the heavy, stifling cloud. "It’s on, but you’re just staring at it, dead-eyed. And it’s showing the same thing for, like, 20 minutes, 40 minutes, you don’t even remember how long; then it snaps to something else. But, what you were staring into is still there, just the outline, a ghost in the picture. It’s like that, except, I… I don’t recognize the ghost. I’m not looking at something I’ve seen." You watched her thin, pursed lips, waiting for her to probe you, to interrogate or judge. You waited for her to engage, to analyze, but she simply flicked her gaze up into your eyes, reading the vulnerability as if it were a language she were fluent in. "My mind, my body, it’s not my own anymore," spilled out into the silent void Grace had formed, a welcoming vacuum for your words to fill. "But we’re not in sync. Like roommates who never meet, we just walk in on what the other has left behind and clear up their mess. Only the post-it notes it leaves on the refrigerator… not exactly humorous." With each word, each confession, your body relaxed. Stressed and tensed muscles yawned. "It wants me to lose everything," you jabbed, words hurried and hushed as if you were afraid of being overheard. "It wants me to alienate every friend, not that I had all that many to begin with. Every branch on my twisted, gnalwed family tree it wants to hack off and burn. I have no home, and I have just enough money to keep me out of a cardboard box. It knows what it’s doing; nasty fucker that it is." Words seemed to be creeping up to the tip of Grace Harrison’s tongue, but she held them back. This was the most you’d talked, to anyone, in months; a grateful relief that she seemed clued in on. They were an impurity that needed to be purged from your body, words that needed to be spoken, like puss being syringed from a wound. You were beyond more flowery metaphors. "I’m… I’m lonely." Your voice finally cracked under the weight of the admission. "I work for maybe 20 minutes a week… that leaves 10,060 minutes - don’t ask me how I know that - to fill, with nobody to talk to and nowhere to be. If I’m not concentrating, if I’m not focused on something, not talking to someone, then it’ll sneak in through the tiniest little crack in my mind and take over. I needed someone to talk to, about anything, so… so I came to you." Grace Harrison sank deeper into her arm chair, not distracted by your words settling on her. She nudged thick, black rimmed Dolce and Gabbana a quarter inch or so up her elfin nose. Still, she didn’t speak, only invited you to continue by way of a snatched look at the clock. "It was meant to be like detox," your doubting words seemed to question. "It was meant to be cleansing… or nourishing, I don’t even know anymore. I should have known by now that everything I touch, or even think about touching, I fuck up. Spectacularly. So many grand ideas, built so high that I never had to see the scrapheap of failures that sum up my life thus far." You began to feel a little more… human. The sun through the window, glass filtering the chill of the wind, licked at your scar, its healing tissue welcoming of the warmth. Each lungful of air tasted cleaner and crisper than the last. "I should be terrified, I should be calling, like, a fucking exorcist or something." A nervous, forced chuckle followed; fearing it was truer than you‘d want to admit, even in such a confessional mood. "But, it’s… I dunno. It’s… it’s not intoxicating, that’s not it. It’s not addictive, I don’t crave it, or itch for it. It’s not safe, either. It’s… easier, I guess. All my life, fuck up after fuck up has been my fault. Every failed relationship, every family fracture, every selfish choice, the blame was all on me until I was piledriven into the ground. Now, I finally have someone to share the burden… even if they do only exist in my head." You heard footsteps, the distant creak of a floorboard under the hum of the air-conditioning overhead. You words sank into a deeper, quieter tone, as you knew they’d be the last to spill off your tongue. "I know how crazy I must seem, or weak," you uttered wearily, punctuated with a labored sigh. "But I just can’t go on knowing that this pathetic existence I wallow in is the fault of nobody but me. I get to share it, now. I can tell myself that it’s the reason I sleep alone every night, or that I go days without hearing so much as a stranger saying excuse me… and I can tell myself, maybe even convince myself, that it has to creep into my mind through the cracks rather than me inviting it in, just so I can feel something, no matter how dark, cold, or devoid of compassion." Peeling yourself out from the engulfing couch, lighter - in more than one way - than when you’d sat, there were only two words left to say. "Thank you." Every word you’d spoken had chipped away at the cemented shoes anchoring you below the suffocating darkness until you could once again float to the surface, into the light. For how long, you didn’t know. "Ugh, sorry it took so long… new staff, borderline retarded," grunted Heather King - Grace’s roommate - as she passed over an Irish-cream latte. "Who was scarface?" "I have absolutely no idea," a troubled Grace answered, watching you depart with unease. "But they are seriously fucked up. Eh, just ignore them and they‘ll go away soon enough. Seemed more pathetic than mental." Grace Harrison, interning interpreter in the United Nations' social, humanitarian and cultural bureau, was paid to listen. Roleplay title is taken from "Grace" by Jeff Buckley.
