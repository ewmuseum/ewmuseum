THE HOME OF ANDY MURRAY, TALLAHASSEE, FLORDIA, TODAY, NOW. “Wait, no. One more thing,” Andy wearily exhaled as the recollection sent a bolt of lightning juddering through his psyche. He reached into the pocket of his jeans, pulling out a black leather wallet that looked surprisingly empty for a man of his wealth. After unfolding the wallet he reached in behind the picture window and gently slid a small, trimmed photograph out. He’d carried the picture of himself, his son Marvin and Jane with him even before their first divorce. At had been by his side, through good times and bad, for the best part of a decade now, and yet he felt no regret as he carefully tore his twice divorced wife out of his life. He tossed Jane’s visage into the pile before slipping the newly-frayed photograph back into his wallet. “Are you sure about this?” Andy heard his girlfriend ask as she approached him from behind. “There’s a lot memories in there I’m sure.” “Yes, a lot of memories,” retorted the Scotsman without so much as glancing at Kayla. “And not one of them good. Burn the fucking lot.” ---- THE OFFICES OF RUEBEN DALLAS, TALLAHASSEE, FLORDIA, YESTERDAY, 10:00am. A decade-long career had brought about many brushes with the clerical side of the business, and as his star continued to rise at an astonishing rate the frequency at which Andy Murray found himself holed up in a corporate office block seemed to increase day-by-day. He always found it rather peculiar that a business that trades in dramatised violence could essentially be run by a bunch of paper-pushing white collar wage slaves in a nice, clean, air-conditioned office block. These men knew nothing of the toil of the professional wrestler; they were completely indifferent towards the physical toll that a life trading blows and travelling across the country took on a man’s wellbeing. No, their world was one of coffee breaks, Microsoft Windows, and memos. So it goes. Andy was not dreading the appointment with Rueben Dallas one bit, despite his general disdain towards the environment in which his agent operated. The two had been friends since the turn of the decade, and the African-American businessman had helped transform The Scottish King of Cool from another faceless, nameless grappler doing the rounds on the US toilet independent scene to a genuine global superstar. “So how the hell is my top earner doing today?” Dallas flashed a perfect smile as Andy gently closed the office door behind him. Andy could still hear the unrelenting pitter-patter of keyboard strokes, despite the two-inch thick wall separating Rueben’s private office from the foyer of endless cubicles. “Eh, you know, taking it easy, playing it cool, all that jazz,” a half-honest Andy Murray retorted as he grabbed his agent’s hand, firmly shaking it over the desk. As the son of a professional wrestler himself, Rueben Dallas knew the ins and outs of the industry like the back of his hand. He’d even went so far as to try out the sport for himself back in 2002, but it wasn’t his cup of tea; he was always far more effective when it came to the business side of things. Rueben was tall and sturdy for a man who pretty much sat behind a desk all day. He obviously still hit the gym every other day, as his muscular build was evident even underneath his inch-perfect black tuxedo. His dome-like head was hand-shaved to the point where not even the faintest hint of a hair was visible which allowed the light trickled in from the window behind him to reflect like a newly-shined bowling ball. It didn’t take long for the light-hearted banter between the two to fade away as the inevitable task of “talking business” came to the forefront. Rueben apologised for being so abrupt, telling Andy that he had a “tight schedule” to adhere to today. Andy believed him; he had no reason not to. ?” so basically, I’ve been chatting on the phone over the past couple of days to Caldera’s people. I think an offer is inevitable.” The clean-cut agent explained to his client. He was referring to Andy’s GCW contract which was up for renewal. When he signed back in December 2007, the Scotsman only inked a 12 month deal with the promotion who understandably, at the time, didn’t want to risk handing a man with such a dreadful injury record a longer deal. They agreed that after six months a full-performance evaluation would be conducted, and The Scottish King of Cool’s contractual situation would be revised as necessary. “Oh?” Andy questioned as he raised an eyebrow. He feigned his surprise in attempt to sound humble, but he knew deep down that Global Championship Wrestling were going to keep him on. With the start he’d made, how could they not? “Absolutely. We’re looking at an extra twelve months on top of your existing deal, bare minimum,” Dallas said, looking up momentarily from the stack of paper that sat upon his oak-topped desk. “Congratulations, chief. Looks like the Scottish bastard is alive and kicking ass in 2008 after all.” Andy’s smile mirrored that of Rueben. “As if there was ever any doubt,” he cockily followed up. “What’s the turnaround looking like?” “As far as I know, Caldera should have the thing ready for you to sign come WorldWide time. That Rampage Tournament victory hasn’t gone unnoticed either; you can probably expect a lil” something extra financially too,” winked Dallas. “Oh, tight,” was all that Andy could really muster as a response. Dallas flashed his client a curious glance, somewhat surprised by the lack of emotion present on his face. “C’mon, dude. You’re about to become one of the most well-paid competitors in GCW! Let’s see a little colour in those cheeks, eh?” A forced smile drew itself across Scottish lips. “Oh, don’t get me wrong, Rueb. I’m delighted, really; thrilled. To be doing so well after as a torrid couple of years is completely mind-blowing, and I’m absolutely amazed at what’s happened since the turn of the year. I’ve just got some bullshit on my mind is all.” Rueben knew exactly what he was referring to. “Bagwell?” Andy nodded. “Awww heck, don’t let that reprobate drag you down, man! You’ve taken down men bigger and better than Chris Bagwell before! What makes this shmuck so special” I mean, remember Trevor Kent? Now that was some serious shit,” said Dallas, referring to a mammoth competitor that Andy had many bloody battles with during his formative years as a professional wrestler. “True, but even Trevor didn’t have it in him to go as low as to tie Jane up in all this fucking crap,” sighed Andy, inadvertently raising his voice with the obscenities. “Let’s leave that for the time-being; I’ll deal with that odious sod in good time. I’ve something else I want to discuss right now.” “Oh yeah?” Dallas quizzed as he leaned forward, resting both arms on his desk. “What’ve you got in mind?” “Well, I’ve been thinking,” Andy began; Rueben got the impression that this was going to be rather long-winded. “I’ve been doing this for, what, must be over ten years now. I’ve earned enough money that’ll allow me to never work again, and I know I’ll be able to provide for my family “til death comes a’knockin” at my door. I’ve performed all over the world, won championship belts in just about every place I’ve been. I’ve experienced stunning highs and terrible lows, you know? I’m at the point where, if it all ended tomorrow, I could look back upon my wrestling career and say “yeah, I had a good innings”.” “Whoa whoa, hold on now,” Mr. Dallas cut Andy off. “You sound suspiciously like a man pondering retirement here?” Andy couldn’t help but laugh at the retort of his agent. “Nah, nothing like that” don’t be silly, dude! You know this is in my blood; even if I wanted to ? and God knows there’s been points over the past few years where I HAVE ? I don’t think I could quit this game. No, I’ve just been thinking of ways I could put a little bit back into a sport that’s given me so much, and well, there’s this old gym downtown, and I’m thinking of putting a cheeky bid on?” Dallas leaned forward further still. “What are you thinking here” Training kids?” “Exactly.” “Hmm, well?” the African-American pondered the proposal only for a couple of seconds. “It’d take a lot of time and effort, you’d probably need to bring in a few helping hands here and there. But you know what” I’d go for it. How much is the gym going for?” ?$80,000. Its pocket money, really, but the place does need a little bit of work. I figure I could work on it with Cayle and Sid when I’m not on the road with GCW. Plus it’s a nice contingency plan, just in case the ol? back decides to go again,” Andy said, referring to this notoriously troublesome back region. Rueben Dallas reclined in his chair, smiling. After seeing Andy’s career stall itself by the man’s complete and utter obsession with competition, he was genuinely glad to see the Scotsman taking an interest in some out-of-the-ring activities. “Andy, man. Do it. Buy the gym, and do whatever you want with it. Stu Hart will be crapping himself.” ---- THE HOME OF ANDY MURRAY, TALLAHASSEE, FLORDIA, TODAY, TEN MINUTES AGO. He’d spent almost the whole day gathering this shit together from various places around the house. Even Andy didn’t know why he had taken some of it with him when he’d moved houses just a few short months ago; it would surely have been much easier to just dump it there and then. Maybe he just got caught up with the hassle of moving house, or maybe Andy Murray was just a bit more sentimental than he thought. This didn’t matter to him now, however, as he carried the final of three cardboard boxes out to the sizeable lawn that sat behind his old-style Floridian home. The night air was thick, muggy and uncomfortable; despite having lived in America for a giant portion of his life, nights like these were something Andy had never really become acclimatised to. Too many bitterly cold evenings in Aberdeen, he told himself. He put the box down a-top the other two and took a minute to pause, taking in the sounds of crickets chirping in the distance. He soon set about grabbing the very same box again, opening the flaps at the top and tipping the contents downwards into a large, rusty steel barrel he had found in the garden shed just a couple of ours ago. After repeating the process with the other boxes, Murray placed the palms of his hands down on top of the barrel’s contents, pushing down and compacting them with all his weight. The Scottish King of Cool could feel sweat beginning to form under his armpits despite the lack of any real physical exertion. Andy didn’t want to spend more time outside in the disgustingly humid Tallahassee evening air than he had to, so after completing his business outside he quickly walked back towards the French doors that lead inside his well-maintained kitchen/dining room. “Ohhh, look who’s back!” Andy’s elder brother, Sid, 33, proclaimed between sips of Budweiser. “Care to let us know just what the hell you’ve been doing out there now?” Andy glanced at his elder brother and offered him the slightest of nods. Sidney Murray was the definition of the word “rugged”; a stout devotee of the independent wrestling scene, he’d been a grappler for as long as he remember, and his weathered facial features were perfect evidence of this. His shaggy, shoulder-length brown hair was tied-up by a blue bandanna, and his unkempt beard looked particularly scruffy tonight. Glancing around the room before offering any kind of verbal response, Andy was glad to see so many friendly faces. Kayla Jarosz, his girlfriend of almost a year now, was present of course, clad in one of Andy’s old AWC sweatshirts that was far too big for her. Even though she’d lived in the state all her life, Andy was still baffled by the fact that she could find an evening like this cold. Cayle, the youngest of the three Murray brothers, sat at the dining table, slowly demolishing a glass of chilled Rose wine. His long, brown hair was held back with a white alice band, and his arm was wrapped around twenty-four year old Nikita Daugherty, the latest person to waltz into the life of the Murray clan. She’d only been seeing Cayle for a couple of weeks but already it seemed as though to two were attached at the hip. Surprisingly for a Pakistani immigrant, she spoke with almost no accent whatsoever; if Andy didn’t know any better, he’d assume she was of English upbringing. “Come on out, guys. I’ve got something to show you,” Andy finally uttered, motioning towards the barrel. ---- COOCH’S BAYOU ROUGE MARKET & EATERY, TALLAHASSEE, FLORDIA, YESTERDAY, 13:30am. “How’s the feast, hon?”” Kayla asked as Andy did his best to wolf down a giant-sized portion of Jambalaya in record time. His teeth slowly obliterated a mouthful of chicken, rice, tomato, sausage, celery and spices before he’d swallowed enough to utter a coherent response. “Feast-tastic, thank you.” Andy responded as he filled-up another forkload; Kayla could tell from the way he enthusiastically tossed it down his throat that it was the first thing he’d eaten all day. She smiled and turned her attention back towards a sandwich that looked incredible feeble next to Andy’s plate of food; but then again, she didn’t have the appetite of the 6’7? 275lb professional wrestler. Cooch’s Bayou Rouge Market and Eatery was one of their favourite restaurants in town for sublime Cajun cooking, and it seemed as if the couple came here every chance they could. It was a rare luxury for Andy to have a complete day off from his full-time wrestling schedule, and he always made sure that he spent a little time with those close to him to compensate for his long absences on the road. If this was an attempt at having a nice quiet lunch then it was going badly. Already three fans had approached Andy looking for autographs; he was exceptionally popular in his adopted hometown and attracted such attention everywhere he went. He always made the attempt to dress down and blend in with the public as much as possible, but when you’re Andy Murray it’s hard not to look like a million dollars. “This is great, Andy. It really is,” she began, observing her partner wash his food down with a refreshing mouthful of ice-cold lemonade. “Right on. Sure beats Ponderosa any day of the week.” “No, I’m not talking about the restaurant. Okay, well, maybe I am, it’s good too, but I’m talking about something more than lunch on a Sunday afternoon,” she began, immediately dragging the attention of the giant Scotsman towards her. “I mean us, Andy. To see how far we’ve come, especially after all that rubbish you went through last year” well, it’s great,” Kayla said, repeating herself through lack of being able to think of anything else. As he gazed into her deep, hazel eyes, he knew she was right. Like Jane she was physically a tremendous argument for procreation with just enough meat on her bones to make most men weak at the knees and a smile so devilish it could probably turn Jesus Christ himself to the darkside, but mentally she couldn’t have been further removed from Andy’s ex-wife. After all the shit that Jane had put him through, he was glad to find somebody so warm and understanding to share his baggage with. Not once had she complained about his absences; while she didn’t necessarily like them, she just put up with them. She knew exactly what she was getting into dating a professional wrestler and took it in her stride. Even though they’d only been together for just under a year, Andy knew that she wouldn’t toss him to the wolves like Jane did. “I know, K, I know.” Andy Murray didn’t need to say any more; the shit-eating grin on his face and the twinkle in his eye did a good enough job of that. “So I’m thinking of putting a bid on this gym just round the corner.” “Oh yeah?” she quizzed, showing no dissatisfaction whatsoever at the topic of conversation. “What for” Isn’t all that stuff in the basement enough?” “Been thinking a lot about opening some kind of training school for young wrestlers. Maybe get Cayle and Sidney to help out. We’d get a couple of rings, maybe book a couple of shows here and there, you know, all kinds of ill-shit.” Kayla giggled under her breath; she still found herself amused by Andy’s idiosyncratic way of speaking, even after almost twelve months. “What, you don’t think it’s a good idea?” “Oh no, I think it’s fantastic. Just eat your food, dear?” ---- THE HOME OF ANDY MURRAY, TALLAHASSEE, FLORDIA, TODAY, NOW. Cayle was the first of the group to express his confusion towards the barrel. He bounded up to it like a rabbit, releasing his grip on his girlfriend’s hand as he began to rummage around. “What is this stuff?” Sid dipped a hand in, and without too much fiddling, pulled out a small, framed photograph. He cleared away from of the dust on the glass with his thumb, and squinted in the poor light, barely able to make out the seven year old picture of Jane. “Dude, what are you doing with this stuff” I’ll take this!” Cayle grinned as he examined an immaculate silver Rolex, the hands of which were still ticking. “It’s got to go,” Andy announced, deadpan, as Nikita pulled out a letter that Jane had written to Andy whilst he was out on the road. “All of it.” A mass aura of confusion seemed to descend upon the group. “I get it,” Sid asked as he dropped the picture back into the barrel. “All this stuff, it’s all you and Jane, isn’t it?” “Gifts, photographs, trinkets. You name it. It’s time to flush that parasite out of my life for good.” From the recesses of his pocket the Scottish King of Cool pulled a book of matches, tossing it to his elder brother. “You want the honours?” Instinctively the group stepped back, not wanting to get caught up in any potential flames. Sid gently slide open the matchbook, “you sure about this?” he asked Andy, who nodded in response. “Wait, no. One more thing,” Andy wearily exhaled as a sudden recollection sent a bolt of lightning juddering through his psyche. He reached into the pocket of his jeans, pulling out a black leather wallet that looked surprisingly empty for a man of his wealth. He’d carried the picture of himself, his son Marvin and Jane with him even before their first divorce. Burn the fucking lot.” Andy nodded towards Sid, who, on cue, stepped up toward the barrel. He struck up one of the matches against the rough edge. As the flame spurted to life he moved the lit match towards the others, igniting them, before tossing the pack into the barrel so as not to burn his fingers. The paraphernalia went up in smoke a lot quicker than any of them had expected, and soon enough a surprisingly large flame behind to lick around the edges of the barrel. Sparks floated up into the sticky Floridian evening, dancing with shadows futilely before burning out and meeting their fate only seconds later. As all remnants of a former life were incinerated before his very eyes, Andy pulled Kayla in close beside him, wrapping one of his giant arms around her waist.
