Those who cull the past are doomed to repeat it Pt. 1

20 May 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 05/20/10 The room is serene. Bland. Sterile.Vanilla. Sitting in the middle, Shaman has his arms draped across the table. An interrogator, a man of his late forties with thinning salt-and-pepper hair and a bulging gut, looks over the table at Shaman. Anger fills his face, as he loudly speaks at his captor. "I am Agent Johnson, Mr. Whitecloud. And, quite frankly, if you wouldn’t mind, I would like to know what in the flying hell were you thinking in England? The English have decided to not press charges. But here in the good ol’ United States, we think you need to at least be questioned." Turning his head, Shaman looks at the agent and rolls his eyes. The agent slams both his palms down upon the table in frustration. "Listen to me, you big jackass! You practically caused an international incident. However, it seems someone in England likes you. We’re still looking at prosecuting you as a terrorist." Looking up, Shaman smiles, and makes a fist. "Just another way the FBI decides to not let things go, isn't it?" Agent Johnson leans forward over the table into Shaman's face. "You know, you are really beginning to piss me off." Agent Johnson reaches down into his bag next to him, and pulls out some folders. He scatters them onto the table, and out slips pictures from Belmont, England. Shrugging, Shaman leans back in the chair, placing an arm over the backrest and stretching a leg straight out. He looks at Agent Johnson. "So, lemme see here. Once again, the United States is planning on blaming a Native American for all of your failures?" Shaman laughs. "You are a bunch of fucking hypocrites. Can’t let something go, no harm no foul?" Agent Johnson looks out of the window, and then back at Shaman. "Your lawyer is here. I think she will like to know what we have. In fact, I think I’ll be preparing some charges. Maybe teach you a lesson about what it means to be an American citizen." Agent Johnson grabs a folder off the table and slams it down. "Now, shall I call you Staff Sergeant Whitecloud? Says here, you served in the United States Air Force from ‘93 until ‘97. Four years of honored service, Para-rescue, the very epitome of an American soldier. So why turn on your own country?" Taking his hand over his face, he rubs his forehead slowly. He lets out a deep sigh. "I’m a traitor now? I was in England. I targeted a house. I burnt it to the ground. I roughed up a few fucking locals. Yet here I am, sitting in your office. Way I see it, the Queen has her restitution from me already. Paid in full." He peers nonchalantly at the agent. Agent Johnson quickly pulls a chair up, flipping through some more folders. Shaking his head, he leans backwards, dropping his head a bit staring up at the ceiling. He stares off into space. BAM!! The Sheriff slams a book down upon the table of the interrogation room, as Andrew sits before him in handcuffs. Andrew looks around, not really caring where he is at, nor what he is here for. In fact, he doesn't really care about much. The Sheriff places a piece of paper down that reads “Grand Theft Auto” across the top, with “jail time possible” written in ink below it. "Have I got your attention now, boy?" Andrew looks up at the Sheriff. "You stole old man Thomas's truck, and joy-rode the damn thing across four counties. You left it in a goddamned ditch!" He throws the folder on the table, nearly hitting Andrew, and fixes his hat. He walks back and forth looking at the young sixteen year old kid sitting before him. "You know, if it weren't for your daddy.” The Sheriff chuckles. “I would have had your sorry ass tossed in juvie." He leans forward and reaches for a book. "I think I know just the right person to deal with you." Turning, the Sheriff walks to a desk and sits down. Andrew pours over the many pictures. Noticing one of a young man boxing, he then turns his attention to a picture of his dad with the Sheriff. Andrew looks at the Sheriff curiously. "Where'd you get that?" He points at the picture of his dad. The Sheriff notices the picture "That was about a year before Vietnam. Your dad and I were the best of friends. I was always around for him, and he had my back." Reaching into a drawer the Sheriff grabs another picture from Vietnam, this one with the numbers 69 written in black marker in the corner, of Andrew’s dad with short hair, and the Sheriff with a pair of sunglasses on. Andrew displays a sense of pride for once, holding the picture in his handcuffed hands. Andrew looks over at the Sheriff. "So how come you never got the guy who killed dad?" The Sheriff snaps his head in Andrew’s direction. He sneers as he speaks. "You stupid punk. I did everything I could, but the guy skipped county, and then state. Your dad died, and I couldn't do a damn thing about it." Andrew places the picture down on the desk. He looks at the Sheriff. "Yeah. Mom died feeling guilty, as well. She died of cancer, and yet she still blamed herself every damn day for dad's death. You know why?" Andrew slams his hands down upon the table and stands. He’s a good 6'6" at 16 years old, easily dwarfing the Sheriff’s diminutive size. "Because people pitied her, and reminded her that it was she that asked dad to go to the store and get a gallon of damn milk." The Sheriff stands, pointing right at Andrew, no longer intimidated by his size. "Sit down and watch your mouth, son." "You’re not my dad, and I am not your son." Andrew proclaims defiantly. The Sheriff looks over at him, shaking his head. "You got that right, because I would have shipped your ass off to military school by now. Teach you some respect." The Sheriff leans back, looking at Andrew. "Be straight with me here, kid. Why did you steal Thomas's truck?" Andrew smiles, thinking of Victoria Carrington. He leans forward "There was this girl at school. Said she would go out on a date with me if I could get a pickup truck." The Sheriff lets out a loud laugh as he shakes his head. "All of this trouble over a girl?" Andrew nods his head shamefully. The Sheriff looks at Andrew. "Well, son, did she even give you a kiss?" Hanging his head, Andrew shakes his head. “No.” "She just made a fool of me, is all she did." Andrew sits there, looking at the table top, rotating the picture with his fingertips. "Her Dad was a Sheriff in the next County. Got me arrested for chasing his daughter. It’s why I’m here." The Sheriff looks at Andrew, and walks over to him, unclasping the handcuffs. "Okay, Andrew. Let’s talk about what happened here, and forthcoming consequences." The Sheriff opens a file up. He looks straight at Andrew, his eyes narrowing. "You have a file of trouble the past 3 years. Growin’ since your mother died. This will be a tough one. That is, if we’re to get you out of trouble this time." With a look of worry on his face, Andrew finally realizes he might not ride off scot-free. Shaman suddenly snapped out of his childhood daydream. Agent Johnson looks over at him and frowns. “Mr. Whitecloud, in all seriousness, we are only strong-arming you here because we want to make something absolutely clear." Agent Johnson stands up and waves his hand towards the mirrored glass. The door opens, and in walks a woman around Shaman's age. She sports a woman's business suit, her hair pulled back. He head refuses to believe his eyes. Agent Johnson looks over at the other Agent, not sure what’s going on. "Mr. Whitecloud, have you met Agent Carrington before?" Shaman nods, not usually used to being in such shock. "Yeah. She got me arrested when we were kids. She said she would go out with me, though." Agent Carrington looks into Shaman’s eyes, her crystal blue and all business. "I see not much has changed." She hands Agent Johnson a folder. "This was the man who got my father removed as Sheriff when I was sixteen. He stole some farmer’s truck to try and impress me." Shaman mutters underneath his breath. "Bitch." Agent Johnson snaps his head around. "That is a damn federal agent, Mr. Whitecloud! She deserves your undivided respect, regardless of your unseemly past." Looking at Agent Johnson, Shaman cracks his head from side to side. "And here I was thinking your mother was a bigger bitch who couldn't pay her way through life in a bordello." Agent Johnson jumps up. Shaman remains still unmoving, as defiant as ever at this authoritative man. Shaman clears his throat and grins. "Get your ass off my bunks, sleeping beauty!" The Drill Sergeant beats the garbage can as he watches everyone get up quickly. "This is the first day of the rest of your miserable sad-sack lives, maggots. I am going to mold you into men, or kill you while I try!" Andrew looks at the Drill Sergeant, as he quickly gets out of his bunk. The Drill Sergeant’s methods have remained the same for three days, Andrew thought to himself. He knew he was working harder than he ever had before. He knew the routine as he began to start his day. Two weeks to graduation, this was his ultimate goal. He was going to be Special Forces Para-Rescue. Finally going to achieve something his dad would be proud of. After lacing his boots, Andrew hurries through the barracks and down onto the drill pad. He stands at attention, all throughout the roll call. Everyday the same routine, but they say to be sure nobody goes AWOL or lags behind. He stands, waiting until the others have finished. His name is called, but he does not move. He just responds. "Recruit Whitecloud, present and accounted for, Drill Sergeant." The Drill Sergeant steps forward, a once over on Andrew, making close inspections of the short haircut and the facial hair. Andrew’s one of the very few that did not have to shave his facial hair, a call back to his Native American heritage. The Drill Sergeant addresses the squadron. "Gentlemen, make the birds look bad!" All at once, a deafening roar of eighty plus recruits sing out. "Off we go into the wild blue yonder, aiming high into the sky!" The voices carry the Air Force’s theme song loudly and proudly throughout the drill pad, out onto the courtyard. The Drill Sergeant strolls back and forth, eyeing the recruits. "That sent shivers down my damn spine. You men could be the Vienna Choir for all I care." Turning, he blew a whistle. "Chow time!" He turns and looks at the squadron. He yells "Hu-right face!!" The whole squadron turns to the right and marches in place. "Fuhwaard maaarch uyuh leeeft uyuh leeft right leeft." They march literally two blocks and then turn, marching another block and a half. The Drill Sergeant begins the marching cadence. The pace of the march picks up as the cadence began. "I saw a birdie flying in the sky" The march, straight and stiff, shoots out the hoofs of the boots stepping, as all seem to stomp in unison as if it were a rolling machine. "I got some white stuff in my eye" Each step, more defined than the last. Every time the left came down, they hit harder as they moved. "Is that water? Is that spit?" The Drill Sergeant heads the column, as he occasionally barks part of the cadence louder as the recruits respond. "OH NO ITS BIRDIE SHIT!" "Oo birdie, birdie in the sky." The recruits fall into line at the mess hall, their march slowed with the ending of the cadence. "Why did you do that in my eye!?!?" Andrew files into the mess hall, as everyone heads for the food line. He grabs a tray as the Drill Sergeant barks out. "Take what you can eat, but eat what you take." The Drill Sergeant marches around the hall, watching each man eat. "You better not waste my food, recruits, or I am going to make sure you see it again real soon!" "Mr. Whitecloud, would you like some coffee?" Agent Johnson responds, looking over at Shaman. Shaman looks up as another woman walks into the room. This time, he recognized the woman quickly. She turns and looks at Shaman. Her gaze turns to amusement, as she speaks to Agent Johnson. "You seem to be holding my client on nothing." She presents a piece of paper from Scotland Yard and she straightens herself as she speaks. "On behalf of Scotland Yard, all complaints have been removed and expunged from Mr. Whitecloud’s involvement. Reparations have been made to those involved thusly dropping any and all said charges. Inspector Hannaford Robinson." She folds the piece of paper and holds it out to the agent. Shaman stands up and holds his hands out with the handcuffs still on them. A chain leads from them to the floor. "I guess we won't be needing these anymore, will we?" Agent Johnson groans as he walks over and unlocks the handcuffs. Shaman turns and walks to the door. He observes the room. "Lovely place you have here, just not my cup of tea, guvna!" Chuckling Shaman walks out with his lawyer. As they come to the front door, Shaman looks over at her. "I knew you were there watching the whole time. You could have ended that session long before it even got that far." She looks at him and simply shook her head. "Look, Mr. Whitecloud. You needed to sweat. Instead I saw you day dreaming and thinking about stuff." Pushing up her glasses on her nose, she looks at him. "What were you daydreaming and thinking about?" Shaman looked at her and motioned for her to walk with him as they stepped out onto the sidewalk. "Many things actually, Times that were tough for me as I grew into the man I am today. Things that defined who I am and what I am going to be in the future. People like Tim Shipley my opponent at World Wide will never understand." Walking Shaman looks around the streets. Everyone hustling by and not stopping for anyone or anything. Like ants drawn to a crumb that is business. "I am the underdog once again. I had always fought for every little inch in my life. At times I had defining moments." Pressing his finger against his forearm. He simply watches the pigment turn white and then back to normal as he pulled his finger back. "My time in the military helped me establish my dreams of what I wanted to do. I wrestled while I was in the military. It was a part of my life I wanted to keep hidden from my friends and opponents. Just something I wanted to keep to myself. Keep it as my own and nobody else’s. I didn't want people to defile something that was mine and mine alone." He actually shows a bit of emotion as he seems to be struggling with what he had just said. "Now it isn't mine any longer. Something I shared with my Father. The pride of doing something that was bigger than ourselves." Shaman notices a sidewalk cafe and motions for the two of them to sit down. He waves over at a waiter and lifts a menu. "Let us get breakfast while I explain a few things to you." Shaman looks at the lawyer with a serious face. He takes a few sugar packets into his hand and shakes them. He looks over at the lawyer who seems to be waiting patiently. "You are to make sure I don't go through those damn situations again. You did well with the work in England, but you failed here miserably." The lawyer adjusts her glasses as she looks at him. "You need to understand that I pay you to accomplish certain tasks. My task is to beat my opponents in the ring. Hated or not, I have to deal with every jack ass in the world thinking it will be a cake walk to beat me." Shaman holds up a fork and looks at it. "I have to always reflect back and see what I am forgetting or leaving out." Shaman looks around the little cafe and grins. "Nobody ever has a clue, do they? No clue as to the constant danger they are always in. Yet here I am, staring every possibility in the face." He plays with a napkin, folding it a few times. "My fears, my dreams, my failures, are just that." Pausing, he takes a short breath. "They are mine nobody else’s. I use them to build myself and become stronger. Do I have doubts?" Shaman looks at the folded napkin and then looks up at the lawyer. "Unfortunately, I have many of them. They are like layers. Each time I pull one back I am reminded of one I had before. I don't seem to learn at times to look back at them." "You see, back in that room, I was reminded of two times back when I was younger." He grins looking at the secretary. "One time I was arrested when I was a kid. Sat in a room similar to that interrogation room. I had a friend of my dad lecturing me about women and being a criminal at the same time." Chuckling he takes the cup of coffee from the waiter as he nods. He drinks the coffee as he looks at the lawyer across the table from him. "His demeanor, with his yelling and screaming, reminded me of my drill sergeant in boot camp." He then places the coffee cup down, and looks up at the sky. "It was kind of unnerving to see that woman, though. The same damned woman who had caused me to change my path. It was strange, realizing she was the end to all of my troubled childhood." He grabs a sugar pack and opens it pouring the sugar into the coffee. "Could be like little grains of time pouring into the ocean of life." The lawyer just looks at Shaman confused. "What is that supposed to mean?" She looks at him confused looking over the top rim of her glasses. Shaman looks up and grins. "It is what it is i guess." Shaman downs the coffee and stands up. "Well you have yourself a good day. I have some information to gather so i can make sure things go my way for once." Shaman walks away humming the Indiana Jones theme leaving the Lawyer to her own thoughts. The Lawyer watched as Shaman left her at the Cafe. She looked down as the waiter left the tab for her. "Son of a bitch he left me with the tab again!" The Lawyer looks around shaking her head. Standing behind some bushes around the corner Shaman waits quietly and watches the Lawyer depart. He opens his cell phone and dials a number. Waiting for someone to answer, he simply watches the lawyer stroll away down the street. "Hey Robert bring the truck to Leon's address in New York City." Shaman nods his head and then speaks. "No dip shit i am not in Newark, i got arrested and had to spend time at a Government building answering stupid questions and daydreaming." He looks at the phone. "See you in a half hour bye." Hanging up Shaman walks away. Unbeknown to Shaman a man well know to him is watching him depart. "Well my Mortifera partner it seems you have to answer to me very soon." Jorge Samuelsson grins and walks over to where the Lawyer is standing. He looks at her and smiles. "Well my dear Valkyrie did Andrew realize that it was you that he had on retainer as a Lawyer?" She smiles and kisses Samuelsson on the cheek. "No dearest, but he is finally coming around. Perhaps you will wake him up after all he attacked you." She smiles as they walk away.
