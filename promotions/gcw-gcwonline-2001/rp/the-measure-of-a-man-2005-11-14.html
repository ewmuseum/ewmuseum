The measure of a man

14 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/14/05 'No, no, Vickie, the girls need you now. They need you to be strong. I just wanted to offer my condolences. I wish there was more I could say or do...' I trailed off, because there was nothing more I could say or do. I couldn't bring the man back, and words failed me. 'I understand,' I said, 'Send my love to everyone and let me know if there's anything I can do to help. Bye.' I snapped the phone shut and wiped the tears away. It just wasn't fair. Why did Eddie have to die? The knock at my door pulled me out of my thoughts and I answered it to find Jeff standing there. I could tell the mountain of a man had also been crying. It was no surprise as he had known Eddie just as I had. 'It's not right,' I said letting him in, 'It's not fair. I mean I still can't believe it, Jeff. I just got off the phone with Vickie, and she's trying to be strong for the girls, but it's hard on all of them.' 'I know,' he said, 'The worst part is it's never going to get easier. You can never get over something like that. You can go on with your life, but you can't get over it.' I nodded, 'I wrestled the man, I spent time with him.' I smiled a bit, 'I remember one night Eddie, Konnan, Art Barr, and myself went out drinking one night, the night before Eddie and Art were scheduled to drop the AAA tag-team titles to me and Carlos. We were laughing and drinking and just having ourselves a good ol time. I remember how Eddie had agreed to miss his frogsplash on me so Konnan and I could win the match.' I wiped my tears away again, just talking about it made me want to cry. 'You know, Billy Joel was right, only the good die young.' The worst part about it was, without even really thinking about it I could name a whole host of wrestlers who had gone on to that great Battle Royal in the sky: Eddie's father Gory, The Von Erichs (all except Kevin), Andre the Giant, Art Barr, Boris Malenko, Eddie Gilbert, Big John Studd, Curt Hennig, Davey Boy Smith, Owen Hart, Brian Pillman, The Junkyard Dog, Rick Rude, Gorilla Monsoon, Road Warrior Hawk. And now Eddie. It just wasn't fair. After I had left AAA I hadn't seen much of Eddie, at least not in person. I caught him on ECW TV, and later on WCW programming. I remember when he, Chris Benoit, Dean Malenko and Perry Saturn had defected to the WWF as the Radicalz, and how in his first match in, he had dislocated his elbow hitting a frogsplash on the Road Dogg. I remember a WCW pay-per-view where he had wrestled in a four corners match for the Cruiserweight title, and then immedately after had to wrestle another match. I remembered so many good things about Eddie, I thought my head would exlpode from all the good memories crowding into my head at once. There weren't enough good things I could say about Eddie, he was a great wrestler, a good friend, and a hell of an athlete, if anyone deserved to die less, it was Eddie. The man had admitted he had done wrong with the drugs and drinking, and had turned his life around and gone on to not only return to wrestling, but return and win championships. He was only the second latino wrestler to win the WWE title. He left us with a lot of good memories though. No one could deny that. Sure sometimes his in ring persona was that of the heel, but even then his performances were without flaw. If I were to name wrestlers who put asses in seats, Eddies name would be at the top of the list. It's been said that the measure of a man was not how much he loved others, but how much others loved him. If that's true, then there is no measure to Eddie Gurrero. Rest In Peace Eddie.
