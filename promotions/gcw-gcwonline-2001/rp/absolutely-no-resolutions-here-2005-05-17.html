Absolutely no resolutions here!

17 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 17th, 2005 In the aftermath, I'm usually to the point of haiku. That, while elegant enough, isn't really what I'm feeling right now. It's sonnet time. Last night in Greece, we dined on pills and life We held up high our heads and then did stuff Like sacrifice a goat to Zeus above And bent over peril; beat away strife Succumbing to stupid is how we thrive And so on, and so forth, until there's a full sonnet. Leaking radiation from my drug-induced fall out, I've got some time to piece things together. I've got vague memories that only seem to come out in poem form. At some point I did At least, I most likely cursed System of a Down A useless memory, all right. Sand grinds between my teeth and clots my nostrils. Water. I slack my jaw and sweat drips down into my bottom lip, salty and holy nourishment that finally collects enough to wash the grit from my mouth. I look down at the cracking earth and spit--damning the Mediterranean climate for not being suited to my fur coat. Other than that, nothing. There should be people around me, a preternatural force with a nice rack, and a wrestler. He's a black guy, red hair. Trey Tremendous. Yeah. Someone else, spry and wily with dead hair that hung pale and limp against her cheeks. Someone important to me, and new. Maybe important because she was new? I look right and Tulz is bundled up tight between rocks, drenched in sweat and still asleep. Left is Trey, back against an ancient tree with his head cocked to the side enough that he'll be in hell whenever he wakes up. Someone else isn't at my feet. Was I gone enough to completely invent a person? No, she has to be around here somewhere. Stand up, Jordan. At the very least, you need a cigarette. Everyone talks to themselves sometimes. So, up it is and a cigarette it is. The first one I pull from my pack is broken at the filter. I try to fix it, rolling it between my fingers to dump tobacco on the ground and shoving the loose paper into the filter, but it refuses to hold so I throw it down and finger another, better cigarette to smoke. Then 'JORDAN!' comes from not quite in the distance to break up all the peace, and someone's coming up fast on me from behind. No reaction time, I take the hit and roll with it, coming out on top of that pale-headed girl I'd tried to remember earlier. 'G'morning.' 'I know you.' 'I know.' 'Glory?' 'Good enough.' 'Sister?' 'Yeah.' 'Alright.' I let her up and start to remember until everything makes sense, bumming her a cigarette somewhere in between. She's talking the whole time, chiming in without realizing how much effort it takes to tune her and the myriad of other voices out. And those vague hallucinations, quick shimmers in the distance and shadowy creatures moving under the sun's hard glare, don't make it any easier. It still comes back to me, as well as the fact that I need to get Trey to Greece sometime within the next day. Or two. 'What's today, Glory?' 'Uh, Tuesday. 17th.' There's something in the back of my head about the show being on Thursday, but I don't trust it enough to take the risk. It can't be that long of a drive. 'What're your plans?' 'I'm going to Greece. Trey's got a match.' 'Mind if I tag along?' Tulz is asleep--she can't be able to know what I'm thinking about now--so I count the rise and fall of her breasts, taking until five before I've come up with a good enough excuse. 'Can't. The bat'll eat you.' 'The bat?' 'Yeah. Bat that lives in my car, eats people under the legal drinking age.' 'Jordan.' 'Alright, alright. There's no bat.' 'So I can?' 'I didn't say that.' 'I'm your sister.' 'And?' 'Doesn't that mean something?' 'Yeah, it does. Means a whole hell of a lot, and it's a great thing. We're gonna have to sit down and catch up someday, trade stories or whatever, but right now I've got a lot of people to haul around Europe in a tiny little rental car, and I'm not sure you wanna be the fourth person in the group.' Her eyebrows drop, blocking me from seeing anything but the slightest white line of her eyes, and the corners of her lips pinch down. She's angry and about to cry and I'm a bastard and that's the way things sometimes go. 'Look, I don't wanna let you down, but there's a lot of shit I've gotta do. If you came looking for closure or something, you're screwed 'coz I'm all out of it. All I know is that a mom that abandoned me abandoned you too, and as sweet as it is that you've found me after all these years, it doesn't mean I can just stop everything I'm doing.' Damnit; said the wrong thing. 'You're just like her.' She turns before the first tear quite drops, and runs off towards the club, bawling the whole way. Just shake my head and sit back down, waiting for Tulz to wake up and let me know that I've officially gone into major moral bankruptcy. Once she's taken everything into account, of course. I feel bad, so as soon as the cigarette's done I pack a bowl to help me feel a little less of anything. It helps, until Trey comes up to me, wiping the gunk from his eyes and yawning in confusion. 'Yo.' 'Yeah?' 'What's up.' It's not a question, so I don't answer. 'You ready for Greece?' 'The fuck's in Greece, J?' 'Your match, dumbass.' 'A match?' 'Toxic.' 'Isn't his name Twizzy?' 'Twisted's the burn-out. You're up against Toxic, the big guy that hates Twisted.' 'They ain't the same person?' 'There's like a six inch difference, jackass.' 'Toxic's... how tall?' 'Almost seven.' 'What?' 'Yeah.' 'Fuck.' 'It's alright. Don't you remember when I beat Risk and Draven? Toxic isn't any bigger than Risk, and there's no analogue to Draven in this, so yeah.' 'You used fuckin' smoke bombs.' 'And nunchucks, if you'll remember correctly. And I won, which is the most important lesson to take from it.' 'With fuckin' smoke bombs and nunchucks.' 'And a really big bo.' 'I don't have any of that shit, man.' 'Yeah, but you've got a brain. Just use it.' 'Yeah, yeah. Where's your sis at?' 'I told her she couldn't come to Greece with us, so she went crying back to the club.' 'Damn man. That's cold.' 'It's business. I don't ask her to stop her life for me.' 'But she did. That's gotta mean somethin', an' shit, it's your fuckin' sister. You can't treat blood like that.' 'I wouldn't know.' 'Man, you got issues.' 'I hate that goddamned band.' 'Who?' 'Korn. Want smoke?' I move the little green glass piece to him and he snatches it from me, hitting it hard and hacking up the better part of a lung in return. 'They had some good shit.' 'Not enough to worry about. Music sucks anyway.' 'Shit, I know you listen to music. Everyone does.' 'All of it sucks, liking something doesn't make it any good.' 'Hell you listen to now anyway?' 'Some of this, some of that. Same as always.' 'True. So what you gonna do about your sis?' He sits down on a rock across from me, musing over the twirling stream of smoke rising from the bowl before he passes it over and establishes a pace of rotation. 'I'll talk to her sometime,' hold. Release. 'Try to calm her down some. Why, what were you gonna suggest?' 'I dunno, man. Somethin' a lil quicker, maybe.' 'Look, if she's really my sister, she's going to have to make damned sure to prove it. If that's through constant rejection until we take a blood test, then that's how it goes.' 'I don't even know, man.' 'Yeah, I know. It's a fucked up place to be.' He laughs. 'What the hell were you talkin' 'bout Lexicon last night?' 'Was it a demented eulogy?' He shrugs at me. 'Let's go with that then. Maybe I'm just looking forward to you beating his ass.' 'When's that?' 'Sometime. I doubt he'll be letting that Eva thing slide anytime soon.' 'Shit, can't blame me man. She was talkin' to me. After all, ladies love Trey Tremendous.' 'You ever buy one of those t-shirts?' 'Haha, yeah. Merchandisin' was the shit. Nothin' in the world like seein' your face stretched across some chick's rack.' 'I do have a pretty face.' 'Y'know what I meant.' 'Yeah, you like my face. It's alright, Trey, perfectly natural.' He takes his turn on the bowl silently and passes it back without a word. Mopey son of a bitch. 'What's up?' 'Shit, that shit with your sister. Just seems weird to me that you'd be so cool not havin' a family. Like, I can't imagine not growin' up with my mama singin' me to sleep every night.' 'My aunt did that.' 'It ain't the same.' 'Was to me. Hell, I didn't realize there was a difference between a mom and an aunt until I was about five. Then, I just knew that my mom was either dead or not a good person, and I was fine with my aunt.' 'Damn.' 'Living's just learning what to do. That's how I got by, you got by with your mom tucking you in at night. You had your siblings, I had my friends.' 'Guess you should learn how to treat your siblings.' 'Guess you should learn tact.' 'Sorry, man.' 'It's cool, doesn't matter much to me. Hey, would you go check on Glory for me?' 'You sure you wan' me to do that?' 'C'mon Trey. You know I'd kill you if you fucked her.' 'Haha. Sure, man.' 'No, really. You wouldn't even see it coming, and you probably wouldn't even think I'd know, but I'd know, and I'd build your life up to the best it'd ever been. I'd support you in everything you did, watching you rise to the top of the ranks and enjoy the riches your success brought you. About the sixth muscle car you'd restored, I would murder you in the most foul, inhuman way possible. They'd cower when speaking my name, even in the darkest depths of hell. I'd kill you, Trey.' 'I got it, man. You'd kill me.' 'Ding ding. So yeah, check up on her for me.' 'Aight.' I once treated a man like a beast And trained him to make one deceased 'The best way,' I said 'To make sure they're dead 'Is to slay 'em when they're still asleep.'
