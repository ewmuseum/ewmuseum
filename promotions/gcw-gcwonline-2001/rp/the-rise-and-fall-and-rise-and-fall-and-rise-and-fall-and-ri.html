Date: Timeless His (now third and likely counting) shot at redemption ended abruptly when Jason O'Neil simply stopped showing up for work. Anything could be blamed, really -- the quality of competition, the moderate pay for someone of his stature, exhaustion, depression, repression, aggression, regression, congestion. With him, there was no truth, no lie. Only shades of gray based in some modicum of fact. FUSE simply didn't work out for Jason O'Neil, and as a result, he took himself to the Eastern Valley Wrestling Alliance, where he was immediately thrown into a match with theirmid-level champion. And the fire lasted for three weeks before tapering off. O'Neil said publicly that "Mountain Man" Jack Elias was simply too stiff in the ring, and the EVWA folks didn't protect the stars of O'Neil's stature. He swore that no one would work for them again, because he had stroke. Weeks later, he surfaced in a tiny New England company that he personally bankrupted without ever setting foot into the ring. The barn he was meant to wrestle in, had been cleared three days before hand, yet, that wasn't good enough for the Scarecrow. Someone of his stature should not to be made to wrestle and change where pigs and cows shit. And so, he walked away, leaving the company in a lurch, and ruining his last chance -- at the time -- of being a professional wrestler. The Sociopath cared nothing for that. There was plenty to fall back on -- his law degree for one, his accounting degree for another. And so, O'Neil set up a small tax practice in Toledo, Ohio, helping tax cheats through loopholes they'd never seen before. A few clients were so bold onO'Neil's advice they wound up in prison, and soon, that dried up as well. There were bottles to drink, and as such, money to pay for said bottles. His friend Jamison visited regularly, and took O'Neil from a moderate house, into a tiny, cramped basement apartment on the bad side of town. It wasn't Jamison's fault entirely. Jimmy Beam, Buddy Weiser, GodDamn Miller, The Coors Family of Beers and that Ruskie wonder, Vodka played parts in it as well and left Jason O'Neil alone with his relics of what he once was, and what he'd never be again. January's snow picked up early one morning, just after 2008 gave way to 2009, the winter wind blowing across the open Lake Erie, taking moisture with it soon-to-be snowflakes swirling high into the atmosphere before falling wherever they decided to. Five inches had fallen since midnight, another and fifteen more were predicted by morning. These were the nights O'Neil always slept best; the cold wind rattled the windows of his meager apartment barely keeping the wind out, the snow almost covering each ground floor window. The sound of breaking glass startled him. A man telling someone with him to shut the fuck up so they can get in and out with the stuff forced him to sit up in bed. He heard his things -- the things that were left -- being moved around in adesperate search for something valuable. He jumped out of bed and grabbed the Louisville Slugger he'd had since little league, his knuckles turning white as he readied his swing. "Cute kid this guy's got. Wonder if she's here," a voice called before being quieted with a shh. "You're gunna wake this muthafucka, bitch." "Fuck him. Bitch owes me money. I'm gunna take what's mine. Fuck him." The door opened and the two men turned surprised. O'Neil swung at the loudmouth, catching him on the side of the head. Had he been a softball, it was likelyJason'd have sent one into the upper deck, the man crumpling to the ground without so much as a struggle, his lungs gurgling. The second man shrieked as he fumbled through his pockets in a panic. "You're next, bitch," he breathed, The man stopped moving as O'Neil held the bat up, almost like alightsaber , as if he were giving the second man a chance to flee. He didn't move, aside from rummaging through his pockets, packets of drugs falling out with each movement. O'Neil stepped forward as the man pulled out a knife, a small pocket job, perfect for slicing leaves in half when you're six years old, and not at all intimidating to a grown man. O'Neil smiled as he advanced. "Here it comes," O'Neil said a smile on his face. He hadn't seen the other two men who stood in the kitchen waiting for him, the first hitting him in the head with a cast iron skillet. The fog hadn't quite set in yet as O'Neil looked into the face of the fourth attacker, who put a bat to the side of his head. ------ BROLL: Jason O'Neil vs. Dusk, FUSE, :38 sec. Coming up in Sports... ------ The doctors worked fast patching up his injuries. Thirty-six stitches to a cut on top of his head, fifteen on his chest, five stab wounds to the gut, stapled up and cleaned out, three broken ribs, a broken arm and a shattered cheekbone later, and the former Dark Messiah lay motionless in a hospital bed. Machines buzzed and whirred, bleeped and beeped, ticked and tocked, dripped and dropped and inhaled and exhaled, each move, keeping him alive, hours after the attack. While the body started to mend itself, the mind stopped, simply content to lay motionless, lost behind blue eyes. He was catatonic, for no medical reason except his own mind shutting down all response to outside stimulus. Scream in his face, he won't flinch despite his eyes being open, looking at a point far off beyond what you can see. "Does he have any family?" the attending doctor asked the police officer. "Not that we can find. His parents died a few years back." the police officer answered. "Didn't he have a brother?" The officer chuckled. "Yeah, one of the guys at the station thought so, too. Guess that's just for TV. The guy just hung up on us when we asked." "Friends?" "There was a number in his phone for Chet Worth. His exact words were 'Fuck that piece of shit. If he ever comes to, I'll beat his ass myself.' Another one to Steven Caldera who hung up immediately, and a woman named Melinda who told us she wanted nothing to do with him." "Jesus. There's no one for this guy?" Officer shook his head. "Nobody. Guys down at his local watering hole say he's a prick. Gets a few beers into him and starts bragging about almost beating Angelo Deville, almost being GCW World Champion. Challenges guys to fights, and usually gets his ass kicked. He's insufferable and a drunk." "There's gotta be something we can do for this guy," the doctor said, exasperated. "Yeah. Treat him and let him go. He ain't worth it." ------ Weeks later, his body lay still, his mind the only activity in the now hollow shell of his body. There were no drinking problems, no drug problems, noroid rage, no little girl he loved with all his heart at the end of a phone cord he couldn't hug. There were only the demons of each that haunted and taunted him to no end. "Come on, Jason. Wake up, and let's grab a drink or two. Just one or two, I promise. That's all we need. We'll be right as rain then.C'mon." said the alcohol demon. "No, no. Let's lift weights. Three hours a day, three million reps. You're not using your dick anyway. Die young, leave a pretty corpse!" "Daddy? I miss you, Daddy." "I miss you too, Jason. Get the spoon, c'mon." The voices converged in his head. "Come on, Jason. Come on, Daddy. Come on, Jason. Come on, Daddy." It wasn't all bad though. He found through his inaction, he could wave most of it off. And sometimes late at night, he could very faintly feel his body harden. He was avirile man, even in his social ineptitude, able to pry the panties off even the toughest target. Maybe a nurse recognized him and gave in to her pleasure, seeing the length and girth of what lie underneath his flimsy hospital gown. There were moments of clarity on how to get his life on track again, to get the cars and women again. There were moments in his mind replayed with better results. For one, Angelo Deville was barely a blip on the radar, O'Neil running him over at Genesis, on his way to the FSW Championship belt. And then, the OSW Championship followed. The RPW and PCW and PWC titles followed, and soon, Jason O'Neil was on top of the world. And then, there were times he just couldn't take it anymore. He ached to move, even as he felt his therapists bend his legs to fight atrophy. He was like jelly, his body simply going with the flow. He wanted to touch hisassailant, to see what she had to give as well. He wanted to punch and kick and suplex and strangle again. But, most importantly, he wanted money again. He wanted his life back. He screamed as loudly as he could muster each time, and he imagined his voice carrying for miles. His cries bouncing through Chicago, on toward Jackson Hole, to LA, and across the pacific, the world feeling his pain. Unfortunately for him, it only echoed through his now silent head. He screamed for his God, who he put his entire faith in, only to be seemingly taken from everything he'd ever believed. He screamed for his life and dreams. His hopes and wishes. But, more than that, he screamed, just to feel something, only to feel nothing. He was locked inside his own mind, and didn't know how to get out. It only served to frustrate him more. ----- The young doctor did what he could to make O'Neil comfortable. He left the television on twenty four hours a day, changing the channel every so often, hoping something -- anything -- could snap him to. No one of his stature deserved to lay in a hospital bed for three months, becoming a shell of what he once was. Mornings were time for Rachael Ray, who O'Neil'd been briefly linked to, who he'd truthfully had a passing affair with, so far as O'Neil told the story. In truth, they'd met once briefly. She smiled at him, and he stared at her chest, looking for any sign of breast. She moved along, rolling her eyes, and in an instant, O'Neil had a sexual history with the woman, knowing her likes and dislikes, and telling all who were interested. Doctor Phil was part of the routine as well, but, to be fair, the Doctor didn't complete rounds in time to turn on something else. Afternoons were for CNN and the news. There may be money floating around that O'Neil had in stocks and bonds, and the doctor figured that bad news regarding the economy might help him come around. O'Neil, however,preferred to keep his equities liquid, and in a cupboard above his sink. Evenings were wrestling shows, the doctor hoping that his former career could bring the Scarecrow back into the world again. These were the times that showed the most promise, especially on X:TV.Every time Steven Caldera showed up, Jason O'Neil's pulse would race, and for a brief second, his fingers on each hand would twitch, the middle digit specifically raising themselves from whatever positionO'Neil's hands were in. And sometimes late at night when other doctors were far too busy to pay attention to the first year resident who finished near the bottom of his class, the Doctor would bring in his own private DVDs, keeping them to a respectable volume as various men pumped in and out of various,interchangeable women. When the more senior doctors were away on emergencies, the Doctor would help Jason with his predicament. Despite being locked into his mind, the Doctor thought the wrestling, specifically the GCW events showed great promise, and soon bought every DVD that GCW had to offer. The Best of Jhub, Shane Lane, Great Tiger were first, and got no reaction, much like the careers of the men the DVDs were named after, while they were inGCW rings. Soon, the doctor exhausted every GCW TV show, DVD, VHS bootleg, and youtube video he could find. He needed to get more serious. ---- On May 16th, 2009, Jason O'Neil opened his eyes for the first time in nearly five months. A nurse smiled at him as he met her gaze, quickly calling for other doctors. A barrage of tests were done as O'Neil lay initially motionless, unsure how his body should react after so long without any control of it. His fingers moved first, clenching his fist, each movement getting easier. The elbows and knees follow the toes and wrists, and soon his shoulder and jaw. "What happened?" O'Neil asked, his eyes looking around the room, trying to grasp where he is. "You were attacked," a doctor answered. His fingers traced his stomach, to find a metal plate covering it. He glanced down, and around his waist lay a belt. He grasped it, though a bit too weak to lift it up. The doctors buzzed around him, barely noticing his movement as they checked the machines that whirred and stirred. A momentary struggle left the plate in his face. Connected to a large leather strap, there in his hands was the GCW World Championship. The nameplate read "Jason O'Neil", and was so shiny, O'Neil could see his face in between the etching. It was beautiful, and it was his. "Where did I get this?" "We don't know. It showed up one day, and we knew you were a wrestler. We figured it was from them." "They... they hate me." The doctor nodded. "Yes. First patient in memory that no one came to see. Well, aside from your landlord to serve the eviction notice, and the police to drop off your belongings, since you had nowhere to live." "No- No- No one came for me?" "Not a single person. Only one that stayed with you was Doctor Madsen." "Can I see him?" "I'm afraid not. We caught him in an inappropriate moment with a patient, and he's been fired." True to his theory, Doctor Madsen gave Jason what he most wanted in life. It didn't matter it was a replica of what he'd been chasing for seven years. This belt was Jason's birthright; he could be denied it in the ring, but, it was only a matter of time before it came to him. Before he felt the fifteen pounds of gold, leather and diamond around his waist. The belt pulled O'Neil from the depths, and bore him into the world anew. Six days later, Jason O'Neil stood outside of Metro General Hospital, overlooking West 25th street. Thirty-six dollars waited in his wallet to be spent at the nearest bar. His credit cards lapsed with time, and he had no where to go. A man of his stature couldn't hit rock bottom like this. Surely, there was another way. With his cellphone amazingly in service, he went through his contacts list, and pressed send as the number highlighted Steven Caldera's name. Good day, you've reached the private desk of Steven Caldera. I'm currently on another call, but, please leave your name and number, and I will get back to you as soon as possible. If this is a talent emergency, please call the emergencyhot line. Thank you. "Steven... It's-- It's Jason. I'm... coming home. I'm going for it, one more time."
