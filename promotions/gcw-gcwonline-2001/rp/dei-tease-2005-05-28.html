Dei-tease!

28 May 2005

Date: 5/28/05 'Man, I got a worst headache.' 'You need soda.' 'Huh?' 'Probably because you haven't had caffeine today. Go grab a Mello Yello or something from the bar.' 'Y'sure?' 'Always.' 'Aight.' Trey left the table and I kept thinking to Tulz, glancing over to catch her facial expressions and get some idea of how she was reacting to the horrible things that ran through my head. 'So, whaddya think?' 'About what?' 'That last idea.' 'Not now, Jordan.' 'I didn't mean now--later.' 'How much later?' 'Tonight?' 'Not tonight.' 'Why not?' 'It doesn't happen tonight.' 'When does it happen?' She shrugged to me. '327 years is way too long to be a virgin.' 'Maybe.' 'It happens?' 'Yes.' 'When?' 'Sometime.' 'I don't have 327 to wait.' 'Not that long.' 'You know, I didn't know you could see the future.' 'I can't.' She raised the corner of her mouth in a coy smile before I even had the time to realize what she meant. 'So I don't have to keep trying?' 'No, you do.' '...why?' 'I enjoy it.' Then I thought something about how she'd already learned to be a cunt within a month of going into the real world, and she just laughed about it until Trey got back with his drink. 'Man, some'ne recognize' me.' 'She cute?' 'Was a dude.' 'Damn.' 'Yeah, wanted autograph. Fuckin' crazy, ain't never had a fan like that before.' I glanced around the room with an odd feeling in my gut. 'What'd he look like?' 'Big dude, taller 'an me. Hair almosto 'is shoulders, sorta shaggy, y'know? Ain't shaved in awhile either.' 'Huh. Any piercings?' 'Nah, but didn't see 'is ears.' 'Shit. What about scars?' 'Man, I wasn't payin' that much attention. Just some dude.' 'I think it was god.' 'You just drunk, man.' 'No, that sounded like the guy.' 'An' a lotta other slack-asses too lazy to get they hair cut or shave. Wasn't your little god-dude.' 'You sure?' 'Shit, you can ask 'im 'fit matters that matter to you.' 'Matters that matter?' 'Matters 'at much.' 'Drunk bastard.' 'Jus' havin' fun.' 'Where's he sitting?' 'Who?' 'God-dude.' 'Ah shit, corner of the bar. Right under the--uh--Budweiser sign.' I glanced over to where Trey pointed me and saw the least explainable darkness I'd ever witness. The red-and-gold eagle shined in sharp lines that let off no ambient light, cast no errant shadows--just lingered there in the dark, glowing lines in a pit of an eerie blackness in an already more-than-sufficiently dank club. A figure hunched over the bar, nursing a tall glass of something with too much head and dragging at a long-cherried cigarette that I intrinsically knew was a Newport. He was more outline than man, occasionally highlighted by the drag of a cigarette, but otherwise an inkblot of a life that wasn't even there at a casual glance. That son of a bitch. I finished the glass I was having an affair with at the time and kicked my chair back as I stood up--Trey bickering to get him another Sprite. 'You dumb bastard! Sprite doesn't have caffeine!' 'Damnit man, now my headache's back.' 'Fuck you; you're coming with me.' And you, I thought in Tulz' general direction--still unsure of how to show possession with her name. Most likely 'Tulz's,' but that just looks bad. Either way, she followed and we made a sad limp of getting to the corner and taking our seats. I sat in the bleak shadow, and it half-cast on Trey. Tulz, however, was gleaming in the soft glow of the bar. A minute passed. Trey ordered a Mello Yello and I lit a cigarette and looked over. 'God?' The guy looked up, half-smiling. 'How'd you know?' 'Fucking asshole. What brings you here?' 'You've been looking for me. And really, stop calling me God. That name has some bad associations to it.' 'Well, what is your name?' 'Jayce.' 'I knew a Jayce.' 'I know. It has a few bad associations to it too, but I'm not the one that came up with it.' 'Who was that?' 'My mom.'He looked back to his drink, clearly thinking about finishing it off. He followed through on the thought before snuffing his Newport, reflexively lighting another. 'Not as epic of an answer as I'd expected.' 'Don't do epic. Not my thing.' 'What is it, exactly, that you do?' 'Control you, Trey, Tulz, Sobias, Danielle--pretty much everyone you've ever met, actually.' 'So you're god.' 'Sort of.' Trey nodded and held his hands out--maybe sarcastically. I looked back to Jayce with an emphatic nod. 'That's good enough for me.' 'I know.' 'I'm really tired of being around mind readers.' 'Not quite a mind reader.' 'Mind...writer.' 'That's got a nice ring to it.' 'So, what's the big idea?' 'You're meeting your creator, and that's the best question you've got? Come on.' 'Well, as our creator...' 'Not 'our.' Didn't create Trey.' 'Huh?' 'That was a friend of mine, I just control him now.' 'What the fuck is going on?' He laughed and took the top off a fresh glass of beer that had slipped my eye somehow. 'If you knew your purpose in life, you'd cry yourself to sleep every night.' 'Fuck purpose--what's the deal with this entire life thing?' 'What about it? You live a good one, and I'm trying to make Trey's better. Why should you worry about how it gets done, so long as it gets done?' 'Because it's my life, and once I saw the puppeteer's strings, I got a little curious.' 'Good answer. Doesn't really matter though, you haven't gone through nearly enough shit yet to know the good stuff. Danielle's still doing better than that ex of yours--was it Amy?' I thought back. Car crashes, stupid teenagers, dying girlfriends, really fun shit. The only name that came to mind was Amy--I nodded to him. 'It wasn't always. I keep forgetting it, so you keep remembering it anew. It's funny, isn't it?' 'If I was Kurt Vonnegut, yeah.' 'It is funny, and you know it. You can act all indignant if you really wanna, but everyone here worth a damn thinks it hilarious. Trey's at least that drunk, and after that long, Tulz has to be pretty cynical.' I was nodding and biting my tongue behind a smile that had apparently always gotten the joke when I felt Trey's hand against my chest, pushing me from the bar so he could eye up with Jayce. 'I don' buy it.' 'What?' 'This act. You ain't done shit to prove it.' 'What do you want, a complete change of your past? Just wipe all those scars away, give you your dreadlocks and your brain back? Let the kid be yours?' 'Fuck you, man. What you gonna do?' Trey was angry drunk, off his stool and hanging behind me in an off-balance boxer's stance. 'Look, if it were entertaining, you'd be married to a supermodel and have a bunch of little kids running around. It's just, well, not worth the time. Calm down.' 'Understandable.' Trey nodded like a gentleman and sat back down. I turned to look at him, mouth agape in awe, and he was back to drinking and listening intently. 'So, Jordan, where were we?' I tasted the copper blood rushing to my head--waves of anger and fear that made their way up my spine at break-neck pace. 'Is he just going to be fine with this?' 'Yeah, for awhile. In about a week, it's going to come back and hit him just like it hit you; how horribly fucked up it was. I can do that, though, and I can do good things. I do a lot of good things, which puts you on a better level than me as far as being assured of a benevolent higher being. Just don't tell anyone that doesn't already know what's going on about it--especially not Sobias. He would lynch you for that one, just keep your hunt up for awhile and then back away and act bored like you always do.' 'You're not backing out this easy. There are questions, and things like that--more questions, in fact.' 'Save the big ones for later. I can taste my stomach right now.' 'Bastard; what later?' 'There's gonna be a later. I've got some timing.' 'I shouldn't be trusting you, but enough wasting time--why the hell did I save a clone of the President? Better yet, why the hell is shit sorted the way it is?' 'Because I don't mean anything on the grand scale. There's still some rules I've got to adhere to. Think of it like GTA--you guys do what I want you to do, but I didn't make the world. You can't blame all that on me.' 'So giving a stoner the giggles is my lot in life? O Fate, why hast thou betrayed me?' 'Not really. Like I said, your reason for existing is pretty low on the scales. You should just be happy that I didn't make you a paraplegic that was good with computers.' Tempted, slightly, to make him a paraplegic that was good with computers, but I somehow understood that he wasn't good with computers so the irony was lost. 'Huh. It can't be that bad.' 'Oh, it is.' 'How bad?' 'Miserable.' 'I'm doing pretty well.' He nodded, agreeing. 'Really, you're just trying to hurt my feelings at this point.' 'Hell, maybe. I think we might have to catch up some other time though.' 'Why?' 'After I finish this sentence, the window behind us is going to cave in so I suggest you guys start moving before I run out of things to say; things that won't turn it into a fragment and rather just a remarkably long sentence full of incredibly poor syntax and a hideous overuse of adjectives for the sole purpose of stringing it out like a heroin junkie.' We moved back in time to get a good view of the blacked out glass shattering and the mob going wild, crowding in on us, yelling and tearing their way across the bar and towards the main part of the room. I lost Jayce somewhere in the swarm, which we all knew was going to happen, and quickly ditched my shirt to the floor. I'd been smart enough to wear a stringy old Grateful Dead shirt underneath, which isn't very smart but at least shows some concern to the idea of being spotted. I slipped my glasses into my pocket and made my way to the door, Trey careful to close his jacket and Tulz trying to mimic me. She got her shirt half-way up her chest before I tugged it down. 'What?' 'Any other time is completely fine, so long as we're not in the middle of a heavy riot. Back to the hotel before the fucking tear gas comes, that's all I ask.'
