Imprisoned in Paradise: Love's Labour's Lost

19 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/19 Immediately following WorldWide? “NOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOO!!!!” There was certainly reason for Seymour Almasy to be anguished in the aftermath of WorldWide. He had defeated Aiden Jackson, true, but only by countout. That meant that title #21 would have to wait. But Seymour’s anguish at this moment was over something entirely different. Over something that Seymour cared about even more than shiny golden belts and wrestling glory. “Where in the name of Yevon did my memory card go?” In his locker room, a puzzled Seymour looked at his PlayStation 2. It was the new slim model, naturally. Not a speck of dust rested on it, nor was a wire out of place. Checking inside the system, his Final Fantasy XI disc was still there, still secure. “This is definitely odd,” Seymour said, to himself. “I know I brought the memory card with me. I was going to do some work in Final Fantasy IX on getting Excalibur II after I played a bit more FFXI?” The missing memory card contained roughly three-hundred hours of game play in Final Fantasy IX, XI, and Tactics. It was also the only memory card that he took on the road with him. His thousands of hours spent playing favorites such as the seventh, eighth, and tenth renditions of the Final Fantasy series were kept safely secured in the Almasy safe. “I’d better find this thing,” he grumbled, clearly displeased. “I REALLY don’t want to have to try to beat FF9 again with no summons and a level one party.” With that, the search began of the locker room. Seymour got down on hands and knees, combing the floor for the small, black device that represented many a Saturday in Seymour’s life. But nothing turned up. “Memory card, oh memory card” Where are you? I know you’re here somewhere! Come on, I need to go beat Pandemonium!” Not even when he checked the television stand. “Stupid television stand! So many nooks and crannies, so few memory cards!” Not even when he checked his own gear, before remembering that his armor didn’t HAVE pockets. “I bet this damn thing’s in my pocket. Hey, wait a minute. Armor doesn’t have pockets?” Well. That meant one thing. His memory card was gone, and he needed to get it back. Fortunately, Seymour knew that one day, such a situation could occur, and as such, he was prepared. Wordlessly, and with the gravest of grave expressions on his face, the Final Fantasy reached into his duffel bag. There, among the other gear that he brought with him, was what appeared to be posterboard, scissors, and a black Sharpie. Placing the posterboard on the locker room’s coffee table, he began to write. ================ LOST: MEMORY CARD MEMORY CARD HAS GREAT SENTIMENTAL VALUE. YEARS OF SAVE FILES OFFERING GENEROUS REWARD FOR SAFE RETURN! CALL SEYMOUR: [detail withheld] “Perfect.” Seymour admired his handiwork, recently attached to a backstage wall in the America West Arena with a few strips of sticky tape. Four others had been placed at strategic locations throughout the arena. If anyone in the stadium found the memory card, they would know to call Seymour on his cell phone. To most, the entire situation would be ridiculous. It was a memory card, for God’s sake. Seymour could buy another one for $25 at the store, and for a man making as much as Seymour did, it wouldn’t even be a drop in the bucket. But that would be a blank memory card. This memory card represented some of Seymour’s happiest memories in life, ever since his divorce. Seymour Almasy had no significant other. He had few friends. In a cruelly ironic twist of fate, his life had gone full circle. After a childhood of neglectful parents, once again, the video games were the only things that kept him company. Slumping against the wall, Seymour thought how ludicrous this was. Once, he had been on top of the world. He was PTC’s Global Champion, considered nearly unbeatable by those in the know. More importantly, he had a loving wife, who followed him everywhere, who was at his side through the good and the bad. “Laura would have been there to listen to me tonight,” he whispered. “To comfort me for not being Cruiserweight Champion. For failing. She’d be there to say that everything was okay, and because it was her, I’d believe it.” Love was hell. There was no way around it. “Mr. Almasy!” Seymour’s heart jumped in his chest at the voice behind him. Someone had found his memory card! A broad smile replaced his frown as he whirled at near light speed to see who was behind him. “Yes,” Seymour asked, looking something like a child on Christmas morning. “I’ve got your match assignment for next WorldWide.” This year, for Seymour, there was only coal. Disinterestedly, he took the piece of paper that the man was offering him, scanning it briefly. He skimmed through a vast majority of the legal mumbo-jumbo, finally finding the only bit of information he even gave a slight damn about. “Eva VanLorne. Wonder-frickin”-ful.” He’d have to prepare for Eva. And quite a bit as well, considering that behind the pretty exterior was the heart and determination of a warrior. God help you if you got her mad’which made him wonder exactly what the hell Borealis and Knight were thinking going to such extreme lengths to piss the woman off. The GCW employee had left. Thank God. Seymour wasn’t in the mood to deal with people right now. He was generally miserable, and thinking about what had happened to Eva had pushed that misery into more palpable anger. Seymour knew that his chosen profession was filled with jackals and assholes. People who couldn’t confine their vendettas to the rules or the ring. People who sought their satisfaction in human misery. And in this case, those two people were the company’s top two champions. It was enough to make Almasy physically ill. “Too much to do these next few weeks,” he said, pondering what calling out Borealis and Knight next week on WorldWide would do. It would add two more people to the list of people who wanted a crack at him, with Rich Rollins being at the top of that-- “Wait a goddamned minute?” The name of the Renegade flashed in Seymour’s mind like a Las Vegas marquee. To the best of his knowledge, Rich Rollins hadn’t been booked tonight, but that certainly didn’t mean that he hadn’t showed up. Murderous thoughts tore through Almasy’s body. If he knew Rich Rollins like he thought he did, Rollins would be out to brag about his misdeed next week, maybe even try and hold the memory card ransom. Suddenly, the coming WorldWide was starting to seem like a really strenuous ordeal. If Eva wasn’t enough to worry about, he had his inner desire to call out LeStatt Knight and Jonathan Borealis for being hideous excuses for human beings. And if THAT together wasn’t enough, Rich Rollins would likely be gloating. Slowly, Seymour let himself sit, back sliding down the wall as he did so. The more he thought about things, the more he realized how alike he and Eva really were. They’d both had their spirits broken by ones they loved. They both now bore scars, both physical and mental. Yet neither would yield, not in the ring, nor in life. Logic told Seymour to deal with Eva this week, and leave the rest for another day. But the Final Fantasy was not a creature of logic. He was a creation of emotion, of chivalry, of what he’d learned from years of playing heroic knights. At the same time, though, Eva wasn’t exactly going to sit idly by and let him be chivalric, as she was rather likely going to try to beat the absolute hell out of him. She was wounded, and backed into a corner. And regardless of whether or not Seymour meant harm, the German woman would likely strike, and strike hard. Seymour sighed. Such was the way things seemed to be working lately. Every situation he was in was one without a chance of victory.
