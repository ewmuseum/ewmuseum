Mr. Happy Face/My Hiroshima, Part 3

7 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 7, 2005 I douse my face with icy cold water, sending the skin on my neck and arms crawling with goose flesh. I open my eyes and let it cool me down, watching as the porcelain sink bowl ripples and distorts with cascading droplets. I needed that. I am still half asleep, mind cloudy with the smog of last night’s dreams. Long forgotten since it’s ? I glance at my wrist-watch, and wipe a drop of water on my shirt ? just after ten in the morning. Christ. This was a mistake. How many people are out there? More than expected. It seems like every fan of Global Championship Wrestling lives in Seattle, and every single one of them shows up with smiles of over-expressive glee. Take pictures. Sign autographs. Not too bad, though. The fans are what make wrestling as powerful as it is - at least in part. The anti-climax of this whole situation is the simple fact that I’m part vampire and the sun is boring holes that pierce through my soul. Call me Rei. Staying up all night, sleeping all day. That just screams “future champion.” Vote for me. I glance out the small port-hole window, dusty and stained from weather. Outside bathrooms are required to be filthy, I gather. A bylaw of public city plumbing decree. The tile floor is broken in places, blanketed by dirt that has long embedded within the crevices. Paint is peeling and aged, several shavings of it disseminated across the minute room. I turn my gaze toward the ceiling and wrinkle my nose at the formation of mold. No doubt where part of the smell emanated from. The other part? I note the many toilet tissue rolls thrown carelessly about before I push open the ancient restroom door and step back out into ? A madhouse. Aberrance. Insanity defined. Everywhere I look, I’m greeted with more examples. Beneath the warmth of the afternoon sun, people are wandering about aimlessly, waiting for their 40 cc injection of GCW entertainment. Children running and jumping, dogs barking and sniffing each other. I notice a parrot flapping his wings and a grinning clown. Global Championship Wrestling is a catering circus. I’m the lion tamer. I’m wearing a baseball cap, hiding my true identity under the brim of the Mariners. I’m not a fan. I don’t even like baseball. I’m just making good on my promise to GCW officials that I’d support Seattle in every way as long as I’m here. I left the banners and stickers and goofy stupid foam hands in a random dumpster on the way from the hotel room. Them: A good face, is a happy face, Rei. I swear to everything religious that’s what was said to me on the phone call that ruined my day. My response? Me: Mr. Happy face is pissed. I considered asking someone in a white coat when my pills would arrive. This whole day had been a buzz of psychosis since I awoke. Greeted into the morning after a long, sleepless night of self-arguing. Being told I had to show up to a GCW promotional event outside the Seattle arena. Meh. My week has gone beyond leaving a bitter taste at the back of my throat. It’s like a cold sore that only gets more conspicuous when you rub Carmex on it. A better example would be that it’s like getting pelted in the balls after having one testicle removed. Choose wisely. I’m still reeling from the phone call, too. Words can hardly convey the feelings. That buried any hope I had of moving on with my life. When she relocated to Portland, it was the beginning of the end. Then the end came. And now the beginning is resurfacing. A wild beast gnashing its jaws and snapping out for one last unfortunate soul. The end is the beginning is the end. Basic Confusion 102. I still honestly don’t know what she expected. My love for her is still strong, I know. I’m at a point in my life now, however, where I can’t afford suffering the slings and arrows of heartbreak, again. I was dying a little more each day and she’s come back to end it. Death comes swiftly. I can count the total number of hours of sleep I had since her phone call on a single hand. Call it guilty conscience. Call it whatever the hell you want. I am Rei. Self inflicting heartbreak. I’m the biggest asshole I know. ~ Miles away, Rebecca was hard at work. Instructions directly from the museum’s director had her running rampant since she walked in from the warm morning sunshine. Filing papers, making phone calls. She found herself swamped beyond normality and hoped this wasn’t a sign of things to come. She snickered, wondering what she would do about it if it were. What indeed. Rebecca stepped from behind her desk and admired her new office. It was evident that she was moving up on the corporate ladder. Fine mahogany desk and matching file cabinets. Glorious overview of the busy city below. Leather reclining chair that just begged to be sat in. She made a mental reminder to work grooves in that chair as soon as possible, then turned to open the door, only to have it nearly slammed into her nose. “Jesus, I’m sorry, Rebecca.” William Curtis. He was the director’s own director of creative control, heading up the development of all the shows and tours. Today, he wore a white suit jacket and black pants. Yesterday, Rebecca noted, he wore the exact opposite. “Let me help you up.” He hefted her up with surprising strength. “I’m fine. I was on my out - Uh, did you need something?” Rebecca asked him as he turned and made his way to the comfortable armed chair in front of her desk. She shrugged her shoulders and went to sit in front of him. “What do you need?” “It’s a little hot in here, don’t you think?” “No”, Rebecca thought. The air conditioner was always set on seventy, and unless someone new had been hired into a secretarial position, that was how it was. Everyone else knew the rules. “It’s fine to me.” Rebecca watched William like a hawk as he got up and paced around her office. Running a finger delicately across some of the books on her shelf, he turned back to her with a smile. “I think I’ll take off my coat. Do you mind?” “Of course,” she thought. Despite his good looks, the man was slimy. Hidden agendas wrote in bright pink across his being. “How’s life treating you, Rebecca?” “It’s fine, I guess. Why do you ask?” She knew damn well why he asked. The way he was walking and strutting, he was playing alpha male. Rebecca considered this in horribly poor taste, especially since her phone conversation with Rei. “No reason. Just wondering.” He was smiling. Beaming with pride. Typical testosterone-driven male ego trip. She suddenly felt ill. “Can you just get to the point, William” I have a lot of things to do ?” It happened so fast, she didn’t have time to react. Like a drunken haze, his clothes were off and thrown aside. He leapt up on the desk and crawled over, clawing her clothes until they fell to the floor. He kissed her deeply and passionately, as she struggled to get away, looking for anything to beat away the frothing dog. His hands slid into her pants, popping the buttons with dramatic snaps, and he kissed his way down her stomach. What happened next was something out of a Wes Craven film. ~ GCW Wrestlers abound. Several of the finest have turned out, plunging into the throngs of people with tainted smiles and deceitful stares. Maybe it’s just me. Who am I to judge? I pull the brim of the cap down even further, keeping my head down. Watching from a different point of view. I feel like I’m standing out like a sore thumb. I’m the thief wearing the light up shoes as the cops chase me into the night. I am Rei. Trying to hide amongst the crowd. I like to think of them as my people, however conceited that may appear. I remember diving into the ring at Annihilation during the announcement of the Cruiserweight division to quite a few cheers. Applause for a man who feeds them mental observations and caprices to whet their thirst for intellectualism in a field where big clubs and body hair make the man. In other words, I R smart n they luv it. Call me Rei. High on the dictionary. Great. I watch as a GCW official approaches me and slips an arm around my shoulder, steering me back into the direction from whence I came. Drained of natural skin color, he’s almost translucent. Sweat stains in his armpits. Long sleeve shirt, for reasons I can’t comprehend, wrinkled and bunched up at the waist, only partially tucked into his pants. “Rei, we realize you're still new to the professional scene and the crowds might be intimidating. How are you holding up” You look a little tired ?” What a riot. This guy had only good intentions, but my somber mood is acid. He’s wearing out his welcome, quickly. “I’ve got a lot on my mind, is all.” As always. The only time I’m not thinking is when I’m thinking about not thinking. Which, if you consider it, is thinking. Therefore, the equation is simple. Solve it on your own time. He smiles at me. His palms are sweaty, soaking through the upper shoulder area of my white 53 Minutes t-shirt. “We’re glad you came. Probably going to put you at the picture booth in a bit. They just love you new guys.” For some reason, that sounds more insulting than he intended it. He is right, I know. I’m one of Global Championship Wrestling’s bright new shining stars. I haven’t even truly wrestled a match yet, unless you count that stint with The Jhub. I don’t. I’m booked this coming up WorldWide in a match against The Rogue Paladin for the Cruiserweight Tournament. I’m not even going to pretend to know anything about the man. I don’t feel like trash talking today. Be sure to catch me in next week’s episode, where I ruin someone’s life one word at a time. Rei Tiu. Headache’s cause and solution. Anyway. I take a deep breath, for once inhaling somewhat clean air. The way mother nature intended. I watch as the people walk around to the different areas. Autograph signing. Wrestlers signing pictures of themselves in a signature that would take scholars to decipher. Photographs. Amateur wrestling in a makeshift ring gathers more people by the second. Sigh. I feel like a love song. Cue mental music. “So if I seem a little out of it, sorry But why should I lie” Everything reminds me of her.” I find myself wondering what Rebecca's doing as I make my way toward the concession stand. I pass several of GCW’s roster, who simply nod their heads. I don’t even acknowledge. “Everything reminds me of her?” ~ Rebecca sprang to life and shot up in bed, nearly throwing her body to the floor beside her. The alarm clock next to the bed had been beeping for well over an hour and she could hear her answering machine accepting a call at that very moment. She was sweating, and her nightgown felt sticky against her skin. Her hair was stringy and frizzy. No time for a shower. She was late for work. Thinking of excuses, she leapt to her feet and sifted through her drawers for something to wear. The dream had offset her day, entirely. Being attacked by William Curtis was one thing; she always thought that man was a worm and wished he would stop looking at her like she was a piece of meat. She thought back to the precise moment when William’s face began melting. How it had resembled jelly-like material and she touched it out of curiosity. Half of her clothes had been torn off, and he was completely naked. His body had begun heaving violently, as if being murdered by forces unseen. Then, she had watched as the goo changed again, the mold forming into a face she longed to see. She remembered Rei staring down at her, no remorse behind the unfamiliar cold eyes. “I’ll never forgive you for what you’ve done.” The sentence lingered on. To her, it seemed like a bad horror movie, but those words weighed heavily on her thoughts. She began combing her hair, trying to work out some of the mess. Sorting out her clothes, she slipped off the nightgown and got dressed, no longer in a hurry. What was the point? She was already late. Any excuse she could come up with wouldn’t be good enough, anyway. A few extra minutes would give her some more time to get her act together. “Always present yourself in the finest of forms,” she had heard on a daily basis. The museum’s director had this terrible fear of being thought a fool because someone wasn’t smiling. It was absurd. Little things like that about the job always nagged at her. She glanced at the telephone and the journal. Thinking about how much of an asshole Rei was. She grabbed for her keys and headed for the front door. Her answering machine let her know of her five messages. Sighing, she knew who every single one of them were from. The museum director. “What would he do without me?” She wondered. Probably harass someone else less detached from his inane babbling. Rebecca smiled as she exited into the sunshine. Rei, for once, was an afterthought, but she knew that wouldn’t last long. ~ Sexy hero pose. Corny muscle bearing pose. Sliding a finger across my throat to signify that the photographer is going down, GCW style! Call me Rei. Global Championship Wrestling’s new Vogue. People are lined up to take pictures with the stars. Several of the crew are taking simple shots; arm around the lucky fan, who is all smiles and braces. Putting the fan in a headlock and making their meanest face. I amuse myself by pointing out its resemblance to a man suffering from constipation. A day in the life of a future superstar. I strike another pose with a small Asian boy and he tells me how much he admires me. I give him a free GCW t-shirt and the cap off my head before he’s on his way. Sweet kid. So I’m here, spending my day entertaining others. Taking glamour shots, I call them. Engaging in friendly conversation. And all I can think about is Rebecca. I’m beginning to think I have a serious problem in the fact that a kid with a slushie reminds me of the time Rebecca and I shared ours beneath a clich? of stars and a full moon. My Hiroshima, indeed. I imagine her disgust for me and can’t blame her. I am Rei. Playing games with her heart. 'Everything reminds me of her.' And it does. I’m sick with regret and I’m trying to chase away thoughts of her by doing everything I can. Handing out t-shirts. I offer to referee a match with two high schoolers with aspirations of becoming the next Rich Rollins or LeStatt Knight. After it’s all done, I find myself heading for my car. An old Volvo. They’re economy efficient and environmentally friendly. I slip on my shades, slight wind parting through my tu-tone hair. I crank up and drive off. If you could see all of this, you’d get a nice lens flare on the screen as I drive off into the sun. Instead, all you get is the mental picture and the following disappointing exit line. Ahn Young Ee Gae sae Yoh. It’s a polite goodbye in Korean.
