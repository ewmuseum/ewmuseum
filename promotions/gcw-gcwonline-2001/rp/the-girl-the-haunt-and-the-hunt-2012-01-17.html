The Girl, The Haunt, and The Hunt

17 January 2012
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/17/12 Dishes are heard clanking together as the diner is busy. Many of the people that were enjoying a meal there were accustomed to their blue collar lot in life. Some came from Downriver, others from the Northern and Western Suburbs. But they all knew one thing. It didn’t matter where they were from. At the end of the day, they were all from Detroit. It was a badge of honor for those that made this region home. As much as they complained about the blight that was around them, as much as they moaned about not having enough hours at work, as badly as they would like to see improvements to public transit and the water department, they still acknowledged that they were Detroiters. Most of the people sitting around, eating the rather unhealthy food, were dressed in their Carhartt Coveralls, their Wolverine boots or whatever they wore for work. Even as the city tries to reinvent it’s downtown, it is still very much an industrial town, one of the last remnants of the Rust Belt that still hasn’t started to show that the times have changed. Near the back of the diner, though, was a man that didn’t look like he belonged. He sipped on a glass of what people from Detroit call pop, but you might call it soda. He may not have been born here, but this, for lack of a better word, was home for him. Unlike those around him, he doesn’t look like he’s seen a hard day’s worth of work in years. His hair is cut in a simple style. The gray around his temples fade into his naturally blonde hair. Scars and creases line his forehead, reminders of what he used to be, and sometimes wishes he could be again. But those years were now passed. A new knee and hip pretty much ended his old career, so now he’s onto a new career. Retirement doesn’t come easy for a man that spent so much time on the road. He isn’t used to an office, so he finds himself picking up the slack that his brother left him when he went back to what they both used to call a career. The man sits in the booth, and reminisces about the days that have long gone by to those that come up and ask him. He still is recognized, even though it had been years since he had been famous. He sits, he waits. A waitress brings him another soft drink. “Your order will be up momentarily.” He pays no mind to the waitress though. It wasn’t like he had ordered a steak that needed to be perfectly cooked. It was a couple of coney dogs, heavy on the mustard and onions, and a basket of fries. He looks out the window, and he thinks of the past. How it has changed him. How everything was different now that he was no longer what he thought he would be the rest of his life. He doesn’t even turn his attention to the young blonde that slid across from him in the booth. “You said you wanted to see me.” the man said, turning back to look at his new companion. “So, what do you have to say?” “First of all, Jason, I hope you ordered an extra dog for me.” She doesn’t even bother hiding her British accent. “Second?” “I hope that maybe, you will, like Paulie has, forgiven me.” “The kid is young. He makes mistakes. On the other hand, I don’t forget very easily, Kirsten. You want my forgiveness; you’re going to have to earn it.” “What else do I have to do to earn your forgiveness? I thought that being a part of your mind game with Alex should have been enough.” The man that once was known as Preacher stews on this for a moment. “It was a start. You still have a way to go to earn my forgiveness. First, you have to earn my trust back.” The waitress at the diner brings some plates to the table. 2 orders of coney dogs and fries. Kirsten takes a look at the plate and gives Jason a slight smile as she takes a fry from the plate and starts to eat. ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- The walk through the airport was rather uneventful for Xander. Fans of the Denver Broncos were arriving back from Boston, dejected that their team was unable to do it again against the Patriots. Many of them didn’t pay any attention to the man that stood taller than most of them as he made his way to the baggage claim area. As he arrives to take his bag, he pulls his phone out of his pocket and powers it on. He looks a little dismayed that Kirsten hadn’t called or texted him yet. He takes his bag and starts to head towards the shuttle to the car rental place when he stops and sees someone holding a sign with his name on it. “I don’t remember asking for a car service.” Xander says to the man holding the sign. “I think I would have remembered that, and I know that isn’t covered in my contract.” The man holding the sign just looks at Xander. “Well, all I know is that I was told to be here and pick you up.” “Uh…how much am I going to have to pay for this?” “Don’t worry, sir. The fee has already been paid.” Xander chuckles to himself and thinks that Kirsten shouldn’t have. “All righty then.” ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- Jason Ryan sits in on a training session in his new gym. Outside of PR work, he tries not to spend much time with the fight gear company that he co-owns with his wife. Instead, he spends his time watching friends training the wrestlers of tomorrow. Sitting next to him, Kirsten Pendleton looks like she’s ready to crack. “So, what is it that I have to do to earn your trust?” Jason looks away from the students doing their drills. “Sit and watch.” Jason then takes a drink from the bottle of water that he had at his feet. “What am I supposed to be watching?” Jason points at one of the students doing a back bump drill. “See that kid in the Iowa Hawkeyes T-Shirt?” Kirsten leans over the railing that separates the viewing deck from the training area. “Would that be the lad with the shaggy brown hair?” “That would be him.” Jason remarks as he sets the water back down by his feet. “I want to tell you a story about that kid. Five years ago, he was a state champion wrestler in Idaho. One of the best in the country. “He was offered a scholarship to wrestle at the University of Iowa. A dream of his. His grandfather wrestled there. His father wrestled there. His brother wrestled there. He was going to be going there as a legacy. “Instead, he was tested the night before what should have been the biggest meet of his life. He had been suffering from headaches for weeks, his sight was blurred, but he was able to wrestle past that. “That night, the headaches were just too intense. So he went to the emergency room, and they found that he had a tumor. The surgery was scheduled for the next day. Kid is lucky to be alive, but in the cruel reality of our world, lost the opportunity to go on and live his dream. “A year ago, he came to my company looking for work. We started talking one day when I came to take a look at some of the new designs for fighting gear. It turned out that he wanted to get back into some form of wrestling, so I told him about the school, and there he is. He’s working as hard as he can to get a second chance. “You want to earn my trust. You have to learn from his example. The kid pulled himself up from nothing and wants to become something.” Kirsten slumps back in her seat. “Well, when can I start to do that.” “When I’m ready.” ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- The ride from the airport was a different experience for Xander. He was relaxed as the driver was listening to music. “What do you do for a living, anyway?” the driver chimes in, trying to get some small talk going. “I’m a wrestler.” the pride that comes from Xander when he says this is evident. Much more so than when he first broke into the business. “A wrestler, really? Make good money doing that?” “Nope, but it really isn’t about the money. It’s about the rush of going out there and performing.” “Really? You must not be very good then.” All the pride that Xander had is gone almost instantly. “Well, I’ve been away for a while, and I’m just getting back in.” “You leave because you didn’t do well?” “I injured my knee in a match.” The driver just laughs. “You got hurt? Isn’t that stuff fake?” This is where the wrestler starts to come out of Xander again. “Listen, some places might pre-determine their results. But I’m with a company that doesn’t. Everything that we do in the ring is damn real. Plus, when you get dropped from some of the heights we do, it really, REALLY hurts.” “I’m sure that it doesn’t hurt as much as you make it seem.” “My last match, I was dropped from a ladder onto a bed that was made of an inch of padding, three inches of wood and an industrial spring. It hurt like hell.” “Yeah, sure. Then how is it that you take these great, painful falls, and don’t seem to get injured.” “We learn how to fall.” “Learn to fall?” “Yeah.” The driver pulls the car over. “Hey, this isn’t my hotel.” The driver turns his head. “I know.” Xander is a little perplexed as the passenger’s side door opens and a young woman with straight, shoulder length black hair and red-lensed sunglasses gets in. “Don’t worry Xander, nothing is going to happen. I want to talk to you about a business proposition. Driver, let’s go to this young man’s hotel.”
