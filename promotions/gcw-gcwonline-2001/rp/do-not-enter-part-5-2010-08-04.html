Do Not Enter Part 5

4 August 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/04/10 Marge Beamer sat in the waiting room of the hospital chewing her fingers nails and watching the TV screen. She did not pay attention to what was going on inside that little box, but she certainly did look like she was paying every bit of attention that she could. Her fake nails were chewed off, followed closely by her real nails. She has not chewed on her nails in years, but the fear and trauma going through her body has caused her to start this annoying habit. Dr. Landis came into the waiting room, stopped in shock for a second seeing Marge Beamer still here after all this time. He gave her a belittling look that she took to heart and turned her head away from his direction. Dr. Landis stood in front a man who stood up the second he saw the doctor. His wife was hit by a car and was unresponsive on her way into the clinic just after Dr. Landis pronounced Ellen dead. He was able to stop the bleeding and relieve the pressure from her brain and it looks like she will make a full recovery. The man jumped into Dr. Landis’ arms in glee. Landis smiled, played the role of the helpful doctor, yet his gaze and negative energy was aimed at Marge Beamer. Dr. Landis shook the man’s hand and walked away and made his way towards Marge Beamer. He is clueless as why to she’s still here. Ellen’s body has already been transported to the morgue, so what other reason does she have for staying here? Her eyes were still staring away from Dr. Landis. He knows she’s angry with him, and during the entire procedure with the hit and run victim Landis has been telling himself, actually convincing himself that there was nothing else he could have done to save Ellen, even though he knew in the back of his brain that he did all he could in covering up her death. “Mrs. Beamer.” Marge doesn’t move. Her fingers still in her mouth, her legs crossed, the top one bouncing violently. “Mrs. Beamer. I want you to know that I am very saddened by this. I wish I could help everyone that comes to see me, but that’s not God’s plan.” “GOD!” Marge sits up and screams. “You talk about God? Where was God at when this murderer butchered my daughter and her friends! What kind of God is that Dr. Landis?” The entire waiting room went silent. The TV was the only background heard. Everyone sat and stared at the doctor with this crazy lady who is now talking about a butchering. Dr. Landis puts his hands in front of him and slowly drops them trying to signal to Mrs. Beamer to calm down. “Mrs. Beamer there was no butchering, we haven’t even found the other kids with her. I told you from my expertise she was climbing something, most likely from a dare, fell and caused severe damage to herself” “BULLSHIT! You heard her she was screaming about this Bryan O’Neil guy!” When Dr. Landis heard that name his body shivered as the entire waiting room started talking. The man whom they all thought was dead wasn’t? “Mrs. Beamer. Please stop with that. I have a waiting room full of people. Please don’t cause a scene.” That put Marge Beamer over the edge. “Create a scene? How on earth are you so loved and adored? Bryan O’Neil just killed my daughter and you are going to lie to me? That makes you just as guilty.” The waiting room started to become nervous. The name Bryan O’Neil, or even the moniker The Lunatic started to escape from people’s mouths. Dr. Landis put his hand out to try and calm down Marge. “Mrs. Beamer will you please…” “Hey everyone! Guess what? The most notorious killer on earth is alive and well and living in Belmont again and this asshole right here, the same doctor you love and adore is hiding it from you!” Landis grabs Marge by her arm and yanks her down the hall as the waiting room begins to use their cell phones to call loved ones. The last thing Landis heard from the room was a guest saying, “Pack your bags now we have to leave!” There was a bit of silence than an explosion, “BECAUSE O’NEIL IS NOT DEAD!” Marge Beamer, obviously lost it hours ago, begins to yell down the hall at the guests. She’s telling them that hell is coming for them, and his name is Dr. Landis. As Landis drags her down the hall he tells a nurse at the station to call the authorities. Landis, still holding tightly to Marge fumbles around in his pockets for keys. He finds them, fingers through them and grabs the one of his choosing inserts it into the lock and tosses Marge Beamer inside the utility room. He flicks the light on and shuts the door behind him “Mrs. Beamer what the hell has happened to you?” “You’re kidding right? Bryan O’Neil killed my daughter and you ask a question like that?” “Mrs. Beamer, for the love of God, Bryan O’Neil is fucking dead! I saw it happen! I put my finger on the pulse! I proclaimed him dead! You have just nearly incited a riot out there now!” Marge begins to weep. Her eyes well up with tears as they explode down her cheeks. Landis’ heart begins to grow soft. This woman has obviously been through some hell and has not resort but this, whatever this is she’s doing. “Look Mrs. Beamer I will prescribe you something that will take the edge off. No one can ever get over the loss of their…their child.” Landis stammers that line out, obviously remembering what happened to his little girl so many years ago. “Please, get your husband and go home, get a hotel, something. Staying here will only make it worse.” “Ron’s gone.” Landis was about the exit the utility room when Marge said her husband was gone. Gone? Where the hell is he? His mind began to race with different locations. Was Ron an alcoholic? Was he drowning his sorrows over a bottle? “What do you mean Ron’s gone Mrs. Beamer?” Still sobbing, “He left right after we found out.” Rolling his eyes in annoyance. “Ok, got it he left, but where and why?” “He said you aren’t telling us something. The murder that nailed O’Neil was your daughters and you are hiding something about it. So he went to go investigate.” Landis’ head began to spin uncontrollably. “Investigate? There’s millions of books written about O’Neil and my daughter’s death. What else does he need to know?” Sighing, “Doctor. He left almost 4 hours ago for the house on the hill. There’s been no answer on his phone.” Landis dropped his keys. He quickly recovers them and turns back looking straight into the sunken eyes of Marge Beamer. “Wait. He went to THE house?” Marge simply nods. “4 hours ago you said?” Once again another nod. Landis never realized that he said what he thought but he responded with, “Oh God no, not again.” Marge’s expression is what made Landis realized he spoke his thoughts out loud. Her eyes grew wide, the tears fired up again, and her hands went to her face in an “OH MY GOD!” expression. Landis, shaking, attempts to unlock the door when he’s met by a flying Marge Beamer. She jumped at him so quickly and force that his left shoulder went numb upon contact with the door. She grabs him by the coat and pulls him closer to her. “You know something don’t you!” “I know nothing Mrs. Beamer. I need to you leave!” Landis was getting nervous. This crazed woman is almost like a rabid animal baring its teeth and sinking its claws into his flesh. Marge is grabbing at him like a maniac, his eyelid is scratched and his septum catches what’s left of one of her nails. Landis turns his back, now she’s scratching at clawing at his neck asking him to explain himself. In what seems like hours(only a few seconds actually) Landis grabs the door handle when he realizes he does not need a key from this direction and whips the door open. There’s a small crowd surrounding the door, some of them are hospital security. Landis rushes from the room looking at no one only screaming at the security guards to take her away. Marge begins her fight against the three guards, scratching and clawing at them. They attempt to hold her down but this woman is too strong or her adrenaline is that of a wolverine. She keeps screaming “Where’s my husband?” at the top of her lungs. Landis never once turns back at the commotion, nor the crowd that has circled Marge. One of the guards, obviously fed up with her insubordinate behavior pulls out a taser and zaps her. She screams in pain as 50,000 volts courses through her body. Her body flops to the ground and begins to jolt in a reaction to the amps flowing through her veins. The guard lets go of the trigger as Marge lies on the ground in the fetal position, mewling in pain. She looks at the guard, “All I want to know is what happened to my husband.” Bryan O’Neil pulls Ron Beamer by the meat hook. It entered Ron’s mouth and exited his left eye. There was not a lot of blood as one may think, just some pus oozing out from what use to be his eyeball. The other eye remains opened and expressionless. What little blood there is seems to be exiting through the hole in the mouth and his nose. Ron died instantly once the sharp end plunged deep into his brain than exited through the eye. The dead weight of a 210lb man is not as easy to haul around as one may think. Even though Bryan O’Neil stands over 6’10” and tipping the scales at over 300 lbs this task is not an easy one. He grunts and thrusts with all his might. He pulls so hard that the meat hook removes itself from the face of Ron Beamer pulling parts of skull and flesh with it. Bryan falls backwards and stares at the chunks of what was once a human on his meat hook and he laughs. His laugh starts to become maniacal. He pulls the chunk of flesh from the hook and tosses it aside. He looks at the makeshift oven inside the barn, than back at the corpse of Ron Beamer. He smiles at the task, stands up on pulls the dead carcass over his shoulder, ready to place Ron Beamer in his final resting place, the atmosphere of Belmont, England. Dr. Landis slams his office door and sits down and begins to massage his temples. This morning he woke up feeling great, now he sits here knowing he ended the life of a helpless teenager, she brings up Bryan O’Neil, and now he’s running from her mother because she feels HE knows something. Life is going to hell in a hand basket and in a hurry. The entire hospital is in an uproar because of Marge and now who knows what can happen to his credibility. Goddamn Marge Beamer and Goddamn Bryan O’Neil! Something must be done to make this disappear, but what? If this was a cartoon or a comic book a little light bulb would appear over Landis’ head. He starts to tear through his desk like a mad man. Books, papers, and notes are flying in every direction. He finds a few journals and starts to page through them only to toss them to the side as well. He keeps saying to himself, “Where are you, you little fuck.” He opens the smallest drawer of his desk where his mace and 9mm handgun is kept as well. He removes the mace and the gun, puts them on top of the desk reaches his hand into the back of the drawer and snatches a tiny piece of paper that has some writing on it. He looks at this paper and stares at it intently. His breathing becomes more rapid and his heart rate has surely increased. He never thought he’d ever have to use this number, but with the present situation being what it is, this is the only way he can ever be normal and ever be trusted again. He picks up the phone and dials. Aron Levy is lying on his couch watching TV. It’s a small house, older than any in the neighborhood. Only 1 bedroom, 1 bathroom, a kitchen and a living room. Aron never had kids, was never married, and never really believed in excess. If he ever had extra money he put it in a suitcase that is under his bed. He’s actually done well for himself even though the entire neighborhood stops by with pies and all sorts of gifts because they think he’s poor or because when he came home from the first gulf war there was not a single soul with a sign or anything waiting on him. He preferred being alone, but yet no one could ever grasp that. Aron was a gracious man however. He took every gift in and offered many to come inside for some coffee or perhaps a beer. He always seemed to call the neighbors bluf because once they took a peak inside this small place none were interested in steeping another step further. He use to think maybe it was just some kind of contest on who can be the nicest person to this lonely man. He took it all with a grain of salt, accepted the gifts, shook hands, gave hugs, than cursed their name when they left his front step. Aron was jolted by the sound of the ringing phone. He often wondered why he ever owned one since it rang maybe but once a year. He stood up, knocked over a few cans of beer, tipped over a full ashtray, cursed at the mess on the floor and walked towards the phone. Sitting all by itself, stained in smoke, was his phone. It stood on a table who’s leg was duct taped back on, along with another full ashtray. He picked it up and simply said, “Yep.” The voice on the other end was a familiar one. Aron’s disposition did not change at all. He stood there, cigarette in his mouth, can of beer in his hand and the phone nestled snugly between his cheek and his shoulder. It was a very quick one sided conversation that ended when Aron told the other person, “Yep. Be there in an hour.” When Aron hung up the phone he stretched, scratched his stomach and looked into the mirror attached to the closet door. He aged poorly, but nearly 2 years in the shit in Iraq will do that to you. His hair was long stringy and graying fast. He hasn’t shaved in a while so his face is rough. He stands there older and little more overweight than the last time he had to do this. He knew he had no choice, so he shrugs, downs the beer, throws the can to the ground and walks into his bedroom. He said last time was going to be the last, but one more time may make him feel young again. Back at the office Dr. Landis hangs up the phone and plops back deep into his chair. He lies his head back and stares at the ceiling counting the dots in the dropped ceiling in his office. He never thought he would ever have to make that call, but drastic times call for drastic measures. He wants his life back, he wants it all back, and this was the only way he knows how. Night is falling slowly upon Belmont. The sun is being succumbed by the clouds and the landscape as it gets ready to sleep on this hemisphere and awake on the opposite. Bryan O’Neil stands at the very crest of the hill looking down upon the city. Those that know he is here are now dead, those that still feel they are safe in this town will soon follow. With the wind blowing his black hair into his face, Bryan O’Neil stands tall looking down on the city, plotting his next move. Pulling the hair from his face he whispers to himself, “Soon. Very soon they will all know I am back.”
