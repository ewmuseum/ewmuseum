Hail Mary

19 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 19, 2005 The End Zone. Local sports bar and grill, more than likely. Currently, Styles is in the middle of passing out drinks to everyone at the table. Granted, the majority of people that are at the table are huge breasted women, but that’s beside the point, no? Varsity’s also here, though he’s keeping a much lower profile. He’s slumped down in his section of the booth, hat tilted down to cover one eye and idly sipping a Smirnoff Ice. Yes, he knows he has a match soon, but drinking something light won’t hinder those chances. Smirnoff Ice ain’t anything but chilled wannabe beer. “So, there I was... staring down the barrel of Big Jimmy Johnson’s fist, when out of nowhere this guy... this guy right here!” Styles, who has obviously had a few too many, points directly in Varsity’s direction. “... This guy right here comes out of nowhere and body checks that big lug into the lockers. BAM! I mean, you shoulda” seen it. I bet they’re still tryin? to get the dent out of that locker, right V?” Varsity smiles a little bit and nods, shrugging off the attention. Apparently, he’s not focused enough to be out here having fun. He’s not sad or anything, he’s just thinking about what’s going to be happening. Which, anybody in his position, should be thinking about. “Meh. It was nothing. I just don’t like bullies.” He looks at his watch. “And... I should probably get goin”. I need to get some rest.” Styles (and the girls) all let out one of those Studio Audience styled “awwwwwwwwwws” because they’re having so much fun. But, that’s because they’re also having so much to drink. Styles is the one to speak up, though, “You sure, man” You been nursin? that thing all night. Don’t wanna? hang for another?” Varsity smiles and starts pushing up from the table, lifting his hat and spinning it around on his head. “Nah. I’m good. But you...” Varsity doesn’t even have to say the rest, holding out his hand. In the next moment, Styles tosses the keys into that open palm. “Be careful. And take a cab back to the hotel, alright?” Varsity adds, before saying goodbye to everyone else. Styles nods and gives Varsity the classic handshake that they’ve been doing for years. Tipsy, but still serious, Styles looks Varsity in the eyes, “Hey. Relax. It’s in the bag.” Apparently, even while he’s too tipsy to drive, he knows just what in the heck is his client’s problem. Varsity smirks and nods. “Have fun.” With a wink, he spins on his heels and makes his way out of the bar. He steps right outside, pulling his jacket onto his shoulders more, keeping the chilly air away, and he makes his way to the Benz parked right in front of the doors. The one with the Enterprise sticker in the window, of course. He slides in, getting comfortable and tossing his hat into the passenger seat. A quick run of his hand through his hair and he’s pulling off momentarily. Making a right at the first light he comes to, Varsity reaches down and flips on the radio. Nothing good is on, as is apparent from the way he keeps twisting the dial back and forth. He sighs, shaking his head and makes a couple of turns as he cruises through the city. He passes by the hotel, not ready to go inside just yet and makes a left... cruising right on down the street. He finally comes to an old parking lot, that’s only slightly lit up. He pulls in, mostly because of the handful of kids that are in the lot playing a little late night touch football. He smiles as he parks the car, his lights beaming on them. All the boys stop, turning to stare at the car, wondering whether or not to run or what. He turns the car off, lights dimming and pockets the keys. The door flings open and he steps out. “Hey! You guys got room for one more?” The kids, whom have already started to run, screech to a halt and turn around. The one with the ball hurls it in Varsity’s direction and he catches it with ease. He twirls the ball around in his fingers, peering down at the pigskin that he loves so much. The first rule of football is to never be intimidated. No matter how good a team is... no matter what they’re record is... they’re no better than yours is. As the quarterback, it’s your job to lead your team to victory. And you can’t do that if you walk out onto that field intimidated. You have to walk out onto that field with your head held high, your feet on the ground, and your heart in the right place. There’s gonna? be times when you’re sacked. There’s gonna? be fumbles. You might even through a couple interceptions. But the game isn’t what matters. A lot of people believe it’s the way you play that decides your victory or defeat. It’s not. It’s you... as a person. As a quarterback, you should know from the first play of the game whether or not you’re going to win. If you don’t, then you need to pick another position. This is a game. That’s all it is. But it’s important. It’s important to you, it’s important to your school. It’s important to your family. And it’s important to me, your coach. You have to understand that just because it’s important to us, that doesn’t mean you can’t screw up. Of course, we prefer that you didn’t, but if you do... suck it up. Fix it. Move on. Varsity smirks a little bit, pulling the ball up and placing his fingers over the laces. His fingers flex a little bit while he’s getting a better grip on the ball. This is it. It’s tied up, 14 - 14. The clock’s done. The timeouts? Gone. You got time for one more play. One more play and we win this. We win this? We go to the championship. This team hasn’t been to the show in 10 years. I think it’s about time we changed that, don’t you think? I’ve got plays. I’ve got playbooks. I’ve got ideas. But, even though I’m the coach, this is not my team. Alex, this is your team. It’s up to you to give them this victory. It’s up to you... Varsity nods to himself, before looking up from the ball and noticing that the kids are all staring at him. He shakes himself out of his flashback and starts taking some steps back. He singles out one particularly small little boy and points at him. “Hey kid....” The kid blinks, reaching up to fix his glasses and mouths a silent, “Me?” “Go long.”
