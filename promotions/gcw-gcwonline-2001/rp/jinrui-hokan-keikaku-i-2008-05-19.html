Ima wa tada sore wo negai tsuzukeru Anata wa ima doko denai moshite imasuka? kono sora no tsuzuku basho ni imasuka? - “You” =========== The cars had long since left the parking lot, for the most part. WorldWide 90 was over, the wrestlers were in the back showering, and everything was still. Everything save for the rather odd presence of a little girl, flitting about between the few remaining parked cars. She wore a yellow dress that clung to her pixieish frame, brown hair down to her shoulder-blades. She couldn’t have been more than ten years old, though the expression on her face appeared to carry the weight of the world. It wasn’t long before arena security spotted her, of course. The uniformed security officers politely asked her if she knew where her family was. Her response was a simple shake of her head. “I have no family,” she replied, expression still heavy with something resembling regret. One officer offered her a hand. Again, she shook her head. “I cannot leave until I have spoken with him.” One officer looked at the other officer, by now more than a tad creeped out by this particular series of events. “Who?” the second man asked, the intensity of the ten year old’s gaze unnerving him considerably. “The one who is Fated,” she replied, as if it is the most obvious thing in the world. “He wears long hair, usually in garish colors, though he may have since changed this. He..” Struggling at the explanation, she forges ahead. “He is the one who carries the sword?” “Oh.” Both officers say the word simultaneously. From that, of course, it is rather obvious. “She must mean Almasy,” one says to the other. Both officers look the girl over again. She has no pen and paper that would indicate her as a particularly ingenious autograph seeker. Indeed, she has no outwardly perceptible motive at all. “You want to go get him,” the other officer asks the first. When the first man reacts with a surprised expression, the officer is quick to explain. “This is some freaky shit. We get Almasy to come out here and talk to her, and it’s HIS freaky shit, not ours anymore. “That’is a good point. I’ll go get him. You stay here with the freaky girl.” The officer walked off, headed back up the arena steps in search of the self-professed Standard Bearer of GCW, leaving the girl in the care of his colleague. Taking a few pacing steps around the parking lot, he let out a deep sigh, shaking his head back and forth. “What a life this is,” he mused. “Security guard. I’m a college graduate, and this is the best I can do?” His eyes opened wide as the young girl stepped forward, placing a comforting hand on his kneecap. “Don’t worry, mister,” he heard, neck craning downwards to look at her. “You’ll be opening your own business to sell wrestling memorabilia in a few years. You’ll make it.” ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ What used to take him thirty minutes took half that nowadays. Once upon a time, Seymour Almasy’s long locks had to be subjected to multiple shampooing and washings in order to get it clean after a hard fought contest, but with his hair now shorter, things were far easier. And yet, he remained in the shower, letting the hot spray beat down on his neck and back, as if it could wash away the feelings of failure that he felt clinging to his skin. It bothered him to his very core that he had been, essentially, right about everything. LeStatt Knight had been there at the end, just as he knew Knight would be. He had cost Brad Jackson, for all intents and purposes, the United States Championship. Almasy had a contingency plan for Knight. He’d had the help of the Movement to take care of Andy Murray, put the Scot off his game. And it had all gone to Hell due to Brad Jackson, the x-factor, the one thing his plans hadn’t truly accounted for. Jackson had beaten him ? after two matches ? but beaten him nonetheless. And that meant no shot at the United States title. Which, in turn, meant that WorldWide had been a resounding failure. The sound of his own breathing was all he could hear amidst the pounding spray. No words were necessary. The fact of the matter was he talked a good game, a better game than he ever had. But the end result was the same. Loss against Digital Mortality. A loss here. Turning off the spray, Seymour reached for a towel, wrapping it firmly around his waist. His good mood from earlier was shot, replaced with a melancholy that he could not break. So confident he had been in victory, even when he was announced first. Even in the worst case scenario he had thought victory assured. But then, as it had so many times over the past two years, life threw a monkey wrench into his plans. At times, it seemed like there was a never ending supply of them. His thoughts were broken momentarily by the sound of knocking on his locker room door. “Who is it,” he called, voice annoyed as he stepped out of the shower to look for something to wear. “Arena security,” came the reply. “Someone’s here to see you.” “It’s not an autograph seeker or a rat, is it,” Almasy retorted. “If it’s a rat, tell her I’m not interested tonight. Clyde Fox could probably stand to get laid though, if she’d like. Don’t think he’d turn ANYONE down.” “None of the above,” the guard responded. “You want to have this conversation. Believe me.” Whether that was true or not, of course, didn’t matter to the guard, who wanted a problem out of his hair. Finally, Seymour gave in, with a call of “I’ll be out in a minute!” Lovely. Something else to deal with that he didn’t want to take onto his plate. Reaching into his duffel bag, he haphazardly pulled out a shirt, boxers, and pants. Almasy dressed hurriedly, though not exactly eager to meet his new mystery person. No, he just wanted to get the fuck out of the arena, deal with this, and go back to his hotel room for a good night’s sleep. Stuffing his feet into a pair of sandals, Seymour zipped the bag, and slung it over his shoulder. Five strides had him to the door, and a motion of his wrist opened it to reveal the security guard. “Let’s go, then,” were the only words spoken, the officer leading Almasy through the backstage corridors to a side exit. Seymour’s eyes were bleary, tired from a long night in the ring, and long nights of no sleep, pondering his current standing in the world. He remained silently even as they stepped out into cooler night-time air, walking around to the front of the arena. Almasy’s gait was slow, lagging behind the other man as they made their way back to the other officer and the mysterious girl. “Who the Hell is that,” Almasy whispered, under his breath, as he looked at the mismatched pair, officer and child, standing together. “We figured you’d know,” the other officer replied, shrugging his shoulders. “Listen, she wants to talk to you. We’re headed inside to go deal with some cleanup. You need us, just holler.” They were gone soon after, disappeared into the arena with nervous expressions on their faces, leaving Almasy alone with the young girl. “Hello,” she said, quietly, voice barely above a whisper “You are the one who wields the sword?” “Used to,” came Seymour’s quick reply. “I haven’t picked that thing up in at least a year now. Time flies..” “You wielded it four years ago. That is what matters.” Almasy cocked an eyebrow. He’d known that SOMETHING was odd from first laying his eyes on the girl. “Yes, I did,” Seymour replied. “The first Divine Blade Imperial. Back when I?” Though he couldn? t bring himself to say it, the memories flew back into his head unbidden. Of angering PETA via his attempts at grinding bunny rabbits for experience points. Of Aerith. Of being the UWF’s good guy of good guys, a state of being so far from his current position in life that it made his head spin. “Four years ago, Fated one, you were sealed into a reality that you cannot comprehend. A horror beyond your imagining.” The words coming out of her mouth were utterly incongruous with her age and appearance. Still, the more he looked at her’the more he realized that something was wrong. She looked familiar somehow, even as young as she was. He knew no children well’and yet the swath of red hair she wore screamed familiarity at him. “You use big words for someone your age, little girl,” Seymour replied. “But you’ve got my interest, and I’ll bit. Go ahead, Explain to me what my fate is.” “Your fate is simple,” she said, her face masked emotion, joy/anger/sadness radiating off of her features. In six months, you will be dead.” The statement was audacious, enough so that Seymour didn’t even take it seriously. “Yeah, right, and then I?” “You will take your own life with your sword,” the girl states, forging ahead, no emotion in her voice. “You will be found in your hotel room by GCW staff. And then, it will all begin again.” Seymour’s attention was now fully on her. What was being described was giving him chills ? not the least of the reasons why being that this entire scenario sounded damningly familiar. Alarmingly familiar. “My life will begin again,” Seymour whispered, throat dry. “And proceed in the same way, so that I am forced to relive my every sorrow, my every heartbreak, my every mistake?” “Exactly,” the girl replied, coolly. “You pick up quite quickly.” “Yes,” he agreed, a disgusted expression on his face as he gazed down at the innocent looking girl. “I know who you are now. I almost missed it.” “I’m glad you still recognize me, Seymour,” the girl said, her pretenses dropped, the expression on her face giving way to a faint grin. “Even a lifetime later. I owe you everything, I suppose.” Before him stood what once was perhaps his second great rival in the sport. A woman who represented his hated opposition, the Dominant Wrestling Organization. A woman who battled him tooth and nail in a match that ended up in Seymour winning his first of three PTC Global Championships. “Kayna Trielle,: Seymour murmured, equal parts anger and fear in his voice. “Roulette.” “That’s me!” was her reply, sounding more bubbly and a lot more happy than she had for most of the talk before her voice became somber once more. “I am sorry, though. I really am. That I had to use you..” “Use me,” he asked, the words half out of his mouth as he realized the horrifying truth of what had happened to him. For it was Kayna Trielle that was doomed to repeat her life until the end of time, to watch her parents die over and over again with no choice but to take it ? in theory, anyway. Seymour closed his eyes, body trembling as he took in the improbable facts of what had to be his current situation. Roulette’s wheel had stopped. His own had just begun. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ His eyes shot open as he sat up in a pitch black room, sweating profusely. Cold, clammy skin clung to the sheets of the bed as he dragged them with him, desperately trying to find a lamp to get comforting light into the room. Fingers fumbled about in the darkness until finally’success. With the light on in what he now knew to be his hotel room, Seymour Almasy could take stock of his situation. It was a dream, thank God ? another dream in a seemingly endless series of them that was making it nigh impossible for Seymour to catch any good sleep. “What’s happening to me,” he asked, weakly, the clock on the nightstand reading a clear 4:04 AM. “Can’t get any sleep, can’t relax..mocking Tessa isn’t even as fun as it usually is. Something must be wrong with me..” With his chances at further sleep destroyed for the evening, Almasy flopped gracelessly back to his bed, reaching for the remote control to feed himself a few hours of mindless entertainment before the sun came up and he could try to start having a normal day. Brad Jackson was going to be dealt with later that very day, Seymour resolved. His pride would not withstand two straight losses to ANYONE, let alone an antisocial loner who Almasy considered a candidate for the 2008 Unabomber Award. Even awake, though’he couldn’t quite understand WHY he was having these dreams. This one was more extreme than the others had been by far. Usually they were anecdotes from his life, his career, or both. All were from years ago. With a final shudder at the fate of one Kayna Trielle, Almasy settled back to watch an infomercial, eyes half-closed and half-opened as Ron Popeil began to shill, confident that no such ridiculousness existed in the real world. ~~~~~~~~~ “There are more things in heaven and earth, Horatio, Than are dreamt of in your philosophy.” - Hamlet, Act I, scene v
