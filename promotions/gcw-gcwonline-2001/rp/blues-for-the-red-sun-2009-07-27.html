Blues for the Red Sun

27 July 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/27/09 He was taking a shower when his phone rang. By the beat's first repeat, he was drying his hands on towel slung over the curtain. He had it answered between the third and the fourth, towel draped over his head to muffle the sound, to block the water. "Mr Black has a room reserved for you at the Atlantis Motel in. In Atlantic Beach." "Huh?" "He would be very happy were you able to make the reservation within the next, say. Five hours?" "I can. Shit. I could manage that, but. There any reason for this or, just. What?" "He believes that it would be in your best interest to meet someone and has made arrangements as such." "What if" "If youre incapable of making it, he has also arranged transportation." "Motherfuckin Sage Black." "So. Will you be there?" "Huh. Yeah. Fuck it. I can do that. Kinda curious, actually." It was settled, like many things in Frank's life, with a decision that’in spite of preexisting mounds of evidence that cast long shadows across his world, in spite of hours spent predicating or predicting courses of action, in spite of a ponderous kink he couldn't quite work out of his head’came instantaneous and unexpected to even the mouth that formed the words. A tinge of willful ignorance, of turning a blind eye to the inner workings of his own skull, that he kept around just for situations like that, for questions provided but not explained, for moments carrying nothing but a base descriptor. Maybe optimism kept it alight. He believed in fun, he hoped for fun; it was as close as he came to religion, in most regards. Besides: it was leave and deal with unknowns, or stay and deal with the knowns he dreaded. He left his Charlotte home with loaded duffel bag lodged behind his seat, traveled southeast at high velocities on weaves through highway traffic at an uncomfortable level of sober, obeying the directions of his demure GPS’which he had yet to cause any harm to or to foster any sort of ill will from. He trusted it the same as he trusted pens to write, brushes to paint, windows to roll. A mechanical honesty. A lack of personal faults; all failures resulting from a definite user or a definite producer or a long run of either. He didn't expect nihilistic kicks from his toaster and he didn't believe his refrigerator could succumb to a deep malaise that would leave its bulb forever dimmed. He just had the faith required to hurtle his twoton machine coat between others that wrapped in jackets or sweaters or blouses or speedos; he just did what came natural with the same kind of smile he'd have worn if he'd come upon it entirely naturally. He drove against the day, he flung himself at the sun for as long as it could guide him, until it passed him over and left him running on subinstinct. Like road maps weren't just hieroglyphics of the scars the species had dug deep into the planet, that they also contained some arcane knowledge about the reason for paths becoming streets, into highways and interstates with clover ramps above spiderwebbed cities; that they would share with him an unspoken knowledge of those magnetic truths that had controlled our expansion as poles to migratory birds. How couldn't he compare himself?: he took aloft on routes his families had tread for generations, fell into it like it was a genetically attained inclination towards returning’only he had replaced an ancestral home, an elephant graveyard, with the umbrella of safety of being a lackey, of having a safety net and a world that paved itself several steps ahead of him. He hadn't kept up with how long it had been since he heard from Sage in any satisfying way, how many checks had come with a signature as their only correspondence; it didn't keep him from having some estimated value of the damages, both caused and attained, in that time directionless. Taking orders had the potential to be less damning, at any rate, than just fucking up on his own wisdom. And maybe that's how he measured time itself; not as the linear progression of clock hands and a slow rate of decay, but the mountains he accumulated of achievements and demerits alike; as the sheer volume of activity incurred, rather than the length of the span that housed it. That said, he had no idea how many days had passed since anything, really. He focused on work like he focused on driving’when he wasn't lulled into a trance, he kept his eyes open for any sort of mischief to keep himself from falling back into hypnosis. An ends to a means, any accomplishments worth only the momentary pride of whatever victory had been found. So he drove. He drove a low, swooping curve across his state, rebounded up along miles of coastline; he sometimes even caught a glint of sun reflected off an ocean that stabbed to reclaim long stretches inland. His head was elsewhere the rest of the time, lost in a darkening sky and tinted memories of a once that never really existed. Stabbing on. Cutting through traffic as ocean became inlet, locked in by shrinking outer banks, by cities built into a sand that never implied permanence. All he had to do was maintain harmony, follow directions like: take this right onto the bridge longer than your destination is wide. His machine hadn't told him; he remembered that much as he coasted silver some fifty, some hundred feet above the water way, piers and docks lining it like hash marks and dotted lines; like when this place is gone, we will take our outline and we will relocate to somewhere similar enough. He crested the bridge, fell face first into a city that may not have been painted in the last ten years, or fifteen, or. He quit thinking backwards; he let the island wash over him with its grays and its turquoises; he drove between knotted patches of forests with trees beaten twisted and short by the relentless ocean wind and he coasted under the shadows cast by its hotels and houses and rentals like rows of headstones. If he continued the analogy’on the long trek to someone you'd rather not put flowers on, for any reason. He kept his foot up, he fell into a creep to avoid roaring violent through narrow streets at the highway speeds that had nurtured him in those hours; his little box told him were to go and he didn't question. If he started doubting it? Lost. No doubt; religious adherence that paid off as well as any faith he'd observed before. The sign, the entrance, up to and including the parking lot, nestled into those gnarled oceanfront forests as though all parties involved were just waiting for the day of reclamation. Parked beside a gray tree, he put the location to memory even if he'd have always be able to spot his wheeled engine in a parking lot before he could pick any specific plant out of a lineup. "Maritime forests are what they're called." He snapped head over shoulder to a woman relaxing by the pool with the sun beaten past her skin, into her muscles; he sensed into her soul. She wore a haze of sweat with the same grace she managed to wedge into her khaki capris, her yellow tank top. That is to say, to Frank at least, something was out of place. "They're dying, though. People are still developing and these are going to be wiped out to the last. I want to say they're oaks, but. Botany's beyond me. Beautiful, either way." She paused for a second, broke her perfect composure to suck her lips into her teeth. "I think they used to call them black jacks around here. It's been so long. Wow. It's a beautiful place you've got." "Looks kinda desolate to me." "I could see that. I don't agree, but I can understand where you're coming from. Teddy Roosevelt used to vacation here. Not, not this exact spot, but he did. Past the aquarium, theres a pretty big memorial forest. It wont save nature and it won't even save here, but. It's something." He left his bag in the backseat and his phone on the floor, he made his descent across the parking lot. "This is Pine Knoll Shores, right?" "If you're gonna be proper about things, sure. I didn't take you for the type. Nice to meet you, Frank. I'm Thirteen." He planted himself on the railing that locked the pool away; she looked to him through large dark glasses. He felt like a zoo. "Sages. Mentioned you before. So youre why I came here, huh?" "I'm flattered." "Hah, I mean. Sage," she nodded, he stopped. "I dont know what he wanted. Like you were gonna make me a champion or some shit." "Do I look like a wrestler to you? No, boy. I'm gonna make you a better person’come on over here." She sat up in her lounge chair, slid her feet into thongs and began backing down the length of the pool. Beckoning. He locked his car remotely and stepped inside the gate, keeping her pace on the inner side of the enclosure, chlorine dividing them as they fell deeper into the overhang of trees strong enough to never break (because they were weak enough to always bend), down past a manmade waterfall that kept the overflow constantly lapping over into a wading pool secluded from the world around, if not the world entirely; canopied by those dread trees, locked away and kept from flight. She sat on plastic patio furniture; he did the same. "Thirteens a. Memorable name." "More like a title than a name, really. You met my brother once or twice." "Wait. That guy at that party?" "Hah. You're precise. But yeah, then. Other times, too. He's. He's good at going unseen." "Shit. Sage said he wasnt even real." "As far as Sage is concerned, no one's real." "That would explain a hell of a lot." "It's what I'm here for. If you don't understand the world, or. I should say, if you don't define the world one way or another, you cant find your place in it. You don't have to be right, you just have to be able to make enough sense of it to paint a picture, to know where you stand in it." Frank nodded, her hand slipped into a purse that was there before them; or, at least, a purse that she hadn't carried while he'd watched. "You hungry?" It was hard to break routines. It was hard to stare her down without wanting to lick lips, without wanting to misunderstand everything in a wave of awe; he sucked his pride down, knew that he'd just be another in a long line that tried and failed. He sat forwards, forearms on knees. "Kinda. What you got?" Little white squares, fanned like a poker hand. She grinned. It was infectious. "My brother, Fourteen, made a bet with Sage, or; more of a deal, I guess you'd say. Before you were Jordan. It was vague enough that Fourteen, being the idiot he is, decided to pretend he was really a devil and take it to the letter: if Jordan White, any Jordan White, wins the world title, Sage owes my little hipster brother a secret." "Yeah, Sages mentioned that too." He scratched at the back of his neck, found the direct center between a smirk and remorse and held it on his face with little regard. "Couple times." "Good. What you probably haven't heard, is that your boss has already told him the secret he asked about, and and Fourteen just doesn't believe him. So, now. As time passes, as Fourteen keeps getting further from mature, he gets more irritating, more incessant about it. He bothers Sage whenever it crosses his mind, basically, and the only way any of us can think of to get him to just go back home for awhile is, well. To put one of those belts on a Jordan White." "Okay. Whys your brother care?" "You ever heard that old song about the Devil going to Georgia?" "Yeah." "My brothers a petty little boy. He likes capitalizing on people's pride. Your boss is a. Duplicitous person. He never planned on fulfilling his end of the deal. Fourteen figured that out, and telling him, outright, was how Sage fought back. He thought it would make Fourteen go away or something. He obviously didn't. I, I don't want to see this explode, so I offered to help Sage. These," she slips the squares against one another, "are what I call helping." "Then, shit. Word up." "You are eager." "I." He dragged his heel across concrete. "Yeah." "It's a good thing, being ready for change. Better than me having to force it on you." "Think you got it in you?" "Boy." She. She sneered. Sneered pure, with the kind of disdain, disgust Frank had never been able to summon so casually. He swooned, in his 46 chromosomed way. "I could take you down to component pieces without breaking a sweat. The things I choose not to do. Mm." Her hand outstretched, her palm wide. Squares in a square. "Take two." He did. Surveyed them like he had any real manner of estimating their worth, their validity. "What is it, though?" "You're a smart guy. Figure it out." "Thats not a answer." "I know." Ruby lips against island skin, pursed into a smile. Frank couldn't argue. Sat them on his tongue. She mimed chewing; he devoured. Consumed. Leaned back into his chair while she stood, pressed her back into thin railing. "You can sit down anywhere, you know. How often do you get to take a walk through an endangered forest?" "Doesnt look like much room for walkin. Kinda. Snug." "Come on. You'd be amazed." He blinked, he reached for his pack; she was over the wrought iron, lifting gold through the shadows. "It's great down here." And she was gone, her smile left hanging out from the dismal bleak briefly, a token, a bread crumb to draw Frank along. He considered. He considered a cigarette. He considered following. He considered winning gold. He considered offering apologies. He considered a world of options, options less immediate, options more pressing, more important to some imagined facet of his soul. He smoked. He ashed. He stood. The world followed. He crossed the rail, tucked chest tight to it as branches clawed his back. The world remained behind; the maw took Frank whole, it divided him away and it isolated him in shades of ash that slipped seamless with shadows, that emerged from shade only to knot reverse into another lock of branches and recede into obscurity again. Like celtic knots, centuries whipped, welcoming him deeper into the tight density of their hidden world. Her voice, a laugh, rode the thickets, crunched dead leaves as it weaved to him. He tried to follow its labyrinth twists and only fell deeper. Supported himself against their bark until his consciousness slipped from his head into the palms of his hands, the palms rough, the palms red. He wondered why he hadn't tried it before. He wondered why he hadn't existed in his legs when he'd moved, why he hadn't been absorbed entirely into every swinging fist, awareness running down the twitch of muscles counter the waves of input riding his nervous system; why he hadn't just extended himself fully into every minute gesture. Why he'd been so compartmentalized, so segregated from himself. "Frank." He turned to her; his vision caught up in staggered streaks that swirled her the shade of stars' surfaces, that repainted her anew with her every slight shift, her every breath. God. He felt so stupid before, so clumsy. "Heyyy." "How you holding up?" "Hah. Im good. Ive never been this good. Neverrr." "You still haven't caught me." "I wasupposed to catchyou?" "Mmhmm." She nodded, face streaming into less than blurs, into fragmented representations of blurs. The breeze washed through him. "No one tol me." "There's a lot of things in life like that." "Huh. Cant I jus. Siddown?" "You could. But, but what if, the distance you lose sitting down is the distance you lose by? It's a serious risk." She sank backwards, drifted on the winds that passed him by. He followed. Staggered slow. Muscles slipping into a murk, a mechanical delay. Some error in transit. A neural bottlenecking, maybe. "Okay. Ill. Follow." "Follow long enough and you might learn to lead. Yourself, at least. What were you like as a kid?" "Wanted,(to) be Superman." "And why'd you give that up?" "Hes an alien. And not real. Anyways. Shit. What were those things?" "Exactly what you think. Now, this might be personal," under wraith awning she crept, under a sun that always found her, even if it would find her alone, "but why would you give up on a dream just because it was impossible?" He had to stop. Reality moved at a different pace than him, time catching drag on some substance less than immaterial, some feature of nature that only affected what he was increasingly beginning to consider an irrelevant and misguided human creation. He had to stop. She accommodated. "Having a goal's always better than apathy, you know. Even if it's only an inspiration to you, it's still something to aspire to. People definitely have actually gone after crazier things than that and, worse things than that. Really. I just kind of hate to think about people giving up on everything they treasured as children. Nihilism, disconnection?, those are more Fourteen's kick than mine. I like to think we're obligated to who we were, really. They got us to where we are. We owe it to them, heh, we owe it to ourselves to live out their dreams. We were all so much more pure back then. Maybe better." "Yourrr voice. Is perfect." "Come on." "No, I mean. I mean. It, (has)nt changed. Wow." "The only thing that's changed is your head. Feels good though, doesn't it?" "Unh. Felt better." "You're coming up. Just let it ride’if you fight it, it's going to go badly. That's, thats just how it goesss" His focus shifted, as it went; something broke through the ground behind her without breaking anything. A ripple in existence. Reality shaking to it, like tall grass to snakes. It turned towards him, cast empty eyes at some undefined point beyond. He had already stopped; all he had left to do was freeze. She talked; it slinked feline and his awareness of such rose to the surface of his being, became the single defining trait of his existence. Sometimes vertebrae, sometimes ribs. Sometimes a sheet of cloth. Sometimes a mustache. Sometimes the stripped down, the barest of, of. Of some thing's essence bared and raped. He felt like a voyeur. He felt like it agreed and wanted penance; it paid him no mind, didn't even show the concern as to regard him poorly. It moved, past, through. His view followed. And, and dominant, she came into his soul again: "oh. I'm guessing you saw him." "Him. What. I, I." "Not everyone can. Or not everyone does; I've never really been sure about the limits of human potential. Every time I thought I had it figured out, one of you was ready to surprise me." "What. What w(as th)at? Am I. (thought lost to a rising undertow) Haunted? Ghost?" "Hah. No, they're not ghosts." "You know?" "I know a lot of stuff. You'd do well to listen." He tried to point at his ear, hoped to maintain some basic level of communication. "Okay. Now, look at yourself. Look at your arms." Arms. Melting. Repeating tracers fading into one another. No. Not fading. All one. Streams flow undivided. "The important thing to remember is, that's you. That's what you look like. You're not just some singular entity at some singular point in time because there aren't singular points in time. There's existence, and the rate of perception doesn't change what's real. If you opened your eyes, if you just looked backwards? You could follow yourself, you could go against the current and revisit everything. Like. Like a infinitely extending snake redoubling on itself to see its pattern." He looked backwards. A progression of Frank. Away from the trees; back to Charlotte, Detroit, Phoenix, Charlotte; beyond Caroline, beyond Leslie and Zoe and Erika and Mike and John and David and Leslie again, repeating like concentric rings; eventually following the line past sweeps and clusters to the beach again. Smiling. Child. He snapped away. He snapped to her. And she was everywhere. Blinding. He closed his eyes for all the good it would do. Something waited in the dark. Heart rate firing. Teeth gritting. Sweating. Staring. Staring. She was kneeling in front of him when she peeled his eyes open, "calmcalmcalmcalm,calm,calm, calm, calm. Calm. Calm." Her nose a pivot, her face twitching breakneck metronome as the world beyond turned to static, fell chaotic into dissonance. Calm? Calm? He didn't know calm. He was on the ground. He clawed at dirt, at leaves. The buzzing of wings in all directions, spatial and chronological. He didn't scream. Not an honorable thing, not a proud thing. Some piece of the connection was past frayed, was simply gone; it broke transmission, ended all control as good as miles premature. She held his face in her hands, whispered incoherency with some prehuman import, grandeur. The buzzing of engines. Gibberish as incantations. He saw the sky where he should've seen her forehead, an abscess more than a projection. He saw the claws descending, filtered through trees that would have never allowed passage to the eyes he missed, the eyes that never took in more than his skull could hold, could process. Peace at a phonemic level. Dreams never prepared him. He'd been a witness; not a bystander to an event that would leave only a fine powder of fatalities claimed. Wide eyed. Gasping. Darkness clustering. He found his feet, rose above them with a force entire existences below words, dug his back against a tree to find balance, turned to its lashing form and tried a sneer, tried to run. Aimed forwards. Foot, foot. Like being underwater’becoming aware that the emptiness of space was a substantial thing in and of itself. The weight of concepts. The staggering and staggered beating of everything breaking against him; of opportunities crashing and dissipating into the rest, of things that would never come again the same as they'd passed before. Similar, maybe. But. Every choice made equaling out to choices permanently lost; some stupid twist of his soul equaling out every choice made to the worst one. "I wouldn't go that way." He understood, he didn't care. He trampled what would fall, rebounded off of what wouldn't in his run for some dream of daylight. The buzzing turned roars broadcast from his spine outwards, echoed inside his skull and his feet alike in case he tried to find refuge in a distant limb. It didn't matter if only he heard them, didn't matter that only he heard them: he couldn't save the world, he could've only hoped for escape from something that had given him ample warning. Unheeded eviction notices stapled deep into his brain. He hit the sand, dug his feet through scant undergrowth; watched the barely things walk in masses, light shifting faint against their occasional contours, water draping them with ceremony. Their sounds accumulated into a rolling bag of clicks, of sinew tearing. He clenched his eyes, back from their intangible herds. The thing in his head snapped at him; he returned to the beach gaping, staring. Sunblind, save long shadows that turned his world into black bars and white bars. Tried to find balance in relation to nothing; balance without a sense of self, a sense of distance, a sense of direction. He stumbled. Fell to his knees in the dim shade of trees, acknowledging neither the protection of the product nor the harshness of the producer. Face to the sky. Like: come on, you motherfuckers. Like: not defiant. Like: defeated. "You only have to lose if it's what you really want to do." She was behind him. He was breathless. She'd never strained. Knelt next to him. "I think you've got more options than that, though." Face to the sky. "You know what they are?" Sunblind was white. She was gold at his side, arm outstretched to shuffling beasts he struggled to remember. "They're all just choices, Frank, afterimages wandering around to see where they went wrong. They're just existence reacting to a void. Choices, change? If you talk to the right person, those are the only truths. The best thing you can do, to be totally honest, as a person? The best thing you can do is dedicate to one thing, one silly thing, without question. I hate seeing people just keel over because of apathy." He turned to her, tanned his face in proximity to her solar surface, sheltered himself from the coming storm. He hadn't noticed the details before, the little things: the way the edges of the blades went orange as they tore through the blue above, the exact spins of the clouds shattering and dispersing in whirls as the machines dragged along on some unseen current, the scattering of animals at ground level, the thrashing of the airborne ones caught in the great drag, the lashing of the rising tides. "There's just something about watching people defeat themselves. It gets to me. Like some of you just don't realize how strong you are. I mean, really. There are higher goals in life than complacency and acceptance." Great engines, if engines there were, stalled. Hung over the horizon, a dead and dirty onyx. Inverted mountains of cold space itself. Scythed mesas made entirely from the black in between. Cirrus spinning around their bases, fading them out. Those of the ghastly congregation that momentarily had necks craned them. "And sure, you can't win all the time, but isn't it better to be outgunned than to just shoot yourself before it even starts? See’you know what I'm talking about, you can focus and that's all you need this far into the game. You're already past the hard part. I think we might be able to get this together, Frank. Come on, get up."
