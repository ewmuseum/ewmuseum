Imprisoned in Paradise: Parasitic

31 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Aug. 31 No one, Seymour Almasy knew, could live forever. But at times, standing before a capacity crowd, cheering for him and chanting his name, that fact became somewhat blurry. Even blurrier, he thought somewhat sleepily, when it was three in the morning. “What a party,” he said, aloud, closing the door to the suite Steven Caldera had bought and paid for behind him. Taking a quick look at himself in a mirror, he noticed that his suit was rumpled, his hair was a mess, and he was clearly ever-so-slightly inebriated. What a party, indeed. Almasy stepped forward into the bedroom, looking around and gasping at the opulence. Clearly, no expense had been spared for him by GCW’s leader. It wasn’t the sort of thing he was used to, in spite of being perhaps Primetime Central’s most visible competitor. Let’s face it, it’s not like the Universal Wrestling Federation has much money to give. Half of its roster gets paid in pizza and cab fare. But the shrewd businessman Caldera seemed to have money to burn. He’d wined and dined the Final Fantasy for weeks. Even offered to take him to the Playboy Mansion before Seymour had politely declined. And in the end, it had paid off for Caldera. Almasy came to terms. Rather lucrative terms, at that. Still though, Seymour didn’t know why he’d accepted the offer. Staggering into the bedroom of the suite, Seymour could only shake his head at the bottle of champagne that had been placed on a nearby table. He read the tag attached, smiling just a little. “To the start of a wonderful partnership-- Steven Caldera. How sweet?” Despite his dubiousness, he found himself popping the cork. Really, tonight WAS something to be proud of. The GCW fans and locker room had seemed to embrace him. Sure, Rich Rollins hadn’t, but that was to be expected, given the circumstances. He chuckled. For once, he’d actually fooled the wrestling world. Probably because of his total lack of association with GCW before yesterday evening. When he’d gone to GW, the reporters had been right there. Same when he came back to the UWF. And, much to Seymour’s amusement, the same when he’d just plain disappeared. “When are you coming back, Seymour,” he asked, aloud, in a slightly mocking tone of voice. “Will you be competing in PTC still, Seymour?” God, how he hated reporters. Seymour Almasy loved his fans. Even when they came up to him at awkward times, like while he was getting a bite to eat, or even in the restroom, he could always make time for those who supported him. Reporters were another story. Bitterly, he poured himself a glass of champagne. Whenever he thought about reporters, it gave him the impulse to drink. Heavily. Over a wrestling career spanning years, Seymour Almasy had gained the world from professional wrestling. And thanks to the reporters, the dirt sheets, and the like, he had lost most of it. Seymour took a quick, stiff chug of the alcohol, swallowing hard. Sure, he still had money. He still had fame. But he had lost what really mattered the most. He had to fight back tears as he dug into his pocket, pulling out his wallet. Opening it quickly, Seymour let his eyes fall on the picture that he looked at so often. His wedding picture. The Seymour Almasy of a few years ago stared back at him, with his arm wrapped around a beautiful young woman. Her hair was done up in curls, a beautiful pink dress clinging to her form. The couple looked relaxed, elegant, and deliriously happy. “I’m sorry, Laura,” he whispered, mournfully. “I’m sorry I let them ruin everything.” Almasy put the glass down. God, every time he thought about it, it made his blood boil. How good everything had been a few years ago. How he had won Primetime Central’s Primetime Invitational Tournament and married Laura, all in the same weekend. How he became UWF World Champion. And then, it had all been pissed away, and it wasn’t even really his fault. Seymour knew that he had made lots of mistakes, that had cost him dearly in his life. Believing that RPGs were real, for one. But those weren’t what ruined everything. It was those goddamned reporters. Always interrupting the happy couple at meals. Knocking on the doors of their apartment. Really, was it any wonder that poor Laura cracked under the strain? From there, they had become friends with benefits. Then just friends. Then, not even that. It was a combination of losing his best friend and a general lack of passion that had caused Seymour to decide to walk away for a bit. “But without fail, they came anyway,” he said, disdainfully. “Wouldn’t leave me alone. All I’ve ever wanted is a bit of privacy’for what happens out of the ring to be my business only. Guess it’s impossible.” Or perhaps not. In truth, THAT was the reason that he signed on the dotted line. Caldera had promised that he would keep the reporters off Almasy’s back, give him the private time that he so wanted. And thus far, he’d been a man of his word. Sure, he’d been mobbed outside the arena after Annihilation, but that was to be expected. He’d stood with Caldera and answered all of the questions as best he could, as politely as he could. “Mister Almasy,” he said, in his best slightly-drunk impression of your average reporter, “what made you decide to come to GCW?” Of course, Seymour had given the proper answers. He’d been well trained from being the subject of hype for PTC’s GTT4 tournament, so he knew how to deal with the men he despised so. He’d taken questions, and then gotten on to the far more rewarding business of greeting his fans. Signing autographs was just the tip of the icebergs. Pictures of all varieties followed, including those with him playfully headlocking fans, and having younger fans struggling to hold up his immense sword. Seymour was unique. He was an impressive competitor in the ring. He was loved by fans the world over. As Steven Caldera had no doubt figured out, he was’marketable. Almasy sighed. No sooner had he debuted than the GCW merchandise stands had rolled out shirts and foam fingers in his likeness. The news of his arrival was being spread to the four corners of the wrestling world. In other words, he was right back in the goddamned spotlight again. He shook his head, looking over to the king-sized bed that seemed to be inviting him. Inviting him quite temptingly, too. It was far too late at night for him to be worrying about his return to wrestling, Seymour had decided. No, better to get some sleep now and be prepared to attack the problem from a new angle in the morning. From a running start, he practically dove onto the coverlet. No sooner had he managed to crawl up to the pillow than he fell asleep, the adrenaline and energy that had sustained him for the evening gone. So ended Seymour’s first day as a member of Global Championship Wrestling. Unfortunately for Seymour, his SECOND day would begin far sooner than he would have liked. ==================== Seymour was suddenly startled awake. Yawning, he rolled to the side, looking at the room’s clock. “Six AM” What in the?” He found his groggy voice cut off by a sharp, ringing sound. After a few blinks of his eyes, he realized that the telephone was ringing. At SIX IN THE FRICKIN? MORNING. Almasy’s blood began to boil as he groped blindly for the phone, managing to lift it off the hook and jam it to his ear. Before he could respond with utter venom, however, a cheery voice spoke. “Good morning Mr. Almasy, this is your wake-up call!” Seymour slammed the phone down. Obviously, HE hadn’t asked for a wake up call. So who had? Was it a practical joke from an angered Rollins? Maybe a GCW hazing ritual? Really, he didn’t care. He was too tired to be angry, anyway. The Final Fantasy rolled to his side, getting ready to catch some shut eye, when he saw a folder that had evidently been slid under his door. Caldera. “Son of a?” Really, the only person it could BE was Caldera. A quick look at the morning’s delivery would confirm that, and so Almasy dragged his weary body out of bed at 6:04 to go take a look. Stooping, Seymour picked up the folder. In it was what appeared to be a travel itinerary, along with pages and pages of other information. Almasy regarded it with narrowed eyes, until he saw that he had indeed been scheduled to compete, and in the main event no less. “Borealis, non-title,” he mused. As per usual, he was being thrown right into the thick of battle. Borealis had captured the US title at Annihilation from Rich Rollins. He was good, and seemingly had all the momentum on his side. “So much for easing into things, I suppose.” Of course he wasn’t going to be eased into things. Caldera had made an investment. One that he wanted to get as much out of as he could. That was the only reason Almasy could think of for the schedule that Steven had set up for him today. A radio appearance. Autograph signings. This on top of the plane flight he’d need to make to Portland, Oregon and the gym time he’d need to put in to be ready for a man of Borealis? caliber. He sighed. Was Caldera trying to kill him? Judging by the schedule, it was a distinct possibility. Sometimes, he wished that wrestling was just that, wrestling. He could show up at the arena, have his music hit, go out and wrestle Jonathan Borealis, give it his all, and go home after a great contest. But that wasn’t how it worked. Wrestling was a business. Steven Caldera was a businessman. And Seymour Almasy was an employee. He had never thought of it like that, really. Seymour Almasy was a wrestler. In the Universal Wrestling Federation, there was no appreciable corporate structure. You got paid, and you went home. Nice and simple. Here, though, it was Caldera’s way or the highway. That much had been made clear right away, and Almasy had accepted it. Heck, he thought it was nice to have an owner who cared, who actually gave a shit about his company. But now’he was beginning to have second thoughts. “I signed a contract,” he said, voice slightly shaky. “I must fulfill that contract. No matter the terms.” That meant doing things that he didn’t want to. Like waking up at 6:30 in the morning. Like going to endless appearances. Like doing publicity. It was at that moment that Seymour Almasy understood. Understood everything. The second that he had put his name to Caldera’s contract, he had become a prisoner. Tied himself down to Caldera’s beck and call. Suddenly, the posh hotel suite didn’t look quite so inviting. The bed didn’t seem quite as soft. The carpets didn’t seem quite as plush. PTC’s 2004 Wrestler of the Year collapsed to the bed in a heap. How could he have made this miscalculation? How could he have made such a fatal mistake? To Steven Caldera, Seymour Almasy represented income. Dollar signs. And so the shrewd businessman would milk Almasy for all he was worth. At that moment, Seymour saw Caldera as what he truly was. A parasite.
