Deceit Abound: Part 4

24 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/24/06 (Takes place 9/05) September 2005 How much could money actually buy? To your average person, it was an age old question that seemed quite pointless to ask. There were plenty of things to burn a million dollars on. Cars Jewelry Clothing Or even just a spacious house. And when it’s all said it done, you’ll have plenty of people wanting to suck off your fortune to keep your social life alive. And your love life? Why even bothering worrying about that? You can buy love too, can’t you? And friends, and family, and everything you could possibly need in life. If only the Average Joe lived the kind of life that Christa Windsor and her adoptive brother Evan did, they’d understand why money mattered none. It happened to be one of those rare September mornings, where the dawning of autumn hadn’t set in. Yet on said morning, charming eleven year old Christa Windsor decided she’d spend her Saturday morning at her breakfast table, poking at her half eaten stack of pancakes as if she had nothing better to do. Only the occasional call from a friend tore her from her syrup drenched pile. But only to tell them today wasn’t a good day to play, while her mother was around. Granted to her, it was a shame that even with an hour left to go in the morning, all her twenty four year old aunt turned mother could think to do is flop on the couch after cooking one small meal. The front door swung open, being only the third thing to embrace Christa’s attention that morning. In came her brother yet again, this time without the smile he’d woken up with that morning. “She went back to sleep?” Why’d he even bother asking? All he’d have to do is peak out of the corner of his eyes into the study, to see Tessa crashed out on the futon in the far corner of the room. Then again, it was difficult for the eight year old to comprehend why, when she’d just lost her job and no longer had to spend long weekends away from home, she’d want to sleep all morning. “She said she needs a few more minutes.” Responded the preteen turned miniature adult, as she finally surrendered to her inadequate appetite, and tossed the remnants of her few bites into the plastic can underneath the sink. “But why?” The curiosity of the youngest member of the Windsor family never seemed to fade, even in times where answers were sparse to his sister. “Like I’m supposed to know,” she answered in typical preteen fashion, “she’s probably got plans?” “With who?” The phone cut the answer to that off, as Christa’s bare feet scampered across the freshly polished linoleum floor. Given the timing where all her friends would either be at soccer meets or on their way to the mall, she figured if she answered it, it’d answer both their questions. “Hello?” Like queen of the Windsor estate, Christa Windsor marched down the hallway, with her head held high, while her younger brother danced in anticipation on his tiptoes, closely behind her. “She’s not here right now.” Now, Evan was nearly taller than Christa, as his anticipation peaked. And even though he fell just an inch or two short, he still tried to peer his head over her shoulders to get his radar in on what was being said. “No, I’m not lying. She’s not here!” Christa protested to the voice on the other line. “Besides, she doesn’t have time for you.” Finally, Evan’s ears were within earshot range of what was being said. The two had to bottle their mischievous giggles, as Christa folded her arms around her developing chest and pricked her chin up as if the person on the other line could see her. “Who is it?” Evan questioned without words, but with the gesture of his mouth. That was a question that’d have to wait to be answered, as their party was about to come to a screaming halt. “Who isn’t here?” Out came the real queen of the house, with her much more muscular arms folded across her chest in a style similar to the princess?. Still clad in the same pair of blue cropped pajama bottoms and the white racer back sleep tank she’d worn the night before, Tessa had certainly looked a lot more like a peasant than the boss of this joint. Still, when her arm outstretched to her smaller version, the phone was deposited immediately into her palm, so that she could put an end to this monkey business. “Hello?” She answered, as the children escorted each other towards the stairs, hoping to escape to their rooms before being told. Unfortunately, that wasn’t going to save them his time. Slithering her fingers, which were highlighted by their pearly covered tips, around the mouthpiece of the phone, she eyed the children on the way up the stairs, which stopped them dead in their path. “Don’t you dare go anywhere.” She hissed low enough so that the person on the other line wouldn’t here her. “I’m sorry about that, dear. I guess they must have though I stepped out side.” Covering the path of lies laid out the children to send her a message, it clearly wasn’t one loud enough for them to understand, as they whispered among themselves in the middle of the carpeted stairway. “Yeah, I’ll be there Monday. I promise. Wouldn’t want to let you down.” To this, the children did some hissing of their own, as they looked down at their mother with a scowl, that she was too occupied to see. “Thanks so much, I really do appreciate it. Take care” As her thumb went down for the off button, her eyes unpleasantly trailed in the opposite direction, to meet with the kids own pairs. Still, she posed about as much of a threat to them, as she did someone the size of Bryan Mayhem. She was never around to uphold their punishment anyways, and the time they had together was too little in the past to give away to moments like this. “Why’d you lie like that?” She questioned, not so much angrily anymore, more so hurtfully. “Why do you keep going back?” Christa retorted with a question of her own. Most eleven year olds wouldn’t dare answer their parents in such a manner, but given that she barely viewed Tessa as one, she felt she had the right to. “It’s not like you ever get anything from it.” The mouths of both Evan and Tessa fell agape. Were her efforts really that unappreciated? After all, all she’d done was to help the three of them out, or so she thought. The only one now that could possibly answer that was Evan, and judging by the way he tried to slink away from both his sister and Tessa, he’d wanted nothing to do with it. “You’re too young to understand.” She clamped her tongue down with her teeth to keep from trying to argue about her choices with a child. After all, how could Christa possibly know what was best for her? “I understand what you and Dawn did.” With that, charming little eleven year old Christa Windsor put herself on equal playing field with a multiple time tag champ that had twelve years on her. For one, after her ill fated trip to Las Vegas a few weeks back, the last thing she wanted to think about was her whole situation with Dawn. Dawn was a good friend, a great partner, and certainly someone the children looked up to. But since she’d up and walked out of Tessa’s life because of things supposedly of better value, it seemed the children’s faith in Tessa had taken a similar path. Apparently, Dawn had stolen at lot more than Tessa’s trust. Gazing her baby blues away in another direction, her chest inflated, and then deflated as she looked back towards her smaller version, “That’s not for you to be worrying about either. Besides, it has absolutely nothing to do with what’s going on right now?” “Yes it does.” This was certainly news to Tessa. “It does?” “Yes.” She answered, a bit more meekly, as she realized this would require an explanation. To make matters worse, it almost felt to Christa as if she assumed the role of the parent, as her mother looked up to her for answers to such a complicated question, “Why?” “The only reason you’re going back is because you think you can find someone else like Dawn or Chris...” Ouch. It was one thing to have someone of her age to be digging into her about her past, but her own kids now? It had to be stopped?.. “No, I’m going back to keep a roof over your head and mine.” Though not shouting, her frustrations were certainly clear by how rapidly her chest moved in and out. Unfortunately, the girl was far too clever to fall for that load of crap, “You’re already rich enough to do that.” “That’s what you think. But you don’t pay the bills”.” “You’re also going back because Chris is there.” She continued on, disregarding any of the words that continued to trickle out of her mother’s mouth. “People are right when they say you can’t do anything alone.” It’d be an understatement to say that Tessa’s heart had sank with that last bit. Now, she tried the more heartfelt approach to get into her daughters mind, “And why would you care if I went back for those reasons?” “I wouldn’t.” Ah yes, even at an early age she was obtaining the qualities of a full grown woman, and leaving her mother absolutely clueless as to what was going on in that little mind of hers. “I just don’t want you to get hurt by that other guy.” In the ocean of emotions that’d filled the moment, this certainly sunk Tessa’s heart the furthest. If only Christa was old enough to understand how relationships really worked, “Don’t worry, we’re just friends for now.” Inside Christa’s mind, the lie detector was blaring full blast, but she managed to keep her opinion to herself as she slowly ascended the stairs, but not before looking down towards her aunt turned mother, with a glimmer of hope that things would turn out better for her than they did with Dawn and Chris. “I know you love him, but please don’t get hurt again?” Swallowing her breath, Tessa bit her bottom lip knowing for a fact that this, like all the others she’d made, wouldn’t be something she could guarantee would follow through. “I promise. No more tears. Now let me get a shower in, and we’ll hit the movies.” Forcing herself to look relieved by Tessa’s promise, Christa nodded her head and headed to her room. She knew much better than to know that life would be happily ever after from here. It never ended that way. Ever. Even with Seymour Almasy taking the new role of the Knight in shining armor. After all, young Christa Windsor knew for a fact it wasn't the money she was going for this time. This time it was about a heart and soul that needed to be mended, and taking whatever gets thrown Tessa's way in the process.
