The measure of a man

6 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/6/05 I opened my eyes slowly and blinked a couple of times then rubbed my eyes to get the sleep out of them. Good God, everything hurt. I sat up and looked around. A few people were up and moving about among the throng of people who were lost. Lost by a force of nature none of them could control. I had called Richard and canceled my spot in the poker game telling him I had something more important to do. Then I boarded a plane for Louisiana. I had seen the pictures like most people on TV of the damage that Katrina did, but until I stepped off the plane and my eyes took in the sight...well, let's just say TV can't convey the truth as clearly as my own eyes could. I couldn't imagine what these people are going through, but I had made the decision to come here when Caldera suggested it. I may have been many things, but a heartless bastard was not one of them. I was a human being, and so were these people. I was just one man out to do what I could. As I was driven to one of the many shelters in Baton Rouge, I stared out the window with a morbid fascination. I looked at the damage that Katrina had left in her wake. Buildings that just a couple of short weeks ago people were sure would stand forever were nothing more than piles of rubble. As I reached the shelter the true weight of what had happened hit me like a physical thing. The smell and the sight were almost too much to take, but as I was led inside the shelter and introduced to the volunteers running it, I was taken aback by something else. Despite all these people had been through, there was hope. Some of them were actually smiling. People who had lost everything, their houses, their cars, all their worldly goods gone and they were persevering. Most of, if not all of them had nothing to go back to. No homes to go back to and had to start their lives all over again, and everyone around them understood that. Because they were all in the same boat. 'Mr. Savige?' 'It's Savitch actually.' I said, 'Away from the ring it's Mike Savitch.' Kimberly nodded. She was one of the many volunteers working here. She barely looked a day over 20, and yet here she was helping the victims of Katrina. 'Sorry. Anyway, we're about to serve lunch.' 'Okay, just let me know what you need me to do.' I joined in the serving line and helped serve the food that the shelter could get. As I looked at the small portions I felt guilty about having so much as a hamburger. But it was all these people had. It made me mad. It was unfair, and there was no one to blame except Mother Nature, and there was nothing any of us could do about that. You can't change the weather, nor can you change the past. I know people talked about it all the time. How they would have stopped the assination of John Kennedy and Martin Luther King, Jr., Pearl Harbor, the Hindenberg, the Titanic, 9/11, and this. But there was no turning back now. All we could do was help the victims move forward. After lunch I made the rounds of the shelter and signed autographs, posed for pictures and helped people. Mostly I helped. I saw a woman, maybe a few years older than I was with a broken leg, hobbling along on makeshift crutches. I moved to help her and she smiled at me. Didn't know who I was, at least not my professional life. She just knew I was someone helping her to her cot. Despite the fact that she had no access to the creature comforts that most of us now take for granted she had a quiet beauty to her that no force of nature could ever take away. I helped a young boy, no more than ten get to and from the bathroom as his parents were still among the missing. He held on to the hope that one day they would come in through the doors and take him away from all this. I didn't have the heart to tell him that it might not happen. It was very possible, as they weren't confirmed dead yet either. I had never claimed to be a religious person, but when I came across a preist leading a prayer group, I was invited to join in and I did. I prayed for the families and friends of the people who were here. I prayed that God, if he really did exist, give these people the strength to carry on. I worked tirelessly throughout the day. Helping people and trying to keep the names to the faces, but there were just so many of them, after a while I could only remember the first letter of some names, but the actual names wouldn't come to me. Only once the night came and people started settling in did I find time to sit down, and then it hit me. I'd been tired in the ring from long matches, but this was different. I was exhausted. I had worked straight through without a break and I could barely stand. My shoulders ached, my fingers were numb, my arms felt like they weighed a hundred pounds each. My back was sore from bending over and stadning up and leaning over and helping carry everyone from young children to grown men and women. My legs, from my thighs down to my ankles were sore and my knees felt like they would collapse under me. My right knee moreso due to the car accident all those years ago. My feet hurt so bad I don't think I could have walked if I wanted to. 'How do you do it?' I asked Kimberly as she came over and offered me some dinner. I greatfully accepted the small plate of mac and cheese and the cup of water and used my leaden limbs to eat. 'I've never been so tired in my life.' 'It's not easy.' she said, shrugging, 'But they need our help, a lot of them have given up hope, others hold on to the small hope that the relatives and friends that they haven't heard from are still alive. All of them are thankful that they're alive. They all know it will take a long time before things are ever right again.' 'Nothing will ever be right again.' I said. 'No matter how much rebuilding goes on one the water is gone. For any of them. This has changed everyone just like 9/11 changed the lives of the families of the victims. No matter what kind of normal life these people may get back to once all this is over, this tragedy will always be a part of their lives.' She nodded, 'We know, but we try to make the best of it. I've seen people who have literally lost everything swear up and down that they will get through this and not let it get the better of them. They know things can never go back to the way they were before, but they hold out hope for the future, knowing that if they made it through this, they can rebuild their lives a little bit at a time. We all know it won't happen overnight.' I thought back to the preist leading the prayer group, I thought to the children who would probably never see their parents again, I thought to the sick, the tired, the injured, and I felt the buring in my eyes. I didn't fight the tears as they spilled down my face. After I had gotten it out of my system I stood up and looked out over the now sleeping mass of people, and it gave me pause as I realized the strength it took for them just to get through a day. I was just one man doing what I could. Kimberly was just one girl doing what she could. Every volunteer at this and every other shelter was as an individual just one person doing what they could. But all of us, as a whole, celebrities and volunteers alike were an entity helping those who needed help. I had helped in two ways. I had donated ten thousand dollars to the red cross, and I had come down here and seen the devestation first hand. I had seen the survivors first hand. I had lent a hand. And I nearly passed out from helping tirelessly for the day. I finally fell into a cot and quite literally passed out. I was just one man doing what I could to help those who had either lost all hope, or were hanging on to what little hope they had left. Just before I passed out on my cot, I felt very, very small.
