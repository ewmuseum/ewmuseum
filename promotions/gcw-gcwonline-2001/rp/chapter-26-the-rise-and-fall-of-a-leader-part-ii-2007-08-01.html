Chapter 26: The Rise and Fall of a Leader- (Part II)

1 August 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 1, 2007 __Start There's a certain rush that comes with the big day. Look at a wedding for instance; everyone, especially the bride and groom, are smiling as the event proceeds. Those clich? butterflies leave their nest in your stomach and begin to flutter until they get trapped alongside your lymph nodes. There it seems like something straight out of a kinky porno with their interaction which ultimately leads to the feeling of weightlessness and overall euphoria. It's often times called happiness. It's that happiness that keeps a person wanting more. That happiness is the driving fluid that makes the machine you are work. If it wasn't for anticipation and a slight tinge of fear a person wouldn't be a person. They'd be something along the lines of a plant or a rock. That build-up, though, brings desire and that desire drives each and every person to the point of actually doing something. It's what makes us human. Chapter 26: The Rise and Fall of a Leader Part II The smoke from the end of his cigarette exited the butt and crawled along the space between his mouth and nose as it found his nostrils. He sucked in the amazing scent in an exaggerated manner making everyone inside the diner look his way. If it wasn't for the bandana he had around his neck, he probably would have drawn more looks his way. It's hard to get more when you've already gotten them all though, right? 'Would you like a coffee or something sir?' The waitress’s voice was an innocent one to the ears. It floated in the way any teenage girl's voice should: young and beautiful. Jay laid the cigarette in one of the rivets along the edge of the ash tray. His eyes shifted along the curves of her body and up to her face. For only being eighteen, she wasn't half bad. 'Jay.' She seemed confused by the clarification. He could tell by the way her eyes squinted and by the way she ran her fingers through her hair. It was normal for them when they didn’t know what to say or do. 'My name's Jay.' 'Oh,' she smiled flashing a set of pure white teeth. 'I'm Candice.' She pointed to the name tag on her shirt like Jay was some retard or something. He brushed it off though and smiled back at her. 'Did you want a coffee?' 'If I was to ask you to sit down with me, what would you say?' Stunned, 'Um, I don't think I'd be allowed. It's the policy here.' Jay nodded and picked his cigarette back up off the tray. 'You smoke?' He could tell she was trying to leave but just as he figured, she was the kind of girl who was too nice for her own good. There was no way she'd scurry off without making sure he was done talking, it was just the kind of girl she was. Normally, Jay hated that kind of girl. He was bored though. 'Not a lot.' she replied courteously refusing. It was probably more policy bull shit. 'Would a drag now be a lot?' He held the remnants of the cig out for her. 'Um,' She paused and glanced around the diner. Everyone had turned their earlier attention away from the outlaw and back to their food. 'I suppose I could take one drag.' She grabbed it from his fingers and Jay could feel her smooth skin on his. Her lips wrapped around the paper and she sucked in the fumes like she enjoyed every second of it. He knew she was lying though. Smokers didn't cough like she did after a drag. It was way too obvious. 'Thanks,' She wiped her lips showing another sign of her poor bluff. 'Of course.' He smiled and took the cigarette back to his own lips. Her lip balm left a green apple taste on the end of it making him take an extra long hit. 'Well I have to get back to work. You want that coffee?' 'Will it get you to come back?' She smiled a false smile for him. 'Well I'd have to bring it to you.' 'I'll take one. Black.' __________ And as human's, desire has to be a double edged sword. Even more clich? than butterflies is the fact that you can't always get what you want. Sure, you may firmly believe you deserve it but who says that you truly have done something of merit to receive it. Just because a person sees themselves with a championship doesn't mean they are going to have it. And there is where desire takes a turn for the worst and is actually put to use. Yes, work must be done to gain what you think should be yours. As dreaded as the word is though, this is the point where you step back from everything and assess the situation. Is it worth it? How much do I want it? Would I kill for it? If yes is your final answer to each of the questions, then you go for it. Trying never hurt anyone. __________ She slid the coffee mug across the surface of the table as if she was trying to make a quick escape. Jay wasn't surprised by the hit and run, he could cost her the only job she's ever had if the wrong person saw them talking. 'So how long have you been working here Candice.' He was willing to take the chance. She turned around and stopped in her tracks. So close. Too bad for her, Jay knew exactly what he was doing. It was human nature for a person to stop when their name was spoken. Often times, if a person wanted to avoid another, they could play it off as if they thought they were talking to someone else. It was common logic without really lying. 'Just about two years I think.' 'You think? That long huh?' He could tell that he made her feel a little more comfortable with a comment like that. With most people, they felt awkward toward new acquaintances. Jokes were an easy way to kill that awkwardness. 'Yeah, exactly.' Her eyes met his and Jay could tell she wanted to ask him something. It was just something about the look she gave him. 'Go ahead.' Again, she didn't know what he was talking about at first. He gave her a moment and in that time she put it together. 'You know I'm underage...right?' He laughed to himself. 'I didn't think you were twenty if that's what you're thinking.' She laughed as well. It was the same false chuckle she gave him earlier. 'Ok, cool.' Silence. 'You think I want to get with you, don't you?' She didn't want to say it but her true thoughts were too easy to read. 'Eh, not really.' 'Well trust me, I'm not that kind of guy. Just trying to make a girls day, you know?' He'd seen it in one of those chick flicks he'd flicked past on HBO once. There was a girl and a guy, both strangers, and the guy was acting quite flirty. Kinda like Jay was. But anyways, he said that he wanted to make her day and she got all teary eyed and they ended up getting freaky later on that night. 'Aww, you're cute.' By the look of her smile, Jay figured the movie was a good outline as to how the good copy was going to turn out. Jay simply smiled back at her trying to blush. Blushing normally made a girl melt inside. They thought it was cute or something. 'I'll be done in an hour if you wanted to talk a little more.' Jay smiled. He had her hooked. 'In that case, I'll need another coffee.' 'Black?' 'You remembered, how cute of you.' Her grin was actually genuine...for once. __________ Now comes the tricky part. Unfortunately, a person can't simply achieve their goal and then leave it behind. When you want something, you want it to stay. Take the idea of a championship. When a person attains that title and calls it theirs, they want to make sure it stays theirs. Who wants to loose something they actually worked for, let alone share it? And so the tricky part is actually the same as the hard part. Maintain. The work never ends when you truly want something. It's a rule or something. __________ An hour exactly went by and Jay was out at his car. He had managed to pick up a small camo green colored jeep with a retractable roof (for those hot days) for only five thousand dollars. Not bad for the condition it was in. Hell, it even had official dirt marks on it making it look like Steve Irwin had actually been the previous owner. He sat in the driver’s seat of the jeep and watched Candice step out from the diner two minutes after she claimed she was getting off. Her apron was shed and her pants changed into a pair of tiny khaki shorts. Looking at her made him think he was making a mistake. Nevertheless, he kept his promise to himself and avoided leaving the Jeep. Sure, it was a mean thing to do to a girl like that but he had a point to prove. All he wanted was to hook her in and make sure she still wanted more. In a sense, it was an ego thing. Jay felt better about himself seeing that he could get a girl, years younger than him, to fall for him. It was refreshing. He looked at her through his rearview mirror and saw her looking both ways for him. Five more minutes passed and still she looked anxiously. That's when the van pulled up. It was a beige color and swept along the dirt trail of the Australian diner until it eclipsed Candice. Jay rolled his windows in case he heard voices. Nothing. And then he thought he heard a scream. It wasn't a full scream. The beginning was a high pitched yell but it died within seconds and fizzled to nothing at all. That was when the van left. Candice was missing, much like a magician would have done, leaving behind a small piece of paper where she stood. Weird wasn't enough to describe the scene. Jay stepped from his Jeep cautiously and watched the Van disappear in a cloud of dirt where it made its way to the main road. He walked to the front of the diner and when at the paper, he stopped and stared down at it. 'What the fuck...' It was addressed to him. He lifted the paper off the ground and undid a piece of tape so he was able to reveal the contents. In bold print, a single sentence scrolled along the center of the page: BEGIN PHASE ONE: TO SAVE OR NOT TO SAVE? 6 MILES Too many questions came into his head at this point. He hardly even knew who she was, much like the woman he met last week, and she had been swooped up for ransom. Out of all the questions floating around, colliding with one another, he asked himself: Is she worth it? __________ And when a person feels a threat enter their realm of maintaining, that is when everything you've done to that point becomes worth it. Competition means that you're doing something right because someone else wants what you have. So you fight for it. Everything you believe in and all the work you've done to that point combines in one and becomes the sole force driving a person to succeed. It's like a super potion or something. This is where you decide how much you really want that something. If you're like Jay Terror, it means you’d do anything for it. So you fight with one thing in mind: If I die, will it be worth it? Because it better fuckin' be worth it. __To Be Continued
