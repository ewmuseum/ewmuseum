Some Bad News

1 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/1/06 Backstage, after WorldWide It had been troublesome and boring to stand about and congratulate Vivica Valentine on her U.S. Title win for Virgil Ambrosi. Not in the sense that he didn't feel it to be at all useful to them as a powerful gain for their fledgling faction. As far as that was concerned, he thought it was quite advantageous. The truth was, he simply didn't like Vivica. 'Congratulations,' Virgil muttered, briefly making eye contact with Vivica before looking past her once again, and returning to being the silent rock he'd been for the majority of this meeting. Vivica was likely put off. He didn't notice. Dawn and Lia ignored him for the most part, but he didn't expect much more than that. After all, he'd only just won a match. It seemed to feel as though the locker room was closing in on him, like the coffin his kind were rumored to sleep in. Even Lia's presence couldn't cure the malaise of being flanked by Vivica and Dawn McFarlane, at bad and apathetic on his scale of company, respectively. He leaned over to Lia, whispering in her ear. 'I'm going to step outside for a moment... I'll see you later tonight.' She turned her piercing jade eyes to him, smiling slightly, affectionately. 'You're leaving? Are you alright?' He nodded, returning the look. 'Only hungry. It's been a while since I've had anything.' She raised an eyebrow suspiciously, perhaps with some kind of inkling of exactly why Virgil was really stepping out. 'Can't you feed later?' Virgil glanced over toward Dawn and Vivica, who were prattling on about something or other that didn't particularly concern him. 'I won't be gone long, I promise.' Her brow furrowed, and it seemed as though she had every plan to be as stubborn as him tonight. 'Virgil... please stay.' He closed his eyes for a moment, slightly annoyed by her persistence. Then again, he didn't expect much difference from her, after all, their similarities were one of the reasons they got along so well. 'I just need some fresh air. I've been stuck in the arena all day.' She forced a sigh, and looked away from him. 'Fine.' Virgil took that as a cue to slip quietly out the door, annoyed at himself for having to push the envelope, but realizing that he wouldn't have been able to keep his somewhat rude apathy to himself in that situation. He did have every plan on being back shortly, after all. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Outside the arena Virgil felt rather relieved as the first breeze whipped around him, still a little cold for him, being used to the more swarthy climates of his Barcelona homeland, and his adopted living quarters in Las Vegas and formerly New Orleans. It seemed as though the streets in every Japanese city they'd visited never ceased to be busy, but that was something he found he rather enjoyed. His bent to criminality that had been a gift of his transformation from human to vampire had somehow led him to Asia, a place rife with markets for illegal arms trade, and he'd come to enjoy the cultures of Japan, China, and Tibet the most. In Japan, for example, he could walk down the street on a dark night, and people would go out of their way to cross the street, so as not to walk past him. He was never certain if perhaps they were just more intuitive than the brash Americans he lived around, or if most foreigners of his appearance got that kind of treatment. In the busy daytime traffic, however, it wasn't an option, but most people didn't seem to notice him, despite the imposing figure he cut at six-foot-four, with dark sunglasses even on overcast days. He politely declined an offer of lunch from a smiling old man at a noodle cart, despite the fact that they smelled rather pleasant. The man only nodded, and kept at his business, not missing a beat with the throngs of people pushing around him. Such was life around here, he supposed. It was slightly comforting to be here, despite the occasional mild case of xenophobia Japan seemed to be afflicted with. He glanced behind him, and noticed someone seem to duck behind an oblivious businessman, who was talking into a cell phone at a speed that even Virgil couldn't quite make out individual words. Obviously, someone was following him, and doing a pathetic job of it. With so many different people around, he couldn't be at all certain of what it was, but the fact that he couldn't feel a certain sense of power emanating from behind him was enough to make him feel rather confident that he could deal with any threat posed. He glanced behind him again, and the man he'd seen half of previously didn't make any attempt at evasion, but the game was over for him, at least as far as stealth was concerned. Virgil ducked through the packed sidewalks to an alley, and turned a corner, waiting patiently, fingering the grip of his pistol. A short, black suit-clad Japanese man turned the corner and froze. Virgil smiled. He didn't even have to use the gun this time... this one must actually be human. 'You were following me.' The man bowed, deeply enough to show a modicum of respect, but nowhere near that of submission, Virgil noted. That, of course, meant he was likely associated with one of the clans of Cathayans, various tribes and collections of vampires of the East, mysterious and dangerous to most, but only the latter in Virgil's case. Considering how he'd simply walked out of a rather important deal with a Tibetan faction, he wasn't entirely surprised that one of their emissaries knew where to find him. 'Yes. I only have a message for you.' Virgil raised his eyebrow suspiciously. He felt slightly patronized. After what he'd done, he knew very well how many people 'wished a conversation with him'. 'I assume Wang-xiensheng would like to speak with me regarding our deal.' Wang had been the mouthpiece for the aforementioned Tibetan faction of Cathayans, who had purchased numerous weapons from Virgil in the past, despite the rocky interactions between the two. The messenger nodded, and handed him a card, with an address like any other scrawled across the business card in blocky Latin script, an obvious side effect of writing too many Chinese characters in one's lifetime. This likely wasn't going to be the most pleasant of meetings, but considering now he'd been found, it was a little too late to not show up. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ A dry goods warehouse, inside the city It was night by this point, something that irritated Virgil to no end. He wished that it would be possible to deal with Wang and be done with the whole business, but considering he was closer to Wang's territory than his own, he realized it probably wouldn't be the best of ideas to kill him. Right now, however, it was tempting. Besides being an inconvenience, he was a late inconvenience, something which in itself bothered Virgil to a degree. It likely meant he wasn't alone. 'Ambrosi.' Virgil had no use for a dramatic whipping around to face the sound. That would have been a show of weakness, and he never made the first show of weakness. He glanced in the direction he thought the voice was coming from, and smiled. 'Hello, Wang. Are you hiding from me?' A smaller form of a man seemed to materialize from the shadows of the various crates stacked high in the warehouse, and as Virgil set eyes upon him, he found himself surprised by the man he saw. No longer the same Wang who had been so cool, calm, and collected. Now, without his trademark sunglasses, he looked worn. Virgil wondered what had befallen him since the two had last spoken, but realized he'd never know, and didn't really care in the first place. 'You surprise me. Usually you're at least polite enough to speak Mandarin.' Virgil narrowed his eyes. 'Don't talk to me of honor. Did you think that pathetic excuse for a sharpshooter would have a chance against me?' Wang shook his head vigorously. 'I have no clue what you're talking about.' A lie. A pathetically told lie, at that. The fact that Wang was being so animated proved to Virgil that something unpleasant had been planned for him, something he wouldn't be around for, if at all possible. 'Of course. Random Cathayans often attack me with weapons marked with my inventory numbers. How absurd of me to associate him with you, my dear friend.' Wang forced a sigh, crossing his arms behind his back. 'You shouldn't have broken our deal, Ambrosi. You have little room for complaint, as you brought these consequences on yourself.' Virgil scoffed. 'I hope you're better prepared to deal with me than that. None of your clan has near the resourcefulness or power to be more than a roach beneath my boot.' Wang smiled, a devious, wicked sort of smile that sent a slight pang of fear through Virgil's body. 'Perhaps we cannot, but one of your kind does walk our lands, in search of you and your companions... which is why I called you here, Ambrosi. While we cannot destroy you, she can, and I only wanted to watch you quake in terror.' Virgil rolled his eyes. 'Ridiculous.' A pause, and Wang's smile grew a little wider. 'The mother of your race deserves a little more respect than that. Where, Virgil, is your sense of filial piety?' Virgil's teeth gritted. Lilith... what business could she possibly have with them? Hadn't she made enough of a pointless show of power by attacking Cyrus, several months ago in Bratislava? Why could she possibly be in search of Lia again? He noticed, through his agitation, that Wang had started to laugh. 'I see you're rather upset by this, Ambrosi. Had you not burned your bridges with us, we could have destroyed Lilith as we destroyed your clan's elder. A simple flick of the wrist for us... but your irresponsibility will be the demise of you and your companions.' Virgil found he had little left to say to Wang, and although he could hear him laughing quietly as he darted through the doors of the warehouse, botched arms deals and empty threats meant nothing. The news he'd been given, however, warranted as much speed as he could manage. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Back inside the arena The arena was more or less empty at the moment. The crews had long ago finished their business of cleaning up after the event that was WorldWide, and it seemed as though most people had gone back to their hotels for the night. However, it seemed as though a few of his fellow GCW members were still lingering, as he heard the occasional familiar voice coming from the rooms lining the long corridor that sat underneath the seating. They had to still be here too, then. He walked quickly through the cavernous hall, glad that there was no rush to pack everything up to impede him. At this point, with news this dire, he wasn't certain he could have maintained his charade of patience very well. The doors passed quietly by, and his inner monologue was applying itself to complaining about their room being the farthest from the door. However, after a few more, the proper number came into view, and Virgil reached for the knob. Virgil burst rather dramatically through the door of the room where Vivica's after party had been going on, and looked around, noticing the room to be, of course, completely empty. The expression on his face soured. The one time he'd actually wanted to find all of them at once... 'Shit.' He turned, and slammed the door behind him, sending a dull thud echoing through the empty arena.
