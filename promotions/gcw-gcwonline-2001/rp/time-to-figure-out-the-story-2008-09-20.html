Time to figure out the story

20 September 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/20/08 It is difficult to recap the last few months of my life without making it seem like the plot for a bad movie. I get together with my ex-girlfriend who is pregnant with another man’s baby, and then proceed to break up with her on the eve of her due date. The breakup causes her to go into labor and she dies during childbirth. Her son is born deformed, but instead of taking care of him like a responsible man, I abandon him to the maze that is social services. Then it all comes to head when my brother threatens to disown me, and I am attacked in my own home. If that weren’t bad enough the only apparent reason for the attack was to take everything relating to me and Erika. But I did not have time to spend trying to figure out who attacked me and why. My brother’s speech, as well as his threat, made finding Erika’s kid and bringing him home my top priority. Unfortunately it’s a lot harder to take a kid from the hospital when you’ve already denied being his father, don’t know the kid’s name, and aren’t a relative of that kid. To make a painfully long and embarrassing story short the last time I headed to the hospital the doctor threatened to get a restraining order against me. Now if I was smart I would probably have headed to a lawyer to discuss my legal options. In fact there were a million other things that I could have probably have done that would be considered smarter than what I actually did. I called the hospital and pretended to be Erika’s cousin, the only relative whose name I knew of. Unfortunately her cousin is female’causing the conversation to get somewhat awkward. “Hello, Lenox Hill Hospital, how may I help you?” “Hello, my name is Naomi. I was just told that my cousin Erika had come in last week due to give birth. I was hoping you could tell me what happened to her?” “Ma’am’are you alright?” “Well of course, but I won’t be if you don’t tell me what happened to my cousin.” “Well, you’re voice seems unnaturally high miss. Have you been inhaling some sort of substance?” “Why you bastard! I’ve been cursed with the voice God gave me for my entire life. I couldn’t get a date to the prom, get asked out by the boy I’ve been in love with for years, and I am still living at home. Now I am trying to find out what happened to my cousin and you are making fun of me, I should sue!” “Miss, please come down, tell me your cousin’s last name and I’ll find out what happened to her for you.” I proceeded to tell him and waited for him to finish typing. “Ma’am, I hate to tell you this, but you’re cousin died in childbirth. She gave birth to a boy she named Brian, he was picked up by his father today, Greg Bifaro.” I slammed the phone shut as I visibly began to shake. Brian’the boy’s name was Brian and he was in his father’s hands. I should have felt relieved; after all I no longer had to feel any responsibility towards the boy. He had his father to take care of him, I could call Sean, tell him what happened. He would probably agree that a son deserves to be with his biological father, so who was I to get involved? Except that his father was the same man who abandoned his pregnant girlfriend. The same guy who made it clear to her that he wanted absolutely nothing to do with his child, and that was before he knew the baby would come out deformed. Not to mention that there was a very good chance that he had, for some unknown reason, arranged for a steel pipe to make its acquaintance with my forehead. Of course that last part was purely a guess, but I had a feeling that it was fairly close to the truth. It was a foregone conclusion that I was going to confront Greg; I just had to find him. But I figured that it could not be too hard. After all, detectives manage to find people who try to hide every day. How hard could it be for me to find a man who wasn’t even trying to hide himself? An hour later I had a whole new level of respect for detectives. The hour I spent trying to find Greg went something like this. I began by typing his name into google, and spent about ten minutes clicking on links that led me to various porn sites whose existence I would rather forget. After that I tried Wikipedia. I learned about Greg the lawyer, Greg the doctor, and Greg the rapist. Unfortunately one of those was murdered, the other was eighty, and the last one was in jail for rape (I give you one guess as to which is which). I spent another ten minutes on the toilet, another ten eating a bagel, and the last twenty catching the Jerry Springer Show. Clearly I was not equipped to be a detective. But there is one huge advantage to living in New York. It is stocked with Private Investigators, and so I confidently walked out the door to find one that could help me out. Five minutes later I walked back through my door to look one up on the internet. Later at the detectives office?. “So Mr. Black,” the investigator said to me as he leaned back in his charge, “what is it that I can help you with today?” To be honest I did not really like the look of the detective. By the looks of his wardrobe he thought he was living in some sort of black and white mystery movie. He had on one of those detective hats that went out of style thirty years ago. A suit which wasn’t too bad, but it came along with a fucking trench coat. Who the hell wears a trench coat when he is inside? But from what I gathered on the web he was one of the best detectives in New York City, so I figured I would forgive the wardrobe. “I need to find a man by the name of Greg Bifaro. He should have a baby boy with him named Brian.” The detective studied me piercingly for a second, as if he was trying to read my mind. “Why exactly do you want me to find this guy?” he asked me gruffly, “because I am telling you right now I don’t do kidnappings.” I slowly shook my head. “Let’s just say,” I answered him with no hint of doubt in my voice, “that I want to thank him for giving me this lovely gash on my forehead. But if you could also dig up some information on him, that would be great to. See I don’t want to only thank him when there might have been people who helped him.” I forced myself not to fidget as the detective leaned forward and placed his crossed his hands as if he was in deep thought. I was ninety-nine percent sure that he had already decided to take my case and was just trying to increase the suspense-as well as the price- but that did not make the waiting any easier. “Tell you what,” he finally broke the silence, “I’ll take your case, for my regular fee. Now I just need a picture of a guy.” “Well,” I coughed with a blush rising to the top of my cheeks, “that may be a problem. See I don’t have a picture of him’or a description. I never saw him so I don’t know what he looks like, but I do know that the kid he is with has some sort of deformity.” “So,” the detective muttered, “I have no description to work with and all you can tell me about the kid he might be with is that the boy has some sort of mysterious deformity.” He did his best to sound disgruntled, but I could tell by the smile that he barely managed to hide that he was counting dollar signs in his head. “Because of the increased difficulty of this case,” he told me after I refused to say anything, “I am going to have to unfortunately increase the price. Not only will you have to pay my regular fee, but I am going to need a flat payment up front.” My eyes nearly bugged out of my head when I heard what the initial payment was. I would still be able to pay all my bills for the month, but barely. Plus I would have to push back getting that new fridge I wanted. But I suspected that even if I shopped around, only the second rate detectives would give me a cheaper price so I was more or less stuck. Clearly nervous at having to pay such a large sum, I wrote out the check and gave it to the man who happily placed it inside his safe. “Alright sir,” he told me as I stood up, “I’ll be in touch with you soon.” As I went home I began to feel incredibly numb. Everything was out of my hands now as Brian’s future, my relationship with Sean, and my confrontation with Greg all rested in the hands of one badly dressed detective. I wanted to lose it completely, but I knew that I would not help anyone by doing that. I tried taking deep breaths, meditating, watching TV, but nothing seemed to distract me. Finally I just hired a hooker to take my mind off things for a few hours. Hey, you distract yourself anyway you know how.
