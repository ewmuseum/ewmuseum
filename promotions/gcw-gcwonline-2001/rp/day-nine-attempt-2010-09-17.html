Day Nine: Attempt

17 September 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/17/10 Searing pain shot through my eyes, and my hands immediately clawed at them to try and get the offending substance out. I was under attack by a nameless, faceless adversary, and I had no chance in Hell. My limbs flailed around as a dozen thoughts filled my mind. This was my first ever shot on Pay Per View, High Stakes 2010, and this was how it was ending. I was powerless to stop it. With nothing else to do, I closed my eyes, tried my best to not get destroyed, and let my mind wander... DAY ELEVEN: ATTEMPT Halifax, Nova Scotia, Canada I looked around at the hundred or so people in the small armory, poised on the top rope. I was facing away from the ring, so looking at the crowd was easy. Casting a quick glance over my shoulder to ensure that my opponent was still down, I screwed up my courage and leaped. By wrestling standards, it was a dangerous move, a blind 180 turn 450 splash. Over the years, it had acquired a name: the Phoenix Splash. And since I was billing myself as the Canadian Phoenix, it was a move I needed to have in my repetoire. "OOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOHHHH!" I heard a loud roar from the crowd as I landed across my opponent. On recoil, I hooked the leg, and three slaps of hand on mat later, Gerard Roy had won his first ever match in the Halifax Wrestling Alliance. It was supposed to be a great moment. But all I could feel in my heart was a crushing sense of despair. ~~~~~ Perhaps the worst thing about it was that, even as I remembered it, I couldn't remember for the life of me WHY I was so upset. All I could recall was that I was, and that it was bad enough that the Canadian Phoenix's first match was damn near his last. I was upside down, all of the blood in my body rushing to my head. I was at the mercy of whoever this was. Escape was impossible, and I tried to brace myself for impact, to make the damage about to be done to my back or neck less severe. Even as my body was doing what it was trained to, though, my mind was returning back to the memories that this entire experience was somehow bringing to the forefront of my mind's eye. ~~~~~ In the movies, it was so fucking easy. All you did was exert a thimbleful of pressure on the trigger, and BANG! That was all it took to kill yourself. I knew this. And yet, as I stood, gazing up at the night sky for what I fervently hoped would be the last time, I found myself utterly unable to do it. Human beings had impressive survival instincts. We were, after all, evolved animals. We worked hard at avoiding dying any of the hundred or so myriad ways that we exposed ourselves to in every day life. Maybe that was why I couldn't do it. Because pulling the trigger went against everything I was hardwired with. Because LIVING was the one thing that mattered to every human being, from Neanderthal to metrosexual. Doing this would defy thousands of years of human experience and logic. "Don't you fucking dare!" And just like that, with the arrival of another actor upon this stage, the window that I wouldn't have had the guts to take anyway was closed. I tasted the bitter tang of metal in my mouth as I looked out from soulless, defeated eyes. Truth be told, I was astonished that anyone had found me here, on a rickety pier a few miles away from the arena. "And why the fuck not," I retorted, creatively. "You don't even know me! You don't know what I'm carrying with me!" "Maybe not," he shot back, "but whatever it is, it sure as shit ain't worth your life!" The other man moved closer to me. Somewhat courageously, I thought, given that I was holding a gun. In the dim moonlight, I recognized him as one of the guys on the show I'd worked earlier in the evening. Shaking my head bitterly, I pulled the barrel of the gun out of my mouth and threw it over my shoulder. It landed in the lake with a soft splash, and I took a step closer towards my savior. "You have no fucking clue, Mark," I spat. "No goddamned clue at all who I am, or what I've done, or why I wanted my corpse to fall off of this pier into the fuckin' water." He glared right back at me. He had balls, I had to give him that much. A Hell of a lot more than I did, when it came right down to it. "Maybe I don't, Gerard, but I *want* to know! If it'll get you off of this pier with me, tell me! Spill the whole thing! You have my word I won't say anything! I swear on my kids!" I flinched at his offer. It was tempting. It was so fucking tempting to just spill the whole thing and give another person a share, however slight, of the burden I carried with me everywhere I went. "It's too long a story," I finally said, forcing back the impulse. "You've made your point though. I'll come back with you." My wrestling boots echoed off the pier as I walked back to solid ground. I must have looked ridiculous, wearing white and red boots, no shirt, and a pair of package-hugging short tights, to say nothing of the phoenix-styled mask. Mark breathed a sigh that sounded an awful lot like relief. "Whew," he said. "Was afraid that was going to be a lot harder." In spite of it all, I couldn't help but chuckle. "You're just lucky I'm a huge fuckin' coward, or you'd have had a harder mission on your hands." "You're a lot of things, I guess, but you ain't no coward. At least, judging by some of that shit you pulled tonight. You're not a greenhorn, that's for damned sure." I didn't relax then, not ntil my feet hit pavement. I breathed out deeply at that point, somewhat relieved that I'd avoided making another mistake in a lifetime full of them. "Never said I was one, did I?" I fired right back, mouth curving into a smirk. "Neither did anyone else. Just new to the area." "Not used to seeing the guys we bring in for the opening match actually being any good, that's all," Mark said. "You're the first guy who got a "please come back" chant in a few months." "Does my heart good to hear that," I replied, the gun and what I'd almost done nearly forgotten. "You think they'll bring me back?" He grinned at me. "Our owner's the biggest fucking cheapskate on the planet, and he was running around backstage telling everyone that he wanted to see "that Phoenix kid" again. I think you're gonna be okay." As I took that in, he took a step closer, and gave me the hardest pat on the back that I'd ever had in my life. "And if you ever want to tell me what's eatin' ya, feel free, man. There's no place on Earth I feel more comfortable than with the boys in the back. You make friendships that last a lifetime." I looked up at this man who I'd just met today. Words weren't enough, but I managed a weak "thank you, Mark." Judging by the solemn nod he gave me, it was enough for now. "Let's hit up Applebees. My treat for some half priced appetizers." ~~~~~ What could I say? He'd saved my life AND was going to get me some cheap, filling, unhealthy midnight snacks. It was, I'd realize much later, the stuff that lifelong friendships were founded on. That story faded from my mind just as the birdies stopped tweeting in my poor abused cranium. Reality awaited. ~~~~~ "Gerard? Gerard? Are you okay?" A feminine voice was about all I was aware of once I regained coherence. My eyes were burning, vision blurry. I remembered enough of what happened to know that it wasn't due to sweat. "As fine as I'm gonna be after getting misted and dropped on my head," was the most witty thing I could think of to say at that point in time. "Anyone get a glimpse of who did it?" I'd been in the company about a month. Probably less. I couldn't imagine that I'd been targeted by anyone. I was probably a randomly picked target for someone to make an impact. Didn't mean I liked it any less, but the idea of someone with a grudge against me already didn't fill my heart with joy. "On commentary," I heard the voice of my yet-to-debut-on-TV valet Autumn say, "they were speculating that it was AXEL Action." "Oh, goodie." I shook my head, groping about blindly until I found a wet towel. I wasn't about to look a gift horse in the mouth, so I took it, and wiped my eyes as clean as I could. My vision wasn't perfect, but at least now I could see that I was backstage in GCW's usual makeshift triage unit. "Just what I always wanted, an angry guy who spells his names in all caps coming after me. I had my share of those when I actually, you know, wrestled in Japan." She chuckled at that, and stooped down next to me. "You know, I think I like you a lot more when you're not spouting Zenisms." "Next thing you know," I retorted, "you'll tell me that you like me better without the facepaint, too." "Actually, I do, but that's neither here nor there." I sighed. Apparently, there WERE people in this world who could be as sarcastic as me. "Anyway," she went on, "they ran tests on you. You'll be okay. Probably a good lump on your head, but otherwise..." Running a hand over my stubbly head, I could feel said lump. It would need a steady diet of ice, but the docs were right. I'd be okay. "Good. So that means I can go figure out what I'm gonna do about this AXEL Action problem. If he didn't have a problem with me going into this, congratulations to him, because he's got one now." Standing up was a new and exciting challenge, one that I achieved with a minimum of stumbling and making an idiot out of myself. Thanks to some timely assistance from the wall, I was up and halfway down the hall a few moments later. I had just come up with a few choice words to give Mr. Action when I felt a sharp poke in my ribs. It was, of course, my lovely valet. "If you'd just talk like that when you get interviewed," she hissed in my ear, "you wouldn't need me." There was a hopeful tone in her voice at that, as if me not needing her would possibly accelerate her chances of getting called up to the main roster. I couldn't blame her for that. "Yeah, because the world really wants to hear another sarcastic guy. But wait, this one's CANADIAN! That makes all the difference in the world. Fuck, there are already a dozen sarcastic Canadian guys in the sport." Probably more than that, but a dozen seemed like a nice round number. "It's better than spouting a bunch of Zen cliches that make no sense." Autumn was, it seemed, very very good at getting to the heart of the matter. "I've been traveling on the road with you for a week now, and Gerard Roy's a pretty interesting guy. Moreso than Bhava, I'd say." My jaw dropped, and eyes went wide. "Bhava's a mysterious Japanese mist-spitting Zen Buddhist who talks in riddles," I replied. "He's got mystique. I've got a ring name that makes no sense and a last name that no American who hasn't seen a hockey game can pronounce." "Even if all of that was true," Autumn replied, "it's you. I've been watching wrestling for six years now, and the one thing I learned long before I ever decided to train was that it's always the guys who are themselves who make it. Bhava's never going to main event NC-17. Gerard Roy may never either, but he's got a better chance than Bhava does." A low groaning sound came from deep within me. Autumn would come to know it in the months to come as the sound I made when I knew someone was right, but didn't want to admit it. "Let's get out of here, Autumn," I said, waving the proverbial white flag on this conversation. "We can talk about it over some half-priced appetizers at Applebees? My treat?" She looked at me for a few moments. I knew the look on her face, because it was the same look that my friends knew me for: the "what can I say to get the biggest rise out of you?" look. "Is this your terribly awkward way of asking me out on a date?" She smirked at me and shook her head. "Because if it is, you're going to have to do a Hell of a lot better than Applebees." I shook my head, mirroring her gesture. "Mixing business and pleasure never ends well. No, not a date. Just some food and discussion. We can sit in the bar and probably catch the Windsor/Terror main event to boot." "That sounds like an evening," Autumn said. "Professional wrestling, carbonated beverages, and fried food. Reminds me of college." "Maybe we went to the same college," I chuckled, leading Autumn down another corridor and towards, presumably, an evening of artery clogging goodness.
