Sharp-Tongued

31 March 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/31/08 Airports were a way of life for Seymour Almasy. So too, therefore, were delays. Short delays. Long delays. Delays of all sorts for all the dumb reasons you can imagine. The giant flight board in Nagoya’s airport indicated a two hour delay for Seymour’s flight to Tokyo, a fact that made Almasy grimace. “It might be faster to just take a train,” he winced, scratching his chin thoughtfully. Settled into his seat in the waiting area, Seymour surveyed the scene, finding lots of equally grumpy patrons. Most were typing away on laptops, or yammering on the phone, but there were a sizeable amount of young men and women playing video games on their DSes or PSPs, whatever their handheld preference happened to be. Usually in Japan Seymour could count on being approached by fans, but as had been the routine for the tour, he mostly just caught people looking at him. Probably taking in the fact that he’d chopped his signature mane down to nothing. Or the fact that he’d turned his back on them. There were the diehards, of course, the people who cheered him, who understood why he’d done what he’d done. GCW as a whole didn’t especially like him to begin with, let alone now. Reaching into his small travel bag, Seymour unearthed one of his more recent purchases: an English copy of the new Square-Enix game, Crisis Core: Final Fantasy VII. He swore at the title, low under his breath. “Goddamnit’it’s been over a decade since FF7. A fucking decade. Ten years?” He was fifteen when the game came out. Fifteen years old. A high schooler. And now, at the ripe old age of 25, he’d been around the world multiple times, had some of the wrestling world’s greatest prizes strapped around his waist, married, divorced, broken, and built back up again. “What a fuckin” world this is,” he muttered to himself, halfway to pulling the PSP from his bag when a voice and a hand on his shoulder interrupted him. “I knew I’ve made it in this country. Got my own mini-me!” Seymour’s eyes followed the voice up’and up, and up a little bit more, until he found himself staring into the face of a familiar man. Though not familiar to the hordes who knew of Seymour as only a Primetime Central talent, those who followed his farther travels knew well the six foot five blond man standing next to Seymour. “I’ll be damned,” Almasy chuckled, rising to his full height, still six inches shorter than the other man. “Andrew Sharp. Thought you were dead, or at least out of the game.” “You know what they say,” the former ACW World Champion offered with a shrug, “the game’s our life. Can’t get in it easy, can’t get OUT of it easy.” Andrew grinned, offering his hand to Almasy. “Been touring Japan for the past year. And you” What’s life after ACW been treating you like?” Seymour fought back the urge to say “sheer hell.” “Meh, you know, the usual shit in the business,” Almasy replied, non-commitally. “Remember those six months or so that you decided to be a complete twat?” Sharp nodded. “Yeah. Fun times. Beat you what, three times during that period too?” Seymour gritted his teeth, but nodded. “Yeah. Guess you could say I’m going through the twat phase of my career.” Andrew looked at Seymour, his jaw dropped. “Wait, YOU” Mister Overcome the Odds, Mister “No Matter What I’m Going to Do The Right Thing?” The guy who’d always sign autographs when we were eating dinner on the road?” Sharp chuckled. “You’re pulling my chain, Seymour.” “Have I *ever* pulled your chain, Andy?” Seymour asked, a bit impatiently. “You and I rode up and down the roads of America in very mediocre rental cars, and I can’t remember bullshitting you once.” “I can,” Sharp retorted, immediately. “The time you told me they didn’t have any more cars left when in fact you’d gotten one so you and your ACW girlfriend could go make out in the back seat?” “I seem to recall,” Almasy replied, “you not being especially upset about that when you got to the hotel and told me about the girl that gave you a ride. “Tongue like a hummingbird,” was it?” “Touche.” Sharp sat down next to Almasy, placing a hand on his friend’s shoulder. “So what brings you here” Manga run? Going to try and pick up more cosplayers? “No, and no,” Seymour replied, scowling at the dubious look on Andy’s face. “Yes, yes, I know, I’m as shocked as you are, but I’m here on business. Signed with Global Championship Wrestling, and we’ve been touring through Japan for the past month.” “Ah, GCW?” Sharp pondered. As a student of the game, he kept up on as many promotions as he could in spite of being a far more monogamous superstar than Almasy would ever be. “They’ve got a big PPV coming up’ah, no wonder you’re here. Who ya got?” “The PRIME Killer,” Almasy replied, with a soft chuckle. “AKA, the guy who’s been following in my footsteps for years upon years by now, Digital Mortality.” “Niiiiiice,” Sharp mused. “Sounds a bit more high profile than taking on El Caballero in Korakuen, but I love the small crowds. You working the Dome?” “Yeah, I am. Caldera’s not the sort to settle for small arenas. Helps assure him of the size of his package when he goes to bed at night.” “Like Lord Lowell, eh” But I digress. Anything on the line for you two?” Seymour smirked, reaching into his bag, and coming out with the GCW United States Championship. “Just this bad boy right here,” Almasy said, patting the plate of the title. “Congrats,” Sharp said, ogling the belt with the practiced eye of a man who loved titles. “When did you win it?” “Welll,” Seymour began, “I didn’t so much win it as, well, took it. Used it to smack Digimon in the face, and kick this entire thing off for the PPV. Belongs to Tessa Windsor, but meh, she doesn’t deserve the thing. I clearly do.” Sharp fell silent for a few moments. “You stole the title?” he asked, almost disbelievingly. “Yeah. Digi’s Tessa’s friend, so now he’s going to come at the PPV and try to get it back. Of course he’ll fail, but that’s neither here nor there.” Andrew looked into the eyes of Seymour, as if trying to divine the man’s soul. “You’ve changed, Seymour,” he said, simply, even as Almasy shook his head in denial. “You’ve changed.” “Look, I don’t know what’s wrong,” the former ACW World Champion said, “but I look back on that time of my life and wonder what the Hell I was thinking. Sure, it got me the ACW World Title, but it cost me a ton too. It cost me self respect. It cost me a lot of things that I’ll never ever get back. ? Sharp got up from his seat next to Seymour, nodding at him. “It wasn’t who Andy Sharp from Pikeville, Kentucky was,” the 6’5? man concluded, beginning to walk off towards what presumably was a flight of his own. “And it’s not who the Seymour Almasy I’ve always known is.” The Standard Bearer watched his friend leave, a half-dozen thoughts in his mind. “Shit really does change faster than we think,” Almasy mused, looking around at the airport, at the people, the culture, and the country that had irrevocably changed his life. Though an immense amount really had changed, the more Seymour pondered it’the basics were still the same. He was still Seymour Almasy. He still knew what he was doing. Still had all the ability in the world. But then, if so, why did everyone doubt him? ===-===-===-=== Time waits for no man. The world is set to write me off as a has-been. As a legend ready to be put out to pasture. As a man whose worth is gone. Maybe that’s what offends me more than anything else. More than being turned on. More than being persecuted. The fact that the world thinks I’m done, when I know otherwise. Ten years ago, Final Fantasy VII came out and changed my life forever. It put me on the path that I have followed to this day. Without that game, I wouldn’t be here. No one would know my name. Since FFVII, spinoffs have come. Advent Children. Dirge of Cerberus. Crisis Core. All worthy in their own ways, and yet all inspiring the question of when there’ll be a real sequel. I was the spinoffs for the past few years. Still there, still good, but not quite what was expected. Never living up to the hype. Never living up to my own expectations. Sadly for everyone, the sequel’s here. But here’s the catch’the hero’s the villain this time. And the second twist is that, well’you know how the villain always gets his comeuppance in the end? Not so much here. The road to the top starts with you, Digital Mortality. Seymour Almasy, Jr. The UWF’s great hope after me’a hope it never lived long enough to see mature. You want to be me, Digi? You want to take my place? You want to be the guy with the weird name who does well in tournaments? You want all that, Digital Mortality? Then come to Japan. Come to the Tokyo Dome. Come to a land in which I’ve competed dozens of times. Step up to the plate. Look me in the eyes in the middle of the ring, and then you can tell me if I’m washed up or not. Step into the Battle Arena and come to grips with the cold hard reality of the situation. Seymour Almasy is faster than he once was. Stronger. Smarter. And damn sure meaner. This isn’t PRIME, Mortality, nor is it the UWF. This is Global Championship Wrestling. Rampage will live up to its name. It is where my rampage over the promotion begins. You want to stop it? By all means, try.
