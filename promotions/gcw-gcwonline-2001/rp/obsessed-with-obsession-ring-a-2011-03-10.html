Obsessed with Obsession - Ring A

10 March 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/10/11 Monday 22nd February I am Mitchell Hall, and I have OCD It was a sentence that I knew I would have to get used to saying, but right now, it was one that I was struggling to come to terms with. I stood on one of the darker and dingier streets of New York City, leaning up against the crumbling brick wall of the doctor’s surgery from whence I had came not five minutes previously, still trying to digest the diagnosis that I’d been offered… trying to digest the truth… The wind whipped around my ankles, biting at exposed skin and sending a chill right through me – a chill not dissimilar to the one that had descended upon me as I’d heard the Brooklynite opposite me utter those three simple, yet painful letters. O. C. D. Even on their own, without any context to define them, they sounded dirty, ugly. With context, it wasn’t just the words which sounded or felt like this – I did too. "You’ve got OCD." It hadn’t even sounded like a diagnosis when the doctor had informed me of the truth that had, until now, remained hidden; it had sounded like an accusation. Of course, the doc had done his best to reassure me with his facts, figures and support. Information overload, in the hope of confusing me with enough leaflets and helplines so that the ugly truth wouldn’t hit me. But hit me it did, and it hit me hard, after taking just a single step into the brisk New York air. Everything came into focus, everything had to be looked at from a different angle, through a different lens. People would undoubtedly tell me "It’s OK, nothing’s really changed." In essence, they were right, nothing had changed. Yet in my mind, everything had changed… I shook my head, as if physically trying to remove these thoughts from inside it – this wasn’t helping. At this rate, I’d become the first person ever to become obsessed with a disorder about obsession! With an effort, I pulled myself up from the wall and straightened up, running a hand wearily through my shoulder-length hair. Thinking these things through was doing me no favours. I needed to talk, but not just with some patronising, overpaid doctor. No. I needed someone who knew me, someone who understood how my mind works even better than I myself did. Slowly, I began walking down the busy street, in no particular direction, trying to avoid the harried-looking commuters as they made their way home from another busy day of nine-to-five. I pulled my cell phone out of the pocket of my jeans, a piece of technology which was outdated even by my standards, and cycled through the phonebook until I found the number I was looking for. Smiling in spite of myself, I pressed the call button, and brought the phone to my ear. After a short pause, I heard a tiny ’CLICK, and a voice on the other end of the phone that instantly reduced my problems to something barely recognisable. "Hello? Mitch, is that you?" Charlotte. I could almost picture her standing hundreds of miles away on the other end of the phone, leaning casually against the wall of our old Georgian home, her blonde locks falling perfectly haphazardly about her face. A blonde bombshell, whom I’d managed to convince that I was someone who it might well be worth spending some ‘quality time’ with (which, when you see the two of us side-by-side, is no mean feat!) when I was at possibly my highest point, professionally and personally. She’d seen me through the best of times, yet she was also someone who I knew I could turn to when things got rough. In short, I trusted her implicitly, which is something I’d found very difficult to do in my forty five years on this earth. Oh yeah, and she’s damn good in the sack as well! (What?! You wouldn’t want me to lie would you?) "Mitch?" For the second time I shook my head loose, realising I’d been lost in dreamland again. "Yeah, sorry hun. Just wanted to give you a quick call to let you know… well, how things went…" Although Charlotte said nothing, I felt the atmosphere on the other end of the line change tangibly. After a short pause, I heard her soft voice: "… And?" I took a deep breath, considering a question which was both infuriatingly simple and desperately difficult to answer. "Well… I got a diagnosis…" I said slowly. Another pause, longer than the first. I expected a response from Charlotte, but didn’t get one, forcing me to continue on. "And, well… I can’t pretend it’s completely out of the blue, because it isn’t. To be honest, it should’ve been obvious to anyone who had half a brain. How I didn’t notice it before I’ll never know. But we know now… yeah… we know now…" My voice tailed off, but thankfully, Charlotte intervened, her tone still soft and sensitive. "Mitch, hun, you’re rambling. Come on, it can’t be that bad…?" "Depends on your viewpoint, I spose…" I said in a low voice. "And what’s your viewpoint Mitch?" Charlotte replied matter-of-factly. "I don’t care what anyone else thinks, I just want to know what you are thinking." "I don’t know what I’m thinking" came my honest response. "I’m thinking I’m an idiot for not realising it in the first place. I’m thinking that all of our plans might be completely worthless now. Hell… I’m feeling pretty worthless right now…" "But why?!" Charlotte replied with just a hint of exasperation now creeping into her voice. "Look, I don’t know -" "I went in to see Doctor Lawrence." I interrupted quickly, "And I just asked him to give it to me straight. I knew he had the results, but I knew he’d want to do all the bullshit that always comes before a diagnosis. But I didn’t want all that. Like I said, I just asked him to give it to me straight. And tell it to me straight is exactly what he did……" I paused again, breathing out a stream of air that turned to smoke in the cold New York air, before revealing the diagnosis. "I have OCD, Charlotte. Obsessive Compulsive Disorder." Yet another pause, infuriatingly long, allowing me to ponder just what Charlotte’s reaction would be; exactly how would she try to drag me out of this pit of despair that I found myself in? What I got instead was a wake-up call. "Oh well, at least we know" came Charlotte’s reply, brutal in its briskness. My mouth actually dropped open a couple of inches, and I came to a complete standstill, even causing a couple of suited-and-booted bankers to clatter into me as they made they walked behind me on the New York City sidewalk. One particularly angry looking suit turned round to snarl in my general direction. "What the f…? Prick…!" "Shit… sorry! Wait… what did you say?" came my disjointed response. "What? What d’you want me to say?" Charlotte replied. "I don’t see how this changes anything. Sure, now you know exactly what has been causing you to act the way you do. But that’s it. I don’t think there’s any need to get down in the dumps about it." "But I am down in the dumps." I replied. "Oh pull yourself together!" Charlotte said lightheartedly, though I secretly knew she meant every bit of it. I suppose I was just enjoying playing the martyr, but right now, I had no intentions of climbing out of this pit… "Right. OK then." Charlotte’s voice came again, when she realised she wasn’t getting a reaction from me. "What’s changed in your eyes then Mitch? Is it gonna be any different between me and you?" "No, but…" "Right. And is it gonna be any different between you and your Dad?" "No, but that’s not what…" "Right. And are you going to let this get in the way of you making a fucking huge impact at this new company you’re talking about joining?!" "No! Just stop and fucking listen will you?!" my voice rose in volume; it was safe to say that Charlotte had indeed, now, managed to get a reaction from me. "It’s not about any of that." I continued in a low voice. "It’s just… y’know… hard…" "That’s what she said…!" I barely registered Charlotte’s reply, as the jumbled thoughts that had been whirling around inside my head even before Doc Lawrence had said those three magic letters came tumbling out. "Before, you know, before I knew anything was properly wrong with me, I barely thought about it. I knew that I had a few idiosyncrasies, a few little quirks that made me a bit different. But who doesn’t? Yeah, I overanalyse stuff to death. Yeah, when my mind gets fixed on something or someone I find it difficult to concentrate on anything else. Yeah, I have a bit of a thing for hygiene. But so what? I’d learnt to live with it, and so had anyone and everyone who mattered to me. Surely that was all that mattered, right? But now…? I dunno… it just seems like now I know… now everyone knows… it’s gonna be a barrier. It feels like everywhere I go, whatever I do, this is going to be something that could stop me from achieving the things that I want to with the rest of my life. OK, maybe the condition itself won’t do that, but it’s the little things. Like, I know I’m gonna be on the outlook for it now; whatever I do, however small, I’m gonna be looking for the OCD in me. And it’s gonna drive me insane! And I just… don’t know how I’m going to be able to stop that from happening… I’m scared Charlotte… For the first time in my life, I don’t feel like I’m in control of my own mind any more…" For several seconds, nothing but the sound of breathing on the other end of the phone, and the distant sounds of New York City, still in full swing, yet coming to me from afar, as I zeroed in on the solution that I knew, I just knew Charlotte was bound to have. "Listen Mitch, I can’t even bring myself to imagine how hard it is for you right now. But you can’t let it beat you. You just can’t. Problem is, I don’t know how you can do that. Nobody does, Mitch, apart from you. Ignore it? Try to fight it by changing yourself? Or embrace it? Only you’ll know, deep down Mitch, which path is the right one to go down with this. You’ll have to get there in your own time. And you will get there, Mitch, because you’re a strong person. I don’t care what some West Coast doctor says, you’re as mentally strong as anyone." I sighed, conceding the inevitable. "I guess you’re right…" I said quietly. "Besides," came Charlotte’s voice again, encouraging in its tone this time. "You’ve got to sort yourself out pretty damn quick – aren’t you going to that new wrestling federation tomorrow to smooth things over for your debut?" "Yeah, GCW. Pretty big step up for me, all things considered…" "Are you ready?" "Damn right." I said, the competitive fire inside me instantly rekindled at the thought of throwing myself into another hornet’s nest of talent, bullshit and notoriety - a potent combination for any professional competitor if there ever was one. "Yeah, Scorpio’s ready. The question is – is GCW ready for The Blue Scorpion?" ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ Tuesday 23rd February *Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap* Monotonous. Repetitive. The only sound that could be heard as I made my slow progress through the darkness. The relentless ticking clock of fate, perhaps, slowly but surely eating up the seconds until a certain Blue Scorpion finally got the recognition that he deserved. Or maybe the "tap tap tap" was Mitch Hall’s conscience, questioning exactly why he was going into all of this again, instead of retiring for good, and enjoying what was left of life. *Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap* I looked down. "Or maybe," I thought to myself, "It’s just your footsteps, and you need to stop blowing every little thing out of all proportion. Tsk… dickhead." Sure, I was talking to myself as I wandered steadily towards the glorious bowl of memories that was Madison Square Garden, and it would be obvious to even the most dim-witted of individuals that very few, if any, of my thoughts, were rational. Surprisingly enough, however, tiny things like intense psychological struggles and deep moral debates were the least of my worries. Well, at least for an hour or so. No… I had much more important things occupying me at that particular time. First and foremost, I had a job to do. And also, a decision to make. One which had, up until now, divided my ever-so-slightly damaged mind. I continued onwards, until at long last, my destination loomed large in front of me. I looked up and around my foreign surroundings, and knew the significance of entering. By stepping into this foreign setting, I’d be stepping into a different world, with different rules, and a totally different pecking order. Then again, I’ve always liked ‘different’. Maybe this different place could fill the recent void in my life… maybe it could even become home? I smiled wryly at my own clichéd thoughts, before heading somewhat inconspicuously into the arena, for my very first taste of GCW’s flagship event, known worldwide as… well… Worldwide! Not even my 28 years experience of everything that the wrestling world had to offer could have prepared me for the onslaught that my senses were about to endure. Chaos. Bedlam. Absolute fucking pandemonium. Road agents, runners and many other unnamed wrestling jobsworths were buzzing around like some crazy insects on a do-or-die mission, selfishly winding in and out of the mass of humanity, determined to complete the worthless, meaningless task that had been entrusted to them. Wrestlers of all shapes, sizes and ages, in varying states of preparation for the upcoming show, yet all parading themselves around as if they were ‘it’. Sickening. I stared round, my mouth slightly agape, slightly disbelieving of the mess that surrounded me; the mess that I had got myself into… or maybe a mess that I had been dragged into…? Yet even amongst the shouts, scuffles and general craziness, a little voice inside still managed to worm its way in, and make itself heard: "Home sweet home…" Once again, I smiled and shook my head at my own thoughts, allowing my shoulder-length dark hair to fall free from the baseball cap which had, until recently, been keeping it neatly in place. "Certainly doesn’t feel like home right now…" I muttered, as I thought back to better times, and a better place. A time and a place where it felt like my name finally meant something, where I’d finally made an impact on those that mattered; I’d left a little part of myself behind, etched into the fabric of professional wrestling, never to be forgotten. Yet how easily are these accomplishments, these triumphs, forgotten? Who can say how much the name of ‘The Blue Scorpion’ will mean in twenty, ten, hell even five years time?! No, I might not have been sure what this place was exactly, but I knew damn well why I was here – to finally, after nearly 30 years of toiling in obscurity, cement my legacy as an immortal in the great sport of professional wrestling. And on first glance as I looked round at the weird and wonderful array of gimmicks in front of my eyes, I was beginning to think I might not have a hell of a lot standing in my way. Finally, my musings were interrupted by what I could only presume was one of GCW’s finest – pasty-faced, sweating, and looking like he hadn’t washed in a week, Ross (as I was reliably informed just seconds after he scurried up to me) was apparently here to welcome me into the ranks of GCW, and get me acquainted ahead of my first show. Offering a forced smile, I resisted the temptation to crack a joke about the somewhat hostile smell which was currently emanating from my newest acquaintance, and instead allowed myself to be led towards the general insanity of the GCW Worldwide backstage area. Over the course of the next hour or so, I was introduced to a variety of GCW alumni; Steven Caldera, the well-renowned CEO and my new boss, was mechanical and baffling unenthusiastic in his welcome. The long-reigning World Champ, Jay Terror, gave me approximately thirty-seven seconds of his all-too-precious time in order to feign interest in my arrival, and busty backstage interviewer Sally Ford looked me up and down and suggested we organise a ‘private’ interview as soon as possible. Fools. All of them. Insolent fools who had absolutely no idea of what my arrival actually meant for this company, and as such, no idea of the respect that they should be affording me. No matter, clichéd though it might have been, the phrase ‘actions speak louder than words’ was never more appropriate than here and now. They would learn, in time, and along with some new friends, I was planning on educating a hell of a lot of people with just a few, very loud actions, starting at GCW’s biggest pay-per-view, and a mindless clusterfuck of an afterthought that might just be The Blue Scorpion’s fast-track pass to something close to the respect and notoriety that he deserves, wherever he lays his hat! Obsessions? You bet it; obsessed with success. But I’ve learnt, through experience, that there are a hell of a lot of worse things out there to be obsessed with…!
