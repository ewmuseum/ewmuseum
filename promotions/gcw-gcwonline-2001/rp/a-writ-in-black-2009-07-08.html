A Writ in Black

8 July 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 8, 2009 Excerpts taken from a journal… Date: February 16th, 2009 I’ve always wondered about the nature of man. What makes a man into who he is? How many layers do you have to strip away before you get to the bottom of what that man is and what he can become? The armies and intelligence agencies throughout the history of mankind had it right. Strip the man of what he knows, break him down and then build him back up into the perfect warrior. All of us can become the perfect warrior, but in order to really accomplish it, we have to break ourselves down and build ourselves back up. You have to get to those reptilian instincts that have long since buried by thousands of years of evolution and are just struggling to get out. We have become complacent as a species. We have forgotten what it was like to be hunted every single day by something more ferocious than what we can ever hope to become. Our reliance on police and government to protect us may have made the society stronger as a whole, but it has taken the power from our hands, from the hands of the individuals. It has made us weak. It has made us fragile. For years we have been coasting by our lives. Floating in a sea of nothingness, like grains of sand, going with the flow. Its time to break ourselves down. Its time to go against the current. I am here to break that mold that everyone has been living in. I have broken myself down and built myself back up. It is my job to start breaking people down. It is my job to make them writhe in their complacency and then to loathe it. It doesn’t matter who it is, because everyone is the same in my eyes. Everyone is flawed, especially in this crooked, corrupted and self-satisfied business. People run around contented to be the ones the public cheers for and on the other side people who long to be jeered because it makes them feel special in some way. They all revel on the strings that their puppet masters pull. I won’t be held up by the will of anyone. Not by those who pay to see us break our bodies down night after night, nor by those backstage who think they are more powerful than what they really are. I’m an island and I’ll stand alone. You all might consider this my manifesto. A writ in black. Part 1: Identity Crisis As I walked through the deserted street something overcame me. The realization that what I was about to do might not be the greatest idea in the world. It was not even a marginally good one. The hospital (as they liked to call it on the inside) was in an isolated spot, far from any form of civilization. When the town’s Mayor had announced that they would be host to an asylum many years before, there had been a public outcry. The citizens of the town didn’t want a place full of crazy people and killer maniacs close to them and it is understandable. I don’t want a place like that close to me and I was one of the crazy ones. The structure came into view not too long after and I couldn’t help but wince a little at its dreary exterior. It stood there staring at me intently as if welcoming an old friend. I had a lot of memories in that building and none of them were very good ones. I questioned myself for the thousandth time as to why I was coming back to this place which had sucked my life and soul. There was never a straight answer in my head. So I walked on, almost as if marching to the sounds of drums, towards my old friend; my old enemy. Going up the steps towards the entrance was surreal. I had never really gone up those steps. A steel door on the back of the structure was used for the internment of patients into the hospital. I still remember the feeling of uselessness as they pushed me through that door and into the belly of the beast with my arms tied behind my back. Helplessness. They thrive on it in that desolate place. You can never give an inch. You can never let them break you. I never did. The receptionist recognized me as I walked through towards the reason I was coming back. People talk about confronting your demons or some such nonsense. I was not there to confront my demons, but to let them free; to let them roam beyond those white padded walls where I had first come to know them. As the guards checked my body for objects that could be turned into weapons I couldn’t help but smirk inside. It hadn’t been too long since I was on the other side of the security protocol. No more… for now. The room was exactly as it was back then. There was a man sitting with his arms tied to a glossy steel table. He was looking up to the ceiling with growing interest. His brow furrowed when he detected something of interest or at least these would have been the observations made by someone who didn’t know the person bound. There is a sort of rapport that one creates with someone they meet in a place like this. It is a sort of relationship that is hard to recreate in real life. It is almost like those relationships created because of a mutual tragedy. Not quite, though. “It seems to me that the ceiling is trying to tell you not to believe in what the men in coats tell you. They seem to get it wrong every time,” I said in jest and as a way to appease the strange and dangerous mind that sat before me. “Quite right, young man. They also tell me not to trust strange men who out of nowhere come to visit,” he told me with an edge to his voice. His glasses were slightly askew and he was trying in vain to fix them upon his nose they way they should have been. I dare not fix them yet. “I am offended that you don’t remember me, old friend. It wasn’t that many moons ago that we sat together playing friendly games of chess and sometimes hazardous games of the mind,” I countered. I instantly noticed a difference in his demeanor. “Well, well, well, if it isn’t you. My lost sheep! How is a shepherd like me supposed to exist when a member of his flock is missing?” “There are many more minds to guide inside these walls, old friend,” I told him as I fixed his glasses. The Doc, as we all knew him by, had once been a young bright mind in the field of psychiatry. Sadly for his future in the business, he developed an affinity for electro shock therapy. Too many patients passed through his hands before he was institutionalized. Even then, he had been moved three times because he had convinced other patients and even staff members that they had to lobotomize themselves in order to meet god. It’s not too much to say that the state wanted the guy locked away for life. The only thing they could actually do was send him to this place. This was the end of the end. After this place, it was a chair and a nice cozy hole in the ground. “So, how have you been, Doc?” I asked with actual concern in my voice. He had become a sort of mentor and friend since I was interned in this hell on earth. “What is the smell of hope?” I couldn’t help but chuckle. It had always been his way to answer a silly question with another. We had had this discussion many times before. There were questions that had answers and there were questions that just didn’t deserve one. ‘The smell of hope’ was one of his classics. He had a profound hatred of questions that he thought didn’t deserve answers. I learned that the hard way. “Ok… ok… I’ll get right down to business.” We talked then. When you spoke to the Doc it was like a game of tennis or table tennis. If you didn’t keep your end and were defeated too soundly, he would get bored and you wouldn’t get anything out of him. If you were one of the few able to out play him at his own game, you had better be careful. So the back and forth went on until I was able to get all the information I needed out of him. We said our pleasantries and we parted ways. I was exhausted. The dark blue skyline that could be seen through the window announced that it was already past my time to be there. There was one last thing to do. I told the orderly where I wanted to go and he led the way. The room was exactly how I had left it. The bone white padded walls were yellowing after years of use and abuse. I remembered my time in that room. The times I sat in the middle of it with my arms bound behind my back. In that padded room I felt as a modern version of Miss Havisham. The days went by but I was stuck in that decaying state where self-consumption was the inevitable result. Luck was on my side when this place released me. “Hey, can I talk to you for a second before you leave?” I heard a voice ask from behind me. It didn’t surprise me. In fact, it was surprising that it had taken that long for him to talk to me. “Sure thing, doc,” was my answer. I didn’t really mind that much because I was no longer a patient at that place but I had once dreaded to hear that voice. Dr. Jonathan Hill was a good natured man who really wanted to help those patients that most needed him. He was not the head man at the institution; he was much too young for that, but he still went out of his way to try and help. There was a naiveté about him that always made me cringe when I spoke with him. It felt like talking to a six year old. “I still think you shouldn’t leave. I believe that you will revert back to doing what got you here if you don’t spent an amount of time here that will actually help you to get to a place where you won’t help yourself,” he said as we walked towards the front entrance of the institution. “Or others, right?” “Or others, yeah.” “That is what you are really worried about. And you know what, you should be worried. In some weird way, you get me. You understand what I am capable of. If it wasn’t for those friends in high places I have been able to make, I would still be here being drilled with stupid questions every day.” I smiled and put my black cowboy hat on. The receptionist also handed me back my black trench coat. “Don’t worry, Dr. Hill. I’ll make sure not to hurt anyone too bad.” I turned my back to the good doctor and to that prison of the mind and left. I accomplished what I had set out to do. I faced my toughest opponents and survived. That place had taken something from me, but I had regained it. I took it back. That place was in the past now, and while it would always be looming there in the background; it would never have a hold of me any longer. It was time to get started. It was time to fulfill my destiny. It was time for me to destroy those that had driven me to a place where I had lost my identity. It seemed a fitting vendetta.
