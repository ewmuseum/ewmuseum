Pandora's Box

8 September 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/08/2009 "... The next match that night saw the unlikely duo of Cade Carson and SurReal taking on the team known as Mortifera, in what promised to be one of the more unpredictable bouts of the evening. Going in, many believed that, while new as a tandem, Mortifera -- Shaman and Jorge Samuelsson -- would have the slight advantage. However, the team of Carson and SurReal surprised onlookers by appearing, for the most part, the more cohesive of the two, despite the fact that neither men had ever wrestled together before. In fact, if one witnessed the match with no prior knowledge of the four competitors involved, one might mistakenly walk away from it wondering which pair were tag team partners, and which pair had been thrown together specifically for that match. The unlikely duo walked away with the win when SurReal scored the submission victory for his team, with Carson running interference to ensure the submission hold would not be broken. During the course of the match, SurReal looked the more formidable of the two. Carson displayed very little in the way of offense, and in fact, if it weren't for the fact the just recently formed duo of Samuelsson and Shaman looked all out of sorts for most of the match, Carson's sub-par performance might have cost his team the win. SurReal, on the other hand, looked like a force to be reckoned with. At any point that the team of Carson and SurReal held the momentum in the match, more often than not it was SurReal who was the one leading the charge. Whereas Carson looked tentative, shaky even, SurReal wrestled with a confidence that bodes well for his future within Global Championship Wrestling. For Carson, the night ended up being bittersweet. Before he even had a chance to celebrate his first victory in GCW, Carson was brutally attacked by his tag team partner, as SurReal took Carson by surprise with a quick kick to the midsection, and followed up by executing the Jackhammer on Carson. SurReal then exited the ring to a chorus of boos, as Carson lay unconscious in the center of the ring. The two men are scheduled to take part in a four-man contendership match for the Television at GCW's Warpath on September 9, 2009. SurReal's actions no doubt have raised the stakes of that match-up, and now Cade Carson has two weeks to prepare for his vengeance for the man who sent him to the hospital after their match ..." Source: A clipping from the Wrestling Underground magazine - August 26, 2009 edition ~ ~ ~ Cade tossed the magazine article onto the kitchen table, discarding it with a sneer on his face. Ever since that match over a week prior, the magazine article had been part of Cade's daily ritual that he used to keep himself motivated while he trained for his match at the pay-per-view. But now, the anticipation was killing him. Cade was champing at the bit to get his hands on SurReal for real. "He's a dead man," Cade snarled. He looked up at the other person currently in his apartment's cramped kitchen, a tall but scrawny man in his mid-twenties, with square rimmed glasses, shaggy medium-long hair pulled back in a ponytail. Wearing a t-shirt of some obscure superhero Cade wasn't familiar with, and a pair of tattered jeans that looked in sore need of a washing, the man looked like he should be at a comic book convention, standing in line to get his comic book collection autographed. "You can quote me on that," Cade said to him. The man -- he'd introduced himself as Ralph -- stopped chewing his gum and looked down at Cade as though he'd just realized Cade was in the room. He cocked his head to one side. "Pardon me, sir?" "Aren't you going to record this?" Cade asked in annoyance. "Uh, yeah, sure," the man stammered. Reaching beside him, he picked up his small camcorder device and brought it up to eye level. "I didn't know you were ready to begin." Cade rolled his eyes. "Well, I'm ready. So, start recording." Ralph nodded. "Okay, you're on." Cade took a moment to compose himself. He came up with this idea to record his comments to SurReal after his friend, Darius, had joked that Cade should storm down to the local television studio and interrupt the evening news broadcast, tearing into SurReal on live television. While the suggestion was made in jest, and it certainly wasn't advisable -- Cade wouldn't be able to get his revenge on SurReal if he was in jail for the night of their match -- the idea behind it was sound. So, he decided to improvise. Running his hand over his smoothly shaven skull, Cade took a deep breath, let out, and then began. "Did you think I'd just go away?" Cade asked the camcorder, but his words were aimed at SurReal. "Did you really believe your little stunt would be enough to put me out for good?" He snorted in amusement, a smirk blossoming on his ruggedly handsome features. "You may think you sent me a message. When you blindsided me, kicking me in the gut when I wasn't expecting it, and then drove my entire body into the canvas, leaving me unconscious in the middle of that ring, you probably believed that you'd knocked me out of our match at Warpath." "Instead," Cade continued, "all you accomplished was to make me focus that much more on repaying you for what you did. You awoke something inside of me, something that may have remained dormant otherwise." He paused. He stood up from his seat at the kitchen table. He made his way out of the kitchen, through the living room, and toward the glass sliding door that lead to the balcony. Ralph, holding the camcorder as steady as he could, followed in his wake. "You see, SurReal," Cade stood on the balcony with his back to the street. He leaned back on the balcony, propping his elbows up on the railing and adopting an almost casual pose, picking up where he'd left off. "Before you attacked me, I'll admit that I lacked focus for our match at Warpath. Sure, a part of me understood that it was a golden opportunity, that winning it meant earning a shot at the Television title. But there was still another part of me that didn't have the absolute hunger to win it." With a slow shake of his head, Cade leaned forward and wagged a finger at the camera. "Your actions changed all that. Before, I just saw that match as me versus three guys I'm not all that familiar with. Three guys who I don't like, but who I certainly don't dislike. But now?" He smiled wickedly. "Now you've made this match personal." Narrowing his eyes, Cade turned slowly to gaze at the horizon, where the sun was just beginning to set in the distance. Ralph remained where he was, so now the camera focused on Cade's profile rather than straight ahead. "I have a purpose now, SurReal. It's making sure that you pay for what you did to me." He paused, cocking his head slightly to the side. A cruel smile making its way onto his expression. "Do you hear that sound, SurReal? It's the sound of my retribution making its unstoppable charge towards you." Turning to face the camcorder, Cade's chestnut eyes seemed to glitter blood red. "You failed when you didn't put me away for good. I won't make that same mistake. At Warpath, I intend to walk away from our match victorious. And if I have my way, you'll be lucky to even be able to walk again." Cade stood staring into the camcorder lens for what seemed like eternity. Finally, he scowled and said, "Uh, you can stop recording any second now." "Oh," Ralph said, pressing the 'stop' button. "Sorry about that." Cade shook his head. He turned to look out over the balcony again, his elbows leaning on the top of the railing. After a few moments, Ralph joined him, the camcorder still grasped in his hands. "You wanna see how you did?" Ralph asked, holding the camcorder up to Cade. On the screen Ralph had cued the playback of the recording they'd just made. "No," Cade said, shaking his head. He didn't even glance at the screen. Ralph frowned at his reaction, but didn't press further. "Okay. So, what do you want me to do with this?" "Make two copies and mail them off for me." "Who do you want me to send them to?" Ralph asked. "One to the Global Championship Wrestling head office. They might want to use it in any promotional stuff they're going to running as advertisement for the upcoming pay per view." "And the other copy?" Cade smiled devilishly at that. "To SurReal," Cade said. "Unlike what he did to me, I'm not going to blindside him. I want him to know exactly what's in store for him." ~ ~ ~ "So what, exactly, are we looking for, again?" Cade glanced up from what he was doing -- flipping through a dusty book with weird symbols on the cover, and looked in the direction of his friend, Darius Drannen, who was currently holding a similar text in his own hands. "I don't know," Cade responded. "Yet." Rolling his eyes, Drannen said, "Thanks. That was helpful." After Cade's 'interview' with Ralph, he and his two companions had driven to Father Michea's church. Father Michea was away for the week on some kind of official church business, and he'd left Cade the keys to the front doors just in case Cade needed a quiet place to go and meditate. But it wasn't meditation that had brought Cade here today. It was curiosity, sparked by something he'd himself said in the interview with Ralph. During his diatribe directed at his opponent, Cade had made mention of SurReal awakening a drive inside Cade that he was best to have left dormant. Afterwards, Cade thought on that and, for reasons he couldn't quite explain, was suddenly compelled to delve deeper into the past of the man who had become his mentor and paternal figure. Deep within the bowels of the church, Cade knew that Father Michea kept a room full of mementos he'd collected over the years, gifts and trinkets given to him by those who attended his masses, as well as photo albums and video tapes cataloging the years he'd spent as spiritual adviser to this community. Cade had always been curious to browse the basement store room, ever since he was a young boy. But Father Michea had forbidden it. But with Father Michea away on business, Cade could peruse at his leisure. There were a lot of questions Cade had about Father Michea, and perhaps this would provide him with at least some of the answers. "Ah, shut yer bellyachin'," said Thibbledorf Stitskin. The man known to his friends as "Rumple" -- short, powerfully built -- was laying on his stomach, flipping through a third such book. Drannen shot him a dirty look. "I'm surprised you're not the one complaining." Rumple shrugged his wide shoulders. "I kinda like lookin' at these here religin comics." Cade snorted at his friend's choice of words. While the books they were currently browsing through contained a collection of pictures and diagrams, they were hardly 'religion comics'. Drannen chuckled. "I'm surprised you can even read what's written in there. Or are you just looking at the pictures?" Rumple shot him a dirty glare. "Ye just keep it up, Darius." "And, what?" Drannen taunted. Rumple displayed a wicked gap-toothed grin. "And ye'll be eatin' me fist for yer lunch." Cade ignored the exchange of barbed comments that were going back and forth between his two companions. Instead, his eyes were focused on the text in front of him. It was a passage telling the story of a young tribal boy who, at the coming of age into adulthood, was sent into a cave to battle the -- Cade assumed this part, but his translation could be wrong -- 'Demon of One', which was said to dwell in one of the various caves surrounding their village. It was some sort of rite of passage that all males had to take in order for them to go from child to adulthood. The story itself wasn't what had captured Cade's attention, but rather the description of the creature referred to as the 'Demon of One'. Being a religious text, Cade assumed initially that it was just another name given to the Devil. But as he read further, he realized that wasn't the case. Instead, the creature was described as a messenger who had gone astray, and whose false promises were corrupting the thoughts and actions of many who listened to it preach. But before he could finish the story, his concentration was broken by the call of his friend from across the room. "Hey, Cade. You might wanna take a look at this," Drannen said. "What is it?" Cade asked. Drannen held up a photograph. The distance between them made it impossible for Cade to identify the people in the picture, but he could see that there were three people in it -- two men and one woman -- and they were standing at the front of Father Michea's church. As he made his way over to Drannen and got a closer look at the photo, his heart leap into his throat. It ... It can't be. It was a picture of Father Michea, about fifteen years younger, with his arms draped around Cade's parents. His mom and dad looked to be somewhere in their mid-thirties, which, if Cade's math was correct, was around the age when they'd disappeared, vanished without a trace, leaving Cade an orphan. Judging by the clothes each individual was wearing, Cade assumed the picture was taken in the summer time. But Father Michea didn't even know my parents. Cade found it difficult to process the information running through his head. Too many things just didn't add up. For one, the first time he'd ever seen the priest was when his foster parents had brought him to the church that Father Michea presided over. That was long after his parents had ... left him. And he couldn't recall ever attending any sort of service or mass with his parents. While they were genuinely nice people, they weren't the religious sort. Before he could think more on the implications of what he'd just learned, his thoughts were interrupted by the sound of Drannen's voice cutting through the fog of his mind. "Who's the couple with the old priest?" Cade stared down at the picture longingly, his throat suddenly dry. He choked back a tear, hoping his friends wouldn't notice his moment of weakness. Then when he felt confident he could speak without his voice cracking, he replied. "My parents." "Your parents?" Drannen asked, surprised. He peered closely at the picture, noting the closeness of the trio in the photo. "I didn't realize they even knew Father Michea." "Neither did I," Cade answered in a soft voice. He added silently to himself, Looks like Father Michea's got a lot of explaining to do.
