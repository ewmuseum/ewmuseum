Hello, this is OnStar

1 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 1, 2009 Rain. Thick rain spit from the clouds above dousing the highway Jay Terror currently cruised along forcing two hands on the wheel. The wind shield wipers worked overtime sweeping a sheet of water out of view and onto the road beside him. Already puddles had started to form within road dents causing severe side to side swerving. To make matters worse, he noticed a woman's face planted close to her windshield beside him. Shaking his head, he turned up Big L and leaned back in the driver's seat. In all honesty, he didn't know where he was going. Sure, he was supposed to end up in Hawaii at some point, but that didn't mean much. His arrival at Shockwave was inevitable. People expected him to show up and team with Chris and Marcus. What he didn't know was where he was going before making it there. Where was he going to clear his mind. Unfortunately, he wasn't given much time to decide on his destination as he glanced through his peripherals. The woman beside him was now wide eyed and losing control of her beat up Chevy rapidly. One of the puddles caught her tire awkwardly and she swerved right crossing over the dotted line. Within seconds Jay's car and hers collided sticking with one another rolling quickly off the concrete and into the soggy grass. Both cars dipped into a field finding their final destination within large stalks of corn. Pulling his face out of his recently exploded air bag, Jay gasped for air. An eerie silence settled over the wreck broken only by the slight patter of rain drops on glass and metal. Jay was first to move shoving the airbag down to a reasonable level. His hands wiped his face and ran through his hair while deep breaths continued to circulate in and out of his lungs. As he gathered himself he heard a voice. A woman's voice. "OnStar has realized that you have just been involved in a crash. We've notified 911 and help should be on the way. Are you ok?" Jay searched for the voice and found its origin within the confines of a small red blinking dot. He sighed, "Yeah, I'm fine." "What's the nature of the crash?" "Nature of it?" "How severe I mean." "No idea. Some woman driver swerved into me." "Is she okay?" He laughed slightly, "What's it matter?" The woman from OnStar paused a minute before trying again, "Have you seen if she's injured?" "She ought'a be." "Sir - " Jay interjected, "Look, I haven't seen the cooze. For all I know she's on fire. Fact is, I'm not surprised that she couldn't handle her vehicle." Masking her emotions, the operator went on, "Sir I'm only trying to find out the nature of the crash and the situation you two are in. Could we keep profanities to a minimum?" "Sure." "Are you able to move?" "Hardly. There's an airbag up my," he paused for decency, "backside." "Remain calm and still then, emergency services should be there momentarily to help you and her out." "Aren't I calm?" "You do seem it, yes." He grinned again and let out a heavy sigh. A box of cigarette sat on the floor of his feet staring at him like destiny itself. It's red and white face beckoned for Jay but he resisted for the moment. Yes, cigarettes were his niche but they were one niche he meant to toss. "How much do you get paid to stay on the line with me?" he asked while staring at the box of 27s. "Sir, I'm not going to release that information with you." "It'll help me cope with this -" as his tongue extended for the next piece of his sentence the woman from the other car became visible in his window. Her hands pressed against the glass and her eyes waddled back and forth in their sockets as she searched for Jay. "What the fuck?" "Is everything okay sir?" He ignored her and rolled down his window. She was crying which added to the wet mess the rain had made. "I'm so sorry. Really, I don't know what happened and it was slippery and the rain just kept coming and that hole in the road and - I'm so sorry!" Jay cocked his left brow and shook his head, "That was a mouthful." "I know it's just - well, I've never been in a crash before and I just got my license suspended for speeding and this isn't going to help that and," she stopped to catch her breath as a pair of tears slowly trickled down her soft skin, "I don't know what to do." Jay kept his eyebrow raised and backed away from her as he examined the situation. The girl seemed to be no older than twenty five and she obviously wasn't the sharpest tool in the shed. Her makeup poured down her face resembling war paint just below her soft blue eyes. Jay shook his head and grinned, "Get in the car, huh?" "Yeah?" She said surprised. "Just kick through those cornstalks and climb over through the other side." Without hesitation or fear she walked around the front end and into the stalks. The battled her as she squeezed past and opened the passenger door. Her jeans were a dark flavor thanks to the rain and her hair hung over her face like long thick bangs. She pulled it to the side and sized up Jay. "Thank you." "Yeah." "I'm really sorry, you probably have places to go and people to see and I screwed that -" "You talk too much girl." "Jackie." "Jackie?" "My name's Jackie in case you were wondering." "Right. Relax for a minute, yeah?" She nodded, "Yeah, sure. I can do that." Jay sighed again and looked through his windshield. The wipers were doing their job keeping a space clear for Jay to look through. Unlike she thought, he didn't have a place to go or anyone to see. All he had was a free day of roaming to clear his mind and prepare for GCW's resident wenches. "How old are you?" She seemed surprised by the question, "Twenty-three. How come?" "How fast were you going?" "When?" "When you got your license suspended. How fast over the limit did he clock you." "She actually." As if living in the moment again, she stopped to think, "Thirty over in a fifty five." "Have somewhere to be?" "No. I just tend to forget how fast I'm actually going, you know how it goes." "Figured." Her eyebrow extended now with obvious perplexity, "Figured what?" Jay looked back at her now examining the similarities she had with Troy, Windsor, and Valentine. "Woman drivers. They just don't - well they just don't understand driving I guess. Something about missing the vital brain functions to get from point A to point B successfully." Her other eyebrow followed suit and raised allowing her eyes to open as wide as possible. Jackie's jaw slumped leaving a gaping hole. "You really just said that. Really?" "Nothing against you in general. Just, damn. I don't know who hands you people your licenses." Visibly more upset, she leaned forward. "I may have made some bad choices while driving but that doesn't provoke the stereotype guy." Sarcastically, "Yeah, exactly." "Look, I don't know who you are or who you think you are even but this crash could be just as much your fault as it was mine. I mean, who says you didn't make me come across the dotted line?" "Physics dear. Physics." "Fuck physics and fuck you." "Real classy Jackie." Shaking her head, she turned toward the door gripping the handle tightly. As fast as she could make it out, she stepped back into the rain and around Jay's front end. As Jay watched her trod through the soggy grass, his phone went off in the armrest. "Yeah?" Steven Caldera's voice came through the speaker talking in Jay's ear. "I'll be a bit late, having some car troubles." He said something about a plane, "Yeah yeah, so it goes. Any chance you can send me something - helicopter maybe?" Jay watched out the front as three emergency vehicles pulled up beside their cars. The girl was already walking toward them. "C'mon, just grab my location off the OnStar dot you put on this car. Have the thing land in the field here. Not like I didn't run down half the corn stalks already." Seemingly agreeing with Jay, the line went dead and Terror snapped the phone shut. Emergency services headed toward his car. Before he opened the door though, the OnStar voice spoke again. "Sir, I sure hope you apologize to that girl." He swiveled his head, "Don't you have a job to do?" With that he opened his door and stepped out. A police officer approached him. "Hi there, I'm officer Greenly, how are you feeling?" "I'll be better when I'm outta' here." "Fair enough. Any injuries or anything substantial you'd like to tell me to help this situation?" "Women drivers. Revoke all their licenses." The officer seemed irritated by the remark, "Sir, I'm going to ask that you cooperate with us and keep your personal views out of this until it's settled." "Just saying." "If you could share with me how things happened then I'd appreciate it. We have an officer with the woman now and EMTs around in case you need medical assistance." "Two sides of the story?" "There always are." He shook his head and snorted slightly. "Ok, here's how it went. We were both driving side by side basically, it was raining pretty hard. Fact is, she lost control and swerved into my side driving us both off the road into that lump of stalks." "Do you know anything about her?" "Jackie. Her name's Jackie. That's about it though; she's some kind of feminist." "Did you guys talk already?" "Had her in my car, yeah. Just chatted about the accident and all." He paused and looked up from his notepad. Nodding his head he approved. "Ok, if you can wait here, I'll only be a minute. I have to discuss the story with my partner." "Yep." A minute turned into fifteen and by that time the rain had subsided and helicopter blades sliced through the clouds. Caldera Enterprises branded the sky as the chopper swung around landing in the field beside the wreck. All of the officers, four by now, watched as the blades came to a halt. Jay merely smiled. "What is that?" "My way home. Hopefully someone else can assist you guys here." "The officer who had heard the story from Jay stepped forward. "You aren't allowed to leave this scene yet sir, we aren't done here yet." "You got my story." He looked across the field at two men in suits walking his way. "I'm sure one of these gentleman can handle the rest of this." "Sir, I advice you listen to us now." One man in the suit approached the police men as Jay followed the other back to the helicopter. Jackie watched with angry eyes as he climbed into the back seat. The man left over immediately began talking down the officers. Sitting across from Jay was none other than Steven Caldera himself. "What did you do now?" "Woman drivers man. That's all I have to say. Woman drivers." Caldera nodded as if he knew exactly what had happened. As the blades started again, Jay watched from above still seeing Jackie's blue eyes drilling holes through him.
