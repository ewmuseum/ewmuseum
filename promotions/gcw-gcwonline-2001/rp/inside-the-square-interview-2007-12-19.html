Inside the Square Interview

19 December 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: December 19, 2007 “Alright, set up camera A over there and Camera B over there.” The director was pointing in different directions as a crew was setting up video recording equipment. “Barber, the subject is going to be sitting in this chair. So make sure that both cameras will be able to see him. Atkins, move your ass and get those lights hung. This is not what I need this close to Christmas.” A backdrop is being lifted into place as a photographer shows up and takes some light readings. “You, yeah, I don’t know your name, but you see that backdrop over there, that’s where you’ll be. People, we are on a tight schedule. We only have five more minutes before the subject gets here.” The director is really trying to keep his composure. “And where is Paul. Tell him to get to the set ASAP. We need to get this tape to Philly tonight, and we aren’t going to get the interview done unless we get everything set up.” The director’s phone rings. “James Stanton here. Dorian, boobie, where are you”... Don’t call you boobie again. Got it? You’re still at the house?... Don’t do that to me kid, you nearly gave me a heart attack. Well, everything should be ready by the time you get here.” The director hangs up the phone and puts it in his pocket and starts to rub his temples. The lights finally are raised to where they needed to be. “We don’t have time to test the lights, Adkins, just make sure they turn on. People, he’s going to be here in just a few moments.” With that, the door to the warehouse that has been converted to a studio opens up, and with it a rush of old air. Dorian Ryan walks into where the interview is going to take place and takes off his jacket, handing it to a PA. “So, this is where we’re going to be doing it. You know, I have a plane to catch a little later, I have to get down to Baton Rouge before the show starts. So let’s get the photo shoot done quickly, and then we can do the interview.” “Well, I was thinking that we can get the photo shoot in after the interview.” “You take the pictures, and then we talk. That’s the deal that I set up with the representatives from your company. Otherwise, you’re going to be short one article and one DVD interview for your Magazine. “He’s got you by the balls there, boss.” “Barber, stop talking and get that other camera set up. Tell Adkins that the lights are working fine and to get behind the other camera.” After about a half hour of photos being taken of Dorian Ryan in various serious and goofy poses, he sits down in one of the chairs that was set up in the interviewing area. He’s got his jacket back on as Paul Brenner walks up to the set. “Dorian Ryan, it’s an honor. You know, before I took over as primary interviewer, I covered your debut in MSW.” “Really?” “Yeah, it’s good to see that you’ve started to come full circle. Now, I just want to let you know that everything that I ask you is going to be on the record, but I want you to answer honestly.” Dorian turns in his chair a bit and looks at Paul. “That’s fine. Just make sure that my answers don’t look bad when you edit it.” “You’ve got a deal. James, are we ready. OK. This is Paul Brenner with Inside the Square Magazine, the date is December 19, 2007 and I’m interviewing Global Championship Wrestling’s Dorian Ryan. Begin interview now.” Dorian sits up a little and takes a drink from the bottled water that the PA placed on his table for him. “Dorian, back at GCW Dangerous Games, you made your return to wrestling after a near one year absence. What was that like for you?” “You know, there is one undeniable thing about wrestling. It’s that no matter what, if you have impacted the lives of the fans that they’re always going to remember you. The fans had a rather good reaction for being away as long as I have. “People talk about how you can’t go home again. I proved that to be the opposite. I returned to what has become my home within professional wrestling. I came back to GCW when I was given the opportunity to, and when the time was right.” “Where you worried about ring rust, seeing that that was your first match back?” “I didn’t worry about it. Even though I wasn’t actively taking bookings over the prior year, as soon as my release was finalized from Caldera Enterprises, I started to wrestle some smaller indy shows under my old “Prime” moniker. I’d get in the ring about once or twice a month, just to keep in shape. “In fact, I had wrestled an indy show at the Mall of America the week before I got the offer to return to GCW. Now the shows that I wrestled in, the competition wasn’t quite as good as what we have in GCW, and the matches were a slightly slower pace. But there was nothing wrong with that.” “Let’s talk about why you left GCW initially. You were set for a match with Shaman when you just, walked away.” “When I left GCW, it wasn’t because I wanted to. I’m a wrestler. That’s all I’ve known. My older brother was a wrestler for almost a decade before I started lacing up my boots. Walking away from wrestling did not do anything for me. But I walked away because I cared enough for my wife to put things in proper perspective. “She needed help, and my traveling around the country, being on the road sometimes five days a week for different things involving the wrestling business made it hard to take care of her in her time of need.” “Now, I know, after watching a recent World Wide, that your reasons for returning are a very touchy subject. Why is that?” “Because it is. Simple as that.” “What brought about your return to the ring, to GCW?” “I’d prefer not talking about it.” “Eventually, you’re going to have to.” “Not to the media, I won’t.” “Fine. Let’s talk about what looks to be becoming a small rivalry with a former ally of yours, fellow former Ascension member, Tessa Windsor.” “What’s there to talk about” Tessa made her decision to walk out on her Fianc? and his tag team partner in the middle of a match. I’m sorry, but if you love someone and you have a problem with them, the last thing you do is leave them to get hurt. “I was brought up in this world to cherish two things, the people that you love, and your brothers-in-arms. I’ve never walked out on a tag team partner, I’ve never let a partner fall?” “What about Jaden Li?” “Let me finish please, like I said, I’ve never walked out on a tag team partner, I’ve never lets a partner fall to the numbers game out of spite or malice. What happened with Jaden Li was unavoidable. It was a situation where either I dropped him and still had a chance to win that match, or I hold on to him and we both take the plunge and lose. I’m pretty sure Jaden didn’t complain too much when he got his share of the winners purse that night. At least he got out of the ring before the structure collapsed.” “Going along the lines of that, you seem to be a veteran of so-called “gimmick” matches. Let’s take a look at the list of matches that you’ve been in, just in the US. Triple Threat matches, reverse battle royals, regular battle royals, the first ever Dead Pool match, No Holds barred matches, Ultimate X matches, and of course, Scaffold Matches. Is this what you make your career out to be, gimmick matches?” “There are those that say that your career is remembered only by your last major match. Me, I want to be remembered as a guy that is willing to take risks. Willing to take my rival’s personal vehicle and turn it into the Zamboni from Hell. Willing to do whatever it takes so that for even the five, ten, fifteen minutes that I’m out there, that the fans say they had a great show. “That’s what this is, a show. Night in, night out, city after city, I do something that everyone in the crowd dreams that they could do. Do I take unnecessary risks sometimes” For me, they are necessary. “There are two things that a wrestler never wants to become. Crippled, but more importantly, they don’t want to be come an afterthought.” “Can you give me your final thoughts about your upcoming NC-17 match with Tessa Windsor?” “Tessa, my brother taught me many things, so I’ll put this in a way that he would have put it. I don’t want to save you, but if need be, I will destroy you.”
