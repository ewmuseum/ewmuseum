Rebuilding: Tearing Down

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Timeless It’s been a few weeks since the fire that ravaged Dorian Ryan’s life was extinguished. But after that time, losing just about everything, he still hasn’t completely recovered. He finds himself drifting aimlessly through the streets at night. He turns down an alleyway to find a man being beaten by a group of six men. They see Dorian and head toward him. Six on one. Their funeral. But before Dorian is able to fight with these men, a cop car pulls up to the alley and shines its light down it. The six men run off. There will be no fight for Dorian Ryan this evening. Dorian walks towards the man that was beaten to a bloody pulp. The cops scream something at him, but he doesn’t hear. The sounds are muffled. The blood from the man’s face stains the cement. His body is covered in black, blue and red. Dorian kneels down before the man, who doesn’t get a good view of his face. To the man, Dorian looks like the arch angel Gabriel, ready to take him to the other side. “I’m not ready to die.” “I’m not here to end your misery. I’m here to see that you get back up.” With those words, Dorian reaches out his hand and brings the man back to his feet. “Now to get you to the hospital.” “You sure” I don’t want to be a bother.” “I assure you, it isn’t. You need medical attention.” Dorian walks the man over to the cop car. As the man gets in, Dorian closes the door and watches as the cop car pulls off. Dorian then continues his walk down the street. A couple of the men from the alleyway appear before him before he makes it too far. “Well, well, well. Look who it is. Someone that doesn’t know to keep his nose in his own business.” The men go for an attack. Five minutes later, three men are laying in the concrete, Dorian sitting on the stoop of a brownstone, holding a piece of his torn t-shirt over his eye, soaking up his blood. The cops come up and ask Dorian questions about what happened. “So, you said that these three men tried to jump you?” “Yes.” “And they ended up like this?” “Yes” “I don’t believe it.” “Because you probably don’t like professional wrestling. I fight guys two times the size of these guys.” “I watch pro wrestling.” “Well, then you know that a pro wrestler is a better fighter than someone that’s just a street punk. We have years of training. I’ve been in fights with more people than this. And I’ve made money doing it. Now, are you going to continue to ask me stupid questions, book me, or let me go?” “You can go.” Dorian arrives back at his apartment. He sees that there aren’t any messages on his machine. The boxes have yet to be unpacked. He looks at the picture of him and Andrea and a tear wells up under his swollen right eye. Dorian wipes the tear away and goes into the bathroom and turns on the light. He sees that his right eye is nearly shut from the combination of the beatings he had at Dangerous Games and the fight that he was just in. “Damn!” Dorian turns the water on and splashes some of it onto his face. He pulls his torn t-shirt off and tosses it aside. Bruises are very visible on Dorian’s torso. As he stares intensely into the mirror, he sees that some new bruises have popped up. He touches his left ribs, and winces in pain. He then spits a little bit of blood into his sink. “Damn! This is not good. Dorian, you’re a fucking idiot for getting into a fight this close to a title match. Damn it!” Dorian turns the light off and exits the bathroom.
