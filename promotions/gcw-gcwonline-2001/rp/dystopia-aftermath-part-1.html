Dystopia (Aftermath part 1)

Date: Twilight He hadn’t been able to stop shaking since he’d gotten off the phone with Alex. The news had struck hard, harder than he ever could have imagined. A man was essentially dead, and it had been at his hands. Worse than that, only a handful of people knew he was responsible. Just minutes ago he had been ready to reveal the truth, now he wasn’t exactly sure he could. Sheena would be furious, and heartbroken, she might do something stupid like call the police. That couldn’t happen, and not just because Stevens himself didn’t want to go to jail. That’d almost be preferable to living like this. The problem was, what would happen to Alex, Jeff, Cindy, all three had lied to the police about the incident. They’d all be guilty of obstruction of justice, possibly even worse. No, Sheena couldn’t be allowed to go to the police. That made speaking to her all the harder. He should have called her, after all her brother had just ceased brain function, but he couldn’t bring himself to make the call. How can one listen to their lover cry, knowing you’re the reason for her tears? Knowing that you can’t even tell her you’re the reason she’s crying? This was hell. After the call he’d left pleading a need to be alone. Drake would understand, would know they’d speak again soon. Stevens had left the house quickly, headed down the gravel drive, and now wandered aimlessly towards where he’d parked. The walk should’ve taken no time at all but Stevens found himself quickly lost. Everything seemed so unfamiliar, so different from when he’d entered. It was almost as if the world had completely rearranged itself in a matter of hours. The sun was just beginning to set, obviously he’d stated at the Nutt House, far longer than he had intended, or maybe he’d just been walking longer than he’d thought. He couldn’t tell anymore, everything had seemed to move at a snail’s pace since the phone call, while at the same time rushing by with a swiftness unseen by man or beast. Try as he might, he couldn’t retain a thought in his head for more than a few seconds. There was so much to think about, so little time in which to do it, he felt that soon he’d reach a point of overload. Fearing that perhaps his brain would suffer a similar fate to old John Henry’s from too much work too quickly, Stevens stopped, sat, closed his eyes, sought peace. It wouldn’t come, not in seconds, not in minutes, not in hours. Possibly it would never return, he feared that never again would he simply be able to close his eyes, and stop thinking for just one moment. Through closed eyes he saw Lance’s face, covered in blood, eyes glazed over. The air was still, no sound disturbed the countryside, and yet he could hear the screams, just a kid begging him to stop. Other voices came, his friends, his family, desperately trying to calm him down. Nothing had worked, he would’ve never stopped. There was too much rage, too much hatred fueling the fire. It wasn’t until he was lifted bodily, planted on the ground that he came back to some semblance of reality. It had struck him then, the pain he’d caused, the horrible weight of what he might have just done. He’d been scared, more frightened than he’d ever before been. The kid wasn’t dead though, he was breathing, at least that was a relief. Until he found out the kid might never wake up. The fear came back, like a cold hand running up and down his back. He couldn’t imagine feeling more guilty, it made him ill, grief twisting his stomach into knots. At least there’d been hope, the kid could make it, could come out of it at any moment. More than anything else Stevens thought that hope had been the one thing keeping him going, the one reason he was still able to get up and function on a day to day basis. Now, hope no longer existed. Lance would never wake up, never open his eyes again. And it was all Michael’s fault. Sooner or later he’d have to come face to face with that one simple fact. Michael Stevens had killed a man. He opened his eyes to see what on any other day would have taken his breath away. The last dying rays of the sun setting, setting the trees ablaze with colors. It was a glorious moment of the kind that gives poets their inspiration. Just then the breeze picked up, the scent of honeysuckle filled his nostrils. He didn’t belong here this place was too perfect, too serene. He was nothing but an ugly bruise on the surface of a beautiful world. Standing he took one last look around the idyllic setting, and began to walk. He had to be somewhere else, could no longer stand the stifling beauty. He quickly picked up speed as he moved, no longer walking he ran. The idea was moving as far away from this place, as fast as he good. The destination wasn’t important, the only thing that mattered was getting away from this place with it’s promises of picnics and happy lives. Things he didn’t deserve, things he should never have again. It wasn’t long before he felt the dull pain in his chest from the pumping of his heart. He must’ve sat longer than he thought as he felt a stiffness in his legs that shouldn’t have been there, but soon this fell away. He could only run, peace was behind him, forever now, as it should be. As the sun continued to lower everything became gradually darker. He ran into the night. He ran to escape the day. ~To Be Continued~
