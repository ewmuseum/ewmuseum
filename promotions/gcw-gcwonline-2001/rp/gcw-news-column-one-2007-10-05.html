GCW News: Column One

5 October 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5 Oct 07 (K)Night Fall? By Alejandro Fonseca I have been sitting here in front of this computer screen for hours now trying to think up the best way to start this column off. I thought at first to give you a taste of who I am in my debut, going into detail on my schooling, my experience, and why I was chosen for this job but scratched that idea realizing that very few people probably care. You came here to read wrestling news not my autobiography. So I moved onto an idea of just moving right into the meat of this story, screw introductions. Can’t do that either can I? You should probably get to know me a little bit so you can take me seriously shouldn’t you? Well if not then just skip the next paragraph or two. My name is Alejandro Fonseca, born in Columbus, Ohio on May 21st 1986(Yeah I just recently turned 21). I was hired here at Prowrestling.com just a week ago and I was assigned to write articles about a specific federation, Global Championship Wrestling. I should have guessed this would be the fed I was given, after all I did spend a couple of months on the roster back in the beginning of 2007, before tearing up my knee at a house show. Only the truly die hard fans may even remember the moniker I went under, Sero Blake. I learned a lot about the business and the way of life for the athletes in my short time there and am grateful for the opportunity Steven Caldera gave me but it wasn’t meant to be. Perhaps this job is, we’ll see. Well now that you know a little about my back story I can dive into what my column will be about. I met a lot of wrestlers in my time in GCW from Vivica Valentine to Lia Ambrosi to Steven Caldera to Toad, head of security; BUT there was one superstar I never got the chance to meet. LeStatt Knight. The former World Champion had just taken a leave of absence right before I arrived so I was never able to catch a glimpse of him up close which I was kind of disappointed about because some of his performances in 2005 were a big reason why I even decided to get into the business in the first place. In 2005, when GCW re-opened it’s doors, there were a number of familiar faces that chose to come back. Off the top of my head I could name Michael Hardy, Toxic, Octavian, Jason Dread, Tristian Mayhem, Khan and Michael Stevens of those who came back right off the bat and who were well respected in the GCW realm for their contributions to the 2001-2002 run which was incredibly popular. One name that came back as well but to little fanfare was LeStatt Knight. When Knight came into GCW in 2001 he was very young and very, very raw. Not to mention the fact that he was nowhere near the physical specimen that he is today. After doing some research I’ve actually found that the man is about 40lbs of solid muscle bigger than he was in 2001. So while all of the other names, along with many exciting new faces were coming in and expected to do great things, LeStatt Knight wasn’t thought to be much more than filler. Has the wrestling world ever been more wrong? Look I don’t need to run down the specifics because any true wrestling fan remembers what LeStatt did in 2005. He won a mini tournament to become the United States champion which he held onto for (at the time) a record shattering time however it wasn’t just about the length of time he held the belt but the amount of times he defended it, almost on a weekly basis. This fact makes some believe that his reign is the greatest ever, even greater than Vivica Valentine’s impressive 247 day reign. After dropping the title to Trey Tremendous, Knight continued his path of destruction by defeating Jason Dread for the World Title at Dangerous Games in a hellacious “I Quit” match that finally put Knight in the driver’s seat ? and he wouldn’t lose another meaningful match until NC-17 of that year when Michael Stevens (after numerous attempts and FOUR Inferno DDTs) finally ripped the World Title from his grasp. 2005 was without a doubt one the most dominating performances by a single superstar in this industry’s history. But something changed after that loss to Michael Stevens. It wasn’t all together noticeable at first because the man would still throw in a dominating performance here and there to throw us off track but he slowly changed into something far less terrifying. Some say that it was the loss of Michael Stevens as an enemy as the two pushed each other to limits no one else could. Others claim the rise in talent in GCW pushed LeStatt to the back for a long time with superstars like Vivica, Lia, Dawn, and Seymour Almasy taking the spotlight. 2006 wasn’t quit a kind year for his career however he did manage to win back the World Title from Dawn McFarlane. As bad as 2006 was, 2007 was far worse for the most dominant wrestler in GCW’s history. Knight has gotten no direct World Title opportunities in 2007 thus far and blew his only chance at Warpath by getting caught off guard by a school boy from Valentine. From that moment Knight never seemed to put in the effort required and soon it would seem that his desperation took over because then he sold his soul and joined Caldera’s Establishment. This move screamed desperation to me because Knight had ALWAYS been the first to criticize others for taking a short cut to glory and then he signs on to be Caldera’s right hand man? It didn’t make any sense. Their first appearance together seemed totally anticlimactic as well, coming in during a match between Twizzy and Jordan White to screw Twizzy? Sure Twizzy is a legend but this didn’t seem grand enough for such a revelation in my eyes. Since when has Knight or Caldera cared about anything but winning the World Title? I figured Knight would veto this decision from the jump and go right after the two competitors in the World Title match. And it has all went down hill from there. After Jordan White won the Warpath tournament, which Knight was heavily favored in, Caldera really had no choice but the back the youngster. Steven is a business man so him going with the hot hand is no surprise but Knight not really doing anything to help his cause? Big surprise to this long time fan. Yes Knight has gotten inducted into the Hall of Fame and yes he has destroyed Twizzy in this time frame but when you add in a loss to Chris Storm and the fact that he couldn’t even put away a guy like Shaman two weeks ago ? things aren’t looking great for Knight and this past week his actions didn’t do anything to help his cause. WorldWide 75 has come and went and surprisingly Knight had a fairly big part in it. While it was nice to see him back on the show doing what he does best, a certain part stood out to me. When Knight came down to the ring and gave his in-ring promo I couldn’t help but see a man who knows he is being taken lightly and is doing anything he can to try and recapture his past glory. Claiming the number two spot in the battle royal? Challenging a new person every week after Dangerous Games? Why do these things if you are established and secure in yourself as Knight seems to be? The answer? Maybe he isn’t. It has been almost two years now since Knight has been the single most dominate force in GCW’s locker room. He almost appears as GCW’s version of The Undertaker now. Skills may be eroding but hes a strong locker room presence and will always be a big draw for the fans, not to mention the loyalty he has shown to GCW by not leaving in 2006 when the going got tough throughout the fed. Where some superstars left, (Trey Tremendous, Eva VanLorne, Rich Rollins to name a few) Knight stood by what he considers HIS company and I applaud him for that. All GCW fans should applaud him for that but is he really the man to carry GCW anymore? Does he still have the fire inside of him? At 27 years old (He’ll be 28 on November 10th) you’d like to think he is still in his prime but perhaps he wore down trying to be the greatest. Perhaps it’s time for him to step down and allow for the younger studs like Jay Terror and Tyson DeBough to take over. Then again maybe this last gasp is just what Knight needs to get his career back on track. If he can win Dangerous Games and secure his spot in the Main Event to NC-17 then he is well on his way. Maybe. If not and he truly is done, I can only say I am thankful for 2005 and the memories he left all GCW fans around the globe. This is Alex Fonseca Reporting for Prowrestling.com “Your source of Wrestling news”
