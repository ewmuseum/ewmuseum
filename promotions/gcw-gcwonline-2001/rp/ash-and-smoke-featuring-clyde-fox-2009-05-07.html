Ash and Smoke (Featuring Clyde Fox)

7 May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5-7-09, Rampage R2 The illuminated moon decorates the horizon as a single set of footsteps traced the barren landscape. The smell of ash and smoke was quite apparent, even noticeably rising from the ground at several points. The roads beyond were pathways to nothing, as mere fields grew between the squares of the town that once was. Towards a hilltop, several lines of gravestones lay carefully maintained, with smoke rising from the ground all around. A figure splits through the smoke ever so carefully. Scuffing his shoes along the broken pavement, Clyde Fox strolls down the lane. Head hung over and eyes drooping down, Clyde can't help but feel like the kid who always gets left out during recess. “Why does something bad always have to happen to me? What did I do to deserve this...?” Fox posts up by an aged slab of rock. Pebbles and dirt tumble down the side as Clyde leans against it. It wasn't bad enough that he had to experience the loss of a friend by himself, let alone the solitude of such a monumental site. Fitting, yet at the same time it was so very depressing. “You’ll want to be careful there,” a voice from the shadows surprises the already nervous Fox. He turns around to see the silhouette of a taller, long-haired individual with a coat flapping in the wind behind him. The shift in the clouds inhibiting the moonlight break just enough to allow a solitary beam to identify the stranger as the Elven one himself, Lantlas Anduril. “Life may seem so absolute to one who perceives only nothingness around him, but a spirit is still identified by that rock,” Lantlas informs him. Frightened, Fox fidgets with his hands as his sweat-stricken forehead reflects the moonlight. He quickly turns away from Lantlas in a shy but confused manner. “Okay... do it. Do what you have to do to me. Just make it quick.” Fox pushes his derriere out as he trembles in fear. He thought he was going to be safe all alone here. Little did he know, he still was. Slightly confused, Lantlas takes a small step back from the trembling Fox with two hands raised in the air. “You must have me confused with someone, albeit I don’t know many other living creatures would be found in this particular part of the world… You requested my presence, and I was merely and punctually responding to your call, though it escapes me what could possibly have given you the reason to contact me.” Embarrassed, Fox exits his weird posture. He grinds the grass beneath his shoes as he turns back around and faces Lantlas. Fox examines him closely... pointy ears, elf aura, friendly eyes... clearly Fox had made a mistake. “Sowwie. I thought you were the mystical rape elf.. I know you... you're Atlas Underarmour. Thanks for coming. I just could use someone to talk to... I'm too old for kids help phone.” Lantlas’s is clearly feeling disrespected at the insinuations and the mispronunciation of his name. A hearty sigh escapes Anduril’s lips as he shakes his head, walking towards a steaming fissure in the cemetery between rows of stones. He suddenly turns around with a fierce step in Fox’s direction. “Kids help phone? Mystical rape elf? Is this some kind of joke? I’ve taken enough jokes over the years. What exactly is it when you look at me that you come to the conclusion that I’m some kindhearted individual who wants to help people? Are you just wasting my time for a cheap laugh?” Lantlas lets several hard sighs, and turns back around. He kneels towards one of the stones, clearly dated and crumbling with the effects of time. His long hair dangles in the wind, covering his face. Startled, Fox backs down. He cuts the cute routine and summons his serious face. Unmasking his true feelings, Fox walks over to a similar crack in the ground streaming hot air. He places his hand over the open sore, but quickly pulls it back in pain as the steam scolds it. “Ouch…” Fox says as he sucks his fingertips in sadness. It was just another excuse for him to create some sort of self blame... self pity... self anger towards everything.. The steam continues to blast upward as a very rattled Fox collects himself. “Look man... sorry but I think we got off on the wrong foot. I'm just very unstable right now, especially with the passing of a great friend. But here... this place makes me feel like I'm at home. I mean sure, there's big rocks and boulders all over the place but really... this place is ugly. And I feel just as ugly,” Fox admits. Anduril turns his head slightly in Fox’s direction. “Home…” he muses as he rises to his feet again, and begins to pace along side the markers. “These people once called this town home, as did many who no longer reside within the limits. By all accounts it should be ugly; very few humans still remain here. I find it strange that a human such as yourself would feel at home in a ghost town, almost a place with no life in and of itself as you grieve for your friend.” Several feet away, Lantlas turns toward Fox as the moonlight shimmers off the sword at his side. “Normally I see humans exaggerating their mannerisms in order to receive more attention. It seems to help sear their wounds and give them some pity simultaneously. Yet you… Such an interesting case…” Lantlas continues walking along the next row, passing by Fox. With his back now towards the moon, he glances down at the ground, noticing one of the markers near him is of a girl who lived to be only twelve. He removes a small blue flower from his pocket, and places it at the young girl’s grave. He then watches it float away in the breeze. “You come seeking the aid of a stranger, all but a leper within the business…” Lantlas continues. ’Someone who barely knows of the existence of your friend. Now you’ve opened up to me, your true feelings that you mask with feigned stupidity, yet you feel at home here. Quite interesting to me, I haven’t figured you out yet. The only conclusion to which I can come is that you find some sort of solace in that you must think I’m aware of death and its presence...” Lantlas mumbles off a bit, before turning back a bit toward Fox. “That, or you’re playing a joke, which considering the lack of any human authority within several miles, would be a really bad decision on your part.” Somewhat dumbfounded, Fox pulls himself back and tries to make sense of everything. The setting couldn't be more perfect. This was meant to happen. Fox was here for a reason. THE reason. He was at the point in his life when he knew he'd have to face things head on. He had come to that fork in the road. Left? Right? It was time for the young Fox to decide. “Ghost town... heh. True. I'm not like other humans. In fact... I don't think I am one. But that's another story.” Like never before Fox's words flowed in utter brilliance. “I feel I can trust you. I don't know why.... I'm not joking, my pointy-eared friend. Ever have the feeling that a situation just feels... mmm... right?” “I really hope you’re not coming on to me, that’d be creepy,” Lantlas finally released a bit of tension with a small chuckle, quite out of character for the normally silent warrior. Lantlas’s steps returned in Fox’s direction. “I don’t know you, and I don’t know what you’re going through, but it’s not as if you have to worry about me running to the rumor forum. In case you didn’t notice, I don’t interact much.” Lantlas turns to see the flower drift back along the path of his feet. As he kneels down a few feet from Fox, he twists the stem of the flower in between his fingers, and then lets it go in the wind again. “There’s more to this than losing your friend, isn’t there? It isn’t just grief you’re suffering. Tell me what ails you.” Watching the flower spiral around in the air like a graceful tightrope entertainer, Fox gathers his thoughts. He then reaches up and plucks the flower out from the air. “You know...” he begins, “I've always been different from everyone my whole entire life. The outcast. The loser. The loner. I mean, I've had friends but I never really was liked. I guess losing someone who showed a little interest makes me feel like I lost a lot more.” Fox huffs and accidentally blows the flower from his grip. It flutters off quickly as Fox is left with nothing. “I want to be normal. If not normal... I want people to like me for who I am. What about you? Do you ever feel that way?” “The curse of being immortal is that you’re forced into that position. You surround yourself with those who love you, or who at least make you believe they do, but the sad reality is that eventually they will be gone, and you will still be here. It’s being outcast by birthright, loner by lifespan… Being liked loses its appeal after you’ve existed through generations of careless humans who betray each other on a whim for a cheap high or a slight gain in their professions. You see things like that and lose the interest in being desired by them.” Lantlas continues, walking through his beam in the moonlight. “There’s no use in wanting people to like you for who you are, because it’s not going to change regardless. And when life is linear, when the horizon never changes, and the days run together in their never-ending monotony, you’re left with the only thing of which you’ll never run out… Time. That is the gift of humanity, of mortality. You’re given a specific amount of time to make the choices you will, to love the ones you love, and be who you want to be, and then you’re left with a stone like this where those you’ve affected can visit you and remember you, pass down stories through generations.” Lantlas motions to the first stone that Fox nearly destroyed. “Even when the town is but ash, here still will you be, preserved and respected, as your friend has now discovered through his transcendence of life itself.” Fox is left in awe as he slowly pieces it together. He looks around him... stones of remembrance everywhere. It doesn't even have to be a monument. Fox could find meaning in the most irrelevant of things. Tree branches could hold the essence of life reaching skyward for its potential but never quite making it there. “Wow…” Fox's head turns on a dime as he realizes the futility he holds in his body. Self realization or not, it would make Fox a force to be reckoned with regardless. “I don't want to betray anyone. Especially myself. I wuv myself. I... I should show it more. Embrace life. Like you said; my time is limited. I should probably go... I have so much to do! So much... to live for!” As Fox returned in the direction from whence he came, Anduril remains stoic in the beam of moonlight. The flower he released into the air in memory of his fallen daughter brushed up against him a third and final time, and Lantlas falls to the ground. “I love you, Emerald,” he whispers into the air as he releases the flower once more. As he fades back into the darkness of the town once known as Centralia, dawn awaits the return of life and light to the world. Lantlas’s hope was that brightness could return to his own life as he had helped Clyde Fox to find within himself.
