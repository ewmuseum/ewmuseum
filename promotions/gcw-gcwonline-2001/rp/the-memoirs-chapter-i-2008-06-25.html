The Memoirs, Chapter I

25 June 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 25, 2008 The following is an excerpt from the memoirs of Chris Bagwell. All of the proceeding was written by Chris Bagwell, himself, in a private setting. It expresses the personal views of Mr. Bagwell and is completely confidential. The information provided below is not to be reproduced nor republished under any circumstance. ---- June 25th, 2008. There comes a time in every person's life where they reach a crossroad. They are faced with two absolutely appealing alternatives, but both with specific consequences. Each path on the crossroad would bring glory, but with pain. And most importantly, either direction would end up completely changing that person's life. I'm at that crossroad. It was only four or five years ago that I reigned at the top of the game. I'd beaten everyone there was to beat and I'd accomplished every achievement one could want to accomplish. I made the decision to leave the career behind that had filled up my bank accounts. I made the decision to return to a normal life... A boring life. I'm still not sure what brought me back. I've asked myself that same question each and every day for the last six months. I was set financially and really hadn't much else to prove.. or so I thought. When I spoke to Steven on the phone, I'd intended on keeping a low profile in his company. I didn't want the spotlight or the big matches. I didn't want title belts or to be involved with high profile matches. I wanted a paycheck. I wanted to sit in the back of the locker room, collecting a paycheck, and just be a filler for an already stacked show. I had no problem putting people over. I was willing to do the job and to give some of these younger guys some credibility. But this became a sport where credibility wore thin. It was like a silk dress in a tornado. Any single person's credibility could be shattered with one fierce blow. And I had too big of an ego. When it came right down to it, my ego was what got in my way. I'd been at the top before. I'd tasted the spotlights and loved the view from the highest step. I couldn't help but want it again.. want it more.. once I got another little taste. And so here I am. I'm at a crossroads. I never dreamed of coming back into this game and being able to overcome some of the places' legends. My debut here, although notable, was hardly impressive. I came up with the short end of the stick during a tournament designed specifically to propel one of us new guys to the top. I dropped the ball -- there are hardly any excuses. And for some reason, unbenounced to me.. The ball rolled back. It rolled right back into my hands and it allowed me to take another shot. Like any other male with a little dirt on his ego, I took full advantage of that shot. I had to tie the score. I keep telling myself that I did it for personal satisfaction -- just to know that I can still do it. But that was all bullshit. My Rampage loss to Andy Murray put me in the EXACT spot I wanted to be in when I signed the papers with Steven. I didn't want the fame or the opportunity. I could have slipped underneath the radar and fell into the cycle of wrestling dark matches. All the while getting paid. It was like collecting a welfare check. All I'd have to do was sit around on my ass and let the people putting money in my pockets laugh at me a few times a month. It was Easy Street, America and I had just gotten offered a real estate deal of a lifetime. But I'm addicted. Its that God-damn adrenaline. I felt the rush years ago and I started feeling it again. I loved hearing people curse me off or get really upset over the things I'd say or do. I loved being able to affect people's lives merely by living my own. I loved the absolute disgust these people would develop over a guy they'd never known. I loved the attention. And the attention fed me the adrenaline to satisfy my addiction. And my addiction has launched me forward. It's launched me to an area of Global championship Wrestling that I'd never ever intended on going. It's put me damn near in the main event. And so I'm at a crossroads. In four weeks, I'd accomplished more with this company than I had in the previous six months. I can't help but reiterate my victories over Primates, Valentine, Murray, and then Mayhem. I'm building myself up to be something -- or rather, someone -- worth fearing. I've become a legit threat to the top guns. I've become a slow developing rash on the Championship belt. And I've earned credibility. Its ironic how you can spend years upon years to build your credibility up, and then the instant you back out of the limelight -- it vanishes. People forget who you were and what you did. Your name may still ring a few bells, but its nothing more than a distant memory for most. Then it all comes back. I felt like I just snapped my fingers and I was back where I was. To some, I was a guy who climbed up the ladder in less than six months. I was skipping rungs. But to me, I was just feeding my hunger and feeding my high. The same credibility I wanted to help other younger guys build began building within me. My career has become quite the paradox. I don't suppose there is anyone to blame but myself on this one. It was me who signed the paperwork. It was me who couldn't accept defeat. And hell, it was me who went after a man's ex-wife. And there came my crossroad.. Jane. I may not be liked because I'm an asshole. And there's a ton of truth to that. I'm a jerkoff, a dickhead, a genuine asshole. I pretty much only care about myself and my own personal well-being. But beyond all the black in my heart, there's a soft spot. I can't ruin lives. I sympathize with Andy over his divorce. I know what its like to lose the person you care the most about. I remember how it felt to wake up every morning and to kiss her -- and then for her to just stop being there. I never thought I could relate to a guy like Andy, but I really can. And, unfortunately, I did that by sleeping with his ex-wife. You could add the word scumbag to my list of names. I made it very personal. I'm practicing the art of taboo behavior with her. I absolutely hate that I did it. And thus why I need to make a career-altering decision. I don't think I could live with myself knowing the kind of emotional hell that I'm putting Andy through. But I just love the rush it gives. I know that if I push forward, I'm going to ultimately get hooked. I'm going to crave more and more attention. I'm going to crave more hatred and crave more anguish. I know that if I push forward, I'm going to ultimately return to the man I once was... A full blown self-centered sadistic sonuvabitch. I know it. I can already see myself changing. I'm already becoming more and more willing to do whatever it takes to get into someone's head and to get under their skin. When I was wrestling Mayhem just this past week, I'd whisper in his ear how I'd violate Lia. He's got an obsession with her. And each and every time I'd whisper something, he'd go off. And each time he went off he got sloppier and eventually gave me the perfect opening. I couldn't match him physically, so I took him down mentally. It felt so good doing it in the ring. I felt like I had such power. But I felt like shit afterwards. I hated that I had to exploit his emotional weakness for the sake of winning a match. I hate it because I know it doesn't end when he walks out of the ring. It probably won't end for weeks. It'll linger in his mind, eating him away. Mayhem was my punishment. Bryan Mayhem was supposed to pummel me. He was supposed to knock me around the ring and make me remember why I hung up the boots. Mayhem was supposed to ruin my momentum and set me back in my place. Mayhem was my punishment for what I'd done with the whole Jane saga. But I punished him. And I'm so afraid of those tactics taking over. The more I talk about it, the more fearful I grow. But, strangely enough, the more accepting I become of what has already consumed me. I'm a cradle of contradictions. And now they feed me Shaman. Another legend. Another icon. Another massive beast. Another attempt at punishment for the nonsense I've pulled. Is it sickening that I actually want Shaman to rip me apart? That I'm hoping he'll leave me a bloody heap? I need a reason to escape from this profession once and for all. I need to get out soon. Get out now. But the thought of letting myself lose to a guy who's culture has him wearing headresses and feathers just makes my stomach cringe. Or, rather, should I say my ego. It just won't let up. I feel like there's an alter ego inside of me that encompasses my own personality. And there are even times when I feeling my personality getting choked.. Strangled. Just the thought of all this is starting to give me that rush. My leg is twitching while I write, and my body growing extremely antsy. All I can think about is my last altercation with the big Native American. All I can think about was... is my impending revenge. All I want to do.. is crush him. There comes a time in every person's life where they reach a crossroad. They are faced with two absolutely appealing alternatives, but both with specific consequences. Each path on the crossroad would bring glory, but with pain. And most importantly, either direction would end up completely changing that person's life. I've decided on the path I'm to take. ---- This has been an excerpt from the Memoirs of Chris Bagwell.
