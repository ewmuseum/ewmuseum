Forever May Not Be Long Enough

28 August 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8-28-09 It was an aura of solitude. The time passing into midnight could only mean the arrival of a dreadful anniversary. Through the pale moonlight peeking ever so slightly between the trees, the reflection on the trickle of the slow river could be seen. Thus the stream of life, where a colony of the species once existed, the last of a bloodline lay upon his knees in sorrow. The last was me, the species were the Elves, and I, for the first true time in my existence, was alone. At any moment, to feel the emotion I once could've achieved simply by looking into a set of eyes, I now struggled simply to maintain the composure I once sought. Staring at the pair of eyes looking back at me, I merely saw emptiness. What had I become? Drifting away in the silence of the moment, there she was again… Lying lifeless in my arms, her chest not rising and her heart not beating, the color vanished from her adorable face… Why had the world taken away such a beautiful little girl with such a long life ahead of her? What lesson was there to be learned in this? One could argue that there was a plan, but in what logic of any plan does a little girl lose her life after nearly escaping the trenches of death at an even younger age? I remembered her, jumping about after every match like she’d just seen the pinnacle of achievement. She adored her Ace Anderson doll, even after he’d gone far by the wayside psychologically and performance-wise. Even my enemies couldn’t help but smile every time she would go skipping by, her braids in pigtails and a little tune dancing away from her tiny lips… It was hard to even want to be a part of this world anymore… A world without her, a world without hope, and a world without the most precious life I’d ever known. Opening my eyes, the water trickling down to almost a standstill, I took in the surroundings. There was a slight breeze grazing the back of my hair, and the leaves on the many trees whistled along with the passing gusts of air. It was a warm summer night, and yet for some reason, I suddenly felt a chill. … “The deal was 5200.” “Yeah well, it’s 6000 now,” a tall, blonde-haired man in a suit informed his darker, shorter client. The expression on his client’s face was not exactly one of content. Though smaller in stature, the impression the darker man gave was not one of helplessness. There was a certain aura of confidence that could not be ignored, and a casual, knowing smile crept across his face. “So short-round, you gonna pay up or what?” “You know, there are several rules of business perhaps you should’ve learned before getting in too deep,” the client snickered. “I didn’t come here for a lecture, I came to get paid,” the tall man snapped. “There are two things you should really know about being a businessman,” the client continued, ignoring the remark. “The first is never changing the price at the time of the transaction.” “Oh, and what’s the second, wise-ass?” Instead of responding, the client just smiled again. As the tall man tilted his head trying to figure it out, the client let out a bit of a chuckle. “What?” “Too late,” the client laughed. Before the tall man could react, a powerful strike knocked him into the brick wall, and he slunk to the ground unconscious. The client reached down, pulled the cash from the tall man’s jacket pocket, and grabbed the suitcase he was carrying. “The second rule is learning who your business partners are before you fuck with them!” he screamed. The client turned toward the dark alley, anticipating the appearance of his unidentified cohort. “Gonna stick in the shadows all night or what?” Without warning, a fist suddenly flew at the client’s face, bashing him right across the jaw. “Where the fuck have you been?!” a familiar female voice of rage screamed out from the darkness. Immediately recognizing the possessor of said voice, the client shook off the blow to the cheek and smiled through the slight pain. “Nice to see you too, Sis.” Walking out from the shadows, Devon Drake gave the fallen business associate a kick in the gut before she stood face-to-face with her brother. “Tomas, I’ve been looking for you.” “How did you know I’d be here?” Tomas inquired. “It’s three in the morning, I figured where else would you be? Setting up situations where you know you’ll have the chance to kick someone’s ass, making business deals that you know they’ll powder or try to take advantage of it…” “That’s what I do,” Tomas cackled. “Except now, they can’t touch me.” “So I’ve heard, Judge Eldenshade,” Devon replied with a bit of a sneer. “I’ve also seen bits and pieces of you on the internet here and there,” Tomas continued. “I noticed you’re still using your adopted name, and that Lantlas fella is still your managerial expertise, is he not?” Devon turned a way for a second, dreading the topic arising as she knew it would. “Do I still manage the pointy-eared wonder? Of course I do. I’d hardly call myself an expert anymore though.” Tomas motioned with his head for the conversation to continue as they walked away from the scene of the crime. “While I usually find the suffering of others to be somewhat entertaining, you are family after all, and while I haven’t met the guy, I know what ails him.” “Tomas, you really don’t have much of an idea,” Devon shook her head in disagreement. “I realize you’ve kept up with what happened with his daughter, but to know everything that happened before that just truly is a hint to what must be going on in that head of his.” “What do you mean?” Tomas asked. “The guy has lost everything, Tomas,” Devon explained. “I screwed up, and then Neko messed his head up. He lost his daughter; his career fell apart several times, although he’s managed quite well considering the circumstances… There was the whole Jade thing, and then between Chances, Adrienne, and me, we finally got him to go home, but then this little bitch Christine made his life there miserable too. Then, on top of it, he lost his daughter, the one true unconditional love he held in his life.” Devon paused for a few seconds, remembering fondly the love Lantlas held for that little girl. She also recalled her own status as Auntie Devon, and how she’d look out for her when Lantlas was doing what Elves do, whether in battle or elsewhere. She too had grown attached to the little one, but through her natural way of healing, she’d never let anyone know it of course. “Seems to me that not all is lost,” Tomas hinted. “What did you say?” “What’s to say this little token of information I happen to have on the guy who’s responsible for it won’t improve your standing with the guy?” Tomas smiled again. “Wait, information on the guy responsible?” Devon reacted, confused. “You’re forgetting of my status so quickly, Sis,” Tomas grinned. “You think when I heard about the ‘mysterious’ death of a 12-year-old girl that I wouldn’t be looking into it?” “Tomas, you son-of-a-bitch…” “That’s me,” he responded. … Covering up a bit after the cool chill hit the small of my back, I noticed my hair waving a bit in the front, despite there being no wind anymore. Also, for some reason, the night seemed to get darker, yet the moon hadn’t faded behind a cloud. Something didn’t quite seem right. I saw a flash before my eyes, and I suddenly knew I wasn’t alone. There was something, or someone, in the night around me. As I rose to my feet, standing across the river from me was none other than Neovan, my long deceased brother. Shaking my head to make sure I wasn’t seeing things, my brother was still clearly standing there after I went through the measures to assure myself this wasn’t a dream. “I know, it’s been awhile,” Neovan casually greeted, as if nothing had ever happened. “Neovan… What… What are you doing here?” “I’m always here, bro,” Neovan replied. “You don’t think that once I helped you a few years ago that I’d be gone forever, did ya?” “Well… yeah, kinda…” I sputtered. Seeing my brother had me in a bit of a speech bind. Before I could come up with another stupid question to ask, it was clear he hadn’t come by himself as two more figures stood at either side of him. It was Captain Jeremiah and Lenina, my adopted parents, standing with their only biological son. The one thing that was clear to me out of this was that my mother had passed on since we’d last seen her leave the air force base ten years ago, and was finally reunited with the man she loved with all her heart. Nice to see that kind of things work out for some people, anyway. “Hello, son,” Captain Jeremiah greeted. “It’s been a long time,” Lenina added. “We’ve missed you so much, Lantlas.” “Mom… Captain… When…” I stuttered some more, unable to form a comprehensible sentence yet again. Though it’s not every day someone gets to see their reunited dead family standing before them, I was still disappointed with my ability to verbally express my feelings of the situation. “When did I pass away?” Lenina finished my question for her. “Yes, Mom. When?” “The minute your father died,” she responded, surprisingly with a smile on her face as she gripped her son’s arm. “Don’t be sad for me, son. We’re all together now, as we were meant to be.” “Yes,” Captain Jeremiah agreed. “Eternal love knows not even the bounds of death, and our son has watched out for his brother as his brother once cared for him once they were alone. We couldn’t be more proud of what you were able to do in your situation, Lantlas. We mean that.” This was all too much. They were proud of how I handled the situation… Proud… Handled… Situation… “Proud of how I handled you abandoning us, mother?!” “Watch your tongue,” Neovan warned. “No, I’ve waited a long time to say this,” I disregarded his request. “Now that you’ve passed into the realm of existence where your long lost love has transcended, and your son is there, and the whole entire Anduril family can be together in harmony and bliss, you have the nerve to come back here and tell me how you’re happy, how you’re so proud of the way I was able to handle being left on an air force base at a young age with a little brother and no money because you couldn’t handle the responsibilities of your own children?! You’re proud of the fact that I survived hungry nights, sometimes making sure that Neovan ate when I couldn’t, and the only way I was able to escape that place was to join an organization, a profession, that allowed us to be physically dissected by German professional wrestlers at their will…” “And from that, we were able to make it to America, to find a profession that sought something different, something unique… I became able to support my brother, despite his own shortcomings in our industry of choice, and it only took several years of hell to accomplish. This didn’t happen as a test of will, Mom, this happened because you left us alone and I had no choice! Death was the other option! I didn’t survive for you, I didn’t keep my brother alive for you, and I did it despite you!” The three remained stoic in their presence, not even hinting at emotion at my verbal tirade of their shortcomings. “You feel better?” Neovan asked. “No, I don’t,” I admitted. “Of course you don’t,” Lenina snapped back. “Because I did the exact same thing you’ve done countless times throughout the last few years.” “I never abandoned my children!” I screamed. “Didn’t you?” Captain Jeremiah inquired. “Were you not out wandering the night while your friend Devon kept an eye on her, so you were able to deal with the things that had occurred in your lifetime? Did you not rely on others to keep an eye on her while you progressed in your chosen profession, cementing your warrior status and the calling of your own heritage? And when you felt like you could no longer go on, did you not rely on her little heart in order to find a reason to continue living?” “I never walked out on her,” I yelled as I jumped the river and tried to swing at my father. I found myself instead grasping at air, as the three of them had vanished. “You didn’t have to physically disappear in order for you to abandon her,” Neovan answered my fury. “But you know what, no father is perfect, not even an Elven one. After all, you know who I really am.” “She meant the world to me,” I cried out. “Emerald was my little girl. She didn’t deserve to be taken so young.” “Neither did my daughter with Eldarin,” a new voice responded. Out of the corner of my eye, I saw a long white flowing dress slowly approaching the circle of Anduril. It was Narenwyn, the beautiful Elven wife of Eldarin back from the days of origin. “Little Ellie died even a bit younger at the hands of bloodthirsty humans. Her spirit was trapped within Emerald’s body from the day she was born, and deep down I think you knew her fate was sealed. You couldn’t have saved her more than you did, Lantlas.” “Why not? Why couldn’t I have saved her?” I pleaded. “Being of the Elven race, it is so easy to forget about fate when it does not apply to you,” Narenwyn explained. “Emerald, though possessing the spirit of the young Elven girl, was herself human. Humans fight their entire lives to avoid their fate, but as you’ve seen in your own experiences, the lines cannot always be shed. Her story was written before she even lived it, Lantlas.” “But in her time on Earth,” Neovan interjected, “you took what could’ve been a life of torture and abandonment, and gave her something beautiful.” “What was that?” “Lantlas,” Lenina voiced in response, “Emerald spent five years of her life with a father she loved more than anything. She had a family, one she adored more than anything, and she died knowing that there was no one in the world who her father loved more than her. The circumstances were unfortunate, but she died happy, Lantlas. She really did.” … “So let me get this straight,” Tomas began, “you loved the guy, but couldn’t be with him because your Elven counterpart, who is also technically you, told you that it was a bad idea?” “Yes, Tomas.” “So you cheated on him and broke his heart, I suppose in order to make him stronger or something?” Tomas again asked, very confused. “Yes, Tomas.” “And then, when the two of you got closer together, you palled around with one of his enemies and almost paraded it in front of him after he had lost Jade?” Devon turned to punch her brother in the face again, but Tomas was quick enough to catch her wrist in time. “You’re lucky you’re my sister or they’d never find you,” Tomas smiled. “You’re lucky you’re my brother or they wouldn’t have anything to find,” Devon snapped back. “And to answer your question, Lantlas should’ve known me well enough to know that B Boy was a mere plaything, a toy… Like someone that dumb would ever mean a damn thing to me.” Tomas’s smile disappeared and grew quite cold. He stepped in front of her and they stopped walking as he looked her dead in the eye. “Did you ever stop to think that in his wake of tragedy, logic may not be the most prevalent force working up there? Maybe as he was attempting to mend his broken heart, all he saw was you with another guy, and it didn’t cross his mind that he was just a, how did you put it, a ‘plaything’. Possibly the details didn’t matter to him, and seeing you with the guy was enough to break him up inside.” Devon was about to respond, but realized she’d been beaten. When it came to getting what she needed, Devon was never shy about fulfilling her desires, but it had never crossed her mind that Lantlas may not have seen it that way at the time. “You know, a few weeks after that happened, he shot an arrow at someone that went after me… right in the middle of a crowded arena. At the time, all I could think of was what a crazy Elven bastard he was, but it never occurred to me that it could’ve been more than that.” “The guy shoots an arrow at someone who tries to harm you, and that wasn’t hinting enough?” Tomas laughed. “Like you’ve never done anything that a girl hasn’t understood,” Devon sarcastically remarked. “Of course not, who the hell do you think you’re talking to,” Tomas boasted with a cocky grin. “I’m Tomas Eldenshade, and I rule with an iron goddamn fist!” “And somehow you managed to wear a robe and get people to call you ‘your honor’”, Devon mocked. “Tell me, does lightning strike the courthouse every time someone can actually get that one out without hysterically laughing?” “Only ones that end in stories with the words ‘and there were no survivors,’” Tomas answered. … “Vati?” The rest of my formerly living companions drifted into the distance, as the one for whom I’d longed to hear her voice sailed through my body like an electric shock. I dropped to my knees, and all of the memories I’d attempted to suppress for so long came rushing back like water rapids. Suddenly, I was alone in darkness, a simple figure approaching me through the shade of the moonlight. As she finally was within the distance of a touch, those beautiful blue eyes reflected the gleaming waters of the tiny stream aside where she stood. I was frozen; no words could escape my lips. “I miss you, Vati,” her cute little voice greeted me. So much did I desire to reach out to her, grab her tight, and never let her go again. Nothing had completed the emptiness of my soul like the deceased void of unconditional love. My little girl, forever she should stay as she was when she left, was inches away from me and I couldn’t even muster a hand gesture. “Is okay, Vati, I know,” she continued, obviously knowing of my struggle to communicate my long buried feelings. Her broken English brought a tear to my eye, as it always made her seem a bit younger than she was when she died, and I didn’t want it any other way. Even if she had become a full grown woman, I would’ve always seen her as the cute little pig-tailed girl playing with the flowers in the meadow, and forever that sentiment shall remain in my mind. “See Lantlas, she’s okay,” Neovan interrupted our moment of reunion. “Yeah Vati, Uncle Neovan him take good care of me!” Standing up from my kneeling position, my glare returned to that of my former brother. “What is all of this? Is this just to remind me of what I will never know again?” “No, Lantlas,” Narenwyn answered in Neovan’s place. “It is to remind you of all that awaits you once your time has finally come.” “When you have accomplished all that you were meant to, all that the Earth needs you to, and all those who were meant to be touched by your existence have come to be… When that time arrives and you can finally find peace within yourself and lose the residual guilt over all those you believe you’ve lost, you will find them once again.” “Like I found Jade? As it was in a plane of non-existence, where the incapabilities cursed my very name?” I angrily replied. “No Vati,” Emerald answered. “You will come home to me.” “All of us will be here,” Lenina added. “None of us are going anywhere,” Neovan continued. “You are the last Son of the Earth,” Narenwyn informed. “All of the previous, even the children of God, who have known you and affected your life and who you have become, will one day come to meet you again. A plane of existence that you have tasted… But as a living being, still shall not be able to fully embrace… You know of this place, and as our times have come to an end, we can return there. You, though, shall not cross into the permanent afterlife until your life is complete.” “And you still have so much left to do, so many people to touch,” Neovan told me. “Forever may not be long enough for all of this to occur, but as an Elven Warrior, the Son of the Immortal, the Child of the Earth, you’ve been blessed with forever.” “Forever is a curse,” I interrupted. “Humans live a little while, they grow old with each other, and they die, never appreciating the fact that their lives are short enough to not truly grasp the concept of eternity.” “Lantlas, imagine for a moment they had the ability to change that. Since the beginning, man has attempted to achieve forever, and the Earth has blessed us with the ability to protect the humans from ending their own existence. So much knowledge that has not been changed in your mind, do you realize that you know of fact more than any book can explain, more than any writer can tell? You were there to experience it, instead of passing it down through the corruptible word of man through generations and human futility. You know so much more than you are aware,” Narenwyn interjected. “You know of the beginning, and you know how things have come to be. You have also been blessed with the time to heal from pain instead of carrying it to the grave, and while that may not seem quite plausible through everything you are feeling at the moment, this too shall pass. Even as an immortal, you are yet so young.” “And me be here to visit you at night, Vati,” Emerald cooed. “But I cannot hold you anymore, Emerald,” I finally replied to her. “I love you so much, and you meant more than anything in the world could ever hope to.” “Vati, I still here,” she answered. “I go with you everywhere and when time come, you come back to me.” “We’ll all be waiting,” Neovan added. “You carry all of us with you wherever you go. Anytime you need us, you can come here, to the land of your ancestors, and the reassurance you’ve so desperately needed shall await.” “I love you, Vati.” Emerald faded away, and I was once again alone with the sound of small amounts of water rushing around the rocks. Solitude returned as the moon began to fade in the horizon and dawn approached. “I love you all.” … Several weeks passed, and much had occurred in my revived appearance in GCW. Devon, naturally, had gotten under someone’s skin as only she was able. Dan Black, a competitor along the same ranks with which I had achieved, obviously had some personal issues, and Devon had hit him at just the right moment. Still, that left no excuse for the actions he’d chosen to take. He assaulted her, and the fact that he was able to score the amount of offense he did led me to comprehend that I was dealing with no average slouch here. Not since the likes of Oliver Ranken had I known someone with the fortitude and athletic prowess to be able to put away my manager, and like Ranken, I had to take a similar approach in dealing with him. I wasn’t aware of when the GCW was going to require my services again, so I took the chance that they would see my action as a notion of participation. Intentionally missing the arrow by a few inches, I’d fired the warning shot to Dan Black. His actions were unacceptable, and I wasn’t going to stand for it. His response, I should’ve expected with as many years in the business in which I had put, and he got the better of me with an underhanded cheap attack. Devon herself had to break up the assault in the return of favor, and with being at less than a hundred percent; I had a challenge to answer. I’d long struggled to find my place in Global Championship Wrestling, and while that was seemingly impossible, Dan Black’s challenge was far beyond professionalism. The United States Championship match that would be awarded to the victor also held little regard in my motivation. While I was proud of my championship accomplishments in my resume, the belt itself was little more than a shiny way to hold up my pants. Dan Black had awakened a portion of my psyche reserved for the worst of sinners. Those who dared to cross the line into territory unbeknownst to any human who had yet unleashed the true being of the Elven Warrior. The green eyes of justice not blinded by human compassion, the warrior from within would emerge to swing the swift sword of retaliation for those who truly deserved the wrath. He’d put his hands on Devon, and while I knew things would never be as they once were, I responded the same as I would have when we were life partners. The knight riding into battle with the utmost intentions of revenge for a delusional human who believed it was he who sought vengeance, the scene had been set for the confrontation. Returning at Worldwide would provide me the avenue to accept the challenge, and the chance to look into his eyes and see the nothingness that had brought him to the point where he existed in my realm. And when that moment would come, when I would be able to look the most intense enemy I’d known in years in the eyes and be able to read his soul, I wondered if I would be able to stop myself from truly unleashing the Elven spirit. Would I have the tact to leave it as a wrestling match and not bring it into true battle form, where one who had crossed the line as he had truly deserved to be? The time would be soon approaching, as the return he likely unexpected would be brought forth, and reality would sink in as the result of his actions. A cold hard reality, like the tip of the sword, would not be swayed by words, and the moment of truth would soon arrive. … “The hell you’re not going to let me do anything about this,” Tomas exploded on a bruised and battered Devon. “You’re my fucking sister!” “It was necessary in order to bring him back,” Devon calmly replied. “He is your friend, you are my sibling! I will not just sit back and let this stand!” “You will,” Devon informed her raging brother. “I got in over my head, which I can admit to maybe only you, but the warrior I’ve been waiting to see has finally returned. You should’ve seen the look in his eye as he carried that flail… It was the Lantlas I knew from so long ago, the one I loved…” “Devon, why are you trailing off?” Tomas changed his tone slightly. “Why would you refer to that in the past tense? I thought you were finally planning to do what you wanted to?” Devon sighed, rubbing one of her many wounds as she stared off into the night. “My time has passed, Tomas.” “The hell it…” “Tomas, listen… When we meet up with Lantlas, and I formally introduce you, you must keep the context of the conversation to why we truly needed to reunite… Bringing Emerald’s killer to justice. What I have told you, what you now know, you must keep it that way. At no point will the feelings I’ve expressed to you be released to his knowledge, do you understand that?” “No, I don’t Devon,” Tomas argued. “I don’t understand why if you truly love this guy, you won’t finally do what you know you want to.” “Because my time with him has passed, and we both know it, whether or not he would see it that way.” “Please explain,” Tomas requested. “The actions I took are irreversible. No matter what I want, no matter what I do, our place is in the past. I cannot go back to 2003 and relive that time, no matter how much I may want to now. What I did changed the work of fate into what it has become, and while we both may suffer for it, it cannot be changed.” “Why not, Devon? Why can’t fate give you a second chance?” Tomas inquisited. “Simple, I ruined it,” Devon answered. “He may love me with all his heart and protect me as he has against Dan Black, but I will never be able to return the love he needs, no matter how much I want to. I am unable to do that because it is no longer meant to be, and eventually I will be able to understand why. I don’t now, but I am somehow assured that I eventually will. Somehow…” “Claire Eldenshade, you disappoint me,” Tomas honestly admitted. “The hell with disappointment, there are bigger issues facing the day.” “No,” Tomas stood in front of her offward gaze. “Everything in life is a choice. I have made many that would imply to others that I am a careless evil human being, and for the most part he’d be right,” he slyly remarked. “However, those are actions I chose to take, and it is merely through the gifts I’ve received that these tactics have been able to carry through. You’re an Eldenshade, you understand, we do not think like others do, we do not act as others may act. The gift of being an Eldenshade may not take the same course as yours has, but it has brought us back together after many years, and I cannot idly stand by and let my sister make the same mistake she apparently has several times before.” “You don’t have a choice,” Devon responded, though her brother’s words clearly affected her state of being. A small tear escaped her eye, which she quickly slid away in order to avoid showing a point of weakness. “This is how it must be.” “Will things ever change?” Tomas asked. “As many of those who have crossed you know,” Devon replied, “some things never change.” “Forever’s a long time to encapsulate, Claire.” “Forever may not be long enough,” Devon answered as she threw out her cigarette and disappeared into the night. Tomas shook his head and attempted to hide the disappointment written all over his face, but for the time being, his disagreement with his only sister was not going to be something without objection. As Tomas himself knew, sometimes second chances were not plausible, and to see his sister clearly avoiding the opportunity to do so, it struck deep within his own psyche, and angered him on a deeper level than he would freely admit. For one who was never given the chance to remedy the damage done to his own heart, willful ignorance caused him seething rage, and he would sooner give her his own dose of Dan Black’s actions than see her give up. But for now, it was all he could do… At least until he met Lantlas himself. Then, the choice would be his.
