The Devil Whiskey (Choices Part 1)

undated

Date: Before and after midnight The place was getting ready to shut down, he could tell by the dirty looks the cashier was giving him. The guy looked like he just wanted to go home, and really who could blame him, it was almost midnight after all. Stevens hadn’t seen another soul enter the building in almost an hour, he’d been standing there a long time. He stood in front of the whiskey rack, staring at the bottles lined up before him. One bottle in particular had caught his eye, and he hadn’t been able to look away. There it was right in front of him, a release from his problems, for one night at least. The amber liquid would burn going down, but it’d most certainly get the job done, half the bottle and he’d be numb, finishing it off he’d actually feel good, maybe. He hadn’t really felt good since that night. Yeah, that night. He felt so childish, just pick up the bottle, pay the man and leave. It was all so easy, the simplest of solutions to his problems. Buy, drink, forget, simplicity itself. So why couldn’t he bring himself to even touch the damn bottle? Everything in him screamed that he should take it, drown his sorrows in a flood of the fiery liquid. Yet he couldn’t move, frozen in indecision. If he ran away like this, would he be any better than Lance? He’d beaten the kid into a coma, and for what, because he’d tried to help his sister forget her problems for a night. Sure there was a pretty big difference between whiskey and the trash he shot her up with, but when all the chips were didn’t they amount to the same thing? Release from the troubles surrounding you. A night’s diversion from the harsh truth brought to light in the day’s glare. God knows she’d needed it, and all her brother had done was given it to her. Just because he didn’t approve of the method, he’d acted like an animal. What right did he have to pass judgment on anyone? He could barely get through the day without a drink, and he sure as hell couldn’t get through the day without at least a pack and a half. That was different though, right? Alcohol, and cigarettes, sure they were addictive, and weren’t good for you by any means, but they weren’t killers. Ok, maybe they were, but that was more of a slow death, and you can’t exactly OD on booze or tobacco, well you could but alcohol poisoning was for frat boys. Fuck, he’d been going around in circles like this for nearly two hours now. He hadn’t had a drink in nearly a week, just couldn’t bring himself to touch the stuff since that night. He wanted a drink so damn bad his hands were almost shaking. A drink would make everything alright, a drink would make him sane again. His hand inched closer to the bottle all green with the red metal twist top, the tips of his fingers caressed the glass. Abruptly his hand dropped back to his side, he couldn’t do it. “Listen, buddy, I gotta close the shop in five minutes. No liquor sold after midnight, state law, so either buy it or scram.” The voice from behind the counter shocked him back to reality. Immediately he became intensely aware of his surroundings the tunnel vision on the bottle fading away. “Huh, what?” “I said either buy something or get out, I’ve got a family I want to get home to.” Family, something Sheena didn’t have much of, and what little she did, he’d taken away. That was all the convincing he needed, reaching up he grabbed the bottle by the neck. He couldn’t take feeling like this any more, not for another damn minute. Heading towards the door, he tossed a hundred dollar bill on the counter. “Keep the change asshole.” Walking out into the street, he resisted the urge to twist open the bottle and start drinking right there. He really didn’t want to deal with the cops, in fact they were almost the last people he wanted to deal with right now. Sheena had to be the first, he’d barely spoken to her since that night, and she’d stayed behind in California when he’d been forced to head to Portland. He missed her, but he was glad she’d stayed behind. He couldn’t even look her in the eye after the lie he’d told her. Of course she believed him, why wouldn’t she? Everyone else had backed him up, and there really wasn’t any reason for her to think they’d lie to her. That’s what hurt the most, the trust in her eyes when she looked at him. God he wanted that drink. Ducking into an alley he clawed open the bottle and raised it to his lips. Almost immediately he lowered the whiskey not even tasted save for a few golden drops on his lips. Backing up against a wall he slid to the ground, lighting a cigarette. Fingers running through his head, he banged the back of his head against the wall several times, his words punctuating each blow. “Fuck, fuck, fuck.” He should give her a call, see how Lance is doing. There probably wasn’t any change, but he was sure she’d appreciate hearing a friendly voice. If he was going to call her he definitely couldn’t have that drink. If he were drunk he’d just end up saying something stupid, like the truth. Maybe that’d be for the best, he’d probably lose her completely, but maybe he’d be able to look at himself in the mirror again. That’d count for something at least. It’d been so damn hard putting a smile on his face for the show, acting like nothing was wrong, like he was happy. When questioned he’d told Alex that he’d done it, because he had to stay in character for TV, and it was almost the truth. The reality of the situation was he just happy to be away from everything. The smile might have been fake, but it had been a relief to think about something other than that night for two hours, and his new found powers of intimidation did amuse him, at least a little. He couldn’t help but think how much sweeter everything would be, if he could’ve just kept his anger in check. If he would’ve thought logically about the situation instead of acting out of pure emotion, none of this would have happened, and he could’ve truly enjoyed the effect his match with Lestatt had on everyone. It was fun being feared for something other than his brain for a change. The match itself had been nothing more than an extension of his frustration with the whole situation. He didn’t know if he could match that intensity again, but he’d try his best when the time came. Hell, knowing his life, another crisis would pop up right before the show and he’d have a whole new set of aggression he needed to get out. And he’d do it again. Yeah, there it was, the gorilla in the room. No matter how much he’d tried to ignore it, he still ended up seeing it. From now until forever he’d remember what he’d done to Lance, and knowing he was capable of something like that was scary. What if he boiled over in the ring sometime? What if he stopped wrestling and just started hurting people? That wouldn’t be good. Lifting the bottle again, he stared at it. All the thoughts could go away, he’d just have to drink. Run away, like he always did. That’s what he was best at after all, whenever things get too rough, hide behind the drink. He’d been doing it for so long he didn’t remember how to fight his own psychological battles anymore. Without the fog of liquor he couldn’t function. Too many regrets, too many bad choices. Choices, yeah in the end that’s all everything came down to. The choices we make stay with us forever. And right now he had a choice, run and hide, or stand on his own two feet and work this out for himself. The bottle promised relief, an easy way out. Well, this is where the easy way had gotten him, alone in dark alley that smelled like piss and worse. Pushing himself up to his feet he held the bottle in front of him, one more time. This is it decision time, wallow in your own filth or try to rise above it. In the end, it wasn’t any choice at all. “Fuck it.” He walked out of the alley and onto the street, the sound of the glass bottle shattering against the wall still ringing in his ears. He had a phone call to make. ~To Be Continued~
