Not the Center of the Universe

13 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/13/2006 For what seemed like the tenth time in as many minutes, Michi Noketsuna looked to his right to where Dawn was sitting, and noted that she had not moved a muscle. For the last few days she’d drug him all over Sapporo and the surrounding area, moving in an ever widening circle out into the rest of Hokkaido. The result? Nothing. Neither of them caught so much as a glimpse of Dawn’s mystery man, and if anyone else had caught that elusive glimpse, they simply weren’t talking. Whoever this “Kanzaki Reito” was, they had found no trace of him. From the highest official searches to the most illegal back alley questioning, nothing had turned up hide nor hair of this guy. Michi accepted the setbacks with a quiet grace, his usual poise uninterrupted by the constant failures. Dawn was not faring so well, seeming to slip deeper and deeper into a funk from which he feared she would never emerge. They were on the railway now, moving quickly back towards Sapporo itself’and she had spoken not a word since boarding the train. Instead she sat against the thin padding of her seat, elbows on her knees, and stared at a spot somewhere between her hands and the floor. Only the gentle rise and fall of her shoulders assured him that she were still alive’although he doubted she was anywhere close to fully functional. It pained him to see her like this, to be unable to offer the hand she needed to pull herself out of the spiral she was walking. But he could not simply watch it happen. Taking a deep breath he turned back to her, forcing as much joviality into his expression as he could manage. “You ready for Rampage?” She turned her head up slowly, blonde hair falling away from her face until she was staring at him with those striking eyes, like pools of liquid jade shot through with small tendrils of crimson. There was a vacancy to her expression he found disconcerting, a blankness in her eyes that reminded him heavily of staring into the eyes of a ghost’until she blinked. Nodding slowly she leaned back against the seating, her eyes never leaving his. “Yeah’I’m ready.” Her voice was as dull as her eyes had been; lacking the flair and emotion he had come to associate with his former training partner. He didn’t want to risk pushing her, but the tone of her voice lacked any conviction at all, leaving him to wonder if she was possibly hiding something. “Are you sure” We’ve been out and about so much; I’m surprised you’ve had any time to train.” He regretted the words as soon as they left his mouth, but there was no helping it now. Her mouth curved upward into a wry smile, one that entirely failed to reach her eyes. “I’ve been getting in as much prep time as I can between trips’I still know where my priorities lie Michi. I can’t fail again.” “Again?” He cocked his shaved head to one side, running his hand bemusedly over the course stubble atop it. “I thought this was your first time in GCW?” “It is’but it’s not my first time in the ring with Seymour Almasy.” Of course that was it. Michi cursed himself for not seeing it sooner, and shifted to look at her without craning his neck. Each person at the school had been watching her career in Global with great anticipation, and each person had let out almost the identical sigh of regret when Dawn had dropped the TV Title to Emoticon, the masked Almasy. Of course her failure was bothering her now, on the eave of her second confrontation with him’and there was still more. “You ah’you looking for some payback?” He asked, tugging lightly at the collar of his jacket with one finger. “Nope.” The word hung in the air like a brick, leaving Michi staring in dumbfounded silence at his companion. It took him several moments to recover and find his voice, and he wasn’t ashamed to admit it held a tone that suggested she might have lost her mind. “No” Did I hear you right? You, Dawn McFarlane. Don’t want any payback on the guy that’s made your life hell for the last few months?” “Ah ah.” She held up one finger, smiling a decidedly predatory grin. “I don’t want any payback YET. This tournament is bigger then that, bigger then any sense of personal aggrandizement I’m going to get out of it. No matter what he might think, my world does not revolve around Seymour Almasy’and this tournament is more important to me in a lot of ways.” Michi looked at her skeptically, the corner of his mouth twisting in his own wry smile. “Uh-huh. Since when did Seymour Almasy mean absolutely nothing to you emotionally?” “Since I decided to stop letting him matter.” The reply was cold and emotionless, spat out from somewhere around her stomach. “Seymour’s been in my head for months, fucking around and dong whatever he goddamn well pleases in there. But I want this Michi, I want it worse then almost anything in recent history. I want to win this thing and make everyone choke on their stupid preconceptions; make everyone really see what we’re all about. And I’m not allowed to let Seymour Fucking Almasy screw this up for me.” Rubbing his head again, Michi settled back against the uncomfortable seat, his broad shoulders well above the rim of the bench meant for a man who was much shorter. He mulled the next question around in his mind for a few moments, breaking the silence before it lapsed into a level he found personally uncomfortable. “So you’re more focused on the finals then yeah?” “Hai.” Her affirmative came out in Japanese, a habit picked up from an increasing amount of time spent in the Far East. She looked sheepish for a moment before elaborating. “I’m not underestimating Seymour by any stretch of the imagination, but his time is long since passed. We’re starting a new era in GCW, and I won’t let anyone stop us.” “So’what if you have to go against Lia in the finals?” The question was unfairly loaded and he knew it. The first and only time Dawn and Lia had gone against each other in the ring had been a disaster rivaling a personal bomb for Dawn, and he could only imagine it had been similar for her partner. The Steel Rose had walked away the victor then, moving on to capture the 2005 Master of the Mat tournament and become the first ever Mistress of the Mat’but she had done so at the expense of the woman she loved and things had been strained for some time afterwards. “It doesn’t matter.” Dawn reply was light and almost upbeat, and she turned on Michi the first genuine smile from her that he’d seen in days. “It’doesn’t matter?” He was bewildered, and it showed in the tone of his voice. “Nope. We talked about things already, spent the last few days working them out.” She tucked an errant strand of hair away from her face, blowing a few rebellions hairs out of her eyes as well. “If we both get to the finals it won’t matter which of us wins, because it’ll be a victory for all of us. No matter which one of us gets branded the champion, our whole group can hold that title high. We’re not about personal gain right now, we’re about showing the rest of GCW that we’re here to stay, and we’re not going to balk at sweeping away the old corruption to do so.” “Old Corruption” You mean Seymour?” It always made Michi a little uncomfortable when she talked like this, but he hoped it would help keep her mind off the tiring search. “She nodded firmly in response. “Definitely. I mean it’s not just him obviously, there are a lot of “Veteran” GCWers who fall into that definition, but Seymour is most definitely in the top five.” “So it’s not so much that you don’t care about him?” Michi started, understanding starting to dawn on his handsome face. ??it’s that I don’t care about him in a personal capacity.” She finished, her eyes alight with fervor. “This tournament isn’t anymore about me beating Seymour then it is about Lia beating Rollins. Right now it’s the final goal that matters and the people between us and there are just obstacles along the path we need to get around.” “I bet Seymour sees you in the same light.” His reply was intended to be light hearted, but judging from Dawn’s reaction it wasn’t taken well. Her eyes narrowed and carefully trimmed nails dug into the fabric of her cargo pants, bunching the dark fabric up in small handfuls. “Seymour’s only EVER seen us in that light, bumps on the otherwise smooth road to keeping his career intact. He claims to have honestly loved Lia and I, but I can’t believe someone can fall in and out of love so quickly and with so many women. He’s like a stray cat, rubbing up against anyone who’ll take him in for the night.” Michi’s reply was lost in the train’s sudden deceleration, the squealing brakes overriding anything her might have said. It was just a routine stop, but he thought the conductor had stopped with perhaps a little more vigor then was entirely necessary. People flooded in and out through the open doors, the train taking on far more passengers then it let off. To his right an elderly woman struggled to find a place at the hand rails, her arms weighed down by a half dozen plastic bags containing the days worth of grocery shopping. Dawn slipped from her seat and motioned wordlessly for the woman to sit, deftly blocking the path of the young man who had sought to claim the spot before it could be occupied again. A smile slipped onto Michi’s face as he watched, struck again by the seemingly random contradictions that made up the woman he knew as Dawn McFarlane. For the last several minutes she’d spewed venom that would make most women blanch in consternation, while simultaneously picking apart the career and personal life of someone she worked with’only to moments later clear out of her own seat to offer it to an older woman. The smile on her face was one of genuine pleasure as she bowed low; shrugging off the others woman’s thanks as though it had been nothing at all. But he supposed, in her mind, it really hadn’t been. She caught him staring and placed her hands on her hips, staring back in mock irritation. “What’re you grinning at?” Holding up his hands to mollify her he chuckled at her reaction. “Just shaking my head at you. What would everyone in GCW think if they saw the big bad Dawn McFarlane giving up her seat for an old lady on the subway” Your bad guy reputation would be forever tarnished.” Dawn shrugged, her amused smile slipping into a thoughtful frown. “We’re not the bad guys here Michi, we just see things differently then other people do. People need to see what they’re made to wallow in, and no one else is willing to step up to the plate and take charge. People have been eating the crap guys like Almasy and Rollins feed them without realizing how bad it is’just wait until we show them what it could really be like. It’ll be a breath of fresh air.” Shaking his head and leaning back, Michi tried to alleviate the misgivings he felt in hearing Dawn speak that way. “From helping old ladies to wide eyed zealot, you must be looking for a side career in acting.” The benefit of knowing someone for a long time is you know when they mean something personally and when they don’t. Rather then take offense Dawn just grinned and leaned against the nearest pole. “You’ll see Michi; everyone’s going to see come Rampage.” He shook his head again’and managed to miss the stealthy hand that slid up the back of Dawn’s duster to squeeze her rather shapely behind. Her face contorted for a moment in surprise, features scrunched up in irritation and shock that someone had dared. She knew young women often ran the risk of being molested on public transit in Japan, but it had never happened to her. A quick glance over her shoulder revealed the offender, his greasy hair hanging low over his face and the shoulders of his trench coat. Was he smelling the hand he’d grabbed her ass with? Michi turned back to her as she reached behind herself, keeping her eyes fixed forward on her longtime friend. Seizing the offending arm by the wrist she twisted violently. “The winds of change are coming Michi. Rampage is going to be our big break.” *CRAAAAACK* “AIEEEEEEE!”
