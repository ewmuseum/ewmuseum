Judicial Jeopardy: Take 1

21 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/21/2005 He sat on the corner of the steps to the Panama City Beach District Courthouse. Shining brightly against his forehead, the sunlight drew forth beads of sweat, which he continued to wipe away. He was provided the opportunity of going inside the court house and enjoying the comfort the facility's air conditioning system provided, however he took the road less traveled. Sure it was cooler inside, but he would have to deal with the cigarette smoke and all. Furthermore there was far too much crap going on with the many asshole officials that filled the place. His distaste for his surroundings began during his teenage years. He and his high school friends had obtained elephant dung from a traveling circus frequenting the area. After placing the crap in paper bags and setting each a blaze on the many porches along Ocean Boulevard, one of the less courageous youth ended up stool pigeon and confessed for everyone. Needless to say after all was over with, months later the said youth carried a beating he soon was not to forget. Only nearing the age of thirty, he was far from being technically considered old, but yet now years later he saw things in a different light. Jake Summers realized he should've been more mature and perhaps chosen those he associated with more wisely. Nowadays things were slightly different; our 'golden boy' was an adult whose mischievous youthful ways had long passed by. It was already mid-afternoon and his attorney was already thirty minutes late. There's a lot to be said for having a court appointed representative, but Jake sacrificed the opportunity for a cheaper rate and took his GCW tag-team partner suggestion instead. Now as he endured a heat index that pushed triple digits, he paced back and forth in front of the courthouse steps, questioning whether he had made the right decision or not. 'How do I get myself into these predicaments? Where the hell is that sorry son of bitch?' he asked of himself, while he tapped his foot against the white marble steps. 'The bastard was supposed to have been here thirty minutes ago. Man it's hotter than two alligators frigging in a mud puddle. I knew better than taking Ryan's advice.' he continued. As she approached, he tried to ignore her, but the sudden urge in his loins suggested he was unable to escape her sway. Along with the assistance of a bright green dress clinging to her bosom and shapely backside, the movie star looks and swimsuit model figure his estranged wife possessed, was enough power alone to get a rise out of the dead. He watched on, trying to conceal the feelings he still had for her. The woman knew how to work what her momma gave her and was well aware of the effect she had on her husband. Simply stated she blew his socks off when they first met and he still loved her very much so. She continued up the steps with her lawyers in toe. Before entering the cold confines of what soon could become the sarcophagus for their marriage, she turned to bask in the glory of his physical demise. At this point the gel he sculpted his short blond tresses with had dissolved. His 'do' to say the least, looked as if he'd just risen from bed. The business demeanor he sought to set forth for their hearing had quickly changed to one of leisure. The navy blazer that lay on the steps, the bright yellow tie tucked his back pocket, as well as the half button whit shirt he wore were all sign that he had literally lost his cool. During their marriage she was constantly reminding him he needed to manage his temper. And he lived by the creed of never letting them see you sweat, therefore the first thing that came to mind was to smile. Smiling when she was trying to control him or angry at him always seem to work. It worked better than ever having to say anything in response, better than ever having to argue. The less he said the more it seemed to affect her. 'Figures!' she said storming into facility. He never thought things would come to this, but over the past few months he had gone through a hard ordeal. First he was indicted on money laundering, but thanks to a federal plea bargaining he got off on probation. He had almost completed the condition of a thousand hours community service and that was one less thing he had to worry about. Furthermore he was able to travel out of state again therefore now he could make more money wrestling. Whether he was convicted or was not convicted, the bad press he received from the whole ordeal drove numerous of his real-estate customers away. As result of such he soon found himself going toe to toe with judicial system in bankruptcy court. Seem no one wanted to sale their homes or businesses through an ole wrestler/sports entertainer whom escaped the law by the skin of his teeth. 'To hell with them, I don't need them in the first place.' he said grabbing up his coat and tie, but suddenly stopping in his tracks. 'Mr. Summers,' called out the voice from behind him. Jake turned, only to be met by the menacing stature of a freckle-faced thirteen-year old boy with a pen and magazine in his youthful hands. The adolescent was wearing a 'Chicago Bears' t-shirt and multi-colored shorts along with an oversized pair of sunglasses. 'Hey mister can I please have your autograph?' questioned the bright-eyed, bushy headed youth. Jake pondered whether or not he should ask for money. Sure he could use the money; it's not like his was growing on trees these days. But then again, that's something Ryan Stephens would do. Ryan was indeed his friend, or so he thought, but there were a few little quirks about the man he disliked. Nevertheless maybe with the money he could buy some cigarettes or a couple of coffee. He cared for neither, but a draw of tar and nicotine or the rush of caffeine, either at this point could have help calm his nerves. Although the angel, the nice guy in him won this go around. 'Sure kid. Whom should I sign it to?' the GCW tag-team champion asked of his young fan. 'Gee golly Mr. Summers...' the teenager said awestruck in the presence of his idol. 'We're on vacation, didn't think I'd meet anyone famous though.' the kid continued. Jake smiled, basking in the glory that at least someone still admired him. He always had his wrestling fans if nothing else. 'I'm not sure if I'd call myself famous anymore or not,' Jake responded. 'So what did you say your name was?' 'Everyone calls me Chase.' the young man said more googol-eyed than a gal at an N-Suck concert. Chase he thought, there must be a thousand chases, but why did that name sound so familiar? Wiping the sweat from his face, he shook his head and took another look at the child. The kid was older, but yet familiar. 'Aren't you?' asked Jake. 'Something wrong mister?' the boy asked. 'Just under a little stress is all.' Jake said taking the magazine as the kid handed it to him. He studied the cover, PRO-WRASSLIN' MONTHLY... October 1997. 'What the hell?' he said under his breathe. There he was, 'Golden Boy' Jake Summers on the cover, eight years younger, looking as confident as he ever was nonetheless. Beside him standing was his mentor, his former partner, Darren James. The headline read, 'THOROUGHBREDS STAMPEDE!' Wrapped around each of their wastes were the NHWF tag titles. He smiled thinking how great life was back then; a wife less and hell of a lot less headaches than he had these days. 'I thought that would bring a smile to your face.' noted a much older man voice from a distance. Suddenly Jake turns, shielding the bright sunlight with his hands. 'Darren?' he says as his eyes abruptly looked downward at the near bald man in a wheel-chair. A huge smile appeared on the faces on each man. 'Darren James! What the hell are you doing here?' he said with much excitement in his voice. 'How did you know...' he let out before abruptly being cut off by the elder gentleman. 'Good to see you ole buddy.' responded Darren, 'I see you met my son?' Jake accepted the offered handshake of his old friend and former tag-team partner. 'Yeah he's grown some since I last saw y'all. What are you guys doing in Florida?' responded Jake Summers. 'Chase is following in your footsteps... he had a national amateur wrestling tournament. We figured we'd swing by on the way back home to see what my ole friend was up to these days. It's been a while since we've seen each other.' the former tag-team partner of Jake Summers' said. Summers continued to stare at his friend in the wheel-chair. A combined look of amazement and sadness appeared on Jake's face. 'It's not that bad... My back just finally gave out on me. The doc said I had a deteriorating spine, but I told him I'd carried one too many young guys during my career,' Darren said jokingly. Jake laughed 'Yeah I hear you. More like one too many of those sick bumps you were notorious for,' Jake said laughing along with his friend. 'So how did you know where to find me?' Darren wheeled himself closer to his friend. 'We stopped by your place. The maid said Faith was at the courthouse and I know how inseparable you two are. So we figured you were with her down here. We asked her where the building was and we were off.' answered Darren. 'Ahh... well I'm here, but I'm not necessarily with her.' offers Jake. Jake finished signing the magazine for his friend's son, before he turned his attention back to the father. During all his years, Darren had very seldom showed any emotion. But at this moment the look on his face showed that he cared. It's amazing how growing old softens you. 'It's a long story my friend and unfortunately at the moment I'm pressed for time. And with my damn lawyer late I'm not exactly in the mood to tell all.' Jake continued, reluctant to tell his friend of how his marriage had fallen to pieces within a blink of an eye. 'Maybe we can hook up later this evening. You can tell me all over a beer. I still have your cell number. We're staying over at the Holiday Inn.' Darren said smiling as turned to wheel himself away. Before Jake could respond Darren and his son were fading into the distance. Jake watched as the young teenage wheeled his father towards an awaiting mini-van. 'A mini-van? Man a lot has changed.' he said, turning back towards the court house steps that he made his way up. Appearing out of no-where was the slim figure of a man with a receding hairline. In one hand was a briefcase; in the other was an umbrella. There's an old tale about a rainmaker, but that story had already been told by someone else. 'Mr. Summers... My name is Franklin Felix Foxworthy IV, Ryan Stephens told me you'd be waiting.' said the lawyer, whom had finally arrived. Franklin Felix Foxworthy the Fourth was more than a mouthful to say, but the man was far less than what Jake had expected. While Jake assumed the guy would look more like Robert Shapiro, he got a dead ringer for Michael Jeter. 'Heaven help me now!' Summers beckoned before we faded to blackness.
