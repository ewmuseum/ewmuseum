Blood and Thunder

16 June 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 16th June Three sisters weird; clad in shadows and shrouded by darkness. A trio of terrible soothsayers come forth from hither and yon to stage a ceremony as old as time and as precious as the providence it would soon bestow. Not witches, no; marvels. Seers. Clairvoyants. Beings drawn from the blood of The Almighty himself. Long, gaunt faces and frail skeletons centuries old, with crooked snouts and dull, green eyes, all clothed in heavy black robes weathered not by wear and tear. “We meet again, we three,” said The First, her voice nought more than a ragged cackle. She wrapped her bony fingers around the staff on which she leaned and stretched a black-toothed grin across her ghostly white complexion. “To what cause doth we owe this congregation?” hissed the second, serpentine. “The time to decide has come,” spoke The Third. Taller than those beside her and undoubtedly the authority of the group, there was a stern certainty to her otherworldly chimes. From beneath her cloak a withered arm emerged and pulled back the robe's hood. Long, straggly threads of seaweed-coloured hair blew freely in the night's deathly cold chill. “Reaching the end of his journey is he. We hath spoke often of his trials and hardships, but now we must be certain.” Waves crashed against jagged rocks below them, thunder rumbled through distant hills and valleys, and splints of wood and strands of straw hissed, crackled and popped beneath the vessel between them. Each consumed the speechlessness and pondered carefully. Their decision was one that had monopolized hours – nay, days - of their time, yet, for all their deliberation, none could find the words. “Well, what say you?” The Third croaked suddenly. She dragged a poisonous gaze across each of her collaborators and twisted her dry, cracked lips into a twisted sneer. “Where doth the fortunate son travel from here? Above, or beyond? Here, or there?” she paused. “To rags, or riches?” “The son is strong of heart, but weak of mind,” said The First. “He hath come far, but now he wavers.” “But he is bold and true,” The Second interjected. “His character holds all that the incumbent lacks. Tall he walks and sure are his strides, but now he strays. We can allow now longer his fortunes to be blown in the wind.” “Is he worthy of the problem with which he is tasked, though? I see nought, sister, that convinces me we should allow the son to travel this path any longer.” “He hath strayed, yes; but he will become lost without this, of that I can guarantee. He hath towed the line because we hath allowed him too, and this can continue no more,” The Second paused. “If we are to vote, then I say we give the son fortune. His battle hath been hard and long, and he hath earned it, truly. If have no recollection of a soul more worthy than he. He must not be denied.” “Sissssster,” hissed The First. “With these 'must''s you speak as though the power to decree is yours alone. I remind you of the ballot; the decision belongs to three and not one. I say we bring him atrophy. Too much time hath already been wasted debating the path this soul should take. Your argument that he is worthy doth not sway me; I say hour is nigh to cast this one into the fire and bedone with it.” The Second gritted her worn, decaying teeth together as a gust of ethereal wind blew through her fatigues and tickled the edge of her crooked spine. “We doth not 'waste' our time. We are in agreement that the son's road hath been a long one, yessss, and it is without question that we have expended much effort on this soul's fortunes, but to cast him aside now – after so much time hath passed – would be beyond all reason.” “Your assertion is flawed,” spat The First. “The son’s own soul is tainted; he has been corrupted by he whom he seeks to usurp and thoughts of glory have left him forever soiled. Now he stains where he strides and alienates those he seeks to call ‘friend’. What use is there in con-...” “It would be a grave mistake to confuse a man's destiny for something undeserving!” The Second growled, digging her heels into the loose soil beneath her feet. “Hath thou completely forgotten the wishes of The Almighty? Our duty is to ensure that all is just; that fair is not foul, and foul is not fair.” “Your attempts to patronise ar-...” “Enough!” roared The Third, clenching tightly her staff and driving it down into the ground. Through strands of unkempt hair she glared at her accomplices with such acrimony that both knew it was time to back down. “Is this what we hath become? Bickering and sniggering! 'Tis not the way of we three!” As The Third shook her head, pondering, a sudden burst of wind shot up form the cliffs and rattled the cauldron before her. The thunder rumbled ever closer and forks of lightning shot from the heavens, and The Third felt tiny droplets of rain begin to hit her sagging, dead skin. For all her authority, she was no closer herself to deciding the path of the fortunate son. The Second glanced at The Third; the wrinkled, sagging skin on her face painting a mask of contemplation. “What say you, sister?” The Third raised her brittle hand to her chin and scratched her blackened fingernails against her skin. Slowly, she raised her bowed head. “It was my hope that we could come to a union, but I see now that that is an impossibility.” Another pause punctuated the equilibrium; The Second for, The First against, The Third undecided. Finally, The Third's cadaverous eyes widened and her tone lifted. “A trial, yesssss. A test.” “A trial?” questioned The First. “Yesssssss. An agreement between we three cannot be reached. There remains one option; the theory of ‘free will’ to which the mortals cling so tight. A chance to shape his own fate.” She glanced at The First. “A chance to question those who doubt him,” she glanced at The Second, “and a chance to justify those who believe in him.” Slowly, The Second nodded her head. “Yessssss...” “We will pluck him from the material realm?” questioned The First. “Test him in a world of our own?” The Third nodded. “And his destiny will be his own? Success will bring him fortune, and failure will bring him ruin.” “Yesssssss...” “Then make it so.” “Then we are agreed!” cackled The Third. She reached deep into the cold pocket of her robe and pulled out a handful of dust. Sharply she flung it forward, sending thousands of minuscule particles flying into the cauldron and at the feet of these sisters weird. A thin plume of white mist became to seep from the top of the vessel as The Third brought up her staff and dug it inside the confides of the cold, steel vat. “If he overcomes, triumph shall be his...” said The Second. “... and through failure we shall bring him decay,” The First added, and slowly they drew closer, gazing into the vessel's murky depths. Three agents of fate, bound together by the struggle of a man with more ahead of him than he could possibly comprehend. * * * * * The hotel bar was busy, yes. Although the Scotsman had done a decent job of locating his family a reasonable distance away from the crowd that a Global Championship Wrestling pay-per-view attracts, there was still barely room enough to squeeze another person through the door. And oh, how it toyed with Andy Murray's patience. He sat at the bar with his head in his heads, pushing his palms into his temples and staring aimlessly at the half-empty glass of Scotch that sat before him. One would assume that working in a profession such as wrestling, where peace and tranquillity is at a premium and noise and pomp rule the roost, a man would find himself at home inside a noisy bar. After all, what are a few hundred merry punters compared to tens of thousands of screaming fans, a booming PA system, and earth-shaking pyrotechnics? But no; Murray was agitated. The only things on his mind worth a second thought was the GCW World Championship and LeStatt Knight, and the abundance of drunken hotel guests and overzealous wrestling fans in the room were making it impossible for him to focus on the one thing that truly mattered. “And, oh my God! It's gonna be big and puffy, just like in the movies, and it's going to be worth thousands of dollars; it'll be the prettiest thing you've ever seen!” enthused his fiancée besides him. It had been her idea to come down to the bar; “have a drink,” she said, “take your mind off things,” she said, but the sentiment had been a resounding failure. Sitting in his room letting thoughts of Rampage stew and marinate in his head would do him no good, but neither would sitting in that boisterous goddamn bar listening to Kayla speak of the one thing that was on her own mind; the wedding. After NC-17 and the vanquishing of Jay Terror, nothing had been more prominent in Andy Murray's inner cortex than the coming together of his third marriage, but as he drew closer and closer to his career's crowning glory it had become much less of a concern. He loved her, yes, and their coming together would surely be one of the most joyous days of his life, but if let his mind stray from the task at hand for a split-second then he world surely fail. This was LeStatt Knight, after all; an evil so terrible, so vile, that the mere mention of his name was enough to leave the foul taste of bile in the mouths of even the most virtuous of men. And Andy Murray was nothing if not virtuous. Leaning back, Andy dropped his paws to the bar and took a swig on the double measure. The harsh smokiness of the whiskey licked the back of his throat as he turned to his right, observing his younger brother Cayle scoping out the legions of wealthy womenfolk that occupied much of the tastefully-decorated bar. The youngest of the Murray brothers sensed Andy's presence, and said, without visual contact, that they “should go five-star more often, 'cause these New York ladies are F-I-N-E.” “We should really start thinking about the honeymoon, too,” Kayla continued, jostling for Andy's attention. The Scotsman, clad in a simple white button-up shirt and bootcut blue jeans, turned back to the bar as his partner continued to prattle. “And we need to set a date! This year, next year... what do you reckon? I'm thinking fall, but we could stretch it out 'til early 2010...” “Whatever makes you happy, Kay,” Andy said. Cocking his head back, he polished-off the last of the whiskey and slid the crystal glass towards the barkeeper. “Look, I don't mean to sound rude or abrupt, but can't we talk about something else?” “You know that if you think about this match too much it's only gonna drive you crazy, right?” Andy sniggered, figuring that if he had to endure any more wedding talk it might just have the same effect. “Maybe so, but we can get married whenever we want, Kay, whereas I'm only gonna get one shot to topple good old LK. We can talk about the wedding after Rampage is done.” Kayla huffed; both disappointed and annoyed that their union wasn't as high on Andy's list of priorities as it was hers. “It's just a sport, you know,” she said, screwing up her deep, hazelnut-coloured eyes. “All it is is wins and losses, but me and you? We're talking about life here, Andy...” “I don't wanna hear it...” he mumbled, but she didn't hear. “I know that the title's supposed to be a big deal, but this is our weddi-” “I said 'I don't wanna hear it!'” Andy suddenly snapped, bashing his clenched fist into the bar and rising from his perch. Tightening his facial muscles into a fearsome portrait usually reserved for the likes of Chris Bagwell and LeStatt Knight himself, Andy watched his petite fiancée's sympathetic eyes sag as she admitted defeat. But The Scottish King of Cool was in no mood for reason. “In a few night's time I got the fight of my goddamn life on my hands! Can't be thinking about no marriage!” The volume of his words drew gazes from those around him – including Cayle – and he shook his head and grabbed his hooded sweatshirt as he grew uncomfortable with the attention. He was Andy Murray; he was a hero, righteous and pure. He wasn't supposed to air his dirty laundry in public. Pulling the jacket over his shoulders, he shook his head and stepped away from the bar. With long, purposeful strides the aggravated Scot made his way through the gathered masses, who parted much like the Red Sea at Moses' behest. Soon he was out of the bar and into the lobby, and he thought he heard Kayla call his name and tell him to “wait!”, but still he continued. He needed time alone; to clear his head and gather his thoughts. Andy glided through the door as the porter held it open and stepped out into a cold Manhattan evening. Pulling the grey hood over his head and tugging on the drawstrings, Andy walked out onto the pavement. “Where are you going!?” There was no mistaking the sound of Kayla's voice this time, but he chose to keep walking. She scurried out onto the concrete, realising she'd pushed him a step too far with the wedding talk, but The King was too far gone. He stepped out onto the road without look, and she spotted the danger immediately. “ANDY!” she blared as loud as her small lungs would allow. “LOOK OUT!” If Andy had reacted a fraction of a second earlier, the car would have missed him. But he didn't and it didn't. The driver slammed his foot down on the brakes and the vehicle came to a screeching halt as the large frame of Andy Murray bounced over the hood, leaving a sizeable dent in it's wake. He rolled over the bonnet and came to a suddenly stoppage when his body bounced off the cold, wet tarmac with a dull thud. He came to rest on his back; body splayed out on the road, glazed eyes helpless but to stair at the bright lights of the glowing buildings illuminating the New York skyline. “Ohgodohgodohgod!” Kayla trembled as she trod towards the vehicle as fast as her heeled shoes would allow. She reached the car as the driver slid out of the door and bawled obscenities at Murray for not looking where he was going, but she barely heard them as she knelt down at his side and raised a fluttering hand to her mouth. “Andy...” The Scot's head lolled slowly from side to side, and his eyes fluttered open and closed like a butterfly's wings. Kayla put one hand on either side of his head to steady him. “Andy!” she shrieked. “ANDY! Oh God. Oh my God...” Cayle too emerged from the hotel and burst through the gathering crowd. He too dropped to one knee. “SHIT!” he barked, “what happened?!” “He didn't look where he was going,” the driver, feeling less sure of himself now he'd seen that Murray was losing consciousness, said with a wavering voice. It barely registered with Cayle, who watched his brother struggle and toil on the tarmac. “S'alright bro, s'alright,” he said. As Kayla began to weep and sob into her clenched fists, Cayle took notice of the intrigued group of people that had began to surround the downed wrestling megastar. “Well don't just fucking stand there!” he roared. “Do something! Call a fucking ambulance!” Snapping out of their morbid curiosity, at least six of seven of the dozen grabbed their cellphones and dialled 911. But Murray was losing it. His blinking slowed and his movements ceased. He was drifting ever so slowly. “Come on bro! Don't give up on me!” Cayle struggled to say as he pushed down on his brother's chest, but it was no use. At last, Andy Murray's eyes closed and wouldn't reopen. Cayle leapt up from his lifeless elder brother and snatched a cellphone away from a dithering spectator. “Gimme a fucking ambulance!” he yelled into the phone. “NOW!” On the ground, Kayla crawled closer to Andy Murray. Her jeans soaked through with rainwater, her hair a tangled mess, she felt the salty taste of her own tears leak through the corner of her mouth as she held her partner closer than she ever had. * * * * * Taste came first. Well, not quite taste, but the feeling that someone had torn his tongue from his throat and lined the walls of his mouth with coarse sandpaper. Dry and rough, as if a sponge had been stuck in his mouth and drained all it's moisture. The Saharan aridness stretched from the tip of his tongue and all the way down his throat. He swallowed dryly and it felt as though he'd just thrown a handful of thumbtacks and razorblades down his barren gullet. Touch returned to him shortly after, and the feeling of a chilling dampness soaking through his attire and filling his head with dreadful thoughts of pneumonia. But it wasn't the cold or the wetness that caught him first, no; it was the motion. A constant swinging and swaying, as if he was laid face down the face of a giant pendulum. The source of the irregular careening became apparent when his ears popped and hearing returned. It was muffled and blurry at first, but soon he recognised the hefty swoosh of the ocean's swell, and when he opened his eyes his suspicions were confirmed; he was ocean-bound. Vision was slower, but soon it came. At first all he could make out was a faint, brown outline on the dull night's sky, but soon he made out the wooden surface of the deck and a ream of heavy rope sitting just inches from his feet. Rubbing his eyes, he sat upright and craned his neck from side to side, looking in awe at the giant sails that filled with air above him. Each bellowed and filled with air with every powerful windy puff and flopped to a limp, flaccid form as the wind changed it's direction. Had he not been so dumbfounded by his situation, he might have found himself entranced by those majestic sheets of heavy calico. He tried to pull himself to his feet but found his legs to be as reliable as those of a newborn foal, and the choppy seas didn't exactly help matters either. Almost immediately he fell back to his knees and crawled over to the side of the ship. Reaching over the side and tugging on the barrier, eventually he was up on his feet. It hit him as soon as he was vertical; a sudden dizziness and an overwhelming bout of nausea. Quickly he turned around and hunched his form over the barrier, hurling a thick syrup of bile from the pit of his stomach into the ocean below. The harsh acidic taste in his mouth almost made him want to hurl again, but instead he wiped the remnants from his mouth, turned, and spat on the deck. He was breathless; sweating profusely and struggling to get a grip of himself. He was not at all used to seafaring, and being thrust into such an environment had hit him with all the tenderness of a freight train. As he regained his composure he had another chance to look around. The ship was a fine one indeed; not overly big and not particularly long, but lovingly crafted and put together. Two small flights of stairs led the upper deck, with a small door sitting in-between them. Above it hung a small, gold plaque, splattered with salt-water and fading through age. It's elegantly-carved text displayed a name of French origin, beginning with “P”. “So kind 'a you to finally wake up!” The voice called at him with such suddenness that he almost jumped. Piercing through the crash and bang of the ocean, it was loud enough for each syllable to be clearly audible, despite the conditions. In a panic he turned his head to the source. There, above the door and at the ship's wheel, stood the captain. He pulled hard on the wheel, bringing it down to the left and looking out across the picture of black seas and white crescendos, doing battle with the elements without a crew. His observer squinted, trying to comprehend what he was seeing before him. This was surely the ship's leader, yes; but he wasn't exactly dressed like Lord Admiral Nelson or even Captain Ahab. He wore a long, brown trench coat of suede that had been soaked into a deeper tone than it's natural hue, and below that a pair of drenched black dress pants and some scuffed black shoes. But it was the head that was most striking about this character; beneath a brown fedora his head was entirely obscured by tightly woven white bandages. He pinched himself. Closed his eyes and told himself that he was dreaming, but when he opened them the bandaged captain still stood before him and he was still the weather-beaten craft. “What the he-...” he began, but found himself unable to finish. “Going on a journey, me and you!” said the Captain, his excitement tangible. Again he tugged hard on the wheel, and his observer imagined a maniacal grin beneath those bandages. “Prize is just ahead of us, see! So close, so close…” As the Captain shrieked and snorted on the upper deck, his observer clung tight to the banister and began a slow, careful ascent. As he got closer to the Captain he saw the man was tall; at least 6'5” and built like a man who'd spent his life battling a force he'd never truly conquer. “What's going on?!” shouted the bewildered soul. “Just told you, didn’t I?” replied the Captain. His voice was rough, hoarse; sounded like a man with a sixty-a-day smoking habit, and yet he spoke such ease, defying his nicotine-ravaged throat and blackened lungs. “We're going to the edge of the Earth, the end of the line,” he croaked. “Gotta keep chasing, gotta keep going! The prize beckons us.” Slowly he paced towards the Captain. “We're going where!?” “There!” The Captain thrust his hand dead ahead and unleashed a shrill, painful fit of laughter. The man cast his gaze forward as barbs of lightning clattered down from the sky miles away, obscured only by a dearth of rain-clouds and the thick mist caused by the Heavens opening themselves. He was no seafaring man, but even he could tell that heading in that direction was a bad idea “Have you lost your mind?! That storm-...” “Hey, who's the fuckin' captain here, huh?!” barked the peculiar figure before him. “Shut yer hole, sit down, and enjoy the ride, kid. Besides, I never said this voyage was gonna be a pleasurable one...” “What are you trying to sa-...” “I'm saying buckle-up, because it's gonna be a bumpy one! We're going straight into the heart of that storm and we ain't coming back without the prize!” “What?!” The man shrieked. “You’ve lost your mind! Turn around before you see us killed!” “Can't. Too far gone now. There's only one thing left for it; you and I? We're gonna plunge ourselves into the darkness, and we ain't ever gonna return! We may go to our doom, but one’s soul is a small price to pay for a prize so glorious, so full of wonder…” The Captain paused. Slowly he turned his head to the side, acknowledging his observer with what he thought was eye contact for the very first time. “But you know a thing or two about that already, don't you Andy?” * * * * * Paramedics swarmed like worker ants as the ambulance’s back doors were flung open unceremoniously. Staff poured from the Accident and Emergency entrance and scurried towards the vehicle, helping those inside to jostle the stretcher free from its lodgings. It came loose with a metallic clink and the legs popped out as the paramedics slid it from the ambulance and pulled it out onto the asphalt. Calls of “keep clear!” and “coming through!” filled the air as the hospital staff dragged the fallen Scot inside the hospital with his brother and fiancée in-tow. They powered through the building in a breathless whirl, dodging concerned loved-ones and skipping past doctors and nurses. Soon a doctor joined the group. Distinguishable from his colleagues by his short, white coat and blue doctor’s scrubs, the practitioner glanced once at the black-haired Scot on the stretcher and then up to his co-workers. It went by in a matter of seconds of Kayla and Cayle, and soon they found themselves being ushered away from the theatre they’d instinctively tried to follow Andy into. One of the nurses turned up her hand and politely asked for them to wait outside, while the green-clad honeybees swarmed beyond the glass. Cayle couldn’t have been edgier. The youngest of the Murray brothers ran a hand through his dark brown fauxhawk, then suddenly turned round and launched his size eleven forward, taking out his frustrations on a nearby waste paper basket. “Goddamnit!” he snarled through gritted teeth. He was breathing heavily and pacing back and forth like a dog guarding its territory. Pausing only to watch the cluster of practitioners attempting to revive his elder brother, Cayle clenched both of his fists as tightly as he could and bashed one against the wall. “Fuck’s sake. Just a couple days away from Rampage and this happens,” he paused, shaking his head. “I should’ve planted one on that asshole driver’s jaw. Stupid fucking prick.” Kayla had sat herself down. Head in her hands, briny tears leaking through the gaps in her fingers. “If big bro doesn’t make it to the show there’s gonna be trouble, Kay. There’s gonna be trouble.” He breathed an exasperated sigh. “Idiots think they own the roads these days! Fuckin’ clown should’ve been looking where he was going.” Choking back a snivel, Kayla lowered her hands and placed them under her chin. Her eyes were red and puffy, and streams of black eyeliner ran down her light complexion like blots of ink. “You shouldn’t blame the driver…” Cayle snapped his head round. “Why not?!” he barked, speaking as if she’d been talking from a hole in the side of her neck. “He’s the one that put big bro in the hospital!” She glanced down at the floor, her lips trembling. It was hard to come to terms with. “I did this,” she said in a shilly-shally Midwestern accent. Straight-away his facial muscles warped with confusion. “What the hell are you talking about?” “It was my fault,” she sniffled. “If only I hadn’t been so pushy…” Cayle looked dead ahead and exhaled. As his brother’s partner returned to her emotional cocoon, Cayle dropped his tattooed arms to his side and prepared to be the unlikely Agony Uncle. “Don’t be silly now, Kay. Wasn’t you that got in that car and ploughed into him…” “But if I’d j-j-just listened to him for a s-s-second this would never have happened!” she wept, her soft voice breaking and cracking with every whimper. “Oh God, Andy. What have I done to you…” “Hey now,” Cayle said as his brother’s fiancée lost control of her tear ducts. His own body rife with concern for his brother and anger for he who’d struck him down, Cayle dropped himself on the chair beside Kayla and wrapped an arm around her shoulder blades. “Wasn’t nobody’s fault but that moron on the road, Kay. Andy’s a little tetchy at the moment, sure; but you weren’t to know this was gonna happen. It was a fluke, a one in a million.” “But I still drove him away!” she garbled. “I just di-...” “... and if Hitler wasn't an asshole, World World II never would've happened. Not another word of this, ya hear?” Cayle interrupted, sounding a tad more brusque that he'd intended. “S'bad enough having the big guy Stressing every second about this clash with LK; last thing we need around the house is for you to lose your nerve too.” She raised her head. Sniffled. “But who says he's even going to get to Rampage? He was hit by a car...” “We can't be thinking like that, girl. Just keep it together, they're doing their best...” Kayla swallowed. “Okay...” “I know you're trying to blame yourself for this – shit, I'd probably do the same if it were me – but it just... happened, you know? No use letting something like that dig it's claws into ya, because it'll tear you apart, Kay.” Cayle paused. “You keep strong, you dig your heels into the ground and pray for Andy. 'Cause when he wakes up, he's gonna need all the strength in the world to beat that bloodsucking prick.” Lifting her hand and wiping a stray teardrop from her cheek, Kayla looked into the rugged features of the youngest Murray brother. “But how do you know he's even gonna make it that far?” “'Cause he's my brother, that's how.” Another pause. He noticed the smudged make-up staining her sunkissed complexion and immediately recalled the Kleenex he'd stuff in his pocket earlier. Cayle pulled it out and handed it to her. “Looks like you could use this,” he smiled. “Thanks...” she half-smiled in return, taking the tissue with a thankful hand. Gently she rubbed the blotted make-up from her face as the medics in the other room tried to revive her husband-to-be. * * * * * ”What the hell are you talking about?” Andy called, struggling to keep his balance as a hefty wave crashed into the side of the ship, sending a splattering of salt water onto the deck and swaying it to the side. “Think you know EXACTLY what I'm talking about, Mr. Murray!” came the Captain's retort, as smug as they come. He'd turned his attention away from Andy again, now focusing on the choppy seas and the growing storm ahead of him. In Andy's estimation, it was getting worse. The angles of lightning draw closer and more numerous, and a few electric whizzes and pops had become audible even over the sea's turbulent pandemonium. Light was at a premium with the moon all but eclipsed by thick clouds, but he could see the waves growing bigger, and shuddered to imagine what horrors would await them as they drew closer to nature's terrible cacophony. “You've lost your bloody marbles!” Andy shouted, clinging tightly to the mast behind him so as not to be flung overboard. “Ain't the first time I've heard that one, kid!” he said in his dreadful crow. “If there's anyone gonna be questioning another man's character it's gonna be me, though.” Andy snorted, his frustration growing as the Captain's stubbornness showed no sign of relenting. Instead of launching another unsuccessful protest, he waited. “Tell me about CJ Woods, Andy!” Andy couldn't see, of course; but from the tone in his voice he could tell the Captain was grinning behind those bandages. “What of him?” “Well, you did him wrong, didn't you?” The subject wasn't one that The King was entirely comfortable with. He sneered, casting a macabre gape at the drenched man before him as thunder ripped through the air and stormclouds gathered. As if on-command, raindrops the size of gobstoppers poured from the skies, landing on the deck with a heavy pitter-patter, but still the Captain continue unpertrubed. “Come on, Andy!” the Captain bit. “What's wrong?! Cat got you tongue?!” “It's none of your concern, what happened.” “Oh but it is, Andy. It certainly is,” beneath his bandages he summoned a chortle that was surely the devil's own laughter. “I already know more than you could possibly imagine. All the kid wanted was to be friend, Andy! All he wanted to do was buddy-up with a man he'd put on a pedestal – a man he'd IDOLISED – and what did he get for his troubles? Some bumps and bruises, a bloodied face, and a couple of cracked ribs! Some 'hero' you turned out to be...” “But I didn't mean to-...” “Oh come on!” the Captain barked dismissively. “You meant every last second of it! Don't feed me that bullshit! If you actions were not your own then you would not be standing here today mourning them. You put that kid through hell, and for what? Your own personal gain. Nothing more.” Slowly, Murray rose to his feet. “There's more to it than that.” “No, Andy. There really isn't.” The Captain – whose fedora, miraculously, was still in-tact - shook his head and pulled down on the wheel, hard. Even from behind he cut an imposing figure. Still wary of the ebb and the flow, Murray carefully made his way towards his antagoniser. “But he wouldn't stay down, damnit!” “What, did you expect him to just roll-over and die?! Ha! CJ Woods is a proud man, a fighting man; he was going to fight 'til the death and you knew it! But make no mistake, his demise was nobody's doing but your own! LeStatt may have been there, yes; maybe he was calling the shots, but the man that put CJ Woods in the hospital that night – the man who DROVE him out of the sport all-together – was Andy Murray,” he paused. “Am I wrong?” Biting down on his lip, finding it harder and harder to tolerate his condescending agitator, Andy stepped forward and latched a white knuckle grip around the palisade in front of the wheel. He a swallowed painfully a mouthful of warm saliva; the Captain was right, yes. But Andy would never admit it. “And what of Tessa Windsor?” Already he could sense where this was going. “Save your fucking breathe.” “No Andy, I will not,” the Captain snapped. “How can you call yourself a man if you can't even confront your demons? Nobody's perfect, Andy; there's no such thing as a man without sin, and if you know what's good for you, you'll come to realise this before our journey's over...” He reminded Andy of someone, the Captain; something about his voice and the way he carried himself as he swung the wheel from side to side, but he couldn't quite put his finger on it. Not that the Captain was giving him much of a chance to contemplate such things, either. “Tessa Windsor,” he began, “another soul tossed by the wayside because everyone's favourite overgrown baby went and threw his toys out of the pram again. You know what I'm talking about.” “The title...” “Yes. That very strap of leather and gold, of which you wish to make your own! Do you have any idea how long Tessa Windsor had worked for that shot? A damn sight longer than you, anyway, and still you snatched it from your grasp... and why? For the very same reason that you drove that dagger straight into CJ Woods' back; personal gain.” “Enough!” Andy growled. The cold was beginning to get to him. Every gust of wind that swept across the deck send chilling barbs coursing through his veins, and his arm trembled – shivered – as he raised it to his brow to brush away the moisture. “Heh!” snorted the Captain. “See, it's not nice when someone attacks your character, is it Andy? Not a very pleasurable experience at all...” No words came to Murray. Instead, weakened by the storm, he slumped down on the deck, his head hanging low. “You, sir, are a phony. You're no better than those you seek to conquer, and until you get your priorities straight, you never will be. For so long you've prided yourself on being the nice guy, on playing things cool, and yet in the past couple of months you've stomped a hole straight through the careers of two honest, hard-working athletes that had never so much as said a bad word about you before. Who knows how long it'll take before Tessa Windsor finds herself back in the title frame... heck, who knows if we'll ever even see CJ Woods again!” “ENOUGH!” His dry throat cracked as he bawled, and Andy's burning eyes shot upwards to the bandage-clad seaman who, at least, looked down upon him. “Awwww, come on. We're just about to get to the juicy parts...” * * * * * “You know, we've been together for so long now; I thought I realised just how entrenched in this sport he is, but I can see now that I have no idea.” As her tear ducts slowly dried, Kayla Jarosz found herself still sitting alongside the runt of the Murray litter. Cayle was hardly build for being a shoulder to cry on; his rap list of misdemeanours was longer than Kimbusa's Pizza Hut bill, and his own wrestling career had been marred by a series of professional blunders, wreckless acts, and two incarcerations. But he was there, and that's all that was important. “Even I ain't ever seen him actin' like he has been,” Cayle mused. “I've been with him when he's been fighting for titles before – hell, I even won one with him – but this? This is a whole new side to Andy Murray. It's kinda like this match has consumed him, you know? S'like, he probably can't think about anything else. And shit, I can't say I blame him much; this is LeStatt freakin' Knight we're talking about here.” “... are you worried about him?” The words hit his inner-cortex with great impact, jolting his focus and making him think. His jaw sagged as he carefully formulated an answer. “Honestly?” he paused. “No. And you shouldn't be either...” She nodded appreciatively. “I know that... it's just, the way he stormed outta the bar. That's not him. That's not how he acts. Even when he was fighting Terror and Bagwell, he always had time for us, but right now there's nothing on his mind but this goddamn match, and I'm worred that if he doesn't wi-...” “What do you mean 'if he doesn't win'?” Cayle intruded. “Don't you go losin' faith in big bro, now...” “I'm not... it's just everyone keeps talking about how bad this Knight is. How he's unstoppable, unbeatable. From you and Sid to the guys on the television; everyone's making it seem like Andy's facing the impossible. And I don't like it... I don't like it one bit...” “Well hey, that's nothing new. That's how you're supposed to feel, bein' close to a guy who's about to go up against something like that...” Cayle's words wavered momentarily; he too knew the implications of stepping into the ring with a beast like Knight. “But you can't let that chin of yours drop another inch! The half-empty thing never got anyone anywhere. Me? I ain't even thinkin' about what it's gonna be like if he don't make the belt his own...” As the twenty-six year old's words bounced around inside her head, Kayla had no time to concoct a response. The interruption was a welcome one; through the door to Andy's room emerged the doctor. Red-faced, beads of perspiration where building around his receding hairline and tiny bags were emerging beneath his eyes. It had been a hard shift, clearly. Both stood up at the sight of the doctor. “Doc...” Cayle said with a subtle nod, praying for good news. “How is he?” an unrestrained Kayla followed-up. “How is my Andy?” “Well, guys, the good news is that his condition hasn't worsened,” the doctor said softly, delicately. Has face was a curious one; his face was tight and shiny like a taut, compacted ski slope, and his skin tone was a lot closer to red than it was white. His small, beady blue eyes darted from Kayla to Cayle and back again, and from the position of his thin eyebrows Cayle estimated that he was anxious. “But we haven't been able to revive him, still.” “Oh God,” Kayla said, raising a dainty hand to her lips, immediately assuming the worst. “What's the short and narrow?” Cayle quizzed, finding it easier to keep his cool than he had imagined. He looked through the glass; in the room his brother's body still lay motionless, but the scene had calmed somewhat. Now only two nurses monitored him directly, while another two stood at the back of the room, cleaning and preparing equipment. “Doesn't look too deadly to me...” The doctor sighed. He raised a hand and scratched his forehead. “Look, there's no easy way of saying this to you, but we don't have a clue what's wrong with him...” “What?!” Cayle's brows tightened. He was getting choleric. “But you're the doctor, aren't you? Ain't it your job to figure out shit like this?” “That's the thing, sir. Medically speaking, there's nothing wrong with him, per say...” “Come on, he's laid-out in there...” “Yes, but aside from a couple of scratches and bumps, there's really not a lot doing. If our tests are correct he's not got a concussion, his heartbeat hasn't slowed anyway, and a coma is completely out of the question...” the doctor swallowed hard, he was nervous. Clenching his muscles and feeling completely helpless, he prepared to throw years of medical school and hard graft out the window. “It's... it's like he doesn't want to wake up. Like something's keeping him from coming around...” * * * * * In a world not of our own, a sudden, dizzy wave overcame Andy Murray. A familiar acrid sensation rose from the pit of his gut and travelled up his windpipe faster than he could contain it. Suddenly a stream of vomit and bile came forth; spewing through his parted lips and splattering against the worn wooden surface, only to be washed away by the torrential deluge from above. “Oh for crying out loud! You'd best be thankful it's raining; the stuff's a nightmare to get out of the boards,” mocked the Captain. He was a picture of remarkable calm and tranquillity, even in the face of his own annihilation. It was worrying to Andy; jarring, almost. He could make no sense of it. “Look sharp, kid! No long now. Not long before clash heads with such an impossible force for the very last time. The storm draws nearer still, and the prize is within our grasp. Coming closer. Closer. Closer...” His words fell on death ears. Andy cocked his head back against the wooden barrier, trying his best to control his breathing in such wretched conditions. Now he was shivering all-over; trembling and shaking like a wet dog. Keeping his own thoughts in-line was proving to be a hassle, let alone those of the Captain. “So where were we?” the Captain continued, only half-expecting a response. “Ah yes, the life and times of Andy Murray and his many deviations!” Andy groaned, although the Captain would not hear it. “You realise, of course, that those who's strides you've broken are not strictly reserved to the world of wrestling, yes?” “Fuck off.” The Captain refused to acknowledge the Scot's venom. “What of the woman you once called your 'wife'? Jane. Twice divorced you were because you couldn't let go of the sport. You put it above the one thing that should be most precious in any man's life – his family – and everything came crashing down. TWICE. Now you don't have a goddamn clue where she is. She's without her son, she hasn't spoken to him in months and you don't give a flying fuck. And what of the boy, Marvin? Forced to grow-up under the guardianship of a father he hardly sees, and a mother he may never seen again. That's no way to bring up a child...” His body yelled at him, screamed at him not to rise, but he couldn't help himself. Slowly, feebly, Andy began the arduous process of pulling himself to his feet. “He spends most of his time in the company of a woman not of his own blood, and it's your fault Andy, yes. You brought this misery upon him, and forever should it weigh on your conscience...” “Mention my son again and I'll toss you over the side of this here vessel...” Andy thrust an accusing finger into the Captain's just, but the apparition chose to ignore it. “But Marvin and Jane are not the only ones. Your father, too. You think that a weather-beaten fisherman from the North East of Scotland wanted to see his son grow up to be a professional wrestler? Gimme a break. You were as big a disappointment to him as you are to yourself!” The red mist was descending. “Shut the FUCK UP!” Andy roared at the guard, who tilted his head sidewards. “The list continues. Soon, Kayla will grow resentful of your immersion in this stupid little thing you call a 'job', and she too, like all the others will leave you. And what will you have left? A championship belt, a dead, empty mansion and nobody to keep you company but your delinquent brothers. Not exactly what you set out for, is it?” That was it. The Captain had questioned his character for too long. Summoning what little energy remained, Andy grabbed him by the collar of his jacket and thrust him violently away from the wheel. But the Captain was strong; he beat his arms away and stood firm, his well-built body heaving under his soaking fatigues. “I told you to shut up...” “And I told you to LISTEN up!” Now it was the Captain's turn to throw a finger in Andy's direction. “This is a game, Andy. A test to see if you are truly worthy of what lies ahead,” he said. “It's blatantly apparent that you are NOT. Persist, if you will, with your charade, but keep one thing in mind when you're blurting out those oh-so headstrong words; this is a game, and you can't beat me.” Wait. Those words. In that form. With that exact pronunciation. He'd heard them somewhere before. “Say that again...” Hesitant at first, but soon stepping forward, the Captain stood just inches from the Scot. Pulling his hand upwards, he thrust his finger into Andy's chest with each word. “You. Can't. Beat. Me.” It came to him. Andy felt another bout of nausea swelling inside him, but he forced it back. “Who are you?” he began, but sensed he already had the answer. “Who are you, really?” Slowly, the Captain took a step back. Pulling his fedora hat from his head and tossing it across the deck, he looked up at Murray and waited. “I am the culmination of everything that you cannot comprehend. I am the biggest threat to your livelihood that has ever existed. I am that one, final roadblock on what has been a truly remarkable ascension.” He stepped back again. “I am what you seek to overcome,” the Captain continued. “I am the one true evil that has driven you to betray your friends and alienate your family. I am your Goliath. Your greatest foe. Your magnum opus...” A frosty chill shook his spine, and Andy Murray backed away until he could go no further. His antagonizer had a name. “LeStatt...” * * * * * Outside the hospital, Cayle Murray cursed his brother Sidney for not answering his phone and snapped his Nokia shut. Switching it off on re-entry, he navigated the hospital corridors with haste, eventually making it back to the corridor in which his brother was housed. Kayla was on her feet, now; exchanging a few brief words with the doctor, who soon stepped away from Andy's room and set off down the corridor in the opposite direction. “Did you get Sid?” Kayla asked. The puffiness around her eyes had began to clear-up, although it was still blatant that she'd been crying. “Ain't picking up. I'll try again later,” Cayle said, before pointing towards the departing practitioner. “What's he saying?” “He, uhhh...” she began, her words tentative and uneasy. “He said we can go in now.” The younger Murray nodded. “You ready?” Even though she was unsure of herself, Kayla nodded. She allowed Cayle to lead her into the room where her love lived. He'd been stripped of his clothes from the waist upwards, and a few white IV leads had been sunk into his chest. Andy's skin had turned a ghostly shade of white, but his barrel-chest still heaved every time he drew oxygen into his lungs, and that was a reassuring sign indeed. Slowly she padded over and looked down upon him, grabbing hold of his heavy hand and raising it to her cheek. His skin was cold to the touch; so much so that it shocked her initially and she dropped it down onto the overstrached bedsheets. Soon, though, she'd grabbed hold of it again, warming it up between the palms of her hands, and looking up at Andy Murray's closed eyelids. “Oh Andy, wake up,” she began, finding it hard not to crack. “Please wake up...” Behind her, Cayle Murray gazed at the grim sight that lay before him. “Think that's in someone else's hands, now,” he said, deliberately lowering his decibel so that she would not hear a word. * * * * * ”You piece of shit...” Andy Murray clenched a fist as the rain poured heavily upon him. He furrowed his brow and flashed a fierce snarl at the figure before him, who reacted curiously, laughing. “Always with the anger, Andy!” his foe croaked. “Even now on the brink of your own destruction, you cannot overcome yourself. What's the matter with you? Finally coming to terms with all the bad things that the squeeky-clean Mr. Nice Guy of GCW's done, huh? Is it finally dawning on you, all the people that you've had to kick in the teeth on this long, torturous journey?” “Can it!” Andy stepped forward as he spoke. He would not allow this apparition to get the better of him again. “Relax, Andy, for it's almost over. I hope, after all this is said and done, that it was all worth it. CJ Woods and Jane will never come back to you, and soon Kayla and Marvin will follow... but hey! Andy Murray won the GCW World Championship, so everything's fine!” “I'm not gonna warn you again...” “Just remember, in a year's time, when your career's in tatters and your personal life is thrown into disarray, that you brought this all on yourself, and that the only one to blame when everything goes wrong...” the Captain reached forward, slamming a gloved palm into Andy's chest. “... is you.” “That's ENOUGH!” All at once Andy sprung forward. Grabbed the Captain by the throat and pulled him close so that he was head-to-head, face-to-face with his greatest enemy. “What are you going to do, Murray?” LeStatt's mien taunted him. “Kill me? Oh go ahead, give it your best shot! It won't change where you're going, won't give you that happy ending that you yearn for...” “SHUT. UP.” “Do it.” The voice was that of Knight. Murray was sure of it. “DO IT.” Even in the cold, Andy Murray felt his face flush with red heat. He tightened his grip on the Captain's collar and lifted him from the ground. “DO IT!” “SHUT UP!” From the Scot; one last, primal roar, so barbarous is split his lungs with blood and thunder. WHAM. Andy dropped one hand and flung it forward, nailing the Captain on his bony jaw. He was struck with such force that he immediately fell to the deck and scrabbled backwards on his hands, but Andy wouldn't allow a retreat. Murray stepped forward. Fell to his knees and grabbed LeStatt's jacket once again. WHAM. WHAM. WHAM. Blow after blow to the bastard's face. Soon small, red blotches began to break out on the bandages under which skin had been broken. WHAM. WHAM. WHAM. There would be no remorse. No mercy. For under his fatigues, LeStatt Knight was doing to him what he'd done so many times before. He ridiculed him. Made him look a fool. He was laughing. Smiling. WHAM. WHAM. WHAM. But Murray kept going. And going. And going. Soon blood-stained bandages came tearing from the Captain's broken face. In places the material merged with flesh. They bound together, becoming one. Spurts of crimson flew up against Murray's own face but he cared not. His knuckles were bust and burning with white hot pain and the air in his lungs quickly depleted, but he would not show mercy. He could not show mercy. Punch after punch after punch, reducing the face of LeStatt Knight to a bloody pulp of torn skin and tattered bandages. And eventually, the laughter ceased. Andy Murray fell to his side, exhausted. His energy reserves were depleted. Empty. All that he could do was sit upright against the palisade and stare straight into the face of his haunt. It was only then that the visage – still alive, miraculously – curled his lips into a crimson soaked smile that the bone chilling realisation hit Andy. The hair. Short and black. Those teeth, pearly and white. Immaculate, though splattered with blood they may be. And those eyes beneath what remained of the bandages. Blue. Serene as a summer meadow. “Jesus...” Andy murmured... “oh Jesus Christ...” And all that the apparition could do was laugh that same diseased cackle. For the glint in eye that looked back at Andy Murray was not LeStatt Knight's. It was his own. It was too late for him now, though. As horrible, horrible reality became to dawn on him a mighty wave, several stories high, rose tall above the ship and consumed it entirely. * * * * * “Comeoncomeoncomeon...” whispered Kayla, still clutching Andy's hand close to her. The fact that the two hours Andy Murray had spent unconscious was not a long time by any stretch of the imagination provided her with no comfort. She may have overcome her own guilt at what had happened, but she was still besieged by worry and uncertainty. She cusped his hand closer still; raising it to her mouth and kissing it. With Cayle hovering over her shoulder, Kayla prayed, wished, for even the slightest sign of life from her fallen future husband. And it would come. Cayle noticed before Kayla did. Andy's eyes. Bolting open suddenly and without warning. Suddenly his body snapped upright like something out of 28 Days Later, only to fall back down on his bed straight away. Kayla gasped, hopping to her feet, terrified by the sudden movement. When she got over her shock she looked down to find Andy Murray laying in his bed, head cocked to the side, breathing heavier than a man who'd just traversed the Sahara in a day. “Andy...?” she quizzed. The fact that he offered no verbal response was unimportant as Kayla Jarosz suddenly felt a great weight being lifted from her shoulders. “Andy!” She hopped forward. Threw herself upon him. Wrapped her thin arms around his torso and squeezed him tightly. His mind soon followed his body. Andy came-to slowly but surely. Struggling at first to comprehend his environment, but soon latching onto the silhouette of his brother's rough form against the room's lighting system, and then the presence of his bride to be on his chest. Rolling his head back, Andy closed his eyes and breathed a colossal sigh of relief. “I thought you weren't waking up,” Kayla said, but found Andy had soon raised a finger to her lips. “Shhhhhhh.” Snatched back from the grim jaws of death, it occurred to Andy Murray that he had never felt more certain of the path ahead of him than in that instance. * * * * * The visage disappeared before them with a crack, fizz, and pop. Each of the three recoiled as the cauldron's glow faded into a bleak nothingness, standing together as Mother Nature tore through their surroundings with a brazen fury. Together they stood in the dull moonlight, with plenty to contemplate and much to consider. “So it is done,” said The Third. The leader was sullen and sombre. She pulled her robes tightly, protecting her skeletal form from the harsh elemental barrage. “The trial is finished,” echoed The Second. “But what of the fortunate son?” asked The First. She looked at her sister for answers, but The Third's head remained bowed. “Hath he failed, or hath he succeeded? Hath he found ruin, or hath he found fortune?” “Yesssss, what of the son?” purred The Second. Slowly, The Third raised her head. “It is uncertain.” “Hmmm. What say you?” “I am unsure. Such ambiguity, such uncertainty. The trial hath produced results of which I cannot be certain….” A pause between the three, sudden and abrupt. The Second was the one to break it. “But how doth we know now what lies ahead for the son, sister?” “It matters not what we know now, sister,” began The Third. She glanced up; her cold, unfriendly eyes making contact with those of The Second, and then The First. “It hath been done. The fate of the son has been decided, and though the path ahead is not as clear as we wished it would be, our duties are fulfilled. Stand now doth he on the cusp of legend, but on the brink of destruction. He is one with himself, now. He is on his own.” * * * * * “The drama's done. Why then here does any one step forth? — Because one did survive the wreck.” - Ishmael.
