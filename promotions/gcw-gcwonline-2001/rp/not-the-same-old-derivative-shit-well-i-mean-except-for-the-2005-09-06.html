Not the same old derivative shit! Well, I mean, except for the shitty and the derivative bits

6 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/6/2005 Not everyone gets perfect. Hell, one in a million comes close. As a literary device, any cheap bastard can be perfect. That's the price we pay when we leave the real world--the normal rules no longer apply. Mortals that're more than gods get to fuck any sort of heavenly whore they want, and never have to worry about losing out or slipping from whatever course they're on. They can be stuck in any eternal rut or everlasting glory, their entire lives subject to their individual creators and the whole of their purpose left up to the assuming reader. Life's not that easy though, away from the black and white of the narrative. No one can escape the laws that life follows, where the line between joy and misery's as thin as a bit of intoxication or a bad phone call. Living and dying by the random direction chance chooses to take. Or, hell, maybe everyone's around to fulfill a story, each life an unimportant piece in establishing a common theme. Fearing this, or maybe just fearing entropy, we slip around and try our best to create man in our own fractured images; everyone picking up a part of the puzzle and running with it until a greater sense of establishment comes together. The ones of us that want purpose, at least. Some of us are just bastards, happy to fuck with and destroy any sort of life--even if it's just a shard freed from our own heads. Take your pick; no one after this gets to. Sugar pills are, after all, delicious. Some people though, in or out of the text, are just born stupidly kicking. Let 'em. They'll keep stomping their ways through life, blindly destroying until they happen to catch a glimpse of a maybe something more. Words, words won't ever hold them back if they were truly born into it. It takes something more than fire stolen from the gods, sometimes they've just got to go up in flames themselves before they catch on. Trey cracks the sidewalk as he bursts along, dwarfing the population of Seattle. They tremble in awe when he passes, shuddering for the sight of something more than fame--almost like an immortality that he lets bleed into the world around him. Even the sky cowers, too shocked to let rain fall. And he can't help but smirk, indulged in his own mucky self-esteem. It's almost to the breaking point--completely ignorant of the turns his life's taken over the course of a month. There's no help for the bold. Read: the self-centered. Because, really, Trey's problems matter to the whole world and aren't just a matter of kicking a few ugly habits. No, it couldn't be that easy. He laughs and runs a hand through his hair, stopping before a crosswalk to light a Marlboro. A child rips against its mom's arm to move to Trey, cheering out the kid's roar: 'I wanna be like you when I grow up!' 'Shyeah. Right.' And he stares at the mom, shaking his head with warning. He thinks for just a second about how too young she is for the moment, her skin still soft and unbroken with work. Passing judgment without ever actually considering 'whore.' The thin lines we dance on to keep our images glistening. 'Mommy, it's Trey. Can I get an autograph? Please? Please?' The boy's voice rises in a shriek and drowns out his mother's argument until it's too late and Trey's on the far side of a line of traffic and burning out enough to make even Neil Young grin. The journey continues, waves of people parting to let a man possessed through. He hears his name a thousand times stereo and smiles to each voice. Eyes dart away instead of making contact. He calls it quiet reverence. A man bows in front of Trey, blocking his path. He's dressed in drab beggar's rags and his stringy white hair comes out in clumps. Unshaven, unwashed, he looks up to Trey without a trace of sorrow running in the million creases his face makes. Time, as it often does, seems to stop to try and account for the moment. 'Tell me a story, Trey.' The words come out slowly, cracked and soft, in the voice of a preacher. 'Don't have many.' Trey steps to the side, the man's eyes not following. 'You blind?' 'Yes.' 'Shit, man. You want some cash?' 'No. Tell me a story.' 'Like I said, man, don't have many.' The man turns his head again to the ground, silent. 'Alrigh, alrigh. Let's say I grab us a couple of beers and we relax for awhile. That cool?' 'Infintely so.' Trey looks to one of the swarm congregated around him. 'Where's the nearest bar at?' 'Two blocks, that way.' 'Thanks.' The elderly man stands and follows Trey competently; never leaves his wake or stumbles along. The shouts of recognition quiet and the people disappear back into their lives. Lunch break must be over. And, of course, they make it to the bar and Trey orders two mugs of Bud. 'So, what kinda story you want?' 'Anything.' 'Y'know Jordan White?' 'Well.' 'Well...' the tale begins. It's the same kidnapping story that's been spoken and written too many times since his return to wrestling. The man listens intently, a slate devoid of reaction. It finishes differently, more recent than any other time he's had to remember it. Accepting that he played along with the Croatoa bit, that he was forced to accept what was inside of him. That he was, most likely, too stuck between stupid and weak to deal with it rationally. 'Hum. How does it end?' 'That's all I got.' 'Not good enough.' 'Well, shit. You tell me.' 'You'll realize your mistakes and rectify them.' 'My mistakes?' 'You started wrestling.' 'S'my life, man.' 'And have you ever been convinced of that?' 'Don't matter if I'm convinced, it's what I do. It feeds me, keeps me solid. Hell, s'how I made my name.' 'Your name being your most holy attribute? No, you're not that stupid. You're naive, you're misguided, you're hellishly self-absorbed--but not stupid. You wouldn't have gotten this far, otherwise.' 'Look, man, you just wanted to talk some shit to me, lemme know. Think I might have somethin' better to do.' 'My point. You hear me, but do you listen? Do you wear a mask and feign ignorance? Stay if you care to listen; leave if you don't. It's a simple decision.' Trey makes it. He stays seated. 'Impressive.' 'Still thirsty, man. You gettin' to a point?' Trey orders them another round. 'No, never. Unless you want there to be one.' 'Wouldn't mind it.' 'Find yourself.' 'In a bar, with a hobo?' 'Setting is only important to writers. Are you a writer?' Trey shrugs then, remembering: 'Nah.' 'Perhaps. Liken yourself to a warrior? You've won countless battles over the years, but always lost the one that mattered. How many friends faded away? How many loves were torn? How many times have you laid restless when trying to sleep, just wondering what else could've happened?' He starts counting. 'Was that rhetorical?' 'It was whatever you took it as. I don't need an answer, maybe you do. Maybe it'd help, or maybe it'd just destroy you. You might not even be strong enough to survive if you admitted it. There's only one way to know.' 'Shyeah?' 'But I'm just a hobo.' 'Seem to be pretty smart, though. What else you know?' 'I know much, and appreciate your kindness. I must be leaving, though.' 'You didn't get to the point, man. Can't leave yet. I mean, shit, you just started.' 'A thousand apologies, but there are things to tend to. There'll be much time to finish things--next time. Until then...' 'You ain't really blind, are you?' 'Why trust everything but that?' Trey hears his name (Trey, if you wondered) from across the room and turns around. His eyes dart around, looking between people and jukeboxes and wood paneling until Jordan's face comes from the crowd. His black fur coat shines in the neon rainbow of a world of advertisements, for once tightly shut around his body instead of floating behind. He comes to Trey and sits across from him in the booth, putting his Aviators on the table and finishing off the mug. 'Wouldn't expect to find you here.' 'I was talkin' to someone.' 'Yeah? Were you?' Trey looks around the room. 'Thought so, at least.' 'Anything good?' 'Yeah, s'alright. Just this crazy ol' dude, I guess. What's up, man?' 'Not much. Keeping track of the employees, mostly.' 'Shit, shit. You want some brew?' 'I'll cover it, don't worry.' And it comes time for Jordan to call for six foamy mugs. 'What the hell you been up to? Should've asked you last week, but, y'know, didn't really come up.' 'Yeah, for your moping act. Nothing major, though.' 'Shyeah? Where's Tulz at?' 'She's gone.' 'Damn. You cool?' 'Yeah, yeah. Doesn't really matter. Just a chick, wasn't like I was trying to marry.' 'Gotcha, man. Damn though, s'hard to believe you ain't been, I dunno, like fightin' ghosts on Jupiter or some shit.' 'Yeah, I saved Fidel Castro's life from Jupiterian ghosts. Now that you mention.' 'How'd it go?' 'Easier than you'd expect. You feeling up to Knight?' 'What, between Stevens wantin' to kick his ass and Borealis ready to stab me in the back again? I don't even know.' 'Yeah, I wouldn't worry about it if I were you. Things tend to take care of themselves.' 'What ya mean?' 'That you're more than talented enough to get whatever the hell it is you want. I just hope you're smart enough to have your priorities straight.' 'Heh. What should they be?' 'Want I should act like your manager for once? Well, fuck. The world title's the only thing that should matter to you, you scummy bastard, and if you so much as think about something else during the match,' he drinks, 'I'll have to kill you myself.' 'Hah. Seriously, though.' 'Don't ask me. It's your life.' 'You're the boss.' 'Don't remind me.' 'C'mon, y'know you love it.' 'What, never actually interacting with a company I own? Shit, never get used to it. I'm a god-damned multimillionaire now--you've known me more than long enough to figure out how far that is from how my head actually works.' 'I guess.' 'If I had a head for business, I'd have started dealing instead of wrestling. This is all just, well, sheer fucking luck. Still trying to figure out if it's good or bad.' 'We both like all that extra money.' 'Yeah, that, but people rely on me. Sure, there's the chairmen that make sure I don't fuck up, and the investors, and all the other checkpoints along the line; but it still falls on me. Sort of. Alright, it's mostly a bullshit burden I pretend to bear. That any better?' 'Hah, yeah. I gotcha, man. Business is business, and some of us are just perfectionists.' 'Who?' 'Dunno.' 'That's what I thought.' 'So, what you gettin' into tonight?' 'Not much of shit, getting out of here pretty soon though. Maybe find out where Zodiac's staying, give her a shovel revenge?' 'Holdin' a grudge?' 'Not so much as I'm humoring psychoses. Looks like you'll be busy anyway.' 'Yeah?' 'Whatever you started at my house has to be finished up someday. That's more your deal than mine, though.' 'Guess so. Hit me up sometime, been good chillin' again.' 'Yeah, I'll do that. One quick thing before I exit stage left--still fucking around with that white bitch?' 'Who?' Jordan puts a finger to his nostril and snorts deeply, then smirks. 'No need to play dumb.' 'Oh, shit. Nah, not really. Ran out.' 'Good a reason to quit as any.'
