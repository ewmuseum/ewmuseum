The truth about heroes.

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Aug 29/05 It's all starting to look so familiar again. The faces. They come, they go, but some remain my constant. I've spent alot of time and alot of energy proving to myself that I can beat any of them if I so much as put myself to the task. The gnawing ache in my arm tells me I did a damn fine job tonight, I was electric. It's dull, would be nearly gone if I could bring myself to take those two little tylenol's in the bathroom. But I'm enjoying it. The pain in my body lets me know that I did what I had to, and now I can rest. But the throbbing in my skull reminds me that it's just a beginning. I've hurt more for less, but at least back then I could say wether I won or lost. This time though, it's not so easy. By the look of this memo though, I can see who the boss thinks won. Another week, another list of matches I'm not on. I could be arrogant, I could say it's because I'd dominate the low cards, humiliate the mid-cards, and I'm just not popular enough for the main event. But who would I be lying to? The Marauder? He knows all my secrets already, and he keeps them all. We both know Savige is getting the push and I'm not. And I was so close... When that feeling of elevated weightlessness is all around, even the sounds of the fans drowned by the roar of wind in my ears, I almost had it. I hit him with it, but it came too late, the wrong time, the wrong match maybe... Mike is a fighter, I'll give him that. He put on a show like few men would have, but he's a machine, and machines are predictable. This is far from over, this is far from settled. I'm just getting started, I was just starting to find my focus. And then she had to show up. Zodiac came, as I somehow knew she eventually would, and as always with nothing but kind words for her dear friend mister Lexicon. It's the truth in those words that makes them hurt, like a knife in the gut. I think I want to have a smoke. The room falls away like a dirty shirt, the door closing on its own, not like it matters, only thing inside is a bed and dirty wrestling gear. The hall holds people, stragglers, bums and junkies, prostitutes plying their trade in the dirty apartments rented by the week. Their faces hold glazed eyes, lost, alone, afraid and too stupid to realize that the terror is worse than the drugs. The drugs just make it easy, it's the fear to succeed that keeps them here. The fear of failure, the fear to try. Some people are like that, a man can't change the world. I give my favorite hooker a couple bucks. Not enough for a hit, but enough for a coffee and a sandwich down the street when she feels like coming down. It's not like I need the money, it's not like I need anything but a place to train and a ring to fight in... The air is warm, and the sun setting, the streets aren't much better than the apartments. Los Angeles is so full of beautiful people that they'd rather forget about the places like this. The people like me. It's how the system manages to keep going, the money goes to war or foreign aid, and the internal strife is covered in a blanket of embarrasment and ignorance. Sasha's on the corner again. She's only fourteen. I did a few things to her pimp that a man should never do to another man, I haven't seen a black eye on her since. Although her pimp has taken to walking with his dog alot more. 'Zeta.' She calls to me and hurries across the street, her heels clicking with each step. 'You comin over fer dinna tonight sugar?' 'Got some things on the go, be there if I can.' 'Either way, love to have ya over. You got a few bucks you can spare?' Those innocent blue eyes flash at me and for a minute I forget about holding her hair back so she can puke out a few bottles of pain killers and alchohol. For a minute I forget holding my back against the door as a stray john hammers it from the other side, still horny, still angry, and not quite done beating the crap out of her. For a just a moment, I forget hearing her cry about having to call guys 'daddy' because it brings back the memories of her real father. But just for a moment. Because beyond those innocent eyes is a junkie's complextion, her fingers twitch and she just can't manage to keep her balance steady. I give her ten, half of what I have left, and tell her that Carl upstairs has some pure stuff he's keeping stashed for his favorites. Better than letting her sniff something bad. 'Got a smoke?' I ask her before she runs off to get a fix. 'Sorry sugar.' Gun shots echo down the street and people run. It's a dangerous neighborhood, guy like me could get shot just like that. Then it'd be over, all of my dreams, hopes, and aspirations would be wrought into a single defining moment. Shot in the bad part of town, headlines would probably figure me a user, a pimp, or a john. Maybe all three. Not that it matters much. While the rest of the roster was posing for pictures, signing auto graphs and smiling for the tabloids at that ludicrous FanFest. I was here. In the bad part of town, hanging out with users, pimps, and johns. You'd never see Rich Rollins down here, giving a few bucks of his hard earned cash to help a little girl get through another tormented day. You'd never find Michael Stevens pulling an overdosing junkie out of a trash can and shoving his fingers down the idiots throat to get puke all over his shoes. You'd never see Jason Dread down here carrying a half dead hooker to the nearest hospital so that he can be accused and sent to jail for a couple days. You wont find Eva down here, giving her jacket to a dying homeless man, so that for at least a few moments before he dies, he can find warmth, a smile not filled with pity, and an ear to talk to. It's their stories that make them so spectacular, it's their trials that make these people extraordinary. They aren't the dredges of society, not to me, to me these are some of the strongest people alive. Through the worst of the worst these people look for a way to survive, the laws of nature dictate that it should be only the strong. But the modern laws of man don't reward their kind of strength. My feet stop. A weak voice calls, choking on it's own fluids. An even weaker hand clutches my foot, gripping the worn leather like it will let him live a little longer. Blood stains those fingers, stains my shoes, it is all over him, he's dieing. Probably took a few to the chest, just too stubborn to lay down and let the cold take over. Slowely I lower myself to one knee and grip his shoulders tightly, flipping him onto his back I take a seat. He reaches for something, his aim off, I grab his hand and he grabs mine, holding it as tightly as he holds onto his last few breaths. 'What's your name?' 'Le-Le-Lenny.' He manages to sputter, blood filled spittle marking his lips and chin. He coughs. 'Been a crappy day Lenny?' He tries to chuckle and agree, but all that he accomplishes is a couple more wet coughs. My other hand rests on his chest and pats it lightly. 'Don't worry Lenny, I'm not gonna let anybody else hurt you.' The corners of the kids lips turn up a bit, he's trying to smile, his eyes glazed and sightless. He can't be older than twenty, tall black kid, his shoes are gone. 'There's somebody coming to help you Lenny. All you have to do is let him in. Somebodies going to come real soon and take all that pain away.' 'P... Promise?' His voice trembles as tears stream down his face and fall to the pavement below. The white and blue mask dips in a reverent nod. 'Just let it all go buddy. All you have to do is wait now, he'll come real soon.' Marauders voice drifts off as the grip on his hand loosens. Marauder settles the kids hand on his chest and reaches forward tentatively to pull his eye lids closed. Nobody wants their body found and their eyes to be open, because if your eyes are open, it means you saw it coming, it means you probably deserved it. I check his pockets quickly, the thugs hadn't even stuck around long enough to do that. Poor Lenny had two bucks and a pack of smokes. I put the filter between my lips and search for Lenny's matches, strike one quickly before pulling that first drag. It burns its way into my lungs, screaming death, and I settle my back against the alley's wall. You'd never find any of the GCW roster down here, holding a kids hand as he dies. But they're our modern day heroes, our great role models. They can be the heroes. They can the idols, icons, legends, and famers. But me... I'm just a wrestler.
