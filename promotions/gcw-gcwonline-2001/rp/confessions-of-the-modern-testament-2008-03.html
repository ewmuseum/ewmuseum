Confessions of the Modern Testament

March 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Begining of March "The descent of an angel cometh to thee in the time of confusion." An irresistible glow glistens off of the rim of the holy water dish, bolted to the corner of the doorframe, for the sinned to bless themselves before they enter the house of God. The dish is filled to the brim with this water, worthy of any dimension of God’s people. A water that stings the lips of those who do not deserve his grace. The sting runs from their lips to the back of their throats’and then into the pit of their stomach, where the pain can only be released in death. Those who feel this pain suffer a fate that He brings them before “their time.” A time they feel will be much later than sooner. But is it death that should be the only liberator? Or should pain from an instrument of the Almighty be able to deter them from their ways and let them live on in a life of change? Questions based off of a theory that was written by man, but said to be commanded by God Himself. So who is worthy to answer? Father Emanuel, the author of the Modern Testament? Or is this up to the interpretation of the disciple’the student of the script? When does the student become the mentor and the mentor the student of the practitioner? “Forgive me Father for I have sinned. It has been one day since my last confession in the presence of the Almighty. Today I engaged in violence to protect the innocent.” Taylor bows down his head looking at his clutched hands, still red around the knuckles. “My son, you are engaging in the acts of God, our Father.” Father Emanuel blesses himself at the mention of “God”. “You do as He commands, what he wills of you.” “It is different Father, I do not feel as I once did...” “My son, you are forgiven of your sins. You did a righteous act of God, protecting the innocent from the grip of evil. Now let us pray...” Zacharia has studied under Father Emanuel since the creation of the Modern Testament, since he was a young boy. Father Emanuel studied as a sociologist and psychologist when he was in college, but the tragic death of his parents in a bank robbery changed his course path. He became a priest, studying The Bible from cover to cover for years until he was just out of the covenant, when he had a vision from God. This vision guided him to a new theory in Catholicism. He felt commanded by the Lord to write a “Modern Testament” in comparison to the Old and New Testaments of the day. This struggle with his views and the vision resulted in a defined work where God was no longer taking on the previous all forgiving mentality, but more of a vengeful attitude towards sinful acts upon the innocent. The Modern Testament mentions the modern world and how religion is being lost to the corruption of media and politics. The people of the world are seen as lost and confused in their faith; the lines of right and wrong being blurred and bent in every direction. This take over of evil has been impenetrable all over the world by any means of enforcement and justice. It is now the time for action from faith as it once was in the times of the past. It is not understood that God seeks out individuals whom are virtuous and stand up to their faith to this understanding of purification. This is not to be misunderstood as reckless crimes of destruction and death, but commanded and chosen moments of fate where a chosen mercenary of the Lord takes justice into his own hands. Father Emanuel is a preacher, an educator of his work, which was commanded by God. Zacharia Taylor was chosen at a young age to be a practitioner, the first mercanary under the Modern Testament to take his faith to achieve this religious cleansing. “Forgive me Father for I have sinned. It has been one day since my last confession in the presence of the Almighty. Today I took the life of a murderer when I caught him in the act of taking the life of a store clerk.” Taylor bows down his head waiting for the words of his mentor. “My son, under the laws of the Modern Testament you are doing everything our Lord wants of us. You are blessed with the guidance of the Lord, and took action in his name. You do as He commands, what he wills of you.” “Father, I do not know what is coming of me. I find these offenders on the streets, almost as if their victims are calling out to me as they take their last blow before losing consciousness, their last breath before death.” Zacharia takes off his hooded sweatshirt to reveal a blistering burn across his chest. “Am I being punished by the Lord for not performing his will to the best of my ability” Am I being punished for acting injustly?” “My son, not everyone is perfect, even a practitioner like yourself in God’s eyes...” “But, for certain it was a mistake I must have made! My Lord would not let my body be scared while fulfilling my duties to the Lord. My hands are always battered, always swollen and cut. Is this too a sign...” “Sign” Sign of what Zacharia? Your hands are the instruments of God. They fulfill the vengeance of our Lord on those of who have sinned and will not repent, as said in the Modern Testament. Every scar and every spill of blood on those tools you use in your acts are as much as damage on a spade digging into the earth. Every tool has its purpose, and every action has its consequence.” “But, Father! Is it not said in the Modern Testament that blood is a sign of weakness and evil” Is it not the spilling of blood that purifies the soul? Every person is born with original sin, and what is left after the baptism can either be suppressed or grow.” “Zacharia, if you are trying to say that you are as destructive and evil as those in which you punish, you are wrong. A minor mistake, one of which you have made tonight does not condemn a soul for eternity, but the minor spilling of blood or damage to the body helps suppress the evil and promotes virtuosity. It is not only death that brings the soul back to righteousness. The wounds that you are inquiring of are those in which the Almighty feels fit to release your anger and hatred. This is His way. You are not evil if you receive a wound on your skin in your actions, only human.” It was at these last words that made Zacharia shutter in the darkness of the confession booth. “Only Human.” It is hard to think of himself as being “only human,” like everyone else. He is as much at risk as anyone else on the street, living out their days under the ever approaching deathly vale. Bleeding is a natural reaction to a puncture in skin, so is it truly evil to spill blood? It is hard to determine absolutes in a text that was created out of emotion and a religious vision. The mind has capacity for so many things; any restriction to the mind promotes destruction of theory and thought. If all the blood that was spilt from these criminals was the spilling of evil, but the blood from his own body was not evil, how can a text say that it is all evil? If it is not evil, as Father Emanuel is saying, than it is weakness. If I am weak, how come my Lord does not change it so? Is it because I am “only human” and weakness is a human characteristic? “My son, let us pray. Our Father, creator, and ever-lasting power of the heavens, grant us your strength and wisdom to fulfill your requirements of Zacharia Taylor. Present to him Your Almighty power, and take back the ill-guided and evil souls of this world. Use his body to Your will, his hands are Yours. Let all innocent blood that is spilled be repaid with the blood of the offender. Our Father, grant us this power and guidance. Amen.” Zacharia blesses himself and looks up to the heavens for a brief second and then grabs his cross to kiss it. He then walked out the door of the confession booth and walked up to the alter with Father Emanuel to receive the body and blood of Christ. The weather slowly deteriorates outside as he sits on the pew behind the alter meant for alter boys and visiting clergymen, contemplating what was discussed. He pulls out the copy of the Modern Testament that rests to the right side of the bench, and rummages through it without direction. One of the artist renderings of a mercenary of the Almighty is displayed in sketch design standing in an alley of a building. The mercenary stands with his back slightly to the reader, his face masked by the hood over his head. The offender stands over the victim, blood flowing like the life-stream of the earth pass the feet of the practitioner. The offender is skewed by darkness, not baring only human characteristics, but also demonic, animal characteristics. Each man stares at one another, but pictures do not tell the future of action, only an exact moment in time. The scent of rain is in the air, carried from great distances by the roaring wind. Trees are half bent over by this Godly power, but it was still ever so quiet. Zacharia walks down the street into the local park with the intentions of finding a place to meditate on the issues he is facing with his faith. It is hard to believe that there is any questioin or confusion for he has studied the Modern Testament for years. Thunder booms across the sky, but there is no sign of storm around him, only the trepidation that the storm will come all too soon. A bench sit under a tree to the left of the walking path next to some brush and a declining hill. He sat down and bowed his head in thought, then began to talk out loud. “My Lord, I am at your mercy, and forever at your will. I am confused whether I should be taking such actions on these men. I want to be a defender of the innocent, but I do not want to be a slayer of evil. Not every man I have ever killed has been as evil as some, but in what seems like a mistake, I take their life. Is this the will of my Lord” My hands are battered and scared, and my body faces only a few scars from my own recklessness. Protect me my Lord, and I shall do Your bidding until You see fit the day to take my own life into Your graces. I just fear that it is becoming something else, something like a pleasure in this justice of cleansing. When I see blood spilt from their bodies it is nearly a...” Suddenly, as if it is a sign from God to not finish his thought, the ambiance was diminished in an unnatural way, taking away all sound. A flash of light in the distance from a bolt of lightning lit up the world, but there was no sound that followed. Rain starts to fall all around him, but all he can hear at that moment was the sound of a gargling, or bubbling of some kind, as if someone was using mouthwash. A near silent groan and then a pleasurable moan followed from behind the brush of his bench. What sounded like kicking of feet started to ease away into nothingness, but another moan followed. Zacharia moves off of his bench and then to the side where the tree grew and where the brush started. He moved it aside to reveal the source of the sound. Anger and rage immediately filled his heart, but his mind was frozen in time at what he saw. This was not the picture from the Modern Testament, this was something that only the offensive people of the world could muster up. He snaps back into time by the sound of thunder breaking into his mind. He takes off his hooded sweatshirt and blesses himself and looks up to the heavens for a brief second with his eyes closed. Reaching for the cross around his neck, he brings it to his lips and then begins to walk forward to what was down below on the hill. The rain was forever drenching, soaking all who were in its presence to the bone. The temporary relief came from the phone booth in which he made a call to the police for the poor innocent soul which was taken for only self-gain and dominance, not for the evil bastard that now lay next to her. The rain is soothing to the wounds on his reopened cuts on his hands. The warm blood is cooled by the rain and washes off onto the street, even the blood that isn’t his own. He runs behind the church to the back door, closest to the preparation room for Father Emanuel and his fellow clergy, but an extra robe hung in the corner for himself. He looks into the mirror above the sink and stares into his own eyes. “What is coming of me” Justice was served, but was it for personal pleasure? I suffer a blow to the lip once again because I froze. I do not understand why I freeze in the middle of every fight. Is it the blood that makes me ever so still? Is it the pain they feel that makes me hold up for a minute? Is there a difference in the will of the Almighty and will of my own? I was taught to think they are one in the same; vengeance of the evil is a duty delivered by God, and those who do it are chosen by Him. Is this becoming more than will, and maybe, no! I do not want to believe this! I AM NOT ONE OF THEM! What have I done? Will Murray and Mathews meet the same fate because of my, my... because of my infatuation?” As he says this his hands fall onto the edges of the sink to support him from falling to the floor in pain. His eyes are dilated and eyes very wide. Why are his eyes dilated? As the question is pondered the door opens with Father Emanuel and a manuscript in his hand. He was at first startled by the site of Zacharia but now he smiles and greets him. “My son, what perfect timing. I was hoping you could give me some incite on this topic of the Modern Testament (the name sends a chill down the spine of Zacharia) for you have much more knowledge on this than I do.” “Father, I am just a practitioner of the book of God, not a writer of knowledge or a wealth of incite. You, my mentor, wrote those words of knowledge.” “Zacharia, sometimes the mentor furthers his knowledge by the knowledge and independent thinking of his student.” “Father, what is it that you ask of me, what is it that you seek for your manuscript?” “I was reading through book 21 where the mercenary of the Almighty is mentioned and the image in which I had drawn up so many years ago struck me. I wonder, does this scene come up often, or do you not confront in this manner?” Father Emanuel lifts the book in the light, revealing the picture that Zacharia was looking at before he went out into the park. The image was sending rapid breaths through his chest, and the blood started to pump ever so faster, pushing more blood through the open wounds on his hands and lip. He falls to his knees and inches towards Father Emanuel. “Forgive me Father, for I have sinned. It has been only but a couple hours since my last confession...”
