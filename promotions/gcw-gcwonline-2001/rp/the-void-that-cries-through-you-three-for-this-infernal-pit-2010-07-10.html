While, in the ring below, sweat gushed from every pore as skin burned under the blistering lights, an air-conditioned Staples Center Skybox was an all together more comfortable vantage point to watch the violence unfold. Like the gladiators of old, the debuting Triumph Frost and Rasa, the brash and the stoic, fought as obliviously as they did violently for the occupant’s approval. In turn, index, middle and ring finger softly drummed against his glass. In it, a syrupy glacier of deep-frozen vodka flowed between ice cubes; his treat on a rare night off. Imported from the outskirts of a small artisan town on the Russian-Finnish border, whose stubbornness to embrace industrialization meant that he was drinking one of only 569 bottles distilled in the past year. Yet, despite dispatching an aide personally to the Russian tundra to collect the named and numbered bottle, a drop was yet to pass his lips. His fingers drummed three final beats against the side of his glass, in time with the referee’s count. The man was impressed. Impressed, yet curious, also. Impressed, curious, and nagged, taunted, by a memory. Underneath the grotesque mask, bound and buckled by leather, there was a human mechanism at the core of Rasa; one whom Christian Zenith was convinced he knew. No face to put a name to. No voice to recognize. Just a motion, a meager betrayal of body language, echoing out from deep within muscle fibers to the creases of its leather prison. The bar was sunken into the bowels of the city. Shitty, cancerous, worm-ridden bowels. Their eyes long removed from daylight, its patrons saw only what the flickering, dying amber bulbs could reach through the smog of dust and stale smoke. Only a putrid waft of the condemned bathroom’s splintering door recycling the air. At the bar, a small, weasely man scorched his throat with a shot of the house’s battery-acid whisky. The rickety still which pissed out the vile concoction was just days away from exploding and gutting the joint. The closest it would have come to redecoration in thirty years. “Another,” he demanded, voice as harsh as the spirit which croaked his voice. “Still got some insides that shit ain’t dissolved yet.” He felt the heavy air begrudgingly shift; the arthritic screech of the heavy front door drowned out by the frenzied, skull-splitting clickity-clack of colliding pool balls. As the door slammed shut behind Christian Zenith, it triggered a wave of nervous panic throughout the bar, conversations stalling and jukebox stuttering. Anyone ballsy enough to finish their sentence felt the words slashed from their tongue with a razor-sharp shhhhhhhhhhhhh. If the gold coin he was chasing tumbled into the sewer, then he would have to peg his nose and wade through shit. To sterile silence, Christian strode to the bar, resigned to the fact that he’d have to throw out a perfectly good pair of shoes. He already felt the grime begin to dissolve his soles, the rubber tacky. Above him the light fizzled, a shadow filled the void it left, and Zenith felt the point of a knife prod the small of his back. “What shall we do with you then, Sir?” the small man hissed. “Shall I cut you down from those legs and make an invertebrate of you? Maybe butcher you up to feed to the rabid mutts we hear howling in the back alley every night? Toooo laaaaate… Rasa cackled through your bones. “Too late,” you groaned, defeated. The ropes that had bound your wrists ensnared Kevin Crane’s neck. Your prey already frozen in panicked fear and teary regret, he was little more than a damp slab of meat for your knuckles to tenderize from gristly cheap-cut to succulent fillet. You looked away, but Rasa kept your eyes open, front and center, albeit narrowed by the vengeful snarling mask that it hijacked your facial features to construct. The raw flesh around your wrists sizzled as cold, salty sweat splashed up your arm, then blubbered tears, then a crude ooze of blood and phlegm. It didn’t let you hear Kevin sob, “it was fate!” It didn’t let you hear Kevin plead, “I was meant to find out!” You could only lip read the half-formed, "I lov-… I lo-…” It had mastered your senses’ parental controls. Rasa was aware of three who knew its true face. The first, it had to look at in the mirror; flesh hanging from its cheek. The second was wallowing in a pool of urine and blood at its feet, broken and neutered. Pathetic. The memory trickling down his trouser leg. The third… the third Rasa knew would have to wait. It still felt its host’s authority squeezing it, putting it back on its leash and suffocating it under their vile, compassionate personality. The third was its hosts lifeguard, to pull them back from Rasa’s murky depths when they felt their lungs collapse under the heavy darkness. At Belial’s feet lay the shredded remains of a heavy moleskin coat. Deathly still in the moonlight, it was as if someone had sculpted a sneering goblin from somber grey stone. The odor of stale smoke, ingrained into the coat’s every entwined fiber, had never smelt so rancid. It was a stench that the perfume of crisp, fresh currency could not smother. Still, respectful nostrils resisted a sour twitch. Bushy ear hairs preserved Henry Moloch’s final, wheezing pleas, and would whisper to him in his sleep until the day he died. From its depths you frantically clawed, nails splintering as rough stone filed them to raw bloodied, print-less fingertips. Your desperate calls echoed, bouncing angrily from wall to wall and merging into a hellish din which punctured your eardrums like a fierce fork of grounded lightning. Where nails could find no traction, fists and feet found no give, knuckles flaying to the milky bone and toes bruising the black and purple of a cruel night sky. Sanity spilling out of your wounds and into mottled, dying tissue, you slammed your head against the chamber walls and - for the fleeting second of impact - you felt the chamber#s pulse run through you; it was alive. “Hey!” you screamed, pitch fragmenting as your throat was stripped raw. “Let me out!” Above, your organic prison’s nostrils filled with natural gas, sucking it down into the chamber to which Rasa had banished you; cast from the heavens into the gaseous pit of hell. Your flapping lungs struggled to inflate, the air polluted by the hum of rotten eggs. As your itching eyes watered, tears trickled down the chambers walls, cleansing them of you tacky, crimson blood. You gulped the thinning air down, each mouthful lightening your head and tingling your fingertips. A fizzle from above. A flash of light. The zingy, damp scent of ozone. Your wounds felt it first; shredded fingertips, lacerated, swollen forehead and butchered cheek cauterized by a white hot flash. The walls were draped with churning sheets of fire, their ferocity dampened only by a suffocating deluge of sooty snowflakes. The pulse of the walls tried to detune you. This was where Rasa planned to wilt your resistance, purge your fighting spirit out through every streaming pore. Breathless, you roasted, eyebrows crisping and crumbling, lips blistering as they kissed the rampant flames. The pulse of the walls was interrupted by concussive blasts of static, trying to sever the connection. To one knee you buckled, flames lashing your back, bringing you to heel. The soot storm overwhelmed you, desiccating ash clogging your nose and plastering your mouth with a bitter, charcoal paste. A quicksand of soot and sweat set around your feet like cemented shoes, and you began to sink. But Rasa would learn that it had not yet grown stronger than the spirit which birthed it. Through the molten crust you burst, hurling yourself up into the swirling air currents of the chamber. Up scorched, rising air you scrambled, a sizzling jet trail of soot and sweat crystallizing in your wake. The chamber walls contracted, a glowing, snug, molten embrace. Your clothes dissolved, polymers fusing and crumbling to dust in the firestorm below. Your skin melted against the walls, bubbling fat beneath sticking. There was light. There was air. The pulse was loud, clear, and frenetic. You were free. You opened your eyes… and saw your life in flames. Flashes of blue blinked at you from within the churning clouds, subconsciously luring your hands deep into your jeans’ pocket, where your lighter weighed heavy.
