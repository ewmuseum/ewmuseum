Date: 03/10/10 There Ain't Nothin' Crazy 'bout Those Eights A Garbage Bag Production *** Not everything's all white in a madhouse like it is in pictures. In fact, there's barely any white at all. It's just that all those weak colors blended together make it look white; colors so weak they ain't worth remembering. Ain't nobody even all that mad, either. Most of them were a bunch of poor suckers who'd tried to off themselves and failed miserably. I don't remember how I'd ended up with them, but the nurses mistook me for one of the patients. I figured I was some kind of volunteer, but my memory had gone funny on me. Nobody seemed to know what I'd signed up to do -- a little bit of this, a little bit of that. I guess that's volunteerin' for you. I'd gotten to know some of the residents. There were all types of folks here, all with sad stories. It made me feel bad listening to some of them, because I couldn't remember having any of my own. I'd heard them all, and after a while, I didn't have the guts left in me to hear another. They gave me a room, on account of how busy I was, and found things for me to do. There was always work to be done there, especially when they let everyone out. I helped keep an eye on them, and cleaned up after they left, and when there wasn't much to do, I'd keep to myself in my room, for the most part. I assumed my job of keep everybody occupied from thinking of offing themselves again. There wasn't much to it. I sat in on card games and watched some programs with them. I tried to learn everyone's name. That was coming along. The last thing I remembered before I found myself here was that it was June. The weather was perfect. My career was progressing well. I was the happiest motherfucker on the planet, and then all of a sudden, I woke up here. In March. *** Benji Graham didn't look like a Benji, but maybe he did once. Over all his pathetic circumstances as a pitiful human, Benji feared going to hell over all else. Through all the bad he'd been through, he believed in worse. It was enough to get him by. I wished I could have shared his faith. *** Jacob had enough cards to choose from, and none of them seemed helpful. He laughed to himself while rubbing his temples, figuring he'd have to sit through a long, drawn out game until the tides turned and he notched another victory that meant very little to him. He made his first million at thirty-one after dropping out of college to do freelance coding for an early online virtual environment. Candice saw something in him then, and she dropped out with him. It wasn't his rebellion that drew Candice to him. His motives were far from rebellious. Jacob was too waywardly ignorant of the world around him to rebel against it. Jacob's father was understandably upset, having paid large sums of money to push his son through law school. The lines and lines of code appeared as gibberish to Candice, and as little as she cared for virtual reality, she was taken by Jacob's rhetoric about what those lines of characters and commands stood for. He wanted to create an environment where people were free to do as they pleased without being judged or punished because the characters in this environment were rendered incapable of doing anything of consequence. He said it would be more real than reality itself. Candice appreciated Jacob's cold honesty about his feelings on reality. It was as if he were trapped in his own virtual environment. She could act as she pleased, and it would never cause him any harm. With all the currents of fate backing her, she found that she couldn't win against a man like him. It hurt her more than she thought it would. Jacob's dream was realized, and he felt a sense of ownership, exploring the world he created. Characters multiplied and flourished, but then the juggernaut released new versions, each one offering newer features and objectives beyond being that changed the world Jacob created. The first person he told about his first million was his father. He felt it would be traitorous to cash them, so he had Candice handle the checking accounts. There wasn't much emotion exchanged between Jacob and his father in their last conversation. Mr. Barnaby didn't seem too upset about it, so Jacob made sure to mind his composure. "Well, nobody lives forever," shrugged the elder Barnaby. The younger Barnaby had lost many things, despite having never lost himself. He barely noticed he was slipping away. Some people say that's the best way to go. *** Gino threw down a King of Clubs. He had two hearts left in his hand. One was a seven. The other was a nine. There were no eights. He decided not to use the money to pay his gambling debts. Instead, he'd play for another big score so he could pay off his debts and get a real set of wheels back all at once.
