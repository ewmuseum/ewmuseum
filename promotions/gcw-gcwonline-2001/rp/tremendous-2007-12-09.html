Tremendous

9 December 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/9/07 When the phone rang, the bathtub wasn't full of blood by any stretch’it was full of water that happened to contain a swirling red haze that diffused into more of a burnt orange than a pink as it radiated from the newly formed hole above his bent and bruised knee. Crimson clotting under his fingernails, he held the flesh away with two pinched fingers as the other hand pulled taut a hair once ingrown; the strand twisted around and through a congealed glob that had been pus and was then a singular body halfway buried within his own. He sustained a dry heave; his throat retched and shuddered his whole. The ringing, even incessant, didn't stray him from the moment of release after years of slight irritation; didn't knock away the feeling that his body had been sharing with him the onesided pleasure of minor betrayals It was a nonfactor. It was him tightening his eyelids. It was toes curling in the stained water. It gave up before he did and left him to the qiet’save dripdriprippledrip’obsession he'd taken upon himself. And time allowed it, and he got to the dark blood, the old blood that had been there since the hair first decided it had no interest in sprouting. It came out in sheets, contorting a layer of fat he didn't even know he had as progress continued to rip the manmade hole wider. He had been cringing for some time. A nonabsorbent pad and four strips of white medical tape later, Jordan stood with all of his weight to his left side and tried to keep his right knee straight enough to scab up. A towel wrapped around his head absorbed all the stray water; he thought about getting another haircut and if the oozing breach was really the kind of thing that could affect a man's performance. Of course it was. He imagined he'd seen the bone before he'd wrapped it tight and sealed it against water. His phone let out a shriek at steady intervals; he shaved and didn't draw more blood. He had nothing else to do. He checked the missed called; no fragment of the number summoned no name from his memory or the phone's. He called it back. "This Frank?" "People I dont know usually call me Jordan. Whos this?" "Old friend of Sages." "Whos this?" "Trey." "Tremendous." "Miller." "Yeah, I know who you are. What you want?" "I want to talk about Sage." "Aint talked to him in awhile." "Thats fine. I havent talked to him in a long fuckoff time." "Well. Why?" "I'm working on something." "The kinda somethin that I shouldnt be helpin out on?" "Maybe. Not making any promises." "Fuck." He ran his hand over his face and stared at his reflection, not passing selfjudgment. "What do you wanna know?" "Not on the phone. Maybe over drinks, though. You know where Bar Charlotte is?" "Man, not meetin there. Anythin else you know around here?" "Been losing a lot of blood lately?" Jordan glanced past the table’s edge to his knee before he found Trey's line of sight ending at the stitches across his forehead. "Hah. Well. It happens. LeStatt Knight, yknow." "Yeah, I saw that. Saw you limping your way in here, too." Jordan let out a cringe at that, tried to betray himself but only worked at building another man's reputation "It feels good beating that sonofabitch, doesnt it?" He offered his glass up in toast. Jordan clinked his against; both drank. "Nothin against him, but. Fuck yeah. Startin to look like somethin out there again." "You're good, don't get it wrong. Lot of people are though." "Yeah. You ran the fuckin game before you, uh. Retired." "Hah. Before I got shot cause of Sage. 'Giveth and he taketh away.'" "Still tryin to warn me about him?" "Not even. You made your choice few months ago." "True. Is about Sage though, right?" "Definitely. You know how wrestling is though, shit gets in your blood. Couldnt just not talk the old shop when I had the chance." "Thats word. So, you wanna get to the disparaging talk on my boss?" "Nights long, drinks are on me. Just sort of guessing youre not going to say much sober." There may have been some truth to that. "So, when was the last time you heard from him?" "Who? Fuckin, old boy? Sage?" "Yeah." "Man, wasnt that long ago, really. Hits me up, usual, like once or twice every couple weeks. Just business shit, all of that." "Like?" "Fuck. Money, advice, that sort of shit." "Sage cant give advice worth a damn." "Nah, Trey, he for real cant. Dudes got some, some aight ideas, but hes gettin real bad about just not gettin shit across well. Can pick up on most of it but, shit, man." "He stays fucked up; always has and he always will. Thats Sage for you; used to be Jordan for you. Bwah. I cant get used to that. I dont even have a clue how youre managing." "Not that different from you gettin called Trey Tremendous." "Maybe, maybe." Nas spoke; the side of Trey under direct lighting gave a selfsatisfied smirk as Jordan answered his call. "Whats good? What you mean? Fuckin Christ, girl. I already told you I was havin drinks with a business associate. Yeah. Yeah, I said business associate. No. Nah, but he used to be. You dont even watch the shit, why you care what his name is?" Trey laughed and drank, wary of letting his Crown and coke water itself down. Animated and sinking into his own torso, Jordan’each sentence confusing the last’stooped over the table and dropped his voice below the volume of the crowd or the jazz group that superseded them with a cover of Coltrane’in the sense that the saxophone followed some base element of the original rhythm and left the others to fill in the rest with a swaggering melancholy that didn't altogether betray the piece. Trey slipped into a headnodic daze through the breakdown; only found the smell of cigarette smoke mingled with sweat with expensive booze with imported perfume again when Jordan, deflated, forced him down. "Girls." "You need to try women sometime, Frank." "Same difference. Women just older." He lifted a Black and Mild from the chest pocket of his black denim jacket, biting into the plastic tip to keep it still for the flame and the ritualistic sacrifice following. Shaking the match out, he flipped it into the ashtray and busied himself with drink; he lifted the lowball glass with his hand almost covering the top, taking a drink and then twisting his wrist in loose circles to swirl the rum. It was not mixed; there was no reason but habit. "Says the guy that just got done getting harped by a girl. Not my place to intercede, though. Who am I to qestion your credentials?" "Toure the vet. Ask ever the fuck you want." "Huh. No, no. I was just messing with you. Anyway I could get one of those Blacks?" Jordan slid the pack at him. "Why you wanna know so much on Sage?" "Writing a book." Fire. "On?" "Me. Sage. People Ive known." "Some biography shit?" "Not really. Its more like, its more like taking advantage of my life." "Sounds good." "Thats what Im hoping, Frank. You like jazz?" "Its alright." "Huh." Trey nodded his head, tapping fingers and the tip of his stunted cigar against the table. "Just looks like the kind of place that fans of the genre come to. You know?" "I feel you. Ex of mine showed me the place, I can chill on the environment." "Ah. Yeah." "Whose idea was that whole Rollins thing last week?" "The clips?" "Yeah." "Sage. I mean, I mean. Lemme get it straight. I started talkin about it, just rememberin how shit hed been in all those old ACWF matches or whatever. Sage had all the rest’the fuckin, knowin who Id need to talk to, knowin where Id need to go, all that shit." "So you had the idea, Sage" "Wrote the cuecards." "Nice. Its all good though, fuck Rollins." "Yeah. Sage, Sage liked Twizzy. Dont nobody like Rich." "Its his fucking ego." "And him makin you lose that US belt to fuckin Eva, way back." "Mother fuck. You remember that?" "Always was a fan of your crew." "Shit. So, you actually still remember all that shit that went down with Rollins and Eva and all?" "Yeah. Bunch of bitches." "I heard that. Damn. Did not expect posterity to smile on me." "Shit happens." "It does." "Speaking on last week, what was up with your theme song?" "They hit the wrong cue." "Ah, shit. Saw you were using some Saul Williams for a minute. Good to know you didnt just go back to Aes." "Nah. Cant hear a song but so many times." "You ever met Saul?" "Hah, nah. Aint really met anyone famous yet." "Hes a cool motherfucker." "Word up." "Its all Sage, then?" "What you mean?" "When you get on the mic’its all Sage talking, right?" "Nah, nah. Not really. Well, sort of. I mean. I talk. He just lets me know where I need to go with it." "No script to flip, then." "Yeah. Cant forget a line if I've gotta come up with em myself." They said their goodbyes and left in opposite directions. Jordan dragged feet on the sidewalk, burying himself somewhere deep in his jacket with a coat like a hermit's shell. Warmth was walking into the hanging knots of his own breath before they dissipated, was wreathing himself in a seatbelt and fighting with the idea of just going home. Relatively early; in that he spent most nights awake longer than this, regardless of how those stolen hours were spent. He cranked the Caprice to turn his heater on, rolling the stereo knob and hunting for a sound he could ride until he could find whatever decision might stumble past. "Next, the formation of words that fit. At the table I sit, makin it legit. And when my pen hits the paper’ahhhh shit." Jordan chuckled and changed discs, hunting for the next movement. "Word up the formation of words that fit’thats what I usually disturb you with. A lotta rappers never heard of this or know half the time it is’you doubt the Illa Fifth, what could you accomplish?" He respected the reverence hip hop had for its history. Many days he found himself wondering where that solidarity was in other communities; many days he came up lacking. People. People humored the past from what he had seen; they didn't respect it. They'd toss around "greatest of all time" regardless of how comparisons stood; they wouldn't look at the field and they wouldn't look at the way styles had birthed styles like the journey of genetics. Whoever dominated the now, then, seemed to dominate the before and the yet to come in the same breath. People lacked scope in their vision. He found them lacking in relevance. He found them poorly versed on why they made it to where they made it. So wrestling had become more of an art to him with time. Shit happens. Jobs could be art like art could be jobs. His car was still in park three songs later, gasoline burning the environment to ash gray as he linked from track to track to draw a spiderweb that spanned for miles and years in all directions’except forwards, of course; not to say he didn't bob his head to the beat of a song he'd imagined would one day come to be. He found his mantra. He held it tight to his chest and disengaged the parking brake. Never do what they do. What they did? Maybe it was caring or maybe it was caring that much. Maybe it was forgetting; maybe it was that they didn't remember to begin with. His neck locked into backandforth upanddown permanent agreement. He dreamed about the beach that night’about buildings without walls on their ground floors and all painted cement gray; about girls with hair the intensity of the sun; about hard 90 degree angles at all corners; about red cars with the tops down; about uniform studio lighting. He didn't have time for any of it.
