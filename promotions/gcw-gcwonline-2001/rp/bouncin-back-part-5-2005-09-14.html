Bouncin' Back (Part 5)

14 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/14/2005 [The camera slowly fades in and we see a small, virtually empty bar at night. Some soft music can be heard playing from a jukebox at the far corner of the room. The soft buzzing of a few tvs mounted in the wall overtop of the bar can be heard as well breaking the silence. The camera switches to a close up of the bar countertop where a black, gloved hand can be seen reaching for a large, half-empty, mug of beer. All of a sudden a shadow falls on the countertop. The camera rises and catches sight of a middle-aged, African-American bartender with a slightly graying beard on his face.] Bartender: Well, well, well. Lookie what the cat done drug in again. [shakes his head in disappointment] Why the glum look, son? Didn't we have this talk before? You got a lot goin' for ya, boy. [An awkward silence falls between the two men and the bartender just shakes his head in disappointment of the customer.] Bartender: Always up in here lookin' for pity. C'mon! Lift ya chin up! You up in here lookin' like a baby suckin' on that damn beer. [The customer remains silent.] Bartender: You up in here with that damn woe is me attitude. Shame on ya, boy! Shame on you! [The bartender turns his back on the customer at the bar and begins shining up some of the mugs and straightening things up a bit around the area.] Bartender: Did I ever tell you that this ain’t the only job I got, man. Hell nah! After my shift here I gotta go straight to the construction site and work their too. You think I’m happy doin? what I do for a livin?”” Hell nah, boy! Hell nah! [The camera turns and catches sight of Vince Jones who is sitting there guzzling down some beer as he rolls his eyes.] Vince Jones: Yeah, yeah, yeah. V knows this sh*t already. You come home each night feelin? like sh*t! Yo feet be hurtin?, yo back be achin?, barely got anythin? left in yo damn tank to tap on yo wife’s phat ass. [The bartender nods his head in agreement.] Bartender: Ya damn right, boy! [He suddenly whirls around and looks at Vince in anger.] Bartender: But don't you be talkin' 'bout my wife's ass like that. She might have phatty, but that ain't you. That's mine to be talkin' 'bout so step off! [The bartender sets the glass in his hand down, reaches in his pocket and whips out his wallet. He opens it up revealing a picture of a beautiful black woman with long, brown hair.] Bartender: See that? That's all mine, not yours. [The bartender suddenly appears a bit angered by Vince's reaction to the picture of his wife and quickly puts his wallet away.] Bartender: Aight! Don't be starin' too hard, son! [The bartender takes a step back and leans against the countertop behind him and folds his arms across his chest.] Bartender: Now listen to me close, son. I got somethin' to tell ya. You may have neva heard this before, boy, but Imma tell ya anyways. Now I may not like havin? to work so much and not bein? able to do everything that I wanna do, but I’ve never missed a day of work from either of my jobs in ten years. Why? Cuz what I do has purpose. [Vince slams the mug down in frustration.] Vince Jones: Yeah, yeah, yeah. You tell V this sh*t all the damn time he be comin' around this joint! If you don’t go to work yo kids ain’t getting? nothin? to eat. If you skip work you and you family out of our apartment cuz you ain’t makin? the rent. Bartender: Damn right, son! That’s why I’m here right now, my life and the welfare of my family depends on it. Unlike you, I’m part of the black population that don’t got it easy in this country. I ain’t got a name like you. I ain’t got money or fame; but, what I got is mine and its all good. [Vince Jones shakes his head and folds his arms across his chest.] Bartender: So, I hear you've got a new gig? [Vince looks at him in confusion] Vince Jones: A new gig? What you talkin' 'bout now? Bartender: You know what I mean, a new gig, a new job. Word around the block is that you're GCW boy now. [nods his head in approval] That's respectable, boy. That's very respectable. Plannin' on walkin' out on anymore matches? Hear you facin' some Ricky Thunder guy or somethin' this week. So, you plannin' on givin' him an easy win like you did with that other guy back in LOW? You plannin' on bein' a punk and no- showin', huh? [Vince Jones sits there in silence with his head hung low.] Bartender: Huh boy? You got yo head all low. Is that what your plan is for this week? Cuz if it is you might as well quit while you're ahead, man. Just hang up the boots. [Vince Jones raises his head and looks at the bartender in fury.] Vince Jones: Will you just shut the hell up about that sh*t?” The Violence come out here to the damn bar to get away from his damn problems, not be bothered by 'em! Sh*t! [The bartender is a bit surprised by Vince's reaction and takes a step back.] Bartender: Sorry, man. No harm, no harm. [An awkward silence falls between the two of them as the bartender turns around and goes back to his work.] Bartender: So, you never told me. Vince Jones: Told ya what? Bartender: Why? Vince Jones: [in confusion] Why what? Bartender: [shrugs] Why'd you walk out on your job like you did? [Vince Jones just shakes his head and sits there in silence.] Vince Jones: Business... [The bartender nods his head as he continues wiping down the beer glasses.] Bartender: Business? Like what? Vince Jones: None of your business! Bartender: Well, all I can say is this... [Vince rises to his feet, tosses a twenty on the counter, and heads towards the door.] Vince Jones: You've said enough for the night! [The bartender turns around watches Vince as he makes his way to the door.] Bartender: Hey... [Vince stops in his tracks.] Bartender: Good luck against, Ricky Thunder. Knock 'em dead out there. Do it for your people, son. Do it for your people. [Vince just shakes his head.] Vince Jones: V's out. [The camera slowly fades to black as he exits the bar.]
