Date: 2 December 2007 .:: ::: ::. CANTO VII ...and the rest unto their dance // with it mov'd also; and like swiftest sparks, // in sudden distance from my sight were veil'd... A halo of glittering gold enveloped everything she looked upon, or so it seemed. It had the effect of making everything within her field of vision seem halcyon, sparkling with... what was that turn of phrase? 'The peace that passes all understanding.' For some reason, she was reminded of that feeling, the one that came after she emerged from Sly Fox's sweatlodge in the bright Nevada desert, squinting post-vision into the sunlight. There was one major difference, that being the doubt that lingered in her mind right now. It hung there, a stereotypical dark cloud, a wraith attempting to dig in talons full of mouldering soil. There had been a certain ease about this whole ordeal that made it seem somehow... suspicious. All this talk of justice, of doing what was “right”--it had a certain air of simplicity about it, and while it may have been so in a book, justice in the flesh was rarely a cut-and-dried notion of right and wrong. 'You have questions...' Phoenix, as she had rather aptly begun calling the creature she flew on, seemed to have a knack for figuring such things out. She supposed that was a singular talent of beings that sprang, fully developed, from one's psyche. Or perhaps it was simply a power afforded beings who were... well, it was a little much to get into assumptions about what form of locomotion a spiritual journey might take. “None that I could not answer on my own.” It was a hedge, something she said because she'd been quite overwhelmed with moral lessons lately, and didn't feel like dealing with any more. It was also a hedge that would not work. 'There's no reason to shy away from your doubt, and no reason not to receive answers if they are readily available to you...' There was a brief pause, as though Phoenix expected her to argue. Truth be told, she would have liked to, if just for the sake of somehow putting this bizarre experience into perspective, putting it firmly outside of herself, and refusing to allow it any internal logic. 'Look at where your kind began... created in the image of the Maker.' Her stomach turned, because that required thinking about what she used to be. A weaker version. Lia 1.0, the version that had been traded for what she was now, at the cost of leaving behind concerns such as eating, drinking, dying... she hadn't minded the trade at the time, nor ever since. She wasn't like those of her kind who forever weeped to be human again. 'Think globally for a moment, if you will.' There was a hint of 'if you can' to the statement as well. Rebuffed, she focused on Phoenix's words as he turned, wheeled to the side streaming fiery feathers and flapping his wings where the updraft wasn't strong enough. 'They were made in the image of the Creator, and, like the Maker, they had free choice. They chose knowledge over trust’trust is greatest relationship the Maker has ever made. Knowledge is an empty chorus, a symphony that will always be incomplete. How do you explain a rainbow without stripping its beauty? And can any of your kind truly explain the journey the soul takes after it dies? Or what would make the soul forsake that journey, and remain bound unnaturally to the earth realm?' It was philosophical high-mindedness, but she supposed Phoenix had a point. 'I'm sure you've reasoned yourself to the point where it makes no sense to you now... where you cannot understand why a Creator, a loving parent, would give his creations free choice, but then allow them to make the wrong choice.' This time, Phoenix paused for an answer. Though it felt like a setup, she gave one. “I've often found myself thinking that. Even when I was much younger, just a whelp recovering from the trauma of my rebirth, I did not understand. I still do not understand. If going back to that thinking is understanding, I prefer to remain in ignorance.” Phoenix turned again, and her hands burrowed into the spectral downy feathers, though she was fairly certain Phoenix would not allow her to fall. There was no frustration radiating from the being, whose emotional state she had started to detect almost through symbiosis. 'I have no reason to be annoyed by your response, Lia... after all, it is the cycle of your kind to return to those beliefs. When one cannot learn from one's mistakes, one is doomed to repeat them, I believe is the nugget of wisdom passed around down there. Everyone must come up with a way to cope. Some are contrite their entire lives, foolishly thinking that one soul will make up the balance of thousands of years of bad choices. Others, the more independent personalities like yourself, decide that a belief so fundamentally logically flawed is not worth consideration. You pass over it, and move on to the next step, or so you see it. Both are coping mechanisms’one is simply effective for submissive personalities, and yours is certainly not one of those.' Twirling upwards again. She squinted her eyes into the light as they spiralled. They were past the sun, they had to be, but she was uncertain what they were flying towards. A knot formed in the pit of her stomach. 'It will make sense to you only if you can return. Return farther than the distance it takes you to get to your birth, return to the time when such choices were not an option. Return to Paradise’these gates are not barred to you, if you only avail yourself of the key.' Phoenix wanted her to be a child again, the child of a time before sin. But she had been born into sin, a taint that had washed over her since the very beginning, one that had made her choice to accept her Sire's gift obvious. Should she have gone wailing back to her Maker? This didn't make any sense, none of it made any sense. She didn't know if it ever had, even back in those halcyon days. The heaviness of thought began to overwhelm her, and she buried her face in Phoenix's feathers. No matter how she fought, she was lulled, and her eyes soon closed, heavy eyelids dropping down over green eyes that reflected the amber around her. In those eyes, there was confusion, but it was overwhelmed by the shimmer of gold, by the truthfulness of the past. .:: ::: ::. Chicago, Illinois She had been irritable ever since they arrived in Chicago. It might have been the wintry weather, though she was usually fond of snow, unlike her husband. It might have been the lodgings’a stodgy, conservatively decorated hotel room that was quite in opposition to the openness, the warmth and decadence of their Nevada mansion. Or it may have been that she was absolutely sick and tired of listening to Virgil and Dawn snipe at each other. She was standing in the kitchen, and she'd forgotten completely what she came in here to get. The only thing obvious now was the voices, overwhelming her in nauseating waves. “...have to always make those microwave... things?” Virgil was saying. The sneer was obvious in his voice, probably because he'd always had an insane amount of distrust for microwaves. Anything that radiated something he couldn't see into his skin, into his food was something to be warily and studiously avoided. “Well, you know, it doesn't do me much good to have to smell blood all the time either, you know” You may think mixing it with wine covers it up, but it's quite clear, thank you. But you know, I deal with it... it's kind of part of living together for the time being.” She couldn't handle it anymore. They'd been at it ever since she'd stumbled in from sleep this morning’or closer to this afternoon, if the truth were told. She'd been the last up, which was strange enough. Even Damek and Jirina had tired of nesting in the blankets with her, and had trotted into the suite's living room to beg from Dawn. Lia banged the cabinet shut, much louder than she had meant to. “Now I understand why we don't ever need to live in the Estate again,” she snapped, storming into the room with her hands on her hips. “Obviously, time apart hasn't changed either of your habits... or your contempt for each other.” Dawn's eyes met Virgil's before they both looked up at Lia. “I was just joking,” he muttered, looking away from her and out the window, which was suddenly very interesting. Dawn painted on a tense smile. “Yeah... just a little kidding between friends, Lia... nothing to worry about...” She didn't say anything for a moment. From behind her eyes, pain exploded, a headache that had been budding since before she woke up’since last night, in fact. Her fingertips pressed into her temples as she glanced down at Dawn, the other hand still glued firmly to her hip. “Maybe I just can't take it anymore...” A note of resignation hovered in her voice, discordant and exhausted in spite of the fact that she hadn't really done much recently. “Sit down.” The words came from Virgil, and they were not a request’they were an order. He shifted to the side on the couch to make room for her. From underneath the end table, a thick stumpy tail thumped against the carpet as she came close, did as she was told without a second thought. “Maybe you want to talk about it?” Virgil's English was a little bit off in phrasing sometimes, she noted. Perhaps because he'd taken to not using it much around the house anymore, not now that he didn't have to worry about TV cameras being in his face all the time. “There's no want about it,” Lia said, closing her eyes. “Well tough,” came Dawn's voice, cracking through her facade once again. This was a double-team, and she had the feeling that it might have been purposeful. “How many times have you tried to tell me that things are only worse if you hold them in” At least give us a clue, Lia. We're both concerned you're spreading yourself too thin right now, but how can you expect us to help you or even just to respect what you're going through and let you handle it if you don't tell us what's up?” Damn their logic, both of them. “I'm just concerned about this... situation. The three of us all know what kind of man Christian Zenith is. I'm surprised that there haven't been any other incidents... I'm uneasy about all three of us being in the same place, because, truthfully... don't you think he probably has an inkling that we're here already” He could be just waiting to make a move.” “There's no reason to assume that...” Virgil's basso rumble slipped in under the radar. Maybe that's it. Maybe she was exhausting herself with paranoia. “No, I think being careful is sensible,” Dawn said, and when Lia looked through her cracked eyelids, she saw the Steel Rose pensively resting her chin on the tip of her index finger and thumb. “He may be waiting for an opportunity to make a move.” “Or he may want us to come to him,” Lia filled in, voice flat, accent heavy. “He wants to decide that he's done with us on his terms. If nothing else, Christian Zenith is a patient man. It would be completely like him to wait years to get his revenge.” There was silence for a long moment. Of all people, Lia had probably known Christian Zenith the best’or at least known his business side the best, which meant that she'd had the most insight into his scheming, his manipulation... and she knew just how dangerous he was when angered. There had been significant risk involved in making an alliance with him, but then again, was there not significant risk in any alliance? “So... maybe we just show him that we can't be intimidated. It worked last time.” 'Last time' had been when they all left. There was a fundamental flaw in presuming that they'd had a confrontation last time. Perhaps Christian had thought they'd sink without him and come running back, looking for shelter. Perhaps over time, that had turned into a seething anger that was waiting to visit itself. Or perhaps he was secure, knowing that he had a tracking chip embedded in Lia Ambrosi's neck, and had the ability to exact his revenge any time he saw fit. “The ball's in our court now,” Virgil said, shifting on the couch. “If he knows we're here, then so what” He can't track our movements anymore. He may not even be expecting you to come for Markus--? “We don't even know that he has Markus,” Lia broke in. “That's why we need to come up with a plan to involve all three of us,” Dawn finished. Lia had staunchly disagreed when they'd first gotten together in Chicago. She had adamantly refused to allow Virgil and Dawn to be part of anything that might put them in danger. It had always made her something of a matriarchal figure back in the days of Dies Irae’her complete willingness to take the difficult tasks on herself, as opposed to letting one of her teammates potentially take the fall. Despite the fact that she was on her own now, at least where GCW was concerned, her personality hadn't changed so much from holding a World Title. “I don't even know if I want to look for him, aside from my own reasons.” The silence was longer this time. Lia had never been able to break through to Markus and make him understand that what he had in Nevada was more a family than a loose group of protectors. Even Rune was more inclined to let Lia nurture and teach him than was Markus, and Markus was actually of Lia's own bloodline. “What's going on with Vivica?” Dawn was the one to ask. Virgil paid perhaps a bit closer attention, but had grown greatly distasteful of the wrestling business since he had left it. Dawn seemed to be much the same’both of them had settled into lives that were quite complete without ever remembering that GCW existed, except to know that Lia held their World Title still, and was making quite the name for herself there. “There have been some... issues.” Virgil huffed. “Obviously, there have... what sort?” “I caught her talking to Octavian a week ago, when she was supposed to be meeting me in the locker room. She told me she'd have my back that night, against Kimbusa, with the Establishment ringside as lumberjacks... and I turned around to find her with a chair in her hands. Had I not ducked...” A growl came from across the room. It sounded animalistic enough, but it actually emanated from the Steel Rose's half-parted lips. “I knew it would come to this one day... You can't depend on people's loyalty for anything. Vivica, of all people’you take her in your hands, help train her up from nothing so that she could stand on her own two feet without Christian around, and now she wants to act like you've done nothing for her, just turn her back and join that group of... of... well they're just scum!” “There's no proof,” Lia said, her fingertips massaging at the bridge of her nose. Even with her eyes closed, she felt like she could see explosions of gold light pounding in rhythm with her heart. “Proof be damned,” Virgil said, “She could be hiding the proof. And at the same time, wanting you to save Markus. She was practically begging after he was taken.” The tension in the room had suddenly become quite high. Lia wondered, briefly, if it was only bleeding out of her and into her companions. They were, after all, the two people emotionally closest to her in the entire world. No one knew her like they did. Which meant that both of them likely knew the agonies she was experiencing trying to figure out whether or not Vivica was betraying her. And Dawn and Virgil already felt like she'd done it once before. She was torn, to tell the truth. Torn between rescuing a childe that had never been particularly grateful for her gift to him and telling him to fend for himself. Torn between an openheartedness that left her open to assault from all sides and exceptional cruelty that was perhaps cunning enough to see her through to another day. Choices, choices. .:: ::: ::. CANTO VIII ...so in that light // I other luminaries saw, that cours'd // in circling motion rapid more or less, // as their eternal phases each impels... It would have been easiest to keep her eyes closed. That much was obvious, but it wouldn't have pleased Phoenix. When one was at the mercy of one's enemies (for so she had started to conceive of the being that carried her higher and higher at every swoop of wing), one didn't have much choice but to do whatever would keep one alive. Around them now, swarms of light zoomed back and forth. It was like some cosmic speedway, full of shooting stars challenging one another to races. One shot overhead, trailing a silvery tail, and her eyes took it in with the wonder of a child seeing fireworks for the first time. They were all around, closer than she had realized at first, and they seemed to be swooping ever closer. She clenched her fingers tighter around Phoenix's feathers as one swooped close, blinding light causing pain to thread through her eyes. She resisted the urge to bury her face in the great bird's back’fear was unbecoming, her master had always taught her that. And suddenly, it was as though her entire body were being set ablaze. She sat bolt upright, threatening to scream... and then realized that the warmth radiating through her was nothing to be frightened of. It was... comforting, if somewhat odd. Her jaw dropped, the scream falling limp before it ever came out. In front of her hovered a flickering ball of light. “What...” 'Gavrilo will work... though I have many names.' She was trying to grapple with the strangeness of it speaking to her, but it went on. 'So long it's been since we had anyone up in the spheres just for a visit... pardon me, I don't mean to make it sound like a lark. Obviously, you're here on serious business, but it's just... exciting, you know?' 'The young woman has questions, Gavrilo.' Phoenix's words confused her. By no means was she a young woman, and proclaiming such made it seem as though Phoenix, Gavrilo, the spheres, the lights, were all timeless and unchanging, an unbelievable story that was somehow still happening despite its complete unlikelihood. 'Ah, questions... yes, she seems like the type who'd have questions.' 'I was explaining to her that the concepts of justice between our two realms are often quite different...' 'Well yes, what you've got down there is basically the corrupt representation of what's going on up here. Not that they can help it, bless them. They certainly have enough to worry about without trying to bring back something that would completely change the way things are run down there.' 'Gavrilo... questions?' It turned to look at her, this ball of light. Somehow, she knew that. She didn't have any questions’but suddenly something fell through her lips with the ease of a raindrop from a cloud. “If justice is so simple, then explain the divine right of kings... explain... explain how those in power remain in power, with scruples such as they have.” There was a moment's silence in which the ball of light glowed brighter. 'Feisty this one... not an easy question, though there's a simple answer for it. See, good rulers can be born of bad parents. Vice versa’some of the best lineage can produce the most horrible monsters to ever set foot on a throne. What it boils down to-and I'm sure you could have answered it yourself if you thought about it long enough- is personal choice. You choose to be the kind of person that you are... you choose how you'll rule, so to speak. It's not our fault if someone makes a bad choice... it's just that rulers have many more expectations laid at their feet than do normal people.' That line resonated within her. For all the criticism she'd taken from people recently, it was this part that was the truth. She made her own choices, best as she could, but there was always someone out there to complain about it. 'I guess the bottom line is... your choices always have effects on other people. But other people also follow their own morals, and some of those morals are... more selfish than others. You're taking a lot of flack? Use your moral compass... figure out which choice is right, who's got a real complaint, and who's blowing hot air, so to speak. You can't be guaranteed that you're making the right decision, but somebody who's astute like you is bound to have a better chance knowing right from wrong.' She could feel the sleep coming over her again, the all-encompassing. Was that truly all there was to say? There was no more lesson than that? “So what you're saying is that...” But those were the only words she could speak before her lips closed, along with her eyes, lids falling shut over the lush verdure of her irises. But the thought had not died, and it echoed silently inside her, sound trembling through the empty hallways of her soul. I'll never know... .:: ::: ::. Chicago, Illinois You can never truly know the inside of somebody else's soul. That's something we'd all do well to remember, no matter how many times we want to believe these little stories that we concoct. She's my best friend, he's my husband, she's my mother, she'd never... But sometimes, wanting to believe something isn't enough to make it true. Sometimes, wanting to believe something doesn't do anything other than make us look like the desperate ex-lover who wants to pretend that his woman still loves him, even though she's now married to another man. I could never be that pathetic, that's what we all want to tell ourselves, but how many of us are lying when we say that? How many of us fail to remember that solipsism, not unity, is the only fact of life that we can count on, except maybe change. I'm no different. I can be just as much a dupe as the next person, because it turns out that losing whatever humanity you had left doesn't change the fact that you want meaningful contact with another being. Sometimes, you don't want to feel alone, you want to merge with another entity, if only for a moment. You want to feel like somebody sees the world through your eyes. I guess Vivica and I were always an unlikely pair. We've had completely different goals since we first met. I don't know why Christian had the brilliant idea that we'd work together as a tag team, unless he just thought it would get Vivica out of his hair. He never seemed like the type who'd want any woman, even his fianc’e, around for an extended period of time. She's always had more trouble getting noticed than I have. I don't know why that is’maybe my personality is harder than hers, maybe I'm just the type of person who wouldn't have let my intended take the spotlight. Maybe that makes me a bad person. Or maybe it doesn't. Maybe Vivica just took more time to come into her own, and there's no reason to blame me for that. Who can read a book that's written in a language they've never seen before? I'm starting to realize that it doesn't make my life easier to just assume that I know someone. I should know that by now, after everything that I've been through. Maybe I'm an idealist at heart. I took the simple solution, and pretended that I could unify disparate personalities under one banner. I tried to make us into a family. Hah. Families may not be blood related, but there are times when that blood tie makes up the difference between people who would really prefer to shred one another into ribbons, take whatever commonality they had between them and rip it to bits, laughing the whole time. Maybe it was under the surface, but I didn't see it. How do you hide something like this from your best friend? Or am I paranoid? Am I just plain scared and paranoid? Is it easier to assume the worst about someone than to try to understand their complexities, their real motivations? Stress can do a lot to change someone's personality, and what's more stressful than losing the one you love? I should know. I should be sympathetic. But I'm not, and over what? Over a World Title? Over the idea that I might be helping someone out who really wants to see me fail in the ring at NC-17? This situation has become a riddle with no answer. No clever turn of phrase will provide the truth, and all I'm left with is the unsettling feeling that I thought I knew Vivica... and I really don't. And if you can't trust someone who claims to be your best friend, then who do you trust? HOW do you trust? Markus had better be glad that I've got some affection for blood ties, otherwise I might just leave him to his own devices. Or at the very least, I wouldn't have come here for him. Here, of all places, to confront the jackass who put a tracking chip in my neck and probably kidnapped Markus when we had our backs turned. What the hell is my life turning into? It's a soap opera, without a coherent plot line, but replete with looming villains around every corner. At the very least, I've still got Virgil and I've still got Dawn. I don't know what I'd do without them, if the truth were known. I'm not even sure if either of them know how much they mean to me, especially given the fact that god-damned pride tends to prevent me admitting it. They've never failed me, even when they've doubted my motives, my choices... loyalty like that... no, LOVE like that... is not something that you can come across just walking down the street. It still hurts, though, when layers are sloughed off one by one. Christian turned out to be exactly what he appeared’self-serving. Seymour Almasy? A turncoat weakling. Hope and Keiko disappear just when Hope most needed assistance, and the KB has turned its back on me. I can't talk to Blaise anymore, and Ian Childress? Out of reach. There are points where you start wondering... if you give a piece of yourself to each of these people that you let through the door, what's left when they leave? I can cling to Virgil and Dawn ever more tightly, but what happens if they decide they have to go too? Notice the desperation in my tone. Gods help me if I were ever the last being on the planet. I'd drive myself into a pathetic state. .:: ::: ::. CANTO IX ...o ye misguided souls! // infatuate, who from such a good estrange // your hearts, and bend your gaze on vanity, // alas for you! 'Momma!' The little voice pricked at her ears, white-hot droplets that singed where they touched. 'Momma!' It sounded more urgent now, desperation mixed with fear in the voice of the tiny female specter. Guilt pulled at her first, then fear. Her eyes snapped open. It was too bright, lights whooshing past at a thunderous rate of speed and obscuring everything around her. Her lips answered with a word unbidden, unspoken for years upon years. “Elena?” She raised a hand to her mouth to prevent it from coming’but it was too late. It echoed, it sounded louder than she had possibly spoken it, and every thread of her being vibrated with noise. And fear. There, too, was fear, the fear of seeing that little face again, with mother's elven features and father's eyes. Her beauty had been obvious, even at so young an age. A light approached her, almost timidly. It shied away to the other side. She got the impression of a small child, hiding behind a tree, peering at her. Her heart skipped a beat, fingers inadvertently clenching Phoenix. Was she scared? Scared of a small child? “...Elena?” Her voice was tentative, and shook with emotion that she had long ago tried to suppress. But she found that she couldn't control this, not in unfamiliar territory where nothing was under control. 'Momma?' She swallowed, and somehow found her voice. Again, it was almost out of her control. “Elena, it's me... It's Momma.” The little light buzzed closer, and she saw eyes, though they were not really there. Blue eyes. Her father's blue eyes. “Momma, you look sad...” She touched her fingertips to her cheeks, wiping away moisture that had not been there moments ago. “No, little one... Momma's not sad, just... surprised to see you is all.” The little light dimmed for a moment, flitted almost too far behind her for her to turn her head comfortably to see. The words came again, this time softer, almost as though she was fading away. 'Momma, why didn't Papa come? Did he forget about me too?” The innocence of the child's question stabbed her far more deeply than the question itself. Of course she hadn't forgotten, that was the answer, and neither had Elena's father. Virgil had never forgotten his baby girl... But even as she thought the answer to herself, and willed her lips to say it, she realized that she wasn't so certain. She wasn't certain if the answer was correct for either of them, but more so herself. If she hadn't forgotten.. 'Then why are you so selfish?' She wasn't certain where the voice came from. It sounded like her own, brutally accusing her of staining the sanctity of her child's death by forgetting to mourn her each day that she woke without her. But would it not have been worse for her child to lose both parents to this plague-filled vampiric existence? What would have happened to Elena if... Those questions were moot points. They were excuses. And she had relentlessly provided herself with them for years to assuage the guilt of her own choices. What kind of mother would abandon a child for her own gain? .:: ::: ::. Chicago, Illinois The snow fell relentlessly outside, but that hadn't stopped Lia from throwing open the sliding glass door of the suite to sit on the balcony. A tiny flake was nestled in her eyelashes, but she blinked it away after only a moment. It had been too close for any of its ornate crystalline structure to be visible, and it melted on her skin, trickling down her cheek. She had woken up at 1:30. It was 2:30 now. The Chicago skies looked positively magical, surreal and glowing with tiny flecks of white illuminated by the myriad streetlamps. She didn't think she'd be able to sleep anymore. There was another person in the suite who couldn't sleep anymore. This sort of thing was common, as Virgil Ambrosi was, for several reasons, cursed with the inability to sleep by himself, at least since he'd been re-united with Lia. His feet made no sound on the stiff carpet as he walked toward the balcony. They scratched slightly, however, when he stepped onto the cold and rough cement balcony. He hated cold weather, and his feet hated cold stone, but he figured it would ultimately be worth it. "You picked a strange night for sightseeing..." She jumped a little, but tried not to betray her surprise, because there were times when she hated being caught off guard. Rather than finding herself able to relax in Virgil's presence (which was the norm), she found her shoulder muscles still tight, her brain still whirring with images. “I couldn't sleep anymore.” That much ought to have been obvious. It was also a hedge. "I noticed... the bed got cold." He noted the somewhat defensive expression on her face. She was hastily constructing walls around a mental fortress, but his siege would be forthcoming. "There must have been something important, to drag an exhausted one such as you from sleep." She waved a hand dismissively. “Just those dreams again... the strange ones that I've been telling you about.” They were silly dreams, all told, but they seemed to happen with increasing frequency and intensity. Every time her eyes closed, she was being carried away into that strange realm again. It had started to make sleep just as unattractive a proposition as being awake. He nodded. He knew all too well the vivid dreams she'd been having lately. He'd been wondering if he'd start having the same dreams at some point. "What happened tonight?" For all that they were dreams, Virgil couldn't help but be interested in them. They were far too coherent to not mean something. She chewed her lip for a moment, biting down on the lip ring that she all too often forgot was even there. Her eyes flicked over at him for a moment, wondering if he was too sleepy to be persistent. “More prattle about justice... Evidently, I haven't understood whatever these dreams have been trying to tell me yet, which is a shame, because I'm getting tired of having them. And they were so much more personal tonight...” He raised an eyebrow. A rather tired eyebrow. "That doesn't sound as specific as the others." She didn't know if she had the strength to broach the topic. But perhaps in a bleed-over from the dream, it felt like her tongue was taken control of, and she was speaking before she could control herself. “Do you ever think about Elena?” The question was bare, almost accusative in its nature. The tone of the question struck him, moreso than the question itself. That followed later. He wasn't sure how to respond, the name of his daughter conjuring fuzzy memories by far not forgotten, though the emotions surrounding them may have been. "I've not forgotten her. I still remember how she looked." “She had your eyes...” Lia said softly, turning her head to the side, resting it on her knee in a way that she didn't have to look directly at him. The guilt of the encounter with what she presumed to be Elena's spirit still weight heavily on her, a lead weight dragging her under the murky waters. Virgil began to wonder where they were headed. It was difficult for him to be without the latest information, and it was particularly difficult when it was information he cared about. "Why do you ask?" “Because I encountered her in my dream tonight...” Lia's voice was barely making it above whisper volume, and perhaps was all the more impacting because of that. “I wonder if I made the right choices.” Virgil shook his head, somewhat sullenly, even for him. "With Elena, we had no choices..." “I didn't have to choose this life,” she said, her voice suddenly splitting into ragged notes of discord. “I didn't have to keep myself alive at the expense of everything else, forget that I'd ever had her, and focus selfishly on my own desires for centuries.” He took a couple of steps closer to her, settling in next to the railing, looking down at the street below. "If you'd chosen death, you wouldn't even be alive to remember her now... what's more important is realizing what's important here, while keeping those memories, don't you think?" “She asked me why we had forgotten about her...” Emphasis on the we. They were both stained in this situation, in this case, with the blood of someone too innocent to have defended herself. What was training in arcane arts, becoming something more than you would have been able to be... if you forgot about the ties that bound you together with others? Virgil sighed. Children couldn't know what sorts of pain everything they did had the ability to inflict. It was probably an innocent enough question, but it cut rather deep. "I still don't know... I feel as though I'll have to remember her for another thousand years to make up for it." Lia instinctively reached for his touch. She leaned forward in her chair, the tip of her nose finding his ribcage and nuzzling up against it. Her eyes drifted closed again, and she had the curious sensation of floating. She realized for the first time how exhausted she really was. “We can't leave Markus...” It was a non-sequitur, only not so much, not if you considered the memories that Elena dredged up, and the effect it might have on someone like Lia, who had distributed such precious little of her blood over the years. Virgil's cold hand reached out and rested against her cheek, his long fingernails barely grazing her neck. "We have to find him first." She shivered, goosebumps rising down the line of her spine. “I don't think it'll be too hard. The odds that Zenith doesn't have him are small.” She paused for a moment. “He won't even care if we find him... I'm not sure he cares about much of anything, aside from...” That thought wasn't worth pursuing. Her emotions were twisting inside her at the moment, and she was casting her lot about anywhere she could cast it just to find some comforting thoughts. "He'll be thankful when he learns what he's being spared from." Virgil didn't particularly care for Markus. He thought him an ungrateful whelp, saved from the barely living by death. It was a slight clich? in some circles, but the term fit him aptly. "Wherever you have to go, I'll come with you." She slid farther forward, her arms wrapping around his hips in an embrace that was almost too casual to convey how much she appreciated his presence at the moment. “I know... I appreciate it.” He returned the gesture with the arm closest to her, smiling slightly. There were scant joys in life these days, but her touch still remained one of them. "We should go back to bed... your eyes are half-closed already." “Yeah...” she murmured softly, pressing her lips to his side. “I guess you're right.” Standing up was a monumental effort, but she managed it. He braced her with his arm, which the trials of life had left near the solidness of a steel girder or so. He leaned down and kissed her, something which to him always seemed a little casual, even though it wasn't. He was rarely seen being affectionate. He took a step, guiding her back inside. Though Lia had been quite stubborn of late, this time, she didn't resist. Though the memory of the dream hadn't faded from her consciousness, there was something to be said for gathering strength, and returning to fight another day. He slid the door to, flipping the latch back to the locked position. The snow continued to fall outside, but these lonely droplets were not for Lia Ambrosi tonight. An arm wrapped around her waist pulled her back into bed. It didn't take long for the natural protective curve of his arms around her to seduce her back into sleep. .:: ::: ::.
