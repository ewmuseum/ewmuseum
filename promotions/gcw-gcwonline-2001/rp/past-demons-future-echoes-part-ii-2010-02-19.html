Past Demons, Future Echoes [part II]

19 February 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02/19/10 The sweat stung as it dripped into his eyes. Reaching for the bandanna wrapped around his forehead he twisted it tight, watching the build up of sweat rush out from it. This was one of the very few times over the course of his life where he would trade the world for Irish weather. Deciding that putting the bandanna back on would serve little purpose he slipped his rucksack from his back and knelt down to pack it away. If any of his friends saw him now they would laugh, he looked more unlike himself than he could ever remember; cotton socks tucked under hiking boots, a pair of long shorts and a black vest top. Were he not alone in the wilderness he would probably feel ashamed, but out here in the baking heat of the Amazon rainforest he had no energy to contemplate vanity. Reaching further into his rucksack he grasped a half-empty four litre bottle of water, he wasted little time unscrewing the cap and taking several large gulps. Right now it could have been Bushmills (Ireland’s finest whiskey) because it tasted so good that it couldn’t possibly just be water. Satisfied that he had taken on enough fluid for the next push he prepared to climb to his feet. Where that push would take him would be a mystery. He had long since accepted that he was lost, should he manage to complete his objective he would do so with the luck of the Irish, because he certainly couldn’t remember the way. He no longer had any choice but to push on in hope of finding some kind of civilisation, this far into the vast jungle his cell phone carried no signal, there was no hope of gaining any help that way. Suddenly the hair on the back of his neck stood up and a horrible feeling crept over him. He was lost, but he was no longer alone. He climbed slowly to his feet and attempted to discreetly scan his surroundings. There was nothing there. Had it just been his mind playing tricks on him, it wouldn’t be hard to believe, heat such as this is well known to effect perception. But despite the lack of evidence, the feeling would not shift. He slowly set off once more, considerably more alert than he had been in the last few hours. The more he walked, the more paranoid he became. His eyes nervously shifted from side to side. Every so often he was sure he had glimpsed movement in the tree’s flanking him and those straight ahead, but the more he strained his eyes the less he saw. It was only at this stage that it occurred to him that he had made a fundamental error. He had failed to bring anything in that rucksack that could be used to protect him against whatever it was that was slowly encircling him. Even the small knife he had for cutting aside any obstacles in his path would provide little use against a predator, or as he suspected, a group of predators. How had a man so obsessed with preparation, a man who always likes to know what he is up against, managed to overlook something so critical? NC-17 was so close he felt as though if he reached out he could touch it. Every time he heard that alphanumeric combination he thought of one thing: Opportunity! Indeed on the greatest stage of them all he had a very real opportunity to get one over on two of the people he detested most in the world. And yet here he was, in the middle of one of the most dangerous places on the planet and he had not had the basic common sense to bring with him some form of protection. How could it be that he had put himself in a position whereby he may not even be alive to compete at NC-17! After all, the Amazon is home to Black Caiman, Jaguar and Anacondas, not exactly household pets! His train of thought halted to a stop, he had been so busy tormenting himself that he had stopped paying attention to his surroundings. He struggled to force his body to override its natural instinct to panic, but it was harder than before. Gripped with fear he kept his eyes fixed in front of him, because in his peripheral vision he could now see several shapes on either side matching him stride for stride. Beyond any doubt now he knew for sure he was not alone, and at last he knew the identity of the threat, because he was being flanked by one of the tribes that made their home in the jungle. He had no idea if it was the tribe he was looking for, but somehow he thought that it might be better not to stop for a chat just right now. The sounds of calls from either side were coming to him now, various thinly veiled primal grunts communicating in a code that meant nothing to him. The battle inside him was lost... panic had won! Without waiting another second he broke into a run, mustering as much pace as he could given the conditions. The shapes of the tribesmen flanking him effortlessly matched and exceed his pace, as he knew they would. However, his panic was only to last for a few more strides. From behind a large tree just ahead of him stepped a giant of a man, tall, of broad shoulder and intimidating. Long dark dreadlocks covered the tribesman’s face from view as he set his feet, blocking the path ahead. With nowhere else to go, he continued running, hoping that perhaps he had enough momentum to throw the man who blocked his path off balance long enough to pass him. The last thing he noticed was a large staff raised into the air and swung forcefully, on a collision course with his ribs! He spent the next few moments clutching his abdomen, gasping for breath that was not forthcoming. There are few feelings as uncomfortable as having the wind taken out of your sails like that. He couldn’t figure out what hurt more, the pain of being struck by the staff, or his failing attempts to draw breath. Rolling onto his front he raised his back, pulling himself to his knees as air slowly filtered back into his body. He opened his eyes to see the shins of several tribesmen surrounding him. With a deep breath he raised his neck to meet the eyes of the dark warrior who had felled him. He didn’t know how to describe the feeling that took control of his entire body as his assailant spoke. "Greetings Seán O’Neill!" - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - Ten days previously... 4th February 2010 Northern Ireland The boos rang out around the sold out Waterfront Hall in Belfast, the two thousand locals in attendance were most unhappy with what they had just saw. He shared their anguish as he slowly sat up, his elbows resting on his knees, the crushing disappointment coursed through him. The ring bell rang out and a voice carried across the public address system. "Here is your winner... IMPULSE!" It was as if someone had cranked the volume up ten-fold as hostility rained down on the American. To be fair, Impulse’s entertaining style and impressive victory deserved a much better ovation, however on this occasion he had committed an unforgivable act. He had beaten the Belfast born SurReal in front of his home crowd, and for that alone, he was being booed out of the arena. Running his hands through his long hair and letting a long demoralised sigh issue out of him the Irishman finally staggered to his feet. Small pockets of support became audible amongst the jeering still aimed at Impulse. He motioned a sign of gratitude in the direction of his support before quickly making his way out of the ring and out of the main arena itself. Right now he wanted the group to open and swallow him so the last place he wanted to be was in a room packed with two thousand plus people looking at him. He scurried through the curtain and wasted little time making his way back to the locker-room. There he found his opponent going through a quick warm-down routine. He struggled for a few seconds between the decision to talk to him or not. But there was no point in being bitter about it, on the night he had simply lost to the better man. "Hey there lad, congratulations, that was a fine show you put on out there, my jaw’s still stinging!" He rubbed his jaw with a quick smile before extending his hand for a quick shake. "I’m going to have to start counting up these defeats ya know, this is turning into a most unwelcome streak now!" The American placed his hand on SurReal’s shoulder. "It'll come, sir," said Impulse, "Next time - and I hope we do have a next time - it could easily go the other way. Now, if you'll excuse me, I'm off to a... hooley... tonight." The word seemed to cause the Irishman a bit of confusion. "A what?" "Hooley, you know, a party." "I can honestly say I’ve never heard that word before, is it some kind of New York slang?" Impulse greeted the question with a laugh, pulling his kit bag over his shoulder and making off towards the door. "I thought it was Irish slang. Let's compromise and call it Australian. Have a good night, sir." With that, Impulse headed out the door, leaving SurReal in a dangerous environment; alone with his thoughts. They weren’t lingering on his loss though. That was over, there was little could be achieved from over analysing a performance that wasn’t up to scratch. Impulse had been right, perhaps on another day the outcome might have been different. Regardless it mattered little now so there was no point in dwelling on it. Instead his thoughts were focused on his next match, because no matter how emotional tonight had been, it paled in comparison with what was next on the agenda: Lori Equinox, Donny Diamond, Glass Tables and NC-17. This was going to be his first appearance on the event that overshadows everything else in Global Championship Wrestling, and it was against two men he needed no extra motivation to want to beat. It had become his obsession. Despite not having had his arm raised in victory for months he knew that if he was on top of his game at NC-17 that neither Diamond nor Equinox would beat him. He wanted this win, no, he needed it. He thought little of Equinox, over the past year they had competed against each other so many times that he was increasingly more sceptical that Equinox had anything new to throw at him. That said, it was hard to imagine that Equinox would not be going into NC-17 feeling the same way. The hatred between the two men will probably never disappear, they have put each other through far too much for that, but it had almost been put on a backburner recently. Diamond had thrown himself in between them and right now, he was the far greater concern. After all, he is supposed to be dead! He had been thinking about that almost non-stop for weeks now, deep inside his head a voice had been nagging at him, offering a solution he was hesitant to take. SurReal is notorious for scouting his opponents, his attention to detail in his match preparations is second-to-none. But this was a different kettle of fish altogether. Donny Diamond is dead and buried. Was this really the Donny Diamond he had faced all those years ago? It looked like him, it acted like him and it spoke constantly of the things he had done. Who else could it be? But he is dead. No matter how many times he reminded himself of that he still couldn’t get his head around it. Whether intentional or not, Donny Diamond had succeeded were so very few before have done so, he had thrown SurReal off his game. And yet that voice continued to whisper to him Seek him out, he will know what to do... How could he just go walking back there though? It’s been seven years without a word between either of them. He knows how to beat Diamond... Undeniable, no one had bested Donny in the squared-circle as many times. It can’t wait any longer, the time has come, seek him out... He conceded at last that there could be no alternative. If he was going to walk into NC-17 in the right frame of mind to compete against his two greatest rivals, he needed help. Find Kaiowas! - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - "What the hell was that for?" No sooner had the words left his mouth when several large knives were extended in his direction from the tribesmen that encircled him. They looked to the dark warrior for approval, but none was forthcoming. Instead he made a gesture for them to lower their weapons. He issued a command in a language that O’Neill did not understand, which prompted the two men at his side to lift him onto his feet. "Why have you returned?" The question should have been easy for O’Neill to answer, but he had not come this far to have a quick chat under such heavy guard. He decided that going into to detail at this point would be counter-productive. "I need your guidance old friend, I was hoping we could talk under more civil circumstances." There was a brief silence as the warrior considered the request. "You recall how it was when you first came amongst us O’Neill?" He nodded, assuming correctly that Kaiowas referred to his being blindfolded and led to their camp. It was obvious now that to achieve his goal he would have to undergo the same treatment again. "So it shall be again." With that he instructed one of the tribe to blindfold O’Neill before another man pulled him onto his back, the tribe set off at a blistering pace, including the man carrying him, SurReal noted to himself that this wasn’t the most comfortable ride he had ever been on as his bearer clambered up inclines he could not see and rounded obstacles he could only imagine were there. Once he was sure he heard the rumbling sound of a waterfall but that also fell into the background against the low incessant breathing of the scout tasked with carrying hm. This continued for around twenty or so minutes, which gave O’Neill plenty of time to think about what he would say to Kaiowas. His thoughts also dwelt on the tribe themselves, he had forgotten how loyal the Ktulu are to Kaiowas. After all, he is not their chief as such, but he stood out amongst them like a god amongst mere mortals. They both feared and respected him in equal measures, his skill in battle was legendary, he was the warrior who had freed them from the darkness of Pultra. Not even the elders of the tribe would dare speak out against him. When you have an ally such as Kaiowas on your side, it is wise to keep him there. When they finally came to a halt, O’Neill was readying himself to fumble his way off his carrier’s back when he was saved the trouble. The tribesman who had bore him had obviously not be given any instructions of special treatment from Kaiowas, as he simply dropped O’Neill who hit the ground below him hard. Son of a bitch... He had worried that things might be strained between Kaiowas and himself, it had not occurred to him however that the entire Ktulu tribe would be so coarse with him. He was helped to his feet again but before he could remove his blindfold he had been lifted under the armpits and was being dragged away. A few seconds later they came to a halt and he could hear the tribesmen retreating back. And then another sound, one that sent a shiver down his spine. A growl, almost a snarl, and he had a fair idea what had made it. Plucking up the courage he slowly peeled off his blindfold. Standing before him, as he had suspected, was a large panther, her coat was as black as night and there was a look of savage nobility about her which on this occasion did nothing to calm O’Neill’s nerves. "Nice kitty!" O’Neill’s broad Irish accent quivered as the panther sniffed at him. Out of the frying pan and into the fire was the thought that occurred to him. Luckily his worries were about to pass. "Easy Rula, you remember SurReal don’t you?" SurReal took the chance to scan his surroundings as the big cat hesitated, he had been brought to a simple rather bare hut, made from everything the jungle provided. In the roof above there was a hole to allow smoke to escape and sunlight to enter. The panther looked past O’Neill, to where Kaiowas’ voice had come from and her expression changed for the better. She strode past him without a second glance and left the hut passing Kaiowas who still stood at the doorway. "Still not comfortable around animals I see O’Neill." "Forgive me, Rula’s just that little bit more deadly than the animals I’m used to!" Kaiowas nodded, and it almost looked to O’Neill as if he had smiled as he gestured to him to take a seat. Of course, he had probably just imagined that, Kaiowas for all his power was not one for smiling. They both sat down cross legged as a young boy scurried into the hut with a platter of fresh fruit. "Gotta say, I love what you’ve done with the place Max!" The boy almost dropped the platter on hearing these words and Kaiowas shot O’Neill an ominous glare. He hated it when O’Neill called him Max, and to do so in front of the tribe would be taken as an insult. SurReal took the message on board and made a mental note that today it would perhaps be wiser to refer to him only as Kaiowas, even calling him Massimilio Junior (his full name) might have been risky. Once they were alone again Kaiowas turned his attention straight to business. "So, tell me O’Neill, why have you come?" For the next few hours O’Neill went into great detail as he explained to Kaiowas everything that had happened in the last year and a half. From his return to active competition as a wrestler, through all the problems he had endured with Lori Equinox, right up to Donny Diamond’s return at Worldwide 122. For the most part Kaiowas listened in silence, only registering an opinion when a name he recognised was mentioned. He listened all the more intently now that O’Neill talked more and more about Diamond. The fate of one of his great rivals from his own time in a wrestling ring was of great interest to him. SurReal went on to explain about how Donny had died, how he had found the news article written in tribute to him after his death. It had been his hope, that Kaiowas, who had a much greater understanding of things that most of us would consider unnatural, may be able to help him to understand how it is that he could fight a dead man. It was also his hope that Kaiowas would want to see his old rival suffer at the hands of anyone. When he had talked for so long, that he might as well have taken Kaiowas on a day-by-day account of the seven years that had passed since their last meeting, he stopped. There was nothing more he could tell the Brazilian. Kaiowas now knew every bit as much (and as little) as he did about the entire scenario. Finally the time had come for Kaiowas to speak. "So as I understand it, you are fighting two men, yet your thoughts dwell only on one. This is unlike you O’Neill, I question the wisdom of dismissing one opponent who could be every bit as dangerous as the other." Kaiowas spelt out the last few words of the sentence, obviously trying to emphasise the significance. SurReal knew that himself, but somehow it had more impact having heard it from Kaiowas. He had wondered often enough in these last few weeks if his obsession with Diamond would come back to haunt him. He hated Donny Diamond, but he hated Lori Equinox more. If Equinox was to get a victory over him at the biggest event in the business, simply because he didn’t see it coming, well, that would be impossible to accept. "You’re right of course. I will heed your advice, I certainly need to take a harder look at how Equinox has built himself up to this match, I’ve not given him the respect he deserves as an opponent. But there is still time to deal with Lori. Tell me my friend, what do you advise with regards to Diamond?" The Tribal Warrior’s mood darkened as the topic moved to his once bitter rival. The room seemed to dim and SurReal looked up to observe that a rather threatening cloud had slowly driven across the sky, blocking the rays of the sun. Surely a coincidence? Kaiowas dipped his head, the long dreadlocks now covering his features as he took his time pondering his words. As the seconds of silence crept along and turned into minutes, SurReal began to feel uneasy sitting here, in the presence of someone so still. On a few occasions he felt like piping up but thought better of it. However as soon as his mind relaxed, Kaiowas spoke up, out of the blue. He spoke slowly, and there was tangible hatred when he spat out the name Diamond. "You say Diamond is dead. And I think that perhaps you are correct, death is not always final when you are dealing with a man such as Donny, one who has firmly rooted himself in the occult. To beat him, you must look beyond the facade of death itself." It was always surprising for those who did not know him to hear Kaiowas speak with such wisdom, by all accounts he is a gruff individual, stern and not always particularly forthcoming. He is a man who chooses his words wisely. But Seán O'Neill knew that behind the muscles and intimidation there was a man so deeply educated in the ways of the world that he rarely said anything that was untrue. If Kaiowas told him to look beyond death, then that was exactly what he must do, the question now was how does he do that. He didn’t need to ask the question though, Kaiowas knew already that O’Neill’s brain would be in overload trying to come to terms with the concept that death is not always final. Perhaps in terms of the soul, he might have understood, it is not unreasonable to suggest that the soul lives on after death, most of the religions in the world today are based on that premise. This wasn’t a soul living past death though, this was the entire package, as it death had simply changed it’s mind. Kaiowas turned to face the sky, as if pulling insight from the heavens. "Face the man Seán, not the fear. If you let your understanding of the mechanics of death stand in the way, you cannot win. Push it aside and deal with the physical. You have spilt Donny’s blood before, I suggest that he might not seem as unbeatable should you repeat it." O’Neill smiled for a variety of reasons, these words were kinder than Kaiowas is in the habit of giving. And he had even called him by his first name; somehow that had made the message easier to take on board as well. And of course, he was right. All of this built up frustration and paranoia about Donny Diamond really boiled down to one thing. Ever since he stormed back into his life, he had never been able to properly get at Donny. Every time there had been a confrontation there had always been some factor that had stopped him from getting his hands on him. At NC-17, there would just be the three of them. Nothing to stop him at last, suddenly the prospect of tackling Diamond looked considerably less daunting. "As always you have found a way to help me Kaiowas. I can’t even begin to thank you. You have put my mind at ease at last my friend." Kaiowas nodded, but it was clear that their discussion was at an end. He had taken considerable time to listen to O’Neill and advise him as best he could, but he was a loner now and could not suffer the company of men for too long without their humanity grating on him. He was like no other and in this way he did not have time for many but SurReal had once been part of the Tribe, the one thing that Kaiowas always strove to protect. It didn’t concern O’Neill though, he had already given more than enough. He understood that Kaiowas had to be the way he was to have faced the things he had and survive through it. "Go now O’Neill, you have much preparation still ahead of you. I will have one of my men take you to the edge of the jungle, you will find your own way from there." They both stood up and Kaiowas clasped SurReal at the elbow of his right arm. SurReal, realising he had forgotten the tribe’s form of the handshake, quickly reciprocated, and there was unspoken feeling of kinship between them. Old bridges may not have necessarily been rebuilt, but stepping-stones had been put down in their place. As harrowing as it might have been and as cold as the early interaction had been, O’Neill realised that for the past 7 years since Kaiowas stepped away from the ring, he had missed his old friend. No matter how cryptic he could be at times... Before long, he was on his way out of the Ktulu camp, following closely behind his guide. He did not bother to look back, he knew inside that Kaiowas would not be there. He felt as though a great weight had been lifted from his shoulders. His mental block over Donny Diamond had been lifted, he would soon be on his way back to America. And when he got there he would have a full week to reassess his match at NC-17. He could prepare properly now for Diamond, and just as importantly, he would have to prepare for Equinox, the time to neglect that had passed. Although there was still much to do, he felt that he was ready at last. In the right frame of mind for this, possibly the biggest match of his career. At NC-17 he would be walking away with the victory, nothing was going to stop that now.
