Returns and Other Thoughts

1 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Nov. 1, 2005 It was desolate, dark and deserted now. The next day, Joe Louis arena would be converted back into the brash and raucous home of the Detroit Red Wings. Gone would be the already half-dismantled wrestling ring that was currently occupying the middle of one of the NHL’s most famous arenas. The fans that had filled the complex to its capacity were also gone. There was, however, still two remnants of the exhilarating event that had taken place not more than three hours earlier. The buzz of a crowd that had seen one of GCW’s most exhilarating events to date was still present. And, sitting in an empty seat, surrounded by shadows, was Toxic. He sat smugly, enjoying the atmosphere that he had been missing for nearly four months. Had it been worth it? That’s what everyone would be asking him for the next month. Was it worth to take a rebel stand against the boss? How did it feel to have to sit at home and watch his “colleagues” achieve their moments of glory while a starving rage chewed away at his guts? And was it worth it to sacrifice four months worth of a premium salary for the sake of principle? Money wasn’t the issue. He countered his conscience quickly. Of course it wasn’t. He had no concern about being reduced to rags. Toxic had long ago reached a state of financial security. Money was never the issue. What’s done is done. It doesn’t matter what the rest of the world thinks. No, Toxic had never really cared what anyone else considered the right thing to do. That was for the weak and feeble-minded. That’s not really what they want to hear about though. Tomorrow I’ll hear enough “why” questions to make Bob Costas grimace. He thought, feeling slightly amused. He stood up, and as he did several crewmembers looked over, clearly startled by the noise. As impossible as it seemed, the crew did not notice the 6’10? behemoth sitting only a few rows away from them. “Um’I don’t think you’re supposed to be here.” One of the men informed him. “Call the cops.” Toxic responded. The big man buttoned up his coat and headed for the exit. Had the three months off put him at a disadvantage? Surprisingly, this thought had crossed the mind of a man who never thought about consequences. Yet it had felt like the opinion that some skeptical magazine writer had planted there. The thought then had disappeared quicker than Corey Feldman’s career. Tomorrow there would be no skeptics. He had dominated tonight. Sure there had been a few close calls but at no point did he feel like he had lost control. He stepped alertly out of the arena, and into the brisk and chilly Detroit night. Being a native of California, Toxic wasn’t particularly fond of the cold. Tonight though, he welcomed the refreshing feeling that swept over his face. Toxic breathed in heavily as the wind rustled through his hair. The site of steam seeping softly through the holes of a manhole cover wouldn’t normally seem so poetic, but tonight was a different night. In an alleyway across the street two homeless men were shouting complete jibberish at each other, all the while keeping a firm grip on their stereotypical brown bags of liquor. As he passed another corner the red glow of brake lights reflected off the pavement. He watched a prostitute climb into the passenger seat of a black Buick before it sped off in the direction of a seedy motel. Disgusting. He thought fondly. On any other night he wouldn’t be able to tolerate this type of scene. People groveling for any penny they could get. Women, selling their bodies to every fat slob with a five-dollar bill. Every repulsive person he could think of seemed to be manifesting before his very own eyes. He could never understand how anyone could feel sorry for these people. They had their chance, just like the rest of us. He mused, watching a stray cat scamper across the street, barely avoiding the oncoming delivery truck. It was this same mentality that told him he had to come back. For as little as he cared about the other wrestlers in GCW, he couldn’t just stand back and watch a reign of mediocrity take over what was once his kingdom. His reputation was on the line. Of course there had been obstacles that had nearly thwarted Toxic’s return. There had been no easy way of getting around the suspension. He would’ve loved to be able to walk into any arena of his choice and start right where he had left off. It takes a little more than that. He grinned. That’s where the easy part came in. Find the person who was most vulnerable. He had done that without any problems. Then all it took was a match. Once he had his match, the suspension was automatically lifted. Then came the statement. Toxic made sure he left that arena with the satisfaction of knowing he’d served every superstar notice. I won't let the average clog up the ranks of the elite anymore. He thought grimly. Two headlights quickly caught Toxic’s attention. A cab! I’ve had enough of this wasteland for one night. He hailed the cab and hopped inside. It smelled like a urinal, but at this point he could care less. “Sheraton, on fifty-third.” He ordered the driver. As the cab silently sped off in the direction of the hotel, Toxic gazed out the window. Signs of weakness were everywhere. Poster-children for every contributing factor of a malignant society dotted the landscape. A social worker’s own professional gold mine. This place was bottomless pit for pity and empathy. There was only one thought that crossed Toxic’s mind though. God I love being me.
