Business Proposition

2 December 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/02/2006 Saint Ann, Jamaica The day becomes dusk, and the nightlife begins. The streets become crowded, and the clubs start to open. There's cliques of younger people passing by; some dressed up, some more laid back, some with cigarettes, some without. You have your group of drunk girls over here, your group of loud dudes over there, and your group of underage kids trying to pass for 18 in front of you. It's a typical Friday night downtown, it's Gus Barty's night downtown. But tonight is a little different. Normally, he'd be making way into one of the many clubs he's familiar with, share a few drinks between good friends and sexy acquaintances, and work on seducing a new lady partner for the eve. However, tonight is special. Real special . Real special with a real deal. It's easy money and an easy task. It's what anyone could basically ask for. Barty never thought he'd be pushing drugs in his life. Sure, he sells weed quite successfully, but he does it just to provide good times. Selling pills and coke never really appealed to him. And it wouldn't unless he was approached by Reggie Bale, a man you don't say no to, the man who is practically in charge of all drugs in the downtown area. He's the real deal for any ecstasy or coke flow in the clubs, he's the life of the party, he's the main man. Tonight, Reggie Bale requested to have a 'business' meeting with Barty. And that's where our story begins. Barty sat in a chair and waited to be directed over to Reggie Bale's room. The chair was just a regular guests' chair, but it was velvet and comfortable, and it had a swift modern design. There was nothing else in the room except for a noisy clock and two doors. One door lead to a staircase, the other to the 'business' room. The second door had something about it. Every time Barty looked at the door, he heard his heart-beat rise. When he looked away, the sweat on his forehead would dry. A thousand thoughts crowded his head and ultimately created a 'mind-fuck', if you will. Was he ready to open up shop with this man? He was called in by a tall stocky man who seemed to be some sort-of a bodyguard. Barty wasted no time, and made his way on in to the 'business' room. When he walked in, he saw a rather large stone table; occupied by no less than 7 people. At the very end was Reggie Bale, and to the sides were just associates. They stared through Gus Barty until he was properly introduced by the doorman. He looked nervous, but he wasn't; after all, what do you say to break the ice in this situation? Doorman, 'This is him Reg, Gus Barty, the guy you've been lookin' for.' The doorman left the room and closed the door. As he closed it, everything in the world became mute to Barty, everything except for the sound of the closing door. That sound echoed in his head a few times before he grasped reality again. He nodded his head towards Reggie Bale. Reggie, 'So this is Gus Barty?' The short black man stood up, and slowly approached Barty. He was inspecting him closely, studying him, trying to learn him. Reggie, 'A man with dreadlocks...' He grabbed some of the locks on Barty's head. Reggie, 'A man with style...' He slightly pinched Barty's shirt. Reggie, 'and a man with good people skills.' He padded Barty on both shoulders as if he was trying to lift him up. Reggie Bale turned to his associates and stood with his arm around Barty. Reggie, 'This guy, my friends, can be a very important aspect to the business. This is Gus Barty.' The room was silent for way too long; at least, longer than it should have been. Barty looked into each associates eyes, and finally relaxed. He thought now would be a good time to make a greeting. Barty, 'What's up? Yeah, that's me... I'm Gus Barty.' Reggie, 'Yes you are.' Reggie took his arm away from Barty and walked back over to his seat. He began to explain why Barty was here. Reggie, 'Gus, the reason you're here is simple. I've noticed, wait... we've noticed that you've got some public attention down in our clubs. Usually, that sort of thing really pisses me off, but hey, you don't steal clients. And then it dawned on me... He leaned back in his seat, and brushed his legs up onto the table. Reggie, 'Why not let you make a few deals for me? You know, nothing heavy, just a few deals inside to get all of the foreigners and locals hooked. A lot of people like you, they may do it just to be close to you. Who knows...' Barty stood there with his eyes a little squinted, listening to Reggie. He stood proud yet loose, and nodded his head along with what Reggie had to say. Reggie, 'I run a business, Gus Barty, with these people that surround you. I have very little competition, but either way, I need to make sure my business runs smoothly. If the people see somebody so loved selling off something so 'great', they're bound to just get some. And instead of looking for it, they can just go up to the main man, which would be you, and make the transaction quickly and securely. Then, when it's time to call it a night, you'll climb that flight of stairs outside, make it up to the third floor, walk in to my office, and hand me your earnings. In return, you'll get a 1/3 profit, and you won't be bothered until the next night. You'll only work Thursday nights to Saturdays, and you'll have all the protection you'll need. Let alone, all the say you need too. So what do you say?' Barty stuttered. Reggie, 'Gus, please, take a seat, relax, take a load off.' So he chose the seat directly in front of him and sat down. He loosened up a bit, and took a moment to look back into everybody's eyes before answering. Barty, 'Listen Reggie, I'm down with helping out a little bit, it's my nature to give to the people. However, I just don't know if I'm the right man for the job. I have no experience with this sorta shit.' Reggie Bale cuts him off. Reggie, 'You've sold schwag before, right?' Barty, 'Well... yeah but...' Reggie, 'Then you won't have a problem Gus! It's the same thing, but it's just a different package. No difference in process. And trust me, you're the perfect man for the job.' The associates sat there continuing to be quiet. They would not let Barty out of their sight. Barty, 'But the thing is, I sold weed because it doesn't hurt nobody. I'm a lover man, I'm not lookin' to ruin any lives. I just want people to have a good time.' Reggie, 'Well that's your choice, but I'm not asking for you to force it at all. Just, if anyone asks, which they will, you'll have it. It's that simple buddy.' Barty sighed. Reggie, 'Look, whether you want to or not, you're sort of already involved. It's just your decision if you want to fight or if you want to accept your new responsibility.' Barty, 'I just don't know man, I mean, there's kids in there, there's sons and daughters and brothers and sisters, it's just... He paused. He had everyone's attention, but he started to think more. It was a huge decision he had to make on the spot. Reggie, 'Gus, come on now. I'm not asking for a lot. Besides, you'll be safe if you accept. If you decline, however, well, I don't know, I just can't let that happen. I'll tell you what, Gus, you come back tomorrow and you let me know. No use in rushin' things, right man?' The night turned to morning, and Barty's room lit up with sunlight. His alarm clock went off, and bolted his eyes wide open. He lied there motionless, still with a lot on his mind. What was he going to do? Barty, 'I'll fuckin' do it...' He said to himself. Barty, 'I'll just fuckin' do it.'
