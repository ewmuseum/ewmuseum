Rigid and Flawed, Incomplete

15 April 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/15/11 Room 812, Tower 1 Baywatch Resort, North Myrtle Beach, South Carolina April 10, 2011 The sun rises on all of us. It doesn’t die at night, and this morning isn’t its rebirth. It isn’t a god worth praising, yet I always seem to greet it with a reverent smile. Today was no different. As it pierced its rays of light around the curtain, my eyes were already open and looking up at the ceiling. I’d been awake for a few minutes now and just enjoyed that moment of obliviousness that was granted every time I slipped out of a deep sleep, but part of its beauty was its fleeting nature. ‘This isn’t my ceiling,’ the first thought through the fog. ‘I don’t smell like peaches,’ the second. Yes, it would appear that I had verged away from the path most often travelled and found myself laying here, only, where was here? Maybe more important: who is she? Slowly I unfurl the sheets and set my feet on the ground. It’s not all that bright in the room yet, curtain beating sun, but I can see enough of the room to note that this place is not my fashion, except for maybe the bottle of Blue decorating the nightstand. And before I can accuse the alcohol for my whereabouts the night prior comes rushing back. The glaring omission is the girl’s name. Silently I rose and crept across the carpet, past the open door and into the living room. The same tacky themes followed from the bedroom, with seashells painted into the couch and a 32 inch flat screen hanging on the wall, but I don’t think I will be watching the wall, not when the ocean sits just outside those patio doors. It is massive, filling up most of the view and calling to me. And even as quiet as I may have tried, the doors still rumbled as I slid one behind the other. The sweet smell of the sea breeze was the first to hit me. Next the rolling noise of the ever crashing tide. The sun is young, but strong still, forcing my eyes to squint as I find quant wooden chairs side by each. I imagine for the briefest of moments that heaven should be like this, but like all thoughts it subsides and is replaced. Next in the queue is to think about the times as a kid Dad would drive the family down for vacation, a happy memory for sure. I didn’t want to think about why we stopped coming down, so I remember the last time I was here. I remember who I was here with. ----- June 21, 2005 We are standing beside the ocean, the tide washing over our toes as we gaze out to what seems like infinity. I take a few glances back at you, but you don’t break your stare. “Amazing, isn’t it?” you asked. I don’t exactly remember what my response was. I am sure I said something dumb that make you laugh, though. It was enough to steal your attention away from the coasts of Africa that you couldn’t see on the other end of the Atlantic. You were smiling that heart-melting grin. Part of me will never stop missing the way your painted lips parted. There was a sea of people on the shore all taking in the sun the way we were, but this moment felt so exclusive. It was us against this world and never did that feel so much the case as when the breeze played with your brunette hair and we stood hand in hand. I won’t guess at how long we stood there, but it wasn’t long enough. An eternity would not do, but nothing is forever. As you turn to take us back up the beach, toward the shoreline plotted with high rises and three story walk-ups, I follow, but only until we are out of the water completely. Sorry if my sudden stop jerked you arm at all, but I think as you turned, the last thought you had was your shoulder. You looked blown away as I knelt on my left knee, hand busily fishing for the felt box in my pocket. You were crying even before I got the ring out. “Kelly, will you,” I didn’t even get all the words out of my mouth. “Yes.” ----- The question you ask over and over, “What have you ever done, Alexander Redding?” the laugh track that plays through out the scene of carnage and very near regrets. Our last fight, and the final throws of ‘us’. ‘Us’ died that night, but it had been cancerous for how long, exactly? Drunk on indulgences that I will forever damn you for, but you hadn’t the courage to tell me. That night everything I though ‘we’ stood for shattered like the vase hitting the wall. You mislabelled me as wanting to be famous, that somehow the side of your life you shared with me ought to keep me satisfied. But it wasn’t the fame I dreamt about, it was simply the respect. Respect not yet presented to me, but future considerations of respect none-the-less. A matter of success, you preached, I could never attain. The threat of the long trips and tired visits home probably weighed a bit heavier than any of that. The thought that I was selfish to follow a dream is just as foolhardy as the one that we’d be forever, when eternity is impossible. I knew that when I walked out of that door I was never coming back. You knew when you drew blood that it was over. And now it’s just a memory, violent and short. ----- Back in the land of the living, I am, heaven forbid, happy. Nicked up and bruised, sure, but that’s become expected. I’m not sure I’d recognise this body without its woes. But it is accurate to say I am content sitting with my feet hung up on the bottom of the railing staring out at the massive and feeling tiny in comparison. Alone, despite enjoying the company of a sleeping beauty in the other room. It had become this odd thing, this ring I wore. I wore it for God knows why, but I wore it for a time too long. I became used to twirling it about my finger in times of boredom and could feel the attention it gained from territorial women. I hate jewellery, but never seemed to go anywhere without the stupid hunky of metal, that all was until a few months back now. It was some hotel room in some city after someone had gotten through giving me their best in the ring. My mind a bit loopy, I reason it was misplaced when I was showering. A couple hundred worth of gold and I couldn’t have been happier to loose the thing. It was a tie to the past when I have tried so very hard to not be the fragile kid I was, but it never left me. To this day I still reach to my ring finger with my thumb only to flick at the scar tissue built up thick. And that was just the physical representation. I’d spent nights like the one just past before. You didn’t expect a red blooded man to stay celibate for four years? Each time they had been little more than the act and the awkward morning after. Without analyzing it too much, there was little beyond that that I had sought out. I was far from the romanticised idea of a boyfriend worth waiting for. And honestly, the more personality they revealed, the more I would regret not being able to remember on the rare times I could just sit with my thoughts. It was a clear meeting of intention; I was sure of it, or as sure as a drunk could be. But then the morning would come and I would be hung up with this shame. It was not a regret for the actions I had done, nor was it for the actions I’d take next. It was this indescribable but very real feeling. And it was only now that I dare to place it. Today the first morning after I walked away from my fiancée that I had been without that flood of feeling when I woke up. I had finally stopped blaming myself for not waking up next to Kelly. As irrational and twisted as it was, it was the simple truth. The next breath I took was something magical. I found myself innocent. I was free. Free from the shame. Free from the mistakes of the past that I could not change. Free to be the man I dreamt about becoming, the one that was respected; loved or feared. I picked myself up and wandered back inside. I got to the kitchenette and peered into the contents of the fridge: beer and water. I decided it best to take the bottle of Nestle Pure Life back out to my oasis perch, to toast to this new day. A toast to the moment I could be at peace, watching the Earth rotate so that the sun rose along the horizon. A chance to sit and enjoy before the impending awkwardness of dealing with the girl sleeping in my king size bed. And this day would come to a close just like every other, to be replayed in my mind. The moment once real and fluid became an imprint on my grey cells, rigid and flawed, incomplete, but packed with the feelings, sights and smells, a thousand miles from home and any worry. After all, I remember all the feelings and the day they stopped.
