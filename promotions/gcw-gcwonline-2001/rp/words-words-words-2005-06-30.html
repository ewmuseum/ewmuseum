Date: June 30, 2005 Smoke gave me a halo when Trey interrupted with the real world; the bad news from the warfront. He moved like a scared soldier, reporting in for duty with, well, a general in my general position of life. 'They're comin' here.' 'Who?' 'Sobias an' Marauder.' 'Lexicon.' 'You don't know it's him, man.' I looked at him--nothing fancy. 'Alright, alright, shyeah. It's Johnny. But fuck, man, what the hell are you gonna do?' The case was heavy in my lap. 'I don't know. Why are they coming?' 'I talked to Sobias. He said he had a deal he wanted you in on.' 'The deal? He's swine, an arrogant bastard not worth his weight in shit.' He tried to sit down. 'No. Wait until Johnny sits down, then sit on the outside edge. Hold him in.' 'You're insane.' 'You said that a week ago.' 'I said you were goin' insane a week ago. You're there, man. Got a nice ass house, up on a hill. Everyone aroun' town respects you so much that they call you Mr. White. Shit, you set the bar for-' 'Stop. Go to a dark corner and wait, and don't talk to the hot one--she'll try to steal your soul.' 'Shyeah. I gotta piss.' 'Good call.' He pushed out of sight just as the curtains parted again. I disappeared back into the booth, waiting for the shadows to reveal the owners. 'Jordan, I know you're in here. Come on out.' Sobias' voice bellowed through the room, vibrated the clasp on the case open. I swear. I pushed it down my knees, further under the table, before they came into view. 'There you are. Want something to drink?' I waved the bottle. 'Who's that?' 'Marauder.' Under the table, Sobias caught a glint of my gun. 'Sit down.' 'Let's sit down, Zeta.' The man in the mask nodded and sat across from me, Sobias filling up most of the booth next to me. His face had lost all humor at the sight of the deadly stupid hand I was playing, and it showed in his voice. 'What's up, Jordan?' 'You called him Zeta.' 'Yes. He's Zeta Acosta.' 'Now's not the time for fucking around, Sobias. Who's under the mask?' I looked over--the mask was too good, concealed any trace of emotion. 'Zeta.' Trey came out of the bathroom, wiping his hands on the legs of his pants, then leaned his head up and noticed us. He made his way over quickly, knocking chairs and tables aside as casually as he good, and sat down beside Johnny. 'Yo Sobias, J, masked guy. Sup.' 'I see you didn't tell Jordan about the deal.' 'I mentioned it to him.' 'I see he didn't seem to like it.' Glanced down at the gun, seeing who it was pointed at. 'Why not, Jordan?' 'No one explained it to me.' 'Well, it's pretty simple--' 'Let Lexicon talk.' He swallowed hard at the name. 'I'm not Johnny Lexicon.' 'So the man in the copyrighted mask speaks. What do you have to say for yourself?' Thud. I let the handle of the psychotic shotgun pistol drop against the table hard enough to make sure someone knew--everyone knew. His eyes trailed down slowly. 'Please. Go on.' 'Jordan, listen. Sobias wants to sign me to Anarch--' 'Not what I want to hear. Take the mask off.' 'A luchadore doesn't remove his mask in the presence of others; to do so is to lose the pride and honor it entails.' 'You're one to talk.' 'How can you judge me?' 'Because you've fucked me over a thousand times, Lexi, and because I'm a little tired of it. I saw the goddamned reports you faked to keep me going, the game's been fucking played, and I think I get the right to pass judgment on you by now.' His eyes wavered. 'I'm not Lexicon.' 'Then fucking prove it.' 'Sign me to Anarch. Let Sobias take me into GCW. That'll prove everything.' 'Take my mask off. That'll prove everything.' 'Jordan,' came Trey from the corner. 'What?' 'Put the gun down.' 'Glad someone noticed it.' 'Everyone fuckin' noticed it, J. It's drawin' attention man--you don't want the fuckin' heat, do you?' 'I'm rich. The cops won't touch me. Lexicon has to level up.' 'You don't have to wave that goddamned thing in his face, man. Ain't no way he can think straight--no one trusts you right now, not with that thing.' 'Sobias?' He snorted and went to the bar. 'Well. There's the answer. You've lost your muscle, Johnny, so where were we?' 'You were puttin' the piece up.' 'I didn't ask you, Trey.' Something damp smacked me in the face; I instinctually pushed it to the side and then looked up of my own accord. Johnny's face, leaner and draped in shadow, stared back at me. He took my bottle and downed a few shots, then smiled. 'That what you wanted, Jordan? Here I am, Johnny fuckin' Lexicon, and you know, I'm as tired as you are of this whole thing. I fucked up, yeah, but you've fucked up too. I know you've hit people harder than I hit you, and you don't have to deal with them waving guns around in your face when you're just trying to make a living off of what little you've got left. Yeah, I let you dance around in a silly fucking mask and wrestle when you shouldn't have. That. Pipe. Smoke. I paid a doctor off to tell you that you weren't as fucked as you really were. Milk. Spilled. Cry. So, do something about it--anything. I'm trying to change things, but I think I'll leave it up to the psycho with the gun for right now. Whaddya say, old buddy?' Sobias came back with a beer that had to be as warm as the desert sand that surrounded us for miles, only taking brief concern at Lexicon not wearing his mask. I put my head down and held the gun tight. The mask was in my left hand--I put it to Lexicon's chest, leaning across the table, and he slowly put it back on his face. 'Welcome to Anarch.' His head raised and he quickly extended his left hand to shake. I waved the gun again and laid it back in its box. 'Thank you, Jordan. You won't regret this.' 'I don't plan on it. Trey, let the man out.' Trey stood, breathing deeply while Sobias laughed. 'I thought you were going to shoot the poor fucker.' 'He said what I wanted to hear--didn't give me any bullshit about finding himself while lost in the desert, seeing the wrongs of his past transgressions, that boring shit.' 'I'll see you in Boston.' 'If you make it, Torm.' They left quickly, only slightly panicked and showing very few signs of being frantic messes, and Trey sat back down, laughing to himself. 'Y'know.' 'No, I don't.' 'You're not so bad, man. I mean, yeah, you did just kick them out after threatenin' their lives, but shit, you were right about that waitress.' 'What, she steal your wallet?' 'Nah, she was just making this evil ass eyes an'...' He stopped, one arm reached behind his back. 'That bitch stole my wallet.' 'No, I stole your wallet. You want something to drink? I'm gonna be here awhile.' 'Nah, not right now. I'm tryna find my fuckin' wallet.' 'Really, I took it. You left it on the dashboard of the rental and I grabbed it.' 'Shit, for real?' 'Yeah. Now, want something to drink? You've got a big night coming up.' 'Might as well, man. Ain't a big night though.' 'Yeah? Just the chance to lose both your titles and end up a chump,' I took a gulp from the bottle, 'or come out on top, the indisputable future of GCW. Take your pick.' 'Hah, that's all cool but, damn, what're you gonna do with Lexicon?' 'I made that pretty clear, man.' I broke and let 'SENORITA' ring through the bar to call the waitress. 'Marauder's under Anarch contract now. I'm getting into the fed. All's well that end's well.' 'You're insane.' 'No; you're the one trying to blow off Dangerous Games.' 'Shit, I know you don't come down on Eva an' Erik like this.' 'They don't seem to like me much anymore.' She appeared again, her face colder than before. 'Cuatros cervezas, por favor.' With that, she nodded and left us alone again for a moment. 'Besides, what're tag titles in a company that's got a handful of active teams?' 'What's a US title in a company that's got no one to compare to the champ?' 'They're not gonna buy that, you know. You gotta do something other than just get cocky.' 'Yeah, what I always do.' 'Point. Well, if I trust you to not fuck up...' 'I won't fuck up.' The bottles appeared on the table while I blinked, and the woman slipped away quickly. Something in the air. 'You're smart, kid. Got a real future.' 'Hah, fuck you man.' 'What? I was being serious.' 'Hard to tell.' I grabbed one of the glass bottles and held it over the table; its lukewarm temperature was a bad sign. 'To the rich getting richer.' 'To the rich gettin' richer.' The bottles clinked together and we drank solemnly. Nothing about the situation was particularly depressing, warm beer's just hard to drink without getting a tad morose. I lit another cigarette and dwelled on that as the conversation passed away from shop talk and to casual banter that avoided all pressing matters for the sake of relaxed conversation, but neither of us seemed to put our hearts into it and we sat in ambivalence as the empty bottles lined up on the table in long columns, witness to our harsh aversion of reality. Neither of us let those real concerns slip away from our minds; we had just silently chosen to wait until life caught up on us, as it has a tendency to. There was no need to lengthen the process by filling in the blank time with paranoia, so we drank long into the night.
