Initiation and Emancipation

23 January 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/23/11 ”Did I really used to live like this? This is supposed to be a child's room? Not a single poster or drawing. Not a single stray toy. Not even so much as a crease in the bed linen. Nothing. It's weird that I spent the first thirteen years of my life in this room but it looks like it's never been lived in. Of course some would say I hadn't lived at all until I left and I'd be inclined to agree with them. I wasn't miserable here, I just always suspected there was more to life than what I'd been shown here. As I grew, so did my curiosity. I had to know if I could be something other than what my parents wanted me to be. I made up some crap about wanting to train Puroresu with Uncle Shawn in Chicago; Dad would never say no to me furthering my training. Shawn had nothing new to show me in the ring... but, oh man, the things he DID show me. The little things most people don't notice: cars, food, music, the simple pleasure of being a complete asshole to another human being once in a while. For the first time in my life it felt OK to be less than perfect. It's your flaws that make you unique; tell you who you are. Five years later, looking at this room, I have no idea who the kid who lived here was, and now I have to pretend to be him. Getting into the character of a kid with no character. Heaven help me.” Standing, staring into a full-length mirror in what was once his bedroom is Alexander Edwards. However, this clean-shaven young man, with every last strand of his dark brown hair slicked back into a tight ponytail, sporting a crisp, black, custom made Armani suit, is by no stretch of the imagination the real Alexander Edwards. Alex never wears suits. They're restrictive. He has always been utterly confused by his father's insistence on wearing one to the arena every single day of his 20 year career. “Why would someone who has to share a locker room with twenty muscle-bound monsters intent on ripping your head off, wear something so impractical for fighting?” Of course his lifetime of training in the etiquette of a “proper” professional wrestler, gave Alex the answer before he could even finish asking himself the question. He couldn't help but smirk at how deeply his training was ingrained, even after so many years away from his parent's tutelage. “Be a champion. In form as well as function.” One of the Edwards' most highly regarded beliefs. That whether in the arena or simply walking the street an Edwards must dress like a champion at all times. Anything less than Sunday best is disrespectful to the sport; and Alex's preferred look of long hair allowed to hang where it pleases, 5 O’clock shadow and casual clothing, certainly did not fit the mould. ”At least it covers my tattoos; no need to explain those today” Alex tells himself, whilst channelling every ounce of his willpower into breaking the habit of tugging at the collar of his “costume.” It must be a nervous tick, because despite his distaste for formal attire, Alex knows it's unlikely that he finds the best suit money can buy, quite this uncomfortable. He addresses the mirror; as if he were making a deal with the unfamiliar reflection staring back at him. “OK kid. It's just a stupid tradition. Doesn't mean a damn thing. Give the old man what he wants. Then go home and move on with your fucking life already.” He inhales one more tentative breath before turning sharply on his heels and storming purposefully out of the room. Mere moments later he bursts through the solid oak doors of the trophy room of the palatial Edwards estate. It is an extravagant circular room with a white marble floor and blood red walls trimmed with intricate carvings gilded with gold. A grand ballroom which had been re-purposed decades earlier as a monument to the glory of the Edwards Legacy. Alex is halted in his tracks by the sight that lays before him. Not the small family gathering of his father, mother and sister that he had expected, but dozens of well-wishers: close family, distant relatives, family friends, seemingly anyone who may have so much as held Alex as a child is here, and he knows them all. As the brightest and best professional wrestling had ever seen. It seems that Alex will now be forced to give his little performance to a crowd. “Happy Birthday Alexander.” Booms the voice of Alex's legendary father “The Legacy” Ewan Edwards. Who even in his advancing years still cuts an impressive figure of a man, at 6'3” 250 pounds of solid muscle. He greets his son, soon to be a man, with a firm handshake and a pat on the back. “Thanks Dad. This is... elaborate.” “It's not every day a man turns eighteen.” “Certainly not with half the Hall of Fame at his party.” “Well, you're no average eighteen year-old. Let's get started.” ”Let's get it over with.”Alex mentally corrects his father as he nervously approaches the centre of the room. These are not the nerves of a groom awaiting his bride at the altar, but the nerves of a man facing a firing squad with no blindfold. With the exception of the gathered crowd, Alex had always known what to expect on this day. Today is the day that Alexander Edwards the man, ceases to exist, and is replaced with Alexander Edwards, the seventh incarnation of “The Legacy.” Ewan addresses the congregation with all of the theatricality and ritual befitting such an event. An event that has only occurred on five previous occasions. An event eighteen years in the making. “Our family, our friends, our illustrious colleagues, we thank you for joining us on this joyous occasion. Where we celebrate Alexander's arrival not only into the world at large, but also into OUR world. For six generations the Edwards family has dedicated itself to the pursuit of excellence in professional wrestling. Not for ourselves as individuals, but for the next generation. We pass down all we learn, so that our children, and in turn our grandchildren grow stronger, and the Edwards name continues to prosper long after we are dead and gone. Look around this room and you will see the fruits of this labour...” Even Alex himself was compelled to join the gathered crowd and truly take in his current surroundings. Just as in his five-year absence he had forgotten the Spartan nature of his own bedroom, he had also forgotten how intimidating this place had always been. Not due to the sheer vastness of it's size, or the opulence of the décor, but rather the amount of history contained within. Every surface adorned with a glittering award representing a significant achievement in the world of professional wrestling. The perimeter of the room lined with pedestals bearing trophies of every conceivable shape, size and importance. But most daunting of all is the family tree. Six generations of the Edwards dynasty, not scrawled on paper with ink as one would expect, but laid out, larger than life, across the walls with championships in glass cases and golden plaques detailing the accomplishments of those that held them. Champions one and all. Alex swallows violently as the true importance of the commitment he is about to make. That which he was so quick to dismiss as silly tradition, was so very real to the people he cares most deeply about. People who would be crushed by his failure. Calm down Alex. This changes nothing. You're good at what you do. You've got this. Alex chants to himself, over and over in his mind, like a mantra, as his father continues the initiation. “....but all of these accolades you see before you, which other men have coveted their entire careers, are but mere trinkets to us. Souvenirs we collect along the path of our true purpose. Our true legacy. The legacy that we leave to professional wrestling. In a sport that each passing year becomes more and more overpopulated with brain-dead strongmen who rely on nothing more than brute force to succeed, shallow acrobats who stun their opponents with their cheap tricks, and the psychopaths, freaks, weirdos and criminals why bring pollution to the pure beauty of our craft with their presence.... we stand proudly to represent, the sweet science, the art of pure wrestling. But more important than how we conduct ourselves inside the ring is how we conduct ourselves outside of it. As the living, breathing embodiment of valour, honour, integrity and respect. As champions. In form as well as function. As more than human; as “The Legacy” itself.” This is the part of the story Alex could never forget even if he tried. The driving force behind his departure to Chicago. The removal of choice; forced compliance with a certain way of dressing and behaving. The intention behind it is noble, but does it justify the the stripping away of his hard-earned individuality, his personal hopes and dreams, so that he may better serve his family's legacy? Alex didn't think so. But is it any better to betray the people who brought him into this world, people he loves, for such a selfish reason as an unwillingness to conform? As Alex stands torn between loyalty to himself and to his family, he doesn't notice his father approaching him. “My son.” Ewan gushes, obviously relishing saying those words today, and placing his hands on Alex's shoulders snapping him out of his trance. Alex's brow furrows in confusion as for the first time today, no, for the first time since he left, Alex looks directly into his father's eyes and sees something he has never seen in his life. Tears. “Dad.... You OK?” Alex asks tentatively. Unsure of the generally accepted protocol for when your notoriously stoic father is openly weeping. “I just can't get over what a fine young gentleman you've turned into over the last five years. I knew from the first moment I held you that you were going to be a warrior and lo and behold the moment you could stand on your own two legs you out in that ring training with your old man. Learning faster than I could teach. Leaving home when you're just a boy to go perfect your craft? I've never seen anything like it. I owe you an apology though. I was a little concerned that spending so long with that troublemaking brother-in-law of mine would corrupt you. But here you are, standing tall, looking sharp; the very image of an Edwards. I'm sorry for doubting your resolve for even a moment. You stayed true to yourself Alexander. I'm proud of you my son.” Much like the ceremony itself had been, this tender moment, which should have been kept between a father and his son, had become public domain thanks to the invited guests, whose clapping and whooping now echoed throughout the hall. Alex was oblivious to the uproar that surrounded him, enveloped in a sudden wave of calm and clarity. Thanks to his father's heartfelt confession all of the doubt within Alex had vanished. He knew exactly what needed to be done. Ewan sees the look of contentment that he has brought to his son's face and pulls him into a tight embrace. “Dad... don't.” “Modesty too? You'll be a true champion son.” Ewan replies, laughing heartily, pleasantly surprised by the humility of his son's character. “I said DON'T!” In one quick motion Alexander places both his hands on his father's chest, pushes him away and violently breaks the embrace; sending Ewan tumbling to the ground, halfway across the room with relatively little effort. The crowd falls silent. Alex stands above his fallen father and speaks earnestly, almost coldly. “I'm sorry, I truly am. I'm sorry for knocking you on your ass, but not for the decision I've made. I tried. Honest to God I tried to be the person you wanted me to be. To believe what you believe; to think the way you think. Because all I ever wanted was for you to be proud of me. But you know what? Now that you've said it. Now I've heard you say the actual words to me, it means nothing. Because you're not proud of me Dad. You're proud of the lie.” “Lie?” Whispers Ewan. His voice an undistinguishable mix of shock, anger and confusion. Alex pulls the band out of his hair and shakes it loose. Rips off the suit that has been smothering him for what feels like an eternity, revealing the numerous tattoos that completely cover his arms. A seemingly insignificant transformation, that in this instance has turned a son into a stranger. “Nice to meet you Ewan. My name is Alexander, my friends call me Alex. Today is my eighteenth birthday. My interests include body art, rock music and cooking. And when I grow up I'm going to be the greatest wrestler who ever lived. Because I love it. Because it's what I want for ME. Not because I want to save the world, or set an example, or make everybody else dress and act exactly like me. Oh.... and certainly not because I'm locked into some almighty fucking Legacy.” A seemingly everlasting pause envelops the room. “Now I think this is the part where you're supposed to disown me. Tell me I'm not worthy of my name.... so shall we get it over with?” Alex prompts his father, calmly, evenly. Genuinely wanting the day to simply end. Ewan picks himself off the floor, and breaks his silence, attempting quite successfully to seem unaffected. “The name is a gift you were given the day you were born Alexander. It's not something I can just take back for a refund like a faulty toy or a sweater that doesn't fit. Like it or not, you ARE an Edwards. The name is yours, you may do with it what you will.” Ewan's expression which until now has remained as unyielding as stone, begins to crack. This new expression which Alex can see creeping across his father's face is the very thing he had hoped to avoid at the start of the day. A face split almost exactly in half, the eyes struggling to hold back a flood of bitter tears of hurt and disappointment, but his lips contorting into a murderous snarl. “If you wish to make it completely fucking worthless, that is your choice. I no longer have any say in the matter.” Ewan is the first to leave the room, followed shortly after by his wife, then his daughter. One by one the room clears of those who gathered earlier to wish Alex well, each one now giving him a look that could kill as they depart. Alexander is left alone with his thoughts as a smirk crosses his lips. “Now I hop my happy ass on the first plane back to Chicago, and never look back. Maybe I wont be charged for going over my baggage allowance, now I've lost the anchor that's been hanging round my neck my whole damn life. Was it wrong to hurt my family the way that I did. Maybe. But remember - “It's OK to be less than perfect. It's your flaws that make you unique; tell you who you are.” One thing's for damn sure. Mom'll never call me her “Little Angel” ever again. But who wants to be an angel anyway...I'd rather be a Saint.”
