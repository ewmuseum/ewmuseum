Smoking by Pregnant Woman May Result in Cool.

2 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 2nd, 2005 Tic. Trey's eyes race left to right under their lids. Tic. Fish heads strung up around the room. Tic. A reek of vomit and disease. Tic. Solitary cockroach scrambles across a wall and pauses in the light of a window. Tic. Looks over, offering up the lime green egg sac it carries as a gift or a weapon. Tic. Trey studies it and wonders how it could survive a nuclear blast. Tic. It's as well built as any creature on earth, one of the best designs chaos spewed up. Tic. The bug races in the trenches the venetian blinds cast and disappears to the shadows where Trey can't follow it. Tic. He throws his head back on the pillow and closes his eyes, arms dropped taut to the sides of his body. Tic. 'Breathe.' Tic. Bruises and cuts course his form, laid over generations of old wounds. Tic. It was never the drugs. Tic. 'Are you breathing?' Tic. A car outside screams and he quits counting the seconds after five minutes without the roach. Tic. He sits up and puts his hands to his head, further from sleep than from death by atomic warfare. His feet swing and slide across the shaggy carpeting before going down on a wet and slimy bare floor. Something squeezes between his toes. Tic. Flipping the light by his bed on, Trey admits to being awake and accepts his duties. He dresses in a wifebeater and baggy jeans, then pulls a leather jacket on top of it. Tic. The light makes him shine for a moment. Tic. 'Stop breathing.' Nothing happens and his eyes clench shut, cheeks swell as he passes to the door and out. It locks itself behind him. His footsteps echo down the hallway, repeating like the flickering yellow bulbs that cast spiders and snakes and hell on the black and white zig-zagged carpet. Staying in the light the best he can, Trey slips down the stairwell. Cracked ancient portraits line the walls, dead contributors to the hotel, and cast their disapproving glare with doughy frowns and unaware eyes. His stomach curdling is the only sound in the lobby (massive, dim, painted a vibrant red and filled with expensive looking furniture) save the whistling of the man behind the desk breathing. The man's centuries older than the paintings or their dead subjects, a corpse thrown in a maroon vest and trying to look happy for his resurrection. 'Breathe.' He nods at Trey in passing. Trey lifts his head slightly and walks through a set of glass doors, through the anteroom, and past the heavy wooden doors that lead to the outside. The city madness eats him instantly--those shrieking tires, those faceless voices, those twisting shades--and latches into his central nervous system. His heart jumps with every step that leads him through the night and into the glow of an auburn street lamp, where he stops and thinks empty thoughts with a passive calm. He runs a hand through nappy red hair that sticks straight up in a crude afro and wants to light a cigarette, forgetting for a moment that he doesn't smoke. The moon ticks by. Waxy fish swim past. If he said something, it would be: 'I don't mind smellin' like a fish; just don't wanna smell like a lot of 'em.' He moves dry through the ruins. Faces pass in the dark, briefly highlighted and fading away to their Point B's, eying Trey with their bullet holes and saying nothing. He's deaf to them anyway; his ears still ring from the games he's played and nothing's louder than the roar of pain that his muscles give up as feedback for every yard he takes in his robotic march. The gears need oil and creak/whine out for it. Trey strains them anyway and splashes on through the puddles of the night. Billboards above him advertise liver failure, lung cancer, and horrible car accidents in no real order. It's unclear if they lead him to the bar he stops at, outfitted as an authentic Irish Pub in the middle of not-Ireland. He's sick to his stomach before he goes inside to a world of smoke and ink and boisterous laughter. Someone recognizes him, screaming, 'Tremendous!' until he gets something back. Trey raises a fist and grins at the liquor-bloated maggot writhing on the stool, causing the other vermin to cheer in approval. They paw at their glasses and bitch for more shots to guzzle and throw hard drink down into their organs. 'GCW', cheers a man with constantly twitching feelers like fat pink caterpillars burrowing from his mouth before he walks over to Trey, who's found a nice hole in the corner. 'Nice job out there,' he slurs, 'lemme buy you a drink.' 'I got it.' 'No, you don't understand. I insist. It's on me.' So Trey laughs and accepts, not ready to deal with the man any longer. The lager eventually comes, carried by a large brunette with breasts tearing out the front of her mostly-buttoned white shirt. She smiles at Trey and runs a long, thin tongue over her top teeth. When she turns, something brushes against his leg. He doesn't look down. The jukebox scratches out a drinking song and the patrons follow with it, a rasping cacophony. If the sun should rise tomorrow, Before my ship is seen 'roun' your way Then hold out your fear An' think of me dear With a hope that'll never fade He's not sure if he's heard it before, or if it even exists, but it spins around his head regardless and gives him hope that he's been forgotten. His drink's empty and he orders another from the grotesque waitress. Her thick lips pucker and smack incessantly, causing the fat of her face to ripple and crease until it becomes a blasphemous deformation of the human form. Words come out all slurred, and Trey just nods until she leaves for his drink. 'Are you breathing?' 'Hree ye go, loonely.' He nods to her and pulls his feet off the ground as something scrapes under them. The room's rancor rises steadily and drowns out the song that had drowned out the television before it, with Trey alone in his head. A mighty gulp brings him closer to level, and he begins to eye the pub around him. The beasts are orgiastic, wrapping around one another and shining with Crisco sweat as they thrust into oblivion. It disgusts him enough that he drinks until he can look at it with an impartial eye, if no closer to understanding. As the mass daisy chains around on the floor, a sole organ begins to stand out to Trey's mind. He focuses on its melody as it rises and falls, though it drifts and slips from him, until finally succumbing to the bleating of the drunks in depraved communion. 'Hey.' The noise dies. Trey looks up to a pale round face with a button nose and ocean eyes; dirty blonde hair pulled back into a frayed ponytail that let a few strands scrape along the collar of her thick brown sweater. It feeks like burlap in Trey's mind. 'Yo. What's up?' 'You were amazing tonight.' 'Yeah. Thanks.' 'Ah, cheer up. You were the most determined person out there; at least deserve a couple free drinks.' 'Big fan or somethin'?' 'You could say that.' 'Groupie?' She smirks. 'Big fan. Now, about those drinks...' 'I'm buyin'.' 'I can't let you do that.' 'Not the first person with that problem tonight.' 'But probably the best looking.' 'Won't argue.' 'Something hard?' 'Huh?' 'The drink.' 'Oh, shyeah. Beer's slow.' She gets up to order and leaves Trey to deal with the monsters by himself. He zones out, staring at a dark spot on the wall. It moves when he focuses, darting a few feet up or down in sudden bursts like spasms. After the game has played through several times, the spot stops--knows something--and darts back off to the shadows that spawned it in time for the doll girl to stumble back to the table. She plants drinks in front of him, six shots of something clear, and throws her head back laughing. 'Enjoy.' 'Breathe.' 'Thanks.' He kills the bit of beer sloshing at the bottom of his glass and downs a shot with one quick motion. Face twists, throat knots up, liver begins to cave in. Wiping the drool from his face: 'Sorry, I ain't caught your name.' 'Alyse.' 'Damn, don't hear that much. So, you live round here or somethin'?' 'Used to. Here now on break from college.' 'Shit, cool, cool. What you doin' in school?' 'Art degree at NYU, minor in communications. In case I fuck up.' 'Like it?' 'Yeah, it's alright. Don't really like the teachers--they're mostly stuck in the Bronze Age.' She forces a laugh under her breath, cuing Trey to smile before she goes on. 'But I'm not letting it stop me. I'm going to revolutionize sculpture.' Trey hits another shot that Alyse matches. 'Sounds good, kid. Got anythin' to show off? At your house, whatever.' 'Yeah, actually. There's a piece I'm working on, I'd love to have you look at it.' 'That'd be straight, yeah. Just gimme a minute.' He shakes his head and empties another shotglass before rising for the bathroom. Forced to choose his steps carefully, Trey nimbly slips through the gyring mire of mucus and sweat with little on his clothes at the other side. The heel of his shoe fights, caught in ooze that stretches and finally pops. He enters a small hallway, haunted by a glowing neon ghost, and follows a green arrow to the men's room. The wooden door creaks open and he steps inside. 'Stop breathing.' Plunged into darkness. The faucet drops bombs that explode in the sink, reverberate in Trey's skull until his mind pours from his left ear like undercooked bread pudding. His head droops down, body unresponsive and useless. There's no thoughts now, save the ringing of primordial urges that the brain's evolved to control. His lungs want oxygen, his stomach wants flesh, his hands want throats and the entire lower half of his body devotes itself to the singular urge of 'fuck.' A fly buzzes past his face. His brain slides back into his head with a plop and Trey fumbles for the lights. Flicking the light switch brings nothing but disappoint, so he shrugs and leaves, sure to walk around the pile-on instead of barging through it. Alyse looks up to him with bloodshot eyes. A few frayed stitches hang off the side of her face, and empty glasses fall in front of her like dead soldiers. 'So soon?' 'Light was busted.' 'That sucks. You could use the bathroom at...' Breathes. 'My place.' Blood from a broken scab on Trey's knee begins to dry. 'Sounds good.'
