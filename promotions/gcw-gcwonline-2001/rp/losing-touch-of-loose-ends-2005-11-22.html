Losing Touch of Loose Ends

22 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11-22-05 ~<>~<>~<>~ Somewhere amongst the mountains of Vermont? Rich Rollins had expected the battleground to be inhospitable. He had anticipated a ragged landscape screaming for a sacrifice, waiting in the hopes that the brave few who would dare mar its timeless surface in bloodshed would return once again for a final lesson in sufferance. He had expected to see Hell personified. What he hadn’t expected was beauty. And Lord, what a beautiful landscape it was. Rich Rollins squatted on the precipice of Mt. Equinox, his long hair whipping irritably in tune with the unhindered gusts of wind that ravaged his lonely perch, gazing idly over the rolling foothills spread below. The day couldn’t have been more perfect; a single sliver of silver cloud hung high in the sky, casting its shadow on the rocky ridges below as it moved in lazy spurts across the horizon. Even with a harsh winter coming on, the once-flourishing flora still managed to steal a man’s breath, rolling over the fading hills like a painting in a children’s book. For once in his life, Rich Rollins felt safe. Such a deceptive feeling it was. “You know you could very well die here,” came the voice of Rollins? imaginary mentor. The Renegade didn’t care to acknowledge Dave Olson’s condescension. He had finally learned to tune out the snide antics of his petulant hallucinations. Reaching into a small hiking pack, the Renegade withdrew a bottle of vintage Perrier-Jou’t Belle Epoque he had received as a gift from Primetime Central’s offices after winning the Global title. Giving the landscape a final salute, he smashed the champagne into a rock at his feet. He grinned. “God, I hope so.” ~<>~<>~<>~ Chicago. The Gaslight Tavern, two days earlier? “Have you considered taking rappelling lessons” May come in handy, you never know.” Slowly drowning himself in a schooner of whatever the joint tried to pass off as beer, the Renegade shrugged. “What for” If I go over a cliff, I seriously doubt I’ll have a cord and harness handy.” Cynthia O’Doyle would have laughed had there been anything to laugh about. Her brow furrowed, narrowing eyes made of pure malachite on Rollins? slouched frame as he continued to inhale his bitter beverage. “Rich, if something were to happen to you” I don’t know what I would do.” Newborn kittens didn’t have eyes that sincere. Rollins snorted. “I’m sure your husband has a shoulder or two you could cry on. Isn’t being an understanding judge of character part of his job description?” Her face shattered like a broken mirror, the hurt plainly visible. Those doe-like emerald eyes began to well up, but her back stiffened and her composure returned before the levy had a chance to break. “You don’t have to be such a bastard,” she intoned dryly. “He is your psychiatrist. Couldn’t you at least pretend to have some shred of common sense, just for one day?” “Believe me honey,” Rollins said, meeting her challenging glare with his own icy indifference, “I’ve got enough sense stuffed away in this thick skull of mine to know that it’s a no-no to tell my shrink how many times I fucked his wife this week. Besides, his services I pay for. Yours I get for free.” Cynthia’s mouth froze open in wordless shock. Rollins? biting remarks had indeed struck a chord; slowly turning her face away from the Renegade’s callous scowl, the psychiatrist’s wife smeared a tear across her cheek as Rollins sat in silence, taking another swig of foul pisswater. With an intentional smack of his lips he smirked. “Does he give you the spouse special on hourly rates?” “Fuck you.” Her voice was wounded, but it still bristled with barbs sharp as steel. Spoiled bitch, Rollins almost blurted. He had to forcibly restrain his urge to laugh in her face. The woman was nothing more than a pencil-pushing head peeper’s lonely trophy. Before Steven Caldera had insisted he look into “anger management” and “stress relief” consulting, Cynthia O’Doyle had been trapped in a world without windows. Before Rollins met Dr. Paul O’Doyle, Mrs. Paul O’Doyle was nothing more than a walking, talking Barbie doll. Whenever playtime came to an end, back in the toy chest she went. Just like any other doll. “Don’t you have an appointment to make?” Cynthia refused to look at him. Her voice trembled. Taking down the remainder of his stale beer, Rich Rollins slammed the still-sudsy schooner on the table top. “I’ll be sure to tell Paul you said hello. He appreciates sappy shit like that.” Pushing away from the table, the Renegade left Cynthia O’Doyle to find a way to soothe herself. It was going to be a long day, he could tell. ~<>~<>~<>~ The offices of Dr. Paul O’Doyle? “What is this man’s name, the one you see?” The first thing Rich Rollins had noticed about the room were the clouds. Fluffy white mountains of hand painted clouds covered the gentle domed ceiling, watching over an office that struck Rollins as very feng shui. Matching sets of furniture dotted the enormous space in half a dozen small circles, each one lacquered and stitched in a different theme. The question Dr. O’Doyle usually started with as an ice breaker: “Polka dots or stripes?” Rollins was a leopard print fan himself. Unfortunately he had settled with the desert camouflage loveseat and Spanish silk pillows, but in all fairness, the pattern of his couch was presently of little interest to him. The clouds were so much more entertaining. “Rich,” the good doctor probed again, “You still with me?” Rollins? mind fell from the ceiling and back to his body. “Huh?” “Your imaginary friend,” Dr. O’Doyle repeated patiently, “What is his name?” Rollins hesitated, but he didn’t know why. “Dave Olson.” O’doyle cleared his throat. “And who is Dave Olson?” Shifting the weight off of his lower back, Rich Rollins sank deeper into the deceptively soft cushions of his camel-colored couch of choice. “He’s my rain cloud,” he replied. Dr. Paul O’Doyle didn’t have a pencil and scratch pad to doodle on. He didn’t have an obnoxious pair of horn rimmed classes to push up his nose. He didn’t even mumble “um-hmm” while Rollins spoke, feigning interest in problems that in all honesty he could do nothing to solve anyway. Dr. O’Doyle had built his reputation as a new age thinker, using a simple technique that had worked wonders for his patients. Like any good psyciatrist, he offered nothing more than piece of mind for a hundred dollars an hour. He just had one difference. He listened. He really listened. So Rollins continued: “It’s like I’m Winnie the fuckin” Pooh, and no matter where I go I can’t seem to shake this goddamn rain cloud hanging over my head.” He sighed. “What’s wrong with me, doc” Most people have to pay good money for hallucinations like this. I don’t want the baggage anymore.” O’Doyle leaned back in his own camo recliner, pursing his lips in thought. “You’re profession isn’t exactly low maintenance, Rich. It’s a high-stress job, being a wrestler.” “Tell me something I don’t know,” Rollins laughed. “Maybe you’re projecting your fears into an alter ego as a way to cope,” the good doctor offered. “People do it all the time. Your psyche just took it to the next level.” “That’s the whole reason I invented the Renegade in the first place, doc.” Rollins traced a cloud’s outline with his eyes, a cloud that vaguely resembled a woman’s face. “My head already has two minds fighting for the same territory. I don’t need a third wheel.” “How’s your sex life?” O’Doyle asked. Rollins couldn’t help himself. “I don’t know, your wife isn’t too keen on pillow talk.” The comment gave Dr. O’Doyle pause for a brief moment, but his laughter quickly cut into the silence. “You’ve got a pretty sharp defense mechanism there, buddy. I can see why you’re good at what you do.” God, he was so clueless. Rollins tried to change the subject before his mouth started running too fast. “I’ve got a big match coming up next weekend against a guy I’ve wanted to cut down for years.” “You mean Seymour Almasy?” “Yeah.” The Renegade tilted his head just enough to be able to see the good doctor’s inquisitive expression. “You follow wrestling much, doc?” O’Doyle smiled broadly, meeting Rollins? mischievous smirk with a shrug. “I used to watch GCW when you were World champion, man. Why do you think I called Caldera for this job” I requested you, not the other way around.” Rollins suddenly felt very guilty. Here was a guy who was not only offering his help, but he was a fan to boot. And here was Rollins, sleeping with his wife. It was a cruel world, Rollins figured. Might as well make the best of it. “Have you ever considered retirement?” O’Doyle asked. Trying to keep the sarcasm in check, Rollins merely chuckled. “Only every day of my life.” “So what keeps you around the ring?” O’Doyle was genuinely interested. The woman’s face had disappeared. In her place was a new face, a face that Rollins had come to think of as his unavoidable destiny. It was the face of Seymour Almasy. “Unfinished business, doc.” Rollins frowned. “There’s always a loose end, and I aim to see them all tied off.” ~Fin~
