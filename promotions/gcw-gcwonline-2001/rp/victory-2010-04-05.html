Victory

5 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/05/10 “Victory at all costs, victory in spite of all terror, victory however long and hard the road may be; for without victory there is no survival.” Winston Churchill It was a victory of sorts. Joey sat in Starbucks with a White Chocolate Mocha and his Hewett-Packard Laptop in front of him with a smirk on his face. Sure, to the fans and those around GCW it was clearly a loss. Slash ended up one of the two losers of the three-way match. The fans were stoked about T.A Giles who clearly has the masses already paying very close attention to him. Joey Harris knew from the very beginning, as soon as he began to study the roster and prepare for his opponents that any match with T.A Giles debuting will be an uphill climb. It was exactly that. Joey was more disappointed in the lack of effort from Bishop Steele than the loss. In this business its not about the wins and losses, its about writing a story in the ring. Giving the crowd something to connect to. Joey didn't have a chance to talk to Giles following the show, but he certaintly felt like it was the two of them carrying the weight of the three-way match. He expects more from his match this time. Despite the loss, Joey sits quietly in the Starbucks, unknown to the strangers coming in and out of the door and Joey has a deep sense of relief. For the first time in his most recent memory his mind did not play games with him. From opening bell to closing announcement, Joey was ... Joey. Joey taps his cup quietly as he waits for Preston to arrive. His mentor called him early Monday morning to congratulate his performance. Joey didn't expect the call. He really didn't imagine that Preston would still be watching wrestling. Hearing the old man's voice brought Joey the closest to tears than he has been in a long time. Well, probably ever since he realized that his ex-girlfriend, Siobhan, was finally leaving him. That was the last time Joey remembers crying, the moment he realized that the alchohol and his fucked up brain finally ended the last meaningful relationship he would ever be in. If Preston saw thes how, he wondered if Siobhan happened to turn the station and catch WorldWide, probably not, he thought. There is no way she would come within a ten-foot pole to this business after what he did to her. The smirk slowly disappears as the memory flashback of his fist crushing against the side of Siobhan's face when she was desperately trying to get him to see a Psychologist appeared in his imagination. He would take that moment back if he could. He would take alot of moments back. He was glad he had no regrets about the 28th. WorldWide came and gone, Joey flew under the radar and he stayed sane. It was a win. Joey looked up at the sound of the bell which alerts the Starbucks staff that a customer has come in. Joey smiled widely when he saw the old man walk in. Its been a long time since Joey saw him, and age has taken its toll on the old man. He is walking with a cane now, and his facial features are dominated by wrinkles. Yet, when Joey saw his eyes he still saw the fire that drove Preston to push Joey as a kid to perform in the ring. The same passion that Preston seemed to have with whatever project he took upon hismelf. The old man might be old on the outside, but Joey could still see the Preston he remembered. Joey jumped to his feet and shook the old man's hand. Preston grip was a bit weaker now than what Joey remembered, and Joey noticed that there were scabs on Preston's head. "What happened? Helen beat you?" Joey said with a smile on his face. Preston took a seat across from where Joey was sitting, "Nah ... doctors says its goddamn skin cancer. Sumthen 'bout the sun and its effects on skin. Ah well...everyone has their time." Everyone has their time, a quote that Preston said ever since Joey knew him. Joey used to think that Preston was being pessismistic saying it all the time. But now he realizes that Preston was actually being optimistic. "Damn doctors," Joey said sarcasticaly, "its been a while old man, its nice to see you. How is Helen?" Preston looked down at the table briefly, "Helen is wit' Jesus, Joey. Two years ago. Fucking heart attack." Joey was speechless for a moment, how did he possibly miss that? "Hey kid," Preston who noticed that Joey was feeling guilty, "its okay. We never made 'da effort to call you either." Joey nodded quietly. "Ya did good out 'der boy," Preston said changing the subject, "nice to see ya on 'da tube rasslin. You look like yer in good goddamn shape. Shouldda won that match in my mind." Joey smiled, "but I did old man." "Ah yes. Always 'da optimist. Same ol' Joey," Preston laughed, "yer a good kid." "I guess so", Joey nodded, "maybe I can get you tickets to the next show? I've got a second match here. Hardcore rules, your speciality." "Nah... nah," Preston shook his head, "I dont travel out of 'da city. Imma old man. Jus' keep yer head straigth and stay on 'da tv" Keep your head straight, those words resounded in Joey's mind. Surely he can keep doing it. If he lasted WorldWide 128 he can keep it going. He thinks. ---- Joey paced around his Super 8 motel room at 3am, sweating profusely and feeling numb. He slams his fist on the hotel desk a few times in frustration, and then kicks the old leather desk chair that the Hotel provides causing it to slam against the radiator. Grabbing his head, all Joey wants to do at this point is scream but he was determined to keep his compusure. Its been a few weeks now of peaceful sleeps but tonight he was awakened with memories of his past. This time it felt like he was right there and watching himself at six years of age. He could feel the flames that consumed the only home he would know until his preteen years, and he felt the feelings of loneliness and abandoment when he heard that his mother and father were not able to escape the house. Why did I escape?, Joey thought. He could never get that answer. He remembered only pieces of what happened that dreadful day when he was six. He remembered the firemen rejoice that he was still alive. He remembered the stench of the smoke from the fire that stained his close. He remembered sitting in the hospital while the psychiatrist tried to console him as a young boy. what he could not remember is how he got out of the house, and why his parents were stuck. "Stop it God damnit!" Joey shouted, not caring about those neighboring his hotel room. Joey walked in front of the full length mirror and stared at himself for a moment. Get your composure, do not go down this path, he kept repeating to himself. He was on the brink of blacking out. He was close to losing himself. Joey gripped the bureau beside him tightly as if he was holding on to his own sanity. If he blacks out, if he allows his mind to play these tricks there is no telling what would come around the corner. With his left hand Joey threw a punch into the mirror with velocity that the entire wall shook. The glass shatters and falls to the ground and Joey can feel blood drip from his knuckles. "Please...not now..." Joey whispered. He stumbles around the corner into the bathroom and wipes sweat from his forehead. Feelings of anxiety have become so strong that Joey's entire body is shaking. In a few minutes, Joey would entire the Hell that crippled his entire life. He reaches over and turns the cold water on and begins to fill the tub. Turning and grabbing the ice bucket by the sink, Joey dumps an the ice in the tub. Please, God...somebody...Help, Joey thought. The bath continued to run and Joey climbed into the tub. One of the only solutions he found was that the cold water would stop whatever was happening to him. The psychologist recommended making drastic external changes that would bring his thoughts to peace. Joey leaned back as the cold water covered his trembling body. He began to run his hand across his body, feeling the scars on the left side of his body. The constant reminder that he indeed escaped the fire. Tears filled Joey's eyes as he began to feel intense feelings of helplessness. As though he can do nothing to stop what is inevitable. As he sits in a Los Angeles hotel room, only days away from his second GCW match and knowing that the only man that ever paid attention to him would be watching caused Joey to feel guilt. So many people were counting on him, and he wasn't sure that he could deliver. He began to think of his opponent, SurReal, and the Hardcore match that was coming up. Joey was certain that if he can not pull himself together that WorldWide 129 would be a very bad night for SurReal and any of those close to him. Joey dumped his head under the cool water and felt the rush of his blood circulate his body trying to keep warm. "Fucking hardcore match," Joey screamed as he came above water again. Of all the matches to put him in they chose that stipulation. The one type of match that he would have to let himself go in order to sell it to the fans. To make this match believable, Joey would put down some of the guards that he holds so tightly to. He feels bad for SurReal, because he is certain that his opponent has no clue what that means. In fact, Joey has no clue what the means. It is like they are trying to rid of me already, Joey thought again. With all the new signings and big names that are coming they could easily dispose of this rookie. He was sure that Caldera and Johnny felt they had no use for him. He will have to force them to pay attention. Somehow Joey had to let the real Slash come out and then after seven minutes try to pull himself back together. He wasn't sure that was possible. ---- "Sixty seconds!" "Alright thanks John," the interviewer nodded at the production staff and then looked at the wrestler he was going to interview. He didn't look as big as he had expected, when he heard he was doing a interview to promote GCW's show on local news he had expected someone that was massive. Instead he stared at this man they call "Slash" and wondered what the big deal was. "Nice to meet you, uh... Slash?" "Thanks, you too", Joey nodded. He was exhausted and his head was pounding. He had to do this public apperance as part of his contract, but he just wanted to run and hide. "Just three short questions for the news tonight, okay?" "Of course," Joey said, "I'm going to do it in character of course." "Whatever that means," the interviewer shrugged. He noticed that Joey was sweating profusely, and could see the sweat stains on his dress shirt. "Are you okay?" Joey wanted to scream "NO" at the top of his lungs but instead he simply nodded. "In five, four, three, two..one..." the staff member pointed at the local news reporter who put on his fake smile. "Thanks Paula, as you mentioned I am here with one of Global Championship Wrestling's newest stars who will be competing this week in a Hardcore match. It's an honor to sit here tonight with Slash!" With all the strength that Joey could muster, he tried to shake the cobwebs from his mind that have been there since the morning and put on his trademark smirk. "It is an honor for you," Joey winked after saying it and the reporter just smiled. "So why Slash? What does that name mean?" "Well boss," Joey said sarcastically, "I seriously think I was drunk when I chose the ring name on my application". Joey lied, it was really the nickname his father gave him. His Dad always commented on how he never knew "which" Joey would come out each day. His Mom always got angry at his Dad when he called him "Slash" because she felt it was encouraging Joey's unpredictable behaviour. "Really?" Joey shrugs, "That's what some of them think. Just ask Tim Shipley." "So you have a hardcore match coming up, how do you prepare for one of those?" Joey wipes the sweat from his forehead, "Well it is really quite simple. You don't. My opponent will probably agree with me on this one. When you got no rules, and tons of weapons, all preparation goes out the window. You just get in the ring and give it all you got." "Okay?" The reporter stutters a bit, he was expecting better answers than what he has gotten so far. Joey could tell he was starting to feel uncomfortable, but he had no energy to try to make him feel better. "Either way, boss, the people in good ol' L-A are going to appreciate what Slash can bring to the ring. And my opponent, SurReal is going to find out very quickly why I keep telling people that I'm a big deal." "Well thank you, Slash for your time." Joey leans over and shakes the reporters hand. "It was your pleasure," Joey said confidently. His Slash character was suppose to be cocky and edgy. He was trying his best to put on a good show, even though he felt like Hell. "And we are off!" The production crew member shouted. Joey leaned back in the chair, grateful that it was over. The reporter got up from his chair and quickly exited. "He is a weird one", Joey could hear the interviewer say from a distant. If you only knew, Joey thought.
