Better Off Dead: Not Much of a Part 2 but Rather a New Beginning

29 August 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/29/10 He's a bunch of fists in an empty weight room, hitting targets as they appear. There's sweat falling fast and forcing his eyes shut. Even closed, he finds his target and strikes harder. There's an elbow that follows a fist and a shoulder that caps off the attack. Taking another drag from his cigarette, he allows his mind to float and his body to sway. If Daniel Franco had taught him anything about fighting, it was that things weren't over until you can't hit the other guy. Whether that meant you were on the canvas or he was, the fists couldn't stop. In the swirl of toxins and heavy panting, he imagined that it was Franco he was hitting. It was retribution for a black eye and payback for asking Caroline to marry him. Franco fell first but before Jay knew it, Chris Storm came from the shadows. He was a new bag and fell like a sack of trash out the third story window. He waited for Tessa to come out, checked behind his back even, but she never came. She was smarter than the rest and better than them too. Terror took one last tug of toxin and held it in for as long as he could. Before he knew it, he could feel his legs curl and his back strike the mats. He was looking at the ceiling beside Franco and Storm wondering why the birds flying over his head were making such graceful circles. In the remaining seconds before he forgot everything, he could remember a voice calling him. They sounded concerned. --- "Tell you what. I'll answer yours if you answer mine," Jay said through a cigarette. The one to answer was a petite figure. She wore typical interview attire in that the skirt was enough of a tease to draw answers. "That's not how this works, Jay." "Maybe not with Kevin Jacobs, no. You though," he exhaled slowly. "You're a fine wine that'd I'd rather enjoy this with." "How sweet. Still, not enough to bring me home to mom. Let’s talk about High Stakes." Jay smirked, "You know, mom makes a mean tuna noodle casserole too. It'd be a shame if you went living your life without ever getting to taste that secret ingredient." "My mom used to force tuna on me. I'm afraid I'll be able to sleep tonight without having tasted the ranch dressing she adds to give it some flair." He maintained his smirk and swirled a glass of whiskey around the neck. Jay watched the whirlpool for a second, finding entertainment wherever he could, before looking into Stacy's young eyes. She seemed fresh out of journalism school even if her job seemed a bit much for someone of those specifications. "High Stakes then? What do you want to know?" "What's it feel like? What's going through your mind?" "You know, I figured there's be more charm to these questions." "Charm?" "They seem. Already written I guess." She shut her eyes feeling the pressure of the interview. She knew it wouldn't be easy to dig into Jay Terror, but her professors taught her that those stories would be the ones that made her famous. "They're standard. Sure. They still mean something though." "I feel stupid. I feel annoyed. I don't know. It's one of those things where you sit back when you're older and say, why was I so worked up over this?" "That's a terrible answer." "To a terrible question, might I add," he added. She shook her head and took a sip from his daiquiri. It wasn't typical for her to drink on the job, but she felt it couldn't hurt getting onto his level. It was another trick she'd learned on campus. "Then let's play on stupid. What's so stupid about fighting for a world title?" "The fact that I have to fight for it. The fact that a girl is holding onto it. The fact that people are willing to watch a massacre. More?" "You're still bouncing around the question." "Because it's my turn to ask." She sighed and gave in to the pressure. "OK. Ask me something." "Why the hell would you write for a wrestling magazine?" He let the question sit as he took a drag from his cigarette. "It just doesn't seem like your world." "It's a job." "So is porn." "Maybe I think a bit more of myself then them." "Still doesn't answer anything though," he reminded. "And you never answered me." She paused to regain her composure. "Fine. It was my brother. He always wanted to be a wrestler when he was younger. I figured I'd follow the dream somehow for him." Jay took down a heavy swallow of whiskey. It wasn't the answer he was expecting. "He ever make it?" "I think it's my turn to ask a question." "Fair enough." --- Sitting in the audience, Jay Terror felt out of place. The average age in the room had to sit around sixty-five and none of them wore a bandana around their neck. In fact, the majority of the people managed to find clean pressed suits for the occasion. Jay on the other hand dug out a pair of khakis he'd been hiding in the back corner of his drawer and a wrinkled white tee-shirt. In passing, he'd overheard some mentioning respect, but he didn't interrupt them to discuss the topic further. Instead, he listened to the speaker currently on the podium. She introduced herself as Jeff Foster's daughter. Jeff Foster, as was told, was a member of the Concord facility who revolutionized entertainment. He was a kind hearted man that, when behind closed doors, found himself helping the rest of the roster grow and advance. In his time, he'd managed to win various titles and was considered to be one of the best home-grown talents they had. This of course was through the eyes of his daughter. From stories that Jay's father had told, Terror understood that Foster was a swindling fool. He was a drunk and helped people with pain medication since he typically held the bulk of it. He was a regular guy who stumbled onto success and subsequently died of an overdose. This, although through the eyes of his father, was the story Jay felt more comfortable with. From him father, he learned that there was no such thing as good and bad in the business. Everyone was bad in their own way; it just depended on the degree they took it. The daughter left the podium and was replaced with a suited man. His hair was crooked and his face was heavy. "With that, it it our great honor to induct Jeff "Fearless" Foster into the Concord Wrestling Association hall of fame. With his generosity and his flash in the ring, I'm sure no one will be able to forget what he did for us in this state." Terror looked around at the people again. Some were dozing while others seemed proud to be there. Some were already in the hall of fame, others were next in line. Still, what got Jay the most was the fact that people cared. It was an odd idea for him to grasp at times. It seemed that wrestling was merely a trend, a passing fad that fell off when someone retired. To him, there was no bigger case than LeStatt Knight. He watched him rise, lived the legend, and learned from him. As time moved on though, Knight wouldn't be remembered. He'd be some name in some book that did some things. It was the same with anyone else who'd ever retired in the business. Nonetheless, Terror turned his attention back to the stage. "And without further adieu, I introduce to you all, Jamison Talley!" It was Jay's name and his time to speak. As he stood, he could remember the letter that had showed up in his mailbox. The seal alone gave him enough information. After reading that his father was going to be inducted into the hall of fame, he wasn't sure whether to gloat or reject the invitation. He loved his father, but he agreed that a legacy was born of memories, not reminders. He took his steps onto the stage and found the podium. The man with the crooked hair shook his hand and fed Terror to the dogs as the applause slowly died down. --- "How does it feel knowing that Tessa Windsor has the better record over you?" Terror tongued an ice cube and sucked it into oblivion. "You don't do your research, do you?" Stacy took another sip from his drink. "I've done enough. Besides, maybe your answers have changed since the black out." "The blackout didn't count as a win or loss. All it did was reignite the flame. You know what it's like to take a drag from a cigarette after a long day? One of those days where you couldn't find two seconds to light one up? It was like that. I got a taste of ecstasy, a taste of my defining fight. When it comes down to it, this fight and this war will define both of us. Records don't matter as much as who wins the next installment if you ask me." "Look at you using your words." Jay lit another cigarette. "So your brother never became a wrestler. Did he?" "Is this your question?" "Yes. Yes it is." She took another drink, an obvious sign of how touchy the subject was. Jay looked intently though, showing no sign of backing down. "He never made it. Died in a car accident before he could graduate high school," she said with fierce finality. She took another drink. "What will this title mean to you?" Jay never said sorry. He only smoked his cigarette and admired her open top button. "It'll mean that I did what I set out to do. That's about it." "You have to have more." "That's just what it is though. I joined Steven Caldera a year ago and told him I'd succeed. I told him that I'd win the world title and show him and everyone else that I'm the greatest in the game today. So far, I haven't done that. Beating Tessa Windsor means that not only do I take another battle. I take the world's favorite champion and the breath from their lungs. It's uplifting I guess." "And you'd like knowing that you broke the spirit of a country?" "My turn, Stacy." He took a drink from his glass of whisky again and set it back onto the table. "Why not just become a wrestler?" "Me? Look at me?" He did. "You've obviously seen Tessa. You're no bigger than she is. Plus, women's wrestling is all about eye-candy these days." "You're a pig." "Just answer the question then." She finished the end of her drink and looked around for a waiter. None was nearby. "My dad told me that wrestling wasn't for me. Told him that it wasn't for him too. I guess he just didn't like it." "That before or after his death?" "If you lose, what's next for you?" He laughed slightly and pulled the tobacco from his cigarette again. "Good question." --- Terror tapped the microphone with his index finger to check for sound as he pulled loose a script. In essence, it was merely a list of points and random words. Still, he used it to keep his pace. "Hello," he started. "I guess we're all here to remember some of those big guys in Speedos, huh?" The joke didn't land as he had expected. "Well, my dad wasn't one of them. I remember this one night when he came back late. It was after a show and he had obviously lost some blood. I was still awake watching the TV in the living room when I heard him drop his bag on the hardwood. Back then, it was exciting to see him come back. I remember running to him and hugging his leg. He rubbed my head and messed up my hair before lifting me up. He said to me that one day I'd be coming home to my son doing the same thing. He told me that I'd be famous and have a family, and that there was nothing better. Today, I took care of the fame. The family though, I'm still working on. That's not the point though. Frank Talley believed in two things. One was that a life on the road was the greatest thing a person could do. The other was that all was possible through family. My father was a kind man and he is the reason why I'm proud to be standing here tonight." Jay paused and looked down at the script. There were words he thought would sound right but in the moment, he changed his mind. He looked back up again and scanned the crowd. "I remember recently I was at a show in Arizona. I was facing a larger man much like my father had in his time. As most know, he wasn't the biggest guy on the roster. That said, I felt like I was following in his footsteps more than ever before. I felt like if he was watching, he'd be proud of what I'd accomplished so far." He stopped again, feeling as if he was rambling about himself too much. "What I'm trying to say is that Jack Talley was more than Concord. He was a father, he was a good father, and he will always be more than just a wrestler. No matter how much blood he lost in a night, he always had the strength to come home and lift me up into his arms. He always had the voice to tell me about my future. That's who Jack Talley is to me." Jay blinked a few times and tucked the speech back into his pocket. It was short, scattered, and liklely one of the worst that Concord had ever seen. Still, none of that mattered as he made his way back to his seat. Some congratulated him for his father, some told him his speech was touching. He hardly listened to any of them honestly. "And thank you, Jamison. With that, we welcome Jack Talley into the Concord Wrestling Association hall of fame." --- "I guess I'll have to find a new job," Jay answered sarcastically. Stacy had managed to get a new drink and took her first sip. "You know the drill, Jay." "It's just. Where are the hitting questions about my past? What about the speech I gave for my father? What about anything else? This all seems so unoriginal and beat to death." "It's what the readers want. Where do you go from a loss, Jay? The people want to know." He inhaled the cigarette smoke and shut his eyes for a moment. "I don't think I'm going to lose." He opened his eyes. "I've been isolating myself, training harder than ever before. I'm just at this point where I could fight while asleep. I don't sleep even. You know?" "Sure. But a loss is possible." "If I lose, I'll likely do the same thing I've done before. I'll confront Tessa, shove my fist down her throat, and beat the children out of her until I get another title shot. I made a promise that I'd win that belt and I need to do that at least once." "At least once?" "So what did your father have against wrestling?" "You're still playing that angle?" He smirked again in his sly and cunning way. "I'm just trying to learn about you. Find out why you'd take a job like this." She took a drink from the daiquiri. "My brother. He died on his way to a wrestling show." "That's it? Why did he hate it beforehand?" "He thought he'd die doing it in the first place. We lost our mom early in life and he didn't want to lose anyone else." She paused to think back. The flashback was almost visible for Jay. Snapping out of it, she flicked her brows. "Did your father die of wrestling?" --- After the ceremony ended, eight former wrestlers became official hall of fame members. Of them, four were able to attend. Three had died and another was hospitalized earlier in the week for a failed liver. Finally able to escape, Terror took a turn outside where he lit a cigarette. To his surprise, one of the inductees had already beaten him to the reprieve. "Kevin, right? Heavenly Kevin?" "Tell you what. I haven't heard people call me that until tonight. It's almost a weird experience." "Not the worst name I've heard though," Terror cited. "No, certainly not," replied Kevin. "Good speech for your father up there tonight. I'm sure he would have appreciated it." Terror spit on the concrete. "Probably not. I fumbled it and talked more about myself than him." "He talked about himself to." "Sure. Plenty." "Just saying. You reminded me of him up there. It was a cool moment for the people that knew him." "Thanks." They each dragged on their cigarettes and let the silence linger between them for a moment. Terror initiated the conversation again. "You miss him?" "When I think of Concord, I think of him. So, yeah. He was a good guy. Had his stumbles but everyone does. You know?" "Yeah. Sure." "You must have been thinking about him a lot this week." "A bunch. Sometimes I find it's weird that he's gone. Feels like yesterday that we were working in the basement on fundamentals." "It's those moments that you have to remember. They're the ones that'll last." "I guess so. Just wish he could have seen where I got to. Seen me hold a title." "Just win one. I believe in life past death and of all people, Frank Talley is surely watching where you go in this business." "Heh. You got a point." They both finished their cigarettes between small talk before opening the door and entering back inside. Jay thanked him for the words, shook his hand, and walked out of the induction hall wearing a smile. --- "He loved wrestling. He did wrestling until he couldn't. It was the steroids though. It was the pain killers. It wasn't the slams or the chair shots. Just the politics backstage." "I'm sorry," she offered. Her speech was beginning to slur. For what started as an intrview, Jay felt it went more like a date. He finished his glass of whisky. "I'm gonna' go. You should too. I got a match to get ready for." "Underneath all that rubbish you wear around your neck and on your arms, I kinda' figured there was some sense in you." "It's somewhere." Jay helped her out of the booth and walked her to her car. It took her two tries to turn the ignition on and Jay stopped her before the door could shut. Instead, he called a cab and waited for it. Watching her drive away, he had a cigarette hanging between his lips. He hated interviews and the people who conducted them, but something about Stacy gave him confidence. He slid into the back of his limousine giving serious thought to the business and the road left to be travelled. --- Terror woke up in a hospital bed with his head taped together and an IV in his arm. At his side was James Bryan. Blinking a few times, Jay mouthed something that didn't come out. Instead, Bryan spoke up. "You were training too hard Jay. No water, a whole boat load of nicotine, and traces of HGH were found in your blood stream. I don't know what you were trying to prove, but you might not be fit for High Stakes." Jay shut his eyes and coughed. "I needed help. Something. Anything. Just needed to get over the hump and perform." "You're lucky you woke up." "I know." "There's a drug policy at GCW, Jay. You should know about it more than anyone with what you father went through." "It's just. He made it to the hall. He did everything that I always wanted to. I just thought." "Your dad made it because he worked as hard as he could. He may have done some things that people look down on, but he didn't leave it at that." "I just -" "You're lucky. Leave it at that." James Bryan looked at his watch and then at Jay. "High Stakes is in two days. I need you to rest until then. This is the match you've been waiting for and I don't want to see you blow it." "I can do that." "Good." "Thank you, James." "Just rest. Take this as the storm clouds clearing and a new day beginning." James Bryan left with that and Jay shut his eyes. For his father's sake, he had a title to win and a promise to fulfill.
