Lifesblood for the Downtrodden, Part 5: Without You

15 December 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 15th December "But boys will be boys, and girls have those eyes, that’ll cut you to ribbons sometimes?" ? The Gaslight Anthem. I A foul mood clouded his psyche, hanging over his head like a grave spirit. Sitting at the breakfast table shovelling spoonfuls of Cheerios down his throat, Andy Murray felt nothing like the international wrestling superstar that 2008 had turned him into. And Kayla Jarosz knew it. She looked up from her mug of coffee, staring deeply at her other half. Head hung low, focused entirely on his breakfast and nothing else, this was not the charismatic extrovert she had come to know and love. Baby blue eyes that once sparkled with an unbridled enthusiasm had been replaced by cold, grey orbs of blankness and she hadn’t seen him smile for days on end. They hadn’t said a word to each other all morning. An awkward silence, to say the very least. Not exactly what you’d expect from a young couple who’d hardly seen each other for the past couple of weeks. Something was wrong, definitely. And oh, how it worried her. Ever since she had met the man GCW fans know as The Scottish King of Cool, he’d always seen like the chirpiest man in the world. Even with LeStatt Knight’s claws dug into his brain last month, Andy Murray had never sunk like this. "So, uh. How are Cayle and Sid doing over in Japan, then?" she’d been thinking up an ice-breaker for about sixty seconds and that was the best she could come up with. "Hmm." Not even a proper word; just a noise and a shrug. That’s all he could manage? Kayla took another mouthful of milky coffee into her mouth and put the empty cup down on the table with more force than she had intended. That was it; he’d been blanking her all morning. She’d had enough. "Andy, what’s going on? You’ve hardly said a word all week?" This seemed to perk the Scotsman’s interest, at least momentarily. He glanced upwards briefly. "Nothing. I’m fine." "No you’re not," she began with a little trepidation, "you’ve been mulling around the house like a ghost ever since you got back. I’ve been with you long enough now to know that this isn’t you, Andy." Again he looked up. "Come again?" was his uninterested retort. She sighed. It was beginning to get more than a little bit frustrating. Kayla leaned forward, elbows on the table, glaring at the Scot with the warm, hazel eyes he’d found solace in so many times before. But this time? Nothing. Immediately Andy’s gaze shifted downwards as he continued to fill his mouth with cereal as if it were some kind of mechanical process. Kayla couldn’t wrap her head around it. Here was a man who, career-wise, couldn’t have done much more over the past twelve months. Chris Bagwell. Zacharia Taylor. Bryan Mayhem. Vivica J. Valentine. LeStatt Knight. Great wrestlers, all of them. Champions. Hall of Famers. All had fallen at the hand of the Scottish King. He was so close to greatness that he could practically reach out and touch it, and if 2009 was to be anything like 2008 then a World Title reign would surely not be too far away in Andy Murray’s future. His popularity with the fans was at an all-time high and merchandise sales were going through the roof? ? what could such an outrageously successful human being possibly have to mope about? All right, recent battles with Jay Terror and The Establishment may have left him with some anger and ire, but Terror was nothing to Andy. Even Kayla knew that. If he could put down a psychological nightmare like LeStatt Knight, he could surely wipe the floor with a scrawny little leech like Jay Terror. "Andy, talk to me. I-?" she tried to continue, but the words trailed off. She was lost. "What’s there to talk about?" What’s there to talk about? So many things were wrong with that statement that she didn’t even bother considering them. A frown worked its way across her features. If only he could hear how cold he sounded, how distant. "What isn’t there to talk about?" she snapped. Andy looked up once again, surprised by her tone. "Look, whatever you’re doing to yourself, you need to stop. I can’t go on like this for much longer, it’s killing me?" Still not even the faintest hint of sympathy behind those eyes. He parted his lips, as if to respond, but nothing came out. Kayla folded her arms, her frown deepening. In her mind she tried to pinpoint when the last time she was actually able to hold a conversation with her boyfriend was? ? it didn’t take her long to work it out. The night before the trial ? well, what was supposed to be the trial. "It’s something to do with Jane, isn’t it?" "What the fuck do you know about Jane?" Andy suddenly barked, flashing her a look normally reserved for his fiercest on-screen rivals. The mere mention of his ex-wife’s name seemed to ignite a fire behind those cold, dread eyes, but not the kind of fire that Kayla had intended. "The tria-" He didn’t let her finish. "And don’t even mention the “trial” either. I’m through with that, it’s over. Done. Don’t fucking mention it again." Suddenly Andy stood up, pushing his chair backwards with such force that it fell to the tiled floor behind him. He stormed across towards the open plan kitchen and threw his dishes down into the sink. Kayla was shell-shocked. She dared not speak for fear of what her partner’s response may be. "I’m taking Marvin to his mother’s. Call me when you’ve got something worthwhile to say." He disappeared upstairs, calling for his son. Kayla didn’t notice. Such was her surprise that she was almost dazed. But she had an answer, at least. Jane Murray. Always the root of the problem. Ever since Kayla had known Andy, she’d recognised Jane to be nothing more than a cancerous presence. Mother of Andy’s child or not, she’d tried to bring the man’s career to ruins. Along with Chris Bagwell and an army of sleazy reporters, she’d tried to end the man’s livelihood. Which was exactly why she couldn’t understand why Andy dropped the lawsuit. He was going to sue her; take everything that she had. It was supposed to be punishment for what she had tried to do to Andy’s career, and yet Andy had pulled out literally at the 11th hour. Kayla knew how conflicted Andy felt about picking at the bones of his son’s legal guardian, but it was nothing more than she deserved. "Bye," she attempted to call as Andy and son came down the stairs, walking with great haste towards the front door. Marvin managed to squeeze out a quick "goodbye" as Andy slammed the door hard behind him. Kayla stood up, shaken. It was the first major argument she’d had with Andy since they’d been together. She didn’t like the reaction that mentioning the Jane situation had brought out of Andy; it would be enough to make a lesser woman suspect that something was going on between the two of them. She dismissed this thought immediately. Andy Murray may have been in one hell of a grumpy patch, but he was no adulterer. II "What was that all about, back at the house?" Marvin Murray asked his father as Andy killed the engine of the Mercedes Benz with a flick of the ignition. Andy turned and sent his seatbelt flying across the surface of his Motley Crue tee shirt with a quick push of the release button. "Oh, nothing," he said, muted. Quickly and nervously he shifted his glance away from his son. "Just a little disagreement is all. Don’t worry about it, little man." Marvin accepted his father’s explanation with a nod, but it didn’t answer all his questions. Even his ten year old mind could see that something was going on in Andy Murray’s head that he didn’t want the world knowing about. "Do we have to do this? I don’t really wanna go back in there," Marvin said as he pointed out of the window towards Jane’s modest single-floor home. Andy couldn’t blame him. Having been holed-up in the GCW wrestler’s utopian suburban retreat since Jane decided it would be a good idea to swallow a bunch of pills with a bottle of vodka, coming back to this ramshackle residence must’ve been quite a comedown for the kid. "Come on now, it’s only for a night or two. Besides, I bet Mom is really looking forward to seeing you." Marvin folded his arms, unconvinced. "Yeah right?" A sigh escaped the lips of Andy. He himself hadn’t exactly had many kind words for his ex-wife over the past twelve months, but it troubled him deeply to witness his son’s growing dissatisfaction with his own mother. "Look, Marv, I know she’d had her troubles. But she’s still your Mom, and she still loves you very much." Andy leaned towards his son, placing a hand on his shoulder. "I know she hasn’t exactly shown it recently, but, for this weekend, forget about all the stupid stuff she’s done. She’s had a rough time. She needs you just now, more than ever, son." And that was all that Marvin Murray needed. "Okay, Dad," he nodded, which was enough to bring the first smile to Andy’s lips in weeks. The Scottish King pushed his door open and slid out of the Benz. Marvin did the same, pulling out a rucksack with him. Andy looked up towards the house as Marvin scurried up the steps. There she was, standing on the porch, door open behind her. Waiting for them. "There’s my little man!" Jane beamed as an embarrassed Marvin scuttled towards her. "C’mere you!" She knelt down and ruffled his hair, kissing him on the forehead. "Hey, mom," was all he could manage as a response. He never was one for such public displays of affection, even at his young age, so he felt even more uncomfortable when she pulled back and looked him dead in the eye. "Ohhhh I’ve missed you!" But she couldn’t hold her gaze for too long. He caught her eye like a piece of silver attracts a magpie. Leaning against the black Benz, mirrored Aviators covering his eyes, Andy Murray watched on, expressionless. Strange, she thought. Andy usually made himself scarce at times like these, dropping Marvin off and leaving him to head back inside of his own accord. But today he stayed. "Why don’t you run inside for a moment, Marvin?" Marvin was only too happy to oblige, disappearing into the house as Jane rose to her feet. Her eyes met with Andy’s and he shifted his vision to the dirt beneath his boots. He wasn’t the only person feeling more than a little bit uncomfortable at the moment. "Hey?" Jane’s voice drifted across the distance between the two of them. Andy looked up. "Hey." The word hung in the air. Lingering for longer than either of them would’ve wanted. Jane smiled but it was gone before it had fully developed. It was the first time that she had seen Andy since he’d dropped the case and she wasn’t sure what to do. If he had con ahead with the case, she would’ve been ruined; her already perilous financial situation would’ve hit catastrophic new lows to the point where she’d no longer have been able to provide for her and her son. He would have ruined her, pure and simple. But he didn’t. That was what mattered. For some reason, Andy Murray had dropped the charges and was standing before her now of his own will. After all that she had done, surely he hadn’t forgiven her? ? had he? There was one way to find out. "You, uh. You want a cup of tea?" She wasn’t sure what she was doing, but it felt right. "Oh I dun-" "Come on. I’ve just put the kettle on." No. No. NO. Andy looked towards the ground, then upwards. Smiled. "Why the hell not." He pushed his giant frame away from the Mercedes and hopped up onto the porch. They shared a glance, quick and brief. Awkward. Real. III</font This was odd. Very fucking odd. Little over a week ago and Andy Murray was about to sue this woman for all that she was worth. He was going to drive a stake through Jane’s heart, destroy her. Take her money, her home, her possessions. Her custody of their son. For months they’d been at war. Ever since the dissolution of their marriage, there’d been mudslinging between the two of them and resentment to the point where Jane found herself on GCW television, using Andy’s career as a new medium to get her own back on a man who she’d grown to loathe. ? and here they are, now. Sitting over the kitchen table, exchanging polite chit-chat over a pot of tea. If Andy had the time to slow things down and ponder the ridiculousness of the situation, he’d probably leave right there and then. After they’d gotten over the initial awkwardness, the conversation was flowing. No uncomfortable silences, and almost no striking of nerves. Nothing like Andy and Kayla earlier in the day. They exchanged niceties and tiny compliments with no real substance like a pair of people who’d only just met; she smiled at his attempts to be funny, and he listened intently to every word that came out of her mouth. At war they may have been, but they were childhood sweethearts, and Andy felt like he was eighteen again. And she looked good, too. Well, not as mind-blowing as she had when they had first met, but she was looking a damned sight healthier now than she had been for a long time. It was easy to see that the disintegration of the case had brought her back to life. "So, uhhh. I guess I should say “thanks”," Jane eventually said. It was inevitable that the conversation would take a slightly more meaningful route. "For dropping the suit. It would have ended me?" Shamed, Jane hung her head momentarily. Andy pondered, carefully picking the word out. "Don’t worry about it." "But after what I did. Well, I deserved it. I tried to pull you under, and if you had went ahead with things? well, I could have understood why. I just let that prick get too close?" "Well, I’ve gotta admit, Jane. What you did ? what you were going to do ? was shitty. There’s no doubt. But I would’ve recovered, I would’ve bounced back. I see that now. I’m not a monster; I’m not going to be responsible for the mother of my child to be tossed out onto the streets. And it’s not like I need the money?" A warmth rose beneath Jane’s chest. Even though she couldn’t believe how hospitable her former spouse was being, she immediately felt that a giant anchor had been removed from around her neck. She cracked a smile. Mouth the words "thank you," and Andy nodded. "So how’s the wrestling treating you? Seems like I can’t open a newspaper without reading about the latest Andy Murray success story?" Andy was glad of the subject change. While it felt good to clear the air, he was still deeply ashamed of the fact that he’d even contemplated the lawsuit in the first place and didn’t want to be reminded of it, as Kayla had found out to her peril earlier in the day. "Well, seems like everyone and their aunt wants to kick my ass these days, so I must be doing something right," he smiled. "And it’s given me the lifestyle of a prince, so “spose I can’t complain." "Uh-huh," Jane leaned forward, her head cradled on the palm of her hand. "And how’s home life? How’s? Carla, is it?" "Kayla. And yeah, it’s, uhhh. It’s okay." Jane picked up on the drop in his tone immediately. His gaze fluttered and his voice wavered. She’d known him for fourteen years; there was no way she wasn’t going to pick up on the signs. "Is it? Really?" she asked, her voice warm and understanding. "She just? I dunno." Conflict began to rage inside his skull, no doubt multiplied by the fact that he was sitting just inches from a woman he had devoted many years of his life to. "I don’t think she understands sometimes. I don’t think she likes the fact that I dropped the case..." "Oh," Jane cleared her throat. "? but she’s going to have to live with that. Look, Jane," gently he placed his hand on top of hers. "We’ve been through some shitstorms recently, and I don’t wanna fight no more. I’m tired, I’m getting old, and for my own sake, I can’t go on much longer holding this grudge." She looked up, eyelids trembling. The moment. Inescapable. She wrapped her fingers around his palm, gripping tightly. The leaning-in was practically instinctive. The meeting of the lips, so na- No. No no NO. Andy Murray recoiled, leapt up from his seat and backed off. What the hell had he done? Jane wiped her mouth, holding the back of her hand up against he lips. Andy was sweating bullets, pressing his hands into his skull. "Shit! Oh shit." "Andy?" He turned, left the room. "Andy?" Jane heard him run through the hall, slamming the door behind him. The ducts in the corner of her eyes began to swell. What the hell had just happened? IV</font He’d barely been able to maintain focus as he drove back to the house, but he made it back in one piece. Almost. Gritting his teeth together and clenching a fist, Andy pushed his feet down into the pedals of the stationary vehicle and growled. He’d kissed her. Soon he leaned forward, head bowed into the steering wheel. Get yourself together, he thought, compose yourself. It was like a scene in a Hollywood blockbuster; the kiss, that moment. A forbidden love rekindled by a beautiful mistake. It was tearing him to pieces. He began to rue deciding to go inside Jane’s house; he should’ve stayed in the car and drove off, like he usually did. But noooo, he just had to "clear the air," didn’t he? "Stupid motherfucker," he growled, bashing his fist into the dashboard. But what is this happened for a reason? Maybe this was a goo- No. He wasn’t even going to entertain those thoughts. Twice he had married Jane and twice they had divorced; he wouldn’t allow himself to be drawn towards her light again. He couldn’t. But one thing was for sure; Kayla had to know about this. His conscience would never allow him to live in sin, he couldn’t lie to her like that. She had to know. With a heavy heart, Andy Murray left the car and looked up towards the front door of his Tallahassee retreat. This wasn’t going to be fun. V</font Andy Murray wasn’t the only person whose head was in turmoil. Kayla had had plenty of time to herself to think about what happened earlier on. She shouldn’t have antagonised him by bringing up the Jane situation; of course it was an open wound. She didn’t know a great deal about the woman and her history for Andy, but she knew enough to know that suing an ex-spouse isn’t something to be treated lightly. Sure, Andy’s reaction may have been a tad extreme, but she should never have went there in the first place. Their eyes met across the hall as Andy entered the house, and there was silence between the two. Nothing but heavy stare of two people so very in-tune with each other’s frame of mind. "Andy, I’m sorry," Kayla finally broke the deadlock. "I shouldn’t have brought that up-" "Shhhh," Andy said, bringing his finger to his lips as he stepped forward and hugged his other half. "Don’t be stupid. I know I’ve been acting a little strange recently, and I shouldn’t have taken it out on you. It’s okay. I’m the one who needs to apologise?" She took a deep breath, burying her head in his shirt. Finally she felt at ease. Her boyfriend had come around. The argument wasn’t pleasant in the slightest, but if that’s what it took to bring the Andy Murray she knew back to life, then it was worth every moment. If it was only so simple for the Scottish King. You’ve got to tell her, Andy. You must. "K?" She looked up. "Yeah?" God, the look in those eyes; it was enough to melt even the coldest of hearts. Andy swallowed. "I-?" he stuttered. No. He couldn't. "Nothing." He pulled her back in. She was in heaven; a warm, loving embrace from a man she would walk over hot coals for. He was in purgatory, arms wrapped around a woman he had effectively just cheated on. What a fine mess.
