An Ode to Debts and Spontaneity

11 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 11, 2005 Now, what do I want out of this? It's a hard question, honestly. Spent so long as a semi-automatic hate machine that thinking ahead became a thing of the past, but I get a hint that blind leaps aren't the best thing to take around forces of nature. Wait. Where better to take a blind leap? Can't figure that out right now, so it's on with the show. I look over at Trey and smirk, lighting a cigarette. Nowhere to go but up, right? He asks if I'm ready to kick it in, assuring me right on my earlier assumption. 'No time,' I tell him, and turn the door knob. 'Wait here.' It twists smoothly and the door pushes open without a hitch. In front of me is final, absolute proof of my sanity--rows upon rows upon rows of men and women wearing that same almost-toga as a uniform, each looking at a small box and writing with both hands at the same time. None looked up at my grand entrance, so I go back outside and shut the door behind me. 'Howah zit?' 'No time. Follow me.' I kick the door with all my force and it goes down slow, hits the ground without much of a noise at all. There's no stop in the rhythmic scraping of pen on paper, and I still didn't catch the attention of anyone. Trey's rapt, on the other hand, and stands in complete childlike awe at the world around him. The ceiling hangs much closer to us, and on either side of the room I notice stairways that twist up through holes in the floor of the level above us. I can picture them rising into infinity without too much of a stretch, or at least past the boundaries of this dimension. My stomach roars--empty--and I finally have a good regret. 'Wha now?' 'Without direct opposition, I'm finding it a little hard to smoothly cause a scene that'll disrupt their ongoing affairs. I've yet to be convinced that they even possess the cognizance to recognize us.' 'Huh?' 'Excuse me, sirs.' I turn and look up into the aqua blue eyes of a blonde girl that has me by an inch. I recognize her face and name from the tapestry, because I surely knew there was going to be a nametag when I looked down at her breasts. 'May I help you?' Trey opens his mouth and I slap him. 'Yeah. Just general inquiries and the like. Who would I see about that?' 'I can help you with all your Tallying related questions, yes.' She smiles with unreal lips and immaculate teeth. 'First, yeah, how exactly does this work?' 'Every tallier is assigned several people to mark for. They view the thoughts and actions of these people and tally them accordingly.' 'On good and bad?' 'Basically. We take into account the situations involved, of course, to guarantee a fair review.' 'So, who would my tallier be?' 'We greet a person with their corresponding agent.' 'You can read my thoughts?' She nods. I look at the ground and nod. 'Oh.' It just keeps getting worse. I give up on the formal aspect of the situation and, hey, Tulz, if you can read my mind, say 'okay.' 'Okay.' I'm sorry. 'No problem.' Okay, that was a lie. 'I know.' Is it cool if I just do this? 'If you'd like.' Alright. So, Tulz, what are you doing later on? 'Jordan.' Wait, can I take that as what I'm taking that as right now? She looks at me hard, and I think I know what she means when she says my name again. Could I at least see my karma debt? 'Right this way.' She leads me off, up a staircase and into a dark room, and I turn to see Trey being talked to by his far less attractive agent, or whatever. I still prefer tallier, as I can pretend it's some form of pirate. With that in mind, I read the list she pulls from a pouch on her side. Anyway you can tell me what this means? 'A review?' Sure. Start with that kidnapping thing in Mexico, and just hit the major events. 'You saved a clone of the Presid-' It was a clone? 'Yes, a clone of the President, which put you into the positive. After that came going into hiding after inciting a riot, which almost dropped you into the negative. Be glad no one died. While hiding from police as the Marauder, you lost all contact with your close friends, but kept the level about even by actually being a good person for once. That incident at Denny's in Louisiana gave you a nice boost.' The what in where? 'You tipped with a hundred and a five, then didn't really care when you found out.' Kick ass. Let's speed things up. Where am I at now? 'You're brainwashing and manipulating a man who had forgiven you for the first time you brainwashed him. You've also secured him a better life by doing so, and in turn maintain in the positive. Saving Danielle from Casey, and not trying to use her gets you a pretty decent boost, so we're in your debt.' Is there any way at all that I can trade that in for two hours with you? 'No.' One hour? 'No.' Thirty minutes? 'Stop.' Does this hurt my karma? 'Not particularly.' Ah. Fifteen minutes? 'Stop.' At this point, it's hard keeping my thoughts away from my talking. Ideas come from the back of my mind in tidal waves that I fight with blankets of music. I hum 'Spacegrass,' and then think about just how attractive she is and just how much of a bastard I really can be. It's that stupid bit of reality coming up that I can't fight whatsoever, and I know that she knows it now. So yeah, welcome that awkward moment that I always figured would happen if I went after Jean Grey. So, hey Tulz. Can I smoke in here? 'If you'd like.' She smiles at me again, and she knows both times I thought about it in detail as I light the cigarette. Their debt to me apparently consists of letting me wander headfirst into the most gauche moment of my life. And yeah, thanks for that word, Robot Chicken--I'll remember to never say it out loud. If I had a dictionary, I'd call it ironic. I’m too out of it right now to be sure if I'm right. 'It's ironic.' I'll have to look that up. Time's drying up, Tulz; what do we do? My plans don't really seem to be appropriate for the situation. 'Your friend should be ready soon.' I'm really disappointed. Isn't there some way I can get repaid for this whole karma thing? I'd like to get reparations for all my good deeds in one quick moment, instead of this maybe never I'm seeing. 'Not getting arrested for stealing that car-' Trey bought that car. '-which really did cover everything up. You're right, you're right. Sorry for the misunderstanding, I'll correct that.' About the repayment? 'I'll see what I can do, Jordan, if you'll follow me.' To kinky se- 'To the main area.' Grumble grumble. Follow her out, think about the lovely engravings on the walls and the intricate tiles inlaid to the stone floor. What an amazing painting that is! Right there. Glorious. 'I appreciate the effort, but you don't have to pretend.' Thanks. In return, I won't outright say 'nice ass.' 'You haven't said anything outright in some time.' Well, the first thing I do say won't be 'nice ass.' Deal? 'Sure.' Someday, I've got a bastard to thank for not making every chick I meet a telepath. There's nothing else to do now but completely give up on that attempt and move on with my life--completely shaken of the idea that I can change the way things are going to turn out. It's a waste of effort now, and I can't help but keep the smoking up. I create a rhythm, pipe, cigarette, pipe, that sees me half-stepping to a stop every time I need to get a good flame going. The hall's incredibly drafty, and yeah yeah, I think you're right about the irony, Tulz. So, where'd you get the name? 'I came into existence with it.' Blah. How old are you? '327 years.' Don't speak. 'Okay.' I mean it. I'm about to make a proposition to you and I don't want everyone around getting in on the idea. How often does karma actually work out? You can talk now. 'Rarely. Humans overlook it, misuse it, muddle up the whole affair.' Exactly. What say we kick this kooky joint and hunt down the guy that made you and me in the first place? We can get answers, we can make things work, and you can be youthful and foolish. You on your 327th year here? '203rd. I worked in public relations prior to this.' You just proved my point. I don't think I've really gotta convince you, if you've really got full access to what's going on in my brain at any point in time- 'And I do.' --nod a little more often. I don't know what kind of repercussions I could bring down for trying to pull this off, so yeah. Like I was saying, since you know my head, there's no point in trying to convince you. You've known what's been going on for awhile, so, gimme an answer. In nod form. She doesn't turn, and I stare at where her shoulder blades would be if they weren't covered by that garment I've finally declared a tobe--no, a roga. I imagine eyes, a nose, and a little mouth cheering with joy. Neither of us is getting any younger, and one of us seems to keep getting older, so have a little heart for man's concept of time. Her hair bobs up-down slightly, just enough to answer my question. Coming down the steps, I see Trey weeping his eyes out, and walk over to him. Tulz breaks away to talk to another rogaed figure, a guy with a stately beard that hangs half-way to the floor. They're all so goddamned tall, these bastards. 'Jordan, man, my life... man.' C'mon now, it'll be cool. Fuck. 'C'mon now, it'll be cool.' He's a babbling mess when Tulz comes back, whispering in my ear and rushing us out the door that’d already been repaired from earlier. I drag Trey, who seems to understand speed's necessity as he starts to kick and pedal along of his own accord. The three of us push the door open and run across the field, diving headlong into the tangled nest of branches and spines and creatures like shadows in the night. 'Follow me,' she says, and we do, further into the hellish woods. Vines lash at my face and forearms, and Tulz moves nimbly ahead of me. We hit a clearing--or a small circle where the flora doesn't have so much control--and Tulz stops for the first time in what seems like days to me now. My heart burns and sweat keeps my clothing stuck on numb muscles. I light a cigarette and try to think, and when I look back up, Tulz is a good five inches shorter than me and pulling at my backpack. 'Shoes.' 'Cool.' It's all I can say anymore. They weigh a ton, I think, but it's alright. Too asphyxiated to think. Can only run.
