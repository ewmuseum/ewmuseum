Coma Black

27 March 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3-27-09 Deep within the Elven soul lays the instinct for conquest. So embedded for the reasons upon the necessity to take action when all other means have failed, it was so created to avoid dominion by evil men. With the world in chaos and the Earth in fear of being destroyed, the Elves were created to protect the Earth, and humanity from destroying it along with themselves. But then, the Elves departed from their Earthly plane, and only a few of the Earth’s children remained; a faint glimmer of hope to save a dying planet. Because hope could not be placed squarely on the shoulders of the few still on the plane of existence known to all to live there, a second world was created. A carbon copy of the first, one where the way of life could be preserved shall the prophecy of the Elves fail, and man destroys the Earth. . Although intentions were pure, the darkness could not have been foreseen. With the Elven race being the only ones capable of traveling between the parallel universes, a utopia could not be created. That was the idea of heaven, and it was not to be. The spirits still remaining on Earth sought their voices to be heard. Those who had not ascended to their place of creation feared being kept silent forever, and were able to obtain simultaneous existence, along with the beings that unknowingly had carbon copies of themselves in another plane of reality. . In the other world, the spirits could be seen, heard, touched, and communicated with. However, remaining in their continuous pattern of death and haunting could not be escaped from. For instance, a child who was killed by an oncoming train could be seen by the human eye, but for every night through eternity would continue to relive being killed by the train. To any who traveled these universes without the ability to survive doing so, it would be a moment of delusion, for the human mind could not truly comprehend what it had seen after signing their death certificate by traveling to this alternate world. . This world was created to protect the Earth’s children, and one day it may do so. When the last of the Elvens have completed their Earthly missions, or when the unfortunate ending awaiting the planet ensues, the remaining Elves will forever be bound to the alternate reality. The humans in the alternate existence, unaware of their situation, would live, love, and die like the rest until it would be saved, or destroyed like the original world. . Sometimes a human believes they feel a presence, and occasionally, they see someone who isn’t there. Sometimes humans hear voices in their head, and while it sounds like their own, it is beyond their own thought comprehension to how it couldn’t be. Those humans have connections with their spiritual identicals, and occasionally their maps connect on a level powerful enough to transcend the mortal boundary. . When Emily DuPrae was on her deathbed, clutching the written word from the being she’d so loved with all her human heart, she was slowly drifting towards the eternity that would await her. What some experience as going towards the light, Emily, for reasons beyond her knowledge, was being sent to a world where she could one day reunite with her bereaved loved one. In another world, in another time, where the two had only encountered once, Emily would reach the soul of her counterpart and lie dormant until the day would arrive where her awakening would be pertinent. . The Emily DuPrae of the alternate world met her loved one once, a chance encounter from an unknown transition by the Elven being when he was under the delusion that it was all in his mind. That Emily was unaware of who the blue-haired presence was, but once he would return to this world, the true Emily would be resurrected in spirit, and the souls destined to spend eternity together would finally cross in a world without the torment and tribulation both had experienced in their lifetimes. . Chances, learning she was no longer Devon, knew this all too well, long before it was even going to happen. Unlike anyone else in that plane of existence, however, she had to earn her spot there to avoid the desolate sea of nothingness that would’ve otherwise awaited her. Being unknowingly lost in the Seven Gates of Hell, repeating the murderous rampage of an asylum massacre, is beyond any fire and brimstone conjured by the darkest and most fearsome of men. . Charging into the abyss of the Seven Gates of Hell, her objective only being realized when she would achieve it, the lost souls of the inmates swarmed her. Given the weapon of a soul reaver, something that would come in handy for her Elven friend a few years down the line, she could only see red and swiped away at anything within striking distance. . “Oh, this is new. First psycho dead elves and now you guys again. Fuck me sideways next time. I'm sure it'll be more fun for the lot of us,” she sarcastically muttered as the screaming of souls of destruction cried out around her. One attempted to disturb her from her mission with a knife, and she kicked it out of his hand without batting an eyelash. “No, no, no! You're doing it wrong. You do it this way, idiot,” she laughed before easing his pain. . Finally reaching door 714, she checked her surroundings to assure of her solitude. As she grasped the door handle, one more foolish soul attempted to thwart her by charging violently with a bloody stretcher. She shrugged, “that’s not the way you did it last time,” and kicked the front wheel out, sending the unaware specimen plummeting through the floor and out of her way. “You know, I should bring some of you with me, might help the cause... Nah, you'll take away all my enjoyment of squashing those insects.” . Breaking down door 714, Dr. Virgill awaited her inside. The door shut behind her, and her eyes glowed. Suddenly, it was fourteen years prior, and she was being accosted for her self-defense against her ill-intentioned father. She swung her blade of death and disposed of the mirage of Dr. Virgill. All around her, dark thoughts from her past were brought to life, spinning around her before she could comprehend everything she was seeing. . “Devon Drake is a menace to society, and should be locked away from human contact,” one of the images from her past reflected. . “She deserves all the blood on her hands to be repaid with her own,” another, sounding like her mother, added to the spinning circle of memory. . She closed her eyes before they again turned a shade of red, and she screamed out loud as she destroyed every last thought that had plagued her mind before she’d been plucked for a mission gone wrong in an alternate reality. Finally, all that lay before her was an image of a blue-haired being, fair and wise. Coveting the life of a young girl, easing the thoughts of the assured destruction she was sure awaited the meeting between the two. At last, her rage subsided, and she realized her mission had not been completely in vain. Somehow, the Devon Drake of the world in which she’d lost her life would know of this fair being’s heart, his intent, and his long-awaited destiny given to him by the planet that created him. . Meanwhile, it would be seven long years before she’d have the pleasure of removing his na’ve head from the clouds and sending him back to what he believed as reality. Keeping her mouth shut about everything would be the toughest part of that stage of destiny, but in the end, she would live without fear of incarceration or death. The world would be her oyster, and she had many years before she had any obligation or consequence to anything whatsoever. . Sitting outside a maximum-security prison, she could only recall the definition she’d been plagued with since she’d been old enough to speak. “Psychopath: A person with an antisocial personality disorder, manifested in aggressive, perverted, criminal, or amoral behavior without empathy or remorse. Yeah, I totally fit that, but, I'm not dead, and you are... Well, will be soon. Have a nice day!” . In a world without consequence, it was ripe for the picking for the damned, the condemned, and the swift finger of justice. . “. The urge to make one suffer, for some reason, led to a smile creeping along my bleeding lips. For some reason, I’d taken under consideration that everything that had happened was a direct result of wrath upon me, that I’d deserved the unjust treatment I’d incurred for some misdeed long in the past, or some kind of curse placed upon me years ago that had constantly decided to become apparent at the worst of times. What the hell was I thinking? Could I honestly look at what had happened, what I had done, and what I had tried to do? Could I look myself in the eye, after all the effort put forth, and say that I deserved what had happened? Would anyone deserve to be treated in such a manner? No! It wasn’t right, it wasn’t just. I was tired of being under the raincloud of delusion that led me to believe that what she had done to me was a result of me not being good enough or not trying hard enough. Nothing could excuse her lies, her deceit, and her ultimate betrayal! Nothing! I wasn’t taking personal responsibility anymore! I felt a fire burning within me that hadn’t existed in years. Glancing in the mirror, I could see a trace of green building around my iris, and my smile grew a bit wider. The part of me that I had tried to shun, to bury, to hide, was finally arising again when I needed it the most. “Pleasure to see you again, Eldarin,” I remarked as my eyes began to fully change to the eerie lime green that indicated my pure Elven side, the vengeful spirit that lurked within me and taught me who I really was, made himself apparent. In the mirror, I saw Eldarin begin to respond to me. “I knew you would come back to me sooner or later,” he snickered. “I admit it, you were right,” I growled as I glanced down, unable to look him directly in the eyes as I confessed this fault. “I should’ve listened.” “The one true good thing about being Elven, being immortal, is timelessness. The curse of being Elven, being immortal, is timelessness. Do you understand, Lantlas?” Eldarin inquired. “No, I don’t follow,” I replied. “Being given an endless timeline gives you what humans do not have” The ability to experience things on a bigger scale, and if they don’t work out in your favor, not be any worse off because time has passed. When a human man falls in love with a wretched wench and loses everything because of her, he suffers consequences in addition to the loss of precious time on this planet. You, who will have centuries in front of you should you choose to keep pursuing your ultimate purpose, are not limited by precious time and simple aging,” Eldarin explained. “And the curse?” I asked. “The curse is that you’re left with the heartache that humans can easily pass. With the Elven emotional vulnerability, you are unfortunately stuck with the memories, the nightmares, and the searing pain that envelops your soul every time even the slightest moment captures that essence that you once felt. You’ve been through it before, just not on this grand of a scale.” “I have?” “Yes,” Eldarin reaffirmed, “there was Devon, which brought out the fury of the warrior. There was the Neko, who buried herself deep within your consciousness and dominated it for several months. There was Jade, who simply passed away and broke your heart in the process. However, this is different. Not only is this a betrayal on the level of Devon and Neko combined, this is the destruction of a sacrament you held dear. Into this, you put commitment, love, and perhaps most important, hope. The cataclysmic destruction of the hope you felt is most likely the hardest part of this process.” “I still don’t understand,” I responded. “Why is the destruction of hope the most painful part of this?” “Simple, Lantlas,” Eldarin retorted, “you’ve never hoped for something this much before. You believed that when you committed your life to this human female, that the tribulation and anguish of heartbreak was finally over, that for the rest of her natural life, you would have someone who loves you as much as you loved her. You instilled that hope within yourself because that’s what the sacrament is supposed to be; a fairy tale ending to a lifelong journey of wrong decisions and heartbreak, but unfortunately, you didn’t pick the right one.” “Obviously, I didn’t,” I agreed. “The human heart is capable of such sadistic behavior, hence why half of their marriages don’t work. You’re not exempt from this as an Elven trying to marry a human. You’re as subject to the pain of a failed marriage as much as any human man, only the consequences in your case are different as I previously described,” Eldarin continued. “So what can I do, Eldarin” I’m at my wit’s end. I’m not sure where my path is taking me, and I feel lost, even in this profession.” “Focus your energy on the tasks at hand. Take your concentration away from the betrayals of the past, and focus all of that pain built up within your heart on your enemies. Bring out the warrior, fight like you used to, and no one will be able to stop you,” he proclaimed. He was right. While the ultimate result may be unfortunate for those on the other side of me, it was no longer about that. In order to survive, this needed to be dealt with, and I was in a position where I could fight other men without fear of repercussions or imprisonment. Those against me this coming week, I hadn’t the faintest idea who they were or what they had done, but in my mind, they were all male versions of her and had done to me exactly what she had. I soon found myself back in the forest, a stream rolling beside me and the green hills untouched by man. Except this time, taking in the familiar location of what I believed to be Cathdesalantis, one thing stood out. Something that hadn’t been there before in my previous visions through my own mind or Eldarin’s. In the midst of the honeysuckles, a stone and brick dwelling permeated the horizon. Falling just on the other side of the river that divided the territories, I’d never once remembered seeing this house. What about Wormwood’s eyes led me to this vision, I wasn’t sure, but stirring of life began to startle me out of my observation. A horse riding off in the distance required my attention, as curiosity always got the best of me. As I sped off, following the galloping sound, lifeless bodies lay all around me. Burned beyond recognition, I could only assume this had been the work of humans. Knowing where I was also indicated to me that these were my fellow brethren, fallen in vain to human greed and stupidity. When I finally caught up with the source of the riding horse, I’d descended upon a human settlement. One man rode in the town, mounted on the horse, with the blood of the slain decorating his symbolism of human religion. “I return to this town with news of victory!” To the news they were receiving, the commoners cheered and applauded the Knight. He took a slight bow in each direction, basking in the glow of his own glory. Unbeknownst to the head Knight, a little girl was tugging on the strings of the horse’s saddle. The crowd began to grow quiet as they realized a child was trying to get their hero’s attention. The head Knight finally glanced down in her direction, and grimaced at the thought of a peasant girl touching his prestigious horse. “You stand in the realm of Greatness, commoner,” he warned. “What is it you seek?” “Where’s my daddy?” The little girl, green-eyed and innocent, looked up at the man covered in protective metal with the most anticipation her heart could muster. “I do not know who your father is, now be gone.” “Daddy go to battle with Knights,” she cried. “I seen him leave with you, Mister. Why isn’t he here, Mister” Why?” A few eyebrows raised in the crowd of the people. Another voice echoed from the left side of the square. “Where’s my brother, sir Knight” He rode to battle with you as well.” “Unfortunately,” the Knight announced, “the rest of the Knights died in defense of the Crown, but I assure you their death was not in vain! The entire village of Cathdesalantis has been destroyed and burned to the ground! Those vile creatures cease to exist!” “Daddy no come home?” The girl was in hysterics and convulsing on the ground. “Will someone not help her?” arose a voice from the crowd. “She’s fine,” the Knight muttered. “Help the little girl, she’s lost her father.” “Be quiet,” the Knight commanded. “Someone do something!” “I order you all to be quiet!” The girl pulled herself up with the horse’s leg, and it let out a moan in disapproval. The Knight, in pure reaction, swiped his sword down from the side and struck the little girl in the face! A screech rang out from the injured child, and blood splattered the wooden wall behind her. The townspeople’s attention suddenly focused in the direction behind the head Knight. The Knight noticed a shadow growing near his own, and also recognized the silhouette of two swords being held at each side. I couldn’t believe what I was seeing?. It was Eldarin! This must’ve been the confrontation I’d read about! Turning his head into the setting sun, the Knight locked eyes with the one he’d left for dead. With the glow of the sun on each side of the rebel leader, Eldarin stepped closer. His clothes completely covered in blood, he walked up to the little child as the Knight backed his horse up several feet. He removed his helmet, allowing his blue hair to flow in the wind. The crowd gasped, as they realize one of the creatures of which the Knight had spoke survived their raid, and was now lurking near one of the children. Eldarin’s hand slowly drifted to the injured face of the child, and he wiped the blood away from her eyes and leaned his head in to check for a breath. In a delicate manner, he lifted the child to a cradle position. Her eyes very slowly drifted towards the blue-haired stranger handling her ever so carefully. “You have blue hair,” she choked out. Eldarin nodded, as he slowly rocked her back and forth. All the eyes were focused on the two of them, and not a sound was made. “I like blue.” The girl fell lifeless in Eldarin’s arms, and his eyes drifted towards the head Knight. A crowd circled behind him as he gently set the girl’s body on the ground. Eldarin took the end of his long-sword and crossed the dirt in front of him. The townspeople looked confused for a second, but the rest of those from the square very slowly started filing behind the Elven Warrior and away from the Knight who had just murdered an innocent little girl. “Now, we end this.” The Knight dismounted from the horse, and drew his sword to the ready position. “So I did leave a survivor,” he mused. “How foolish of me. The people of my colony will get to witness the fall of the final Elven Warrior.” I laughed to myself, knowing the outcome of his arrogance. “Sir Knight of Supposed Honor, serving under whichever crown with which you associate, it has occurred to me that a soul and a heart cannot exist within the boundaries of that body. Therefore, I will have no problem ripping it out in vengeance and symbolism for what you’ve done to our community. The difference is, not a single Elven child ever did so much as think badly of your kind when they first started arriving from the waters. Not a single Elven woman ever thought a male creature of any supposed intelligent kind would ever bring a sword to an innocent face and spill their blood for no reason other than war-mongering. My warriors never imagined they would have to watch their city burn and defend themselves for no crimes committed, no sins for which to pay, and no damage on the land on which they lived. You brought all this here, and because of you, many children now have lost fathers in your own town. Many wives will no longer hear their husband’s opening the door, nor will they continue to live with the support of the Crown, as I’m sure that monarch will do nothing to aid the bereaving. For your sins against my people and your own, this is your judgment day.” Eldarin swung full strength into the Knight’s face. The Knight cross bladed the attack, and the two were inches from each other’s face. Pushing with all their strength, each strained to gain the upper advantage. Eldarin pulled away and swung low with his other sword for a knee attack, but the Knight blocked as well. Eldarin rolled through with the attack, dodging a stab from the Knight. A second swing from the Knight drew Eldarin blocking from the upside down position. Eldarin swung his feet up and kicked the Knight’s arms away, and jumped to his feet just in time to block another slash attack from the Knight. Eldarin backed up a few feet, and tossed the long-sword to his right hand, and a big smile crossed his face as he dropped the short-sword. The Knight’s face started to grow whiter as Eldarin began hitting powerful attacks that the Knight could barely block. They were growing stronger, to the point where the Knight began to hear the splintering of wood. The Knight was backed up against the wall stained in the child’s blood, and while the Knight quivered in fear before him, Eldarin looked down at his wrists, gripping the long-sword for a few last seconds. Turning the corner of his eye, the corners of his mouth turned up in a slight grin, and Eldarin swung his sword so hard that the Knight’s blade broke, and a sharp piece flew directly into his throat. Blood began spewing everywhere, as the Knight grabbed his throat and gagged for a few seconds, before dropping near the feet of his horse. Eldarin spooked the horse by smacking its rear end, and the horse ran away from the village and into the forest. Eldarin turned to the Knight’s townspeople, all breathless over the display of swordsmanship they’d just seen, and even more shocked that the mighty Knight was finally defeated. One finally had the courage to speak up. “Are you going to kill all of us?” Eldarin stepped a few feet closer, and a couple of the people closest to him flinched in fear. Eldarin kept walking, and they started to back out of the way. Eldarin walked slowly as a line formed between the population of the colonial village until Eldarin reached the gate. The sun disappeared on the horizon, and the dark blue sky almost made Eldarin’s hair blend with the surroundings. He turned back one more time with a stone cold stare. He then disappeared into the forest, never to be seen again by any of the people in that village, as I’d heard the story go. Observing the colony in silence, I couldn’t help but notice how familiar the slain knight seemed, and despite knowing his misdeeds had earned him that fall, I couldn’t help but feel a tingling of remorse. The little girl had also reminded me a bit of Devon? looks-wise, anyway. If it were actually Devon, she would’ve killed the Knight herself. I snapped back to reality, returning to my room with an unexpected rush of air. So many things to take in and wonder, especially why Eldarin’s eyes had taken me back to the death of a little girl and the fall of a bloodthirsty Knight. Not to mention why the little girl reminded me of Devon in such a precarious situation, but one thing at a time would have to be determined. Eldarin’s eye light disappeared from the mirror, and the sword gleamed in the background. Still sporting a sick smile on my face, I removed the sword from its sheath and held it front of my face. The light reflecting shone into my eyes, and as I once more stared into the mirror, no longer seeking answers but preparing to make them myself, I knew what I must do to survive. Eliminate all opponents and adversaries until none were left standing. Until the time when the pain was gone and all that was left was the desire to be the warrior I was destined to be, the fuel inside me would be laced with the ashes of the heart she burned. Son of the Immortal, Child of the Earth.
