A reputation is a terrible thing to waste

12 July 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12th July See the man, Andy Murray; thirty-one years dying, yet still very much alive. Torn from a glamorous world of glitter and gold, and left alone in one of dusty gym halls and half-filled concession stands. A man of worldwide reputation and numerous accomplishments who’d transformed himself from perennially injured also-ran to household name in eighteen short months, yet a man, who, to his Just Wrestling counterparts, was no more than another name on the booking sheet. Stood alone inside the Kaiser Convention Center, casting his bright azure blues across five thousand empty seats. Place was small. Claustrophobic, kinda. Chairs were packed tightly into the limited floor space with scarcely enough room for one more, and the short distance between the fans and performers was such that nobody would be able to escape the clasps of overzealous punters. It was a thousand miles from the places he’d become accustomed to wrestling in, and lightyears away from his comfort zone. Leaned back against the tense ropes, resting his giant frame. Soon this empty room would become a boiling pot. A cauldron ignited by the primal fury of a few thousand partisan indy wrestling fans. For the moment, he was fine – lucky, even. They were still fond of him here. His Just popularity levels were nowhere as stratospheric as in GCW, of course, but he was still one of the good guys. Andy knew, though, how fickle this indy kids could get. Another loss to a lesser-name opponent and they might turn on him. These fans were of a different breed; they would not cheer for a man on name value alone, and were just as likely to get on the back of a big-name flop as they would for a devious villain like Violence Jack. He’d stressed about it, yeah. Expectation weighed heavy on his shoulders; one-and-three was the worst start he’d ever made in a new promotion. For a man supposedly at the peak of his powers, it was embarrassing, humiliating. He'd just captured the top title in one of the continent's biggest promotions, and here he was mailing-in performances in front of two thousand people, against wrestler's he'd never hear of again. Andy stepped through the ropes. Slid down from the apron and hit the floor; his legs wobbled, feeling as steady as those of a newborn foal. Five thousand empty seats watched his every move as he slowly paced up the ramp, and Andy Murray felt butterflies swirling in his stomach for the first time in almost a decade. * * * * * [i]They bundled him into the car like a fugitive and soon they were off into the night. Flanked by two GCW security team members, he slumped into the Mercedes' leather interior, a tired wreck of a man. Andy's limbs hung from his body as if his hands and feet were iron wrecking balls. Every muscle in his body ached and the slightest movement in his right leg sent barbs of white hot pain shooting behind his kneecap. He could feel stale sweat drying on his brow and he'd barely been able to catch his breath after the match had finished, but it didn't matter. The shiny prize of gold and leather held tightly to his chest made it all worth the while. He'd fought for so long and finally the prize was his. LeStatt Knight; a force of incomparable evil in the wrestling world had fallen by his hand after six months of toil and struggle. Overcome was the foe that many thought unbeatable, unstoppable. A feat that even he thought he'd never accomplish. The pay-off of twelve years hard work – his moment in the sun, at last. As the car sped away from the garage, Andy barely noticed the gathered masses through the tinted windows. They stood around the arena, chanting and screaming his name, but sounds of the arena still rung in his inner cortex. The bell. The fans exploding. Joey Andrews. “Here is your winner...” The journey to the hotel passed him by, and before he knew it he was shuffling from the elevator door, headed for his sweat. Bag slung over his back, belt resting on his shoulder, Andy reached the door and slid the key card down the reader. Stepped into a room full of grinning faces. His brothers, fiancée, and son. Dropped his bag and began to realise what he'd accomplished as a champagne bottle popped and his family jostled to embrace him.[/i] * * * * * It was a modest room of a decent size. White walls, grey floors. Couple of tables and some chairs. Just the kinda place you expect to find tucked away in the deepest reaches of a small wrestling venue. The man they call “The King” sat awkwardly on a plastic chair, shifting his 275lbs weight back and forth, struggling for comfort. A couple of men in Just Wrestling polo shirts stood before him; one operating a laptop, the other hovering around a tripod-steadied hand-held camera, lens focus on a young grappler standing in front of a Just backdrop. Kid couldn't have been any older than twenty-five, but he was buzzing. Full of piss and vinegar, he could barely keep still as he barked textbooks insults and “witty” quips into the camera, trashing his opponent for the night. Andy was feeling uneasy. He hadn't cut a promo like this in a long-time, and he was going in blind. He knew next to nothing about Brock Alyas, only that losing to him would surely result in the full-on implosion of his fledgling Just career. The youngster was about to wrap it up. His words were growing slower, his facial expression more intense. Andy clasped his hands together tightly, and when he pulled them apart he found they were clammy. Sweating, now. Pulse quickening. Ridiculous, really; a man used to performing weekly in front of tens of thousands, frightened by a camera lense. GCW's pressure was nothing like this. This was closer, more personal. In GCW his reputation was assured – he could do just about anything and the fans would still eat from the palm of his hand – but here he had to [i]fight[/i] for it. Perhaps he'd become complacent. For the past year he'd cruised through the opposition with only a few minor hitches, but now he had to put his head down and graft. The first four matches had been a shock to the system, but he would turn it around. He had to. Kid wrapped-up his promo and bowed. Said something along the lines of “how [i]good[/i] am I?!” and posed like he was on Broadway, but the staffers paid him no notice. Swaggered away from the set. Andy stood and extended a hand as he swaggered towards him. “Hey, And-...” he began to introduce himself, but the Just wrestler who's name he hadn't caught swung by him as if he wasn't there. Blanked Andy Murray, world-renowned wrestling entertaining, entirely. Andy watched him leave the room and shook his head. It reminded him how unimportant he was in this world. “You're up,” came a voice from the short distance across the room. Andy looked up and found both technicians glaring at him expectantly. Stepping forward, settling himself in front of the camera, and hastily prepared something to say as the cameraman's countdown began. * * * * * [i]”Take me with you,” he thought he heard her say over the top of the Springsteen track that crept through his headphones. She stood before him, dainty hand resting on the worktop, saying the last thing that he wanted to hear. Andy did well to disguise his sigh as he paused his iPod and dropped his blue dufflebag on the table. His bride-to-be shifted herself from the kitchenette and drifted slowly towards him, the look in her round, hazel eyes enunciating a not-so-humble request. “I know this isn't gonna be an easy trip. Let me help you.” “Kay. Don't.” “Please,” she said, stopping just a few inches from him. “You won't even know I'm there.” “It's not a good idea,” responded he, dropping his head. “This is an important one. Not the most glamorous match I've ever wrestled, but I really don't wanna lose it. Can't have any distractions.” “But you've only been home two days.” “And I'll be home again in another two,” Andy said with a flash of the perfect pearly-white smile that had helped make him famous. He turned. Pulled his bag up. “I know you're just being supportive, but family on the road's not a good idea when it's not a big event, Kay. Trust me.” Andy learned forward. Kissed the suntanned skin on her smooth forehead. “Okay,” she nodded. Swallowed. Knew there was no point pursuing matters further. Battling with the Jay Terrors and Chris Bagwells of the world had made him far too thick-skinned to back down from something like this. “You don't get as long as we do when you're alone out there, though?” “'Course I do, but that's the nature of the beast. Ain't like I'm gone all the time – just a couple days here and there,” he paused as his train of thought meandered. Stopped to re-compose himself. “Truth be told, a few more days to recover from Rampage would've been nice.” “Then why are you going?” she asked. “You're a champion, now. Why d'you have to go fight some guy in a bingo hall?” The terrible start he'd made and the damage it might do to his reputation if he didn't right it. He explained it to her, but Kayla would perhaps never understand. Still, they exchange well-wishes and she watched him go as she had a hundred times before. Often she wondered where they would be in five years if Andy continued to pour so much of himself into the sport. She knew what had happened to his last marriage and such prospects terrified her. The strain of the career on a man's family life was well-documented, and sometimes, when he was away on those cross-country trips, she wondered if she'd made the right choice. But, as Kayla looked down and saw her future husband's visage beaming back an advance copy of this month's Pro-Wrestling Illustrated cover, title over his shoulder, behind the “MURRAY – BEST IN THE WORLD?” tag-line, she remembered why sure endured these long, lonesome hours.[/i] * * * * * “... so saddle up, Bubbles, 'cause this is one Long Hot Summer that you ain't gonna enjoy!” He maintained his pose for a couple of seconds, flashing a cocky smirk into the camera's eye before it's operator told him he'd cut the feed. The staffers thanked him for his time and the laptop user turned back to his computer quickly, desperate to ready the day's promos for show's 3pm deadline. Andy left the room in his own time. The promo had went well; it had alleviated some of his concerns, but not all. The true test would come that night. Just a few nights removed from an epic forty-five minute clash with LeStatt Knight in front of a global audience of millions, Andy Murray would lace-up and grapple with Brock Alyas for the entertainment of a fraction of GCW's crowd. A walk in the park on paper, but that's what Ivor Avalon was supposed to be, too. No longer could he rest on his laurels; tonight he'd have to dig deep and show some of the qualities that made PWI consider him one of the world's finest. Not so much a big fish in a small pond, more a shark in a goldfish bowl. Breaking The Educator's streak in AWC. Defeating PRIME champion Devin Shakur in PTC's Elite Title Tournament. Besting the “unbeatable” LeStatt. Some of Andy Murray's greatest accomplishments had came when his back was against the wall, when the odds were against him, and Just Wrestling would know it soon.
