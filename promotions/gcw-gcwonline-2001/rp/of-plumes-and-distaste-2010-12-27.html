Of Plumes and Distaste

27 December 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/27/10 Sometimes I wonder if it was all worth it. Maybe it’s the lack of nicotine or just the random bored moment between shows, but could I have made a mistake? Usually it’s only a passing thought, nothing more than a sigh, because when it comes down to it, there is no going back. My life has been decided, my future staked, and forever I will be known as that snake. What I’ll never understand though, is how some people believe they can ignore their decisions and reset everything they once had. Without hesitation, they believe that the booing will become cheering as it once was, and that all things will be back to normal. It’s a fantasy world, a desirable one admittedly, that’s no more than a mask. For what it’s worth, I’d rather see things through my own eyes rather than through a pair of mesh holes. --- There was a line stretching straight out the door at the local FYE and Jay Terror couldn’t help but sigh. He’d had two cigarettes in his two hour stint, but neither was able to satiate his hunger. When it came down to it, there was never a proper remedy for autograph signings. For the entire afternoon, Terror had tagged merchandise for the same people that spit on him while he marched down to the ring. Cleveland was especially filled with those who simply had something to say. For the general population, Tessa Windsor was a popular piece while others found it worthwhile to mock his loss to Johnny Borealis. Naturally, Jay found it best to focus on his next cigarette. There was something comforting in aiming forward and, for the most part, it allowed him to slip in and out of conversation. His responses were static and he could tell it only added to his line’s distaste. Thankfully for them, Jay was not alone. Vivica J. Valentine sat at the table beside him passing silent judgement with her CERULEAN blue eyes. Even outside the ring, there was a thick layer of tension built up between the two. She was happier than he was, peppy even, and carried herself as a world champion was supposed to. People admired her charm, talked about how she had helped them chase their dreams, and how they were happy to see her back to her old self. She drew smiles from the largest of men and for some reason, Jay Terror couldn’t stand it. Although James Bryan was to thank for their pairing, he wasn’t ready to blame him in the case of a personality explosion. Naturally, he was meant to get along with everyone no matter what the circumstances were. Frankly, he found it impossible. While he hated Andy Murray, he had the same face and same outlook no matter where he went. With that, there was a sense of civility through appreciation. Valentine on the other hand was different every week. If she wasn’t looking for approval, she was bashing the world for their lack of encouragement. For a long time, Jay stared at her handwriting, and watched as she made long swoops at the bottom of her J’s. He studied her warming words and shook his head at each lie. It took a tap on the shoulder to break his trance and upon looking at the table, he found a fresh pack of cigarettes staring at him. Terror grinned, pulled one loose, and violated the store’s rules by torching the head of it. “Hey,” said the man in front of him. Terror looked up, noticed him, and nodded. “Thanks.” “Are you going to sign it?” He raised a brow in surprise and reached into the box for another stick. Grabbing his pen, he scribbled his name across the carton’s Marlboro logo. “Thanks.” The man turned to Valentine as he marched past and said aloud, “Kick his ass this week. God knows he needs it.” Jay pulled the cigarette free from his lips and watched a plume of smoke float into the ceiling. He didn’t hear him. --- There are times before I fall asleep that I can hear Jordan White’s ankle snap. The sound reminds me of fresh fall leaves being stepped on, and often it plays over and over as if someone were crossing an entire field of them. To this day, I believe wholeheartedly that the people that watched me leap onto that chair will never be the same. Some said that he was defenseless and didn’t deserve the brutality. Others say that I’m a monster and should be taken off the television so their children can watch again. When it comes down to it though, it was Jordan’s fault for not kicking out. He got exactly what he deserved for letting Vivica Valentine pin him in the center of that ring. If it hadn’t been for Steven Caldera’s impromptu title match the following night, I would have surely ended his career for wasting my first chance at true success. Steve was smart though and understood that it wasn’t my fault that Valentine retained the title at Warpath. To this day, pinning Vivica and raising that title for the first time still sits on the top of my greatest achievements list. Whether Jordan knows it or not, I have him to thank for truly surfacing the greatest rage I’ve ever felt. Unfortunately for Valentine, history has a funny way of repeating itself. This time she can thank Johnny Borealis for everything I plan to do to her. --- Jay picked up Gia just outside his hotel room for a decent rate considering he was driving a Mercedes. She was a talkative girl with an obnoxious lisp, but Jay chose to turn the radio up louder. They listened to an entire Godsmack song before arriving at the Quicken Loans Arena and when there, he continued to hum the guitar to himself. “You some kinda’ athlete,” she asked. “Something like that,” he responded. Her questions continued until Jay reached an open area. His eyes were focused on the wall and, more noticeably, a ladder. He grabbed the metal monster and carried it to Gia. “Grab this,” he demanded. “I don’t think you understand the usual protocol.” “It’s my money.” “And my nails,” she hissed. “I’ll pay for them if they chip.” “You’re something else,” she said before grabbing the ladder around the steps. Her sass made him question her Cleveland roots, but he liked it for what he had in mind. “Now ram the top into me as hard as you can.” “What?” “As hard as you can. Just run it into me.” “I’m not running anything into you. This is stupid.” “It’s my money.” “Most guys don’t,” she started. “I’m not most guys,” Jay interrupted. “Look, I realize that you’re not right in the head but you gotta’ understand that I am. I got things straight and I know that hittin’ you in the stomach ain’t gonna’ be a good thing. You can have your money or whatever, I just,” Gia trailed off. Jay grinned. “Then hit me in the face.” “I’m not hitting you anywhere.” Terror reached deep into his pocket and found whatever cash he had left. It was wrapped tight and still managed to look thick. “I don’t know how much is here, but it’s yours if you ram that god damn ladder into me.” Even with Terror’s tone rising, Gia showed grit as she continued to hold onto the ladder. Her emotions were unwavering, but it was easy to see that the money had caught her eye. “You’re not trying to win an assault case, are you?” “Don’t worry about that.” “I am worried about that,” she spat. “All I’m getting is a ladder to the chest or wherever you feel’s worth it. No case. No paperwork. Just a hard shove.” He could see her greed and before he knew it, Gia turned the ladder topside forward. While she didn’t appear the be strong at a glance, she showed a hidden strength in her first swing. The ladder connected with Jay’s ribcage and he doubled over nearly instantly. “How’s that,” she asked with a sense of thrill in her voice. Jay coughed and replied, “again.” She knew the word well and continued to thrive on the adrenaline high. Her second swing was to fulfill an earlier request which dropped Jay to the floor. He grabbed his jaw and allowed a slight smirk to form on his lips. “You want another,” she questioned. Jay turned to his back and looked up into Gia’s eyes. “Hit me until you feel like you earned that money.” She slammed him in the gut three more times before feeling the weight of the ladder. She tossed it aside, unaware of the way his ear rested on the concrete, and found the stack of money from the ground. Jay’s ears buzzed but in that moment, he never felt so alive. --- What’s funny is that this opportunity coincides perfectly with my war on Andy Murray. There were times that I couldn’t stand the sight of both Vivica and and Andy holding hands backstage. It was pathetic and a distraction at best. This week though, I wish that the spark had never died. If there’s one thing that would break Murray, it would be me smashing his girlfriend’s face into the mat with the weight of my foot. Unfortunately, times have changed and egos have run their course leaving me with one thing left to crush. All good memories will die under my boot leaving a cold spot in Andy Murray’s heart. --- Terror’s body hurt, but he’d paid plenty to see the date through. Thankfully, she liked being in control, perhaps more with the day’s events, and took the top with a fierce energy Jay hadn’t seen in weeks. She was rapid and Jay watched her perform her actual day job. Ten minutes in, his phone vibrated on the bedside table and Jay shoved her off. “Hey,” she shouted. Jay grabbed the phone, checked the name, and flipped it to his ear. “Maggie. Hey. How are you?” Gia scoffed. “Does your lady want to know my name?” Jay ignored her and listened. “Good. This a bad time,” Maggie asked. “No. I’m just watching TV,” he replied. “I’m not a TV, asshole,” Gia continued. She got up from the bed and found her jeans and underwear. “I just wanted to call and wish you luck tomorrow. With Valentine. You know,” Maggie said. “Thanks. I appreciate it.” Gia found her shirt and slid it over her head. Jay continued to listen. “Also wanted to make sure you were alright. With the whole John thing and. Well. Just everything. You seemed out of it against Johnny.” “I’m not. Not anymore. Just a bad week I guess,” he answered. Gia stood and grabbed the box of cigarettes off the nightstand. She flipped him off behind his back before walking out the door. Jay hardly noticed. “Just don’t let anything get you down. You’re better than that.” “You coming to the next show? Maybe we can do lunch,” he pestered. “I’ll try my best. I’m sorry. I have to go. John and I are going to dinner.” “Yeah. Of course.” “Jay. Don’t do the same things you did to Tessa. People might start thinking that you’re dangerous.” He laughed exaggeratedly, “I’ll try not to.” “Good bye.” “Good bye.” He flipped the phone shut and turned back to the bed. While slightly perturbed that Gia had left, he was more worried about the cigarettes she had taken. With a shrug, he found his place under the comforter again and stared down the wall wondering just how dangerous he was.
