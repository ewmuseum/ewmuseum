The Skye Is Falling (Part One): Paper Heart

3 May 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 05/03/11 Balloons of blue and yellow filled the nearly empty movie rental store. Unlike most closings there were no "Huge Liquidation Sale" signs or any signs proclaiming "ALL THINGS MUST GO!" Just like her musical career, Amanda Harper's Blockbuster career, went out on a whimper. No hugs goodbye or tears of sorrow, just a rip of her name badge off her blue polo. Gripping it in her fist, she then threw the badge like an Aroldis Chapman fastball straight into the ground before saying her famous last words to anyone within earshot. "Fuck this, guys." In true revolutionary leader form, she looked back at the people she never considered her friends, "I'm not sticking around for this shit. I'm gone. Peace." Throwing her tiny frame into the door, she shook the glass walls of the lobby that stood between herself and freedom. This freedom however was bittersweet. This freedom wasn't free. Biting down on her spider bite piercings, she was forced to face the truth. She couldn't hide behind the facade of "having a job" any longer. All that was left was her own failure, and with slight hesitation, Amanda Harper had no one left to blame as she looked back at her own reflection in the tinted glass. Sometimes it was hard to wake up in the morning and feel as if she was destined for anything greater in life than eternal failure. Even the Blockbuster job fell way below the lofty standards she'd set for herself in high school. The tiniest taste of musical success was all it took to convince her that she'd be a rockstar in just a few short months. Once upon a time she was in a band, she was in high demand, she was somebody. Then everything came crashing down. The guitarist left for another band, one with some radio success at that, and everyone else just came unglued. Life was harder than it seemed, and reality had crushed her dreams and aspirations. With a sigh, she pulled her phone out of her cargo pants pocket, and with disappointment saw that she'd missed nothing at all. It was now that most people would admit defeat, man-up, swallow their pride, and make that phone call back to "mom" and "dad". Again. Because that apartment she so badly wanted Vivica to stay in? It would be up for rent in just about a week after a rather messy eviction. Her life packed into the backseat of her mid-sized sedan, which was so junked up the make and model now mirrored her own future. Unspecified. Lightning flashed across the desert sky and rain poured down on the pavement below as she left that hell hole behind once and for all. She didn't want to look back, only forward as she took one step and watched as a couple of teenagers slammed their car doors shut and sprinted towards the safety of the porch. As they jumped over a small puddle and onto the raised pavement, Amanda side-stepped in an effort to make a path to the door. It wasn't the door they were running to, however, but a new shiny blue machine that almost went unnoticed to her emerald eyes. Blockbuster Express. 1$ DVD Movie Rentals. Corporate office sure had some nerve putting that piece of shit out in front of the store before it was even closed. Amanda even felt guilty about the smirk the crept across her face at the disappointment of the teenage couple, who quickly found out that the machine was not yet operational. Forced to go inside, they walked right past the green and black pigtailed Harper, who went to go check out the machine for herself. Sure she was far from a model employee, she even blamed her disenchantment with cinema on the fact she was forced to work at that place, but what was it that this machine had that she didn't? What did this machine have exactly that made it more important than her home, her bills, her livelihood. That miserable job was all she really had, and it wasn't enough that it took it away from her. It had to stand there on her last day to properly tell her to go fuck herself on the way out. Well, what was keeping her from giving it a little payback herself? Clinching her fingers tightly into the palm of her hand, she snarled before banging a hammerfist down on the side of the machine. Listening to the sheet metal dent into the cabinet was like music to her ears as she did it again and again. It wasn't long into her orchestral arrangement of boiled over emotions that kicks started being sprinkled into the melody. People walking in and out of the various stores located in the shopping center began to stop and stare, most shying away but still captivated all the same by her symphony of anguish. Parents covered the eyes of their children as she screamed out in a release of stress that was like an orgasm for her soul. Tears ran down her face, flowing rivers of mascara as she heaved air in and out of her lungs. And with one symbolic wave of the conductor's wand, she ripped her blue polo off over her head and threw it against the machine. Taking a few steps backward, she turned around and faced her public who looked back at her as if she were a freak of society. An outcast. Another soul lost to whatever devilish vice of choice may be the flavor of the weak. If they only knew her story, and the things that machine symbolized... they would've joined in the masterpiece themselves. Yet society feels it is their place to judge. Their place to become the jury of their peers in the courtroom known as life. The telling eyes surrounding her as she walks past makes her forget for just the slightest second where she is going, now only wearing a white tank top over a black bra and khaki pants. If she would've had that second back, she would've spent it trying to stop the tow truck that went roaring past her. Strapped to the back of it, the piece of scrap metal that she called her car. Strapped to the back of that, all she had left. "No!" She screaming, almost running over an elderly woman before jumping in front of a car going towards the exit. Waving her arms in the air, she watched the traffic light turn yellow. "DON'T YOU FUCKING DARE!" But just like that the light turned red, and the tow truck turned left onto the main road merging into traffic. She had no choice but to sit back and watch as life just slipped away. Even in a loss, it wasn't the fact she came short once again that stood out, but the loss of a friend. "Thank you for calling Verizon Wireless. Two weeks ago was the last time she'd heard from the girl she once knew affectionately as Jupiter Skye, and that conversation alone was enough to make the blonde-haired fighter nervous. The last words Amanda spoke to her still haunted her as she looked up into the night sky. "I don't know if I can do this." She'd lost her job, her home, her car... and now her phone. For all she knew Amanda Harper could be living with her adopted parents or dead somewhere in the middle of the desert. Taking a deep breath, Vivica just slid the cell phone back into her side pocket before dropping down to take a seat on the moist grass. With eyes closed and mind reeling, she ran her fingers through her crimson and gold locks. There had to be something she could do. A voice rang out from behind her amongst the clacks and clangs of tearing down the set. Turning her head, Vivica was surprised to see Amy Campbell come out from inside the giant talent tent, a bit concerned for her new locker roommate. Just two weeks ago, "concern" was the last thing Vivica thought Amy would ever feel about her, but forced time together has made them realize that they aren't so different after all. This would force most girls apart but for Amy and Vivica, it was almost exactly what they needed. Someone predictable. Someone stable. Someone just like them. "Kind of." Vivica responded unspecificly before looking back out across the Washington Mall. "Kind of?" "It's a long story filled with unflattering choices." Valentine answered, "I'd rather not remind you why you should hate me." "Like I'm a saint." "I see the way Desade looks at me, I'd rather not stir the pot." "Alex will be Alex, plus I've got time." "I don't." Viv responded, trying not to look back at the Red Raver who has now plopped a seat next to her, making things increasingly uncomfortable. Trying to ignore the presence of her red-headed roommate, Vivica pulled her phone back out of her pocket. No missed alerts, and ever worse, it had only been a minute and a half. "You sure? Worried about someone?" Aimz chimes in, breaking the awkward silence. "A little." "Boyfriend?" "Nope." "Lover?" "No." "Girlfriend?" "..." A glare from Vivica answers for her. "Just a friend?" "Just a friend." Vivica reassures her before adjusting for comfort. "I remember once," Aimz persistently carries on, "you told me you didn't have any friends." "Just the one, really." Vivica glances over at Amy, just a second before the former SCCW star pats her on the back. Valentine closes her eyes and takes a deep breath before shaking her head. "Well..." Amy uses Vivian’s shoulder as a brace to push herself up before rubbing the top of Vivian’s head, further messing up the battle-tested hair atop Valentine's head. "...now you got two." As much as she tried, Vivica couldn't hold back a smirk from crossing her lips as Amy began to walk away ahead of her, going towards a security fence about 100 yards away where GCW fans gathered trying to get a peek at what was going on. Desperately hoping to score an elusive autograph. "Aimz!" Vivica shouted, causing the Red Raver to stop in her tracks. Slowly turning around, Campbell looked back at her almost knowingly. "You don't need to say it, Viv." Amy smiled, "You're welcome." ~*~ "I am, I am, I said I'm not myself, but I'm not deaaaaad and I'm not for saaaaaale..." A sweet serenade leaves lips of green as she strums up and down on the acoustic guitar. A crowd of people gather and clap along as she continues on. "So keep your bankroll lottery, just have your deathbed motorcade." Applause erupts from the handfull of people, leaving the girl to smile as people begin to toss change into her guitar case. Just outside of the Fremont Street Experience, Amanda Harper looks past the small crowd to see the other performers who surround her. People who have been working the same sections of the same streets, over and over again for decades. A parade of broken dreams and heartbreak, all trying to get a little cash before the entertainment starts. "Do you do requests?" A woman with a question catches her attention as she re-tunes her guitar. "If I know it, sure." Her jaw drops and almost hits the floor in mid-sentence before she jumps up off of her plastic milk crate and almost tackles the woman in front of her. Vivica J. Valentine almost tumbles over herself in the exchange, caught a bit by surprise as people look on. Talking among themselves, some of them recognize the GCW superstar and start pulling out their camera phones and those who don't quickly begin to speculate. "Christ, Viv! What in the world are you doing here?!" "I haven't heard from you in weeks," Vivica responds with a smile, "I didn't have a clue what happened to you. I ended up getting a hold of Dave and Cindy..." "Of course." "And here I am. I'm just glad you're okay." "I'm performing again." Amanda pointed down to the guitar she dropped in a spasm of joy, "I got away from Blockbuster, finally and now I'm doing what I've always wanted to do. Going after my dreams, I mean... I figured it's what you would do." "Well I'm not much of a musician." "And I'm not much of a fighter." She returned with as she picked her guitar back up off the ground and swung it over her shoulder, before sitting back down "I could be if I wanted to though. If you hear a story from anyone around here about a girl who assaulted a Blockbuster Express machine, it totally wasn't me." Vivica laughed, noticing the crowd disperse to other performers. "I was framed." Amanda laughed, "I can't believe you came all the way out here. This is crazy!" "I can't believe you're out here performing on the street, isn't this dangerous?" Pausing for a moment, Amanda looks to her left and watches as an older man in a cowboy hat performs an old Hank Williams song for nearly the same group of people that just left her performance. She didn't have nearly enough money to put a down-payment on an apartment. Hell she barely had enough for breakfast in the morning. Looking up into the eyes of her best friend, her eyes then move back to the group of people now applauding for the song they just enjoyed. Why was it always so hard to trust in somebody? Maybe it was because she didn't have anyone for most of her life or the fact that everyone she ever let in seemed to hurt her. It was a rough life that Vivica didn't fully understand, and that's why what was just about to happen may not have been fully appreciated. "Alright." For once in her life, Amanda Harper had something to believe in. ~*~ With her foot down on the accelerator, the only noise other than the roaring engine of her rental car is the snoring of Amanda Harper from the passenger seat. A series of very emotional weeks and sleepless nights have taken their toll on her, leaving her fast asleep with her head propped up against the passenger side window. Valentine. It means she felt safe. A quick turn of the wheel and a slight jump of the curb sent shockwaves through the Pontiac G6, bouncing Amanda back before going forward again and bouncing her head off the passenger side window as Vivica came to a slow stop. "The fuck was that?!" Groaned Amanda, rubbing her head and trying to figure out exactly where she was after a deep sleep. "Ooooooow." "Sorry, a little harder to make that turn at night." "Where in the hell are we?" Amanda looks out the windshield towards a white garage door. Vivica answered as she pulled the keys out of the ignition. Power locks clicked open, the handle pulls to a thunk, and she pushes the driver's side door open before unfastening her seatbelt only to hear the snoring of Amanda Harper filled the air once again. The sleepwalker begins coming more to her senses, "This place is pretty nice." "Come inside." Viv smirks and flips a light switch as soon as she walks into the room. "Aren't you being Miss Cryptic." Harper steps inside and notices the marble entry before it turns into carpet, usually being the international sign for 'take off your shoes.' Her eyes lock with Amanda's, whose joyful expression begins to fade into awkward silence. Each passing second seems like an hour as they just stare back and one another. She couldn't just leave the poor girl standing there. Vivica knew what she had to do, she had to tell Amanda how she felt. And she would, as soon as she figured it out herself.
