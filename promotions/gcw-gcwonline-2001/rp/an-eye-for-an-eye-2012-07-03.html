An Eye For An Eye

3 July 2012
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07/03/12 Gun-shy It’s sweltering. My four hundred dollar Charvet shirt is clinging to my back and drops of warm, viscous perspiration are trickling off my nose. Exhaling a current of steam, I can see Joel and Derek shooting me sideways looks, wondering how in the hell we’re going to get out of this alive. We can’t stay in here. Aside from the fact that this courthouse is an oven, the three of us are aware that we’ll never wait out the horde outside. The ravenous, salivating mongrels that need to tear me to pieces to live another day. I can feel them, eager to sink their teeth into me and angry as all hell that I’m making them wait in the scorching sun. My two partners have my back, I know, but that still means they’re following my lead. They’re out there. Loaded and ready, each one aiming at the door with their finger on the trigger, wanting- needing to be the first. It’s a lynch mob and they’re all stirred up into a frenzy, and God knows what’s keeping them from charging the gates and busting down this oak door and gorging themselves, fighting over the scraps of what’s left of me. Pressing my ear against the door, I can hear them cocking their weapons, shuffling their feet in anticipation. With a pleading look, I turn first to Joel. He opens his mouth to say something wise, but nothing comes out as he turns his gaze to the floor, ashamed that he can’t help me. Derek, on the other hand, doesn’t wait to make eye contact before providing the top-notch legal counsel for which I pay him so handsomely. "They’re waiting," the words find their way through the muggy air. The look on his face is a perfect mix of trepidation and anticipation. Six hundred dollars an hour and he tells me the obvious. Thanks, dick. "No way we can slip out the back, is there?" My joke falls flat. Derek shakes his head ‘no.’ Joel readies himself for that whole once more unto the breach moment. The three of us came together to accomplish the impossible. Now it’s time for the hammer to fall. "Alright then, guys," I hold my hand out to Joel and without making eye contact he places the metal piece in my palm. The beating sun, the stifling courtroom, and Joel’s body temperature have warmed it to the point where I wince as I unscrew the safety. Tilt my head back, pull the trigger, and let the shot of whiskey slide down my throat. Eases my nerves just the slightest bit. I replace the cap of the flask and hand it back to my agent, "The ride had to end sometime." Flipping out the Oakley’s, I give my team a wink before hiding behind the shades. If I’m gonna go out, I’m gonna go out my way. And those bastards out there are going to see exactly what I want them to see. Placing my hand on the door, there’s no turning back as I give it a shove and a breath of hot air comes wafting in at us. I’m not even in the daylight yet before they start shooting. My million dollar pearly whites do their job as the cameras click and beep and suck away what’s left of my soul bit by bit. Police officers line the stairs and physically hold back a sea of microphones, news cameras, and individual bloggers, reporters, and civilians just hammering away with their DSLR’s. Capturing every little drop of sweat gleaming on my brow. Johnathan Fitzwillis was dead the second that door opened. Johnny Borealis is all that remains. Joel Owens and Derek Harmon fan out on either side, trying to tell reporters ‘no comment’ for me, as the three of us try to make our way to the waiting limousine. I thought it a touch tacky to bring a limo to a court date, but my jowly agent insisted that it would help my image. Right now it’s an oasis on wheels, ready to shelter me from the blazing heat and drive me away from the circling buzzards. We move at a good clip, but still I feel it... "Johnny! Johnny, can we have a word, please!?" they beg. "Johnny! Do you have any take on the situation?" they plead. "Two minutes, please! Can we just get two minutes and one word out of you?" they bargain. ...the pull is too much. Against my better judgement, and the urging of my advisors, I can feel my feet move slower and slower. We’re at the edge of the sidewalk and I’m pushing myself to just get in the car and drive off, but the situation already has a mind of its own. Johnny Borealis is already turning toward the crowd, their microphones outstretched like the hands of beggars. As my head turns I can see Joel’s pitiful face, his eyes screaming at me to just let it go this one time. But how can I resist? The spotlight’s on me. One step from salvation and Johnny Borealis turns to face the masses. All I had to do was keep my mouth shut and make it from the building to the car. All I had to do was say nothing and drive off, into the sunset, and everything would be done and left behind. And instead I smile, I give in because I’m weak, and I give them what they want. Me. All of me. "A word? A take?" a sick joke, "For you guys, I’ve got all the time in the world!" And I’m on autopilot. Watching from behind my eyes, locked in by the Oakley’s, as Johnny Borealis throws chum to the sharks. Every blurb they could want, every charming anecdote they could need. In a matter of seconds I’ve got them smiling, laughing, playing along like we’re all old friends. Joel and Derek resign themselves to relax a bit, slouching in defeat that they couldn’t do the last bit of their duty. They’d represented me, Joel through public relations and Derek through legal representation, and they’d done a smash-up job. The icing on the cake was to keep me from shattering everything we’d worked for, and they mourn in silence as I melt it all away. Out of the corner of my eye, I see them. I see the family of the man I killed, getting into their minivan and sedan, far away from the media circus. No cameras on them, no microphones, no one asking their opinion. They just keep their heads facing the ground, knowing that the money I’d agreed to pay wouldn’t bring their son or sibling back. Oh sure. Johnny Borealis is giving Johnathan Fitzwillis a little public relations spin of his own. I’m doing this for them, he says. This way they get some privacy, he says. I’ll take the bullet. It would only hurt them, he smirks as he lays out the plan before me. And I wish I could believe a word of it. Keith Jankowski’s family climbs into their meager cars and drive off quietly. And nobody cares. ---- Wait. Did I ever catch you up on ‘Dinner’? She stared at the newspaper clipping in complete shock. Her jaw agape, trying to process what I laid out in front of her. Carried around for weeks, it was my little ‘get of out jail free’ card. A jagged cutout from a newspaper that announced the death of the only man whose testimony could save or ruin the rest of my life. Our meal screeched to a halt and she just stared...not sure what to make of the fact that I’ve been connected to two people who were killed by automobiles. At least I didn’t throw Jeff McCormick in front of the one that killed him. "I-..." Doctor Lynn Nicholai was supposed to have a nice, pleasant meal and give me a psychoanalysis once-over. This knocked the wind out of her sails, "I don’t know what to say." "I’d appreciate it if you straight up told me what to do," I spooned some chocolate mousse into my mouth, "Cuz I have no clue." I could see her try to sift through her thoughts and find the words. Tasked with trying to find out why I’ve been avoiding my defense attorney, Doctor Nicholai was promised a laid back, pleasant dinner. No one hid their intentions...she was supposed to determine if there was something wrong with me, and I was supposed to tell her whether I thought there was something wrong with me. I guess we were supposed to come up with a conclusion that satisfied all parties. What she didn’t expect was for me to whip out a newspaper obituary and tell her that I have no fucking idea what defines ‘the right thing to do.’ Keith hired a private investigator to take incriminating photos of me in order to convince me to stay away from Kate. I found out his private eye broke the law and I hired him right out from under Keith’s nose. Private dick discovers papers that prove Keith handled toxic accounts, accounts which cost thousands of people their homes, but it’s unclear whether he had any idea. I got drunk, had the investigator make those accounts public, and Keith lost everything. I burned my copy of the files from sober guilt. Then I had a rare bout with honesty and told Keith the truth, he started punching me a whole lot, I reflexively threw him into the street...truck smashed him dead. Two days later a random truck kills the investigator. What’s hard to follow? "Mister Fitzwillis," she began with a name that I barely respond to any more, "The only person that can tell you what to do is you." "Figures," with a swirl of some red wine, I gulped it down like a barbarian. "I can offer you someone to talk to, perhaps have regular sessions," she shook her head again, staring a hole through the newspaper clipping that I’d held tightly to for so long. My little dirty secret, "But this is an awful amount of weight for you to carry around. I...I’m sorry. I’m so sorry." I shrugged. As far as I was concerned, the hard part was over. I’d already told her the worst thing in the world, right? Now I just have to wade through the apologies and small talk and wait for her to tell me that I should bring this evidence up at the trial for Keith’s death, that it would clear my name. Or I should wait for her to tell me how bringing all these things to light would only hurt Keith's memory? Things that would only drag his name through the mud some more, that I should keep buried, be happy, raise my daughter, and simply bear the weight. "Weight’s nothing. I’ve got no issue with weight," with the clink of the spoon I pushed the glass of mousse away. She barely touched her turnover, enraptured by the story of a stranger she just met, "I can carry that, you know? I can deal with that." "The fact that you disappeared for three months might beg to differ." "And what do you think I was doing that whole time?" there was no anger in my voice, just a matter of fact, "I dealt. I didn’t want my dealing to weigh down on somebody else." "That works in a bubble, John," her doctor voice returned. The commanding, sexy tones of a woman who is used to directing the conversation. I’d been able to silence the voice in the back of my head that was looking at her like a piece of meat, but against my best wishes it boomed loud in my ears again. I yearned for her to take me somewhere dark, quiet, and secluded and tell me exactly what to do, "In the real world, everything you do affects someone else. Even disappearing." "Oh I know. I have a couple dozen messages from one Derek Harmon," I held up my iPhone and waggled it back and forth, "Those suits of his don’t come cheap." "I was talking about your daughter," she wasn’t amused and chose to drop a bomb, "And Miss Walsh. You think those three months might’ve been tough on them?" "Them? Nah," I downed the rest of my wine and the snarl on my face told her exactly what I thought of her little tactic, "No. Kate just watched her fiance get run over by a truck, then watched the father of her daughter pump on his chest and breathe into the guy’s mouth for ten minutes and get pulled off of him by the police." I could see the regret in Lynn’s eye immediately, but that didn’t mean I was going to let up. Neither did the glances from the restaurant’s high-class clientele. "Why would it scar my daughter to come running out of the house, Doctor? To see that scene," her hand came up as if to tell me to please calm down, but that just flared my anger, "To see her mother wailing in the street. And then I go off to digest what happened and I don’t have to the guts to come back. I leave the two of them at the drop of a hat for the third time in my life." I hadn’t even realized I was standing up, towering over her. We’d begun dinner with her poking, prodding, trying to find her way into what I wouldn’t tell Derek. Well there it was, laid out for the entire restaurant to see. The vulgar details ready for every gossip and eavesdropper to pick apart. Harmon chose a public place to entice me, to make me feel like it was more relaxed than a private session, for a more intimate approach to a once-over screening. "You...tell me, Doc," I almost retched right then and there, the disgust welled up in my throat, "You tell me why my first response is to run away from the two of them when they need me the most." The maitre d’ came over and suggested, none too politely, that it was time for the two of us to pay our check and leave. I don’t think I could have agreed with him more, and just as Doctor Nicholai was pulling out her credit card I slapped down a crude stack of cash. Then I left, because that’s the only thing I’m consistent with. ---- Before that first scene, without all the gunfight metaphors and the bullshit. "I think we can agree that this is better than dragging it out in court," the Jankowski’s lawyer had finished scribbling a number down on a piece of paper and pushed it across the table to Derek. My counsel reached to the middle of the battlefield and lifted up what had been written. Keith’s mother and father sat across from the two of us. Their remaining children were in the hallway, sitting awkwardly next to my agent, who is apparently my only friend in the fucking world. Kate had opted not to attend. A shame, since I made it to her fiance’s funeral...albeit from a distance. With such care that the paper didn’t even crinkle, Derek looked at it for the briefest of moments before holding it out for me to see. My eyes were glued to the newspaper clipping in the middle of the desk, and I didn't need to see the number for the settlement to know what it was. Thirty pieces of silver. The number itself doesn’t matter, it was a man’s life I purchased. It’s all I can do to wave it off and nod. The obituary photo of the private detective, Jeff McCormick, stared back at me. Given the opportunity to save Keith's parents the embarrassment of knowing that he and I traded cloak and dagger stories to squabble over a woman, I instead laid it out before them. Before today it was a question of whether I meant to throw Keith in front of the car, but at least they knew it was because their son was drunk and depressed and I pushed his buttons. Having shown them that newspaper clipping, providing my own testimony to their lawyer, I may have sealed my fate to be at their mercy. But at the same time, I'd smothered any innocence they might have believed their son to have. I'd crushed the last vestige of their boy's reputation, replacing it with a story of jealousy and petty infighting. "My client and I wish to spare the family any further pain and suffering," Derek was completely oblivious to the irony of his words, "If this is agreeable, we can be on our way." Their lawyer leaned over to them. Keith’s mother had been staring a hole in the top of my head the entire time. The single oscillating fan did little to stir the air, and the wronged parents who had to bury their child stewed and steadily drove hatred into me. Amidst the nodding and low voices and signatures and initials, I couldn’t even bear to look at her and actually apologize. ---- The reaper. Cold water cupped in my hands. It hits my face and snaps me back to the here and now. Looking back at High Stakes, over two years ago, it hit me that I was about to get everything I ever wanted. At the time I proceeded to throw up in a trash bin, and tonight it’s been no different. Out there. Waiting. There are tens of thousands of people that need to see me ascend. "You’ve got this, John," a shaking voice is hardly reassuring. I’d survived worse after all, not even that long ago, "You’ve earned this. You’ve earned every single moment of this." It replays in my head. Half a lifetime of pain, of strict dieting, of working out every single day. Half of my life devoted to perfection, to maintaining the body of a man in his prime. Every single day I’ve fought aging and I’ve fought laziness and I’ve fought other people and I’ve fought every want and need that’s ever arisen. Knee surgery, bankruptcy, contract disputes, owing this company over a million dollars, and having to start from the bottom time after time. Every single time getting that much better at it. "No," I don’t talk to myself often. Maybe that’s why my voice sounds so alien to me. Maybe that’s why I barely recognize the face I’ve worked so hard to keep at perfection. Somewhere in an attic there’s a painting of me getting older, "Not the past. Here and now. That’s all there is." If only that were all there is. "John?" Her voice. It cuts through everything in front of me. The mirror might as well splinter, as the face in it cracks apart. She rips me back ten years and she shoves my face into a dead man’s mouth four months ago. The shadow I can’t escape, Kate has made her way to my locker room for the first time in my life. It’s all I can do to hold in the vomit. "It’s you." That’s all I know for sure. "Your..." she points. Her finger circles and dances and she’s trying so hard to find the words, "...the security. They said you’d be back here." I can feel a tear squirm its way out of a duct. Thank God my face is still wet from the sink, and I have the presence of mind to snap to and bury all anguish into a towel. I hold it there in the darkness for a split second too long before I can find the courage to look back up at her. She’s so beautiful. "This is the first time you’ve ever come to see me at work," is the lamest thing I could’ve said. "Thirteen years." "Fourteen," a quick correction "I was wrestling when we first met." "I don’t know if you can count just...running around in a training gym," a half hearted attempt at a laugh pops out of her throat. It wasn’t meant as an insult, so I don’t take it as such. All I can do is stare as her smile fades. I want to hold on to it as long as humanly possible. I want to remember her smiling. "I, um-" her hand touches her mouth, then her eye, and I can hear her sniff a little bit, "Sorry." "Don’t be." I mean it when I say that. "I wanted to call you but..." she bites her lip, cuts herself off from some excuse, "I’m here instead. I thought it’d be best to..." Every time I expect her to fall to pieces she absolutely amazes me. With a clenched fist, Kate swallows whatever grief or anger is welling up and she stares at me plainly. "I brought someone to see you." With that she opens the dressing room door. The light pours in from the hallway and the happiest little girl in the entire world bounds in after screaming "Daddy!" My daughter...absolute perfection. And I can’t even tell that I’m on my knees until she puts her arms around my neck. I bury my nose in her hair and smell what it’s like to be happy. Sarah is telling me all about her day and her week and school and what she’s done since I saw her last and it’s everything I can do to compose myself. A glance up at Kate and she’s smiling like this is always how it was supposed to be. There, on the floor of a musky locker room, a grown man is caught up in the ecstasy of laughing with the love of his life. Moments before I’d been staring in a mirror, trying to bring myself to live in the present and to be where I was. I don’t think I could have ever guessed what that was really like...playing and rolling and hugging my daughter. Pure euphoria. After a few moments our laughter subsides and she’s told me her stories. The smile, a real smile, not worth a million dollars but priceless in every way, is slowly fading from my face. The aftermath of happiness. Sarah has told me all about everything and she turns to her mother, who hasn’t said one word. "Sarah," Kate’s smile is so genuine, I’d have thought that we were all one big happy family for the last ten years, "Your dad’s got to finish getting ready. He’s got a big night tonight. Give him a hug and tell him you love him." Like she needs to be told. "I love you Daddy!" and she squeezes me as tight as she can. "And I-" she’s off the ground and I twirl her in a circle, "Love you, Pumpkin!" I set her down and plant a kiss on her forehead, and she turns and runs into the hallway. Kate watches after her with a smile on her face. Sarah takes a seat in the hallway and Kate lets the door close. Holds her hand on it a moment before turning to me, smiling from a moment passed. "This is so great!" I turn in a circle because I need something to do with all this joy, "After the match I can take you guys for some food. Fuck the media. Alright? I don’t care. This is everything I could-..." Kate’s just staring. "You know, I don’t think for a second that this makes everything alright," snapped back to reality, I’m still responsible for her fiance’s death, "I won’t kid myself. I know exactly where I stand, Kate. I just...God, I haven’t seen her. Or you! And I just-" She shakes her head. Side to side. Very, very slowly. "Look. I’ll do what I need to do," she needs reassurance that this wasn’t a mistake, "I don’t know how, but I’ll make sure Sarah is happy. Whatever it takes, okay? Now I’m gonna be paying off Keith’s-...his parents...I’m gonna be giving them settlement money for a real long time. But that doesn’t mean I can’t save and-and-and get some other income...right? Put it toward...Sarah’s..." Her head stopped shaking. Still as stone, she looks at my eyes like she’s looking for something, anything, that still looks like a person. The silence could kill me. "Kate..." my hand reaches up by itself, and my palm holds her neck right underneath her hair. In another life I would kiss her and we could be happy, "...I’ll make it right. Whatever I need to do, I will make it right." "Come with us." Those three words open up anything that I could ever close off. The words are purer and bring more joy than any amount of ‘love’ or ‘adore’ or ‘need’ phrases ever could. She states it plainly, openly, and the answer is obvious. "Yes!" my arms are around her I hold her close, then I back off and my hands are on each shoulder, "My God yes! I-...absolutely! I don’t care where, I’ll get tickets. I’ll drive. The minute I can get out of here, I-" "No. Now." I was halfway to my bag, ready to pull out my phone and order plane tickets to anywhere. Her words stop me dead and when I turn I can see she’s not smiling. "I...can’t," my eyebrow peaks. She has to know, "I can’t leave. This is...do you know what tonight is?" "Yes. I do. That’s why I chose to come here, tonight." For the first time I can see her jaw shudder. For half our lives she’s watched me flounder and flail. I’ve left her twice, once when I didn’t know she was pregnant and once when our daughter was six years old. She’s been with me at my absolute highest and she’s seen me as low as a man can go. She watched as I threw her fiance into the road, saw as a truck barrelled into him, and stood by shrieking as I futilely tried to keep him from dying. After everything we’ve been through, Kate is capable of forgiving me. It just comes with one gesture. "Kate...I..." everything in me wants to shake her and yell. Why now? She’s had four months to do this. She’s had two years since I returned to GCW. She chooses now? "This is everything I’ve ever worked for, Kate. I...I mean...this is...this is everything I have." My words echo off gray concrete and steel lockers. The stark, cold room in the middle of a strange city, a room that I have to share with strangers that I can barely tolerate...this room is everything I've put in to my life. It's hard, it's empty, and it's work. And it's temporary. "I mean," one hand grabs my boots, "Except my wrestling clothes, of course. Not quite your style-" "John-" "To Hell with John!" My boots get thrown. They hit the mirror and it cracks. In retrospect it’s more symbolic than it needed to be. An eternity passes before her hand is on my shoulder. The same as a moment ago with Sarah, I never even felt myself falling to my knees. But here I am...on the floor yet again, with one of the angels in my life trying to save me. "You and Keith were all Sarah and I had," her voice is ambrosia, her touch is divine, "It was an accident, and you’ve paid your dues. Don’t make me lose you too." A lesser man would hate her for wanting the best of both worlds. The truth is, I left her once and came back. I left her again and came back a second time, except that she’d found someone else. I stuck around, it ended up with him dead, and left a third time. If I were a betting man, I’d say she’s making it clear there won’t be a fourth time. For too long I look up and stare her right in the eye. I need to hold on to this. I can’t let this moment go, because it’s the only time it will ever exist. "Sarah and I are going to our car in the main parking lot," she calmly lays out the rest of my life, "We will drive by the front door in ten minutes. We are going, and we aren’t looking back, but we will take you with us if you’re there." Plain English. Even I can understand that. "Please...darling," I can barely find the words, "Can I see Sarah for a moment-" "You don’t get to do that to her," she’s quick to cut me off, and for the first time her anger flares, "If you choose this...if you choose Johnny Borealis...then her last memory’s going to be a goddamn happy one. A happy one." Her hand leaves my shoulder. I thought Johnathan Fitzwillis died outside of the courthouse, shot to death by cameras while hiding behind a fake million dollar smile and a pair of Oakley’s. That’d be the way to go, wouldn’t it? Blaze of glory and all that shit. But here I am. In an empty locker room, face to face with everything I’ve ever wanted. And out there is everything I’ve ever wanted. And for the first time in my life, it hits me that I can’t be everything to everyone. Johnny Borealis has his first shot at the GCW World Heavyweight Title. Johnathan Fitzwillis has his chance to be happy. "For one moment, please don't think about what you want," her eyes are pure pain, "...Your daughter needs you to do what's right." But her words don't match her face. She says what she needs to say, but the tone she uses is...absent. Hopeful, but not hoping for me to follow through. The door closes, and it's the first time I know what's right. Wrestling versus show business versus staying at home. Letting Keith take my place versus fighting for what was mine. Testifying the private detective or letting Keith's innocence live on. I never knew what was the right choice in all those matters. But this time...this time it's different. Kate raised our daughter for six years without me knowing anything about her. And for six years, she was happy. The harder I tried to be what I wasn't, to be a father to her, the worse I'd become at it. Sarah's ten years old. The meaning behind Kate's words was obvious...if I don't go with them, then I'm not welcome to come find them. Leave them alone. Maybe when Sarah is eighteen, she might decide to come seek me out. But eight years without me in her life...well...she was happy for the first six, wasn't she? Every single fiber of my being tells me that's bullshit. Pick yourself up. Get off the God damn floor, and go find the two women you love. Go. Be happy. ---- But we already know how that turns out. Don’t we. Almost exactly ten minutes later, my foot hits Triumph Frost’s chin with all the force of a life left to rot. Damn near knocks his head clean off. The crowd doesn’t know what to think, and neither do I, but that doesn’t stop me from doing what I’ve spent my life preparing to do. Holding the World Title in my hand, I’d imagined what it would be like to see my reflection in it for the very first time. Jay Terror once told me it was worth everything you’ve ever given up. Jordan White once told me it was empty and that the person that held was what mattered. They’re both wrong. It’s the death knell of a wasted life. It’s the freeing from guilt. Johnathan Fitzwillis is dead. Long live Johnny Borealis.
