The Truth Comes Out

23 April 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4-23-07 Keisha Anderson had worked for the District Attorney's office for most of her adult life, she knew full well as soon as she graduated from college that she wanted to put criminals where they belong ? in prison. With her grades, they considered it a privilege that she would even consider passing up all of that money she could have made being a defense attorney for a huge corporate firm, but they never thought to even question her decision. They knew her past, and knew that if they brought up the fact that she had lost so many loved ones to violence; it would only bring out the worst in her. Jayce couldn't believe it when he saw her walking toward him, it had been so long since they had talked. The day of the funeral for his brother, which happened to be her boyfriend, was the last time he had even seen her. So it was kind of strange to get a message from her to meet him, but he came nonetheless. ?I'm glad you're here,? she reached out and touched his arm with a single finger, her voice was as soft and sensuous as he remembered it to be. ?You said it was important,? he replied, his arm never moved as he felt her touch. ?You look like him,? she said, as a tear trickled down her cheek. It had been hard on her, the loss of Dominic Bradley, and though they grew a part she never forgot how much he loved his younger brother. He always talked about keeping him out of trouble and making sure that he headed down the right path. Jayce turned away from her, the last thing he wanted to do was let those feelings come flooding back into his mind. ?I'm sorry,? she apologized, as she slowly pulled her hand away. ?I know you too were close. All he ever did was talk about you, telling me how proud he was of you, how smart you were. He would've done anything for you.? ?I know that.? ?He would be so disappointed to see how you turned out.? Jayce looked up, his eyes wide and his jaw skewed slightly to the left. ?What?? ?We know all about the Prophecy of Fates,? she had his full attention by this point. ?We've been collecting information on the group for the last two years, but it wasn't until a few months ago that we found out about Russell Braun's affiliation.? ?I don't know what you're talking about.? ?I've seen the pattern, Jayce.? He shook his head, but there was nothing he could do to keep the memories of all of the people that died at the hands of him and his mentor. ?There was a racially motivated murder within a fifty mile radius of each city you wrestled in.? He glanced over at her, some black whore that his brother was probably going to see when he was killed, and he tried his best to keep from doing what he had been trained to do. ?The connection is there, and we have enough evidence to bring you in for questioning. But...? His nostrils flared with each inhale of breath, while the warmth of each exhale brushed against his lips as it escaped through his mouth. ?But what,? he asked through gritted teeth, unsure if this had all just been a set up to get him in a position to be taken quietly and away from the eyes of a stadium full of fans cheering his name. ?We want to take them all down.? ?All,? he asked. She noticed that he had no genuine idea about what she was talking about. ?There's others like Braun, Jayce. Probably like you too, since I doubt you just helped him have an alibi ready if he ever got caught.? ?What others?? He racked his mind as he thought back to when Braun had first taken him to the compound he was trained and raised on. There had been other children his age, and other adults that shared in Braun's beliefs ? his prophecies. ?You really don't know about them, do you?? No,? he responded with genuine ignorance on the 'others', as she called them. A flicker of light somewhere behind her caught his attention, just before a splatter of blood splashed against his face. He hardly felt the sting in his shoulder where the projectile had grazed him. He dropped down as quick as he could, before he looked for a place to duck behind. Son of a bitch,? he screamed as he watched his brother's last love fall to the ground in a heap. Another flash of light caused him to leap to his left, since most people's first reaction was to jump to their right. Professional assassins had been trained to always aim slightly to that side, in order to catch someone who followed their first instinct and made a fatal mistake. He scurried behind a large oak tree that was a few yards away, as pedestrians that had decided to spend their afternoon in the park began to scramble around like chickens with their heads cut off. =?=?=?=?=?= From an empty apartment in a building that had more rats living in it than people, the shooter focused on the duo having a conversation more than two hundred yards away. The scarlet mist that escaped the district attorney's face looked like a cloud of dust and sent a calm sense of satisfaction coursing through the shooter's veins. The shooter paused briefly to catch a glimpse of the man she was raised to admire; the first of her kind, she had been told. He was so small, she thought before she gently squeezed on the trigger. It was hard for her not to take him out first, but he was not the intended target ? not yet anyway. Before the shot, she had taken stock of his features, stared deep into his crystalline blue eyes and followed the lines on his face like a trail on a map. She knew how simple it would have been end his life, but she only continued to shoot in order to send him a message; the family had other plans for their lost child. Big plans. =?=?=?=?=?= Jayce listened for the recoil of another shot, as he breathed deeply in order to regain his composure. ?Son of a bitch,? he muttered as he glanced toward the woman that was just doing her job, as she laid there with a puddle of blood where her face had once been. The sounds of sirens blared in the distance, and if what Keisha had said was true; this was the last place he needed to be with one of the lead prosecutors of the case against him dead a few yards away. The blood that covered his body, most of it her blood as he noticed the flesh wound on his shoulder bled profusely, would be hard to explain. How would they know he had not set her up, if they knew of their past connection as well. It had been a while since the shots had been fired, so as he hoped for the best, he leaped out from behind the tree and made a mad dash for his rental car. There was no way he could be caught, he couldn't allow it to happen. Not now, and not like this. He had to figure out who killed her, who tried to kill him, and he had to do it fast. He had to do it before the World crumbled down around him, but first he had to get away.
