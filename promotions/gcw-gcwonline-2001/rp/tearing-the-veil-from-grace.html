Lowell, West Virginia 12:20pm He could tell from the look on her face as he watched her--lips tens, drawn back in a long, thin line, eyes slightly narrowed--that she was displeased. Had he been in her position, he thought to himself, he probably would have been displeased as well, because this was just another lead that had turned up nothing. That had turned up, literally, dust. A disdaining snort, like that of an untamed horse, passed through her in one motion, accompanied by the tossing of her head. She looked back at him, her eyes blazing indignantly. 'A fine mess, that's what this is,' she snapped peevishly. He didn't have to agree or disagree with her; pronouncements of that sort rarely invited, or warranted comment, especially when he agreed. His cerulean eyes cast downwards, towards the ruins of... whatever this had once been. He presumed it was a church, but that was only because he held in his hands a manila folder that informed them that it had been. The Lowell Falls Church of the Redeemer, to be exact, under whose eaves met not evangelical Christians, as the name implied, but a secret sect of Satanists, deluded into thinking the man that taught them had a direct satellite hookup with the devil. What was left of the place... was nothing, to be frank. A pile of ashes- not even any beams from the roof, no metal- leaving Virgil to believe that the building must have been an old one, if it had no steel beams in its construction, no leftover metal from any part of its destruction. 'Lia,' he said, his eyes catching on something. He pointed her in the direction of something- a pile of ashes laid out in far too uniform a way to simply be debris. Her jade eyes cast over at him in their cutting way for a moment before flickering in the direction he indicated. 'Shit,' she murmured, and with that utterance, took a few steps closer to it. “It” being a sigil, rendered finely in ash on the ground. “A sigil?” he murmured. She cast him an exasperated look. “Yes, a sigil” but what of?” That seemed to be a question without an answer, for the moment at least. Virgil hadn’t spent his early years as a vampire poring over magic books, and frankly, whatever knowledge Lia herself had of sigils had long been replaced by still more esoteric and stomach-clenching knowledge. “There’s another,” he said, breaking the silence and pointing a little further down. Lia, following the line of his finger, took a few more steps due north, trailing along a long row of evenly-sized ash-inscribed sigils. They looked far too perfect to have been done by a human hand, but yet no other interpretation made sense. “They look like the sigils of Solomon’s spirits,” she said, only halfway to herself, and he nodded, “Only I don’t understand why that would have any significance at all to my brother?” Virgil raised an eyebrow. “Given what we know, would it not make sense that perhaps these signs were a part of their ritual work, left behind when the church burned” Only? they would have to have been here after it did so to leave the sigils written in ash?” She shook her head. “Nevermind the end of the story. That doesn’t matter, just that” obviously, if the group house is burnt to a crisp, he’s not here anymore.” He could feel the frustration emanating from her’and it was not because he was so attuned to her emotions, and the small indicators that betrayed them even when she tried hard not to let them through. This time, the emotion was a thick, the tension palpable, the frustration almost painful in its poignance, as though he had a direct satellite linkup to her brain. “Now what?” The question was desperate, desperate enough that he could sense raw anguish coming out of it just from the way she spoke. She shook her head vigorously. “It doesn’t make any sense. Why on earth would he be hanging around a place like this trying to teach backwood hicks the rudiments of Satanism” What personal gain could he possibly get out of a task like that?” Again, silence for a moment. But Virgil shook his head. “I think the question is, what sort of personal gain would his master get out of doing things like this.” “I’m sure I don’t care!” she interjected, her eyes flaming with the indication that she had not quite accepted her brother’s subservience to the master he now served. That would be something she’d have to deal with on her own time, when she was face to face with Laszlo Vlaszy again. But right now was enough, without him having to reminder her of the myriad uses that someone like Laszlo’s master might have for someone like Laszlo. She already knew, and had already considered the terrible possibilities ten times more than she had the good. And that was part of what made their search so earnest, so desperate. Virgil shook his head. “It doesn’t matter.” “You’re right,” she reiterated, “It doesn’t. And we can’t stand around all day trying to figure out what the hell happened here. We’re just going to have to go back home and call it a day” maybe trying to get in contact with Blaise about this development, and see if there’s anything else we can go after next week, after Warpath.” Virgil raised an eyebrow. As far as he knew, this had been the best lead they had, the best lead that presented itself among a cadre of the shady, the shaky, and the outright bullshit information in the file of Laszlo Vlaszy, or as he sometimes styled himself, Vasek Kral. But he didn’t have to remind her of how frustrating this had been, or how frustrating it was likely going to continue being. Lia, sighing and grumbling under her breath, turned from the scene and started back towards her bike. Virgil lagged behind for a moment, his eyes scanning over the sigils, trying to memorize their look, and their order. Perhaps, if nothing else, this would play into something bigger later’some clue that Laszlo had left behind. “Yeah?” He turned at hearing the sound of his paramour’s voice, only to find her holding her cell phone up to her ear, some few yards off. He caught up to her in a few long strides, but even despite his enhanced hearing, could really only hear her end of the conversation. “Blaise” everything’s alright, right?” There was a brief pause. “Good” You needed me for something?... Ah? oh!... Of course, of course. Virgil and I can be there in a few hours? I really appreciate it? alright, nazdar.” With that, she snapped her phone shut, and seemed to notice him standing there for the first time. Her eyes were backlit by a latent sort of fire, her interest piqued again. “We need to go,” she said breathlessly, “Blaise has something that he says he needs to show me, so we’re going to go meet him in Kentucky. About the middle ground between us and them right now.” Virgil bowed his head briefly in assent to her wishes’not that it would have mattered, had he said no. “Lead the way.” That she did, practically flying back down the path to her waiting Ducati. He shook his head, smiling somewhat ruefully’certainly a luxury he rarely allowed himself. She’d be hard to keep up with today, he could tell from the frenetic energy that was practically radiating off of her. But, he supposed it was understandable’had he been able to so easily lay hands upon the answers to the questions about his past, he no doubt would have acted just the same as she did. .:: ::: ::. Bernard, Kentucky Days Inn Conference Suite Breathlessly, Lia pushed her way into the suite, which had had an extra key-card waiting for her, upon Blaise’s request to the front desk. He was seated on the couch as she practically whirled in, followed at a more stately pace by Virgil. She bowed her head slightly in greeting. “I apologize if we’ve kept you waiting for too long.” Blaise shook his head. “Nah” Only been here the better part of two hours, and you guys were coming from a ways away. I had some things to get caught up on myself. Have a seat? you too, Virgil.” He gestured to the chairs surrounding the immediate area where he was sitting, as well as the seat next to him on the sofa. Lia sat down next to him, still keeping a somewhat respectful distance, and Virgil perched himself on the arm as he folded his arms over his chest’as always, a presence apart from everyone else in the room. “I called you here,” Blaise said, “To look at this?” He gestured towards his laptop, leaning over to tap a button. The screen lit with a halfway blurry image, which then lept into movement. On the screen she could see what appeared to be a wide angle view of some large building. Something was swarming over a body that wore a uniform like Blaise did when dressed for hunting. But these things were tearing it to shreds. One of them looked towards the camera, Lia recognized them... Nosferatu Vampires. Considered to be nothing more then feral beasts by many of her kind. She looked as suddenly something jumped down from high up, he landed in between all of them and then spoke. “J” m’t zpr’va z v??e otec...” He spoke with a strong Eastern European Accent as he pulled out two things from their sheaths on his thighs. Both looked like a large hand dagger, with a protective backing to where his hand gripped them. But two other serrated blades came out from each side, making it a three bladed terror on each hand. He moved like a professional, deftly dispatching each and every one of the other vampires with the unique weapons. It took less then thirty seconds, and they were done. He then turned his attention onto the camera and Lia heard Blaise speak. “Well, have to say thanks. Would be rude otherwise.” The man answered in what could only be Czech, but it was low and out of the volume range of the microphone. “What?” He gave a look of disgust and then tried to speak English. “I've come to speak to you. Tell me know, what do you want with the Vampire?” Again Blaise answered, showing that he had to be the one that was wearing the camera. “Same thing you wanted, apparently. They were breaking the rules and tempting fate with displaying their nature to normals. The Order tries to prevent that.” The man shook his head, it wasn't the right answer from the look on his face.. “Not these... beasts. No, the Vampire named Shadow. What is your relationship with her?” Blaise's answer was a bit more serious. Lia could make out the shift in the picture that showed that Blaise had shifted his stance. With his movement, her entire body tensed. “Professional, purely professional. What's it to you?” The camera dipped to look at Blaise's hands, as if he was making sure his blades were in his hands and ready. “That does not explain things enough for me, Lupine.” “Well, you obviously know more about me then I do you. If you know that, then you would know about my relationship with her, and you'd not need to ask me.” “You are resisting me?” ? There was a brief cut, as though the video had been edited to show only the pertinent pieces. Lia swallowed, hard. **CRASH!** The large glass window at the top of the back wall crashed in. A single figure came into the hanger and landed on his feet. The man who fought with Blaise backed off a bit. The newcomer Lia didn't recognize, but he wore the same uniform that Blaise and Ukyo usually did. However, Virgil said something under his breath. “Malik...” The new man on the scene spoke to the first one. “Lazslo, long time no see. Why would you have your leash off and be over here?” The man that he'd just addressed as Lazslo nodded in recognition to him. “Personal, I needed information from your man here. But he has been resistive in giving it to me. I see you're still your father's lapdog.” Both men started doing what would be a macho posturing. Even on digital film, the air about them both seemed to shift as if the very reality around them was in a fight to tear to pieces. “Lord Korwyn Malik, you know that you don't want to fight me right now. The last time we met, we set caused Mount Pelee to erupt. I don't think this area would like it if we started warping things to fight. Instead of thousands, there are millions here.” Malik nodded. “Well, I'd have to agree. But if you persist in your mission now, that's exactly what will happen.” The man named Laszlo put his weapons down and backed up. “You have convinced me, for now. But know that I'll not take kindly to anyone interfering in my mission. Including your hunters.” The man then turned and with his strange powers, slowly faded away as he walked. Lia sat up ramrod straight, her fingernails clenching into her knees. “I?” It was impossible to come up with articulate words at the moment, impossible to completely stem the tears of relief and recognition’it had been so long. “My brother?” Blaise nodded. “So you can confirm then, that this is The Black King, this is your brother?” She nodded vigorously. “Yes, it’s him” I? When was this?” “Couple of weeks ago,” Blaise responded, his tone flat and carefully measured, ??and since you can confirm his identity that brings us to the next step.” She looked at him, plainly puzzled through all the high emotion. “The next step?” Blaise nodded. “Someone’s here for you to talk with.” At that moment, a slim, well-put-together figure stepped into the room, dressed in an ash-gray business suit, her long dark hair tied back from her face. “Aine “Annie” Leanan, Princess of the Unseelie court, and Head Researcher fer th' Order o' the 'Ammer. I'm th' one who ye've been talkin' t' t' get things done.” she said, by way of introduction, and offering Lia a hand. “Lia Vlaszy,” she said, somewhat flatly as this all started to hit bottom and make sense to her. The gravity of the situation was beginning to overshadow anything else that was going on at the moment’her brother, right there, that close to Blaise, who was sitting next to her on the couch at the moment? “Well, no reason to sit and get gloomy,” reminded Annie’s somewhat cheery voice, breaking through her thoughts and pulling her back to the present, “Come with me, Ms. Vlaszy, you’ve got to meet Korwyn.” “Korwyn?” she said, standing up and casting a gaze back at Blaise that said plainly “I’m not comfortable with this.” Blaise only nodded. But Virgil spoke up. “Korwyn Malik” He's a vampire hunter. And a good one at that. He's better then an entire cell of the Den put together.” Something about that name and the information made her slightly queasy. Especially when Blaise spoke up. “He should be. He's one of the few things on this world that's older then you. And the only first generation Vampyle that's survived. That's why, like Laszlo, he's got the Mark about him.” That didn't seem to help, but Annie spoke up to calm them all down. “Virgil, I give me word t' King Oberon an' Queen Titania, tha' no 'arm will come t' Lia. Korwyn is under strict orders from 'is father t' fill ye in. We ga' t' much crap goin' on 'ere riogh' now. We need t' all get on th' same page 'ere. Lord Malik won'na even 'ave 'is blades on 'im. An' I'm sure Lia can resist the allure o' a vampyle. 'E'll na be makin' a gobshite ou' o' 'imself 'er else 'e'll be speakin' t' Lord Ishram.” Virgil’s face sunk into an uneasy, cornered glare. Lia, fearless as usual, only nodded that she would go along. .:: ::: ::. Bernard, Kentucky Days Inn Conference Suite Feeling like she was sleepwalking, she followed Aine Leanan down the hall towards one of the conference rooms. Aine turned back to look at her, and smiled slightly. “Ye’ll 'ave t' pull yerself together befer ye go in t' talk t' Korwyn. I’m afraid 'e’s rather 'ard on your kind.” Those words pulled her back together. Her eyes hardened into an icy jade once again, and she drew herself up to her full height. Rather than the emotionally husky tone she had first greeted Annie with, now she seemed possessed by a cold composure, her voice hard and needle-sharp. “Why should I trust him” What will make him talk to me if he hates my kind so much?” At that, Annie smirked. “Tha’s wha' I’m 'ere fer. T' make sure tha' everything goes alriogh. 'is father ordered this if I needed it. So 'e'll be a good boy... I 'ope.” She opened the door to the conference room, and gestured with her free hand. “In ye go.” Lia stepped in, her eyes immediately fixing on her adversary. He sat behind a table, his arms folded. He had an ancient elegance about him, and a deep, Arabic complexion with dark, thick hair’combined with a thick crop of muscles that were evident even underneath his uniform, that made him quite handsome. And from the cocky way he stretched his legs out as though no one else was in the room but him, she guessed he knew it. As she got closer to him, at Annie’s bidding, she could see the air shimmer about him, electric and fluid, almost as though the molecules were shying away from his presence. It lent his outline a hazy halo that almost made his presence in the room seem unreal. “Korwyn, this is Shadow. Shadow, Lord Korwyn Malik first son of Ishram, and Colonel of our Hunter forces.” Korwyn looked at her, unashamedly glanced her up and down for a moment, at which he couldn’t help sneering. “You expect me to trust in an alias?” “Whether er na' she tells ye 'er real name is up t' 'er.” Lia fixed her pure, cold eyes on him. “Lia Vlaszy.” Korwyn arched a thick brow. “Vlaszy, hm” I know you. Barronessa. The sister to the pawn? Annie, you didn’t tell me I’d have such an honor, speaking with Cain’s filth so closely. A pity I’m unarmed.” The catlike yellow of Lia’s irises flared as her pupils narrowed. “You wear his mark yourself,” she said, her voice low, growling, and dangerous. “Blasphemy!” Korwyn spat, banging his hand down on the table, his face finally discomposing itself from its fine lines to show annoyance, “Do not profane my father’s name in such a manner!” “Nor you mine,” was the soft, composed rejoinder. “Know you this, our sin was not that of murder, but of questioning a god that was too forgiving,” Malik said with daggers. The standoff was two demons, glowering at one another across the table, a baring of teeth by two ancients, one far more so than the other. Annie sighed. “Alriogh', that’s enough o' tha'. If the two o' ye sit 'ere snarling old prejudices at each other, all day, it’s going t' take two more centuries t' finish this conversation.” Lia composed her features once again. “What am I to tell her?” Korwyn said, looking boredly down at his fingernails. “About my brother,” Lia said, answering him directly with a defiant and stubborn tone. “Your brother?” he said, his brown eyes switching up to hers, and practically sucking her in, “Ah yes, Cain’s fetch-puppy.” She snarled, but Korwyn remained impassive. “Did you want the truth” Or did you want me to tell you of his noble service? He serves Cain the way a dog would serve its master, running his errands for the old man who is too lazy and feeble to rise from his own throne to do it himself.” Lia shook her head. “That I refuse to accept about my brother.” “Well, it’s the truth, whether you accept it or not.” “My brother has too much pride to submit to being a simple errand boy.” Korwyn stood up, pacing around his side of the table to approach her, who had had the impertinence to refuse to sit down to speak with him. He peered closely into her eyes. “I would presume you insane, but perhaps you are just ignorant. Ignorant of how your so-called father would treat his children if he could get them all to stop fighting amongst each other and do his bidding” or perhaps just ignorant of who your brother really is, and what you may rightly expect from him.” “I have never met the founder of my own line, much less the father of my kind. How could I claim anything but ignorance” No one knows of Cain’s true nature.” Korwyn arched a brow. “Save your brother, who wears his mark more strongly now than when he was a lowly mortal.” Lia, for a moment, was baffled by this remark. How could Laszlo possibly be more deeply connected to Cain than she herself was, especially if they were sired by the same man, who had certainly had not the ability to alter his own generation? As though reading her mind, Korwyn continued. “You are wondering, I’m certain, how that is possible. It is certainly possible when you are sired by the father himself.” She couldn’t keep her jaw from dropping. Of all the things? no, that couldn’t be possible. She almost thought she saw a smile come over Korwyn’s face, self-satisfied and cocky. He took a step closer to her, so that they were no more than two steps apart. “Have I helped you on your quest, Bloodsucker” But if it's any consequence or comfort. He's not much of an errand boy unless that errand includes killing important beings or people. Or lots of them. He is the Hand of Cain, but not the hand of peace. But he is his hand of war and death. He is as I am from my own father, the man who questioned the god of the Jews.” As she watched, slowly the halo around his outline stretched convex, swelling towards her. On its surface, there were vague reflections, the room around them, her own face, but deeper than that. Detailed tiny depictions of figures, some tormented, others writhing to rise away. It looked almost as though she could have put her finger through the bubble and stepped inside a world that would have contained only herself and Korwyn, no Annie, no physical surroundings, nothing but him? Slowly, her fingertips stretched out to touch the surface, so intricately detailed, and still swelling towards her. She felt like she was reaching across an infinite void, stretching so slowly through the thick air that she would never get there. Her fingertips grazed it, and she closed her eyes, absorbing a great, sharp shock that went through her body. “KORWYN!” Annie’s voice brought her back to reality, broke the spell. Lia’s head snapped to the side almost involuntarily, to see the pretty face of Ms. Leanan split with annoyance. “What th' fok do ye think ye're tryin' to do” Aren’t ye th' big man, Lord Malik, tryin' t' intimidate 'er with yer little light show?” She took a couple of strides towards them, intending to pull Lia away. Korwyn smiled, finally, a cold and calculating look. “The child seemed rather interested in it. For all her worldliness, there certainly seems to be much she hasn’t seen. A dangerous way to be in her condition, when meddling with beings so much more powerful than herself?” “I don’na see ye volunteering fer the charitable task o' teachin' 'er either. She 'as opposed.” “Why should I?” he said, following Annie’s remark immediately, “What consequence is it to me if another one of Cain’s foul brood dies by the hand of a better” Even the better if by the hand of one of my own?” He trailed off into a grin. Annie’s face blanched pale with anger, and she grabbed Shadow by the wrist with a grip that was incredibly vise-like. Annie looked at Korwyn as she held Shadow, “Yer a bastard ye know tha'? We're on th' verge o' a war. But na' th' one yer used t'. This is some 'ard shite. Cain 'as released 'is 'and on th' world. An' Cassius an' 'is little society o' zealots are preparin' fer somthin' big. She's fought The Den befer, an' she's 'elpin' Blaise ou'. Ye need t' get the two thousand year old chip off yer shoulder!” Annie, turned her attention onto Lia, pulling her chin up to look her in the eyes, “Never you mind him, Ms. Vlaszy. I think we’re done here.” Annie started to pull her out of the room, but Lia struggled in her grip, turning back to Korwyn. “Wait! Laszlo” where is he?” Annie paused, but didn’t let go of her prisoner’s arm. Korwyn shrugged disinterestedly. “What do I know, or care” If I meet him again, we will finish the battle that we started many years ago? And as for you, I’m sure I’ll be seeing your pretty little face again as well.” It sounded like a veiled threat, but Lia’s head was spinning far too quickly to try to comprehend it at the moment, and she didn’t much care, being that she was still too far away from her personal goal to care what Korwyn Malik thought of her, her bloodline, or her kind as a whole. Frustrated tears clouded her eyes and she stumbled as Annie led her. Annie turned back, then paused, seeing her upset. “Wha’s wrong?” Lia glanced at her almost suspiciously for a moment. “It’s no use to know what he is if I don’t know where to find him” I know he’s alive, but it’s not enough to see his face on a digital screen?” For some reason, the stricken countenance of this semi-charge made Annie’s own feelings bend towards sympathy ever so slightly. “We, er I should say I, 'ave ways of keeping track o' 'is real location.” “How?” Annie let her loose, for the moment trusting that Lia would listen to her as she walked down the hall back towards the suite. “Power spikes. When 'e manifests 'is abilities, it’s almost like 'e creates a vacuum around him for a period of time, until th' energy can regenerate. He’s been localized in the US Southeast these past few weeks.” That realization hit Lia like the shot of a cannon. ??And so have I?” Annie smiled, slightly grimly. “Maybe you’re not the only one looking for a twin.” .:: ::: ::. The Maryland Inn Annapolis, Maryland A bed had never looked so inviting as it did now. Virgil and Lia had chosen Annapolis as their resting point, close enough to their destination that it wouldn’t be impossible to get there when the time came, but far enough away that they were relatively assured to meet no one they had no wish of seeing’and the people who actually knew where they were staying ran no risk of being among that number. The Queen-sized suite they had rented had a large, four-poser antique bed, which had perhaps been half the enticement of staying there. The other half, at least for Lia, had been an antique desk and work area, large enough to spread out her things and get some thinking done. Thinking wasn’t on her mind at the moment’the situation with her brother had taken up so much time lately, and so much energy just today, that she moved towards the bed with near the mechanics of a robot, wanting only a place to lay her head until it was time to get up again. Virgil likewise followed her in, not so tired himself, but certainly ready for some time alone with her, which he seemed to so rarely have these days, whether they were among the GCW rabble or out on their own. His intelligent blue eyes read her movements as she started unlacing the black velvet corset she’d been wearing all day’she was near exhaustion, he thought to himself, and a swell of pity rose within him as he leaned over the bed to help her. That is, until the annoying ring of her cell phone, and its accompanying angry wasp noise as it vibrated against the wooden desk arrested his hands halfway there. He let them drop onto the thick coverlet as she answered, and fell to examining its navy eyelet lace pattern, in somewhat of a snit at the idea of pressing business conveying her away from him yet again. “Hello?” she asked, hardly sounding herself, ?” Oh, hi Dani? No, you didn’t wake me up, in fact, I just walked in the door.” His visage fell further into a shadowed storminess. So much for having any time alone with her tonight. ??Yes, well, I’ve been out all day and I’m a little tired, so I could meet you at about nine tomorrow morning? It won’t?... I see? Well? I’d be coming from Annapolis, so it would take a little while, but I can be there in? forty-five minutes tops? Will that work?... Good. I’ll see you then.” She flipped her phone shut and sat down on the edge of the bed. A moment of silence went by before she groaned under her breath and flopped backwards onto the mattress with a lack of grace that surprised him. “Virgil,” she mumbled, half insensibly. “I’m really, really sorry.” She sounded so piteous that it was almost hard for him to stay annoyed. His hand crept across the bed and stroked the side of her face. “Cara mia, don’t worry about it” I understand.” He could hardly believe that the words were coming out of his own mouth, not as much as he’d actually wanted her to be able to rest tonight. “I have to go meet Danielle in D.C. She called me about a meeting that her grandfather wants me to have with him.” “Grandfather?” Virgil asked, a hint of scorn in his voice that he seemed to have great difficulty repressing at any given time, “Why can’t it wait?” She shrugged, somewhat difficult a gesture lying down. “I don’t know. But, they are our allies, and it’s my vow to come when needed or called. You can stay here. Get some rest.” She forced her tired limbs up from the bed and reached up to the top of her corset to pull the laces tight again, wearied by the idea that it could be hours upon hours more before she could take it off and lay down for some rest. But that was how her life worked, some days’ricocheting from no information whatsoever, a trickling stream that contained tidbits of nothing to a roaring river, awash with rapids of activity, and between them, deceptive amounts of information lodged in the silt underneath the navy depths. .:: ::: ::.
