Ballad of Bagwell: The Beach, The Bitches, & The Beer; Act II

8 March 2009

Date: Written: March 8, 2009 ACT II SCENE I _______________ TWO DAYS AFTER WORLDWIDE 107 "Welcome to McDonalds! What can I get for you today?" I stood before a sixteen or seventeen year old boy, staring off up at the many menus above his head. In between the golden arches were various types of hamburgers and about a billion different choices of chicken. But as hungry as I was, I just couldn't seem to concentrate on any of the menus long enough to make a decision. "Sir," the register boy said to be with question in his tone. His voice wasn't very hard spoken or commanding. Come to think of it, he actually sounded like a little girl and for that I decided it be best to not even acknowledge that he was trying to get my attention. Naturally, I received a chorus of sighs from the customers in line behind me. Normally, I would care and try to hurry through with my ordering but this wasn't normally. The people behind me had to be pushing three-fifty and had to have been packing about ninety six percent body fat. They were absolute slobs and they were sighing because they couldn't get their greasy, fast food fast enough. In my eyes, I was just doing them a favor -- I was giving their metabolism a chance to catch up before the gorge themselves again. Ah, who am I kidding. I'd probably react the same regardless of who was behind me. But that's not the point. "I'll have a number ten. Medium, please!" Rebecca nudged me to hurry along and even so much as placed her order to put some additional pressure on me. I think she was worried about getting her arm chewed off by the elephant family. "That's a ten piece nugget meal; and what would you like to drink?" "A sprite will be fine," Rebecca responded joyfully. "Any sauce for the nuggets?" "No thanks!" After about forty questions, the register boy glanced back over at me. I suppose he was trying to insinuate that it was my turn to order, with that stupid looking maroon visor on his head. My eyes continued to scroll the menu above the counter before I heard another grunt. Obviously, I immediately made my decision, "Well I guess I'll have that double quarter pounder meal. And I hope to God it's the last one so that Free Willy and Miss Piggy behind me can starve to death and start eating themselves." The boy's face dropped. I don't think he was quite expecting a response like that. There was an awkward silence for about thirty seconds before he was able to read the next line off his cue cards, "a-a-and what would you like to d-drink with that, sir?" My guess was that he was getting nervous. I'm sure Free Willy had a look of abomination dancing all over his face by this point and the register boy was well in tune with the reality that he'd never be able to break up a fight if one broke out. Rebecca nudged me again, probably wanting me to knock it off. "What's that commercial you always see Jessica Simpson in," I asked with the utmost rhetoric. "Was it ProActive?" Since the brainiac behind the counter couldn't catch where I was going, I had to embarrass him. "Have you ever tried that, buddy? I mean - for the landmines on your face? Better yet, do you even wash your face with soap, because it looks like you use the chicken grease from the bottom of the pans back there." "HE'LL TAKE A SPRITE, TOO!" Rebecca shouts. Without saying another word, the little McDonalds minion rings up our order and points at the display screen to show us our total. He's too ashamed to even open his mouth again - and I really don't blame him. His face really was pitiful and the fact that he's working the register at a McDonalds doesn't particularly help his cause. I couldn't imagine how treacherous his sex life must be. Rebecca throws a twenty dollar bill on the counter just as soon as another employee carries our tray of food over. "Keep the change," she tells the counter clerk to my dismay. She snatches the tray up and hands it to me, then proceeds to grab my arm and pull me away. "I can't wait until we end up with spit in our food, Chris!" Before I can even think of responding to her, I lock eyes with Free Willy who is finally able to place his order. I lean in to him and whisper a bit of advice, "I heard the fried whale is pretty good here" Becky rips me by the arm, nearly causing me to drop the tray. "I'm surprised he didn't just rip your head off your shoulders," she say softly. "I'm not. He's too hungry to jeopardize getting thrown out of here. I betcha I could slap that fat sonuvabitch across the face and he still wouldn't step an inch out of line." And then I thought to myself, "although an inch really wouldn't put him out of line at all." "You're such an ass," she says with a smile. "That poor kid at the counter looked like he was about ready to piss his pants." "Good!" I shouted with my utmost sincerity. "That kid's a little terd. I bet you every night he goes home and wonders to himself why he always gets picked on - even by complete strangers! It's suckers like him, who don't speak up for themselves, that allow guys like me to thrive." I try making it a point to speak loud enough so that the register boy can hear me. He doesn't really react to much though, so I just assume he does for my own peace of mind. "And who have you bullied around," Rebecca challenges. "Mister tough guy!" "Dan Black, for one," I say without the slightest hesitation. "The dumb Jew might be big, but he's still a little pussy. He tries to compensate his size for his lack of balls. He thinks that by being big, it'll intimidate others from messing with him." "Poor Dan," she sympathizes. "He's sweet." "Case in point. You're saying somebody that calls himself a Bulldozer is sweet. Tell me that isn't an oxymoron." Finally, I decide to indulge myself with my five star rated McDonalds double quarter pounder with cheese. Even as the first bite slides down my throat, I can already feel my stomach hurting; the grease coating my insides; and the gas building up in my ass. You've gotta love fast food. No sooner am I able to take a sip of my soda do I get a nice bump in the backside. Although it wasn't much, it was still enough to send some dribble down my shirt. Rebecca's eyes light up as I snap my head around. Who else do I see but Free Willy and Miss Piggy scooting on by with enough food to feed the entire country of South Africa. "You fat fuck, watch where you swing your third stomach pal!" I guess it was the straw that broke the camel's back because fat boy slammed his tray on the table and started marching towards me. If I was the register boy (or even Dan Black for that matter) and wanted to run, I sure as hell had plenty of time. "Ughh, Chris," Rebecca grunted. She was anything but pleased that I had to ruin our classy date to Mickey D's. "You got a problem pal," the chunky monkey mumbled to me. I rolled my eyes at him, trying so hard to not laugh in his face. "Whaddya think that you come over here acting all big that I'm going to say 'No, sir, no problem at all?' I sure do have a problem, tugboat. You just knocked my soda all over my favorite shirt." The reality was that it was by far not my favorite shirt. As a matter of fact, I hated the fucking thing. It was just some cheesy white t-shirt with the GCW logo poorly slapped on the front. It was a 'signing perk' that Stevey-boy tossed my way when I signed my contract a year ago. The damn thing looked like the register boy's friend printed it off the computer and ironed it onto a cheap white shirt from the dollar store. But what fun would it have been if I told the fatass that I didn't mind. "Well maybe you should quit making comments about me and my girlfriend and you won't end up with a wet t-shirt," he instructed. Looking at this guy's four chins made me want to vomit. But he was so pronounced. He stood in front of me like he was next month's centerfold for GQ magazine. You had to give the guy some credit, I suppose. He was sure as hell confident with his body - or mass, rather. So I decided to give him a consolation prize. "Ah, I get it man. I'm so sorry. Here I thought you guys were grunting at me on line before because I was taking too long. But now I get it..." Rebecca quickly started to gather her things. She knew me well enough to know this wasn't going anywhere good. "You were just having a hard time breathing. I know the feeling, buddy. Standing in one place for too long really winds me, too. So here - as a token of new friendship - this is for you." And so I took my half-eaten double quarter pounder with cheese and plopped it into his hand. "It's a double quarter pounder. But since I ate half of it, I guess that makes it just a regular quarter pounder now." I thought I was being thoughtful by giving the rest of my food to the hungry, but apparently Shamu thought differently. He let my burger drop to the ground and splatter all over the floor. "You mother fucker," he shouted as he whirled his left hand around at me. I slipped off to the right side of my chair and watched him stumble forward. I then gave the big man a nice hard inner elbow to the back of his head, forcing him to tumble all the way forward and hit the floor. Meanwhile, Rebecca scampers out the side door and heads right to our car to get it started. "See what you did, fat boy? You just made a complete and utter fool out of yourself in front of your girlfriend, a couple strangers, and pimple face with that stupid visor over there at the register. Was that really worth it?" I could see one of the employees racing over to the phone from the corner of my eye. Either he was calling animal control to report an escaped rhinoceros or he was dialing the police. I wasn't in the mood to gamble, so I made my final mark. Fat boy was getting up angrier then ever. It was the perfect opportunity to add insult to injury, "Well I've gotta jet, Big Mac. Normally this is the part where I'd sexually assault your girlfriend or tell you how I'm going to fuck her until she bleeds - but to be quite honest I think her love handles are a few hundred inches too big!" A sticky something was instantly wailed at the back of my head, probably from Miss Piggy. I saw Rebecca in the car ready to go, so I quickly bounced out of there. On my way out, Fatty tried making one last swoop at my feet but obviously that didn't come close to working. We whipped out of the lot, second time we were on the run in two weeks, only to spot sirens about a mile down the road heading in our direction. Another great adventure and another close call! SCENE II _______________ NOVEMBER 1993; HIGH SCHOOL Preface: Chris Bagwell began his high school career only two months early during that September. Although he was confused at where he'd fit in, he managed to make friends with who many considered to be one of the more popular kids in the school. Yoko Shorskiney was a junior jock on the varsity football team. He was well-respected by all of his teammates for being such a dynamite cornerback and wide-out. After Chris mouthed back at him in the weight room on the first day of school, Shorskiney took a keen interest in him. He claimed to have liked Chris because of how he presented himself - afraid of nothing. While in reality Chris was petrified, Shorskiney decided to take him under his wing and show him the ropes. He wanted to teach him how to really handle high school. It was two months since I started my freshman year as a young, naive fourteen year old. To my surprise, my first day went significantly better then I'd ever imagined it to be. Not only did I manage to get myself on the freshman football team, but I got in good with probably one of the most popular guys in the school. Yoko had been showing me the ropes on how to not only survive high school, but how to command it. He taught me that when you walk, you should always do so at a slow and casual pace and that you should always stare the world in the face. He said that it displayed confidence; that you're telling everyone else you're ready for anything. He also told me that when I talk to people, to lean back and talk in a soft yet stern voice. That it would draw them to lean in closer to me and really pay attention. He said it gives you control over conversation as well as gives you a higher personal value. A lot of what he'd filled me in on dealt with posture and portraying confidence. Confidence was never really something I lacked, but it was always something I had trouble showing. I always assumed that showing confidence would make you cocky and people weren't usually attracted to cocky folk. I didn't want people to hate me because they thought I was too cocky. I wanted everyone to like me and say "Wow, that guy is really cool" every time they saw me. But my dream was far from a reality. Towards the end of the first marking period, Yoko and I were spending some additional time in the weight room to work on my build. He'd essentially taken me under his wing and started training me to become a dynamite cornerback for the freshman football team. Ultimately, his goal was to get me to start on Varsity in my sophomore year. A stretch, yeah, but if anybody could've pulled it off it would have been him. "Make sure you keep your chest straight and your back slightly arched," he shouted at me as I struggled to bring the weighted bar down to my pectorals. "Thatta boy!" "So I Yoko," I said to him with the most curious intent. "You really think I've got a shot at starting varsity next year?" I was eager and willing to learn - everything you needed to become successful, right? "Well," he said. "You definitely have the athleticism but I don't quite think you've got the attitude yet." Apparently my entire perception of what it took was wrong. "You're a nice kid, Bagwell," he went on. "But nice ain't gonna getcha very far. You gotta learn to take charge of everything you do and really dictate what everyone else around you does." I wasn't exactly sure where he was going with it. I mean, I did get the notion that he wanted me to be in control of every situation but I couldn't figure out how one would even achieve something like that. It wasn't like I was in a relationship and the world was my girlfriend, where I could simply tell her "Sit down, Bitch" and with the flick of a backhand she'd be on the ground. There was something more to it. Something that just wasn't as clear in the eyes of a fourteen year old. "You don't have a clue what I'm talking about, do you?" He could see right through me - I was like a set of open blinds. "I get what you're saying," I studdered. "I mean, like I know what you're talking about. I just don't think I get how you're supposed to pull that off." His smirk said it all for me. If a picture was worth a thousand words, then his smirk was worth ten thousand. It told me everything I needed to know. Well, I guess not everything, but it did tell me I was about to get an eye-witness account of exactly what he meant. "Watch me," he instructed. There he went already, controlling my actions. Yoko strut across the weight room and I watched as the other kids sort of stopped what they were doing. Although he didn't say a word, his body language screamed that he was about to make a scene. Of course, he picked out the smallest, skinniest kid in the weight room to stop in front of. The kid was struggling on a weight machine and really giving it his all to get in shape. Despite the fact his posture was completely off - you had to give him credit for even being there in a room full of jocks. "You done?" Yoko asked. But his demeanor was anything but inquiring. He wasn't actually asking the kid if he was done, he was telling him he was done. "Just one more set of ten reps, bro, and I'll be all finished," the kid answered. Yoko smiled and shook his head as the kid began his final set. "One," Yoko began counting. "Two. Ten." He grabbed hold of the weight bar and held it with a single arm, stopping the kids workout. "Thanks, pal. Nice workout," he shouted. The kid looked like a scounded puppy. He slipped off the machine and walked away with his head lowered. You know damn well that he was absolutely livid inside, but what was he really going to do? Yoko sat down at the machine and then dropped the ringer. "Actually," he said. "I don't think I want to use this machine anymore." He stood up, just as the other kid was escaping to the locker room, and walked away leaving the machine unattended. I was in utter shock. Yoko had completely punked out that kid for no apparent reason. However, I didn't really see how it influenced anyone else. That was until Yoko stopped off at another machine on his way back to me. "Are you done here," he stated to another kid. "Y-y-yeah," the kid mumbled. "Just finished up." I knew the guy wasn't finished because I just watched him sit down at that very machine. He was afraid and so was every other person in that weight room. That's what really got me the most. We were in a room full of probably ten or twelve people and they all seemed to fear Yoko. But the thing is, if a fight ever broke out there was no way Yoko could take all ten or twelve of them. It was a simple numbers game - but apparently people don't think in those terms. A classic example of who would take the fall first. "Forgot it, chump," Yoko encouraged. "You need that machine more than me. If you spend any less time on it, you're going to start looking like the Pillsbury Dough Boy." The lad smiled the fakest of smiles a person can wear. He even so much as forced out a small chuckle; so bad that it sounded like a squealing pig or something. "So you want me to be a dick to people," I asked as Yoko finally strut back over to me. "What'd I tell you on the first day of school?" I wasn't sure if he was being rhetorical or if he really wanted me to answer, so I took a steady pause to be on the safe side. "I'm going to train you in the art of deuchebaggery." Thankfully it wasn't the latter or I would've seemed like an idiot. "You see," he went on to explain, "when you belittle others, you're capitalizing on their insecurities. When you do that, you're putting yourself in a position of control because you're making it out to seem like you're perfect. And even if you're far from perfect, you're pounding the idea into their heads that you're the most ideal a guy can get." It sounded stupid but it kind of sounded true. "What if they don't have any insecurities," I asked stupidly. "Come on Bagwell," he sighed. "Everyone's got 'em. Whether it be a little chunk, a couple pimples, a hairy chest, or a funny odor coming from their balls. All you gotta do is find something and harp on it and those people will do anything you want with the hope that if they please you, you'll stop mocking them." I didn't have much to say so I just nodded my head. It kind of made sense in a twisted sort of way. I could sort of relate to what he was saying, too. There had always been times where I felt a little awkward in different situations and the best way to break that feeling was with a joke. In this case, Yoko was just saying to use somebody else as that joke. Cruel, maybe. But this was high school - hell it was life. Survival of the fittest, right? "There will even come a point when you'll do it just to get a kick out of the reactions you get. And that's not a bad thing because then you're making a statement to everybody else around you. As long as you keep that same wittiness you showed me on the first day of school, you'll be golden," he added. He brought up a good point - I already had it in me. Although I didn't plan on snapping back on him on the first day, I still did it and the entire weight roomed 'ooh-ed' and 'awww-ed' at me. I took command over the whole room in an instant. I can definitely still remember what that felt like and I certainly remember liking it. It was all about creating higher personal value; making you feel better about yourself at the expense of others. I surveyed around the room watching everyone else constantly looking over their shoulders as they worked out. They felt intimidated and uncomfortable with Yoko being there, knowing that at any moment he could march over to their machine and boot them out. The power he posessed over them was incredible and damn near addicting. And what he said to me next would end up changing my life. He took the innocent mind of a fourteen year old boy and made that boy make his first major decision - a decision which would shape the rest of his life. You might call in manipulation, but I still refer to it (to this day) as opportunity. And when opportunity knocks... "Alright Bagwell. It's your turn.." You answer. SCENE III _______________ TWO DAYS AFTER WORLDWIDE 107; AFTER THE MCDONALDS INCIDENT "I can't believe you called him Free Willy!" We made our valient escape from the McDonalds parking lot and Rebecca was absolutely hysterical. When I ran through the doors and hopped into the car, I expected her to give me shit about starting more problems with complete strangers. But she was right on board with me. It was hard for me to really grasp the concept that she was serious about being in the limelight, even if it was in a negative connotation. But slowly I started to see, more and more, that she was serious that night we ditched out on Abigail, Shane, and Timmy. She really was going to stand by my side through and through. "I really wanted to leave that one alone, too," I reassured. "But that fat pig had to sit back there moaning while we were on line. That's what really threw me over the edge." "Yeah right!" She didn't quite buy into the fact that I was trying to be a nice guy. And who would? Nice just wasn't my thing. You know how some people are allergic to chocolate or others are lactose intolerant? Well I had a similar ailment - I was allergic to kindness. I think I'd melt or crumple into a billion tiny pieces if I ever started actually being nice to people. Purely a disease, I swear. "The second we walked in, I knew you were going to say something about that register boy, too," she added. "The whole time I had to bite my tongue because I was running through everything you could've said to the kid about his face." "Seriously," I exclaimed. "The kid's face looked like a game of connect the dots, Bek. And if that wasn't enough, the look in his eyes just screamed 'make fun of me.' He was a tool and I had to make a statement out of him." "Oh to 'increase your personal value'," she joked. "Bingo!" I shouted enthusiastically. It was a motto, if you will, that I had lived by since high school. Build up your own personal value and you'd be able to dictate everything that goes on around you. I still remember laughing at the idea when I was first exposed to it, but it really seemed to work. I sort of modified the concept of it through the years and learned that it wasn't just a person's insecurities that you could control, but their emotions too. Take Clyde Fox, for example. The kid had a heart of gold and so he was always willing to be your friend despite how much you may have dicked him over in the past. He was also always eager and willing to learn a lesson from a 'friend.' Tell me that didn't have opportunity scribbled all over it. "Well then when's your personal value going to get expensive enough to buy you that World title belt?" It almost felt like getting a sledgehammer smashed into my chest when she asked that. That question practically haunted my dreams as I just couldn't seem to catch a break in the ring lately. "Don't even get me started with that," I warned. But she egged on, "stop being a little girl! Answer the question!" The truth is I really didn't have an answer. I'd lost my last two big matches to Jordan White and Vivica J. Valentine, but I really wasn't prepared for either one of them. In my match against White for the title, I was so focused on Valentine that I overestimated every single one of Jordan's moves. In the match against Valentine, I had already stabbed a dagger through my whole concentration when I battled White the week prior. It was a damn catch twenty two. "I don't know, Bek," with a dash of disdain in my voice. "Again Jordan White screws me out of a victory the other day at Worldwide. I cleared house in that ring and he stumbles out of the corner to snatch it all away." That's what I hated about those stupid four way matches. I do all the work and then some prick comes in and gets one lucky kick that takes you off your feet. Next thing you know, he walks out the winner because he pins somebody you just knocked out. It was bullshit. Pure and complete bullshit. And the fact that it was Jordan White made it even worse. He was beginning to become a nagging pest; a pain in the ass - a hemorrhoid. "Do what you do best," she suggested. "Get under his skin." The plan was genius, except for one tiny dilemma. "That's the thing, Bek. The bastard doesn't give a shit about anything. Anything." White wasn't like anyone else I've dealt with in the recent past. Everyone else had relationships I could ruin or friends that I could assault. But Jordan had neither. He had no real friends and he never so much as expressed a wink of interest in any woman. Maybe he was gay? And if that should be the case, I'd have even more reason to want to castrate the guy. "There's gotta be something you can do," Rebecca insisted. "What about Tessa?" "What about her," I asked curiously. "I don't know, Jordan seemed somewhat protective of her during your match. I don't even know if he threw a single swing at her." Rebecca did have a good point - if there was one thing White never did, it was hit a woman. Hell, he even bugged out when I beat around Ayake in the weeks before NC-17. He seemed to be against the whole bullying women thing, whether or not they are wrestlers. We kind of see eye-to-eye in that sense except that I just straight up don't think they should be allowed in a wrestling ring...period. "I guess it might work. He did get pissed off when the whole thing went down ith Ayake." "Worth a shot, isn't it?" she added. And she had a pretty good point; what was the worst that could happen if I fucked around a little bit with Tessa? White wouldn't give a shit and the rest of the world would think I'm an asshole for bullying her? Half of them think I'm an asshole already, so it's not like much would change. And that's when another brilliant idea popped into my head. "You know who else would be a perfect culprit?" Rebecca smirked, pleased to see me getting into my zone, "Who do you have in mind?" "What about my old pal, Sally Ford? I doubt Ayake will let me within 500 feet of her but I'm certain I can con Sally into an interview. I'll just damn near rape the bitch until Jordan White comes racing to her rescue." "Damn near?" she said, sounding a bit dissatisfied. "Fine," I acknowledged. "I will just rape the bitch." And just like that, her dissatisfaction transformed right into complete gratification.
