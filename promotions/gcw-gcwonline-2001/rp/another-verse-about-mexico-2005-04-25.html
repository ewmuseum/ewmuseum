Another Verse about Mexico

25 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/25/05 Tired of the taste of airplane liquor at noon. It boiled over in my stomach as the people in coach were served their flash-heated meals with the roaring of an impatient mob. A stewardess apologized to me when she passed through towards the divider, intent to quell whatever rebellion had started. I wasn't prepared to fight off the impending riot--thanks to airport security--and took stock of what was available. The arm rest could be ripped off with a little effort, or the rum bottle sitting on the cart smashed and lit ablaze. If the worst happened, of course. Trey had found inner peace two rows back, chin down to his chest and eyes lost behind the wall of sleep. It suited him, brain and body spent from what best accounts to stupidity. First class was half-crowded, but I took most of it as a joke. The landing strip was carved out in the desert, just south of the border, and any notion of a first class would be abandoned as an absurd fetish the moment the wheels started spewing up dust. The majority were suits, button-ups and pinstripes, with fine creases and vacant salesmen smiles. They'd panic at touch-down, struck in abject fear that the jet would somehow trip and tumble end over end off a rocky crevice. They were a cowardly and superstitious lot. Sin coursed through my veins like a virus, spreading to my fingertips and clutching them tight against my palm. That desert stretched out forever below me, gold and copper and amber waves of vile discourse. I wondered how long we'd been up--how much longer until we landed--when the claustrophobia of old man's cologne kicked in. The outside world was too far away--could've been being bombed into oblivion while I choked on Old Spice. Not sure who had it worse. A bear with thick golden hair on his arms pushed past me, shoved his way into the aisle and shoved that stench into the depth of my brain. No salvation. No redemption. The plane banked right in a hard gust of wind, passengers yipping out instinctively. Rattled to the core by human noise, I was quick to order another drink. The liquor let the incessant whining of addiction slip out of my head long enough to grow hate for the surroundings on their own accord--not influenced by nicotine-deprived bitterness. The pilot's voice came over the intercom, short and deep: 'All passengers buckle up; we're coming in for a landing.' A different stewardess passed down the aisle to make sure that people had followed orders and I called out for her as she passed, curious about how much more drink I'd be allowed. She smiled and handed me a few bottles that I quickly stuffed into my pockets. More rattling. The plane jerked side to side in resistance to deceleration as we circled high above the airfield; which appeared to be a few squat gray buildings and a long line laid out in the sand for a landing strip. The golden bear was still gone, either ignorant or deaf to the captain's proclamation of landing, while I downed a little bottle of Bacardi quickly and set in for the good part. We descended from the skies like a hungry warrior desiring the refuge of human comfort, prepared to kill if all hospitalities weren't quickly extended. Christ, that was my stance on it at least. The others were getting together whatever they'd spent the flight working on and shoved stacks of papers and manilla folders hastily into briefcases while we approached what I hated to call a tarmac. The plane jolted as the first wheel touched down, and a few of the hysterics dampened their screams under heavy Prozac. The rest of us waited silently, knowing full-well that if the plane lost control we'd have to accept it as reality and enjoy the ride--we bought the ticket, after all. I laughed at the bear in the bathroom, imagined him doused in a viscous goop and wracked with tears for his condition. And, that simply, we had landed and were being taxied across the landing field--away from the buildings--while a stewardess read off a list of precautions to make sure that none of us cashed out in the final moments. It was simple enough to sit there and wait until we began filing out down the staircases. The ground surprised me, hard asphalt covered in a sand that piled up quicker than it could be swept away, and I waited for Trey to step out in mild amusement. A few people lingered at the steps for further instructions, but I had packed light and moved with my carry-on. Clumsily, I fumbled to light a Newport in the harsh winds as Trey walked over to me, his face contorted in a yawn. 'Ah shit. What next, J?' I shrugged and looked out over the airfield. There was a small car lot, presumably rentals, further away than the main building. 'Need a ride.' 'Where to?' 'Few hours off. Lexi's supposedly wrestling for kicks in some low-rent federation down here.' I hacked and wheezed from the first real hit of the cigarette, body feeling useless for the week of hedonism and excess. 'Yo.' 'What?' Trey rubbed his hand under his nose to indicate and I followed suit; wiping away a thin stream of blood that dripped out of my cracked-dry nostril. The arid climate didn't suit me well then, and never had before. 'Thanks.' With that, I started the trek to the car lot, Trey in tow. I lit a second cigarette off the burning filter of the first before letting it fly, curious as to what my plan even meant for Johnny, if I found him. Fuck that--spontaneity is the name of the game. The menthol cooled and cleared out my throat even while I was being baked under the glowing sun, when Trey caught up to walk beside of me. His face was worn and weary, clearly preoccupied. 'Trey, what's up?' 'Whaddya mean?' 'You've been quiet as shit since Worldwide.' 'Oh, shyeah, that.' 'Yeah, Stevens get to you?' 'Hah, nah man. S'just, shit, I ain't even realize I was gonna have a singles match--shit came outta nowhere. And damn, I forgot who dude was. Y'know that made me look like a jackass.' 'Fuck it, who cares? Just a long-winded prick.' 'Shyeah, shyeah, but still. Shit, ain't I already have a match with him signed before Worldwide?' 'Wasn't signed yet.' 'Ah.' 'And, fuck, face it Trey. Once you beat Knight, there were a lot less names you had to worry about remembering. All curtain-jerkers to you.' 'You know I got lucky there.' 'Bullshit. Knight beats the people that he intimidates, and the only reason anyone here's scared is because, well, they never saw him in 3WA. You saw him gimping around when the playing field was tougher, so what power's he hold over you? You would've won either way.' 'Shit, I dunno.' 'Fuck. You took the eye patch off.' He ran his fingers across his eye and smirked, nodding. 'Shyeah. S'all healed up now, s'I figured I might as well.' 'It's been healed for, fuck, weeks now.' 'Looked cool.' I nodded and let it pass--we were on the lot then, anyway, so there was an easy excuse. The cars all shared that thin glaze of sand, their paint jobs faded and cracking underneath. Nothing stood out as any better--or any worse--of a deal than the rest of the lot, so it came down to pure preference in deciding. I passed through the rows, mostly American and Japanese cars from the 80s and a few pick-up trucks, without coming to any good conclusion other than that a voice deeper than Trey's was calling my name. I turned and looked at, well, the instigator of a back story: Sobias Torm. 'You son of a bitch.' The large Chicano hung in the doorway of the rental office, drenched in sweat and probably reeking of cheap gin. He puffed on a filterless Camel and set out into the sun to catch up with me. He was lazy, clearly, and dressed in a wifebeater and gray shorts that barely passed the half-way mark of his thighs. Ragged sandals on his feet, he looked me up and down before handing over a case I instantly recognized as my own. 'Danielle said you might need it.' I laid it on the hood of a car and opened it enough to make sure that everything was in order and smirked. 'I told her to send it. You know you can't explain something like that to airport security.' 'Not anymore, at least. Christ, is that Trey with you?' 'Yeah. I'm a manager now.' 'I already heard the news; thought that maybe he'd want a real attorney instead of whichever shitheads Anarch's got working for it now.' 'Can't you stay employed without leeching off of me?' 'Haha. I'm very much employed, Jordan, and you should know that. Just because you're not getting arrested every other day doesn't mean I get to sleep and fuck all the time.' 'Not that you don't.' 'Not that I don't, no. So, why the hell you out here, Jordan? This isn't your normal vacation routine.' 'Why the hell are you out here?' Trey looked up from an old Datsun and started to make his way over. I put my hand up slightly and he stopped, nodded, went back to looking at cars with me in his peripheral. 'You don't make it easy to keep up with you, Jordan.' 'Okay--why did you wanna keep up with me?' 'I'm your attorney, and you've fucked up.' 'How now, brown cow?' 'Johnny.' I lit another cigarette and looked up at Sobias; his wild mane of black hair blocked the sun, and his face was a stone-sort of serious that one doesn't often see when things are going well. 'Christ. How'd you know?' 'There aren't many circles to run with around here. When a delusional pro shows up, people hear about it.' 'So he is wrestling.' 'You knew that. Anyway--you running your mouth about him on Worldwide a few weeks back sealed the deal. Does Trey know?' 'Yeah.' 'That's not good.' 'I can trust Trey.' 'Maybe. You might be able to trust Lexicon, too.' 'Huh?' 'He's lost his fucking mind. Anyone with the facts could figure out what you tried to do.' 'Isn't it your job to keep anyone from hearing the facts?' 'I can't cover your ass every time you get stupid, Jordan. I can help you out on this though.' 'How?' 'I know where Lexicon is and I know you goddamned well. If you can find him, you're making sure there isn't a problem left to come out of it. That is, if you don't blow his brains out with that little toy of yours.' I ran my hand over the case. 'It's a precaution.' 'Doesn't seem to work,' in reference to the sling. 'It's a new precaution.' I caught Trey's eye again, waving my hand low as I reached for a cigarette. 'So, what exactly is it that you want outta me?' 'You're still a motherfucker, Jordan. I came out here to save your ass. You said it yourself, that's my job.' 'Yo, J,' came Trey, behind Sobias for a second. 'What's up?' 'Not much. Trey, you know Torm--right?' He nodded. 'Well, meet Anarch's new head lawyer.' My lawyer let out a laugh from the depths of his broad stomach. 'You never were good with money.' 'Easier to have you on their payroll than mine.' 'And you always were a caustic fuck.' He laughed again and thumped the remains of his cigarette to the ground. 'Been pretty impressive lately, Trey.' 'Heh, thanks.' 'Even more amazed that you managed it with Jordan as a figurehead. You haven't choked on every big match you get.' 'I don't train him.' 'That explains it. Well, look; I've still got shit to settle up with inside, so Jordan can fill you in on the details. I'll be back in ten minutes.' He laughed to himself while looking out across the nothing of desert, then turned and went inside. His drunk amused him greatly. 'What's up?' Trey barely turned his shoulder to talk to me, keeping his body aimed towards Sobias. 'The details, I mean.' 'Aren't many. Sobias knows exactly where Lexi is, what he's been up to. Said he wanted to help me out with finding the bastard.' 'That why you gave him the job?' 'No, gave him the job so he wouldn't be as apt to fuck me over on this. That, and he's one hell of a lawyer.' 'Really? Smelled like a Quickie Mart.' 'Don't judge a book by its gut-wrenching, milk-curdling demon reek. He's as decent as the lot comes, but never mistake him for anything but a blood-sucking villain--he's just on our side. That's all that ever matters, especially with lawyers.' 'Hah, shit. Aight, just, you gonna need me to stomp his ass anytime?' 'No.' 'Cool.' 'Probably not, at least.'
