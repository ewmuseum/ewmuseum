Date: over a couple days Failure. I was never quite used to it. It was something that came foreign to me from the day I was born. That was the root of my upbringing. And while the overbearingness of expectations have become the thing of emo songs, to me it was nothing. Being goal oriented from the start is a good way to carry it over through the rest of your life. And as time went on, I only got better and better at what I put my mind to. A shock to many in the wrestling community, I am a solid student who has an Associates Degree in Liberal Arts. Looking at the way I talk on camera and with the way I usually exuded my personality, nobody would get that right off the bat. Nor would they understand my interest in mathematics. It transcended into a love for the up and down Stock Market. There was a sense of reckless competition within the dynamic, given that the science of numbers was concrete. To make malleable the figures somehow gave them meaning an exciting meaning. There was a story beyond the figures, but they overlapped, neither acting as an overpowering notion. I am far from someone who needed to use a bunch of words to encompass my essence. Perhaps that is premeditated. While everyone else is searching for the who and why to what they were, I use my time to be tangible. I did not get to where I was by sitting on my butt. I went out and shined bright through whatever came my way. While many in the wrestling business needed to be created by people to garner the kind of kinship that they so inherently desired, I simply went out executed without someone holding my hand. In many ways, it is the curse I suffer through. I am totally self reliant, and that pisses off many people who simply do not have my “talents” and need to painstakingly give everything they have to get only an quarter of what I can do without really trying. It pissed them all off that I had defeated legends and usurped the considered best. There are different ways of being excellent in this world. You could kiss the ass of those around you, or you could be Terrell Owens and accomplish what others who get more praise. If you do, however, you will never be perceived as anything more than a dirty scoundrel. My favorite baseball player ever is Barry Bonds. It made me laugh when Bonds shattered the home run record and received a tepid response while Mark McGwire was still cherished as a treasure for what he did. It showed the true nature of the media, who without as much as a second thought tried in vain to no sell the revolution in an American lexicon. It has only gotten worse as the media seems content in tarnishing the American Pastime than accept as valid the accomplishments of someone who had the balls to give them the finger rather then get on his knees. My favorite football player, outside of Reggie Bush, is Terrell Owens. Owens broke his ankle and weeks later caught nine passes for over a hundred yards in the Super Bowl. Owens is also reputable for his charity work and his ability to connect with the fans. However, he is reviled because he has the gift of gab. Terrell came from humble beginnings, but in no means is humble about his abilities, claiming himself unequivocally the best in the game of professional football. I believe it was Tully Blanchard who said, “People don’t like people that are successful and talk about it. It irritates them.” And no truer words could be spoken. The Four Horsemen toured the global landscape talking about a life of excess that they had earned a right to via the success they had attained through their triumphs. Everybody hated them because they were arrogant. Nobody took into account the plausibility of them working harder then everyone else. It does not matter. Even if I kissed your asses, you'd all still hate me. And for me, at this point, I am unsure if it's even worth it. Even if I have to fail. The only thing that stops me is spite. *** The summer heat was arduous, the landscape with a certain lull to it. A gazelle sped across the expanse, cutting through the golden strands of tall grass thirsting for water. The sky bled a velvety crimson against the setting sun, yet the heat still pounded upon the Earth. It tore the life from the land, turning it into a mixture of dying tall grass and barren trees. Some of the animals tried to find respite in the hollow trunks, but there was no salvation from the drought. The gazelle was proud, galloping with purpose. Water was near, he could feel it. The hills were just a little ways away. There had to be something there. Maybe so? Maybe not. But exponentially, the enormity of the situation did not allow for laziness, nigh death come approaching just around the corner. Unbeknownst of the gazelle was a family of tigers just waiting below the hill. A pack of five strong laid still, something awry. The majority had an acerbity, perhaps out of desperation. Their heads sunk low, barely having the strength to hold themselves up. Their coats were patched, flesh showing with sores sticking outward, a soft whimper of desolation gnawing upon their jaws. Rising up, their rib cages extended. Akin to starving children, they were the embodiment of despair, their disgusting appearance making sick the dying sod beneath them. Licking their lips, they sensed a kill coming and with it delirium. The leader was different. Female, with a brilliant coat with rich black v-shaped stripes extending from head to tail, her muscles were strong. Pacing back and forth, she waited, the trap set. The gazelle sped over the hill, then down it to a break. Full of vitality, she looked upon her mangy pack with narrow sullenness, her fangs reaching from her jaws as she let off a powerful roar. The pack whimpered, the chase on as the gazelle turned to escape it’s fate. Giving chase, the lead tiger came within inches of a pounce, only to draw short. The blades of grass broke in pitch, the roll of the leader allowing abdication to the fearful gazelle caught unawares by the astuteness of the hunters. Their leader fallen, the pack surrounded her, drawing back and poking her with their snouts. Shaking it off, the leader peered down upon them quickly and roared, drawing attention back towards the gazelle. Getting to her paws, she began again, running with full force. Her reluctant sect merely was caught in her dust, watching through tumultuous eyes their hopes crashing on across the space without them. Feeling lost, if only for infinitesimal seconds, it was enough to send them crashing over the plane in depression. Chasing after her, they limp along as perverted hyenas, derelicts forbidden by the laws of nature. The gazelle was not so lucky as to find emancipation, a second resounding pouncing trampling the beast. Elementary, the fangs of the tiger sank into it's throat and a panicked burst of blood exploded from the gazelle's mouth in its last moments. Finally, victory. The celebratory notion was quickly taken out of her mind as she stared out, looking at her family. Ragged and run, she hoped they'd finally feast. The killings was rather meager, but even a little bit might perk up their mindsets and get them going headlong towards life rather than eminent death. Pattering feet accompanied the head of the table, her powerful jaws getting grip of the Gazelle's skull and effortlessly tossing it over towards them. Grabbing hold of the midsection as she joined them, she ripped into the flesh of the kill, hungrily chomping away as the deep red soaked her face. The night sky came over, the look over the land now starry and full of hope. Looking before her, they gathered. And what a feast it would be. The elastic sinews proved mighty difficult for them, their aching jaws unable to gain a grip thanks to the rotting teeth found within their maws. She had a sigh of relief, her family at least seemingly interested in staying resolute and alive. 'CAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAW!' But as she pulled her head up, she saw them dropping mouth fulls of chewed flesh and tendons before her feet. Above, vultures began circling the clique, diving down on top of her brethren. A roar escaped her lips as the vultures started tearing away at the sores upon their backs with great malice. And in their eyes there was abject happiness, tears cascading down their brows, furled heavily in pain. Redemption in servitude, self demolition the new creed. Over the expanse, a dark shadow arose from the brilliant moon's reflect. The laws of the land perverted, she could only weep at what they had become. *** Blood trickled from his skull, the gash under his left eye swelling several sizes. He tried his best to keep his left eye shut, failing for whatever reason. Nothing seemed to be working. His entire body just ached. Sweat pouring from him, the straps to his singlet slung over to his waist. Hands resting on his knee braces, he felt a stabbing pain going through his lower back. He tried so hard to push himself up to ease his back straight, but failed to do so. The white leather PRIME 5-Star Championship belt draped over his right shoulder, he could only feel a sense of shock, followed by desperate mutterings of despondency. Easing off his right arm, he extended it before him between his legs. A single strip of black tape surrounding his index finger, Brandon tried his best to close his hand. He couldn't. He would've gotten up and roared, gotten insane with rage that his own body had failed him. But the depression was just setting in too hard for him to stomach even the slightest of outpourings at this juncture. The backroom was devoid of but a simple light on the gorilla position. The fans had all poured out of the Staples Center, their thoughts now focused on the locked Hoyt Williams/Tchu Universal Title match to come. The new Jewel In The Crown had exploded upon the scene, maiming Karina Wolfenden before reaching the finals and conquering Amy Campbell. In some ways it was a mirror of the fallen contender, who had exploded upon the PRIME scene earlier in the year and engaged in conflicts with the company's elite over the time span, slowly completing 'a circle of injustice' from the Dual Halo. He'd destroyed the K-Wolf before anyone else. Gone over an hour and sent Nova out of PRIME, possibly forever. He'd solved the riddle of the Deville. And from there, his run of conquest was piling up large. Main event win upon main event win stacked high in his record. He was the franchise, the face PRIME was sending out nightly as the linchpin. Even as he was pumping God knows what through his system, he was still decimating the field. And before him, the last piece of his circle...Hoyt Williams. A man who hadn't put his title up for nearly four months, who'd been opening shows and losing matches left and right to midcarders. Brandon buried his head in his hands, still in shock over the colossal upset. How could he have lost?! Brandon was simply all around better. And his fluid move through the competition akin to a knife through butter made it all the more crystal that Youngblood's reign of supremacy was just about to begin. But destiny decided to take a detour. He didn't study him, simply letting his training take a slide for a moment. He'd talked trash for months about how Williams was the absolute biggest pile of crap in the business. How he'd only gotten his shot on size and the fact that his coke snorting father had some lineage in PCW. How he was a mockery of wrestling, how his 'fat' could not carry the weight of such a title as the Universal Championship to any grand height. It was Los Angeles. Home field advantage, and the fans had cheered HIM on to the point where it was as if Youngblood was the fearless good guy against the most evil of villains. They'd tired of Hoyt's stalling, his tactics of maintaining his championcy Hoganesque to the core. They wanted the representation of pure wrestling to hold the belt. But he overlooked him. His talk crashed around him the second the three resounded through the shocked Staples Center. With Hoyt and Duke Williams laughing down on him, Brandon could see his world crashing around him. His father had died just days earlier...taking his own life rather than suffering through his brain tumor. He'd dedicated this match to him. Dedicated it to the man who had instilled his sense of goal oriented success from birth. His father was frowning upon him now. Wrestling was already moving on without him. His loss still shocked him though. His talk was empty. All of it. For the first time in his life, he did not make good on his promises. His shot at immortality denied, Brandon simply sat there. He could feel the ease come off the shoulders of the PRIME roster. Youngblood was mortal. And it made him sick. *** The arrival in Las Vegas was sudden. He was wanting to get himself to the venue early to gain a comfort level, something he usually lied to himself about over time. Brandon Youngblood stared out the window of his room in the Mandalay Bay, the lights off within. His feet up on a seat, Brandon sat back with a Dr. Pepper, blinking his eyes rapidly. The warm glow of blue from his Sony Vaio notebook was the only visibly light from within. The sound was muted, as it usually was during intense study. The analysis of the matches by the color and play by play men seemed more akin to a commentary track in a movie; an added element with no tangible need and basically diverting to atypical potty humor with sexist jokes laced within. Every match had a story. Flashing on the screen was the main event of WorldWide 44, where Shadow and Vivica J. Valentine took on the team of Eva VanLorne and Seymour Almasy. Nobody knew just how deep Brandon’s study of matches went. Within the context of the matches, the nuances other people miss are picked up. No, it is not akin to simply knowing to avoid, but rather understanding what is coming natural from the opponent. And sometimes it is not so much figuring out flaws as to what to avoid. Shadow, the perhaps favorite to win the Dangerous Games match, was a deadly speedster with striking ability. Within his study she showed an understanding of submissions. Perhaps sambo? Her lethality was akin to a blitzkrieg, dropping an avalanche of blows that could incapacitate an unsuspecting opponent. And her slender frame belied her power. She could not bench press five hundred pounds, but her striking power was off the charts. And in facing her before, he understood the capabilities of a well placed Ebon Goddess blow. In a sense, he was more at ease. In their first encounter, he was caught unsuspecting with it. Not because he had not seen it, but simply because he had never felt it. There was a sense of warrior anticipation to him. After all, two people do not beat the hell out of each other for over a half hour without having some level of equality and respect. Right? It wasn’t like Vivica. Valentine was green, head-longingly throwing herself around like a rag doll. Her efforts, while at times mesmerizing, were still sloppy and flawed. It’s what made Shadow deadly, that level of skill. She did to him what nobody had done save for Mike Randalls; legitimately rocking him back on his heels. And Randalls had done that near the beginning of his career. Shadow had done it against a much more seasoned and honed Youngblood. The study was requisite. He needed something to occupy his time. The last time he was on this sort of trek he was too busy popping pills and drinking to his fill. Over time, he’d honed his craft to something akin to mathematics through meticulous investigation. He’d spent thousands of his own dollars on tapes. He’d procured most of the PTC library. His idle time at the BloodWorks was used writing into databases what he studied. And over time, he’d gotten a sheet on everyone. From the Angelo Deville’s to the Cave Hulk’s. If they had done something in a ring, Brandon knew about it and had an breakdown of it. In study, he found meaning. He took it seriously. Perhaps too seriously, as he had literally nothing else in his life. Ever since Hoyt caught him off guard in predetermined celebration, Brandon was overtly cautious. Never again would he take an opponent for granted, even though his demeanor would tell you different. Which is why, in the shadows of his room, with an entire city of sin surrounding him, he felt the need to pop in another Almasy match. This one from ACW. Much better than a pill. His eyes felt heavy, a cold sensation coming over them. He tried to maintain his alertness, the honking of horns and the screeching lights trying to fight at his side. Could he defeat Shadow again? The numbers were on her side this time. And Dangerous Games was basically made for her. It was a night custom built to elevate her to the stardom she so craved. Maybe it was possible for a dog to learn new tricks. Perhaps it was possible that he did not want it as much as she. He'd tired from his war with her. Seymour Almasy, as much as he despised him, was no match for the unbridled vileness that encompassed perhaps his greatest challenge. Chivalry was not dead, not for Dies Irae. His life made a living Hell at her call, she seemed the only one truly composed whilst it all happened, testing the corner of his will. He had to lose at some point. And he could feel the hatred of his detractors breath hard upon his neck. 'It's payback time Brandon!' 'You can't stay undefeated forever!' 'You're average at best!' 'You overrated piece of shit!' 'And ease back through...' *** It was a soft whimper, and with a sudden feeling of a world pulling apart from him, he opened his eyes. And before him was void nothingness, save for the few. Before him sat a small obelisk with a board on top of it's apex. At top, right underneath the board to north and south were lions carved into the obsidian structure, with the east and west having cobras. The minute detail of the images showed of painstaking crafting, most of which was considerably difficult given the flawless perfection that the figures enveloped. A light akin to a spotlight shone over the table, with charcoal and pearl pieces resting upon a chessboard. 'Checkmate Brandon. I know you deep inside.' The tone was feminine in nature. Out of the darkness appeared an alabaster figure, her striking green eyes peering towards him with a callous grin. 'I know you lack the depth of perception to truly understand why you can fail.' Her skin was hugged like a second skin by a leather outfit, the silver piercings of her lip and nose catching the light. Looking through it, he saw the black bit imperfections in the rings. Standing his ground, he just stared towards her emotionlessly. She frowned, her eyes curled up and her lips slightly quivering as she belted out sarcasm. 'What's wrong? Can not accept your reality?' 'I've accepted it. Long ago...' Brandon's inner voice of defiance was kicking in. She could merely smile, her right arm swaying fluidly upwards. A black chair appeared next to the chess board. She looked him over as she rose, patting the cushion of the seat. 'Then sit down and accept your defeat.' Her rhythm was hypnotizing, and in that moment he understood the David Koresh qualities she exuded were indeed real and dangerous. Brandon sauntered over, looking at the chess board. He'd been done. Caught dead to rights, his life was indeed in checkmate. He was soulless...empty. He had no other choice but to finally accept that as requisite fact. Looking forward in her eyes, he saw a self satisfied sense of superciliousness. Her conquest near and dear, she could not keep from beaming. He looked down at the chess board. Surrounded by a knight and five pawns. And with them stood a queen, the coloration skewed slightly, showing it was cut of different granite than the rest. The options of escape did not exist, as he was not a chess player. He did not understand the decorum of the game. Seemingly sensing this, she put her right arm around the expanse of Brandon's shoulders and turned her head towards him. Her left hand delicately grasped his chin and pulled his head towards hers, meeting in a breathless stare of unadulterated lust. 'I'll give you the time to figure a way out of this predicament.' She wanted more than anything for him to admit weakness to her. And in doing so he could be rebuilt. Her release allowed him to peer down the board, looking for a way out of his quagmire. He tried to rationalize an excuse. A possible last gasp effort to try and squirm his way out of the odds and live another day. The rule makers of the game made it certain that he could not truly hope to garner a victory in this contest. Finally, the Diamond would lose, giving to those lusting for a piece of his unconquerable spirit the El Dorado they had desperately desired. He could feel the beady eyes shielded in the darkness beat upon him, their Cheshire smiles of satisfaction taking him out of his lull. And in that moment, he became mutinous. Grabbing hold of the chessboard, he looked into the green orbs of his rival. His face could barely contain his grin and unease erupted within the ethereal jury. With a forceful flick of the wrist, the pieces on the chess board went skyward. Her eyes shoot saucer size, watching as the assured victory fell, connecting with an invisible plane of ground. 'NOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOO!' She screamed, falling to her knees as the pieces weave and wobbling end over end before pinning and remaining still. 'NO!' A universal roar. Satisfaction was denied on this day. Falling lifelessly to the plane was the chessboard, the binding almost separating upon collision. She stumbled forward, nearly falling upon her flushed face as she grabbed hold of the chess pieces. She could not hold onto them all, always losing grip of the white king centerpiece even as she would manage to grab hold of the rest in her clinched fists. Her frustration began to cause her to breath heavily, and the thoughts of defeat raped her mind of all composure. Tears began to well in her eyes as she looked at the pieces, dropping them with unsteady hands. She let go, the pieces all falling to the plane. Her lips quivered, strands of water falling with dire fluidity down her pale face in fear. Looking over, her opponent had changed. An exquisite white suit with silver cufflinks donned his body comfortably, a pair of silver shades covering his gleaming lime eyes. A sardonic smile formed upon his face as he kneeled down at the blubbering woman. The smile quickly passed to disdain. 'I don't follow your rules!' He growled through clinched teeth. 'I make my own!' Grabbing her face, he pushed her over onto her back. Raising up, he brought his black Italian dress shoe up and sent it cascading upon the pieces, smashing them to dust. The woman screamed upon the impact, pushing forward onto her hands and knees, grabbing onto the obsidian powder. It stained her hands. Adjusting his black tie, he looked down upon the grieving woman, a sinister smile tickling the corners of his lips. 'You can't beat me.' Looking up, he could see the mascara pooling in wetness down her cheeks in streaks. 'You can't destroy me.' She was biting her bottom lip, her dream dying before her very eyes before the soulless beast she so hoped to surmount. 'And even in your house...I reign supreme.' The Diamond merely turned, walking into the distance. He could hear the sobbing of those surrounding him, inconsolable as the destruction of Youngblood went averted. The distance was cold and perpetual. 'GET BACK HERE!' She screamed as he walked out of sight, clutching her chessboard and powder pieces close to her chest. 'GET BACK HERE AND FINISH THIS!' What she did not understand that it was over. In many ways, it was over before it even began. And the distance felt comfortable to the Diamond, mirroring his existence seamlessly. The rules broken, the time arranged dying before the sea of vultures, their wasted aspirations the only thing to prevent them from starvation. *** 'I won't deny ya...I'm a straight ridah...' 'You don't wanna fuck with me...' 'Got the police bustin at me' 'But they can't do nuttin to a G' For a fleeting second. That was all it was to him. Staring at his wrist tape as he worked it around his wrist, he wrapped it around the thumb a few times before snapping it free. The resolute crack of the knuckles followed, dead to the Diamond as his mp3 pumped out his psyche up beats. 'Let's get ready to ruuumbllle!!' Clad in his white singlet, he punched his arms in front of himself, the athletic tape skittering across the teal carpet as he did. The breath escaped his lips, the sweat already glistening on his shaven skull. Another fight, another night. Adopting a wrestling stance, he looked game ready as he floated on the balls of his wrestling shoes. Rolling his shoulders, he brought in his hands with a constant twitch firing off on the tips of his fingers. His talk. His training. His doubts. They all fueled the furnace of his hatred. And in his world, that was all that mattered. He was not scared. He was not dreading this. For weeks, he'd staked claim to this night. Start off with Almasy...end the number one contender. Other's said they had nothing to loss. Brandon had gotten sick of the clich?, to the point where it made him chuckle. If you had nothing to lose, your 'win' would be meaningless. Urgency was the only palpable notion worth a damn. He had EVERYTHING to lose. Grabbing hold of his BloodWorks hoodie, he threw his right arm through, swaying it back and putting his left through. Drawing the hood up, he went to make his approach to the gorilla position. Looking back, he looked down at the floor, his shadow extending far behind him vicariously in the light, always caught behind him. It was always caught behind him, no matter what it did. And he closed the door on it.
