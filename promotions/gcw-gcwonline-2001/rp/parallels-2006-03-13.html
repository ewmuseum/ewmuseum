Parallels

13 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/13/06 It's a process. Varied, but generally similar. The party gathers and somehow, someway, a story begins to unfold. An outside force rallies the party to action and the first steps are taken down a path. Along the ways are tests and tribulations, obstacles by design or chance to draw out the essense of character. Instance by instance these obstacles build as the party grows from the experiences, leading steadily toward the goal. The end of the quest. But before final victory can be achieved, there is the penultimate moment. The quiet time just before battle that echoes with the permenance of ultimate glory or terminal failure. Parallels The quietness of the hotel room closed in around Jim Brandt, leaving him unsettlingly alone with only his thoughts and the maddeningly loud thump of his own pulse in his ears. Sleep would not come easily this night. The pulsating glow of Tokyo's neon jungle shown dimly through the small window of his 20th floor room, adding its ominous glow to the ill-tidings that had set upon him. The feelings of nervousness had him worried, mostly in the aspect that he was not prone to them. In all of his previous matches he had not felt such doubt. He'd been in the ring with many men, many dangerous; some reputedly lethal; but he'd not as of yet even batted an eye in the face of such challenges. Even before GCW, at tournament or on-march at a faire he'd been stalwart before the charge and the impending clash. Even back in high school, the state finals. Much ado had been made about him lining up play after play across from a future D-1 defensive lineman. It hadn't eaten at him the way this match was. He'd been indifferent to the pressures, and it had even made him popular for a week or two. Indifferance was the order of the day to that as well, as Jim well knew it would be superfluous and short-lived. Days after the game it was back to the normalcy of clique-driven social discrimination, and late-night binges of Mountain Dew and Cheetos with Dave and the rest of the dice dealers. He'd be the tank, the Meatshield, the Trainwrecker. He'd occupy and demolish the front and middle ranks, leaving the principle for Dave to solo in archetypical heroic fashion. It'd been that way from as far back as Jim could remember. They were the shield and the sword; each powerful in their own way, but together greater than the sum of their parts. Knowing that each was vital to the other's success they played their roles perfectly, without jealousy or resentment. The fat man behind the counter at Thing for Thinkers had often said, 'Always two there are.' upon seeing them; but the truth was that Dave was the only person on earth that Jim Brandt believed took him as more than an grotesquely obese geek. If for that reason alone, Dave Montgomery was the closest thing Jim had to a brother. Dave Montgomery was burning the proverbial 'midnight oil'. His entire being seemed to be energized in the face of the upcoming challenge. The nature of the match appealled to him two-fold. Before the physical challenge lay a true mystery. A puzzle to be solved. A riddle to be answered. A game within the game, and one that Dave would rise to meet. The windows of his laptop seemed to dance under his control as he searched the net relentlessly for a clue that might lead him to the answer of whom would be Seymour Almasy's mystery partner. With a career like Almasy's, he could choose from literally hundreds of candidates. Smark columns and industry blogs bristled with conspiracy theories and opinions on the matter. Although none could be considered 100% likely or correct, many did address critical issues and bring up interesting notions that had to be considered. Recent traumas had taken a toll on the Final Fantasy no doubt, the extent of which could only be imagined. A terrible thing. For some time now, it had vexed him on how to properly convey condolences to Seymour without it seeming forced or 'professionally driven'. Matters that were made more complex with their upcoming confrontation. It would be something to be dealt with further on, perhaps when situations were less heated or recent in memory. Regardless, the strain might have effect on whom Almasy would choose. As base as Dave felt when taking it into consideration, it was a cold hard fact that had to be dealt with. Also for consideration was the rising presence of GCW's newest hire's, Almasy's overly familiar past with them, and their unending ability to work themselves into opportune situations. Also, the too obvious to be true but too simple to ignore aspect of MacGregor remained in play. These decisions and others played into a thought process that narrowed that field of likely candidates down to a handful or two. That small wonder being achieved, Dave now focused on gathering as much material as possible on the probables. Unprepared was no way to proceed in what would be to date the most important match of the The Guild's existance. He had to find an edge, a plan of attack. He had to, because he knew that the crux of the match would lie squarely on his shoulders. It is no secret that so far in GCW, Dave had been viewed as the lesser member of The Guild. So many times, the supreme efforts of his friend Jim had been squandered by his failings. Dave would not, could not, allow that to happen again. The sudden realization of what had been gnawing at him washed over Jim's thoughts like a sheet of ice. Cold, black, unyielding. Months ago, they had stood at the very precipice of achievement. A title shot against The Regulators. They were favored, prepared, and supremely confident. Only a short drive and a flight seperated them from the completion of their quest for glory. It was a flight they had never boarded. The drive was a familiar one for Jim, Southward on I-35 to Kansas City. Early morning traffic was light, and he had been paying more attention to finding a radio station with some sort of decent music than the road on that December morning. The first sign of danger had come far too late with Dave's panicked voice echoing in his ears.. 'Truck!' Ahead a semi had jacknifed across both lanes, a victim of the icy winter storm the night previous. Jim tried futilely in the scant seconds remaining to stop the 1982 Chevy Wagon. Realizing that he could not, he tried to turn the vehicle, to take the impact on his side. The car had spun viciously out of control and around again, the passenger side smashing into the trailer. The impact had knocked Jim unconscious. The following hours were a blur of panic and guilt, adrenaline and fear. He sat in the waiting room of the hospital for hours, unaware of the people staring at his bloody shirt and bruised face The words, 'He'll be O.K.' were of some relief; but it had taken weeks for the image of the x-ray and the two screws now in Dave's skull to leave his vision every time he closed his eyes. As much as Jim blamed himself, Dave tried to take responsiblity becasue he had shrugged off driving duties that morning. Jim had feared that accident would have driven a wedge between them. Those fears were quickly assuaged, only to be replaced with the deeper fear and understanding that no matter how hard he tried, he would not be able to always protect his brother in arms. And now, on the eve of their greatest challenge yet, Jim Brandt understood the true root of his fear. Against a grieving and possibly unbalanced man and an unnamed partner who would likely would be as serious and dangerous as any imaginable, Jim would play his role. He would sweep aside the front rank, and leave Dave to the principle. Unable to protect him, Jim would have to watch as dave would roll the dice the only way he knew how. All or none, glory or failure, life or death. All these years Jim had tried to protect him, at cost to his own efforts and success. He had let himself be held back for another's shortcomings. Failure was no way to repay friendship, loyalty, or brotherhood. When Dave decided to stay in Eagleville after high school to go to community college and write for Palladium, Jim passed on his football scholarship to Northwestern Missouri State. When Dave spoke out against the local SCA authority, Jim followed suit. When Dave came up with the crazy idea of going to wrestling school, Jim stuck by him. Jim even tried to blame himself for the accident when it was Dave's idea to leave early to beat the traffic, knowing the roads would be icy. Every bridge he'd come to, Jim had crossed it unquestioningly with him; and for every decision he'd been repaid with failure. Not this time. He would not fail again. No matter what happened; somehow he would find a way. Finally, after these many hours of frenzied thought and planning, a calm settled over Dave Montgomery. His path was clear and he was filled with a sense of purpose. He would live up to the trust placed in him. He knew now, somewhere deep down, that if was required of him he would lay bare his soul to repay the friendship shown him. Because that's what heroes do. Because that's what heroes do.
