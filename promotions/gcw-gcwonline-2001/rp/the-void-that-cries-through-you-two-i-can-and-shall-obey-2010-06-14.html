Wheeze followed scoff followed wheeze followed scoff followed wheeze. As he slipped deeper into his threadbare armchair, the tar-lined lungs of Victor Crane coughed and grumbled him out of his whiskey-hastened slumber. Clinging on to the waking world, grit encrusted fingernails dug into the charred fabric, remnants from the times he’d dozed off with a lit cigarette between mustard-tinged fingers. Snorting and scoffing, Victor tried to pass off twenty minutes of gaping-mouthed, phlegm gargling sleep as a blink. When eyes closed, bowels opened, flatulent whistling and spluttering accompanying every gulp of Victor’s diaphragm. The living room smelt of a moonshine distillery in the shadow of a sewage treatment plant. A festering world from which Kevin Crane was all too eager to escape. His eyes, watering from the fecal stench engulfing him, were transfixed on the flickering television. The reverberating crash of spine again ring, punctuated by the thunderous roar of the crowd, purged the snoring from his ears. He imagined the air flowing up through his nostrils was dense with the salty tang of sweat and the sweet, tinny nectar of fresh blood. If he toppled off the fraying edge of the sofa, he would tumble down into ringside, at the tightly laced boots of his idols. “The fuck did I tell you about watchin’ this shit?” Victor Crane croaked. A crust formed where viscous dribble had flowed over his bottom lip and onto his buttoned-down, checked shirt. “Put the game back on. Proper fuckin’ sport. Man’s sport. Not this fairy shit.” “Game finished ten minutes ago,” Kevin replied, making it their longest conversation in the past three days. His words were devoid of emotion, numbed by their own venom. “You ain’t fuckin’ right for watchin’ this,” slurred words chastised. “Told you before. Pansies play-acting in tights like a pair of fuckin’ queers. Makes be fuckin‘ sick.” In the years since his mother’s death, Kevin Crane had developed a thick skin. The kind that only forms once others have been lashed and torn away. “You wanna end up like Barney’s boy, huh?” The vitriol continued to pour out of Victor. He was a broken man, old ahead of his years and wallowing in the hollow grandeur of his spiteful dominance. “Poor guy can barely look any of us at the plant in the eye since we found out his boy takes it up the ass from some rag-head queer. Would be stoned in the fuckin’ street back where his little pansy boyfriend comes from. Fuckin’ good on ‘em, too.” A throaty, satisfied humph brought an end to Victor’s rant. One day, Kevin Crane told himself. One day, he’d hear the spluttering snores stutter into an asphyxiated gasp. He’d hear the creaky springs of the stained and singed chair fall silent. He’d hear the last, wheezing breath escape his father’s tar-pit lungs. He’d watch as his father, the father he once knew, a good man, could escape the bitter, decaying prison he’d been trapped in for the last three years. “Now put the fuckin’ game back on,” Victor grumbled, his barbed hook not enticing a bite. Withered fingers clawed at the glass beside him, but with cheap whiskey having rotted his coordination, it tumbled onto the brick fireplace, shattering into liquor-tipped shards. “Game’s over,” Kevin reminded his inebriated father. His tone was submissive, molded by the aggressive spite is his father‘s rants. “You slept through the end of it.” “Don’t you fuckin’ talk back to me like that, boy,” hissed Victor as he lumbered out of his chair, bones creaking almost as loudly as the rusty springs. “Now put the fuckin’ game back on and don’t let me catch you watchin’ that queer play-acting shit no more. Hear me?” Victor Crane knew he’d fallen asleep. Deep down, he always knew. He knew when he awoke with a blanket over him to fend off the biting cold nights. He knew when he awoke to the revitalizing scent of a fresh cup of black coffee drifting up crusty nostrils. He knew when he awoke, damp and warm, the sour stench of urine exploding his senses like smelling salts. But the thought of Kevin, his only child, seeing his father like that; it made it easier to lie to himself. “Game’s over,” Kevin repeated, doomed to let the inevitable run its course. Rage reanimated the decaying body of Victor Crane. A feral snarl bared rotting brown and amber teeth. Under bushy, charcoal eyebrows, anemic grey eyes narrowed, deep crow’s feet spreading from their corners. Through the barrage of wild, uncoordinated blows, Kevin could feel his panting father’s warm, stale, smoky breath suffocate him. The mottled brown, dormant bruises that decorated Kevin Crane’s forearms were revived as rings and knuckles thumped down onto them, drumming a dull throb through to the bone. “You show your father a bit of fuckin‘ respect, boy,” Victor wheezed, his black lungs burning. “Your mother would be fuckin’ ashamed of you.” There were rules. Kevin took it all. Every last bit. The belittling words, the numbing blows, all of it. He took it all for his mother. Not in her name. Never in her name. Untrimmed fingernails slashed up at Victor’s face, digging into his cheek and tearing the skin back. Clutching at the cerated wound which wept warm blood down his face, the darkest, most inhuman side of Victor Crane took over, clawing up to the surface and trampling over what good was left inside his withering body. When Kevin could bring himself to look up, he didn’t recognize the man looming over him, whiskey-tipped shard of glass clenched in a bloody fist. A blood-curling scream erupted from deep within Kevin Crane as he felt the burning point of the glass dagger tear into the flesh of his cheek. The victim’s swollen-shut eyes were two black hole suns, into which his fracturing facial features had began to collapse. He was still conscious, emitting a low, constant murmur; inaudible to all but the two men stood a few feet away, admiring their handiwork. The much larger man removed his black leather gloves, tacky with irony, chocolate-syrup drying blood. “Do you know what we do?” the larger man asked in the high-pitched wheeze which squeezed from the sooty bellows of his barrel chest. “We’re often misunderstood,” the smaller man’s words stabbed at the eardrum; hissed and spat sharply. “Undersold, even.” The smaller man mopped his leathery, tanned brow with a fresh handkerchief. “I know… who you are,” the victim gasped, a layer of rich, irony treacle coating his throat. “We didn’t ask you that,” the smaller man sniped. “First, you don’t answer any of our questions, and now you’re answering ones we didn’t ask. You’re not really endearing yourself to us, young Master Trenter.” The larger man cracked his knuckles and teased excavating a cigar from deep within his moleskin coat, only to resist the pleasure at the last second. “Who we are isn’t important.” The much larger man’s tight-chested words whistled as he spoke. “Irrelevant. We could dream up a terrifying back story to frighten everyone, embellish every little detail of our previous work, conjure up a pair of catchy names and - et voilà - that’s who we’d be.” “Now, what we do? That is important,” the smaller man said, eagerly. A serpent’s tongue traced his thin, pursed lips. “That is, after all, why we paid you this little visit.” “The world is full of tricksters,” sighed the much larger man as he finally gave into temptation and chomped the end off of a cigar. “Deceivers and charlatans, thieves and illusionists. They’ll use sleight of hand, gain your confidence, misdirect you… fool you.” “There’s not a tatty shred of honor shared amongst any of then,” the smaller man seethed. The disgust which filed down his tone to a sharp edge deepened the wrinkled etched into his leathery, perspiration-beaded brow. “Now, I ask you, what would the world be like if there wasn’t people like my associate and I to maintain the balance?” “And that is what we do,” the larger man finally answered his initial question through a smog of cigar smoke. “We slow down the sleights of hand, we shatter the illusions and reverse the tricks. We find the things that have vanished, Master Trenter, even if we have to peel back every layer shrouding them.” “But, alas, we must admit not being above a ruse or two ourselves,” confessed the smaller man with an apologetic sigh. Around his fabric-wrapped hands he began to tense a short length of wire. “Hazard of our business. His face was an asphyxiated shade of blue-purple, with yellow-brown bruising beneath the dead surface and violet lips split by slashed of dark crimson. It appeared Rasa had made you its next face. “What the fuck?” You tried to remember if you’d dreamt last night; if you’d let your consciousness wander off distracted and left an empty shell for Rasa to pollute. It was an morphing ideology without consequence, which imprisoned you within its torturous, suffocating suit and festered in the darkest depths of your mind. You felt colder since you’d first buckled and zipped yourself inside it; distance from the warmth of humanity growing larger by the day. The chilly prickles of dread scurried up your spine, numbing your feet to the cold tiles. You scuffed your peeling, blistered feet across the floor. A deep breath of dry refrigerator air flushed the last sleep-weariness from you. You grabbed a carton of orange juice, just as you did every morning. Minute Maid, just as you always bought. You poured it down your throat straight from the carton, just how you always drank it. You were so predictable; a loyal consumer. You went to screw the cap back on the juice carton, but numb fingers could only prod and poke at it. Then there were two cartons. Something felt… wrong. “Ugh…” Your tongue felt dead and swollen, laying lifeless at the bottom of your dry, sticky mouth. The carton dropped onto your feet, juice splashing up your grazed and bruised shins. “Ugh uh ugh?” You felt your center of gravity shoot to your head, your legs buckling underneath its weight. Your bare kneecaps thumped down onto the tiles, a shuddering jolt which wiped your consciousness away like an etch-a-sketch. Triumph Frost appeared to mock you with a blood-smeared grin as your skull bounced off the kitchen floor.
