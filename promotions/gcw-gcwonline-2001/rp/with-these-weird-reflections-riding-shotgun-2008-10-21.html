With these weird reflections riding shotgun.

21 October 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/21/08, but it takes place before Terror's. Blame a time warp. "Hey yo, this is Frank, an Im not gonna be answerin my phone for the next. For this week. So, shit. Leave a message, I guess." It's on and it's not silenced or set to vibrate: the ringers are mostly replaced by a recording of nothing, of ambient noise that doesn't stand out against the world around it. Recent, down on his life, Frank had asked Sage what the opposite of being empty was. The only answer Sage gave him was being hollow. It nested in his inner cortex until it had the wings to take flight. He is blind to the world. His head is down. He believes he is moving his mouth but he stopped an hour ago and he is only reciting into himself. He, blind, in stereo with a sullen reflection. His mind is a temple and he is gutting it; he examines his every filament and what he finds is remembered. The object is discarded and the memory filed. He believes he can retrieve himself when he returns; he believes he can leave and he believes he can return. He is not frightened--he was frightened, but it's been removed and he is coming closer to being and to being of a singular purpose. His upper lip is drawing into a snarl. He is a red lotus. He has his reasons. He makes babysteps towards emptiness--not of disrespect for the void--because they are all he can dedicate. But he is learning. His blood is rushing rapid. His breathing is nil. Subsets of his personality disappear without notice. He redefines natural and learns to run. Glass houses lying in piles. Forked tongue running along endless rows of perfect teeth. For a blinding second, he tells himself that he is wrong and that what he's doing, even if it works, is too high of a cost to pay for what's at stake. He shudders and it leaves him and he makes up for lost progress. He's asking himself questions in words that float by without definition. He does not know why he lets them call him Jordan White. He does not know why his greatest talent is one he can't justify using. He does not empathize with the motivations of his peers--when he looks at them, he sees mostly plastic devoid of meaning or purpose. He's exiting the realm of knowing and rewriting his brain towards the other as he leaves. He will have a career to answer questions in if he can dedicate and he has told himself that with increasing frequency since his ideal path narrowed into oblivion. He forgets if he's more talented than Terror; he discards that talent has any bearing and leaves a null in his victory equation. He does not breathe for the majority of the time in which he replaces red for red. When he inhales, he takes beyond his fill faster than he needs and lets it sit until it turns into a stale heat pounding his ribs for attention above the other suffocating abscesses in residence. He builds a home on the far side of his heart and he faces away from its teutonic pumping. He is interested in spaces without it. His phone rings and it's the hotel; the only number allowed sound. He believes he has lost the sight of some distant mystery cresting the horizon when he acknowledges the outside world, and he acknowledges it with blame already at the tip of his tongue. "This important?" "Mr Black, a very distressed young lady is looking for you. Should I send her up?" "Whats she look like?" "Blonde. Thin. Vapid. Emotionally unstable." He shakes his head. "Fuck it. Might as well." "Very well then." He lowers his head. He thinks about red. He recites Terror, terror, terr-r, terr'r, terr'r t'rr'r in his ribs until it transcends into a singular sound. His teeth grit. She knocks, four raps. Pushing, the scattered center of a beige and bromine hotel room. He hefts himself to the cramped foyer and he opens the door; Caroline is already coming inside and he's left to close the room and stand still, concern himself with her issues while being incapable of fully concerning himself with her existence. She has had him at ends since Tokyo. "Youre bleeding." He touches his face; she's right. "Huh. Didnt realize--whats up?" "Its raining like hell and I spent most of the day trying to figure out where the fuck you were staying because youre not picking up your phone and Im really really not in a good mood. At all." She paces. He scans the room, sits down on the bed. "Well. What up?" She's against the wall, her palms sliding flat down it as she collapses to the floor. "Jays a fucking asshole." "Yeah. An?" "And I want you to kick his ass." "Aint the only one." "I didnt think I was." She buries her face in her hands, roughs them backwards through stringy blonde hair. "I went to see him the other week, after his apartment burned down. I, I thought he was dead, you know? He wasnt, I mean, he," her head drops between her knees. Her voice muffles. "He might as well be. I miss the living hell out of what he used to be. When he was my Jamison, when he was nice. When you were friends." Frank stifles a laugh. "Now, its. All he cares about is that stupid fucking belt. All he cares about is his ridiculous little wrestling career--he doesnt give a fuck for me and, fuck. Im being stupid, but, I dont know. Its like, I really believe if you take the title away from him, he can just be himself again. Like hes just going to remember who he is." "Maybe hes just an asshole." "He wasnt. Hes not. Hes not being himself. Calderas changed him." "Shit, people change all the time. How you gonna say if hes bein true to himself or not?" She contorts. Her mouth narrows. "I know him. This isnt right. This isnt him." "I never met Jamison. I got nothin to tell you." "You were friends, Frank." "Hold up. Where you get the idea we were friends? I mean, you been watchin too much GCW or somethin?" "I dont know, its" "Its a bunch of bullshit. We werent friends--you should know that, shit. Talked to him once outside of a GCW event--once, and you were there. Back in Tokyo?" He's nodding. "Yeah. That was the closest I came to this mythical Jamison Talley youve been on about. Only name kid ever gave me was Jay Terror; only thing Ive ever seen him as. Some asshole outlaw kid with too much to prove to the world. Chip onna shoulder type person, yknow?" "What the hells wrong to you?" "Nothin. Youre tryna use me as moral support an Im apparently not in the mood to hear it." She pulls her lips into her teeth. "You want to talk about it?" "Nah. Aint my thing." "Then. What?" He laughs. "Nothin. We got nothin to offer each other--I say we just go our separate ways. I got shit to do, C. Spent the last couple months makin sure your ol boy was shook out of his fuckin mind before Dangerous Games, an, tell me if Im wrong, hes shook as hell right now. It would suck, dont you think, if I let myself get distracted this far in." "Im asking for like, an hour of your fucking time." "An I aint got it to give." "Frank" "Look. You think me takin the belt from your asshole ex would turn him back into whatever you think hes supposed to be? Fine, whatever, lets go with that. You wanna actually see? You probably oughta let me just focus on the match and, understand, that I am real tired of havin to deal with your problems an my problems at the same time. Im no fuckin support, girl. Im a goddamned wreck." He drops elbows into comforter, angles his neck to her. "If it helps, pretend Im doin it for the good guy reasons, instead of the selfish fuck off ones. She pushes to her feet. "Asshole." The door slams behind her. "Huh." He nods and shifts beneath the weight of it. He's sighing, his bluff only entering a personal history that keeps a ghosted tally mark of sacrifices quietly made and loudly forgotten. She's gone; he falls off the bed and he crosses his legs and he looks into the mirror without hesitation or regret. His forehead is bleeding and he has no recollection of why; he considers it a sort of stigmata and closes his eyes. He hopes to trap himself in the reflection; to make the trade with the opposite self he has been too afraid to regard. Time slips around him. He thinks until thinking is not an aspect of his identity and he begins to move. Standing. To the sink of the kitchenette; he splashes warm water in his face and wipes with paper towels that come away red, then pink, then white. He puts his phone in his pocket. He pulls on a hoody and zips it shut and he leaves. He is without reason because he's blocking off reasoning with all of his will. He just moves automatic, from the hotel and into the narrow London streets; he watches his feet, and a desk clerk scowls his way. Rain smacks him intermittently and the sky is dark above; he follows the claustrophobic avenues and alleys like an animal in a maze, a mouse drawn by a faint smell that increases if he makes right turns, that fades when he decides poorly. He buries himself under the hood up and his face is most of a shadow; his suede shows are wet through and through and it sloshes against his feet as passersby barely regard him. Their eyes turn and focus and some open their mouths but suture them tight without letting a word roll free; Frank Black's face is twisted like wearing it is a burden of its own. They all, to the last, they continue to walk. He stalks and the legs of his pants are siphoning the water from his socks and his shoes and it runs a dark rise up his calves that weighs him down, that slaps loud against his thigh distinct from the similar rhythm of his soles breaking puddles to find ground beneath. He shivers and aims another cigarette; his head is down and he takes turns that leave the rain angling at his back, smacking against canopies, getting lost in trees. "Wont be able to get way with that for long." He shrouds his smoke in his palm and turns. He pulls his hood lower. The woman adjusts her umbrella and sways to him; she pouts around a cigarette. "Be illegal to smoke in yer own home before too long. Borrow a light?" And he fumbles it to her. She glows orange in the fire, sucking smoke as she snaps it closed and turns blue eyes up. Her umbrella shifts to shelter him. "I know you. Yer that Jordan White wrestler, arent ya?" He nods. Takes his lighter back. "Not the talkative sort, huh? Its alright. Only recognized you coz theyve been advertisin it fer the last month. Dangerous Games, Jordan White versus Jay Terror. Ridiculous names ye got there, innit? Bryan Mayhem? Garbage Bag Johnny?" "I dont think he was, uh. Christened that." "Hah. Fair nough." She twists her heel against the sidewalk and turns to watch it. Frank's eyes follow. "How many of you smoke?" He shakes his head. His eyes wander. "Some." "It cant help any." He shrugs. "You seem a little distracted." He shrugs. "Guess that match has yer mind, huh?" He shrugs. "Damnit, boy. Say something." She smiles. "I like yer accent." "Caught me at the wrong time." He looks over his shoulder. "Ive gotta go." "Well. Sorry fer botherin you." "It happens." He tries to grin and he shakes his head; he's away, then, further into an auxiliary network of the city's veins, where things stagnate and the water mixes with dirt into a noxious clay that makes every step taken an aggressive motion, a declaration of something of indefinite import. It's unclear if he knows why he stomps, why he looks to have a planned path which the world can't sway him from. His cigarette is ruined by an awning leaking rivulets. It falls into a puddle and is crushed beneath his heel. A gray man shouts into the quickening rain ahead; people break away in packs, cross the street in aversion. Frank doesn't stray; the man grabs him, spiderleg fingers around his biceps. "World dont got forever, boy. Theyre spreadin their touch!" Frank soaks in the downpour. "The wicked ones are here, theyve always been here! Wicked ones rule the world, like a game. Theyll end it, soon, theyll take everything away from us and start themselves a new game in the ashes! Look! Look!" A waxy finger points up, up; beyond the brick buildings and the sharply angled roofs, above landmarks miles out of view, into the gushing clouds. "There, son! Theyre up there. MI6, the CIA, the KGB, theyre all the same. There are rulers and they are sinister, all sinister! Its us against them, boy! Its us against them until the end of time, and they dont care how they win! Theyre converting us, theyre playing to us; theyre making our boys mindless killers! Capitalizing on our weaknesses! Our greed, our lust, our fears! Theyll have us destroy ourselves and theyll laugh and play it all again! We cant fall into the traps. Theyd never let us escape." He looks Frank in the eyes. "Already touched you, havent they?" He has one hand locked on Frank then; he lets it go and shoves Frank away and retreats at speed. Howling. "No hope for you, boy. The lizards already have you. But you wont catch me. Oh no, oh no no n" He rounds a bend and his voice is lost. Men and women stare at Frank; he turns his head to them but offers no defense, no consolation. If they'll think he's a reptile or just an assailant of old men, they'll think that and there will be no argument. Hands to pockets, he is in motion again, taking a right where the man had disappeared left and finding the world around him victim to a certain shade of decay; narrow houses with second story window panes shattered, paint peeling, gnarled ashen trees with their dead digits reaching for the heavens. His breath breaks across his face. He does not make a conscious action of navigation; he does not make conscious actions. And when his phone rings, he stares at it briefly before sheltering it by his ear. "Yo. Frank here. Whats goin on?" "For the second time today, there is a morose looking woman here to see you. As you requested, I have called you directly at some nominal charge to the hotel.." "Again? Shit. Whatre my options?" "You can either quietly, and quickly, escort her to your room for whatever bizarre consolation you offering; or we remove her from the premises. Its not conducive to our general appeal, you understand, to have such a regular flow of destitute women crowding our desk." "The hell is she doing?" The sound of a palm against the phone, muffling the ambient. "Currently? Bawling, rather loudly. "Shit." He kicks an empty bottle in a shallow arc; it lands in a gutter and clatters into a storm drain. "Look. You may as well just shove her on out of there--I seriously wasnt expectin her back. Least not this fuckin quick. Im payin you guys, righ? You can deal with her." "Im not sure you understood me--much as Id like to have her ejected under even false pretense, this is an entirely separate, possibly related, despondent female than your earlier--and exceptionally quick, I might add--visitor." "Fuck. She give a name?" "Well, the information you gave us doesnt indicate any spouses, so Im not entirely sure the given, Mrs Leslie Black, is to be taken at face value." "Redhead?" "It would appear." "Fuck." "Im afraid thats not currently a possibility, given present company. What do you want done?" He straightens his back and leans his face to the sky; his hood drops. "Send her to my room. Tell her Ill" "Ooh. About that: it is against hotel policy to allow visitors unaccompanied." "Well. Send somebody up with her." "Unaccompanied by a guest of the hotel. If she went up with an attendant, she would be the attendants visitor and they arent allowed in rooms currently rented out to guests." "You really dont like me, do you?" "Not in the least. Youre a celebrity and you force me to deal with raving women. You are very low on my list." "Thanks." "So, if you arent planning on returning from whatever debauchery youre inflicting on this city," "How long do I have?" "A couple of years, Id guess." "Before you kick her out." "A few minutes; we are past the point of expecting her to restrain herself, you see." His eyes close. He has spent a month focusing himself. He has spent a month blocking, redirecting. He sucks air. "Im on the way." "Ill let her know." "Do that." "Be quick about it, though." He closes his phone and shudders in the English rain. He comes of terms with several things in a rush. And, for the first time in weeks, he lets himself forget about Jay Terror. He grips his belt, jerks his pants to his waist. Shoulders roll. He tenses and fires into the darkening day. Backtracking at triple speed. Kicking at stones, bricks, asphalt. He burns through the evolution of a city. He is only halfaware, running on muscle memory, assuming some electromagnetically based sense of navigation. Rain explodes in sheets against his bare face. His legs pump. He sidesteps a man carrying groceries, flings himself on the outside of a lamp post to avoid a group of stragglers sharing two umbrellas among the four or five of them; he doesn't count. He just moves. A car slams its horn and flashes its high beams, spotlighting him against a dreary world. He's gone from there, too. He retreats, returns; he's defaulting as fast as he can run, dodging the public, weaving gridlocked traffic. A cyclist swerves wild to miss a collision and loses control. He bails to his side and his bike flips headlong over a bench. Frank slides to a halt and jogs back, helps the man up and goes for the bike. "Sorry, sorry about that. Im seriously not an asshole." He's righting it and the man's grabbing the handlebars away with force. "Why the hell are you running in weather like this?" "Why the hell are ridin a bike in weather like this? An on a sidewalk, too?" The man looks to the trees, relents without a word and they part ways. Frank feels for his phone, pushes spikes of hair out of his face and picks up speed, splashing mud wild across himself as he cuts the corner off of a block with a detour through a miniature facsimile of a park. Something else to cling, something else to fight. He clears the fence and he's back to speed before he spots the hotel in the distance; he hurtles headlong into traffic, steadies himself on a passing post, crosses the final street like a finishing line and throws himself across the hood of a sedan at the entry way. A parking attendant sneers. Frank holds a finger up and hunches over, hands to knees. He tries to catch his breath. "Sorry bout the car. If anyone complains," he breathes in, "ask the desk clerk for my name. He knows who I am." He points in a general direction and smiles before pushing through the first doors, bursting through the second. A room turns to him; buried in soaked clothing, drenched to the core, hair splayed, maniac. The crowd is split; some prefer the intrusion magnificent of the sopping mass, others maintain a circle of distant fascination with the girl hysterical. There is silence. A gray man behind the desk coughs into his fist. "Mrs Black, I believe your escort has arrived." He pushes through them, the gaping, the bored, the halfconcerned and the quarterconscious; he takes her by the wrist and they move wordless to the elevator, treading on the murmuring of the staff and the patrons. The doors slide shut behind them. They are at the third floor and she wraps her arms around him, puts her crown to his sternum. They leave on the eleventh; he's carrying her and she's a constant whimper at his chest. Into his room, she drops her grip and turns, her hand to her forehead. Frank plants his shoulders into the door. She swivels and locks again to his torso. Her voice is cold, timbre weak but rising on every syllable. "Adam has Lisa." He pulls her in. He runs a hand through her hair. "Youll get her back." "No, no." She pushes away a second time and collects herself; she drops her jacket sopping across finely upholstered trash and she collapses on the bed. He follows, his shoulders straight and his chest out; he piles his jacket over hers. "Hes filed for separation. He used those photos, the articles, about me and you, and now. I dont know, Frank. Right now, I cant see my little girl. I cant fucking talk to my baby because. Oh, holy fuck." She is red like fire, now. She sits up. She snarls. "Im going to fucking kill him." Frank genuflects at her feet. She bites her lip. She shakes her head; he brushes drying strands of hair from her eyes and they kiss and she is, again, the red he can't escape. He is thoughtless and it washes over him naturally. She pulls away. "Ill be alright. I. I know I can get her back. I just, god. Everythings so fucked up right now, Frank. Everybodys so fucking cold and. Really, really? I just needed to see you." "Enough to pretend to be my wife?" "I thought, you know. Like hospitals." He smiles up to her. "You didnt go with sister, niece, cousin--you went straight to wifey." "Sister would be weird." Her lips are curled and her face is blushed raw. "Are you alright?" "Im okay. Nothin important." "You always do that." "What do I always do?" A smirk. "You always downplay your life when I ask about it. You always act like my problems are more important." "Maybe they are." "They definitely are." She grins. "But theyre my problems, and, god. Sometimes I just want to think about anything else." "Im guessin nows one of those times, huh?" "Mmhmm." She grabs him by the neck of his shirt and stretches it wide as she falls backwards. He slides on top of her. He tries to talk and it's all reflex; his shoulders roll, his head shakes, he feigns innocence. "I dunno." "Bullshit. Youve got to have something to talk about. Like, where were you when I got here? Hm?" "I was walkin." "Why?" Her breath is hot against his raincheckered face. "I was lookin for somethin. I, I really dont think I know what. It was just a stupid kick." "See? You do have something on your mind. What kind of stupid kick?" "Really? I mean, with what you" "Dont talk about me. Okay? Talk about yourself. I need that right now." "Okay." He leans in and she presses lips to his forehead, grazes without action. He faces her. "I missed you. I missed you to the point that I thought the only thing I had to care about was fucking wrestling. I missed you like I was losin my motherfucking mind, girl." "Are you alright?" "I hope." "No, seriously. Are you alright?" "I dont know. Things are always weird." "They dont have to be. They could just be, sometimes weird." "Youre" "You. Not me." He slips down, rolls to his side and squeegees his palm against his face. He rests his head at her lap and she runs hands idle through his hair. "You didnt call." "I tried. Not then, I mean. But Adam was." "Dont defend yourself. You say you got a reason, Ima trust you." "Thats sweet. Its maybe a bad idea. But its sweet." "Hah. Well, I mean. Since I last saw you, Ive just been. Its like, Ive just been tryin to redirect everything I had for you into. Shit, this is gonna sound fuckin off, but into somethin ugly, cruel. Its the job, I think. Like, hows somebody supposed to do that shit all the time and walk away normal? All we do is we beat the shit out of each other an the fans cheer if were good at it. I used to be, shit, I was patient, I like to think. Was I?" "Mmhmm. You put up with me." "I still do." He cranes his skull backwards against her abdomen and rests his arms on her thighs. "But now, its just, its gettin to feel more and more like violence would solve all my problems real simply. Like, I should threaten waiters when the cooks takin too long, or Im supposed to start stompin the guy in front of me at the DMV. I sound fucking retarded." "No. Keep talking." He closes his eyes. "I think I went out tonight cause I wanted someone to talk shit. I think I wanted to hurt somebody." "But you didnt." "Nah. I didnt make it far. Mile an a half, maybe." "Still. Youve had a bad," "Horrible." "-horrible couple of months; you got pissed off at the world. It happens; all you did was want something. You didnt do anything stupid. Jesus, Frank, youre a celebrity. Theres a million ways you couldve imploded, but youre still here." "I feel bad blamin the environment, like Im too weak to control myself, but, fuck. Everybody acts like youve gotta be willin to die to win that belt, and, fuck me, I bought into it. I wanted to kill Jay Terror--no good reason, just, big dumb violence." "Hee. Thats such a funny name." "For real." "Still, though. Why did you want him dead? Did you cave to expectations that much? I mean. My Franky never worried about what the world though of him." "I thought, think, whatever; Caroline made it sound like he wanted my head onna stick, so its like. He bugs me, an all I can do is return in kind." "Ooh. Call it selfdefense." "Hah. It aint, though. I know Im more talented than the kid--or, fuck, everyone seems to say I am, so its like, if Im not fucking up, I know I can take this match. I just. I dont know. I gave em my heart and now I think its eatin my soul alive." "Oh, shit. Dangerous Games. This week. I kind of forgot about that." "Hah. Its cool." "I dont know. You make it sound like Im just going to distract you." "If you are, its cause I made my decision." "Dont." "What?" "Dont give me any reason to think Im going to make you lose." He laughs and shifts to his side, rolls his neck and makes eye contact. "Nah. Last chance at that gold thing or not, I needed to level the fuck out." "Last chance?" And he explains to her, in greater detail than he has explained to himself.
