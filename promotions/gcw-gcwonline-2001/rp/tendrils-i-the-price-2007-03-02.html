Tendrils I: The Price

2 March 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/2/2007 Some Weeks Ago Makeshift KB Headquarters Unspecified Location, Western US It was almost a month ago that Blaise McClancy had turned to Lia Ambrosi (then Lia Vlaszy) and said that Ian Childress never did anything for anybody without asking for something in return. As a remark, it had flown in sort of under the radar. It was fairly typical of beings of his caliber (and her own caliber, for that matter) to do favors for others in full mind of the fact that those favors would one day be returned. Perhaps it was a holdover of living in a very different time when one gift would be reciprocated for another- when nobody either expected or got something for nothing. Ian had kept his end of the bargain. Two weeks ago, Lia Ambrosi had walked out of NC-17 with thirty-or-so pounds of gold resting on her shoulder, after having defeated two of the most difficult opponents she had ever faced. It meant more than getting to the GTT4 semi-finals, and more than holding any singles title she'd ever held in WMW, GW, or even GCW up to this point. It meant more than being a former PTC Global and Extreme Champion, and it meant more because this time around, she had bled for it, literally bled for it, and proved to herself that in situations like that, she could come out on top, unlike what a lot of people thought. Flash in the pan. Lucky shot. Dark horse surprise, who was really rather overrated when you looked at her in the full light. Lia Ambrosi was none of these things. But, everything came with a price. Though Lia had done the grunt-work on her own, the training had been Ian's, the attitude adjustment had been Ian's. And now, it was time to pay the piper. She wasn't sure exactly what he had in mind, though she knew basics. Right now, it was like sitting on pins and needles, waiting to hear the pronouncement come out of his own mouth. Though she trusted Ian Childress as her boss, there was still something a bit on the shady side about him at times, as though you could never really get to know or understand a man like that. For someone of her usually piercing mental acumen, being unable to read a person like a book by the first few glances was disconcerting. The office that she was waiting in was furnished to the nines. The chairs were a deep cherry velvet brocade, so plush that you could have easily fallen asleep in them (that is, if anybody could have possibly been in a mood to sleep while waiting to see G. Ian Childress). The walls were a cozy deep-toned wood, warm and embracing, and were covered with bookcases and ornate paintings. It was, overall, a place that she wouldn't have minded being stranded in, had she not been in the predicament she was in right now. There was a folder on the thick-wooded desk. She could see that it was unmarked, save for a yellow sticky note with her name on it. The temptation to lean over and have a peek at it was almost unbearable, especially considering the way her nerves were hammering around inside her body at the thought of what she might be potentially getting herself into. She had worked up the nerve to give it a small once-over. Lia Ambrosi, clad in the crisp black KB dress uniform, leaned over the desk, snaking a finger towards it, hoping to flip open the front cover and see if she could read anything without having to disturb the folder's placement, which Mr. Childress would surely have noticed. But like all things which seem easy, as soon as the tip of her fingernail had touched paydirt, the door of the office squeaked open. Lia sat back in chair, sitting up with the straight, military bearing that she was used to greeting her commanding officer and until this point trainer with. He had never asked her to stand when he entered the room- a formality that was, all in all, rather silly, he felt, as he didn't want everyone playing musical chairs every time he walked into a room. But she showed him the requisite respect in the way that she best could; attentively, her sea-foam green eyes followed her commander into his black leather desk chair, which groaned a slight greeting at its owner's weight. "Captain Ambrosi," he said, with a tone that was clipped and disinterested- for the moment, this was KB business. "Commander Childress," she said, nodding her head with deference to him. "Punctual as usual," he remarked, but in such an offhand way that she knew it wasn't a conversation starter, just something to fill the silence for the moment. She nodded. Ian Childress allowed her to soak in the silence for a few long and uncomfortable moments as he rummaged through the drawers on his desk. She would have almost sworn it was purposeful, stringing her along like that. His next remark made her feel like it truly was. "Why don't you inform me of the circumstances that made this meeting impossible until now," he said, folding his hands on top of his desk. They fit neatly into each other- much as most of the things in Ian Childress' life seemed to. Or at least, he hoped to give off that impression. His words sounded chastising, but she knew better than to think that they were. Had Ian been angry with her, she certainly would have known by now. She cleared her throat briefly, thought over the tiring events of the past two weeks, and began her story. "You know Hope, of course... and I'm certain you're aware of the problems she's been experiencing in the past few months. She's come to me for help, and until NC-17 was over, I wasn't really able to readily give it to her- or at least, she felt that she couldn't disturb me by asking. I met up with her after the show, and she was on her way to Germany, to confront whatever this presence is that is attempting to take her over alone. As I didn't think that was wise, Virgil and I accompanied her." "And you found there?" he asked. He didn't sound as if he was any more interested in the story than he had been before. "A small and not very well concealed enclave of vampiric slaves that Leandra had sired for herself, including two young twin girls, which greatly upset Hope. Leandra bade us to the throne room, and tried a few parlor tricks attempting to get me to bow to her whim, both literally and figuratively. I had to show her the ropes of being a vampire, it seems, since it took a literal physical battle for me to convince her to back off." "Hope was harmed?" Lia shook her head. "Not so much harmed as disturbed. Especially about those little girls. I brought them with us just to make her feel better, like they would be getting adequate care." Ian nodded. "Yes, I'm aware that they're in our care now." Having children of his own, Lia would have expected Ian to be a bit more compassionate. But then again, there was obviously a reason he wasn't showing any emotion today, and she wasn't going to play psychotherapist if he didn't want to offer it up. "It sounds like you had and have the situation well under control, Lia. Why was this such a concern to you?" Or, perhaps he really was disinterested. "Several reasons. One, being that the coven of vampires she has created is a serious breach of what my kind calls the Masquerade, and what your team tries very difficult to enforce. Two, this is a very personal situation that affects one of our team members, and as such, I thought it needed to be treated with a little more care than if she was some miscreant off the street who came seeking our help or stumbled into our hands. Three, Hope is having difficulty controlling her transformations. It seems strange that the being calling herself Leandra would be able to come and go at will inside Hope's body, though I suppose stranger things have happened," she said, well aware that her boss was host to a friend who didn't often manifest himself, "Whatever it is, it's vampiric crossed with some sort of daywalker, and like the legends say... that makes her doubly dangerous. There are only a few of my kind that can walk in the day, so that either leaves us only a few options as to what kind of blood she's harboring, or means we're dealing with something completely unexpected." Ian let go a long sigh. "It seems like we deal with more and more of the unexpected the longer the days wind on. Something like what Hope is experiencing may be just like a routine case of possession, or we may have something else on our hands, like you say. I think it would be wiser to treat it as the former until we see any indication of the latter, because if we don't do something to treat the former, as you should well know, it can get out of hand. If we have to take other measures, so be it." He seemed so cavalier about the question, as though Hope's fate was a fate belonging to someone who was far remote from GCW and the desk of the Order's leader. That grated on Lia's nerves slightly, something she presumed that Ian read from her demeanor fairly easily. Ian chuckled in a manner that could have almost been described as 'unamused.' "So. Should I go on, then? Or were you wanting to sit here and talk case history about something that is well in hand some more?" There were times when Ian Childress could really get on a person's nerves. She gesture towards the folder with her fingertips, with the elegant grace of a ballerina. "By all means. I'm eager to see what you have in store for me." He paused to shoot her a look as he flipped the folder open. "Don't be so eager until you actually see what it is. You could get yourself in a lot of trouble that way." She bit off the response that was brewing, and lapsed into stone silence. Ian Childress, satisfied, opened up the folder, and rotated it so that she could see the 8x10 glossy photograph that rested on top of the large stack of papers. The man in the photograph had a familiar look to him, though he wore a dark gray suit with an unfamiliar looking insignia in its lapel pin. Lia couldn't lock in on him- it was right there on the tip of her tongue. "This man is Joseph Sinclair," Ian said, breaking the silence. She looked up at him. His blue eyes were cold, measuring. The name sounded familiar for some reason. Dread twitched at the pit of her stomach. "You might be familiar with the Society of Leopold, an offshoot of the Catholic Church." Suddenly, the dread had a reason to multiply. The Society of Leopold were perhaps the most elite, and certainly the most vicious organized group of vampire hunters in existence. She had had a run-in with The Den, an off-shoot organization, and Virgil had lost an estate because of that run-in- the both of them had very nearly been killed when a detachment of Den hunters tried to burn down the house with the both of them in it. "I am," she said numbly, knowing that Ian expected a response to assure himself that she was paying attention, though she didn't really feel like giving him one. "Joseph Sinclair, you might say, is a little bit higher up that then Society of Leopold. As in, he supervises, at least partially, the work that makes the two organizations operate together, the one a supernatural military arm of the other." "Hunters," she murmured softly, not wanting it to be audible, but scarcely able to help herself. For ever vampire with the instincts still to feel, the word sent a shudder down the spine. "Yes, hunters," Ian echoed almost with exasperation, "But more than that. Sinclair is a hunter, a priest... and my half brother." Jade orbs widened in disbelief as she looked up into the hard eyes of her commander. It was not an order she could turn down, especially after having cut a deal with him like she had- but it was an order she could try to talk him out of. "Your half brother... why would you want to kill him?" "Personal reasons aren't important," Ian rejoined, raising his eyebrows at what he no doubt considered very unamusing impertinence in an inferior officer, "What's important is that you agreed to do this task for me, and this is the task." She took a deep breath, and nodded. Though it had been several years since she had engaged in killing anybody for a payoff, they weren't skills that she had lost. "What have you got on him?" Her voice had returned to that cool businesslike tone that reassured Ian that her head would at least be in the right place by the time she went to do the job. If it wasn't... she'd be killed. "This folder, for one," Ian said, tapping his fingers on the photo, "But this dossier is to be considered only your starting point. The Order has volumes of information on Joseph Sinclair, and this folder is meant to be both an overview and a guidebook. In it, you'll find all the information you need about where to access files on him, including the security clearance codes for the classified documents. Speak with Aine about it when you have the chance." Finally, he pushed the folder across the desk to her. She took it carefully in her hands, handling it as though it were a precious glass sculpture. Her eyes scanned over a few pages of it before she folded it shut and nodded to him. "Alright. What else is there, Commander?" She was brusque, like him. It almost brought a smile to his face to consider their rather similar temperaments. Then again, considering what his temperament had gotten him into, that might have been a good thing or a bad thing. He cleared his throat for a moment, and zeroed in on her with eyes that held in them a glacial fury. It was not directed at her, but was rather only an expression of his inner intensity. He held her there with his eyes, and she waited, waiting for what it was that he had to tell her. "You need to take this assignment very seriously." "I do." "No, listen to what I say," he rejoined, "You need to take this assignment very seriously. It will have large ramifications if you do not accomplish it in the manner in which I am about to tell you." She sat up a little straighter. "The Society will be aware of our presence. They have been, for some time, expecting a big move from us, and you should have no doubt that if you alert them in the slightest as to who and what you are, they will be onto us, and they will move faster than we can stop them to crush the Order. It's in your hands right now- your job is to take out the castle wall in front of the proverbial King." She knew what he meant by that, but didn't want to say it herself. "What do you mean?" "This is not just a personal job but a strategic one. I have been waiting for some time to put this move into play, and now is the best opportunity I may ever have. You are the one who has to do it, because it suits your skills, and the Order's needs." "You want me to take out a Paragon..." she said, numbly. Suddenly, her entire body felt like it was weighted down and sinking to the bottom of a river to be mired in the sludge there. "Don't play weakling, it doesn't suit you," Ian said harshly. Her head snapped up from staring at the front of the manila folder. "I didn't pick anyone else because they didn't have all the qualities latent that I needed. You've been honed by my training. You are one of only two of your kind in the Order, and they will never be expecting an attack from this direction. In the past, you've been a master of disguise, and you will have to employ those skills again in order to appear as one of their allies, infiltrate their inner sanctum, and carry out the deed. Joseph Sinclair, also known as the Hound of Longinus, will be a difficult target. You may have to take out more than one person to get to him, but your main objective should be at all times to remain undetected, and unaffiliated." "If I don't?" She was almost afraid to ask, but some morbid part of her forced the question out anyway. "There is no if you don't," Ian said icily, "I've made no backup plans. This is my strategic move, and its success will create a distinct advantage in strategy for our side. You've joined on as a Captain in this brigade, and I believe you highly qualified to be successful in this undertaking. But think to yourself about what will happen if you fail- it won't be just you that is wiped from the face of the planet. Our hand will be played- they'll know what we have before the poker game even starts. And they will know that we've spent our surprise card, and have nothing left but a direct attack. And if we make a direct attack against them when they have their fortifications one hundred percent operational... we will fail." He spoke with the air and authority of a man who knew the gravity that his own words carried. They were frightening words for Lia, words that put such pressure on her that it seemed impossible to breathe in the room right now. But Lia had made a promise. And Lia Ambrosi did not break promises. "I'll get to planning, then." She was prepared to leave- but, like all the other KB members, she would not do so until dismissed. This left her firmly in Ian's thrall for a moment longer. "Don't make any moves for a few more weeks, Captain. Give yourself time to digest the information and prepare yourself for the task at hand. It's not going to be easy in and out; it's going to require some creativity. I don't want you telling anyone about this situation, even your husband. He will be informed when the time is due for you to do your job, but breaching this level of security could compromise the mission, plus... you don't want to know what happens in the Order when we think people may talk. Let us just say the old adage 'there are fates worse than death' might apply to our special agents who will deal with you in that case." Lia nodded. His implication was clear- and even after her relatively short time with the Order, she had heard rumors of the beings that he spoke about. Beings that could easily devour the intellect of anyone attempting to breach Order security. "You're dismissed, Captain." She stood to leave without any further words. What could she have said, after all? As her hand touched the ornate antique doorknob, he spoke again, and she froze. "Let me know if I can assist you in any way." She wanted to tell him that he'd already assister her plenty. Lucky for her, she was smart enough to keep her mouth shut. .:: ::: ::. A Week Later Hradpou’tn? Las Vegas, Nevada Her brother Laszlo found it amusing somehow that she had named her new house Hradpou’tn?, or Desert Castle. It was somehow fitting, he thought, as he sat on its back porch watching the Nevada sun spill its golden rays over the last of the flat horizon. The sky above it was warmed to a magenta, tapering off to a dim bloody crimson at its edges, as though the sun were a blade, slicing through the Earth's flesh each day to leave a wound that somehow magically cured itself before the sun rose again the next morning. She had invited him here some weeks ago, after finding out that he had secretly been in attendance at her wedding, but had chosen not to come into the ceremony. Perhaps the presence of the Hand of Cain would have disturbed most of the few beings present, but Lia was put out- she would have enjoyed it, she said. It was strange how, now, in what might have been the twilight of their kind's existence, if one believed the rumors that were almost constant, the two of them were getting along so swimmingly. It was rather like two old friends gathering to drink martinis on someone's back porch in Tucson, only all parties involved vastly preferred a warm cup of what the kids today call vitae, or blood in the old-fashioned jargon. Virgil was inside, preparing them what he called his cocktail. Even Lia was not allowed to know its ingredients. It was perhaps one of Virgil's sillier idiosyncracies, but then again, she would not have married him (twice, to boot) had she not found those little things altogether rather charming. "How is work?" She asked her brother the question with a slight gleam of a smile. It was that manner that most Kindred used when they were amused by their own imitation of human interaction patterns. "Oh, you know, same old same old. I run around carrying messages and gathering information when people think I'm not looking. The same things that all paragons must do, I suppose. Though I won't speak for what sort of grunt work the rest of them are doing, I certainly think I got the better end of the bargain, considering my master is at his very least, not an insane zealot." "There seem to be plenty of those to go around." He nodded in response. It was perhaps a slightly sour note to end the conversation with, all things considered. It was often the zealots that bore the most ill will towards the Children of Cain. It had long been an irony to them that those who claimed to serve a God who commanded his childer not to kill could so often find themselves doing it. "It's sad about Anezka," Lia said softly. Sad was really not the word for it. Anezka was, had been, the twins' younger sister. Another Czech Vlaszy child in a long line of Vlaszy children who served the Lord Vaclav Ionescu, and it seemed a shame that after surviving the tumult of all the years safely squirreled away from all those who would have put a price on the head of a Vlaszy, she was gone. Laszlo nodded moodily. "It is." There was silence for a moment, but in the space of twins, that was nothing that needed to be immediately filled. Laszlo was running over in his mind whether or not he really wanted to tell her everything he knew. Finally, he relented. "I've found some documents..." Or he relented halfway, at least. He didn't finish the sentence, instead turning to look at her with a somewhat sheepish or apologetic look on his face, as though he couldn't bring himself to say the rest, and needed prodding. He'd never been the best at expressing himself, even as a young man back in Zdice. "Oh?" she asked curiously, although trying to seem nonchalant in this situation was sort of like trying to act innocent when you were a cat with bird feathers sticking out of its mouth. "I traced Anzha to a small convent in southern California," he said, using the name they had always called her when they were children, "That was apparently where she was living before she..." He didn't have to finish the sentence, of course. "And the documents?" He wouldn't have mentioned them if they weren't something more than just a map to her location, after all. Laszlo made a displeased face, his smooth, lineless features contorting into something that looked altogether more bestial. "Tied up. They're among her personal effects- a family Bible of ours, and a collection of journals kind of like the ones you used to keep. Well, perhaps they are, I don't know. I haven't had occasion to track down and read hers, of course. The convent was somehow tied up with the remains of the Cappadocian clan, which, as we know now, is what she was sired into. And for some reason, they don't believe I'm her brother, and they won't let me have her things without some sort of documented proof." Laszlo snorted. "Documented proof! They want the Hand of Cain to have documented proof of his lineage from a time over a thousand years before birth certificates existed." Their faces took on a look that was a male-female mirror of disappointment. They looked surprisingly alike in some expressions, not so similar in others. This was the depth of hopelessness, however, because it was a tie to the past close enough to mend, but it seemed that neither of them had the correct needles to do it. "What will you do?" she asked simply. Laszlo shrugged in that way he had that somehow managed to seem predatory. "I'll go see them myself, again. Perhaps the Mother Superior can be... convinced." He let the word slide through with a sort of threatening grace. She didn't like to think about too many people getting caught up in his crossfire, not when they were between him and something that he wanted. One way or the other, and she could tell from the look in his eyes, he would have those documents. "It's a shame... they would be nice to have... to read..." It was the same sort of wistful remark that anyone might have made on the occasion, but it was loaded. Laszlo cast her a wary glance. "Why do you say that?" She sighed slightly. "You remember my visions? Master Ionescu?" Laszlo's lip curled. He had never particularly liked the way their master looked at his sister, but then again, he hadn't had much control over it, either. "Yes... what about them?" "The last time... he told me to get my things in order here, because I was to be called somewhere else. Spiritually, I assume, though he tends to speak in metaphors about those kinds of things. I think he means another journey, like the one my astral body took when Samma... Soma... wanted me to be his consort." Laszlo's lip curled further. Of all the paragons, he probably liked Sammael the least, but then again, that was like picking a bastard out of a pack of assholes. "And what will that consist of?" "Finishing up these last few things with Anezka, settling my mind about her, for one thing..." That was the easy part out of the way. The next part... she'd have to hedge carefully. Because she knew her brother wouldn't take a cop-out for an answer. "And for another, making good on a promise that I made someone." "A promise?" Lia nodded. "Mr. Childress, to be exact. In return for the training he gave me, I owe him a boon, if you will pardon our archaic terms for it." "What sort of boon?" "I can't say." The tone of her voice was even enough that he couldn't tell whether she didn't know, or was refusing to tell him. That was the damnable part about working with someone who knew how to handle people as well as a Tzimisce did. He supposed they weren't considered the perfect hosts for no reason, even if their parties did often have a not-so-splendid ending. "I see. And what sort of journey did Master Ionescu say you would be taking?" She shrugged. "He didn't. I don't suppose he really knows, or at least it would make enough sense if he didn't. As I understand it, he's sort of stuck where he is, spiritually, at least now that he gave up his chance at rebirth to watch over Virgil and I." "Such a sweet story," Laszlo practically snapped. He was never going to feel any other way about his old master, that much was plain, and they both knew it. Yet, Lia continued to bring him up as though they were still children one day destined to be his blood dolls. "Laszlo..." Her voice contained a warning-note’she would have no patience for his ill-temper this evening. After all, they were gathered for fellowship, not for argument. Argument could come at another time, if it was necessary. Footsteps approached from the distant kitchen, apparently Virgil finally making his way back from mixing the drinks. He was not often considered personable by most people who knew him, but had taken an especial liking to Lia's twin brother, more than likely because he was one of the only solitary beings around who was actually male. He stepped onto the patio, holding the tray out to Laszlo first, who took his cocktail with a nod of thanks. Next, he handed one to his wife, a small cactus bloom garnishing the glass to differentiate hers from theirs. He sat down in his chair next to her, quietly content- or at least that was his surface veneer. Virgil too had been going through his own turbulence lately, but he didn't want to talk about it, and trying to make him do so would have been impossible. "When do you think Rune will be up?" Lia said, turning quietly to him. Virgil shrugged. "Offhand, anywhere within the next hour. The instincts still haven't worn off him yet, so not until the light is dead. I left him a note to come meet us out here." "Good." Simultaneously, they all sipped their cocktails, Laszlo having waited until his hosts drank before he did it himself. It might have been considered a rude habit in some company, but when one was the Hand of Cain, one got used to expecting treachery and poisoned blood around every corner. The veranda lapsed into silence, though this time, it was a comfortable silence. Lia had to conclude that, though at the moment, she didn't know what was going to happen next in her life, there was a sense of completeness and stability that she had very rarely, if ever, had since being turned so many, many years ago. She didn't know what she had done to deserve it, but she intended to hold on to it. There was, of course, though, the element of the manila folder. It still sat in the study, burning a hole through its locked drawer with the false bottom. Virgil would never see those papers, and Lia would never let on that she had them, of course. She was looking forward to the day when she could burn them, and pretend they had never existed. Then, perhaps all this tranquility might feel permanent. But considering the nature of the task she was about to undertake, she had serious doubts that any of their lives would ever be tranquil again.
