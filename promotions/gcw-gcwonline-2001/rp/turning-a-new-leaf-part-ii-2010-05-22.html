Turning a New Leaf: Part II

22 May 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 05/22/10 The ceiling lights were more incriminating than warming, the kind that were meant to break the strong-willed. Still, Jay Terror smoked his cigarette ignoring the numbing effects of the incandescent bulbs. To his right sat officer Harvey and on his left was Officer Moore. Both wore serious eyes with hints of a smirk on their lips, most likely due to his celebrity. As it stood, Harvey was the mouth, the man with the most to say, while Moore sat and watched, forcing his uneasy stare over Jay's apathetic posture. "Look," Jay reminded. "I told you everything. She was lit out of her mind, but I didn't rape her. She consented to it all and must have forgotten the next morning. I was out, couldn't fill her in, and I'm sure she jumped to the worst case scenario." "If she was so lit though," started Harvey. "Then how was she able to consent?" "You don't have to be sober to say you wanna' have sex." "You just have to understand what we're working with. How did she get so high?" "Drugs." Harvey waved his hand looking for more. "Drugs that you furnished? What kind of drugs? You gotta' give us more if you want to clear yourself, Jay." "I didn't give her anything." "Did you do them with her?" "Not ecstasy, no. Not into that kinda' high. She asked and I kept to the Jack Daniels." Harvey and Moore exchanged glances for a moment. They were a good tandem, the kind of partners that had worked together before. Moore finally spoke. "Were you too drunk maybe to realize she said no?" "Girls don't say no to me." "Very confident in yourself," Moore went on. "It comes with the bandana. I've been around the world and done some cool things in my time. I'm not the everyday guy. When a girl gets the opportunity, she takes it from my experience." "And you've had lots of experience with this?" He smirked thinking of the women he'd had. He couldn't count them but he liked it that way. "With women, yes." Moore grabbed his own box of cigarettes out and lit an American Spirit. His motives were obvious but Jay wasn't buying into the similarities. As hard as Moore tried, he wouldn't be more friendly. "I want to believe you, Jay. I'm a fan of what you do out there and think that you've really done well for yourself. Fact is, the famous people are sometimes the ones we have to focus on. Some of you guys think you're untouchable and that nothing can happen to you. It's a breeding ground for bad behavior." "So because I'm on TV that makes me more susceptible to rape?" "Not exactly." "It's what you said." Moore pulled on the cigarette and coughed into his arm. He was only an occasional smoker. "What I mean is that we can't simply take your word for it. She was in your room, on drugs, and in pain. We need to hear witness reports, view security footage, and find out all we can before we're sure." "Then let me sit in a holding cell so you can collect the evidence. I have a pay-per-view to attend and I'm not missing it." "We can't promise you'll be able to wrestle, Jay." "And I can't promise that I'll be cooperative. Find a way to make it happen, Jack." Moore's name wasn't Jack at all and he shook his head at the tone used. Harvey took over again. "Did you know Michelle Genny before this?" "I've only been to Hawaii once before. Didn't see her the first time." "Did she seem like a likable person?" "She was on drugs; of course she was likable. We danced and she talked and it was all harmless fun. Hell, I figured I could get a tour around the island at least the next morning." Harvey snorted in a laughing sort of way. "That's one way to see Hawaii I guess." "Exactly." "Who do you fight this week?" Jay drew on the butt of his cigarette and dabbed it out into the ash tray. He found another and lit it to continue his habit. "In all honesty, I don't mind the back and forth. I'll shoot shit with you on any day. Fact is, if I'm not clear by the time the event rolls around then I won't be fighting anyone. Let's keep the questions dealing with Michelle and maybe later we can share some stories." Officer Harvey placed his half-smoked cigarette into the tray beside Jay's filter. He leaned back and crossed his arms taking on the role of a true police officer. His face was less friendly, his questions more pointed, and his objective was quite obvious. --- The remainder of the press conference seemed rushed to Maggie McVay as the questions were aimed toward Jay's removal. It had been some time since a GCW star had been taken out of their questions by police hands. Clyde Fox was cut off by James Bryan in the end, stating that the answers the people wanted were unknown at the time. As the media filed out of the room, Maggie McVay stood back. She was in as much shock as the general public, but she wasn't prying for the answers as hard. Instead, she knew the circumstances. Jay Terror had been framed, made to look like a fool, and now he was in police hands. She feared for him on the surface but knew deep down that there was little to worry about when it comes to Jay. He wasn't like most people, especially since he had bought a new pack of cigarettes before the press conference. Making his way toward Maggie was James Bryan, his head swayed with uneasiness. "How're you doing, Maggie?" "Just hoping he wraps things up soon. I'd hate to have this go on." "You're confident he didn't do it then?" "You're not?" "No, I am. Don't get me wrong. I was just curious what you knew." "He's Jay and someone finally caught onto the idea that his sexual tendencies could be enough to turn him in." "And to think he was turning a new leaf." Maggie flicked her brows. "He was trying to. In the end though, you can't take the deviant out of him. It's how he keeps going, like his own gasoline tank." James Bryan took a seat beside her. He turned the chair. "I want to go down there and help him out somehow. I mean, he's innocent and might as well miss Shockwave at the rate these officers are moving." "There's not much you could do though," Maggie replied. If there was a way, she would have been the first at the police station. The bond between the two of them was stronger than most and seeing Jay walk free would be truly uplifting. Unfortunately, her words weren't the key Jay was looking for. Instead, she was forced to painfully wait through the Hawaiian Police's slow start. "Will you replace him if he can't show up?" "I'm not sure. If anything, I guess that match would become a three-way, maybe a handicap match. We'd have to get with Johnny." "A three-way then." "Why's that?" "Johnny. He's got it out for Jay. I'm convinced of it. Ever since he's shown up, he's let Jay merely tread at the top." "He has ideas though, just wait to hear them out." "Maybe. It's just frustrating to see Jay suffer like that. He wants to be the best. It's all he talks about. In my mind I know that he's the best too. I just want him to have his chance to show off." "He'll get it. People like Jay aren't denied forever. Soon enough, he'll have his shot and he'll prove that he's matured enough to stay at the top of the federation. It's just how he is." "I know," she said, drawing out the ending. "I just want it now." "Now we have to prove Jay's innocent though. That's the first step." "You have a," Maggie stopped in the middle of the sentence as her phone lit up. It vibrated in her hand and she flipped it open hoping the unknown number was the police department with good news. She placed a finger up for Bryan. "Hello," she greeted. "I thought I took down your number, Lyle. How did you get mine?" She looked confused and began to nervously twirl her brunette lochs. "Some things came up, I won't be at the sunset." Lyle continued to press on the other side and Bryan leaned in to try and catch a word. "One drink, that's it though. Only because I need to clear my head." She finished the call and shut the phone into her pocket. "You have a date?" "Somehow, I have something. I took his number down earlier but he called me still." "That's odd." "I'd say. Now I'm roped into having a drink." "Well do it, clear your mind some, and I'll go find out how the investigation is moving along. If they aren't viewing the security tapes yet then I'll be sure I get a complaint ready." "Thank you for the help." "It's my job. Besides, I wanted to see the new leaf he was working on. Sounded like he finally found where he wanted to be." "I hope." --- Jay Terror sat in the room alone, a prison the officers felt was more fit than a holding cell. He'd already been through almost all of his cigarettes and was eyeing up the box of American Spirits left behind. As much as he hated to admit it, he was a nervous smoker. The nicotine was his religion, the one true thing he could turn to in his time of need. For years he'd smoked the same sticks, he'd relieved thousands of pains, and he'd gone on without worrying about the harm he was doing. Finally, things seemed to be catching up with him. He was slower in the ring, less aware of what was happening, and out of breath far too early. Tessa Windsor was the tell-tale sign. Still, he couldn't stop. He'd built his career on the tobacco and climbed the ladder without giving up his famous habit. To blanket it, he liked to believe that he'd already done the damage. Nothing worse could happen. Be it a weak outlet, he lived off it nonetheless. Still, while sitting under the lights he couldn't help but wonder what his life would be like without the products of his affection. He believed that he'd be the world champion already. He pictured himself carrying around the title as if he had the same prestige LeStatt Knight had. On top of that, he saw himself in more power, able to create matches and design destruction however he pleased. They were pipe-dreams perhaps, but they were all things that he wanted. As he finished another Marlboro, he crushed it in the ash tray and sat staring at the pillar of smoke climbing out of it. He watched for a solid minute, hoping he could zone out, but it didn't last. The situation called for another and he lit his second to last stick to calm himself once again. --- Maggie McVay walked out of the limousine with slow steps. To say she was scared was an understatement. When it came down to it, she couldn't pin point how he had been able to get her phone number. It was the start of a horror film and each step closer to the bar she took felt like it could very well be her last. In her moments before opening the steel double doors, she thought of the life she'd led. For a while, she'd forgotten who she was. Her past had become a blur and she could only trace her roots back to sorrow. The fact was that after her husband, Steve, had died, she found little drive. Honestly, if it hadn't been for Jay, she would have never watched a wrestling event again. Thanks to Steve and their former gym, the sport was branded in her brain though. She was meant to be ringside and she was meant to give pointers to the struggling stars. Perhaps, she wondered, that was why she felt at home alongside Jay Terror. She felt passion when she helped him, as if she was in her zone. As it stood, helping him had cleared some of the fog that had settled on her past. Like any mourning widow though, the days weren't as easy as that. There were tears sometimes, some days more than others, but through it all she tried to think of anything positive. Jay had been the one who urged her to date again. He claimed that getting back out onto the market would help her find true happiness again. It was what Steve would want he persuaded. Taking his advice though, was not something she felt confident in as she grabbed the door handles. Lyle was nice, yes, but behind every nice guy stood human tendencies. She pulled open the door and immediately, she ran into the music like a wall. The bar was packed, club-like almost, and she struggled to even step inside. When in, Maggie scanned the people for Lyle. His height and blonde hair was her beacon. --- James Bryan stood outside of Jay's hotel room with Officer Harvey. Harvey leaned against the wall while Bryan stood free. "I'm sorry Mr. Bryan, there's not much we can release about this situation." "I'm with his employing company. I work for Steven Caldera and it's in his best interest to learn everything I can." "Unfortunately, this doesn't work like that. We need to get the evidence, get the facts straight, and find out how all this went down. Rape is a touchy subject and we can't go giving details so easily." "I know what rape is. He didn't do it though." "Were you there?" "I beg your pardon?" "Were you filming them? Do you have proof that this was made up? If not, you're going to have to let us do our jobs." "He has a wrestling event to attend though." "And my daughter is receiving communion in the morning. We all have things to do Mr. Bryan, but just because he's famous doesn't mean that he's going to get to do them." "I understand that. It's just," James Bryan started before being cut off. "It's just not your place to get into. We have a firm hold on this and will see it through until it's over." "Right." "Get yourself a drink maybe, take a load off. I'll come to you if I have any more questions." "Right." James Bryan began to walk away from Officer Harvey but stopped with the buzz of his phone. The name on the screen read Maggie McVay. "Maggie, hey. Aren't you on your date?" He grinned but changed his expression after a second of listening. "Evidence? Really?" Bryan reached toward Harvey and lowered the mouthpiece of his phone. "Officer Harvey, I think we have something you should see." He turned around before entering the room and shook his head, "Like what?" "Michelle Genny." "What about her?" Bryan placed the phone back into his pocket and walked toward Harvey. "Come with me." --- She was on the floor when Maggie spotted Lyle, lying in a puddle of spilled margarita. People were laughing, trying to get a glimpse of her exposed parts, and Lyle sat trying to help her to her feet. As she grabbed his hands, her blood red eyes whirled Maggie's direction and she knew that she'd found what she needed to. McVay kept a low profile and grabbed a table to keep out of site. She watched Michelle Genny from a distance, taking mental notes of her side effects. What truly made her wonder though was how she was free enough to be out like this. Surely, the officers would see the same. On top of that, the lack of protocol following was enough of a threat to get Jay free again. A half hour passed and she took occasional photos with her cell phone. Most of them captured her drink consumption and her influence on Lyle's as well. Some were of the way her eyes glowed, as if in another haze. Finally, a hand grabbed onto her shoulder. It belonged to James Bryan. In tow was Officer Harvey. Immediately, he picked out the Hawaiian local who'd screamed rape. Harvey leaned on the table, "How long have they been here?" "At least an hour. I'm pretty sure she's on the drugs again." "And who's the guy?" She shook her head, "Just some guy. She keeps feeding him drinks." "You sure it's not the other way around?" "I'm sure that she did the same with Jay, so no." "How many has she had?" "I have pictures of at least four in this last half hour." Harvey looked stunned and called into his radio. He approached carefully but James Bryan and Maggie didn't bother sticking around. They took seats inside of the limousine each knowing one thing: Jay Terror would make Shockwave and show the world a new side. --- A week removed from the incident, Jay Terror and James Bryan sat in a coffee shop within New South Wales. Terror ate a muffin while Bryan drank a cup of coffee. "You took a good step this past week. Seems like you really did turn the leaf." "I just went out and wrestled. It's been a while since I really wrestled." "Well keep it up. If things go your way again, you may be in for a treat soon enough." "I can only hope," added Jay. For once in his life, he didn't demand his opportunity. Instead, he felt content taking his time and showing people that he truly did deserve it all. Although failure was possible, he believed that it wasn't probable. That alone was enough to continue with the strategy. "It's just finally time for one on one with Tessa." "Been a while, yeah." "You think I'll beat her?" "I think I'm already crossing the line talking to you this much lately. I don't know if you'll win but I can tell you that it'll be competitive." "I'll take it." If competitive is what he needed to do, then he'd do it. New leaf or not, he was ready to reveal who Jay Terror was.
