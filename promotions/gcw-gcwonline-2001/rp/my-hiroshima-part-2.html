My Hiroshima, Part 2

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 1st/2nd Sigh. “Rei doesn’t know what to say,” Rebecca Hendley was telling herself, repeating it over and over again ad infinitum. It was beginning to lose definition. Rebecca forced the thought from her head as she listened on the phone. Only a malignant silence. She couldn’t remember when she’d called. Not that it mattered, anyway. The only thing she had heard from Rei was his sporadic breathing, probably when he snapped to and suddenly thought to inhale. Rebecca surfaced the memory of the last time they’d talked on the phone. A whir and confusion of pointless thoughts and random quotes. They would be in competition, testing each other’s mental capacity concerning life’s meaningless. It always brought a smile to her, she recalled. Even now, midst the drama, she was smiling as she thought about some of them. Smokey the Bear’s zip code. Campbell’s Swastika soup. How donkeys kill more people annually than plane crashes. Sigh. She missed those days. Filled with musings, playful fighting ? physical contact. She missed Rei’s look and feel. His touch. Rei treated her like fine porcelain when he caressed her; careful not to stroke her into shattering. His demeanor had a gentleness that was the bitter antagonist to his violent profession, and she adored that as much as she admired it. Above all however, she missed what even Neanderthals had mastered - communication. Rebecca found herself wondering if he was actually listening on the other end or if he was just there physically. A zombie clinging to the phone, listening to the language of the living. Is he ever going to speak? “A simple how are you would suffice,” Rebecca thought. She had no doubt that Rei still harbored feelings for her; his reluctant silence was the preaching parrot perched atop his shoulder. Reading him like a novel was one of her charms over Rei. No matter what he did, she knew the higher truth. He couldn’t lie, cheat, or steal. Rei was a chapter book she knew almost by heart. “Rei?” The name sounded almost foreign whispering off her tongue. She always had him in her thoughts, but she hadn’t uttered the name to him in so long. He didn’t hear, of course. Saying it quiet was all she could manage, right now; it hurt too much. Rebecca was a different person around Rei, she knew. Even her tone changed. He melted her heart and warmed her soul in an instant. Best friends equivocating on the verge of being lovers. Dancing on the fine line of intimacy. She knew no better way to describe it. Thinking back to how Rei would recite, she smiled. That was his narration. Rebecca had adopted it as her own simply because she could think of no better. Rei always had a way with verse. “Except now,” Rebecca said to herself. She couldn’t get mad. Anger was far removed from her emotions. What replaced it was far worse and more exhausting. Disappointment, frustration. Heart-breaking sadness. She always felt high above such sentiments, especially considering how Rei escalated her to new heights. Never letting her fall, always catching her mid-flight. Now, she was free falling. Rei was her heroin and he had disappeared, leaving her hollow and insensible to the world. Maybe he had a grasp on her, too. Many sleepless nights haunted her. Lying awake, tossing and turning, reflections swimming in her head too fleet-footed to actually contemplate. Her bed and room, once a comfortable and safe haven, transformed into the bowels of despondence. She had taken the pictures of them that adorned her room down, tears slipping down her cheeks. It was another needle of pain with every picture, each one stinging a bit more and reminding her that ? It was over? That was impossible. Reality obscured. They were beyond simple break-ups and stupid adolescent bull sh**. A relationship that could endure and sustain, that went beyond limitations of a feeble mind and the idea of love. “We were best friends first and foremost,” Rebecca thought. Surely, Rei had thought about it as much as she had. What they had transcended simple relationship and companionship. Theirs was special and wasn’t meant for foolish games. “How have you been” Are you alright?” She spoke softly. He didn’t respond. Was she reaching for meaning where there may be none? Rebecca had searched for purpose and definition in things since she became of age to wisely question. Curious about everything, she wanted to comprehend it all. That was a trait she inherited from her parents and her best memories of them hold hands with their logical minds. They were religious in the sense of being spiritual. Seeking higher explanations, they were more scientific in their discovery of knowledge. Their ideas were complex, though she grew to understand. Her parents offered rejoinders, where religion posed only the questions, and they were forced out of the Catholic church for being radical in their ways. Even today, when Rebecca spoke to them, she felt like a wide-eyed child. Learning the secrets and history of the world through virgin eyes. Besides Rei, her parents were the only people she turned to in times of turmoil and tragedy. Her friends were always there, but were often too removed to even help themselves. Beside themselves. She thought about Jacob Finnergan briefly. The mutual friend between them. He was the common link that had chained them together. She and Rei had met each other through Jacob, and how Jacob and Rei met, Rebecca was still unsure. She just knew that it had been the beginning of the best months of her life. The irony? Now, these past few weeks and months had been some of the worst. “Jacob told me you were back into wrestling.” Jacob was the only way she could find out anything about Rei, anymore. Rei had stopped calling shortly after his last wrestling stint, when his mood turned increasingly dark and somber. She suspected it had something to do with her move from California. After that, her attempts at contact were in vain and she developed quite a relationship with the automated voice on his answering machine. Work at the art museum always kept her busy and she had no time to travel to San Diego. Portland seemed continents away. Her promotion from presenter to art and d’cor manager consumed more of her time than she liked. Visits to San Diego became infrequent and it got to the point where even phone calls became scarce. Rei’s job had him traveling all over the country every other week. Shortly after making a promise that nothing would come between them, Rei fell off the face of the earth. Seemingly. The promise that broke the chain. At the beginning, she felt it was an obstacle they could easily overcome. Weeks later, she feared everything they had was beyond repair. Rebecca blamed herself. “It was my over-achieving attitude and my strive for success,” she often told herself. Often aiming for the moon in things, she usually landed among the stars. Unrealistic, it may be. Rei thought this was her one drawback. “Being an over-achiever pushes everything else to the pits of your mind,” he had said. She took it for simple jealousy and selfishness, then. Now, she thought there might be a portion of truth in that morsel. Once again, she blamed herself. ? Silence, still. She honestly didn’t know what she expected. It had been quite a few weeks since they had talked. It was surprising enough he even answered the phone. How much longer could she mask her frustration? Usually able to hide her emotions, she found that Rei was able to bring them out simply by giving her the cold shoulder. Imagining him sitting in San Diego, she thought of the smug grin that might have adorned his face, as he counted down the last seconds before she lost it and forcefully interrogated him. It was far from the truth, she figured, but her imagination was making up fables as she grew more ? Desperate. A strong word, though soaked in truth. Desperate for happiness only Rei had given her. Desperate for the life they shared. Clich??s and desperation aside, she simply needed it. What else could she want? She posed that to herself, once again sifting through the cluttered thoughts, seeking a response with which she could live. It wasn’t there. “Rei ?” Still no response. More interrupted breathing. Rebecca had loved Rei soon after their meeting. He had a personality that illuminated; he was her beacon of light when she was blanketed by darkness. Everything had fit like a jigsaw, all the pieces resting where they should. Now, feeling like her world was imploding on herself, she was powerless. She hated that feeling. The feeling of helplessness. Hated it almost more than the thought of a life without Rei. Tears were welling up inside her. They were part of her life as much as eating or showering now. It was a sad truth she didn’t have the courage to face. “I love you, Rei.” The words didn’t linger long before she hung up, cutting off their line of communication. Pressing the phone against her chest, Rebecca let the tears free. They streamed down her face, soaking her silky peach-colored nightgown. They streamed down still, sliding from the sides of her face, hitting her emerald shaded pillows with tiny splashes. She glanced out the window for the first time that night, watching the dark clouds cover the night sky. It was the only thing she and Rei had shared in a long while. A smile formed briefly, and Rebecca turned in her bed, letting sleep take hold. It wasn’t long before dreams spread their wings.
