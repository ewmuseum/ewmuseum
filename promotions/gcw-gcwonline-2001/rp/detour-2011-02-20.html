Date: 02/20/11 "Please take your seats. The captain has turned on the seatbelt sign." I really hate flying with these...people. What people? Regular people. The worst kind of fucking people. The Fox News-watchin', trailer park-livin', mouthbreathing inbred fucktards that ruin everything. Lexi booked me coach. Again. Man, I really need to learn to do this on my own. She's off in France or Belgium or some stupid-ass European shithole promoting her new fashion crap and...ugh, I can't even care enough to finish that sentence. I swirl the empty ice in my drink. I need another one. I hit the stewardess button. Hit it again. Ten more times, and she finally shows up. I hold up the glass and shake the ice, the universal sign for "get me another fucking drink, bitch." She huffs. "Yes, sir?" You have got to be... "Refill. Now." She hangs for a moment, before snatching the glass out of my hand and storming off. Yeah, you do that lady. I dunno if she recognized me, since now I've got my own row to myself, but I tell you I'll be goddamned if I was gonna stay in that other seat. Placing me next to some Chinese lady and her tiny little brat. The same little brat that kept spewing up what smelled like three-day-old Egg McMuffins in the seat between us. You take two hours of that, and you're a better man than I am, I tell you. So yeah, I fuckin' moved. I'm seriously considering buying this airline when we land, firing everyone, and stripping the planes and selling them to the Russian mob. This guy I know, Evgeny...man, he cannot get enough of Boeing's engine guts. I think he's got mental problems. The glorified waitress returns with my Glenlivet. I reach for it, and she pulls away. "Eight dollars," she replies. Huh? "Eight dollars," she repeats, and yeah, I heard you the first time. I reach into my pocket and hand her my Black Card. She looks at it like it's the first time she's seen one, but for some reason, her jaw's not dropping. And then, she asks it. "What am I supposed to do with this?" Maybe she's never seen a credit card before. I doubt that highly. "You put the tab of that drink on that," I inform her. "It's called a credit card." "Sir, this is an airplane." She hands it back to me. "We don't accept credit cards." I pause. "You're joking." She shakes her head 'no'. "This is two-thousand and ten," I state. "Eleven," the stupid know-it-all sober bitch corrects me. "Whatever. Fine." I hand it back to her. "Go ring it up and give me my damn drink, woman." She tosses the credit card onto my lap. "Sir, if you want this drink, it will be eight dollars. I will also need to check your identification." Oh man, she's really grating on my nerves. Time to turn on the charm. I lean forward. "Look..." I need a moment to check her name-tag. She doesn't have a name-tag. Why doesn't she have a name-tag? Time to improvise. "Sugar, we've got off on the wrong foot. Let's just start over, and we can figure this all out, okay?" I reach for her hand. She's not buying it. She leans in. "Sir, do I need to get the air marshal?" I reach into my pocket and fumble around. I'm sure I've got cash in my pocket. I have to. Let's see...condom, cigarettes, lighter, cell phone. Other pocket...wallet. I open it up and hand her a twenty. She hands me the drink real shaky-like, and it nearly spills everywhere before she walks off to the back of the plane. I turn back to holler. "Wait, my..." She's gone. "...change." And so I take a drink. Fucking people... ****** "Ahh, this is your captain speaking. We, ahh...we will not be landing at LaGuardia as originally expected." Everyone groans. Wonder why. "Instead we will be landing at Newark International. We will be making our descent in, ahh...just a few moments. Thank you for flying with us this evening. We're sorry for the inconvenience." Everyone murmurs, but there's still some noise. Beeping. Ruffling. "Yeah, sorry you're all so damn poor." Laughing, followed by worry. "Whaddya mean it's still o--" I'm a few days early to New York, which sucks. I'm a West Coast guy, born and bred as such. The bright shining sun all day, every day. The Wharf, the Haight, the Rock. The glitz of Hollywood a 45 minute flight away. None of this Village hipster bullshit or Yankees-Red Sox speak or even the "facks". It just ain't my thing. I mean, sure, I'm cool with staying at the Plaza and chilling at Marquee and all that. But it just ain't home. And right now, I kinda miss home a little bit. My home, my bed, my tank, my bud Murr Jr. I hope he doesn't get into the wine and start fucking the Victorian couch again. That really was a lotta monkey cum. I really should get someone to watch him more often. I can't even imagine half the shit I don't know about that he gets up to when we're not around. "Crew, prepare for landing," the captain booms out, and I do the same. ****** I am an intimidating dude. I know I am. When I get off the plane, I've got no bags, I'm dressed impeccably, and everyone looks. But in their eyes, it's not awe or amazement. It's fear and worry. Some of them might know who I am and what I do. Most of them haven't the slightest idea. But every time they see me, they stop and watch. Mothers move children out of the way, while dads huddle the family behind them. They treat me like a thug or some bastard celebrity that fucks pornstars and does blow in five-star hotels. These people, the ones that know my name and to the ones I'm a stranger alike. They all fear me. Maybe not fear, but they certainly all take pause. And I wonder if I like it or not. I feel a rumble in my pocket and it's my cell. It's a text from Lexi. "Hope u made it ok. Paris roxx! Ttyl." 'T-T-Y-L.' What the fuck does that mean? 'T-T-Y-L.' It sounds so stupid, I can't simply extrapolate what she's saying. Why the fuck do people talk like this? Why not just say what you're gonna say? And why even bother with text messages? Why type for twenty minutes to convey something to me that takes fifteen seconds by actually properly using the fucking phone and dialing the number? I get so flustered I just shove the phone back into my pocket. I'll let her worry that I overslept and missed the flight or that my plane crashed. That'll teach her to send me stupid shit. I spot an airport bar and decide to leap in for a quick drink. It's probably morning since I took the red-eye, but I'm about to dare someone to call me on it. I leap right up to the bar and order a scotch on the rocks, and I gaze up at the morning sports scores. It's more basketball trade news, and I'm just not that interested in that stuff. Tell me when they trade the guy. Otherwise shut the fuck up about it. No one wants to hear the process of how I go about taking a shit, so why do I wanna listen to this nonsense? I drain down the scotch and call for another. The balding bartender gives me a weary eye, but when I pull my black card out from my pocket and toss it on the bar, his disposition switches from "judgmental asshole" to "might suck my dick if I asked him to." At least some people still have respect these days. 'T-T-Y-L.' I can feel it growing in my head like a black cancerous tumor. This isn't going away anytime soon. Great. I finish up my second drink, close the account and decide to finally get out the door of the airport. The air hits me, and there's a lingering putrid stench that nearly singes along my nosehairs. I can't put my finger on it, but I imagine something along the lines of what 'mediocrity' might smell like. I pace along the waiting area, where families hug and people meet for affair hookups or drug buys or whatever people do in the airport waiting areas. I don't care. They're not me. I just want a car to take me to a club or a bar or a liquor store or a strip club that sells alcohol. Wait. I look at my watch, it's only noon. Let's re-think the strip club angle... I check the area, and there's no driver waiting for me with a sign that says "Frost." That's...oh yeah, we're not in New York. Stupid fucking shitty goddamned commercial airline companies. I've really gotta learn to book my own flights. It can't be hard, can it? If Shatner can do it, I bet I could, too. Suddenly, the area becomes familiar. We were just here. I mean, I didn't really hang out in the area or do much 'round here. Lexi's security team ushered me in to the arena from New York and right back to the Waldorf immediately after the show. But I recognize the area. And I might as well check it out while I'm here. I didn't get to do it last time. It's gotta be at least kinda decent. I mean... Jersey can't be that bad, right? ****** Five minutes in, I find the nearest fucking taxi. I leap in and toss him lots of money. "Get me the fuck out of Jersey. NOW!!!!" And you best believe he does. ****** I sit in a coffee shop in SoHo after having checked into the hotel and enjoy a bitter black coffee and an overpriced dry blueberry muffin given to me by some aspiring indie film-making pretentious dickwad with horn-rimmed glasses, 'vintage' clothes, a leather wristband and an ironic tone. Still better than Starbucks, though. I leaf through the fashion magazine I grabbed off the rack with Lexi on the front cover. I really can't believe she's doing as well as she has. It doesn't hurt that she's got a whole shitload of my money behind her, but still. When some fashion magazine I've never heard of says she's one of the new fresh faces of fashion...that's probably got to mean something, right? But that cover. She flashed a shit-eating grin like she rules the world and struck an elitist pose that I don't even think I'd have had the balls to try. What a bitch. She actually did what I dared her to do. Man, I love her so much. I've really missed her. It's gotten me to thinking about what I do more and more. If this is really what I am now. Really, it can't be about the people. I mean, I can't stand the people. The chick with the tits. The snatch-haired bitch. Clown raper. The guy that wears the red bandana and sunglasses everywhere. Fucking Murray. But I'm asking myself something each time now, and lately the question hasn't been "do I like it or do I want out." It's been "is this now a part of me." The lights, the cameras, the announcers, the accolades...the attention. I don't have to do this. It's not even really a job. I could walk away tomorrow, and I don't think I'd feel any more indifferent than before I was even brought into this by Lexi. I only really stuck around for her, because she seemed to enjoy all this. She's hopping 'round the world, living her dream. I ain't mad at her about it, really, but this was all because of her. I guess I like it. I don't know anymore... The hipster douche comes and asks if I want a refill of my coffee. Of course I do, stupid. Can't you see it's fucking empty? So I shake my head 'no' and leave. ****** I've never been in the arena outside of the show. I just come in, do my thing, avoid all of the assholes as best I can manage, and get the hell out the door. But here, ringside...I never realized how close it really was. It's so damn close. Yeah, people have spit on me. Taken swings at me. Thrown things at me. Thrown things on me. Threatened me and my wife, both homicidal and sexually in nature. And I know it's always been close, but not this close. I've been thinking about the future a lot. Whether I might just walk away from this, that I've had my fill. I'm still young, and I've done a lot. But I could see myself leaving. Moving to France with Lexi, or just following her around on her fashion jaunts. Maybe do a little runway work myself. Or maybe just buy a football team, move it to Los Angeles. Spend my days meddling in the day-to-day affairs, building my own personal Madden All-Star team. I could do anything. But is this what I really want? Fighting date rapists, weathermen, and mentally stunted Scotsmen in arenas across the globe? Getting vicious voicemails and scathing e-mails about how I skipped another autograph signing or 'fan fest', whatever the fuck that is? That, no, Murr Jr. cannot have Sally Ford's home address? Or maybe just to keep hearing people curse my name like I was Satan himself? How much is too much, you know? And as I get up from the ringside seat and slide into the almost-completed ring, I walk around the ring and I imagine it's months down the road. I'm in the ring, a full-capacity crowd. Everyone boos. They never cheer, always boo. I can hear the disdain in their voice. The hatred. The pure, unadulterated hatred. And they all look at me, and there's something new about me. Something I have now. Something I've taken. Taken from either something they love unabashedly, or someone they at the very least respect. But I have it now, and they don't like that. It's in my hand, and the sound of their venom, it grows. Louder and louder. I walk towards the turnbuckle. Louder and louder. I ascend up, make the climb. Louder and louder. I look at it for a moment. Revel in it. Louder and louder. I hold it up, for all to see. I show them my prize. My trophy. And I can't hear myself think, it's so loud. When I come back to reality, I know I'm still on the turnbuckle, facing those people that aren't really here, hearing the sound that is not echoing throughout. And I know I'm by myself, fantasizing about something that I'm not even sure I really want. But I just decide to enjoy the moment. For just a little bit longer.
