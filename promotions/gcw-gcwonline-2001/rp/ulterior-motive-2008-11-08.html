Ulterior Motive

8 November 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/08/08 PROLOGUE When Jacob McKail managed to sleep, he didn’t dream. Not anymore. The days of hope and happiness were long behind him and there was no longer anything in his life that would warrant a dream. In his unconscious state, only nightmares awaited him. They lurked in the darkest recesses of his mind, waiting to tease and torment him in a variety of frightening and soul-destroying ways. There was a particular recurring nightmare that scared him the most. It started with darkness. He couldn’t see a damned thing, but he could hear their angry voices, feel their heavy grip latch onto his arms, wrists and shoulders. He recognise a few of their voices, but no idea where from. Then, the light hit him and he saw the faces of those he’d wronged dwell around him, hundreds if not thousands all around him, with angered faces and murderous eyes. They shouted, whispered and sang unspeakable things that they were going to do to him. And then the light went away. He would like to say that’s where his nightmare ended and he bolt upright in bed sweating, but it would be a lie. After the darkness took him once more, they fought each other to get to him first and then they gave him the punished they deemed appropriate; the punishment he himself knew he deserved. I CAGED ANIMALS “Ugh...” McKail managed, struggling to force his eyes open. “Oh fuck...” “I’m with ya buddy,” the vaguely familiar voiced sympathised, placing a friendly hand upon his shoulder. “Feels like a dog shat in my head.” Desperately trying to ignore both his nausea and crippling headache, McKail glared at the hand and then at the perpetrator. It was Mike from his local bar. He looked a little rough and when McKail finally took in his surroundings, he realised it probably wasn’t just down to the booze: they were in a damned prison cell. “What the fuck...”” McKail muttered, painfully trying to re-ignite his admittedly sketchy memories from the night before. But it was useless. He couldn’t formulate anything approaching coherent picture in his head and the pain was too excruciating push himself any further. He gave up and, massaging his forehead gently, leaned back against the wire mesh. The cell was large, but it was also crowded with a variety of scumbags and villains he didn’t recognise, as well as those he did. Other patrons of the bar. Actually, it was all of the regulars. What the fuck happened last night? He found himself sat on a wooden bench, propped up against the wire mesh of the prison cell and from the jolt of pain shooting down his neck; he’d been there a while too. His ass even hurt and really hoped that was just down to sitting on it for too long. “Don’t worry man,” Mike began, “I don’t remember much either.” Well that’s not comforting at all, Mike, McKail thought, not even bothering to formulate the words. It seemed like a waste of time. “But apparently there was some sort of fight at in South Street,” he explained, obviously trying to remember the story one of the others had told him. “I don’t know what over, but it spilled out into the street and the police were called.” He paused, looking about ready to heave. McKail edged back as far as he could without nudging into the hairy Hell’s Angel type sitting next to him, making sure he would avoid chunks if Mike actually blew. Mike managed to contain his dinner well enough to continue. “What the hell we were all doing in South Street I don’t know, but they just threw us all in this cell and are waiting for us all to sober up. It’ll be nothing to worry about.” Yeah, I bet... Opposite McKail, a rather large Mexican stood from his wooden bench, pulled his jeans down and proceeded to take a dump in the communal toilet at the back wall. It wasn’t long before everyone in the cell could smell what he was cooking and identical disgusted faces quickly spread as far as the eye could see. “Aw, Jesus!” Mike cursed, raising his dark grey t-shirt over his nose to help filter out the odour. A number of people all around copying both his curse at his makeshift filtering system, but it was the bald Hell’s Angel type sitting next to me who spoke up first. “You’re a fucking animal you know that?” “I had to take a dump man,” the Mexican shrugged. “What do you want me to do, brake one off on the floor for you all to admire?” The man sitting next to me shook his head. “You hold it in, is what you do.” The Mexican shook his head and waved him away. “Man, you’re crazy. I do that I explode.” “I could live with that,” the man sitting next to me told him. A few other people scattered around the cell nodded in agreement, whilst Jerry, another one of McKail’s regular drinking compatriots hurled over a Puerto Rican transvestite who was sat in the corner of the cell trying to chat up one of the supervisory police officers standing guard a few yards away. The little fruit looked used to it though and didn’t put up much of a fight even after Jerry stuck a finger up at him afterwards for his troubles. Mike chuckled at the scene and leant in closer to McKail. “Did I ever tell you about the time Jerry picked up a prostitute on the South Side?” McKail shook his head and rubbed his eyes with his thumb and forefinger. “Am I gonna wanna hear this, Mike?” “Probably not,” Mike told him, shaking his head, “but I’m gonna tell it ya anyways!” McKail rolled his eyes. “Great.” “Well, it was the summer of eighty-eight and--? “Jacob McKail?” A cop called, walking into the holding area. McKail raised a hand. “Yeah?” The cop had someone unlock the cell door for him and drew closer. “We need to ask you a few questions.” He unlocked the cell door. “Come with me.” McKail nodded and painfully stood, trying to hold his own dinner down. “Okay.” II THE ACCUSED The officer had escorted him to what the Desk Sergeant had labelled “Interrogation Room Four”, shoved him into a particularly uncomfortable metal chair facing the grim and rusted Iron door. He then unlocked the cuff on his right hand and locked it together around a rather sturdy steel metal railing that the underside of the table, which was positioned in between him and the door. The officer stood in the corner of the room just behind him and waited. What for, McKail was about to find out. “Jacob McKail, born the twelve of March, nineteen seventy-four, in The Bronx, New York,” the man in the cheap white shirt and god-awful brown tie announced, directly upon entering the room. He glanced up from the paper file he was reading from and glared at McKail questioningly. “That you?” McKail nodded. “Sounds like.” He appeared young; early to mid thirties at the most, but his face looked worn. Premature wrinkles had already enveloped his forehead and the circles under his eyes would put Keith Richards to shame. His badge clung onto the site of his belt desperately and told McKail that this wasn’t just a drunk and disorderly rap, at least not for him. “I’m Detective Bullock,” he gruffly greeted. A Detective? McKail silently questioned. What the hell does he want with me? He thought about it for a while, but came up with nothing. What the hell happened last night? “August two thousand and six, you were charged with a DUI in some damned hick town. Got a suspended sentence and a relatively small fine,” the detective continued. “Well, relatively small for a big wrestling star like you anyways. ? If he really knew what I got paid, McKail thought amidst the haze and agony of a considerable hangover, he’d probably reconsider that last statement. The detective took a seat opposite him and threw McKail’s file down on the table. If he was trying to make some kind of point or gesture, McKail didn’t pick up on it. “So,” the detective began, his eyes burning holes in McKail’s face, “where were you around nine twenty last night?” McKail thought about the question for a time, unable to force memories to burst through the cloudy torture of his spectacularly brutal hangover. Ultimately he shook his head, he decided he’d better before his head exploded and redecorated the interrogation room an interesting shade of red. He took a guess, “Doyles Bar up on--? “Do you realise it’s a felony to lie to a police officer, Jacob?” the detective interjected. If McKail wasn’t sure before, he was damned certain now. This fuck has it in for me, he decided himself, but why? “I’m not sure.” The detective developed a mock confused expression and eyed him warily. “You’re not sure?” McKail shook his head. “No, but I assume I was at the bar.” “You assume?” the detective asked. McKail nodded. “When I get back in town, that’s where I drink. Last night was no different.” “Well last night must’ve been different, Jacob,” the detective told him, “you and your buddies were picked up on South Street twenty miles away from Doyle’s Bar disturbing the peace at ten. The bartender said you left his place around nine. At nine-twenty a young single mother was killed in a hit and run seven miles away from Doyle’s and on a direct route to South Street. Is any of this sounding familiar?” McKail’s stomach sank to his ass and suddenly he felt sick. “What the fuck are you getting at?” “The bartender’s statement and the complaints called through by the residents of South Street put you in the area where the girl was killed,” the detective began, “by the time we pick you and your buddies up, none of you can even remember your own names, where you were or how you got there. Most of you still can’t. Get the picture?” McKail shook his head, trying to force the memories from last night back into recollection. Perhaps it’s not the alcohol that’s making me forget, he thought, perhaps the memories are just to painful. What if I killed that girl.. “No, we didn’t, we couldn’t...” He muttered, then paused as the detective’s words finally took meaning. “What do you mean most” The detective grinned. “Pat Maguire confessed about an hour ago. Said you and the rest of your drinking buddies got all tanked up in Doyle’s and then went out looking for some action. Identified you as the one who drove them down to South Street and said you hit something along the way, though he didn’t know it was that girl. Any of this sound familiar yet?” McKail shook his head. “No.” III THE LEVERAGE McKail didn’t even remember them dumping him back in the cage. His mind was busy, desperately attempting to process the information Detective Bullock had fed him and having little luck. He still couldn’t remember the details, but there was something of a familiarity with the story he was told. Did he knock down that girl? He didn’t know. He told himself over and over that it wasn’t something he would do, but the truth was he didn’t believe his own constant reassurances. Deep down, he conceded the eventuality was at least plausible and that’s what scared him the most. He glanced around at some of the familiar faces from earlier, but he couldn’t see any of his friends. The Hell’s Angel guy he sat next to before Detective Bullock had requested an audience told him they were hauled out of the cage five minutes before he was brought back. Looks like they’re trying to separate us, he decided. But why? Some kind of tactic? Were they trying to get the regulars from Doyle’s to turn on him? What the hell was going on? “Jacob?” Fuck! He silently cursed. He recognised the voice immediately and consequently his gut churned. It wasn’t Detective Bullock, nor one of the other cops, it wasn’t even one of his drinking buddies. It was Caroline; his late wife’s sister and the only person in his life who gave a damn about him anymore. “Caroline?” McKail asked, glanced up at her stood just beyond his cage. The other inmates noticed her too and by now were shouting insults, insinuations and anything else their sick and twisted minds could conjure. The cop who’d escorted Caroline into these pen of bastards and reprobates, withdrew his nightstick and rattled the cage with it aggressively. “Keep it down in there!” After a time they did, but McKail had been ignoring them anyhow. “What the hell are you doing here?” He saw the expression etched into her face. It was a soul-destroying combination of pain and a pity; she’d been trying to get him sober for the better part of two years now and almost succeeded. Almost. “Detective Bullock sent for me,” she told him. She was trying to act strong for him, but despite her best efforts was failing. “He said I might be able to help with the investigation. He explained what happened.” “Bastard!” McKail cursed, angry now. This has nothing to do with Caroline, his mind raced, so why the hell has Bullock brought her in here? To get under my skin? Is he trying to make this personal? McKail composed himself. Getting angry would only give the bastard what he wanted and he suspected the only reason Caroline was even allowed to see him was to provoke such a reaction. He’s playing me, McKail decided. But why? He drew a blank, but decided to play along anyhow. It would at least buy him a little time to think this through. “He’s only trying to help, Jacob,” she told him. I doubt that. McKail thought, but nodded anyway. “I know.” “Tell me you didn’t do it, Jacob,” she pleading. She knelt to meet his gaze. “Tell me this is all some kind of mistake.” McKail shook his head. “I can’t.” Tears began to well up in Caroline’s eyes and she quickly wiped them away, hoping he wouldn’t notice. He did and it was making worse, it was clouding his judgement even more and the memories of last retreated further, just out of reach. “Is that because you’re ashamed to tell me,” she began, “or because you don’t know?” “I don’t know what happened last night, Caroline,” he told her. Play along, McKail. “Oh god, what if I killed her” What do I do then?” The officer drew closer and placed a reassuring, but firm, hand upon her shoulder. “It’s time to go, ma’am.” She nodded the best she could and then shot McKail one last lingering look. “It’s going to be okay, Jacob.” She stood and was led away to the exit. “I promise.” IV THE TRUTH McKail had been waiting ten minutes in the interrogation room before Detective Bullock strode in, taking a side from his “world’s best dad” mug and grinning smugly. He sat opposite McKail, rested his mug on the table and leaned closer. “So Jacob,” he began, his smile widening, “Has it all come flooding back yet?” McKail remained silent, but Bullock continued regardless. “You know, getting drunk in Doyle’s and then taking to your car to go search for some action with you buddies. Mowing that poor innocent girl over and leaving her to die right there in the road.” His smile had faded now. “You remember any of that yet?” “No,” McKail admitted, “but then I’m not likely to, am I?” Detective Bullock shook his head, almost bemused. “And why’s that?” “My alcohol induced blackout,” McKail replied. “I’m sure you’re aware of it.” “Of course,” Bullock said, “but that doesn’t mean much of anything does it” According to Caroline, this isn’t the first time you’ve had one of these blackouts.” Bullock paused to study his reaction, unsatisfied he pushed harder. “Caroline said that when the two of you were closer, you had a blackout then too.” McKail remained silent, but maintained a confident grin. It was putting Bullock on edge and he knew it. “Would you like to know what you did?” “I already know,” McKail told him. “Caroline told me.” Bullock’s grin widened to produced a toothy smile. He looked like a predator that’d caught its prey and for all he knew, he had. “She told you most of it, but not all.” He paused, studying McKail’s reaction intently. There wasn’t one. “Have you always been a violent drunk, Jacob?” McKail leaned forward, his face still placid and expressionless; an easy enough task, with enough facial hair. “Why are you doing this, Bullock?” Bullock smiled, weakly this time. “Doing what?” “Trying to pin this on me,” he replied. “I didn’t kill that girl and we both know it.” Bullock shook his head. “Do we?” “Of course,” McKail answered. “If I had done this, then you’d have arrested me already. But you haven’t. You’re dancing around the subject. You’re trying to provoke a reaction, trying to break me and get something you can convict me with.” Bullock shook his head. “No, Jacob I’m really not. Are you forgetting your friend’s confession?” “No,” McKail told him, “but then he isn’t really a friend. None of them are. We just drink in the same bar together. I’d be more surprised if you couldn’t strong arm one or all of them into confession against me.” “We have all their statements, Jacob,” Bullock said. “More than one of them point at you.” “No doubt,” McKail replied. “But you haven’t anything concrete, have you” What’s wrong, Bullock? Were there too many holes in the story? Did they not quite match?” “Come on, Jacob. You’re smart enough to know I can’t got into details,” Bullock shrugged. McKail’s smirk widened, he had Bullock on the ropes and he knew it. “But what makes you so confident, eh” What makes you so sure I can’t touch you?” “Because you have no real witnesses,” McKail told him. “Nobody saw me driving that car. Well, nobody without a vested interest at any rate. Hell, you haven’t even mentioned the car once. Who’s car was it” What make was it? What colour? Can you even prove that we got to South Street by driving?” “If you don’t cooperate with us, Jacob,” Bullock began, sounding less and less confident with each passing word, “the judge won’t be so forgiving. I reckon if you made guilty plea, you’ll only have to serve five years max.” “Yeah, right,” McKail told him. “I won’t be serving a second, dickhead. Get me my lawyer, you’ll be out of a job within the hour.” “Why you--? Bullock lunged across the desk and headed for McKail’s throat, knocking him backwards to the floor. Just then, a number of uniformed officers piled in through the door and a sturdy African-American woman took up the rear. She wore a grey business suit with a white blouse and stood there with arms crossed and shaking her head, disgusted. She oozed with authority, that much was obvious. The uniformed officers restrained Bullock and one unlocked McKail’s cuffs, helping him up to his feet. The woman watched the detective struggle with his own brothers in blue with a disgusted expression plastered across her face. She then turned her attention to McKail. “Mr. McKail, you are of course free to go and on behalf of the precinct I’d like to offer you our most sinc--? “Don’t bother,” McKail interrupted her, rubbing the red-raw sores on his wrist left by the handcuffs. The woman chuckled uncomfortably. “But you must understand, bad press such as this could shut this precinct down. The area just can’t take--? “I don’t care,” McKail muttered. The woman shook her head. “But I’m sure there’s--? “I ain’t gonna report this,” McKail put her out of her misery. I probably deserved this, he silently reasoned. This is what I get for being a drunk. This is what I get for hurting anyone and everyone I encounter. This is what I get for living the kind of life I lead. “But I have to know why.” The woman developed a confused expression. “Why?” “Yeah,” McKail muttered. “Why did he do this” Why me?” The woman nodded. “One of our informers told us that someone in the department had gotten in heavy with one of the local black market gambling rings.” She glared at Bullock, who was now pinned against the cell wall. Crying. “Wasn’t too difficult to figure out who.” “They were going to kill me,” Bullock whimpered. “I had to do something.? “Anyway,” the woman began, turning her attention back to McKail, “seems he bet against you in you “American Dream” or whatever the hell it is and couldn’t afford to lose. So he decided to shift the odds in his favour a little.” “Bet against me?” McKail questioned. “They do that?” “If you know the right people,” the woman told him, turning to exit, “you can bet on just about anything.” END
