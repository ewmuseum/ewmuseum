Only one way to be cleansed

25 June 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06/25/10 In a dank, desolate room, with all windows blacked out, Shaman stares at a wall. The wall, rough with torn wallpaper on it, stands within arms-reach of him. He leans forward and extends his arm to touch the broken wall, feeling trapped by the things he wants to say, but remain unsaid. This day, though, he wishes to bury demons. Demons from his past. He clears his throat and pulls back, a piece of the wallpaper tearing off in his grip. “Chris Storm," he cracks into the night air. "Once again, we meet in the ring. Your immature rants and quips against me are abundant enough to last three of my lifetimes.” Clenching his fists, he looks upwards, as he closes his eyes, focusing slightly. “Do you remember our first meeting, Storm? For the title?" Shaman laughs. "You had to cheat to gain your victory.” He lays the stripped wallpaper piece along the floor. On the back of the wallpaper reveals posters of days past. One shows Chris Storm, standing next to his partner in Banned & Exiled, the partner's head scratched away and distorted beyond viewing. Shaman grin. Yet, as he looks at the poster, he suddenly notices his own former partner, blotched out to oblivion. “There's one demon left, Storm," Shaman calls out. "Did you really think I'd forget?” He brings his palms to his temple and massages them. Shaman's mind grasps at the thought of cleansing the demons of the past. “Perhaps a reminder of the beginning would serve better for your feeble mind.” Shaman raises a remote control as a flat screen TV comes to life behind himself. --- World Wide 70. White staggers backwards, only to bump right into Shaman. Shaman immediately hooks White and hoists him up, promptly dropping him on the head with a violent Desert Spike! Bryan: Desert Spike on Jordan White! Good God, Shaman just annihilated the number one contender! Yale: Not sure that was in Caldera’s plan, JB! Shaman stands and considers a cover, but instead turns to see Ambrosi charging in. Ambrosi leaps through the air, but Shaman catches her and slings her over his shoulders! Shaman immediately drives her down with a massive death valley driver, planting her hard. Shaman sits up with a broad smile on his face, but behind him Markus Stone crawls over top of White! Briggs drops for the count, and a moment later Shaman leans back for a casual cover on Ambrosi! …1! …2! …3!!! The crowd roars, but the roar quickly subsides as confusion takes over. Storm pulls Stone out of the ring, as Shaman raises his arms in victory. --- Shaman pauses on his victory. “Kind of ironic, isn't it? That I handled my other two problems first, only to leave you to be the last. You need to understand that this must be buried, Chris. Once...and for all.” Shaman paces around the room, and opens a door. The bright sunlight illuminates the otherwise pitch-black room. He reaches down, where a can of gasoline sits. He pours the contents onto the floor, and exits through the door. The rays of the sun blast on the wooden frame of the shed that, at first glance, looks rather decrepit. From his pocket, he pulls out a pack of matches and sets it ablaze, tossing it into the shed behind him. “You need to save the pretense and all the rest, and realize that this comes down to you and I." The blast of heat hits his back, but nary a flinch flows from Shaman. "I am not going to lie to your face, claim how hard I have been training for this moment. All I can tell you is what we both already know; I have wanted this for a long, long time. I have wanted to bash that juvenile skull of yours out through your arrogant fucking ass forever.” The flames finally engulf the frame, as the shed begins to buckle under the fire. "And don't think I won't make the most of my opportunity." His swings around to watch his work. He turns his head towards the right of the structure, spotting a sledgehammer laying about. “I've wanted to duct tape you to a chair. Listen to you breathe through your nose. Watch, helplessly, as I turn bone into dust with swing after swing. Metal to sinew. Marrow to pulp.” He approaches the sledgehammer, placing it onto his shoulder. He spots a coil of barbed wire, and picks it up with his free hand. He grips it tightly, as the razor wire cuts into the palm of his hand, blood dripping to the ground below. “Suffering is in the cards, Chris. I know you have the same planned for me, but I won't give you the chance to strike first. I can't. I know you well enough to know what you're capable of. But this is not days or weeks or months or years ago. This is now, and I tell you this now, Chris. Vengeance is a callous mistress.” Shaman walks up the wooden steps of the deck, and makes his way to the railing. He peers down at his furious masterpiece ablaze below. “You and Stone thought you'd make a mockery of me once more. Not even once pinning either of the true members of Oblivion, rather the guy whom we did not even want there.” Shaman reaches into his pocket, pulling out a wrinkled picture of Kimbusa. He balls it up and throws it into the flaming wreckage. “It was a damned spectacle of a match. We should have won, or at the least retained the title without little argument." Shaman quickly grows even more perturbed. "But because you jackasses were the Establishment favorites...there was no room to argue.” Jacob, Shaman's trainer, walks up behind him and spots Shaman's handiwork. “I'm assuming you've a rational explanation for that?” Shaman looks over his shoulder at him. “A simple cleansing ritual. My ancestors believed you can cleanse bad spirits by burning the object of which they lived within.” Jacob groans. "Naturally." Grinning, Shaman looks at his hands. “Perhaps I could just simply burn Chris Storm like a Roman candle.” Jacob shakes his head furiously. “This ain't exactly what I envisioned when I came back, you know.” Shaman doesn't respond, entranced by the dancing flames. Jacob observes Shaman's obsession, as he raises a can of Pabst Blue Ribbon to his lips. “You've obviously made up your mind about this guy," Jacob says. “I hope you can control yourself a bit, at least. He isn't Bryan, you know.” Shaman immediately shoots Jacob a look, before calming down and returning to his opus. “And what makes you think I'm not in control, old man?” Shaman extends his wingspan. “I've welcomed the beast with open arms." Shaman brings his arms back down to his sides, and turns to Jacob. "You think I've yet to show the world that I am the man to be feared?” Jacob takes a cautious step back. Shaman senses Jacob's fear, and swings around to the shed, which is closer to cinders now. “At least you've noticed my return to form.” Shaman cracks his knuckles and his neck. “I understand my path, now. The pain I intend to inflict will be immeasurable.” At the glass door of the house, Jacob spots Suzy looking on. Jacob motions her to go away, checking over his shoulder to make sure Shaman isn't paying attention. He isn't. Jacob approaches Shaman. “Just don't be forgettin' to save all this for when it matters, and leave it there. You don't wanna be bringin' any of that home with you. Do you?” Jacob awaits Shaman's assuring response. He gets none. “This is a piece of the puzzle, one that fills an important gap. And I don't wanna put it off, only to be left wonderin' why I waited so damn long to fill it.”  Shaman turns to watch the fire once again. "And I will fill it." --- Jacob shuts the door behind him inside Shaman and Suzy's house, where a curious Suzy awaits him. "So," Suzy inquires. "Is everything going to be okay?" Jacob shines a half smile. He searches for the words, but none come. Suzy walks up and hugs her father. “You need to know I am in total support of him this time. He finally regained his passion. The ire I fell in love with the first time I saw it.” She pulls out a crumpled letter and opens it, and places it in her father's hand. “He wrote this, a few months back." Jacob unfurls the letter and reads it. Suzy watches him. "He was doubting himself. Doubting the way he did things. Every time he thought he had a chance, something would cause him to make a mental mistake. Cause him to hold back.” Jacob looks up at his daughter, as Suzy snatches the letter back. “I am not going to worry about him simply because he's found the drive to set things right for himself. If this Chris Storm guy is one of the focuses for this, then I feel sorry for him.” Looking over her father, Suzy stares out the door at Shaman. --- Shaman hops off the deck and slowly walks across the yard once more. His mind races over his past. He struggles to move it. A few weeks ago, he thought his career was falling apart. Suddenly...it happened. He succeeds. He breaks through the ceiling. Content engulfs him, as he talks to himself. In his own eyes, he sees several images of himself. He knows they're only his imagination, his hallucination, but he speaks to them anyways. The first image is of a man in his early 20's, standing there with a wide-eyed hope and unaccomplished dreams dancing inside his head. The image is of himself. The younger Shaman. The one he longed to be once again. Shaman takes a deep breath as he speaks to his twenty-something self. “It's been awhile since I imagined you. You smashed through the wall that stood before me, all those years ago. What a grand day it was, when we raised that title above our heads. You remember. Don't you?” His younger self stands amidst the flames of the shed. But his eyes are different. They are not filled with longing, or desire, or hope, but vitriol, anger and hatred. Shaman finds himself drawn to his younger self. “You brought me many titles and tons of glory." Shaman stops short of his past visage. "But where was the payoff in losing who I was? You may be gone, but I have not forgotten. I have to do what I will need to do against Chris Storm. To finally close that chapter of myself.” The visage finally smiles at Shaman. Shaman becomes infuriated. "Why do you look at me like that?!? You are dead! You are the past!" The visage motions for Shaman to follow him within. Shaman passes through the flames, following his younger self within. --- As the shed burns around him, Shaman walks around. The heat singes his skin slightly, but he pays no attention, wiping the smoke away and moving to the back. At the far end of the shed, a mirror stands, where Shaman spots his younger self. The younger Shaman smiles at his older counterpart. "All this time, you're still the fool. You have all that you need. A beautiful woman that loves you. A daughter that brightens your life. The father figure you've longed for." Shaman screams. "You don't know what I want!" The younger Shaman laughs. "I am you, fool! I know exactly what you want, and you have it all. So why won't you let go?" The flaming wood structure slams mere inches away from Shaman. "I have to finish this, once and for all." "Don't you get it?" Younger Shaman is not pleased. "There's nothing to finish at all! You've done more than you've ever imagined. You've accomplished it all. There's nothing left to prove." Shaman shakes his head furiously. "You don't know anything about me!" "Andrew!" The younger Shaman calls out. He pleads his final case. "You need to let go." "No!!!" Shaman grabs the fire-breathed hunk of wood and throws it through the mirror. It smashes through the wall, leaving a gaping hole, as the mass of the place begins to collapse upon itself. Shaman bolts through the hood, sprinting a few yards, as the shed falls to pieces. Suzy and Jacob rush up to Shaman, who gazes down at the smoldering ruins. He simply utters over again. "I'll never let go. I'll never let go." As Suzy hugs Shaman for dear life, he never turns away from the smoking remains of the shed.
