Recruited to Global Championship Wrestling

24 April 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/24/08 I’ll be the first to say that professional wrestling is a very unique profession, so much so that anyone who develops a taste for it can find it all most impossible to leave. But to have an organization close a matter of weeks after I became one of its champions made me seriously consider a drastic career change. Normally I would have been able to use the momentum I gained as a champion to at least get a try out with another organization. From there it would have been easy to secure a permanent spot on the roster, and begin to work my way up. Unfortunately the federation that I had been in, known as Hostility Wrestling Federation, was not very well known to the rest of the industry and there never seemed to be a federation that had a spot for me. Well that is not precisely true. Almost every indy organization in the country called me, unfortunately what they offered to pay me wouldn’t pay a single month’s rent. Besides after being in a major federation, if one relatively small to the rest of the big names, going back to the independent circuit was too much for me to bear. The independent circuit is for all intents and purposes similar to dating the fat goth girl while dreaming of the head cheerleader. If your lucky and work your ass off, you might upgrade, but it’s more likely that you’ll wake up and realize that you’re dying of AIDS. (To all the stupid people, I know the Indy circuit does not give you AIDS. I was making a metaphor.) Regardless after a month of living off the little that I had managed to save during my time in Hostility, I realized that I running out of money had the potential to be a very bad thing. I was in the midst of considering whether or not I should put my degree in economics to use while watching Days of Our Lives when a commercial for Global Championship Wrestling came on the air. Instantly I flashed back to the first time I heard of that federation, and the first time that I met Chis Bagwell. *Two months ago* “You’d think they would give me a week off after I won the title in an Extreme rules match,” I muttered angrily to myself as I changed in the locker-room. It seemed like every muscle in my body was aching which wasn’t a surprise considering that I had just survived a triple threat match with two men who were hungry for my title. Only the fact that neither would let the other win gave me the opportunity to catch them both by surprise and barely get the pin. I was forced to run after that because the wrestler I hadn’t pinned was coming after me with a steel chair in hand. I don’t mind fighting a guy who has a weapon in his hand, but I prefer to do it without wondering whether the other guy in the ring is going to get up and hit me from behind. With that unpleasant incident finally done with I concentrated on getting dressed so I could go home, have some tea, and watch the Daily Show. “Not a half bad job, right up until the point that you ran away,” a voice sneered at me from behind. I turned to face my accuser, but a hot retort died on my lips. Generally speaking I am bigger than most guys I meet, and that stood true for the man in front of me, at least in terms of muscle. But his aggressive stance, the dangerous glint in his eyes, and the malicious smirk on his face made it clear to me that I did not want to face this guy when I was not 100 percent and he was. “Who the hell are you” I asked gruffly managing to keep what I really wanted to say off my lips. “Chris Bagwell,” he said cockily, “and I’m here to make you the offer of a lifetime. I’m with GWC, a federation a hell of a lot bigger than this shit-hole that you’re working for. I’m prepared to let you leave the minor leagues, and I come join the group I’m forming.” “Let me get this straight,” I scoffed, “you want me to give up my championship here, so I can come be your lackey?” *Present* To make a long story short I turned down his offer. He told me it was my loss, but he left his number with me. His last remark before leaving was, “Trust me, either you’ll come to your senses or this sorry excuse of a federation is going to come crashing down, either way you’ll be calling me soon enough.” Even though every ounce of pride and stubbornness I had fought against me I managed to pick up the phone and dial his number. In the end it was not a very difficult decision. Frankly I could suck it up and get back to wrestling, or I could end up working in a suit and tie. “Chris Bagwell,” I began with a slight tremor in my voice, “It’s Dan Black. I’ll admit, when you’re right, you’re right”.
