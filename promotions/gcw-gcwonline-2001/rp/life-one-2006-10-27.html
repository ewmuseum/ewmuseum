Life: One

27 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 27 Oct 06 To say that life isn’t fair would be one hell of an understatement. I have made it no secret that I find the way that life and death are chosen is, well, bullshit. Some almighty being feels the need to keep people on this earth that can barely even pass as human beings yet take the lovely ones way before their time. Some people might say that this is good because they are going to a better place. A place without war, without drugs, poverty or hardship. I call it selfish. Yes God is a selfish being, I said it. We are suppose to believe he loves us? He leaves his “children” to fend for themselves against evil men and yet takes the ones from us that can really make this world a better place. What kind of being is that to praise? I have always been under the impression that God has had something against me, some grudge that I could never quite comprehend. How else would there be to explain what he has put me through? For awhile I thought that he was punishing me for all of my failures as a person. Karma was a bitch afterall. It wasn’t until recently that I really thought that through. Only conclusion I could come up with? What the hell did I deserve punishment for? At least prior to my wrestling background began. I was innocent in my youth. I got good grades, I opened doors and pulled out chairs for young ladies, I answered everything with sir or ma’am. I volunteered for things, I loved my uncle and aunt ? basically I was the model child. So why punish me? So then I figured, well God is all powerful right? Well maybe he could see what was going to happen in the future? So I’ve thought back on everything that has happened in my career as a wrestler and business man. Yes I have done some questionable things in my wrestling career even I admit that, however none of it was without purpose and none of it was so despicable that I should have a life long sentence. Then there was Victor. ? But fuck that asshole he deserved being burned alive. Not to mention he killed my brother prior to that. I will NEVER regret doing that and I was being punished far before the blood on my hands soaked in. Now I’m sure this the point in my tale where people would start wondering, “where the hell is he going with this?” or maybe “Yeah okay asshole we’ve heard all this before, stop bitching.”. I certainly understand where you all are coming from. BUT at the same time guess what? This is my space so you all are going to have to fucking deal with it. The point is that I have been under the impression that bad things most always happen to good people. There is no balance in this world, no reward for doing the right things, saying the kind things. It’s all sorrow and pain. When I realized this is when I learned to just let go. I figured that if I was going to be punished for every misstep, then why not enjoy life as best I could? And yes now comes the entire point. The day that I finally realized that being a good person does nothing for you. It was early 2002 while in my first stint in GCW. I wasn’t even really a big name at the time, just kind of scratching the surface. So it did certainly amaze me when Steven Caldera called me into his office to tell me that they had gotten a video request in the mail, directed towards me. I sat down and watched this tape. It was a small African-American family, two teenage boys, one young lady that looked to be in her early 20’s and the obvious mother figure. Normally this wouldn’t mean much but it was the setting that really made this tape stand out for me. It wasn’t a nice living room with a fire place or a dining room with an incredible dinner laid out in front. It was a hospital. The mother had been diagnosed with cancer. In the video they all took turns describing their mother, all of 38 years old. She was a single mom, their father had left while she was pregnant with the oldest girl. She worked two, sometimes three jobs to make sure her children had the things that they needed. She always smiled, a bright and beautiful smile and she had this way with people. Always capable of making them smile. And of course she was a wrestling fan. A fan of LeStatt Knight to be exact. She wanted to meet me. When the video ended I was conflicted. On one hand I felt for this family and especially this mother who fought so hard and was so much to so many people. Yet it seemed like another sterling example of what I hated about this life. That wasn’t her fault though. With that I knew what my decision would be. “When does the plane leave jefe?”” _______________________________________________________________ I remember walking up to the hospital feeling nervous. I wanted to be everything this woman dreamed I could be and more. She deserved to get what she wanted even thought I couldn’t help but think that even more so that she deserved to live. What she didn’t deserve is to be disappointed. I don’t know how many strange looks I got as I walked into the hospital. Being 6’6? and 270lbs. Will get that sometimes however. Although I also should have had one of those clickers from the “Axe” commercials because of all the female nurses that felt the need to undress me with their eyes. Of course it was a few years ago, so maybe the details of this part of the story are a bit sketchy. I wasn’t given a room number or even a floor that she’d be on, so to the front desk I went. “Hello nurse?” She turned around ? froze a moment and smiles awkwardly. Another click to my resume. “Uh ? Hello sir, something I can help you with?” “Yes I’m here for?” “Carol Hardison.” The extra voice made me turn my head. It was an older female nurse, one that didn’t drool over me. A welcomed surprise. “Yes that’s it.” I said with a smile. The older nurse looked over at her much younger counter part and rolled her eyes. “Nurse Abby I’ll take care of this young man can you go and check on Mr. Gregory?” She turned to me and gave me a knowing wink. “My name is Tammy.” “Roberto,” I reached out and shook her hand. “Pleasure.” “Likewise.” She began to turn. “Follow me.” So we began to walk through the maze that they called a hospital. Tammy kept me talking the entire time which actually helped since I was getting more and more nervous about meeting this woman. She told me more about Carol Hardison and her three children, Iesha, Paul, and PJ. She told me that Carol loved her children, that she’d do anything for them. She told me a tale about Carol kicking nurses out of her room because they were interrupting her “future husband” on TV ? that of course being me. “I guess I can see why she loves you so much.” Tammy said with a chuckle. “Well thank you ma’am.” We reached the third floor. Now my stomach was in a knot. I didn’t know why I was so nervous exactly. I mean I was the so-called celebrity here. Maybe it was anxiousness? Maybe it was just the fact that I couldn’t believe that someone actually made it their dying wish to meet me? “She is just up?” A high pitched scream rang through the air, echoing through the hospital hallway. Tammy and myself immediately looked up. “Excuse me for a moment.” “Of course.” I stood in place. I had no idea where Carol’s room was so I had no other choice. Tammy ran to the desk and began talking to the nurse that was behind the desk. It was obvious something bad had happened. Not a surprise considering that I had been in a hospital after all. That’s when it happened. Tammy glanced over to me for just a brief moment. It couldn’t have been any longer than half a second but something about that glance sent a shiver up and down my body. I can imagine that at that point all emotion and color left my face. I felt as if my body was slowly leaking all feeling. Then she did it again. This time I had to act. I walked up to the desk and faced the two nurses. “What happened?” Her eyes gave away the story. Perhaps I didn’t want to hear the words. I already could hear the crying. So I followed the sobs until I found the room I was looking for. There in that room was that family from the video. Young PJ with his head on his older brother Paul’s shoulder, tears falling down both of their cheeks. Beautiful Iesha bed side ? her head resting on her mother’s stomach. And Carol. My number one fan. A hero to so many people. An Angel. And now in every sense of the word. I looked around that dim room and saw the pain in their eyes. In that moment I felt what they felt. I had never even met these people yet I felt like they were with me every step. And a tear fell down my cheek. I watched as it splattered on the floor beneath me. Then I knew. It wasn’t worth being good.
