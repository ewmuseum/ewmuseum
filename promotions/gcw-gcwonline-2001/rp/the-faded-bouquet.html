Date: varies .:: ::: ::. Las Vegas, Nevada 6 October 2006 4:07 pm A clammy wetness rolled over the Cupid's bow and onto the rose-tinted fabric of her upper lip. It squeezed in between her upper and lower lip and squirmed onto her tongue, flooding her mouth with an acrid tang. Her eyes were squinted shut in pain, but along with it, she could feel the next wave of sweat rolling down the crest of her forehead. 'AHHHH!' The bestial growl of pain broke the seal of her lips as she twisted and writhed in his grip. Her fingers clawed at the mat; it was sickeningly soft in her grasp, and it withered when she tried to use it for leverage. 'Give?' The question was guttural, a barely snapped out syllable. It crawled inside her and twisted her guts. She shook her head in a sharp, but miniscule gesture. 'No!' She knew what came next- and it wasn't long in coming either. Ian Childress torqued her shin a little harder over her ankle, contorting her limbs in a way that she didn't think possible. Agony followed in short stead, and a fresh wave of heat coursed through her. The sweat beaded to clam up on her skin in preparation for a second-wave assault. 'Give?' This time, there was real aggression behind the question. He was pushing it. He knew it. Maybe he thought desperation and pain combined to create miracles, but there was no will left. Her head thudded to the mat, and she spoke as a vanquished foe. 'Give!' Ian seemed surprised, so surprised that for a moment, he continued to hold his patented Fool's Gallow. As the word sank in, he grunted his displeasure, and unceremoniously dropped her to the mat. She lay there for a moment, smelling the vinyl and foam of the mat, smelling her own sweat, smelling the all-too-nearness of relinquished hope. 'Get up.' His voice was cold, and it caused an immediate reaction. Her struggling limbs suddenly found their strength, and she pushed up with her arms. It was much easier to stand on her left leg, the one that hadn't been torqued in the hold. Her right ankle stung and ached as the blood coursed downward into it and she winced as she turned around to face her teacher. He was unforgiving, as she had expected he would be. His polar blue eyes narrowed, and zeroed in on her, and she noted for the first time how insignificant a glance like that from Ian Childress could make you feel. Or maybe it was just her internal landscape--she was already feeling insignificant enough, without his help. 'Do you want to tell me what just happened?' It was possible that he'd noticed the crestfallen look on her face. Or perhaps he just knew when he had pushed a student farther than she was ready. He stood with his hands on his hips, looking away from her with an air of great disappointment. His tone had changed nearly imperceptibly; it was still brusque and businesslike, but with the hint of a father's tone before he's gotten quite used to calling himself a father. She shook her head vehemently, not in answer to his question but in deep, boiling frustration. 'I don't know. I was just trapped, I felt trapped, and so I gave, because I couldn't figure out what to do other than sit there and have my ankle torn off.' Ian smiled with a grim gesture that really seemed more a baring of teeth. 'It's designed for that, of course, but you already knew its effectiveness. You do remember what you were supposed to do, don't you?' She nodded, but Ian continued regardless, unnecessarily driving the failure back into her skull with the railroad spike of disdain contained in his voice. 'Figure out how to use the hold against me. Figure out how to get out. You'll never be able to use it effectively if you don't understand the possible counters, and the best way to do that is to be put in the situation yourself, with only your own resources to get you out of it.' 'I know, but-' 'And you tapped,' he continued, speaking a little louder to emphasize that he wasn't finished, 'You gave up. Do you not remember what happened with Almasy? He figured out how to get out of the hold.' It took all she had to not double-over with the force of that blow. It was like a scythe slash right to the stomach, right to the pit of her insides. 'I remember,' she returned. No more words would come out, so neatly had that one word scalded and cauterized anything productive that she could have said. She turned away from him and limped across the gulf of mats to the chair that held her bag and towel. The towel felt coarse under her fingers, and like sandpaper when she pressed it to her face to mop off the sweat that had collected. Its light blue surface darkened with the moisture, and her eyes probed the fibers, searching for the answers to her malaise inside a book that could not possibly provide any. 'You haven't had your heart in it lately,' he said, and he was standing close behind her right now, and she couldn't believe that her attention had been so unfocused that she hadn't heard him moving at all until he'd been standing there. She turned around to look him in the eye, prepared to offer an excuse of exhaustion, but it too refused to come. Her chin jerked up defiantly. 'No,' she said plainly, 'I haven't.' 'Why?' he returned, breaking his stoic stillness to fling his arms wide in a gesture of utter lack of understanding, 'Why on earth would you be giving up now? You've just walked away with the biggest win of your GCW career, and you mean to tell me that it means nothing to you? Maybe you should have just given it to Brandon Youngblood if that's the case!' She winced at that jibe, too, only now, she could feel the ire rising in a red tide behind her eyes. Her hands suddenly clenched around the towel. 'It's NOT that, and you should know that. I'm happier than I could possibly describe that I finally get another chance at my goal, but...' She closed her eyes, took a deep breath, and reined herself in. There was no need to start this argument, not here, and not with him, who couldn't possibly understand where she was coming from right now. But Ian pressed on, full bore. 'But what?! There IS no but, Lia, you're right where you need to be right now.' 'We'll have to agree to disagree,' she said, politely calm even though his words were eating straight through her with every second that they hung in the air. He growled in frustration as she turned her back on him, her fingers meaninglessly arranging the objects in her bag as a way to defer the tension. 'Listen to me, dammit!' he snapped. It dimly registered in her consciousness that Ian Childress rarely swore. She turned as if compelled to look at him over her shoulder, and her body slowly followed. 'How long are you going to dwell on Almasy?' he said, ruthlessly pursuing the question in spite of her last reaction to it, 'Huh? How long is it going to take before you realize that he's small change as far as you're concerned.' 'He's not small change,' she said, her voice ragged and quiet, 'He's the PTC Global Champion.' 'AGH!' Childress growled, throwing his hands up. He composed himself, and continued, 'Pay attention to what I'm saying, Lia! Whatever he is, he's GONE now. Gone. You're training yourself for something new. You went out there and you made a mistake. It happens. But if you dwell on that one mistake, if you forget how much and why you want this, all of this work is going to come to nothing.' It sank in quickly, like a tank dropped from a plane into the ocean. She knew that, despite his words, it would be a long time before she could shake the memory- no matter how hard she had to try to mask it in front of him, and in front of everyone else. She nodded quietly. 'Understood.' He turned away from her, as though looking at her might bring him dangerously close to the raw bitterness that leaked from inside her, into the open air like a toxin. 'You know my policy,' he said gruffly, 'If you're not trying, you're not training. We're done for today, so I'll see you on Monday.' Her fingers fumbled to lay the towel inside her canvas bag and zip it up. The GCW logo swirled in brilliant gray and teal on the side of it, but it brought no pleasant reminders, no relief. Her spirit within rebelled at his chastising tone, and something, perhaps only her own pride, prevented her from answering him like a deferent young student. She shouldered her bag and exited the training room without so much as a sound. .:: ::: ::. Moapa-Paiute Reservation Valley of Fire, Nevada 30 September 2006 The dimmed light of dusk made Laszlo's dark hair shimmer like raven wings. It was yet another detail of her brother that caught her off-guard. So long without him had made aspects that had been familiar to her in their childhood seem completely foreign and fantastic in this setting. He glanced at her with green eyes that were the mirror of her own- looking at him produced a strange effect, almost as though she was looking into a mirror, save the fact that the lines of his face were carried into full masculine sharpness which contrasted with the delicate femininity of her own. She smiled. 'I'm glad you've come,' she said, for what must have been the umpteenth time. Below them sprawled the Valley of Fire, its dramatic rainbow-painted cliffs muted by the silence of the evening. They paled in comparison to the brilliance her twin brother's presence cast- after all this time searching, he had been within her grasp, and only playfully eluding her. A slight smile changed the slant of his lips. 'It was inevitable, at some point,' he said, and it was like being home as he spoke, the familiar Czech lilt matching her own, 'It was only choosing the right time.' She started to nod as a gust of wind whipped through the canyon once more, pushing her dark tresses into her face. Adept fingers caught them in their wild celebration, and tucked them back into place behind her ear again. After all this time waiting to find Laszlo, she was finding now that perhaps it was only his presence she was seeking, and not his answers. Nothing immediately came to mind to ask him, though she knew there was much he could probably say to any question she fielded, and a thousand questions that she had scripted for this very moment over the long months that she had searched. Finally, she turned a slow, bemused glance on him, looking up into a focused gaze that had been monitoring her the way a predator is wont to monitor every move of its prey. 'Why did you wait so long, if you knew I was looking for you?' she asked, tilting her head slightly to the side, 'You couldn't possibly have been unaware that I was expending all my energy trying to find out where you were, if you were still alive...' It seemed to her unnecessarily cruel for him to have known all these things, and still only watched her search for him. Almost uncomfortably, he turned his gaze away from her, sliding his hands into his coat pocket. 'There was a right time for it, as I said. I had other concerns to take into account.' She knew well what he meant by that, and it sent a chill sweeping up the back of her neck. Laszlo being thick into business meant that Caine, the reputed father of her entire race, was actively pursuing his ends. It was something that she had been taught to fear for her entire life, something that, if the old books were right, meant that the protracted bloody battle of Gehenna was coming soon. She could feel the tiny velvety hairs on the back of her neck standing. 'Caine is moving?' The suspicion in her voice betrayed to him far more of her fear than she would have wanted him to see. His face faded into serious lines, as it often did when he considered the work of his master. 'Caine is always moving,' he said, in a tone as nonchalant as though he had just spoken of the world being round, or of the sun rising in the morning. The thought bit into her hatefully. If Laszlo had been allowed to come now, then it meant that Caine had a reason to focus on what was going on with her, or the little group that surrounded her. It meant that, once again, the balance of her life was threatened. She turned her eyes to him resentfully. 'What does he want with us?' She found it impossible to believe that Laszlo would be allowed to take care of personal issues when he had a job that was so vital- surely Caine would not allow his right hand to be caught up in a family celebration at a time when strings were tightening behind the scenes, vague, but threatening strings that Lia herself knew nothing about, save the rumors she heard from Ian or Blaise. Laszlo scoffed, though whether at her or his master was impossible to tell. 'He wants nothing with you in particular, nor the assortment of half-breeds and mortals you keep company with. But... he does want a word with the man you've agreed is to be your teacher.' 'Mr. Childress?' 'The same,' said Laszlo, nodding, looking out over the bluffs with the far-off blankness of someone who was absorbed in their own thoughts, 'It seems that he feels the game is-' He choked on the words, and shook his head violently. 'Damn the work! I didn't come here to talk to you about work, I came here...' 'Why?' she pressed, perplexed at what seemed to be a genuine hatred for his current position. 'Because it's important that we don't lose track of each other again,' he said, turning serious face to her once again, 'Not now that we're the only two left.' Her heart dropped into her stomach like a stone. 'But what about Anezka...? They told me she was still...' He shook his head. Her throat went dry. 'She was... at one point,' Laszlo said soberly, 'I don't know the details, just that she died in Palermo, Italy. Not long ago. She... well, so the information I have says, she was embraced into the Cappadocian clan, and may very well have been one of the last of them living.' 'They knew the family?' Laszlo nodded briskly. 'My best guess is that someone found out that she happened to have the name Vlaszy, and found that a very convenient excuse to get rid of her... so it's just the two of us now.' Her own guilt insisted that she should have cried. But she hadn't seen Anezka for a very, very long time now, hadn't even known that she had made it out of the wholesale slaughter of the rest of the Vlaszy line. Whatever Anezka had been doing in times past, she hadn't worried much about finding the rest of the family. It was more Vlaszy blood needlessly spilled for only the crime of bearing the name, but in a way, perhaps Anezka was better off dead. 'Old names are a rarity now,' she said quietly, abruptly dragging him out of his thoughts, 'Wherever they are found, they're quickly scratched out. If it need be such a secret, maybe it's safer for Anezka that she's gone, that she doesn't have to endure any more of our bad luck.' Laszlo nodded without saying anything. Whatever Lia's attitude, it was still sobering to realize how the blood had dwindled now- how much more precious it was now that the two of them were the only ones to bear it. She realized her own mistake soon after she spoke. Deep within her, something distilled, and spread a cool, somber eulogy through her limbs. It was too much to bear. She cut the silence with a meaningless question. 'How have you been?' He shrugged the question off. 'As well as I can be, I suppose. I don't have much time to consider myself. Being on call to do the dirty work of someone else is a full time job. My existence isn't really all that interesting. I want to hear of yours... it's a consequence of the job, I suppose, but what you might call a mundane life fascinates me utterly.' The sudden sadness in his voice was striking, but she knew he wouldn't want her to pick up on it. Laszlo wasn't the type to wear his emotions on his sleeve- or talk about them freely, even with her. 'I... well, things have settled down lately. Everyone is beginning to get along, that is between Dawn, Vivica, Virgil and I, and the KB... the Order, as you would know them. It isn't something I ever would have expected, but I'm not going to take it for granted.' She paused for a moment to gather her thoughts, wondering how to delicately hedge around the bad parts, all in the name of a good story. 'Everyone is situated in the Estate now... getting on pretty well with each other, for once. And as far as my personal success...' She forced a smile and could do nothing to make it look real, other than will it to be convincing. 'Well, I've had some setbacks lately, but I've got a chance to make that all up coming up this week. I've done all I can to be ready for it, now it's just a matter of going out and making it reality.' She trailed off, feeling like she'd just read him the text of an interview, and expected him to believe it. He smiled and shook his head. 'And personally?' Caught in her own trap. She sighed, and looked down at her hands. 'Personally, not so great. There are enough people behind me right now, encouraging me, that I know I'm supposed to be fine... but I'm having a harder time making myself believe.' He raised his eyebrows, a mirror-image mimicry of his sister's own facial expression for disbelief. 'What little I've been able to keep up with, you don't appear to be having any problems.' 'Easy for you to say,' she returned, as devoid of emotion as possible, because this was a path she really didn't want to hike right now. Laszlo chuckled quietly. 'Fair enough,' he said, and let it rest there. Silence came between them for a few long moments, an impenetrable rough brick wall that again reminded her of their separation. He just didn't know what it was like to be in her position, nor could he, not from where he was sitting right now. A wry smile twisted at his lips as he gave her a sidelong glance, and caught her still brooding. 'I suppose what they say about us is true, then...' He trailed off. It was a hook, she knew, and she wasn't sure she wanted to hear the answer that she knew was coming. 'What do you mean?' 'That old gem- the Vlaszy's are Kingmakers... but that doesn't make them Kings.' A wave of nausea rolled through her with the force of an unleashed gale. It might have been a family joke at one point, but now it was a long-unspoken dread. It was the last bullet to the head of a wounded soldier who, until this point, had somehow kept the will to keep crawling away from his enemy. .:: ::: ::. McFarlane Estate 19 October 2006 7:38 pm The sun was dying over the horizon in an ignominious bloody streak. The colors chasing it out of the sky were ugly- dim mustard yellow, a dirty olive, and a cold, unfeeling navy. None of them spoke to her, none of them culled the beast that was raging within her as she stared off into the distance. She stood at this window, a window that had come to be the bane of her existence over the past three weeks, four weeks. She'd lost count. It was from this window that she had watched Seymour Almasy walk away from the McFarlane Estate without so much as a word to the people who had taken him in. It was to this window that she had returned, barely speaking to anyone in the house after Almasy had taken everything from her. Plenty of people might have thought that was an over-dramatic proclamation, but because of him... she'd lost the one thing she had been striving to achieve. And she lost it as a joke, a two-week note in PTC history who would retain the shame of being the shortest reigning Global Champion ever. That was a sting that no balm could remedy. Lightning flashed sharp on the horizon. It stabbed like a flamberge into the woods in the distance, and she saw a tiny plume of smoke follow the strike. Thunder growled behind it. Her eyes combed through the strands of the clouds- it looked like a long storm, heralded by ominous, thick clouds that hung threateningly low. It was coming on fast, and she viewed it with dread, because it meant that everyone would be shut into the house for the night, and she would once again feel like a moth under a microscope, pinned in between the unforgiving glass of the specimen slide. At the end of November, they were telling her, she'd face Dawn McFarlane and Lestatt Knight for the GCW World Title. It was a hollow honor, because it brought with it curling fingers of dread and doubt. She and Dawn hadn't had any words on the subject yet, but she knew they would. There was the usual Sword of Damocles too, the question that everybody was asking in their head- would this finally be the match that split Dawn and Shadow up for good? Her fingernails scratched along the wooden window frame as though she was trying to grasp at an answer where there was none. None, save expectation- and she knew plenty of people were watching and waiting for her relationship with Dawn to implode. She had questions of her own. Dawn had been furious after that episode of KillZone. She had pledged, as always, to take up her arms and avenge the woman she loved. It was at that point that the application to GTT6, which had lain unopened on the kitchen counter, had suddenly disappeared- and not into the trash. But mysteriously, not long after the drafting of brackets, after the welcome packet... she'd withdrawn her entrance. No explanation. Whatsoever. Of course, Lia wondered what had made Dawn change her mind, but she didn't have to look very far to figure it out. Because it wasn't long after Dangerous Games had gone down that Dawn had pulled her application. Lia didn't have the strength to ask the questions. As though a release of something that she herself could not express, the sky released the first drops of rain. They spattered big and fat upon the winding walk, over-dramatic crocodile tears that mocked her intensely. Moments later, the bottom fell out, thousands of liquid soldiers kamikaze-diving the dirty concrete. Another bright spark of lightning lit the landscape. The following thunderclap shook the house from the foundation upwards, and a tiny part of her thrilled in the idea of its walls falling in, and being trapped Samson-esque inside its sprawling columns. 'Don't be ridiulous,' she snapped at herself. She turned away from the inside of the sitting room, tired, sick to death of looking at the opulent furnishings. There were days when every moment in this house was an agony, a reminder of the announcements of other people's grandeur that did not come when you said the name Shadow, much less Lia Vlaszy. Every moment, there was something else. She walked out of Dangerous Games the victor... and they hated her for it. They'd raised her hand, and not Brandon Youngblood's, or Tessa Windsor's. The win came with a grudging acknowledgment of the skill it must have taken to get it, and then an eager introduction of another topic, like Eva VanLorne's return. She was alone again. It didn't taste any less bitter to her now than it had when she was in WMW, being ridiculed because she had the wherewithal to call bullshit when she saw it. It was no different than the thousands of reminders that lined up in her mind of all the times when she'd been told 'you're good... but not quite what we're looking for.' She could have walked away. But if she'd walked away, they would have won. Any naysayer would have said 'I told you so,' and immediately forgotten about her existence in order to gossip about the latest return to PRIME. For Lia Vlaszy, resilience was not just a character trait, it was a necessity. But there came a point where even she had to ask herself how much was resilience, and how much was allowing oneself to be kicked in the face repeatedly and coming back eager for another boot tread to the bloodied nose. It had all come to a head with Seymour, and that was still the blow that stung the most. It wasn't even so much the title that stung. She'd genuinely liked Seymour when she met him as Emoticon, but it had never been about anything but him in that context- he had outright refused to understand her position on anything, so long as his own needs were satisfied. In any case, she had always been secondary to him- she knew instinctively that if Dawn McFarlane had ever given him the time of day, he would have ditched her as quick as he possibly could, and gone to lick the boots of his preferred goddess, rather than continue on in the company of the devil who made him toss his inhibitions to the wind. They'd had their disagreements, but in the end, it had all come to dust. She'd extended a hand to him when nobody else would. She had promised him that if he'd join her in Dies Irae, he would have what he wanted- the ironic part about it was that he did. Seymour Almasy had exactly what he wanted right now- it had just been Shadow's misfortune to underestimate how far he'd go to get it, and who exactly he would put in the line of fire. She owed it to her own naive stupidity. It had been stupid to trust him, stupid to give him another chance after she'd seen just what kind of viciousness he was capable of perpetrating in order to come out smelling like roses. Everybody's hero, Seymour Almasy, was a ruthless, calculating little son of a bitch... but only when no one was looking. Still and all, Lia had considered him a friend. She'd asked him to join Dies Irae not because of a strategic advantage, but because she had genuinely wanted him there. She had wanted his company, wanted his thoughts... wanted his friendship. That was all over now. Water under the bridge. Stupid to dwell on something we all knew was coming. That was what everyone around her was saying, at least, but they had no clue to what depth remarks of that sort cut their leader. Every time she heard Vivica or Dawn or Virgil sneer about how they knew he'd come to no good, it was another charred score-mark on the prison-door that kept her walled into the position she currently occupied. They wanted her to forget about it. Ian especially had pressed her to move on with her life, nevermind what had happened, nevermind how much it had hurt, nevermind all the people saying I told you she was overrated, nevermind all the people saying I told you he was a snake. It wasn't that easy. Had she been so shallow as to just see him as another shock troop, she knew it would have been easier for her. But there was no closure here- and it wasn't Dies Irae, but just Lia that was left with the wounds from the encounter. Wounds that nobody had bothered to help her heal, because nobody but her sincerely believed they were there. But perhaps there were other reasons for such a cavalier attitude towards her distress. Lurking deep in the back of her mind was the unpleasant thought that maybe they didn't care because they, too, were Seymour Almasys. They might not have been brave enough to hold up daggers behind her back, but maybe they were brave enough to drop some arsenic into the wine they offered her. Maybe loyalties were still bought and sold with so many pieces of silver. BOOM! The loudest clap of thunder yet snapped her line of thought. Her fingers clung to the window-frame, betraying her surprise- or perhaps her desperation to cling to something that made sense. She started again as she heard a tiny mechanical noise behind her- the tinkling of a music box playing the familiar tones of 'Svat? V?clav,' a song she had known since she was little. Two steps took her to the table full of knicknacks, and one more sharp motion clapped her hand down on top of the offending ceramic box, only jump-started to life because of the violence of the storm. Her fingers clenched around its painted orchids, delicate purple blossoms that stood out around the black-painted ceramic. The unvoiced wrathful scream of the past few weeks raged through her veins, but refused to break free. The impulse of her hand tightened her fingers around the box, and her forearm threatened to hurl it to the floor and shatter it into a thousand pieces. But suddenly, as the rain poured on mercilessly outside, it was as though all will to do so was washed away, her soul returned to a pale listless gray. It wouldn't have done any good to be angry, nor would it have done any good to bury it all underneath layers and layers of lies and pretend to be happy. Reaction was out of the question. It was only then that she finally began to understand the sense of second-best that hovered around her like a guardian angel with a razor-sharp halo dangled precariously closely to her neck any time she got delusions of grandeur. The Vlaszy's were kingmakers, cavaliers, those who hovered perilously close to the top, but were never afforded any time in the spotlight. It was a mantle she had to bear. A scarlet placard that read 'not good enough.' But it would have been no use to shed the garments that bore the hateful missive, because underneath all that, her own skin bore the same curse. .::: ::: ::.
