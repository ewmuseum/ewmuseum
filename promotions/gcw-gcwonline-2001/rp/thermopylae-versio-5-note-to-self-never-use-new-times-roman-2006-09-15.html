Thermopylae (versio 5- note to self: never use new times roman when rping)

15 September 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 15/09/2006 Now. A Bar. Portland, Oregon. The bar is a dive. I can tell by it's run-down exterior, but I don't really give a fuck. It's the closest one to the arena and I'm just lucky I made it this far under my own volition - it was touch and go there for a while. As soon as I enter the establishment, I'm first struck by the rather potent smell of stagnant ale and sweat that almost makes me want to chance it further a long the street. Almost. The place is filled with an eclectic cluster of people, ranging from leather-clad bikers, to old, yet respectable-looking, drunkards to the college frat boys who probably only came in here because was the only place who'd consent to serve them without asking for identification. Hell, the woman behind the bar was too busy finding a daddy for her kids, to be discriminate about age. In short, it has all the signs of a post World Wide blow-out, where members of the audience came after the show had finished - the wrestling industry always did seem to bring the strangest people together. I head for a stool at the other side of the bar, right by the jukebox, becoming increasingly aware the floor is sticky and it made increasingly difficult to walk. I develop a confused expression and glance around, but nobody else seems the least bit phased by it and so I simply shrug and continue. I make it to the barstool and then slump down onto it, quite surprised by the volume my contented sigh as I do so. A few look around, appearing mildly put out by the noise, and it seems to grab the attention of the female bartender. She approaches my side of the bar and leans over the counter, her hand propping her chin up and glares directly into my eyes. She develops a playful smirk at top corner of her mouth, or what passes for one whilst chewing gum aggressively, and the she sounds very much like a cow grazing in a field. 'Whatcha havin'?' She asks. 'Gimme a beer, any beer.' She stands and extracts a Bud from the cooler behind her and flips the lid off with the bottle opener attached to the underbelly of bar. She places it down in front of me and retakes her position, leaning closer toward me and resting her chin on her hand. Seems she's turned her attention to me from the other toothless yokels vying for her attention. I think about telling her that I ain't gonna be nobody's daddy, but I figure it'll hinder the service of alcohol some and so I keep my mouth shut. For now. 'Don't I recognise you?' She grins and worn lines develop, the kind that can only come from having little sleep and looking after numerous children by yourself. I grunt something under my breath and take a long, completely satisfying gulp of beer. It's only a matter of time before the alcohol gets into my system and the pain is somewhat dimmed. Hell, after the match I had with Tessa Windsor earlier in the night, I damn well need it. Two Hours Ago. Rose Garden. Portland, Oregon. At first, I can't believe what in the hell is happening. Windsor makes the cover and the ref makes the count. His hand slams against the mat two times and is damned close to hitting it a third and final time too, but I manage to get it together quickly enough to raise my left shoulder just moments before impact. But it doesn't matter, the bell sounds regardless and the match is over. I develop a confused expression, or what passes for one in my current state of exhaustion, which is somewhat heightened when 'Defy You' by Offspring hits on the PA system. Upon hearing it, the fans go wild, chanting Windsor's name repeatedly, completely enamoured with the woman. What the hell is going on? She won? Bullshit! I got my damned shoulder up! I should be angry, I should just get to my feet, grab the ref by the scruff of the neck and shake him until I get some answers, but that's not going to happen anytime soon. Hell, I'm having trouble just breathing, never mind actually moving. Windsor rolls off my chest and onto her back, but that's about all she can manage. Seems she's just as spent as I am and it doesn't exactly come as much of a surprise, we have just been in one helluva war after all. I wait for the ref to confirm what the fans in the arena and all around the world have already come to believe, by raising Windsor's hand into the air valiantly, despite her beaten and bloodied condition. But it doesn't happen and I‘m not surprised. For the longest time we're left to wonder why, but then out of the corner of my eye, I see the ref, head perched through the expanse between the middle and top rope, talking to the timekeeper. Something's going on here, I can‘t tell what, but I smile regardless. I fucking knew I got my damned shoulder up! Andrews takes a live mic off one of the production staff. 'The referee informs me that Tessa Windsor did not pin McKail's shoulders to the mat!' No shit, Sherlock. 'However, the bell was not premature.' Then what the fuck is going on? 'Due to television regulations, this match has reached it's time limit!' The chants of bullshit from the crowd immediately sound and if I had the energy, I'd probably join them. 'Therefore, this match is being declared a time-limit draw!' Fucking bastards. The fan's chanting becomes almost deafening and they most certainly want blood. For fear of his life, Andrews passes the mic back to the production guys and quickly retakes his seat with the timekeeper, in the hopes of blending right back into obscurity. My panting begins to regulate back to normalcy and I manage to muster up enough energy to roll onto my side. Every muscle in my body burns and I have to question whether it was worth going through all of the pain and anguish for a damned time limit draw. I look upon the face of Tessa Windsor, who's glaring at me, undoubtedly the very same thoughts rummaging around in her head and see the respect for me in her eyes. I look upon the crowd, who have now begun to applaud our efforts, now finally coming to accept that no matter how much chanting they get through, this match will not continue. I see the respect they show both of his for what we've accomplished here today. Neither of us have gained a shot at the United States title or elevation up the card, we've accomplished much more. We've put on a damned fine match for the paying audience and sometimes that is enough. Sometimes. I roll out, under the bottom ropes and, with the aid of a few guys from backstage, I make my way to the backstage area. Along the way, I even hear some chanting my name and I can't help, but smile. At any other time, I would've ignored the praise, feeling it was irrelevant, but tonight, and only tonight, I don't think it is. Now. A Bar. Portland, Oregon. 'He's Jacob McKail!' A plump guy declares, sat on the bar stool just over from me. He's wearing a shirt and tie, under his brightly coloured V neck sweater and looks like a damned college professor, certainly not the type to strike me as drunkard. However, with his nose being bright red, him already slurring his words and not far off falling of his stool, there can be no real doubt in my mind. 'Get him another one on me!' Strange, I didn't even realise I had fans. I glare at him and raise my bottle in appreciation. I don't much feel like talking at the moment. He echoes the gesture, chuckling. He kinda reminds me of Friar Tuck from a Robin Hood flick I saw one time. The jukebox behind me whirs into life and the deep, unmistakable, tones of Johnny Cash begin to play - ‘A Boy Named Sue' if I'm not mistaken. I smile, the song always has that effect on me. Even now. As he walked passed, I can see the man who has selected the song wore blue denim and a green hat that made him look like a trucker, hell, he could be a trucker for all I knew. I can see his lip curl at the sight of me, sneering as he goes by. Seems he's taken a disliking to me, I'm not really surprised, I have that effect on made people. Usually it's because I search for it, but not this time. 'Get him a Martini or some shit,' he says, re-taking his stool at the other side of the bar. 'A pussy's drink should suit him just fine.' I smile. 'There's no call for that,' Friar Tuck responds calmly, only slurring a few of his words. 'Sure there is,' The Johnny Cash man replies, his friends laughing along. 'This pussy could even beat a woman in the ring. I was there. Time limit draw? What a fucking fag.' 'It was a good fight,' Friar Tuck says, still defending me. I don't need defending, but he persists anyway. 'Leave the boy alone.' 'And now he's getting some old fool to stick up for him!' he Johnny Cash guy continues, 'What a fucking pussy!' This is precisely why I'm here. Despite my veneer of a man desperately needing a drink - not much of a veneer, I'll grant you - I came looking for this. The unpredictable. A true brawl. The perfect training. I had thought that I'd have to instigate things, but apparently it's my lucky day. I hurl my emptied bottle of Bud across the bar and watch it smash against then moron's thick skull. The Grazing Cow behind the bar, lets out a high-pitched scream and ducks down behind the counter, out of harms way - she knows what's coming next. The moron's friends stand from their seats and crack their knuckles, their intentions clear. A rumble ensues. One Hour Ago. Rose Garden. Portland, Oregon. I'm lying on the connected wooden benches within the GCW locker room, waiting for the damned doctor to show up. The officials have probably sent him to aid Tessa Windsor first, hell, she is the GCW Cruiserweight Champion after all, in their eyes she's worth a lot more than me. They're probably right. I feel bitter, thinking back to an hour ago, to my match with Tessa Windsor and how I couldn't get the job done. Story of my life. A time limit draw, I had everything to gain and nothing to lose, or so I thought walking into the match. Now I'm reminded, yet again, it's not always like that. I had the victory in my grasp and I failed to capitalise on it. Now, I'm filled with resentment because of it and I'm worse off than I was before. I force the thoughts from my mind. Important times are at hand and there‘s no room for bitterness in my life right now. Dangerous Games is just around the corner and such negative thoughts will only hinder my preparations. As if I'm not hindered enough already. I'm with GCW and with the unaligned group of workers that refuse to join either warring factions and there are very few of us left now. Now the place is dominated by a couple of stables known as Dies Irae and the Ascension, both of which have snapping up talent to join their little war for the better part of three months now. I don't want any part of their colliding ego‘s, but my options are running out. The battlefield has already been earmarked, the first major skirmish has been arranged and strategies have been devised. Dangerous Games. A thirty man Darwinian fight for ultimate survival and the perfect platform for either side to score a decisive victory. The theory with these rumbles has always been no friends, only enemies, but in this particular event, grander things are occupying many of the competitor's heads. The last thing any their strategies will encompass, is every man for himself. Then there's me. A man alone between these two teams of giants. How the hell am I expected to compete under those odds? I'm not and that's the whole damn point. Already tonight, Shadow, self proclaimed leader of Dies Irae has singled me out and targeted me as the next victim. I am in this one alone and I realise that more than any of the others. That's why I'll win. That's I'll be more prepared than anyone else when Dangerous Games comes rolling around. I sit up from the bench, painfully cradling my gut. Seems I bruised a rib, maybe worse. It doesn't concern me now, all that concerns me now is accomplishing what I'm destined to do: walking out of Dangerous Games as the winner, ultimate survivor. I stand and pull on some jeans. Can't wait around any longer, I've gotta find me a bar... Now. A Bar. Portland, Oregon. I lean over the bar, ignoring the grazing cow still cowering on the floor, in the far corner, open the cooler and extract two bottles of beer. I don't choose a particular brand specifically, but I come out with two bottles of Bud. Good enough. I flip open the tops and place one in front of Friar Tuck, who's still chuckling to himself, despite it all. Johnny Cash is still playing on the jukebox, seems the idiot who started the fight paid for around five songs, or at least fifteen minutes worth. That's how long the brawl lasted. Bodies and blood scattered the floor, mixed in with broken wood, spilt beer and smashed glass. I'm the only one left standing. Well, the only one left standing in here. A few of the bar patrons had run out screaming like girls when the fight first broke out, mainly the college kids. Friar Tuck is the only one who didn't fight and I made sure nobody laid a hand on him - he did buy me a beer after all. I slump back down on the stool, wincing as the pain from my ribs plays up again and take a mouthful of frosty cold beer thankfully. The alcohol will take the pain away - it always does. 'The Battle of Thermopylae.' Friar Tuck declares, his joviality fading some. 'What the hell are you talking about?' I reply, as the Grazing Cow finally emerges from behind the counter, still shaking. 'Is it over?' She asks, reaching inside her pocket for a cigarette. 'Yeah,' I answer, developing a confused expression. I don't hear sirens, there should be sirens, shouldn't there? 'The police are taking their time, aren't they? I expected to be in the cells round about now.' 'The police don't come round these parts,' she says, 'the gangs see to that.' I nod and don't ask about it - I don't much care. 'Earth! render back from out thy breast. A remnant of our Spartan dead! Of the three grant but three, to make a new Thermoplyae.' Friar Tuck, took another sip of his Bud. 'Lord Byron in Don Juan, Canto three, stanza eighty-six, seven.' 'How many has he had?' I ask the Grazing Cow, concerned. 'Not half of what he usually drinks,' she replies. 'He's a History Professor at the local college. Always does this shit, rambling on for hours on end. Nobody understands what the hell he's on about half the time and can't shut him the hell up.' I shake my head and take another mouthful of Bud. 'The Spartan king, Leonidas, and his troops held off an attack by the Persians in a mountain pass, right by the Greek city of Thermopylae.' He takes another gulp of beer and I attempt to look interested. 'They say the Spartans had an army of around five hundred soldiers and were heavily outnumbered by the Persians, but nobody can be quite clear about the accuracy of the numbers. Some say sixty thousands, others say one million, others say up to five million...' 'Bullshit!' the Grazing Cow interrupted. 'An army of five hundred defeated an army of five million? Impossible.' 'They didn't win,' Professor Friar Tuck corrects, 'they were killed to the very last man, but they took a rather large portion of the Persian army with them. They stood up against overwhelming odds and paved the way for the Greeks to win the war at the battle of Salamis. Tragic really.' He finishes his beer, solemnly and stands. 'I should be getting home.' The Grazing Cow develops a confused expression and glances at her watch. 'It's only ten? You usually stay until I throw you out.' Professor Friar Tuck nods and turns to leave, putting a friendly hand upon my should. 'Good luck, friend.' I nod. 'Thanks for the beer.' As he disappears into the night, he replies, 'No problem.' I finish off my beer in one swig and stand from the barstool. 'I'd better be leaving too.' I tell the Grazing Cow, throwing a few bucks on the bar. 'I've got what I came for.' 'Who the hell's gonna clean this mess up?' She asks, some of the bodies stirring now. I don't respond, instead I make for the door, or what's left of it anyway, and head toward my motel. This was a good warm up, but it's not enough. I need to be ready for when my own battle of Thermopylae comes around.
