Overture

25 March 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/25/10 4:25 P.M. Miami, Florida. Rickenbacker Causeway. A 1970 navy blue Oldsmobile 442 speeds down the highway towards Miami Beach. Weaving in and out of traffic, it causes motorists to angrily pound on their horns, a vengeance suited only for the motorists that throw caution to the wind. Motorists like the man behind the wheel of the car that looks like it was time warped straight from the seventies, the classic muscle car Oldsmobile 442. Triumph Frost. Wearing a white T-shirt and faded navy vintage jeans, a cigarette hangs from the side of his mouth, his left arm draped out the window, his right casually gripping the steering wheel, eyes shielded by sunglasses. His wife, Alexis Frost, a stunning blonde straight out of a model shoot and sporting a shimmering green dress, holds on for dear life, a look of sheer terror on her face as her husband plays “Frogger” with two-thousand pounds of pure Detroit steel. “You think you could, I don’t know, slow it down a bit,” she queries, her hand gripping the door. “Before we die and all?” Triumph only responds with a sneaking smile, and a flick of the radio, turning it up nearly full-blast to “La Vida es Una Lenteia” by Unaesta, a funky Latin ditty that engulfs the sound system. “You worry too much, babe,” he snorts, as he weaves his way about the fine lines of life and death. He tosses the cigarette out the window. “I just want to continue living, you know?” Her voice cracks, as he nearly misses a Sonata. “Jesus, Tri! Chill it out, will you?” As he continues his jaunt towards South Beach, Alexis sheepishly looks at him. “What are we doing in Miami, anyways?” she asks. Triumph gives a shrug. “Dunno. Just felt like something different.” “You’re supposed to be training, you know,” she sternly remarks. Triumph turns to her, his left eyebrow raised. He pulls up the sleeve of his shirt, and flexes his arm. “You think I need to train, baby?” he chortles. “Check this shit out!” “Babe, babe, babe! The road!” Alexis clutches for dear life. “Fuck that. Tell me I need to train.” Triumph keeps his eyes fixed on her, and still somehow manages to keep them both on the road. “Jesus Christ, will you watch the road?!?” Her knuckles whiten from clutching the door handle. “Tell me.” “I-I don’t…” Her eyes meet Triumph’s. His face never wavers from his shit-eating grin. “Tell me.” Alexis is near tears. “You’re good, alright? You don’t need to train! Now watch the fucking road!!!” Triumph widens his smiles, and puts both hands on the wheel, his focus back on the road. He pulls out a pack of Camel Lights and grabs a smoke. Her breathing remains heavy. He pulls out a lighter and sparks up the smoke, taking a drag. “You need to chill, girl,” he says, smoke billowing out from his mouth. “Worry too goddamn much.” Her fear turns to scorn. 6:34 P.M. Miami, Florida. Bodega liquor store. In a stuffy liquor store that looks more off the grid than anything else, Triumph walks from the back of the store, where Alexis is chattering away on her phone, up to the cashier. A scruffy man-child in his early thirties, he wears a black-and-red attendant shirt and a pair of headphones. Triumph eyes the man’s nametag: “Abdul”. Triumph looks over the man, who’s name is clearly not “Abdul”, and scoffs. “Pack of Camel Lights,” he calmly remarks. “Abdul” ignores him, rocking out to his headphones. Triumph looks around the empty store, and incredulously back at “Abdul”, who remains oblivious to the world around him. “Pack of Camel Lights,” his voice louder this time. “Abdul” pays no attention. “Can you take me higher,” sings “Abdul” softly, as… BANG! Triumph slams his palms flat on the counter. “Abdul” slowly removes his headphones, as the distant sounds of Creed’s “Higher” blasts out. “I was rockin’ out, man,” huffs “Abdul”. He sighs, his eyes barely open. “What do you want?” Triumph bites his lower lip. “Pack. Of. Camel. Fucking. Lights.” “Abdul” looks at Triumph for a few seconds before he nonchalantly reaches overhead and pulls down a pack of smokes, tossing them on the counter. “Alright, man. No need to be mean about it, okay?” His voice reeks of disinterest. “Eight twenty-five.” Triumph’s eyes light up like a pinball machine on Tilt. “You’re fucking shitting me!” “Don’t blame me, man. It’s the economy or somethin’, I don’t know,” “Abdul” trails off, and starts to slightly sway his head back and forth. “But it’s like five a pack at the Seven-Eleven!” Triumph retorts, mouth still slightly agape. “So?” “Abdul” continues wavering about. Triumph closes his eyes, and his mouth shuts. He grumbles as he grudgingly reaches into his pockets. He pulls out a twenty and tosses it on the counter. “Abdul” eyes it and looks back up at him. “I don’t think I can break that, man,” he spits. Triumph rubs his forehead. “Then just give me two packs and keep the fucking change,” his hand in the air in defiance. “You don’t gotta swear so much, man,” “Abdul” scoffs as he grabs the second pack. “I’m right here, you know?” The man rings up the cash register and Triumph grabs the smokes and pockets them, as Alexis saunters up, closing her phone. “Ready, babe?” she asks him. He nods quickly. “Abdul” puts back on his headphones and starts humming “Higher” again. Triumph just stands for a moment. “Really?” he asks, but it’s too late. “Abdul” has returned to oblivion. 6:41 P.M. Miami, Florida. Bodega liquor store parking lot. Triumph walks out to the car, Alexis following close behind. He sits on the hood of his 442 and begins to pack one of the boxes in his hand. “So who were you calling, anyways?” “I,” she beams, “just got us two first class tickets to Salt Lake City!” He blows on the pack, excess tobacco flecks flying everywhere. He chuckles. “We leave tomorrow morning,” she tells him. “Why,” he postulates, as he pulls out a cigarette with his teeth, “the fuck would I ever want to go to that godforsaken city?” He pulls out a lighter and begins to light it up. She stands, hands on hips. “How many times do we have to talk about this?” “Talk about what?” He takes a long drag from the cigarette. “You keep saying that we talk about stuff, but I don’t remember any of it. Like when you told me your grandmother died? I don’t remember that happening.” She laughs incredulously. “We went to the damn funeral!” “That didn’t happen,” he reiterates. “It was two months ago!” Alexis goes on the warpath, tapping her finger to her palm at each relevant point. “You got shitfaced and started a fight with my uncle. You passed out during the service. Then you called my mother a whore and hit on my cousins.” Triumph eyes her for a moment. “That doesn’t sound like me.” She lets out an annoyed grunt, and throws her hands in the air. “Whatever. Look, you’re part of a company now. You need to make appearances. You need to show up.” She gets closer to him, poking him in the chest. “You need to par-ti-ci-pate.” He swats her hand away from his chest. “Knock that shit off, I get it, okay?” He jumps off from the hood, rolling his eyes. “Jeeeesus.” She walks right up to him. “I’m serious, okay?” She grabs his hand, and slowly caresses it. She shines her puppy-dog eyes up into his. “It’ll mean so much to me.” Triumph continually tries to look away, but is mesmerized and slowly returns to her hypnotic gaze. He closes his eyes and breathes softly. “Okay. For you.” She lets out a squeal, taking him aback a bit, and kisses him on the cheek. “Now let’s go find a five-star restaurant and get the most expensive thing on the menu.” She skips off and jumps into the car. He tosses his cigarette down, stamping it out with his sneakers. “God…damnit.” He jumps into the car and revs it up. 10:37 P.M. Miami, Florida. Passage Bar, Hotel Victor. A cute waitress saunters by, as soft hip hop music flows through the bar. Triumph and Alexis sit at a table, Alexis with a Mojito and Triumph a bottle of Corona. She sips her drink nervously, while he surveys the bar. He notices her nervousness. “C’mon, what?” She peers up from her drink. “I don’t like drinking in public,” she whispers. “When, you know, we’re…” Triumph throws his head back slightly and sneers. “I know the people who own this place. I know fucking everybody that matters.” “Yeah,” she continues. “But people will--“ “Will what?” He leans forward, grabbing her hand. “Do you see me? No one will say shit.” A group of guys walk by, looking right at Triumph. He nods his head towards them, and they pass by. “See?” She starts to loosen up, and clutches his hand. He pulls away, and takes a sip from his beer. She pulls out her iPhone and starts going through it. The waitress passes by again, and smiles at Triumph. He returns the smile. Alexis’ eyes dart up from the phone. He gives her a ‘what-can-I-say’ look, and shrugs with a smile. She playfully pouts, sliding the phone along the table. He looks down at it. “This guy,” she beams, “looks promising.” On the phone is a fan site for Bryan Mayhem. Triumph eyes it intently for a few seconds, and then lets out an inappropriate laugh. He looks back at Alexis, who is none too pleased. “You can’t be serious. Holy shit, this guy looks like a sex offender!” He looks back down at the phone. “You think he just walks around without a shirt like that? That’s just creepy.” He picks up the phone, going through it. “And who wears clown makeup like that? That asshole posse of clowns, the ones that are insane or something, I forget what they call themselves. Fuck those losers.” Alexis snaps her fingers and gets his attention back. “Hey. He’s a good start, okay? It says here he’s the, uhh…” She grabs the phone back, and starts cycling through the site. Triumph lounges back, as if he’s too cool for school. She finds what she’s looking for, and points at it. “Here. He’s the Hardcore Champion. It’s not, like, the best or anything. But it’s something!” Triumph scoffs. “I don’t give a shit about some fancy belt. I just wanna turn people into quadriplegics or at worst, a loss of feeling in their legs for six to eight months.” He takes a sip from the bottle. “I don’t need titles.” “Well,” she says, “you need to try something.” “I haven’t seen ‘Bad Boys’ in awhile,” he contemplates aloud. “When we get back to the room, we should totally watch that.” She stares at him for a moment, but shakes her head in disbelief. She goes through the phone again, and then slides it across the table. Triumph looks down at it. “Rick Mad?” He slides the phone back to her. “Now I know you’ve lost your mind.” “Why not,” she asks. “He’s working the show. If you don’t want to, err, bash his brains in,” she moves about in her seat, obviously uncomfortable to say things like that. “Well, we’ll find someone else.” The gears in Triumph’s head slowly turn about. He takes another sip from the beer. “Can we still watch ‘Bad Boys’?” She grunts. “Yes, we can still watch ‘Bad Boys’.” He slams his hand on the table, startling people. “Well! Goddamn, girl!” Alexis slinks down in her chair. Triumph takes a large gulp of the beer, finishing it off, the lime tumbling down the side of the glass. A sly smirk runs across his face. “Sounds like fun.”
