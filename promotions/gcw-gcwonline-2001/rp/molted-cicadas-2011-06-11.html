Molted Cicadas

11 June 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06/11/11 September 2002 Braided Cross Ranch, San Saba County, Texas The warm breeze gave a tenor peel to the baritone crackles of the campfire. Cicadas chirped in the wiry brush. They were far enough from the fire to from being trampled and burnt, but they contributed into the cadence. Somewhere deep in the prairie were several spitters. Their muffled hisses and shakes warned travelers this mild night. Above all of this, the Braided Cross Ranch could be heard laughing, alive in this early fall evening. Four teenage boys stoked a massive bonfire as two older cowboys supervised. The boys would shove their brands deep into the fire, pushing the ashes of dry wood and branches. They pulled out their sticks and play fought with them, the glowing press floating in the night slightly illuminating each face. The older hands chew their Red Man, spitting gobs to their sides. One of the cowboys was short and stout: his face red with broken capillaries, an ashen colored mustache and white sideburns under a white straw hat. The other man was younger but more run down, his face showed every year in the wrinkles and wind burnt features. The wool collared jacket swallowed up his lithe figure as the dirty v-neck t-shirt clung to his chest. Not far away from this gathering was a massive split-level ranch house. The moonlight faintly shined off the smooth adobe siding, making it an eerie citadel in the night. In one corner of the house, a dimly lit window had a young Will O’Galloway with his hands and face pressed up against it. He made cupping binoculars with his hands, thinking it would let him to look farther at his older brothers’ bonfire. Will’s room was spartan; a twin sized bed lofted over his desk, miscellaneous file cabinets lining the small walls. Out of all the younger O’Galloways he was the only one with their own room, and this room was his life. His mother had him on a strenuous schedule preparing him for the GED. Tonight he would be studying social studies if his brothers, father and uncle not distract him from his desk. He was the runt of the O’Galloway family. Whereas Will was small, skinny, and bookish, his older brothers were strapping, ruddy and skilled with the various ranching tasks. The third brother, Cian, broke his arm while trying to break in a painting horse and still managed to get the horse cut before heading to get it set in a cast. Will’s only exposure to the fieldwork was trying to wrestle and tie down a calf that got loose. The result of that was the steer escaping to the Johansen’s watering hole a few acres away and Will somehow getting himself tangled in the rope. Still Will dreamt. He was tired of the endless studying for the GED and preparing paperwork for his admittance into the seminary program at Columbia. His mother never gave him a chance to choose otherwise. The O’Galloway clan believed in the act of tithing to the point that the tenth child would be a gift to God and would serve him. His childhood has consisted of endless coursework, the Psalms and appeasing a set of overbearing parents. Will moved away from the window and spun back around in his chair. With a sigh, he opened his U.S. Government book and returned to his studies. Before he could focus on the book, he heard a sudden rap on his window. Startled, Will twisted around to see Finn O’Galloway waving to get his attention. At 19, Finn held an odd position among the boys on the Ranch. Unlike the older boys, he was not in charge of the operations and drives, however he wasn’t treated like a greenhorn like his younger brothers. Finn was skilled enough on horse and roping that he was considered a jack-of-all-trades. The faint light of Will’s desk lamp could not do Finn justice, around San Saba he was considered one of the more handsome O’Galloways and their mother figured his marriage could get them connected to a stronger ranching family. Finn did not seem to mind the girls’ attention, but often felt uncomfortable due to the meddling of his Ma. Will felt a connection with Finn that was not there for his other siblings. His older sisters would baby him. As he was the youngest, they felt he always was going to get hurt out mending fences or castrating calf, so they protested whenever Da needed another worker. This meant that ‘Uncle Ulf’ would come to help, even when he had his own ranch to manage. His older brothers felt he was incompetent and a sissy. The O’Galloways were a hardy stalk, and they did not need some scab ruining their legacy. Even worse: Will finding some way to cause harm towards the ranch. Finn was empathetic. He saw that Will was excluded from the boys’ adventures and tried to spend time with his smaller brother. Will got his playful sense of humor; and Finn enjoyed the stories he would tell from the Bible. Finn roughed his way through high school, but respected the ability of the prodigious baby brother of the family, where as the other members saw Will as a freak. Finn exhaled onto the window and wrote, “sneak out front” and grinned at his youngest brother. Will nodded and Finn backed away from the window, and slipped into the darkness. Excited by the opportunity, Will nodded and grabbed his denim jacket. He opened his door slowly to see if Ma was still moving about. The wood paneled hallway was dim but opened out into their living room. Careful not to step on a part where the carpet ‘popped,’ Will crept to the front entrance and opened the screen door and slipped out. The night sky opened up in their front yard, no brush or scraggly trees blocking his view of the stars. Will took a breath and smiled when the mesquite burning bonfire hit his nostrils. The smell was of when he was younger, the oldest sisters would set him on a table near the fire as they cooked dinner, leaving Will to play with his blocks and create worlds in his head. The whole O’Galloway family was at peace; men working, women taking care of them, and William exploring his world. This peace would exist until he felt a creeping slither on his boot. “WHAT THE--” Will jumped three feet to the side as Finn snickered from the ground. “Now Will, you know that a snake ain’t going to get you at this time of night. It’s like you saw a ghost!” Finn cackled. Will blushed and stammered for a second before his heart reentered his chest cavity. As soon as he regained composure, he grew a bit angry. “It-tt-t’s just I don’t like being startled, that’s all. It’s stupid and mean,” Will replied. Finn composed himself as he stood up, still chuckling and smiling from his trick. He was so much larger than Will, where as Will was barely five foot five and exceptionally skinny, Finn towered over six feet and was broad. As well, Finn’s curly strawberry blonde hair was more indicative of the O’Galloway family rather than William’s shaggy dirty blonde. Finn walked over to a stump and sat down and Will followed him. “Of course,” Finn said, “You looked like you were dyin’ in there, surrounded by books.” Will said, “Seriously. I can’t wait until I’m done with this. Did Da or Uncle Ulf have anything special cooked up in the back.” “Nah, just the twins playin’ with the brands. I think Da just wanted them to get tired so they wouldn’t stomp all over the house,” Finn rummaged around his pocket and pulled out a rolled cigarette and a match. He struck it on the stump and held it in front of the cigarette in his mouth, and took several drags to get it lit. From the burning smell, Will knew it was not just the usual pocket snuff they usually had, “Want a puff?” Will reached out and took the joint and tried a smoke. He quickly weezed and coughed and Finn laughed again shaking his head and taking back the joint. “Figured. We will haveta rub juniper on you so Ma doesn’t smell it on your clothes and try to find our hidden bushes in the hills. This is it, isn’t it? One last test before you enter your program.” “Yeah. I guess,” Will stated. He looked up into the stars, he found Orion quickly and then continued, “I don’t know Finn It seems like they’ve always set me on doing this, but I don’t even know if I want this, you know? I have to go to New York and get through Columbia, and then enter a seminary. I don’t feel that religious. I don’t feel like this is right. It is like she’s shoving me along so it’s the right thing. Tithing her youngest into the church. It’s so stupid and unfair. I never got a choice. This is it.” Finn took a deep drag, had a light cough and ashed, “I know it sucks Will. You never got the chance that we did. Or really any opportunity to prove yourself on the ranch. Ma seemed like she destined you for the church, or college or whatever,” he paused a second thinking of the difference between the two, “I don’t know Will. I’m actually kind of jealous of you. Your opportunity.” Will was aghast. After all the years of being the little brother and the runt of his family, he stopped in his tracks. He blinked several times; Finn did not notice and continued his explanation. “It’s crazy. None of us have had this opportunity, ya know? Other than driving down to Mexico or whatever, we haven’t gone anywhere. But you Will? You get to go to New York and shit. Man. That’s crazy, I’d kill for that.” Finn paused in the middle of his thought process. Ryan and he looked up to the stars. It was not the most uncomfortable silence. Rather, Finn and Ryan sat and enjoyed the quiet. Their family was enough off in the distance that they could not hear their hoot and hollering, so the boys had their peace. Just the bonfire in the air accented by the chirping cicadas and the breeze through the brush. “I mean. Fuck William. The biggest city in the nation. Like other than goin’ to Juarez or whatever, we aren’t gettin’ out of this life. You get it to go to New York. And what if you get to Columbia and you decide you want to go into art, or business, or whatever. Ma can’t do anythin’ about it. You get this giant opportunity. I’m so envious, I’m goin’ to stay here in San Saba, and will ranch or tend cattle my whole life. And I fuckin’ hate it man. I’m tied down to the Braided Cross. You could do anything, Will. You’re gettin’ out. She can’t control you from here.” Will nodded as his brother continued. “Think about it William; the most alive city in the world. And it’s yours. The women. You don’t gotta settle for Karen Johansen or hope some cute girl will see you at the calf wrestlin’ competition and is impressed that you’re able to throw one down and tie them off in a minute.” Finn killed the remained of the joint and tossed it into the bushes near the stump. He got up. He was exasperated, and quite jealous. He knew his mind did not work the same as William’s did. Will could analyze and figure out the most difficult situations. Finn could decide how to cut calf and what routes would work best to drive them to proper grazing. Unless Finn’s life changed, it would be like this. He would eventually find some farm girl that found him cute. They would marry and settle down. Will was different. Will could break out and do something. Go and serve other parishes, maybe become some Catholic scholar and teach others. Maybe Will would never get married or start a family or even spend nights on the range. The 4H offered him a full scholarship, so it didn’t really matter what his degree was in, Will had the opportunity that Finn and the rest of the family didn’t. The O’Galloway family was a self-fulfilling cycle. Find a wife. Build a ranch. Have kids. Teach the boys how to tend, the girls how to homestead. They find their partners and it repeats. Even now, with the Internet and bank transfers and the rise of online colleges this is how it is. This is how it will be. The Braided Cross might get special guns that brand without heat, or computers to monitor steer’s health. This was the life the family would live however, regardless of how the world changed around the O'Galloways. Will and Finn’s life would not change then. They would continue to look up and the stars and listen to the wind, cicadas, crackling fire and snakes in the wilderness. Will would move to New York and attend Columbia. It would be later in the next year where both started making the changes that would shape the dynamic of the family.
