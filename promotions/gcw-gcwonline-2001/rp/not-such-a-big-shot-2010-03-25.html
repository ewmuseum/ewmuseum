Not Such A Big Shot

25 March 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/25/10 People complain because the meteorologists on television are always wrong. Whether it be because the high temperature was only 78 instead of the forecasted 80 degrees or that they canceled their plans because it was “supposed” to rain and didn’t, meteorology is a constantly scrutinized profession. Most people take it in stride. They brush that scrutiny off and go to work the next day; not Aaron Fujita. Aaron would tell you to spend hours looking at and deciphering numerous computer models, deciding which is the most accurate, factoring in hundreds of atmospheric variables to determine high and low temperatures. Once you did that and came within two degrees of an actual high temperature on your forecast, Aaron would just brush the comments away. Aaron Fujita, clad in dress khakis, a blue button-up dress shirt and matching tie stands at the trunk of his charcoal gray Dodge Charger. He reaches in and retrieves a backpack before forcefully closing the trunk. He turns and begins walking from his car on this sunny Chicago day. As he walks, he puts his right hand into the air and locks his car with the wireless remote. In front of him is a large building with the letters “WGN” on its façade. Aaron approaches the front doors and reaches down to check the identification card hanging from his front pocket. “Time to feel like a big shot.” He says to nobody in particular. Aaron steps up to the door and pulls the card down on its retractable clip and passes it in front of a reader to his right. He lets the badge retract and grabs the door handle to open it, but the door remains locked. He sighs and pulls the card in front of the reader once more, only to see a red light flash, denying him access. He pulls on the handle in spite of the warning from the little red light. Suddenly the intercom dings and a voice comes over. “Can I help you?” The voice asks. Aaron sighs and turns to face the camera that is position amidst the entire system. He pulls the badge down in front of the camera and lets it retract before speaking. “Aaron Andrews here for my internship in the weather center.” Aaron replies. Aaron puts his hands into his pockets as the intercom goes silent for a few moments. He fumbles around with his keys to pass the time and seems to get irritated as each silent minute passes. Finally, the guard’s voice fills the intercom again. “Step inside please.” The guard says. A clicking noise is heard, which must be the door lock unlatching because Aaron reaches down and pulls the door open. He enters the building and approaches the security station to the left. He reaches it and places his hands on the counter, looking to the guard. “What’s the problem?” Aaron asks. The security guard continues flipping through a clipboard as Aaron awaits his answer. He does so, but doesn’t give the young man the courtesy of a look while speaking. “Your badge isn’t activated. It took me a few minutes to find you on the list.” The guard replies. Aaron sighs and looks towards the next door. He passes his badge in front of the reader again, but he gets the same red denial light as before. He pulls angrily, but the door remains shut. The security guard leans over the counter and stares at the aspiring meteorologist. “Hey knock that off!” He yells. Aaron abruptly stops his yanking and turns toward the guard with a frustrated look on his face. Aaron straightens his tie and steps back from the door. The guard shakes his head and reaches behind the desk for the button to unlock the door as Aaron calls out. “I want my badge straightened out by the time I come in next.” He barks. He gets no response and turns to walk through the door. He takes a few deep breaths and begins the trek down a long hallway towards the weather center. He seems to become calmer and calmer with each step he takes as he progresses down the hall. He reaches the door he is looking for and sighs once again. “At least Tom and Bill respect me.” Aaron says aloud. Aaron pushes the door open and enters, looking to his right. At a cluttered desk amongst numerous computer monitors is a man in his fifties, furiously punching numbers on a calculator. He doesn’t notice the door opening or closing and doesn’t take note of Aaron’s movement, so Aaron pauses and looks in the direction. “Tom Skilling, good afternoon.” He says. Again the man Aaron speaks to doesn’t turn to look before speaking. “I have to keep going on these numbers guys, I need you to be quiet.” Tom replies. Aaron shakes his head and walks past that portion of the medium sized office to another set of desks. He places his stuff down and reaches in to turn on the computer. As he takes a seat and gets comfortable, another man in his late thirties walks up and pats Aaron on the shoulder. “Too bad about your NCAA bracket kid, losing your entire final four already. What were you thinking putting Marquette, PITT, Villanova and Georgetown that far anyway?” The man asks with a laugh. Aaron sighs and looks to his computer screen. He begins entering a log-in to the system before he replies to the remark. “Don’t patronize me, Bill. I thought they all had good teams. At least I picked Northern Iowa over Kansas.” Aaron says. Bill continues to laugh as he walks a few feet away and begins collecting papers from his desk. “Yeah, but that was a stupid pick too. You picked Northern Iowa over the number one overall seed.” Bill says. Aaron slams his fist down onto the desk as an error message pops onto the computer screen. He clicks out of it and enters his log-in information again, only to receive the same error message. He stands and looks at Bill, who has transitioned his laughter from NCAA talk to Aaron’s frustrated state. “Shut up. I’m going up to HR to get my log-in and badge straightened out.” He says with a clear ire in his voice. Aaron moves back towards the door and opens it to exit, but his eyes light up as he hears Tom. “Hey, um, uh, Aaron?” Tom asks. Aaron stops short of walking out the door and turns back, happy he gets to interact with the highest paid meteorologist in the world. “Yeah Tom?” He answers. “Will you get me a Diet Mountain Dew while you’re out there?” He asks. Aaron’s shoulders drop and he sighs once more as he turns to exit the office. “No problem.” Aaron says; his mood obviously less than improved. The young student walks out of the office and begins towards a stairwell, upset with the start to his night. “My life sucks.”
