Sex, Drugs, and Paperwork

21 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 21, 2005 Tulz was right--it happened. We were hours into drinking and smoking, really giving the authentic Jordan White smell to the CEO office at Anarch, when my thought train finally broke and left us to our own ingenuous devices. I might as well have discovered gravity, with gravitas towards the leaps and bound I made for mankind. Three hundred and twenty-seven years old. Fucking Christ. This is monumental--this sets precedent for the future of human sexual interaction. Soon, everyone will be finding quasi-immortals to corrupt and lead into a life of all-sorts of devious hijinks, and they'll all have me to thank. I should get a fucking plaque, but that isn't even the major event of the night. As dawn crept through the windows, highlighting wisps of smoke that bunched in clumps near the ceiling, I felt that I should peel my ass off the plushy leather chair (which still had Johnny's increasingly irritating initials stitched in it at that time) and start rifling around in the file cabinets that I had come to own. Tulz raised her head up from the length of the polished-and-sweat-smeared pine-finished desk and smirked at me, then content to collapse again. Her chest rose and fell in long/deep breaths, skin a golden color under the cheesy ass mood lighting, and I almost felt content--always a shortly lived emotion. The carpet was soft under my feet and smothered my toes with every step of the long walk across the office and to the massive oak file cabinets that Johnny kept his contracts in. I was curious--my name was in one of those files and whatever Lexicon had to say about me probably mattered--so I rummaged, ripped, and tore my way through until I figured out that my name wouldn't be under J for Jordan. W for White was the obvious choice, so I took it and was horribly disappointed. I wasn't a Wade, a Wallace, a Weiner, or a Wilson. Something was clearly afoot. Tulz eyes dreamily trailed me as I made my way back to the desk, pulling Lexicon's embroidered smoking jacket off a chair and onto my, well, me on the way. Her flushed face, eyes thin slits, didn't tell me anything that I didn't already know about myself. I wasn't bored, but my shoulder was only bearable thanks to the classic rock star stand-by--heroin--and even that was waning as morning broke. I came to understand the dull hum at the base of my neck as an intense headache and thought 'plot device' heavily enough for Tulz to smirk. Still content, I jerked a cabinet on Johnny's desk open. There was a leather attach?, a rusty brown color with a shallow snakeskin imprint to it and--guess what!--JL in gold on the front, back, handle, and clasps. The lock was one of those classic types, five numbers and a few wheels to spin. I didn't trust luck, and I never want to know Johnny well enough that I can guess the codes on his lock, so I broke the defeated bottle of rum on the edge of the desk and started gouging away at the face of the case until the wooden frame had splintered enough to pull its contents out. Tulz watched with rapt attention, stolen from the dreamstate by all my lovely banging and noise-making. There were two manilla folders inside the attach?, held shut with elastic bands that pulled vertical across them, and each had a name, in impeccable cursive, on a little slap-on label. There was Johnny's and there was mine. Fuck Johnny; I tossed his to the side, missing the fireplace by feet, and pulled mine open. By then, Tulz was laid out on her stomach across the table, staring down at the papers I dumped into my lap and reading them in time with me. The first few were contract-related, bullshit that didn't deserve all the effort Johnny'd gone to in keeping it locked away. Schedules for appearances that I hadn't made, promo photos, records, graphs, charts, the mundane followed as my fingers slipped around on something at the back of the stack. Since my fingers were slipping, I knew it wasn't another boring sheet of paper and put my attention to it. Two sets of X-rays with my name on them, White, Jordan X, and a series of numbers. They were clipped to the back of a stack that I began to read. Once upon I time, Jordan got a medical report. It said that he could wrestle, with a probability that he'd suffer irreparable damage to both body and mind. Jordan White let the notion slide, as he'd never been one that knew when to stop--that was why he kept people around. They were relatively sane and often stepped in the way to prevent him from injuring himself in some stupid pursuit of a self-righteous notion. At the heavy goading of Johnny Lexicon, this lovable rogue donned a mask and went out, threatening to destroy himself. As a result, he couldn't wrestle again. So he thought. Lexicon, however, wasn't a very nice guy. As I, now back to first-person, read, things started to fall together. There were duplicate reports, all information the same until it got to the actual diagnosis. My neck was tight enough that I wasn't sure it had ever fit me. No, I hadn't been risking lingering self-destruction then; I'd been running along in it for six months before I ever went to the doctor. Sticky notes with the same handwriting as the folders marked out various points that needed to be changed, issues that had to be resolved, and covered the whole of 'what Jordan needs to hear to keep wrestling, but not sue my ass.' I watched the shiver run down the length of Tulz' spine and looked back down to the papers. She was quick to light a cigarette and placed it in my lips as she rubbed the top of my head. Her fingers trailed down my chin and came to rest at my jaw, slowly pulling my head up so she could stare into my eyes. Fuck that, I thought. I took a long drag and looked through the pile of clothes for my cell phone, calling Trey when I found it. His voice was slurred with sleep. 'Hey--who's this?' 'Jordan.' 'Yo, J. What's up?' 'Johnny's alive.' '...huh? Did you find him?' 'No, but he'd better be alive for his own fucking sake.' 'That doesn't even make sense.' 'Then you don't understand the universe.' 'Shit, you don't make sense.' 'Look, life's a big circle of conflicts and resolutions--and a new conflict with Johnny's come up. If he's not alive, I can't make sure he suffers enough. Get it?' '...what the fuck? Dude, you're fucking losing it. Johnny's dead and you..' 'I don't trust the phone lines.' 'See? You're fuckin' paranoid. He's dead, what could he do to you?' 'He's not dead.' 'Get some sleep.' 'Where are you?' 'Miami, man. You got any clue what time it is?' 'Six a.m.--same as here. Look, we need to meet up.' 'Sleep.' 'Fuck sleeping. We've got to figure out how to find Johnny...' '...if he's alive.' 'My will to see him suffer could keep him alive for a couple eternities right now, so don't worry about that.' 'So, you're gonna kill him? Like, again?' 'He's not dead, and he hasn't gone through nearly enough shit to justify dying anytime soon--not this lifetime, at least.' 'Sleep,' he roared. 'Get sober and hit me up when we're both awake. You're ramblin' again, not makin' sense.' 'I'm making perfect sense, you're just too damned tired to realize it. I'm catching a plane, meet me at the airport.' 'Man, don't be so loud--Erika's gonna wake up.' 'The hell with Erika, this is serious.' 'Always say that, J.' 'Only when it's serious.' 'Fuck. Look, aight, shit. When you gonna get here?' 'I'll call when I know.' 'You're gonna forget alla this when you crash out, y'know?' 'Probably; so fuck sleep.' 'You sure we can't just talk this shit out now?' 'I don't trust the phones.' 'Shit. Aight, aight. Just don't pull no psycho shit when we meet up.' 'I'm not psycho.' 'Riiiight. Later man.' I hung up and looked to Tulz. Her soft hands were still wrapped on my face, her eyes locked on where mine would be if they were ever completely open and away from shadow. 'I'm sorry,' she said. 'Why?' No answer. I smiled--no need for pity. Trey, however, wasn't so much sorry at the news as he was completely confused by the doctor's results that I was shown. 'Don't get it, man, why Johnny'd show you reports that said you weren't straight to wrestle.' 'He knew I wasn't stupid enough to buy that I hadn't fucked my body over by then.' 'Shit. So Lexicon...' '...outsmarted me, yeah. Got the best of me, yeah.' 'That don't happen much.' 'It never happens; especially not on this fucking level.' The humid air stuck the collar of my shirt to my neck with sweat as the sun cast on my back. I milled over the food in front of me, questioning its worth. 'Christ. This isn't even right, Trey.' 'You gonna get checked out?' 'Wherefore?' 'Huh?' 'Why?' 'See what happened to you, I guess.' 'So I can live down to the damage? Fuck that.' 'Man, might be a way to help it. Shit, you might've got lucky, not had shit happen to you.' I laughed. 'No one's that lucky.' 'Fuck, man. What about Lexicon?' 'He's not lucky at all.' 'Nah, not that. I mean, how you gonna find him?' 'He's Lexicon. If he's alive, he's trying to make a name for himself somewhere--start working to get his empire back, or build another one. If he's alive, he's going to making a lot of noise. If you didn't notice, Anarch has a lot of ears. The guy can't do much for money, other than wrestle or donate plasma. Pretty sure there wasn't a plasma bank near him. Anyway, if he's alive...' Silence. 'Trey.' 'You'll find him.' 'Ding, ding, ding.' 'Then what?' 'Destroy him.'
