History then and now

19 May 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 05/19/11 The morning air is heavy with mist, the ground wet from the moisture in the air. Most like a wet blanket covering the history of this land that seems to wish to forget. The world seems to not grow old or even move forward here, the air is thick with sadness and torture. Shaman quietly walks through the now wet stoned street. He looks side to side, as if he is watching for ghosts unseen. He stops at the gate and looks up. Staring at the gate as its imposing iron looms over him as the shadows seems to cast and ancient yet foreboding warning to all those who dare to enter. He reads the sign to himself just barely audible. “ Arbeit Macht Frei. “ Shaking his head slightly as he walks through the main gate at Auschwitz Concentration camp almost 70 years later. He looks to his friend walking with him nearby. “Work liberates or work makes you free.” His friend clasps a hand on Shaman's shoulder tightly as they pass along the guard tower nearby. He stops looking at the tower. Tilting his head he can almost visualize the guards watching him as he passes. Then he turns closing his eyes listening as if he is going to hear the whispers of thousands and thousands of ghosts tell him something profound. The friend looks at him and speaks. “What is it my friend?” “The weight of the sadness still in the air is heavy, almost like a thousand hands pressing down on my chest.” Placing a hand on his chest he clenches his jacket slightly. Turning he faces another building, this building houses the ten meter room of hair of victims. Pinching the bridge of his nose he squeezes his eyes shut and then opens them once again. “It is hard to imagine what these people went through.” Slowly he walks along with his friend as he points at several buildings as they both remain silent. Nodding occasionally they take several turns and stop at the execution spot of Rudolph Hoss. He stands quietly observing. For some reason he simply spits on the ground at the base of the gallows. Turning Shaman walks away angry, his step faster than usual and he comes into a clear opening after a few minutes of heavy walking. He stops and simply stares at the gas chamber looming before him. The most photographed image of Auschwitz. “The weight of this place reminds me of the weight of the war myself and Bryan Mayhem have been waging with one another for years.” His friend hurries up beside him, he simply stares at the converted bomb shelter turned gas chamber. Walking forward he reaches out to touch the nearby electric fence, which is no longer working of course. Reaching out he touches it almost as if he feels the electric jolt go through his soul. Turning he looks at his friend. “Late 2006 , September if I remember correctly Bryan had met with an attack from myself. No longer was he the only beast on the block. I had learned a longtime ago show yourself as quickly as possible and go after the biggest dog in the park.” Smiling a bit he walks placing his hands into his pocket. Kicking his heals on the ground softly as he walks, he clears his throat as a bit of steam comes from deep within his lungs joining he misty air. “Come January that year I was poised to take something precious from him. Hoping to throw a wrench in the works Kyo Kushinaga was thrown into the mix. Of course history simply shows that I was the dominating factor in beating Bryan Mayhem and taking a title from him. The Television title to be exact.” Slowly he removes his hand from his pocket and then clenches a fist looking at it. His eyes focus on the scarred and battered knuckles. “Wouldn't be the only thing I took from him would it?” Motioning Shaman walks faster with a bit of spring in his step. His eyes dart from side to side looking for something that is never really there. He knows they are there deep within his soul. The ghosts of days long past. Much like his past that cannot seem to let him go, the past between he and Mayhem. “Just less than a year or so later Bryan and myself formed the most devastating tag team to ever walk through GCW calling ourselves Oblivion. We gained tag team titles during that reign and I took away Mayhems identity.” stepping through the gates slowly he grins sadistically. He turns an eye back towards the former concentration camp and he lets out a slow breath. His eyes focus for a moment and he speaks to those gone. His heart in it one hundred percent he turns and heads down the gravel road with his friend in tow. “I had to leave that place since it would dishonor their memory wanting to release my rage and anger talking about a war in GCW. There are somethings I will not do, there are some liens I will not cross. Disrespecting the dead is one of them.” Stopping they reach the vehicle that took them there. Shaman slowly opens the door and sits in the passenger seat. He turns and points to a side road, His friend starts the vehicle as they drive off down the road towards the other concentration camps in Auschwitz. His eyes focus on the railroad tracks where the road runs close too. Each railroad tie moving faster and faster as a line of historical events seem to spring from them. Imagining the black and white images of the masses of people being transported here. Gypsies, Jewish, Russian, and the list goes on and on he thinks to himself. Bringing his hand over his mouth he rubs gently. Letting out air slowly through his nose he simply watches. Wondering how the people of the nearby towns could turn such a blind eye to what was going on there. Then a thought turns inwards as he begins to wonder about something else. “Has Mayhem had the blind eye turned on him, was this the reason he had been so easily defeated by me several times.?” Rubbing his fingertips together slowly he thinks to himself. “No Mayhem believed in his own bloody crusade and hype, now he has to figure out why I have not disappeared like the rest.” Laughing a bit he leans back in the seat roughly. “Perhaps it is time I put the petulant child in his place. It is simple really, win or lose the answer is simple at its utmost most truthful answer.” Shaking at the sheer enjoyment of finally figuring out this simple revealing fact. “The answer you seek is simple Bryan, I am your own monster. I am that which you have sought out. I am your very own monster that you cannot run from.” Coming to a stop at the gates of Auschwitz III-Monowitz the work camp. Stepping out of the car Shaman walks to the gate slowly. His eyes following the rows of barbed wire. “I'd almost forgot the old adage, to stop a monster you have to fight it with a monster. The truth will be in our fight come World Wide Bryan my dear friend. You know we are the rivaled history in GCW. We are the legendary match up everyone has been waiting for.” Standing straight he adjusts his jacket. His eyes narrow as he lower his head concentrating once more on the tasks at hand. “This time my brother in war, we are fighting each other and not the glory of GCW. Come Bryan with the rage you have shackled for so long, allow me to cleanse you of every little thing that is holding you back. When it is said and done feel free to cry.” Turning he loses all emotion, his face returns to a stone like edge. His jaw tight as he enters the work camp. His friend slowly makes his way around the car as he follows. Shaman simply looks on now that his mind is clear of that which was bothering him the most. Now to him this was the time to pay respect to those he had wanted to for a longtime. Disappearing into the thick mist no sound is heard but the silence and heavy weight of sadness emanating from the concentration camp. Some following quotes from Auschwitz and this roleplay was a tribute to those who suffered there. We know that a man can read Goethe or Rilke in the evening, that he can play Bach and Schubert, and go to his day's work at Auschwitz in the morning. ~George Steiner During the first 3 years at Auschwitz, 2 million people died; over the next 2 years - 3 million. ~Witold Pilecki “Since Auschwitz we know what man is capable of. And since Hiroshima we know what is at stake.” ~Viktor Frankl
