A Long Process

30 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5-30-05 The fire burned out a long time ago. Ryan Stephens had become all that he once detested. The sleek Armani, the wealthy acquaintances, the watch, the money clip- all if it had become his persona. He had shoved her aside over two years ago. She had been a liability during a title run that meant more than her, more than anything. When it was over, she was gone, and frankly so was he. A mere shell of the fiery youngster with all the heart and resilience in the world. ******* “Don’t forget you have that meeting tonight with the Barratas,” she said hesitantly. Shelly Tallent a sharp woman. Her thick frame spun around from Stephens? desk as he gazed down upon the manila folder she had dropped before him. A perceptive person could discern that she had once been an attractive woman, but a couple of years of frustration and a pack of cigarettes a day will dilute the charm and the beauty. Her black hair was pulled up into a bun with a pencil and her dress was in dire need of an iron. She had piercing green eyes and big pouty lips that used to spit venom in the courtroom. “Shelly,” he said sternly looking up from the folder and scouring her attire. “Yeah?” “Your grace period is over. It’s time to pull your shit together.” She shot a glance over her shoulder and his gray eyes met hers, but she merely sneered, shrugged her shoulders and replied. “Some could say the same for you.” She knew him. Once upon a time they were law school acquaintances. She couldn’t help but fall for his infectious ambition and he tolerated her because of it. He had dropped out and disappeared and she had gone on to be the first female and in a major firm in Houston before getting caught embezzling, over billing and various other crimes from the company. She had managed to work a deal and get off without jail time. She lost everything in the process including her sanity, and he read about all of it. To his old self it would have been disgraceful and disappointing but to the one-labeled Scandalous it was fascinating. He stumbled across her at a convention in Houston hotel bar and she went back to Austin with him. He had saved her ass and she was at his mercy. Where had she gone wrong she frequently asked herself? and where had he gone period. She had hoped he would restore her faith in mankind. Instead he did quite the opposite despite her continuous effort to find the kid hidden beneath all the bravado that he’d become. She slammed the door behind her and fish tank next to the massive bookcase rattled. Stephens smirked at nothing in particular and opened the manila folder. In large letterhead the top of the memo read: RE: PTC CONGLOMERATES. He seemed satisfied before even reading the ensuing text, which he would immediately do so very dutifully. ******* Scandalous really didn’t even begin to do justice to the man Ryan had become. Since almost killing his young prot’g? and one half of his illustrious tag-team Polar Opposites, Chance McMillian, he had orchestrated various other affairs, all resulting in a large profit in his pocket. While the National Wrestling Council spiraled towards extinction, Stephens sat idly by or so it seemed to most. Very few know that he had helped Wyatt Connors decimate the council. Ryan’s favorite was probably getting a lot of the council guys to join his new federation Wisoming Wrestling. It was a ludicrous name for a ludicrous fed and an ingenious idea. After receiving the financial backing for the company and holding a few shows Stephens took virtually all the money, none of which any of the wrestlers had yet received, and split. He was to say the least a conniving bastard. ******** “You know he’s back. He’s wrestling in the PTC Infinite Gauntlet tournament.” Blaise Barrata was the wealthy spoiled son of John Barrata whom Ryan did a lot of business with until he had swiped Wisoming’s money and disappeared. Blaise was a tall handsome man with jet black hair pulled back in a ponytail and excellent taste in clothes. His father had asked him to ditch the broad but he enjoyed her company too much, and because of it Ryan was very hesitant to do business with them now that he had returned to the states. They had a meeting tonight, but Blaise wouldn’t bring her. “I know. He turned up on the GCW roster two days ago. He can’t stay away,” she said. Robin Boca was a pharmaceutical sales woman. She had been a thirty-year-old when she was eighteen. She was tall and slender always dressed in fancy business attire and high heels. She prided herself on looking professional and looking good. Blonde highlights in her light brown didn’t signify any “blonde” moments. She was a smart and classy woman with soft facial features and a gorgeous smile. She was Ryan’s ex-wife and Blaise Barrata’s newest interest. “And why do you suppose that is?” Blaise asked. “Because that fiery young kid who refused to stop until his name was mentioned in the same sentence as Eli Flair, Big Alan Steele and JW Oswald is still within that cold, meticulous man somewhere. A tiny flicker, if sparked just right and start a forest fire Blaise,” she replied calmly. She was somewhere else. Recalling old times, her hazel eyes staring at memories. “I don’t know why he even bother’s anymore. He’s as wealthy as Tyler Nelson and he’s associated with Barrata Inc. He’s had his mediocre run. Some will remember him, others won’t, but he’s got all the money he’ll ever need and he can make a bigger name for himself behind the scenes, running his own federation. Why wrestle” Does he think he can win you back with some star-studded performance, a championship belt?” Blaise cackles in delight. He’s a rational man, smarter than he appears, but his arrogance proceeds him. Robin looks up slowly at Blaise. Part of her liked part of him. She couldn’t decide if it was genuine or if it was all just a show now. She was in so deep that she didn’t know if she would be able to pull herself back out. “You and I both know he couldn’t win me back if he became the Universal Title champion and defeated Doctor Curiosity, Angelo Deville and Archibald MacGregor all in the same day,” she said reassuringly, but with slight evidence of indecision. Blaise didn’t notice. “If he was up to more corruption though you’d think joining GCW would suffice. This gauntlet is unlike him.” She rattled of the names of superstars like she herself was a fan, she wasn’t. She watched because she was certain he’d turn up again and now all her worst fears and all her best dreams were beginning to culminate and it started with this Infinite Gauntlet. Blaise’s father wouldn’t let her attend the meeting tonight, but she needed to talk to Ryan? if he’d let her. “He’ll lose!” Blaise stated simply with an evil smirk. ******** “You know exactly what he wants, and exactly why he’s back.” John Barrata was a very powerful man. He was bigger and stronger than his son was but his face was battle hardened and scarred from his days of association with various mob circuits. He wasn’t in the mob, but he had fought alongside some of them and now he had connections and was in good standing with various Italian families. Ryan Stephens was a very flustering subject for the black eyed, leather skinned man of nearly sixty. His greasy slick black hair matched his freshly shined shoes. He had thought Stephens would be an easy score, but Ryan had turned out much sharper than expected and was frequently a few steps ahead of him and it didn’t help that his stubborn son wouldn’t relinquish Ryan’s ex-wife. He was perched in a large brown leather chair in one of his living room’s with Tim Scannel, one of his many employees. “We should just kill the bastard,” Scannel urged. “I don’t operate that way anymore Tim. We’ll wait,” the elder Barrata explained. “For what?” Tim was a sniveling little bastard. He was full of all sorts of useful information, but was annoying as he was intelligent and he didn’t fit in with the others due to his small frame and fear of violence, though he hid this disposition well. “He wants creative control over Fox Sports so he can coerce the PTC heads into letting him broadcast all their shows on his station. He’d manipulate them all and certainly use his experience in the field to add various features and upgrades. He’s a smart man.” John paused. He adjusted the Rolex on his left wrist. “There lies a problem within his vision, at least from my standpoint. I’ve watched him the last few years and he’s a solid competitor, but he can’t win the big one and his inability to do so will keep him from garnering the respect he needs from the fans and other wrestlers. The greatest mastermind in the world can’t manipulate that many people unless?” “Unless what boss?” “Unless they’re good enough to make others believe that they are indeed the answer. The PTC is lost and Ryan knows it. He knows that they’re vulnerable to hostile takeover especially with the loss of CORE. BUT he has to surround himself with the right personnel and he has to make them believe that they can confide in him. A upper mid-carder won’t obtain the respect he needs to make this happen. That’s why he’s back. For the first time in a long time, he plans on winning. And I for one, am not quite sure he can,” Baratta said. “In other words this is going to be a long process.” “Precisely!” John Baratta dismissed Scannel and went to his office to begin preparing for the meeting later in the evening. He would draw Ryan out and worry more about the sore on the roof of his mouth if Ryan started winning, if people starting watching. He had seen the old videos of Stephens. He, like others, feared that once the heart and desire of Ryan “Streak” Stephens merged with the intelligence and skill of “Scandalous” Ryan Stephens that the possibilities where endless. He prayed the two would never come together. ******** I had to go for a walk to clear my thoughts before the meeting this evening with the Barratas. I had to make sure I was still ahead. Unfortunately this stupid wrestling kept creeping into the confines of my head. I needed to begin participating again to my face back out there. It’d been December since this Wisoming incident and God knows how long since I had actually wrestled a match. It was probably sheer ego that made me think I would be able to compete in the infinite gauntlet match with only half of the training I was accustomed to putting in prior to a match. Assumptions and rumors were popping up at an alarming rate. Suspicions should be confirmed. I was up to no good, but none of them knew what I was up to, they never did until it was too late. Winning was the last thing on my mind despite what others might try to tell you. This wasn’t a last hurrah, just another chapter in a long storybook. I’d probably try to avoid as much contact as I could during the match, just stay out of the way until the most opportune times. Winning was overrated especially at the cost of exerting too much energy, but I certainly didn’t want to be one of the first ones out either. That just wouldn’t do, and if for some reason I did win by happenstance than by all means, even better. Soon enough my name would be synonymous with all the business opportunities that engulfed PTC. There was people to manipulate, others to beat senselessly and money to be made. I sniffed the air and felt an overwhelming sensation of self-satisfaction. The dawning of a new era was just around the corner, all I had to do was lift the veil. As I patrolled the sidewalks of downtown Austin I witnessed it all. I was programmed to see everything as a business opportunity. Ideas came to me in droves. This was a land full of money to be had and people to outsmart. As I watched the UT Austin girls walk by in their tiny white skirts, fake tans, Dad’s cell phone glued to their ear, I thought of conning one into being my weekly entertainment’but then I thought of her and I quickly shook the thought from my head. On a hill near 8th street I thought I saw two men in navy suits and cheap glasses staring at me, plotting even, but then again I had become a paranoid person over the last few years. The gauntlet couldn’t come soon enough, neither could be GCW debut. It was time to set the gears in motion. As I meticulously ran my sinister plan through my head over and over, admiring its? flawless nature a haunting image kept seeping in. I had dreamt last night, as I had the night before, and the night before that of a young kid in sky blue tights pounding me into an oblivion. In my dream his face was blurred, but I knew it was McMillian or any other person I had betrayed. It was someone that I was supposed to recognize but didn’t. Every night I was haunted by the kid in the blue tights, with yellow boots executing high flying moves with no restraint and me gasping for air, for life. Each night the dream ended with a tiny lighter showing a flicker of a flame and then I would awake. I shoved the images from my mind. There was no time. I focus on my plan, on my upcoming match and most importantly my meeting with the Baratta’s tonight. That prick Blaise better not bring her I thought. I shuffled along around a corner and down towards a bus stop when the collision occurred. It happened so fast, but I witnessed the soft blonde hair collide into my chest and a Styrofoam cup empty the contents of a hot cappuccino al over my suit. I was speechless and angry, but my rational took over and I scoured the surrounding areas to make sure it wasn’t some sort of diversion. “I’m sorry,” a tiny voice stammered. “I am so sorry.” You would expect her to start wiping my suit, but she didn’t. I could tell that she feared the scowl I was sending her away, and yet I could also tell that she noticed my panic as I surveyed the area. The burning had not yet set in. “I’m so sorry mister. Are you okay?” she asked genuinely as she bent down to pick up the cup. Still, I said nothing. I glanced around again and then watched her. Her skin was porcelain, her hair bleach blonde. I took her for a college kid, but she didn’t fit in with the ones I had seen. I was certainly suspicious as she stood up and searched my eyes for something, any indication that I was fine so she could be on her way, never to see me again, to relive her embarrassment on her own time where my frightening gray eyes and sinister scowl would not be present. “Sir?” “I am fine.” It was then that she took me aback. She reached up and pulled the pen out of my coat pocket. She grabbed my hand. I yanked it back from her and looked at her startled. “What are you doing?” I demanded. She still looked frightened and confused. “I am giving you my number, I will pay for the suit to be dry cleaned.” Poor thing, she didn’t know any better I thought, or this is certainly a good act I told myself as I looked around again. Everyone had gone about their own affairs. She reached for my hand again and this time I let her take it, enamored by her beauty and perplexed because I wasn’t really sure why she was beautiful. She scrawled the number on my hand and carefully placed the pen in my pocket. “Again, I’m so sorry!” she reiterated. I merely nodded, as she spun and walked away. She bounced with each step, the blonde ponytail bouncing in unison with her steps. I would call her, but not for my suit, and not today. I had to go home and change before I met with the Barattas, but I certainly didn’t care. In due time, I’d own them, the rights to PTC televised events, and maybe even this girl. I smirked as I trudged back up the hill towards my home.
