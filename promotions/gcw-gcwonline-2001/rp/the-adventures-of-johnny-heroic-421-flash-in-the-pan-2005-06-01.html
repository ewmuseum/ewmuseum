The Adventures of Johnny Heroic #421: Flash in the Pan

1 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/1/05 Times like these where there's only two choices. Get up or stay down. 'Get up, Mr Hero. Get up and let's end this right now.' Stay down, you jackass. Stay down, for all your worth, and just let it end. Accept it. Move on to whatever it moves on to. Disappointment floods me as my whole life doesn't flash before my eyes, but only the last couple minutes do. I pull up to the old gas station on the east end of town, the rough end of town, to fill the Heroic Cycle. ...that's what I call my motorcycle. Because everything I own is called the Heroic Something. Heroic Boots, Heroic Cove, Heroic AirHorn, Heroic Hot Wheels Model of the General Lee...everything. And it's a badass motorcycle, too. So badass that it's the only motorcycle you can find that does ten miles to a gallon. There's more fuel needed to push those pipes of raw, complete awesomeness than most semis. I built it myself one morning. Yeah, I have a motorcycle building power too. 'Johnny Heroic, I'll be damned. What can I get for the city's most presigous hero on this fine day?' 'Lenny...how's the wife?' 'The wife's good. Gone and made a pie for me to give to you. But I er...lost it.' I don't need the incredible power of perception, which I have by the way, to know he ate the fucking pie. I don't care. Nancy's pies taste like shit anyways. And while I have the pretend-Nancy's-pie-doesn't-taste-like-shit power, I'm not a fan of using it. 'What'll it be?' 'Give me the most expensive shit you got, and fill the thing up.' 'You know what's funny?' 'What's funny, Lenny?' 'The most expensive gas that I got is still nowhere near as expensive as the bottles of pop and cans of beer that people got no problem buying.' 'That's fuckin hilarious, Lenny. You should do standup.' 'Eh, go piss yourself wet in the woods. Ey, Heroic...you're like a hero and stuff. Why don't you go over to them freakin oil company owners and give them a couple of reasons to lower the god damned prices for people like you an' me, eh? You're paying a good thirty bucks a day to fill this crack whore you call a motorcycle, and I'm even sick of filling my own car outta my own pumps. I'm rippin myself off here.' I take a few steps away to look at the night air. Gorgeous tonight. Take a look up and I can even see a few of the stars up there, through the city lights and the pollution. There's just a few of the brighter ones, but they're there. Not that I miss the stars all that much. I mean, I could fly up there anytime that I want to. Hell, I could use my abilities to turn my lungs into a filter for the toxins in the air and clean the skies above this city in a few deep breaths if I wanted to. ...yeah, I got that power too. But for all that I try to do to change the world, there's few things I like better than just looking up at the stars. But at the same time, I don't know why I do it. Because it only depresses me. I interrupt Lenny as he's rambling on about OPEC and me destroying economics on a global scale. 'You know what sucks about looking up at stars, Lenny?' Lenny was busy juggling his automated brain between talking about things nobody's going to do and actually filling my gas tank, so it takes him a minute to process what I actually said. He's gotta look over at me to make sure that I was the one that said it. 'What, eh, sucks about lookin up at stars, Heroic? They're pretty and all.' Naive Lenny. Naive everybody. 'Most of the stars we see right now are actually dead. Burnt out. Their fire has been extinguished from the universe. But we don't know that unless we're told it at some point. We think that just because we see something that it exists in the here and now. That those stars are telling my eyes that they are where they look like they are. And they're telling absolutely everybody the same thing. But the fact is that most of those things up there aren't stars. Because if we could trace the light back to their source instantaneously, we wouldn't find a star. We'd find nothing, or a black hole, or something. Not what we'd expect.' '...I don't follow, Heroic.' I don't expect you to, Lenny. I really don't. That's the difference between me and the normal man, I guess. I'm a full time superhero. Not one of those losers with a secret identity who actually works an everyday job, thus to solidify themselves as the everyman that is beside everybody else. I'm Johnny Heroic. That's my name, that's my identity, that's my job, and that's my life. Johnny. Heroic. Nothing. Else. And I feel like I'm one of those stars up there in the sky. Like some day, somebody's going to recognize my brilliance, my light, and my power. But by the time they do, they're gonna come looking for me and I'm going to be gone. I'm mortal. Simple as that. Hell, if the stars in the heavens cannot escape Death, then I, even as a superhero, can't. Or maybe I'm wrong. Maybe it's worse than I thought. Maybe I am dead. The source of what can be seen has been gone for a long time, and all that I am is the representation that lives on. The light, the heat, and the idea remain, but the mass of blazing hot attitude has been gone since before anybody, even I, knew it existed. And as much as I'd like to think about this further, there's a rocket propelled grenade coming from my right- KAAAAAAABLAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAMMMM!!!! Standing there, facing away, I see the flash of light that is the gas station going up in flames. With a simple thought I redirect the heat and the shrapnel around my body so that I'm standing there, now staring at the ground, silhouetted against the fires of a gas station that was blown to hell with metal and plastic and glass and concrete and flames going off behind me like a firework. Like a star in the sky. And within seconds, it too is nothing but nothingness, expended and blown out. And not having moved, a silent mourning escapes my thoughts. It'll be hard to tell Nancy that Lenny got blown to pieces right in front of me today. Well, technically behind me. But she doesn't need to be bothered with semantecs, her husband just became part of a crater. 'Whoever you are, that was a big mistake.' Yeah. I really liked that bike. 'Why don't you come out so that I can make you pay for it.' The mistake that time. Not the bike. It's a few seconds, but I see a figure walking toward me from the darkness that seems to surround this gas station fully. The burning fires are still raging, but they are calming slightly. The star is already staring to burn out. And my super sensative sense of smell detects Fortune before she gets any closer than she needs to be. Her scent is ambrosia, and cannot be mistaken. 'Fortune. You've made your final mistake this time.' She steps into the light so that I can see her and plays her only trump card. If she hadn't just tried to kill me, destroyed my bike, and vaporized Lenny, her sex appeal might work. Wearing her skin tight black bodysuit with slits, holes, and designs cut into it revealing teasing amounts of skin underneath, many men would even forget about the rocket. I see through it. The ruse, not the suit. Though I could see through the suit if I wanted to. I have that power too. 'No! No, Johnny no! You have to believe me! It was a mistake! I went too far this time and I cannot be held accountable. It was only because I love you!' 'You don't even try to act sincere anymore, do you?' 'You wouldn't buy it if I did.' It's no mystery. Fortune knows me and knows me well. 'Your mistake wasn't as much what you hit with the rocket, but who you failed to hit.' I can already feel my fist clenching when I didn't tell it to yet. It's almost like my body knows what to do without me having to tell it, because it's done the same routine so many times. But I put a great deal of control into not losing my senses completely. That's when mistakes are made. But God damn, I really liked that bike. Fortune, meanwhile, looks upward at the stars and puts on her high quality acting voice. 'You know, most of the stars we see right now are dead. Burnt out. If we trace the light back to the source...they're not what we expect.' Her mocking tone is almost too much to top it all off when she looks over at me and puts a gloved finger to her lips teasingly. 'You'll be seeing stars in a minute, you little bitch.' 'Name calling! Now really, Johnny-boy...don't you think we should be past threats and name calling and simply move to-' At a speed faster than thought, I move in. I don't as much want to pummel her to death as I want her to realize how stupid she is. Then I'll beat her senseless. For now I rush up and grab one wrist and hold it against the small of her back, take my other hand and put it right up on her neck, ready to give one good squeeze. She brings her free hand up with surprising speed and instinctively puts it on my hand about to choke her straight to Hell. 'Johnny-boy, now seriously... You're supposed to ask the lady to dance first, not just take it. Though this could be misconstrued as an outright sexual advance.' It's with the most minor of body language that she changes this scene to a dance, a sexual suaree...not at all a man about to kill a woman. Her body presses against mine hotly. Her foot lifts and touches the back of my thigh. Her free hand begins to caress my hand. And she closes her eyes and smiles, only to open them again and be looking at a stone faced Johnny Heroic. Hero twenty-four/seven. I don't take breaks, no matter how turned on I am. 'If you're expecting me to drop my guard, you're extremely-' I eat my words, and almost swallow my tongue, as excruciating pain rips at my backside. Letting go of Fortune wasn't a voluntary action, but it happened anyway. And even though I didn't try to let my guard down, it seems that Fate saw an opening to move in for the attack. I turn and see her. Dressed the same as her twin, but wearing white instead of black. 'Hi Johnny-boy. What? No date for me?' White spots cloud my vision as I look at her. I know Fortune is behind me, and she's going to unleash on me anytime now. I just need to stay on my toes. 'You girls actually think you can take me? That was a sucker shot.' 'Is that so?' I realize as I see her kick coming up that Fortune is doing the same thing behind me. They're going to whale me at the same time with their stilettos. But what's worse is I realize I didn't see it coming soon enough and I can't act fast enough to stop it from happening. I explain my powers all the time, but I'll do it again. To the world, I have the ability to control electromagnetic fields. I can control the very atoms of my body or, with severe concentration, atoms outside my body. Their magnetic pulls and poles, their electrical charges, their electromagnetic strengths, and their very structure. Atoms and molecules are my mind's play things, and I can more or less do whatever I want. The reality of the situation is that I'm the luckiest son of a bitch that ever walked the planet. I don't control atoms. I don't control electromagnetics. I just get fucking lucky every single time I try to do something. Think of it as the cosmic coin always ending up in my favor. I ask the universe if it will speed up my arm and give it an electromagnetic field strong enough to make my punch incredible, and it happens. I ask the universe to slow down my perception of time, and it happens. But I don't actually control anything. I just get lucky every time I ask. Except... 'Your luck has run out-' '-Johnny Heroic.' 'You put up a good fight-' '-considering you're fighting the best without-' '-any powers. But in the end-' '-it's pointless.' Fortune and Fate circle my battered body like a couple of dogs circling fresh meat. They finish each others sentences due to that creepy mind connection that twins have. Freaky. I was always afraid of this day. Most superheroes would consider this the day that their powers 'went away' or 'wore out.' I know the fact. Fact is that I was just wrong. I asked the universe to allow me to beat their asses, and for once it said 'no.' And like the dead star, the one that's burnt out by the time you see it, I had no idea this was how it was. Fortune and Fate lean in close and gently put their hands under my chin, lifting my head up to look at them. And they look like angels. The two most gorgeous faces I've ever seen, side by side. Each one looking at me for a brief second like they actually cared about how I felt. I almost forget that the two of them are the most cold blooded killers I've ever known. 'You're lucky, Johnny-boy-' '-that you aren't wanted dead.' 'Come down to the Plaza East when you-' '-get back up. Bring your powers-' '-if you can find them. If not-' '-you're just...out of luck.' They let my head fall and one of them lands a kick to my ribs. And it all goes black.
