Clearing the mind.

18 July 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 18 The front lawn of the McFarlane Estate was, for all intents and purposes, Seymour Almasy’s training ground. It was free from Cyrus, who rather frightened him, and free from Dawn and Vivica, who didn’t seem to want to associate with him. In the front lawn, he was alone, so he could prepare as he saw fit. Seymour was rarely the sort to put in gym time before his matches. His figure was lean, not bulky, the product of running, jumping, and lots and lots of situps and pushups, rather than any great attraction to the weight room. His workout, instead, was his own. With “Fight With Seymour” from THE BLACK MAGES? album blaring on his iPod, Seymour swung his trusty sword in various patterns. He favored this sort of workout, as it was a: fun, and b: tiring. Swinging a fifteen pound sword around wasn’t the easiest thing in the world, but he was deceptively strong. With every swing of the blade, he thought of his life, and its recent happenings. The mood in the Estate was noticeably somber after Warpath. Shadow and Dawn had both lost their respective championships, and wrestling pundits had begun the predictable predictions of doom for Dies Irae. Seymour chuckled at the thought. Dies Irae was more than a wrestling stable. It was a union of people who cared for one another, about one another. A bond beyond the sport. Well, for everyone except him. For the most part, it didn’t seem like anyone really liked him that much. At that thought, he stopped. Yanking the headphones from his ears, his iPod unceremoniously was thrown to the green grass of the lawn. His sword was treated more respectfully, placed carefully in its scabbard, before Seymour fell to the lawn, stretching himself out on the grass. “The Hell am I even doing here,” he demanded of himself. As far as Dies Irae went, he fit in wrestling just fine. The stable was constituted of the best of the business, and he certainly was among them. A match against the returning Johnny Borealis might have scared most competitors, but Seymour wasn’t afraid. He’d beaten Borealis in his debut match for GCW, after all. The agenda would be simple. Beat into Borealis a healthy respect for Dies Irae. One did NOT cost the group’s leader a title without paying, and Seymour’s new style was designed to make people pay in a brutal and violent fashion. That was easy. Out of the ring, though, he was less confident. Out of the ring, he was the black sheep of Dies Irae, the silly human who liked to listen to RPG music. A quick glance up at the Toronto sky was calming. It was a beautiful day here, even at the epicenter of the force that caused GCW such chaos. That always made Seymour smile; the fact that Dies Irae was so, well, normal. Even though they were vampires, and whatever Dawn was, and whatever Cyrus was, they got along. All except him, really. “Wonder if there’s still lingering discontent from before I joined.” It was a legitimate thought. Before he joined, he had been one of the group’s staunchest foes, the only man truly willing to raise plowshare against them. Of course, he’d had no help’part of the reason he’d turned to begin with. “Or maybe it was Emoticon,” he mused, recalling the black latex clad persona he’d played in Global Wrestling. A persona that, true to life, was rather interested in one Dawn McFarlane. “Why, oh why does she have to have legs that go into next week?” It was a fair question, really, because it was probably the source of contention, at least between him and Dawn. It didn’t help that, even though he was lodged in the basement, he could still hear the collared former World Champion and their leader engaging in special private Dies Irae business at strange hours of the night. A heartfelt “BAH!” came from deep within Seymour’s soul. When this upset, there was only one thing to do: comisserate with those possessing equal misfortune when it came to women. And for that, he needed to go read some harem manga. --------------------------------------------- McFarlane Estate Library The library of the estate was admittedly impressive, to any lover of books. Important tomes stood neatly in vast rows up and down the library, with sturdy high backed chairs and stout wooden tables there for book lovers to read on. This was true of all but one corner of the library. In THIS corner of the library, the shelves were a lot smaller. There was no Milton or Chaucer here, instead, there were entire series of manga and RPG strategy guides. In the very corner itself was a bean bag chair, and plopped in that bean bag chair was Seymour Almasy, reading the fourth volume of “Oh! My Goddess”, with a contented look on his face. Still, as he read the misadventures of Keiichi Morisato and the goddess that he loved, he couldn’t help but feel something to be slightly amiss. As if someone was watching him. In truth, someone was. Every so often, Cyrus, the library caretaker, peeked out above his book of Eastern European genealogy to look at the white-haired man sitting and reading those silly books Lia had asked him to put in the library. He had done so, but with the caveat that they be kept separate from the fine literature. He had no idea what a Final Fantasy was, but was pretty sure they shouldn’t be filed with actual books. Soon enough, Seymour realized that he wasn’t alone. And so, every so often, he peeked out above his manga to look at the seven foot tall, blue-skinned, pointy-eared vampire. Curiosity looking at curiosity. It was only a matter of time before one blinked, and that one was Seymour. “Cyrus, is it,” he asked, re-filing the volume of manga neatly where it belonged, and reluctantly rising from the undeniable comfyness of the bean bag chair. The taller man merely nodded, and went back to his reading. Unfortunately for Cyrus, Seymour wasn’t giving up on this conversation that quickly. The Maester of Misery quickly crossed the library, and took a seat in one of the stuffy chairs, looking at the other man/vampire/thing with obvious interest. “Whatcha reading?” “Eastern European genealogy,” came the slightly terse reply. “I doubt that you would be interested.” Seymour had to concede that point. “Probably not,” he admitted, “but to each their own, right” I like to read my manga. Thanks for making that little section for me. It’d be rather difficult for me if I had to find them all in those big stacks of books.” “You are welcome.” Somehow, Cyrus didn’t feel the need to tell Seymour that he’d segregated those particular “novels” on purpose. Besides, he’d been genuinely thanked. “You enjoy living here,” Seymour asked, eyes now focusing on the unique looking individual. A glance into his eyes was slightly unnerving, as they were all black. No pupil, no iris, simply black. Yet another reminder of how painfully normal he was. “Indeed,” Cyrus said, neutrally. “I cannot live back home anymore for various reasons, so the Estate serves its purpose well.” Seymour thought about that’and supposed it was true for him as well. “I could live back home if I wanted, but my home has too many memories.” The Long Island home he had bought with Aerith was still under his ownership, but for obvious reasons, he didn’t especially want to live there. Too many remembrances of both the joys and atrocities that had occurred in that house. Cyrus felt no need to pry. Really, he didn’t know Seymour. This was the first time that the man had ever spoken to him. Usually, Seymour tried to avoid him, which he understood completely. Most humans found his appearance rather off-putting. “It’s tough sometimes,” he said, looking at the much taller man. “I feel so alone here.” Cyrus nodded. Really, the blue-skinned man rather liked being alone, but understood that others did not. “The denizens of this house are rather private people,” he replied, with a shrug of his shoulders. “Yeah,” he said, with a touch of bitterness, “except with each other.” Cyrus was about to open his mouth to say something, but he realized that Seymour was more or less correct. Lia got along with everyone, Vivica got along at the very least with Lia and Dawn, Dawn with Vivica and Lia. The vampire pondered a thought for a few moments, and almost rejected it before finally speaking. “Have you spoken with Virgil at all,” he asked, leveling a calm stare at Seymour. Virgil. There was a thought that he actually hadn’t had. Ambrosi was the Hardcore Champion of GCW, a man who could mix it up in any environment. Seymour knew that. He’d waged war with the man in Global Wrestling for twenty minutes before nearly accidentally murdering the vampire with fire, setting off Lia’s “Emoticon Must Die” sensors. Conversation with Virgil had been sparse since that day, naturally. But now, they were in Dies Irae together, and one would assume the hatchet, if not buried, was at least off rusting in a corner somewhere. “No,” Seymour admitted, with a shake of his head. “Perhaps you should. You two seem to have more in common than would be readily apparent. Besides, with the exception of myself, you are the only two males in the house.” “Yeah, I know,” Seymour groused. “Means I never get enough time to do my hair, because Vivica’s always hogging it.” Cyrus almost laughed at that. Almost. He managed to keep the stoic look on his face, even as he gestured up to his hair, kept back in a tight ponytail. “Try this,” he said, no humor in his voice. “I have learned to adapt to the, ah, unique situation of living with three women.” Seymour pondered it for a second, before realizing the wisdom in Cyrus? words. “Not a bad idea. Hey, thanks for chatting with me. Going to head out and raid Dawn’s stash of hair ties so I can tie mine back. I’ll leave you to your reading.” “You are welcome,” came the reply, as Seymour turned to leave. Cyrus took a quick glance at the area where Seymour had been, and found it neat and tidy. This was good. It meant he didn’t have to go over and touch those silly books. As the door to the library closed, Cyrus was left alone, reading about Romania. For him, it was the perfect way to spend an afternoon. ------------------------------------------------------------------ So, Mr. Borealis, we meet again. Much has changed since our first one on one meeting. Back then, you were the established GCW star, the company’s United States Champion. Had that belt been on the line back then, I would have been a champion my first night in GCW. But I’m not bitter, Johnny. No, that injustice, looking back, was but the first in a string of them that I suffered. The first in a chain of events that led me into the arms of Dies Irae. And now, you’re making your illustrious return here, and look who you run into again. Your old pal, Seymour. A man who is going to make you regret ever making this mistake, Borealis. You see, Dies Irae exists now. The era of Eva VanLorne and yourself is over. No one cares anymore. This time, there’s no Eva to distract you. No excuse that you can give when all is said and done. Just you and I in the ring. One on one. Both of us with something to prove. I’m sure you want to prove that you’ve still got it. That you can come in off a layoff and pick right back up like you never left. That you can beat a Primetime Central legend in your first match back. I, meanwhile, am going to prove that you are antiquated. Past date. Spoiled. Expired. Any other such synonym you want to use. I, like yourself, have been around for several years. Unlike you, I adapt to changing times. My arsenal has grown and expanded, my philosophy changed. I’ve gotten smarter and stronger, while you have gotten weaker and softer. My sights are firmly set on the GCW World Championship, the one big prize that I’ve never gotten a shot at. And any individual in the way of that prize will be dealt with accordingly. You’re not the sort to go Ascension. For that, I give you credit. You’re too selfish to prostrate yourself for a cause. I, on the other hand, am not. I have sat back and waited while Dawn was our standard bearer. And now that LeStatt Knight has managed to become a fluke champion, it is my turn. For soon, I will wave the banner for Dies Irae and this company as its world champion. I will stand for honor. I will stand for pride. And I will stand for the elimination of men such as yourself from this company. Unfortunately for you, you will not be there to see my ultimate triumph. You, instead, will watch on television, either from a hospital bed or your home. Because, either through simple defeat or the shattering of bones shall you realize the truth of my final words. It’s over, Johnny. IT’S OVER!
