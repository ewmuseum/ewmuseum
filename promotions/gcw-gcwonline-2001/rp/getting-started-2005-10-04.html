Getting Started

4 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 4, 2005 After all of the time we have spent in mom and pop backyard leagues over the last few years, it’s nice to finally be able to say we are moving up into greener pastures. We’ve dwelled so long on just making ourselves known to the locals, that we forgot what our original plans were. We pushed aside our goals of the future, to shine in the limelight of today. But now? Now it’s time to hit the big leagues. Of course, I’m getting a bit ahead of myself. More than anything else, introductions are definitely in order. My name is John Cantellop, and I am the agent of Jayce Bradley. I also handle all of the narration for these promos that seem to be all the rage as of late. Something that, up until now, has not been very necessary in Jayce’s career. Who really wanted to know how Jayce spent his day? But like I said, we have moved beyond wrestling for peanuts in an elementary school playground. Now it is time to see what my boy can really do. Can he achieve the goals he set for himself a few years ago, or will he fall face first like a toddler trying to take his first few steps. The future is there for him to chase after like a storm chaser in Florida, but only time can really tell how prepared he really is. This first match--his debut match--is up against a man that is no stranger to the sport. Apparently he has wrestled a bit and made a name for himself in Canada. A nice country I’m sure, but how many people do you know actually pay attention to anything that happens on that side of the border? I know Jayce doesn’t. I don’t. To be honest, I don’t even think many Canadians follow what has been going on in their country. The only thing on their minds right now, is the return of their native sport. Hockey. It’s like the natives have gone mad. With baseball, football, basketball, and Texas Hold “Em Poker going on, I don’t think many people in the States even noticed that there was no hockey being played. That’s how important that is to us down here. But I’m straying away from the topic at hand, because this Frost guy doesn’t even play Hockey. He’s not even Canadian. He’s some Irish dude with a European accent. Weird, I know, but it’s not like you can choose your accent. Not to mention, you would think he’d get a bit of a Canuck accent’him growing up there and all after leaving Reykjavik. But I digress. Not to mention, that we are about ready to get under way. Jayce has been over in the corner of the studio, mulling over the right words to say. He doesn’t want to seem inexperienced in his promotional debut, and I didn’t want him to sound like an idiot using big words either. When we were driving over, I turned to him and told him that he needed to be relaxed, act normal and let the words flow freely from his mind. The last thing we wanted was for this thing to sound rehearsed, or forced. These little snippets need to come from the heart, but you need to have a little bit of knowledge on the subject as well. Jayce managed to nod in my direction, signaling that he was ready to get started. I could see the nervous look on his face, and after spending as much time together as we have, I noticed that his fingers kept fidgeting. I still remember the first time I saw him doing that, too. He had this match against some boxer, who happened to be talking a lot of crap about Jayce the whole week. He kept telling everyone how Jayce was too small to hurt him, and that he was going to prove to Jayce that he was nothing more than a lucky son of a bitch. Jayce tried to laugh it off, he kept telling me that the guy was just trying to intimidate him and that it would not work. But the closer it got to match time, the more his fingers would fidget. Of course, he will deny it every time. No one ever notices their own tells, which is why everyone has them. If you could notice them, you would try your damnedest to keep from doing them. Take Frost for example. On the outside, he acted calm and collected in his promotional piece. But did anyone else notice that a simple drop of water was driving him insane, to the point where his friend had to come over and settle him down. It was really simple for Frost to place the blame on that leaky pipe, but who is to say that Frost did not use that leak to conceal his insecurities. When Jayce and I watched the promo, one of the first things he wanted to comment on was the fact that Frost was being a tad bit over-confident. While I am no expert in this field, I watched quite a bit of these things as I geared up to help Jayce. I noticed these things called traps, where someone would act a certain way to get an argument started over what they wanted to debate. I felt this was one of them, so I told my friend to forget about it. I told him not to follow in Frost’s footsteps, that he needed to strike his own chord and’okay, I didn’t say all that. I just told him to do what felt natural and go with it, without focusing too much on what Frost said. And here we are. Jayce is mic’ed up and ready to go. I give him the thumbs up and we’re off. Wish us luck. ---- It’s A Start. ---- I’d like to welcome you all to Jayce Bradley’s promotional debut. There’s nothing fancy to see here. It’s just Jayce sitting on a stool, in front of a white Gee See Dubya backdrop. Even his clothes are pretty plain. A pair of khaki shorts, a white short sleeved tee shirt, and some black flip flops’pretty basic, unimpressive stuff. But then again, none of you are really here for the scenery’are you? “I’ve gone’Global.” I told him it was a cheesy way to start his promo, but he figured he could get a few laughs so he went with it. Which is not a bad thing, I did tell him to do what felt natural after all. But yeah, I would have preferred something a bit more serious for an opening line. “It seems like just a few weeks ago I was slamming some punk onto some trash can lids, and now here I am about to debut for an honest to God, real wrestling federation. Seriously, I almost creamed myself I was so happy.” Now that I think of it, I should have given him a script. “I wonder how many of the wrestlers on the roster felt the way I felt” Could someone like Lestatt Knight get so happy that he creamed himself? Probably not. The guy is so cold and calculating, he probably knew as soon as he walked through the doors that he was signed.” It felt weird to stand behind the camera and watch Jayce look and talk like an idiot in front of what could turn out to be thousands of Gee See Dubya fans that will end up watching this. But hey, he needs to get his cherry popped sometime, right. “I wish I knew what it was like to feel like Knight. A champion. Someone so full of confidence that it seeps through his pores like sweat on a hot summer’s day.” Man, that was a good use of a metaphor. Hopefully someone puts that on their Jayce Bradley fan site. If he ever gets one, of course. “That’s just not me though. Sure, I have a cocky, arrogant side when I win. It’s just this part of me that seems to break down and question myself when I lose, that I have a problem with. I’m what you would call a sore loser. I don’t like to lose, but it is a nature of the game. No one can beat everyone, and those select few that can’well, they go on to bigger and better things than just mid-carding an event.” I almost wanted to go out there and pat him on the back, he was doing so good. Of course, he could have done without the sore loser comment’someone’s going to play that fiddle until the strings bust. “Can I ever be that good?” I would answer him, but it was more of a rhetorical question. Besides, I would have missed him rubbing his chin with his right hand if I did. He leaned to the side as he did, and went a bit out of frame. “Only time will tell. In the meantime, I plan on doing my best inside of that ring. If nothing else, I expect everyone to know that I am trying my damnedest to make an impression. Even if I end up sucking harder than a whore on a cold winter night.” That was harsh, hopefully no one gets offended. “Because anyone can say that they are going to do something, it’s backing it up inside of that squared center when it actually matters. Much like my match this week. I’m facing My Cavity Frost, and he is already talking about being the future of this company.” Man, I really need him to memorize these name better. That or I need to make sure I write it down for him, I could have sworn I said the name right. Macavity’No, I understand why he made the mistake, it’s awful close to the same thing. “Comparing himself to the likes of some guy named Octavian, and saying that I should give one hundred percent in my loss to him.” I think he is calming down. Right now when he shrugged his shoulders, it seemed as if his fingers were not fidgeting as much as earlier. “My loss. Well, at least I’m not the one sounding like a cocky prick. My manager says I do that a lot, but he likes to over exaggerate.” No, it’s the truth. When he wants to be, Jayce is a cocky little prick. Mark my words, you’ll see it soon enough. Right now, he’s just trying to break the ice’fit in, if you will. “Well, Frost, I hope you win this week. I hope you walk out of that ring with your head held high after getting your arm raised in victory, because if you don’t’no one is ever going to believe anything you have to say from here on out. And while I want to win this match so badly, I’d really hate for you to look like an ass on National Television.” I need to make sure this thing is going to be on National Television. If it’s not, Jayce is the one going to be looking like an ass. I don’t want that to happen. “I guess I’ll be seeing you soon, Mr. Frost.” I had to pan back a little bit, making sure I got a good upper body shot for when he waves at the camera. And there it is. “See ya later, Gee See Double You.” And with that we’Cut. I couldn’t believe it. We had finally finished. Jayce motioned that he was going to grab something to drink, but the only thing on my mind is how I was going to have him prepare for this match. How was I going to make sure that his first match’wasn’t going to be marred in failure. The pressure was on, and time was running out. Somehow, I need to make sure that Jayce will be ready for a match of this magnitude. Which means I need to get to work? Like. Right. Now.
