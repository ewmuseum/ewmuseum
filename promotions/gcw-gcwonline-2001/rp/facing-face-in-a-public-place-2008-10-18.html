Facing Face in a Public Place

18 October 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/18/2008 The sky bleeds red, pink and orange as a woman dressed in a black and white dress suit walks down a pedestrian-filled sidewalk. Cars frantically jockey for position a meter away. The woman has quick feet as she too jockeys for position, showing masterful balance and form for someone wearing heels. In her left arm is a clipboard with a pen dangling from it, swinging to and fro as the woman steps left, right and forward. In no time at all, the woman reaches her destination: a local Mom & Pop restaurant called “Jim & Jane’s.” The woman opens the door, a jingle accompanying her as she steps inside the restaurant. She takes a look around, the lights illuminating her long blonde hair, deep blue eyes and cherry red lipstick. She’s “quite the looker,” as some would say. Others would just call her plain “hot.” Despite her beauty, this woman is clearly all business. She quickly finds her latest scoop sitting in a booth in the far corner. A few men at a nearby table catch a look of her as she heads over to the booth. She rolls her eyes in disgust at the goggling, and quickly takes a seat opposite a man who could not be farther from her in terms of appearance. He wears a ragged suit with holes in it, not one part of the suit matching another. His features are etched in stone ? or, rather, some sort of highly durable plastic ? and they certainly aren’t nearly as pleasant to look at as those of the woman sitting across the table. In fact, the man, who is known simply as Face, looks downright creepy. The two look at one another for a few moments, each one studying the other’s appearance. The woman takes some pleasure in the fact that Face is focusing on her face, rather than her chest, like all the other men she interviews seem to do. After the woman takes in what she can from appearance alone, which is a lot, seeing as how Face’s attire is as unique as a thumbprint, she places the clipboard she was carrying on the table in front of her and opens it up to reveal a stack of fresh lined paper. She removes the pen from its dangling string and clicks it open on the table as Face takes out his lighter and turns his attention toward it. Flick. Fwoosh. Crack. The sudden sounds of Face opening and closing his lighter cause the woman looks up from her clipboard, just staring blankly at Face as he flicks his lighter open and closed once more. Flick. Fwoosh. Crack. Woman: What are you doing? Face turns his attention away from his lighter and towards the woman, but continues to flick his lighter on and off like clockwork. His voice is just as harsh and raspy as always as he gives his reply. Face: Waiting for you to ask me a question. I’m assuming, Ms. Hunter, that you will ask me questions...being that you are a reporter and all. The woman now identified as Ms. Hunter rolls her eyes in a very similar fashion to when she was goggled at by the men a few tables down. Ms. Hunter: I meant with your lighter. Face’s lighter opens and closes once more. Face: Well, Ms. Hunter...it appears that I am opening and closing it at a certain interval. The obvious answer reeks of sarcasm and Ms. Hunter doesn’t like it one bit. She opens her mouth to retort, but Face leans forward and cuts her off. Face: So, why didn’t you accept my invitation to that nice place just down the road? Hmmm? An eyebrow is raised on Ms. Hunter’s face. Ms. Hunter: What, you mean that old, abandoned building? I’m sorry, but I’m not an idiot, Mr. Spencer. I’ve- Face: It’s Face. Ms. Hunter: Whatever. My point is, Mr...Face, is that I’m not just going to meet a shady-looking masked man, for the first time, in an abandoned house where no one can immediately get to me if trouble arises. I’m not some dumb broad. Face studies the reporter for another few moments before leaning back into the booth’s cushiony seat. Face: Interesting. The last person I met of your...kind...was not quite as smart. Ms. Hunter, misinterpreting the comment, takes offense to that and snaps back at Face. Ms. Hunter: What’s that supposed to mean: my kind? This sudden outburst, while not very loud, grabs the attention of a nearby waitress. The waitress walks over to the booth, attempting to eliminate any chance of an escalating argument happening in the restaurant. Despite her tiny frame, she has an aura of authority about her, obviously having dealt with these sorts of situations before. However, when she reaches the booth and sees Face sitting there, flicking his lighter open and closed, she is at a loss for words and that aura of authority turns into the timid nature you’d expect at first glance. The waitress manages to catch herself eventually, though, and speaks as if her word is law. Waitress: Folks, I’m going to have to ask you not to raise your voices in this restaurant...and, sir, I’m going to have to ask you to put that lighter away. Ms. Hunter nods and apologizes, but Face just turns to look at the waitress. His permanent fiery grin is not a welcoming one and the waitress is clearly creeped out, but she tries to hide it. Face once again flicks his lighter open and closed, somewhat more deliberately than usual. Face: We’re fine. We don’t want any food or drink. Now, go run along to that table over there. Face points to a table where a little boy has a pouty face and his arms crossed as his mother asks for him to eat his soup. The waitress looks over and frowns, knowing the kid could blow any minute now. Face: His mother wants to try and get him to eat healthy because she’s bought into the crap the bottom-feeding scum of the media are constantly throwing at her...But, she doesn’t realize that the kid’s just going to whine and complain because his mother and father used to let him have, quote, unquote, less healthy foods. He was happy before, but ever since his daddy died of a heart attack from high cholesterol, his mother has been trying to push a diet of greens on him. The two women just stare blankly at Face as he continues. Face: The death was hard enough, but now another cog has been added to the ever-growing machine that will eventually pump out teenage angst. The mother and her son will become more and more distant, despite the fact that they both, deep down, love one another. Unfortunately, people are too damn stubborn, so neither will show a sign of weakness and they’ll eventually get into an argument, resulting in the boy becoming a druggie and dropping out of high school. He’ll run away and have no friends and he’ll be some anti-society anarchist, whose protest will consist of wasting what little money he can get to maintain a comatose state. He’ll rot away his insides his whole miserable life until he dies, ironically, of heart failure at the age of 37...alone, depressed and full of hatred... The mouths of both Ms. Hunter and the waitress simply hang in disbelief, their eyes wide open and centered on Face, who spoke of this horrible fate in the most casual tone possible. What’s more, he didn’t skip a beat with his lighter the whole time. After a few more moments of shock-induced silence from the two women, Face casually adds in another suggestion. Face: He’ll want a grilled cheese sandwich...with ketchup on the side. The waitress eventually snaps out of her stunned state and turns to the boy, then to Face, then to the boy again. Without a word, she walks over towards the boy’s table. Ms. Hunter searches for words and, after a few attempts, finally manages to speak her mind. Ms. Hunter How the...Hell...What...What was that!” How could you possibly come up with such a horrible story? Face: Story? No, Ms. Hunter; that was a tale of reality... Ms. Hunter: But...how would you even- Face: A combination of observation and knowledge, Ms. Hunter...things most people lack these days. The details of the tale may turn out to be different, but there are always signs of general, and even specific, behaviour within people. Their body language; their expressions; their attitude; their reactions...these are all pieces of the puzzle. It takes a highly developed mind to put them together, though, so don’t feel so bad for your mental inadequacy to do so... Still utterly confused and lost, Ms. Hunter points over to the table with the mother and her little boy. Ms. Hunter: So, in the time that we’ve been here, all you’ve been doing is studying the kid and his mother from across the room? A short cackle is let out from beneath Face’s mask. Face: Not just them, Ms. Hunter. For instance, it’s glaringly obvious that you have a problem with men drooling over you. Of course, being a hypocrite, like that the majority of society is, you strive to be a typical blond, blue-eyed fantasy Barbie because, apparently, that makes you a stronger woman... The hue of Ms. Hunter’s face becomes redder as anger grows within her, but Face casually continues. Face: But, you didn’t always have such intentions with your look....You were one of the first girls to develop a chest and you used it to your advantage all throughout high school. You sought the attention of boys and got a kick out of leading them on, only to crush their hearts into itty-bitty pieces when they thought they were close. Unfortunately, when you went to college to get that nice writing degree of yours, you found your strategy backfired. You drew the attention of hopelessly drunk and obnoxious frat boys, who treated you like the whore you were pretending to be all these years. The hue of Ms. Hunter’s face remains the same red as it was before, but now it’s more out of embarrassment than anger. Of course, Face is not done yet. Face: After getting banged more times that a villain in a Batman cartoon, you decided you hated men who even glanced at you in what you considered a sexual way. Of course, you couldn’t drop your image; No...Heaven forbid you looked like an average female...or, rather, what used to be an average female. So, you continued to dye your hair blonde and wear blue contacts, thinking you would continue to trick everyone into thinking you were “naturally” like that. Ms. Hunter raises a hand up towards her eyes and hair. At this point, she is in pure shock and dismay. As hard as she tries, she can’t find any rebuttal, or an explanation. Face: Ah, so I’m quite right about you, aren’t I, Ms. Hunter? Hmmm? Well, you’re veil was not so thick. Of course, most people’s aren’t. Some are better at hiding details than others, but there is always a chink in the armour. It just takes a keen eye to see it... In one relatively short conversation with Face, Ms. Hunter has gone from strong and focused to timid and lost. Face chuckles dryly under his breath. Face: You see...Ms. Hunter, it is obvious that I am no ordinary man. However, most people see me and think I’m some bum off the street. They think I’m crazy and that I just ramble on about nothing. They fail to acknowledge any truth in my words. But, in the back of their heads, they can’t help but think about what I’ve said. They can’t help but see that my “ramblings” are, in fact, truths. On the outside, they continue to deny it all; but, on the inside...it slowly gnaws away at them. The free hand of Face begins to claw and grasp at the air as Face stares directly into it. Face: It gnaws and it gnaws until there’s a big enough hole ? and it doesn’t have to be very big at all. And that hole is where I strike. I break them...from the inside out, Ms. Hunter. You can see it now with Mr. Danger, can’t you? Hmmm? He will never admit it out loud, but he knows now...He knows that, deep down, he really doesn’t have what it takes to accomplish anything in GCW. Danger knows that this is all just an act of stubborn pride; an attempt to make his life worth a cent. He is tricking himself into thinking otherwise, but it has been gnawing away at him this whole time. Danger has left himself wide open, but he continues to push forward with his foolish pride. He is playing right into my hand, Ms. Hunter...and it will cost him dearly. Face’s lighter suddenly cracks closed harder than usual, startling Ms. Hunter, who has been in a trance-like state this whole time. However, she finally breaks her silence, now searching for answers from this masked man in front of her. Ms. Hunter: So, let me get this straight...You target people’s weaknesses and then you break them down, psychologically...and then you take advantage of that and beat them down, physically. Face looks up as if he has to think about the answer to that question, but it’s obvious that he doesn’t. Face: Yes, Ms. Hunter. That’s exactly what I do, although, I don’t physically beat down everyone. Playing with people’s minds is enjoying enough on its own. Ms. Hunter: So you enjoy all this? You actually enjoy watching people suffer needlessly? Face’s head tilts at an inquisitive angle. Face: Surely, Ms. Hunter...the answer is obvious at this point. Of course I enjoy it. In fact, I enjoy nothing more than seeing people squirm like ants under a magnifying glass. I enjoy having people come at me with blind arrogance, only to walk away hollow and crushed. You see, there are far too many people in the world these days whose egos far exceed their actual abilities. If anything, I’m doing the world a favour by bringing them crashing down to Earth again... The sheer cruelty of Face really begins to show and Ms. Hunter is left shaking her head in bewilderment. She raises her voice slightly, garnering the attention of the waitress once more. However, the waitress decides to let it go after what happened last time she came over. Ms. Hunter: But...you can’t just ruin people’s lives like that! You can’t expect everyone to be perfect. Hell, you’re certainly not perfect! Surprisingly, Face laughs at that last comment. In fact, he exaggerates the hilarity of it by slamming the table a few times with his free hand as he does. Then, he suddenly stops, staring directly into the eyes of Ms. Hunter. His voice deepens slightly and his tone becomes much more serious. Face: Don’t lecture me, Ms. Hunter, on the imperfections of humans. I know full well that no one, including myself, is perfect. But, that doesn’t change the fact that I can find imperfections within everyone I cross and brutally take them down for them. Everyone is more than welcome to try the same thing with me. The problem is, when people do that, they step into my world. They open themselves up for an even more brutal attack. And yes, I can and will ruin people’s lives. The best part about being in GCW is that I don’t have to stop at breaking people mentally. For instance...if I was to break your arm right now... Ghostly is not white enough to describe the colour of Ms. Hunter’s skin as Face suddenly juts forward, his face stopping inches away from Ms. Hunter’s. He sits back down shortly afterwards, but Ms. Hunter is still frozen in fear. Face: But, I can’t. I’ll get sued, or go to jail. However, in a wrestling match, I can beat someone to a bloody pulp if I wanted to. Hell, I shattered some guy’s elbow into a million pieces and the only repercussions from that was that I got a chance to crush the hopes and dreams of Lia Ambrosi at Dangerous Games. And look at what I just got Danger to agree to: A no disqualification match! He’s an old man with bum knees and he agreed to enter a match with absolutely no rules with me. That, Ms. Hunter, is why I love this sport. Everyone in it is full of themselves and they are all willing to go to whatever lengths to prove their worth. And I’m allowed to mentally and physically beat them down over and over again until they have nothing left. As long as I don’t continue to simply injure everyone like I did with Danger and Stevens, I can keep doing this for as long as I want. In fact, it’s encouraged in this sport. Ms. Hunter: So that’s the only reason you’re in wrestling? To be able to physically hurt people after you mentally abuse them? Face: Exactly...Ms. Hunter. Everyone else is out there to prove they are the best. I’m there to prove that they’re no better than all the rest. Ms. Hunter has finally had enough of this and gets up from the booth, taking her clipboard and pen with her. She begins to awkwardly walk away, her heels clicking loudly as she does. Face doesn’t bother going after her or even saying anything, content with what damage he’s already done. The bell above the door rings as Ms. Hunter leaves Jim & Jane’s, forever changed by this encounter. After a few moments, Face gets up out of the booth as well. He walks towards the door, his lighter flicking on and off continuously as he does. A feeling of immense satisfaction courses through his veins, but his mask allows no emotion. Before he exits the restaurant, he looks over to the table with the mother and her son. Sure enough, the boy is eating grilled cheese with a side of ketchup.
