The Change: Gerard Wilkins

30 November 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/30/2007 Gerard Wilkins doesn’t have time for a lot of hobbies or interests. Being the junior editor of Louisiana Wrestling, a magazine that updates all of the local talent, he spends most of his day writing articles, reviewing articles, and placing those articles in the proper format. By the time his day is done, Gerard tries his best not to think about wrestling; although it’s one of his favorite things in the world, sometimes there can be such a thing as too much of a good thing. So Gerard slings his briefcase into his 1994 Ford Taurus, drives three miles to a coffee shop, and fully intends to spend a good hour reading the paper and not concentrating on anything having to do with wrestling. Things don’t always work out that way, though. Gerard looks up suddenly at the noise of the bell above the door clinging more urgently than usual, and the fervor of the door opening is matched with a loud slam. Jumping in his seat a little, it takes Gerard no more than a second to recognize the newest entry in the Espresso Yourself Coffeehouse. “Foster Nackedy,” he says, a smile forming his face. But the smile is quickly erased when Foster gives him a sour look, measuring him, then shakes his head and continues to walk to the counter. Gerard isn’t severely hurt by this reaction, because as a member of the media he is used to being viewed in an unfavorable manner. But he does find it surprising, seeing as Gerard had conducted a very friendly interview with him a few months before. Scanning his memory (he’s a good memorizer, and the article was only five hundred words), Gerard tries to remember if there was any indication that Foster could be this angry and rude? BACK IN THE GAME How Arabi product Foster Nackedy came out of a three year retirement to take the PTC community by storm in the recent Chris Williams Memorial Cup It doesn’t take long when talking to Foster Nackedy to realize he is more than a wrestler. He constantly mentions the conflicts he has with living the average wrestler life in a big company (as he was part of the National Wrestling Council from 2001 until its closing in 2004) and being a normal person, living with his wife Magen and four-year-old son Connor. He’s not shy about his domestic issues; in fact, it’s one of the first things he says to me when we start the interview. “It sucks that Magen and I have had so many splits, so many offs and ons, because we are truly a couple that loves each other and would do anything for each other. The problem is, there’s a fine line between Foster Nackedy the wrestler and Foster Nackedy the man. Foster Nackedy the wrestler has one goal, and that is to overcome any distraction and be the best. Obviously, some of those distractions come from Foster Nackedy the man. So you see the conflict.” The conflict wouldn’t be so big if Foster hadn’t come back in such a big way. After a few failed comebacks in smaller federations, Foster applied for the Chris Williams Memorial Cup and took the world by storm, topping Dusk, James von Doom, Tsunami Oni, and former PTC Global Champion Kimbusa before falling in the final rounds to Digital Mortality and the Illustrious Face-Eater (the eventual tournament winner). Even though his ring performances made everything look easy, Foster says that outside of the ring it was anything but. “I had fights every day while in that tournament: with my friends, with myself, and especially with my wife. We actually split up over my applying to the tournament. We were able to work it out, but none of it was easy.” When I ask him if everything is fine now at home, he says, “Absolutely. We’ve reached a new understanding and we’re stronger than ever.” With his showing in the CWMC, so are the chances that he will join a PTC fed. The only question is, which one? Foster seems hesitant about that question, so I steer the conversation back to his personal life. He seems excited in general, but when his son comes up, a smile lights his face. “He’s my world. He is everything. If I have both wrestling and Connor in my life, I don’t need anything else.” Hopefully for him and his fans, this union of personal and professional Fosters will make some major federation very happy. Gerard found out a few days ago that Foster signed a contract to GCW. He assumed that Foster Nackedy would look happy in light of this news. He is mistaken. As Gerard wonders what could have changed Foster’s demeanor so much in such a short period of time, the answer walks through the door after him. Magen. Her small stature emphasized by the big overcoat she is wearing, Foster’s wife slides into the room and sidles up to Foster, whispering up to him. Foster shakes his head and says something back, but Gerard can’t quite hear what he said. After a few moments, though, the couple heads towards a table nearby Gerard, and he lifts his paper to his eyes and perks up his ears. “You can’t just run, Foster. You can’t pack up and leave now. Things were going so?” he hears Magen say, and Gerard can hear the pain in her voice. Foster’s voice, however, is rough and uncaring. “I’m not running,” comes Foster’s reply, sounding hollow and low. Despite its lack of strength, it still takes Gerard by surprise. He remembers Foster’s voice as warm and, while being a deep baritone, lively and almost chipper. None of that is coming through now. “Yes, you are!” says Magen. “You have suddenly gotten cold and distant again, almost immediately after everything started working. It happens like this every time, Foster. We reach an agreement, we are happy, things start going good, and you suddenly get scared. You run. And you don’t have to keep doing it!” Gerard risks lowering his paper, and when he does he sees Foster, whom he expected to look irate. Instead, he is shocked to see that Foster is merely staring at his wife with no discernible emotion on his face. This actually scares Gerard more than if he were angry. “No, you misunderstand. I’m not running, because I’m not going anywhere.” Magen’s face contorts, eyeing her husband with raised eyebrows. “What are you talking abo?” “I want you out of the house. You can go back now, pack up your things, and move in with your mother. I’m not running anywhere, but you better be by the time I come home.” Gerard stares at the scene in horror as Magen Nackedy cannot respond, moisture forming at the corner of her eyes. “Foster, I don’t?” “Just go, okay” Just get out of here.” Again, Gerard notices that there is no anger coming from his voice. No emotion at all. It seems as if his absence of emotion elicits a rage of it in Magen. “Fine, you asshole, but if I go, Connor is coming with me. You hear me” If you don’t see me, you don’t see him.” Gerard takes a moment to reflect the odd nature of celebrity: even though he has never had a true conversation with either of these people, he completely understands the conversation. Magen has used their son Connor as a weapon in every argument they’ve ever had. It has always been the way of getting Foster to come back to Earth, to once again put the family first? “Fine,” he says. But not anymore. Gerard nearly drops the paper in surprise as Magen Nackedy looks at her husband; with confusion, with anger, with tears. “Foster?” “Go.” Magen takes a long second before leaving the coffee shop, likely heading home to make a call to a friend, and then packing her bags. Gerard stares at the local legend for a few moments, contemplating how someone who had looked so happy a few months earlier could somehow drive his wife to tears. It isn’t until Foster Nackedy clears his throat angrily that Gerard realizes he had been caught staring. “You got a problem, old man?” Gerard quickly shakes his head, then stands up and throws his coffee away, deciding to go home and perhaps watch some TV instead. Anything but wrestling, though. Between work and watching how wrestling has obviously changed what was a decent man, Gerard Wilkins just wants to ignore it for awhile.
