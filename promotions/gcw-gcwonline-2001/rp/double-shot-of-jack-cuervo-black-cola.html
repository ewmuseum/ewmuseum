Date: Written 6/15/09 Fame is a vapor, popularity an accident, riches take wings. Only one thing endures, and that is character. - Horace Greeley ---- I. Deception. Who could have ever thought that in a single word, one could sum up the entirety of all problems ever to plague the earth? Consider history for a moment and some of its greatest occurrences - when Brutus stabbed Caesar, when Hitler captured control of Germany, or when Ginger left the Spice Girls. Each incident had an underlying theme of deception; a commonality of trickery amongst the millions of people who believed in them. Deception is not accidental. Deception doesn’t just happen coincidentally and gets dubbed as something negative. Deception is real, precise, and well-planned. Deception is conceived within the minds of individuals who hold no consideration for the well-being of others. Deception is derived from the egos of the hungry - the power hungry. And in such a case...deception was death. When I died, the world around me crumbled. Superstars mourned and fans scrambled in panic. Since December of two thousand and seven, there has been constant ramble about being able to silence the Human Hype Machine and in one night, it was achieved. Chris Bagwell was dead. The outspoken, misbehaved Rajah of Ratings was to speak his last word. Yet nobody could handle it. As much as people wanted me dead - they needed me alive. You needed me alive. Why do you think criminals exist? Why aren't those sentenced to life in jail just taken into a back alley and their heads blown off with a shotgun? Why aren't prostitutes stabbed to death or rapists neutered? Crime is an easy thing to solve in life. Kill the bad, reward the good. Stop producing weapons that are available to the public and start instilling punishments on people that actually mean something - that actually leave an impact on people for life. The theory is simple - but why don't we do it? It's because we thrive on evil. Americans, Europeans, Asians, Africans, Australians, Eskimos, Monkeys, Gerbils, and Shamans - you name it. Every nationality and every heritage preys on evil. Better yet, they pray on evil. Because without evil, there cannot be good. And that's why I did what I did. I deceived the entire world by faking my death in order to maintain the balance of life. I was the evil to your good. I was the yang to their yin. I was the devil...to our God. ---- II. "God, I just want to fuck the shit out of you..." My lips were pressed against her ear and my words slipped softly through them. Her hair smelt soothing, like a field of strawberries as it pillowed against my face. Her body fit into mine like the last two pieces of an intricate puzzle. The lights were dim and room was glowing only by candle. The mood was right - but my hormones wrong. "You really are a charmer," she reminded me with an obvious hint of sarcasm. "You know that?" I brushed my hand down her side, appreciating every curve in her well-kept figure; the cushion of her breast to the firmness of her ass. I wanted one thing and one thing alone and I could feel it stirring up inside me. "We both know that isn't true," I whispered back. "I can be romantic when the mood strikes me." There was a momentary silence. "And what would you call the mood striking you now," she challenged as I moved my hand from her ass to her thigh. "Is this your romance?" I laughed. I had to, I couldn't help it. "That was a mood killer," I added. But she was quick to reply, “…because telling me you want to fuck the shit out of me is such a turn on." She pinched my hand in joking manner before rolling over to stare me in the face. I kissed my lips to the tip of her nose and gave her a smirk. "I was nearly dead, you know. I'm just trying to savor life - God gave me a second chance." "Yeah!" She chuckled with enthusiasm. "He gave you a second chance because he didn't want you to die hard." She was witty and she was "Clever. Real clever." Although she never actually said it, I always had this feeling that Rebecca wasn't overly thrilled with what I'd done. She enjoyed the person I played on television, but mostly because it was damn near a reflection of my actual personality. And although she hadn't any real concern about the feelings of the fans or the other guys backstage, she did seem partly perturbed that I'd keep even my friends in the shadows. It wasn't like me to throw my friends out there like that - particularly Zacharia Taylor. She definitely tried a number of times to hint that I should fill him in but I knew I could count on his real emotions to sell the whole thing. Everyone knows he's an emotional roller coaster and if he didn't get really worked up, I don't think anyone would've bought it. Shit - look at how well it went. I definitely got a rise out of it. Not necessarily the same rise I was working on now, but you get the idea. "Hey, all I'm saying is that you shouldn't isolate yourself from every friend you have. You never know when you're going to need one..." she went on, proving my point. "--I knnnoowww you're self-sufficient but it never hurts to have someone to lean on." I'm not exactly sure how we shifted from dying hard into having somebody to lean on. I felt trapped inside a corny, overplayed fifties ballad. Naturally, there was only one way to handle the whole situation. "I already have somebody to lean on," I said as I kissed her nose. "...and in our case, even someone to lean in." My head tilted and lips slid down her face to meet hers. ---- III. They fuckin' hate me... It isn't DaVinci's Code nor a thirteen second solution to a Rubix cube. It's not a derivative of some sum of a quadratic equation or an optimization algorithm for the best case of 'Big O.' It's simpler than that - much simpler. They fuckin' hate me. I never really took into account the impact I made on people's lives. I mean, I've always known I was making a statement - but I was never really sure what I was saying. It's always been a matter of tweaking the right emotions with me; like choosing the right cologne. You never wear a heavy scent in a bright setting - it just gives off all the wrong vibes. You're not really sure why it does, or even how it does for that matter. All you know is that it does - and it just doesn't work. Just think about it. We've all done it. And I tend to think that's how I've lived out most of my career. With over ten years lacing my boots, I still can't give you a solid motive or even an inkling of an understanding about the mind frame I leave people in. I'd even so much as be willing to spend an entire afternoon with The Mighty-Mighty Bosstones so they could tell me about the 'Impressions That [They] Get.' All this time, I've been a blind man walking around...without a seeing-eye dog. Of course I've heard them all boo. I've listened to the criticisms by other industry professionals and my apparent 'colleagues.' But you never truly know when someone's being honest or if they're still stuck in their imaginary persona spewing stupid catchphrases or blowing lines of bullshit. And perhaps that's been my problem all along. Either I just haven't cared enough to react or I just haven't considered the possibility that these people aren't just talking out of their asses. But today - today is a different day. Today I've gotten my vision back. You really start to take things into perspective when you're told that a single additional failure is going to cost you your job. Every accolade and achievement is worth nothing - absolutely nothing - should I decide to take a three second break on my back in Madison Square Garden. And just like that, over ten years of my life will have equated to... Nothing. It isn't really my accolades that leave me in a rut, though. My ego is strong enough to fuel the fire behind those for centuries to come. What really concerns me is the darkness that lies beyond a career with this very company, because if I were to leave...I'd be leaving more than just the company - I'd be leaving the industry. What other option would I even have? ---- IV. "So have-ya given an ounce of thought to the 'what ifs'?" It'd been years since I sat down around a coffee table with Ryan White, especially to talk about our careers. Back in my early years, he'd been a staple in my evolution and some might even say he's to thank for giving birth to the Chris Bagwell you see before you today. Granted, I've evolved a lot since the days when Ryan and I were joined at the hip. Nevertheless he's still one of those founding father figures in my life. "To be honest Ryan," I muttered with a head of steam. "There really aren't any 'what ifs' to think 'bout." Ryan was well aware of my modesty. "I guess that was'a stupid question. I should've known you'd gimme an answer like that." He raised his right hand in a slapping gesture. "I should give-ya a whack in the face for it." He smacked the inside of my arm with a smile. "Seriously though," he persisted. It's not like I'd never considered the possibilities before. I have. I think it was more a realization that I just didn't want to face. This was all I knew and just the mere thought of having to do something else didn't sit well with me. Besides, I was never really good with change. "Seriously," I said sternly. "You know me, man. I don't really think about that kind of shit. If you walk into a match with your head lowered, thinking you're going to lose - you are going to lose. And that doesn't just pertain to the business - it pertains to everything in life." He nodded his head, showing his agreement. "Pessimism breeds failure," I went on. "I don't fail. I won't fail." My choice of words alone made me transparent. Ryan sat with a grin on his face, continuously nodding his head as if he agreed with everything I said...and maybe he did. But he didn't believe me. He didn't believe I was giving him the truth. Hell, he hardly believed I was giving myself the truth. "So," he pried into me, "how many times didja run through that speech in the mirror?" He gestured with a smirk. "You don't have to spew the bullshit to me, Chris. I know it's bothering you a little bit - even if just in the least." "Eh," I said with hesitation. "It ain't like that, Ry." "Let's get hypothetical for a minute, then," he replied. "Shoot." "Suppose Jordan White beats you - what's next for Chrissy-boy Bagwell?" He spoke with such flare, almost to the point where I couldn't take him seriously. "Prostitutes," I muttered with a face as straight as a board. "Lots and lots of prostitutes." He couldn't help but laugh. "You sonuvabitch! You haven't even thought about it!!" "I told you, man. I really haven't considered the possibility. I left the business for a short time and literally did nothing with my life. I came back and I'm in a groove - and a damn good one at that. I've knocked out Tessa Windsor and even Jordan White in my last two matches. Both are big names. Both are top stars. What's there to think about?" "And what about Valentine? Or the first dance with White?" I knew he'd bring those minor details to surface. I really hadn't had the chance to even think up a good excuse. "I had a stomach ache that week. I couldn't concentrate on the match." "Ohhhh," he gasped. "Well I guess you better not forget to take your vitamins this time around. Wouldn't want to get another stomach ache, would we?" All I could do was smile. I knew it was a lame excuse but I had to say something. Besides, I couldn't imagine what he'd think if I told him that I really thought Shaman put a voodoo curse on me and made me see orbs circling around the ring in both those matches. To the normal person, it was just as believable as saying Wolfeinator was gonna skip Donniepalooza this year for a tea party. "On a serious note, though," Ryan interluded. "Aren't the prostitutes the ones that got you killed a few weeks ago?" "Huh?!" At first I was a little lost. The combination of Shaman, Wolfeinator, and Donniepalooza racing through my thoughts had my head doing cartwheels. But eventually I got him, "Ah, Shockwave!" "Shockwave..." he said with a wink. "I didn't touch those broads," I confirmed. But he obviously didn't believe me. "My name ain't 'becca, Chris." I batted my eyes, hoping to give off an impression of innocence. There's just something about doing that, though, that only works for women. "I railed the one - big deal. You shoulda seen what Fish was doing to the other. Jesus Christ." "Fish? Brian Fisher?" "You got it." "The hell you doing hanging around with a guy like that?" "I like booze, he likes booze. He likes hookers and I.. you get the point." He couldn't help but shake his head at me (at least he did so with a smile). "You were heartbroken and love struck for how many years while you searched for a girl you thought was dead. You find'er and you're still out there fuckin' prostitutes? Man, oh man. I tell ya.." I slapped a grin on my face and shrugged my shoulders, "I like a fresh set of titties...even if they are a little worn down..." ---- V. That night I ended up on a bar stool at a place they call Jack n' Bills down in the Seaside Heights area. It'd been a number of weeks; maybe even months that I'd sat at a bar alone and drank the night away. But I had a lot on my mind - justifiably so. To make things clear, Jack n' Bills wasn't some fancy upscale joint that seemed to be stigmatizing the area. Nah - Jack n' Bills is a shitty looking hole-in-the-wall right on the boardwalk. The floorboards are rotted, the bar top is stained, and you could damn near peel the sheetrock from the walls. But it was exactly what I wanted. Exactly what I'd needed. They got their name from it originally being a place where the owners envisioned you could go to "drink Jack Daniels and play some billiards." If those two pastimes weren't some sort of indication of the clientele you'd see there on a weekday night. Yeah, trash. One hundred percent pure white trash. But it did the job right. Granted, pool was never my cup of tea but I never minded shooting a few balls around. It was the Jack Daniels specials that lured me in after meeting up with Ryan. The mask I was wearing while we spoke was started to disintegrate and I needed an outlet. Preferably somewhere where nobody would see me. The aura J&B's gave off was a somber one. It was the perfect setting for a guy like me to really sit down and be able to reflect on things. The alcohol - well that was merely acting as my psychiatrist. "I'll take a double shot of Jack and a Cuervo Black and Cola," I mentioned to the bartender in his passing. "Rough day?" he asked. I responded with a shrug...and that said it all. "You could say that." As he ventured off to make my drink, I had to wonder - was it really that clear? Did I have depression written all over my face? The shitty part was that I hadn't the slightest clue. I've never been sad or upset. I've never seen nor felt depression. I'd always been so self absorbed with my accomplishments that the mere thought of dissatisfaction made my stomach turn. The bartender slapped two double shots of Jack in front of me and brought my Cuervo Black and Cola over next. "I just wanted one double of Jack, my man," I said hastily. "I know," he confirmed. "The other one's for me." He lifted his glass and toasted it towards me. "Cheers!" he exclaimed as we pounded back our drinks and slammed the glasses to the table. I shuffled in my pants pocket and pulled out my wallet. "Whadda-I owe ya?" "That rounds on me," he said as he walked away. It was at that moment I finally understood the hidden message that Andy Murray and LeStatt Knight were portraying at Worldwide when they shared the night together. I hadn't been in Jack n' Bills for years and most of my regular bartenders from back in the day were long since gone. But this guy made me feel like I hadn't missed a happy hour. My first round and it was on him. For no good reason except that he can relate to how I was feeling. I felt like I'd rediscovered the thought process of the human psyche. Maybe, sometimes, we don't like people because we can't immediately find a common ground with them. Maybe we're just too judgmental on first impressions that we miss out on the opportunity to meet someone who could truly be grand. While I gulped down my Cuervo Black and Cola, it got me thinking - have I been missing out all of these years? Could I have missed out on a better life if I'd just opened myself up a bit beyond stereotypes? I polished off my first drink, slammed the glass to the table and exhaled, "Nahhhhh!" You know when you're drinking; you hit that crossroad between being sober and starting to feel a nice buzz? It's the border between where it's you talking and then it's the alcohol. That's exactly where I was at this point. Surprisingly, it didn't take much for me to really get a good buzz going, nevertheless it was there. How in hell could I ever co-exist with someone like Andy Murray, someone like Jorge Samuelsson, or shit - even someone like...White? I couldn't. Ever. "Another Cuervo Black & Cola?" "And another double shot of Jack," I added. "And get one for yourself - this time on me." The bartender winked, appreciative of my gesture. It was the least I could do for his generosity on the first round. I mean it's not like the money for those drinks was actually coming out of his pocket, but I guess its the thought that counts. At least that's what they tell you. "You must've really had a long day, kicking one of those back that quickly," he noted as he brought the second round of drinks over. We raised our glasses, cheered, and drank. "Yeaahh, you could sure as hell say that again." After swiping away our empty shot glasses, he extended his hand, "Name's Jordan." If that wasn't irony at its best. "Chris," I replied with a hint of hesitation. "Pleasure to meet you, Chris." "Yeah, you bet." I was kind of at a loss for words. Here I was just bickering about Andy and LeStatt sharing a few Shirley Temples at Worldwide and I find myself having a drink with some asshole by the same name as the guy who could potentially put an end to my career. It's because of that prick I'm even here, doubting myself, in the first place. But I suppose I had to make some sort of conversation. "How long you been working here, Jor--Jordan." Oh, how it pained me to say that name in friendly company. "I guess it's been about five years now. Started as a way to pay my way through college. Money was good, college was bad - next thing I know I'm still serving drinks." "Money's still good though, I'd imagine?" "Eh," he mustered, "it has its nights. Summer is awesome. Holiday weekends are great. The off season can get a bit cruel, though. Why, you looking for a job?" He said it with a cackle, obviously kidding, but it still hit me akwardly. The sonuvabitch already ironically shared White's name, but could he possibly be foreshadowing something, too? Too many damn literary devices for my tastes. "Ha," I forced out before taking a swig of my Cuervo. "Let's hope not!" Our conversation was steadily heading down the fast track for nowhere. Don't get me wrong, he was a nice kid - but something about sharing a drink with a guy named Jordan didn't work for me. "Ah boy," he sighed with disdain. "Here comes trouble." I looked over my shoulder and noticed a group of guys in their early twenties circling around one other kid who seemed to be there by himself. I wasn't really paying too much attention to what they were saying, but I did hear a few mentions about a girl. Typical. Always is about that. "These guys are assholes," Jordan confirmed. "They like to muscle around anyone who even looks at the one kid's girl." I smirked. Déjà vu? He must've seen the light go off above my head, "You've seen their kind?" I nodded. "Seen them? I've preyed on them." The whole scenerio reminded me of a time long ago when I first encountered a guy who'd ultimately become near and dear to me. The night I met Zacharia Taylor, I came to his aide when a bunch of pricks - just like these guys - were fucking with him because he glanced in the wrong direction. It got me thinking. Taylor and I go way back and I really fucked that whole friendship up. Rebecca was right. I let him crumble when he thought I was dead and then I FedEx'd a chair across his head just to spite Jordan White. You don't make very many friends in this business. And considering my time could potentially be limited, I guess it would only be right of me to make amends. The bartender stepped away from me for a minute to go clear up any altercations that might occur with the immature, scumbag muscle heads. I took that as my cue to get the fuck out of there. I pounded back the last of my Cuervo Black and Cola and slipped my money underneath the glass. As I walked out, the bartender felt it necessary to bid his farewell, "Nice chatting with you, Chris. I'll be seeing you soon." I dipped my head and jerked my wrist, giving the poorest farewell I think possible. As much as I wanted to hate him, he really wasn't that bad of a kid. Despite having a piss-poor name, he was alright. Hell, he did cover me entirely on my first round. I guess he meant well. But the fact is, as nice of a guy as he might have been - I'm still Chris Bagwell. And I cheaped him out on a tip. ---- VI. Taking all things into consideration, you almost have to find it funny to see me in this sort of predicament. Three weeks ago, I faked my death and my escape from the wrestling world. I gave myself a little vacation from all the bullshit. Now I'm faced with the possibility that my vacation may become permanent - that the death of Chris Bagwell from wrestling may actually turn out to be true. But the irony lies in my adversary. I've stepped toe to toe with everyone from Tessa Windsor to Bryan Mayhem, Seymour Almasy to Thom LeStrange, Clyde Fox and Jay Terror. Every single one of them, from Andy Murray right down to Vivica J. Valentine, has had a bitter hatred towards me. They've voiced their opinions time and again; never saying anything complimentary. And all its ever done was feed my ego. The more I hear my name in your mouth, the better I feel. I get off on people getting pissed off. You spit at me - I smile. You curse at me - I laugh. You hit me - I get a feeling of fulfillment. My fame is only temporary; it will not last. And the same goes for just about everybody else. Once you dip out of the spotlight, you're quickly forgotten about. My popularity is purely an accident. If I had spit in the face of LeStatt Knight when I joined this company instead of Vivica J. Valentine, my entire career would've went differently. People would've loved me. They would've thought of me as a hero among men. Shit, if I were to walk down to the ring at Rampage and dent a chair over LeStatt's head to give Andy the win - they'd start cheering me then, too. Popularity is all about perspective. But the one thing that truly endures - the thing that really lasts - is that of character. The moments that define you are those you'll be remembered by. Those very same moments are that of what boosts my ego. And we all know I've got a whole helluva lot of them. I fucked Andy Murray's wife. I beat up a kid with Down syndrome. I sexually assaulted an elderly woman. I disgraced a crippled old man. I convinced a gullible Clyde Fox that raping Sally Ford was okay. I hospitalized an innocent female backstage interviewer. I blew a chair up over my best friend's skull. And everyone hates me for each and every one of those things. They despise me for doing what I've done. They call me sick, twisted, and a scumbag. Everyone, that is, except one... Jordan White. That's the ultimate irony. The one many who's never done a thing to feed my ego; the one guy who quite frankly has never given the slightest shit about what I've done; that's the man who holds the responsibility to end my career. In 2008, I was labeled the baddest mother fucker on the planet...and Jordan White didn't care. He's never cared. He didn't care when I challenged him for his World Championship a week before NC-17 and he didn't care that I defeated him at Shockwave. That sonuvabitch didn't so much as skip a beat when he was told that Chris Bagwell was dead. All along, I've been sitting around contemplating my career and my life thus far. I've been worried about the impacts I've made and if I messed up by not being concerned with anyone other than myself. I keep asking the "what if's" and I've lost touch with the one thing that's gotten me to where I am... My ego. There's a skeleton key to all of this - one minor angle that's merely been overlooked. Jordan White is apathetic. He doesn't care about defeating Chris Bagwell. He doesn't care about stealing my job away from me. He doesn't care about championships or fans. He doesn't care about the industry. But me... I thrive on it. Imagine the power my ego could gain by shelving one of the industry's biggest stars. All this time, I've been wondering about what I might be forced to do next, when the reality is that I'm not ready to quit doing what I'm doing now. I enjoy fucking my co-workers' wives. I intend to do it again. I enjoy sexually assaulting elderly women and get aroused in teaching naive little boys to do the same to our backstage female interviewing staff. I have no remorse about smashing a chair over my best friend's head and I'll gladly do it again. I want to keep beating down retarded kids because I know they can't fight back. Shit, I'll even make my own You Tube channel just to timeline my activities. I enjoy being racist. I enjoy being sexist. I enjoy being anything else that society hates that ends with an "ist". That's just who I am and this is my only outlet to show that. I don't care who you are or what background you came from. The hell if I care what religion you believe in, because either way I spit in the face of your God. I live and I act for one person and my time is far from over. I'm Chris fucking Bagwell. Let that be known.
