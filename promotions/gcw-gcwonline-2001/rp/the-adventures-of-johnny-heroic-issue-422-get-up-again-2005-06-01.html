The Adventures of Johnny Heroic Issue #422: Get up again

1 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/1/05 Snap to in one...two. Yeah, I feel like I've been snapped in two, alright. Eyes crawl open and I can almost count the number of bloodshot vessels in them. Mouth tastes like brass, and the searing pain in my side tells me I have a broken rib or two. Fate and Fortune have nasty kicks to them. Especially when they land those kicks to an unprepared, unguarded vigilante who just lost his ability to use what is so commonly referred to as 'powers.' An attempt to sit up makes the wiser of me as I squeeze back a yelp of pain and bring a hand to my side. One's ribs are sore enough having lain on them on the sidewalk for a couple hours, never mind the fact that they just got the shit kicked out of them. But at least my arms and legs still work. And laying there, the shooting electrons in my brain replay useless little parts of my life bit by bit, but they slowly become more coherent. Little flashes of memory, little frames of consciousness flare into my brain and before I can connect them to a time or place they're gone again. Third person views of myself which I know I can't have come in anyway, and I'm wanting to relive moments past. But I can't. I can't hold on to any of them. They disappear as soon as I might begin to realize what meaning they might have. But a little bit of mind clearing paves the way for thought. Good, clear, rational thought. And I can remember Fortune and Fate standing above me, though I see it in third person. I don't know why I see everything in third person when I imagine it. I guess, as weird as it sounds, it's hard for me to picture anything happening in the world through my own eyes, but rather I see it as a straight on shot of everybody included. And I remember Fortune and Fate's speech about being told not to kill me. But though I replay that in my head, it's not what I'm thinking about. 'So you're giving up, Johnny?' Kid Justice stood in front of me, looking up at his hero. The hero of this city, of this whole fucking world. Johnny Heroic. And he stands there with no powers, a lame super suit, and eyes full of dreams of becoming me, becoming better than me, and it takes everything I've got not to simply crush everything he believes in. 'It's not a matter of 'giving up,' kid. Giving up is something you do when you're in a contest that can be won or a race that can be completed and you quit. It's something that you regret later on in life because you stepped out when you could have made a difference. Fact is, kid, there is no contest. There is no winning this race. There is no difference I can make.' 'What are you talking about? You're Johnny God-damn Heroic! You're the best one out there to make a difference.' 'I might have useful powers, kid. But I'm still just a man. And just like every other man, and just like you, I can't be more than one place at a time. I can't save more than one person at a time. I can't beat more than one person at a time. No matter how fast I am, death and dying are always ten times faster. And there's nothing I can do about it. And just like every other man, I'm gonna die. And then what? How long are people going to remember Johnny Heroic before he simply becomes forgotten in the annals of time? A hundred years? A thousand? Ten thousand? How long will it be before all the actions I commit to here and now don't mean a God damn thing? All the people I save will still die some day. I simply prolong their futile running from death. All the bank robberies, car jackings, and bomb threats don't even come close to showing up on the radar of history. So tell me why I bother.' Kid Justice strives for making everything just and righteous, as the name implies. The problem with being a 'hero' and striving for 'justice' is that they're relative things. What's just to one man is unjust to countless others. He who is one man's hero is another's villain. Different perceptions, different takes on everything that can be thought of. And Kid Justice just doesn't get that. But he looks at me and he sees right through all my bullshit and he cuts apart my argument with the truth. 'Because you have the power to bother.' Kid Justice has absolutely no powers. But for every power he doesn't have, he's got the drive to make up for it. He's a hero in training not because he doesn't know what it means to be a hero, but just because he doesn't have the means to be one. And I keep thinking that he's going to understand one of these days. One of these days he'll see why it's futile and it's better just to forget this whole 'go out and save the world' bullshit and one of these days he'll look at me and admit that I'm right and that there's absolutely nothing that one or two or a thousand people can do to save the world. But instead of admitting that I'm right, he goes and throws the fact that I can try in my face. The one man on the planet with my abilities is me, just as the one man on the planet with anyone's abilities, anyone's problems, and anyone's faults is the person whose they are. We all have our problems, we all have our setbacks, and we all have our own versions of 'powers,' and you can't compare on person's to another's. Johnny Heroic lost his mother at a young age. John Q Public didn't. Who's got the worse problem? That's not something that can be judged with names and dates and numbers because there is no comparison, because each has his own. Johnny Heroic can manipulate atoms and electrons. John Q Public can only manipulate his genitals. Who's more powerful? Both of them are simply slaves to the universe and completely dependent on fortune and fate to be merciful so that he may get by just a little bit more relative to how he's doing now. There is no comparing the two of them. There is completely powerless and there is all powerful. Both are unattainable by man, since he cannot be either one. And they are separated by an infinity of increments. Every man is somewhere on the spectrum at any given point, always fluctuating. Every man has his area where he normally fluctuates from, with a most powerless point and a most powerful point. But for as much as we fluctuate and as separated as every person is, we're all set amounts in an unlimited space. If you look at it from one angle, in comparison to the universe as a whole, everybody is almost powerless, even the most powerful of us. How can we compare to the vastness that is omnipotence? Any number that is not infinity is just about equal to zero. From another angle, we are almost all powerful. Any number when compared to zero is almost infinite. 'I also have the power to choose not to, kid.' 'So the fact of the matter is you're quitting.' No. 'No.' 'You're walking away. You're stopping what you're doing. You are quitting.' ...yes... 'No.' Kid Justice turns his back on me. Hypocrite. Nice way of telling me not to ever quit on something that seems hopeless, only to walk away and quit on me. But I don't say anything, because he's doing exactly what I want him to do. 'Hopefully, one of these days, Johnny, you'll come back.' But instead of hearing his words and thinking about them, they jog the memory of my favorite jazz song. One of these days, baby... One of these days you'll crawl back to me. One of these days you'll fall back to me. In the sun of your face I'll shine onto you. One of these days, I want... You... Back. Inch by agonizing inch I push myself off the ground. No longer does Johnny Heroic have the power to heal or to ignore pain or to do anything but be what he is. A man. But for the first time in a long time, I'm someone with direction. I was the world's most powerful man with no idea where I was going or what I was doing. Now? Now I'm nothing, and there's one place to go for someone like me. Fate and Fortune. And their boss, my arch-nemesis, Destine. One of these days, you'll come back to me All broken and bruised and loving me for it. I don't know her name. I don't know what time period she lived in. I don't know what band she sang for or the name of the song or why it appeals to me so much. All I know is that the singer is an angel. An angel because nothing I know of her taints her voice to my ears, because I don't know anything about her. Nothing she's done or said that was wrong is anything I know about, and she's that much more majestic because of it. All I know is that she is someone out there with a gorgeous voice telling me everything I want to know. You'll fall down and ask me to hold you again, And baby you know that I'll heal all your pain. One of these days. I stand up full. Pain and pleasure both melt like ice, and there's only one place for me to go. Johnny Heroic, for the cause of righting the wrong one more time, takes step by painful step to his arch-nemesis' lair. Step by painful step, I know that luck has run out. Sometimes, though, you don't need luck or powers or hope or even fortune, fate, or desire to push you foreward. You do it because you have to. You do it because there's an insurmountable force that you cannot ignore that tells you what to do, and for no fucking good reason you do it. You're addicted to it. And Kid Justice's words echo in my head. And they're more true now than ever. 'Because you have the power to bother.' I always had the power to bother. Control over electromagnetic fields or not, that choice was always there. And towards Destiny, to take Fate and Fortune into my own hands, step by step I go. One of these days, baby... I want... You... Back.
