The Name of Impact

11 May 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/11/07 Some people, when outside the ring, outside the arena still like to call themselves by their ring name. A wrestler who used to be one of Davis' best friends refuses to be called by his real name. Maybe it's an ego thing. Maybe they're trying to live vicariously through themselves. The real world is hard and crushing, but in the ring, in the arena the cheers are lie diamonds: beautiful and unbreakable. Impact. Davis and Impact haven't seen each other in over three years. They do call each other though on their respective birthdays to keep in touch and continue some semblance of friendship going, but realistically they haven't been friends in some time. Impact still forbids Davis to call him by his real name, which is something Davis is always pretty uncomfortable with. Even though Impact hasn't actually wrestled in two years. Not one match, not one move, not one workout. He used to be large and muscular, but now he's large and fat. He works at a warehouse in Allentown, Pennsylvania packing up boxes of merchandise. What goes in the boxes he doesn't even know, nor care. A man who used to live vicariously through himself now lives vicariously through Twizzy, he's only connection to the wrestling world besides VHS. The two men shake hands after Impact answers the door. He lives in a trailer off the highway on the border of Bucks County and Montgomery. Davis sits down on an old red couch. He is the first person to sit there in over a month. Impact goes to the fridge and pulls out two beers, his blood hound Roger following him closely. "I'm so glad you came down from the big city today, Trash." Trash is Davis' old ring name from when he and Impact were a tag team. "We have got to do this more often." The excitement in his voice is anything but unnoticable. Company is one thing, but a pro wrestler sitting on his couch means that he can talk about wrestling for hours. All the gossip, all the off screen turmoil, who's fucking who, all that crap. Impact wants to know. Davis doesn't respond. He looks around the trailer and does not envy Impact his freedom. There are water stains on the ceiling and posters of metal bands and Impact himself on the wall. Most of them are coming down. Other than the couch there is an easy chair that faces a 19" TV set and an X-Box 360 on the floor in between the two and a lot of VHS tapes stacked next to it. Nothing about the trailer makes it seem livable. Impact hands Davis his beer and Davis immediately takes a sip while Impact asks the first question. "You think you're going to win Warpath, Trash?" Surprise jumped into Davis' eyes. He had no idea that Impact was following his career that closely. "I don't know, man. And you know you don't have to call me Twizz or Twizzy or Trash or anything. Call me Davis." Impact chuckles. "Yeah right." Davis lets out an untraceable sigh. "Well, I don't know about Warpath, man. I've got Shaman and he's rough. Real rough. Plus Jordan White's still in the tournament. Everybody's a threat." He takes another sip of his Ying-Ling. "I'm getting older, brother. There might not be much more time for me in the ring." "And you got your ass handed to you by Mayhem last week. Damn! That was tough to watch, Trash." A little rage and hunger rises in Davis. "That was Caldera. He fucked me over so bad. What an asshole." "At least he gave you a job," defends Impact. "Some job." Davis takes a long drink. "Well, you kick some ass, Trash. You go out there and beat Shaman. You beat White. Beat Steven Caldera and Bryan Mayhem and LeStatt Knight and every piece of shit they throw at you." Impact gets to his feet and starts swinging his beer bottle, which is now empty, around like a sword. "Take The Establishment and shove their heads up their asses. Just fucking do it, Trash. I want that win!" Davis is very taken aback by Impact's outburst and Impact can see it. "Uhh," he starts, "you want to watch some of our old tag team matches, Trash?" The smile is so childlike, so eager. "I don't know," says Davis. "I've really got to get going. If you want to come to a show, just let me know and I'll set it up." He starts to get to his feet, but Impact grabs ahold of his t-shirt. "Oh come on, Trash. One match. I have the tape of us beating The New Breed." Davis gives a genuine half-smile. "All right, one match. One more beer." "Great!" Impact darts over to the fridge. Davis sits back down on the couch and starts to pet Roger. "Hey, weren't you called Misdirection back then?" "Yup." "Hey, let me ask you," Davis sets up, "what's your real name?" Davis is handed a second beer and his old friend smiles. "Impact."
