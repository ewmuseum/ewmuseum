Basic Training: Part Two

3 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12.03.05 Fade? “Hell here we come.” One of the younger of the seven boys mutters as they march forward to the door of the bus. As the men spill off the bus, they are quickly placed into a line. The first of our group of seven steps out. The drill sergeant immediately gets into his face as he walks by. “Fall in, soldier!” He yells as the first boy passes and is followed by the six others, one by one. The last off the bus is the biggest of them, the twenty-two year old. He falls in line all the way at the end, right as he steps off the bus. The drill sergeant steps in front of all the men and stands at attention. “Welcome to Fort Leonard Wood. For the next nine weeks, your asses are mine!” The man begins to pace back and forth in front of the new soldiers. He gets closer and closer to them with each pass. Finally, he walks so close that he’s almost nose to nose with them when he turns his head. He slows his pace and starts at one end, stopping at each recruit to make sure they are standing properly. The first few are good, the fourth a little slouchy. “Stand up straight, Private!” The sergeant yells. The boy quickly straightens up, much to the pleasure of the sergeant. He moves on down the line, not finding many problems. He reaches the first of our group of seven, the seventeen year old. He stands at perfect attention, bringing a small grin to the face of the sergeant. “Identify yourself, Private!” “Alan Greenburg.” The private answers, wiping the grin from the sergeants face. The boy sees this and allows a look of worry or fear to come across his face. The sergeant steps back and turns his head to scan the line of twenty men. “Wrong format, Private! Can anyone here tell me what this newbie did wrong”!” He yells forcefully. Nobody moves. Nothing. No, a cricket isn’t heard chirping. In it’s place is the crackling thunder of a storm approaching. The thunder rumbles a low tone, spanning a time of four or five seconds. The quiet only aggravates the sergeant, as if he needed further aggravation. He walks quickly over to the biggest of the group, our friendly twenty-two year old, located at the end of the formation. “How about you?” “He moved his mouth?” The larger private says, drawing small, unable-to-be-concealed chuckles from nearly all the other new recruits. Surprisingly the sergeant lets out a chuckle as well. Why did the bigger man do that? He should know better. The chuckle quickly disappears into the humid Mid-West air. Another crack of thunder is heard, this time louder, closer and longer. The sergeant steps up into the face of the Private, stopping any chuckling that is still in the air. “Think you’re funny, boy”!” The instructor yells. He has been “nice” so far, but now he’s getting angry. Disrespect like that just doesn’t fly in the ARMY. In his own mind, he’s willing to let the first few things from their mouths slide without punishment. But they don’t know that. This is how he puts the fear of anything and everything in them. But that should already be in the mind of this twenty two year old. The sergeant steps forward, pushing face to face with the large frame of his newbie. The Private is a little bit bigger than the Drill Sergeant; about an inch and three quarters taller and more muscularly built. The drill sergeant doesn’t back down though, he just presses forward. The two men become nose to nose. The sergeant presses his body weight forward coming chest to chest with the bigger man. The bigger man is taken back by this, literally. He loses his balance falling a few small steps backwards. “The ARMY isn't funny, boy.” The other boys all have their heads turned to the left to see what is happening. The sergeant continues his intimidation, staying in the face of the bigger man. Finally after advancing on the private, causing him to fall backwards and hit the bus, the sergeant backs off. At first, he stares straight ahead as the bigger man steps back up into line and gets back into attention. After a few seconds, the sergeant looks at the other recruits, not pleased with their curious looks. “At attention Privates!” He yells as he storms forward and walks the line of recruits. They all snap their heads back to a forward-looking position as the drill instructor steps by them. He steps back a few steps and looks at all of them, landing on the bigger man again. “For the next nine weeks the last word out of your mouths will be “sir”. Do you get me?!” “WE GET YOU SIR!” They all shout. The drill sergeant smirks again with amusement. His gaze has once again settled on the older private. The initial smirk is because of the good response on the last “test” he has given the recruits, but this smirk is at the stance of his target. He makes his way swiftly over to the private as another crack of thunder hits. It’s very loud this time. “What’s your name, son?” The instructor asks the bigger recruit, settling into a position a few feet in front of him. His smirk remains. He stands there, waiting for a response, looking the private up and down. If this wasn’t the ARMY, you’d think he was eye-fucking his private. “Sir, Ehren Moore, Sir.” He responds. “Where are you from?” The sergeant inquires. “Ur-anus, Sir!” The private replies with a small smile. This causes chuckles from the recruits again, causing the sergeant to back off and gain another stern look. The tension in the air is even thicker now, seeming to grow with the impending threat of thunderstorms. The sky that is seen through the windows of the bus has slowly changed from a light grey to a darker grey. Off in the distance, dark streaks can be seen from the clouds all the way to the ground. Rain. It’s coming. And it’s moving in quickly. “Shut your holes!!” The instructor yells as he steps back up into the private’s face. He pushes forward hard but Moore, as we now know him, has planted himself. The sergeant doesn’t displace the private at all, simply stands there, face to face. This brings a few “oohs” from the other recruits. “Seems like you’re a little mistaken, Private! The first time is an oops, the second is stupidity! This is not a joke! For the next nine weeks I am going to be riding your ass like stink on shit!” The recruit is a little taken back by the sudden hostility of the instructor. It was just a joke. But he knows he shouldn’t joke. After where he’s been? He knows. The instructor, now highly pissed off steps away from Ehren Moore, but not backwards. He remains only inches from his recruits, sidestepping his way down the line. All of the seventeen, eighteen and nineteen year olds aren’t amused any more. Their smirks are gone. The chuckles are gone, replaced by almost continuous rumbles of thunder. “Trust me, you worthless pieces of shit; this isn’t home anymore. In my camp, there is only room for one sense of humor!” The instructor rants. He stops at the first of our group of seven. The seventeen year old. He steps another inch forward, pushing the young man’s frame backwards. “Greenburg, do you know whose sense of humor I’m talking about?” The private takes a big gulp of air. Saying this is a little overwhelming for him is an understatement. His face turns beet red, his cheeks quiver. He struggles to get the air to his throat to strike his vocal chords, majorly startled. As he opens his mouth to answer, a crack of thunder hits almost simultaneously with a flash of lightning that lights up the privates fear-filled expression. The private jumps at the big boom, falling out of stance. He quickly takes a deep breath and stands back in position. “Yours, Sir!” He finally manages to say. “That’s right!” The sergeant yells, causing the private to shake a bit more. The instructor steps to the side and begins to walk the line of recruits again. “My you are going to learn to love my sense of humor! Do you know what I find humorous”!” He yells in the face of the last private in formation. “I do not, sir!” The private firmly answers. The sergeant doesn’t show any signs of pleasure at this nice response. He just steps back and eyes the group. “Who here thinks they have what it takes to take me down?” He asks. CRACK Another loud thunder strike is heard. Nobody answers. They’ve all been intimidated too much. The sergeant pans the formation for a volunteer, but doesn’t find one. They all stand as still as ever, not wanting to lose their balance. “I’ll just have to pick somebody then.” The sergeant says softly, but forcefully. He pans the formation again and settles on his new found “friend.” The sergeant smiles and points to the last man in the row, Ehren Moore. He moves his index finger in a “come here” motion for a few seconds, causing Ehren to sigh and step forward. He approaches the sergeant, who has tossed his large hat to the ground a few feet away. He settles himself in to a ready stance. “All right, tough guy. Try me.” The two circle each other a little before Ehren throws a forearm sloppily. The sergeant ducks the weak strike and drops to a crouching position, swinging his leg around to sweep Ehren’s legs out from underneath him. To the surprise of the drill instructor, Ehren hops over the sweep and swiftly turns, under hooking the crouched sergeant. Ehren uses his agility and strength to pull the sergeant down onto his head allowing him to fall to his back. Ehren still has a hold on one of the sergeant’s arms, allowing him to keep control. As the instructor lands, Ehren quickly kneels and places a knee across the throat of the sergeant. The recruits? jaws drop. Ehren’s jaw drops. He looks down and locks eyes with the furious drill sergeant. Ehren quickly stands and jumps back into formation. Even he has a look of worry on his face. As he steadies himself in line the drill sergeant takes a deep breath and climbs to his feet as the rain finally begins to fall. It’s slow at first, but is quick to pick up in intensity. The instructor places his hat back on his head. In just those few seconds, the thunder has begun to rumble loudly and the rain is coming down really hard. Puddles are beginning to form in the dry dirt at the feet of the recruits. “Private Moore! Drop and give me push ups!” The instructor yells, very upset. Ehren drops to his knees as looks up to the angered man. “Push ups! Now!” “How many, Sir?” Ehren asks fairly innocently. “I do not recall saying, Private! Just do the damn push ups!” The now irate sergeant yells. Ehren obliges and begins going through the up and down motions. He counts off with each repetition. As he continues and nears the tenth one, the sergeant looks to the others and then to the sky. “All right, Privates. Get your gear and head to the barracks!” “Yes, sir!” “Fourteen.” Ehren mutters as the sergeant speaks. He stops his up and down motions and kneels, beginning to stand. He is quickly stopped by the firm hand of the drill sergeant forcing him back down to the ground and into the puddle of water forming underneath of him. “I never said anything to you, maggot!” The drill instructor yells as he passes by. Ehren sighs and goes back into his repetitions. He continues to count out loud as all of the other recruits scramble around in the rain to get their bags from the undercarry of the bus. “Private Greenburg! Take Moore’s stuff to the barracks. He is a little indisposed at the moment.” The private nods and walks past the downed Moore. As the recruits pass by him one by one and make their way towards the barracks, the sergeant yells one last time. “The time is fourteen thirty-eight! Grub is at Eighteen hundred! The rest of the night is yours, but grub is only served once!” He yells as he slams the baggage doors on the bus. The bus pulls away with a loud release of air. As the bus drives out of sight, the sergeant begins making his way past Moore, on his way to his own tent. Before he passes the bigger private, he places hi large boot on the back of Moore and presses down. “Welcome to hell, maggot.” With that the sergeant smiles and snickers, walking away. The thunder is still loud; the rain still heavy. Water is gathering in the area and Ehren Moore is left there. He’s left there to do only one thing. “Twenty-five. Twenty-six. Twenty-seven.” Push ups. Fade?
