The Breakdown of the Bicameral Mind: Chapter 3

22 November 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 22nd, 2009 You sat there all alone waiting. A lone lamp post across the street was your only companion. The little light that it gave out was drowned by the furious fog that embraced that night and everything in it. You shivered as you looked around for any landmark that would give away your position. There wasn’t anything that you could recognize except for the darkness and the fog. Two things you were very much familiar with. Everything seemed so strange to you. There was something about the air that you were breathing that appeared bizarre. The air felt stale as it coursed through his body and back out, turning into a cloud of cold air. The thought that you had finally done it passed through your mind. There was no going back to whatever there was left. That’s the moment you saw the shadow walking through the mist towards you. You held your breath for a split second as you decided what to do, but the air around you became stifling and you couldn’t move. The shadow came closer with each passing moment. Its presence filled you with unease. For a moment, your throat would work. No words or sounds came out. The shadow was right in front of you. Eyes baring you down to your core as you stared in uncertainty. The fog had gotten denser with each step the shadow took. Now, even though the shadow was right in front of you, there was no way to recognize who or what it was. “It’s been a while,” the shadow said with a voice that was forceful, cheery and eternally sad at the same time. The fog around her seemed to dissipate according to her will. “Why are you here?” You finally saw the contours of her figure and a face that was unforgettable. While you finally knew who it was that was standing before you, there was still no way to answer her question. You had no idea where you were or why. “I don’t know where I am. But if you are here, it might only mean one thing. It was my time,” you answer her with a hint of disappointment. “Your time has not come yet. Trust me, I would know. I think this is just a construct of your subconscious. A place where your mind can escape and separate itself from what your body is going through. You must be either very close to death or in such excruciating pain that your mind created all of this just for you,” she told you. She sat down besides you, took a hold of your hand and rested her head on your shoulder. The feeling of her skin on yours was exhilarating. It was like a jolt of electricity passing through your whole body. It was like nothing you had ever felt. The mere presence of Death was like an aphrodisiac to you. “So, does that mean that you are also a construct of my subconscious? Am I just crazy and hallucinating or are you real?” you asked her. You were dreading her answer. Your whole reality depended on her answer. You waited for it to come. You waited. She only nestled against your shoulder and intertwined her fingers with yours. There was another bolt of electricity. This one shook your whole body with such force and violence that it almost threw you off the bench you were sitting in. There wasn’t enough time to compose yourself before another jolt of electricity, even stronger than the one before warped the place around you. She was no longer by your side and everything around you seemed to be melting away. Another one. ---- “We have him back. That was too close.” Dr. Matheson and one of his orderlies stood huddled around the body of Antonio. They were checking his vitals and making sure that he was alive after the test they had run. The doctor checked his pulse, probed and prodded until he was sure that there was no permanent damage to the brain or any of his internal organs. After that was done, he moved towards the table and scribbled furiously on his notepad. The orderly grabbed a hold of the knocked out Antonio and lifted him on his shoulder like a rag doll. “Be careful with him. We don’t want him to die on us. That would ruin everything I’ve been working for,” Dr. Matheson told the orderly without coming up from what he was writing down. The orderly grunted assent and left the room with Antonio on his shoulder. For a moment, Dr. Matheson was left by alone with his thoughts. Everything revolved around his future in the world of psychiatry. He would become even greater than Freud or Skinner. His name would be synonymous with his profession. Doubts about his methods never crossed his mind. Understanding that only those that dared to go the extra mile to get what they want was the first part of his own evolution as a man. There was a slight knock on the door and a nurse popped her head inside. “Dr. Matheson, Miss Porter is ready for you in your office. She seems more rattled today than any other time, for some reason. We gave her the medication you prescribed but it doesn’t seem to be calming her down.” “I’ll be right down. I just need to finish off a small bit of paperwork and I will be with her. Tell Bill to wait with her until I arrive,” Dr. Matheson ordered. There was no time to think about his future glory. Work beckoned. The doctor put a hand on the device he had just used on Antonio and softly caressed it. “Don’t worry girl, after all the bad things that have been said about you, I will make you a household name soon. Very soon,” he said as he left. He flicked the light switch and turned the lights of the room off, leaving it in a menacing darkness. He closed the door and left. ---- I woke up in the room that has come to be my home for the last few weeks. My head hurt like a bitch and the room was spinning around me like a kaleidoscope of white. The pain in my head was exactly like the one after a good and long night of drinking, except without all the good and alcohol. I tried sitting up, but as soon as my head left the padded floor it got worse. Trying to stop what was coming up now was impossible. I felt the bile rising up my neck and in the back of my tongue as I retched whatever it was that I had eaten the day before. It was a while before I was able to compose myself. I could still taste whatever it was they had fed me even after spitting out everything I could. The stench invaded my nostrils and almost made me throw up again. The attempts at recollecting what had happened before were unsuccessful. It was like a wall had been built inside my mind that divided what they wanted me to remember from my real memories. I hated it. I hated what was going on with a passion. I didn’t know which part I loathed more, the prison I was trapped in or the loss of control. Either way, both were incredibly taxing on my sanity. So I worked on trying to recall what had happened. I tried to build something close to a full picture by slowly putting the bits and pieces together like a jigsaw puzzle. I really couldn’t remember much. I remembered the experience of being in that desolate place with her, but I didn’t know how I had gotten there in the first place. Was that a dream or something else? She never answered me. She left me to fend for myself in this prison of the flesh and of the mind. She left me with a dark reminder. Memento mori. ---- It was days of intensive torture sessions for the doctor and his crew to begin to see changes in their patient zero. The constant stream of drugs and tranquilizers had left him almost as a vegetable. No longer in control of his body, he went through whatever experiment the doctor could come up with. At first there were some good days and some bad days, but it seemed like the fresh supply of good days was basically over. At a minimum, throwing up through the mornings and passing out through the evenings was his schedule. Days and hours and seconds and minutes were no longer consequential. Places where there should be some sort of recollection of memories were exchanged by moments of intense darkness and endless black. Colors and shades where now indistinguishable. It was all a big clutter of feelings and colors and… things. He sat at the usual place, at the usual hour in Dr. Matheson’s office. With orderly Bill at his side to make sure that everything went smoothly, the daily therapy session was in order. Dr. Matheson sat on his desk reading the results of the current slew of tests that Antonio had gone through. His chest swelled with pride as everyday he saw his masterpiece unfold. The doctor was like a modern day Michelangelo as he saw the progress of his Sistine Chapel. “We are making great progress, Antonio. Together not only will we make your troubles disappear but I will turn you into the perfect specimen. The perfect human being. The modern day Adam. And I will be your god. Standing over you and pulling the strings into a new era,” Dr. Matheson smiled as he said this. He leaned back, put his hands behind his head and grinned foolishly. Something inside of Antonio heard what was being said. Somewhere deep within him, those words stroke the right chord. The hollow shell that was in front of the doctor couldn’t distinguish the words from anything else. Antonio’s body was slumped in his chair. His head was slumped forward so he would not choke on his own drool. While his body was fine, his mind was battered and bruised. The damage was most likely permanent. “I know you might not fully grasp what we are undertaking here with your…” the doctor coughed a bit, “…help, but you will soon. You will understand, won’t you?” Bill grabbed the back of Antonio’s head and slowly and carefully moved it upwards and downwards simulating affirmation. The wicked grin on Dr. Matheson face grew wider at this. Bill chuckled. And somewhere deep down, Antonio listened. He listened and he remembered. He remembered. “Oh. Before I forget, since you are our Adam, I decided that we couldn’t finish this project without our Eve. So, I decided that you two should finally meet, if you are to be the future of this world,” he said as he scribbled some notes on his notepad, his demeanor turning back to business mode. Bill the orderly opened the office door and a nurse brought a woman in wheelchair inside the room and parked her right besides Antonio. Her long black hair covered most of her face but it was pretty obvious that she was on the exact same condition Antonio was in. Bill grabbed both their heads and turned them towards each other. “Antonio, this is Vanessa. Vanessa, this is Antonio. Adam, this is Eve. Eve, this is Adam,” the doctor said trying intently to see if there was any reaction at all between the two subjects. Outwardly, there was absolutely no reaction. Inside though, a voice screamed at Antonio in recognition. It was unmistakable. She didn’t look exactly like she had always looked but there was no confusing it. She had not abandoned him. It had to be her. Dr. Matheson looked down at his notes and before he could notice, Vanessa Porter winked at Antonio before she returned to her previous state. There was a hint of a smile on Antonio's face. Death was by his side.
