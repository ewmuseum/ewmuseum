Date: Shockwave '09 “nnnnnNNNNNNYYYYAAAAAAAARRRRGGGGGHHHHH!” I scream, as the blood filled urine trickles, as if it were in lumps, from my urethra into the bowl. Hear a man in one of the cubicles startled so badly by my scream that he bangs his head against the wall. The dull thud of his head hitting the wall echoes in the remnants of my shriek. Probably not the most enjoyable noise to hear mid-dump, but I didn’t have the will to fight the sting nor the courage to fight the blood-curdling scream. Put my hand against the wall to brace myself against the pain, grit my teeth and hold my breath. The excruciation is almost unbearable as I try my best to push through and finish my business. My insides felt like they’d been turned to pulp after the progression of the last two weeks. Resilience was a matter of fact when people talked about me, CJ Woods, the underdog rookie who was continuing to fight against the odds. But when it came down to the crunch, I was feeling battered a broken. Physically and mentally. I, too, am human. The door to the toilet facilities in the hospital burst open. “Hello” Is everything okay in here?” calls the voice of a woman. Her voice timid, as if she understood she shouldn’t be in the gentlemen’s facilities but felt the urgency based off the gruelling scream she’d heard ring through the thin plaster walls, carrying down the hall to her nurse’s station. I secretly hoped the maternity walls have been better sound-proofed than this. “I heard a scream. Is everyone okay in here?” “Next cubicle,” says the guy to my right. “The scream came from him. Scared me half-to-death!” KNOCK! KNOCK! KNOCK! is the feint wrap of her knuckles upon the melamine door. “Are you okay, sir?” “Yeah. I’m okay.” I could hear the hesitation in her voice, even before she spoke. My spider senses tingling that she does not believe a word I say. “Are you sure” It doesn’t sound like you’re okay.” Flush. Zip. Step away from the toilet and unlatch the door, brushing by her as she steps aside. “I said, I’m okay so that means I’m okay. Just fighting some internal bruises and stuff.” Make my way to the hand basins as she looks over me, clearly worried about the state I was in. Her eyes scouring my body for evidence of wound, rash or plague. As if on cue, her eyes bounce from traipsing over my body to the bruises on my forearms and face. Aghast with shock, her innocent hands rises to her chest as if protecting it from what she might hear. “Have you been fighting recently?” “Look, sweetcat, I’m a pro-wrestler. It’s part of my thang, ya dig” You get bruised. You bleed. You get broken. The fans cheer. Money in the bank. Job done, right?” Frowning, she lets out an exasperated sigh of disapproval. “Maybe we should check you out, nonetheless. That IS why you’re here, isn’t it?” Shake my head. “On my way to see Morag Woods, actually.” “Room three-ten?” “You some kind of psychic spook or somethin?” But thanks for answerin? my next question. “Preciate the concerns but you can stick “em in your pocket and hang onto “em for someone else, yeah” Right on.” Ruffle her hair on my way past, still grimacing as the sting which remained with my pee-hole. First Chris Bagwell. Then LeStatt Knight. Dan Black had a turn next at me. Somehow, I managed to overwhelm him and slink through with victory, but I’d taken my bumps to get there in the end. Collapsing after it all takes a toll on your ego, but to be able to stand again, that relinquished any grasp embarrassment had upon me. The stingy blood-wee was something I wasn’t so sure I could empower myself to battle, however. Get out into the hall and read the numbers on the doors. The kids were with Morag’s parents in their multi-million dollar apartment in Manhattan. They liked visiting the grandparents. They were good people. They managed to remain civil to me although I’d moved away from their daughter’s life. They were good, wholesome people. Her condition now was concerning. Hopefully they’d finished their diagnostics and tests to figure out what in God’s name was going on with her. It was touch-and-go for a minute there. Her convulsions frightening the Hell out of poor brave Leslie and her brother just a little under a week ago. ==================================================== One week ago? “Craig, I’m afraid her situation is critical. She’s had a brain haemorrhage but we do not yet understand what has caused these tests,” the emergency room head of staff advises me without emotion. I felt my hand clutch to my mouth out of reaction, my guts twisting like turbines inside and the nausea rising in my throat. My right arm, which held onto little Alex (whose attention was taken by those rushing around us in ER) clenched tighter, as if looking for something to support me. Leslie watched the man talking before me; he didn’t even acknowledge her presence. He didn’t even bat an eyelid as Alex decided it was time to stick his small, yet chubby, index finger so far up my nose it made my eyes water. “This is a serious situation, Craig. We’ve managed to stabilise her for the time being but we’ve put her in an induced coma to do that.” “Is she going to make it?” He sighs. Looks into one of the fluorescent bulbs in the ceiling, hoping their familiar hum will give the strength to make something up. He had a good poker face, but it wasn’t great. And when he said this was a serious situation, that look he was struggling with told me just how serious it was. “I wouldn’t like to speculate on her survival chances at the moment, Craig. Let’s just say that now she’s stabilised she’s got a better chance of making it through. It’s really up to her at the moment.” “She’s dying?” “She’s not in a great condition. This is one of those situations where it’s good to let family members know that now might be a good time to visit.” “So she IS dying then. Why don’t you just come out and say it instead of beating around the bush. Bet you’re scared I’ll sue. I just want her to be okay, jackass.” Push by him, dropping my shoulder into him as I walk by. Their egos and worry for their bank balance and reputation really get to me. It was rare to find doctor’s who cared. And bedside manner is almost a myth in this day and age. I couldn’t understand how people could be son involved in the public health system when the only health they were worried about is how portly their wallets could be. Trying to rush as best I can, whilst poor little Leslie did her best to keep up with my stride, we finally get to the room. My heart skips a beat and I lose my breath. Seeing her on that bed was like someone belting me with a four-by-four lump of timber and for the life of me, I struggled to catch my breath again. I clutch Alex tighter, fearing I might drop the boy thanks to the shock which was coursing through my body like a formula one race car. Leslie brushes off my grip, swatting at my grasp with her left hand to make sure she could free herself immediately to go to her mother’s side. She scales a chair nearby the bed, standing on it and leaning on her hands on the bed, looking over her mother. “Mummy?” she asks, without any answer. “Mummy, wake up. Why are you still sweeping?” A tingle in my eye, followed by the tear streaming down my cheek. Another tear rolls down the other side as the lump in my throat threatens to choke me. My knees begin to wobble and the whole world around me begins to grow dark. Tunnel vision throws out my centre of balance as I feel the Earth spinning. On cue, a nurse strides in and plucks Alex from my grasp. Just in time for my knees to buckle. Just in time for my kneecaps to greet the linoleum. Just in time for me to wail. As if out of a movie. Landing on my knees is the invitation for the tears to flow. And they do. Cascading like a great waterfall, the downpour unforgiving as my breath tries to fight through the saliva I’m near choking on. I didn’t leave Morag because I didn’t love her anymore; I left her because I love her. And seeing the woman you love lying on a bed, with tubes coming out from everywhere, is an overwhelming experience. An experience I wouldn’t wish upon my worst enemy. My whole soul aches for her mercy and Leslie comes over to comfort ME. Instead of me comforting her. The three year-old. Her podgy little arm wraps around my neck, she draws me close to her chest. Her free hand strokes my face awkwardly as she holds me tight, slick as it smoothens the tears on my cheek. Being brave for the both of us. “There, there, Daddy. It’s okay. Mummy’s just sweeping,” she soothingly says. A three-year-old sympathising with her father is almost heart breaking enough. But to have to be held in her arms for consolation felt embarrassing but right all the same. The wisdom beyond her years astounded me and as I look through my own curtain of tears, I see the nurse has begun to cry as well. The moment so heart-warming she couldn’t find the strength to fight through her own emotions. To see Morag laying there, in the wake of whatever attack she’d been assaulted with, shattered my whole world around me. Smashing it to smithereens.. ==================================================== “The fuck’re you s’posed to be, feller?” says the man sitting in the seat by her bed. I have barely made it through the door-way to be awestruck by the audacity of that voice. Shifts his position in his seat and stands tall by Morag’s side. I glance from her to him, then back again a few more times to reiterate I’d walked into the right room. Surprisingly, I have. “I’m sorry. Can I help you?” “I asked a God damn question first, cowboy. Who the fuck’re you and what’re you doin” in here?” says the man. The hairs on the back of my neck prick up and before I realise, my hands have clenched themselves into fists. The man has noticed, which he reflects with a grin. His appearance is that of a man who cares not for the swift discharge of hand-to-hand combat. The scars and shape of his face brag that rite. “I’m her husba” her ex-husband and I’ve come to see how she’s doin?. But never mind me, killer, who are you?” His face contorts into what some people might describe as a smile. The dribble of urine which passes through my scathed pee-hole causes me to flinch and only describe as watching Lucifer himself grinning. It can’t be called so much a grin but more as a gloat. His large calcified hands brush his leather trench-coat backward as they slide into his jeans pockets. “Well, lookee what we got us here.” “Are you monologue-ing or have you got a friend in the closet” Not that I’m not cool with you and the closet or nothin?. Just sayin?.” Stifles a laugh and bounces his neck from side-to-side, cracking it in the motion. Exhales heavily through his nose and I could swear I saw flames. “You some kind of wise-guy, are ya?” Shake my head. The tension was growing and I wasn’t ready for any type of tension. My body has gone through enough. Too much for me to put it through more before getting to Shockwave. “Come for my money.” “Oh” She borrow a nickel for the paper or somethin?” You’re gonna have to get yourself some crayons or somethin?, asshat, coz I haven’t got a clue “bout what your gums is flappin” over.” Within a split second, I find myself pinned against the wall. He knocks over an empty drip bag holder as he forces me against the wall with a tremendous thud. My head aches as it collides and I can feel the dampening sensation of a headache looming through my skull like a big, black rain cloud. I groan. He slaps me. “Pay a-fucken-tention, asshole!” He slaps me again. “I come for my money and I get my money. That’s how shit works. Is this makin” sense to you now?” My spine is tingling with fear. My breathing increases rapidly as I fear these my be my last few minutes if I cannot grab my wits about me and find out just exactly what is going on here. “See, she told me she didn’t have none to pay me back. Blames her husband on runnin” out on her.” “I DIDN’T RUN OU-? SLAP! “DO I LOOK LIKE I GIVE A FUCK, SMART MOUTH?” I quieten down pretty quickly. “She owes my ten large, cowboy. You got it on you?” “No.” “When can we arrange for that payment to be made?” “Whoa, feller. Ten large” Plus, she’s not even my wife anymore. Why should I bail her out of anything?” “Coz you don’t wanna go lyin” in the bed next her, too. Or do ya?” Brains stops right on the dime. The facts are starting to come to me now. The man notices me glance from Morag to the tubes and back to him. His cold dead pan eyes let me knows that he understands exactly the conclusion I’m drawing. “Oh, you’re clever. That’s right, motherfucker, pay up or I do you, too.” “What’d you give her?” “Why should I tell you?” “COZ SHE COULD DIE!” I scream at him. Shrugs at me. Plainly shrugging it off with that matter of fact matter somebody with a heart of stone would have. The care within his body wholly and solely reside on him and him alone. He lets go of my shirts, smoothing it with his calcified hands. Making sure he hasn’t left any wrinkles. The feeling of his cold palms through my shirt make me feel even colder. That should net you a nice enough pay cheque, yeah?” “Fuck you.” “Awww,” he sighs, patting me down one more time as he smiles sadistically at me. “You got some cute kids. Bet I could make my money back with them.” He says it plainly. Like he were talking about trading baseball cards. He shoots a wink at me, knowing full well the reaction it was cause within me. He’s pre-empting me before I can do anything. As I lunge forward, ready to punch him square in the face, he’s already side-stepped. As he steps to the side he reaches one leg across my body, putting his index finger beneath my nose and using my momentum as he raises it. My legs fly out from underneath me and I land square on my back with all the wind knocked out of my body. “See you after Shockwave, cowboy.”
