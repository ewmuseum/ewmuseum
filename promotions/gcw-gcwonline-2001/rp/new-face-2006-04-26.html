New Face

26 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 26/04/06 A cold chill ran through the air, as the plane struck the ground at JFK. Tyler Xing had heard many things about America. He’d been warned about the carnivorous nature of their economic behemoths, the dog-eat-dog attitude of the streets, the inequality plaguing their streets. He’d also been enlightened about the opportunities provided in such a land where talent talked and money listened. He’d never really had money, having devoted himself so thoroughly to his life pursuit in the monastery since he was sixteen; there had never really been any need or use for it. But now, he was intrigued by the possibility of holding a thick wad of American dollar bills, and being able to splash out on himself in the land of the free. The last few years had been amazing. Being 26, he was now really and truly a man in every sense of the word, and he’d found a lot out about himself at the Tan Wui monastery just outside of Beijing. All his teachings, which so many students found boring and impractical, had helped him cope with the death of his parents, the struggle of solitude, and the temptations of the city of the world’s largest country, China. It wasn’t that his parents? death hadn’t hurt him, it had a great deal, but it was more the case that he felt untouched spiritually by the grief he let his mind advocate. Nights of meditation had allowed him to commune with his father in spirit, and his soul was at rest’of this Tyler Xing was sure. But now, it had been time to leave the monastery. It had been his father’s wish for him to study there, to protect himself against the impurities of Western Culture. But, as much as he was his father’s son, his father was gone and Tyler “Nightwater” Xing saw a land of opportunity as the perfect place to make his mark on this ever-ailing and fragile world. What had surprised him most, however, from his view on the plane, was not the size of the United States of America, or the lack of people there compared to the bustling and over crowded streets of Confucian China. It was, instead, the fact that his father and his teacher at the Chin Woo academy had both been so wrong about the United States. To them, evil lurked in every corner; all men were to be regarded with suspicion, and women condemned for their scheming plans of world domination. It was, however, a much more reasonable place. A young man of African descent welcomed Tyler into the airport after departing the boeing 747. He had never seen a man of black origin before, except in the archives of film, which was very harshly restricted by the Chinese Government even today. He held onto the sweaty palm of the man, wearing his light blue shirt and black tie, and looking as comfortable in his position as a baby sleeping in his cot. This was all so fascinating for Xing, that he forgot to let go of the handshake, and a couple of security guards rushed over in an attempt to explain to Tyler the ritual of handshaking in the developed world. Tyler took their explanations with a pinch of salt, and a little humour about how ignorant they must be ? but then most Chinese were ignorant about the Americans, evident from his father’s attitudes. He was ushered into the long queue awaiting entry into the United States. The Chinese Woman at the counter, by the time Tyler got there looked exhausted. She shouted with a shrill voice, corrupted by the presence of too many cigarettes? “Next!” Tyler quickly moved into position, retrieving all the relevant documents. He had the Visa from the American Embassy in Tywan; and his papers and Chinese Passport. The woman looked down at the passport, and back up at Tyler. He was sure a glint of a smile appeared on her face. Now, Tyler had always considered him to be a reasonably good looking male, but was presently shocked when her eyes strolled down his torso and to his crotch area, where a further smile crossed the young maiden’s lips. She returned her gaze to his eyes, and handed him the papers and passport. “Be careful now. The streets are no place for a man of your’prettiness.” Tyler stepped back. Americans were indeed strange, he decided. But, politely, he returned her smile. Tyler was rushed through the security like a conveyer belt implement, and finally made it out of the claustrophobic airport with his brown carrier bag. Imagine his delight, when he saw the scene from his favourite movies, as a stream of yellow cabs awaited Tyler’s arrival from the airport. He stood, politely, waiting to be invited into a cab, as people rushed past him into cabs and they all drove off. Finally, he decided that there would be no invitation, and opened the door of a brown vehicle with taxi imprinted above the bonnet. “Hi” Tyler exclaimed immediately, extending his hand. “Yeah, alright buddy, introductions on the first date, not taxi ride.” He withdrew his hand and sat into the cab, which smelt like the Beijing Sportsdome toilets had smelt to him when he’d competed there just last October. Strange that a cab could smell so decisively like a changing and shower room’but this was America, it was bound to be strange. Tyler offered the Italian man driving the car a piece of paper, suggesting where he was to go. The man nodded, and the cab took off. Xing couldn’t contain his excitement as the car sped through the streets of New York. He’d seen the pictures’but to be there in person was quite exhilarating for his virgin eyes. The huge towers, like monuments to capitalist greatness, stood proudly in the sunlight, reflecting the gleam onto the city with pure altruistic decadence. The people walked around, some in a hurry, some without purpose. Men and women of all cultures and all descriptions adorned the streets of the world’s greatest city, and none of them seemed to care about the other, for good or bad. “So, this is multi-culturalism?” Tyler quietly asked himself. “Father, you were so wrong. This place is?” He was cut off by the Italian American’s interruption. “Buddy, you alright there” Talkin? to yourself?” Tyler nodded, the grin on his face inextinguishable. He briefly apologised before turning his gaze to the location of Ground Zero, and gasping at the extent of the damage, and the speed of reconstruction. “This your first time in New York pal?” The question caught Xing off guard, as a group of extremely good looking Chinese Women walked past on the sidewalk, and one sent a glance his way. He almost swallowed his tongue, before turning back to his driver companion. “Yes. My first time. America and New York.” He smiled into the rear view mirror. The man glanced at him through the mirror also, with a raised eyebrow. “Yeah” Where you from?” Tyler smiled again “The Tan Wui Monastery near Beijing.” The man shrugged. “Where’s that” Japan or something?” Tyler was shocked. He couldn’t understand how anyone wouldn’t know where the largest city in the world was. Everyone he knew in China, knew that Washington, New York, California were all in the United States of America; that London was in England; that Paris was in France? “Yes” he replied decidedly politely. “So, if you don’t mind me askin”, what you doin? in the big apple?” Tyler thought for a moment, and then realised that the big apple meant New York. “I am here to wrestle, see?” He shoved a piece of paper at the driver, which had offered auditions to prospective wrestlers looking for a place on the wrestling entertainment stage. He grinned impressively, as the man shook his head. “Well, that’s something you don’t see everyday. Which company?” Tyler thought again, and again realised what the man was asking. “Global Championship Wrestling?” That was where this innocent man was heading. Into the mouth of the corporate dragon of America, and onto the screens of the viewing public. He had left his contemporary safe life in China, for the world of risk and opportunity, and into the GCW he had gone. He was to become a wrestler, a good wrestler, perhaps a great wrestler. When he’d heard stories about Rich Rollins and Seymour Almasy on the television sets he’d encountered, and heard about the American wrestling industry, he had been instantly excited. And now, a member of the wrestling organisation’the excitement brought a tear to the young Chinese hopeful’s eye. His new life was starting’a new land, and a new occupation. But he was, and ever would be, a warrior of Chin Woo. Tyler Xing was now settled in his hotel room in Alabama. The first event he would appear at was near the hotel, in Mobile, so transportation wouldn’t be a problem. He’d spent a few days in New York, searching the sights and watching the intriguing people, before being whisked by his duties into this completely different state. “Just like China” he thought, “The people are so different in the south”. His first opponent had been scheduled, and was a man named Machaeveli St. Romani. He was excited about starting in the business, and about taking this opportunity with both hands. He didn’t have much of a wrestling past, so when he’d been accepted into the organisation, it had been quite a shock. But what he was good at, was Martial Arts. Having studied since the age of eight, Tyler Xing was a great Artist of the Chinese Discipline of Tan Tui. He’d been narrowly beaten in the semi finals of the Chin Woo 6th World Championship. But now, he was here to prove himself to a much larger audience, in a sport he knew little about, in a country he’d never stepped foot’it was exhausting to even think about. But Tyler was sure, that through the teachings of Chin Woo, through the principles and concepts he’d mastered at the monastery, and with the blessing of his teacher and the enthusiasm and drive of his spirit’he could do no wrong. Where there was injustice, he would be there. Where there was an opportunity to extend the Chin Woo ways, he would be there. When push came to shove, and he stood in the ring with foreign assailants’Nightwater Xing would carry the ambition of his people, and the integrity of a true warrior of Chin Woo.
