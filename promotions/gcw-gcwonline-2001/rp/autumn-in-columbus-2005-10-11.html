Autumn in Columbus

11 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10-11-05 Autumn in Columbus, Ohio, rolls in with a thick wave of pale, dismal cloud cover that remains until spring. The air grows chill and foreboding, as if the sky is waiting on a terrible storm that never seems to come. The breeze blows subtly, hardly noticeable but penetrating and inescapable. For the remainder of the year, the city will go about its business in a sunless gloom, ever shrouded beneath this unyielding, merciless shadow. The offices of Caldera Enterprises, Incorporated, lie in the center of downtown Columbus, at the center of the shade. Crowded by neighboring buildings that tower overhead, the lonely fifteen story structure remains proud but dormant in the cool air. A quiet chill hangs about it, as its occupants bustle in and out, silently seeking quick passage through the season. Business remains brisk but somehow more subdued, and the intensity and sheer velocity of the summer rush had finally given way to the hushed calm of winter. The lush greenery has begun to fade to a sad brown, with even the vibrant fall colors muted by the hanging gloom. The top floor of Caldera Enterprises houses the impressive office of President Steven Caldera. In the summertime this office boasts a wide view of bustling downtown Columbus, with massive windows surrounding a world-class contemporary wonderland of technology and comfort. The bright summer sun shines into the office most of the day, keeping the spectacular office brightly lit and warm. But the cooler months had descended, and now the office runs with quiet efficiency, the heavy drapes closed to block out the darkness. The brilliant light afforded by the hanging chandeliers and sconces is adequate but obviously artificial, bright but somehow lacking the same luminescence of natural sunlight. Alas, the sun had descended into a five month slumber, leaving the lonely President and Owner of Caldera Enterprises, Incorporated, to go about his business in solemn isolation. Steven Caldera, having recently turned thirty, had accomplished tremendous things in his career. Now his pride and joy, the crown jewel in a global, multi-billion dollar operation, had reached a new peak of excellence, and he had finally found the opportunity to leave it to its own devices. It was with a happy heart that he had told the fans of his soaring promotion that he was no longer needed, that Global Championship Wrestling had finally assumed so much talent that his watchful eye was becoming little more than a nuisance and a bother. He didn’t want to play puppet master any more; for years he had dreamt of running his promotion from behind the scenes, doing only what was necessary from the cozy confines of his own private office. But now just over a week removed from this monumental decision, Steven Caldera had begun experiencing something unexpected. It started subtly at first, still hidden behind his glee. But as the novelty of more free time had worn off, he started to feel it. It began as a throb in his arm, an ache in his back. It progressed until he was forced to break every thirty minutes to walk a lap around the office. It was the undeniable effect of loneliness. All his career, Caldera had demanded to do things himself. His unparalleled creativity and brilliant professional wrestling mind had led him to a life of great success, but great isolation. His insistence on having complete control over every aspect of his operations, his constant micro-managing of every tiny detail, had driven those away that might have been his colleagues. He had finally begun to delegate and get rid of some of his more high-profile duties, but the seclusion had set in. He had earned the reputation of being a brilliant businessman and a sharp thinker, but difficult to work with, or for. Despite his active attempts to become more friendly and shake a few hands, people still had trouble connecting with their reclusive boss. It had come to the point that most of the people working in Caldera Enterprises had never even seen Caldera himself, and the popular rumor currently floating around the office was that Caldera himself did not exist. Were it not for his hours of archived wrestling footage, Caldera might have believed the rumor himself. Now that he had left the weekly production of his wrestling show, WorldWide, Caldera found very little need to interact with anyone but his secretary. CEi had expanded into the realms of music and movie production, as well as management and consulting. He had hired the best of the best to run the separate divisions of his company, preferring to handle the wrestling portion personally. But now, no longer dealing with the daily issues of the wrestlers, having handed that job down to young Tiffany York, he found himself spending his time signing paperwork and reviewing financial numbers. These more menial aspects of business he had mastered years ago, to the point that he was one of the most respected businessmen in the country. But they had never held his attention; his true passion was professional wrestling. He loved the sport almost to a fault, to the point that he was often criticized internally for failing to devote enough time to the less successful aspects of the company. Of course, every branch of Caldera Enterprises was highly successful under the direction of Caldera and his partners, but Caldera himself was rarely seen at any of the other offices worldwide. His passion was wrestling, and that’s where he devoted his time. Sitting in his throne-like leather chair, Caldera loosens his red silk tie and sighs. The massive surface of his elegant desk was covered in meticulously organized stacks of paperwork. Legal briefs from a movie shoot in Taiwan. New contract offers to potential music clients. He even had paperwork detailing the procedure for purchasing an NBA team. He had been hard at work all day, but the lonely feeling had caught up with him again, and he was finding it harder and harder to work through. When he had first started in the wrestling business, his phone was ringing constantly, with people wanting to interview him, offer him jobs, or simply be around him. Before he had branched out, Caldera was regarded as one of the best pure wrestling minds in the world, and one of his secret passions was meeting fans on the road and chatting with them, letting them pick his brain over a few drinks at the local bar. But now his phone sat silent, the only noises in the office the creak of his leather chair and the light gust of wind on the windows. Caldera clears his throat, and the sudden loudness actually causes his to jump in his seat. He hadn’t received a single caller at his door, his phone only ringing once; his secretary informing him that she was taking her lunch break. He had built such an efficient, well-oiled machine around him that his presence was hardly needed. Oftentimes he considered vacationing in South America to pass the winter, and wondered if the company would even realize he had left. He could spend his time lying in the sun on a warm beach in Chile, in a place where he might once again find some genuinely interesting people who would be genuinely interested in him. Shaking the vision of sunny beaches and bikinis from his head, Caldera forces himself to pick up his pen again. But moving his hand back to his paper, he finds himself completely unable to press on. With a grunt he drops the pen onto the desk and rapidly pushes out, spinning away from the desk. In his hurry, he clumsily bumps his knee onto a sharp corner of his desk, causing him to cry out in pain. Suddenly very aware of the pathetic sound of his cry, Caldera scowls in frustration, rubbing his throbbing kneecap. He had once been one of the toughest technical wrestlers in the world. Now he found himself becoming clumsy in his isolation, his body and mind no longer moving in unison. Standing slowly from his chair, Caldera steps down off the raised section of the office where his desk sits in a place of honor. He had positioned it centrally in the room, against the wide panoramic windows to create a halo of light around himself during the daytime, a dominant seat to intimidate his visitors. But the sun was hidden away behind the shroud of grey cloud outside, and his visitors no longer had use for him. A small door sits in an inconspicuous corner of the room, its handle painted to blend in with the wall. Caldera turns the knob and pushes his way inside, entering his private stateroom. A king-sized bed, complete with silk sheets and a velvet cover, occupy a bedroom worthy of the finest home. Passing through the room and pushing through another door, Caldera enters his bathroom. With a large walk-in shower, a Jacuzzi bathtub, large walk-in closet containing an impressive wardrobe, sauna and massage parlor, Caldera’s secret hideaway was meant to ease the time spent at the office. In the early days of Caldera Enterprises, Caldera found himself spending weeks on end at the office, and even considered selling his home and moving to the office full-time. Having never found the time to marry, and being on some level a loner, Caldera enjoyed the freedom of working until he could work no more, then moving only a few feet to his right to sleep a few hours before showering and returning to work again the next morning. Caldera reaches into the shower and turns the lever, producing a thick stream of immediately steaming water. Caldera undresses and steps inside, sighing a breath of relief as the hot air fills his lungs. Rejuvenated, Caldera can think of nothing else to do but let out a thunderous scream. The sound of his voice fills the shower, ripping through the entire bathroom. Caldera finally reaches the end of his breath and stops, satisfied that at least for a moment he had heard the sound of someone’s voice. Chuckling again in spite of himself, Caldera tried to force away the obvious conclusion of just how bad things were getting. After spending nearly an hour in the shower, standing motionless while a constant stream of searing hot water pounded his flesh, Caldera manages to force himself out. He turns off the water and dries himself off, walking to the bathroom mirror. Fogged from the steamy shower, Caldera wipes clear a large ring in the center of the mirror, looking at himself closely. His black hair was dripping and mangled, his face looking worn and unclean. Though only thirty, Caldera suddenly became alert to how old he looked. His complexion seemed white, as if he was a specter, lingering too long in a world to which he no longer belonged. Then, suddenly, as if suddenly his heart had caught fire, his face flushes with color. His body tenses, his still fine-tuned physique suddenly looking impressive like the young wrestler of a decade past. A familiar thought unexpectedly flickers into his mind, fleeting at first, but gaining strength. Caldera shuts his eyes tight, as if trying to shut the notion out. He knew precisely what it was, and he had promised himself and his employees that it would never happen again. But the idea gains momentum, until it begins running through his mind like a freight train, barreling over all notions of reason and restraint. After a moment’s battle, Caldera finally wills the thought from his mind, rapidly bringing himself back down to earth. “No” No,” Caldera says aloud, shaking his head surely. “It’s over, that’s all there is to it.” Deep down inside, Steven Caldera had always remained a professional wrestler. It was his biggest regret in life that, though it was beyond his control, he had suffered a series of injuries that prevented him from ever competing regularly in the ring again. Even stepping into the ring one more time bore the risk of severe permanent injury. But injury wasn’t his concern. It was a matter of evolution. That part of his life was over, and he was entering a new phase. There were greater things he wanted to accomplish, and to get there he had to put aside the fun, grit his teeth and do what was required of him. He had made a promise, and under no circumstances could he allow himself to break that promise. “I will never go back,” he says aloud again, staring at himself in the mirror, deeply engaged in his own dire stare. “There is nothing that can bring me back.” Caldera stands unblinking, leaning in close, as if doing battle with his own will. His own magnetic blue eyes stared back, still unwilling to yield their desire. Finally Caldera drops his head and shuts his eyes, shaking the images from his head. Once more he raises his stare and is somehow relieved to find the same subdued, lifeless eyes staring back at him. “Nothing.”
