First in Line at the Unemployment Offices

13 June 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/13/2009 His world has a name for people like him. But he. He's a walking "fuck you" to everything they can think of to attach to his soul. He tells himself this when he is lost. This is Frank Black: falling down into these pits of loathing when he's whatever enough to drop his apathy and to actually slip into life, curious at how it all plays out. This is Jordan White: attacking people at random on national television, speaking ill of the dead, and acting like he's too good to give a fuck. He doesn't see the point where the two meet, and he's just unwrapping his second pack of the day and it's something he's found a way to actually, honestly, truly feel good about. Like he's earned this cancer more than he earned the first 20, like he can find some secret aspect of enjoying them more than the average man. Delusional is about half of what Frank Black is, and it's just not fair to the other half of what he makes up that there's this major section of his brain dedicated only to everything he doesn't want to be, consumed into his personality as some cruel twist of luck or fate or whatever applies to the people that believe in little, with self rarely included in the sample. He doesn't really have friends, Frank Black, because he wasn't human enough to deal with the empathy, and he's named it a thousand different things but it's always going to come back to that one tiny point. He can relate; he just can't find the avenue that brings himself to care. This is Frank Black. He's trashed consistent and he's stoned magnificent. He doesn't walk; he swaggers. He doesn't smile, he smirks. He's a former champion and he carries this on his shoulder like anyone cares when the word "former" gets affixed; like anyone cared when it wasn't, really. His locker room is nothing but stale smoke at the end of a show, and it's been lately that he's mostly drunk enough to need a taxi to take him to sanctuary when they close the curtains and let him free for the night. He interviews infreqently and he's always glib when someone does manage to rope him down from this upper echelon he's believed himself onto. He flirts with the staff and doesn't realize it. There are parts of him that despise everyone he works with, and these don't mesh entirely with the parts of him that think he's above their level. The average show costs his employer, JXW Enterprises or Incorporated or it doesn't even matter, just upwards of fifty thousand dollars for every three minutes of Jordan behind a microphone. This is Jordan White. Frank Black thinks they're paying too much. His employer disagrees and these people he pays have put together charts that show, conclusively, that every three minutes of GCW's Jordan White brings in tenfold more money than he could lose if he were profane with a perfect efficiency. Frank Black isn't sure this justifies anything, but his apartment has three massive televisions and he's not about to voice the first concern about his paycheck or any other amount of greenbacks that revolve around the character he plays when cameras roll. He doesn't see Jordan White as himself acting out without social limiters; he doesn't see Jordan White as what Frank Black becomes when people cheer, or at least allow, for any inane action he starts and about half of the ones that he manages to go through with. Frank Black is no longer holding his morning coughs in until he can insulate himself in a bathroom with multiple sources of running water; he's hacking when he wakes up and he's not even shrugging to regard Leslie's little grunts and laughs. He just doesn't care like he used to and he doesn't know if it's him or her or the general way of his existence that's leading it; either way, he's not surprised and he spent years preparing for a second chance with her; like he wouldn't have spent just as long preparing for the day it all fell through. He loves her. Putting it bluntly, he's loved Her since he met Her and he'll probably love Her until he dies; his problems all stem from her not measuring up to Her and, if pressed, he would guess that all fell back on him anyway. Jordan White has a clever, productive, and selfsufficient girlfriend that's willing to, in the middle of an intense legal battle over custody of her child, travel halfway across the country on the drop of a hat to accommodate his schedule, to accommodate for spending some amount of time with him beyond the bare minimum. He smiles for cameras and he promotes a line of clothes in brief commercials and bizarre billboards strung up across his nation and the nations of many that have nothing to offend him. He told his boss to fuck off and the world cheered and he'll tell the world the exact same syllables as soon as they give him a reason. The world should know this, but the world doesn't really think through many of the things within its boundaries; so they still cheer when he acts like he's lost his mind, so they still cheer when he goes brutal animal with a steel chair against people that barely deserve acknowledgment, much less condemnation. But Jordan White's a job and Frank Black is a life and, if they were actually distinct from one another, neither would want those lines to blur. It's four cigarettes later, it's Frank Black chaining addictions between shots and his girl's with the lawyer and he's got nothing better to do but submit to every whim that crosses the twitching gray goop he used to presume as a mind. He's not so sure anymore. Relegated to just another organ in a machine that would work fine without his intervention--kidney status, with the space to drop to spleen. Entertained in spurts, but never well fed. But if, beyond those things that swirl and the universal secret he has tripped across and collected, he has any room left between his temples, it guards the knowledge that he will not discuss the rest; that to start is the same as to tangent, that he does not have the words available to, in conversation with the uninvolved, make any progress and is better off with anything that takes several words to say nothing. He has also come to understand a second option: that it is not qite the pressure at all, that when he opens his mouth and lets flow what will, he's venting only the air from his stained lungs and comes no closer to relieving himself of whatever burdens, assumed or otherwise, have taken residence at the peak of his spine. It defined him or it grew to define him; that notion that, all faults aside, if he could talk this girl into lounging on his level, why wouldnt he?, which became, eventually, an inability to believe he had come across anything he had honestly, that he had ever earned in the proper sense of the word; just conned and suckered every step of the way. That his life, borne on the same random gusts that spread rubber ducks to sea, was the byproduct entirely of forces outside of his control and that he, passenger at best and hitchhiker at nearly all other points in time, could only hope the right driver would for a momentary lapse in judgment decide that he could tag along. It wasn't entirely literal, his status as a passenger, and he would sometimes try to find a name better suited to his condition but all attempts were cumbersome, culminating finally in a long string German flavored conjunctions, entire sentences daisy chained heterogeneous and none reaching his heart in the proper way for him to ever consider them Right. But; what effort there was behind a good con. What talent it took to lie like that.
