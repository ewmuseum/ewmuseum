1.5 - Funeral

21 January 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/21/11 Though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil; for Thou art with me; Thy rod and Thy staff, they comfort me. – 23rd Psalm Prayer 1 Even though he had been awake for nearly a full hour, there had been nothing able to clear the thick haze that was seemingly settled over Kevin Erwin’s eyes. The world seemed to be slightly blurry this morning and it was doing nothing to ease the nauseous feeling in the pit of his stomach. Standing in the shower, the hot water pounded against his bare flesh, but even that did nothing to quell the rumbling of his stomach. “Fucking whiskey,” he mumbled to himself. He ran a bar of soap across his body, doing his best to scrub away at the hangover that was nagging him. Already through two glasses of water and nearly a full bottle of Advil and still his head was throbbing and his stomach aching. In search of refuge from the pain of last’s night’s indiscretions he closed his eyes, and rested his forehead against the ceramic tiles of his shower. The tiles felt cool against his skin, and it was the first reprieve from sickness that he had felt all morning. As he stood with his eyes closed and the water continuing to pour around him, he wondered if it was really the hangover that had him feeling so out of place. He pondered if it might actually be something else that was causing the nauseous feeling running through his entire body. It seemed like only days ago that Special Agent Rios had been assigned to his detail. He still remembered all of Rios’ features on the first day that he met him. A bright smile, intelligent glowing eyes, and a sense of being young and carefree that was absent from most agents. But Rios was new to the FBI, new to the game. And new meant something else, Erwin had heard it all. Too inexperienced, too naive, not ready, that’s what the higher-ups had told him when he requested Rios for undercover work. But, he hadn’t listened to them, instead he rebelled and pushed harder for Rios to go under, even though it was obvious that he wasn’t ready. The minute Rios came to him that day a week ago and said that he wasn’t ready. Erwin knew what that had meant, he had seen it before and he saw it that night, the fear in the young Agent’s eyes. The second he saw that, he knew that the kid wasn’t ready, but still he sent him out there anyways. Erwin was snapped from the painful memories by the sound of his cell phone vibrating on the bathroom floor. The phone had been vibrating ever since last night. He had several missed calls, someone had been trying to call him all night. But Erwin didn’t answer, he found all the company he needed from a man named Jim Beam. It was Erazo, that much he knew, somewhere around the third or fourth call last night, he had managed to stumble to the phone to check the Caller I.D. But still, he never bothered to answer it. Apparently, Officer Erazo didn’t quite take hints very well. He opened his eyes and there was still the pain in his stomach and an ache in his head. Erwin still wasn’t sure if it was the liquor, or guilt or something more, but whatever it was, the pain was crippling him. He turned the knobs of his shower and stopped the rushing water, before stepping onto the cold linoleum floor of his bathroom. He grabbed a towel and began to dry his body, hoping that he would be able to wash away some of the pain and some of the guilt. But as Kevin Erwin looked at himself in the mirror, still dripping wet, with the towel hanging in his hands, seeing his own reflection staring back at him, he knew that he couldn’t wipe away this guilt. Not this time... 2 “Why the hell won’t he answer me?” Miguel muttered under his breath as he flipped his cell phone closed for the third time that morning. He had been trying to reach Unit Chief Erwin all morning, but all he had gotten so far was his voice mail. Miguel checked the clock and saw that he had only about a half hour to finish getting ready. He was expected to be at the funeral for FBI Special Agent Rios at noon, and it was a twenty minute drive in normal traffic. With half of the cops and local FBI Authorities in the San Diego County area going to this event, the traffic was surely to double the drive. He threw his cell phone on the bed and walked towards his closet, searching for his black ceremonial Police issue uniform. He spied the suit easily sticking out on a hanger, clearly Angelina had found it for him earlier. Thinking of his fiancé brought a sudden heaviness to his heart. He thought about the pained look in her face last night when she found out that he had been drinking. He thought about the hurt in her voice when she said goodbye to him this morning. Most of all, he thought about how peaceful she looked last night and how warm she felt cuddled in his arms. The thoughts of Agent Rios brought darker thoughts to his head. Miguel began to think about what if it was him whose life they were honouring today. He had always wanted more action, hell he still did. He remembered how jealous he was when he found out that Rios would be the one working undercover on the Los Aztecas case, the thought of it now was almost enough to make him sick. Miguel thought about Rios’ family, his young wife dressed all in black. Her makeup ruined and running from the crying and tears. He thought about his two young sons, dressed in their best suits, still too young to really understand why daddy wouldn’t be coming home. And as selfish as that was, he thought about Angelina and he thought about their unborn daughter. Could he bear to put them through that? To put them through the pain and the tears of the black dresses? Miguel wasn’t sure if he would be able to and he prayed to God that he wouldn’t ever have to. But the more that he thought about Kevin Erwin’s words, the more he thought that he might have to. Miguel quickly began to dress himself, staring in the mirror, he looked into his own eyes. He searched his soul for the answers that he wasn’t sure he had. The whole time, Erwin’s words still echoed in his mind. ”Can I count on you?” 3 Captain Phillip Toms was already on his way to the Greenwood Memorial Cemetery and Funeral Home when his cell phone began to vibrate. He quickly reached inside his jacket and clicked the Accept Call button to answer his phone. He pressed his cell phone against his ear and quickly spoke. “This is Captain Toms,” he spoke into the phone’s mouthpiece. ”Captain Toms, this is Chase. I’m on my way to pick up Erazo now, we should be arriving just after 11:30,” the familiar voice of Tommy Chase answered. “Very good Chase,” Phillip replied. “You and Erazo worked with Rios, so you’re not going to be part of the security detail, you guys are going to be seated along with me.” ”Yes sir,” Chase answered. “Okay Tommy. We’ll see you and Miguel soon then,” he said before closing the cell phone and returning it to his pocket. Toms returned his attention to the road and saw that he was almost to his destination. He clicked his signal and entered the turning lane, waiting for the chance to turn into the parking lot of the large cemetery. Rios wasn’t from San Diego, he was actually from Los Angeles, but the service was being held here, before his body was sent to be cremated. Toms watched as two senior citizens in a Lincoln Town car lurched their way past him, before he turned into the lot. He quickly found a parking spot, realizing that he had arrived well before most of the other guests. Toms parked the car and stepped out, a warm blast of the morning’s heat greeted him as he exited his air conditioned, climate controlled automobile. Just as he closed the door and was turning to walk towards the reception area, he felt his phone begin to vibrate once again. He reached into his jacket once again to pull out his cell phone. However, when he looked at the screen he realized that the phone wasn’t ringing. His other cell phone was ringing. “What the fuck are they calling me this early for?” he mumbled to himself, as he pulled out the other cell phone. He clicked the accept call button and brought the phone to his ear. He opened his mouth and prepared to answer, but he was cut off by a harsh and intense voice. ”12:45 we’ll be visiting. Keep your head down.” And before he could even speak a word, the line was dead once again. Somewhat stunned and surprised Captain Phillip Toms took a quick survey of his surroundings. Other than a few squirrels and birds, there was no one around to hear or see his quick and very unusual phone call. He quickly replaced the cell phone in his pocket, and straightened his jacket. Quickly, he turned on his heels and headed towards the reception area once again. 4 The parking lot was already beginning to fill when Kevin Erwin’s black FBI-Issued GMC Yukon pulled in. He glanced around at the faces nervously, feeling slightly ashamed of being here already. Erwin studied the faces, but saw none that he recognized, but that did nothing to help relieve him of the nerves that he was feeling. He found a spot far from the front of the parking lot and pulled the SUV into the spot. He killed the engine and reached for the door. His stomach was still doing somersaults and all of the Advil and Pepto Bismol in the world, was doing nothing to stop it. The pain in his head was finally beginning to quell, but he felt as if he removed his dark sunglasses, the bright midday sun might bring that crashing back full steam as well. Erwin reached for the door of the black truck, but hesitated momentarily. There were still no faces that he recognized, no one to hide from, but still he felt the guilt and the shame wash over him. He considered himself a strong, usually well-composed individual, and he definitely wasn’t easily rattled, but this was the first time a man under his command had been killed. Needless to say, Kevin Erwin wasn’t dealing with his first time very well. He withdrew his hand from the door handle, the door making a clicking sound as it latched itself back into place. He reached down into the pocket of his pants and felt around for his speech. He pulled out the heavily folded and slightly sloppy looking piece of paper and began to run his eyes across the words. He did his best to play back the speech in his mind, but he stumbled through the words, his mind racing too quickly to even say, let alone comprehend the words in front of him. He placed the papers on the passenger seat beside him and reached into the inside breast pocket of his jacket. He pulled out a small sterling silver container and quickly unscrewed the lid. The scent of cheap whiskey flooded his nostrils and it immediately aggravated the queasiness in his stomach. He shook it away and quickly pressed his lips against the flask. He tilted his head back, and let the warm liquor burn all the way down his throat and into his stomach. He waited a few seconds, to see if his body would reject the poison. He figured it was about a fifty-fifty proposition on whether or not he would vomit. Instead, the booze had the opposite effect on him. He began to relax slightly, and for the first time all morning, his stomach began to settle. Erwin shook his head and did his best to try to put the rest of his body at ease, he took another look at his speech, the pieces of paper scattered haplessly across the leather interior of the SUV. Immediately, something began to clutch at him and his body began to feel sick again, quickly he drained another shot of whiskey from the flask and again he began to relax. He clasped the lid of the flask and returned it to the inside of his jacket, hoping that at least until after his speech, he wouldn’t need it. 5 Miguel walked beside his partner Tommy through the sea of strangers and unknown faces. They did their best to push their way through the crowd, without making too much of an intrusion during this time of grieving. The two officers marched in stride towards the ceremony that had been set up for Agent Rios. There were already thousands of people gathered, many of them fellow officers and friends, few of them family, who felt the deepest loss of all. As Miguel approached the middle of the cemetery, the arrangements came into view. There were flowers everywhere, and a large black and white picture of Steven Rios from the day he graduated from the FBI Academy, surrounded by a wreath. There was a cherry-maple casket in front of the picture and wreath, with the top spread open. Miguel kept his head down as he walked behind his partner, walking towards one of the officers in charge of seating and keeping the crowd organized. “Chase and Erazo,” Tommy said to one of the officers as they strode up. “We were on the same team as Rios.” The officer simply nodded his reply and offered a handshake for his condolences before directing the two of them to their seating arrangement. As the two of them walked towards their seats, Miguel stole a glance inside the casket. Rios looked like he was at peace now, wearing a fine black designer suit with his FBI badge clipped onto the breast pocket of the jacket. Without warning, Miguel found himself reliving the night of Rios’ death. He thought about the sound of the motorcycles, the way that the engines had started from a low rumbling but turned into a sound so loud that is was like a monster. He remembered the way that the submachine guns made the midnight sky light up, every shot fired illuminating the pained and terrified expressions on Rios’ face. Miguel shook the thoughts from his head as he took his seat, but it was enough that his partner noticed the discomfort in his face. “Miguel, what’s wrong?” Tommy asked. Miguel said nothing, he simply shook his head and waved his hand. He raised his eyes from the ground and immediately caught the stare of who he assumed could only be Rios’ wife. She was a beautiful woman. Young, with beautiful hazel eyes, long flowing black hair. Her skin was well-tanned, but very natural, toned by the sun, not by some artificial machine. She was gorgeous, but the pain in her eyes was unmistakable. Miguel stared back at her, but in an instant her eyes moved along, but somewhere in there, Miguel had felt a connection with her. He wondered if she knew exactly what had happened to her husband, or if she could ever face the awful truth of his demise. Other members of the team tasked to investigate Los Aztecas were beginning to file in heavily now, the seats behind and around Erazo and Chase were beginning to fill. In the sea of faces, Miguel found himself constantly staring at Rios’ wife. He wondered what he would say to her, if he ever got the chance to speak to her. He wondered if telling her the grisly details of her husband’s death would bring closure or more pain to an already mourning family. A minister stepped to the podium and began to speak, reading from the Lord’s 23rd Psalm of Prayer, dragging Miguel back to reality. Though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil; for Thou art with me; Thy rod and Thy staff, they comfort me. 6 Captain Toms stared straight ahead, his eyes unflinching but his mind was running wild. Things were beginning to get wild quickly. He couldn’t even begin to fathom what made El Guerra think that this was a good idea. They were already on the FBI’s Shit List, especially after killing an Agent, and now they had this planned. His hands were shaking nervously, but he did his best to hide the anxiety from those around him. He sat in the front row, not far from Rios’ widow and children. The kids looked stunned and confused, they were still too young to completely understand. For her part, his wife seemed strong, but she was beginning to lose control. The tears were beginning to flow now and her makeup was beginning to run. But even all of that couldn’t hide her true beauty. Toms found himself staring at her, her allure a welcome distraction from the ramblings of his mind. The Unit Chief of the FBI Kevin Erwin, had just begun his speech. He was talking about how bright of a future Rios had, talking about his past in the FBI Academy and in college. But Toms nervously looked back to his watch, it was already 12:37, they’d be showing up any minute now. His nervousness and impatience began to grow, and Toms found it harder and harder to control his emotions. His leg began to shake, enough that some of the police around him were beginning to notice. He was beginning to sweat, but it was easy enough to blame the scorching mid-afternoon sun for that. But the nervous shaking and glances at his watch would be harder to explain. He nervously glanced around the ceremony, trying to spot anyone who might notice him. He saw nothing, most of the faces were strangers, and everyone seemed to be hanging on Erwin’s every word. ‘What a fucking idiot,’ Toms thought to himself. ‘It’s your fault this kid is dead, you brought him into this mess.’ But still, the entire audience seemed to be hanging on his every word. Even Toms had to admit, there was the sound of sincere conviction and sorrow in Erwin’s voice. His natural charisma beginning to shine through, inspiring those in need of any bright spot during a painful time like this. Toms checked his watch again. 12:38. He stole another quick glance around, hoping that no one would notice. He pretended to listen to Erwin’s speech but his mind was elsewhere. Racing a hundred miles an hour down a speedway, heading straight towards impending danger and death. After a pause in the speech, Toms stared at his watch again. 12:40... 7 FBI Unit Chief Kevin Erwin was still giving his speech, but Miguel couldn’t even focus on the words. His eyes kept wandering to Rios’ crying wife and children and his mind kept replaying the nightmare of his death. He watched as Erwin began to lead the crowd in prayer, a prayer for the safety of Rios’ wife and sons. And a prayer for the apprehension of those responsible. Miguel looked around at the gathered mourners, searching for a familiar face. He looked at Erwin, but Erwin was either unaware of his presence, or was being very careful not to look in his direction. That’s when he spotted Captain Toms across the crowd, he looked nervous, and was visibly sweating and shaking. Miguel wondered what was going on. That’s when Kevin Erwin’s voice began to take over in his head. But it wasn’t the words he was currently speaking, it was instead the words that he had spoken to Erazo in confidence during their meeting earlier in the week. ”There’s a leak in this department somewhere Miguel. I’m just not sure where, and to be honest, I think it might be Captain Toms.” Miguel shook the thoughts from his mind, but never took his eyes off of his Captain and mentor. Erwin was crazy, Toms wasn’t a traitor, he bled SDPD Blue. Hell Toms had already taken bullets for the San Diego Police Department. Miguel saw the Captain check his watch, which didn’t really register in his thought process, before he turned back to Erwin. In the distance there was a humming, a faint rumbling somewhere far away. But whatever it was, it was getting closer. Miguel paid it little attention at first, before something in his brain clicked into high gear. He recognized that sound. It was the sound of motorcycle engines. And it was getting closer. As the rumbling began to grow louder and louder, and in turn closer and closer, a visible discomfort began to wash over the crowd. Members of the Police Department began to look at each other quizzically, wondering what was causing the commotion. While family and friends of Agent Rios cursed God for interrupting the wake for their dearly departed friend. Miguel looked at Tommy, who was just now beginning to notice the sound of the engines drawing closer, he then turned his attention back to Captain Toms, who now seemed to be in full awareness mode. His head was swivelling quickly from left to right, surveying the scene, searching in all directions for the source of the noise. But then again, he looked at his watch. The engines were now a dull roar and were closing quickly. It was like a thunder rolling towards the funeral service and the entire crowd was becoming audibly nervous. People began to whisper and shuffle their feet, necks began to crane, searching for a glimpse of what might be happening. That’s when Miguel heard a sound that was all too familiar to him. He felt like he was dreaming, but the chaos around him quickly reminded him that he was not reliving his nightmares. The sound of gunfire tore through the cemetery and echoed throughout the open air. People screamed in terror and began to run in a hysterical panic. The sound of thousands screaming was almost enough to drown out the horrible sound of bullets whizzing by, but Miguel could barely notice that, all he could hear was the roar of the engines and the buzzing of machine gun bullets. Bodies began to fall, people began to cry out for help, but Miguel was too stunned to do anything. It was exactly like the scene from a few nights ago, but here in the middle of a funeral, in the middle of the day. The hysteria had brought a new feeling to the cemetery, you could almost feel the panic and fear in the air. After a few seconds, Miguel heard Tommy calling for him. “Erazo! Fuck Erazo! Listen to me! Shots fired! Shots fired! We’ve got to get these people out of here!” Miguel had a stunned look on his face when he turned to his partner, but he quickly turned that to a look of determination. He stared at his partner and nodded emphatically, before standing to his feet. When he looked across to get instructions from Captain Toms, the strangest thing had happened. Toms was nowhere to be found, he had already disappeared into the waves of chaos. Miguel looked at the scene around him, masses of humans running for their lives, terrified for their safety. This was terrorism at its most carnal of definitions. He had no time to think about the reason for this attack, he had no time to think about anything. He turned to Chase and immediately they began to hurry through the crowd, searching for any victims of the gunshots. But no matter how busy he made himself, Erwin’s accusations of the Captain kept running through his mind. As much as it killed him to admit it, Miguel was slowly letting the truth dawn on him. “He knew...”
