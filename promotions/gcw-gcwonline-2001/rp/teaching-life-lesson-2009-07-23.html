Teaching Life lesson

23 July 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07.23.09 “Run FASTER,” I scream at the panting group of boys as they run around the gymnasium. Its been hard to adjust to life in hiding. Not only did I have to change my name and move to a quiet little suburban town; but I’ve also had to take charge of the worst high school wrestling team in the fucking world. To be fair, it’s not entirely their fault. They’ve had the misfortune to be stuck in one of those rare schools that emphasize academics over athletics…but still. There’s no reason that these pussies shouldn’t be able to run at full speed for ten minutes without vomiting. The other problem I’m having is the shitty state that the athletic equipment is in. The gym equipment is falling apart, and most of it’s broken. The treadmills have a tendency to send out smoke if you try to crank them up above six miles per hour. And the free weights…well if I try to talk about them I truly think I am going to break down and cry. The entire reason I’m in hiding is that a few weeks ago, someone tried to poison me. I’m fairly certain I know who it was, but my uncle disagrees. Regardless, to avoid any further attempts, I became Alex Goldberg, high school gym teacher. Frankly, I am beginning to think I would have been better off just taking the direct approach. It would be very hard for Lorenzo to try and poison me after I make his head a permanent part of his house’s décor. Unfortunately, I don’t particularly like going to jail, and there’s a small chance the asshole isn’t the one who tried to kill me. So until my uncle figures out whom it was, I am stuck in this suburban paradise. I hope to hell you heard the sarcasm in that last sentence. “Alright bring it in,” I bark out as it becomes clear that the boys can’t take anymore. Gratefully the five would-be wrestlers drag themselves into a circle around me. As I stare at them I feel two things, contempt and despair. These boys are not what you would call star athletes. In fact, in a regular school they would be the water boys and the star athletes’ personal punching bags. But I am their wrestling coach, and I will not abandon them until the season is over. Besides, torturing them with brutal training regimes is fun and oddly a great stress reliever. The five boys around me range from freshman to juniors. To be specific there are two freshman, two sophomores, and a junior. One of the freshman, named Steve, is going to one day be bigger than I am. Unfortunately for him he falls into the gentle giant category, and it’s a challenge for me to bring out any aggressiveness in him at all, much less the type required to win a wrestling match. The other freshman, George, is a small, one hundred pound squirt, who could probably give Bagwell lessons on cleverness. Some of the moves he manages to pull out frankly amaze me, but from what I’m told he’s a practiced superstar. Basically when it matters, he turns into the most incompetent idiot possible, and that’s not very conducive to the whole trying to win thing. I’ll skip the descriptions of the sophomores. Suffice to say, they’re twins, they’re huge (in the fat kind of way), kind of dumb, and basically should be bullying the underclass. Once I’m done with them, no freshman with lunch money is going to be safe. No, I’m not in need of professional help. Lastly the junior is named Ben. He has no confidence in himself, a lack of social skills, and is apparently the school outcast. However he is as strong as an ox, and really that’s all that I need. I’m well aware that conventional coaching wisdom says that I should give them a speech about doing their best and the value of hard work. I should say that the important thing is that they give it all they got, and have fun. The only problem I have with that idiocy is that I prefer an approach that actually works. “By show of hands,” I say with my arms crossed across my chest, “who here has gotten laid?” To show them I’m serious I raise my hand, and wait for someone to follow suit. It is no surprise that not one of them raises a hand, and more than one of them are starting to blush in embarrassment. “Well that is pathetically sad,” I tell them frankly. “Now on the other hand, who here is smart enough to compete academically with the top half of your class.” Again not one of them raises their hands. They are all looking down at the floor, ashamed at their failures. I admit that there is a sadistic little grin on my face as I drive the point home, because I know that by the time I am done with them they’d be willing to train all day and night. “Here are the facts boys. Socially, you five are lower than the nerds who inspect their snot after they blow their noses. Not to mention, you look especially dumb when you’re compared to the all the Asians in this school that sleep in the library and masturbate to calculus. But I can offer you a chance.” They look up at me in surprise. They have long ago written themselves off as abject failures, and no doubt believed I was about to quit on them as well. But despite the fact that they have no talent, an unsupportive school, and a dearth of proper equipment, they do have something no other wrestling team in the nation has. They have me as a coach, and it is now my mission to turn each one of them into …well into me. Can you think of a better role model? I didn’t think so. “I’m willing to help you boys out,” I continue confidently. “Not only can I turn you into a team that can actually win a few meets, but I can turn you into the kings of the school like you’re supposed to be. But for that to happen you have to do everything I say, without question, without hesitation. If you don’t, I’ll take that as proof that you don’ have what it takes and simply move on. Deal?” They all look at each other, all wanting to agree but all scared to take the first step. Slowly, Ben, the only junior in the bunch nods his head. He is hesitant at first, but soon becomes firm in his decision. Then one by one the others follow his lead, accepting my deal. This is going to be so much fun. “Ok boys, first things first,” I announce in a crisp business like voice. “Ben, who’s that kid that’s always shouting shit at you in the beginning of the school?” “Ling Chang,” Ben answers with traces of anger and bitterness poorly hidden in his voice. “He’s one of the smartest kids in school, and he picks on me because I’m not as smart as he is.” I blink and wonder whether I heard Ben right. I’ve seen Ling Chang, in fact I have me in my class. My freshman gym class that is, and he’s little for his age. The kid can’t even do a single pushup…I’m kind of speechless. “Well, you know what I want you to do about him?” Ben thinks about it for a moment, and then nods seriously. “I know coach. You want me to talk about it with him right? Make him my friend and stuff like that?” Holy hell. That, if nothing else, is proof that there is something very, very wrong with America’s educational system. “Ben,” I say in a quiet voice, “I want you to remember what I am about to tell you. In fact,” I raise my voice, “I want all five of you to remember what I am about to say. Forget about everything you heard about talking shit out. The next time someone attacks you with words, you answer him with violence!” The boys actually let out a little cheer at that. These poor guys have been so repressed, forced to repress their instincts to beat down the arrogant little pricks that taunt them. I understand that the school wants to raise responsible citizens, but holy shit! There is something wrong when I have to tell a teenage boy that it’s ok to solve his problems with his fists! I continue to dispense badly needed advice for the better part of an hour. First of all I ordered an immediate stop to all homework, if these kids can’t compete academically they might as well not waste their time trying. The rest of it was all wrestling related stuff. You know, six am practices, lifting every day, information about supplements, all that good stuff. “Hey coach,” the George asks, “what can you tell us about girls?” I stare at him for a little bit, carefully formulating my answer. I had to make sure that what I told him would get the key facts into his head. That what he needed was confidence, even a touch of arrogance. But at the same time, he must always remember to treat women with the respect that they deserve. “Kid,” I say to him seriously, “tell you what. If you guys manage to have a winning season, I’ll hire every one of you a hooker.” Eventually the kids leave and I am left alone, which is a situation that I’ve been trying to avoid as much as possible. It’s one thing to intellectually know that I’ll have to adjust to living in a quiet little suburb, but it’s another thing entirely to actually have to do it. It was different in Ohio, where I could train all day. I didn’t have to hide who I was, all I had to do was concentrate on improving my skills as much as possible. But its kind of hard to do that when your disguised as your average high school gym teacher. I’ve occupied myself as much as possible, to the point of actually getting into this whole school thing. But eventually, I have to deal with the fact that I am completely alone in a strange town. I can deal with being alone; I can deal with being in an unfamiliar environment, but both at once is difficult. It’s hard to feel alone in the City if you know it as well as I do. Between the secret spots, the cafes, the stores, and everything else that exists in that amazing place I never truly had the time to contemplate the fact that my friends had gradually disappeared. But there is nothing to do here, so I am left with a choice. I can either sit here, get drunk, and cry over some sappy movie on TV. Or I can do the one thing that I have never become skilled at. Make some friends. Only problem, is that I have no idea how to even start. It’s easy for me to pick up girls, (because of Bagwell as much as I hate to admit it), but making platonic friendships is another matter entirely. You know what…fuck it. I’ll make friends the same way I deal with everyone else in my life. I’ll fucking beat them into friendship! Ok, maybe that is a tiny bit excessive. But damn it, it’s the start of the a plan…or something like that.
