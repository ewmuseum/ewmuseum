That Happy Ever After

31 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 31/08/05 I remember when I was little being adamant that I’d seen God. This belief was further fueled by my mother, who deeply wanted to believe in God. But she was too afraid of what my father would think, she would need his approval first. So for the next few days she kept quiet and watched. The beating I received was all the answer she needed. I bet she was too afraid to even think about God. “Do you ever think that maybe the memories of your past actually help you?” Cal asks as I look up at him blankly. “My old man used to have a racing horse, beautiful thing, fit as anything, but she had the meanest temper I ever saw. It didn’t matter how well we treated her, how well we trained her, she never won” I sit back and listen, he has a voice for stories. At least when his cough doesn’t kick in. Sometimes when he coughs, I believe I hear more than a smokers rattle there, but I try not to think about it too much. I watch as he speaks. “Then one day we made her mad. From that day on she never lost” Unlike me Cal talks about his past, he shares his stories. “Cal, all I feel is that my past clouds my judgment and makes me lose my focus” I fold my arms. “Maybe this is where you get your fire, maybe that’s why no one can figure you out” he’s been thinking about this for a while, I can tell. “Not even yourself” he adds. I think about this. “If it wasn’t for your past, you wouldn’t be who you are today. You wouldn’t have had the success you’ve had” Cal said, his lips curling slightly with a smile. “You wouldn’t be as strong as you are now. You wouldn’t know what you know” his confident grin surpassed any of mine recently. I take a moment to look around me, the place is still huge, four rings situated nicely in their own space. Across the floor two guys are working together on their left hooks. Further down an older guy is working with a speed bag. I can hear the faint sound of others talking around the corner. It’s nice that Cal still gets business. “I’m sure there is a happy ever after some where down the line for you” Cal says with a smirk. “Someone out there must still be able to tolerate and love you” he laughs. “Gee, thanks” I roll my eyes. I wasn’t meant to be here today, Cal had already given me his last piece of advice for the week in the bar last night. But part of me needs to reassurance today. And it’s not like I’ve got many ways to turn. The day that I saw God, I was probably too young to even question what I’d really seen. But I know that I only needed to see him once, it came as a shock to find out that all I got in return was a black eye from my father. What annoyed me even more was that my mother never thought to warn me. I spent the following days thinking dismal thoughts about her. I sulked and slammed doors, when she entered a room I would leave it, I’d drop crumbs on the floor so she’d have to clean them up, I knocked the odd book from the bookshelf on purpose. And I spoke about God. After a while I got tired of defending him, all I ever got for my loyalty to God were marks left from the belt, bruises I needed to find decent explanations for. My parents were the ultimate Jekyll and Hyde . They’d switch when it suited them. My father sent me to self defense classes, but I wasn’t stupid enough to use what I’d learnt against him. I was told that no matter how hard my life seemed under their roof, it was nothing compared to what I’d go through if I went out into life alone. I believed them. Even then the world was no place for a kid on their own. Especially a kid who didn’t even have God on their side. It took one beating too many for me to lose my newfound faith. I forgot about God then. I haven’t believed since. “Well you know what they say” Cal reaches forward and waves his hand up close to my face. As my eyes meet his I let out a deep sigh. Cal smiles, running a hand through his hair. Which is what I’d call salt and peppered. “The past always catches up with you. Maybe you’ve shut parts out for too long...” “I feel like I’m going crazy” I murmur, getting up from the chair. I back up away from Cal, stepping into the ropes. I almost forgot I was in the ring. The gloves down on the floor in front of my chair. “I can’t go crazy Cal. The one clear advantage I have over my opponent this week is my sanity”. A small laugh escapes him. “You can’t underestimate Typhoid Mary”. I shake my head. “No, I won’t” “A joke, huh?” “Yea” I smile. “Well, no.. from what I remember she is somewhat insane. But I’ve seen her before, I know she’s good” To me she was proof that sometimes even fruit loops are too good for some people. If I could learn anything from her, it was how not to fall for the wrong guy. Independence is the key. Cal steps forward, he bends over and picks up the two gloves. Holding them out to me, he says. “You’re problem is that you’re too serious about your wrestling. You might think you struggle to focus, but we both know that’s not true. In fact, it’s other aspects of your life that suffer because of your dedication to your work” I take the gloves and put them on. Hoping to stop him in his tracks. He continues. “Maybe you should think about that... let your guard down a bit. Start living your life. I can assure you there are some people out there that you can trust, people you can let inside. You’ll feel all the better for it, believe me”. He moves behind the bag just in time. I remember reading a long time ago, about the pros and cons of boxing. The thing that stuck out the most was the thought that a boxer could beat almost anyone. In the time it takes for someone to position themselves for a kick, a boxer has already struck out. I met Cal Pistone about nine years ago, when I was around the age of fifteen. He was my savior back then too. I’d finally stood up to my father and I had no choice but to leave. I wanted to box more than anything, so I found the nearest trainer. Well, the first one I came across that was a safe distance away from my past. Cal, with some reluctance, decided to give me a chance. I spent two years with Cal, until I got the taste for wrestling. Then I left, without even a goodbye. I moved on. I never thought about Cal Pistone again until earlier this year. Once again it seemed like I’d spent forever with a speed bag. Working on my foot work, my weight shifting to my right foot and I’d hit it with the back of my hand. Then I’d come over and shift to my left foot and hit it with my right hand. Over and over. It all came back so easily, but that didn’t stop Cal from putting me through the works. With my heart beating in my ears, he’d heave a sigh, “You’ll rest when you’re in the ground” One. Two. Three. Flex the knees when you fire a jab. My first fight this time around was an eye opener. I realised just how cocky wrestling had made me. I didn’t protect my face, I didn’t keep my left up. She got the upper hand. My teeth bit open the inside of my cheek. Cal stood patiently in the corner, the towel in his hands. He was as sure as everyone else that it was all about to be over. Until she came up and over again, when I thought she’d broken my nose, I broke hers. There is no feeling better than being the force behind a knockout. Well, apart from the moment in wrestling when you win a title. I like wrestling, because unlike boxing you can always go for it all. In boxing you can only win so many before managers don’t want to put their fighters in against you. Sly money only works for a short time too. But then, in wrestling it’s not so easy to take “em out in the first round. “Are you nervous about your match?” Cal asks. His question forcing me away, I watch the bag as it continues swinging, Cal stepping away from it too. “No, not so much” I say. “You sure?” “It’s just been a few months is all” “I ain’t no wrestler, but I know you’re ready” Cal says, climbing out of the ring. I just stand still, taking the gloves from my hands. “You’re fitter than you were when you came here. You look tougher, you’ve built yourself back up. You’ve accomplished what you came to me to do” How I ever got to Santa Rosa all those years ago I’ll never know. I had Anita dragging behind me all the way, it took weeks for me to get as far away from home as I could, and even then it was never far enough. It was never my intention to stop at California, but for two years I stayed put. Making money and training to fight. In the years since then, I’ve come and gone as I’ve pleased. Never staying in one place long enough to make connections. Never staying in a situation long enough to allow it to progress. And the relationships I have put myself into, I’ve never stayed in them long enough to let them go anywhere. “It won’t be long until you leave again, I know that. I know better than to expect you to stick around, especially with this new contract you signed” Cal smiles softly as he looks up at me. “I’m lucky that you’ve been here for months..” “Yea” I look at the floor. “I’ll watch your fight. But you need to get gone now, get a bit of rest” he nods his head. Still looking up at me for a long moment. We both know this is the last time he will see me stood in his ring.
