Date: 02/20/11 Top of the 9th … The man behind the wrestling phenom known as Dynasty has seen better days. The former center of the baseball world, who would be outcast for his inability to cope with the fame and fortune, finds himself in yet another logjam of a situation. Unlike any pressure he’s felt before, lives are at stake this time and he’s playing against some rather nasty competition. After a casual scrap, our hero made the mistake of forgetting his past and found himself in the custody of an old Detroit gambling tycoon, Walters Watts, known best by The Trick. His reach is far and his villainy much further. A deal Danny could not refuse; the Trick was sending Danny to Canada to complete a business deal in exchange for his life after WorldWide 141. Watts’ henchmen, Roach and Cesar, were to keep an eye on the situation and tag along. The odd grouping of two thugs, a budding GCW star, and Lacy, a southern runaway, arrive in Windsor. The intentions of all parties are not clear but can be guessed upon. Our protagonist’s goals are to do the deal so he can get on with his rising fame and successfully participate in WorldWide 142, filming in Cleveland, against the massive Shaman. His job in Windsor was to find Big C, the conglomerate druglord of Canada, and make sure the deal goes down without a hitch. When Big C made his move on Danny’s life, it became resoundingly clear that this was not just any deal. - it was set up from the word Go. The Trick had made a deal with Big C to send Danny and $250,000 to Windsor as a double package. When Big C got rid of the problem, he could collect his reward and both parties walk away winners. The one variable they didn’t count on was Danny fighting off the attempt and fleeing with the money after putting a bullet into Roach’s head. When word of failure travels down from Windsor, The Trick travels North to assess the situation. He learns that Danny left Cesar barely recognizable and Roach, found out to be The Trick’s son, with a bullet in his head. Meanwhile, Danny and Lacy continue to march toward NC-17, bouncing around Ohio. WorldWide 142 and 143 were nights to remember for the young upstart, who recorded his second and third wins against the Shaman and always dreaded Donny Diamond. After WW143, Danny got a call from Big C, doing so against his will, in which he tells Danny everything he said was a lie. He tells Danny that he does owe the Trick money and he owes him big. That is the last phone call Big C ever made and would also be the last night Lacy found herself by Danny’s side. Consider this the top of the ninth inning. The bases are loaded. There are no outs.
