Warpath: Emotionally Numb

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 27th July 2013 FIVE YEARS FROM NOW? “Don’t forget, hon,” Caroline began, clearing away her half-emptied cereal bowl from the breakfast table, “when you’re finished eating, you need to clear out the garage.” Chewing bacon, Jacob McKail rolled his eyes. ?I need to clear it out? Last week this was pitched to me as a joint effort.” He swallowed. “Quality time together, you said.” Caroline smiled that same damned smile that always somehow prevented him from getting upset with her and placed a gentle hand upon his shoulder. “But I have to take Alfie to Soccer practice, then I need to go food shopping.” McKail shook his head. “Likely story.” “Likely story, huh?” Caroline said, scooping up McKail’s unfinished breakfast plate and dropping the remnants of bacon and scrambled eggs into the trash. “You do like to eat don’t ya?” McKail glared at her incredulously, knife and fork still in hand. “When I get chance.” “Well, I thought I’d better cut your breakfast short,” she told him. “You gotta be at the gym later, right” You got that World Title defence coming up this week and you damn well better be in good shape for it too.” McKail slammed his eating utensils down on the table and stood. “Married to a goddamn slave-driver here!” “Yeah,” she confirmed, “and you need one to just put your pants on straight in the morning.” McKail wandered into the garage via the kitchen’s adjoining door and glared at the carnage before him. “Oh goddamn it!” “Language, hon,” Caroline warned from the kitchen, with subtle hints of mischief entwined within her voice. “The walls have hears - Alfie’s awake.” “Yeah,” he muttered, half-heartedly, “sorry.” McKail fumbled through the cardboard boxes and himself bombarded with nostalgic memories, each of which unlocked with every fresh artefact he laid his eyes upon. Then, at the bottom of a particularly mildew-stained cardboard box, he came across a little black notebook that he never thought he’d see again. The thing was a diary - no, that’s not the right word for. It was more eclectic than that, not as structured. It was more a collection of random thoughts, observations and occurrences taken from a severely depressed and alcohol-addled mind. He cast his mind back to the dark period of his life when he wrote the damned things and wondered just what the hell he was thinking. But, as he recalled, he wasn’t thinking, he was barely even conscious. He was drinking heavily and he barely slept and just needed to do something to keep his mind occupied. Besides, what the hell else was he going to do at three in the morning? McKail grabbed for the small book and immediately flicked open its time ravaged pages. It was so long ago now, he barely remembered even how his mind operated back then. However, before he continued he briefly considered whether this voyage back into some of the darkest times of his life was really something he wanted to do. He was happy now; he had a wife and child and loved them deeply. His career had become successful and he’d done something he should’ve done years ago; he mourned the death of his wife and moved on. What the hell could be the harm in taking a look? He asked himself. It isn’t as if he’d just fall right off the tracks just by reading a few pages of what would probably read as sheer gibberish. Besides, it’s allure was too compelling for him to resist. He’d already made the decision to read it, he just needed to convince himself of its harmlessness with a reasoned debate. Warpath coming up and it’s been hell getting here. Reed’s under my skin. Has been for weeks and he knows it too. Bastard. Ever since I tried to break his neck he’s changed. He’s-- McKail pulled himself away from the book and gently massaged his forehead. The passage trigged the relevant memories he’d buried deep within his mind immediately and already he could feel the despair, the anger and the hopelessness seep from the pages into his mind. Is this something I really want to do? He asked himself again, but he wasn’t so sure of the answer anymore. Then, his eyes latched himself onto the half-emptied bottle of fine scotch he’d hid here back when he was trying to give up the booze and get clean. He scooped it up and held it in his hand, glaring at it longingly. It was the first time in three years he’d even thought about alcohol and now, there was nothing he wanted more but to crack open the bottle and lose himself in inebriety just like in the old days. For McKail, it was never about the taste of the damn stuff, he just wanted the comforting numbness that came right along with it. “What the hell are you doing?” McKail whirled around, his eyes wide like a deer caught in the headlights. It was Caroline. She was standing at the door, arms crossed and shooting him a look that made him feel worthless and guilty, like some kind of criminal. “Just clearing up,” McKail told her. “I found this whiskey, must’ve stashed back here when I was trying to give it up.” Caroline sighed. “Are you lying to me, Jacob?” She wanted to believe him, but after all the shit they went through together to get him clean, McKail could hardly blame her for being a little bit spectacle, but that didn’t mean he had to like it. He shook his head. “No! For christ’s sake, Caroline...” “Okay, okay,” she replied. Her voice was soft and soothing and McKail felt his anger just slide right away. “I’m sorry. I just had to ask, you know?” McKail nodded, numbly. He wanted to be angry with her so it’d make it easier to forget just how bad he wanted a damned drink right about now, but she was right and he knew it. She walked over to him and rubbed his arm gently, making sure to take the bottle of whiskey out of his hands whilst doing so. “You’ve beat this once, hon,” she whispered. “Neither of us want to go through it again.” *** Warpath coming up and it’s been hell getting here. Reed’s under my skin. Has been for weeks and he knows it too. Bastard. Ever since I tried to break his neck he’s changed. He’s focused. He wants this now. He wants to fight me. Probably wants to hurt me too. I don’t blame him. Before, he just wanted my forgiveness, but I still can’t understand why. He cost me matches, he cost me title shots, hell, sometime he even cost me my pride. But it was never malicious. There was no intent there. I look back at what’s been leading up to this and I know it’s all my fault. This is going to be a war and the blood spilt will be on my hands. Fuck this, I need a goddamned drink.... McKail put the small book down upon the kitchen table and rubbed his eyes. It was two in the morning and it seemed his insomnia had made something of a return. He’d lay in bed for four hours just thinking about the book and in the end, he gave in and came downstairs to read it. It’s got a hold on me, he realised. But am I strong enough to shake it off? His mouth was dry and the memories of Mary had already begun to linger. The next step, he recalled, was finding a way, any way, of making himself forget, making himself emotionally numb. He grabbed his car keys from the sideboard and made his way towards the door. The next step was alcohol and there wasn’t a drop left in the house. He had to get some, he had to forget. It hurt too much; the pain was unbearable. He had to make the pain go away. He made it to the door and was about to unlock it, when he saw the picture hung upon the wall. It was his wedding picture. He looked into the eyes of the man he was back then and saw nothing but happiness. Caroline was standing next to him, looking into his eyes and smiling; she loved him. Despite it all, she loved him. The amount of crap she went through with McKail over the years, it almost surprised him how the picture captured the love so distinctly. No, he told himself. I’m not going to do this. I’m not going to ruin my life, just like I did back then. I’m not the same man as before. I’m different now. He put his car keys back on the counter and grabbed the little black book from the kitchen table, taking it to the yard. He allowed one last look at the small book and dumped it in the trash. The garbage collectors would come in the morning and take it all away and he’d be free again, he’d be happy. Whatever happened to him now, he sure as hell wasn’t going to become that man again no matter how appealing running away from his problems might seem. He wanted to live, not to just exist.
