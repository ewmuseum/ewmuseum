The Skye Is Falling (Part Three): Already Gone

17 January 2012
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/17/12 The following events take place before the Rampage Pay-Per-View. Beep. Beep. Beep. Beep. Pulsating electronics were beating in time with her heart while she marched through the sterile corridors. Equipment became a serenade of chirps and beeps in her ear before she tucked her dyed red hair behind it. In perfect health she walked through a place that most people feared, but in a way it felt like home. It seemed like three or four times a year she'd be in a hallway just like this one. To Vivica J. Valentine, a bleach white hospital was normalcy, but not today. Doctors and nurses passed by her in the halls, not as much as giving her a nod as they went about their own business. It wasn't like she would see them anyway with her eyes firmly planted on anything that wasn't another human being. With a nervous twitch, she looked down at her right hand as it began to shake. A hand that felt much more comfortable holding the usual GCW World Heavyweight Championship than the three "Get Well Soon" balloons it held currently. It wasn't until she looked at the balloons themselves and the absurdity of the situation that it hit her... She didn't belong here, and not just in the hospital with a green foil wrapped box complete with a card tucked under her arm, but here in reality. Surrounded by real people saving real lives and uniting real families. It was all completely foreign to her, what it was like to live a normal life. The people she passed lived on a different plane of existence. A life she gave away over ten years ago to pursue whatever fleeting interest caught her attention for those precious few weeks. How did she even get to where she was today? What was she even doing here? Every sensible thought in her body was screaming for her to turn around and never come back. She'd learned a long time ago it was a lot easier to run from reality than to face it head on. Living in GCW-Land just helped her with those things. Never having to force real and meaningful relationships outside of those that are equally beneficial to their business careers. Maybe that was what drew her to Amy Campbell so quickly. Outside of GCW-Land, they probably could've still been friends. Go to the same stores. Like the same movies. Play the same video games. Unfortunately, there was no way of really leaving GCW-Land. Sure you can take a vacation or a hiatus, but your mind is always there planning your next move. It was silly to ever think she could leave and have a "normal" relationship with a "normal" woman like Amanda Harper. It was the guilt that kept her from running away screaming. Just couldn't shake the feeling that she was the one who caused all of this. Hiding who she was before laying Pandora's Box out there right at Amanda's fingertips. By telling her she wasn't allowed to be part of her world, she turned GCW-Land into the forbidden fruit and every church-going youth knows how that one turned out. Room 409 is when reality began to sink in. The symphony of beeps and chirps became more than a weird orchestra of background music, they became a lifeline being held onto tight by a girl who had wanted nothing more than to be loved and accepted. Seeing Amanda the way she was made her physically ill to the point where she didn't really want to go inside. Leaving her box at the door and running away, the world would understand. She was the World Heavyweight Champion, and the burden that came with that had started to become a crutch. Not this time. Putting one foot in front of the other was like trying to learn to walk with different gravity, legs shaking and wavering, not wanting to bring the truth. As she tip-toed inside the doorway, she could feel the universe begin to gravitate towards her. It seemed like much more than three pairs of eyes that were fixated on her, almost like millions of people were watching at home. Waiting for her next move. Hanging on to her next words. "She's been coming in and out of consciousness for days." A voice spoke in a masculine but youthful tone. Cerulean eyes darted towards a man who looked more comfortable in mascara then she ever did. The familiarity consumed her. "You're Vee, I take it?" "Call me Vivica, please." She hesitated, looking over at Amanda in horror as she took a seat at the end of her bed away from the others. A once beautiful face now covered in lacerations stitched tight on a canvas of purples and deep reds. Burns now destroy once beautiful cheekbones. It was like something you'd see in a horror movie. Suddenly being "fearless" wasn't that big of a deal. "I'm sorry, I can't place your name right now..." "Mark LaJoie." He answered, "I was in Fallout Shelter with Amanda." "Oh..." Vivica's memory returned. He was there the night they first met. "It's been a while." "Yep." "I'm Ashley." The girl next to him responded sheepishly, the long blonde hair and Hot Topic wardrobe reminded Vivica of a seventeen year old version of herself. She looked like a deer in headlights, and if there was a facial expression for a traumatizing experience, she was wearing it. "I used to work with her at Blockbuster, we kind of fell apart after the place closed." "And you?" Vivica locked eyes with a taller man with a shaved head. His eyes hadn't left her since she entered the room. She watched as the man gripped his hands together tight before standing up, shaking his head, and then simply leaving the room. "That's Aiden." Mark responded keeping his voice down, "He was the drummer." "Not very friendly." Vivica huffed in passing, "How's the band doing?" "Non-existent." "That's... surprising, what happened?" "Jupe lost her job, Aiden and I got evicted out of our place. Had to sell our instruments to put a down payment on the only apartment in town that would let us take out a lease." Mark shifted in his seat, trying to find some form of comfort "Then she just lost interest." "She was always talking about that band." "She was always busy hanging out with you." Mark's green eyes narrowed, "Couldn't come to practice, Vee wanted to go to the mall. Couldn't hang out, had plans with Vee that night. Vee was coming over. Vee was supposed to call. Annoying as shit, no offense, so we just moved on but nothing ever felt right. "Busy with me? We only hung out two or three times, ever." Vivica sharply answered. "Every day it was something." "I didn't even have time to talk to her everyday." "Just because she's unconscious doesn't mean she can't hear you." He responded, "I don't think she much appreciates you coming in here and lying in front of her face." "Maybe I'm not the one who is lying." "Guys, not here, please." Ashley softly chimed in. "If she meant so little to you then why the fuck are you here, huh?! Didn't have time for her and she worshipped the god damn ground you walk on and you just. Who the fuck are you, anyway?" For the first time in her life, 'I'm the GCW World Heavyweight Champion' didn't seem like it was a good enough answer. He was right, who the fuck was she? Turns out in reality things like championship accolades and provocative photoshoots in men's magazines don't carry the same kind of clout as being there for somebody when they needed you the most. That thing she was so in love with was just a belt, and to a select few people it meant everything but to the rest of the world... it was just a tacky belt. "Nobody special." She spoke running her right hand through her golden and red locks uncomfortably, looking back at the man who couldn't even look her in the eye. He was too busy checking the breathing tube of his friend. Making sure she was comfortable. Giving her a fighting chance, like a good friend should. "I tried my best. It's all I can do. When she gets through this I'll make it up to her... that much I know." "It's too late for that." Ashley spoke up, catching the attention of the room. "Never too late." Vivica responded. "Can't talk like that here..." "No, you don't get it." Ashley fought the knot tied in her esophagus to eek out the line as pools filled up in her eyes, she was overcome by memories. "The look in her eyes... the way she acted... the things she'd say. It wasn't like her. Without the medicine it was like her soul just... left." She couldn't fight it anymore as streams flowed down her cheeks. She never whimpered and screamed, just fought back the emotions the best she could before looking over at the near lifeless corpse that laid in the middle of the room. Ashley's emotions were contagious. Vivica could feel the knot tying itself in her own throat as the following words were spoken... "Before the truck even hit her, it was like she was already gone." -----~*~----- The Skye Is Falling (Part Three): Already Gone -----~*~----- The Vegas strip is a lot of things to a lot of people. What tourists see as new and exciting has become a constant reminder to those who lost their lives Vegas' appeal. Locals watch as the sheep are herded from planes into hotels, then from hotels into casinos to throw their money away. Yet the Vegas strip never loses it's appeal, even to those who know it's nothing more than a death trap. After all, Vegas is a way of life, either you learn to live with it or you cease to live at all. Even though constantly surrounded by such crippling depression, Ashley McCormick managed to always keep a smile on her black painted lips. Walking as part of a crowd down the strip, she was on her way to interview for job, bussing tables for the Golden Steer Steakhouse. While her youthful lack of experience kept her from dressing the part for such an interview (dark eyeshadow, black lipstick complimented by tight jeans and a black AFI t-shirt), her seemingly undying optimism always seemed to give her a chance. She was the ultimate case of not being able to judge a book by it's cover. Ashley had never met an animal she didn't feel she could nurse back to health, and hardly a stranger that she couldn't give a few cents despite the complete lack of an income. While her parents had money, she had learned at an early age as soon as she was old enough to get a job she'd be on her own. The youngest of three kids, she'd already seen it happen twice, and her parents were pushing to finally get a house all to themselves despite Ashley's inability to even get inside of 90% of the places she passed down the street. Just like the white winged dove, she was on the edge of seventeen going on thirty, and reality was soon going to get the naive Miss McCormick like a truck. Until then, she was armed with unyielding optimism and years of built up good karma despite being relatively shat on in both judgmental upper class public schools and the retail work place. Maybe it was her own sixth-sense of finding the walking wounded that drew her eyes to the freshly dyed mane of red hair staring off in a daze across busy lunchtime traffic. A familiar face below unfamiliar locks, Ashley spotted the often-unnoticed Amanda Harper from half a block away. Almost zombie-like in her appearance, the lady once known as Jupiter Skye didn't even seem to sense the busy traffic as it passed. Lifting her lime green converse sneaker off the ground that once matched her hair, she took a step onto the curb. Ashley's heart skipped a beat as she shoved her way through the sea of humanity, shouting at the top of her lungs hoping to catch Jupe's attention. "AMANDA!" She screamed towards her former co-worker, but the girl was too lost in her own head to hear the cries of a concerned friend. As she shifted her weight onto the other foot, a hand grabbed her left arm and jerked her back towards the sidewalk, tripping over the curb and landing into the arms of safety. "My God! Are you alright? What were you thinking?!" She saw nothing back at first but cold emerald eyes before she answered, somewhat confused "Ashley?" "You could've gotten killed!" Getting Jupe back to her feet, she could feel she wasn't shaking or frightened. Her heart wasn't beating any faster and her breath smelled like alcohol. She saw the world from behind bloodshot eyes, almost matching the color of her newly dyed hair. Amanda Harper was numb to the world. "What are you doing here?" "I was on my way to a job interview and..." Ashley could hardly hear the sound of her own voice over her heart thumping against her ribcage, "...Jesus Christ. You scared me." "I was fine." "You weren't fine." Ashley responded concerned as the sea of humanity swept past them, either completely unaware or apathetic towards the situation that just occurred. "You almost stepped right out in front of that car." "Heh..." was the only answer Jupe can muster, "If it happens it happens. It's out of my control." "Are you feeling alright?" "Never felt better." "Let's get somewhere a little quieter." "I'm fine here, thanks." Amanda replied, shrugging her shoulder away from Ashley's arm, "You must be busy, don't let something unimportant ruin your day." "You're not ruining anything." Ashley tried to guide her to walk down the sidewalk, but Amanda was hesitant, perhaps distracted, "Let's just sit down for a minute and talk, you don't seem yourself." "I'm definitely me, but... you ever wish sometimes that you weren't anymore?" Amanda reluctantly started to move forward due to Ashley's lead but never made eye contact. Her interest was everywhere else but here. "What's wrong?" "It's probably nothing, I'm just stressed out." Amanda tried to hide the turmoil that built up inside by flashing a smile that never fully completed itself, beaten down by dried tears and unkept promises, "I'm just unlucky. This world doesn't seem to have any justice. You give it everything you have, you do the right things, and it just shits on you and never gives you anything back. Sometimes you wonder if fate just doesn't want you to wake up the next morning." "Don't ever feel that way. You're amazing! You're beautiful, and you're a great singer. I wish I could sing like you. You could be famous some day. At least, I think so." "I thought you, of all people, would know what it's like to be a freak." Amanda stopped, the tone of her voice almost accusing, "People must not look at us the same. You have no idea what it's like going through life with people looking at you the way they look at me. With those judgment eyes. Don't think I can see it, do they? Assholes." "No one is looking at you at all..." Ashley notices as he looks around at the crowd, "What are you talking about?" "I'm talking about how I'm proof that there isn't a God. Look at me, I'm a mistake. I'm a freak." Amanda stumbles backwards to do a little spin for Ashley, almost backhanding a couple as they walked by. "You didn't do that on purpose did you?" Ashley says, "There are plenty of people who love you, Amanda. Let's get you home, alright?" "No one loves me." Amanda responded, "Not like they do her. I'll never be good enough to be worth shit. I'm just a freak girl." The word stuck Ashley like a gunshot to the chest, almost taking her breath away as she tried to comfort her friend. She didn't quite know what to say or do, but she did know that Amanda needed some serious help after being stuck inside of her own head for too long. "No one looks at you that way, honest." "I see it in their eyes." Her dead expression glared back at Ashley and it was an image she'd never forget, "They don't give two-shits about people like me. I'm on this planet to make them feel better about themselves. People can look at me and go 'at least I didn't fuck up like her'." "I give a shit about you." Ashley reassured her, putting her arm around her back once again, "As a matter of fact, how about we go somewhere. That job can wait." "Don't throw your life away on me." "You're worth the trouble, Amanda." Ashley forced a smile, "C'mon, let's go." -----~*~----- "I never made it to that job interview, instead I took her to a clinic..." Ashley paused, her voice nearly a whisper, "they said Amanda has borderline personality disorder, once she sobered up. BPD they call it. She isn't like us, she doesn't see the world in the same light. She sees everything in black and whites. Right. Wrong. In her mind there's nothing in between." Vivica felt like her lower jaw was sitting on the floor as Mark shifted in his chair to look better at Amanda. His head rested on fists being structured by his elbows. Closing his eyes it was almost like if he didn't look, the feeling inside of him would just go away. Waking up from a nightmare was never that easy. "Wow, it's just..." Vivica stammered through her thoughts with the grace of an elephant, "how didn't I see that? It's clear as day right in my face." "That's what I thought." Ashley said, "All the signs were there." "I can't believe she didn't tell me." Vivica shook her head, "I can't believe she didn't try to talk to me at all. I knew things went bad but... everyone fights." "She wasn't herself." "Maybe she didn't try to contact you because she knew you were too busy off playing war games with silly red bandanas." Mark forced his way into the conversation, "Just a thought." "I thought you didn't know who I was when I walked in the room." "You think you know a lot of things, but that doesn't mean they..." "Mark, please." Ashley interrupted, "She can hear you, you know." "She knows the truth." He scoffed under his voice as Vivica looked back at the bed ridden woman she met as Jupiter Skye. The sight of her took the edge off of Viv's anger. When you look a tragedy like that, suddenly kicking some asshole's head off of his shoulders isn't so important anymore. There was just no justice in this world. "After that, I left home... my mom wasn't too thrilled about it, but I was going to college anyway." Ashley broke the tension, "I moved in with Amanda to keep an eye on her. I was scared for her. If anything had happened, I wouldn't be able to forgive myself." -----~*~----- Sobriety had brought out a side in Amanda Harper that the world hadn't seen since she was a little girl. A driven young singer, with aspirations that dwarfed anything that anyone around her had thought she was capable of. Whether it was out of spite or a new-found respect, she had begun to look at Vivica J. Valentine in a different light. As the blonde-haired heroine soared through the air on her television screen, Amanda was taking mental notes about building a brand. She was mentally laying down the foundations of her own empire. "You started watching it again, huh?" Ashley announced her presence in the room. A room that was paid for in full by the actions of the charismatic high flyer on the television screen. "You told me you never wanted to see her again." "I want drunk, I said dumb things." Amanda responded, taking her attention away from the television screen and looking over her shoulder, "I figured I could sulk, or I could learn something from all of this." "Maybe you should reach out to her." Ashley walked further into the room, dropping her purse on a dark cherrywood table at the end of the couch. The black lipstick was gone in favor of a stunning cherry red. A little black dress hinted that she'd just walked in the door from working at the Golden Steer. "She must care about you in some form, she keeps paying the bills." "She's busy." Amanda shot back, "Plus, I fully plan on being busy myself. Auditions, touring, singing in front of people... neither of us will have time. In a few months here I'll be gone and she can have the house back." "She only bought it for you, I'd guess." "Well, I don't intend on being a charity case for much longer." Amanda turned the television off and stood up, facing her new roommate, "I'll be able to support myself, and then we can get our own place, yeah?" "I could go for that." "Our own furniture, our own style." Amanda smirked, "We can have canals instead of hallways. A dream house!" "I don't know how to swim." "Then no canals!" Amanda walked past, "No worries about things breaking, we can fix it ourselves. Be our own bosses. Hell, you could help me get a band back together. Maybe we could hit it off big, take some vacations and travel the world. No more ramen. No more hoarding ketchup packets from work." "Sounds like a pipe-dream to me." "No, that isn't even the dream to me." Amanda corrected her, "The dream to me is just getting back up on stage and blowing people away. It's a feeling you can't explain getting up there in front of people and just opening our soul to them. It's incredible. It's like you connect on different levels and you just lose yourself to the waves of fans. I feel like I could rule the world." "Sounds amazing." "It's like the crowd is my life-force. They're my everything. I miss it so much." "You sound like Vivica." "No, I can be better than Vivica." Amanda pointed back at her new best friend, "I can make a difference in this world. All I need is the chance. If people could only see I'm a person and not a girl with BPD..." She paused for a moment. "That medication is draining us dry, Ashley." "You need it, so we work for it." "No, I don't need it anymore. Look at me, I'm fine!" Amanda shot a big smile back, her eyes alight from behind washed-out red hair dye and locks of chocolate brown. "I didn't take my medication this morning and I've never felt better." "You better take that." "I will it's just..." The woman once known as Jupiter Skye's expression changed drastically, "I wanted to know what it was like to be me again. Not a medication-induced altered version of myself, but me again." "You have a disorder." Ashley slightly dipped her head as she gazed into Jupe's eyes, "When you're on your medication. You are you. The real you that's been trapped behind this disorder that holds you hostage for so long you've lost track of what's real." "I don't think that it works qui--" "You need it, Amanda." There is a long awkward pause as Miss Harper eventually just shakes her head. "It means a lot to you, doesn't it?" Ashley nods, her expression showing more than her words could ever do. "Then I'll take it..." Amanda put her hands up in surrender, "But don't say I've never done anything for you." Those words brought a smile to Ashley's face as Amanda disappeared into the next room. In her head were thoughts about her canal-less dream house, and how she was sure Amanda could do just about anything she put her mind to. There were so many connections to that wrestler girl on television that it was impossible not to see why fate had brought them together. If she could only get her shit together like Vivica J. Valentine, Amanda Harper probably could rule the world. -----~*~----- "She might not have tried to contact you, but she talked about you everyday." Ashley added, "At first in the state she was in I didn't even think you were real. I thought you were a figment of her imagination until she had me watch the DVDs." "Yeah, I've been a ghost." "You're incredible, by the way." Ashley nervously smirked, "Never seen anything like that. Until today I thought there was a small chance she made the whole thing up. Some doomed television crush." "Wrestling is the only thing I've ever been good at." Vivica answered, taking a deep breath, "There is something about life... friendships, relationships... it's not for me. I've tried a million times. Every single one is a bigger failure than the last. I gave up on the world for good, until Amanda came along." "She feels the same way about you." "I guess she's more like me than either of us would ever admit." "Just not the good parts, right?" Mark added, "The multi-millionaire, worshipped around the globe, and looked upon as an icon for any teenage girl who has ever wanted to tell their parents to go fuck themselves parts. I'm sure life is tough." "I don't want to be the one who bursts your bubble but that camera lies." Vivica stated, "I don't even own a home. I've been living out of GCW-paid hotels for over a fucking year. I only own enough shit to pack in a suitcase or a storage unit in Chicago. I don't have a family. I don't have any friends except the one's you see on television and the one who is laying right there. My world isn't fucking lollipops, rainbows, and sunshine." "They why do it at all?" Ashley was interested, "If it's so miserable, why go on?" "Because I love it. It's all I've ever wanted. I know it sounds crazy to you guys but that crowd is my family. I love them. I care about them. We share something that you can't really put into words." Vivica looked down at the floor, "The World Championship is just verification that all the things I've fucked up in my life is worth something. That my life is worth it. That I belong." The look on Ashley's face painted a story that no words that could've crossed her ruby red lips would have given justice. The pain of being judged and misunderstood. Mark couldn't let down the facade no matter how much he may have agreed. "Probably why Amanda felt so close to you." Ashley responded, "She had a rough childhood and a lot of personal problems she probably decided not to share with you. Wouldn't even tell me. Found out from a guy I know that went to St. Jude's with her. Drug problems, alcohol problems, sexually abused... all the other kids thought she was a freak." "Sang like an angel though." Mark spoke and shook his head, "When she would sing none of that shit would matter. It was crazy. I'd never seen anything like it." They all simply sat in silence, listening to the vital signs ticking away. It was all they had to let them know that their friend was even still alive. Not a one of them had gone more than a few minutes without checking back to make sure she was still there in breathing. A silent bond is what kept them in the room despite what their differences may be. They all secretly hoped that she'd just open her eyes and look back at one of them with a smile. It wasn't going to happen anytime soon, so they were left to their own devices. Stewing in their own thoughts. Trapped in their own minds. "It was my fault." The words left Vivica J. Valentine's mouth as if it were a startling revelation even to herself. It was like taking a giant weight off of her chest. "I get it. There were a lot of things I could've done differently. I realize that." "No," Ashley argued, "I should've kept a closer eye on her. She was depending on me. I took the medication for granted." "There was no way you could've known." Mark chirped in, "She's your friend, you're going to trust her." "She was sick." Ashley snapped back, for the first time showing a spark of fire in her eyes, "It was my job to take care of her. I couldn't imagine the thoughts going through her mind out there all alone. Can you imagine how she must've felt? So scared, alone, hopeless..." "You said she could hear us though, right?" Vivica responded, "Then she knows that the world is a much better place now that she failed." -----~*~----- Cool desert air blew through her tattered hair, screaming upon the sands they passed. True brown roots scraping through facades of red. A facade that was hid behind for so long that even she didn't know the truth between names and hair colors. Only the faint noise of tires mulching gravel and faint flickering lights in the distance were her company. A flickering beacon of hope had come to save her. A taste of heaven lie just on the horizon. Her breath appeared and blew away in front of burning emerald eyes, turned red from hours of self-realization. Auditions gone wrong. Dreams smothered before given the chance to breathe. She was the victim of a world that just didn't have room for another angel. She could picture the cross pendant that once hung around her neck, a symbol of faith that has since abandoned her. A God grown cold and sick of her humanity. Uncertainty began to cloud her judgment, heaving breathing began to match the volume of a purring engine. Bile rose from the pit of her stomach and she trembled. Blood mixed with smeared lipstick engulfed her taste as she clamped down. This fluttering in her chest brought back memories of a distant past that felt like it could've been a different life. Now in this one she takes one last drawl of precious oxygen. It was time to stand and feel her worth. "Sorry..." choked from between trembling lips, putting one foot in front of the other and into the light. She closed her eyes and felt the warmth. The spotlight she so desperately craved was finally on her, and not even the metallic screeching of metal on metal could take away the chants in her head. Finally she felt alive. She was free. She was loved. She would be remembered. -----~*~----- As the tears dripped down Vivica's face, Mark could have swore he saw a smirk come across Amanda's lips.
