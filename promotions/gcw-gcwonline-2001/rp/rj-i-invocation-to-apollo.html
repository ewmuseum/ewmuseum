Weeks Ago Hradcany, Outside Las Vegas, Nevada Pretty teal blue eyes transformed into limpid pools, with sorrow, loss, lurking translucent at the bottom. Vivica J. Valentine's hands fluttered like butterflies to pluck the nectar streaming from her lower eyelids. They came away covered in eyeliner. Black, the color of mourning. From blue, the color of sorrow. Endless loop. She looked helplessly up at her long-time tag team partner, beyond that mirror to her mentor, beyond that mirror to her best friend. “I didn't even know,” she said, repeating the last thing she had said before the tears came. “I needed to tell him...” 'You have to tell him when you know. Life's short, Vivica’for you, especially. He would've liked to know.' “I know,” Lia said, her boots tapping forward across the desert-rose colored sandstone tile. She sat down on the couch next to Vivica, resting a bloodless and cool hand between her shoulder blades. The fire had started off the property, on the guest house. Though any hint of it was long gone from Vivica's nose (no matter how long the memory it would stay in her brain), the smell lingered to Lia. Acrid, choking, something to gag on out of pure, instinct-bound fear. Fire was a fearsome thing for her caste, and rightly so it should've been for humans as well, despite their relentless belief in their ingenious wild-taming 'advances.' Lia was indeed a creature of instinct, more so than almost anyone else she kept company with. It was a raw, tense existence, always on edge and wondering when the next premonition would come. Wondering when the scent of death would be so strong that you couldn't shake it off. She wouldn't have traded it, though. It was only instinct at the moment that allowed her to give her friend any comfort. “He wasn't there,” Lia said softly, voice stoic. She could not have cried over Markus Eaux D'Artifice as she might have over any other being that bore her DNA. She felt no guilt for that fact. “At least you know that.” Vivica glanced up into Lia's eyes. Suspicious. She didn't quite believe in Lia's instincts, and Lia supposed that was well enough. If you couldn't feel it yourself, that life force lingering on, no matter how far away, fear of losing the person you loved made it much harder to hope. “How can you be so sure?” Lia shook her head. They had been over this twice already, but she would continue telling Vivica the answer until it became clear. She tapped the side of her nose. “I didn't smell him in there. Had he been caught in the blaze, it would've stank to high heaven to me, clearer than any radar signal.” Vivica winced. “There were tire tracks,” Lia said, sighing as she was forced to take another tack. Logic was rarely as convincing as instinct, however. “I know,” Vivica said, throwing her hands up as she propped her elbows on her knees. “I know there were tire tracks, but that doesn't mean anything. It just means that somebody drove up here to set the fire, it doesn't mean that...” What it meant was that the picture was starting to get much uglier. It was someone who had a vested interest in making sure the secure little Ambrosi clan at Hradcany knew they were vulnerable. Or, it was someone who had a vested interest in getting Markus D'Artifice the hell out of the picture. The first one was likely, Lia felt. The second one seemed like a certainty to Vivica. The front door of the desert palace opened, and neither Vivica nor Lia was able to suppress the muscular twitch that came with nerves wound tightly round their cores. But it was only Virgil. The lord of the manor (for that was how he always conducted himself, despite the fact that Lia had both found the house and purchased it, and that did not bother her in the slightest) approached the living room, since he could see them sitting in it from the foyer. His long black hair was pulled back from his face, but the usually sleek strands had been tangled by the wind to the point where he did not look as dapper a gentleman as usual. Though he took a moment to brush some of the desert dust off of the sleeve of his shirt, his expression did not change from the harried look with which he'd originally entered. There was a problem. Both of them could feel it in their bones. “We've an interesting problem,” Virgil said gruffly, his accent sounding all the more anachronistic by his attempt to update his speech by using the contraction. “We've had a problem ever since I got here,” Vivica murmured, looking away from both of them. Before he could move to rebuke her’and the snarl on his face said clearly that he had planned to’he and Lia shared a look. It was not the time. Virgil held his wife's gaze for a moment longer, then let go of the irritation which seemed to crop up all the more frequently when Vivica was around. Or perhaps it was the fact that, over time, he had started to look at Markus like he looked at Rune’like a son. Like his son who had died a long, long time ago. “Tire tracks don't lead anywhere outside of the compound,” Virgil said, his voice sandpaper gruff. “But we missed something the first time we were out. There are footprints around back. Two going in... three coming out.” Vivica suddenly jerked her head to look at him, her eyes wild with hope, tears trembling on the rims of them. “One set dragged,” Virgil concluded. Vivica's shoulders acted as a deflated balloon, leaking air through too many holes. “It doesn't mean anything,” Lia said quietly. Because the two of them were looking at each other like one of them was going to pull a knife. “It does,” Virgil said. He glanced between the two of them. “Those tire tracks... I'm surprised neither of you picked up on it outside. Military grade. Not something you'd usually see on a van.” 'Unless it was a particular type of van.' It was a moment of synergy between the three of them, because they all thought it at once. The stuff out of movies, comic books. “I...” But Vivica couldn't get the words out just yet. Heavy with the weight of the solar system, Vivica's head dropped into her waiting hands. “I don't understand...” “Who the hell would plan something like this?” Lia's and Vivica's words overlapped. Virgil glanced between them with a quick shift of his ocean-deep eyes. “That's what we've got to figure out,” he said, nodding to Lia as the syllables slip-slid fluidly over one another, heavily accented. “The why will come later. The who is the first concern.” Vivica's fingers scrunched into her sleek blonde hair, crinkling up the perfect smoothness. She spoke from behind her hands. “You guys have to help me...” It was a plea, despite being worded as a command. It wasn't usually their territory. It was an outside chance that The Order would... “Please,” she said, the word that was inherent in her tone slipping through her lips. “Please...” Desperation was not something that Lia and Virgil Ambrosi were unfamiliar with when it came to needing to know where your paramour was. It was not an unfamiliar scent, an unpleasant musk hanging heavily in your pores. There was only one answer to give. .:: ::: ::. Air rushed around her, enormous, and whipping her hair from side to side with the currents. High up’she'd never been so high up in her life. The clouds looked like they were miles below, indistinct puffed masses that were no longer even potential eighteen wheelers, or castles. The air that pushed into her lungs was mint ice, stealing any words she might have said, though there were none to come to mind at the moment. And everywhere, the golden glow of sunlight, glimmering threads that seemed to warp and wrap around her shoulders like a mantle of finest sable. And then, the tilt. Her fingers gripped the surface beneath her, softer than eiderdown. And she looked down to see beneath her the great back of a bird, feathers that looked as though they were trailing fire through the sky. Its eye was golden and staring. A bird of prey’but there was no fear, and instead, a warmth flowing through her, despite the subarctic chill of the sky. Alone, alone, the raging, swirling sea of clouds below, and nobody to see this but her. A personal chariot ride through the heavens. It was to the heavens that they tilted now, upwards ever upwards, her fingers clinging fast to the lion's mane of feathers that streamed backwards, blowing with the breeze. And something told her, softly in her ear, that there was no reason to fear. 'It is your journey... fear is a rejection of all that you have earned.' Earned. She had earned this? She didn't know what she'd done to do so. It didn't seem a question worth asking. .:: ::: ::. Las Vegas, Nevada The Strip If any time in Las Vegas could be said to be sleazier than any other, the night-time was definitely it. Not dusk, not just as the sun dipped below the long line of the desert horizon. Full-blown night, when any view of the stars was obscured by the neon lights of this whorehouse oasis eyesore that could be seen for miles around. It was not pleasure that brought Lia Ambrosi here. Never had been’rather, necessity. Because at some point, a vampire frankly had to eat. Las Vegas was a good stomping ground for more than one reason’because it was home, yes, and close to Hradcany, but also because there were enough transients and miscreants here that nobody would miss a few. Same tricks, different cities, the whole fucking world round. Dressed in black, the svelte shadow stalked her hunting grounds. She was expert at this by now, with eyes that noticed everything and nothing at the same time. A look that lent itself to interpretation as 'goth,' and thus concealed the reality. Sleight of hand’oldest magician's trick in the book. She and Virgil often hunted together as a matter of course. It was odd, because she knew of no others of her kind that had done this. She was sure there were other incidents of it in recorded history, but really, what did it matter? They made an apt pair, selecting victims for one another, joking, keeping watch, and often times barely making it back through the door of Hradcany before they fell into one another's arms to sate another kind of need, inspired with a wild lust by the other's predatory skill. Tonight she was alone. It was not because they had fought. Nor was it because Virgil had chosen to stay home. Simply, she had asked him for some time alone while she took care of her business. That was the excuse she gave, at least. The real reason was This Fucking Headache. Hunger took a backseat as Lia slid down an alleway, thisclose to crumpling to her knees in pain. Her hand gripped at the tight, tight muscles at the back of her neck. The source seemed to be there, so logically, it was a tension headache. Logically, that tension came from the fact that she was dealing with the disappearance (or really, kidnapping) of Markus Eaux D'Artifice, and its detrimental effect on Vivica. This had been all-encompassing. There were no clues, either. Nothing to tell the Sherlock Holmes God-Dammit Obvious Truth about the situation. Whoever had designs on making sure Markus disappeared had designs on damn well making sure that nobody knew who had done it. Dropped enough clues to make the abduction obvious, but no other evidence. She and Virgil had combed the property dozens of times. Jirina and Damek were on high alert. They patrolled the grounds on sly, heavy paws, peering around corners and snuffling in the dirt for something that they had as yet to pick up. They were more intelligent than average dogs (for reasons Lia chose not to disclose), but even they could not scent anything out of the unusual, not so long after the event, and after one rare rain had come and washed away whatever might have been left in the dust to smell. “Fucking krava!” she spat, grasping desperately at the back of her head. Her green eyes flickered with animal pain, and closed for a moment. She was dizzy; the pain gripped at her and spun her sideways. Her left shoulder slammed against the unforgiving back brick wall of Cho Hung's Chinese. It smelt awful, acrid and unknown meats billowing their greasy smoke to the sky, and her empty stomach contracted. She gagged, but nothing came up. It would have been awfully convenient to be able to take an aspirin. Flickering images. ...the back room of a lab... sterile white lights overhead. Did they flicker, or was it her imagination? ...no, they did not forgive. They glowed down cancerously on her skin, radiating godknowswhat. ...there was an unknown... She came up gasping from the flash of pain, gripping harder still at the skin at the base of her neck. But she knew now... Her fingers took on a life of their own, sharp nails clawing at the skin. Digging deeper’ripping. Rip. Tear. She felt the first trickling of blood ooze down the soft skin. There would be a scar, but it was no consequence. If she could just... Get a little deeper... Who the hell was this pissant anyway? Didn't anyone work in this lab that was more important than a graduate research assistant? Why did they all seem like they had virtually no clue what they were doing? She supposed it wasn't her place to judge. That didn't do anything to change her intention to do exactly that. “Ah!” She had been surprised by the sudden prick. Pain did not faze her, but she hadn't been aware of the girl's presence. “What is it, exactly, that you are doing again?” Her words were drawn and formal because the needle was large, and it had a hard pinch to it. She didn't enjoy having that sensation unless she was giving it to herself. She only trusted her own medical training, if the truth were told, however rudimentary. “I've explained to you a little bit about the process... This is going to be the best way for us to extract the vital DNA that we need to make this experiment work. I'd have liked to pull it from somewhere else, but Dr. Hodge agrees that this is the best way to do it.” The girl suddenly remembered her bedside manner’which was a good thing, because Lia was about to throw out an elder vampire's favorite word: 'whelp.' “It'll only hurt for a little bit in any case. I apologize in advance for it, but there's not really any way to help it.” She only remembered now... only... just... how could she forget? ...That it felt a lot more like something being put IN than something being taken OU T. Stupid. “Ahhh, do prdele!” It had been stupid as all bloody hell to forget about it. She couldn't make any justification, aside from getting complacent. Her body knew when something was wrong. It knew and she didn't have to ask it to know. Just a little bit deeper. “Vyser si voko!” The obscenity flowed naturally as her fingers, the tips of them, closed around it. It. She pulled it free of her skin, feeling the blood dribble freely down the back of her neck, streaking the inside of her shirt. It would smell like blood for days. But at the very least... it would not smell like the raw stinking shit of betrayal. Between her fingertips. It was small enough to be no cause for alarm, but really what good was it to think that way when one tiny computer chip could make so much difference. So much difference in feeling like your movements were secure, in feeling like you could enjoy the sanctity of your house. Could there be sanctity when a very powerful enemy knew exactly where your haven was located? When he could follow you as just a blip on a radar screen any time of day or night he pleased? How many times had he watched her... walking along the Strip with Vivica, wrestling, how many times had he peered menacingly through the window of her sanctified bedroom? She was beginning to wish she'd never met Christian Zenith. And slowly, yet another reason to hate him dawned on her. Because if he knew exactly where Hradcany was... and it had been him that created Markus as he was in the first place... “Zmrde zkurvenej!” The words came out an unattractive bellow, but she was all fury right now. The chip was clenched in between her fingers so hard that it felt like the indentation that it created would be a permanent feature of her skin's topography. Trailing blood and a dark cloud of ire, she nearly flew chest-first into Virgil, who grabbed her by the shoulders, and noted immediately the rage in her eyes. He seemed to have the good luck to always catch her like this. “What...” “THIS is what!” She held up the computer chip to him. It was covered in gore. His usually stoic face twitched’because he didn't recognize the item, but he could clearly smell that the blood on it was hers. “That fucking worthless no-good sister-sleeping sonofabitch!” “Who?” “Zenith!” she spat exasperatedly. “Putting a fucking TRACKING CHIP in me like I'm his pet!” For a moment, Virgil could say nothing. He only observed her, chest heaving, her comparatively small frame so alight with anger that he thought she might bodily explode. “That means that...” But she was already ahead of him. She snapped the chip between her fingers, and let it tinkle harmlessly to the ground. No more would she be a flashing red dot on somebody's map. She looked up at him, eyes narrowed. “It means exactly what you think it means. He knew. Knows. Knows where we are. Has for months. Fucking toad! He's been sitting on his throne waiting for the right moment to play tyrant!” She threw her hands up, but then took a deep breath directly afterwards. The control was coming back’because if she didn't control... if she showed... “Don't tell Vivica,” she said sharply, and turned to stalk out of the alley towards the car, a fresh meal forgotten, and, more than likely, everything else off the agenda as well. Virgil wasn't far behind her. He made sure, as he left the alley, to look down with satisfaction at his own boot heel bearing down on the tiny chip as he ground it into silicon dust. .:: ::: ::. They had to be miles upon miles away from the ground now. Space loomed above, a slowly straightening inky arc dotting with the glimmering spheres that humans in Dante's time had believed revolved slowly around them. They soared now through the deep, impenetrable blackness. She'd gotten so used to the motion that it felt almost as though she was standing still, in the midst of this great gaping hole in the sky. But it was no hole’everyone thought holes were empty. This was not emptiness. This was meaning. 'We have arrived.' The voice had not changed tone the entire journey. It was only calm, only stoic, only what it was and nothing else, the announcer of pure truths and undebatable claims. Her eyes flickered open, slowly coming to rest on the scene before her with the unfocused innocence of a waking newborn. A bluish mist hovered, shroud-thick. She couldn't see through it, but that didn't matter so much; she was content to look at it. It was not threatening, in any conventional sense of the word. Robbers did not lurk here, nor tramps, nor thieves, but sibilant secrets, multiple meanings that lit the wick of the night sky's lamp of knowledge. The woman came slowly through the mist, delicate sable velvet enfolding her features. She was like an angel in the snow, her lips and cheeks a pale hue, flushed with gentle red where the wind might've drawn streaks of blood to the surface. She spoke, her voice like a thousand angels' tongues, ringing with virtue. 'I am Philostrata.' Lia's eyebrows quirked. She didn't quite look the part. 'I choose that name for myself. F’nix has brought you here to see me.' She was puzzled. She did not know this woman, this so-called Philostrata with the waving blonde hair peeking from underneath her hood. 'I am a vow-breaker.' The words were said without guilt, two bloodless white hands sliding from beneath the mantle's sleeves to clutch against one another. 'My husband... I promised I would never leave him. It was part of my promise to him, in front of my parents, in front of his family, at the altar, kneeling with my hands clasped in his. I am yours forever...' She sighed softly, the whispering breath of requited love. 'I couldn't have known that there would be it. The cancer. We never saw it coming. By the time the doctors diagnosed my headaches as something more serious than hereditary migraines, it was too late. I slipped away in the night, infested with the black plague of my own time. He can't even be angry with me, because it wasn't something I did of my own volition’I wouldn't have undertaken that pain just in order to break my vow to him. He has to live down there... with grief, with anger, with guilt.' Her eyes came up to Lia's, bittersweet in their expression. The slight tinge of pain quickly faded. 'I was only cognizant of one thing’I knew I was breaking my promise. I fought not to do it. Because I repented, I am here, in the realm of the virtuous vow-breakers. A simple mistake, you may say, but we would do well to remember the power of our words before we say them.' Cold fear ripped through Lia. For the first time, she felt the ice of the environment, numbing, paralyzing, horrifying. “What if I want to get better?” She didn't miss a beat. 'Improve for yourself, not because you fear the punishments of what comes after you cease to exist. Know what is right in your heart, and take it into you as the truth.' She faded slowly into a glimmer of nothingness, gone as though she had only been made of gossamer in the first place. Lia realized only then that her hands were shaking. Slowly, all that her eyes could bear witness to faded to gold threads, knotted together with inexorable tightness. The weave was flawless, each thread laid out precisely where it was supposed to be. Lia got the impression that she was within a giant and perfect tapestry, the most perfect story ever woven into cloth. The inexorable pull of peaceful relaxation guided her low onto the bird's back, and shut her eyes with its gentle fingers. .:: ::: ::. The Everpresent Things often came to her in dreams. It was a gift, perhaps a curse, of the Vlaszy line that more often than not, sleep was not peaceful. It was fraught with hidden meanings. One's mind had to be active at all times’there were advantages, such as the fact that it kept ones senses terribly acute to always be in such a state. But the downside was that at no time did she truly sleep. Lia Ambrosi was as the Order was wont to describe her: the sharp blade of the hand of the Nephilim. Useful for her acuity, intelligence, quickness, efficiency. The tool that one used if one needed a job done quickly and well. Or perhaps she was as her dreams told her. Intrepid voyager. Inheritor of things great and small, great and small understanding, knowledge, way, truth, light. An empty vessel waiting to be filled because it had been purified in the cleansing fire. But there was yet another. That woman in the ring. Fast. Lethal. Of uncertain origin’one of those people who played off of mystique, told improbable stories that were not under any circumstances to be believed. A tough opponent. A World Champion. A notch on someone else's belt. The truth was, there was no way to escape being defined. It happened to even the most elusive, the most iconic. In fact, it had to happen for one to become iconic. You could not be ripe for mass consumption if you did not have a mass-produced, clearly read meaning. Perhaps that was her problem. She had always refused the yoke of others, no matter what sort of yoke that had been. There had been plenty of others she'd known who had borne expectations of the world without complaint. She had only to look as far as Seymour Almasy to see an example of the man who becomes whatever it is that other people want him to become. You please the masses. You become a star. You become marketable, recognizable, in short, you become a celebrity. But what are you, truly? Do you become only the sum total of your image? Are you simply Seymour Almasy, The Final Fantasy, the one man who can become a video game clich? in the wink of an eye? Is there something left of a you, the you under there? One could not answer these things by looking at Jordan White, who could claim at precisely no time to have been one thing or another that was self chosen. The path was clear. Everywhere, it was clear. “Choose what's laid out for you. This is the easiest way. You don't have to do anything, just let us choose. Become a number. Become nothing more than a sword in Ian Childress's hand. Become an example of the redeemed to trot out in front of the unrepentant. Become the victim so that we can slide in a mass-produced pretty boy that will look more conventional posing with the World Title.” Fuck that. There was no way that Lia Ambrosi was going to go into the future tied to someone else's railroad tracks. When it came down to it, the only person who could untie her would be the same as the person who was bound down to cold metal, feeling the thudding of the approaching engine deep in her bones. That was what they wanted, wasn't it? Obliterate Lia Ambrosi, any mention of her. It's easier to trot her out as something else, to set her away on a shelf as a has-been, a cast-off toy, to make her into something that's outlived its usefulness. Tricks of the powerful puppet masters working their shows. If you can't get the marionette to dance properly on its strings, then it's really a useless thing to keep, isn't it? No matter how prettily made up it is, isn't it? The audience will look away, will forget about what it was that you once showed them, so long as you come up with something flashier to replace it. You know the old saw--'how can they see with sequins in their eyes?' In the dreams of her dreams, the fantasies about what she'd see when she closed her eyes, Lia Ambrosi knew a great deal more than anyone who might have tried to use her for their own ends. She wasn't just the hidden agent of the Order. Truth was, she knew they didn't trust her very much. And she knew that her recent outburst endangered her chance to keep her Captaincy. That was the proof’a sword did not refuse to slice. A sword acted as the bloody bowstring as notes of agony skittered across the page. She was not a sword. Case closed. Redeemed? How could she have possibly been redeemed? She didn't care how many dreams she had with mysterious birds telling her what she'd earned. She didn't know if this was a prophecy’it could have just as easily been a curse. But she claimed no loyalty to the life they wanted her to enter. Had Holy Church asked her to turn and relinquish her hold on everything earthly to serve as an example, her answer would have been a resounding and unequivocal no. The repentant did not refuse to acknowledge their redeemer’because to them, to her dreams, repentance could only come from a place outside of the self. She was not a repentant Good Example. Case closed. And she was furthermore not finished. Jordan White might have been hand-groomed by Steven Caldera to take a place on the throne of greatness. Lia Ambrosi did not have to be groomed. Lia Ambrosi had taken what she wanted and deserved within the confines of the wrestling ring by her own merits, and nothing else. She'd gone through giants, tyrants, and the supposed greats of the sport. Repeatedly. Lia Ambrosi did not have to be prepared for her task’she simply was. She was not an unqualified interloper who needed a hand's boost to become a champion. It came from within. Case closed. The honourable opinion of the court (that court being of only one judge) held that no created definitions were true. Lia Ambrosi was to be handed her scimitar, and allowed to carve a swath where she would, when she would, as she would. Where battle approached, she would tie the fasteners of her own bracers, and daub her own cheeks, she would face the adversity with her own ferocity. 'Benign Apollo, this last labor aid...' Forget that. Wipe it out of your mind. The meek don't inherit a goddamn thing.
