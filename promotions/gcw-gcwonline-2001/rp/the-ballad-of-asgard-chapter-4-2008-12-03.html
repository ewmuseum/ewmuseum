The Ballad of Asgard: Chapter 4

3 December 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12-3-08 Feelings of inadequacy have always been a part of the human condition. It doesn’t matter who it is or in what societal position they stand in, the doubts about ones impact on those close to one and to the world are always creeping in. One has but to look at the vastness of space and the stars to realize how little impact our deeds have on the universe. It’s a big world out there and what people might hope is to have at least a small impact on the lives of those they love, to be remembered by them. This is especially difficult when the person you love has seen it all, literally. From the moment the first human consciousness realized that it would eventually decay and die, she was there. She turned on the lights in the world. For millennia she has been reviled, worshipped, disdained, loved in what some may call sick and twisted ways, and everything in between. She keeps doing her job though and she does it well. There were others like her. Others that sprang into existence the moment that light was turned on, but none of them will be there until the end. Only her. Only Death. Besides her, everything was inadequate. Antonio had come to understand that and in a way, revel in it. Not only was he traveling around with Death, but he was seeing places and meeting people he would have never had a chance to otherwise. Even more important than that, he had left a mark in her as deep as the chasm that divides the human world from places like Asgard and Mount Olympus. “Where are we headed exactly” I thought Odin’s wife was in Asgard,” Antonio said as he looked up towards his companions. The trip had been a long and interesting one. Watching his two companions argue and insult each other constantly gave him a warm fuzzy feeling inside, because he saw that these gods that had been in existence for a long time still bickered like humans. “Really Dee, where did you find this guy” Where is this? What is that? Is he just going to keep asking question after question? It really is driving me insane.” Loki moved right behind Dee with a really extravagant expression of exasperation. “How can you be any more insane than you already are?” Antonio shot back quickly. “You’ve had us wandering around for weeks without end to some place where you claim the main instigator of the crimes that have been going on is hiding, yet we haven’t seen any proof that she is the one in charge.” Antonio stood up from where he sat and kicked an old dried branch. His frustrations over not understanding exactly what was going on kept getting the better of him. It hadn’t been long since he had snapped at Dee. Loki, of course, wouldn’t back down from anything, even less a battle of words with a mere mortal man. He had matched words and wits with demons and gods in all his years. “You will just have to trust me, my dear bag of bones. I know Frigg almost as much as anyone, if you know what I mean. She fears me and is trying to kill me, I am sure of this. Odin would never try to murder a blood-brother,” Loki said proudly with his head held high. “That is still absolutely no proof of anything and frankly I’m tired of you disrespecting me?” “Enough!” That was the only thing Dee had to say for Antonio and Loki to stop arguing. Her voice still reverberated on the cavernous woods they were traveling in. Death had learned to be patient with time when it came to her job, but not with much else. All the time her two companions had been arguing, she had kept guard and with her keen vision and hearing had sensed a threat. It was still approaching. “Get ready. Someone is coming.” The three of them got into a natural defensive position as they waited for the arrival of whoever it was that was coming. The rustling of leaves and dead tree branches was louder every second that passed. It was as if an animal was stalking its prey in the darkness. The sounds of nature in the dark made it even more eerie. Before long, a monster of a man appeared from the darkness. The little moonlight that crept in through the trees glinted from his helm and from his sledgehammer. Antonio was used to seeing big men in his line of work, but he had never seen anything quite like what now stood before him. The golden locks of hair fell over his shoulder and his chest. “Argh” not you! What are you doing here?” Loki shook his head. “We can’t be dragging around a lumbering muscle head like you.” “It’s nice to see you too, Loki,” the god said. If there had ever been a stereotype of what he thought a Viking would have looked in those times, it had now walked from his subconscious and stood right in front of him. “Death,” he acknowledged as he gave a small bow. He turned towards Antonio and gave a questioning look to Dee. “This is Antonio. He is my friend and he has been given the mission, along with myself, of finding out what exactly is disturbing the human world and the world of the gods. Things are not looking too good,” Death explained. She tried to sense if someone else was coming but everything seemed quiet and calm. The huge deity walked towards Antonio and extended his hand. “I am Thor, the god of thunder. I have heard about your deeds. I am sure that the valkyries will one day fly over your death and a place will be open in Valhalla.” Antonio shook Thor’s hand and he couldn’t help but shiver a little bit. It was like his whole skeleton went freezing cold. Even though the words that the god uttered seemed true and sincere, there was something that felt wrong. After that uncomfortable moment, Antonio tried to mask what he had felt by disengaging the handshake and moving towards the spot he had been sitting in. “So, god of thunder” that must be an interesting job. What brings you to this neck of the woods?” “Odin informed me of the current situation and he thought that you might need our help. What else have you found besides the incident in Ireland?” he asked as he moved towards Death. “Figures. You have always been Odin’s lapdog. He only has to call and there you are licking his hand and letting him pet your belly. Pathetic,” Loki said with disgust evident in his tone. Thor was used to hearing Loki’s verbal attack, so he ignored him for the time being. He had a mission and he had let Loki’s antics get in the way of what he had to do before. Thor had changed drastically through the years; he wasn’t the same, all brawn and no brain. He would eventually deal with Loki, but there was a matter more important at that moment. Trying to ignore Loki’s insolence as well, Death gave a small rundown of what they had found in Mount Olympus. She told him about the body of Hephaestus and about what they all were thinking might be what was stolen from his room. Thor listened intently to every detail as he rubbed his beard with one hand. Inside his head, a plan had already started to form. Antonio stared at the cackling fire they had built in those dense woods. He passed his hands over the flames in order to keep the cold from his extremities a little bit. The story that was being told he had lived through and he couldn’t help but get the feeling he had earlier from his mind. The darkness seemed to be closing in around them. “So basically, we are relying on what Loki says and chasing ghosts” Death, you know very well that Frigg wouldn’t do something like this. She might dislike Loki, but who doesn’t?” Thor was speaking the truth. If there was someone who the gods all collectively disliked for some reason or other, it was Loki. He had burned many bridges in his lifetime. “I still think we are close to something. I can feel that it’s right up my alley. There’s an abandoned house not far from here and I think we might find something on interest there,” Death told everyone. “We should get going and get there now before anything happens in there.” They walked towards their destination. The tension in the air could be cut by a knife quite easily. They finally reached a clearing and in the middle there was a cabin that looked like it should have crumbled to the ground a long time ago. It looked like any other wooden house, but it had this particular shine to it. Antonio could easily tell that it was not a normal house. “Be wary, we don’t know what it in there,” Death warned as she approached the front door to the cabin. Thor had drawn his Mjollnir, his hammer, and was ready to strike, whatever came out of there. Loki seemed uneasy. If there was anything that worried him was to see that strange look on the trickster god’s face. He had never seen that look. “Are you okay?” Antonio asked him, clearly worried. “I’m not sure. I don’t know if I want to go in there. It smells wrong.” And he meant that. His usual demeanor was all but gone. The door creaked open at the slightest push from Dee. As soon as it opened, they were hit by a waft of foul air. It was like a slap to their faces. It was pitch black inside and Loki quickly lit the candles around the cabin. It wasn’t hard to spot the dead body lying on the floor on a pool of its own blood. There was no one around and whoever had done the deed had probably left that place a while back. Dee inspected the body. “It’s a deity. I don’t exactly know who, but it explains why I never really felt it. His statue is probably now in the Hall of Forgotten Gods. Can any of you identify him?” Dee stood up and moved around the room, looking at everything, taking it all in. Loki didn’t need to look for too long. He knew who it was. He had known something was wrong from the moment the cabin was in sight. “I know who it is,” his voice was full of a strange sadness as he said it. “Byleist. He is my brother.” “Brother” I never knew you had a brother,” Death said in surprise. Existence is a huge place and even though she has met everyone once, in their subconscious, she couldn’t remember them all. Gods were also different; they never really died, just forgotten. “Were you two close?” “Not really. We were brothers, but I had a purpose. I had things to do. People and gods hated me or loved me or whatever. Nobody knew Byleist even existed. It was sad, but it was life,” Loki explained. He put his hand on his brother’s head and whispered something under his breath. “What I can’t seem to understand is why someone would bother killing him. He was already on the verge of leaving this world. It makes no sense.” Death was deep in thought. Thor was listening intently to everything that was being said, a small smirk on his face. Any time Loki was in pain was good for him. It was almost time. Antonio had been quiet all that time. “This is obviously the same weapon that did the deed in Mount Olympus. Help me out with something” this weapon? it couldn’t just have been conceived out of nowhere. There had to be something that explained how to build this weapon that can kill gods. Is there any place we can look where it would tell us how to build a weapon as powerful as this one?” “Hmm” interesting. As far as I know, the only ones that had records like that are the Council of Elders and basically their leader, Odin. Maybe we should go back to Asgard and ask him about it. It’s not very easy to get information from them that they don’t want anyone to know,” Death said as she inspected the body once again. “I wonder” could the person who was behind all this be a part of the Council?” The question hung in the air for a while. Everyone was visibly nervous, especially Thor. He didn’t know what to do. “I don’t think anyone in the Council could be behind this. What would they gain?” Thor was trying to defuse what had now become a conspiracy theory of Priory of Zion heights. “Listen to what you are saying here” you are saying that one of the most powerful and important gods to ever exist has created a weapon capable of killing deities, through a proxy, and has sent him to kill forgotten gods like this one? That seems like madness to me.” His rationalization of the problem made sense, but Antonio couldn’t help but feel like there was something terribly wrong. “I don’t know, I think we should go to Odin and question him about it,” Antonio said. He got agreement from Death and Loki. Thor was still shaking his head in frustration and disbelief. “Let’s go, there is nothing left to do here. We must go to Valhalla once more,” Death said. The three of them turned towards the door to go outside but Thor stood in the same spot he had since he had gone inside. The grip of his hand tightened around the hammer. Death felt it happen before it happened. She wasn’t able to stop it since she wasn’t expecting it. Thunder struck hard on the sky, a sick thud could be heard as stone crashed into bone. For the first time in her existence, Death couldn’t believe what she had witnessed. Antonio’s body hit the ground, a pool of blood quickly gushing from the wound on his head. He was instantly dead. No one had ever survived a blow from Mjollnir. The shriek from Death was heart wrenching. She rushed over to the side of Antonio’s body and tried to wake him up. It was no use. Tears streamed down her face. She had never felt such grief and pain. After a few seconds, it turned into a terrible anger; it was as if she had been possessed. “You will pay for this,” she said calmly, but underneath a storm rage was brewing and about to burst. “You have no power over me. Not here,” Thor said confidently. And he was right. There were different levels to existence and while Death was at the top of the food chain, she wasn’t supposed to get involved in the level of the gods. Her realm was that of mortality. “I don’t know how and I don’t know when, but I will kill you.” Loki had stood with his mouth open, staring as the events unfolded in front of him. He tried to flee, but Thor’s gigantic hand gripped him from behind. The pressure on the back of his neck was too much to handle. “It’s time for your judgment, Loki. I am taking you to him and we will finally get rid of you.” When he turned around to grab Death as well, she had disappeared along with Antonio’s body. “Doesn’t matter, once we get to Asgard, there is nothing she can do. Let’s go. You’re blood-brother awaits you.” They disappeared into the night. That decaying cabin would never be forgotten. The black was finally able to envelop everything once everyone was gone. When it did, the cabin was destroyed by the weight of the darkness, burying the body of that one forgotten god that no one would remember and the evidence of the events that had transpired. There was only Death. And Death never forgot.
