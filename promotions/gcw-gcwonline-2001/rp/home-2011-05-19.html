Date: 05/19/11 If you were lectured to in a convincing enough tone that Heisenberg’s uncertainty principle should be treated as a mere approximation and that antiparticles would cause locally an opposite curvature in space than particles, then you would be a slave to agreement. If they told you twice you might even lose the glazed look to your eyes. At the third time repetition may leave a remnant of comprehension. They might then enquire of you whether quantum mechanics can be almighty, or if Singleton set meets Zermelo-Fraenkel set theory with the axiom of choice. The universe was in balance rather than collapsing upon itself and so you would believe that you could understand it, although never answering it. It had rules and laws and order. If you were to be told of love, however, its uncertainties and chaos would leave their words falling upon barren, untrusting ears. One His grip loosened on your shoulders as you suddenly felt delicate in his hands. “Look, I know I’m drunk,” spilled from your gloss-smeared lips, “and I know that tonight has been another massive fuck-up, but… Jack… I need you to save me again.” Your breath, pungent with alcohol as if bellowed from distillery lungs, soured his face. His teetotal ears declined the words you spoke, sodden with liquor. Ten years ago he’d saved your life. Ten years later you were offering yourself as thanks. Ten years away from one another had replaced the intense, hormonal static between you with unfamiliarity and awkwardness. You questioned your memories; if time had cultivated a rare fond memory into a blossom of exaggerated illusion. “Jack.” The fragile words shattered in your mouth. “Please?” His eyes danced around yours, constantly swerving to avoid a head-on collision which would entangle your gazes. Ten years had been given context; it was the time during which devotion had rotted down to disappointment. It bordered on the cruel how long he paused, your hopes given premature and unfounded lift. “Kari.” He spoke much further down at you than the inch or so in height you conceded to him. “Go home, please. We’ll talk. I promise. But tonight… tonight isn’t… it just isn’t, okay?” On cue, an eager taxi shuffled forward, almost nudging into Jack’s thigh as a hint. Two Through your blurry gaze, the streetlights’ mesmerising reflections danced around the windows like a kaleidoscope. Hypnotised by the dance of luminescence against the night sky, it took the jolt of a pothole to realign your senses. “So,” the driver began his uphill struggle of a conversation with. Narrow and shadowed eyes were framed by the rear view mirror. He turned the radio down from an intrusive blanket of blah to a nagging hum. “Good night, was it?” Bare legs almost slid off the faux-leather as you sunk deeper into the seat. Placing the driver’s small talk on hold, you rummaged through your clutch bag - part of your play at being an adult dress-up - for your hipflask. Holding a pausing finger up for the driver’s mirror-reflected eyes, a glug of vodka seared your throat. “I’m in the back of a cab, alone,” you answered, “drinking, alone. Although, for me, this is a better night than most.” The flask felt light enough for you to empty it down your neck. “Which would make a better night than most for me a night where the one man on this whole planet I think I ever loved bundled me into a cab when I was practically hanging off of him despite the fact that I live like 10 meters away from him and knowing my luck he’ll be the first thing I see tomorrow morning when I open my front door with a goliath fucking hangover and vomit-hair and I really need to take a breath now,” you rambled… and rambled. “Which would mean that I’m… yeah, a giant shitballing shitball of fail.” The last dregs of vodka reluctantly dripped onto your tongue. “Please don’t puke in my car,” the driver pleaded with a tone of inevitability. “I’ve sworn off promises,” you replied. “But, as you said please, I’ll try to… yeah, swallow some back down or whatever, I guess… that’s, yeah, that’s why I’m single…” The words poured from your mouth, none swallowed back. “You leaving then?” the driver bluntly enquired. “Can’t be easy living right on top of someone after, well, whatever it was that you were trying to say. If I were you, I’d be on a plane out of here tomorrow. Not exactly like there’s a whole lot happening around here anymore.” His eyes appeared again in the rear view mirror, waiting for your answer. “You alright back there?” Easily distracted, the lights had stolen you away from the driver’s conversation. “Drink this,” he ordered. A bottle of water was tossed into your lap, drunken coordination meaning you barely avoided slapping it back down under the driver’s seat. “For your head and my upholstery’s sake.” A few gulps of the tepid water cleansed your mouth. A car horn blared, swirling down your ear like water down a plughole, its sound stretching out. The streetlights burned blotches into your eyes which swirled and churned like a lava lamp. “Whoa.” Your head whipped back and forth as he pulled off the road. The light blotches etched into your vision were slowly eroded by dense waves of darkness. The silence brought a chill with it. “I don’t feel so…” you slurred, “anything.” The water bottle slipped from between your numb fingertips. You felt your consciousness drifting away, your eyes squinting as you tried to focus and reel your senses back in. As the car shuddered to a halt, your waning attention devoted itself to the dull amber light above the driver. As your eyelids crept down, you saw the rear view mirror frame his mouth, where a thumb and index finger traced the thin goatee around freshly licked lips. Three A sharp sniff cleared the nostrils of William King, stripping mucus from bristly hairs and into the back of his throat where a rumbling cough churned it like butter. The cold had been tormenting him for days, shaving delicate fibres from his hair-trigger temper. If one were to be unkind and politically incorrect in equal measure, they might describe his appearance as that of an ‘angry, sneering Jew‘. Or, to be more accurate, how Mel Gibson would describe all of those of Jewish descent. But the nicotine-thickened blood which wormed its way through King’s cholesterol-plastered vessels was of Anglo-Saxon descent, and every molecule of oxygen it gifted his body was fuel for the sole purpose of making money. Greed wasn’t merely good - or any other adjective - in King’s eyes; greed simply was. “How much do cattle go for at the nearest market?” he asked. “$500 a beast? $700? The hell if I know. $1,000 tops, surely? That’s what we value flesh, bone and the pair of eyes that look back at us. Stun it, slit its throat and write a cheque. Deal done.” A handkerchief squashed and smeared weeping nostrils. “But if you stand a dumb animal up on two legs and give it a name, they can suddenly cost me millions!” he growled, the glob of buttery phlegm sitting in his throat adding extra gravel. “Two people; that’s all that’s standing in the way. A boy, girl, a patch of land and two miserable little houses. If I was out in the middle east they’d make me a Sheik or a Sultan or whatever the hell they have and I could bulldoze every last sand dune in sight to put up a hotel. But here? I have to get a permit to pull up a goddamn blade of grass.” Tearing his brown pinstripe jacket off, he threw it over his assistant. The armpits of his bulging-buttoned shirt was sodden, and a stream of sweat ran down the small of his back, plastering cotton to skin. “You take the girl,” he ordered. “She thinks she can stand on the doorstep of that run-down dump and tell me it’s her home ten seconds after she inherited the goddamn thing? No. No, no, no. You don’t do that. Not to me. If she thinks this place is going to stay some little sunny ghost town, she’s in for a damn rude awakening. You take her. You show her what this place would be like if it wasn’t for the benevolent Mr. King, understand? You show her. Her grandmother was a-… her grandmother was stubborn, and trouble, and so is she. I respect my elders, but I afford her none of my gentlemanly nature. Handle it, Naveen.” The assistant parted his saliva-dampened thumb and index finger, tracing his preened goatee. “My pleasure.”
