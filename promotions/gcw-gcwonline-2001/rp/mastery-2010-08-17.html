Mastery

17 August 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/17/10 His hands are almost works of art in themselves. They seem to move effortlessly, making the most unwieldy of tools look like a natural extension of the human anatomy. Wood and metal seem liquid in those hands, flowing, moving, changing. He works the railing with a simple rasp, then a riffler, though those tools have long since been replaced by power tools today. But he knows how to use the tools of his father and his father before him with just as much confidence. And he doesn’t want that point lost on the men who are his judges today, as much as they have been his friends and his teachers in the past. Today Troy becomes a master carpenter. Today he gains a title to match a lifetime of talent, of hard work, of skills built slowly with determined practice and attentive learning. It doesn’t matter what it is; framing, joinery, trim work, he can do it all. He can even reshape glass if it doesn’t quite fit in some of these old houses he works on. He is a master. He’s not one of those arrogant shits who just tacks the word onto his business card without it being conferred and demands more money, or thinks it doesn’t matter what anyone thinks of him as long as word doesn’t travel too fast, or thinks that who his daddy is is enough, how long he’s been at it is enough or who he knows is enough. His careful attention to detail and getting things just right extends beyond just the thing he’s working on to his work as a whole, to everything he does. He wouldn’t dream of going about it any other way. Did I mention how incredibly hot that is to me? It’s just a matter of the formality really, these guys affirming what they already know of him. And he’s thirty three. It’s almost unheard of. I’m so proud of him as this final show of skill takes place inside the old theater that he did so much work renovating. The Prospero is itself his crowning glory. He was its lead contractor and its renewed beauty owes so much to his unwavering craftsmanship. That was why it was chosen as the last stop on what has been a grueling five day tour of his work sites, interspersed with various tests of his skills. But now he’s working this railing backstage. Yes, backstage, as in an area that no audience member will ever see. It doesn’t matter to him. When he said, “restore” that’s what he meant. He’ll restore this place to its original 1920 state, or its closest safe and modern equivalent, right down to the last light fixture in the janitor’s closet. And even though it’s open for shows now, these last few details are still getting his attention. I did all the fabric work; curtains, seat upholstery, wall hangings, back before I returned to the ring. And I still admire it and love to just come in and look at it, drinking in the overwhelming presence the old place has, like some grand dame you’ve sat down to tea with and can’t wait to hear what stories she has to tell. I really could do this kind of thing forever if I broke an ankle into a million pieces next week. But it’s been one of the things that brought us closer. And now it seems so appropriate that it should bring him this much deserved honor. Now, as I sit in the balcony trying hard not to bounce around impatiently in my little sundress (nothing glittery, just a nice soft blue batik) and be on my best behavior I can’t help but reflect on how quietly he wins his greatest victories, how patiently he plans and builds his greatest achievements. And unlike my flash in the pan, lit by a million flashbulbs, high flying, do or die, in your face triumphs before a hundred thousand screaming fans (and close to a million at home on pay per view), he needs no audience. He walks away from his greatest work and leaves it for others to enjoy without him there to see it, trusting in his own devices to have left a mark so indelible that it will remain forever. Much as he has with me. I didn’t know I was falling in love with him. Then one day I just was. It was like he’d built a house for my heart, brick by brick and I had blithely been unaware until it was built and then all I could do was marvel at its beauty and strength. I still do. But today isn’t about me. Today I’m dressed as normal as I can manage and my hair is a nice shiny, natural black. I’m the girlfriend with the great fabric skills and that’s all I want to be. Today is about Troy and I’m his biggest fan. The lights come up in the theater. Suddenly the Prospero Playhouse is alive with color and the carefully restored beauty of its stage, seats, boxes and balconies comes alive. Wide eyes take in the rich mahogany scrollwork and the double vaulted ceiling, even the tiny bronzework feet on the chairs right next to them, whimsically cast in the shapes of tree roots and lovingly restored, fastened with screws that fade into the design by way of color and being turned to just the right position. Yeah, it’s amazing. Stand up and applaud my baby! I would, but those guys are standing around doing that nod and look impressed thing. God, I wish guys would just go ahead and be enthused sometimes. But I guess gushing will always be a girl thing. S’ok, I can do enough of it for all of them. ~ “Wooooooo!” Tempest cheered as Troy took slight bow before the applause of family and friends assembled in the backyard of his parent’s home. Sun dappled the flagstones and neat linen tablecloth his mother had brought out for this special occasion through the trellis. His father had built it for the hanging vines of clematis that erupted in dark purple blooms over their heads as the small assemblage clinked glasses and only Frankie Valleta booed, being his usual contrary self. Troy grinned and winged a roll at him, drawing a look from his mother that would have withered the tall oak trees around them. “You boys behave,” she said, her gentle tone still leaving little doubt that these “boys” will still children to her. “Making master is very nice, but I’ve still got a switch with your name on it.” The table erupted in laughter, and Troy and Frank proceeded to argue over just who she was referring to while Tempest shook her head and passed the fresh chick pea salad she’d made. “Aren’t peas supposed to be green?” Troy’s father asked her, looking doubtfully at the otherwise colorful concoction of peas, peppers and olive pieces. Tempest rolled her eyes and giggled as his mother chimed in, “Just eat it, Jeremy. It’s probably the healthiest thing you’ve shoveled down all year.” He feigned shock and stared back at her, sitting back in his chair and leaning back, seeming to pivot around his hefty middle and said, “Keep out of this Nora. I’m just trying to establish whether this is actually animal, vegetable or mineral for starters. I know that whatever Rachel makes will be very healthy and probably even tasty. Of course those wings kept me up all night I’ll have you know. Szechuan indeed…” he glowered at Tempest, but a smirk belied his amusement. To say that she had broadened the family’s culinary horizons would be a vast understatement. “The Prospero’s gorgeous Troy,” said Jeremy, braving the unfamiliar salad. “Practically looks like it did when I was a kid and you could still catch a matinee for a quarter.” “Hey and no small thanks to his expert electrician I think,” said Frank, grinning with a wafting of palms looking for support. He got some in the form of nods from Troy and a few others before Troy said, “Yeah, that old wiring was a nightmare. So much of it was still wrapped in cloth for insulation. It’s a miracle it hadn’t caught fire.” “And grounds!” Frank piped up, abruptly swallowing a mouthful of beer. “Jer, you have any idea how long it took me to come up with some good grounds for all that upper level wiring? It was a nightmare, I…” “Was it hard or were you just screwing around making sparks fly for shits and giggles?” Jeremy interrupted, to the uproarious laughter of the table. “Uh huh, working on your pyro I bet,” Tempest chimed in, with a knowing nod in his direction. “Was not!... much,” Frank said, and snickered. In his eyes the same flicker of mischief so familiar to Tempest stirs. People worry when they see that look. “All right you two, break it up before I end up having to fix something else,” Troy said, laughing at the two. “Well I’m just glad the old Prospero was still salvageable,” Jeremey said. He nodded thoughtfully, staring into his tall glass as if the beer in the bottom held a memory in its golden swirls. “Seems kind of fitting to bring it back to life. You know your sister used to love that place, even when it was so run down when you guys were kids.” Troy looked up, suddenly startled and as he did, Tempest blinked and looked between the two. Troy’s father continued to stare into his glass and his mother smiled wistfully, but also did not look up, her eyes focused somewhere in the middle distance between her plate and the center of the table. “She used to put on her best dress and go down there for the matinee and pretend it was a big deal. She could see the grandeur in the old place even though it was a little rough and dusty, you know?” he mused on, and the table got quiet, so quiet one could hear the sipping of individual drinks. In the midst of this, Tempest turned and looked at Troy curiously and said, “I didn’t know you had a sister.” It was a simple enough statement, said without accusation or irritation or any real weight at all. But the impact was clear and had the heft of bricks to the soul. Troy inhaled slowly and carefully before replying, “I don’t anymore. She’s gone. But… yeah, she loved the Prospero back when we were kids.” Tempest’s mouth opened and closed, fumbling for what she ought to say. A simple glance from his parents made it clear that this was news to them, that their son had never revealed this fact to her. But these are the moments court jesters are made for and luckily Tempest had an understudy. Frank snorted and spat out a mouthful of leafy greens onto his plate and coughed, then said, “Ok, construction guy’s gag aside, who switched the linseed oil with the salad dressing?” Nervous laughter swelled to fill the heavy empty space and Frank poked Tempest lightly with a reassuring smile. She nodded and with a deep breath she reached over and squeezed Troy’s hand. He looked up, having been caught in a momentary daze and smiled slightly, trying to return to the day at hand, welcoming the invitation to do so. “So Tempest,” said Frank, calling her by the name that only half those present chose to, “I’m thinking this party’s a little premature. I’m thinking that once you take down this frost guy and hold not one, but two titles in GCW, then we can have like a master and ‘mastered the competition’ party or something.” Tempest smiled, a little more warmly and said, “Well I don’t see why we can’t have another party when I do that. There should be no limits on the number of parties we can have.” “Here, here!” Jeremy agreed and raised his glass, and giving her a reassuring nod. “Heard that guy running his mouth about you. Well, about you and that other lady you’re friends with. Seems like he’s got a bit of a problem with women. And they say construction workers are bad.” He snorted and drew a round of solid laughter from the male, construction industry dominated presence at the table. Tempest grinned. “You know, that’s what’s hilarious. I get nothing but understanding and support from you guys, but a guy who does what I do can’t muster an ounce of respect for me. I don’t get it. I mean, he should at least understand me a little.” “He understands you just fine, I expect,” said troy’s father and both Troy and Frank eyed him curiously, having heard the insults and general slander about her and indeed women in general from Frost’s mouth.“That don’t mean he’s going to admit anything he knows is true, especially if it scares him,” he continued. “Fear makes liars out of all of us. We say what we want you to believe, maybe what we need to believe ourselves.” He nodded and Tempest just smiled. ~ More than once, I’ve been asked how I deal with being a woman in a man’s sport, how I see those men across the ring from me and as much as they must see me differently, I’ve been asked whether I see them differently. I do. I don’t see them as men until they prove to me that they are. And so often they don’t. They show me their egos, in such desperate need of feeding. They show me their ambition, in such desperate need of satisfying. They show me their avarice, which knows no bounds. And I have no regrets in taking from them whatever it was they so desperately wanted because they need to learn to live without a pacifier quite frankly. No, the men that I love and respect and define the mode for me, with strength, honor, intelligence, caring… I live with them. I’m friends with them. I see them daily and they remind me to beware what stands across from me in the ring. I’ve never had anything but contempt for people like Frost, either men or women. The rich and the powerful whose ennui with having everything they could ever want leaves them ready to carelessly destroy everything around them like children whose parents will always buy them more toys when they break theirs. But when you grow up (and I use that term loosely), everything and everyone is not your toy anymore. You encounter strange and willful beings immune to your money and unconcerned with your influence. Such people already know what having nothing is like and don’t fear it. The only reason you don’t fear your own destruction is because you’ve never come near enough to taste it. Oh, it’s not for lack of trying. But you lack the conviction to really push the envelope. And you lack the genuine experience to have known hunger or deprivation. Your substitute is simply to rinse out your already empty soul with alcohol and drugs and another meaningless tryst. It’s good that you don’t care. You can afford not to care. There will always be another opportunity for someone like you, because someone will catch you when fall, clean you up and give you a new toy. Caring is best left to those who know how to make something of it. It is best left to real men. And real women.
