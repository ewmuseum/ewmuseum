The End of Solitude [part I]

8 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/08/10 “Your winner and still Television Champion.....PAZUUUZUUU, THE DAARRRRKKKNEESSSSSS!” Try as he might, his attempts to summon the energy to lift his head wouldn’t come. He struggled to even manage to raise his left arm high enough that he could rip the bandage off his head and run his hand through his hair. Through his ears he could hear the cries of delight as the bizarre individual holding the Television Championship made his victorious way backstage. There was no other way to look at it, he had been beaten by a freak. As he struggled to his feet, head still bowed in shame, one thought repeated over and over inside his head; “The bitch must pay!” Fast-forward several days, and we find the Tribal War Machine still unable to accept his defeat. He didn’t need reminding that for the second time in the space of two months he had come up short whilst competing for the Television Championship. A Championship he felt was the bare minimum he deserved for his efforts in his first year working for the company. As he skimmed over the schedule for upcoming GCW events he noticed that it wasn’t too long until Rampage, the event which would mark the end of his first term and the beginning of his second. A determination tore through him, by the end of Rampage, he WOULD be the GCW Television Champion, there was no more room for excuses. It no longer mattered who he had to take it from, be it the giant cross-dresser, or that pint-sized interfering little skunk Tempest. His waist had gone undecorated for long enough. He walked from the desk where he had been studying the schedule to his wardrobe, and started rifling through the vast selection of t-shirts hanging within. This was a horde of t-shirts that would give nerds everywhere wet dreams, at least ninety percent of them bore emblems and designs of various comic book characters. GCW fans were, by now, well aware of the Irishman’s obsession with comics, he had worn several of these t-shirts on air recent. Today he chose one that the GCW fans had not seen him wear, a white shirt with the Batman symbol riddled with rust and bullet-holes, a personal favorite. The rest of his dressing ritual took considerably less time, and it wasn’t long before he was fully dressed and making his way into the lounge. His apartment was stunning, not at all in-keeping with his celtic warlord persona. He rifled through the post, discarding everything with the exception of a brown package, his name and address scrawled on the package. This was unexpected, he had been expecting his usual parcel from GCW headquarters but this looked very much unofficial. None of the usual branding or typed details were on display, yet it felt right, it felt as though… He opened the parcel, and as he had suspected inside was a much more official looking DVD marked “Bishop Steele versus Slash versus T.A. Giles”. One of the very few specifics that O’Neill had negotiated when he had signed his contract with the company was that he was to have access to all archives on any particular opponent in good time ahead of their match. It was a condition that the good people behind the desks delivered on every time. This package was lighter than the usual ones, his next opponent having only competed once in GCW after all. Yet something still didn’t feel right. He ripped the package completely apart, sure he would find something in there that wasn’t meant to be. Yet there was nothing more to be found, he decided that perhaps he was too suspicious in his old age. “Hmph, cutbacks…” He set about fixing himself a mug of tea, strong with a dash of milk and two sugars (he would have preferred three but he had been making a conscious effort to try and cut out indulging his sweet tooth) and made his way to the couch. Switching on his television and putting in the DVD. After the usual copyright notices and various GCW logos the match began, and sipping his tea he settled in to scout his next opponent. First point of note; right shoulder badly scarred, possible weakness. The young newcomer makes the first move, making light work of Steele. Aggressive enough out of the blocks, an opportunity he won’t have this time around. Within a matter of seconds though Slash had lost his momentum and found himself under fire from both opponents, O’Neill smirked. Out of his depth already, and to think, he is in the ring with Bishop Steele, nobody should look out of their depth when in the ring with him! The Irishman watches on for a few minutes not particularly impressed with what he is seeing. Although he made sure to take note of Slash’s tendency to use a rather strong forearm, on more than one occasion. His interest isn’t properly peaked again until his young opponent climbs to the top rope, O’Neill’s brow raises in anticipation, and is replaced shortly after by another grin as Giles knocks Slash from the top rope before he can execute his move. Silly boy… Slash’s rash decision to go high risk ultimately cost him the match, O’Neill had of course made a note of the obvious inexperience. There was definitely talent in the boy, but it was very raw at this stage, Slash was not the wily veteran he was, and he intended to make sure the world knew it. Having now watched the limited archive on Slash, O’Neill got up from the couch to clear away his tea. He hadn’t yet turned off the DVD yet was taken aback by a new voice, not that of James Bryan or Dave Yale. He knew this voice, even if he could not place it at the moment, but more importantly, it was addressing him directly. He swiveled on the spot to peer at the television. The screen was now blank, but the voice was definitely coming from it. As he had suspected earlier, the package had indeed been tampered with. “So, by now you will have no doubt picked up on the youngster’s energy. His enthusiasm is to be commended, no?” The voice had a certain cunning to it, a tone not dissimilar to the way O’Neill himself spoke. There was a confidence, an air of self-assurance about it. O’Neill listened on as the voice continued. “And of course, the Tribal War Machine would not have missed out on those scars that adorn the boy’s shoulder, wouldn’t that make an interesting target for a few of those sublime submission holds you pride yourself on.” Whoever the voice belonged to obviously knew how his thought-process worked, presumably another competitor. He knew for sure this wasn’t a mind-game like Equinox or Diamond had ever attempted. Certainly there was a message for him here, that whoever this is knows exactly how to get him. Not in terms of winding him up, there was more behind this intrusion than that. One thing was coming across loud and clear, whoever this was, they wanted something from him. As he listened on, the voice discussed a vast array of points that O’Neill himself had already taken note of. The precision with which this voice was narrating his own thoughts was breath-taking. It was very rare that Seán O’Neill was impressed by anything or anyone, it was rarer still that he encountered anyone as thorough in their scouting as he was. How was it that this person knew in so much detail how he operated? The voice had finished compiling it’s report on Slash, but O’Neill hadn’t yet turned the DVD off, he still stood staring at the screen. Who was the man behind the voice? He wanted to know, and sooner rather than later. Between PAZUZU and Tempest he already had enough on his mind, he needed to be focused on Slash, he needed answers. Less than half an hour before he had made the promise of the Television Championship to himself, losing to the newcomer, especially on a night when the bitch Tempest might retake the title would hardly do him any favors in terms of his ranking and position within the company. He had been taken too lightly for too long. The boy Slash seemed to have little regard for the facts. For it was fact, that nobody on the GCW roster had put more people out of action in the last year than him. Yet the boy oozed confidence, going as far as to make sure as many people as possible got the message that he felt he was a ‘big deal’. Indeed, O’Neill had seen the interview the boy had done for television the day before. "Well it is really quite simple. You don't. My opponent will probably agree with me on this one. When you got no rules, and tons of weapons, all preparation goes out the window. You just get in the ring and give it all you got." First mistake. Did the kid honestly think a hardcore match requires no preparation? Surely he wasn’t that naïve? Was he? O’Neill didn’t enjoy hardcore matches, on a personal level he felt that is was a travesty that any professional wrestler would be so incapable of winning a match without resulting to using a foreign object. That said, he had competed in so many he was almost numb to his own objections now, if it had to be hardcore, then hardcore it would be. Despite his dislike of the match type, he knew all too well that he would hold the advantage in experience alone, he knew he just had to look at it as another way to hurt the opponent. He looked up at the walls of his apartment, each adorned with a large picture of some of the defining moments of his career as SurReal. His attention was drawn to the most recent addition, he was standing victorious over Lori Equinox and Donny Diamond surrounded by broken glass, a momentous victory for him, possibly the first in his GCW tenure that had made people sit up and take notice. So whilst he undoubtedly preferred a straight-up wrestling contest, he was no stranger to winning in different and difficult circumstances. His thought process was interrupted by a familiar sound. “STAND BY TO RECEIVE OUR TRANSMISSION” The unmistakable voice of Captain James T. Kirk, the quote from ‘The Wrath of Khan’ had served as his message tone from time out of mind. He looked over to where his cellphone lay and stretched out to grasp it. Sliding the face of the phone up to unlock it he waited the few seconds it required to open his new message. The first thing he noticed was that the number the text had been sent from was not one he had saved in his phone. His curiosity perked he turned his attention to the message itself. BY NOW IM SURE YOU WILL HAVE WATCHED THE DVD. IF YOU WANT ANSWERS I CAN BE IN NEW YORK WITHIN THE HOUR. JUST GIVE ME A TIME AND PLACE. Answers? There was very little he wanted more than answers right now. If he was able to eliminate the question of who, what and why his scouting DVD had been tampered with, the purpose behind it, and most importantly, what this person wanted from him, it would be considerably easier for him to concentrate on the three individuals who already held his attention. His decision was already made, there was little point in debating it. He was going to meet this person and get to the bottom of this before it became a bigger issue than it had to be. He paused for thought before typing a swift reply of where and when the meet was to take place. With that out of the way, everything else was put to the back of his mind, it was no longer the priority. He turned off the television, placed his mug in the sink and started to get ready to go. ---approximately two hours later--- Now mid-afternoon in sunny Albany, Seán O’Neill sat in the back-seat of one of those world famous yellow New York cabs as it pulled up outside McGeary's, a popular Irish bar in Clinton Square. He paid the fee (without leaving a tip, that’s very much an American thing, in Ireland you generally wouldn’t) and climbed out of the cab, one of the staff was standing outside enjoying a cigarette, he nodded in O’Neill’s direction, he wasn’t a stranger here, it was a little piece of home away from home. O’Neill returned the nod and made his way inside. The bar was fairly crowded for a Thursday afternoon, clearly a fair few people had decided to take the day off work. He scanned the crowd at the bar, no-one stood out, but then again, he had no idea who he was looking for. After a few moments he had taken in the entire bar, there was no-one bar the staff themselves who looked even remotely familiar. Deciding that there was little point in standing in the entrance-way he made his way to one of the few empty tables and sat down. He caught the eye of Ciarán, one of the bar staff he knew reasonably well, he gave a quick nod which Ciarán knew all to well meant ‘the usual’. He was already half-way through his third whiskey when the moment of truth finally arrived. A tall man, suited and booted, walked into the bar, he wasted little time in making his way over to O’Neill and sat straight down facing him. O’Neill looked up at the man. “So, we meet again SurReal.” A thousand different things ran through O’Neill’s head at once. Mainly surprise and confusion, but one thing overshadowed the rest. Questions. Question after question flooded his brain as he tried to come to terms with the identity of the man who sat before him. This was only the first of many. “It was you?” To be continued…
