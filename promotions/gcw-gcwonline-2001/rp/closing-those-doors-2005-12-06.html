Closing those doors

6 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/6/05 For as long as I can remember I've always tended to wake up in the morning with the same thought running through my mind: 'Is the current path I'm on the right one?' As I gradually wake I constantly go through the meticulous day to day process of thinking out where I am, what am I doing, and if what I'm doing is the right thing. I remember there was a time several years ago when I was able to stop that thought process...what everyone and everything thought of me was nothing more than an arbitrary point on the line I was walking. I was strong, I stood tall...and ultimately I was a fool. That story has been ran through countless times in my head...and is the main deterrent to keep me from acting the same way I used to. It keeps my head straight, my thoughts aligned...that philosophy has led me down a fairly good path. Has led me down that path, being the operative phrase. I had those same thoughts the day when I woke up for what was the day I head living my dream for months to that date. Was I doing the right thing by confronting my new-found friend and sooner enemy in a televised spectacle that was going to be nothing more than physical damage to both fighters but ratings and revenue to one boss? That answer bugged me, and plagued me all the way to that night. It was that same night when reality took me by the collar and slapped me harder than what any smitten mother or angry wife could. My memory is hazy on the exact events that unfolded that fateful evening. Like a drunken blackout I remember bits and pieces but cannot put the whole puzzle together. If my memory still serves there was lots of blood, my body ached beyond any imaginable point before then, and the smell of gas...that putrid smell still gags me when strong today. I remember that sick grin he had while striking that match...he was ready to do what he had to do. He had to prove himself in the only way he understood how. He wasn't a visionary or respectable man at that point. He was desperate for a way to win. I know firsthand what path desperation can lead one on. His eyes led my belief away from any semblance of a bluff, and I knew what was about to happen. I swallowed my pride and told him...told everyone those two words which I never wanted to tell myself: I Quit. That's where my memory ends of that night. I'm told I was walking and responding but I have no recollection of what happened afterwards. For my own mental well-being in stifling those old emotions I've chosen not to watch any of that night. Not when the next thing I remember was a couple days later being at that damnded hospital, and definitely not now. Watching the tapes won't change what happened, and it certainly won't help me think any clearer. I paid my respects to a true longtime friend of mine, Michael Stevens, before I set myself off on a journey to god knows where. I was in no condition to fight at that point in time; my ankle was nearly shattered, my spinal injury was re-aggravated, and my heart didn't feel like it was truly there. I don't know whether it was the ego crush finally getting to me or that I wasn't to get to do what's kept me living in partial sanctity for so long now in retrospect, but the thoughts didn't help a damn thing. I awoke every morning to that same nagging question of whether or not I was on the right path. Even my body weeks later still was not in any condition to return and my heart sank when I thought about making a return. Facing the same problems I'd had before wouldn't be beneficial in any way. Like an angel to Lot I had received an offer from overseas. They were willing to rehabilitate, train, and get me back into the swing of what I loved doing all for the simple price of having me over in their federation. It took me less than a few hours to book my flight to Japan. Surprisingly Steven was amicable about the relocation. My contract with him was over at the end of the year, and I had ultimately brought him the largest draw of people which I could have in my time left. With good will I was sent off to Japan. New Japan Pro took me in with open arms...and it is simply amazing how much being over with a new culture for only a week can teach someone. Soon I was rehabilitated and set upon a new start. Things began to quickly fall into place once again, I found myself staying happier for longer...it was as if my whole mood has taken a complete spin for the better. But it only lasted for so long. 'Is the current path I'm on the right one?' That question came back. I tried to ignore it at first, and my first overseas title win helped silence it for a short period of time...but like a bee that was smacked away, that same thought came back with intensity. Before long it was driving me to the point of madness...I realized that where I resided was not where I was truly meant to be. Taking yet another victory from the hands of Tsuru Sawamura, I told those closest to me in Japan what I was feeling. It's hard to share those personal quips with most anyone, as they all took my situation to be nothing more than culture shock. I thought was having trouble finding out what it was going to take to satisfy myself...to make things right. I knew the answer all along. I just didn't want to face it. Getting off the flight back to Columbus I went over everything I had to say again in my head, but it still didn't sound right. I figured my time was over from the way I departed. Even after gaining a title from Japan I didn't believe I had a chance to come back. The meeting went by smoothly. In fact, business was not brought up at all for well over a half an hour. When it finally did, what was currently going on had piqued my interest. Jason O'Neil, a long time adversary of mine, was defacing Caldera's name on a weekly basis, and intended on meeting him the next week at NC-17. In my mind, everything fell into place. I asked Steven one very simple question: 'May I attend NC-17 in your absence?' I am not whether he caught on abruptly, or the smile...that same smile I always get on my face when I have a failsafe plan...I knew I was doing it then, and he must've known what I was thinking. I got the pass and everything seemed so perfectly. My appearance went off without a hitch, and in the moment of taking off my mask and showing whom I really was once again to those people...no, my family, I knew I was at the place I needed to be at the whole time. I took care of my business, and was more satisfied in doing so than what I had since the last ACWF. Though many doors still remain open for me, the one that contains the most ill-will and bad blood for me was the one I stepped through. It is that door that in the coming months I WILL close that door and move on to the others which have alluded me for a long time...or just shortly. How do I know this? I have yet to question myself. 'Is the current path I'm on the right one?' Yes.
