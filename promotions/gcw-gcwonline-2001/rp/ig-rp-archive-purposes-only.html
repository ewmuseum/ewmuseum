Date: Today 4:17AM The road stretched out before him. Miles ahead and miles behind, nothing but black top and yellow lines. This late he rarely even saw another car pass, it wasn’t uncommon in the hours between three and five, the interstates were nearly always deserted, it was nice. Sometimes you can feel crowded and stifled even if everyone is separated by tons of steel and glass. The cd player was down low, he could barely hear the music, but he didn’t want to disturb Sheena or Alex, they’d both been through enough over the last week. Glancing in the rearview mirror, he took a moment to watch them sleep. Curled up next to each other, it was a sweet image. Her head rested on his massive shoulder, his own head pillowed on the window. Normally, Michael wouldn’t be driving, but he’d volunteered, driving was something he honestly enjoyed and didn’t get to do enough. Plus he needed to clear his head, too many different ideas had been bouncing around lately that he desperately needed to work through. Tapping one thumb against the steering wheel, he drug his attention away his sleeping companions and focused on the road ahead. Just as he expected it was empty, no risk of running into someone else, but there was always the chance that if he didn’t stay focused he’d run off the road into a ditch. That wouldn’t be pleasant especially considering he’d half to explain the half empty bottle of wine in the passenger seat. The police usually weren’t that understanding when it came to accidents with open containers. So focus on the task at hand, don’t crash, got it. Not even a week ago, he’d succeeded in becoming a number one contender. The rush of that knowledge was intoxicating, especially now that he could actually enjoy it. The days following the match had primarily been dealing with the doctors over his concussion, and traveling at break neck speed to get to the next show. Thankfully this week no nagging injuries, and he had the week off, so he didn’t need to rush. That was always nice. He’d not told Alex or Sheena, but he was going to swing by Greenwich really quickly. Take a day and enjoy his house with cousin and girlfriend. There wasn’t anything planned yet, but certainly that could find something to do, it wasn’t that hard. Things were just going so good right now, absently he wondered when the other shoe would drop. It’d all have to come crashing down soon, things never went this right for very long. Chances are he’d say or do something to screw it all up, that was the case nine times out of ten, at heart he was an asshole, and he knew it. Changing was difficult, but the effort was being made, now it was more a question on when he’d slip and backslide more than anything else. However that wasn’t something he wanted to focus on, the good times, the good things this should be his focus. Fat chance of that happening. Giving his mind over to free flowing ideas, he just listened to the music. Let his mind wander where it may, he wasn’t going to let anything screw up this perfect moment. Everything was going so well, good music on the stereo, two people who’d follow him to the ends of the earth in the back, a long stretch of road ahead, it was almost perfect. Taking his pack of cigarettes from the consol, he released one from its cardboard prison, and lifted it to his lips. Keeping his eyes on the road he dropped the pack back into the consol and went searching for his lighter. Damn thing was always sprouting legs and running off, never lost but rarely found without effort. That was one of the things that made it his favorite lighter, the challenge. Ah success the feel of the cool metal Zippo was a relief, flicking it open and striking the flint with the same smooth motion. Closing, and dropping the lighter back to the consol, he inhaled deeply. Cracking the window slightly, he tried to get comfortable by leaning back in the seat, just a bit. Listening the music, smoking his cigarette, a strange, quiet sort of dread began to creep over him. A cold chill ran down his spine, maybe it was just a cooler night than he’d thought and the wind was starting to get to him, but he doubted it. Something was wrong, he just wasn’t exactly sure what, yet. Stevens had come to rely on these feelings over the years, it was the same sick feeling in the pit of his stomach he’d gotten the day before a pop quiz in a class he never paid attention in, the feeling of impending doom he felt in the hours before his parents had caught him drinking the first time. Closing his eyes and sighing, he waited, knowing that he’d have to open his eyes soon, he was still driving after all. And then it came, the sound of Sheena’s cell phone. He opened his eyes to stare out at the blank road, knowing full well that phone call could only be bad news. Almost instinctively he began to slow down, feeling that soon he’d have to pull over, try to smooth out whatever had happened. He listened to her grunt, the noise of the phone, and vibrations having disturbed her rest. Under his breath he muttered a curse, he’d jinxed everything by being optimistic. Wasn’t that always the way? “Hello?” Her voice was heavy with sleep, the greeting was more reaction to noise than anything else. She was half awake at best. Glancing again into the rear view, he saw her snap to attention before she spoke again. “WHAT”!”” Alex made a muffled sound in his sleep, turned his head a little further away from the noise, but he remained unconscious, at least someone was getting off easy. Sheena began speaking again, her voice much clearer now, all traces of sleep and confusion gone. She was clearly extremely upset, this was bad, and Stevens knew it. He turned the music off completely, so there’d be no distractions. “You’re where?” Stevens shook his head silently. There went his plans to hang out in Greenwich for a day. He could see it coming already, if whoever was on the other end of the phone was even moderately close most likely they’d all be required to take the trip. Hopefully he’d still be able to get to Worldwide, even if he didn’t have a match he wasn’t going to miss the show, that’d just be stupid. “New York, ok, and how much is bail?” Bail? That didn’t sound good. The real question was who exactly was on the other end of the phone. In all the time that he’d known her, Sheena had never really seemed to have any one close besides well Stevens and Alex. She just fell in with them pretty much to the exclusion of everyone else. She never really had mentioned her family, other than her parents being complete assholes, so he just assumed they were completely on the outs. Since whoever was in New York, her old stomping grounds Stevens was venturing a guess it was one of her old punk running buddies. She’d been surrounded by a huge group of people when he’d first met her at that Alkaline Trio show. If the state she was in at that time was any indication, whoever had probably been busted for drugs. Sheena never really seemed to have a problem kicking them, but Michael realized it wasn’t nearly that easy for most. Not to mention even if you drop the habit it’s incredibly easy to backslide, that’s one of the reasons he never progressed past liquor, too hard to give up anything else. Really the last thing he wanted to do over the next couple days was get some low life punk out of prison, but his conscience stung just a bit, but for a twist of fate that could be him on the other end of the phone. Moreover, if it was one of his old friends from back in the day in the same situation, he’d probably rush to their aid, that’s just how the scene worked. “I’ve got to talk to Michael, but we might be able to help you out. Bye Lance.” Flipping her phone shut, she shook her head for a moment. “Mikey, we need to talk.” “New York City” Or just somewhere in New York state?” “What?” “This guy Lance, where do we need to go, New York City or New York state” We’re only an hour outside of Greenwich and can be in NYC in less than two. If that’s where we need to go that is.” “Seriously?” She seemed a little shocked, that brought a smile to his face. Clearly she wasn’t expecting him to be so easy going about all this. It was nice shocking her for once, instead of the other way around, maybe he really was closing the book on that whole asshole thing. Best not be too hasty about that, he hadn’t met this Lance guy yet. It probably should bother him that in a couple hours he’d have to bail out some dude that Sheena had probably slept with before she met him, but strangely it didn’t. He did want to know who the guy was though, especially since he actually had her number, must’ve been someone fairly close to her at one point. “Yeah, seriously. So NYC?” “Yeah, Brooklyn.” “Got it, so tell me, who’s Lance?” ************************************************************************************* 7:04 AM The argument had been a vicious one, now, nothing but a chilly silence filled the car. Sheena had refused to elaborate on who exactly Lance was, just someone she knew that needed help. Their relationship had always been a fairly open one, so it wasn’t the fact that might have another guy on the side that really bothered him, it’s just that she hadn’t bothered to tell him about it. More than that it was the fact that even now after he had “found out” she wouldn’t even give any details about who he was. Suspicion wormed its way into Michael’s brain, and he wondered if she’d been as clean as he thought. It was really easy to backslide, maybe this Lance prick was so old dealer of hers, who finally got busted. Maybe he was still supplying. He didn’t know, and from the look of things he wasn’t going to find out anytime soon. They’d hit a storm just outside of New York that continued to rage. It had slowed them down a bit, but Stevens wasn’t particularly worried about the delay. At the moment he could care less if they ever got there, but at the same time he had promised they’d go, so he was pretty much stuck. Brooklyn traffic was killer, but they’d finally arrived outside of the police station where the guy was being held. Thankfully there were a few parking spaces available close, guess not many people had to come here this early in the morning. Whipping into a spot, Stevens slammed the gear shift up into park but didn’t turn off the motor. “There you go.” Sheena had remained in the backseat for the trip, and she continued to sit with her arms folded across her chest. Sullen, she didn’t budge from her spot. “Well, are you getting out?” “Michael, I?” “Save it, I told you we’d come, we’re here. You don’t want to tell me who this asshole is, fine, but don’t expect me to get out and bail him out. You want him out, do it your damn self.” He glared at her through the rearview mirror, and to him it was particularly telling that she wouldn’t even meet his gaze through the reflection. Alex looked uncomfortable, the yelling had woken him, but he’d had sense enough to remain quiet for the duration. After the screaming back and forth had ended he continued his silence, sinking deeply into the seat, as if hoping to avoid the notice of either party. During this last exchange he shifted uncomfortably, his eyes darting out the window, perhaps seeking escape. “Come on Mike.” “Save it Alex, I didn’t want to hear it from her, and I damn well don’t want to hear it from you either. You want to go with her, fine. I’m heading for some breakfast as soon as she deigns to get out of my car.” “Fine, if you’re going to be that way, I’ll call you when I’m done, asshole.” Her voice was cold, without feeling, almost dead. Normally he would have expected that angry little whisper she’d perfected a long time ago, this was different. She just sounded drained, completely and utterly used up. For a second Michael considered the thought that he may have been too hard on her, that maybe she should be allowed to have her secrets. Almost immediately he discarded the notion, secrets are all well and good when they don’t involve random members of the opposite sex in jail who need to be bailed out. Without another word she opened the door, and slid out the car. Cutting his eyes to the side, Stevens watched as she walked up the sidewalk towards the precinct. Under his breath he was forced to chuckle, she’d get some strange looks walking in dressed like that. She was wearing an old bridesmaid’s dress she’d picked up at the Salvation Army for a quarter, the damn thing was quite possibly the ugliest thing he’d ever seen, but she thought it was hilarious and bought it. Dragging his eyes away from Sheena he spotted a doughnut shop two doors down from the police station, figures. Turning off the engine he looked back to see Alex still sitting uncomfortably, shifting from side to side a bit. If he’d been standing it would’ve been from one foot to the other, right now it was more like from one cheek to the other. “Let’s go grab a doughnut and a cup of coffee.” “Whatever you say Mike.” The two men exited the car, and started off down the sidewalk towards the little hole in the wall joint. It looked like the type of place that would make Dunkin Donuts look like fine dining by comparison, but it would work. Plus, Stevens thought, at least they’d be close to Sheena if she came to her senses. As angry as he was he still wasn’t going to leave her stranded. Well, not long anyway. “Maybe he’s?” “Alex, I told you, save it. I really don’t want to talk about it right now, I just want a damn cup of coffee, ok?” “Sure, sorry.” Entering the building Stevens scanned the crowd, saw a couple of uniformed officers grabbing a bite, and a few random other costumers. The two men both ordered a cup of coffee and a jelly doughnut, when push came to shove they had really similar tastes. Finding a seat near the door, just in case Sheena happened to call, they ate and drank in silence. Alex had his head down, staring at the table, the big man almost looked like he’d start trembling at any moment. The big lug could be a little emotional sometimes, and clearly all this business was upsetting him greatly. Upon finishing the doughnut, Stevens pushed back from the table a little, and lit himself a cigarette. He took a couple drags, and sipped his coffee, staring out the glass doors towards the street, waiting. After a few seconds he felt a hand on his shoulder, and hear a voice. “Son, you’re not allowed to smoke in here.” “Blow me.” “What was that?” The voice was sounding clearly annoyed and the hand gripping his shoulder dug in a little tighter. In no mood to deal with some moron, trying to be a badass Stevens began to turn getting ready to deal with the jerk physically if necessary. He stopped turning the second he realized exactly who was behind him. “Shit” “What was that?” “I said, shit, as in deep, in other words what I’m in.” “You’ve got that right boy. Now get up, you’re coming with me.” Already Stevens was getting fed up with this guy, he was being an asshole, and the day had been hard enough already. Desperately he tired to restrain himself from saying anything else stupid. “Hey, I’m sorry, I’ll put it out ok” I’m not from around here, and I forgot you couldn’t smoke inside in New York.” “I don’t care where you’re from you’re still coming with me. I’m sick of whiney little punks like you who don’t think the laws apply to them. Even if I gave you a ticket, you probably wouldn’t pay it.” And this was it, how in the hell could this asshole get away with something like this. Having had completely enough Stevens slowly stood and turned around to face the officer. The smug look on his face was telling, there pretty much was no chance at this point that Stevens wasn’t going to be arrested, he might as well let the crime fit the punishment. “Look, asshole. Give me a ticket, I’ll pay it, that’s not a big deal I’ve got the cash, but I’ll be damned if I’m going to go anywhere with you.” “Want to try again for resisting arrest and disorderly conduct son?” “Why not, keep trumping up the charges, this should be fun. Especially considering I’ve got a witness who’ll be more than happy to testify in open court about your harassment. Now why don’t you just read me my rights, and we can get this over with.” “So that’s how you want it son?” “Yeah, I think that’ll about cover it.” Roughly turning Stevens and twisting his arms behind his back the officer began putting on the handcuffs. Alex began to rise to his feet, but was cut off by a sharp shake of the head by Stevens. “Oh we going to have some trouble with you too big boy.” Cutting Alex off before he had a chance to speak Stevens interrupted. “No, don’t worry about it. He’s just going to sit there like a good credible witness. Come on, lets get this over with, hope you have fun with your badge because you’re not going to be having it much longer.” *********************************************************************************** 9:52 Booking and processing had taken a little more than two hours. Thankfully the cop had taken him out the back of the doughnut shop, and through the side entrance of the police station. That meant he didn’t have to face Sheena, didn’t think he could handle that kind of embarrassment right now. He’d already called the lawyer, the man was furious once Stevens had described the situation. Assured that he’d be out before noon, Stevens had a smile on his face as he was escorted to the holding cell. Finding a stop on the floor Stevens folded his hand behind his head, and rested them against the bars. He’d just relax until the shit hit the fan. Glancing around the cage he saw there were only two other occupants. One passed out on the floor, reeking of cheap liquor and stale urine. The other was a slender man maybe an inch or three shorter than Stevens. From head to toe he was dressed in shocking white. Not the dull white you normally see, the white of freshly fallen snow that reflects the light back at you so brightly it almost hurts your eyes. Platinum blonde hair hung over his eyes as he appeared to star down at the floor. His back rested against the bars, and his arms were folded across his chest. Stevens was fairly certain he’d never met the man before but there was something incredibly familiar about his stance. From what he could see of the guy he couldn’t have been more than nineteen or twenty, the visual portions of his face were too smooth to be any older. Taking a closer look at the young man, Stevens could see his knuckles were ragged and one was possibly broken, he’d been in a fight recently, probably the reason he was in here. Upon a better look Michael could see the only thing marring the perfect white of his outfit were the bloodstains around the cuffs of his shirt, and a couple splotches near the collar. A loud ringing noise jolted Stevens out of his reverie. Looking over his shoulder he could see the grinning face of the cop who’d arrested him earlier. He held his nightstick in his hand which he’d just slammed against the cell bars. The younger man glanced up as well, but quickly looked down again. The cop swaggering past Stevens stopped in front of the door. Pulling a ring of keys from his belt he started to unlock it. “You, Sullivan, looks like someone made your bail.” “About fucking time.” Once again, a bit of familiarity, there was something in his voice, speech patterns that made it seem like Stevens must have known him. Wracking his brain he was still unable to figure it out. “Oh my god, MICHAEL!” Sheena’s voice rang out through the police station. The sounds of her shoes slapping against the ground as she ran up made just slightly less noise than her exclamation. Stevens looked up to see the young man staring pointedly at him. After a moment he glanced out towards Sheena as she ran up. “Michael?” The man’s voice clearly held a question. Interesting, so this was Lance, and he seemed more familiar than every, especially the way he said Michael. This was just all starting to get a little too strange. It felt like he was missing some vital piece of the puzzle and if he’d actually gotten some sleep, or even just not had such a horrible day so far, he could’ve figured it out. Slowly standing Stevens met the younger man’s stare equally. Without the hair in his face and the way he was standing he looked more familiar than ever. “Let me guess, Lance?” “Yeah, so you’re Michael huh, Sheena’s told me a lot about you.” A smile quirked at Stevens’s lips, this was really starting to get interesting. Getting into a fight in the middle of the police station would have to go down in the punk rock hall of fame. He’d probably go to jail for real, and blow his case against the asshole cop, but that’s why god invented lawyers, right? “Oh really, nothing good I hope.” “No, not really. Just that you were a complete asshole who constantly made her feel like shit. I don’t understand what it is man, what the hell kind of hold do you have on my sister?” “Wait, what sister?” “Yeah, my sister Sheena, you been in some other conversation?” Glancing over his shoulder Stevens saw Sheena had finally arrived. She stood next to the cop, shuffling back and forth. It all made sense now, she didn’t really get along with her family, and when her brother calls to get out of jail, she didn’t want to deal with it, so she wouldn’t talk about it. He was almost sorry he’d yelled at her, almost but not completely. They were going to have a lot to talk about once he got out of here. For starters why had the cop called the kid “Sullivan”, when Sheena’s last name was Ramone? “Care to introduce us Sheena.” It took a minute but when she finally spoke her voice was little more than a sigh. “Michael, this is my brother, Lance. Lance this is Michael Stevens.” ~To Be Continued~
