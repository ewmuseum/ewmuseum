Azuma Gaiden: Prologue - "Gazing Long Into An Abyss"

10 June 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06/10/11 Waves crash down, like open-hand slaps, breaking across the rocks with that old-time stiffness, methodical and cruel, taking all the time in the world to wear down whatever stands in their path. Mamoru Azuma closes his car door with a grunt, before turning his expressionless gaze to take in the awesome sweep of the Pacific. It's been an eleven hour drive up from Osaka, unpleasant in every respect, and he's relieved to finally be able to stretch his legs. He breathes in deeply, taking in a lungful of the ocean air of his youth. He immediately regrets it. The saline purity of it grabs him by the throat, equal parts nostalgia and nausea, broken dreams, dead fish and who knows what else, and in the next instant he's keeled over in a coughing fit, so intense he needs to brace himself on the corner of his car with his free hand. When he pulls his other hand away from his mouth, he shudders at what he sees, palm full of greenish-brown ejectum. It looks like death and feels like death, and he tosses it off to the side in disgust. He walks on shaky legs across the sidewalk to where it meets the beach, scooping up a handful of sand and rolling it around his palm to remove what's left of the slimy residue. Satisfied, he reaches into the pocket of his trainers and fishes out a pack of cigarettes, flicking one between his lips and lighting it in a practiced motion. He takes a long, hard drag and there are tears in his eyes as he exhales, but he blinks them back and the next moment he is as calm and stoic as ever. He hasn't been feeling good recently, though that in itself certainly wasn't anything new. The Showa emperor was probably on the throne the last time he felt good, and that was at least 23 years ago. The queasiness he's been feeling for the past year or so, though, was the start of new kind of malady, he felt, and it was only going to get worse before it got better. It was just another cross he was going to have to bear. One more to add to the stack. Moving along the sidewalk to where the beach ends and the rocks begin, he leans over the weathered guardrail to stare out across the water. There seems to be a harbor some ways down the coast, looking surprisingly intact for the most part. He decides he'll go check it out later in the day. It could be the answer to one of the last niggling problems he's been trying to work out. His gaze drifts down to the rocks below, and he chuckles to himself. Though they look smooth from this vantage point, he knows that beneath the surface they are as rough and jagged as he has become. He had learned from a young age that appearances can be deceiving. He was only ten years old when he had been playing with friends on rocks not unlike these, when suddenly he had lost his footing, fallen, and sliced open a gash on the lower side of his thigh that required thirty stitches to close. He still bore the scar to this day: his first, but certainly not his last. Feeling the heat on his fingertips from his unfiltered cigarette, reduced now to merely an ember, he flicks it out into the rocks, watching its glowing descent into the abyss. The waves take it out to sea, and as he watches their sweep, in and out, in and out, he finds himself mesmerized, taken in by their spell. It's some minutes before he's able to break free of the trance. Shaking his head, he runs his hand through his short, bristly hair, shorn close to the skull, before turning to make his way to the shoddy-looking restaurant set back from the road. “Irashaimase!” The heavy-set bandannaed women behind the counter greets him with a forced smile and a shallow bow, motioning him to take a seat at one of the half-rusted stools along the counter. He's the only customer here, though that fact hardly surprises him. The wood used to construct the counter is unpainted, and the walls too, and both are starting to show the kind of wear and tear characteristic of the cheapest of the lumber. When pressed for his order he orders a plate of pork-cutlet curry rice and a strong coffee, which are brought out to him shortly. They taste like shit, but he wasn't exactly expecting gourmet. The coffee helps to take the edge off, at least. He hesitantly reaches down to his pocket to pull out the phone rumbling within, and after checking the name on the display, he slams it down on the counter with a sneer. Kazuma Araki. He's in no hurry to answer that one. It can wait, he thinks to himself. If not in this life than maybe the next. He's got a long day ahead of him and he'll call him back when he's good and goddamn ready, so let's see how he likes waiting for a change. For now, he decides, he's better off ordering another one of those strong-ass cups of coffee. ================= One week previous The past decade had not exactly been boom times for Japanese pro-wrestling, but the previous year has made what came before seem like a golden era. In 2010, the world of sumo wrestling had been rocked by a series of scandals, starting with cases of criminal narcotics possession and progressing to allegations of fixed matches and yakuza involvement. The resultant government probe soon extended its scope to puroresu, and many organizations quickly found their operations suspended while the investigations played themselves out. There was a considerable backlash from this, as a number of federations' representatives immediately began picketing outside the Diet, but while a majority of them were justified in their complaints, Go-Rin Puroresu, well, they were another story entirely. Their story begins way back, in the years following the war. It was at this time that Hideyuki “Giant” Araki was beginning his ascent to super-stardom. Able to crush the gaijin imports at their own power-wrestling game, he became an icon in Japan at nearly the same time he burst upon the scene. He was capable of rallying a Japanese populace broken, despondent, and depressed, and through his virtuoso performances was able to rekindle their pride in their homeland once again. Crowds flocked to his towering presence wherever he went. Not only was he physically gifted, but he was savvy as they come, and it showed in the company that he kept. Most late nights he could be seen frequenting the most prestigious of cabaret clubs in Ginza, Shinjuku, and Roppongi in the company of the foremost of politicians and kingmakers. It was here he met the men who would make his dreams of Go-Rin Puroresu, named after Miyamoto Musashi's Book of the Five Rings, into a reality: the Kanezawa-gumi. Anybody that didn't have their head up their ass in post-war Japan knew that the real power players in the realpolitik business scene were the yakuza. Like cockroaches after the bomb, they emerged from the fire-bombed wreckage stronger than ever. The resources they stockpiled during the war gave them footholds in nearly ever major industry and their strong-arm tactics were able to bust down whatever remaining doors stood in their way, both figuratively and literal. It was the rare politician that wasn't in their pocket and the most foolhardy of businessmen who dared to resist their entreaties. The Kanezawa-gumi was a mid-level organization, whose leaders had a predilection for investing their ill-gotten gains in traditionally masculine pursuits: boat races, pachinko parlors, high-end sex clubs. So when Giant Araki approached them with his idea they were only too happy to provide him with the necessary start-up funds. While the yakuza had their fingers in the pies of most pro-wrestling organizations in Japan, you could say that they owned the bakery which put out Go-Rin Puroresu. All of which is a long-winded way of explaining why, when the government shut down the wrestling side of the business thereby depriving him of his usual means of income, Mamoru Azuma finds himself sitting in the back of a conference room, sweating uncomfortably in a three piece suit while attempting to loosen the too-tight silk necktie constricting his windpipe. The man sitting at the head of the table, a typical nth-generation Tokyo University type, is conducting the shareholders meeting in a high-pitched, nasal intonation that is particularly grating on Azuma's ears, to the extent that he has to force himself to remain calm and refrain from jumping the gun. Looking up and down the sides of the long table, the other men seated there seem to be of much the same breed. Pampered, weak-spined individuals, who have never worked a day of hard labor in their lives. Unacquainted with adversity. But that is going to change soon, he thinks, cracking his knuckles under the table. This is going to be the one spot of sunshine in an otherwise gloomy day. “Well, with that said I'm going to open up the floor of this meeting to the shareholders,” the bespectacled chairman says, with an unnecessary titter. “As always, we welcome your comments and suggestions.” The man next to him attempts to stand up in response to the chairman's request, but Azuma pushes him back down in his seat with a firm hand on his shoulder. Seeing him attempting to mount a protest, Azuma kicks the chair out from underneath him with a half-hearted sigh, sending the man scrambling to the back of the room in embarrassment “What is the meaning of this?!” the chairman shouts, pounding his fist on the table, his voice cracking in surprise. “Sit your faggot ass down before I slap you into next week,” Azuma says with a point of his finger, not needing to raise his voice at all, his normal speaking tone skillfully honed to a threat. He isn't surprised at all to see the chairman comply with his request in shocked silence. He takes a few steps toward the front of the room, arms crossed behind his back, in no hurry at all. Through the corner of his eye, he sees a man with a cell phone out, furiously pressing the keys with the fingers of one hand, and he slaps the phone out of his hands with no hesitation. “I don't see any reason why anyone here should be attempting to contact the authorities. I'm here on behalf of a shareholder, just like the rest of you. Attempting to deprive me of my place here is a violation of my rights, and you could be sure I would be seeing all of you in court over that.” “What do you want?” the chairman asks, though he has some trouble getting it out, as if speaking around a mouthful of cotton. “I'm here as a representative of Mr. Kanezawa and I must say he's not happy at all with the way you've been running your business here,” Azuma says, shaking his head sadly. “When he invested in this company so generously after it made its first public offering, he never would have imagined you would have gone back on your word in the manner that you have.” “Just what exactly are you talking about?” a jowled, middle-aged shareholder asks with a quizzical expression. “Ah, the man wants to know exactly what you've been keeping under wraps,” Azuma says, smiling devilishly at the chairman as he draws ever closer. “Is there something you would like to divulge to your shareholders, Mr. Chairman?” “If you think I'm going to cave into your threats, you've got another thing...” Azuma slaps him in the face, in full sight of the roomful of men. It is a ringing smack that hangs in the air like a ghost for long seconds after contact. Nobody at the table says a word. Some are looking on with curiosity, but most are staring into their laps, willing themselves into invisibility, trying to avoid trouble by any means possible. “I don't see any reason why I should listen to the weaselly protests of a man who smells like the lint I scraped out from underneath my foreskin this morning,” Azuma shouts, spittle flying from his mouth into the face of the beaten chairman. “If this were a more honorable age, I would see you gut yourself on the floor before me where we stand.” There are tears in the chairman's eyes as he leans forward and whispers in Azuma's ear. “Please, whatever your boss wants...we can talk. Just please, leave me alone, let me finish this meeting in peace.” Stifling a chuckle in the name of sobriety, Azuma stuffs an envelope full of the syndicate's terms into the breast pocket of the man's suit jacket. He briefly aims a glare down the length of the table, though no one dares to meet his eyes, then turns on his heel to stride out the conference room door. The elevator doors shut before he is able to register another sound coming from the room. ================= Sitting in the back of a dusky izakaya, Azuma swirls his whiskey in its glass, taking a liberal swig every minute or so. The top three buttons of his shirt are open and his necktie lays undone on the table, pinned underneath a plate of shishamo. He is lackadaisically biting the heads off the small fish one by one, and if he's enjoying them, he's clearly not showing it. He's been here for nearly an hour already, waiting for his contact to show. He's had a lot of time to think, and that is never a good thing. He's spent most of his eighteen years as pro on the up-and-up, but that hadn't prevented him from getting a little extra income on the side from time to time. He hadn't gone out seeking this supplemental work. It had found him. And while he had never minded doing a job every now and then when it was just a part-time thing, now that it has taken over his life, the job is starting to make him him feel like jumping out of his skin. It wasn't a question of what he was doing, but of how he was doing it. Extortion, blackmail - fine, whatever. It wasn't as if these guys didn't have it coming. But the suit, the tie, spending his days in stuffy boardrooms with insufferable pricks. This is not the way he wanted to be living his life. And to make matters worse the job was just too fucking easy. These executives, they were all the same spineless cowards deep down. Bent-backed and hobbling under the weight of their out-sized egos, like malignant tumors grown to elephantine proportions, they were unable to move in any direction for fear of losing face. They all caved in after only the most feeble of protests, gladly willing to pay whatever it would cost to get him out of their offices and out of their lives. He never got to do anything interesting, and he always left the job unsatisfied. All he wanted was five minutes in a room with one of them and a pair of pliers... This is as far as he's gotten when he sees his contact finally come through the door. He doesn't like it when people waste his time, and he makes a point of communicating this through his narrowed eyes as Kazuma Araki finally slides into the seat across from him. “Well, well, well. Mission accomplished as usual, my good man.” he says, with a weak chuckle, uncomfortable under Azuma's gaze. The diminutive grandson of Giant Araki takes off his sunglasses and places them into the pocket of his blazer with a cocky flourish. Shifting his hand to the inside pocket, he offers his pack of cigarettes in the other man's direction. “Smoke?” “I've got my own,” Azuma mutters, lighting one as if to demonstrate. “Golden Bats? You smoke Golden Bats?” Kaz asks with a laugh, shaking his head at Azuma's choice of brand. “That's what the homeless men outside of Tennoji Station smoke with the change they bum off of foreign tourists.” “I need something with a bit of a kick to it,” Azuma says, turning his head to the side and brushing a finger across the bridge of his nose. “So about that money you owe me...” “By all means, take it,” Araki says, sliding an envelope across the table with a wry smile. “And please use some of it to buy yourselves some decent cigarettes for a change, something with a filter so they won't rape your fucking throat. You sound like the goddamn devil.” “My respects to your grandfather...” Azuma replies, not bothering to respond to Kaz's comment. He slides the money into his jacket's inside pocket. “You sound like there's something else you want to tell him.” Azuma exhales slowly, adding to the dark cloud of smoke that hangs ominous in the air between the two men. He lowers his eyes, but only for a moment, before making eye contact once more. “Sorry, it's just...I hate this work. The suit, the tie, all this bullshit, I can't do it.” Araki looks taken aback, as if he can't believe what he's hearing. He sticks his index finger into his ear, making a pantomime of cleaning the wax out of it. “Are you serious? What's to hate about it? It's a cush job and the bosses chose you above a lot of the other guys.” Azuma shrugs. “Let one of the other guys have it, then. I don't give a fuck.” “Look, you know as well as I do that it's the new way of doing business. You don't get anywhere by threatening people at knife point in dark backrooms anymore. These days that shit will land you in jail quick. No, you have to take the fight to the boardrooms, where they're exposed to the public eye, and back those suits up against the wall. Zero risk, and you get to keep your hands clean.” Azuma places both hands on the table face up. He looks down at them, staring at them for a few seconds before slowly lifting his gaze to make eye contact with Kaz across the table. “Maybe I don't want to keep my hands clean.” Araki busts out laughing, his arms crossed across his chest, each gripping the opposite shoulder. “Man, you are one crazy fucker, aren't you,” he says some seconds later, after calming himself down. “Ok, ok, I'll tell Gramps what you said. If you want to work the dirty, ugly street jobs like the rest of the kobun, I'm sure there are plenty of jobs available. Hell, this could work out good for me. I'm tired of being an errand boy. Maybe I can get your current gig.” Standing up, Azuma smiles to himself, placing a hand on Araki's shoulder as he walks past. “Sorry kid. Your balls aren't big enough by half.” He pats him on the back a second time and walks out the door, with the same look of grim satisfaction on his face as a terminal cancer patient receiving his morning dose of morphine. ================= That night the tsunami hits, and in the morning Japan has 20,000 fewer mouths to feed. ================= Four days later Sitting motionless on the edge of his bed, Azuma is staring off into the middle distance at nothing in particular. He is sporting a sickly looking pallor, of which the nearly empty bottle of Nihon-shu sitting at his feet seems to be the most likely culprit. The phone rings on the nightstand beside him and he picks it up automatically, without turning his head or shifting his body. “Yes?” He grunts an affirmative. Another grunt. “That should be no problem.” Pause. “I was going up there anyway.” Pause. “Private business.” Pause. And then another grunt. “Thank you as always for your patronage. You can leave it to me.” He returns the phone to the receiver. Other than his hand, he hasn't moved a muscle this entire time. Now, bending over slowly, he reaches underneath his bed where he detaches a blade from the bottom of the frame without looking. It glints as he holds it to the light, appraising it for just a moment before sliding it into a sheath fastened tight against his ribcage. He lets out a deep sigh, post-orgasmic in nature. It was true what they said. Absence really does make the heart grow fonder. ================= Two days later Azuma is surprised at how modern, even stylish, the building is, though upon further reflection he decides he shouldn't be. Death is a lucrative business, after all. And business has been booming lately, though in light of recent events that's only to be expected. Still, it's jarring to see the sheer number of people in various stages of mourning that are thronging the hallway. The man leading him down the hallway is wearing a white mask that covers his mouth and nose, and the eyes peering out above it are spider-webbed in red. Azuma is glad the man is tired. It's likely the only thing sparing him from an irritating question and answer session. No small-talk, no questions asked, no probing into his private business like a finger conducting an involuntary prostate exam, that's the way he prefers all his interactions to be carried out. After turning a few corners, the man opens up a heavy metal door with a key, which he then slides off to the side. The temperature drops about twenty degrees as they walk inside and Azuma cannot suppress a shiver as his body adjusts itself. The man disappears behind a long curtain with a perfunctory bow, leaving Azuma to shift his feet uncomfortably. It's about a minute before he reemerges, pushing a long gurney in front of him. Azuma thought he had steeled himself for the worst, but what he sees laying on the table before him goes far beyond the meager limits of his imagination. Peeking out from underneath a long white sheet is a death's head, barely recognizable as human, its bluish-gray skin distended and bloated, or at least what little remains of it. Fish have devoured the eyes, nose, and most of the skin off of one side of the face, exposing a yellowed jaw full of crooked, jagged teeth, seemingly set in the gums at random. One could only imagine what further horrors lay concealed underneath the sheet. There's no doubt as to who it is, though. The tattoo snaking up his shoulder and onto the side of his neck is unmistakable. The people here obviously already know, or else they wouldn't have called him in the first place. His presence here is merely a formality. “I'm sorry, we've got some paperwork here we need to get sorted out,” the man says, as if underlining the point. Taking out a pen and a clipboard he begins filling out a document. “Please state your name.” “Mamoru Azuma.” “Do you recognize the man on the table before you?” “Yes.” “What is his name?” “Satoru Azuma.” “And your relation?” “He's my father.” “Then as next of kin, please stamp here with your family crest to signify that you have positively identified the man before you as your father, Satoru Azuma,” the man says, handing the clipboard to Azuma. He pulls out his registered wooden hanko with his last name engraved upon it and stamps it on the designated spot on the paper, before handing it back. “Would you like some time alone?” the man asks, after verifying that the document has been filled out properly and all is in order. When Azuma doesn't answer he takes it as an affirmative and politely excuses himself from the room, too busy this week to waste even a single moment. Azuma doesn't move at first. For nearly a minute he stands motionless, not even raising his downcast eyes to look at his father's body. And then suddenly he begins to tremble. It's slight at first but it builds and builds until it reaches a violent pitch, as if a chemical reaction is taking place inside the man that threatens to blow him apart at the seams. His eyes are shut tight enough to make the veins stand out in his forehead, his teeth are chattering, his breath is fast and labored. And then, just as suddenly, his eyes snap open and the trembling stops. His breath is still coming fast and hard, but other than that he seems completely fine. His eyes are now locked onto the table in front of him and he takes a few wobbly steps in its direction until he's standing above the corpse. He stares down at it for the longest time in silence, until finally he begins to bend down towards it, leaning over to whisper into the dead man's ear. “You piece of shit,” he mutters under his breath. “You finally got what was coming to you.” Up close the stench is horrible. The technicians have done their best, but there is still a overpowering smell of rotting meat with a fishy tinge. Azuma nearly breaks into another coughing fit, but he forces himself to endure, leaning in still closer, hissing now. “You may not believe it, but it was me. I was the one who did this to you. No, don't laugh. Don't you laugh. It was me. Because for the past twenty years I've been storing this up, channeling my hatred, waiting for the day I could finally unleash it in the dead of the night, to seek you out and strike you down at your weakest moment. And I got you. I finally got you, you bastard.” He places his hand, shaking with emotion, over his heart, clutching it into a fist. “I may be just a man, no more than flesh and bone, but my hate is primal. As old as the world itself. The concentrated essence of devastation, old world hate, old god rage, bursting molten from the earth's jagged maw, twisting violently at the heart of the storm, surging forth, unstoppable, to crash down upon you and steal you from your bed at night." He rears up now and stares into the man's dead eye sockets, raising his voice to a raspy shout. “I choked the life out of your lungs and ripped your soul from your chest. And I cast it down, down, down to the deep dark, from which there is no return.” He pauses, calming himself. He takes a few seconds before he begins nodding his head, smiling to himself. “You're in hell now. One of the deepest, no doubt...and there I will no doubt join you some not-too-distant day. So suffer there knowing this: that this wave was just the first, the first of a long series, infinite as the cosmos, inexhaustible as the oceans, which will break themselves upon you. Death is just the beginning, old man. Just the beginning” He spits in the dead man's face and turns to leave the room. His entire body language is eerily nonchalant, as if nothing at all had taken place. And who's to say that anything had. The dead cannot talk, after all. ================= As he's leaving the building, Azuma sees a scene that makes him stop and ponder. Next to a traditional Japanese rock garden, two small children are playing a game, running after each other around a lone tree jutting up from the ground. Their laughter rings out stark and clear, cutting through the somber air like a bolt of lightning through the thunder clouds. Watching them, their mother and grandmother, eyes red from tears, both smile slightly for what might be the first time in days. Life goes on. Unfortunately. ================= Though the same can't be said for everyone... ================= They have been heading out for nearly two hours when Azuma gives the signal to cut the engine. The man steering the boat looks tired, as if he's being kept up long past his bedtime. Azuma motions him to go below deck to rest with a slight tilt of his hand, leaving him alone, more or less. It's not that he doesn't trust the man to keep quiet. It's a small town and should anything happen everyone knows where to find him. Not to mention where to find his children, his parents, and the girl he dated in high school. Even so, the less he sees the better. It's a calm night, a calm ocean, everything is still. The moon, nearly-full, looms pregnant near the horizon line. Sunrise is maybe only an hour away. It would be nice if he could relax and just take it all in. But there's work to be done. Untying the heavy burlap bag, he drags out first one plastic covered body and then another. He notices that blood has pooled at the bottom of the second one and makes a mental note to take care with that one, lest he wants to spend most of the next morning in port scrubbing this thing down. With a grunt he lifts the first body and positions it on the edge of the deck. He braces himself and counts to three in his head before jerking the plastic back. It unrolls down the side of the boat like a carpet unfurling, and there's a loud splash as the dead man makes contact with the water. It isn't too long before he's repeated the process with the second body. Looking over the side, he doesn't see any mess. They'll have to do a more thorough check once they get back to port, but for now it appears that he's not completely without luck. While it would be nice if the scent of blood lured in some sharks, it wasn't exactly something one could count on in these over-fished waters. Luckily when these two bodies wash ashore later in the week, they'll just be added to the list of unclaimed bodies, already hundreds of names long, too bloated and malformed by their time at sea to be identified with any sort of confidence. Just two more victims to add to the staggering death toll of the tsunami. Well, another job done, he thinks to himself as he lights up another cigarette. Which means he needs to decide what he'll be doing with himself in the coming days. There shouldn't be any heat coming down over this, at least none that he knows anything about, but he figures that it can't hurt to lay low for a while, just to be on the safe side. Maybe he could get worked into a tour overseas in one of the major American promotions. Those spots were in high demand lately, but it's not as if he isn't owed favors by some influential people. He makes it a point to ask Giant Araki about it as soon as he gets back to Osaka. Just the thought of getting away seems to lift a burden off his shoulders he hadn't even realized was there. The more he thinks about it, the more appealing it seems, and his mood lightens for the first time in a long while. He calls the captain back up above deck, instructing him to take his time back to port to avoid looking suspicious, before depositing himself in a deck chair facing east. He lounges back, enjoying the show. Somewhere over the Pacific, two fronts are meeting, intermingling, feeling each other out in a the first awkward steps of a dance that will culminate in the destruction of a dozen Indonesian villages some three days from now. There's no sign of that here, though. Not on these tranquil waters, on this halcyon morning, where the first rays of dawn are streaking across the waters, bouncing and refracting off the surface in a playful two-step as they shimmer from gold to blood-red and back again. The sun is rising, the sun is rising, oh, the sun is rising.
