Chapter One: Solitary

11 December 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/11/10 When you are alone, time passes in funny ways. Minutes become hours, hours become days, days become a lifetime. Stuck in a room by yourself with nothing but your thoughts can drive a person insane. You think that you can deal with being by yourself but eventually you break. Everyone breaks. He has sat in this same cell for the last seven years, the only daylight coming from the small, rectangular window. Fresh air and wind have become foreign memories of a life that he has left behind. They said he was too violent for general population and stuck him in Colorado State Penitentiary's Solitary Confinement unit until his sentence was up. His sentence is up. But as he sits on his small, plastic mattress, he has broken. There was, at one point, a small twinkle in his eye that didn't look forward to every day but at least accepted the promise of it. Now that little twinkle is gone and replaced with a blankness, the empty spot that now fills his soul. But the department of corrections, the fine gentlemen that they are, plan to release him onto the streets back to his own devices; his former vices. The wall behind him has carving marks in it, noting how many days he has sat in this same room, nothing to do but think, devise, plot... Hate. Two entire walls have been covered with one wall segregated to its own masterpiece. There, in big, bold words, reads his signature "Fathom my words, fear their actions" saying that used to strike fear into the world but now only holds a mantlepiece to the broken man. "You," the words say through the small hole that opens in the door. "You have company." Fathom looks up and sees the guard staring at him through the window, the only remembrance of life on Earth he receives. Assuming his position, he turns and puts his hands behind his back as he approaches the door where they cuff him. The door opens and they quickly get in and lift up the cuffs on the legs of his orange jumpsuit to get the shackles onto his legs. "You celebrities getting your special attention," the guard says. "Nobody else here can even get visitors." The men walk together through the desolate room, desperate eyes locked on them through the small windows in the doors. Everyone watches, everyone wishes but this will not be them. Not yet. But they all wish and hope as Fathom walks through the doors that he has not walked through in seven years. Waiting on the other side of the door is a small man, a man that Fathom should remember. His smile says everything that one should know about him: fake, lying and greedy. But that doesn't keep his gold tooth from gleaming in the fluorescent light. His appearance, a cheap black suit with a white shirt conveniently missing a tie, says that he is down on his luck and sees his former protege as his last shot. Ladies and gentlemen, this is Terry. "My boy!" Terry says, going to hug Fathom but caught by the guards. "How have you been?" But there is no response, Fathom hasn't talked in so long, there is probably no voice left in his body. His mind runs a mile-a-minute but his mouth hasn't moved since he walked into the one bedroom asylum. Instead, he continues to stare at Terry blankly as if his presence in this room is not needed. Everyone in the room looks around uneasily, not sure exactly what to do. "Can he hear me?" Terry asks a guard who simply shrugs. "What the fuck did you do to him in here?" Terry looks around and goes to his briefcase, pulling out a small packet of papers inside a manilla folder and walks back towards Fathom. He shakes them as he walks towards Fathom, letting out a small, audible laugh that reeks of betrayal. "Maybe this will snap you out of whatever it is you are in," Terry says. "Your first booking once you get out of here. It is for Global Championship Wrestling against Alexander Redding. Are you in?" So much has changed on the wrestling scene since he entered this place. Seven years ago he was the PTC Extreme Champion, facing the biggest superstars in the business and now he is being proposed a match against someone he has never heard of. His blank look doesn't budge as he ponders his future, his past, his... Everything. This isn't the life that he wants. He turns away from Terry and nods his head. "Hey, what? Where are you going?" Terry asks, still waving the papers. "We signed this contract, you have to fight." The guards oblige and begin to take him away. He has no interest in fighting anymore, he has fought his biggest fight and lost. This fight, of course, was against himself. But Terry isn't going to let the fight go down so easy. In one last- ditch effort, he makes the statement that will change the world. Or at least Fathom's. "Sara is dying Fathom. Your money ran out." This statement stops him in his tracks and the guards who are guiding him nearly fall as his body lunges them back. All of the emotion that left him in year three has returned, filling him with a fuchsia that begins to burn him up. The chains rattle as he slowly turns back towards Terry who now has a serious, pleading look on his face. "I didn't want to tell you," he continues, "but you need this fight... You need this job." The guards look uneasy at this scene and wait to see who makes the first move. Fathom turns around and continues his walk towards his cell as the guards wrap their arms back around him and continue the walk back to his asylum. Terry, confused, holds out his arms. "Where are you going?" he says. Terry sees his money train leaving the hallway and getting ready to turn the corner only for him to utter these words. "To train. Alexander Redding, mark his soul, is standing between me and my life. Poor him." He disappears around the corner and back into his hole as Terry's face slowly morphs back into a smile. A demonic, treacherous smile.
