The Wrench in the Works

16 June 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 16th Tuesday, June 16th, 2009 5:45pm Larry's Gym Cameron Winter had never thought that, of all places, she'd be in a run-down gym in the Bronx the day before her first pay-per-view gig. The building was old, though nice – it was mostly the fact that the air-conditioning came from a laboring wall unit that made it seem seedy. It leaked dank-smelling water all over the concrete outside, water she hadn't even wanted to step in with her cross-trainers on. That having been said, it hadn't been a bad suggestion. The place had been recommended to both her and Johnny Fives (or Nate, as she had gotten used to calling him) by their manager, Phillip Taylor. “Why not a gym in Manhattan?” Cami had asked him. “It's going to take us so long to take a taxi over to this place that we won't get back to our hotel until two in the freakin' morning.” Taylor shook his head, running his hands over his rapidly thinning gray combover. “Have to trust me on this one, Cameron. I've seen a lot of people go out there in MSG and totally bomb their first match because of the nerves. What you need tonight is a place where you can go to unwind, let out all that last minute tension, and focus on winning.” It was the part about bombing that had gotten Cami's attention. Her first singles match had gone 'less than well.' That was if she was being nice to herself. If she wanted to be truthful, she'd gone out there and bombed it completely. She wasn't about to embarrass herself like that again. “So... it'll be quiet?” “Quiet as a funeral home.” Cami winced at that. Taylor didn't have the best head for metaphors. “If you say so...” He'd been right, though, as he'd been right about most things. Cameron had been a little wary at first when she'd met Phillip Taylor through GCW's talent liason office. The idea of a manager had never sat very well with her, being the type of person who had her own idea about what was best for her career. Still, there was something to be said for not having such a stubborn insistence on doing everything yourself. Letting Phillip manage her calendar a little bit, letting him explain some of the ins and outs of the business that she didn't understand yet had been beneficial. Not that she was likely to admit it. Her fists thudded against the punching bag as she hammered at it. She had taken a liking to the punching bag, not particularly because it offered useful practice, but because it took smarts. This particular piece of equipment forced you to understand your body's natural rhythms, forced you to become one with the equipment and focus in on that one, exact moment. It was cathartic, in a way. She wasn't the type of person (not yet anyway) to pretend that the punching bag was someone's face (she was likely to pretend it was Phillip Taylor's, she thought, if anybody – or maybe her father, though that was almost sacrilegious), but it cleared one's head. Ten minutes of stretching. Ten minutes of jump-rope cardio. Twenty on the treadmill, pushing for endurance. Fifteen at the punching bag. Five minutes cooldown. She would have gone for longer, but Phillip had insisted that she not push too hard today. 'If you're not ready by the night before, you won't be able to make it up.' He'd said that to her more than once. She was having a hard time getting it through her head. It probably wouldn't have been terribly surprising to anyone to learn that Cami had been a proponent of the 'study every night until you can't study anymore' scholastic school. 'Bookworm' was one of the kindest epithets for it. The fact was, she supposed, as her dark eyes darted over to check the clock -- Four more minutes... The fact was that she was a little obsessive about doing well. Cameron was an over-achiever in the highest sense of the word. Salutatorian of her high school class. Graduated with an honors' diploma, French Honor Society, National Honor Society, FBLA. All that, and you still couldn't get into Yale. She swallowed, and went at the bag a little harder. Her fist slipped the rhythm, and she barely ducked out of the way from a bloody nose. Shaking her head, she stilled the swinging back with her gloves until it was no longer vibrating. “Mm,” she murmured, taking a deep breath. She had to get herself together. No use dwelling on the past, Cami... She'd been telling herself that for about five years now. It was amazing, she thought, starting up her rhythm slowly. Amazing how nothing she had learned in high school had really ended up serving her at all in any capacity. She'd been on the fast track to Yale, until she'd gotten turned down – and that had been her ticket into the world of corporate law, just like she'd dreamed of since she was thirteen years old. All-state volleyball had been a passion. Not the kind of passion that could get you into a good school, though. She'd had offers, offers from SEC schools (Florida and South Carolina). FSU too. But she hadn't accepted any of those. Instead, she'd ended up in community college in Orlando, forty-five minutes' drive from her hometown of Deland. She'd played volleyball there for a little while, before she'd just lost the spirit for it. French? Not so much. You couldn't get a job from majoring in French. She'd taken it as a minor for her Associate's Degree, but there was no big future in that. Translators made a pittance, teachers even worse. That sort of thing wasn't for her. It was funny, then, that she found herself in the position she was in right now. She'd blame it mostly on her father's death, which had brought more questions than it had answers. Not the death itself – it was nothing suspicious. Post heart-attack cardiac arrest, nothing surprising after years of eating whatever you wanted and not bothering to take care of yourself. Just the aftermath – going through his files and records, finding out that after all this time, Theodore Winter had had a good many secrets. That made her think of Nate again. Nate was a wildcard in Cami's life, not the type of person she ever would have hung around. It wasn't that she would have called him a hood rat or anything. Although, to be fair, she reflected, it was entirely likely that she would have had she met him in high school She'd mellowed considerably since then. (And this was a fact that might have scared anyone who might have considered themselves “normal,” being that Cami seemed to most like a tightass). But, whether she liked, understood, or cared about Nate's background, he was here. Her ticket to whatever the wrestling business held for her. They had to do it together, because there was frankly no way either one of them was making it alone. If nothing else, it was a lesson about just how strong blood ties were. Either they'd learn to live with each other, or it'd be another busted tie. Her mother might as well have been one, considering how unhappy she was that Cami was, as she put it “joining the equivalent of a traveling circus.” She'd gone a minute past her allotted time, she saw, as she looked back up at the clock. That meant she'd have to spend an extra minute cooling down, and then she might possibly be late for meeting Phillip and Nate at the restaurant down the street. She picked up her towel, scrubbing it across her face to mop off the sweat, and fretting briefly about how frizzy her hair was likely to be. She'd had her last relaxer fairly recently, so it still held pretty well, unless she got super sweaty. As usual, no one really seemed to pick up on the fact that she was biracial, and she was just as content to keep it that way. That probably would have rankled Nate, she suspected, but they didn't know each other well enough to talk about things like that. They were hardly past pleasantries some days, although their talk about wrestling seemed to get fairly deep. It was their prime means of relating to one another, she thought, finding that just as strange as she always had. Something she hadn't cared a pin about while she was growing up had suddenly become everything. Stretching felt good. Her tired muscles burned gently as she bent at the waist, stretching long over her right leg. Had she thought twice about it, she would have realized that there were at least a few guys giving her lithe, muscular form a look or two as she did so, but Cami didn't exactly think of herself as a sex symbol, despite the popular conception of women in this sport. She and Phillip had already had words about that. Not to mention she and Nate. She frowned as she stretched over her other leg. She realized, now that she thought about it, that there was some truth to the things that they were saying, to the reality of women in the wrestling business, even in this modern day and age. Precedents had been set a long time ago, and bucking them usually didn't get you anywhere. But part of Cami still hoped that Rampage was her big opportunity to show people that she wasn't just another helping of T&A. That, above everything else, she was talent, that gender didn't matter in considering Cameron Winter. Phillip had told her, more or less, that that was a tall order. One nigh impossible to pull off, unless you were that one in a million phenom. Much to Cami's chagrin, he had had no hesitation in telling her that she was not, in his mind, a one in a million phenom. She looped her towel around her neck as she walked over to her bag and stepped back into her track pants, to cover up the short volleyball shorts that were customary workout wear. She wasn't going to walk out on the street with that much skin showing. Unlike some of the other women she'd seen walking around backstage, she liked to think she had some dignity. That was another thing she and Phillip had had words about. “First thing,” he'd said, “you're gonna have to get over thinkin' that your shit don't stink. People get tired of that attitude real fast in fresh meat. You wanna have that attitude in this sport, you gotta pay your dues, you gotta earn it.” “So I've got to be Lestatt Knight, in other words?” Taylor snorted. “Best get that out of your head too. The odds of a woman getting that big in this sport are slim, doesn't matter how good she is.” “Vivica J. Valentine,” Cameron had started to say, but Phillip held up his hand, and she clamped her jaw shut. “They don't call that girl the Fearless Phenom for nothin', alright? Now I'm not sayin' that you can't get there eventually. But right now? You gotta watch your ass, and you gotta learn to be able to kiss ass, at least a little bit. You don't, you're gonna get drummed out of this company so fast your head'll be spinning.” That had been a bitter pill to swallow. In fact, she couldn't have honestly said that she'd swallowed it yet. She shouldered her bag and walked back out into the street. Honking traffic assailed her ears immediately, but she'd learned to tune it out relatively well. Had to, at least, considering she wouldn't have gotten any sleep for the past week if she hadn't. New York City was an entirely new animal from what she was used to. Deland seemed sleepy by comparison, and Deland had been a metropolis compared to the surrounding towns. Camponotta's was three blocks down the street. 'Blocks,' however, was a relative term in this city. She was winded by the time she got to the door. As soon as she walked in, she was assailed by a whiff of garlic that made her frown. The place was supposedly a 'meat and three' according to Phillip, but the only thing she saw on the buffet was a bunch of over-buttered vegetables and pasta dishes. In fact, it seemed like butter was a part of the air quality of this place, like she was swimming through it when she walked in. Then, she wasn't exactly on a gourmet budget. Phillip was sitting over in the corner, chewing on the end of his unlit cigar. Probably wondering why she was late. Dangling her bag loosely over her shoulder with one hand, she approached his table, and extended a hand to him professionally as usual. “Sorry if I'm late,” she said. “Workout ran a little overtime.” Phillip checked his watch and shrugged, not shaking her hand. He considered it too formal. “Sure you didn't just plan it that way? I don't want you bustin' your hump the day before this shindig and pulling something, alright? Sit down.” Cameron slung her bag onto the bench next to her and flopped down on it. “I didn't do it on purpose, you know. Not to mention, it's a hell of a walk here. Not used to these long-ass blocks. What are they, a mile-and-a-half each?” At that, Phillip chuckled, a laugh that Cameron could have best described as “crusty.” “You got a lot to learn, kid.” “No shit,” Cameron said, cracking a smile for what felt like the first time all afternoon. Got to work on that, she thought. Too damn serious, gonna break my face if I don't start smiling more. She looked around the somewhat smoky dining area, and tilted her head. “Where's Nate?” Taylor shrugged at that, twirling his cigar between his fingers. “Seen him already. Said he wanted to head in early tonight, so he left already. You're my round two,” he said, patting his pot belly with his other hand to indicate that he'd already eaten. Cameron made a face. “So is there anything here that won't clog up my arteries? I've got a family history of heart disease, you know.” “Yeah, I know, heard that story,” Taylor said, standing up. “Do me a favor and don't judge a place by its looks. I happen to know that this place is pretty easy on the body, organic shit and all that. Fresh made. Go to it.” It wasn't an option. Cameron stood up and made her way over to the buffet line. “Some meatloaf there,” Taylor said. That wasn't an option either. “Carrots and beans. Fruit salad. And some of that pasta, too.” “Pasta?” Cameron said, raising an eyebrow at him. “You said you played volleyball before, right?” “Yeah, why?” Seeing that she wasn't immediately placated, Phillip leaned over and spooned the pasta onto her plate himself. “Carbs. After your ass is wore out tomorrow, you'll thank me for makin' you eat heavy tonight.” She shrugged. They went further down the line, where Phillip snapped up a bottle of water and a banana. He pulled out his wallet as they got to the cashier. “I don't mind paying for it you know...” Cameron started. Phillip smiled. “What, I can't take care of it myself? My treat, alright? It's the night before you first pay-per-view event, I think you deserve not to worry about your dinner. And if you gimme a hard time, I'll rough ya up. After tomorrow night, of course.” That, too, was not something that was going to be arguable. They were silent when they got back to the table. Phillip mostly seemed to be watching Cameron, who had found her appetite. She didn't realize, sometimes, how hungry she got. It had always happened that way for her – she'd get into the zone, focusing on studying or working out or something and not realize that she'd missed lunch by three hours. “You know, Cam, I've been thinking about something,” Taylor said, breaking the silence. Cameron, her mouth full of pasta, finished chewing, though she looked up at him. “What's that?” “Not now, of course, not in the next coupla weeks or anything, but... I've been thinkin' that maybe you should go solo.” Cameron's well-plucked eyebrows shot up. She didn't even think about finishing the mouthful she was chewing. “What?!” “I mean Nate's a good kid, don't get me wrong,” Phillip started, putting his hands up in his defense. “I like him, I like you. It's not like I got somethin' against you two teaming, but to tell you the truth...” He ran his fingers across his shining bald head, which seemed now to be gleaming with sweat. “I've been in this business for a long time, Cami. I read people who are in it pretty well. And the truth is that I know. I know when I see the makings of a star, when somebody's got the natural talent to shoot right up the ranks, and I know when I see somebody who's got the drive to make it big through their own elbow grease.” “I thought I wasn't a phenom,” Cameron said, not hiding her obvious sarcasm. “You're not a phenom,” Taylor continued, his gestures getting more excited as his conviction rose. “What you are, though, is somebody who's got a mind for this business. Wrestling's, you know, a different animal. It ain't the corporate world, but it's got some of those same elements. You gotta be ruthless. You gotta know the rules well enough to know which ones to break. You gotta be daring, but not stupid.” Taylor shrugged, loosening the collar of his shirt. “Nate? Nate's brash. Not sayin' Nate's stupid, but he's got a lotta moxie for what the people in that locker room see as a dumb kid. You gotta understand, Cami, you are shrewd. You know what it takes to succeed in a high-pressure environment. Nate? Nate's in it for the laughs, I think. Nate likes the idea of an easy rise to the top. But you? I look in your eyes and I see it, Cami, you know it's not gonna be easy, that there are a lot of asses to kiss to get where you wanna be. A lot of shit to put up with.” His palm slapped the table, as if emphasizing the last part. “You got what it takes. Nate, I don't know. I just don't see it in him. I think that if you pursue this tag team thing too far, he'll drag you down with him. Seen a lotta kids like him. They come in fired up, they crash and burn. It ain't gonna be pretty, but you don't have to be a part of it.” Cameron swallowed, her eyes thoughtful, stormy. “I... don't know. It's not that I don't trust you,” she said, running her free hand through her hair, whose flyaways were, she frowned, out of control at the moment. “It's not that at all. I know you know what you're talking about, Phillip, but... I can't just cut him loose, you know? I can't make a stupid decision in my personal life just to get ahead in my professional life. This tag team... it's.. it's a good thing for us, okay? I'd kind of like to see where it goes before I give up on it completely.” Phillip watched her for a long moment. Then he shook his head, allowed himself to smile. “You're a good kid, hey? Just keep it in mind. I'm with you, I don't want you makin' any hasty decisions that you're gonna regret, but just know that if things get a little rough, there is a future out there for you, least from where I'm seein' it. Maybe you can talk to him, Cami. Impress on him how this whole thing works. If you catch 'em early, kids like Nate can get straight, but they gotta have that temperin' influence.” Cameron raised her eyebrows. Given that one colossal failure had sent her spiraling into a few more, she wasn't sure how good of an example she was. “I can try anyway...” Taylor plainly thought she meant that she understood how difficult of a case Nate was. It wasn't that at all. It was the need, deep inside her own self, to find something to succeed at, after embarrassing herself so badly in the past five years. She had to do this. As she looked back down into her plate to finish eating, that fact was impressed upon her. She had to do this, but she didn't know if she had to do it with Nate, and Taylor's words weighed heavily on her. She knew, as his words sank further in, that she'd have a hard time sleeping tonight. And Cameron was a confident person – it wasn't because she was scared of rising to the occasion at Rampage. It would be because she knew she was going out there with someone that December Revolution's own manager didn't fully believe in. And she knew that she'd be up tonight wondering, wrestling her own mental match, over whether he was right.
