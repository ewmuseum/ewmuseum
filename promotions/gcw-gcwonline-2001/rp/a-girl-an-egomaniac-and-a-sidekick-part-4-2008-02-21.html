A Girl, An Egomaniac, and a Sidekick: Part 4

21 February 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/21/08 (takes place 8/02) August 2002 A woman’s worries never rest. With as many outfits as Tessa had packed away for press conferences and possible nights out on the town with new found friends, it’d be easy to believe she’d have at least one she saw suitable for a night out with a guy she hardly knew. Still, Tessa found herself struggling to narrow her selection down between a lacy black halter dress, which complimented her hour glass figure well and, a baby blue sundress patterned with white flowers, which brought out her eyes. Even she was starting to become frustrated with herself, often reminding herself that “personality was more important” than her attire. If only she could totally convince herself of that. “Man, why didn’t I pack that cocktail dress?” Her reflection was questioned as she diverted her attention to her hair, “It’d be perfect for this occasion.” Whatever this occasion was supposed to be. He defined it as “time alone to make an apology”. Was that considered a date? Or was it just a random meeting between two professional wrestlers who happen to be of the opposite gender? But then again, what did it matter what it was? She couldn’t control his feelings, and she certainly could not predict what was going to go on, so why worry now? Worrying would do absolutely nothing! “Okay, I’m going with black!” She declared to her reflection, sticking the final bobby pin into the bun that rested on the back of her head. She then edged her hands along the bathroom counter, in search of her makeup. Then, that ping of uncertainty hit again, “You better look your best if you want to impress. I bet with his kind of money he could get just about any woman he wanted.” She repeated it to herself, “Any woman he wanted!” Yet he picked her. A woman who had yet to make much of a name for herself, save for a few small wins in a tournament. A woman who didn’t grace the cover of magazines, or have much to her name other than a small house and an average car. Then, she reminded herself that it was never officially a date, and that she were getting her hopes up again. Her eyes glanced over to the clock and noticed that it was hour til they were supposed to meet, which meant her fussing would have to end. The black dress, a pair of pumps in the same color, and a diamond necklace would have to do for her tonight. “Please make a good first impression.” ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- Chris sat in the back of the restaurant with a menu covering most of his face. There was nothing he hated more than being interrupted while trying to have a dinner. Though most of the people there didn’t give two shits about a jacked up wrestler, to Chris it felt like all eyes were on him. In reality, there was only one pair of eyes on him. “Hey.” Tessa’s whisper could barely be heard over the chatter of others around her. Yet Chris, even without seeing her, could feel her eyes deadlocked on him. Placing his menu down, Chris rose from his chair to greet her. A frown accidently spread over her lips as she noticed him in casual jeans and one of his WIW t-shirts that read “I Rule the World”. Realizing her glum expression, she tried to mask it with a cheerier one, but he picked up on it quickly. “Something wrong?” Her hands quickly waved off any sign of distress, “Not at all.” But in her mind, things were else wise. “So,” she said, crawling into the seat across from him, “what was it you needed to speak to me about?” Though her eyes tried to keep on her menu, she couldn’t help but feel awkward as Chris? eyes bore into her. “I want you to quit that pathetic place you call a wrestling promotion. What was its name again, XCWF” They waste your talent in those idiotic bra and panty matches.” It certainly got her ears to open fully, “I’ve got talent?” This was coming from a man who was verbally tearing her apart only two weeks back, “And I can’t just quit, I’d kind of be unemployed, you know?” Chris scoffed, “I don’t see you being unemployed if you accepted my offer.” Tessa lifted up her eyebrows. That was when Chris reached down beside the table and grabbed something out of a bag Tessa hadn’t noticed until just now. Inside, he had a set of papers, which he offered to Tessa. “A contract?” she asked, stunned. “A very hefty contract,” Without even blinking, he reached back into that same bag, this time pulling out a pen, “and the opportunity of a lifetime.” Her hand gravitated towards the pen, but stopped right before as the logical half of her brain kicked in, “That’s nice and all, but why would I want to just up and leave the company I’m with now?” The urge to laugh nearly caused Chris to lose his serious disposition. “Are you kidding me” Have you taken a look at your career there? How far are you going to get in this business wrestling against the same women week after week?” He pushed the contract toward her again. “What I offer here” he pointed to the dotted line, “is a chance to wrestle with the big boys week in and week out.” Her writing hand crawled closer to the pen, but there was something about the man before her that bothered her conscious, thus she had to go against her gut instinct, “I’ll think about it.” The answer annoyed him, it was visible. “Think” Hmph,” he snorted, “what is there to think about” You have two options open to you. You can either continue to have mud wrestling matches for ogling males in a promotion that doesn’t give two shits about you, OR,” his finger slammed into the contract, “you can sign with us and actually make a difference. You’ll be given a chance to be something more than just a Women’s champion.” So much for that date she was hoping for. She should have known she had no chance with one of today’s hottest rising stars, now she was stuck trying to stand her grounds with him, “I’m still going to think about it. Why leave a place where I’m already making a name for myself?” He shook his head, “Fine, think about it.” Getting up from his seat, he grabbed his bag before reaching into his pocket. “Think it over dinner” he said as he tossed down a few bills on the table for her. He then started to head out, but stopped as he passed her. Tilting his head down toward her he whispered to her “For the record, this conversation never happened.”
