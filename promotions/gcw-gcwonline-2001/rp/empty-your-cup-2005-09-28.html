Empty Your Cup

28 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/28/05 'Every now and then, you have a good idea.' Every now and then. Not often, though. With a small sigh, the duffel bag with workout clothes inside of it comes off the ground and into my hand, and my other hand clicks the little button that locks the car. A small chirping sound indicates my vehicle is safe, and that's at least one less worry in my life as each step brings me closer to better days. Days when training and working out meant something deeper than just beating someone in one match or another. It meant a sense of pride, of serving the team, of serving the people at the school or in the family that looked up to you and wanted to be you. Wanted to be Johnathan Fitzwillis. Wanted to be Johnny Borealis, athlete extraordinare. With the demeanor of someone twice his height, a quarterback who was faster than most receivers on the team, someone who could move smoother and stronger than any other athlete near him, and a marvel to behold on the mat, on the field, or in the gym. Now look at me. I'm not even to my mid-twenties, and already so much has started to fade away. I used to be able to get drilled into the cold, hard ground by four linemen at once and jump up, ready to do it again. Break my arm, be back in lacrosse in a month. Now I stumble off the mat, backstage, and can barely move without something reminding me of ass kickings from months ago. And I feel...small. For the first time in my life, I feel like I'm only six foot. I feel like two hundred pounds won't cut it. I remember coming into this business and looking at guys that were nearing four hundred pounds, which is a fucking fifth of a ton, and not even noticing. I towered over guys that were almost seven feet tall. Now I almost feel like good enough just...isn't good enough anymore. So there's two options. One is to magically gain six inches and a hundred pounds. The other is to return to what made me feel so big and bad. 'Funny. I thought it'd be bigger.' Grand Master Chan Fo. Teaches Kempo. He's supposed to be the best. As in, the best ever. He's praised round the world for having been one of the youngest masters of martial arts ever, as well as being one of the greatest. Some say he's up there with Bruce Lee. That's doubtful of course, but we'll never find out since Bruce Lee proved that he isn't greater than death. And for everything I've heard about this man, for all the talk that goes around about how great he is but how humble he is, how phyisically and spiritually superior to others he is, how wise he is, I honestly imagined his school to look like a school. Instead of a dojo, this place is a poor little Japanese restaurant. On walking in, I can tell right away that I'd rather eat the prepackaged sushi that they sell at Tops than this Soylent Green style filth. They folk here make the attempt to look good, but their priorities are in the wrong place. They got a huge statue to some ancient Japanese dragon god bearing down over the entrance; what's seen as a blessing is interpreted as a curse. Overbearing good intentions will suffocate someone all too easily. The extraordinarily hot young waitress comes right up to me. 'Table for one?' 'I'm, uh, not really here to eat. I was wondering if I could see Grand Master Chan Fo. I'd like to respectfully request learning his art.' I speak slowly and deliberately. I assume she's heavily Japanese with a limited Enlgish dialect. I'm wrong. She sounds like she's American born and raised. 'Master Fo doesn't just teach anyone. Table for one?' She says it like it's what she tells absolutely everybody when they come in here. Not missing a beat, not even wondering who I am or what I'm doing looking for training from someone like the greatest master on the Eastern Seaboard. 'Look...it's really important that I learn from Master Fo. It means more to me right now than just about anything in my life. You see, I'm-' 'I know who you are, Mr. Borealis. Big fan.' 'Really? Why...thank you.' A little charm and a slight lowering of the voice can get you a lot of things. Assistance, flirting, martial arts lessons- 'Table for one?' ...a table for one. All kinds of things. The sky's the limit, it really is. 'What do I have to do to have Master Fo teach me what he knows? I mean...you do realize that I'm not actually what you see on TV, right? The arrogance, the eagerness, the impatience...it's all part of the act. The magical facade of professional wrestling. In reality, I'm patient, humble, and I actually do want to work harder than anybody.' Did I ever mention that I definitely have the superpower of being able to lie like nobody else on the planet? Honestly. I could lie to a nun so well that I'd have her joining the Manson Family just because of what I said about it. It's a gift. It really is. She smiles politely. Trained as a waitress to put up with the best of flirting or the biggest pain in the ass the same way. There's no breaking her. She's probably make a great female wrestler. 'Table for one. Tea for two.' Sigh... With hardened acceptance, I bring my gym bag into the restaurant with me. Take a seat at the small table with the cheap place setting and the feeling that I don't belong. Maybe it's the twenty Japanese people that are staring at this six foot giant that just walked in. More likely it's the wondering of what's going on anymore. It's been eating away at my insides lately. It just seems so backwards...my life, that is. Whenever I'm doing well in the business, my home life is shit. And as my home life improves, my reputation in GCW takes a dive. It's the constant struggle. The up and the down always balance out so that I'm always at some sort of equilibrium...some sort of purgatory with little change. Any improvement only brings down a different area, and any clusterfuck on the left brings about a clearing of skies on the right. Kinda like Yin and Yang. Order and Chaos. Which may be why I think Grand Master Chan Fo can help me. I sit here, so engrossed in my own thoughts, that I only notice the old man approach at the last possible moment before he sits down. Something most distracting indeed, to keep me off my toes. Or perhaps I've gotten that dull. 'I have heard you are looking for a master of the martial arts.' Of course I recognize Chan Fo. Who wouldn't? Or, at least, who would seek him out and not know what he looks like? I have a few magazines that deal with buying training equipment and clothing. Any jackass that wants to open up his own Karate school can just order whatever the hell he wants, call himself an instructor, and charge people money for it. And every single one of them would recognize this man. Just like the legit ones respect him. He's gotta be about seventy. A small, old, Japanese man. He dresses simply, wearing only an olive green button up shirt and khakis that are stained with various things from the kitchen that should probably have been left in the back alley. But I guess if you grew up in Nagasaki a poor Japanese child, you'd probably find yourself eating cat to this day because you couldn't bear to let good food go to waste. So I can't blame him and not hate myself. The man's got smiling eyes and gentle hands. But that doesn't fool me for a second and there's not a chance that I'm going to look at him and doubt that he could kill me in a second. But maybe that's just my way of approaching the situation. I don't know. I'm gonna shut up now and let the master speak to me. 'Tea?' He holds a metal pot and two wooden cups. 'I really don't mean to be a pain. I was just wondering-' 'Do you want some tea?' The eyes still smile, but the voice is stern. Don't waste his time, Jack. Story of my life. '...I'll have some tea...' He sits down. Not slowly, but smoothly. Everything he does is smooth. He pours himself a cup of tea. The tan liquid actually seems quite appealing and relaxing as he fills his cup. He then moves the teapot smoothly, the time between tipping it up, stopping the pouring, and tipping it back down and pouring it in my cup is seamless. Like he's directing a God damn orchestra. And then he fills my cup. And he keeps filling it... And filling it... And it's overflowing... And for a moment, I watch as the hot tea begins to spill onto the table, puddling, inching its way off of the table and toward my lap as I move my chair so that it spills harmlessly on the floor. It's then that I realize it's a test. So, I state the obvious. 'No more tea will go into my cup, Grand Master Chan Fo.' Almost as soon as I start talking, he stops pouring. He is completely serene and unconcerned with the mess made. After all, it's only spilled tea. Life goes on. His eyes look at the pot as he puts it back in place and speaks. 'That cup...represents you. It is your mind. It is your soul. I cannot place any more tea into your cup. It must first be emptied.' His eyes comes back up to be and they still smile, even though he is still serious. Again, I state the obvious. 'I don't understand.' 'You are Johnathan Fitzwillis. My grand daughter told me you were here and who you are and I asked her what she knows about you so that I could come here and speak with you. She has told me that you are a professional wrestler. A man who is paid for fighting. A man that takes pride in his ability to hurt others and withstand pain. And she said that you were interested in speaking to me about taking lessons in Kempo.' The old man drinks his tea and I don't say anything. I won't until I'm sure he's done. Sure enough, he continues as he pours himself another cup. Mind you, it's scalding hot. Too hot for me. But he's a tough old Japanese guy, so I'm not surprised that he's downing this stuff like it's nothing. 'You come to me, Mr. Fitzwillis, and you know how to fight. You know about me. You know what works for you and what doesn't work for you. And she told me, based on how you move in your matches, that you have had experience in the martial arts before.' His eyes ask me what experience I've had, even though he only words a statement. 'I took Tai Kwan Do as a child. Got my black belt at seventeen, but I dropped out of it in favor of a full career as a wrestler. It wasn't as good as I thought it'd be. Taught me a lot of kicks and how to do them well. Taught me how to punch a bit, and how to take a hit. But I wanted something that concentrated more on practical fighting. And I talked to someone I know, and they said that Kempo was one of the more solid forms of realistic fighting.' 'Kempo...the way of the fist... It may teach the moves that you use to entertain...but it does not teach to use them except to entertain. It teaches to avoid the fight completely, and methods on how to do so. It teaches that fighting is a last resort, not a recreational activity. And it teaches to end the fight as fast as possible if you are so unfortunate to be engaged.' 'I see...' 'Do you, Mr. Fitzwillis? Because I will not teach you how to use Kempo so you can use it in your profession.' 'I wouldn't-...I'm not trying to-...' 'Lemon?' 'What?' 'For your tea.' I'm racking my brain to keep from yelling at this old man right now. Sitting here, in the middle of this restaurant that I so obviously don't belong in... Half my life is falling apart while the other half is on the knife's edge between happiness and tragedy. I'm ecstatic to have a child. Being a father is the only thing that I can wake up in the morning and not feel like I have to constantly fight for it. I have to fight, tooth and nail, to keep my US Championship. I have to fight against my worse judgement and, frankly, Kate's better judgement to stay with the woman I love. But the only person in the world that accepts me without question is this little girl...for whom I've missed the first five years of her life. She still wants me around and she will always be my daughter, and that makes me the luckiest man in the world. On the other hand... I'm Johnny Borealis. And I fuck shit up. Like I'm addicted to it. Loving relationship with father? I'll fuck that up. Free ride to college? I'll fuck it up. In love with an amazing woman? I'll fuck that up too. Perfect body, friends with the devil, TV Championship, trust of the fans, you name it and I'll screw it up like nobody's business! And now I've got something irreplaceable. Something completely loving and forgiving. But something that isn't un-fuckup-able. And for this reason, I need to get my shit together and get my head on straight. And if that means being a jackass at work instead of at home, instead of the other way around, that's fine. And if it means taking lessons from the best God damn Kempo master I've ever heard of, I'm also fine with that. 'Look, I can prove myself to you however you want. I won't use what you teach me for anything but my own betterment. I won't-' 'I asked if you want some lemon...for your tea.' His old eyes don't smile. They understand. But he said what he had to say because he had to say it. No other reason. It's like when someone asks, 'How you doing?' and you always answer, 'Good.' Or if you're not good, you always go into the most detail to tell them why your life is shittier than theirs. It's just what you have to do. But this old man understands. He just wants me to go through the motions like I have to. 'No thanks. I drink my tea straight.' And I do just that. For the first time since I received my cup of tea, I take a sip. It's cooled down a bit, which is good because I'm not quite the raging tea drinking monster that Chan Fo apparently is. Bitter. Warm. Delicious. Just like me. I suppose the motion is more symbolic than it has to be. My cup is empty. I try to empty myself. Try to become light and ready to learn, not judging anything he will tell me on my prior experience. I wish to be a true student and nothing more. And I think he realizes this. 'Do you even realize that you have already had your first lesson?' 'Really?' 'Yes. And I do charge for the first lesson.' Old bastard. 'How much?' 'You buy the tea.' He motions to the cups, but smoothly continues to motion to the puddle on the table and the one that has formed on the ground. As I said earlier, the man doesn't like to waste anything. I'm buying the whole pot. 'Maybe if I had stopped you from pouring sooner, I wouldn't have to buy the whole pot.' 'Lesson number two. Don't be cheap. That one's free, ironically.' His eyes smile again. The old man's got a sense of humor. A polite one to start with, of course. But I can almost see beneath the surface someone who sits around with his version of 'the guys' and tells jokes that'll make sailors blush and drink like fiends. Again, story of my life. 'So you will teach me?' He seems to consider for a moment. There is a big difference between giving a lesson and really getting serious with teaching somebody. And he knows the responsibility he has. He's probably taught hundreds, even thousands of people. Some of them took in his teachings well, while others may have been failures that abused what he taught. He has become cautious. 'What will you use? And what will you refrain from using?' 'My muscles have grown stale over the years, Master. When I took Tai, my kicks were amazing. I could take brutality on the field or on the mat like nobody else. I was something to behold. But recently, I've had to rely on less than practical methods of fighting to be the winner. With muscles getting tired and balance waning, it's been harder and harder to stay at the top of my competing edge. I want to move like I did when I was younger. Better. Faster and stronger. Put some more muscle on to add some power. Build up speed, both reactionary and wind-wise. And everything I've heard about Kempo says that it might be the right choice for that stuff. As for how deadly it is...I'm not going to abuse it. I have only the utmost respect for fighting. I mean...it's my life. I'm not just some punk off the street that wants to know how to beat someone up. I'm a professional and I take pride in how and when I use my talents. And there is no chance in hell I would ever use what I learned from you in a wrong way.' He nods his head. Lets out a 'Hmmmmm' that seems kind of calm. He's thinking. Not about what I said, but about what he should do with it. How it should be processed. 'Now...tell me why you want to learn.' 'I just-' 'You told me what you would and wouldn't do with my teachings. Tell me what caused you to get out and decide that you want to take this on.' He drinks another cup of tea while I'm hit with the weight of that statement. Images blur in my head too fast for me to discern between them anymore. A picture of my mother blurs into a picture of my daughter...my career turns into my youth which turns into my weekend which turns into booze and strippers which turns into my friend the devil which turns into worrying about my car. The mess upstairs needs sorting, and as of late...I've been at a loss for how to do that. How to get my life back in order. How to stop the images from flying by too fast for me to separate where one ends and where another begins. And to understand what they mean in the first place. ...and that's all been so difficult since Kate convinced me to stop taking painkillers. Turns out they weren't killing the pain at all. They were just feeding the flames. And every time I feel like I need a white angel to help me out...I get flashbacks of six years of torment that I brought upon myself and five years of lonliness that my daughter didn't deserve and it comes to me that I need something to stabalize my life. Oh, and for the record, Kate and I are having sex again. I just thought I'd throw that in. And yes. It is out of this world. 'I need something with order. Something that won't change in a month. Or a week. Or tomorrow. ... I could wake up in the morning and not be a champion. Hell, I could wake up and not even be a wrestler. I could get out of bed and my girl and my baby girl could leave me. Anything can happen as long as the world keeps turning. And we need things to find out there in the world that are constant that we can grip onto and see some form of reality with. ...I used to have a friend that I depended on for this. He was my only link to the outside world from inside my perspective, and I didn't even realize that it was all an illusion that he was showing me until I was already in over my head. He was a real devil, that one.' Perhaps I'll go into Don and him being Satan with my new teacher some other time...but not now. 'But I look at you, Master... You've been around, teaching martial arts for half a century. All kinds of people, rich and poor, famous and meek, have come to you. And I think that's about as constant as it can get.' We sit in unbearable silence for a moment. No smiles in his eyes. No smile on his face. He is deep in thought and consideration. Perhaps my third lesson is to be the essence of patience and wait, above all else, for Master to come to a decision. But as I look at the clock and see that it strikes a full eight minutes of unnerving silence, he finally breaks the wall of silence and floods me with his decision. 'I will take you as my kohai. My student. I will be your sensai. And you will work for me, when you can, as part of your tuition.' 'So I'm not paying any money?' A hearty laugh from the old man shatters any stereotypes I just had of him. 'That is a good one, Kohai John! You are a funny one!' A little bit of blushing from my own stupidity. It was worth asking. 'Drink the rest of your tea. You can come with me. And we can begin work on your next lesson.'
