Lifesblood for the Downtrodden, Part 1: Things Fall Apart

20 September 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 20th Sept. I Jane Murray Tap tap tap? The gentle pitter-patter of knuckles on mahogany gently floated through the confines of her modest wooden abode while she lay half-awake. Cursed by yet another night of restless slumber, Jane stared straight up from her pillow, completely oblivious to the fact that it was already gone 10am. She should have been up hours ago to fulfil her motherly duties and set Marvin off to school, but yet again he’d been forced to set himself up for the day all on his own. Tap tap tap? If only she could see the mess that she was in. A couple of months ago she was flying high at the side of Chris Bagwell and unleashing a seemingly unstoppable smear campaign against her husband. She’d quit her job to tour the country with Bagwell, and by all accounts it seemed like she would finally have the better of her former spouse. And now she is but a shell of a woman. Kicked to the curb by professional wrestling’s most controversial grappler and on the brink of ruin as a result of Andy’s impending legal action, Jane Murray was well and truly in the shit. TAP TAP TAP! The knocking on the door began to filter into her subconscious but it wasn’t quite enough to blot out her ill-feelings towards both Chris Bagwell and Andy Murray. What the hell were you thinking, Jane? She’d asked herself that very question so many times over the past few weeks that the words had almost lost all meaning. Not that it wasn’t a valid query; what kind of woman in her right mind would choose to shack up with Chris Bagwell? Nope, not a particularly smart move, hooking up with one of the most volatile personalities in the wrestling industry. And then there was Andy. The writ had been filed almost a week ago now and still it sat on her kitchen table, gathering dust. She couldn’t remember the date of the summons but it didn’t matter; she didn’t have the money to hire an attorney, and she didn’t have the assets to put up a substantial battle against Andy. Jane was fucked. THUMP! THUMP! THUMP! Muffled voices coming from behind the door. The knocking had grown far too loud and intense for her to ignore any longer, and she slid her way out of bed ? still clad in last night’s clothes ? just as the door frame stopped shaking. Brushing away the straggled mass of brown hair that had been matted to her forehead by sweat, Jane shambled out of her bedroom and lurched through her house in a manner not unlike a monster of George Romero’s creation. Eventually she reached the front door. “Who’s there?” she croaked as she squinted through the peephole. Again she heard the muffled voices but found herself unable to make out a word. The peephole was blurry and badly needed a clean, but Jane thought she could make out the outlines of three similar-looking gentlemen, all clad in identical grey uniforms. Rolling her eyes she brought up the deadbolt and pulled the door open. “What is it?” she croaked, raising a dainty hand to cover her eyes from the sunlight pouring through the doorframe. “Mrs. Murray?” the guy in the middle enquired in soft, Southern tones. He was tall and gaunt; looked like he hadn’t had a square meal or a good night’s sleep in a long time. Dressed in a dim, grey shirt with a red logo and with a black cap adorned with the same insignia, he looked slightly taken aback by the state that Andy Murray’s former wife was in, and so he hesitated. “Whatever you’re trying to sell, I’m not interested,” Jane replied to the men who’d no doubt heard that very same line used countless times. She glanced briefly at the other two guys; both of them had the same shirt grey shirt but neither of them wore baseball caps. One of them was slightly smaller than the man in the middle and looked a good few years younger, the other was at least half a foot shorter, and in stark contrast to his colleague looked like he’d had a few too many square meals recently. “Quite the contrary,” responded the group’s elected spokesperson, pulling a small piece of paper from his pocket and handing it to Jane. “We’re here to reclaim your debt on our client’s behalf.” All it took was that one word jumping off the page to kill what little buzz Jane Murray had left. “Repossession Warrant,” sticking out like a black sheep, taunting her incessantly. Unable to find words, Jane could only gaze at the gaunt figure, open-jawed. “Bu-?” she mumbled. “Due to your constant failure to clear the credit balance on your account, Mrs. Murray, I am afraid that we are going to have to reclaim goods to the value of $2,245 immediately,” said the repo man with the machine-like efficiency of a person who’d repeated those lines thousands of times prior. “Guys,” he said, nodded at his two cohorts who made an attempt to brush past him and into the house. “No! I won’t let you!” Jane trembled, trying to throw her skinny frame in the way. “Mrs. Murray, the document you have in your hands there is legally binding. I suggest you let us go about our business before I am forced to alert the authorities.” She had no choice. Shell-shocked, Jane let the three men breeze past her and into the house, helpless to watch on as they began helping themselves to her worldly possessions. This wasn’t exactly what she’d planned when she quit her job back in April to join the Chris Bagwell gravy train. II Andy Murray It was early afternoon by the time Andy even thought of leaving his bed. Usually he loathed getting up any time later than eight “o clock, but intensive travelling had left his body clock ravaged. It’s nigh on impossible to keep up with a regular sleeping pattern when you’re in this business; any pro-wrestler will tell you the same thing. You try getting a solid eight hours of sleep every night when you’ve got to plan your rest around flights, training schedules, public appearances, and of course, shows. Still, he wasn’t in any position to complain; it was a rare enough occasion for Andy to even wake up in his own home these days anyway, so his post-1pm wake up wasn’t exactly causing him a great deal of concern. Things were going strong for the man Global Championship Wrestling knows as “The Scottish King of Cool” on both the professional and personal fronts. On-air he’d notched up impressive victories over two esteemed GCW stalwarts in Bryan Mayhem and Shaman, and his constant back-and-forths with LeStatt Knight were the talk of the locker-room. His rapid ascent to the top of the GCW card was showing no signs of wavering yet, and a good showing against former United States champion Tessa Windsor at WorldWide this week would only help matters. At home, too, it seemed like Andy’s notoriously turbulent personal life was finally beginning to settle down a little. His brothers had been working tooth and nail with him on his wrestling school and it wouldn’t be too long before that dream would finally become a reality. The legal situation with Jane seemed to be gaining a head of steam as well as his new attorney, Brendan Perry, began to put the finishing touches on his case. Stretching his arm muscles high over his head, Andy let out a long yawn before sitting upright in bed. The bedroom door was open and he could hear somebody moving around downstairs, so he dropped his feet out of bed one after the other. “Ahhh, fuck!” Andy winced as he tried to put his weight on his right leg, before collapsing back down on the bed as sickeningly-familiar barbs of pain began to shoot up his calf muscle. There’s always a catch. Success never comes for free, you’ve always got to pay some kind of price as Andy was finding out. The Scotsman had been experiencing pain in his right leg ever since his steel cell match with Chris Bagwell in July to the point where he found himself almost completely unable to walk at times. After composing himself Andy reached over to his bedside table and grabbed hold of the open bottle of painkillers. He dropped a couple of small, white tablets down his throat without taking a drink and swallowed hard; it would be at least fifteen minutes before they kicked in, so he made himself comfortable in his bed again, pulling his tree-trunk legs back onto the mattress. He didn’t like using the painkillers, having heard all about the horror stories of dependency and excessive use, but it’s not like he had much of a choice. It was those very pills that he helped him get through matches with Bryan Mayhem and Oblivion unscathed, and it will surely be those pills that help him through a clash with LeStatt Knight, if and when that may occur. Just then his solitude was shattered by the sound of gentle footsteps coming up the staircase. Using a couple of pillows to prop himself up against the headboard, Andy watched as Kayla sauntered into the room, a look of concern loitering around her facial features. “Oh my God, are you alright?” she asked, cantering over to the bedside. “I thought I heard you shout?” “Just the leg, K,” Andy sighed. “It’s all good though, tablets should take effect any minute. Say, what’s that you’ve got there?” “Oh this?” she shrugged, holding the white envelope in her hand out to Andy. “For you. I think it’s from the doctor’s; it’s probably your results.” Andy nodded and gratefully accepted the envelope from his partner. He’d been waiting for that letter ever since he’d stepped into the clinic to get his leg looked at earlier in the week and tore through the envelope like a kid on Christmas day. His eyes darted schizophrenically across the page, looking for something to put his concerns to sleep. “Good news?” Kayla asked hopefully. Andy gazed at her, slowly lowering the paper. As the glint in his eyes began to fade, Kayla could tell instantly that the results were not what Andy had hoped for. “Bad fuckin” news,” he confirmed it. III Jane Murray “Hi mom,” ten year old Marvin Murray said in the chirpiest voice he could muster as he slammed the house door behind him unintentionally. Tall for his age, spectacle-wearing and with a well-kept mane of black hair, the kid was looking more and more like his father every day, from his piercing blue eyes right down to his light-hearted outtake. “Mom?” he said again as he dropped his bag to the floor. Usually Jane would be there to greet him at the door; it was one tradition that she refused to abandon, even in these times of strife, so for her not to be there was a concern to Marvin. He dropped his rucksack to the floor and left it inconveniently in the middle of the hallway, as kids do. Traipsing through the home, Marvin eventually stepped into the living room where he found his mother sat on couch, knees held up against her chest as if she were adopted some kind of defensive cocoon. “What the?” his jaw almost dropped as soon as he realised that the room was almost entirely barren. The LCD Television and DVD player had both seemingly vanished into thin air, and there was now a big dusty space where the HiFi system used to be. Looking for answers, Marvin turned to his mother. “Mom” Where’d all the stuff go?” he asked. “It’s?” Jane began, shaking her head. “It’s gone.” “Gone where” Where did you put the TV? The Wii?” “It’s all gone, Marvin. I’m sorry.” “What are you talking about?” Marvin screwed up his face, unable to fully comprehend what was going on. “Look,” Jane said, leaning towards her son, “Mommy got into a little bit of financial trouble. Some men had to come and take all the stuff away” I’m so, so sorry, Marv?” “Huh” Who took it, Dad?” Jane shook her head, brushing away a wayward tear as it escaped her duct. She knew that it was going to crush Marvin as soon as his mind caught on to the reality of the situation. “But it’s all coming back, right?” he asked with blind hope. “No,” mouthed Jane. “You’re going to get my Wii back?” his voice trailed off. He could tell straight away that he would never see those possessions again. “Come he-? Jane leaned in, trying to grab onto her son, but Marvin violently thrust his shoulder away from her. With great sorrow she watched on; his face began to turn red as his little body began to fill with anger. “Get off of me!” he shrieked. “I hate you! You’ve ruined my LIFE!” Marvin stormed out of the room with all the hellfire a ten year old boy could muster. “Marvin! Please! Come back!” Jane shouted back, jumping up from the couch and jogging towards the door. But it was too late; the front door had already slammed shut by the time she had closed her mouth. Marvin was gone. VI Andy Murray “Motherfuckers want me to take some time off! Can you believe that?” Andy Murray said into the cellphone at his ear before shuffling another forkful of pasta into his mouth. “They think I might need some kind of operation?” “Oh yeah?” retorted Andy’s agent ? Rueben Dallas ? on the other end of the phone. “That sounds pretty serious Andy; how long do they think you can hold out without the op?” “The letter’s not specific; just says that if I don’t go under the knife, I run the risk of doing permanent damage,” the Scotsman replied. It had been several hours since he’d washed down the painkillers and now his calf muscle was almost entirely pain-free. “But these pills I’ve got seem to be doing the trick; if I just keep at them I’m sure it’ll go away eventually.” “Hmm, I don’t know, man. It doesn’t sound good...” “What do you mean?” “What I’m trying to say is this; you can’t just paper over the cracks and expect everything to be alright. Sure, the painkillers will tide you by, but if you skip this operation you’re leg’s still going to be fucked, you know?” Rueben explained to his highest earning client. “A little break might not be such a bad idea after all?” Dropping his fork in his bowl of food, Andy couldn’t quite believe what he was hearing. “Are you drunk, Ruebs?” he deadpanned. “I can’t afford to take some time off, especially now! I’ve got Tessa Windsor to go through on WorldWide and I’ve got LeStatt Knight breathing down my neck. Not only that but I’m higher now in the game than I have been in a long time” if I pull out, my momentum’s going to be slaughtered!? “I know Andy, I know,” began Dallas, “but you’ve got to put all your cards on the table with this one and think about your priorities. Sure, it might cost you in the short-term, but what happens if you totally ruin your leg, huh” Would you rather be a GCW World Champion with a bum leg and a dependency on painkillers, or a strong, refreshed injury-free grappler ready to continue his stunning ascent to the top of the game?” Andy sighed loud enough for his agent to hear. Taking time out from GCW was the last thing that he wanted to do, but it did seem like a logical choice. BZZZZT! Suddenly the sound of his electronic doorbell disturbed Andy’s train of thought. “Hey Sid! Get that?” he called out, holding the palm of his hand over the phone. “Got it,” Sidney Murray responded, pulling his rugged frame up from the black leather couch. “Sorry, Ruebs. Door.” “That’s alright. Hey, listen Andy, I’ve got some serious shit to get stuck into right now, so I’ll speak to you in a couple of days, alright?” “Okay.” “And listen; I really want you to think about this carefully, because if you don’t have that operation, you might live to regret it for the rest of your days, alright” You quite possibly the most popular guy on the GCW roster right now; a little break won’t mean shit, my friend.” “Alright Ruebs,” said Andy, promising himself to ponder his future. “Take care.” “Adieu.” And with a single click of the button Andy terminated the call. With the response from the doctor’s still laid out on the table in front of him, Andy dug his fork back into the mountain of pasta that he’d hardly made a dent in. “Dad?” said the last voice on Earth that Andy had expected to hear. He swung around in his chair to the source as Marvin toddled up towards him, still dressed in his school uniform. Immediately Andy rose from his chair and smiled, before kneeling down and warmly embracing a son that he had found himself becoming more and more detached from over recent months. “Hey! Little buddy, what are you doing here” I’m not “sposed to have you “til Monday?” Andy pulled back away from his soon; his cheeks were cherry red and the glands around his eyes looked to be swollen; he could tell that Marvin had been crying. “Marv” What’s wrong?” “It’s Mom,” Marvin replied. The mere usage of the word “mom” was enough to blow Andy’s good mood right out of the water. It pained him greatly to consider that he had to share this wonderful child with such a wretched human being as Jane, and it pained him even more to know that there was absolutely nothing he could do to change that. “What’s she done now?” Andy asked. Sincerity was never his strongest point, but he was trying his best. “Some men, they came and took all the stuff away! The TV, the CD player, the computer?” It was moments like this that made Andy seriously consider his decision to seek legal action against his ex-wife. If Jane couldn’t keep up with her finances to the extent where the repo men had come-a knockin?, how the hell would she survive a damages case against one of the wealthiest sportsmen in Florida? After all, he just wanted to teach her a lesson, not cripple her completely. “Oh son, that’s awful,” Andy said, “I’ll buy you a new Wii.” Fucking Jane, Andy thought, how could she let herself get so low? While she hadn’t left their most recent divorce settlement with a fortune, she had left with a sizeable amount of cash, and surely she must have had some saved up from work? How on earth she was letting her creditors get on top of her like this was beyond Andy’s comprehension. “Thanks Dad, but you’ve got to stop,” Marvin said, as in the background Sid wandered off upstairs. Andy raised his brows. “Stop what?” “This thing you’re doing to Mom. She’s” she isn’t well, and she keeps talking about court and you. She doesn’t even wake up to see me off to school anymore?” “It isn’t my fault, son,” Andy said. Trying to explain this to Marvin might prove to be a lot trickier than he’d bargained for. “Look, your Mom and I have had our troubles, you know that. But she tried to ruin me,” he placed his hand softly on Marvin’s shoulders. “I don’t have a choice?” “Yes you do!” Marvin stepped backwards, much like he had done to his mother not that long ago. “She’s like a ghost! She doesn’t care about anything anymore!” Andy could see his son’s upper lip beginning to tremble as he continued, wrought with emotion. “Jus-just stop it! Please?” Distraught by the thought of what his mother had become, Marvin found himself unable to hold back the tears another longer. Andy dropped to his knees and pulled his son inwards, hugging him tightly once again. Suddenly his quest for redemption didn’t seem so important anymore.
