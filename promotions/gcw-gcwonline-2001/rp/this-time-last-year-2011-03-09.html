This Time Last Year

9 March 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/09/11 This Time Last Year A Garbage Bag Production * * * The bar was nearly empty. One of the last patrons exited and sunlight slivered past the swinging door. Miranda left from behind the bar to do another sweep, wipe down some tables. I could see the humanity coming back as her shift wound down. I don't remember if I said a word to her all night. It was supposed to be my night. I didn't say much to anyone. “Come on, Johnny.” Rocco looked tired. I hated his nickname, but everything else about him was alright. “We shut down the bar a couple hours ago. Ain't you got some other place to be?” I didn't have the words to respond. I would've never imagined that it would have felt that bad, even thought I was prepared for it. I should've been happy just to have been there, and I was. I knew I had no business being there. “You been on that stool all night, and Miranda told me you didn't have a single drink.” He looked at me, and I knew that if it wasn't for my reputation, I would've been sent off while they were still serving. “It hasn't been all night, Rocco. I got up to smoke a few times.” “That idea ain't half bad.” Rocco sucked his lips in obvious thought. “I could use a smoke. What do ya say?” I reached into my pocket and tossed my pack on the bar. The remaining few cigarettes rattled around inside. Rocco took his jacket off of a hook behind the bar and put it on over his apron. He looked over at me and saw I had not moved. “You coming or what?” “Let's just smoke here.” I waved Rocco over as he started towards the door. “We can't smoke inside, Johnny. You know that.” “Come on, man. Nobody's around to find out.” I could see that Rocco knew I was bluffing and reached for my jacket on the stool beside me. “You're just trying to get rid of me, aren't you?” Rocco didn't say anything. He and Miranda looked at each other across the room. Once they were done looking, Rocco led me out of the bar and locked the door behind me. * * * It was one of those rare moments when I was actually trembling. I could hear the roar through the walls, sitting there on that bench, soaking wet. The room was so white that when the door opened, I thought the wall was broken. I almost dropped my cigarette. “Five minutes, Johnny.” I didn't know his name. He had told it to me earlier, but it was the last thing on my mind. He looked at the cigarette I was smoking, but he didn't say anything about it. He saw I was trembling. “You need anything?” “Nah.” My trembling betrayed my words. “I'll be ready.” “You sure about that?” He eyed me suspiciously. I'm sure that he knew what I was all about. My reputation preceded me. “It's just nerves.” I took a drag of the cigarette as he took a step back out the door. His hand was on the knob, but he was paused, observing me. “It's just... it'd sure be nice to win one of these things, you know?” “There's always next year, boss.” I shot him a smile, pretending to appreciate his half-hearted consolation. “Yeah, I know.” I took another drag as I worked to control my trembling. He was satisfied enough. “There ain't any real winners or losers anyway. Listen, man.” He opened the door to let the volume of it all sink in. “They think you got a shot. Ain't that the real victory?” I looked down at my black boots on the white tile. The door shut, and the man was gone. I watched the door blend back into the wall, and then I looked back down at my boots. They'd walked a long way to look at another ceiling. I shook my head and figured he was right about it. The roar penetrated through the white. Whatever it was, it was never for me, no matter the outcome. * * * I had been banging on the door for a good five minutes, yelling for Rocco to let me back in. When that didn't work, I yelled for Miranda. I could've kept at it for a good five minutes more, but it wasn't getting me anywhere. I sat down on the sidewalk, my back against the bar and my elbows on my knees, until I remembered what I was out there for in the first place. As I lit a cigarette, the door opened. Rocco held the door open for Miranda, and she walked out first. They said goodbye to each other, and Miranda walked down the sidewalk in the other direction as Rocco came towards me. He offered me a hand and helped me up before lighting his own cigarette. “I knew you were just trying to get rid of me, you son of a bitch.” “It worked.” He shot me half a grin, fumbling around with a keychain he'd taken off its clip. “You need a ride somewhere?” “Which way you heading? North?” He shook his head, and my shoulders sank. “I'll just cab it.” “Well, alright.” Rocco looked at his keys. He had the one he was looking for. “Ill see you around, Johnny. Stay positive. You'll get her next time.” “Yeah.” I stood there, a man without conviction. He started back towards his car. “Rocco?” “Yeah?” “I think I'm done.” He looked genuinely shocked, and for some reason, that made me feel worse. “I don't want to do this anymore.” “Come on, Johnny. Now you're just overreacting.” I turned away and looked down the road. There weren't many cars. “It's just a match.” “I gave it everything I could, and it killed me.” I took a deep breath. I swore that I'd never seen a sun so goddamn bright. “I don't have it in me to keep that pace going again. Sorry.” “What are you apologizing to me for?” I didn't know, and I wasn't, really. “Look, Johnny. Just take some time off and think about it a little longer. You'll get your energy back.” “Yeah, alright.” I took the cigarette out of my mouth and shook my head. I didn't want to talk about it any longer. I couldn't help myself. “It wasn't just a match.” “Yeah.” He knew it, too, and he searched hard for something to say to avoid leaving it at that. It took him a while. “Sleep on it, at least. What's done is done. You got too bright of a future ahead of you.” I nodded and kept quiet as Rocco got into his car and started the ignition. He waved at me, and as I waved back, I figured that he probably wouldn't have understood that the past was the only reason for me to keep going, and only to spite the future.
