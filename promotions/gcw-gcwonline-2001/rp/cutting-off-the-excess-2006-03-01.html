Cutting Off The Excess

1 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3.01.06 “Just trim it up.” The burly voice of Ehren Moore barks as he takes a seat into a barber’s chair. The middle aged barber drapes a cape over the front of his large client and snaps it on behind Ehren’s neck. Ehren pulls his arms out from underneath the protective cape and sets them on his lap. “You got it.” The barber replies. His barber shop is classic. Just the one chair next to a long counter with a mirror running the entire length of the counter. Various barber tools and accessories are scattered on the counter. A few pairs of scissors sit in holders next to the clear container holding the blue Barber-cide liquid. A handful of combs sit inside the liquid waiting to be called upon for use. Electric clipper heads sit on an organizer in increasing order. The barber moves past these attachments and retrieves a pair of sheers from the rack they hang on. He takes a comb from the container and shakes the excess liquid from it into the sink before bringing both tools up to Ehren’s head. “And be careful. I just had it professionally dyed.” Ehren instructs. He has never been to this barber shop before. He normally has a girl named Megan cut it when she dyes it, but she only had time for the dye this time around. Normally it’s just him and her in her home shop. He’s not used to the three or four guys waiting for a haircut in the spacey room, all bullshitting about nothing in particular. Ehren’s not typically a sociable person. Not unless the person on the other side of the conversation has long hair and wears a C-Cup bra. Oh. And they have to be female too. “Yes, sir.” The barber acknowledges Moore’s instruction with a nod and begins combing the blonde hair of Ehren Moore. After a few strokes he reaches for a squirt bottle and begins wetting the GCW superstar’s hair with short mists of water. After about a minute of squirting, he sets the bottle down and combs again. He then begins bunching small amounts of hair up and snipping it of with short little bursts of movement from his hand into the scissors. “You’re a wrestler” Ain’t you?” One of the men sitting across the room asks. He sits on a large padded bench. He folds the local newspaper he hold up in half and then in half again, setting it next to him on the bench. He looks forward to Ehren, who looks directly at the man. He does this not because he wants to, but because he’s been instructed to look straight by the barber chopping away at his hair. “Maybe. What’s it to you” Ehren retorts. The barber re-adjusts his head to the side much to Ehren’s pleasure. The man still looks on, waiting for an answer. He knows the maybe that was shot his way means yes. But he’s still looking for a straight “yes.” Ehren doesn’t say anything else in hopes of avoiding a conversation with the man. No such luck. “Yeah. He is. He’s in GCW.” One of the other waiting men says. This catches the interest of the two other men in the waiting area. They close the magazines they’re reading and toss them back onto the small coffee table in front of them. All four men now look towards Moore with great interest. “Yeah. I do. But I’m not signing any autographs.” Moore says, hoping to shoot down their intentions. They don’t even flinch. It doesn’t bother them one bit. The barber continues to cut hair from the top of Ehren’s head, allowing it to drop to the floor after each pass with the scissors. He grabs the squirt bottle again and re-wets Ehren’s hair before bunching more hair up with the comb and snipping it off. “Doesn’t phase me any. I wasn’t going to ask for it.” The first man says with a smirk. He turns to the man sitting next to him and lets out a small chuckle. The other three men chuckle a bit as well as Ehren tilts his head again in conjunction with the instruction from the barber. “Yeah. Why would we want an autograph from some mid-card asshole?” The second man says. Ehren lets out a fake chuckle of his own as the four men continue their own small laughs. All of the men aren’t very old, maybe late twenties at most and they don’t seem intimidated by the large frame of Ehren Moore sitting just a few feet away. Moore assumes they are joking, but deep down on the inside he knows they aren’t. He is an asshole. And he doesn’t give a shit. He loves it. He could almost get off on how much people hate him. “You’re right. Why would anyone want and autograph from a mid-card talent like myself” From a guy whose set to fight one of the fastest rising superstars in GCW? All the while having a chance to win the United States Title and a birth in the Rampage tournament. Why would you want the signature of the man who is going to become the first man to ever hold the United States and World Titles at the same time?” The men’s laughter trails off throughout Ehren’s statement, eventually ending a few seconds before Ehren finishes. The barber adjusts his head back to a straight forward looking position and this time Ehren stares at the men with anger in his eyes. Their silence only lasts a few seconds past Ehren’s short monologue, though. “Because you’re not going to win.” One of the men says. They all break into laughter again, causing Ehren to tense up with anger. He’s never had too much of a tolerance for people putting him down. Who does? But after an incident shortly after his discharge and an overnight stay in a local Washington D.C. jail cell, he knows to control his physical aggression. The barber takes a small mirror from his counter and holds it up in front of Moore, allowing him to see the finished product. Moore nods in approval. The barber sets the mirror down and replaces it with a small brush that he brings across Ehren’s neck, brushing the fallen hair from Moore’s frame. Moore stands and quickly pulls a twenty from his pocket. “I’d love to see you get your ass handed to you by a girl. I’ll be tuned into WorldWide tomorrow.” One of the boys says causing another short burst of loud laughter to come out. Moore walks forward to the cash register where the barber has moved to and hands the twenty over. “Keep the change.” Ehren says in an uncharacteristic action. He knows he has to get out of this barber shop or else he won’t be making it to WorldWide. He’ll be stuck in jail again. He turns to walk out and is met with the first man standing in his way. Moore pushes past and grabs his hoodie from a chair and slides it over his head. The barber thanks him, causing Moore to turn back as a natural reaction. “See you later, jobber.” The man settling down into the barber’s chair says with a smile. “Fuck you.” Moore says as he swings the door open and steps out onto the sidewalk. He wants to get out of there. He HAS to get out of there. He’s got a woman to concentrate on. And for once, he’s not concentrating on getting into her pants. He’s got a US Title to win.
