What I Do.

14 March 2012
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/14/12 “Where are we going, Philly,” the luscious Kathryn Shaw asked, face scrunched up in her “look at me, I’m a sexpot!” pout. Being in a casino gave her the excuse she needed (as if she really needed one) to break out the big guns: the sort of outfit that tended to get her stared at as if she was the new panda exhibit at the zoo. Like the giant panda, she favored black and white. Unlike the giant panda, said black and white covered only what was absolutely necessary. Dragging her along, quite literally, by the hand was her former boyfriend, semi-professional poker player and GCW superstar Phillip Kennedy. The Big Stack, by contrast, was dressed casually: tight jeans, black t-shirt, white Stetson hat. Shooting a glance over his shoulder, he chuckled at the Siren’s impatience. “We’re going to work, Kat. Or at least where I work when I’m not in the ring trying to de-head people with lariats. Few years back, you showed me your line of work. Only fair I show you mine, right?” That expedition, two years ago, had been his Horatio moment; the day he realized there was more in the stars than he could possibly comprehend. Kathryn Shaw, Alexandra Pierce – both were something between contract killers, cosplayers, and saviors. Exactly where on that spectrum he never quite was sure, and that fact led to no small amount of disquiet. “This is where we part,” he suddenly said. “Poker players won’t exactly be big on you railbirding me, so do your best to stay inconspicuous. Feel free to wander over, just don’t let anyone know you’re with me. It’s acting. You’re good at that.” “I am,” she agreed, twirling away from him as the Stack made a beeline for the poker room. 1-2 no limit wasn’t usually his game, but higher stakes weren’t on his list of priorities, and not with Kathryn watching, Having a railbird for low stakes was one thing, but guys had gotten shot for worse with gobs of money on the line. Smaller stakes were safer. Kennedy smiled as he approached the podium at the front of the room, nodding his head at the man staffing it. “Hey, Carlo. 1-2 no limit today. Get me a table by the rim. Trying to impress someone.” The middle-aged Italian man grinned a lascivious grin in response. “Female company,” he asked, and was met by Kennedy’s smirking nod. “You could say that, yeah,” Phillip concurred. “Any seats fitting the bill open? Carlo looked down at his computer, punched in a few keys, and nodded. “Seat 5, table 17’s all yours Mr. Kennedy. Have fun out there, and goo—“ He caught himself, much to Kennedy’s approval. “You’re learning!” Phillip smiled, and sauntered past the podium to find his table. Once there, he plopped five one-hundred dollar bills on the table, and nodded in the direction of the dealer. “Buyin’ in for five-hundred,” he almost drawled, and took in the looks of those at the table. Some were playing with one quarter that, a few had similar stacks, and a few had a veritable mountain of chips for this denomination. All of it was information that could be used later, Kennedy knew, beginning to file it away for a rainy moment in a couple of hours. Once given his chips, the Stack sat down, leaned back a bit in his chair, and began to watch the action unfold. Phillip Kennedy didn’t understand most things. Business and economics were so far over his head that they might as well have been in orbit. Most sciences he knew just enough of to not be a total idiot at a cocktail party. But poker? Poker made sense to Phil in a way that practically nothing, even pro wrestling, did. Even as he folded the first two hands he received, total rags on both counts, he was soaking up information. Watching his fellow tablemates. Noticing the way the cute blonde girl at the end of the table giggled when she threw chips into the pot. Focusing on the serious elderly gentlemen as he contemplated every decision as if it were life or death. The third hand came, and this time, Phillip looked down to find an ace and a jack, both hearts. He smiled; the AJh was one of his favorite hands in poker. Better still, he was in the big blind, and he watched with a tight grin as there were two others besides himself still in the hand. “Raise,” he declared, confidently, pushing three times his blind into the pot, a standard raise. Both prior callers looked at him, as if trying to stare into his very soul. Kennedy merely shrugged, and waited to see their reaction. The first was the cute blonde girl, who now chewed on the end of a braid as she pondered her decision. With an equally flippant shrug, she threw the chips in to call. The second was one of the few at the table who hadn’t done much but fold since Kennedy sat down, which meant no read. He called quietly, as if trying not to draw any attention to himself. The flop came down then, six of clubs, five of spades, ten of clubs. The young woman checked hopefully, and the other man immediately bet. With little more than a blink of the eye, Kennedy called the bet, leaving the cute girl to fold and chew on her braid a little bit more. One on one now, just the way Kennedy liked it when faced with an opponent he’d never met before. The turn came down as the ten of diamonds, pairing the board. Once again, the opponent bet. Once again, Kennedy called. The Stack looked across the table into the eyes of the other man, and made a snap judgment. River. Ace of diamonds. The card made the other man flinch, but he didn’t hesitate otherwise, pushing in a sizeable bet. Kennedy didn’t hesitate either. “All in.” He began to shove all of his chips towards the middle of the table, only for the opponent to disgustedly fold his hand. Watching from the rail as Phillip raked in his chips was, of course, one Kathryn Shaw. She understood how poker worked as a base concept, of course, but something had happened that she didn’t quite understand. She’d have to ask later, of course… -------- By the time the pair had been in his hotel room (considering Ms. Shaw was attached these days, they actually had separate ones for the first time in their career of knowing each other) for roughly forty-five minutes, a rather pleased looking (and fully clothed) Phillip Kennedy was taking in a rather pleasant and familiar sight: Kathryn Shaw in her rather skimpy and colorful unmentionables. “Another hand,” he asked, with a grin, reaching for the deck of playing cards between them on the king sized bed. “Your luck could turn around, you know.” She shook her head. “And I thought I’d get that cute necklace all nice and easy like,” she pouted. “It’s like you don’t even notice what I’m not wearing.” “When I play poker,” the Stack explained, “I don’t. That’s the sort of focus I’ve got. It’s like you out on the job, except with a lot less flirting and death.” She sighed, and shook her head. “I don’t know how getting me to strip down to my underwear explains what happened earlier, though.” “Let’s put it this way, Kathryn,” Phillip offered, in an effort to make his point in a less purely self-interested way, “let’s say you’re playing strip poker with a bunch of drunken Catholic schoolgirls.” “Can I have a moment to enjoy this situation a little longer, Philly Bear,” she teased, and it was very, VERY hard for Phil to not watch her squeezing her legs together just a tiny bit. “I can’t believe I’m saying this, but I’ m being serious, Kat,” Phillip insisted. “Here’s the scenario. You’re holding something marginal, let’s say a pair of sixes. Board comes down ace-deuce-five, rainbow. Schoolgirl one bets her stockings. Schoolgirl two calls. What do you do?” She thought about the situation for about two seconds before replying. “Get them both more vodka.” “Cute, but no,” Kennedy replied. “You bet everything.” “You WOULD say that,” she smirked, shaking her head. “I like my approach better.” “You WOULD say that,” he retaliated, “but both answers will get us a naked schoolgirl in the long run, and here’s why. Because once you bet everything, they’re going to have to seriously evaluate what they have. They’re Catholic schoolgirls. They have some sort of innate morality telling them this entire thing is an awful idea no matter how drunk they are. They’re now stuck with a bet for their moral lives. If they stop now, well, taking the stockings off is bad, but it could be worse, right?” Kathryn nodded, a small grin playing at her lips. “And they have to have like a pair of aces to call, right?” “Exactly. And depending HOW scared they are, they might even fold those. Meanwhile, you? You don’t care if you lose. You’re not afraid of losing. The worst that happens is a few nubile schoolgirls see you naked.” “So earlier, when you bet at that—“ Suddenly, the events of the afternoon made a lot more sense. “Exactly,” Phillip beamed. “When I watched him play that hand, I was looking for any information I could find. He was nervous, I knew that much. Every time he bet at me, he looked even more nervous. He wanted me to go away, I could tell. But he was confident enough to keep betting, so I didn’t think he was bluffing. He probably had a solid hand in that situation, something like JJ, QQ, maybe even two clubs or two running cards to give him four to the straight. But that ace scared him, so I was pretty sure he didn’t have a monster. He raised at me, but after betting the way he did on the flop and turn…” “You thought he wanted you out,” Kathryn concluded, to which Phillip nodded. “Hell yes he did. And so I pushed over the top and went all in because I knew in my gut he’d fold. Now, if he’d known better, he’d have known there’s no way I’d have played an ace that long. But he was nervous at how big the pot was getting. He feared losing his money. He was looking for a way out of that hand, playing not to lose, not playing to win. So I gave it to him.” She looked at Phillip for a moment, and nodded. “So, what’s the point of all of this again?” “I don’t scare easily, Kat, in poker or in life. That’s the point. I know what can happen. I’m going into the first ladder match of my career against that bum Redding and two guys I know jack all about. Chances of me getting hurt? Stupid high. But I don’t care. I want that goddamned contract more than anything since SCCW’s Universal Championship. And if I’ve got to bludgeon people to get it, that’s fine. If I’ve got to spend the night in the hospital? I’ll deal with it, and I’ll smile, Kat.” Serious Phillip Kennedy didn’t surface often, and Kathryn Shaw always found herself taken off guard when he came out to play. The look in his eyes was pure conviction and desire, a look that had been directed at her for much different purposes in happier times. “Do I even need to ask why, Phil,” Shaw sighed. Really, this was a question she knew the answer to. She knew why the Big Stack wanted the American Dream so badly. “Nope,” Kennedy grinned, rubbing his hands together. “Because when I do win it, if Amy survives that big motherfucker earlier in the evening and wins the strap, I’ve got carte blanche to cash it in on her whenever she’s at her weakest. I’ll become the GCW United States Champion, that’s cool and all, but really? I just want the history books to say that I pinned that redheaded bitch’s shoulders to the canvas for a count of three. I don’t care how tainted, I don’t care how poorly looked at that victory is, I just want the W to be by my name. That’s it, that’s all.” And with GCW’s mythical contract in his possession, very little save *another* redhead and her daughter could stop Phillip’s American Dream from coming true.
