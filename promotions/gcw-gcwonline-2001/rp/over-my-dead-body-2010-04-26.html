"Over My Dead Body"

26 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/26/10 ---- MAYNARD SUMMER 1995 ---- Clayton has been rolling the ring between his thumb and forefinger for some time now. He still hasn’t spoken. Back and forth, goes the ring. Back and forth. His body almost completely still, save the rise and fall of his chest with every breath. He occasionally moves his eyes from the ring to meet mine. His mouth moves with a slight twitch as if beginning to speak, but then he thinks better of it and returns his focus solely on the round piece of gold in his fingers. Back and forth. Finally, he takes a deep breath and looks at me earnestly. “Are you sure?” I nod as I shoot him a sincere smile. “Of course, Clay. You don’t understand how much Sara means to me. I know in the bottom of my heart that if I’m not meant to be with her the rest of my life, I’m not meant to be with anyone.” “You mean like a soulmate?” He asks skeptically. Clayton barely believes in love, let alone the idea that there’s one person out there who is perfect for you in every way. One person you are meant to spend the rest of your life with. “Why not?” I ask, reaching out and taking the ring from him and examining it closely for about the eight hundredth time: a thin gold band with a small white diamond at the head of it. On the inside I’ve inscribed the word ‘forever’. Simple. Sara loves simple. I take one last look at it before putting it away safely in pocket. “Well,” he begins with a deep breath in, “because it’s bullshit, Nard.” I roll my eyes. “Not this again, Clay. How can you believe in the idea of the paranormal, but not this? What about a ‘soulmate’ is so hard for you to understand?” “Do you want my reasons alphabetically, chronologically or in order of relevance?” He sneers, clutching my shoulder as though he’s about to give me some sort of life lesson. “And how would you suggest giving me the reasons chronologically?” I shoot back, picking his hand off my shoulder by his index finger slowly and dropping it back at his side. “Come on, Maynard,” he says, avoiding my inquiry. “Do we really have to get into this right now? I might not agree with what you’re planning on doing with this”, he stops speaking for a moment and gulps animatedly before continuing, “ring of yours, but don’t go asking me to talk you out of it!” “How am I asking you to talk me out of it exactly?” He puts his hand on my shoulder again, this time in a more sincere fashion. “Listen, I’m glad you believe in the whole ‘one person out there for everyone’ idea and everything, but it’s just not fuckin’ real. It’s a beautiful little idea perpetuated by Hollywood and endorsed by Hallmark. Nothing more. It’s a fuckin’ marketing angle, Maynard. I’m not saying you can’t or won’t spend the rest of your life happily by Sara’s side, but she’s not your soulmate, man. You’re two people. You love each other. That’s great. Congratulations. But there’s no higher power at work here. No mystical power drawing the two of you together by your souls.” I cross my arms across my chest, smirking at him. “Are you done?” He scrunches up his nose and purses his lips in mock contemplation, taking his hand off my shoulder and strokes his chin goofily. “Yeah, looks like it.” “Well, I appreciate your input --” “Oh, here we go again, Nard!” He throws his arms up in the air and begins to pace back and forth, frustrated. “Why do you even bother asking me what I think, man? Why do you even come to me? You’re never gonna listen to me anyways! Why you gotta waste my time?” “Come on, don’t be like that!” I exhort, trying to stop him from pacing by grabbing his shoulders. Reluctantly, he obliges. “Listen, I’m sorry you can’t see this the way I see it. You will someday, though. You’re going to meet that certain someone. That certain someone who makes you want to be a better person. Someone who becomes the reason you get out of bed in the morning. Someone who becomes the reason you are thankful for every breath you take. Someday you’re going to meet this person, and you going to come to be and have to apologize. You’ll have to say, ‘Maynard, I’m sorry. I’m sorry that I ridiculed and mocked you all those times. You were right all along.’” He scoffs with a half-smile. “Fat chance. Even if you are right, I’ll never admit it. Ever. You can take that promise to the bank.” “I’m serious!” I insist with a snap. “Someday you’ll understand how this whole thing works.” “So, how are you going to do it?” He asks, changing the subject. If I was dealing with anyone else, I would stay the course, but arguing with Clayton is like trying to make a dead horse gallop, so I welcome the change. “Not completely sure yet, actually”, I say, patting the round protrusion the ring makes in my pocket. “One major hiccup before asking her is even a feasible option anyway.” “Yeah? And what’s that?” “Her father,” I say, looking down at my Chuck Taylor’s. “What about’em?” “Well, I’ve gotta ask him. You know, for her hand. In marriage.” “Are you out of your fuckin’ mind?” “What?” I ask, trying my best to sound sincere, but the reality is that I know exactly what he means. He is the stereotypical over-protective father, and she is his perfect little angel. Daddy’s-Little-Girl personified, if you know what I’m saying. He treats her like his little princess, no matter what. Getting him to willingly give her away to her eighteen-year old wrestler boyfriend who will be moving to Iowa in a matter of weeks will be nothing short of a miracle. “Oh, for Christ’s sake! You know goddamn well what I’m talking about! The only way that man is giving his daughter away is over his dead body.” “Well, the respectful thing to do is ask him, Clay. That’s just how it works.” “And if he says no?” I shrug. “I’ll ask her anyway.” “Then why fuckin’ ask, smart guy? Why not just go ahead and ask her?” “Because you’re supposed to ask, Clay”, I say. “He doesn’t have to say yes, but I’ve got to ask. It’s just the way it works.” “You’re a dead man,” he laughs. “You know that right?” “Yeah,” I sigh. “I know.” But it’s going to be worth every uncomfortable minute of that conversation to see her walking down the aisle. That much, if nothing else, is something I can be completely sure of. ---- LATER ---- Okay Maynard, you can do this .. I take a deep breath before before extending my arm and knocking on Sara’s front door. It’s only a moment before the door swings open, her father standing right in front of me. He’s an intimidating man; from his facial expressions, his build to the way he dresses and holds himself. Even now, at eleven in the morning, the man is wearing a suit; he’s a lawyer after all. He looks at me blankly for a moment. “Hello, Maynard”, he begins awkwardly. “Sara’s not home.” He begins to shut the door, but I stop it with my right hand. “Yeah, I know, Mr. Quenton. I’m actually not here to see her.” “Oh?” He says, pulling the door back towards him and looking me up and down. Suddenly, I am very relieved by the fact I’ve decided to dress a little more respectable for the occasion. I’ve done away with the heavy metal t-shirts and acid wash jeans, all accented by a pair of my signature Chuck Taylor’s. I’ve even tied my hair back in a neat top-knot in my attempt to gain some extra brownie points in my favor. “Yeah,” I say, scratching the back of my neck nervously. “I’m actually here to talk to you.”  “To me?” He asks slowly. “Yes, would that be alright?” “I suppose,” he says, his eyes narrowed in suspicion; like he knows what this is about. My heart begins to race as he steps slightly to the left and motions me past him. “Please, come in.” “Thank you,” I say politely as I cross the threshold into the Quenton home. I’ve been here countless times, but it feels so unfamiliar this time. The whole world feels different when your heart is beating this hard. “Can I offer you something to drink, Maynard?” Sara’s father asks from behind me as I lead the way to his living room. “No, thank you,” I reply, taking a seat on the couch. Mr. Quenton elects unsurprisingly to sit down in his plush, brown la-z-boy recliner, his right leg crossed over his left and his left hand on his chin. “What would you like to speak with me about, Maynard?” He asks, “is everything alright?” “Yes, sir. Everything is just fine,” I say, my voice beginning to shake. “I’ve just been doing a lot of thinking lately, sir. About your daughter.” “Oh?” He says. I’m not certain, but I believe his pigment whitened slightly. He swallows hard, trying himself to keep his composure. “What about Sara?” “Well, sir, as you know, I’ve been seeing your daughter for eight months now.” “Yes, I am well aware.” A hint of hostility? “Of course. Well, Mr. Quenton, I like your daughter a lot,” I begin, barely able to contain my quivering voice. I know the reaction I’m going to receive for this next part, so I elect to pause for a moment before continuing. I swallow hard and try to take the deepest breath I can without seeming as such. “Sir, I love your daughter. Very much.” He looks me in the eyes coldly. “And what do you know about love, son?” “E-excuse me?” I stammer. “I asked you, what you know about love?” He’s still staring at me with those hard green eyes. I can feel myself crumbling before him. But I’ve got to stay strong; stick to my guns. For Sara. For our future. “With all due respect, sir; what does anyone know about love?” A small smirk spreads across his lips. “Please, explain.” “Well, isn’t it all subjective?” I say, slowly at first but gaining confidence as I go along. “How does a person know he’s in love? Is there really a way to tell except how you feel in your heart? I don’t know much about love, Mr. Quenton, and I don’t mean to offend you, but I know in my heart that I love Sara. I don’t think it needs further explanation.” “Son, you don’t love my daughter”, he’s not smiling any longer. His voice is like ice. “I do, sir. So much. And the reason I’m here, though I can see now that it’s probably in vain, is to ask you something very important.” “Son, don’t --” “I wanted to ask you ..” “Over my dead body.” “.. if I could marry your daughter.” He’d already answered me half-way through the question. But I wasn’t exactly surprised. Clayton had already told me what I knew to be true: he was never going to give her away. Not to me. But I still had to be the bigger man. I still had to ask him. I stand up sternly, and say with as much respect as I could muster given my anger, “I’m sorry you see it that way, Mr. Quenton. I guess I’ll be on my way. Have a good day.” I extend my hand for him to shake, but he doesn’t move. His eyes still baring a hole through me. I nod cordially, shoving hand into the pocket of my dress pants and turn away from him. “You’re just going to ask her anyways, aren’t you, boy?” Without turning around, I say, “Yes, sir.” “I’d think better of that idea, Maynard.” I turn around now to face him. “Sir, if you had any idea how much I love your daughter, you wouldn’t be saying that. I can’t stand the thought of not being with her. She makes everything in my life better just by being by my side. She makes food taste better, colours brighter, and all those other cliches about love no one believes to be true. Mr. Quenton, I love your daughter with everything I have, and I’m sorry you can’t see that. I’m sorry that you would deny your daughter the love of her life. But I can see by the way you’re staring at me right now that there’s no changing your mind. So I’d best be on my way. I turn around again and open the front door to leave. I barely hear him as I shut the door behind me, but his words still ring in my ears. “You will not marry my daughter, boy.”
