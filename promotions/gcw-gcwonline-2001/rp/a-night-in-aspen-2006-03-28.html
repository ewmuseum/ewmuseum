A Night in Aspen

28 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/28 January 25th, 1999..... The cool breeze whisked through the exclusive mountain resort in a fierce, untamed manner, causing all it passed to shiver irregularly in their overpriced formal wear. The town of Aspen Colorado stayed completely true to it's stereotype. Jason Warr hated every minute of it. Surrounded by trees and light snowflakes he sat on the back deck of a large building, reminiscent of a ski lodge. Most of the other guests stood inside, talking about their promising children, new Mercedes, or latest real estate gain. He despised every second of his fathers ‘gala' mountain get togethers, and often found himself sitting on the back deck around a large table with a few other ‘children', leader of the rich misfits. They'd smoke, harp on how awful it was to have money, play pranks on the socialites, smoke, harp on money some more, and eventually retire to their rooms once the festivities had ceased. But during this current gathering they found themselves in front of half eaten McDonalds, a weak protest to the wide variety of snob food on the indoors, launching pickels at the large bay window. “YES! RIGHT BITCH, WIN WIN!” “Fuck You Harper, that was SO my pickle.” “Pft, you're such a pillow biter, I'm serious. You have to try and cheat at Window Pickle Races? Just because I know how much mustard to smear on mi-” “Shh, Someone's coming.” drifted the voice of Warr, silencing his two arguing friends. Pushing his then green hair to the side he peered down the walkway, waiting for the intruder to show themselves. Emerging onto the scene is your average snooty rich couple. Warr and his friends masterfully act out a very often rehearsed act. “OH GOOD, SOMEONE'S HERE!” Shouted the larger boy, scurrying over to the couple. “What a relief,” Shouted the other, wiping a fake sweat from his brow and trotting after. “I thought we may be killed!” “What's going on here?” the old man shouted, as Warr slowly followed the duo. “A couple members of the wait staff were back here throwing things at the window. It looks like it was just a few pickles, but we were scared nonetheless, sir,” The old couple stands, affronted stares affixed to their upturned faces. “Bah! HOOLIGANS!” he bellowed down over the ledge, as if this stealth kitchen workers had leaped 50 feet to the rocky base of the mountain. He shook his fist. “I told you, Those Puerto Ricans are bad news!” shrieked his wife as the two walked off, causing the trio of boys to laugh loudly. As the three turned the corner back onto the main deck Warr slammed right into his father. He had a serious look on his face and was surrounded by some company. Leaning in he whispered. “You may be able to fool others, but I know exactly how those pickles got on the window. I'd tell you to clean it up but I have a few people I need to introduce.” Introductions. Whoo. He turned on his heel towards the guests, pointing at one. An older woman with White Hair, utterly disgusting in revealing formal wear. “This is..” Warr turned him out, as usual. Boredom overcame him as he shook her hand, waiting to re-join his friends. Their next prank included gathering at the bottom of the deck and heaving snowballs at passerby. “And this is her granddaughter, Angela.” Broken out of his stupor he threw his head to the side to greet the second guest. Her flowing raven hair distracted him for a moment, until he noticed her face. Skin and Bone, but absolutely Gorgeous. Taken aback he fumbled a little and raised his hand, shaking it. His father began to ramble more and more about why _____ was rich and why ________ cared, but Jason didn't. He wasn't in love with the girl, but having someone he deemed attractive to raise hell with seemed an inviting concept. An invitation he would regret more than any he'd ever given. Wrapping his arm around the elderly woman's waste, Warr's father led her to the deck. Trying to swoon rich women into investing was his speciality, and he quickly shot Jason the ‘Distract the Girl' glare. “So.. Having a Good time I expect?” he said, shuffling his hands through his pockets nervously. “Uhm, I suppose so. I mean the conversation is interesting.” Angela replied, eyes wandering around the deck in a shy manner. “Suit yourself,” said Warr smiling. Throwing his hand from his pocket he revealed what he'd been searching for, an M80. “We just sit outside and blow shit up.” She covered her mouth, smiling. “What on earth is that?” she said, giggling. “Let me show Ya,” said Warr. He observed the area, smiling and lit the M80, throwing it over the ledge to the snowy bottoms below. He knew the people at the party would be close enough to see it, but far enough to be safe. Boom. People screamed, but the four in the group simply laughed uncontrollably as the ritzy folks below screamed about terrorists and kidnappers. Warr ran, knowing his father would be all over him for this. As the night progressed the four tipped over punch bowls, played with the power in the building and hijacked a small cart owned by the resort. It was the most memorable night in Aspen he'd ever had, and at the time he didn't know it'd be the launch pad for his longest and most damaging relationship ever. A relationship that would ruin his very existence, and stab at his very soul. It'd transform him into a being far from human. ***** Present.. Stepping through his hotel room he wrapped himself in his trademark leather Jacket, wiping the blue hair from his sweaty face. He'd become accustom to avoiding the gym lately, and found his pre-match work out consisted of sit ups at the foot of the bed, as some crappy foreign movie played out on the Television screen. Warr's quest to resurface in the business was about to begin. A victory over Chris Kannon would be enough to lift him out of the wrestling abyss long enough to get his name untarnished once and for all. Grabbing his bag he walked out the door. Ready to face destiny one more time
