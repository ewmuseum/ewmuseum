Date: 06/26/10 Preface Have you ever woken up early on a warm summer morning just to watch the sunrise? You sit outside, body still groggy, listening to the birds whistle as they soar through the sky. And when that beam of light breaks the horizon, your eyes glimmer. You find yourself smiling, even if you've had the worst of days the night before. It's amazing what can change over the course of a few months. One day, you're laying happily in bed with the girl you swore was the woman of your dreams and the next - well, you're still laying there. Except on this time around, you're laying there with your dick in your hands, too emotionally drained to even find the energy to jerk off. Life comes at you fast. It throws you curveballs that you're never actually prepared to swing at. Life is full of unpredictable obsticles; and it's all the little things that keep us going. And for me, it was an addiction - watching the sun, that is. I found myself growing more and more dependent on spending that half hour every morning staring across the sand and deep into the ocean. It seemed to be the only thing that slowed me down and yet kept me going. As long as the sun would rise, I'd have a reason to live. __________ Chapter I "You feeling alright, bud? You look like you're in pain," I said with the utmost sincerity. We were at a wedding for our friend Caitlin when I took noticed to another friend cringing as he took a step. "Yeah man," said Matthew. "My percs are wearing off and so I'm starting to feel it again." Matthew had thrown out his back about eight or nine months prior while working out in the gym. It was the second time his spine collapsed on him and his doctors warned him that it better be the last or he'd never walk again. So for months, Matthew spent his days laying on his couch and watching television. Thankfully the majority of those months were in the dead of winter, so he didn't have to worry about missing out on much. But as the weather got warmer, he made it a point to get back on his feet again. This wasn't his first night out since the injury occurred, but it was certainly close to it. He wasn't really sure how his body would react being on his feet for that long - but was willing to take the chance. "I appreciate you asking though, man," he said with a smile as he pat my back. "It means a lot." I returned the gesture with a nod, noting that it was "no big deal." But underneath the surface, it was a big deal. It was a really big deal. After splitting with Rebecca and severing friendships with Zacharia, Shane, Timmy, Fisher, and even DeBough - I found myself in a very lonely, empty space. To cope with my self-dissatisfaction, I tried reverting back to my childhood friends to see if they'd be any more welcoming. To my surprise, they were. But it wouldn't take long until they began showing me why I skipped out on them in the first place. Caitlin's wedding was the first time our group of childhood friends had gotten together in quite some time. Years ago, there was a core of about fifteen of us. As time went by, those foundations began to crack until we all ultimately went our own ways. Her wedding gave us the opportunity to rekindle childhood memories - perhaps with a more mature outlook. But it's never that easy. "So you have you eye on any of these ladies?" Matthew asked with wandering eyes. "They all look very beautiful tonight," I answered cautiously. "Yeah they do," he confirmed. "But are any of them going to be the lucky lady tonight?" His persistance wasn't so much annoying as it was suspicious. And while I tried to sympathize with his condition, his lucid intent made that nearly impossible. I wanted to drive my elbow into his spine. I felt the urge to do what the weightroom didn't. I wanted to finish the job and keep him out of my business. "I brought Lisa as my date," I snapped back somewhat aggressively. "I wouldn't disrespect her like that." He responded with a slight nod, "you're a good man." I probably seem like such a dick being a prick to a borderline crippled 'childhood friend,' but there was more to it. Matthew was the type of guy who'd play the great-friends card and really try to work over your trust. Then, at the same time, he'd be trying to weasel his way into the pants of whatever girl you were with or brought around. And if you hadn't guessed, he was trying to swing his moves on my wedding date. Not that she and I were together - because we weren't - but it was no secret that I'd had a crush on her for years. Our relationship was like one of those cheesy chick flicks where two people express interest in one another but are never available at the same time. This was the most available either of us had been in...well, ever. I saw it as my opportunity to really make something of the two of us. I felt like it was my best opportunity. And yet I was being side-checked by a person whom I was supposed to refer to as a friend. We've all had a Matthew in our lives - the kid who doesn't hesitate to toss you under the bus in a moment's notice so that he could score some ass for one night. It's one of those things you thought faded with age and maturity. But apparently not. So as I stood there at the edge of the dance floor next to Matthew, I couldn't help but feel just as alone as I did at home. I felt like these weren't old friends I was surrounding myself with, but rather old acquaintances. I couldn't trust them. Come to think of it - I could never trust them in the first place. I wanted to just up-and-leave. I felt like slipping out the back door unnoticed and catching a cab home; just saying "screw it" all-together. And then she grabbed my hand. "Let's dance," she whispered in my ear, leaning her chest against my shoulder. I smiled. I smiled and I danced.
