Curtain Call

11 March 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/11/11 ''The place is dying, Amy. You know you can do better.'' The last slam filled her lungs with bitter cold breath. In reality, she gasped for air, splitting fingernails when she clawed at the mat, pawing for a rope or a turnbuckle or something to steady her. For something to keep her there until they came. ''It's gone downhill and dragged you along with it.'' At some point, she'd think back to recall what was on her mind as it was happening. It would only make sense that she'd be running back through her re-signed agent's words from earlier, when he was telling her not to go out there, not to go to work that day. He'd warned her that it was more trouble than it was worth, that her pride would cost her her sanity. Even though he'd betrayed her once for the sake of bettering his own business, the man truly didn’t want to see any harm come to her. To him, it was just an industry, something to make money from and thrive in. She prayed the man standing over her saw it the same way. ''You don't have to trust me, but you're gonna learn soon enough that you and I, we need each other.'' In reality, she wasn't sure what Kingsley saw. ''You were my best client. I was the only one with brains enough to market you, and together we made that company. You lived its glory days.'' She should have listened. ''I know you're starting to forget how a title belt feels. I've got an opportunity if you want it, but it could easily go down the shitter if you go out there tonight and he does what you know he wants to.'' In reality, she hadn't been thinking about what Dan Mulholland told her. She should have been doing that before it came to a head. ''He'll taunt you to do something that'll end both your careers.'' In reality, she didn't have a fraction of a second to think about anything but surviving, of staying awake until they came. ''I don't like saying it, but you really don't have a lot of choices here. You've got next to nothing left to your name.'' And sadly, the things Dan had told her were correct. They weren't coming, because there wasn't a 'they'. She'd done a damn fine job of burning every last bridge she needed. Before there was a chance to think about that loneliness, he stomped her hand into the mat where she'd been searching for some grace. The nails were already broken, but her fingers had survived so far. The dull agony when her pinky came out of its socket under his boot nearly made her vomit. Mulholland's words, if they'd ever been in the back of her mind, were gone at that point. Any sense of surrounding, any real footing in reality, was quickly giving way to an overwhelming humanity. The aches became pains and drove nails through her, just like he had. Not Dan, not Jared, not Lance - no agent or ex or rival, but a beast who loomed over her. Every strike was familiar, but once she was certain the rest of her hand might have been breaking alongside that finger, the intensity felt fresh again. She tried to open her eyes, but one was blind from the crimson streaming down from the cut along her eyebrow, the other still seated poorly thanks to his fracturing of her orbital bone months before. That had come just before the nail through her cheek. She still wore a stud in that wound - the same stud that was scraping against her teeth, swimming in the blood that pooled in her mouth and only released when she parted her lips to cry out. Later, she'd try to watch the tape to see if any sound came out. Just like the help, there was no sound. She'd only hear about that later, though. Campbell once sat through a replay of this man nearly crushing one side of her face against a bare turnbuckle. She'd watched when he pierced her right after. But she would never make it to the end of this tape. Then, there was no consideration to what might happen later. It didn't occur to her that she'd still be breathing to one day watch the assault after living it. There was no sense she'd be living at all - not after she felt the point at her collarbone. Her arms flailed out weakly, and something just below where she'd almost grabbed his forearm sliced her maimed hand like a terrible papercut when she pawed for it. She had to breathe. She had to breathe. Blood curdled at the back of her throat when she tried - it gagged her, and some cruel reflex, an angry jab from her gall bladder, drove up bile that burned her throat as it joined the blood. And just like that, another torturous instinct had her trying to swallow it all, but her lungs refused. It stung her throat again when she spat it all up, and it landed on Terrence Kingsley as pure gore. All she'd ever remember beyond the pain was that his roar in that moment sounded almost sexual, the single most disturbing thing that had ever met her ears. What had dribbled back onto her warmed a body that was fast losing heat with its blood, but any comfort was too short-lived to notice. The sharp point she'd made the whole motion to dislodge only dug deeper, and something popped in her ears when it first pierced the skin. From there, he went to work. He sat astride her churning guts, keeping her pinned and killing any hope of escape before he willed it. There was nothing to be done but lay and try to steele her mind against the feelings. Any prayer of that happening was gone when she felt the makeshift blade of glass drag, the papillae between the first and second layers of skin stretching, pulling and finally snapping apart as he cut jagged lines into her. She'd long forgotten why she was even bothering to cling to consciousness, and soon gave into the inevitable: She'd die there, in that ring, as someone who'd once been atop a corrupt company in a disrespected industry. And it was all once upon a time -- she'd end as little more than an 'also ran'. A sad footnote.
