Dealing With The Loss

1 May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 05/01/09 Fuck my life. Of course I can’t even imagine how anyone could fuck it up even more than it already is. Just imagine this...one day I'm just a wrestler within the GCW and my main concern is how to win a title. Next I get involved with a pregnant girl and her baby is stolen. So I hire Max (a somewhat dubious detective) to get him back, which he eventually does. Then, for some reason, someone else tries to kill me and I betray Max to the mafia in return for their protection. Just to top it all off with Max pointing a gun at me before letting me live because of some crazy plan to fight the mob...and you have the world’s greatest clusterfuck. Did I mention Chris Bagwell is dead? We haven’t exactly been friends for some time before his death; yet here I am sitting next to his grave. I wasn’t surprised that his death had hurt, we had been good friends before it all fell apart. But, the length for which the pain has lasted is becoming ridiculous. I thought his funeral would purge the shock. I think it made it worse. The cemetery itself is a pretty nice place - all things considered. Bagwell’s tomb is still covered in the flowers that were placed during the funeral, but they have already begun wilting. If they’re any indication I'm the only whose been there since the funeral itself. It kind of makes me wonder how many people still think about him anymore. No matter the number, I have probably taken it the worst. I haven’t gone home since the funeral...I just hung around in a hotel near the cemetery. I spend all day here and at night I go get hammered. I don’t think it sounds healthy either. “You were a pain in my ass,” I said out loud to the tombstone, almost expecting a response. “You talked too much, and you used me. So why can’t I say goodbye and good riddance?” “Maybe because you know that you might go out the exact same way,” a voice suggests from behind. I whip my head around, coming face to face with my uncle. Uncle Erick is my mother’s brother, and I think I get my size from him. Back when I was a kid I was convinced he was a giant, but even now I wouldn’t want to try and pick the guy up. He’s in his mid-forties now but, looking at him, you’d think he’s still in his prime. Muscled with just a hint of a gut, full head of hair and clear, sharp eyes. He gives every indication that he’s more than ready to keep up with someone younger. I opened my mouth to ask him what he’s doing here, then I realize that I’d called him on a whim (probably while I was drunk). While we had never been particularly close, he has this weird ability to make me feel better whenever I’m depressed. I tried to think of something to say but I fail and instead turn back to Bagwell’s grave. “You’re going down the exact same path that Bagwell went down,” he continues as he sits down next to me. “You’ve isolated yourself from every one of your allies. The fans hate you. But you think you’re the greatest thing they’ve ever seen. Hell, at this rate when you’re dead they might not even give you a funeral; they’ll just toss you in the ground.” That’s another thing about my Uncle. From listening to him you might think he’s had some sort of career in wrestling. He hasn’t; but somehow he’s an expert on various subjects that he has no business being an expert in. “What the hell do you know?” I answer belligerently. “You’ve never been inside the ring; you’ve no idea what it’s like trying to make a name for yourself. All you do is sit in a desk from nine to five and deny insurance claims.” I realized that was exactly the wrong thing to say when a blunt object proceeded to clash with the back of my head. I fell forward and suddenly Erick had me pinned down with his forearm pressing on the back of my neck. I struggled to escape, but he’s too strong and the blow left me dazed. “I know what it’s like to get beaten by something stronger than you,” my Uncle says quietly as I struggle to break free. “I know that you think being a good guy will just lead to becoming destroyed in GCW like you were in the last place you wrestled. But I also know you, and I know that you have to force yourself to act like you don’t care about hurting anyone.” “Maybe that was true when I was fucking nine,” I spit out angrily. “But I’m not some little kid anymore!” My Uncle sighs wearily and lets me up. I get to my feet, stretching out my back and neck as I go, then turn to leave him and the graveyard behind. ”Do you care about winning anymore?” he asks and my feet come to a complete stop. I turn to argue, but the hot words die on my lips. Do I care about winning anymore? At Shockwave I just wanted to make Danger pay, winning was almost any afterthought. But I still want to win the Rampage tournament, and to go on winning after that. Certain, I nod my head. “Yea, I do,” I answer confidently. “And you’re going to do that how exactly?” he asks snidely. “You were on a massive losing streak before beating Danger, and he’s way past his prime as it is? You may beat Face, but at it stands you’d need a miracle to beat Taylor?” I blink at him. “How closely have you been following my career?” I ask incredulously. He snorts though I notice the tip of his ears redden. “Well someone in the damn family has to keep a track of what you’re up to. Besides, it gives me something to watch that is generally decent, and that’s hard to find these days.” I hear the implied message of concern and support in his words and my throat tightens. My parents were not exactly supportive of my career choice, and even now it causes tension between us. At least when we decide to talk to each other anyway. To know that someone was watching and cheering me on meant a lot. “Look,” I say with less hostility, “maybe you could just tell me what you’re leading up to? I don’t feel like waiting for you to get to it any longer.” “It’s pretty fucking obvious something happened to you besides everything else,” he answers earnestly stepping in close and placing a hand on my shoulder. I start at the contact but I force myself to relax and listen to his offer. “I don’t know what it is, and I won’t pry. But I do know that if you keep reminding yourself of it it’s going to tear you apart. You’re career is going to hell, you look like hell, and if you go much farther like this you won’t be able to get back. So I want you to come with me.” “You live in New Jersey,” I chuckle bitterly. “Trenton isn’t exactly that big of a change.” “I don’t live there anymore you idiot,” he answers giving me a light whack on the head. “I bought a house down in the countryside in Ohio. Pretty isolated, but there’s a town half an hour away. I think what you need now is a pretty intense regiment of doing nothing but eating, training, and sleeping. No way you’ll be able to do that in a city.” I look at him gratefully. The idea of having to do nothing but train all day sounds like pure bliss to me right now. But I needed to make sure that I understood the situation exactly. “I’m not going to change what I do in GCW,” I warn, “and I am bit old for farm chores.” My uncle lets out a deep belly laugh, that is oddly comforting, and starts to lead me away from the grave. “I said I have a house-not a farm,” he snickers, “what the hell do I know about milking cows? I’ll try to win you over about that other thing, but I won’t get on your case when you ignore my obviously good advice.” A smile breaks out on my face, the first real one I’ve felt in a long time. ”I guess that I can live with that,” I respond happily. “But, why the hell did you buy a house in Ohio for anyway? You didn't get reeled in by that guy Jeff, too, did you?” “Nah. I know no Jeff. I had'a do something to get away from your aunt...she found my head between the maid’s legs.”
