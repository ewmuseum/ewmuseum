No Excuse

18 July 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 18/07/2006 His muscles burned with pain and he was finding it increasingly to lift his arms, but like he did with most things in life, he ignored it. The pain was inconsequential, transitory at best and it was no excuse to quit; there was never an excuse to quit and never will be. The punching bag recoiled back in a certain rhythm his fists helped to maintain. Now, the skin was peeling back upon his knuckles and each punch was beginning hurt more and more, but he ignored it. The pain was inconsequential and it was no excuse to stop, to quit like a damned coward. At GCW Warpath, despite all that McKail faced, he hadn’t quit and the thought hadn’t had even crossed his mind. Yet he failed to recapture the GCW Television title and that’s precisely got to him the most. A disqualification robbed him of the true victory he deserved. Yes, he’d ploughed through the nobody’s within the number one contender gauntlet, but that wasn’t exactly anything to proud of; anybody with an ounce of wrestling talent could’ve walked through that gauntlet without breaking a sweat. But doing all of that and then going on to defeat the Television champion would’ve been spectacular. If it wasn’t for that damned mystery man it could’ve happened, McKail could’ve been the GCW Television champion once again. But that mystery man did interfere in the match and McKail did lose the match. There was nothing he could do about that now and he refused to use it as an excuse. He simply wasn’t good enough. He could’ve - should’ve - done more. Unlike most of the other whiners rather questionably stationed within the GCW locker room, McKail didn’t intend to hide behind this mysterious moron. McKail didn’t intent to point at him and use him as plausible scapegoat to explain away another loss to Northsky. What would be the point? What good could that possibly do him? McKail actually allowed this moron to interfere in his match. He did. Nobody else. Yes, the attacker took his anger out upon Northsky that night, but McKail should’ve done everything within his power to stop it. He actually remembered, lying on the canvas, propped up somewhat against the turnbuckle, glancing up at the masked attacker heaving the metal chair high above his head, ready to slam down the weapon, head upon Northsky’s head. He could’ve done something. He could’ve prevented it from happening; it wouldn’t have taken much to stop it. But as history dictates, he didn’t stop it. Truth be told, he chair being brought down upon Northsky’s head with a glint of delight. After all Northsky had said and done to McKail, he wanted to see the woman in agony. A true champion, a winner, has no place for such a desire in his soul, just the burning need to be victorious at any costs - including the prevention of your opponents head being caved in. He should’ve done something to prevent it from occurring, he knew that now and he didn’t intend to make that same mistake twice. The pain was become unbearable now and the rhythm of the heavy punch bag recoiling backward, was becoming less and less regular. It would all end soon; the rhythm would eventually stop as his punches become more frequent and more powerful. Then he could quit, but not before hand. Pain was no excuse to quit; there was never an excuse to quit.
