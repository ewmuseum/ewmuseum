Family Matters (Part One.)

23 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 05/23 He admired himself in the darkened reflection of mirrored sunglasses, trying desperately to distract himself from the task at hand. He knew this meeting would someday be inevitable, and suffice to say he’d been dreading it for the past six years. Each time he ran a hand through his untidy mop of blue hair, it was reflected in eyes of the man, who to Jason Warr may as well have been Lucifer himself. However, where the two horns protruding from his skull should have been, sat perfectly styled hair. Black Armani shoes replaced his hooves, and rather than a pitch fork he wielded a copy of the Wall Street Journal. Setting the newspaper down he greeted Jason with an unsettling smirk. The two were seated in the back of a rather spacious limousine, softly gliding down the streets of Manhattan. Black leather was the decor, allowing the man who sat opposite of Warr to nearly blend into his surroundings. With his eyes unadjusted to the darkness the only things he could make out were his reflection in the mans glasses, his shimmering white teeth, and the pinstripes of his suit. Each detail leapt forth at Jason through the shaded features of the limo, as if the man sitting opposite of him desired this effect. “I’d ask how you’re doing,” Jason scoffed, breaking the eerie silence. “But I really don’t give a shit.” With a short pause he stared right into the eyes of the man, finishing his introduction. “.. Dad”. ?” W ?” When it rains, it pours. As I tried to claw myself out of the pit my life and career had become, I received a phone call from a man I assumed out of my life forever. A man whose had an upper management role in hell with his name on it ever since he squirmed his way out of the womb in the early 50’s, and I man I thoroughly loathe with every fiber of my being. My father, Richard Warr. Ever since Christmas Eve six years ago where he revealed he’d been having an affair on mine and my sister’s mother, we haven’t been on speaking terms. I still remember the odd feeling of cartilage snapping under my fist as I buried it into his nose and walked out of the restaurant. So you can imagine the mood I was in when I received the phone call. I’d lost the GCW World Television title in a fashion I couldn’t accept, my long since ignored father had called me out of the blue, and for Worldwide #48 I was booked in a match with a veritable monster whose natural facial expression wasn’t one of happiness. When it rains, it pours. ?” W ?” “I can see you haven’t changed a bit, Jason.” Muttered the shadowed figure of Richard Warr. The creaking sound of a sun roof sliding open caused Jason’s attention to shift to the top of the vehicle, as bright morning light filled the once dark limousine. “Still the immature little rebel who chooses fists over words.” “Nor have you.” retorted Warr with a sleek grin. “Still horribly vain, trying desperately to cling to the last minuscule roots of youth your decrepit body has left.-? “And doing quite well at it,” Richard interrupted, another sleazy smirk climbing onto his face “Would you believe just last week I was carded while trying to purchase Whiskey” I’ve never felt so complimented.” “Is there a point to any of this” Unless you forgot I am a professional athlete. And Professional Athlete’s have to, ya know.. Train quite often. Especially when they’re booked against mammoth Hispanic men who like to hurt people.” said Jason, leering at a man he once called his father. “I see there’s no room for niceties in this relationship anymore, Jason.” returned Richard, smoothing out the wrinkles from his pinstriped suit. “So I’ll get right to the point. There’s a time in a man’s life, where he has to think about retiring. It’s not easy, in fact it’s something I’ve been wrestling with for nearly a decade now. When you were born I figured some day I’d pass my empire down to you, but all of that went down the toilet on that night six years ago-? “When you non-nonchalantly mentioned how you’d been banging your lawyer since before I was born. That night?” Jason interjected. “.. Yes, that night.” Richard said, the faux happiness draining from his features. “And I’d be grateful if you’d not interrupt me. After that night I tried my damnedest to repair the mistake I’d made. I tried to talk sense into your mother, but that wasn’t meant to be. With you avoiding me I no longer had an heir to my throne, so to speak. Sure, I could have passed the company down to one of your sisters, but this isn’t a Young Ladies responsibility. Doing what I do, you sometimes have to get down and dirty, and I wouldn’t be able to look myself in the mirror if I left it to them.” ?” W ?” I still remember the night I discovered that my father wasn’t just a real estate mogul. That he dabbled in less legal activities, mostly to increase sales. But, that wasn’t the case when I stumbled in on him handing a black kid my age 10 Thousand Dollars with the explicit instruction he was to go to a certain address and kill the inhabitants. I never told him I’d listened in to the conversation, never told a soul. But it got more and more obvious from that point on that he wasn’t just funding gang activity to ensure local businesses paid him protection, he also owned several meth-amphetamine labs on the east coast. His shady activities became obvious to everyone in the house, but he kept his nose clean with the police. It was at that point I decided I didn’t want to live in his shadow, and I forged my own path.. ?” W ?” “You’re such a good Samaritan.” bellowed Jason, finding more and more reasons to hate this man every time he spoke. “Not wanting to hand the company over to your daughters because they couldn’t handle it” Which part exactly, the drugs or the murder? Maybe the illegal intimidation. Maybe a happy combination of all three.” “I’m not saying my business practices are what anyone would call fair and just, but I need you to lis-? “No.” Jason said. He’d had enough of this meeting. “I know what this is about. You want to come to me, all apologetic so I’ll forgive your sorry ass for cheating on Mom. So I’ll take over your criminal empire when you “retire”. And the answer is No. Fuck YOU!” Before Jason could reach the door handle and escape this hell, his father let out a low chuckle. It intrigued him enough to stop him from fleeing the limo. “Are you serious” You really believe I’d hand over something I spent my entire life building to YOU?” laughed Richard. “No, Jason.. I asked you here this morning so you could in fact meet the man worthy of filling my shoes.” As he finished his sentence the car screeched to a halt, and the door swung open. A man stepped in, dressed similarly to Richard, with the same smirk on his face. His curly hair fell to his shoulders as he took a seat next to Richard, staring at Jason. “Allow me to introduce your brother, Riley.” ?” W ?” I don’t need this shit right now. I don’t have a brother, I don’t have time to deal with this garbage. Instead of concentrating on what could be the make or break match of my GCW career, I’m sitting here being fed bullshit by a man I hate and a brother who doesn’t exist. Let’s see what’s on the menu for this week. 1. Phone call from father I hate. 2. Being told some weird guy with curly hair is my brother, and is taking over the company. 3. Giant “roided up Mexican is gunna kill me. When it rains, it pours. To Be Continued.
