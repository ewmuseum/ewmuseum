The Duplicitous Son, pt.3

undated

Date: Several weeks ago. Biddenham Manor, Biddenham, England. Several weeks previously. Early evening. The village was quiet. Of course, this was to be expected. Mentioned in the Domesday Book, the village had been little more than a quiet hamlet on the outskirts of a medium-sized river town named Bedford. With the advent of the railway, that fine product of the Industrial Revolution, the village had swelled, as a consequence firstly of Bedford's direct and swift rail link to the capital, and secondly of Biddenham's close proximity to Bedford's railway station. Biddenham today was what one would call a "commuter village". At this time of the evening, after the rush hour traffic had petered out and the affluent inhabitant families sat down to supper, an almost unnverving calm descended upon the beautiful, verdant little village. The only sounds that could be heard were those of far-off automobiles, the vague background hum of cicadas, and the extremely occasional bark of neighbourhood dogs. Many a time, Thomas James LeStrange's father Harry had appropriated the poetry of Shakespeare and Blake when extolling the beauty and tranquility of the village, referring to it has his own little slice of "this sceptred isle... this other Eden." The grandiose form of Biddenham Manor, the LeStrange family home, lay in a silence equal to that of its surroundings. However, here the comparisons were suddenly curtailed. In stark contrast to the serene, calm beauty of the beautiful old houses, the colourful flora and winding footpaths, the palacial home was an ugly, alarming sight, which threaten to destroy the atmosphere of calm around it. As residents took their evening constitutional or walked their dogs, their faces would contort into a scowl as they passed the Manor, their eyes and sensibilities offended by its state of dilapidation. From time to time, some would even mutter derisory words to themselves under their breath... "Such a shame... It was such a beautiful house..." "They should just bulldoze it, it's an eyesore now..." "Pity about that LeStrange boy... I hear he had some kind of breakdown..." "His parents would be ashamed to see he'd let their home fall apart like that..." Thom had not returned home since before the death of his parents. Critics would attribute his absence to the fact that since the tragedy he had done nothing but wallow in self-pity, refuse to budge from his constant state of denial, and fail to control his abuse of alcohol and narcotics. That he drunk and used drugs in itself was no surprise; he had done so since many could remember. What alarmed many was the self-control he was no longer able to exercise. He considered his intermittent indulgences in drugs and alcohol to be a frivolity that was justified both by his social status (the latest generation of an aristocratic line that stretched back for centuries of English history, he was hardly a dregs-of-society junkie who contributed nothing) and by the strict self-control he was able to exercise. However, he was now beyond all control. Money was no issue to such an opulent young man, but his health and sanity were suffering. Any moment he found himself alone was rapidly devoted to his controversial form of self-medication. His problem was now not solely a physical one, of substance dependency. It was now a very real, and very frightening mental issue; the boy had problems beyond drugs. In rare moments of sobriety, those around him had observed confusion, paranoia, and manic behaviour. The GCW had made a stab at helpful measures in suspending him until he returned to full health, hoping these actions would act both as a reality check and a guilt trip. But ultimately, it had failed - LeStrange now needed a full-on intervention. Consumed by this spiral, Thom had neglected to take care of the practical issues of his parents' death. The legal aspect was as yet untouched, the funeral had been arranged by other family members (Thom had failed even to attend), and the house now lay in disrepair, as Thom had not returned to sort through possesions, furniture, heirlooms, and other miscellania. That was many weeks ago. The house was no longer simply dust-coated and in need of sorting through. The grass of the gardens was overgrown, now waist-high. The fountain that stood proudly in front of the house had been reduced to an obscene receptable for urine, beverage cans and other detritus of the strung-out. The vast majority of the windows had been smashed and were now boarded up. The interior was dark and dank, the carpets stained and giving off a fierce odour. The family's possessions were now all missing, and the remaining fixtures were heavily vandalises. The walls were covered with graffiti, strange intimidating shapes and words, almost shouting from the silence. Strewn across the floor were the discarded items of drug paraphernalia of the squatters who now inhabited the once-opulent mansion - syringes, spoons, silver foil, broken glass pipes, and empty baggies. Given the current state of the Manor, it was a surprise that Thom had not returned - given its uncanny resemblance to the hovels he now frequented in America, he may have felt more at home than ever. *** Biddenham Manor, Biddenham, England. The same week, two nights later. Evening. The fire engine raced along the main road of the village, tearing the usual still and calm with a flurry of light, activity and din. Moments later, two more enormous red trucks, sirens wailing and lights flashing, hurtled along the same route the previous had just taken, on through the night and towards their destination - Biddenham Manor. The fine Tudor house was now nothing more than a blinding orange mass, accompanied by the occasional crackle as its centuries-old timbers burnt through and snapped. At the end of the driveway stood a throng of neighbours, looking on with a curious blend of fear and morbid curiosity, entranced by the inferno, simultaneously terrifying and mesmeric. Firemen jumped out of their engines and with the efficiency and vigour engendered by rigorous training, had pumps set up within moments. As the first enormous, powerful jet of water hit the blaze, the villagers held their breath, waiting for the fire to begin to die down with yet another curious juxtaposition of emotion - this time hope and dejection - hope that the blaze would be contained and subsequently tamed; dejected that this marvellous spectacle might soon come to an end. However, this was not the case; gallon upon gallon of water blasted into the very centre of the inferno, but made no difference; the fire kept blazing. Many hours passed before the fire died, the fireman finally being able to reduce the house to smouldering embers. Whilst their efforts could be neither questioned nor faulted, the dissipation of the blaze was largely due to the fact that it had gutted the entire Manor house, leaving nothing physically left to burn, rather than the efficacy of their fire-fighting methods. By the small hours of the morning, the locals had returned to their own homes, never more appreciative of their in-tact dwellings. As the fire-fighters began preparations to leave, a car turned into the driveway, and came to a halt. A diminuitive, elderly figure emerged from the car, and trotted towards the house in a half-walk, half-jog; the most his frail frame could muster. A look of controlled horror etched its way across his withered features, his brow heavy and his eyes watery. The LeStrange family butler approached the chief of the fire crew, extending his hand to his younger counterpart. The chief nodded grimly. "I'm afraid there's practically nothing left. The basic structure is still just about intact, but this isn't a house any more." "Such a tragedy... Such an old, beautiful building, destroyed like that. A family home, gone. I shall have to inform young master LeStrange... Do you knoW what may have caused this atrocity?" "Squatters, we think. This place has been a squat for a while. Electricity was cut off a while ago, we think whoever was in here lit candles and left them burning. Big wooden house like this, doesn't take long for the whole place to be ablaze. We'll be in touch if we need anything." With that, the fireman left George to absorb the information, squeezing the Butler's shoulder in a simple act of kindness and comfort. After gazing at the wreckage of the house for several moments, sighing heavily and attempting to come to terms with what had happened. He reached into a pocket of his overcoat, extracting a mobile phone tentatively; he did not want to have to inform young Thomas, not in his present state. But he knew that he had to. With another hearty sigh, he dialled the appropriate number, and listened to the ring, dreading the moment that his young master answered, and secrectly hoping he would simply reach his voicemail. Unfortunately, this easy outcome was not to occur. "Hello?" There was a long silence as George braced himself. Finally, he spoke. "Thom, it's George... Where are you?" There was another silence, this time on Thom's part. Unaware to the butler, across the Atlantic Ocean, Thom placed a spoon on the floor next to him, careful not to make a noise on the folded silver foil next to him, or to disturb the baggie of white powder next to it. He raised his index finger to his pursed lips in a motion which ordered the two other strung-out figures which whom he shared the dark, seamy room to remain quiet. He needed have bothered - their eyes were rolled up in their skulls, and they were elsewhere. "I'm just... running a few errands. Speak though." "Thom... I... I'm afraid I have bad news... Again." Thom was taken aback, his eyes widened, scared of what might follow. He assumed that this would be a regular call from Derek - a polite request to come to his senses and return home to England to begin to attack the practical ends of his parents' death, which were still left untied, all imbued with the background disgust and pity about his current state. All of this could be ignored, a momentary inconvenience before returning to his normal, sordid ways. But this was serious. "What is it?" "There's been a fire. The house is... Well, there is no house now. I'm so sorry, Thom." Silence. "Please come back soon, you really need to." After a long pause, Thom replied in a farwar, eerie voice. "I'll leave very soon..." Thom's phone clattered to the floor. He sat in silence, attempting to comprehend what he had been told. However he tried, whatever angle he came at it from, it was too devastating. He had acted churlishly and childishly in response to his parents' death. This he knew. But he had always assumed that time was the great healer, and when eventually his veil of self-indulgent pity could be lifted, he would be able to return to Biddenham and come to terms with it all, and begin to re-connect to his family, to his roots, to his history, even though everything had changed. But now even this was not possible. Now there really was nothing. He didn't even know who he was any more. He knew that sounded trite, sounded clich? - but everything that defined him was gone; all the moorings of his identity had been taken away. His parents were gone; his home and all his memories were gone; and his present was no more, as he was unemployed and outcast from the GCW. He was a stranger to himself. He felt like his body was a vessel for nothingness, a hollow frame with nothing within. In an almost trance-like state in which thoughts, feelings, emotions floated near him but could not penetrate through, Thom lifted the spoon, held it out, and emptied his baggie of heroin into it, his eyes glazed, expectant. He flipped his lighter underneath, and cooked up the foulsome substance, before extracting a needle from a pocket and filling it from the spoon in a calm but hurried fashion. Lip quivering with expectation, he traced his forearm for a vein, located one, and carefully guided the needle in, before injecting at last. Silence. Broken by the thump as Thom LeStrange's head flopped and he tumbled to the floor, slumped. Silence. It would be several minutes before the other two figures in the room came to their senses and realised what had happened to Thom. One fled immediately. The other called an ambulance, and then did likewise, leaving LeStrange on the floor, oddly serene in his stillness, dangling in the precipice between life and death, waiting for some external force to ultimately take him one way or the other.
