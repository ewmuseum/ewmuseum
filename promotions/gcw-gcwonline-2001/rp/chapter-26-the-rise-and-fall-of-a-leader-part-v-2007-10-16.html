Chapter 26: The Rise and Fall of a Leader- (Part V)

16 October 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 16, 2007 Start A fuckin? power hungry beast. It’s all he was. You could see it in the beads of spit he drooled out. In his eyes by the way they bulged red. In his body language like when he leaned against the wall kicking his one foot up like it was West Side Story. Power was everywhere in the guy. He’d slap you gently on the cheek and tell you, without words, I’m better than you. Plain and simple: Steven Caldera was a prick. Chapter 26: The Rise and Fall of a Leader Part V Burger Joint Let me get it straight with everyone; Jay Terror didn’t hate Phillip Indisio. Sure, he felt a slight discomfort when talking with him and an overall longing to disappear but he didn’t hate him. It was more like he was annoyed by the guy. “Fuckin” right doggy!” Phillip stated with a piece of burger hanging over his bottom lip. He slurped it up and licked the ketchup off in an endearing sort of way. He had a crude edge to him, an unfiled and unedited type of gimmick. Gimmick was exactly how you’d describe it too. Most people didn’t know the true person inside the body of Indisio and most likely never would. It just wasn’t his style. He was the kind of guy who had worn his Halloween mask for so long that he finally accepted that he was actually a warewolf instead of Phillip. “All this traveling’s got its fuckin” perks Jay. Fuckin? perks out the wazoo man!” He said before stuffing a handful of fries between his lips and chomping away at the greasy delight. He just didn’t care. To him, he was who he was and if you didn’t like it then you’d be better off not knowing. “Yeah Jay, good stuff.” Art added subtly. “I mean, I’ve been to so many different burger joints in the past two weeks that I don’t know what to do with myself.” He sipped on the straw of his drink and guzzled down the carbonated beverage. “Yeah.” Was all Jay said. Phillip was too oblivious for his own good to notice anything was wrong with Jay. Art, on the other hand, had a certain knack for discovering Terror. He had a parent-like functionality programmed in his brain making him the kind of person you spilled your heart to. He had that rough exterior but underneath it Jay knew that he was a genuine guy. A real friend. “What’s there to do in this town anyways?” “Besides burgers you mean?” Art asked. “Burgers can come back into the picture later tonight. I mean action! Where’s the cops and their pig snouts?” Indisio said. Jay remained quiet and to himself in the booth. He’d been to too many Chubby Burgers to listen to their conversation and care an inch. “You want to mess with the po?” Art added. “I wanna” make “em squeal man. Remember the good old days dude?” He took another hunk out of the side of the burger with his teeth and chewed it like he was expecting an audience. Art shook his head, “How could I forget them.” They both looked at Jay and received no reaction. No emotion. His eyes were transfixed on the salt shaker and a small black object wedged inside it. They squinted and opened randomly as he focused in and out of reality. Phillip decided it was his job to bring their friend back to the conversation. He shook him mildly and spoke, “Jay, dude’WAKE UP!” Terror shook his head and his hair shimmied around in a dainty manner. His head turned slowly and his eyes looked at Indisio. They were unfocused and uncaring. “What?” “Don’t tell me some lady’s got you all emo and shit.” “Nah.” “Something’s got your undies tangled dude.” He lacked any sense of emotion and made it clear that his brain and mouth didn’t cooperate. With most people, they have a kind of connection allowing them to logically piece together a situation and format their words so they came out just right. With Phillip, just right was completely wrong. Jay ignored the statement and looked at Art. That’s the man he needed to talk to. Phillip continued, “Art ain’t gonna” pick “em out man. I think you just need to slap a pig upside the face and snort at him a few times. It always does it for me.” And that’s the thing, Jay wasn’t Phillip. If Jay were Phillip, he’d probably end up waking up in the middle of the night screaming and asking himself “why”. Like I said though, he didn’t hate the guy; he just couldn’t stand him. “Maybe.” He tried to settle Indisio with an answer. “Maybe?” He snorted, “Jay Terror doesn’t say maybe. He fuckin” grabs the pig by the ears and rides it down the street yelling lick my nutsack. No maybe.” But the thing is Jay was the kind of person to say “maybe”. He’d aged over his time with GCW and away from home. Terror had grown up and found out that people can’t just go terrorizing towns and making people look bad. He had learned that a guy can’t live his whole life acting like someone just to get a few quick laughs. That wasn’t life. “Right.” He answered stirring his finger along the tabletop. Art looked at him and then at Phillip. “He’s still cool Phil, no worries.” “Cool” The dude’s a wrestler for Christ’s sake. He lost his cool since he left the bandstand man. Now quit sticking up for him!” he chewed off the last piece of burger and looked at his two friends. It wasn’t clear what truly set Jay off. All anyone knew was that he lost it. His eyes finally found focus and zoomed in on Indisio for a quick stare. Involuntarily his arm lifted from his side and he clenched his fingers inward making a tight fist. It was a process that took all of two seconds to occur but felt like a week and a half to the three of them. His knuckles met Phil’s face and a bit of burger spewed out onto the table in front of them. He hung there for another second and looked shocked as the skin of his cheek reddened and returned to covering his cheekbones. The booth wasn’t long enough to keep him in and down he went, toppling to the floor in a heap. Art eyed him up, “Jay?” Jay didn’t hear him and instead scooted outward and to the spot where Phillip was seated beforehand. He was a crazed animal seeking his first meal in days. Terror jumped down and mounted Indisio just to pummel his face with an array of closed fist punches. His nose bent left, then right, then back left again, and then down where it would stay. Blood trickled out of his nostrils and mixed with the small black hairs of the mustache portion of his beard. Art swung out of the booth now and shoved Jay off him. The customers around them were in frenzy and staff ran to the scene to help break up the massacre. One dialed three digits into the phone on the wall and waited for the voice on the other side to kick in. Within two minutes a police officer and two medics were present and they wanted answers. __________ Pushing his weight like he was some kind of deity or something. Everywhere he went, obstacles became the ground for him. His victims? There was hardly anyone that avoided the madman’s wrath in this day and age. Beyond the likes of LeStatt Knight and Jordan White it didn’t matter who you were. Fat people, skinny people, tall people, bald people’everyone other than his golden boys fell victim to the wrath known collectively as Caldera Enterprises. Among all the helpless souls sits one man. His name: Jay Terror. From day one it seemed that Steven had it out for the outlaw. If it wasn’t for his friendship with Daniel Franco he was sure that he wouldn’t be a part of GCW at all. Nevertheless, the pig continues to torment the outlaw knowing full well what is happening. He doesn’t care if he’s injured and he damn sure doesn’t care what happens to him. Like a neglecting father, Caldera scorns Jay and verbally beats him. What’s a man to do? __________ For the longest time Jay had grouped coffee and body odor with all police locations. It was different here though. Something more like vanilla and mixed fruit filled the halls and warmed his nostrils as he was escorted inside. The man behind him, a well built gentleman, gripped his hands and made sure the shackles connecting his arms didn’t come loose. “Over here Mr. Terror.” He said with his burly voice. Jay grunted and found the door the officer had explained. It opened thanks to a man inside and Jay was shuffled along the floor and to a seat against the wall. The room was plain in its nature and only kept a table and two chairs inside. “What happened?” The officer that opened the door asks the burly one. “Assault. They were at that burger place and apparently the guy just snapped.” The burly officer stated. Their voices were hushed now and their backs turned toward Jay. The outlaw shut his eyes and concentrated on the conversation. “He looks familiar.” “He’s on TV apparently. Some wrestler or something.” “Jay Terror you said?” The first officer asked. He nods and looks back to glance at Terror. “That’s what he goes by. Jamison Talley in our databases.” “Let’s see what we have then, shall we?” They turned around again and faced their prisoner. He hadn’t moved an inch since sitting. The officer that had opened the door took the seat across from Jay and placed his hands on the table. He tapped to get Jay’s attention but got no response. “Hello Jamison.” He hated the name and therefore didn’t feel obliged to answer. “Jay, how are we?” He changed his tone and used the typical “I’m your best friend” approach. The outlaw opened his eyes and looked at the sitting officer. His name tag read John. “Great John.” He had forgotten about the name badge on his chest and looked down to see if it was still there. “That’s good to hear. Hopefully we can get you going on to the rest of your day. We just need a few answers.” “I don’t answer anything without my 27’s.” He grunted again and made himself comfy in the chair. “Cigarettes you mean?” “No, ponies.” John looked at the brawny officer and silently asked him what to do. The standing officer spoke, “He had a pack on him when we apprehended him.” “Get him one. Nothing wrong with a quick smoke.” The large officer dug into his pocket and found the half empty box of Marlboros. He slapped them down on the table and watched Jay lash out for them. “Got a light?” He asked. John pulled out his lighter and ignited the flame for Jay. The outlaw bathed the end of the cig with the flame and in a second the paper caught and smoke spewed out. He took the first drag and made it good. “So what do we need to know boys?” His demeanor took a complete three sixty showing just how good the smoke was for him. John took a second to admire the man before him. He’d seen him on television many times and now, as he sat there, he noticed a side other than the one reporters and cameras showed. Jay was, in fact, completely personable and not as intimidating at all. “Well let’s start with today. What was your business at the burger joint?” “Can’t a guy eat?” “Fair enough.” He took in the second hand smoke and remembered the days where he’d share a cigarette of his own with the road. “Your friends then, who were they?” “Old buddies. We were just chatting.” He sucked on the end of the paper and drew in the toxins orally. “Chatting turns to fighting?” “Not usually.” “Then what was so special about today?” John persisted. He was easing himself in like his mentor had taught him to do. “I don’t know.” “How can you not know?” Jay looked at John and studied him quickly. His eyes were stone cold and his face was emotionless. He was one of those interrogators that you had to admire. “The guy just pissed me off today.” “So you broke his nose?” “Did I really?” Jay showed a semi-smirk. John looked at the taller officer and then back at Jay. “That’s what I’ve heard. You’re proud of that?” “Why shouldn’t I be?” He took another drag and this time blew the smoke upward toward the burly man. “That’s fine work.” “I thought you two were old buddies?” “Of course we were. I love the guy.” “Yet you pounded his face in?” He was utterly confused by the motive. Jay dropped the ashes over the floor and leaned his head against the wall. He kept that smirk on his lips and looked back at John, “Friends fight.” “What did he do?” “He didn’t do anything.” He was playing hard to get. John put his hand on his head and leaned on the table. “Then what did he say” Something had to have happened that made you punch him in the face.” “It’s a friend thing. No worries.” “Jay, I know that there has to be more to it than this. Friends don’t just fight like that and break noses. Either something was said between you two or you need to attend a few sessions of anger management.” John proposed. He wanted to know the truth but by the way the conversation was going it seemed to be growing more and more out of reach. “Anger management?” “If you don’t give us any information I’ll be forced to line you up with a set of classes to help your rage.” Jay chuckled to himself and took another drag on the cigarette. It was shrinking. “I told you. I just felt like punching the guy.” John let out a deep sigh and looked up at the burly officer. They opted to exit the room leaving Jay alone in his chair with his box of cigarettes. He took the last hit on his current one and tossed it across the room. Unfortunately, the officer had taken his lighter and now he had nothing but a tease sitting on the table. Outside the room, John and the taller man, whose name tag read Francis, spoke to one another. John was first to talk, “So what do you say?” “He’s a wrestler. He probably has steroids injected in him or something.” Francis replied. “So you think that they were just messing around?” Francis shook his head, “Nah. I bet the friend said something subtle that got processed wrong with all those extra hormones. He has anger issues for sure.” John nodded and ran his fingers through his thinning hair, “Would you agree that he could benefit from some time with Anita?” “Definitely.” Francis answered. It was a happy thought for him to see Jay Terror tortured in a room with the police’s official shrink. False justice at its best. John agreed and both men walked off down the hall. Their job was done. Jay, on the other hand, remained in the room looking at his box of cigarettes. He’d kill for just one more puff. __________ So what do you do with a man that you can’t exactly topple? How do you react to his dictatorship? Simple. You make him remember your name. Hell, you make the bastard suffer. __End
