The Ballad of Asgard: Chapter 2

12 September 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 12th, 2008 As green as the view was, he couldn’t take his eyes off of her pale features as they walked through the almost desolate plains of a small town in Ireland. The emerald green went as far as the eyes could see. The grass still held the last bits of the morning dew as the day and the sun made their presence felt. The air they breathed into their lungs was fresh and without the smell or feeling of dozens of years of abuse. It was one of the places in the world that still kept itself chaste and that had yet to be violated by the unrelenting machine that was modernity. The trees swayed slowly in what looked to Antonio like beautiful painting that had suddenly come to life. It was almost impossible to believe that there existed places like this one after spending so much time in an arid hell. “So, what do you think of “ire?” Death asked as she gave a twirl with her hands outstretched. Antonio stood there in silence, taking it all in. He took a deep breath and exhaled. With it all his doubts and frustrations seemed to have left his body, if only for a little while. “This place is amazing. It is all so green,” he said as he took an even closer look at his surroundings. “I think I see a town down there.” Antonio and Death began to walk slowly towards the town. They were in no rush and they both wanted a break from the pressure and responsibilities of their lives. They passed through a small sign that announced the entrance to the small village of Moydow, Ireland. The small village stood there, stuck in some unknown time. To Antonio, who was used to the hustle and bustle of big cities, the place seemed completely alien to him. People were dressed like the modern people he knew, but there was a feeling about the village that seemed off. As they crossed the first few houses, he felt the eyes that scrutinized the pair keenly hidden in their small fortresses of stone and wood. They heard the faraway scuttles of little feet as the children who were outside at that hour ran inside to hide from view. The ominous slamming of doors and windows could also be heard as they walked deeper into the village. They came upon one of the two pubs in that quaint little place. The sign outside clearly announcing one of the activities permitted in this town, drinking, depicted with a wooden pint with the name of the place besides it. “The Vintage” was probably one of the better names for a pub he had come across in a while. They both went inside. “Well, at least we know people still drink even when it seems like the end is nigh,” she said to no one in particular as the people sitting on the bar looked at the pair with a mixture of fear and surprise. The bartender never stopped cleaning the glasses in front of him. He tossed his towel over his shoulder and asked in a deep Irish accent, “What’s yer poison, mate?” “I’ll have your best whiskey,” he said as he sat down on a stool. “I’m surprised to see the pub open so late. Even more surprised to see people here.” He surveyed the other patrons of “The Vintage”. They all seemed to have the same “These are bad times for us here” it’s been a long hard summer of work and it doesn’t seem to be letting up,” the bartender said as he poured Antonio’s drink and handed him the glass. Antonio saw the patrons look tersely at the bartender but he just waved them off. “To tell you the truth, they think you two are either ghosts or working with them,” he said as he went back to cleaning glasses. The people sitting at the bar all gasped and shuffled out of the place. Death turned around from an old jukebox and was surprised to see the place empty. “Where did everyone go?” “They probably went to tell whoever doesn’t know you two are here about it. Everyone must be scared witless. Not that there was a lot of wit in the first place.” He sighed. “So, are you going to tell me why you are here?” Antonio drowned the last of the whiskey and signaled for more. This was definitely not what he had asked for when he decided to travel the world and take a nice vacation from the drama of real life. “I’m just here on vacation.” The bartender examined Antonio as he served more of the alcohol in his glass. “How were you able to walk into town” There’s been some sort of shield or casing that’s been holding us inside for months now. No one has been able to come in or leave,” the bartender said with obvious surprise in his voice. Antonio pointed to Death. “Talk to her about it, she knows more about this stuff than me.” Death sat down in front of the bartender. “We didn’t feel anything when we arrived in town, are you sure that there is something there preventing people from getting out or getting in?” “I’m as sure as can be. I’ll show you.” Antonio and Death stood up and followed the bartender to the outside. They walked in silence behind the bartender and looked around to see what exactly was going on with the town. They saw more people following their every step and couldn’t understand what exactly they had gotten themselves into. When they finally reached the end of the village, the bartender stopped and pointed towards the border of a small but dense forest. He walked towards it and was suddenly pushed back to the floor with tremendous force. Antonio went over to him and helped the bartender up. “We never even felt that when we walked through,” he said with apparent surprise. While the bartender shook the dust from his clothes, Antonio decided to test the shield or whatever it was. He walked through it and back without any problem. By that time a crowd on onlookers had gathered in the entrance of the village. The story of the two people who could go through had already reached most of the village and most were either scared or curious or a mixture of both. The murmurs between them as he walked in and out of the village without problem intensified. “Who is it exactly that has you working here” And why?” Death was very interested in what was happening and her interest was evident and on the rise with each moment that they spent in that invisible prison. They all looked at each other and after the consensus became that the two strangers were not really sent there to kill them all, they all seemed to start talking at the same time. The explanation was lost on both Antonio and Death until a young man in his early twenties stood out from the crowd. “Me name is Eamon Donelan,” he said as he shook hands with both Antonio and Death. His grip was firm but at the same time weak from all the work and the lack of food that he and the other villagers had to endure. “It is a voice. We all hear it. It said to us that we had to dig near the old monastery until we found special pieces of iron. The voice said that we would know when we found them.” “Have you found any yet?” Antonio asked them. Death seemed to wander off to just about the edge of the forest seemingly having lost interest in the story that the young man was telling. “Yes. We have. We left them here as the voice told us to,” he said as he pointed to a spot close to where Death stood. --- “Come on, brother. It will soon be our time,” Helbindi tried to convince his brother but he was having little luck. Byleist had always been scared of change and of almost everything there was. Helbindi was different. He had grown in the shadow of his older brother for centuries and it had driven him mad. He spent weeks and years reading and rereading the Prose Edda and the Poetic Edda until he was not able to contain his hatred any longer. Byleist was usually the recipient of abuse from his elder brother? even if it was by minutes. Someone who didn’t know their history could never tell that they were twins. They were both so different that their bodies had taken the forms that they had of themselves in their minds. “I’m sick of Odin and Thor taking all the credit. We were there when the people needed us but they no longer remember us, they no longer pay tribute to us. I feel my strength weakening every second I stand here. We will destroy them, brother! We will be remembered!” Helbindi punched the wall of their hiding place and it shook its foundation. “Brother” please? let’s just be. Let us go to the hall of the forgotten gods with our heads held high,” Byleist begged his brother. “No! I will not be forgotten. I can’t.” Helbindi walked over to the blade he had built out of centuries of sweat and pain, memories and experiences. He softly passed his finger by the edge. As soon as his flesh touched the blade, a wound was opened. “Now that they have found the pieces of Odin’s long lost crown, I will be able to defeat brother Loki.” His cackle was terrifying and Byleist could do nothing more than to cower in fear. Helbindi grabbed the hilt he had confected out of Odin’s iron crown. He went over to the blade and slid the hilt hard enough to attach it to the blade. The sword was finally done. “This sword will put Excalibur to shame! Now we only need Loki’s blood. He will suffer.” --- “So, what you are telling me is that a voice has been telling you what to do for the better part of three months” It also told you about the pieces you must find in the ground? hmmm? this is definitely strange,” Antonio said mostly to himself although Eamon had nodded every time a question had been answered. “What do you think?” he asked Death with obvious concern in his voice. In a way, he wanted to be away from that place and getting on with his vacation, but he knew that they had to help those people. Death was too concerned for something he couldn’t see that she didn’t answer for a while. She crouched a little and moved with inhuman precision. Her movements seemed to be in slow motion to human eyes. For a moment, it was as if she was a part of her surroundings. Eamon and the villagers stood around staring at Death as she did her thing. Nobody really knew what she was doing, but they were interested anyway. When nobody expected it, she released a feral scream and jumped in front of something they could not really see yet. “I got you!” she said loudly with a smirk on her face. “You didn’t think you could hide from me, did you” Not even your kind can escape me.” She never turned her head or averted her eyes as she spoke. “What are you doing here?” “Le do thoil,” the voice of the creature pleaded. “What is it?” Antonio asked as he moved towards Death and the creature. What he saw was surprising but at the same time, it wasn’t so much. He had seen so many strange things that standing in front of a paralyzed leprechaun was something he should have expected when he arrived in Ireland. The small creature dressed in green that stood in front of him was not exactly what he had imagined when he had heard about leprechauns for the first time. The reddish hair and beard were dirty and his clothes seemed very old. His face was drawn out and tired from whatever it was that leprechauns do. “This is what has been causing us all trouble. Everyone, come closer and look at him. If your eyes never waver, he will be paralyzed in its place.” Everyone moved towards the creature. Most started jeering at their captor but Death knew that the leprechaun couldn’t have done all that by himself. She moved over to where Antonio stood and continued her conversation with the leprechaun. “It is not yet your time, but not even your pleas of mercy will help you from this mob unless you tell them why you have imprisoned them,” she said sternly. The crowd was getting a little over excited and that wouldn’t be good if they wanted information. “It’s not my fault.” His voice was almost a shriek. “They stole my treasures. They said if I didn’t do it, they would destroy my beautiful treasure.” The creature began to sob uncontrollably. “Who has your treasure?” Antonio asked the creature. “I had never heard of them. They claimed to be brothers of Loki, the Nordic god.” Death was confused. She didn’t know every single god that had ever existed, and she had never heard that Loki had any brothers. In her travels and during her job, she had had dealings with the Nordic god of treachery and lies. Her experiences with him had not been pleasing. She didn’t understand exactly what was going on, but she would find out. “Ok. It’s time to get out of here,” Death said as she prepared to move on. “Why?” “We have to find out what’s going on. I know exactly who to ask.” “I was afraid you’d say that. What are you going to do about the leprechaun” If we leave him here he will probably be torn to pieces,” Antonio said as he looked back at the creature paralyzed in front of an angry mob that was inching ever closer to it. “We’ll take him with us and then let him go on the other side.” As she said that, she grabbed Antonio’s arm and disappeared in a flash. The villagers would eventually tell the tale of the beings who rescued them from the clutches of death and then disappeared into thin air. This is how legends are born. --- They arrived at their desired destination. It was the most beautiful place Antonio had ever seen. If the Irish village had been stunning, this place was doubly so. It stood high atop a mountain, a huge stone structure that shone in the brightness of the sun. Antonio walked towards the golden gates, entranced. “You can go now, faerie. Keep out of trouble or I might decide to come get you before your time,” Death told the leprechaun as she made him disappear. Before he went completely, his voice could be heard. “Go raibh maith agat.” Antonio was still looking at the castle when Death stood by his side. A woman appeared behind the gate. They had walked silently to the gates without Antonio even noticing what had happened. “Welcome, mistress. It has been a long time since you have graced us with your presence. Our king awaits you in Valhalla,” a beautiful Valkyrie announced as the gates opened. They both went inside. “Welcome to Asgard, the city of heroes and gods.”
