After the Goldrush

25 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Posted 8/25/05. Between Warpath and the following Worldwide, technically. He was out of the arena in fifteen minutes; he could walk and that was enough for him to waive the attending paramedics with threats vague, delirious, supplied by a facet of his brain not entirely coherent. He had no name for what swept his body; he thought of it as rage as he limped through fluorescent halls and considered regret as he cranked his car; neither hit the gestalt of the experience. He grit his teeth and let his forehead rest against the steering wheel. He was passive. He felt it in the hollows of his bones, if nothing else. Some stoic cocksucker. He revved the engine, cracked his face into the slightly yellowed smile of teeth bared psychotic; of lips that flared, of gums that ran white from some internal tension. A mass of knotted neurons; some flared pain and revenge, some fired loss, wanton destruction. A baseball bat in a parking lot. High explosives in a gas station. Falling back on old kinks, of bunking down and losing his mind. There was being pragmatic, there was being optimistic or pessimistic or realistic; then there was being wrong. He'd have settled for anything but the last until it came clear as the only potential positive: if he was right, if heart didn't matter, then he simply wasn't good enough. Beaten by enough people that they'd started booking him against moralistic stances to liven things up. Either passion mattered, or all his talk of talent/skill/ability was bullshit that he couldn't live up to. Wrong or weak. Options. He crashed back into his seat. He wouldn't play their game because that's not where it was won or lost. Pointless. Pointless. Tattooed it on his brain. Rolled it in tight loops against a background as empty as he could manage. Took down air. Let it out. Breathed. Breathed. What apathy? He cared. He'd gotten one more chance and given his third failure. He could hide in public better than he could hide the truth. He hated wrestling. He hated the fans. He hated the wrestlers. He hated the legends the same as he hated the neophytes, the announcers and the promoters. He hated pageantry and he hated the game. He'd bathed himself in prepubescent hero worship because it suddenly paid well; coming up for air meant seeing the surface world and never wanting to submerge again. He grinned again. Freedom. Going back to college, getting his degree and doing as little work as was reqired to survive with the bank account he'd built for himself. The thought of leaving was the first joy wrestling had brought him. He found the human joy in dreaming of his enemies--imagined, though they were--reduced to gore and splattered across him, but that lust just dwelled in the ancient depths of his brain. Entertaining that reptilian urge as a 9-to-5 was too far gone. Things came together. They didn't lessen the ache or numb the pain; lit it bright instead of offering up consolation. In the crazed meaninglessness of wrestling, he was among the dying rubble of its sanity. If there was any scrap of humanity not twisted by a dementia so common as to be inherent, it was him and, even then, he only held to it by inches. He reasoned that, if humanity was the only actual prereqisite to be a human, then they were either in- or sub-; it followed that the rules he'd learned in his life no longer applied once he was in the arena, that the entirety of the phenomenon had suckered itself into thinking that any of their personal bullshit mattered. They were animals and they couldn't handle themselves. They had created a sport where Tom Brady could've gotten away with trying to strangle Eli Manning to death with the Superbowl shirt, where Tyson would've broken no rules by gnawing at Holyfield's ear. And he was in it. One of them. He understood Sage. Dirt wallowing, abyss gazing alike. It was a central tenet. That every action or inaction, even the vapid and the inert, built a person. That every minute spent was another progression towards some direction, that he'd never found greener pastures behind the tracts of mud. He had nothing to gain. He had everything to lose. And that was the real zero sum game he'd thought about, had even mentioned when the words were too slippery to keep down. "Son of a bitch." He put his shoulder to the wheel. Phone. Was Caroline. "Holy shit. Frank? Are you alright?" "Nah. Not really." She started a word. Something or other. "Where are you?" "Im at the hotel." "Cool, Im leavin the arena now. I. Shit, what room are you?" "232." "Yeah. Ima be there." "Theyre letting you drive? Like. Already?" "I, uh. Sometimes, yknow, you just aint really in the mood to get permission. If you got smoke or drink or anythin. I would appreciate the hell out of it." "Ive got you." "Word." He hung up, separated her voice from the ringing in his ears and picked at the dried passage of blood streaking from his brow. His right ankle was the wrong color and swollen grotesqe and he tossed it across the floorboard and played at the pedals with his left foot, tested its aptitude and decided it was as good as any could be on their first attempt. There was nothing to do but continue his new path of twisting the mechanical spirit into just a base tool for animalistic tears and primal twists, of constantly escaping one mistake for another. Of having too little control to even note it--distinct from being out of control, having more in common with being just clever enough to see the bars of the cage. So, in his world of limited freedom, he drove with a head haunted by the idea of his soul beginning to share features with those of his peers; he hunted for the apathy that had kept him safe, roared his music louder to drown his ears to anything between them. The inside always won, kept its timbre decibels above whatever he threw at it until he couldn't keep the arms race going, until the pressures within and the pressures without threatened to crush his fragile three dimensions. He had limits; that inner figure did not. He coasted well within his, took odd turns along avenues that made so little impression as to disappear when he would turn around to find his way back, leaving only a narrow crack where the buildings surrounding had slid together for a want to eqalize distance against the sudden absence of a space he'd driven on and considered inarguable. Frustrations that piled. He pulled over at one of a row of deserted storefronts and stepped into the orange of the city with his face to the skyline--hoping it held even the barest sense of permanence--until he found the glowing silhouette of the most recent single bedroom replacement to his single everything apartment. Space was made in his skull for its general direction, coopted without argument from the beast thing that wore him. A grayed obelisk, topped with oxidized copper, facing a shorter building and backed by a taller one. Vague details he latched onto like a moral code, a picture that shifted with every glance he gave it on the way; a picture that replaced itself on fleeting glimpses, that zoomed and twisted as he weaved through streets emptier than even night traffic should have allowed. He'd covered ten minutes of driving in an hour and he parked aboveground before a zig zagged limp inside, past the inattentive and into a rising wait that ran opposite his draining tolerance. Out of the elevator. Conversations and the heavy sounds of motion without words cocooned him down the hall, head low and palm tracing support along wallpaper. His heart rate had slowed into a languid boil. His head hadn't. Posted against her doorframe, he knocked twice and listened. "Coming." She opened her room up to him with an air of trash magnificent to her. Dressed down. Blonde and cotton, radiating something smug. She looked up to him and her smile faded. Twisted into the other. "Fuck." "That bad?" He pushed inside, aimed himself at the bathroom while she played moving barricade. She nodded; trepid at first but acclimating rapidly. "You probably want a drink first. I couldnt find any pot, but." "My room." Posted in the dim entrance between him and a mirror, she stretched her arms wide and blocked him outright. "Drink." "No. I gotta see this." "You really dont. You need stitches or something--you seriously dont need to look at it." "See? Now youre hypin it up. No choice." He faked charm. It worked. Or pity worked. Or she moved, regardless. He jutted past. Collected himself on the sink; Caroline flipped the flight switch on behind him. He sneered. Hair caked in wide gouges. Blood highlighting the crevices and the contours. A map of carnage. Just gore, dark coagulation, lingering lumps of broken scabs caught in the streams. He sneered again. Fingers to his forehead, peeling his bangs away from the hole that had dried them in. He leaned down and he turned the faucet on, rubbing his face to watch the red cells spiral down the drain. Splash. Splash. The water went light pink. Splash. The water went red. He looked up again, streaked and wild. He dug the wound clean with enough action to open it again. "Alcohol." "What kind?" "Rubbing." "I. I dont think I have rubbing alcohol." He looked down, absentminded as he rubbed a few of the specks off the sides of the sink, pushed them under the waterflow and let them join the rest. "Shit. High proof, then. Something thatll burn." "Youre not gonna. Youre not gonna light your face on fire, right?" "No." "Okay." Her reflection left into the main room. Clank. Hands soaked, he rubbed at his face. Dislodging. Cleansing. "Promise?" It took him a second. "I wont light my face on fire." "Okay." Behind him, poised graceful with a bottle of dark liqor, alcoholic beyond reason. "But youre gonna owe me if you use it all." He took it from her, twisted the cap away and poured through the fire spout into his hands, cleaning them with a surgical precision that he felt uncomfortable admitting in the company of others. Swallowing his pride, he sqinted and faced the ceiling. He turned the bottle up. Jesus. Jesus and flames and stinging, stinging, stinging. He cringed. The liqor welled in his wounds. Teeth that grinded. Lungs that skipped on inhalation. "Okay. Okay." He repeated it in choral refrain, a momentary glowing glimpse into the zen of monks. "Okay. Paper towel? Towel? Bandaids?" Without words, she planted terry cloth in his outstretched hand and he bore it to his face. No respect for hotel property these days. He tore at his eyelids until they were raw enough that they had to be dry. He opened his eyes. He shrugged. Little weak flows. Blood solute to alcohol that ran bitter copper to his lips. "Alright." "Yeah. I am going to recommend the hell out of you getting stitches." "Not really bleeding that bad. All scar tissue up there anyways." "Now. Are you playing tough because you think I like that, or are you being serious?" "Its not tough, its stupid. And that aint somethin I gotta play at." He washed his hands under steamy water. She grinned, even let a curt giggle slip. "You carry it well." Towel, ruined, collapsed against the sink. Frank stepped away and pulled off the tshirt he'd worn as a sort of safeguard against the war results, stunned a second at the stains that had crept into the wifebeater beneath. Everything dirtied. "So. Since youre just going to soldier along, do you want that drink now?" "Yeah. That would be the shit." She left him with his other. He set his weight atop the sink on palms and knuckles; forwards, then, peering onto that negative self without connecting. Frank the Warrior, externalized. A false grin of red outlining; he recovered the bottle and took a swig, sqeaked his finger against teeth and gargled the coagulation away. He watched the cracked self swallow the mix and he shook his head. What had become of him set back with a sneer. The clatter of ice. Caroline in the mirror the same as Caroline behind. "I still cant believe you drink that shit straight." "Cant believe anythin I do, personally." He twisted a fall, cupped porcelain in the small of his back and he took the glass. Click on the sink. Bottle tipped. "You are a special case, Frank Black." His scabbed eyebrows rolled. He drank back with eyes closed, wiped tension from the bridge of his nose with his wrist. World fuzzing into something; Caroline lingered on peripheries of being (important, meaningful, useful), but never strode fully into frame. "Well. Sane man wouldnt be where Im at." "Havent met many people lately that were that honest about themselves." "Yeah? Dont look to me for it." "Hah. Lemme guess--heres where you pretend that everything youve dones been, like, political maneuvering or something." "Sall carefully considered gambits. Manipulation or, or some shit like that." "Haha. Come on, Frank. You really need to relax." Another drink. "Nah. Nah. I relax too much. I really need to focus." "Oh yeah, with how laid back youve been the last few weeks, and how just utterly chill you are tonight." "Exactly." He leveled his stare, not relenting to her game. "Think Im gonna be takin the week off." "Totally uninvolved with how smashed up your ankle is." "Yeah, this things fine. Your ol boy couldnt even dent the fucker." She topped her drink off. It was a reaction. "What are you doing this week, though?" "Im goin back to Charlotte tomorrow. I gotta. You ever get the feelin that youre just fuckin up on every level you can think of?" "All the time." "Yeah. If I dont fix somethin, my ass is done." "So, you just gonna leave me to fend for myself in big, scary Canada?" "Oh yeah. Just gotta hope the bears dont get you." "Heh." They were drinking and they continued to drink until Frank left her at some minute hour to return to his room and he smoked, smoked deep and majestic on his balcony with marked face turned to the lights of the world below; his eyes had begun to swell and the ground beneath him to take on a continuity of tail- and headlights that flowed without interrupt in red and white lines along the Ontario grid, and he imagined there to be a sort of traveler god that existed within the streaks of light and he prescribed onto it a rolling series of personality traits, of reasons and failings and prides and even, as his attentions waned and his lungs faltered, thought to give it a legitimate destination that wasn't a cheat like Elysian Fields or Mt. Olympus, but a solid geographic point found as a logical conclusion of the life Frank had vicariously lived into this thing that existed, if it existed at all, most prominently in the center of his own skull. Then he slept. With an unthought/unsaid prayer to his patron deity, he left as day fell (through his west-facing windows) and he fell back into his life as a passenger, into nodding silent while flights delayed and diverted, into the drag of boarding and the nagging regret of going coach when there were other options thrust in his face at every opportunity. He didn't sleep; he sat with his mouth sealed and his face to the window while word rolled through the crowd that maybe he was someone famous and, given that much, his time in public wasn't a day-to-day life as much as it was a selfless offer to give devolving scrawlings of his signature on outstretched cocktail napkins with a line that formed up exactly to the point where a stewardess suggested it might give leverage to the terrorists to have the entire cabin paying attention to a man with white skin instead of brown. To paraphrase. He left with numbers scrawled across his arm, each marked by a series of stars so he would remember, were destitution to crash against his neck, which ones he was obligated to call and which ones were better served floating into the sewer with the rest of the shower. He took from it one thing: they regarded him as a celebrity vessel first and a competing athlete rarely, if ever. And he slept that day away, and the following few the same. He turned his phone back on the day after that, left it to charge and submerged himself in the orange hours of the city that had molded him. He ate, he shuffled through crowds either tired of his presence or that, too wrapped up in their daily lives, just wandered unaware. The balance between hating recognition and hating anonymity. The vagueries of hate unshakable. Messages were waiting his return the same as they waited his absence. He sat down deep in front of a glowing television, began to roll something while he checked. Caroline, asking about meeting up for the next show. His parents, asking how was he doing?, and would he be back in the city soon? GCW, or more correctly representatives on behalf of GCW, clarifying that having refused their medical treatments, he had no rights to level legal action in the case of future lingering side effects. He rolled his ankle with some success. GCW, or just more voices behind the letters, granting him the week as leave. He had deleted them as he came to them and he hung up then, called Caroline to let her know he was going to stay in Charlotte, called his parents to let them know that he was cool, that he'd be by tomorrow or the day after--as soon as he gets the chance, he swears. He smoked after that, put in one of Sage's regular shipment of CDs and perched himself on an inner window ledge. He'd forgotten about weeks off in his run at the title. Relaxation crept about his spine like an old lover that he couldn't qite remember why he left, other than wanting to try something different with no slight intended against the former. He took his time before he went back to the voicemail. It was Sage that he got, whatever he expected. "Frank. I dont want to have to keep saying this with a little regret in my voice, but you looked good out there. Yeah--so you lost and you got fucked up a little. Shit happens--theyre telling me you left without consulting the doctors those fucks keep around? Look, hit me up when you get the chance. And dont get confused why youre still my employee--your jobs never been to win titles. You just have to make money, wear the brand. Youre moving product on an upwards curve, so. Fuck Valentine, fuck Terror. You are the shit. Get back at me." And then it was Zoe with, boiled down to the essentials, how are you doing? He called her first. "Frank? Jesus, are you alright?" "Haha. Yeah. I can walk aight, so. Hows things with you?" "Im good. About got my ass fired today, but. It happens." "I feel that. Look--you tryna do somethin before the weekend? Nothin big, I mean, I just need to get my ass somewhere I can chill, watch TV for a few hours." "Dont tell me you got evicted." "Nah, nah. Just, well. The walls aint that great of company." "Well. Doesnt that interfere with your little wrestling thing?" "No. Got the week off." "You and me both, dude. Gimme a call whenever. Ive got to get to the shower something fierce." "Thats straight. Peace, Z." "Take it easy." After that, it was nothing. Staring at a screen unmoving, sneering at ghosts and wisps alike, head occasionally shaking off some nervous twitch that had accumulated in the scruff of his neck. He was unhappy in a way that was hard to define and he'd already given up trying to classify or diagnose it--and to fuck all with tilting at cures. It would linger and, were it malignant and his mental immunities weak, claim him for his inaction. It was simple. He sat. The hours drained away before he called Sage. "If it isnt the prodigal son." "Yo. Whats good?" "Nothing, nothing; entire fucking worlds of nothing." "Shit. Whats up?" "I dont need to talk about it. I need to not talk about it. Either way--howre things over there? Shit. Hows the ankle?" "It aint broken." "Sprained?" "Maybe." "Swollen?" He regarded it; he poked at its pink bulb. "Little, yeah." "Fuck. Thats not bad at all. You staying off of it? "Best I can." He didn't ignore his limped stroll; he just lied. "Atta Frank. Just let that heal up and. Do you have any idea what your focus is now that, you know. Now?" "Fuck it. Worlds always given it to me before--might as well wait it out. See what happens." "Just gotta be cavalier, dont you?" "Hey. Whatever gets me by." "True enough. Okay, okay. I think Ive got it figured out." "What?" "It. Gimme a call when you know what your plans are." "What about, yknow. This call? The one you already told me to make?" "Well. Your ankles in one piece, but your directions smashed miserable. No point just, dancing around the thing--if youre healthy, I just wanna know what my biggest stars gonna be doing in the next few weeks." "Gotcha. Prize pig." "There you go. Now get to wallowing. Ive got. Ive got some shit to deal with." "Be easy." "Yeah. Yeah. You too." He hung up, still lost. Heroes and villains left him with nowhere to be and nowhere to go. He hung his head and fell asleep. And nothing, and nothing. He dialed numbers with stars the next day, working his way through the list until someone was in the area and free, and he spent the day preening and fucking. He kicked her out in as nice of terms as he could muster afterwards, and he went back to sleep. Sleep black and dreamless. It was Zoe the next day, once he'd washed the other's smell from him and taken the time to watch himself. Scabbed and bruised. Eyes, little dead globes, that sat inactive in his skull. His jaw had taken on a natural slack. He dressed against his face, countered the wounds with unwrinkled khakis, with some sheer, almost silken, button down striped shirt, with boots that he hadn't tried on, much less broken in. His ankle roared demands; he made a concession of low top driving shoes and, in some last ditch hint of remembrance before leaving his house, called her. That he should come on through was the general idea. Halfway there, behind someone accelerating just to tailgate the car ahead of them with brakes down, he took a detour through residentials decrepit and called Sage again. It was the third ring: "Wasnt expecting you." "Shit, I wasnt expectin you would actually answer." "Hey. Magic. You get your head right?" "No. Maybe. I dunno--look, man. What the fuck was up with Thirteen?" "Hm?" "Gettin my ass wasted and all, all fuckin cocky goin into the match. I had that shit--you know I had that shit." "Maybe you did. Nothings that solid, I mean." "No, it aint. But. Man, I cant help but thinkin that girl shook me." "Look, Frank. I dont know what exactly she did to you, but all shes ever done is give people choices. You didnt have to listen." "I thought she was right, though." "And now youre saying she was wrong? Qick turn around--probably easier than blaming yourself for losing it. Right?" "Sage." "Is my name. Come on. Is the loss really fucking with you that hard?" "Yeah. Yeah, I think it is." "Shit. Frank, okay. Ever since I met you, your heads been just a little off from the way things are." "Man." "Listen. Listen deep. I saw your little, that promo you cut for Warpath. Wrestling this, wrestling that--whatever. You really believe that shit?" "Maybe." "Well. Thats cool and all, but theres one thing youve got to remember. Wrestling, okay, thats a sport. But this shits not won or lost on wrestling. This shit is, its fucking prowrestling. Its not a sport. Its a competition and its won or its lost by something that isnt qite the same as talent. You understand? I couldnt take it, so I cant blame you for not wanting it to be that way, but youre gonna have to face up to it someday. If you dont want to deal with all that pompous posturing bullshit, become an olympic wrestler. Become a boxer. But dont, dont, dont ever think you can change this job to suit you. Adapt or die--you hear me? Those are your choices. And, if I know Thirteen worth a fuck"--he laughed--"all she wanted out of you was you to be smart enough to see em. Now, I could be totally wrong here and it wouldnt be a first and it wouldnt even fucking surprise me, but, shit. Frank, man? Straight up--youre one of the most talented motherfuckers Ive ever met, but youre just using it wrong. Youre listening, right? I dont want this connection breaking up, because Ive kept my mouth shut on this for fucking months." Frank nodded, first. Then a "yeah." "Good. Im just saying, there are people in this thatve burned their souls to a fucking husk in the name of gold. All the talent in the world wont save you from a psychopath. Shit. Didnt you get your ass beat by that chicks husband, Adam or whatever? Thought youd have figured something out from that. Like I said. Get your shit figured out. Ive only opened my mouth because it might help you get your shit figured out." "Huh." "Yeah. I know theyre all wrong about your being apathetic or, or whatever shits making the rounds now. But the stoicism, the detachment? Thats even worse than not caring." "Just gotta throw it on me like that, huh?" "Oh yeah. Either that or fire you--but youre still making money and, really? I only give a fuck that you lost because it might be fucking with your head, and I dont want to see you shook." "You realize my ass has lost three title shots now, right?" "Yeah. But you did it in your first couple years, and look at me. I got a couple in my whole fuckin career and didnt win a single world belt and I am, currently, richer than God. If I can do that, then you can fucking well turn yourself around. Got me?" "I think." "Good. Now. Do that. Get your shit together. Call me then--youre a grown man. Im not going to give you support unless I think youre about to hit the fucking bottom." That was that. Route amended, Frank followed mathematic. Angles. Curves. Inclines. Movement. Sound. Fury. Proper significance. Freaks. Miscreants. Fuck ups. Headlights to the world. He parked. She let him inside. Warm. Orange or gold without the sheen, or. Warm. Didn't matter. "Whoa, dude. You got fucked up." He dragged a smile up, fleeting. "Yeah, yeah. Been hearin that a lot lately." "Well, yeah. You look like" "Farm eqipment decided it had a vendetta against me." "Haha. Yes. Something like that. So. Whats up?" "Bout to chill with my old friend Zoe. Parently." To the couch, to the corner of it. She followed with: "well, I was waiting on you. Now Im no longer waiting on you." "Hey, things already lookin up." "Heh. You eaten?" "Lately? Nah. Not that hungry, though." "Bastard. Im gonna fix myself something, then. Remotes on the table." It was. He turned the television on, flipped idle and considered her the same way he considered most things: two persuasive arguments that nullified one another and left him inert. They weren't dressed like friends, and he was leading her on. The other side was: why not? Was: always need a backup. Was: fuck it--play dumb. The channels scrolled their products, lubricated, glistening in high contrast lighting; everything for sale, he nodded to no one and hunted down a show that had almost slipped his mind. He was late coming in, some effect of its liqid scheduling or his inability to catch it live or some other option unconsidered in that moment, but found himself catching up like it was a genetic inclination. Deciphering meaningless images of crazed idiots with titles against their chests. "New(final syllable dragged out fractal. swirling around within itself and against itself. wholly selfabsorbed.) GCW world heavyweight champion" Frank smirked, mainly to himself. He thought about the games in that idiot world.
