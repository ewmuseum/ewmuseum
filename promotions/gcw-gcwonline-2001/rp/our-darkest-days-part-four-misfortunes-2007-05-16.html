Our Darkest Days, Part Four: Misfortunes

16 May 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 16th May '07 May 2007 9:15am To say that the past twenty-four hours had been chaotic for Andrew Hugh Murray would be a gross understatement. He’d woken up yesterday with every intention of spending the majority of the day working; he needed desperately to get back into shape if he wanted to make a return to in-ring competition anytime soon and he’d been slacking off recently. With any luck he thought he might be able to jump right back into the business he loved after a couple of month’s hard graft, provided there was a promotion out there willing to take a chance on an injury-prone twenty-nine year old. What started off as a peaceful enough day soon exploded as Andy and his brother Cayle become involved in a heated argument. Cayle was suspicious at the recent actions of Andy’s with Jane, who had been leaving the house at times inconsistent with her work schedule; leaving early and coming home late. He suggested that Andy should put his mind at ease by following her one morning, or by taking one step further; hiring a private investigator. At first Andy loathed this idea? you would too if somebody suggested you hire someone to spy on your own wife. So he and Cayle had a verbal spat; to cut a long story short, Cayle ended up storming out of the house in protest. As the day grew on Andy had time to ponder the disagreement though, and slowly his resistance towards the idea began to fade. Eventually he ended up picking up the phone and calling the number of a local private investigator; he was pretty certain that his wife wasn’t doing anything suspicious but he had to make sure. However, no sooner had Andy slipped his cell back into his pocket than it began ringing again. It was the Tallahassee Police Department; Cayle was in trouble. For the second time in his relatively-young life he had assaulted someone whilst under the influence of alcohol. Andy was furious; his brother had done time in jail for the exact same crime just a few years after he had joined his elder sibling in America, and he’d done it again. “Stupid fucking idiot,” was the initial reaction of The Scottish King of Cool. Cayle wasn’t the sharpest knife in the drawer by a long-shot, but he wasn’t exactly completely braindead either. Andy couldn’t believe the stupidity of his brother, “how could he make the same mistake twice?” Anger was not the only feeling that was lingering in Andy Murray’s head that night though. The Scottish King of Cool was over-wrought with guilt; he knew that if he and Cayle hadn’t argued in the first place then his brother probably wouldn’t have gone out and broken the law again. He felt terrible, especially considering that he had ended up calling the private investigator in the end. If it weren’t for Andy’s resistance yesterday morning Cayle would be sitting at home right now, not in a holding cell at the Tallahassee Police Department headquarters on East Seventh Avenue. The officer he’d spoken to on the phone ? “Vires” Andy thought he said his name was ? was a good-natured man who’d spoken to Murray with all the respect in the world, but Andy bit his head off. He took out his frustrations for his brother on the officer, and that was another thing that was weighing on his mind. As soon as he put the phone down he felt like shit for cursing a man who was merely doing his job. Hopefully Andy would get the chance to apologise to him later, in-person, when he visited his brother in-between interrogations. He didn’t have a clue what he was going to say to Cayle but one thing was for sure; it wasn’t going to be a pleasurable experience for either party. For now, though, Cayle was not Andy’s immediate priority. He’d arranged to meet the private investigator in a nondescript twenty-four hour diner just outside Tallahassee City Centre at nine-thirty in the morning. Even though he wasn’t exactly in the right mindset for such a meeting he’d feel like a bit of a dickhead if he called it off, given that he’d only just arranged it last night. Nope, this needed to be done, and it needed to be done now. At least when the investigator proved that Jane was innocent of any wrongdoings it would be one small weight off Andy’s troubled mind. With him he’d taken a folder full of information on his wife; a couple of photos, her work address, home address, car registration number, pretty much anything that you’d need to obtain if you wanted to track somebody down. Everything that the PI had asked him to take. The documents and photographs were stuffed crudely into the pocket of his green camouflage jacket which he wore on top of a black hooded sweatshirt, the hood of which was up despite the fact that Andy was inside. He looked terrible having hardly slept the night before signified by the bags under his eyes, and the stubble on his chin illustrated that he clearly hadn’t had a shave this morning either. The cheap espresso he’d ordered wasn’t doing much to wake him up either. A movement at the opposite end of the diner caught Andy’s attention as a tall, well-built figure slid in through the door. The man was clad in a long, black coat and had a black fedora perched on his cranium; just like an old-school Sherlock Holmes-style detective. Andy instantly knew this was the guy he’d been waiting for as the figure glanced around the diner looking for the Scottish King of Cool. Murray gently pulled his hood back so that the PI might see him. Noticing the 6’7? Scotsman sitting alone at one of the tables, the man knew exactly that he was the man he was here to see; perhaps he recognised him from television, or maybe his uncharacteristically downtrodden facial expression gave it all away. Slowly he approached the table, removing the hat from his head. “Mr. Murray?” he enquired in a soft, articulate voice. Andy attempted to speak but no words came out. He responded only with a nod and quickly cleared his throat. Observing the PI as he sat down on the opposite seat Andy could see that he was a man of about forty-to-fifty years of age. His cheeks were sunken and his face weathered which gave Andy the impression that the man had lived a tough life. What was left of his greying hair was combed backwards and slicked with gel, and on his top lip sat an immaculately tidy moustache. “Wesley Easterbrock, glad to meet you,” said the private investigator maintaining the same neutral facial expression that he had walked in with. He extended a hand towards Andy which was reluctantly shaken by the Scottish King of Cool. “I know the setting isn’t exactly glamorous, and I’m sorry for dragging you to such a cesspit, but most of my clients prefer a meeting in a public place to one in their own homes, you know?” “Gotcha,” retorted Andy. He wasn’t quite sure why Easterbrock was telling him this given that Andy had made the decision to meet in the diner when they had spoken on the phone. “He’s probably just making small talk,” Andy figured. “Well let’s get down to business, Mr. Murray. I’m a busy man as I’m sure you are too?” Wesley began, “did you bring the things I asked you to?” With a nod Andy reached into the pocket of his jacket and pulled out a dog-eared envelope which he slid across the table. Easterbrock carefully opened it up and pulled out the contents, shuffling through them. As he examined the photographs Andy took one final swig of coffee; the stuff tasted vile, but Murray needed the caffeine bomb. “So this is Jane” Nice looking woman you have there Mr. Murray, if you don’t mind me saying,” Easterbrock pronounced. Andy glared back at him, lacking the energy to verbally challenge what he felt was an inappropriate remark. “So we’ve got photographs, addresses, plate numbers” perfect, this’ll work just fine. I trust you are familiar with the payment arrangements we talked about over the phone, yes?” “Absolutely,” Andy mumbled as he felt the effects of the caffeine reaching his brain at long last. “The first cheque is on its way.” Easterbrock nodded as he took another look through the documents, picking out a piece of paper that Jane’s work address was scrawled on. “So she should be here right now, yeah?” “Definitely, no question. She’s got a lunch break at midday.” “Excellent, I’ll get started there then,” the private investigator announced. So what I’ll do is get in contact with you twice a day with progress reports; one at 9am and one at 9pm, every twelve hours. I may get in contact with you outside those times if I’ve got anything important to report, but otherwise I suggest you prepare yourself for a phone call at those times; head somewhere you wont be heard by others if you want to keep this hush.” Again Andy nodded, even though his brain wasn’t really taking in everything that the PI was saying to him. “S’easy enough, you must be a pretty wealthy man on the money I’m paying you,” said Andy, not meaning to sound as condescending as he did. Fortunately Wesley wasn’t too offended; “Well compared to some of my colleagues I think I offer a pretty decent rate,” he chuckled. “Besides, how do you put a price on a clear, suspicion-free mind?” Deep down Murray knew this was all marketing garbage, but in his distracted state of mind he was buying into it. “So cases like this, how do they usually end up” You get a lot of fucked-up, paranoid husbands wanting to spy on their wives?” “Mostly, yeah,” stated Easterbrock. “Husbands hire us, wives hire us; the most common result is that the person being investigated is indeed innocent of doing anything they shouldn’t, but you can never be too sure.” Easterbrock could see in Andy’s eyes that he was in two minds about hiring him to investigate his own wife. “Look, I understand where your head is at, it’s a big step to take,” he said, trying his best to make it sound like he understood. “You’ve made the right decision though. Trust me, I’ll get this sorted out, and hopefully soon you wont be losing any sleep over this.” The Scottish King of Cool didn’t want to hear this. “Just get the job done,” he muttered. Feeling a little dejected Easterbrock replied; “Okay, I get it. I’ll speak to you at nine, take it easy.” Easterbrock stood up from the chair and put the hat back on his head. He nodded at Andy who glared distastefully at him as he left the diner. Murray knew Easterbrock was a bit of a scumbag; he spied on people for a living after all, and it pained him that he had to do business with such a man, but he simply had to find out what Jane was up to. Was she cheating on him? Surely not, they’d only just got re-married. That was a ridiculous proposition that Andy simply could not comprehend. Or perhaps it was one he didn’t want to comprehend. ---- 11:00am The meeting with the PI was as awkward and unpleasant as Andy thought it would have been, but his day was about to spiral even further towards the abyss. After passing through the mandatory metal-detector and other standard security procedures, Andy was escorted into the main hall of the Tallahassee Police Department HQ by officer Jerrold Vires. The very same man who had both arrested Cayle on the previous night and called Andy about it. The very same man that Andy had barked down the phone at. The Scotsman apologised to Vires almost as soon as he met him outside the station and that warmed his soul up by a fraction. While it was a very small concern to him given the situations involving his brother and wife, it was still something that had been lingering in the back of his head and it felt great to rectify the situation. Andy had heard from friends that the station was a converted church and judging by its large, open expanse this seemed like a logical theory. A hive activity, the office was almost entirely open plan with numerous detectives? desks occupied the lower level, almost all of which were presently occupied. A set of stairs climbed up towards a mezzanine level, most of which was taken up by the Captain’s office which looked out across the entire lower level, presumably so that the chief could keep a close eye on his subordinate. Murray wanted to take a little more time to take his surroundings in but he was soon ushered towards the staircase by Vires. “C’mon Murray, this way,” the detective warmly stated in a Southern accent that Andy found more than a little bit annoying. H He followed Vires up the staircase, not uttering a word to him as the two of them eventually reached the top. The detective immediately grabbed the handle of a door marked “INTERROGATION ROOM 2” which was directly across from the stairs and twisted it. “In you go, big guy,” he said as Andy took a deep breath in before taking a step and crossing the threshold. Immediately Cayle caught Andy’s attention; still wearing last night’s clothes he looked ? and stank ? like hell incarnate. His facial expression was weary, his shoulder-length brown locks greasy and his head was hung with shame as he stared directly at the table at which he was seated. The previous evening’s interrogations had taken its toll on him; he was a man defeated. “Hey bro,” he monotonously mumbled without glancing at Andy who felt the anger and guilt collide head-on inside him. “How could you be so fucking dumb?” were the first words that came out of Andy’s mouth as he sat down opposite his younger brother. It wasn’t exactly the welcome that Cayle had hoped for. “I know, I know,” he responded dejectedly. “I’m sorry Andy, what can I say?” “You know you’re going to go away again for this, right?” barked Andy. “You fucking retard” I take you in, feed you, put a roof over my head? hell, I even tried to get you a break in the business,” Andy continued, referring to the duo’s ill-fated stint in FUSE. “And you repay me by going out, getting fucked-up and smacking the crap out of some guy again?” Cayle didn’t need to speak; his facial expression said it all. He looked up at his fuming brother; Andy could see that he was sorry, but what he’d done was inexcusable. After everything that Andy had done for Cayle he had let him down again and while Andy did feel some guilt earlier on for what had happened that vanished as soon as he saw his brother sitting there. “What’s wrong with you man, didn’t you learn your lesson the first time”! Do you fucking like prison or something? Jesus Christ?” Stressed, Andy ran his hand through his hair. Cayle opened his mouth to speak but before he could say anything coherent Andy shouted “SHUT UP!”, slammed his fist against the table, and stood up. “I just came here to tell you, don’t expect any more free rides from me, once you’re out you’re fending for yourself, you hear me” I’m done with this; I don’t need my little brother running around like some little rockstar, causing trouble and bringing shame upon our family name,” spat Andy as he felt his face growing red with rage. It wasn’t a feeling that Andy was accustomed to but he was so overcome by his brother’s stupidity that he couldn’t help it. “We’re fucking done, you and I?” That was enough; he’d barely been in the room for a couple of minutes but already Andy was sick of the sight of his brother. He stormed out, back into the sight of Jerrold Vires who hadn’t expected Andy to leave so soon. He’d always been so accommodating towards his brother; never before had Andy been forced into taking such an aggressive stance, but desperate times call for desperate measures. He had been walked all over by Cayle for too long, and Cayle needed to know that. He knew fine that in time Cayle and he would rebuild their bridges, but for now he wanted nothing to do with the runt of the litter, the Murray family tearaway. ---- 9:10pm Andy felt like shit. He’d just gotten off the phone with Wesley Easterbrock, as promised, but the conversation was particularly fruitful. Easterbrock only reported to Andy what he had already known; Jane had left the office briefly to go to a coffee shop on her lunch break as she did every day, but that was about it. She’d returned home at her regular hour; no suspicious activities today. The thoughts dominating his mind at the moment were of his brother. His downfall had really struck a nerve with Andy and his usually cool, calm and composed demeanour had been shattered. Jane had noticed it over dinner and had offered whatever forced level of sympathy she could muster. She despised the younger Murray brother, she always had done, and deep inside she wanted to say “I told you so,” to Andy. She knew something like this was going to happen again, but she knew that this was neither the time nor the place to say so. Murray headed to bed early that night, physically and mentally drained. He’d wake up in the morning and try to flush these negative thoughts from his mind by returning to his injury recuperation program, after speaking with Mr. Easterbrock on the phone of course. Whether or not he would be able to distance himself from thoughts of his brother and wife was another matter all-together. Andy Murray couldn’t remember a single point in his life where he had felt as low as he did right now.
