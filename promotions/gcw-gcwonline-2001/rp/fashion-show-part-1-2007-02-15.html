Fashion Show Part 1

15 February 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 15.02.07 The air outside is cold. There’s a distinct but subtle breeze drifting through the air. I could smell the sea from where I am. It smells wonderful. I loved being at sea when I was younger. It was the greatest thing ever. The buzz I got every time I boarded the little yacht my father owned. We used to sail around the yacht basin most of the time, but every summer we would sail to another town or city on the east coast of England. I grew up in a small fishing town in the North East of England. In a town called Hartlepool. It was a quiet town back in those days. Now I hear its got quite an atmosphere to it. A carnival feel in the summertime. I need to go there soon. See for myself how much the place has actually changed. But tonight I need to focus my attention solely on the major event my model wife and I are to attend at the Hilton Miami Airport hotel. We’ve been invited to a very high profile charity fashion show in the hotel. I hate going, but if I don’t, I get an earful from her about how ungrateful I am. We arrive late, as usual. My wife's fault. As always. She spends hours putting her face on and then decides she doesn't look her best so its back to the drawing board. Where as I spend most of the day eating and watching soccer and then at the last minute I get ready. Great being a bloke aint it? Outside the hotel there are photographers and news reporters each trying to get themselves a story for tomorrows paper. My wife thrives on the opportunity to be seen and be heard, whereas I like to keep myself to myself at these sorts of events. Personal preference. But as usual she ignores that and drags me to the photographers, and she poses, they take pictures and she poses some more. Its tedious business, but finally she's done. I let out a sigh of relief as we head inside. The place is jam packed with people. I head over towards the bar before my wife can drag me somewhere I don’t want to be. I push through a small crowd of people and finally get to the bar. I ask the barman for a Double JD and Coke. The perfect drink. As he’s making my drink I turn my head and look around the room. I don’t recognize a single person. I see my wife talking to a bunch of yippity-yays. I turn back to the bar and pick up my drink. I pay the barman. I can feel a vibration in my pocket.
