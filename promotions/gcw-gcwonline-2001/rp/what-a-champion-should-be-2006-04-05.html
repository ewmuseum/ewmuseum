What A Champion Should Be

5 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/05/06 Being the GCW Television Champion represents everything that a true champion should be - forever putting the title on the line against any and all competitors on a regular basis, competing week in and week out just for the right to be the champion. You don’t find that in the world of wrestling too often these days, which is precisely why it has to be actively enforced by rules and regulations. Times change. Moods change. The world does not breathe life into heroes anymore, just people who do the right thing because it benefits them the most by doing it. The wrestling industry is just the same, how could it not be? There is no honour anymore, just clowns vying for the audiences attention or shallow champions who truly believe that being the champion means they’re untouchable, they think they can do what they want when they want to do it. Defend their title when and where they want to. That’s the way things are, they way things should be doesn’t really enter into it. Everybody has a different viewpoints on the age-old issue of right and wrong. Some people are closer to the mark than others, but it doesn’t mean that they themselves are right or wrong. It’s an enigma our generation, or even the next, won’t solve. When the officials told me that I’d have to defend the GCW Television title two weeks after winning the damn thing (and only in my third match in Global Championship Wrestling), against fellow future GCW superstar Tyler Xing, I think they expected me to contest the decision like so many false champions have done before me. When I told them I was more than willing to fight with everything on the line, I think they were inextricably dumfounded. I tried not to show them that their faux-pas was written all over their faces as clear a beautiful summer’s night sky, but in the end it was irrelevant. It was going to happen whether I wanted it to happen or not, after that it was just the issue of why I was so willing to “bow down to their regulations.” Their words, not mine, but that was kind of obvious wasn’t it? I didn’t even try to explain my reasoning to them. They wouldn’t understand. Hell, I didn’t really understand it until a couple of months ago. I came back to the wrestling industry, after the tragedy, renewed. A new man, a new outlook on things, focused and nothing but determined. It’s about the competition, putting the title on the line every week and battling with all that I am to defend it. With each and every title defence I gain that little bit more experience, I gain that little more skill, I gain that little bit more determination to keep the title in possession. In essence, it’ll improve me as a wrestler, it’ll improve me as a warrior, hell, it’ll improve me as man.
