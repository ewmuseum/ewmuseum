Orchestra of Wolves, Part 1: My Name is Casanova

Date: 24th February "If chance will have me king, why, chance may crown me." - Macbeth, William Shakespeare. I It's a house. A shell. Stripped bare of furniture, personal effects and anything else that would help the barren building meet the definition of "home," it's deserted. As desolate as the Sahara and as lifeless and grim as the Mary Celeste, the once quaint about had had its bones picked and all that remained was the skeleton of a family's former home. Mother and child; they'd spent many months here, through thick and thin, good times and bad. They'd holed up when things went sour on the marriage front. It was tough for her at first, being torn violently from the life of luxury she'd become accustomed to by his side, but she had no choice but to adapt. He was the bread-winner, her partner; and without a share of his accumulated wealth the adjustment from celebrity spouse to nine-to-five single mom was torrid and rough. She made it through, though. She survived. The neighbourhood was a good one; just a mile from her workplace and on the bus route to the boy's school, but it offered more than mere convenience. The neighbours; good people, all of them. A pleasant blend of retired couples winding the final years down in the sunshine and young families raising their kids away from the hustle and bustle of the city. Typical Tallahassee suburb. They helped, they made her feel welcome, part of a community, and soon enough she found herself back in the saddle and in control of her life. It was never the same, though. Not safter the divorce. When he left her it was as if he'd taken a little piece of her with him, because things were never quite right again. There were other men afterwards, sure; but none of them made her feel the way that he did. Long and hard she searched the plug the leak, and a string of failed relationships brought her to the doorstep of his greatest foe. Like just about everything else that had happened in her personal life since 2006, her relationship with the man they call "The Ratings Guru" imploded publicly on Global Championship Wrestling's "Warpath" pay-per-view with the failure of the vile smear campaign that was supposed to demolish her ex's public image and eliminate his chances of overcoming the Jersey native. She'd been played, manipulated; all too eager to seek retribution against the father of her child, she found out first-hand why the call Bagwell the most vile individual in professional wrestling today. Kicked to the curb she slinked back to the house. Like the polar ice caps she deteriorated; her life slowly falling away bit-by-bit, day-by-day. Without a job, without anything to fall back on, she fell into a dark cycle. With the threat of a legal battle she couldn't afford and financial ruin looming like a grave spirit, it took a failed suicide attempt to shudder her life back in motion. He dropped the case; couldn't bring himself to take what little remained of his former spouse's dignity. As much as he wanted to get back at her for the damage that the Bagwell affair had done to his career there was no way a man of his morality could effectively kick the mother of his child and his only son out on the street like a couple of dogs. And they talked, reconciled almost. They began to get along, despite all that happened. It was starting to feel more than a little like the good old days. He hadn't planned it at first, what happened, and it didn't help ease an already troubled conscience, but it had to be done. He needed to flush out the poison; he had to kill vultures that had hung over his personal life for too long for his own sake. So her let her get close, led her on. Got to the point where she actually believed that there was a change of reigniting the flame and that he would leave his life in Florida behind and run away to Dallas with her. But as soon as she walked on the red carpet again it was harshly yanked from beneath her feet. Like Chris Bagwell had done all those months ago, she'd been played by her former husband. He bought the house he'd convinced her to put on the market and sent her packing to Dallas without second thought. He may go to hell for it, but he could share a city with a woman who could only bring him strife for no longer. Cocking his head back against the back door, Andy dug his hands deep into the pockets of his grey cargos. Puffing his cheeks, exhaling, he gazed wearily around an appliance-less kitchen of empty cupboards and dusty worktops. The room was small; the size of a gas chamber, almost. Cheap mahogany panels and a white linolem flooring, illuminated by beams of light peeking through the dirty windows behind The King's head. He'd thought of pawning the house down to his youngest sibling but there were too many memories lingering in the desolate building's nooks and crannies. No, he couldnt do that. As much as he wanted to provide for his brothers, he knew he'd never be able to step through this house's door again without Jane's visage slipping through. He wanted to flush her from his life every way possible, and this was one of them. Shifting his weight forward and pushing his giant frame away from the door, Andy took his last long, slow plod through the house. He moved through to the corridor, passed the bathroom, passed his son's old bedroom, and without so much as a second glance he stepped through the threshold and into the Floridian sunshine. Pulling a pair of mirrored Aviators from the breast pocket of his lumberjack shirt, Andy allowed his eyes time to adjust to light that was bright enough to hinder his vision even with shades. When he regained his focus he saw a neighbourhood of well spaced-out bungalows, some with well-maintained gardens and lawns, some with added garages, some with both. Sporadic trees dotted the sidewalks, the canopy providing shade to those on the sidewalk and days as warm as this. He cast his eyes passed Mr. Johnson's old white Lincoln and the Merrywether's cute baby-blue house, along the street to where Old Man Harris waddled down his drive to collect the mail and a couple of kids bounced a ball across the street at one another and then finally to the mechanical beasts that ominously hovered on the sidewalk in front of Jane's house - his house. "Sure 'bout this?" A man approached. Short and stumpy with threads of dirty blonde hair sticking out from under his hard hat, he spoke in a soft southern drawl that was only just audible over the grunt and churn of the vehicles behind him. His weathered, oily features and badly-trimmed moustache gave Andy a curious look. "'Cause if you wanna change your mind, know's the last chance you gon' get." Andy nodded. "Do it". He didn't hesitate. "Shame. Not that bad a place," said the moustached contractor in the white hard hat and blue overalls. He turned and walked back towards the machines giving a thumbs up motion as Andy took leave from the porch, drifting towards the sidewalk. Murray gave all both of the beasts a good look; the bulldozer and the dump truck. Both painted in the company's green, they spat, hissed and snarled as if they were creatures of the flesh ready to rip and tear at the house. The man jumped into the cab of the bulldozer and slammed the door shut behind him. With the ease of a man who'd done this a thousand times before he shuddered the monster into gear and slowly ploughed the mechnical carnivore towards its victim. II Elsewhere the future Mrs. Murray slipped the last corner of a granola bar between her lips and raised her ring finger to the sunlight. The diamonds sparkled in the sun's glow as she twisted her wrist, taking in the splendid jewel with awe and delight. Kayla had had the thing on her hand for twenty one days and still the warmth that ever glance gave her wasn't beginning to wane. Tanned skin, deep hazel eyes and brown locks so dark they were almost black. A charming, infectious personality and the physique of a swimsuit model, a lazier narrator may have dropped the term "beautiful" here. The girl sat at the breakfast bar which separated the kitchenette from the lounge area of the great room. Which a house, what a fantastic place to live. Light ash cabinets and black marble surfaces provided a rustic contrast to the modernity of the appliances and the excessive size of the refridgerator. A giant window overlooking the sizeable garden beyond the confines of the house's walls flooded this area of the room with natural rays and provided a spectacular showcase for the rich greenery beyond. Behind her the lounge was equally grand in its decorated; with soft, cream carpets, the most expensive dining table and chairs in the city and an insanely comfortable leather suite. The signed posters of a myriad of cheesy '80's hair metal bands was something she could live without, but other than that Kayla found it hard to fault the home that made Tony Soprano's crib look like an African mud hut. Her childhood as a poor, midwestern farmer's daughter seemed like a world away. Finishing off her glass of Tropicana, Kayla slid from the stool and took the dirty glass over to the sink. It was then that a familiarly gruff Scottish voice called to her. "G'd mornin', sis." She turned as the stocky elder brother of Andy - Sidney - wiped the sleep from his eyes and paced towards her. His long, straggly hair tied back into a messy, unekmpt ponytail and his thick beard in bad need of a trim, the thirty four year old was hardly the most presentable man in the world, especially at this time of day. He moved towards the fridge and pulled the door open. "Hey, I ain't your in-law just yet." "Not gonna be long now though, eh?" Sid responded without visual contact as he foraged around inside the the refridgerator like a scavenger. "Damnit, don't you guys have anythin' that's nae 'low-fat' or 'high protein'?" "Ha, good luck with that," Kayla said as she abandoned the glass beside the sink. "You know what Andy's like with his diet these days..." "Right. The superstar can't be anything less than 110%, right?" Sid cocked his head and smiled. "Fuckin' guy's like a machine, pardon my French." Settling for a chilled bowl of the previous night's chilli, Sid pulled the polythene back and closed the fridge door. "He's doing well though, huh?" "Sure is, Kay. Sure is," he mumbled, retrieving a fork from the drawer. Without care he jammed the fork into the bowl and churned the rich and beef together. He shovelled a mouthful of the mixture into his mouth, taking a second to relish the glory of second day chilli con carne. "I mean, he's held titles before, he's been in big matches... but what he did against Terror? Damn. That was almost superhuman." "Oh God, don't remind me," she said, her tone almost dismissive. The process of watching her fiancee getting his living daylights knocked out of him for fifteen minutes of a thirty minute wrestling match was never a pleasant experience, especially in a contest as brutal as the Murray/Terror NC-17 clash. "You want me to warm that up for you?" "Nah, it's good," Sid said, taking a seat at the breakfast bar. "You worry about him?" "Sometimes. A little, I mean. It's hard, knowing that there are guys like Jay Terror and Chris Bagwell out there who deliberately want to make someone you love bleed." The oldest of the Murray brothers nodded, swallowing back another mouthful of chilli. "Know what you mean, but the physical pain's nothin'. You get used to it, so don't worry yerself too much. Half of this sport's about learning how to take a beating, and Andy... well, Andy can take a beating. You could drive into that guy with a 4x4 and he'd still hop up and pin your ass." Kayla folded her arms under her chest and leaned back against the worktop. Sid was right; she knew that physical pain was nought but a minor hinderance to the average professional wrestler, but that didn't make it any easier for her to watch. "I know, I know. Guess at the end of it, though, you reap the reward..." "Something like that." Sid was hesitant, distracted almost. "Oh. You don't agree?" "Well, no. It's not that I don't agree..." he tailed off. "It's just... you've gotta be careful in this business. Get me?" "I'm not sure I do." Her features contorted; she was confused. "Alright, look," he began, abandoning his primal hunger for a moment. "I think Andy's fucking great. I think he can be the biggest wrestler in the world if he wants to, but you don't get to where he is without leaving a trail of enemies in your wake. He ain't quite there yet, but when you reach the top, anyone you've ever crossed paths with in the past is going to want to take a pop at you and then some. He's gotta keep the eyes on the back of his head open, is all." She nodded. She understood. "I mean, take a guy like Thom LeStrange," Sid continued. "Here's a cat who entered GCW at the same time as Andy but hasn't had anything like the degree of success that our mutual relation has. They never finished the shit they had with each other last year. How d'you think a guy like that's going to react when he sees Andy's name at the top of the card every week?" Another nod. "I get it." She paused for a moment, contemplating. "What about guys like Bagwell? LeStatt Knight?" "Bagwell's a punk, a lowlife piece of trash. Andy's got his finger wrapped around that one, but LK?" he shook his head, "shit, I don't think I've ever seen Andy as distant as he was when he was throwing down with that guy. He's a dangerous man, that one. You gotta look after baby brother for me if those two end up facing each other again, 'cause it ain't gonna be pretty." "What are the chances of that happening, you think?" "I'd say it's pretty much a certainty. I mean, LeStatt Knight is the champion, Andy is the hottest thing in pro-wrestling right now. Eventually, they're gonna meet." Taking a break from the speech, Sid scooped up another forkload of cold chilli and put it in his mouth. Kayla, meanwhile, moved away from the worktop, considering her future brother-in-law's words. "You got a role to play in this, Kay. You know that?" "I think so," she smiled. "Baby brother's strong, he's a rock; but his will's not as iron as he thinks it is sometimes. It's part 'a your job to make sure he doesn't crack," he pointed the fork at her. "And make sure of that I will." III "Okay, listen here you rusty cuntbucket; I'm incredibly fucking busy at the moment, so why don't you go play with a microscope, a pair of tweezers and your pecker for a couple of hours and I'll phone you back when I've got nothing better to do than talk with a no-hope fucktard whose last job of note was in the fucking MAILROOM over at PRIME HQ, okay?" It was a Tuesday afternoon and Rueben Dallas was stressed. Rubbing his temples with his free hand as he barked down the telephone, the superagent was completely oblivious to the fact that just about everyone on the floor had abandoned their own duties and were firmly fixated on his over-the-top outburst. The joys of having a glass-walled office. "No. No. Fucking NO!" The bald African-American stood up from his chair, tightening his grip on the telephone receiver. "Oh, you don't like that? Fuck off and die then, dickhead." Violently he slammed the telephone down and lashed out, kicking his desk. "FUCK!" From the waiting area outside the office Andy Murray looked on with a mixture of amusement and bewilderment. Chuckling to himself, he tossed whatever coffee table magazine he'd picked up to the side and looked up his agent's Asian assisstant. "He seems like he's in a good mood!" "Tell me about it," the assistant chirped, removing her attention from her keyboard for just a second. "Don't mention it to him, though! It's about the only entertainment we get in this place..." Andy parted his lips to respond, but before he could do so Rueben threw open the office door. His mood switch was immedate; his anger and fury replaced with a warm, welcoming smile and a wink. "Look at this, if it isn't the hottest professional wrestler in the world, baby!" he said, completely unaware of the fact that his vitriolic outburst had just been witnissed by over fifty employees. "How're you doing, Andy?" "Fucking terrible, mate," Andy shook his agents hand as he lead him into the office. "Fame, fortune, having the chance to slap Chris Bagwell in the face whenever I want. It's a hard life, man." "Ahhh the trials and tribulations of the international superstar, huh?" Rueben smiled, sharing the joke as he slumped down into the leather chair behind his surprisingly cheap IKEA desk. "Fuckin'-A, man. It's a nightmare," Andy responded, trying not to get too comfortable on the red sofa laid out at the back of the moderately sized workplace. "So what have you got for me, Ruebs? I don't have a lotta time today." "Oh you know, the usual post-PPV base-touching goodness! We've got a massive cycle coming up for you, Andy. Bigger even than the last, and we gotta make sure you're in the right place." "When am I not in the right place, Ruebs?" Andy shrugged, feigning naivety. "Well, yeah," retorted Rueben. "Of course. But really, Andy. You're THIS fucking close at the moment," he raised his hand, pushing his thumb and first finger close together without making them touch. "I know you hear stuff like this all the time, but Andy Murray is un-fucking-stoppable at the moment. Take a look at the Main Event; Valentine? Beaten her. Terror? Beaten him. Bagwell? Made him scream like a bitch. Just about anyone who's anyone in GCW has fallen at your hand, Andy. You're hot shit." Andy leaned back in the chair, curious as to where the conversation was going. "Ha, well. I aim to please." "Look, I'll be straight. I think you'll be GCW World Champion in less than six months." Two black, Scottish eyebrows raised. "Six months? I dunno. Get the impression that Caldera's not exactly my number one fan right now, seeing as I just took out his boy and all." "Fuck that, Caldera's a businessman," Dallas waved away Andy's point. "He knows what's good for the company and he knows where the money us. And you know what I think's going on in his head right about now?" He hesistated. "LK/Murray. Round 2." That really wasn't what Andy was expecting, and his morale took an immediate hit. Sighing, his facial features sagged. "Oh." "Hey, look. I know your tangles with that guy haven't exactly been a walk in the park in the past, but how else are you gonna hit the top, Andy?" "Yeah, yeah. I know." Andy's head dropped and he stared at the floor for longer than he noticed. He hadn't forgotten his clashes with LeStatt Knight and they still hung heavy on his mind. The biggest wrestlers in GCW history had left Andy Murray rattled worse than he'd ever been rattled before, and it wasn't a process that Andy was too keen to repeat any time soon. "I dunno if I'm ready for that. Maybe I should leave the gold-chasing to Viv or something right now." Immediately Rueben rose from his chair. "Valentine? Nah, man. She's had her time around the gold, it's time for some fresh blood up there." "I dunno..." "All right, look," Rueben walked across the room and sat down at the opposite end of the sofa. Hunched, he looked at Andy. "I know LeStatt fucked up you head before, but really, what else are you gonna do this month? I mean, you could beat Chris Bagwell for the fortieth time, but where would the fun in that be, huh? And besides, what would prove the critics wrong like walking up to the most dominant force that the company's ever seen and slapping him around like a prissy little bitch?" Murray sighed again. He knew that what his agent was saying made sense, he just wasn't sure if he wanted to take that jump yet. "Still-" Rueben interrupted. "I'm not going to lie to you; if you don't step up to the plate this month, then somebody else will. How would you like it if someone else took a shot at the World Champion this month because you were too busy dithering around in the background? You might not be in a position like this again for a long time, Andy. You gotta grab the bull by the horns and fuck him in the ass." Despite his rather ineloquent delivery, the words were true. Andy looked to respond again, but this time he was interrupted by the incessant ringing of his agent's cellphone. Rueben retrieved the buzzing plastic box from his pocket and picked up the call. "What the fuck is it? Oh. Okay. Put her through..." Apologising to his top client with a shrug of the shoulders, Rueben waited for the call to be put through to him as Andy Murray rose from his chair and shook his agent's hand again. "I'll drop by later in the week. We'll talk some more." "Yes. Yes we will," Rueben smiled as The Scottish King of Cool walked out through the office door. Through glass walls he watched him slink off towards the elevator at the other side of the floor as quiet click confirmed the call had been transferred. "Well how's my favourite Hollywood whore doing today?!" IV Andy Murray cocked his head back, sipping on a glass of cool, refreshing ros? wine. Leaning against the guardrail around the rooftop terrace on his house and with one arm wrapped around the waist of his fiancee, he was at peace. Enjoying the coolness of the late afternoon, the couple look out across the cityscape beyond them. Tallahassee wasn't exactly a city known for an aubandance of skyscrapers and an over-developed city centre, but the house was at least a couple of hundred feet higher than the city itself and it made for quite the view. Rows and rows of green foiliage separated the suburbs from the city, and the mostly white buildings of the centre almost glowed in the dim hues of dusk. A soft, gentle breeze carressed the back of Andy's neck as he breathed-in. He turned his head and his icey blues met with her warm hazels. They kissed. "I'd marry you tomorrow, you know that? I really would." "I know." She smiled. It was all she needed to do. "Speaking of which, we should really get round to setting a date." Kayla paused. "Yeah, well. We should eventually, but, I mean, there's no need to rush it. We're still young, Andy. We've got the rest of our lives." He look into her eyes and felt like the happiest man on the planet. Pulling her closer, Andy looked out from the balcony, admiring the view again. "This view is something else, eh? One of the reasons I first fell in love with this place." "It's... stunning." And his mind began to shuffle. He thought about getting married, about beating Jay Terror. About the GCW World Championship. He couldn't help the grin as it crept across his boyish features. "Someday, m'lady; this will all belong to me." And Andy Murray felt that nothing on Earth could ever be sweeter.
