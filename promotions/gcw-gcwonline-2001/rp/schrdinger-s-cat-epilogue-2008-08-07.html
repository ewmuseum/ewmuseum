Schr’dinger's Cat: Epilogue

7 August 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 7, 2008 He had visited the spot one other time. There was something about the nonwashable stain of blood that still tainted the spot where Burton had fallen he couldn’t get around to understanding. It was as if that exact spot would never have any life as the poison coursed through the veins of Mother Nature. The next place he visited was the cemetery where he had been buried. It was hard watching his own name followed by the dates that documented his birth and his death. It didn’t help knowing the circumstances of his death. He touched the coarse surface of the hot tombstone that bore his name and relaxed for the first time in what seemed like years. Antonio’s trip around the place he had called home for the last few weeks ended where it began. The familiar emptiness and hollowness of the underground subway station permeated the air. The air inside was different than the almost choking dry breezes he had experienced on the outside. “I’m sorry about everything that happened. We really did start with good intentions when we created our little group. It was everyone for themselves and we thought it would be a better way to get a hold of supplies so that our people didn’t die,” Sheila tried to explain as she walked with them towards the broken down subway cart where Antonio had woken up weeks before. “Burton just went power-mad. It was hard seeing him fly of the edge the way he did. I had to end it though. He would have ended up consuming himself and taking us all with him in the end,” she said with a slight hint of sadness in her eyes. It was that special kind of smile you only see etched on the faces of people who had learned not to show weakness for the benefit of others. Antonio looked up from his own memories and looked into the earnest eyes of a woman that regretted the position she had been put in and the things she had done. There was no way to turn back time to when he was betrayed and there was no reason for her to lie now, so Antonio smiled. “You loved him, didn’t you” I’m sorry,” Antonio told her, putting his hand on her shoulder. Even if he really didn’t know this version of Sheila that well, the love she had for Burton was very apparent. “You don’t need to apologize,” she said sternly. “Yeah, I do and you know full well why.” He wouldn’t budge. She knew the reason he was apologizing. Burton had forgotten all about Sheila in order to fulfill his mission of killing Antonio. This mission had consumed and poisoned Burton until his death. Burton had left Sheila to fend for herself as he became more obsessed with killing Antonio. Ending his life was probably the hardest thing Sheila had to do in her entire life. Antonio genuinely felt bad about it; even though he knew it wasn’t his fault. He looked back towards Justine who walked a little behind them while Antonio and Sheila straightened things out. She was pointing things at little Milo as he experienced a place he had never been to before. “I’m sure you did what you had to do and I know that you will do anything to help your people survive this. Take care of yourself,” he said she extended her hand towards him. He brushed it off and hugged her tightly. He face welled up with silent tears. “You too.” Sheila stood in front of the cart as Antonio looked at his ride home. Justine and Milo finally walked over to Antonio. “Daddy! Does it work” Looks old.” Antonio got down on one knee and smiled at his son. “It doesn’t work the way you think. It might look old and run down but it is actually magical. You will see,” Antonio explained with a voice he only reserved for his son. “Are we going on a trip?” “Yes we are.” Antonio looked up at Justine and saw that look he had been missing from his life for a long time. Justine’s eyes sparkled with hope and longing for a world that would be a safe place to raise their son. Antonio had immediately asked her to come with him. It was too hard for him to finally find her and then to have to lose her once more. He wasn’t sure his sanity could deal with it. “Let’s go!” he said as he ushered little Milo inside the cart. He pointed to the exact place he had been sitting in when he first traveled through the fabric of space and time. It was still exactly as he had left it. This time though, it was a welcome sensation as he sat down and hugged his son. Justine sat right besides him and put her arm around his. Her face came forward towards his. Her sweet scented breath gently caressed his features resolutely although nervously at the same time. His lips touched hers softly for a moment, as if in a dream. “I love you,” she whispered softly into his ear. “I never thought I could have you back, but I was granted it. Whoever was responsible has my eternal gratitude.” They all closed their eyes as they waited in anticipation. The air inside the cart was cold. A sudden breeze ran shivers through all their bodies. “I love you too,” he said as he opened his eyes and saw the world he had been born in through the window. He turned around, looking for Justine and Milo but he couldn’t fin them anywhere. The desperation that overtook him was almost bordering on madness. He stood up and searched the whole cart, scaring most of the other passengers inside. When he made himself believe that they had not traveled with him and that they were stuck in that barren place, he sighed heavily. He sat back down on his chair and put his face in his hands. All was lost once more. The feelings he had regained, the sudden urge to live life to the fullest and all the plans he had made for Justine and Milo were gone in an instant, a fleeting moment where he had surrendered himself to hope. “You should meet Hope. She can be a bitch, but she is usually very pleasant.” Antonio’s face was lifted from his hands as he heard the familiar voice speak. He looked besides him and there she sat. He long straight jet black hair was now gone in favor of a shorter look, but the face was unmistakably the same. “I’m sorry Antonio. I am powerful, but there are rules. There was no way you could have disrupted the balance of this world by bringing them here.” “But I was there! I disrupted the balance in there!” The woman sighed as she gave the bad news. “That world is destined to end in the next seventy or so years. It is already disrupted. I just wished for you to meet your son and see Justine one last time. I told Hope not to bother you, but she is persistent.” He knew that her intentions had been good. In a way, he felt satisfied that he had finally been able to see the boy he never had and the girl he had once lost. “Thanks. I really appreciate what you did for me,” he said obviously still distraught. “That’s more like it! Do you want to come with me and do some rounds” I get very lonely and my job is thankless,” she said cheerfully. “That’s because most people like to be alive.” “Touch”! Come on now, this will be a day in the life of Death.” She grabbed his hand and the both of them disappeared from the subway cart as it moved normal people to their normal jobs at their normal hours. Even though he might not be the special person he always thought he would be, he had to admit that his life was anything but normal.
