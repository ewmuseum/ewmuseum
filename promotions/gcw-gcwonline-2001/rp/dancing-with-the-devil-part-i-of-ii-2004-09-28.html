Dancing with the Devil (Part I of II)

28 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9-28-04 ((This roleplay, and the one that follows, were two of the final things I wrote before I left the UWF a few months back. I just wanted to re-post them here because I was quite proud of them, and to give those that don't my character a little reference. Enjoy. =D )) Somewhere in the distance, an emergency siren screams into the waning Chicago sunset, it’s sparkling splendor refracting in prismatic rainbows across the towering skyline. From the vantage point of a posh penthouse thirty-seven stories above the Miracle Mile, the sounds of city life below are funneled through the canyon of glass and steel that is Downtown Chicago. Alone with his thoughts and the murmur of a productive city, a solitary man sits on a lacquered wooden patio chair, its polished surfaces matching a nearby table and full set of similar furniture. The wind whips wildly this high up the skyline, tossing the man’s shoulder-length brown hair in mad swirls around his neck as he absently considers the meticulous attention to detail in the well-furnished outdoor deck. An ornate rosewood railing rises four feet off the smooth marble-tiled floor, accentuating the dark brown shade of a pair of flower pots hanging on the back wall between twin sliding glass doors, their lush contents spilling over the sides and revealing a plant that the man doesn’t recognize. Everything so perfect, so clean. So immaculate and well cared for. All except for a dead body sprawled on the floor at the man’s feet, a bullet hole in his head tinting the tiled floor crimson. A small detail that can’t be overlooked, as it would happen. Inhaling a long, shaky breath, Rich “The Renegade” Rollins trades glances between his two blood-stained hands and the dead man at his feet, wondering not for the first time just where his day had gone wrong. ~<>~ ~<>~ ~<>~ Three Hours Earlier? “Don Dante, my husband has always been faithful to you?” [A choked sob forces the middle-aged woman to pause, her red-rimmed pair of hazelnut eyes blinking repeatedly, defying the tears that threaten to overtake her. Wiping a bit of wayward moisture from her face, the woman tries to pass it off as brushing a strand of her olive-colored hair out of her eyes, tucking it behind one ear. She continues by clearing her throat, her heavy Italian accent making it all the more difficult to understand her pitiful plea.] Woman: We share many things, signore. When I married into your family, I knew I was blessed with an abundance of love and loyalty? we share those traits, signore. We share your brother - my husband, that is - and as such, we share a last name. [Her face hardens, her tone becoming a barely-contained tidal wave of fury.] Woman: But I do not share my husband’s bed, not with anyone. When that little? that little vagabondo (she spits the word, the Italian equivalent of “tramp”) got it in her head that my Bernardo was her private plaything, my entire world came crashing down around my ears. He never comes home at night, always claiming to be running some ridiculous errand for you when I know full well he’s traipsing around with that hussy femmina! I ask you, Don Dante? what is a God-fearing woman to do, a loyal wife and mother, when her own spouse cannot look her in the eyes and tell her that he loves her? [She makes it to the word “mother,” but a mournful sob deteriorates the rest into a half-coherent mumble.] Woman: My Simona? she was my only daughter, signore. Teenagers are smarter than we give them credit for, and they see things we often overlook. It was hard for her to come to grips with the reality of her father’s? lifestyle? and they stopped speaking two months ago. When she found out about this woman - oh Lord, she knew it before I did - she? she? [The woman completely breaks down, an uncontrolled moan ripping itself from her lungs. A handful of heartbeats pass in heavy silence, broken only by the sound of her cries until she musters the strength to continue.] Woman: ? she ran away, without so much as a note or explanation, even though I knew. I knew, signore. When the police arrived two days ago, at first I thought they were going to tell me that Bernardo had gotten into trouble with his secret slut, but no. My Simona? she was found by the Pier, raped and strangled, her body tied underneath a dock and left to rot like a dead fish. Il mio solo bambino (“my only child”) ? [Sitting behind an elaborate black desk, his fingers interlocked patiently in his lap, Alexandro Dante maintains a considerate amount of control as he listens to the woman’s hysterical plea. A well-groomed man of his mid thirties, Don Dante’s broad frame is encased in an expensive Armani business suit, the collar unbuttoned at the top in a gesture that suggests a casual mood.] [But he was never in a casual mood. It was always business, even now when the subject at hand was most certainly personal.] [He allows the woman a good thirty seconds to reign in her emotions, but when it becomes apparent that she has no intention of doing so, Don Dante slowly rises to his feet. He shoots a surreptitious glance toward his advisor and most trusted confidante standing near the door, a man who stands so still he almost fades into the dark red wallpaper. The man returns the Don’s gaze, his eyes broadcasting his thoughts, “It’s your call.”] [Slowly, Don Dante strolls around the edge of his desk and leans back against the polished wood, bending in close to the sobbing creature sitting before him. She begins to stifle herself, rubbing a blotch of mascara across her cheek with a handkerchief produced from her purse.] Dante: What is it you’re asking of me, sister? [The family title, stripped of the obligatory “in-law,” gives the woman pause as he looks up into the Don’s penetrating stare. Her hesitation is brief; almost immediately, she answers.] Woman: I want my bastard of a husband, and his two-bit femmina, to get what they deserve for driving my Simona to her death. Dante: And what do they deserve? [A loaded question. Don Dante already knew the answer, even before her lips begin to move.] Woman: Death. [The Don bows his head, slowly exhaling a deep breath he wasn’t aware he had been holding. Now in control of herself, the woman waits for a response from her benefactor, studying the smooth features of his face with more intensity than anything she had ever known before now.] [Adjusting his head ever so slightly, the Don locks eyes with the woman, his tone grave.] Dante: You are asking me to kill my own flesh and blood, signora. While I agree that his infidelity is unfortunate, he is still my brother. Woman: And my Simona was your niece, Don Dante. Bernardo is responsible for her death, brought about by his own treachery to this family. His only honorable solution would be to take his own life, signore, but he is too much of a coward. Please, I beg you? let my daughter’s spirit find peace. Dante: By murdering her father? Woman: Si. Dante: And widowing her mother? [The woman looks away, biting back more tears.] [Speaking slowly, Don Dante carefully chooses his words now, attempting to soothe this woman’s broken heart. The words are kind, a trait rarely associated with a man who’s sleeves were soaked to the elbows in the blood of his enemies.] Dante: Rest assured signora, this prostitute that Bernardo has been with will be handled accordingly. This may be difficult for you to hear, but I’ve known about my brother’s disloyalty for some time now. However, I didn’t dare risk telling you for fear of destroying your marriage. I pleaded with him to come to his senses, even threatened him with alienation, but I think we can both agree that Bernardo can be a stubborn man when he pleases. It is unfortunate for him that his brand of stubbornness is counterproductive to a healthy marriage, and dangerous to me. [The woman jerks her head, meeting the Don’s knowing gaze once more, her own eyes filled with hope.] Dante: Which is why, with a heavy heart, I will agree to do this thing for you. His actions were the very same as murdering his own daughter in my eyes, and for that? he will meet the Lord. [Overcome with emotional relief, the woman breaks into a soundless sob as she grasps the Don’s palm, kissing the back of his hand between bouts of wordless tears.] Woman: Bless you, signor, bless you! Mia figlia l'aspetta nel Cielo! [“My daughter waits for you in Heaven.”] [Don Dante nods in appreciation, slowly ushering the woman to her feet. Holding her closely, they both walk towards the large swinging double-doors that lead into the antechamber outside the Don’s office, the statuesque frame of the Don’s devoted advisor watching them both like a hawk from the sanctuary of the far wall. Stumbling over herself with overwhelming gratitude, the woman kisses both of Don Dante’s cheeks before finally taking her leave.] [The door closes, leaving only Alexandro Dante and his second-hand confidante.] Dante: Armand, cancel my appointments. I will see anyone who is already here, but today? today is not going to be very pleasant, I’m afraid. [Peeling himself from the far wall, Armand casually circles to the front of the Don’s desk as the Don himself drops into the cushy Italian leather seat behind it, running a well-manicured hand through a head of dark hair.] Armand: Are you really going to murder Bernardo? [His thick Spanish accent makes it readily apparent that Armand is not of Italian decent, an odd trait for a man held in such high regard of a prominent Mafioso family. Remaining on his feet, Armand clasps his hand behind his back while he watches the Don’s mind tick away, considering the possibilities. Toying with a ballpoint pen, Don Dante’s eyes glaze over in deep contemplation.] [Finally, the Don speaks.] Dante: Do you remember what I gave Simona for her sixteenth birthday? [Armand puzzles over the strange question, but realizes there is a point to the answer.] Armand: No Alex, I don’t. Dante: A 1967 Shelby Mustang, her first car. It was baby blue, our favorite color. I remember riding with her as she drove it around my estate, the look on her face when she started the engine for the first time. She had a giggle that would melt your heart, Armand. But? she didn’t giggle when I showed her what I left in the glove compartment. Armand: Dare I ask? Dante: Photographs. Pictures of her father, or to be more precise? pictures of her father and the woman he’s taken such a liking to. Pictures of them eating together, getting into cabs together, all sorts of incriminating photos. I had been watching Bernardo for some time, as it would happen. She didn’t know how to take it at first, and got angry with me. But do you know why I showed her those pictures, Armand? Do you know why I gave her that car? [Still trying to assimilate this information, all Armand can do is shake his head.] Dante: Because she was innocent. She didn’t deserve to grow up in the environment that Bernardo had created with his lust and greed, and that car was her way out. I gave her the keys to the rest of her life, and the decision to make of it what she wanted. I only gave her the car, Armand. Her father gave her the gas. [Looking straight into Armand’s astonished stare, Don Dante’s tone turns to steel.] Dante: I will see that man dead, God help me. He deserves eternal damnation for what he’s done? but I can’t be the one to pull that trigger, lest I join him in Hell. I’ll see him there eventually, I have no illusions of that? but I will not kill my own brother. [As if to accentuate his point, the ballpoint pen in the Don’s hand snaps under the pressure of his thumb. Ignoring the distraction, Armand ploughs forward.] Armand: Do you want me to give it to Vega or Cicco? Dante: No. Nobody in this organization, nobody that can be linked back to me. Armand: Who do you suggest? [The Don sighs, leaning back in his seat and staring off into a distance that exists only for him.] Dante: I don’t know, Armand. I don’t know. Armand: You still have one more visitor, Alex. Would you like me to send him in? [Don Dante remains in his brooding far-off seclusion, barely acknowledging his advisor.] Dante: Who is it? Armand: A professional wrestler. [Jerking back into the present as if struck by a whip, the Don’s eyebrows furrow in suspicion.] Dante: It isn’t? him, is it? [“Him.” Despite the informal title, Armand immediately knows who “him” is referring to.] Armand: No, it isn’t Deville. If it had been, I would have told you. [Slowly letting his manor ease back down, the Don chuckles awkwardly as he dismisses the notion with a wave of his hand.] Dante: Well then, what does he want? To fix a fight? [They both laugh.] Dante: Send him in. [Nodding in acknowledgment, Armand turns on his heels and heads back for the large double doors.] [Twenty seconds later, the first thing Rich Rollins notices as he enters Don Dante’s private office is the shattered ballpoint pen, still leaking ink onto the unmarred surface of the desk.] [Fade to black.] (To be concluded?)
