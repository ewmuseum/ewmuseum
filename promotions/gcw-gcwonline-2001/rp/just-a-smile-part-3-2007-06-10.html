Just a Smile: Part 3

10 June 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 10th, 2007 On Saturday Tessa had to go out to see the doctor, one of the joys of her pregnancy. This, of course, meant that the children would be in need of a sitter. Luckily it seemed she already had someone in mind. It was one of those rare times where I was actually allowed into their condo and I was in their living room, observing Scott as he played with the children. The two boys, Chris and Evan, were wrestling around on the floor and making quite the bit of noise. They were, as young boys are, rather rough when they played, each mocking the type of wrestling typically seen on the television. As a wrestler himself, Scott seemed to be encouraging the boys, rather than disciplining them. Hearing the noise, Tessa came marching into the room to investigate. Her eyes grew wide when she saw the two boys tumbling around on the floor. Her mouth soon followed when she saw that it was Scott encouraging them. Holding the two boys apart by an arm length, her eyes darted between their adopted child, Evan, and Scott’s biological child, (of a different woman) Chris. Then, they shot a rather nasty look to the man that was supposed to be watching them. Despite knowing he was in trouble, Scott gave her a questioning look, “what?” “You know what,” she stated, moving from the children and toward Scott. “I don’t want them wrestling. Our children are not going to grow up to be wrestlers.” Scott rolled his eyes, tilting his chin over his shoulder and looked away from her. “Like us?” “Yes like us!” Her voice nearly crackled as she uncharacteristically yelled. In my short time chronicling Scott’s personality, rarely had I ever seen her even so much as raise her voice. She was, by nature, a very passive person. But here she seemed as fierce as a tiger. But like a stubborn child, Scott just shrugged his shoulders and kept his eyes averted elsewhere. “And what’s wrong with that” It seems like we have a pretty good life.” Tessa’s eyes pleaded, though he wouldn’t turn to see it. “Please,” she asked, “you know what this business can do to a person. I don’t want to see them have to go through the same things we did. I want them to get a college education and a good job somewhere nice.” “But why?” the children asked, only hearing part of the conversation. Like all children they wanted the missing pieces filled in. Sadly that would be denied as Tessa quickly brushed their inquiry away with the every common “I’ll tell you when you’re older”. Her eyes once again fell on her love, still pleading with him. “I just need you to watch the kids so I can go to the doctor.” “Didn’t we hire someone to do that?” Per usual, Scott seemed more concern with himself than with that of others. Looking around, he seemed to be searching for the person that was supposed to help them raise their children, given their extremely busy road life. “The nanny is off today.” Scott blinked twice, not expecting that answer. “Well that doesn’t do us a lick of good; she’s not much use if she doesn’t work.” To be expected, Tessa’s head dropped in annoyance. “Her mother died,” she told him, “she left to go to the funeral.” Even that bit of evidence did little for Scott, who thought “someone should have told her to schedule it for a better time.” “Scott!” she screeched, not surprisingly given his response. “Just watch the kids, ok” I’ll be back soon anyways.” His head tilted toward my way and instantly a lump formed in my throat. I saw the words form on the tip of his lips, but I tried to block the words from my ears. “Why not have him watch the kids?” I cringed. Not because I disliked their children, truth be told I hardly knew them. But I wasn’t here to be a babysitter. I’m supposed to be gathering information for my Masters dissertation. I need to know how a person so self-absorbed and egotistical could possibly create such a loving smile that he gives to Tessa each time he embraces her. It would be a perfect cap to my years as a psychology major. Tessa looked bothered by Scott’s request. I could tell he had just put her in a hard place. On one hand she didn’t want to offend me. On the other, she didn’t know me well enough to leave me with the safety of her children. I can’t say I blame her. I would be in the same situation should the rolls be reversed. “Because I asked you,” she finally replied, turning to leave. She had had enough of this conversation, and she was letting him know it. The chip in the armor finally started to show, as Scott got up and cut her off at the door. His hands went to her belly, feeling where the unborn baby was. “But what if something happens to the baby, I want to be there” his eyed begged. He almost didn’t even need to use his words, it was so obvious. The display of emotion and concern did a great deal to relief Tessa of her stress. Just knowing that he cared lifted her. Placing both arms around his broad shoulders, she tilted her head up toward his, “I’ll be fine. The baby will be fine. There is still almost another three months before the due date.” With a peek on the cheek, she turned and walked out of the door. The condo fell into silence, nothing seemed to move or make a sound. That is, until little Chris came up to his father’s leg and pulled on the bottom of his shirt. Scott peered down, wondering what he would want. He didn’t have to wait long before the child informed him that he was hungry. Scott’s eyes turned my way. I felt a blast of icy cold shoot down my spine. “You know how to cook?” The question caught me off guard. Trying to sputter out an answer, all I could manage was a measly “no.” He rubbed his nose with his knuckles and turned away. “Not much use then, are you?” One of the boys, Evan, looked over to my way as if he had just finally noticed me for the first time. He turned back to his adopted father, “who is he?” he wondered. Scott rubbed his nose again, looking at me with one eye shut. I was about to introduce myself to them, something I had accidentally neglected to do earlier, when he cut me off before I could even utter my first syllable. “He’s your drunken uncle Bob.” Both boys were curious as to my presences now, having the question raised. Chris looked to me, “How come we’ve never seen him before.” Without hesitating or flinching, Scott shot back “because he’s a drunkard and no one likes him.” “What’s a drunkard?” one of the boys asked, still studying me over. Pulling his eyes from me, Scott looked out past me and into another room. Moving by, he told the children “I’ll tell you later, now tell your drunken uncle hello.” In unison the boys looked to me and said “Hi, uncle Bob” then followed their dad to wherever it was he was heading. Rapping his knuckles against one of the doors lining the hallway, Scott tilted his head against the wood, “Christa, I’m making omelets. You want any?” “No thanks!” a young girl’s voice cried back. Scott wrinkled his nose, looked down to the two boys that followed him at each side. “What’s with her” She decided she doesn’t like food anymore?” The boys giggled, “She’s talking to her boyfriend!” “I am not!” a quick snap back came. Both the boys just giggled louder at her cry, but Scott gave a deep look at the door that led to the little girl’s room. “Is he anything like me?” he asked while he placed his ear to the door. It turned out he didn’t need to do that, as the door swung open to reveal a girl about to enter her teen years. She wore her brunette hair back in a ponytail to avoid obstructing the cell phone that was glued to her ear. “No!” she told him, “absolutely not.” “Good,” Scott muttered, turning his attention away from her and back to the kitchen. “I like him already.” As he walked to make his omelets, he gave a little smirk, “cause I was a little hell raiser when I was your age.” “You still are,” I had to reply. Scott just chuckled to my comeback. It wasn’t even that long ago he had been in jail for fighting in a bar. He still had his court date coming up next month for that. Finally entering the kitchen, Scott gathered up what he needed to make the omelets. His cooking was like his wrestling, scientific and methodical at the same time. He would slowly break the eggs, rapidly beat the potatoes, and quickly dice the peppers. Every motion looked planned out and practiced. Never had I seen a man who could seem so careless doing such careful work. It was how he approached all things. Everything the man did looked like work, and if I didn’t know any better, I would swear the man never had a second of fun in his entire life. But deep inside, he enjoyed every bit of it. He had this odd love affair for precision and skill. He needed and wanted to be the best in everything he did. Waiting for the omelets to cook, the two boys decided to take back up their wrestling match from the living room. `Trying to gain an advantage, the boys had much less finesse than their father. Rolling around on the linoleum floor, Chris somehow gained position over the older Evan. Coming back up to his feet, the boy placed both hands on his hips and declared “I’m Vivica Valentine!” Scott stopped dead in his tracks, looking away from the frying omelet and over to his youngest son. “Excuse me?” “I’m Vivica!” the boy repeated, posing for his dad. I cracked a smile, but it was more because of the look Scott had on his face. He was petrified and appalled by the statement. Shaking his head, he threw his arm around the boy and consequentially ended their wrestling. “No” he said, “you are a boy. Vivica is a girl. Big difference there.” He thought of a moment, coming up with a good solution to the problem, “Why don’t you be Chris Storm like your dad?” The other child, Evan, was quick to step up, “because I’m Chris Storm!” Scott looked over at him and gave him a thumb up. Cracking a smile for the boy, it was one of pride, but not the same one he gave Tessa. “That a boy. You win by default.” His attention then turned back to Chris, “then why don’t you be Markus Stone?” “Can I be LeStatt Knight?” That was not the answer Scott had been hoping for. In fact, I’m willing to wager it was the last one he had been hoping for. His nose wrinkled in annoyance, something it often did when he tried to think of an appropriate reply. “How about no?” “But why not?” the child cried, wanting to know why his idea had been shot down without as much as an explanation. Scott was quick to fire back an all too typical response of his, “because I have on good authority, word that LeStatt Knight likes to wear make up and dress in pink.” The boys giggled, Chris chiming in “eww, that’s girly!” Scott shot the boy a warm smile, still not the one Tessa would get. It was odd, how one man seemed to have so many different smiles. It was like there were many different personalities all hidden under his surface, each appearing at different times to manifest and give signs of its existence. “That’s right that it’s girly. And you don’t want to be girly, do you?” Chris shook his head, “no”. “Then who are you going to be?” his father asked, now kneeling beside the boy. “The omelets.” Scott scratched his head at the boy’s enigma answer, until he realized he still had the omelets in the pan on the stove. Catching himself before he cursed, Scott rushed to the stove and shut off the fire, saving the omelet to only a semi-burnt future. Looking over his shoulder, he chuckled. “You two can wrestle later, but for now you can have some lunch.” Giving one more call to Christa, the twelve year old finally emerged from her room to join the rest of her family at the dining table. I’m guessing that must have been a common scene for the family, as Scott had made an omelet for her before hand. Each had a plate in front of them as they ate and enjoyed mild chatter with each other. Sitting back from the table, I played the part of casual observer. The words they spoke had little importance to me. I was concerned about their expressions with each other. Words lie, but expressions were much harder to fake, and often revealed a lie. Unknowingly, a person could reveal volumes about themselves with as little as a wrinkle of the forehead, or a twinkle of the nose. The way they rolled their eyes, moved their mouth, and most importantly smiled spoke loudly about the type of person someone was. I knew this from over eight years of studying in various universities. I had compiled papers, essays, studies, and test to affirm this point. Yet as I watched Scott Drake, all that reference material seemed like nothing more than back-handed rumors and folklore. I had sketch books full of different facial expressions he made. Many of them were typical and expected. His eyes rolled to the left when he was annoyed. His mouth would open slightly when taken by surprise. A twitch of the nose meant that his temper was starting to boil. Dropping his eyebrows meant that temper was about to explode. But his smile remained a mystery to me. As of this afternoon, I had twelve different smiles recorded. None of them were similar. Most of them actually didn’t concern me. There was only one I was bothered by. The one he saved only for his love, Tessa Windsor. Even in the presences of his children, those he loved deeply, he had a different smile. It was warm and caring, but still much different from that of the one for Tessa. I can describe this smile, the one for the children. I had seen it a number of times with other people. The one for Tessa was different though. I can’t recall in all my years, ever seeing one of the same nature. I’ve tried putting it to words, to describe it in my notes, but many times I’ve had to go back and rewrite it. I’m never satisfied with the description I’ve given it. It’s warm and caring. It is completely and utterly devoted. Love pours like wine from an open fountain. It’s sinister and devious. It is animistic to the core, primal in nature. There is a deep sense of savage longing, almost lustful yet loving. I will have to continue my research of it later though. Tessa had arrived back from the doctor appointment. She was, of course, perfectly fine. The baby was doing just fine and coming along as it should. Scott was deeply relieved at the news. Light had already started to fade in the sky, my cue to leave. Though I observed them whenever they allowed me to, I still respected their privacy to night and made my exit. I, after all, had to process all the information from today. In just the few hours I had visited, I had already filled a one subject notebook full with sketches, notes, and observations. I needed to scan, organize, and study each piece. I need to understand that smile.
