a Pharmacy tale

28 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/28/05 After discovering his new secret, Coyle decided to take a drive over to the local pharmacy. With him, he held a prescription to have a refill for his painkillers and money to buy a few necessities. He was driving his busted ol? Volkswagen Jetta, the busted kind that clings when it idols and squeaks when it goes; yeah, that bucket. However, he still drove there with a grin on his face. And he enjoyed the tunes he had in the CD player. Shortly after, his cell phone started to buzz. After lowering the music, he answered the phone. “Marky Rahl, what’s up?” in a cheerful voice. On the phone was Coyle’s agent. His name was Markus Rahl, or as the O called him, Marky Rahl. Marky Rahl is a young cat with a good personality. He has spunk about him, and that’s why Coyle chose him. He can successfully get Coyle in a good mood in any circumstance, and that’s also why he likes him. Let’s not fail to mention, Marky Rahl did get the man a job at the GCW, and he’s Irish. Marky Rahl, “Nothing, just pourin” a little beer in my hand. What are you doin?”” “Wait, what are you doing” Are you pouring beer in your palm you nut?” Coyle answered. Marky Rahl, “You know it, I’m tryin” to get my girlfriend drunk. But hey, that’s not important right now, what’s important is that voicemail you left me. What’s goin? on in your head you crazy bastard, you can’t wrestle in this shape. Hell, you have a chance of injuring? yourself more.” “You don’t understand though Marky,” as he gets a little emotional but positively, “I want this match, and more than anything! This is the perfect opportunity to get X back at his own game. He embarrassed me a few weeks ago at WorldWide. He didn’t even have the guts to face me until I was tired. That little bastard is going to ge?” As he was wrapping up what he had to say, he was alarmed by an oncoming car. Getting caught up with what he was saying, he completely forgot about the road for a brief second and swerved into oncoming traffic. How embarrassed he felt. “How embarrassed I feel right now,” he said. Then, on the other line, you heard laughing. But, then instantly, you heard the same thing happen to Marky Rahl. That only ignited Coyle to have a good laugh. “Look at us all over the place!” laughed Coyle. Marky Rahl, “Yeah, good times. Anyways, I know you want this match, but your getting greedy. You can always fight the kid in a few weeks at WorldWide, it’s just going to be a little dela?” “No, you don’t understand, this is the first PPV I have a shot to be in, I won’t let it slip through my hands. No way my friend, no how,” Coyle interupted. Marky Rahl, “But Coyle, you need to realize you should be in tip-top shape for this, X is a good competitor just a little unlucky. Besides, GCW already told me to inform you to forget about’it.” “Are you kidding me” They really know I’m looking to still go on, but they won’t allow it? Damn..” he wondered. Marky Rahl tried to say something, but he stumbled all over his words. He couldn’t even talk, he was trying to think of a nice way to tell Coyle that it isn’t happening. It didn’t matter though because Coyle is in the zone and he had his mind set on one thing. “Listen Marky, we’ll talk about this later, I just made it to the pharmacy,” Coyle said. With a pause, Marky Rahl agreed. They hung up. Coyle clearly lied to Marky because he was only half way near the pharmacy, but Marky Rahl was being a downer. There was no motivation there even from his own agent. And little things like that bother Coyle. Where’s the support? He continued to listen to his tunes, but he didn’t bother to higher the volume. Instead, he wanted to think about how he was going to definitely make this go down. It seems as if the odds were against him. Maybe because the GCW has a policy with being eligible with an injury and what not and because even his own agent won’t side with him. Whatever it shall be, he didn’t sweat it. He was so certain that his half-plan would work. Eventually, Coyle made it to the pharmacy. On the way in, a few young kids stopped him for his autographs. Though he didn’t want to, Coyle couldn’t resist the little giggles. The kids were cute. Besides, all they wanted was an autograph from a favorite wrestler of theirs. Kids, “Wish we saw you tomorrow night wrestling Coyle!” That touched Coyle. He watched as they ran back over to their parents, and stood their with a smirk. “Cute little boogers, they’ll be satisfied. Sorry X?” he said under his breath. The automatic door opened, but seemed to malfunction. As Coyle walked through it, it closed on him. It didn’t hurt him in anyway, but it caused a scene to be made. It tripped Coyle in front of everyone. And not only that, it was an unavoidable announcement to the shoppers that a star was here. About 14 autographs later, he made it through what seemed like a battlefield in a war zone, and ended up at the pharmacy. At the counter, it looked as if he has walked through a minefield. He practically threw the prescription and insurance card at the lady he was so beat. Pharmacist, “O’Conner, Coyle. Alright sir one second.” Coyle nodded and just started to look around. In the corner of the store, he saw a TV streaming some public channel. Oddly enough, a commercial for FallOut came on. Then, there he was. He saw himself on the TV. Of course across the scene it said “match cancelled,” but it put a tear in his eye. It seemed as if he was getting acknowledged for once in his lifetime. How could someone not be happy after that? Still waiting, the O was looking around. Nearby the pharmacy laid the Hallmark cards. One section was filled with St. Patrick’s Day cards, and it was the most visible section to Coyle. It instantly made Coyle think of his father. For the first time in years, Coyle finally called his father, Patrick, a few days ago. And it was one hell of a long conversation they had. They reminisced on old times, and seemed to have gotten along. Secret be told, Coyle missed his father for all of these years, and decided a bond with him may improve his attitude towards things. And it’s safe to say it did. His moment then became interrupted by the pharmacist. Pharmacist, “Here you go Mr. O’Conner.” She handed over his new cartridge of painkillers, and gave him back his insurance card. Pharmacist, “Sorry about the injury too, I’m betting you wanted that more than anything.” She winked. Coyle kind of thought of that as an insult in a sort of way. So, he just nodded with an awe-struck face. Afterwards, he turned around and started walking while looking down. For a brief second, he looked up, but then shot his vision back down. Then he paused. He looked back up slowly and scrambled over to hide behind a stand. All he was able to see was people peeking their faces over aisles and what not, and whispering to each other. “I still have to go all the way back to the other side of the store, crap,” he thought to himself. “Do I honestly want to get X back this bad,” he chuckled.
