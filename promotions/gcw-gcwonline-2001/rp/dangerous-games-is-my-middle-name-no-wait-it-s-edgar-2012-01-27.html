Dangerous Games is my middle name! No wait, it's Edgar...

27 January 2012
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/27/12 A single man stands in front of a large "American" flag. The flag is approximately 40 feet by fifteen feet, and it looks like it was created by a color-blind child who has never seen the Stars & Stripes and had it described to him in a language he doesn't understand. So unless the American flag has flowers in place of its stars and the stripes are red, blue and different shade of blue, the air-quotes around "American" stand. The single man in front of the eyesore flag may warrant quotations himself. Eddie Whisky is certain man-sized and man-shaped, but at times his mind never left the fourth grade. And his soul is comparable to angry primate. Regardless, Eddie Whisky is all decked out in military regalia, but for which country or what branch of the armed forces is anyone's guess. A chorus of recorders plays "Yankee Doodle" in the background, and at this point you may guess they are doing it poorly. "I want you to remember that no bastard ever won a Battle Royal by getting eliminated. He won it by making the other poor, dumb wrestlers get eliminated instead of himself or herself." Eddie pauses to make sure he made sense. He firmly nods and presses on. "Fellow GCWers, all this stuff you've heard about Eddie Whisky not wanting to fight, wanting to stay out of Dangerous Games, is a lot of horse poop. I, Eddie Whisky, traditionally love to fight. All Eddie Whisky, which is myself, ever does is love the sting of battle royals. When I was a kid, admired the champion Duck Hunt shooters, the fastest typists, little league t-ball players, the toughest bakers. I, Eddie Whisky, which is myself, love a winner and will not tolerate a loser, which will be the rest of you all. I, Eddie Whisky, play to win all the time, even when I lose." Again Eddie pauses, whispers the last sentence back to himself, and continues. "I wouldn't give a hoot in hell for a man or woman wrestler who lost and laughed, on account of you being a laughing loser. That's why I, Eddie Whisky, have never lost and will never laugh, because the very thought of losing is hateful to me. Also, laughing." To accentuate this, Eddie Whisky does not laugh. "Now, an army is a team. I am a one-man army, and as such am a one man team. Now you might say to me, ‘Eddie Whisky, there is no "I" in team!’ Well I would say to you shut up and stop interrupting me, dummy. This ‘no "I" in team’ stuff is a bunch of crap. I can spell ‘team’ with an I if I feel like it! It might be pronounced differently, like ‘tiem’ or something, but up yours!" Eddie starts to get angrier and begins pacing in front of the flag back and forth. "You bilious bastards who say that stuff about how to spell things don't know anything more about how to win a battle royal than they do about fornicating. Now I do not claim to be an expert on the former, but I certainly am one of the latter!" Eddie stops, a look of deep confusion crosses his brow. "Okay, now former means the first one I said, right? Whatever. I, Eddie Whisky have the best spirit, and am the best men in the world. Man in the world. You know, by Jeebus, I actually pity those poor bastards I’m going up against, by the Flying Spaghetti Monster, I do. I am not just going to boot the bastards; I’m going to cut out their living guts—metaphorically—and use them—hypothetically—to grease the soles of said boots. This would be messy in reality, hence my use of literary device. I’m going to murder you lousy bloody buggering bastards by the bushel. A bushel is an imperial unit of dry volume, equivalent to 8 gallons or 32 litres." Eddie begins pacing again, this time with determination as opposed to confused agitation. "Now, some of you boys—and girls, don’t think because you are females that I allow the long-honored taboo about hitting females to prevent me from eliminating you also—in the GCW are wondering whether or not you'll chicken out when I, Eddie Whisky will enter the ring at Dangerous Games. Don't worry about it. I can assure you that you will all pee your pants. Now I confess, I have wet myself in the past too. The chants of ‘Bed Wetty Eddie’ still haunt my dreams, even though it is a misnomers as wetting my pants is not the same as wetting the bed and it is a lazy illusory device to use ‘bed’ instead of ‘pants’ just because it rhymes and don’t even GET ME STARTED ON HOW ‘WETTY’ ISN’T EVEN A WORD!!!" Eddie stops pacing. Flecks of spittle now fly off his lips with each word. "You all are the enemy! I will wade into you! I’ll spill your blood! I’ll boot you in the belly! When you put your hand into a bunch of goo that a moment before was your face, you'll know what to do!" Eddie turns and begins to kick the "American" flag until it falls onto the floor. Once again Eddie Whisky seems to calm himself. "Now there's another thing I want you to remember: I don't want to get any messages saying that you are not going to quit. You're not winning anything. Let me, Eddie Whisky, do that. I will be are advancing constantly and I’m not interested in hearing about how you tried your best or any crap like that. I’m going to hold onto you by your nose and I’m going to kick you in the bum.I expect you to cry bitterly when I beat you, like how those kids who called me Bed Wetty Eddie made poor little Eddie cry!" Off to Eddie's side, a pimply adolescent holding a recorder pokes his head in. "Are we done here?" Eddie Whisky turns and nails the erstwhile musician with a stiff Whisky Kick. Eddie then smiles, slightly calmed down now. "Now, there's one thing that you all will be able to say when you get back home after Dangerous Games. And you may thank Cthulu for it. Thirty years from now when you’re sitting around your fireside without your grandson on your knee--because I destroyed your ability to reproduce on account of me beating you all so bad, you can so to no-one-in-particular, 'I was there when Eddie Whisky took his first step towards winning the GCW title at NC-17.' And then you will be sad because you are alone andtalking to yourself." Eddie prods the young recorder-player with his toe, smiling at a job well done. "Alright, then, you sons-of-bitches, you know how I feel. Oh... I will be proud to beat you wonderful guys and gals in a battle royal anytime, anywhere." Eddie Whisky walks away. "That's all."
