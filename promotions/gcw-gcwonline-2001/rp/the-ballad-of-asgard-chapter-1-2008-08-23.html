The Ballad of Asgard: Chapter 1

23 August 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 23, 2008 It is said that at the moment of your death, your mind shows you through you memory the whole of your life. Whether one person’s life is only in the pictures and in the memories is another thing altogether. Ram’n Rivera would have sworn to anyone who would have listened that his life passed before his eyes like a movie as he fell thirty stories down. To what every bystander seemed like a few seconds, to him had felt like the forty year he had already lived. For a moment Ram’n felt like he was in space, like he was actually floating around. When his body finally crashed to the concrete sidewalk right next to the construction site, the screams of fear and agony of the people watching was almost surreal. A second before they had been watching in silence, almost in awe, as a man fell unceremoniously to his death; now, though, the situation had finally sent the right set of responses through the nervous system that led to their screaming voices. It was not a pretty sight. The body lay mangled in the ground with people around it still in shock and wearing some sort of part of the man’s body on their clothes. The crimson pool that painted the ground was like a warning. A beautiful woman in black moved away from the body and the people around it. Behind her, a man who seemed confused as to what had happened followed her towards where Antonio stood in silence. “What happened?” the man asked in bewilderment at the scene behind him. “You died,” she answered. “W” how? I’m here,” he said as he tried to convince himself that it was not him on the sidewalk displayed for all to see. He looked around his own body probing and inspecting it with his hands. “You fell from the top of that building over there. Your body is right there.” Antonio walked to where the corpse of Ram’n Rivera waited patiently for the police and the ambulances to arrive. Some of the people who had been there when the body fell had walked off drunkenly after throwing up but other passersby were now crowding the spot. Antonio tried to see the Ram’n that stood in front of him, but the corpse’s face was too destroyed for him to find a discernable facial feature. “I” I? I’m dead?” He resigned himself to what he knew deep down and sat down on the floor with his head in his hands. As Death consoled the man who had just died, Antonio walked away from the whole scene. His thoughts were on the few weeks he had just spent in another dimension. His thoughts were on the woman he had loved more than life itself and the son that he had lost before he ever got the chance to meet him. How could he face the characters in his life’s story now that he had seen them in a way that he never thought possible? Was he destined to walk through life as if what he had experienced had not really happened? How could he face his best friend Burton after he had learned that in some universe, Burton had killed him? “Hey, wait up!” Death ran up to him and grabbed his arm once more. Her sister Love had played a sinister trick on her and Antonio. Had made them care for each other in a way that Death should never be able to love. They walked together in silence for a while. The day was surprisingly sunny but at the same time there was a nice breeze that made the day invigorating and just lifted some spirits. As he walked with Death through the strange streets he had forgotten, he remembered the way they had met and what had gone on with them. It all seemed like a lifetime before but it was only almost a year. The first memory he had of her was the real beauty her face reveal. For the first time in his life he had knew what the real meaning of beauty. He thought of the ancient Greek philosophers and their idea that the things we see here are just pale imitations of a perfect mold. The contours of her facial features seemed to everyone she allowed seeing her like they had been sculpted by Michelangelo himself. Her jet black hair fell around her face as if framing it for posterity. Antonio instantly knew she was special. He never suspected just how special. She had explained to him her nature after asking him for help to end a war that might end up destroying the fabric of time itself. “I know it’s been hard for you, these past few weeks. Maybe it was not a good idea on my part, but I know how you longed to see them. I have been in your dreams,” she said sadly. “I just wanted to give you a chance to be with them for a little while.” If he had ever seen someone act so far from ones preconceived notions one had created for that person, Death would take the cake. It was her job to take lives. She never reveled on it. In fact, she had confessed to him that at times he hated it. For years she had hoped to become human, not be reviled and feared by everyone. “I appreciate what you did. It was just an unnerving and weird experience,” Antonio said as they continued their walk through the streets of Old San Juan. A cruise had recently arrived full of tourists and the streets were now filled with camera flashes and weird accents. The inconspicuous couple navigated through the 16th century maze-like streets of the capital of Puerto Rico. The sun shone brightly on the colorful houses that stood as a testament of the passing of time. “They stopped at a small establishment. Antonio had spent many nights in that place back when he was much younger and his passion for politics was at an all time high. Los Hijos de Bor’nquen was practically empty save for a few regulars. During the day, those streets were normally empty, but it was something completely different at night. They both sat at the bar. Antonio signaled to the bartender to get him two Medalla’s, the local beer. He looked around the bar and found it weird to see it so empty and without the usual live music. “So, what’s next for you” I know that the experience was traumatic but you have to get on with this life of yours,” she told him. The bartender placed the two beers in front of them. “How am I supposed to” After living through all these extraordinary experiences, my life seems too pathetic and insignificant,” he said as he drank from the beer bottle. Death grabbed the beer bottle in front of her and almost drank it all in one gulp. She was obviously trying hard but not much was really getting through to him. Seeing him like that was something that she just couldn’t stand. She had never felt like she did for any human being in the history of time. “Ok, how about this. Would you like to come with me for a while” We can do whatever you want until you are ready to go back home,” she said genuinely excited about the prospect of them spending more time together. “Um” let me think about it.” --- The dark and damp atmosphere of the room was enough to choke normal human beings to death within seconds. Midgard was for those who could not afford to be in the hallowed hall of Valhalla or even in the underground world of Svart’lfheimr where the black elves dwelled. “We have to do something, or we will die.” Byleist shivered in a corner as his brother worked hard to finish the sword he had spent centuries making. The awful sound of the hammer hitting steel had almost driven him insane. “I am doing this for our own good.” Helblindi had been working nonstop for centuries, only taking a year or two to rest. He felt himself getting weaker every passing moment. Not because he was tired but because he was being forgotten. The both of them had been lost in time. If it wasn’t for the invention of the written lore, their brother would be the only one of them alive. “Why don’t we ask for his help?” Byleist asked. “He has to help us; he is of our own blood.” The guttural grunt that emanated from Helbindi was enough to make his brother cower in fear. “He does not care about us, brother. He has only ever cared for himself. Besides, when we next see him and get his blood, our weapon will be complete. We will finally be remembered and our tale told in the epic poems of old and new. You will see,” Helbindi’s cackle as his plan came together could be heard by his neighbors in the Norwegian city of R’st. “Loki. Your blood will give life to my blade!” --- “Okay. I will go with you. It’s always been a dream of mine to see the world,” he sighed. “And I really don’t want to go back to my old life.” “It’s settled then!” She drank the rest of her beer and jumped off the stool she had been sitting in. He saw her enthusiasm and couldn’t help but smile. He swallowed the rest of his beer and followed Dee out. He hated calling her by her full name, so Dee was a good substitute. Antonio was beginning to think of the time he spent with his son and Justine as just a dream, something that happened in his mind and would never happen again. He knew that Justine was dead and he would not see her again. He grabbed Dee’s hand, spun her around and brought her towards himself. Their faces were so close to each other that he could feel her breath softly caressing his cheeks. Antonio kissed her on the cheek softly and hugged her tightly. He had never been one to cry, so he tried to show his emotions through other means. They stood there for a moment, lost in time. Their closeness was the only thing that held their crumbling existence together. After a few moments, their bodies separated and things felt right. The worries that furrowed Antonio’s brow were forgotten for a moment as he thought of the many possible places he could visit. “So, what is our next destination” Tokyo? Budapest? Sydney?” “Now that I’ve thought about it, I’ve always wanted to see Ireland.” She smiled. “I love Ireland. On we go.” They both disappeared. Nobody that didn’t have a deity’s blood noticed that the couple that had been there a second ago, was no longer there. Someone had noticed. “Death, my love, you are surely not cheating on your man, are ya?” The sneer on his face was almost like death itself.
