A Chasm not crossed

29 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/29/2006 “What the fuck is this bullshit?” The GCW World champion growled low in her throat, clenching her hand into a fist until the fax paper held within crumpled into a ball several times smaller. Tossing it violently against the wall, she glared daggers at her fax machine as if she could cast it back through the fax line to GCW Headquarters. “As if I don’t have enough to worry about already.” That same glare turned to the now mangled piece of paper lying on the floor, knowing what it said despite the fact that had been rendered illegible. Ms. McFarlane, As with standard company procedure, you have been booked for this weeks upcoming World Wide to defend your title against an opponent who has to be announced. Due to special’circumstances surrounding this World Wide, your match has been decided to be an obstacle course, and of course the Global Championship Wrestling World Title will be on the line. Thank you. “Obstacle course my ass, what the hell is this” Three Ring Circus Wrestling?!” With a snarl she stormed out of the main floor office, slamming the door shut behind her’and managing to knock over the hall table as she stalked out. A frustrated curse bubbled from her lips and it was a struggle to mutter it, rather then scream it at the top of her lungs. Shadow was busy today, busy with a million different things. She was bustling through the hallway, a big sheaf of papers cradled over her arms because, by the gods, she had finally found a lead. But preternatural hearing often brought with it a curse- from downstairs she heard a series of thumps, and then a curse that sounded like it was near-murmured. It was hard to identify the sender of the ill wishes, but, with this group, there came responsibilities. 'Kunda!' she snapped to herself. But, she sighed, and turned her body down the stairs, and towards the office, which was the direction from which the sound had emanated. A slow, murmured string of curses came from her lips, but she cradled her papers in one arm, and composed her face, attempting to purge any ill mood at her work being interrupted before she saw... 'Dawn?' She opened her eyes a bit wider behind her glasses, worn more for effect than need. 'Are you alright?' Cursing again, Dawn turned to Lia with a sheepish expression, looking for all the world like a child who was just caught breaking an expensive vase. 'Ah yeah, I'm just frustrated about WorldWide...I let it get to me.' Shadow blinked. There were times when it seemed so futile to get angry about GCW, but that was, perhaps, just her inner pessimist talking. 'You... are that concerned about two idiots booking you in a match like that? Their idea of obstacle course is probably putting up a TV set with a pornographic video on it that you somehow have to avoid being mesmerized by before going down the ramp and picking up the title off the ground for the win.' 'That's my problem though!' Dawn's voice was halfway between a growl and a whine, 'What kind of place would book any of us into so stupid a match? It's like they're not taking us seriously at all.' Shadow smiled wryly. 'What do you MEAN what kind of place? Haven't you ever worked other places that did the same?' Of course, she was goading her for a specific response. And she knew that if Dawn caught on, she'd remember all those OTHER times that people hadn't believed in what they could bring to the table. A sheepish blush rose in Dawn's cheeks and she lowered her eyes abashedly to the floor. 'Yeah...seems sometimes like the whole world is like that eh?' When had it become so difficult to talk to the woman she loved? There was something between them, an unidentifiable distance she *thought* she knew, but wasn't sure. About the only thing she thought right now was that it was definatly her fault. Shadow shook her head, pulling off her glasses and hooking them in the top of her corset to hang there, a useless set of eyes. 'Dawn, my point is... don't worry about it. There are idiots everywhere we go who want to make us look dumber than they are because... because of whatever reason. But you're only a joke if you let them make you into one.' 'I won't, I promise you that.' Her eyes came back up, but found they slid away from Lia's just before the point of contact. 'I'll make sure they won't try a stunt like this again.' The darker-haired woman nodded. 'That's the way to look at it. Withstand their stupidity, and come out looking the better for it. They won't expect it, and you'll ruin their plans.' 'Thanks. And ah...sorry to interrupt you, I'll try to be a little quieter.' Lia shrugged. 'I was only concerned. It's no interruption to make sure that my comrades are alright, no matter what else I may be doing.' Why did it sting to be called a comrade? Isn't that what they all were? She nodded numbly in response, taking a small step backwards. 'Cool, well I'll uh let you get back to what you were doing then.' Turning to go she paused, looking back over her shoulder in curiosity. 'What ARE you doing?' She held up the sheaf of papers in her hand with a half-smile. 'Poring through these. Looking for any sort of line I can get on where my brother may currently be so I can find him before someone decides to feast on Cainite Methuselah blood. I've... got a pretty hot lead.' Dawn didn't know much about Lia's vanished sibling, but she knew enough to know how important it was. Her eyes were wide as they went from the papers to the other woman. 'Can I help?' Lia sighed, the half-pleased expression fading into one of distant frustration. 'I don't know. I don't even really know what I'm looking for. These papers are shrouded in metaphor. They won't call him by name, so I have to look for some other way that they might refer to him. It almost feels like even if I find someone who MIGHT be him... it's still just a guess.' Resisting the urge to scuff her toe and shrug her shoulders, Dawn nodded. 'Maybe a second set of eyes would help. Even if just to give an outside perspective you know?' Lia nodded. 'If you've got a high-lighter and a good set of eyes... I could probably go through this a lot faster. But it's going to be boring.' 'I can do boring...and...time with you is never boring.' The words felt awkward, but still right and she smiled shyly. A smile flashed over Lia's own face before fading away. 'The office then? More room there to spread out papers... I suppose that's what an office like this one was made for, anyway.' 'Sure! I'll get some drinks from the fridge, and we can get started.' Like an eager puppy, Dawn set off down the stairs, long legs taking them two or three at a time. It wasn't much, but even a little time was enough to push her feelings of awkwardness to the back...for now anyway.
