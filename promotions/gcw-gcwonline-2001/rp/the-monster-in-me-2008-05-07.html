The Monster in Me

7 May 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 05/07/2008 -- ww93 “There was a man I knew who’s wife was cheating on him with his best friend. He reacted badly; worst than most. Hired a man to kill his wife.” The old drunkard at the bar paused momentarily to take a mouthful of Scotch. “Not just any fool you understand. A professional. The kind that cost serious money. The husband was a stockbroker. Earned big bucks on Wall Street, so he could afford it.” He licked his lips and swirled the liquid around, the partially disintegrated ice cubes clanking against the inside of his glass annoyingly. “He sat on a bed in a hotel room with a powerful drink in his hand. The place was opposite the beauty salon his wife visited a couple of times a month; the location chosen by the assassin. The husband watched the world out of the window; he wanted to see what his money was buying.” “A powerful drink, eh” I like this story already,” the man sitting next to him interrupted. He finished his beer off pointedly, shook his head and chuckled to himself. The old drunkard glared at him. “You done” I’m trying to tell a story here!” “Hell,” the man began, placing his glass on the bar and pushing his glass towards the bartender to refill it, “we’ve all heard these stories a hundred times already! You ain’t saying anything we ain’t heard!” “Well fuck you then!” the elderly drunk exclaimed. “I’ll just shut the fuck up and sit here all quiet like then, shall I?” The man chuckled at the elderly drunk’s reaction, whilst the bartender shook his head. “Leave him be, Paul.” “This is all bullshit Mike and you know it!” the man identified as Paul informed him. “He ain’t never known a man who hired an assassin!” “I wanna hear it.” Eyes turned to the lonely man sat at the other end of the bar. He was a scruffy looking individual with long, wild, black hair and out of control facial hair to match. “And who the hell are you?” Paul questioned. “This old fool wasn’t even talking at you.” The bartender shook his head. “Leave it be, Paul. Why do you gotta cause trouble?” “The man was talkin” loud enough for me to hear,” the man at the end of the bar replied. He finished his drink and glared at the loudmouth sternly. “Reckon” that’s good enough.” “Want another drink, McKail?” The bartender asked, already reaching for a bottle of bourbon. McKail pushed his glass forward and then gently massaged his face. He made sure to avoid the large gash stitched up on the side of his cheek, the one he got for trying to do something stupid, to someone stupid. “Like you gotta ask...” The bartender smiled and filled his glass, whilst the hamster in Paul’s head picked up its pace and worked its wheel just that little bit faster. “Wait’McKail” As in Jacob McKail?” The man at the end of the bar glared at him, waiting for him to make a point. But there wasn’t one to make. “I thought I recognised you. Bit of a fall from grace, sitting in here drinking with losers like us isn’t it” You were a Universal champion after all.” McKail silently glared back at him in response, which caused Paul to visibly back down and escape into his own drink. “Just let the man tell his story, will ya?” The bartender asked, his voice calm and reasoned. Paul held up his hands defensively. “Okay, Okay, I’m sorry. Gerry, please will you tell us your story?” Gerry finished his Scotch and slammed his glass back down on the bar. “Fuck you buncha bastards, I’m goin” home to ignore the wife.” “Come on, Gerry,” the bartender began, grabbing a bottle of good Scotch from behind the bar. “Finish your story.” He nodded over towards McKail at the end of the bar. “And I’m not the only one who wants hear it.” Without looking at them, McKail raised his glass in agreement and the old drunkard sighed. “Alright I’ll finish it, but I don’t want hear you interruptin” me again Paul!” Paul rolled his eyes. “Yeah, okay Gerry, I promise. Tell your damned story already, would ya?” Gerry glared at the bartender and then the bottle of Scotch in his hand. “You gonna pour that, or wear it?” The bartender shook his head and cracked a smile, even forced a chuckle from McKail at the other end of the bar. “You’re one rotten drunk, ya know that?” the bartender told him, pouring him the drink. Gerry pounced upon it like a Lion on a Gazelle, devouring it in much the same fashion. “So as I was saying, the husband sat on the bed in his hotel room overlooking the beauty salon and the poor bastard couldn’t help but stare at his wedding ring.” Gerry took another mouthful of the Scotch. “Damned thing made him remember the good times; his wedding, the birth of their child etc and he began to reconsider. It was about the same time he heard the shot. He looked out of the window and saw her. She lay face up, eyes staring blankly into the air and blood pouring out of her, covering the around all around. He regretted what he’d done immediately and realised it wasn’t something he could take back. He watched at a crowd quickly gathered around her body and he wept.” There was silence for a time, before Paul blinked and asked. “That it” That was your story?” “Yeah,” Gerry replied. “Didn’t say it was a good one.” “I kinda liked it,” the bartender told him. Paul shook his head. “And was there some sort of point to that story that I couldn’t see?” “Sure,” the bartender readily replied. Then, after thinking about it for a time he developed a confused expression and asked, “Wasn’t there?” Paul wore a mischievous smile. “What” Like make sure you take your wedding ring off before you watch an hired assassin murder your wife for you?” Gerry chuckled. “Something like that.” “Well, go on,” the bartender told him, “enlighten us.” “Ah,” Gerry began, taking a final mouthful of his Scotch, “that’s the question, ain’t it?” Paul shook his head. “C’mon Gerry, you can’t just leave us hangin?”” “The point is,” McKail began, finishing his bourbon and removing himself from his barstool, “be careful what you get yourself into, because sometimes you can’t get yourself out.” Gerry grinned and raised his glass in McKail’s. “Precisely.” “What the hell do you know about it?” Paul questioned, raising a speculative eyebrow. McKail made his way to exit, shooting the trio with a knowing smile. The three of them glared at his face cut and bruised face in both wonder and shock, only now seeing it clearly. McKail pushed open the door, allowing the unfamiliar afternoon sunlight pour into the musky and decidedly grim bar. “More than you know?” *** McKail pushed away the barely touched plate of chicken and pasta in some kind of tomato-based sauce and extracted his cigarettes. The old drunkard’s story had gotten to him more than he’d like to admit and to his annoyance, it’d affected his already nominal appetite. He didn’t have to be a rocket scientist to see the correlation between that poor bastard in the story and himself. The situation with Jake Reed had gotten out of control and judging by what happened at last week’s WorldWide it was pretty evident to everyone. It’d all started in FUSE. He’d worked his way up the roster and put in his dues and finally it paid off, he was considered a “big deal” and was pushed into the main event. Eventually, he won the Universal Title, but lost in but a few weeks. He never got the title back and it got worse from that point on, eventually prompting him to leave the organisation and consider retirement for good. GCW was his lifeife; he came to reignite his career, but it seemed fate conspired against him. Jake Reed showed up begging for forgiveness for something McKail didn’t know nor cared about, like a ghost the past representing a time he’d wanted to forget. But Reed continued to show up and eventually, cost McKail his chance at reliving his days of glory once more. Naturally, McKail reacted badly. So badly, he trapped Reed’s head in a steel chair and attempted to damn near decapitate the fool. But it didn’t quite work out as he’d planned; things never did for McKail. Reed escaped and McKail ploughed face-first into a steel chair. He groped at the scars scattered across his face as the memory struck him, this time making sure to touch upon the largest of the collection, lightly following the stitches with his forefinger. This anger, this rivalry had already cost him more than he had to give and so the only question now was, Is this worth losing my soul over? “I’ve lost my mommy,” the voice stated, piercing McKail’s thoughts and drawing his attention outward, toward the young boy standing just beyond his diner cubicle. The boy looked lost and scared and tears had already begun to well up. The boy withdrew in horror upon casting his eyes on McKail’s disfigured face. McKail turned away and glared down at his black coffee, ashamed. “Where’d you lose her, kid?” He grunted the question, still glaring at his coffee. “We-we-we finished eating,” the boy quivered, “and I went to the bathroom. When I got out she was gone.” McKail nodded and took a sip of the coffee. “Take a seat, kid. I reckon she’ll be back soon.” The kid shook his head. “I don’t wanna.” McKail chuckled and took a drag of his cigarette. “Don’t let the face scare ya, kid. Just a couple of scratches. I won’t look at ya if ya don’t want.” “It ain’t that Mr. McKail,” the kid replied, his voice still aquiver. McKail raised a speculative eyebrow. He knows me then? “It’s just...you’re a bad man. I saw you on the TV -- I saw what you tried to do to that man.” McKail’s heart sank, but found himself unsurprised. Millions of people must’ve seen last weeks WorldWide, he’d expected such behaviour towards him. Hell, he was so ignorant, people could’ve been acting strangely around him all week and he wouldn’t have noticed. Thing was, he never once imagined the fear might come from but a boy. What have I become? McKail dared look at the child again, groping for words that might try to explain his actions, but could find none. The boy glared at him eyes wide, full of fear and wonder. “Why did you try to kill that man?” “I wasn’t trying to kill anybody,” McKail told him. “I was just trying to hurt him a little, is all.” The notion visibly processed in the boys mind for a time, before cocking his head to one side curiously. “But why” He only wanted you to forgive him, I saw.” McKail shook his head. “It wasn’t that simple, kid.” “Why?” McKail took a drag of cigarette and exhaled. The kid must’ve caught a lungful this time and coughed a little, so McKail dabbed the damned cancer stick out in a nearby ashtray. “Because nothing’s that ever that simple.” The kid grew quiet for a time, thinking McKail’s answer through. After a time, he asked, “So you gonna try an hurt him this week too?” It was a reasonable question given the circumstances -- a question he hadn’t even begun to entertain yet. But it didn’t take him long to reach a conclusion. What happened last week, what he found himself capable of, shocked McKail more than it did most of the GCW fans. The time of backstage attacks was behind him, of that much he was certain. Finally, he glared at the kid and shook his head. “No.” The kid’s face lit up. “That mean you’re gonna make friends with Jake Reed” Does that mean you two are going to be best buddies now?” McKail chuckled and shook his head. “No.” The kid visibly deflated. “But--but why?” “Like I said, kid,” he replied, “it ain’t that simple -- life ain’t that simple.” “Timothy!” A shrill woman’s voice sounded. A woman in her mid-thirties wearing a pretty flowery dress stormed into view and grabbed the kid roughly by the arm. “I’ve been looking for you everywhere!” “But I was just talkin to Mr. McKail,” the boy responded. The woman shot McKail an icy glare. “And what have I told you talking to vagrants! He could have cholera or anything!” “But he’s a wrestler, mommy,” the boy pleaded. “I watch him on TV?” The woman dragged her boy toward the exit. “Like hell you do! Professional wrestlers do not dress like hobo’s, Timothy!” McKail cracked a grin and signalled the waitress for the cheque. While he waited he glared out of the window at the sun setting beautifully on the horizon. He regretted what happened last week, but it was too late to stop anything now. The showdown was on the cards from the beginning, but neither of them had realised it. There was no other choice now; he’d see this out to the end, no matter the outcome and no matter what he’d become. Why? Because life just isn’t that simple?
