A Minute of Your Time.

28 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/28/06 He wandered the streets of Kobe alone, his black windbreaker pulled tightly against his chest to keep the brisk chill from causing him any discomfort. His head was bowed, staring at the ground as he took each rhythmic step instead of the few residents of the city that used the walkway to get to their predetermined destinations. It was late out, but considering the time change it hardly felt that way. It’s hard to change your sleeping habits, no matter what position the sun is in -- or not in, in this situation. Of course, the time change could not be the only thing held responsible for his lack of sleep lately. The nightmares’they had as much to do with the bags that had surfaced below his eyelids than anything else. It had become a reoccurring dream of that horrific night a few days before his fourth round match in the Golden Turnbuckle Tournament, and as much as he wished it would all just fade from his memory, he knew that it was something that would plague his sleep for the rest of his life. He had hoped that walking the streets would help clear his mind, but that hunger within him grew stronger with each passing moment. The temptation of grabbing one of the hundreds of perky Asians that seemed to walk past him -- without giving him a second look -- had almost become uncontainable, but still he trudged along with his hands firmly planted within the pockets of his coat. That part of his life had died along with Braun that evening, and in doing so had granted him a rebirth of sorts. He no longer had to be burdened with helping to satisfy Braun’s appetite for supremacy. Braun’s beliefs that the World belonged to the pure race; the Aryan race, no longer had to be his beliefs. Though the more time he spent thinking about all the things he no longer had to do for the sake of pleasing Braun, the more he realized that he never had to do those things in the first place. Over time, those beliefs became his own as much as they were Braun’s, and it took clutching the cold steel of a pistol against Braun’s chest for him to realize it. The old man had given his own life for Jayce to have a second chance, a chance to live a normal life like he had always wished. And now he did not want it anymore. Now it was the last thing on his mind. Strange how the little things you overlook over the span of your lifetime, somehow end up becoming the most important pieces of the puzzle you call your life. With a giant chunk of his puzzle missing -- the most important piece, the one that sits right dab in the middle -- Jayce was now left all alone. Braun was; for the most part, his only friend, and now there was no one for him to turn to for advice. His birth mother and father; his true family, died in a car accident a few months ago when a drunk driver sped through a red light at a busy intersection. The rest of his family; the one he turned his back on and left behind when his brother was murdered, had all but given up hope on him after years of trying to be a part of his life. Isn’t that the way though? Doesn’t everyone wish to one day be able to strike out on their own and be able to say that they do not need to depend on anyone or anything? A small Asian man wearing a light gray suit brushed against his arm, mumbling something that could have been an apology if Jayce understood the language at all. The bump was enough to bring him back to reality, washing away any thoughts of the memories of his past life away like rain on a cloud filled afternoon. Sure they would come back, but for right now he had to focus on other things. Danielle Northsky being one of those things. If he was going to be a part of this year’s Rampage Tournament, he was going to have to get through her. “The only thing standing in my way is a girl,” a smile parted his dry, cracked lips as the thought crossed his mind. “Some hatchet-packer at that.” He watched the little Asian man disappear around a corner before continuing on his way. “This match is going to be a piece of cake.” The words seemed to echo inside the walls of his mind, as the smile that creased his lips almost doubled in size. =?=?=?=?=?= Jayce Bradley lunged out from behind a small crowd, assembled behind the police barricade that had been set up to keep people from getting to close to the man with a gun pressed against the temple of a young girl; who couldn’t be more than ten years old. The image then seemed to just freeze in time, as the image turns out to be coming from a television set with a recording of the events. “You see the way he just came out from nowhere.” The voice was not coming from the screen, instead from somewhere behind us as if the person was standing over our shoulder. “The guy was just out to get his name in the papers,” a second voice replied, the soft and comforting voice of a woman. “Not all of them are cold blooded killers like that Rick Collins character.” “Rich. Rollins.” With a slight chuckle in her voice, “Yeah, like I pay that much attention to losers like that. Most of them are just high school bullies that couldn’t find anything better to do with their lives anyway.” The recording begins to play again, this time in a frame by frame process as Jayce sprints across the run down motel’s parking lot; catching the gunman; Russell Braun, off guard after a few frames. The two men begin to struggle, wrestling with the weapon as Braun turns to face Bradley. After another few frames, a flash is seen between the bodies of the two men. Bradley’s eyes grow wide with shock, replacing the look of exertion he had as he struggled to remove the pistol from Braun’s grasp. “Wait a minute,” the rough voice echoed out through the silence as the image on the screen froze once more. “Did you see that?” “See what, Nick?” “The old man said something,” a brief pause, as if he wasn’t sure he saw what he thought he did. “Then Bradley kind of nodded.” “Are you sure?” “I don’t know, playing it at full speed you sort of miss it, but at this speed you can see that his head nods a little bit.” “Why don’t you just play it back?” “Because as much as I want to nail this bastard to the wall?” We finally have a full view of the room. Detective Nicholas Pride is seated directly across from the television set in a backward turned chair. The woman; a brunette with her hair pulled up in a bun, stands to his left. Pride lowers his head, shaking it as he does so. “I’m afraid to be right.” =?=?=?=?=?= A clock. A large white clock with a green border. The numbers one through twelve form a circle on the face, the second hand gradually making it’s routine trip past every single one. Luckily, we catch it just as it passes the twelve. “Shadow.” Tick. “Vivica Valentine.” Tick. “Dawn McFarlane.” Tick. “Danielle Northsky.” Tick. “Four of the many new faces to show up in GCW over the last few weeks.” Tick. “Four of the eight contenders in this year’s Rampage Tournament.” Tick. “And my name isn't one of them?” Tick. “I guess the more things change, the more they stay the same.” Tick. “Maybe my invitation just got lost in the mail.” Tick. “Why else would GCW’s cruiserweight champion be left out of something as important as this?” Tick. “I’m going to push that aside for now though, because I have a shot now.” Tick. “So what if I had to challenge someone for their spot.” Tick. “So what if I decided to make it one of the girls.” Tick. “Sure I could have challenged Almasy or Rollins.” Tick. “But why should I take that risk when I don’t need to?” Tick. “I want to be a part of the tournament, not watch it from backstage.” Tick. “And this is the easiest way in as far as I could tell.” Tick. “So what if I had to put my title on the line.” Tick. “It’s not like I’m going to lose it or this match.” Tick. “Especially not to a woman, and especially not this week.” Tick. “I just thought I’d take this minute.” Tick. “To let Dani know that I’m coming for her spot.” Tick. “And to let her know that it doesn’t matter who she has watching her back.” Tick. “Because it’s going to be pressed against the mat when it matters most.” Tick. “And it only took me fifty seven seconds more than I’m going to need at World Wide to let her know that.”
