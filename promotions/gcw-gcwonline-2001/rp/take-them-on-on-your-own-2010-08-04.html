Take Them On On Your Own

4 August 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/04/10 Originally posted at http://prime.e-wrestling.org - 2 May 2010 In truth her decision was by then an open secret, but it still mattered to Roxie Sykes that she tell him face to face. She owed Tim Shipley that much. The two had grown apart of late, Roxie at last finding herself able to empathise with Jody Monroe when the mildly floundering Just Wrestling workaholic’s despairing phone calls never seemed to get returned. Indeed, Shipley had been growing apart from everyone, ever since the moment Steven Caldera tore apart months of careful stitching to weave Garbage Bag Johnny into his tapestry. But for Roxie there had been too many final straws, and as her pale body blanched in the heat of her post-match shower, steam and sweat merging into an indistinguishable haze, the girl who wanted to be God threw off the last of her shackles. Shipley was waiting for her, and he looked set to explode. "Win or lose?" Roxie spat rather acerbically, twisting her long red hair into a fluffy towel for it to dry. His response, though non-verbal, was sufficiently demonstrative to answer her question, and Roxie sidestepped impassively as an entire wall of lockers crashed to the floor beside them, her ears absorbing the echoes of the Englishman’s discontent. "That bad, huh?" "Murray’s back," Shipley snarled. Roxie surveyed his face, various red swellings exaggerated by the volume of his ire. She made a mental calculation as to how sympathetic she could afford to be before firing the first rounds from her Gatling gun of rebellion. "You got some of that cream in your, uh, locker," Roxie offered as she tugged two legs of denim up her protesting thighs. "For the bruises, remember." She shot him a reproving look, but Shipley was engaged in kneading both sides of his head with his fists, the knuckles jutting out like rugged Himalayan peaks threatening the monsoon. Fully dressed now, Roxie stowed her new ring attire in a gym bag. Handmade by Tempest for her GCW debut that evening, it had served her well. "My match went great, thanks," she announced to the room. Shipley grunted. "I know. I watched it. Good--" "No, you totally didn’t," Roxie corrected him calmly. "You spent that time yelling at Clyde Fox. Tempest told me. When she came to congratulate me. You know, like you could’ve done." "Fuck Tempest," Shipley declared with some savagery, looking up at Roxie with his teeth gnawing at his lip. "Everyone knows what she’s about. She’s using you." "Like you’re so clearly not." Now, perhaps, she had gone too far. After all, Roxie was supposed to be building up to telling Tim Shipley something she was sure he would not want to know. And now, as he stood with blazing eyes, no longer seemed the best time. "And what’s that supposed to mean?" he demanded. Roxie flung her kitbag to the floor and squeezed her eyes shut. She was still feeling the burn in her thighs, not to mention the thumping pain in the back of her head. She wanted to go home and relive it all, but with a handful of painkillers and a tall glass of blissful silence. To hear again that almost corporeal surge of support around her. To feel again that sense of closing a circle when she landed the Decree on the hapless Aaron Fujita. To do it all again, and then sleep through the night and the day and watch cartoons and nurse her aching body. "I really don’t want--" "If you’re going to say something like that, you’re going to bloody well explain it," Shipley commanded, stepping forward and glaring down at his – manager? valet? or just his colleague? He couldn’t be sure anymore. She never had been sure, and that was part of the problem. "I’m walking, Tim," and there it was, she’d shot her load prematurely, and now she had no choice but to purse her lips and beseech him with her walnut eyes to listen with an open mind. "I can’t sit here and take orders from you, and from Garbage Bag Johnny, and from the whole rest of the goddamn locker room. I can’t pass the time each WorldWide watering fucking fichus plants. I want more, Tim." "I know." And he’d known it ever since he’d taken her with him to High Octane Wrestling in 2009, plucking her from obscurity as an occasional Just Wrestling ring announcer. Since he’d thrown her, with unimagined success, into that awkwardly comfortable crossover zone: between the girl that guys wanted to be with, and the girl that girls wanted to be. "I thought I could do it here, with you," she told him, urgently trying to communicate the allegiance she still felt to him, trying to revive the bonds that had once pulled them together so snugly. "And tonight I had that chance, and I took it. But when I was in that ring I felt..." "Alive?" Shipley intoned in a hollow, aloof voice that Roxie entirely ignored. "I felt like... I felt like Ellis Nash," she half-whispered, turning sheepishly away. It felt infantile to say it, but it was the truth. She’d spent half her adult life idolising the AWC star. She’d modelled her rudimentary wrestling style after the spot-heavy, inventive spurts of inspiration that Nash had been known for. As her knee had found the soft, accommodating abdomen of Aaron Fujita, her synapses had threatened a sensory overload. Landing Nash’s very own Decree was like a heart attack in reverse. "You felt like Ellis Nash." His words pulled her back, but gently, a leaf fluttering to earth; a soft inevitability. "And still he doesn’t put me in the motherfucking tournament," Roxie reported with considerable indignance, tugging the towel from her head. Whorls of damp hair fanned over her features, but it wouldn’t matter; the cameras wouldn’t be returning that evening. "What do those other assholes have? Frost? Steele? The fucking Messiah?" "We’ve been through this," Shipley muttered, rubbing a tissue across his forehead to collect the sweat. He had a car park horde of angry fans to look good for. The obscenities, the signs, the occasional cupful of piss; to face that looking worn – to face that looking like one had, in fact, just gone out and fought two fucking pay-per-view epics in one fucking night – was to request one’s own guillotining. "You’re on a manager’s contract--" "Uh, I’m totally not," she interrupted. "Yeah, like I said. I’m walking. I put my papers in to the Garbage Bag." "Seriously? You threw them in the bin?" "Sense of humour doesn’t suit you, Shipley. ‘Specially not now." "Sorry." He ran his hand through his hair, encrusted as it was with a night’s ring detritus and the precipitates of too many bodily fluids. Roxie hitched her leg up against the door, pulling up her knee-length boots. "I wanted to win the goddamn tournament, Tim. Johnny won’t let me in the goddamn tournament." "So go to Just. I told Jody to hold you a place. Any day. Any show." Roxie shook her head, tired. "No. Nowhere I’ve been," she despaired. Her voice took on a reedy quality, a sincerity that Shipley was not used to. "There, they know me as a ring announcer. Here, a manager. Apparently it’s too much to ask for a fucking fresh start in this business. So I’m making myself one. Johnny won’t let me in his tournament, well, I’m entering a different goddamn tournament." Shipley tore the tape from his wrists, squatting down on the side of the overturned lockers. "So it’s true? You’re going to... to PRIME?" She gave a small nod, then sat down next to Shipley, placing a hand tenderly on his arm. "Nothing personal." He shrugged. "Nothing ever is in this business, right?" Roxie tried to join in his morose laughter, but it stopped as soon as she started. "I wanted to ask if you’d, uh. If you’d come to ReVolution with me. Just the first time." His face hardened suddenly, and a moment later he jerked his arm away from hers and stood up. "I don’t think so, Roxie." "Tim..." "I’ve got Rampage to prepare for, all right?" The Englishman raised his voice a little. "And now I’ve got to do it without my manager. If you’re walking, fine. Go on. Walk!" Shipley pointed threateningly at the door, twisting his head to ensure she felt his glare. He could hear his blood, pulsing unpleasantly in his ears. "But don’t expect me to hold your fucking hand along the way." He turned away again. Roxie had never needed much encouragement to return the first volleys of a slanging match, but tonight was different. She narrowed her eyes at the back of Shipley’s head. "Don’t gotta be spiteful about it." "Who said this is spiteful?" He ripped the top off a fresh bottle of water, despatching its contents over his head and chest. "I’ve been there," she said, quieter than she could ever remember having spoken, "for you. Every time." "Great. Thanks for your help. I’ll write you a lovely reference. Now, seriously--" He turned, and she couldn’t remember having seen so much pain in his eyes. "Leave. Please. I have a tournament to win." That did it. "And so THE FUCK DO I!" Roxie hurled her entire body at him, throwing his thick frame against the wall. She grabbed at Shipley’s face, clawing, with sharp fingernails and bad intentions. He collapsed onto the bench, Roxie’s weight holding him down. Both felt a fleeting reminder of what high school had been like. "You stupid – fucking – ASSHOLE!" She punctuated the insults with blows to the face, and with each one felt the ambivalent thrill of having severed the last strands of the ropes that bound them. She was on her own, now. Roxie Sykes... wrestler. Out into the light. Watching behind her as Shipley’s shadow retreats. Sinking to the bottom and holding her breath for as long as she can. Posing for photographs behind the sign that read "CLIFF NOT SAFE". Pointing. Laughing. With a yell he threw her off. Roxie’s boot caught on something and she tumbled back, her body defining a huge dent in the upturned bank of lockers that broke her fall. She held her breath. Her arms by her sides, legs drawn out straight, view obstructed by errant strands of hair. Shipley loomed over her, dark thoughts infesting his increasingly obsessive mind. Roxie realised with some surprise that she couldn’t remember having been so scared. She held her breath and waited. But when he finally spoke, his voice was inflicted with the haunted memories of defeat. "I can’t go there," he told her in a hoarse, humiliated whisper. He thought of that day, five years prior – Hin See, and the condescending laugh with which PRIME’s owner had rejected his plea for a roster spot. He thought of how that moment had shaped what had happened since. He thought of spinal injuries and courtroom testimonies and days, months, years spent in the backwater promotions – ever since that day. Ever since rejection had first cut Tim Shipley’s feet from under him. That day had taken nearly half a decade to get over –in truth it hadn’t been gotten over, rather cruelly superseded by the more public, more personal rejection of NC-17. Plus ça change, plus c’est la même chose was the phrase that imprinted itself on Tim Shipley’s mind as he left the room, the ghostly outlines of Hin See and Steven Caldera folding their smug arms behind it. He had just realised what his life was about. Roxie knew none of this, and although she had her suspicions as to why Shipley had for so long shirked the challenges befitting a wrestler of his calibre, she knew ultimately that the rudder of the Ship had been fixed in place long before she came aboard. And so all she felt as he left her, prone and silent as Shockwave went on around her, was that the pounding of her head had become the surging crest of a wave she was ready to ride. And when GCW’s head interviewer Kevin Jacobs stepped inside, the door having been left suspiciously ajar, to see Roxie Sykes sprawled out on an overturned set of lockers, eyes closed, chest rising and falling, he sensed a contentment in her so complete that he turned out the light and closed the door quietly behind him.
