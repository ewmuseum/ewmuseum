Exit Stage Left, Enter The Jester

3 May 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 05/03/11 Late December, 2010 He was stunned. Ryan's date caught attention when she walked out in the daylight in a black turtleneck, fully-fronted tanktop t-shirt, tight pinstriped Dickies. It wasn't the fit of the pants or the cover of the shirt that drew eyes so much as it was the arms full of colorful tattoo work and the casually-worn Rosary. Even still, what had him so speechless was the fact that she showed up at all. ''This is a pleasant surprise.'' She invited herself to sit before he could, doing her best impression of a person who had better places to be as she slumped into the chair across the table. ''Surprised I showed or that your beer goggles weren't as thick as you feared?'' When he spoke again, it was far more confident. ''I never drink enough to make a bad decision.'' ''Sometimes I think my best decisions are drunken ones. Then again, sober life's been boring so far,'' she shrugged and may have continued, but something struck her while preparing for a crack about her companion that day being boring, too. ''Did you tell me your name last night?'' ''I did. Ryan.'' ''Well, hi. I'm Amy.'' ''I know,'' he chuckled. ''Believe it or not, I'm one of those guys who actually pays attention to the girls he approaches in bars.'' ''So you know my name. I'm assuming you still know this isn't a date, right, Ryan?'' He nodded, smiling. ''I knew you'd remind me, at least. So there's that.'' **************** Tuesday, January 4, 2011. The post-Christmas letdown had let the air out of the Bayfair Center Mall. Some of the stores hadn't taken their decorations down; in others, employees shuffled about as if in a daze, long weeks of longer hours leaving a pall of exhaustion that they hadn't quite recovered from. With the economic downturn closing stores by the bucketload, the mail was kind of depressing, actually, especially at dinnertime in the food court. Still, the steak place served bacon chili fries, and it was Amy Campbell's cheat day. Well, perhaps every day was a cheat day for the little tattooed redhead, but her excuse that day involved bacon. Chances to have a meal with her companion this evening were few and far between, and... bacon chili fries. "No, I'm serious--" Campbell began, breaking off to make a lunge for the dark-haired girl's phone. "Okay, give me that!" "Wha--hey!" Quinn Gregory snatched her smartphone away, pulling the thing into her lap. "I was... okay, sorry." "You've had your nose buried in that thing since we got here," Amy scowled. "You're--I'm not keeping you from anything, am I?" Gregory shrugged diffidently, leaning back in the plastic chair. "No, of course not. It's just--" At one time, the relationship between the two women had been close, half-mother/daughter and half-sisterly. Quinn was the only child of Alexandra Pierce, wrestling's Spider in the Web, and Amy's ex-girlfriend. It was the "ex" part of that sentence that had grown to make things difficult. Campbell's breakup with Gregory's mother was some ten months old, and no matter how much they tried to remain friends, the subject loomed between them all the time. "It's just...?" Amy prompted, stabbing her fork into a frankly massive plate of chili, bits of bacon, and French fries. "C'mon. Either I am or I'm not." "It's just..." The girl sighed, raking her fingers through her tar-black hair. "How do you do it? I mean, people dig you. All of my friends, almost every guy I know... they all think you're awesome. But get to have bacon on chill on fries, and I'm stuck with a salad. How do you..." She shrugged, poking at her meal with a muted shrug. "I don't know. How're you you?" ''Guys are only interested in me for the wrong reasons,'' Amy shrugged. ''And you are not going to get a reputation for... for the things I've got a reputation for. I'll kill you before anyone can even wink at you.'' The teen frowned slightly, setting her Droid on the table beside her tray. "It'd be better than having no reputation. At public school, I was the weird girl. At the Ellis Academy, I'm the new girl. I just..." She scooped up another forkful of limp lettuce glumly. "I miss Brandon a lot, you know? I never thought I would, but... but I'm a complete downer, I know." Campbell tilted her head with a sad little smile. ''Of course you miss him. He was your first boyfriend, and now that I know you're still sad over it, he'll be the first person I'll ever murder. It's natural to miss him. It's also natural for those puppylove blinders you've still got on to make him seem a lot better than he actually was.'' Gregory sighed again, glancing away. "He wasn't bad. He's smart and he..." She sniffled a little, looking up at the televisions that hung from the ceiling, playing music videos on a loop. "Do we have to talk about this again? I fucked it up. It was my fault, my deal. He didn't do anything, and you're not going to do anything, okay?" ''Hey, I could do without the cursing,'' it may have been hypocritical, but Amy still scolded her. ''And if you really think you f--you screwed it up, we do have to talk about it again, because you need to know damn well you didn't, and I'm not just saying that as your... as...'' it was her turn to look away. ''As whatever it is I am.'' "You kinda are, though. I mean, you're saying I didn't mess things up, but you don't..." She waved a hand negligibly. "Contrary to popular belief, I'm not perfect. I'm gangly and dorky and too... like, too old for my age. I know this, and I know I spend too much time with Mom's stuff and the wrestling and all that. I messed up. It happened, I lost him, move on." ''You're not gonna get past it and realize he's just being another idiotic teenaged boy, are you? In another year or two, you'll figure it out, but right now you're not acting too old for your age by any means. You're just as lost as everyone else your age. And my age, really.'' Gregory folded her arms, resting her elbows on the table. "There's an easy solution for that, you know," she said, glancing up at the monitors again. "You just won't listen to me." ''God damnit, Quinn... it's not easy. Your mom and I, we....'' Campbell ran a hand through her hair, flailing a bit in her frustration. ''It's not happening, okay? And I try to avoid ever copping out like this, but it is one of those things that's above your understanding as a teenager, okay?'' **************** She'd never expected the random meathead at the bar to have stories like he did, and Amy found herself caught in it. After a tour of duty, he managed to be the only person she'd ever met as an ally who could help her understand Alexandra Pierce more. It was a roundabout way, for certain, but he knew what a kill felt like. Any glimpse into that personal hell might help her to reason through why Alex had done the things she did - why she couldn't be a person Amy should be with. ''That's really all you feel? I mean, I'm not judging here, but isn't there a weight to it?'' When he shrugged, it looked like his shoulders were creatures of their own, trying to escape the confines of the too-tight shirt. ''I dunno. Other guys broke down. The first time, I threw up. Didn't think much about it after that.'' ''The ones that broke, did they talk about it?'' ''I guess they did. We talked about a lot of stuff on late watch.'' She stifled the urge to correct his grammar. ''You just... I dunno. You don't seem as broken as soldiers I've met.'' '' 'Cause I'm not. Most guys come back and they hop 'em up on so much shit. They're on this drug and that drug and need three a day for anxiety, and they're all alcoholics or worse anyway,'' the shoulders made another spring for freedom. ''I don't think about it, it won't bother me.'' ''You said it was a fifteen month deployment. That's a lot of dead people.'' ''Still feels like a lot more friends than bad guys,'' he said somberly. ''Can we get off this for a while? I dunno why you're so interested in--'' ''Just have people close to me who've been through... well, kind of similar stuff.'' Amy closed her eyes briefly as she shrugged, unable to take her mind off of one woman. ''I like the insight when I can get it. What about being a cop? I'm guessing that's less stressful.'' ''In a way. Compared to that year, it can get boring. Boring is good sometimes. We haven't talked about you at all, here.'' ''There's not a lot to say.'' ''That's not what Wikipedia said.'' She stared cold daggers through him for what seemed like an hour. He couldn't get past how striking her eyes were, especially in the daylight; One a deep brown, the other ice blue in a perfect contrast. She sealed her mouth with both top and bottom lips bitten between her teeth, and he knew she was considering what kind of charge she'd get for assaulting an off-duty officer. Without taking eyes off of him or warming the gaze at all, she finally spoke. ''I'll foot the bill. You can go now.'' ''Hold on a minute, okay? I didn't--'' ''You lied. You can go.'' ''I didn't know last night,'' he assured. ''Not until some chick came up and asked me if I was a wrestler too. She saw us talking and--'' ''And it doesn't matter, because you let me think I was a normal person for an hour and a half now.'' ''Why does it matter if I know anything about you? I'm still sitting here.'' ''Hopefully not in another couple of seconds.'' ''I'm not leaving.'' ''Then I will.'' ''Don't.'' Campbell stood up, but the young officer jolted to snare her by the forearm. He tried to move calmly, to avoid causing a scene or upsetting her further, but it didn't seem to work - she tensed immediately at the touch, and looked even more like she was ready to strike him. ''It'd be good for both of us if you didn't touch me.'' ''Please just sit down? I told you some real stuff, I just want a little of the same in kind.'' **************** The teen put up her hands in surrender, because they'd been over the same thing a hundred times. There was no convincing Campbell - at least at that point - that Alexandra Pierce was a good choice. "You're wrong, but... not having this argument again. I know how this ends. With the shouty and the hurt feelings and the sulking car ride home.. Then the guilt and the--yeah, can we just move to the ice cream part, because I've had just about enough of the salad stuff." ''Ice cream it i--wow, the chick in this video!'' she exclaimed, pointing behind Quinn to the mounted TV. ''I... just... wow. Who is that?'' "Uhh..." The dark-haired girl looked over her shoulder and up at the television. "I think they're called the Pretty Reckless or something? She's on Gossip Girl or something. I don't know, I don't watch. Anyway--" ''I know there's the whole 'I generally prefer dudes' thing to consider, but preeeeeeeeetty sure my new goal in life is to find that chick and... well, I'll spare you the details, but she'll love every minute and--'' Quinn looked away from the television, snorting. "I don't even want to know. Isn't she... I think that chick is my age." ''With that bod-err-voice? Impossible.'' "Bet you I'm right. Loser buys dessert." ''She sounds like she's smoked a pack a day for years, and she looks like... like... I mean, come on. You're on.'' The teen's fingers were already flying across the screen of her Droid, almost faster than Amy could follow. "I bet I could look like that if you gave me an unlimited budget for wardrobe and make--" "I think if you try, I'll lock you away in a tower." Despite the mocking tone, Amy was as serious as she could be while stealing surreptitious looks at the screen. "Or at least sterilize every boy who comes near you." "And here we are. The Pretty Reckless, lead singer Taylor Momsen... born July 26, 1993." Her brow came up behind her glasses. "So that makes her about six months older than me. I hope you brought cash, because I am having the biggest Blizzard that Dairy Queen sells." ''... There's no way she--lemme see that!'' Quinn simply turned the phone around to show her, and Campbell responded with a sudden slump to her shoulders. ''... So, we're just pretending this conversation never occurred after your ice cream, deal?'' The girl laughed lightly, taking the phone back. "Because I really want to have long-lasting memories of when... of when you pulled a pedo?" Her eyes lifted from Campbell to an approaching figure behind her, brows climbing. She leaned subtly across the table -- long years of living with her mother had her on a hair trigger. "We've got company," she murmured. "Tall, dark-haired musclehead..." Amy's brows furrowed momentarily, body tensing as she leaned back in the chair, cracking her knuckles. ''Looking friendly?'' ''Looking creepy.'' **************** The cafe was nearly empty, and none of the staff seemed to have noticed the almost-altercation. She could deck him and get away easily, even bail over the waist-high railing in their outdoor enclosure if he proved fast enough to subdue her by the time she got to the door. In spite of that, Amy sighed. He'd been charming, but didn't honestly seem slippery. That's the only reason she eased back into her chair, making sure to be forceful when she wrenched her arm out of his grip. He was actually stronger than she'd ever admit. ''There's still not much to say,'' she picked the conversation back up with a sour expression. ''I'm sober, I'm not as easy as you've probably read, and, like everybody else on the planet, I've done some shit I'm not proud of. Also I'm unemployed, in case you were expecting someone with actual assets.'' ''Are you pushing because you're more into women, or because you actually think I'm an asshole? I mean, you came to lunch--'' ''I came to lunch because last night, you almost convinced me that you weren't just looking to get your dick wet.'' ''If I was looking for that, I would've walked up to any girl at the bar and said 'Hi, I used to serve in the military, but now I'm a police officer who's up for promotion in eight months. I like dogs, I want two kids and I drive a Benz','' the words were matter-of-fact, not just pomp and preening, and he offered a small smile with them. ''And I wouldn't have been so persistent with the one who tried to reject me instantly.'' ''You like dogs?'' ''Ha! See? The line works.'' ''This would go smoother if you didn't laugh at me.'' ''That laugh wasn't at you. You're cute is all, lit up at the dog thing. You have any?'' ''Wikipedia didn't tell you?'' ''I don't believe everything I read. Plus they're not that thorough.'' ''I don't. Well... at my parents', yeah, but the most I've been able to commit to with my lifestyle is the cat my ex didn't fight me for.'' ''The woman, the husband or...?'' He opened his mouth again to try and backpedal when her face went blank. ''Not that I--'' ''Why would you even show up to a date with a girl you have to ask a question like that?'' Instead of an empty statement to fluff an ego he wasn't sure she was really even hiding, he smiled. ''So this is a date?'' ''I'm serious.'' ''So am I.'' ''It...'' She furrowed her brows. ''Don't be so...''' ''So...'' He raised his brows, making a show of how closely he was listening. ''Just don't be so... so difficult, okay?'' ''You can't possibly think that I'm the difficult one.'' ''I... come on, I'm not used to this.'' ''What, dates?'' ''It's not a--look, is what it is. And I need more iced tea, so...'' ''They'll be back around to refill it. Just stay and talk to me.'' Amy laughed. ''You're scared I'll run.'' ''Naw. But if you go to the bathroom, I'm gonna sweat worse than the fat guy on the treadmill next to me this morning. So... yeah, I guess I am a little scared.'' He'd admitted it, but tipped his head to where Amy's hand rested on her empty glass, tracing little circles in the beads of moisture collected on the outside. Her ring and pinky fingers twitched as she went, slicing a less deliberate path than the rest of her hand. ''And it looks like I'm not the only one.'' As soon as she saw that his eyes had fallen there, she pulled that arm off the table entirely, dropping both to her lap. Her tone changed for the hundredth time, having paced right back behind her defenses. ''What're you looking at?'' ''Your hand was...'' He shrugged and trailed off. The cycle between them was frustrating, but he wasn't the kind to give up easily - he just needed to avoid offending her too much. ''I just said I'm obviously not the only one who's a little nervous. That an injury or something?'' ''Is what?'' ''The resting tremor,'' he pointed to where her hand had been. ''Usually those're--'' ''Usually those are what? None of your business?'' Amy snapped. ''I agree.'' ''C'mon, the only reason I even noticed was because I had 'em on one side for a good month after I got dropped on my head in basic training,'' He'd once seen an episode of The Dog Whisperer where the little Mexican guy got a leash on a rabid pitbull by just changing the way he stood to ease tension and broadcast calm to the animal. He tried it for himself, slouching a little in the chair, trying to project some sort of reassurance. The Dog Whisperer can't use words, so there was at least one advantage in this version. ''Seriously, I wouldn't have noticed. Be self-conscious about it, they go away pretty fast anyway.'' There were two reasons he failed miserably just then: If The Dog Whisperer could use words to help calm a crazy dog - particularly a female - he almost certainly wouldn't have used those ones. His second failure was assuming the cause of her problem. ''I'm sure they do. So... I'm going home now.'' A third mistake is what led his brows to raise when she stood and actually walked away. He hadn't anticipated that she was already too walled-up to at least give him credit for effort. **************** Campbell lacked the same stealth and tact the Pierces tended to employ. When told of the intruder, she simply turned around, looking over her shoulder, still giving them her back. ''Can I help y--Ryan?'' ''Hey!'' the big man gave Quinn his charming smile first, but put his eyes to Amy right after. ''Didn't figure I'd see you here,'' again, he looked to Gregory, inviting himself to step up to the table and outstretch a hand. ''I'm Ryan. And you're...'' he faked not quite knowing, ''Quinn, right?'' The teen might not have been as skeptical as her notoriously paranoid mother, but she still cut a glance to Amy before she took the hand somewhat hesitantly. "Yeah. Quinn. And you're... Ryan?" When he let her hand go, it fell to her lap. "Who I obviously haven't been told about." Behind him, Campbell cringed and gave Quinn an apologetic shrug. It was the teen's reaction that drove him to speak again, looking between them. ''I'm sorry. I'm not interrupting something, am I?'' ''No--'' ''Kinda.'' The simultaneous response drew a little glare from Amy, clearing her throat. ''No, you're really not,'' she assured. ''Quinn, this is my buddy Ryan. There's not much else to say.'' ''There's not?'' he asked. ''Really? You're gonna be difficult now? C'mon, Ry--'' ''I don't mean you gotta tell her about going on dates with me, just might be nice to follow 'This is my buddy' up with 'He's a nice guy, so you shouldn't be stabbing him with your eyes like that'.'' "I'm a Pierce," Quinn said, setting her jaw. "You're lucky all I'm stabbing you with is my eyes." Amy forced a laugh, her brows coming up. "She's joking..." She tapped the girl's foot under the table, hard enough to notice. "Ryan's a police officer, and..." "And you've been going out on dates, apparently." It wasn't quite droll -- that implied humor. But she wasn't yelling, so at least that was good. "I guess I should have expected something like this..." She forced a smile, extending a hand. "So let's try this all over with me being at least twenty percent less of a brat. Quinn Gregory-Pierce." It was the first time Amy had ever heard the teen introduce herself with her mother's last name. "Good to meet you." ''You too. Amy mentioned she was gonna spend some time with you when she left this morning,'' the smile was very, very deliberate. ''Your, ah... your mom isn't with you today, is she, Quinny?'' '' 'Quinny'?'' The second time the little redhead kicked someone, it wasn't Quinn, but with legs that thick, Ryan barely seemed to notice. The girl shook her head. "No, she's out of town for the holiday, doing the family thing." She leaned back, looping her arm over the back of the seat. "I'd be there, but school is starting sooner at the new place." "The Ellis Academy, right? Sounds like some fancy stuff." "Eh, going to private school is totally lame." She waved a hand at the table. "Feel free to join us if you'd like. I know, like, nothing about you, and I'm dying to know all about Amy's new man." Quinn sounded... lighter than she usually did. Airier, as if she was intentionally dialing herself down. Even the smile was a bit wider, but that look in her eyes -- the careful, glittering study -- was pure Desade. ''He's not my--'' ''I'm really not,'' Ryan tried finishing the sentence for her, adding a laugh as he took his seat, patting a hand on Amy's thigh. ''She likes to remind me.'' ''--And I wanna clarify that whole 'When Amy left this morning' thing,'' Campbell added. ''He forgot to mention that we went for breakfast. Just breakfast.'' The big man nodded. ''Just breakfast,'' he said. ''Say, Quinn - is your friend always so... I mean, I don't wanna say she likes to keep her distance, but it's feelin' like I need to put a gun to her head to get her to go to admit to liking me, y'know?'' Amy frowned. ''You're gonna pick on me forever for this, aren't you?'' "I think..." Gregory put her elbows on the table, her chin in her hands, sighing dreamily. "I think she's looking for someone to sweep her off her feet. A real Prince -- or, I guess, Princess -- Charming. How did you guys meet? Bonus points if it's cute, because people will be asking you about it so much." ''Prince Charming? Really?'' the redhead rolled her eyes. ''Go ahead, Ryan. Tell her the magical tale of how you convinced me to go to lunch with you a couple of weeks ago.'' ''She, uh. She was just frowning in a bar, not drinking, and I--'' ''A real fairytale. And y'know what?'' Campbell interrupted the false banter by pushing away from the table. ''I think we're running late for that... for my haircut,'' she briefly cursed herself, because she'd actually have to get her hair cut to make that less of a lie. ''So, yeah. We'll... I mean, not trying to rush anyone off, but I'm sure I'll find time for you two to hang out again when... if Ryan and I get to know one another a bit more, yeah?'' He'd 'accidentally' happened upon them that day in the hopes that he'd push her ex's daughter farther away, not to alienate Amy herself, so the former soldier simply raised his hands innocently. ''Yeah, absolutely. Sorry if I...'' he cast his eyes down. ''Uh, yeah. You guys enjoy your day.'' Amy had not been expecting him to lean in for a peck on the lips just then, but when he did it, he didn't stick around long enough for her to retaliate (which, given their short history, would probably have ended in her storming off... again). Quinn watched him walk away, but Campbell just sat there, stunned. ''Oh, God.'' Quinn gagged. ''You're actually into that ape.'' ''I am not! Not that into him. Just... Wow. He...'' the tiniest smile crept onto her lips. ''Huh. He's... persistent.'' Calling him persistent would end up being far, far too polite a word.
