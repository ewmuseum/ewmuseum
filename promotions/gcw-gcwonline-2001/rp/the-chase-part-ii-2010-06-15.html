The Chase: Part II

15 June 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06/15/10 It was like the sky couldn’t make up its mind. If you were to look to the north, you’d be forgiven for thinking it was a perfect day for picnics and pick-up games of softball. The sunshine was plentiful and the skies a gentle shade of blue. But a simple one hundred-eighty degree turn to the south would make most people run for cover. The skies were dark and threatening. Thunder rumbled as lightning struck the ground just a couple of miles from the vehicle in view. Aaron Fujita stood in front of a beat up Chevy Blazer. The SUV would have had trouble being considered new in 2000, let alone ten years later. In his hands was a digital camera, loaded with extra attachments. Alongside him was a tripod holding a mini-DV video camera. Both were pointed towards the threatening sky. “She’s cooking now.” Tyler spoke into the thick moist air. Across the field lightning shot down from the sky and the ensuing thunder rumbled into earshot. Behind Tyler the passenger door of his truck slammed shut and Chloe rounded the front of the vehicle. She walked up to Aaron and immediately turned her attention to the sky as well. The two stood in silence for a few moments watching the wonder of nature before the beautiful redhead finally spoke. “Good inflow on the back end of it.” Chloe’s voice didn’t take Aaron’s focus off the threatening sky at hand. He simply nodded in agreement and kept his eyes fixed to the storm. The flat landscape of east-central Oklahoma provided a perfect view of the supercell. Chloe kept her position to the left of her chase partner and began to shake her while she observed the storm. Aaron caught her gesture out of the corner of his eye and turned to her. “What are you shaking your head for?” Aaron’s question was surprisingly polite. After the words escaped his lips he turned back to the storm and noticed a slight lowering of the clouds. The wall cloud as it is known hangs only a couple hundred feet above the ground and lurks ominously. Aaron focuses his camera lens in on the lowering and snaps a few pictures. He pauses for a few moments and then turns back to Chloe. “We need to get southwest of it.” Her words pierced Aaron’s ears sharply. It had been his decision to stop here to watch the storm. Call it eagerness to see some action on the first day of the chase or bad judgment, Aaron had put them in the wrong place. They were in no danger if the storm did produce a tornado but sitting to the north placed them in a horrible observation position. Aaron knew when he began to unload his equipment Chloe would notice this and show him up. There was nobody around to be shown up in front of, but Aaron didn’t like being shown up period. “I agree. The wall cloud is starting to rotate.” Aaron pointed to the sky momentarily to turn Chloe’s attention back to the sky. He began to take down the equipment, knowing that the appearance of a rotating wall cloud was one of the final steps before a tornado was produced. If the books were right they had about twenty minutes to get on the south side of the storm if they were to observe their first tornado of the trip. “I’ll get on the GPS and find us a quick route around.” Chloe bounded off to the passenger seat of the hail-dented chase vehicle. As she went Aaron turned his head to catch a glimpse of her rear end confined to a pair of very tight jean shorts. As she rounded the front of the Blazer and turned back to face him, he quickly turned his head back and finished packing up his equipment. A few seconds later he dumped it all into the backseat and jumped in to speed off. “You got the route?” He turned to look at the red-head as the engine roared to life. The sun shining into the window from the north gleamed through her hair that partially covered her tilted head. She remained focused on the GPS in her hands, giving Aaron time to examine her beauty. He had always been entranced by it, but never really gave it much thought other than one-sided lust. Every time he caught a glimpse of her eyes he seemed to lose his place. They were a gorgeous green the likes of which he had never seen before. “Go west a half a mile, south three miles and then a few miles back east and we should be in a good position.” Her words snapped Aaron from his trance and he began to pull away. The engine rumbled loudly and the wind whipped into their open windows as he sped down the rural Oklahoma road to their first turn. His eyes remained fixed on the road, but Chloe unbuckled her seat belt and knelt on her seat to turn back and look at the storm. “Hang on.” Chloe braced herself as Aaron made the turn at a high rate of speed. She remained steady in her position as she continued to monitor their target. Aaron tried to keep his eyes on the road, but found them wandering back to the female figure beside him. Normally he wouldn’t think twice about catching a nice view, but for some reason he felt different about Chloe. “It’s lowering further, step on it.” He followed her request by mashing the pedal to the floor. The almost ancient vehicle continued forward, slowly gaining speed. Chloe turned back to sit in her seat properly now that the storm was visible out the driver’s window. Aaron looked left and observed what could now be called a funnel cloud just a few miles from them. Chloe smiled at the site and reached to the backseat, retrieving Aaron’s digital camera. She adjusted herself in the seat as the camera turned on and began leaning over into a position in between Aaron and the steering wheel. She wiggled her way closer to the window to get a good picture of the storm. “Excuse you.” Aaron’s playful words brought a laugh from Chloe as she snapped picture after picture of the storm. Aaron’s eyes once again began to wander and found comfort in a view of the cleavage visible from her black tank top. He fought his male instincts to look but couldn’t help taking a peek every now and then. Finally Chloe leaned back to her seat as they approached their next turn. It was a good thing she moved because one big bump in the road would have brought Aaron the closest he’d ever been to receiving road-head. “I don’t know about this one Aaron. It’s becoming disorganized.” Aaron concentrated on the turn first and then looked ahead at the storm just two miles in front of them. The tornado had never formed and the wall cloud was suddenly beginning to lift. Aaron pushed the truck harder, speeding to catch up with the rotation, about to cross the road. After what seemed like an eternity Aaron brought the vehicle to a stop less than a half mile from the storm. The two jumped out and stared up at the weakening storm ahead. “It’s done. Never produced.” Chloe sighed at Aaron’s words but kept her gazed fixed to the sky above. He turned to look at her and began to feel an urge building up inside of him. What could it hurt to ask her out on a date? She was already here, wasn’t she? “Well that sucks. Hey would you like…” “Shh.” She interrupted him as she turned back to the scanner in the truck. She listened closely to the words being broadcast and then began working at the GPS again. Aaron sighed once himself and kicked a few pieces of gravel as he turned his gaze from the sky to the ground to Chloe and back to the sky. Whatever urge he had ten seconds ago was gone for now. “State police are reporting heavy damage to a town about forty miles from here. Want to head over and look?” Aaron nodded to her question and slowly climbed back into his truck. He fired the engine back up as her door slammed shut. He wasn’t sure what they’d find when they got to their destination, but he was sure that it could be a very awkward ride.
