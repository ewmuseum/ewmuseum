Date: Various .:: ::: ::. McFarlane Estate Week of Worldwide 39 Lia nodded, and looked away from Dawn, out across the rolling grounds of the Estate. The light beige of the dead wintry grass showed through the snow in spots, a reminder of a homeland where the snow would stay on the ground until April, much like here. A place Lia would never stop missing. But though she couldn't be there for this winter, another sense of purpose had taken the place of needing to be in Czechia and look for her missing brother. There was only one thing on her mind at the moment, one thing that could make the difference in how this group went forward from this moment. And it wasn't exactly a pretty thought. 'You don't have to do this, Dawn. Not if you don't want to.' A dead silence followed the statement, as though there really was nothing that Dawn could say to it, or perhaps as though Dawn hadn't expected to be given the opportunity to walk away so clearly. Lia couldn't tell, from the expression on her face, whether she wanted to do so or not. 'Lia, are you cr-... That's nuts. Why on earth would you think I wouldn't want to be here with you, during one of the most important moments of your career?' Lia shrugged. 'You know that I'll be here, doing this, no matter whose support I have or haven't. The fact that three people are aligned with me this time around is sheer luck, though it's luck I wouldn't trade in a million years. Don't do what you think is best for me. Do what you think is best for you.' The look on Dawn's face was somewhat wounded as she turned from her lover, no doubt wondering what it was that made her seem so weak, could Lia have guessed. 'What do you think is the best for me?' It was a typical stubbornly-Dawn question, but Lia nonchalantly answered, her tone level and measured. 'I don't know if I can answer that question. You've such a sense of being beholden to people that I'm frightful to even tell you what I'd like for you to do, because I don't want to influence you to do something for me when this is supposed to be about you.' That arrow seemed to strike some sort of mark. Dawn didn't answer, but instead allowed Lia to continue on. 'I do think that the answer isn't here,' she said, her fingers pressing to her stomach, 'Nor do I necessarily think it's here,' they journeyed upwards to indicate the head. Lia sighed softly. 'The best answer that you have will come from right here,' here, her fingers pressed to her chest, indicating where, presumably, her heart was, 'But sometimes that's the hardest answer of all, because sometimes, one's heart will tell one things one doesn't want to hear. You can't force this, Dawn. You have to be here because you want to be, and because you can be, because you've found some truth in this. Whatever Lilith has said to you, whether I believe it or not, is something that you have to deal with. If you feel like you need to take the time to seek answers on that on your own, I will not stand in your way.' 'How could I spend the rest of my life trying to chase down a being that's only findable when she's decided she wants to be found? Especially if I've got something tangible right here, a real way to make a difference that I'd be turning down to go and essentially chase down phantoms.' Lia's arms folded over her chest, and for the first time, this discussion started to take on a slightly confrontational air. 'Because sometimes the best choices aren't the ones that make the most sense.' That sank in for a moment, and in the time that it was doing so, Lia could practically feel Dawn's gaze on her, reproachful and unrelenting. Dawn's hands on her shoulders brought her back down to earth forced her to look into green eyes that were a strange echo of her own. 'Lia, please listen to me. My place is here. I could have stayed in Japan when Virgil called and said he'd found you, but I came back. I've made my choice. If something changes, you'll be the first to know.' A silent surge of relief went through Lia's entire body, her muscles loosening their tight grip on her bones. She allowed herself a small smile, and then Dawn relented as well, shaking her head. 'You're the world's worst at pushing people away because you want them to be close to you.' Lia smirked. 'Yeah, and my competition for that award is staring me dead in the eyes right now.' 'Hey!' But it was mock offense, not real tension that permeated the room at this moment. Though such things had been known to change in a split second, for the moment, everything had fallen back into place. .:: ::: ::. Unknown Location Unknown Date I can't very well look forward without looking at where I've been. Right now, everything's tied up in a neat little package, ready to be packed up and hauled wherever I want to haul it. A new beginning is an extremely promising possibility. A chance to get away from all the hounding, all the ridiculous bullshit that's been crowding my life for the past... So I can't really remember when it hasn't been. But ever since I got into this PTC business, I've been struggling to find a way to do things on my own. The whole Corvus thing (or whatever he's calling himself now) didn't work out because I started stealing his spotlight and he didn't like that. The whole Soma thing didn't work out because he didn't much like the idea that I'd take the power he offered me without becoming a little opinionless automaton for him to control. The whole Zenith thing didn't work out because... well, in my honest opinion because Global Wrestling wasn't ready to be enlightened. Despite the fact that I've never openly said it, apparently I hold the opinion that I can't hack it on my own without someone guiding or teaching me. Maybe that's a holdover from when I was living in Prague and Vienna, learning how to use my gifts. Whatever my own delusions, it's an inaccurate perception now. Whoever I'm around, whatever influence they purport to exert over me, I am my own. I can't help but thinking that whatever happened to me when I was locked up under that wretched tinderbox of a bar in the middle of the Nevada Desert may have something to do that- because it was then that I made a choice. .:: ::: ::. Some time in December Middle of nowhere, Nevada Despite the fact that Lia Vlaszy's body was very much present in the cell below the bar, aptly named the Hell's Mouth, her soul was quite absent. Anyone who had seen her haunting Global Wrestling in the past few weeks would possibly have noted that she was only a shell of herself, if they'd happened to see her before she escaped through an arena backdoor once her match was over. It's not very likely, however, that they could have guessed where it was had they pinpointed the problem. Quiet literally, Ms. Vlaszy's soul was journeying through hell. It felt like it had been weeks since she'd first starting this journey, but God only knew how long it had been. Endless, interminable span of days, melting into sludge and sliming away before any one in particular could be held up to the light and examined for the lessons that it might have produced. Still, she and the Italian Gentleman journeyed further and further into the earth. Had she not already felt herself to be a tainted being, she would have felt dirtied by all these damned wallowing in sin before her, but living a life as she did often carried with it a certain dehumanizing effect, a blunting of morals. Now they stood on a floor of crystalline ice, she at his side, just out of reach of him should she wish to lace her arm through his. He had been like this for the entire journey- defending her, yet at the same time distant. The encounter with Azrail had proved the only time when he was openly emotional regarding her, and after that, he had gone back to his typical detached half-interest, though she noticed he kept her a little closer to his side than before. She might have thought this place beautiful. Ice crystals hung from the roof, each one formed into a grotesque image that was stunning in its intricacy. Yet her weary eyes did not wish to examine anything up close. She only wanted to go home, though it seemed like it had been so long that she had half-forgotten what home was, save a hazy memory. Beauty was the farthest thing from her mind. Her guide could tell she was tiring, but had he any pity for her, it had been spent long ago. He was only doing his job now, escorting her to the massive stone door that lay not twenty yards away. 'Signor... why am I here?' Her voice was weary, tired of this journey. He turned to her with a slight harrumph. 'It is the end of your journey. If you really wish to know the answer to the question, I am sure that the gentleman inside can help you with that.' She blanched at the coldness of his tone, but took up the weary trudge towards the door. Once standing in front of it, she couldn't help but stop and focus on the details of it. Lettering was carved all over it, various sentences and sayings that were familiar to her, which came murmured through her parched lips as she read them. 'Me duce tutus eris... vox populi vox dei... aspirat prima fortuna labori...' Her fingers reached out to touch one in particular, the letters carved larger than the other. 'What are these?' He shrugged, as though the only emotion he could muster was indifference. 'They are lies.' 'Lies?' He nodded, the brim of his hat briefly coming down to obscure the burning coal-like orbs that took place of normal eyes in his face. 'Si. They are lies that may be familiar to you... each soul approaching this door would see something different.' Her words died in her throat before ever forming, her fingers half clutching at the words in front of her. Amor vincit omnia. Love conquers all. Perhaps she had pushed a bit too far, however, because the door began swinging open. She looked back at the Italian Gentleman with apprehension in her eyes. 'He is ready for you.' Something impelled her forward, though she didn't want to go. She wanted to stay here with him, and avoid whatever was behind this door. But there was no more avoiding of fate by this point. She was pulled inside, and the door slowly slid closed behind her, leaving her alone in this hollowed-out ice chamber with spires and pillars reaching up what looked like miles. The chill was almost too much even for her, who had long grown to ignore any outward feelings of discomfort. She was so caught up in between the near-pain of the bitter cold and the stunning sight of the ornate ice-sculpture before her that she hardly noticed the desk sitting in the middle of it all. Behind it, she suddenly noticed, sat a hulking figure, clad all in black. For that matter, his skin was a coal black as well, the only color emanating from him being his eyes, which burned much like the Italian Gentleman's, only they were a fire-yellow, then a fire-orange, dancing and undulating hypnotically. 'Welcome.' For some reason, the booming, thudding voice echoed off the walls, and sent ice shooting straight up her spine. A smile stretched sickeningly over the visage of the being in front of her, which was perhaps worse than him having no expression at all. 'Are you uncomfortable here? Were you perhaps expecting flames?' 'Why would there be flames in hell?' Her voice was perhaps a smidge too defiant for his taste, but nevertheless, he kept his cocksure demeanor. 'Ah yes... you're quite the elderly soul, aren't you? I forget sometimes that we've had a paradigm shift from ice to fire sometime in these past few centuries. No matter,' he said, waving a hand casually, 'Have you enjoyed your visit?' 'Oh yes, it's just been splendid,' she snapped, the sarcasm dripping off of her words. 'I could have depended on you to appreciate it. You've quite the taste for the macabre, I was hoping you would enjoy the show,' he responded, refusing to acknowledge her irritation. 'Who are you? What do you want from me?' He shifted his weight to lean an elbow on the desk, armor clanking with every movement. She could seen now that what he wore was not cloth, but some sort of black metal plate mail that glimmered in the stark white light of the room, coming from an undiscernible source. He laughed under his breath, laughter which seemed to rumble the cavern's ground. She nearly slipped on the ice as it rumbled beneath her feet. 'I think you know the answer to that question, Lia. Look deep inside yourself.' Her jaw hardened into a stubborn line as a chill wind whipped around her. No matter what theatrics he threw at her, she wasn't going to play his game. The being sighed irritatedly. 'Fine, if you refuse to use that fine head on your shoulders... you may address me as Sammael.' 'You mean Lucifer.' He rolled his eyes, the flamed pupils tilting away for the first time from their piercing fixation on her. 'Lucifer is such a ridiculously vulgar name. Though it's not much better, I prefer the Hebrew. Do call me Sammael.' She clenched her fists at her side, refusing to shiver, refusing to even give any inkling that she was uncomfortable or frightened. Her teeth threatened to chatter, but she willed the muscles to remain in place. She would call him nothing. Sammael raised an eyebrow, only perceptible because the black of his hair glimmered against his black skin. 'My, you've become even more stubborn, haven't you? I suppose in lieu of cooperation, I'll have to do a bit more work, won't I? Let's see if this makes you more comfortable.' There was a sharp pop as he snapped his fingers, and before her eyes, his form morphed into an ugly amalgamous shape, and then into a rather familiar visage. Before her sat a being with golden skin, and shoulder-length red hair. The irises of his eyes glimmered a red-black, and his bearing was regal, that of an African prince seated on the high throne of his wildnerness kingdom. Could her eyes have fallen out of their sockets and rolled across the floor, they likely would have. Her jaw dropped in shock. 'Leinad!' A laughter came through the being's throat, the same half-congenial, half-beastly laugh that Leinad Soma had always had when she had been in his presence. Months ago, he had disappeared, dropped off the face of the earth without a trace, or so it seemed. 'You wonder where I've been, presumably.' Numbly, she nodded. He seemed to feel that she was properly cowed into submission, and so he went on. 'I simply got busy. I'd neglected months of my work down here, you see, and decided that I simply couldn't wait any longer to come back. Aside from that, living topside can get terribly boring, as you might imagine.' Sammael-as-Soma shifted his weight in the desk chair to look at his shimmering half-moon fingernails. 'Besides... I wanted to test you and see how you did on your own. I confess, you haven't completely disappointed me. I've gone to a great deal of trouble to get you down here, as I'm certain that you understand. We have a few things to discuss.' Fear seized her, colder than the icy wind of the room. 'I don't have anything to discuss with you.' He laughed again, that easygoing Soma laugh. 'Au contraire, my dear. I think that you'll want to hear this proposition. After all, you thought you didn't have anything to talk to me about when I offered you my blood, my power... now that you have it, I dare say you're not disappointed with the gift, are you?' 'No.' He shrugged, pressing his palms together. A ring glimmered on his forefinger, the stone a blood-red ruby. 'I think you at least owe it to me to hear me out.' Her eyes narrowed. 'Fine. Make it quick.' 'My, my,' he said, arching an eyebrow, 'You're presumptuous, coming to my demesne and attempting to give orders. I always did like you for that.' His amused expression faded. 'I have always had plans for a being like yourself. It gets lonely and quite boring down here, attempting to do my work by myself, you understand. I needed companionship, but more than that, an aide. Someone intelligent enough not to become my slave, but aligned enough with my ideals to work well by my side. Certainly, you fit the bill for a proper consort, wouldn't you say?' She couldn't answer. It was as though her tongue were bound up in knots at the horror of the thought. Revulsion coursed through her veins, but plainly, Sammael was not finished yet. 'It's the most natural option. You have a good deal of my power now, power that I have not seen fit to give to many aside from you. It's only fitting that you learn how to use it in its rightful context. Join me. Become my Crimson Queen, and no one will ever think you are a joke again, Lia.' The wind suddenly gusted behind her fiercely, as though trying to yank her to his side. First it bent her double at the waist, so chill was its fingers, but slowly, she inched back up to her full height, feeling for the first time, a faint sheen of ice that was beginning to accumulate on her skin. The direction changed, now nearly yanking her hair out by the roots as it reversed. He had known exactly what angle to go for. She supposed that was his job, to always know the weak spot to dig at to make people bend to his will. The question was what sort of price she would have to pay if she took his offer. There was always a price. 'Well?' he asked, spreading his hands apart and gazing at her expectantly, 'There are no tricks here, my dear. You say yes, and become everything you have always wanted to be. Anyone who chooses to defy you will be struck down in a hail of brimstone, if that is your wish.' Her eyes had narrowed so much that she could barely see him. 'No.' Immediately, he shot out of his chair, slamming his palms down on the desk. 'Impudence!' he fairly roared, his eyes flashing flame-colored again. 'You've always given me a choice, Leinad,' she said, her voice carrying surprisingly far across the room, 'I've made it.' 'You're a fool.' 'That may be. But if I'm a fool, at least I die by my own terms instead of yours.' Rage trembled across his every feature, and she could practically hear it roaring through his veins. 'I could kill you myself.' She held her palms open, prepared for the blow. Her defiance only caused him to laugh. 'It would be far more enjoyable to let Lilith take care of the job herself. I thought you were worth something, Lia. I'm disappointed. Rest assured, though, that you have seen the last of me. It's enough for me to know the sort of cursed existence you'll be living. The chance to rule hell, and instead, I suppose you'll choose now to quest after the heaven that has already damned you. Enjoy.' The thunderous sound around her gave her the distinct impression that the walls of the mock-office were about to crash down around her and leave her crushed forever underneath the ice. Her hands clawed up to cover her face, but abruptly, it simply stopped- and as she looked up from her cowering position, she found herself face-to-face once again with the Italian Gentleman. 'I'm afraid you may have displeased him with your choice,' he said, as dispassionately as ever. Lia nodded, almost proudly. 'But I'm free.' He tipped his hat to her. 'Si.' She could have sworn she could almost detect a smile in his voice. .:: :: ::. McFarlane Estate Week of Worldwide 39 The clouds above were threatening snow. Yet Lia Vlaszy still stood on the roof of the McFarlane Estate, letting the wind whip through her long ebony hair which was, as always, unfettered by any sort of tie. She squinted into the slushy precipitation that was currently falling, mind heavy with another week's expectations- but it was more than that. It was impossible at this point to be unaware of what she had given up to come back to her everyday existence. At times, it was terrifically mundane, painfully so. But perhaps there was more to her choice than just choosing routine over mastery. What good was being the Queen of anything if you had to share your lordship with someone you hated? With someone who had abandoned you once, all for the purpose of 'a test' and expected you to come running back like a lost puppy to its owner's arms? Taking orders was a thing of the past. For good. Below her, in the palatial mansion, lay the world. Somewhere down there, Dawn McFarlane, long-legged Amazon warrioress, was pacing the grounds. Perhaps fixing dinner. Perhaps sitting in an armchair reading a book. Somewhere down there, Virgil Ambrosi, though cold to everyone else, was thinking of her. The one being in her life who had fought tooth and nail to get back to her side more than once. And too, Vivica Valentine, the little spitfire whom nobody had expected to amount to anything- nobody except Lia, that is, who had always seen the potential underneath the 'career tag team wrestler' label that had been unfairly put upon her. Now, they all had a chance at a fresh start in GCW. A chance to live up to their collective potential. She had disappointed them all once by disappearing without a trace, and getting herself into a fine predicament that she'd needed help to dig herself out of. Though Lia had been in her own personal hell in the middle of that Nevada desert, she knew it was nothing compared to the collective hell of the three people she now found at her side. Turning into the snow that was now gusting down, she started for the access door that led back down into the mansion, making a silent promise to the winds: she would not fail them again.
