Date: Present Day ---- Present Day. Thom LeStrange's Hotel Room, Tokyo. Midday. ---- The telephone on the table next to the bed rang, disturbing the relative calm of the room. Many stories down, outside the closed windows, Tokyo city roared, buzzed and screeched. However, the room was sufficiently high up that all of this commotion, this activity was reduced a dull hum. Thom LeStrange, dressed as ever in a bespoke grey Prince of Wales checked suit, crossed the room gracefully to answer the telephone. "LeStrange." "Mr. LeStrange, a Mr. Dave Yeomans from Wrestling Hype Magazine is in the lobby to see you." "Ah yes, I'm expecting him, please send him up." Thom replaced the phone, and strolled to the centre of the room. It was a beautiful room; practically palatial but not in an ostentatious or tasteless fashion. It was sizeable and airy, and very well lit, giving it a refreshing air. All of the furniture was beautiful but refined, giving the room a refined rather than grandiose feeling. On the walls hung reproductions of several tasteful watercolours. The bed was mahogany, a four-poster; in front of the window, looking out over the city was a writing desk. The wardrobe unit was also of mahogany, and consumed almost a whole wall; the actual wardrobe space was divided in two, with the middle part made into a drinks cabinet. On the opposite side of the room to the large bed sat two sofas and a coffee table. All in all, a sophisticated and relaxing room. Thom looked about, scanning every corner of the room to check that it was tidy. Looking at himself in the elegant full-length mirror, he adjusted his tie and flattened his hair. Satisfied that his appearance was without flaw, the Englishman sat down on one of the comfortable sofas, and closed eyes, allowing himself a few moments of complete tranquility before his visitor arrived, his breathing becoming deeper, his muscles relaxing, and his thoughts floating to the very dark recesses of his mind. A few minutes later, he was snapped from his reverie by the rapping of knuckles upon his hotel door. Thom leapt to his feet, and crossed the room to open the door in his inimicably graceful walk. "Mr. Yeomans, do come in and make yourself at home." Dave Yeomans stepped into the room, his eyes momentarily widening as he digested the splendour of the place. He sat down upon one of the sofas, and placed his messenger bag at his feet. Thom would, at a guess, place his age in the late thirties, but Dave Yeomans had boyish looks that struggled against the hint of grey emerging at his temples. He was dressed smartly yet informally, in a pair of brown corduroy trousers, a plain white collared shirt, and a grey marl cashmere sweater. As the journalist made himself comfortable, Thom moved over towards the drinks cabinet. "Might I offer you a drink, Mr. Yeomans? The hotel has stocked my room very nicely, and I'm positive I can't consume all of this on my own..." On the sofa, Dave Yeomans chuckled, his eyes gleaming with a strange fervour as he noted the impeccable manners of his host, the implacable wall of manners and bonhomie with which he had been met. "Not for me, thank you Mr. LeStrange. I don't think my job is that tenable that I can drink on the job." Now it was the Englishman's turn to chuckle. "I am quite certain, Mr. Yeomans, that you are being modest. It seems you are the consummate professional. Oh, and let's dispense with the formalities. Please call me Thom." Thom reached for a glass, into which he poured a liberal amount of gin, before adding the contents of a small can of Schweppes tonic water. As the flavoursome mixture fizzed, he returned to the coffee table and sat down on the sofa opposite his guest. The journalist had now extracted a small tape recorder from his messenger bag and placed it on the coffee table. "Well, Thom, I don't want to consume too much of your time, so shall we begin?" "Of course." "Ok... Well there are many pressing issues I'd like to ask you, particularly with regard to current GCW affairs, but as this is, I believe, the first big interview you've given to a wrestling publication, I thought it would be a good idea for the fans to get to know a little more about you first, outside of vital statistics. Could you give us a short personal history? You know, a quick look at the man behind the mask, and so on." "Of course. Well, I was born on the 9th of April. I grew up in the house that is still my home, in Biddenham, a small village near Bedford. I attended Bedford School from the age of seven to eighteen, and from there I went to St. Catharine's College, Cambridge, where I studied French and Spanish. After a year or so of travelling, I trained as a wrestler, and then signed a contract with GCW." "Thanks very much, Thom. Now, I believe at school and at college you were a gifted athlete? You played rugby, right?" "That's very kind of you to say so. Personally, I wouldn't lay claim to having been 'gifted', but I knew what I was doing on a rugby pitch, yes." "And I believe you played inside centre? We're an American magazine, so if you could describe what that entails for our readers, that'd be great..." "Yes, I did indeed play inside centre. I suppose it's rather tricky to explain to someone not familiar with the game of rugby. You see, unlike your American football sport, players aren't specialised; there's no separate offensive and defensive teams, as I believe you have?" "That's right..." "Well, playing centre requires a fairly all-round skillset, but my main responsibilities were tackling the opposition backs in open play, and when in possession of the ball, trying to make crashing running breaks. Quite often I'd have to kick too." "Ah, I see. I guess it compares to part safety, part running back and part punter to our football fans. So, turning to GCW matters, I guess the first item of discussion is last week's WorldWide. Were you disappointed not to have made it to the finals of the Rampage tournament?" "Well of course, any loss is a disappointment, but one can't let these sorts of things nag at one for too long. One has to stay positive. My hope now is that Mr. Bagwell is able to do in the finals what I would have liked to have done to Murray myself. As long as the end result is the same, I suppose whichever one of us it is that administers such a crushing victory is ineffectual." "So you're pulling for Bagwell in the Rampage finals then?" "Well, Mr. Yeomans, the wrestling business is a cut-throat industry, and so as a general rule I tend not to, as you say, "pull" for anyone but myself. But in this case, I must admit I do have ulterior motives." "Ah yes, the now infamous Murray-LeStrange feud. I should imagine you're disappointed not to have a chance to battle the Scotsman at Rampage." "Of course. But I feel confident that there will be other opportunities. I shouldn't imagine the ill will between us will dissipate any time soon. And, of course, I've been given the opportunity to capture gold, and so I've certainly not suffered for failing to reach the finals, I'm sure you will agree." "Of course. Could you say a few words about your opponents in the upcoming match - Clyde Fox, Mark Matthews, and Docta Z?" "Naturally. I shouldn't dream of denouncing my opponents in any respect - they're all fine grapplers and I'd be a fool to underestimate them, but I'd be lying if I said I didn't feel calm and confident. This Clyde Fox chap is a little strange though, I must say. I'm not entirely sure I understand him. In fact, I don't understand him a jot. He seems to keep babbling on about Poke-a-something, about which I'm clueless. I'm not convinced we shouldn't be calling the chaps in white coats, if you know what I mean, but people seem to find him endearing, and one can't argue with public opinion. And no one has separated him from that title thus far, one has to give him that." "So where do you see yourself after Rampage?" "Well, with any luck, with a newly-won title to defend! But one doesn't like to tempt fate, so if you don't my not responding to the question - no comment!" "Thanks, Thom, that should be all I need. I shan't cut into your schedule any longer. Thanks very much for this interview." "It was a pleasure and a privilege, Mr. Yeomans, so thank you too." Both men rose to their feet, and shook hands. The journalist clicked off his tape recorder, returned it to his messenger bag, and moved towards the door. Thom strode ahead of him, to open the door for him. They exhange pleasant goodbyes once more, and Thom closed the door again. ---- Present Day. Kissa Ginza Nightclub, Tokyo. Midnight. ---- Thom LeStrange slammed shut the cubicle door in the bathroom of the Kissa Ginza nightclub, pulled down the seat and sat down. From the pocket of his trousers he extracted a small plastic bag, containing fine white crystals of MDMA. Before doing anything else, he licked his finger and dipped it into the bag, the almost powder-like crystals cloying to his damp fingertip. He placed the fingertip on his tongue, waiting for his mouth to salivate before swallowing the crystals, grimacing at the foul chalky taste, before rubbing the dregs still clinging to his finger on his gums and the roof of his mouth. His almost compulsive drive to get the first dab into his system now sated, he relaxed back into the seat. From the same pocket he fished out a packet of small cigarette papers. He pulled one out, and laid it flat on his lap. He tore it in two, and disposed of one half. He carefully poured some of the crystals onto the centre of the cigarette paper - about two-hundred milligrammes, he estimated. He closed the seal of the bag, and returned it to his pocket. Carefully picking up the cigarette paper with steady hands, Thom ensured that the crystals of MDMA were piled in the centre, before twisting the paper around it into a small parcel. He placed the parcel onto his tongue, and swallowed. Forty-five minutes later, Thom was coming up hard. Before the physical effects manifested themselves, he could feel his mind beginning to race. He became aware of his thoughts moving swiftly, his eyes wide, hardly blinking, darting in all directions to take in everything around him. Whenever he attempted to focus on something, his eyes skittered wildly. He had attempted to check his mobile phone moments earlier, but had been unable to focus on the screen; it had turned into a darting flash of light. He felt surges of energy course through him, as if they were crashing around his insides and slowly working their way up to his brain. At the same time, despite the surge of energy driving him on, a state of serene euphoria was overtaking him, making him feel excitable, but very calm. In an instant, all was well in the universe. All the worries that constantly sat on the Englishman's shoulders were shaken loose, all the resentment that lay within him was appeased, and he felt the familiar sense of self-peace that he had come to crave. Anything he felt self-conscious about, anything he disliked about himself (and yes, even for a man with an ego as inflated as LeStrange's, there was still room for occasional self-criticism), disappeared, carried away on a current of acceptance. Looking around at the hordes of fellow clubbers around him, Thom felt not detached disdain as he usually did in a crowd, but kinship. Now he was dancing, barely able to control his body. Usually, Thom was a reserved and cautious dancer. In fact, usually, Thom was not a dancer. He usually considered it nothing but a primitive mating ritual, a precursor to sex, and not a sophisticated one at that. But in moments such as this, when the euphoria of the MDMA experience was at its peak, Thom danced like a man unleashed. There was no conscious control over his movements, no practiced or conventional moves. Beyond, or rather indifferent to thought, his body shuddered and shook as it pleased, his synapses firing at will with no over-ride from his brain. With a sense of detachment he felt and saw his body moving beyond his control, expressing itself freely as it pleased. ---- Present Day. Thom LeStrange's Hotel Room, Tokyo. Early morning. ---- Thom LeStrange sat upright in his hotel bed, a contented smile on his face. The covers lay over his lap, and he had propped himself upright with a pillow, and he sat, simply smiling and taking in the first light of morning. Beside him, a young lady strayed in and out of a light sleep, snuggled under the covers. Reaching over to the bedside table, Thom grabbed a bottle of Jack Daniel's, now empty except for the dregs. He unscrewed the cap, and threw it to one side. He threw his head back and drained the last of the whiskey, before hurling the bottle across the room. It hit the wall and shattered, prompting the girl next to him to come to with a start. Thom laughed to himself. The girl moaned, a disgusted look on her face.. "What'd you do that for? You're drunk..." "I'm not drunk. I'm just cancelling out the MDMA." "You are drunk. You've done nothing but drink since we finished. You're drunk." "I'm not paying you to postulate. How about some quiet time, dear." "No. Talk. When you picked me up, you were buzzing on pills. Your pupils were like fucking dinnerplates. Then since we got back here, you've drunk a whole bottle of whiskey. Believe me, a lot of my clients are just the same. What's your excuse?" "Alright, alright. Now, you probably look at me, and think - wow, fortunate chap. Educated, smart, athletic, rich. But do you have any idea how disheartening it is to be constantly let down by the world? Imagine if you were born with nothing. The world would be a vast, exciting place, where you've got nowhere to go but up. Now imagine what it's like being born at the top. There is nowhere to go but down. Nothing impresses you, nothing excites you. The best any object, experience or success can ever do is equal the life you already lead. I walk around feeling like a stranger in the world. Now tell me that's not depressing..." There was a silence in the room that lasted at least thirty seconds before the call-girl responded. "Cry me a river, you arrogant, over-sensitive fuckwit."
