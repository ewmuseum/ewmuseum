Outskirts The evening wind was warm, and stank of sunset and humidity, demon’s breath coming on with the rising of the moon. Perhaps the woman who roared to a stop on the sleek black bike, painted with macabre purplish shadows was a harbinger of it. Calvin “Cal” Jackson could swear he saw skulls leaping out of it and howling toward him in the dusk. But that was crazy talk. The truth was, Cal didn’t need to think about anything but what was going on in front of him. He had plenty of skeletons in his closet, and if he screwed this up? out they’d come. He knew Fontaine well enough to know that he’d keep a promise like that. She approached him, her jade-shimmer eyes containing some note of cockiness. Cyrus had told her plenty about Cal Jackson, and how he was running scared because he was protecting his now-vampire sister from the Society of Leopold? but he hadn’t told her that Cal would be such a little kitten. He looked like he was about to faint from fright. Slowly, her delicate petal-white fingers pushed back the velvet of her hood. Her fingernails glimmered in the shaky light of the nearby light pole that was, other than the milemarker 237 post, the only distinguishing characteristic of this meeting place. She smiled at him, and in it, he saw Hell awaken. The urge to cross himself was strong, but he clenched his fingers into the pockets of his leather jacket. “Good evening, Mr. Jackson,” she said to him, with a soft incline of her head downwards. She didn’t sound like she was from around here, her accent a clipped, Slavic-sounding one, deep and smooth in tone. He nodded to her gruffly, narrowing his brown eyes underneath his shaggy gelled hair. The toothpick that he clenched in his teeth threatened to snap. “I’m sure Mr. Fontaine informed you, but I am Ljudmilu Ionescu.” He nodded. “Yeah, figured as much. You fit the description he gave me.” Sick. She looked rather ill, as though she’d been ill for a long time. Like his sister looked. If she told him to dance on the head of a pin and piss blood, he’d have to get his tap-shoes out of the car. The tooth pick spiraled to the gravel of the emergency lane, slow suicide. He leaned against his fox-body Mustang, black, 1986, kept in perfect condition despite its age. “Maybe you better start from the beginning, give me some clues as to who you’re looking for.” She pressed her hands together in front of her, cast her eyes down at them. “He’s a fairly large man, I would say about six-foot-four. He glowered. “Lady, do you know how many BEINGS I meet of just that description” People who can change their appearance are a dime a dozen in the line I work in, and “otherworldly?” You gotta be kiddin? me. I know you’re smarter than that, to come out here with that vague pile of crap, so how about you tell me a little more info if you want my help?” She tried to resist the urge to smile, but instead forced out an irritated sounding sigh. “Vasek Cerny Kral,” she said, her voice quiet, but the name rolling off her tongue silken-soft, “Victorious Black King, in Czech. What’d you say he looked like again?” At that, she couldn’t prevent a smile from slipping through. “I haven’t seen him in years, I couldn’t possibly make a guess as to what he’s looking like now, what sort of style” This is a rather overdue debt.” His stomach churned, churned because that could only mean one thing: vampire. These godforsaken beasts were everywhere. He tried not to show his disdain. “And this guy” I take it he’s a bloodsucker too? Like? you?” “Like me,” she said, her childlike demeanor fading and becoming more what he expected’that unnaturally regal bearing, the proud head, those taut animalistic poses he was used to seeing. Thoughts he didn’t want to entertain. The flowing mane of ebon hair streaming from under her helmet, and one last look at those creepy peering skulls on her bike was the last Cal Jackson ever saw of Lia Vlaszy. It only bothered him in that he had the sense that he’d just been looking Death in the face, and somehow walked away unscathed. .:: ::: ::. Dawn was tapped a bit differently than Virgil’with Virgil, it was an implicit, unspoken confidence. Without troops, she had no pretense to concern herself with. Wearily, she trudged to the library. .:: ::: ::. The Library Over the last several days, the Steel Rose had worn her bottom lip almost raw. There was a lot to think about, not the least of which being the weaknesses Shadow had pointed out to her earlier. As far as subtlety was concerned Dawn knew she was the weakest link...by a LONG shot, and there was only one real way to fix that...by learning from a master.
