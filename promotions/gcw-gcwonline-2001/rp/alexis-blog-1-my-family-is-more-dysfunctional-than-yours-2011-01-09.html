Alexis’ Blog #1: My Family Is More Dysfunctional Than Yours

9 January 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/09/11 (The image is a still one, a picture of Ashley O’Ryan in what appears to be his mid-twenties. Next to him is an athletic looking brunette possibly a few years younger. They’re both grinning into the camera, toasting it with beer, sitting on barstools with their backs to the bar. The voice of Alexis O’Ryan can be heard in voiceover.) Alexis: My parents met in the wrestling circuit, and began dating while they were working for the “Fantasy Wrestling Alliance”. (The intonation of her voice actually does sound as if she is putting that name in quotes. The picture of the couple is replaced by a black background with three large, stylized white letters; FWA.) Alexis: Never say that name in front of either of them. Ever. Ev-er. (A red circle and slash like a no smoking sign appears over the letters, flashing and making an annoying beeping sound.) Alexis: Long story. (The picture of the young couple returns.) Alexis: They became the perfect couple, the kind that is so sickeningly sweet it makes other people gag. Trust me... I asked around. I think part of it was that my father had lost a match, and a bet. The penalty, sobriety. For a few months I think. If nothing else he is... or was... still is, I guess... a man of his word. That whole time he didn’t drink. And had won a couple of titles during that, including the World Title.... (The photo switches to one of the young Ashley with another man, one that resembles Micah. They are holding matching titles... tag titles.) Alexis: And had a falling out with his best friend and tag partner... Stu St. Clair. (Ominous music over the picture, which tears right between the two men with a loud ripping sound, revealing another one, this one of the young woman in the original picture, a smaller belt over her shoulder.) Alexis: About the time my dad fell off the wagon, my mom won her first title - the women’s title - right after her biggest rival, Jenny Ignito, Micah’s mom, was forced to give it up cause no one could take it from her. But it didn’t seem to matter that mom won it, everyone still acted like Jenny was the champion. It didn’t help that when dad fell, he fell hard and was in such a drunken stupor he didn’t give a shit mom won either. So mom decided to go to Stu of all people for advice... yeah, I know. Let’s just say that didn’t end well. Then again, maybe it did... kind of. (Another photo, of Alexis’ parents again, with the backdrop of Vegas.) Alexis: Before the dust had even had time to settle dad had done a complete 180 and proposed. They eloped in Vegas during a pay-per-view and were so quiet about it that no one knew about it for months. (A split-shot picture of Alexis’ parents, showing each one respectively with their old federation’s World Title.) Alexis: Some time during all of this, they both won the World Title. Big deals for both of them, for dad it made him equal to Stu, who had won it before he did. For mom, well, let’s just say that the fed they were in made it very difficult for women to break out of the woman’s division. Only 5 ever did, and of those only 3 won the World Title. Mom did, Jenny did not. (A picture of the woman in a hospital bed, holding a baby bundled in a pink blanket, a young boy next to the bed trying to peek at the baby.) Alexis: Less than a year later, mom retired, because I came along. Does she resent me for that? Probably, although she never admitted it. She had a little experience, helping to raise Colin, but at the time she was still able to travel the wrestling circuit. Stuck at home with two little kids, and dad still out there.... Well, don’t get me wrong, she didn’t do anything bad to me or Colin, never abused us. Unless you count her cooking anyway. (A picture of a plate full of something unidentifiable, accompanied by a Wilhelm scream.) Alexis: I could probably tell you all the restaurants and fast foot joints in Vancouver. At least the ones that were around nine, ten years ago. (It was supposed to be a nice family picture of the O’Ryan’s, but clearly the next photo looked like someone had forgotten to say “cheese”. The parents were glaring at each other, a young Alexis looked ready to cry, while a slightly older Colin tried to comfort her.) Alexis: My dad was still actively wrestling for a little while after I was born, and when he was home his idea of relaxing was crawling into a bottle, and staying there. Mom told me my first word was “Dada”. I said it to Colin. After dad retired it got worse. When they’d been on the road my parents at least had wrestling as a way to let off steam. Stuck at home together, well, let’s just say it wasn’t pretty. Dad was the more passive of the two, but when it came to one thing he would not back down. As far as he was concerned wrestling had destroyed him and he refused to let Colin or I have anything at all to do with it. Mom on the other hand wanted us to be exposed to it at an early age as she had been. It was impossible for dad to completely block it from us, but he drew the line at letting us participate at anything associated with it. (The next picture is of the outside of a house at night.) Alexis: The night it all ended it was late and I was already in bed. My parents had had arguments before, but this was the worst that I remember. I got woken up by them not even yelling at each other, but screaming. Right away Colin was there to make sure I was ok. We heard stuff breaking, glass. I think a few of dad’s shot glasses went through windows. Then the light in my room was on and mom was there, telling me to pack up clothes and things because we were leaving. I thought Colin was coming with us, but mom said no. Since she never officially adopted Colin it would be kidnapping for her to take him with us. That was the last time I saw Colin for months, and the last time we lived together. (The photo flips to one of what appears to be a large training room, with a wrestling ring predominant.) Alexis: We moved to Tampa Bay where mom had trained after she moved from England. The wrestling school was still there, and mom knew some people there. She was able to be involved in wrestling again, as a trainer. That summer we went to Chicago as a friend of my mom’s had opened up another wrestling school there. That repeated every year, except for one when I was able to go to Japan to learn puroresu wrestling. Was I pushed into wrestling? Maybe. I’ve never felt particularly hostile to the idea of wrestling, and away from dad it actually seemed a pretty natural thing for me to get involved him. I never told him, of course. He only found out when he came to surprise Colin. (A photo of another woman, this one with a resemblance to Ashley.) Alexis: My Aunt Raquel told me something once, to use everything I had to my advantage... and I quote “and I mean everything.” She framed her body with her hands when she said that, accentuating the curves to let me know exactly what she meant. That stuck with me. I knew I was going to be underestimated just by my gender, if any place I went to wrestle was anything like that federation that shall no longer be named. So, I’d go with that. Hell, I’d go past that, the whole nine yards. If the average guy wanted to see me as a piece of meat, let him. Don’t expect me to respect him though, easy prey. If all he sees is easy fluff in heels he’ll never see the truth coming. (Another picture of Alexis’ mom, more recent and obviously candid. She’s glaring at the camera, looking to be in the middle of forming a word, holding what appeared to be a piece of fabric.) Mom didn’t see it that way though. As far as she was concerned it was an insult to wrestling as a whole for a woman to show off her body as she wrestled, and while she understood - at least she claimed - about being underestimated, she stubbornly refused to let me try out my theory. There was no way I was going to wrestle in her idea of a wrestling outfit though, a t-shirt and jeans. I don’t care how many titles she won. I could be as stubborn as she was. Something had to give, and it did, on my 18th birthday I moved to Chicago for good. I had some friends I could stay with until I got on my feet there, and months before my mom would be up there. I started scouting what was up there, Ring of Honor and all that. I wasn’t going to be a part of any federation my mom had any say in. (The next image is not a photo, but the logo for the recent GCW ppv, Dangerous Games.) Then, a few weeks later, right before American Thanksgiving, I saw a pay-per-view that changed everything. Dangerous Games. This was what I thought wrestling should be, and it proved mom wrong. Here was a fed where I wasn’t going to be shunted for the gender I was born with. And even better it made my theory all the more of an advantage. Some things, after all, don’t change. Now my... assets... would be without disadvantage. (The logo turns into a male silhouette) I didn’t try the theory in my audition, it would have been counterproductive at that point. I was going in as a wrestler after all, fluff only being a very effective cover... at least I hope it will be. I was in for a surprise though... not that I got in, that was a given... but who else was there. (Back to photos, this one of Micah St. Clair, clearly not the man who was in silhouette.) Right after the meeting with the GCW higher ups I met someone I had never seen, but who not only claimed to know me, but even knew my name. And the biggest surprise of all, he knew about me through Colin... and he had just signed to GCW himself! And the one that told me the news, Micah. Since we had both just come off of our meetings and it was around lunch time he invited me to hang out while we waited for Colin. I figured why the hell not. It didn’t hurt, Micah being cute, either, but that’s a story for another time. (There’s a sudden shifting to the picture and a slight rustling sound. The video camera is lifted from in front of the laptop and is now somewhat of a close-up on Alexis’ face, after passing cleavage in a low cut shirt.) Alexis: Right now, here we are, debut match. It’s funny, Colin and Micah were trying to come in as a tag team. I was welcome in with open arms, turning the Saints into a stable. And first match it’s the O’Ryan sibs back together, doing the one thing we were never supposed to do. So here’s a verbal middle finger to both of our parents. Watch us be the greatest wrestlers you never wanted us to be... oh and mom, the outfit I’ve got now is worse. (She grins evilly into the camera, and leaves everyone with that last image.)
