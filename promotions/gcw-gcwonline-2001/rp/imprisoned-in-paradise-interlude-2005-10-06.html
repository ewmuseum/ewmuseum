Imprisoned in Paradise: Interlude

6 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Oct 6 Training is a subject that creates much debate in pro wrestling. Some men swear by power lifting. Others run for mile after mile, in an effort to defeat weakness and conquer their limits. But Seymour needed something else. Rich Rollins was on his mind. His role as one of the “premiere GCW superstars” was on his mind. And, of course, his contest against “The Violence” Vince Jones was on his mind. Thus, he needed to get away, to train somewhere away from distraction. All Seymour truly needed to know he’d made the right choice was a gaze up at the beautiful blue sky, and take a breath of the unpolluted air. He was miles away from the hustle and bustle of New York City. Miles from his cell phone, so Jonathan Calver couldn’t reach him. The only thing that he did have with him was his sword, the Invincible, which glistened in the mid-autumn sun. Out here, he could engage in training that was quite unlike that of most other individuals. “Calm,” he reminded himself, nodding slowly. “In an often chaotic world, I must remain tranquil. To battle chaos with chaos is a useless proposition.” It was likely a proverb. If not, he’d probably cobbled together a bunch of proverbs to form his maxim. Emotion was irremovable from pro wrestling, and Seymour knew that. It was mighty fuel, but when spilled? You got burned, plain and simple. “Bahamut kata,” he whispered to himself, referring to one of the numerous sword routines that he used as both a means of focus and physical exercise. Slowly, gracefully, he pulled the blade up into a blocking position, and held it there. Gazing into the cool metal of the sword, he could see his reflection. Perfect. The angle of the sword was precise, his grip held properly. Truly, he was having a good day with his blade. He simply held the position, and with his body occupied, he could allow his mind to work. Vince Jones was a familiar name to the Final Fantasy. The two men had never met, but they did possess a common enemy. “Chrystal Dawn Fellini?” he remembered, sword twitching slightly in his grasp in annoyance at the name. Once upon a time, that name had occupied a pedestal in Seymour’s mind when it came to world spanning hatred. She had refused to face him, finally only doing so in disguise and unveiling only after the fact. The sword came back down, and he stabbed forward with it, lunging mightily. Faintly, he recognized that he had over lunged, and shifted his body weight to right the error. “Chryste,” he spat, shaking his head. She had accepted the challenge of the man known as “The Violence”, and she had been victorious. In essence, they had both undergone that loss. And from what he had seen of Vince Jones since then, the man had changed greatly. “Less angry now,” Seymour said, chuckling somewhat at his older memories of the man, of the virulent speech that the man had thrown at Chryste, using some of the least flattering terms he could possibly think of. Now, though, it seemed “The Violence” had changed. From what he had heard and seemed, Vince Jones was more focused. More prepared. More experienced. In general, a more capable opponent in every possible way. Seymour pulled the sword back again, stepping back at an angle and slashing upwards. Muscle memory allowed him to control the blade, putting it exactly where he wanted it. His pace was slow, but the strain on his body was still incredible. Every muscle tensed and relaxed with each movement, as he danced through an array of parries and slashes. The sword was a simple weapon, Seymour knew. When asked if he knew how to use it, years ago, he had replied, “Of course. You cut your opponent with the sharp part.” Sort of like he’d once been in wrestling. He knew the goals, and what to do’but as far as strategy was concerned, he had no earthly idea. A shooting star press was second nature to him, but he didn’t know proper ways to cover an opponent. Finally, he collapsed to one knee, sweating and panting. Lovingly, he placed his blade back in the scabbard at his side, only then lying down in the grass and looking up at the seemingly infinite sky. “It’s been so long since I’ve had the chance to relax,” he whispered, in awe at the simple beauty of the clouds passing overhead. “With my career, and living in the middle of the biggest city in the world, I rarely have time?” Striving to be one of the best wrestlers in the world was a difficult, often thankless job. It meant sacrificing one’s time to remain in peak shape. It meant being alone. Seymour sighed. Say what one would about Vince Jones and his past, Jones was at least better at keeping some semblance of a life during his career. He had Jasmine, a loyal companion and a woman who cared for him deeply. In her, he had motivation to go on when the chips were down. Coupled with Jones? obvious pride, as well as the fact that he was 6’7? and brick-thick, he was an utterly deadly opponent. Seymour took a deep breath. He was tired from his workout with the sword, and so he was resting. But he knew deep down that Vince Jones wasn’t resting. His full attention was focused on this contest, on perhaps his biggest match so far in GCW. The Final Fantasy wasn’t the sort to pretend that people didn’t know who he was. For better or worse, he was known. Championships all over the world and tournament victories tended to lead to that sort of thing. Couple that with the fact that he was undefeated in GCW, and there may as well have been a target on his head. “I’ve beaten Borealis,” Seymour said, counting on his fingers. “And Octavian, and Mary, and Trey.” By all accounts, he’d beaten some of GCW’s best, and done so in a semi-convincing fashion. To most, Vince Jones was nothing to worry about, a lot of sound and fury signifying nothing. But Seymour knew better than that. “Sure, I’ve got “the name?”, Almasy spat, a little bitterly, “but he’s got Jasmine. He’s got Reno Banks. And more importantly, he wants this match like nothing else in this world.” So, of course, did Seymour. But there was an inherent difference, something that Seymour couldn’t possibly overcome in the field of desire. Vince Jones had come from the streets. Built himself from the ground up into one of the best up and coming superstars in the world. Seymour had no idea what that was like. Certainly, life hadn’t been easy for him, but he’d never had to fight to stay alive, either. Jones was a warrior, in every sense of the word. Slowly, Seymour began to stand, but his knees wobbled. For some reason, he felt absolutely exhausted. There were a number of possible reasons for this, of course. He’d been in action every week since joining Global, and such constant action certainly took its toll. Couple that with his seemingly constant appearances, and no one could really blame Seymour for being exhausted. But it was more than that, Seymour knew. Being physically and mentally exhausted were part of his chosen profession. There was something else wrong. That “something else” started with Rich, and ended with Rollins. “He’s so erratic,” the Final Fantasy grumbled, remembering Rollins? blindside assault on him after the Trey Tremendous match. “He’ll leave me alone for weeks, and then attack me when I least expect it. Rollins is utterly impossible to predict.” It was beyond worrying. Vince Jones was enough of a problem. Adding Rich Rollins to that equation was nearly mental overload. All of the skill in the world, sometimes, wasn’t enough to win a match, and Seymour believed strongly that this would be one of those matches. “Enough of this,” he chastised, shaking his head. “I came out here to clear my head, not to muddle it up even more. Besides, it’s too pretty of a day to be so worried.” With a sigh, he plopped back to the grass, stretching tired limbs out in the dewy greenery. If only he could stay here forever, he thought, feeling heavy lids to start to close. Here there was no pain, no constant looking over his shoulder. There was only contentment. Only that elusive feeling of relaxation and pure joy. Just before he drifted off, the Final Fantasy thought of a slight irony. He was lying, flat on his back, shoulders to the ground with his eyes looking up into eternity. If Vince Jones were to get him in this position for three seconds on WorldWide, “The Violence” would be the winner of the match. Maybe that explained why Seymour loved rest and respite so much. After all, he spent his entire professional career and life avoiding it.
