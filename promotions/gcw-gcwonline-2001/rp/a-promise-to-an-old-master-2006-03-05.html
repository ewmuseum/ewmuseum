A Promise to an old Master

5 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03-05-06 When Grandmaster Huo Yan Jia founded Chin Woo in the 19th century, it was seen as somewhat of a victory over adversity. Being the fourth son in a family of 10 is not an easy situation to be in of itself; bur factor in that Master Huo was frequently ill, he was taken advantage of by the other children in the province. Even Huo’s father, a Kung Fu Teacher, refused to pass the wisdom of the martial art onto his son ? it was almost as if he felt dishonour in his son’s weakness. Not to be put off, Huo hid behind bushes while his father taught other students in the courtyard. For ten years, Master Huo practiced in solitude, the art he learnt from his concealed window of green undergrowth. It was only when his father witnessed his son dispatching a group of three on the cobbled roads that linked, like arteries, the provinces locations, that he realised the extent of his son’s potential. He officially accepted Huo, and taught him everything he knew. One day, Master Huo, then only a student of Kung Fu, fought with a foreigner, and immediately gained fame ? it was during that time that there were many of foreign descent in China, and the people had been labelled the “Sick men of Asia”. In an attempt to strengthen his kinsmen so as to better defend his country, and defend their honour and homeland, Master Huo established the Chin Woo school to allow all Chinese the opportunity to learn Kung Fu. It was in 1909 that Master Huo’s fame leapt another level. A European Wrestler was sent to Shanghai to challenge any Chinese that would accept. The news quickly spread over the region, and Master Huo was invited to take up the challenge by his peers. He seized the opportunity, and emerged victorious’his reputation escalated. As word of his victory spread, so too did the Chin Woo spirit. Unfortunately, in August 1909, Master Huo died, but on March 3rd 1910, Mr Chen Ghong Zhe, Mr Yao Chan Bo, and Mr Lu Wei Chang reopened the Chin Woo School. After Master Huo’s death, his brother, Mr Huo Yuan Siang and son, Mr Huo Tong Ker, continued his legacy, continuing to teach at the Chin Woo association. Later, many famous martial-arts masters were invited to teach in Chin Woo. Even though they came from different schools, they all followed Chin Woo regulations. Thus Chin Woo became a famous and popular martial-arts association in Shanghai. Chin Woo sponsored most of the martial-arts tournaments. However, in 1966, Shanghai Chin Woo was forced to discontinue their martial-arts activities due to communist regulations. Those restrictions were later lifted, and martial-arts activities were again alive in the Shanghai Chin Woo. After the death of Master Huo, Chin Woo was reorganized to make it available to other parts of China and Asia. In 1920, Shanghai Chin Woo sent representatives to Southeast Asia. Mr. Li Hui Seng, Mr. Luo Xiao Ao, Mr. Chen Gong Zhe, Mr. Ye Shu Tian, and Mrs. Chen Shi Chao made their first stop in Saigon, Vietnam. They opened the first Chin Woo School there and later in parts of Malaysia and Singapore. To prove their skills, they were often required to give demonstrations or accept challenges. By 1923, these five Chin Woo Masters had opened Chin Woo schools all over Southeast Asia and visited nine different countries. ___________________________________________________________ Tyler Xing had heard the story of Master Huo and the origins of Chin Woo many times before, but each time it brought a new zeal to the forefront of his mind. He remembered now, walking with Master Yuan Bo Phat, the rush and exhilaration that flew through his veins. Master Yuan had been telling Tyler once again of Master Huo’s achievements being a testament to Chin Woo in a modernising China. Scholars such as Francis Fukuyama and Samuel P Huntington could never grasp the true extent of Chinese evolution, because it was so far from their own, and had more to do with the spirit than consciousness or culture. Even Plato, Master Yuan announced, was inherrantly incorrect: “The great Greek philosopher, Plato, was correct insomuch as he correctly identified the existence of the soul. But where Master Plato failed to peer deeply into human reality, and the reality we, as the Chinese have experienced, was in his identification of the components of that soul.” Tyler, then only 18, was listening attentively. He’d recognised early on, that Master Yuan wasn’t like the other masters at the Beijing Chin Woo School. He spoke of deep truths and philosophies that penetrated further than the study of Kung Fu. It was on his words that Xing had built his dreams. “Master Plato had asserted that there were three components to his soul. The component of desire, the component of reason, and the component of Thymos; the struggle for recognition. But, as you will know already from your studies here, we believe in wholly different components. Chin Woo teaches us that it is Wisdom, Benevolence and Courage that constitutes the Chinese soul. It is not that some of us do not seek to be recognised in a world where recognition is highly advertised; but the true master rises above this.” Tyler blinked as the wind savagely slashed at his eyelids. His reminiscing of his younger self talking with the great master brought him warmth in this cold place. Having only arrived in the USA one and a half weeks earlier, Tyler had immediately sought somewhere to compose and equalise himself. He had found that place in Canada. The snow-peaked mountains seemed to bring a peace that no drug could manufacture. The perpetual conversation of the wind kept his senses active, but allowed his mind to wander, to meditate. Having defeated his first opponent in the previous week, it was now Tyler’s prerogative to prepare himself for a much harder challenge. His mind drifted back again’back to Master Yuan. “You see, Nightwater, the struggle for recognition constitutes the very makeup of what the truly spiritual would consider bad karma. Greed, Jealousy and the turbulence of the soul are the prey of such ambitions. Plato was indeed correct in asserting that thymos lead to the human emotions of Anger, Pride and Shame, but we transcend these barriers to calm ourselves on the turbulent waters of time. It is a shame indeed, that such appraised scholars as Fukuyama seem to believe that the removal of the struggle for recognition leads to a corruption of humanity; for it is thymos itself that corrupts humanity’this, if nothing else, you must take with you wherever you go Nightwater.” The name the school had given him, Nightwater, Tyler Xing wore with pride. It was an indication of his personality, quiet and silent in battle, and stealthy’but a warrior who could slip out of the grip of his enemy like water. Hence, the night, used for stealth, and water, were the two elemental forces that were combined to identify Tyler Xing. His master, now giving his obituary of the western world, turned to look at him directly in the eyes. “It is you, Tyler, not I, who carry the burden that Plato foresaw. It is you who must carry Chin Woo in the modern world, not as an organisation, but as the principles it merges. The frailty of the West needs champions’needs heroes. We do not. But you, Tyler, can be their champion. But you must always remember your roots, and your teaching. You must remember that, no matter what treasures a man can place upon you, the soul is the only treasure that you must hold onto as true. The material aspirations of others, relative to the self, are unimportant. You must simply always strive to put yourself ahead of it all’to take yourself, and your soul, to the endpoints of your being, and always return having learned something new about yourself and that which transcends the Earth.” So wise, this old man. Tyler thought to himself, as he mediated on the side of the Canadian mountain. The cold air brought the ethereal blanket of snow with it, covering his black hair with its white gloss. But, here, in this state, nothing bothered Tyler. He remembered back again, to the monastery. He was twenty five now, a true master of his discipline, and in the semi-finals of the Chin Woo world championship. ___________________________________________________________ The smell of sweat, blood, and burnt ambition had filled the air. They’d just watched student Huo Yang Phi, apparently a descendent from the great Master Huo, defeat one of the Chin Woe’s finest’Chang Bi, in a one-on-one bout. This was the Chin Woo championships’and this was Tyler Xing’s opportunity. A plethora of faces stared down at him, as his attire was closely examined to make sure everything was in place. His eyes searched the audience’the white faces amongst the Chinese and Muslim ethnicities gave the appearance of Ying and Yang ? the white, richer individuals, given the lower seats, closer to the action’the “foreign” faces given the back-row occupancies. He would change all that one day. The Chinese man would be the Western Man’s equal in material things, as he was already his superior on the spiritual arena. In the ring, Huo Yang Phi looked ready’ready for anything. No smudge of emotion covered anything on his face. Tyler had been sure this would be difficult, but he was confident. The greatest man who’d ever intervened in Tyler’s life, Master Yuan, had died only a few months ago, and was now a part of the cycle of life. But, the Chin Woo association had decreed that Master Yuan’s body was too pure to be committed to the Earth, and so was cremated. His ashes were scattered on the wind. Tyler remembered his teaching from Geographical studies. Each day, a human being would come into contact with the dust and skin cells of another human nearly every second. So, he breathed in’he breathed till his lungs were full, and breathed out through his nose, hoping to capture some of Master Yuan’s essence, who was almost certainly watching on the air’watching his greatest student, as he made the grand master’s legacy famous. Tyler stood opposite his opponent, as the judge nodded at both competitors. “Fighters Prepare. Fighters Ready. Fighters Start!” The bell sounded, as Huo’s left leg rose into the Mid-air. His expression was a poster for serenity, and Tyler Xing’s arms began to tense, so he loosened himself up. The two competitors stared each other down for what seemed an eternity, but in reality was only a few minutes. Tyler made the first forward move, sending a right hand towards his opponent, and pulling back quickly, so as to test the waters. Huo didn’t move Tyler followed through the next time with a left arm elbow smash towards the right side of Huo’s face, but Huo’s right arm swiftly rose, twisting around Tyler’s left arm, and pulling it upwards. Tyler managed to, with his right hand, connect a forward hand slash into the underarm of Huo, but he removed his grip before the move could take any real effect. Huo rose his two hands in different positions, using his left arm as a protective shield, and right arm in a ready to pounce manoeuvre. Xing knew this as a set from the Tan Tui doctrine, and adapted to counter it. Xing flew in with a right kick upwards, designed to instinctively force Huo’s arm downwards, and would then follow through by dropping his right leg, and back spinning his left into the stomach of Huo. Huo’s arm, however, did not move. It was too late. Huo grabbed Xing’s ankle, and using Xing’s own momentum, lifted himself on Xing’s leg into a reverse spin kick to the side of Tyler Xing. Tyler went across the mat, and out of the ring. It was one point to Huo. This time, Tyler decided he was going to be less aggressive. He adopted a Tan Tui set nine pose, one arm outstretched, to keep the opponent back, the other behind him, to provide leverage to any attack. Huo countered with Tan Tui set six, his leg pushed forward on the ground, into a crouching position. Huo manoeuvred himself forward, and Tyler quickly span round, and back flipped over the assaulting Huo, but was caught off-guard with a rear fist from the opponent, who quickly and swiftly adopted the set five position, sending a right fist into the chest of Tyler. Xing grabbed the fist, and twisted the arm, but Huo countered with a side shuffle, and used his outstretched leg to pull in on Tyler’s left ankle. Xing felt himself falling backwards, and instinctively back-flipped again. This cost him dearly? Huo thrust forwards, sending his right leg into the landing Xing’s chest, and putting him on the floor. That was it. Tyler didn’t need to hear the judge to find out. In one evening, he’d embarrassed himself, and his master’s name. Anger swept through Tyler’s body, but he restrained himself. But the mere presence of that anger was enough for him to realise, he was not ready’not ready for the finer arts of Chin Woo. His eyes met the mat, and the tears began They have never stopped. ___________________________________________________________ Sitting on his perch on the Canadian mountain, Tyler Xing’s eyes opened, a crystallising tear forming in one’s center. He steadied himself again, the rush of vision having momentarily put him off balance, and checked his watch. He’d been up here for eight hours. Time, then, to finish this’and regain his pursuit of himself, this time through wrestling and not Chin Woo. The story of Master Huo defeating the European Wrestler had stayed with Tyler for the duration of his time at the monastery. He’d inquired countless times to his master about the Western form of fighting, and had been to see many Sumo-Wrestling exhibitions from the, most from Eastern Japan, coming over to excite the Chinese people. But, in all truth, he’d always found it dull. But Western Wrestling’in its finest form’there was something in which one could attain true self worth. To be able to out-manoeuvre one’s opponent, rather than out-power, or out-speed him’was the true definition of skill. Tyler, though taking pride in his own skill, realised that this amalgamation of Martial Arts and Wrestling would teach him far more than the limits of Chin Woo now promised. Although he’d lost in the World Championships one year ago’he had known why. It wasn’t his skill, or ability, it was his emotions. They had not yet been perfected. But, Master Huo’s experiences with the European Wrestler, made it clear to Tyler Xing that he was not so out of place as he would feel, with the Martial Artist and the Wrestler. And so, the immediate future held promise. A match with Jacob McKnail’for the GCW Television Championship. Another battle with a man he knew little about’a man who was moulded by the wrestling world. Another battle with himself And another chance, to make good on his promise to Master Yuan.
