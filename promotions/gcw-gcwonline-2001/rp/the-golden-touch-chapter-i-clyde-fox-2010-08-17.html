The Golden Touch, Chapter I: Clyde Fox

17 August 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/17/10 In an industry where you have an exponential number of acquaintances and only a handful of friends, I was privileged to say that I met one of my closest friends my first night as a member of Global Championship Wrestling. December is a notoriously cold month and the year two thousand seven was no different. Temperatures dipped into the thirties in Columbus, Ohio as I stepped out of the executive office of then-head-honcho Steven Caldera with a fat new contract in my hand. It had been a few years since I'd actively competed in a wrestling ring prior to signing on board with GCW. My prime had undoubtedly come and gone and so I was hoping more for a means to pay for my microwaveable dinners. The talent was raw; it was young and fresh. The guys on the roster had physiques I'd previously only seen in weight lifting competitions on Spike TV and the women - well, let's just say you'd have a hard time raping them without ending up in the hospital with a broken arm and a dislocated jaw. In my hey-day, it might've been a place I would've thrived in. But years removed from my last big wrestling stint, I was certainly out of my element. I was a stranger in a place I didn't belong; a Phil Atken, if you would, in a Championship match. I was over my head. But that's what I embraced the most. Peering around the GCW World Headquarters, I tried to seek out a comfort zone. It seemed as though no matter where I turned, I didn't fit in. None of the secretaries in the office were a day over eighteen, a testament to the personality of the gentlemen running the company. The maintenance guys were scruffy, poorly dressed, and quite trashy looking. One of which, I think his name was Johnny, I remember vividly - he had long, natty looking hair in which I swear I saw a bird swoop out of. But if I could've summed that day up in two words, they would've unquestionably been... "Heto fwend!" * * * * * When I turned around, I expected to see a six year old boy wearing a rainbow colored unicorn t-shirt with an etch-a-sketch in his hand and a pimple on his nose. Much to my surprise, I did see a boy wearing a rainbow colored unicorn t-shirt with an etch-a-sketch in his hand and a pimple on his nose. Except this boy wasn't six years old...he was nineteen. "My name is Clyde. Can we be fwends?" I smirked at him. Unsure of what I should do, I just sort of stood there in silence. As I stared back at Clyde and his ridiculous outfit, I was certain that this was some sort of initiation. "Almost had me there, bud!" Clyde looked at me like I'd just ripped away a lollipop from his lips. He bowed his head and tucked away his etch-a-sketch. "That's otay. You don't have to be my fwend," he mumbled. I almost felt heartbroken. I mean, the kid looked really upset that I didn't tell him I'd be his friend. It wasn't often in the sports entertainment business that you had a guy who could act, but this guy was gold. He was pulling off the slightly stupid look with ease. But as Clyde moped away, I started to reconsider my initial evaluation of the kid. It wasn't actually until one of the barely legal secretaries dropped me a dirty look as she waved over Clyde that I realized this kid might not've been joking. "Here Clyde," she said while glazing into my eyes. "Have a jolly rancher." Though I wasn't sure if she was giving me the dirty look for disrespecting Clyde or because she saw me gawking at her cleavage hanging out in her scantily-clad blouse, I made it a point to try and reconcile, "Clyde!" He spun around with a glimmer in his eye. "We can be friends," I said, already with regret. Clyde dropped his etch-a-sketch to the floor and let the jolly rancher tumble out of his mouth. He sprinted towards me like a bat out of hell, leaping up into my arms. Naturally, I caught him - I didn't really know what else to do. The girls in the office all smiled, some sharing a whisper, and it wasn't until that moment that I truly believed that this wasn't an act. There was something seriously wrong with this kid. And I told him we'd be friends. "Best fwends forever," he assured. * * * * * Easter had just passed and the sweet smell of summer was in the air. But summer wasn't what everyone was hyped up about. Sure, there's nothing more enjoyable than spending your days on a bus or a plane traveling across country while the weather hits the nineties - but we still managed to find pleasure elsewhere. The close of spring meant the start of Dangerous Games. Up until this point, those two words meant nothing more to me than a reference to a cheesy early '90s flick starring Harvey Kietel and Madonna. And heaven knows that when you mix Madonna and acting, you're bound to be delivered a barrel of shit. That's how I associated the words Dangerous Games: as a big barrel of shit. But with the way everyone got all worked up and skippy, I knew it had to be more than that. And as I'd come to find out, for some it was a pathway to the future - a way into the spotlight. And for others, it was a way out; a perfect excuse to put your body on the line and suffer a career ending injury. But for Clyde Fox, Dangerous Games was a time for love. "I tink I have a crush, Kwis." It was about an hour and a half after I got done sweeping the ring with Phil Atken on Worldwide episode 97 and I was loading up a rental car out back. We were in Vancouver for the show but had to trek down to Reading, Pennsylvania for Episode 98. "Is that right," I muttered to him without the slightest sense of concern. "Yup, yup," he exclaimed, dancing around in circles. "Love, loveeee love! I'm in loveeee, love, loveee." I wanted to knock the teeth out of his mouth. Unfortunately, he was the only guy willing to carpool with me to Reading. I was back to being a loner - I had no friends. Well, except for Clyde of course. At that point, Zacharia and I weren't seeing eye to eye. He grew apart from myself and The Movement, claiming we were holding him back. Coincidentally, Dan Black started to mutter the same shit and formally 'ejected' me from the brotherhood that I built. Whether I liked it or not, Clyde was all I had and I was forced to deal with it. Under normal circumstances, I was able to stomach his antics pretty easily. But I was still incredibly perturbed with my loss to Andy Murray at Warpath that seeing this kid happy was like tossing a gallon of fuel into a brush fire. "Is this one of your Canadian girls or something again?" I tried to entertain him, figuring he'd shut the fuck up if he got it out. "Nooope," he hummed. "It's somebody you know pretty well!" He wanted me to guess - but I wasn't in the mood for games. "I don't know Clyde. Are you going to tell me or are you going to dance around the subject for another hour?" The tone of my voice killed a little bit of his buzz. With a slight frown and a lot less hop, he whispered, "Sally." I tossed the rest of his toys in the trunk - Gizmodo, Poke balls, and his rocking horse - and slammed the lid shut. Despite not having the slightest concern and wanting to get out of British Columbia, I couldn't help but respond with, "What?!" Surely he didn't mean who I thought he meant. "You know," he assured. "Sally. Sally from the backstages with the michael-phone." "Sally Ford," I asked...more rhetorically than inquisitively? Clyde bobbed his head up and down, "Yes, YES!" His smile glistened and his cheeks morphed red. I wanted to give the kid some sound advice, but I wasn't in the right mindset. "So fuck her," I slipped. He looked at me awkwardly, like he wasn't sure what that meant. "When you want something, Clyde, you take it. Especially with women. You don't wait for them to approach you. They play far too many games for that." "But I like games," he insisted. "These aren't the same kind of games, kid." Part of me felt bad. Unfortunately for him, that part of me was so encompassed by the rest of me that it didn't really make a difference. "These are the kind of games you can't win; no matter how good you may be." Clyde didn't have much of a reaction. He stared at me blankly, sort of just waiting to hear what I'd say next. And since I like the sound of my voice that much, I gave him what he wanted, "So when you see her down in Reading, I want you to make a move. I want you to take her into your locker room and show her what you want to do to her." He couldn't help but giggle, reddening in the face even more so than before. "That's silly." "Nothing's silly, Clyde. This is a serious matter," I said sternly. "You just take her...and show her." He gave me another nod and it was all I needed. "Now get in the car," I said. "We've got a long drive ahead of us." * * * * * "You're a fucking asshole. AN ASSHOLE. You're an asshole, a duechebag, and a piece of fucking shit." It was hardly the comments I'd hoped to get after a show but the kind of comments I was starting to grow accustomed to. Zacharia Taylor and I hadn't seen eye to eye for any number of weeks, despite once being the best of friends. But there was one thing left we did share in common - our friendship with Clyde. "He trusts you, Chris. Clyde looks up to you. He'll listen to any God damn thing you'll say and you told him to do THAT?" I never really liked the state of Pennsylvania to begin with. Be it the trash city of Philadelphia or the emptiness of the countryside. There was never a single time I spent in the state that I walked away with a pleasant memory. And this night, as you could probably tell, was no different. Taylor had befriended Clyde after a long and exhausting war over the Television Title. After countless bouts with one another, they ultimately grew a mutual respect for one another. So when I encouraged Clyde to basically rape Sally Ford, Taylor took offense to it. "Settle down," I mumbled to Zacharia. "It really doesn't concern you. Now does it?" He snarled at me. "It doesn't matter whether or not it concerns me. You're a sick fuck for taking advantage of him like that." "Whoa sailor! Not for nothing, but I think he was the one doing the taking-advantage of," I rebut. Needless to say, Zacharia didn't really appreciate it. "Besides, she's walking fine, isn't she?" "That's not the point, Chris. He doesn't know any better. You're ruining his morals. You're treating him just like you used to treat every other person who you used to call a friend." I tried to ignore Zacharia as best I could. I continued stuffing my duffle bag, pretending like it didn't bother me a bit. "And you know what? One of these days, he's going to realize what you're doing and he's going to walk out on you, too. Maybe then you'll be happy. You'll be left with no one." Zacharia stormed out of my locker room, making sure to slam the door behind him as hard as his arm would let him. After he left, I stood there for a minute and basked in the silence. I knew what I did was wrong - with Clyde, and all. But underneath the surface, I was trying to help the kid. Roll your eyes if you will, but it's the truth. Up until this point, there's not been a single person who’s ever taken Clyde seriously. Everyone has always passed him off as 'too childish' or 'completely harmless.' They've dismissed any possibility that he'd ever, one-day, be a major fixture in this company. And up until this point, Clyde has never had a moment where people genuinely feared his next move. But that night was different - that night, it all changed. That night, when that door closed and Sally screamed - every single person watching that broadcast cringed. Would Clyde Fox really harm her? It may have been a raunchy way to do it, but he finally got some credibility. I lifted my bag from the stool it was resting on and threw it over my shoulder. As I turned about-face, I was met with a guy I didn't think I'd be seeing much anymore. "Kwis..." * * * * * In an industry that requires you to be on the road more often than you can sleep in your own bed, the only thing you can truly count on to survive is friendship. Be it a travel buddy, a roommate, or just a guy to grab a beer with so you don't sit alone at the bar, a true friendship can really play a determining factor in your morale. It's no secret that I've been anything but modest when it comes to telling you what I'm good at; be it a ratings steroid, a suplex specialist, or a master manipulator. Amongst it all, there's one thing I'm not good at... Friendships. The ability to trust and to care for a person; to give and to forgive - that's what makes a true champion. That's the real secret for success; why Clyde Fox won Rampage in 2009 and I lost in 2008. That's why Clyde Fox has seen so much success. And that's why, despite everything I've ever done to him, Clyde Fox remains my best friend. My only friend. He's got the golden touch.
