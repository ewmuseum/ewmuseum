Fragile

Date: 08/04/10 Neat layers of waxed black hair started setting atop Andy's head as he tucked a wayward strand behind his ear and placed the comb on the dressing table. He folded a white collar over his black tie, slid the knot up to his throat, and stuffed his shirt's tails into his trousers, then belted-up and glared at his reflection. Stormy blue eyes contrasted with his skin's placid milkiness, but there was no stubble, no bags under the eyes, and not one freckle or spot to mar his complexion. Not bad, given the circumstances, Andy thought. Today was a day Andy had been counting down to since the diagnosis, and the time spent waiting for cancer to knock Rueben from his mortal coil had been terrible. Not a day passed without dread sheathing its wretched claws plunging them through the lanky Scot, and the past four months had seemed more like twenty-four. Finally, though, everything was coming to a head. In some ways, Rueben's passing was a morbid relief. News of his agent's impending demise had spurred him to a spectacular victory over Vivica J. Valentine at NC-17, but his performances slumped soon after. Andy's mind had been carrying too much weight to the ring. Rueben Dallas – agent, friend, and a man who'd done more to aid Andy's career then even Andy himself – was haunting him before death, and his career was suffering. It was a natural reaction and Andy would've been severely worried if his friend's death hadn't knocked him off-stride, but he wasn't selfless enough to ignore the damage it was doing. If such missteps as his humiliating loss to SCCW's Jared Sykes on home soil were to be cut-out, it'd have to wait until Rueben was gone. Acknowledging the fact sent disgust rippling through Andy's body, but there was no other solution. Scientists weren't going to come up with a miracle cure and Rueben wasn't suddenly going to jump from his hospital bed, full of life and the joys of spring. The only closure was death, and now it was staring Andy in the face. His cell phone's ascending ringtone disturbed Andy's concentration as the device buzzed like a dying wasp on the dresser's oak surface. "Home," said the screen. He'd been expecting a call before the funeral so he pulled the phone to his ears and answered with a humble "hello". "Hey, Andy," Kathryn replied from three states away. "How are you?" She knew what today meant, and immediately Andy could tell she was trying to sound as somber as possible. "Okay, I suppose. Just ready for this to finally be over and done with." Andy sat slowly on the end of the room's king sized bed, mindful of creasing his newly-pressed trousers. "How are things back home?" "Pretty slow, to be honest. A couple of extra email this week but nothing I can't keep track of. I just thought I'd call to check-in like you asked, but, ummm. There isn't really that much to check-in with." "So when I get stuck with the paperwork every bugger in the universe and their grandmother decides to flood me with emails and phone calls, but everything goes dead as soon as I hire somebody to do it for me. How does that work?". Andy's lips curl into a smile for the first time that day. He waits for Kathryn's response and a short burst of laughter soon comes through the receiver. Andy was finding her easier to communicate with as her nerves eased, though she was clearly still a little awestruck at working for one of the biggest names in American combat sports. "Sod's law, isn't it?" "'Sod's law'?" "Never mind. Nothing major to report, then?" "Not really. John Hood's called about five times already this morning and he gets more and more impatient every time. I know you're probably too preoccupied to talk today: what should I tell him?" "Tell him to piss off." A pause. "Ummm?" "Well, be a bit more diplomatic than that. Same sentiment, though." Three soft knocks hit Andy's door just as Kathryn responded. "I'll just tell him you're busy." "He knows what today is," Andy said, rising to his full 6'7" and pacing towards the door. "Besides, I'm in his town. Maybe I'll head round to his office and slap him around a little if I need any stress relief after the ceremony." He pulled open the door and there stood Sidney, Andy's shorn-headed elder brother. The GCW grappler raised a finger, hushing Sid's words before they came out. "Kath, my brother's here. I gotta go." Another pause from a young woman who didn't know what to say. "Well good luck." "Thanks. Call me later if anything big comes up." Andy killed the call with the push of a button and turned back into his room to retrieve his jacket. "You ready?" Sid croaked through nicotine-ravaged vocal chords. Andy pulled his jacket over his shoulders and fastened all three buttons. He looked down at his brother, who, with his beard trimmed thinly and his suit immaculate, looked cleaner than he had in twenty years. "Not as ready as I need to be," Andy said. But the time for pussyfooting was over. Andy and his sibling left the room and went down to the car awaiting them outside the hotel. It was time to move on. * * * * * "'Too busy', eh? That's six times - SIX TIMES - I've called this morning. Nobody I know is busy enough to keep their boss in limbo for that length of time!" John Hood felt his blood pressure rise as prickly heat ran across his scalp like wildfire. Sat at his desk and clenching the telephone with a tight fist, he could not comprehend what the girl on the other end was telling him. "Yes, I know what he's doing today, and YES, I am his boss. I know that fucker has two cells; gimme his private number." John groaned as dissatisfying words filtered through to him. "Of course you fucking have it! You're his secretary, aren't you? Well then, give me the number." Her calmness was infuriating, and though John couldn't remember the girl's name, he wanted nothing more than to leap through the phone line and throttle her. "Fuck's sake. Stop reading Andy's script and tell that ungrateful fuck to buzz me back ASAP, alright? Alright." John slammed the phone down with such force that the plastic almost cracked. He pushed his fingers into sweaty temples and inadvertently rubbed away a layer of fake tan as he tried to massage himself back to serenity. "'Too busy'," he mumbled. "This prick's got some cheek." The office chair slid across marble flooring as John leapt to his feet and swiped a stress ball from the desk. Squeezing it until his knuckles whitened, John wandered over to the room's one wall that wasn't made of glass and looked at the chart hanging from it. A think green line moved upwards at an increasing incline below a bold "MURRAY MONEY COUNTER" heading. John had kept a count of all the money he'd earned from Andy's paycheque since worming his way into Rueben Dallas' office, and made it too big and too obvious for anyone stepping into his office to ignore. The cumulative total stood somewhere between ninety-four and ninety-five thousand dollars, and a win over Valentine at 135 would see the green line push into six digits. John's pulse began to slow as he admired the chart. It took a lot of willpower to deal with his and Andy's personality clash. As Murray's agent, John had the position of power and the ability to push Andy's career along whatever trajectory he pleased. Unfortunately, he'd also failed to judge how difficult it would be to work directly with a man he had nothing but disdain for. Things like the wall-chart and the personal gain it symbolised made it worth the while, though. Still simmering beneath his oily surface, John slowly walked towards the office's floor-to-ceiling windows and peered out across Houston's dingy morning. A few feelings for Andy Murray were running through his head, and remorse was not one of them. Rueben Dallas was not a man John had cared for or respected, and his death mattered little. All Rueben's fall had been to him was a opportunity to finally wreak vengeance upon a man who'd slept with his wife and ruined his career as a sports broadcaster. Rancor had driven him for the best part of a decade, and he wasn't going to let one man's passing get in the way of that. But this wouldn't stand. John couldn't have Andy ignoring him, funeral or no funeral. He wasn't going to sit idly by and let Andy have the satisfaction of knowing he was winding his agent up, no. John had to do something about it. He walked back over to the desk and picked-up the landline. "Kate," he said, "I need you to find out where Rueben Dallas' funeral is being held. Now." * * * * * "Sure are quiet today, boss," Sid said, gazing at the blur of Texan concrete on the other side of the window, tapping his fingers to the tune of whatever seventies rock tune was on the radio. "It's a big day," Andy responded, concentration focused on the busy road. "I haven't been looking forward to this." "At least it's gonna be over. He was a good man, Andy. Shit like this shouldn't happen to the good guys, but it does. Remember we put Dad to rest?" "Fuckin' horrible." "Yeah, but we got through it. Together. And we'll do it again today." Silence fell between the Murray brothers as Andy rounded a corner and Sid returned his gaze to the world beyond the window. The sky's tones shifted from grey to white and back to grey again, the colours of a television tuned to a dead station, and Andy was sure the heavens were about to open. "Thanks for coming, Sid," Andy said, carefully adjusting one of the air-conditioning nozzles away from his face. "I hate doing stuff like this along." "I'd have come anyway," Sid said, turning. "Dude was a helluva guy. I know Cayle was fond of him, too." Talk of their younger brother soured Andy's tone. "Cayle made his bed, now he can lie in it." "I know, man. I know. Hell, if I were you, I'd have thrown the fucker out months ago. A guy can't just sit on his arse and leach off his brother all his life, especially at his age." Sid was trying hard not to push such a sensitive subject on such a difficult day. "Have you spoken to him since?" "No, and I'm pretty sure he's trying to forget my existence at the moment too." Andy's words came out colder than he'd intended, but he was at the end of his tether with Cayle. He'd housed, fed, and clothed his younger brother since his release from prison in 2007. No longer could the babying continue: at twenty-seven, Cayle Murray would have to finally take control of his own life, whether he liked it or not. "You heard from him?" "Nah, not once. Couple of guys I know say he's wrestled a couple of matches downstate, though. Money won't be great but I guess it's something." "I suppose." Andy paused. "Just as long as he doesn't show up on my doorstep begging for pennies next week." "What would you do if he did?" "What do you think I'd do?" That was the only answer Sid needed. He sighed and adjusted his posture on the cream leather seat. "He's your brother, though. I know he's done wrong, but you can't just shut him out forever." "Cayle's welcome in my house as soon as he can prove he's self-sufficient, but until then he's getting nothing, I'm afraid. Maybe that's harsh, but, I dunno. Sometimes that's what you've got to do." "I can't say I bla--" Sidney started, but lost his focus as Andy gritted his teeth together and winced. "Uhh, are you okay?" "Headache. Pounds like a motherfucker." "Got some Aspirin in my bag if you--" Andy waved his brother away. "Nah, I'll be fine. It'll pass." "You got a lot on your mind, though. Stress can bring these things on." "It's only a headache, Sid. Jesus Fuck." "All I'm saying is you've got a lot on your plate. There's this, John Hood, Shipley, Valentine at WorldWide..." "So maybe I've got a few things to deal with; who doesn't? Today'll be over in twelve hours, Hood's easily ignored, and I'm through with Shipley. Valentine? Just another match." "Not really 'just another match' though, is it?" By now Sid had stopped tapping rhythms on the car's interior; his full attention had turned to his brother. "You guys dated. That's pretty heavy." "Would you fucking stop?" Andy said, momentarily taking his eyes off the road as he raised his volume. "What does fighting Vivica J. Valentine have to do with a bloody headache? I had too much dodgy coffee at the hotel, that's why my head hurts." Sid raised his hands innocently, knowing that he'd probably pushed his brother too far. Andy's attention returned to the road and Sidney looked dead ahead, gazing at the open road. "Sorry, bro. Didn't mean to push you." Andy said nothing; just kept driving. * * * * * For Roy Lahenne, midweek afternoons were about three things: coffee, cigarettes, and watching the world go by. Gently rocking back and forth on his front porch with porch with a smoke dangling from his mouth and a fresh pot of Joe on his table, Roy was content. His fondness of the blackjack table meant his retirement fund wasn’t sizeable enough to afford the luxuries many in his old profession enjoyed after their working life, but he had all he needed. A clean house in a quiet neighbourhood, a loving wife, and enough money to put food on the table and tobacco in his rolling tray. What more could a man of his age desire? He sat serenely, watching the same surroundings he watched every day. Cars purred along the street and a cluster of school-kids on their summer break strolled by. The postman, lagging behind on his morning runs, shoved a small white envelope into Roy’s letterbox and they exchanged waves. It was then that an old red car pulled up at the sidewalk. Roy leaned his crooked back forward and squinted to recognise the vehicle, but the bulbous figure that emerged from it needed no such effort. Floyd Holland closed his car door and waddled up the garden path with all the elegance of an elephant on stilts. "Well, well," Roy croaked hoarsely. "Mr. Holland." "Roy," Floyd said between wheezes. He placed a meaty paw on the handrail and hauled himself onto the porch with considerable difficulty. "You busy?" he asked, retrieving a handkerchief from his pocket to dab sweat from his brow. "’Busy’? Don’t know the meanin’ of the word these days." Roy smiled and sipped thick, black coffee through cracked lips. Though the rest of his body was failing him his taste buds were as sharp as ever, and he relished every mouthful. "How you doing, big guy? Must’ve been a couple months since you last strolled through this neck of the woods." Floyd slowly lowered his ballooned torso onto a cushioned wicker chair. The furniture creaked and cracked under his weight, and the lawyer exhaled as he finally came to rest. "Alright, I guess. How’s Martha?" "Keepin’ me on my toes as always. Today she does lunch with a couple of the neighbours." He paused for a moment, giving Floyd time to notice the calm emanating from the empty house. The only sounds were the tweeting of birds and the papery flip-flap of a paperback’s pages flapping in the desk fan’s breeze. "Bliss." Unbuttoning his top shirt, Floyd took a couple of seconds to enjoy the fan’s oscillations. The sun struggled to peak through impenetrable layers of thick cloud, but the air remained moist and balmy. For a man of Floyd’s size, summer in Houston was a nightmare. "How’s work?" Roy asked. It had been ten years since Roy had left the legal profession, but he was still interested in his friend’s fortunes. It’s difficult to completely detach from something you’ve dedicated more than forty years of your life to. "That’s actually what I wanted to talk to you about." "I knew there was somethin’". Roy smiled. "What’s it this time? Difficult client? Struggling for work?" Floyd shook his head. "Nothing like that. It’s a client; I guess you could say he’s ‘difficult’, but I can think of more appropriate terms," he spat. "Ohhh. Such indignation, Floyd: that’s unlike you," Roy said, leaning forward as much as his rigid frame would allow. "We get to deal with all kinds of bastards in this business, and I dare say I’ve dealt with a few more of ‘em than you have. Spill." "Did some work for this guy recently. Loud suits, gelled hair, wears sunglasses indoors, loves his own reflection: a real pompous piece of shit, y’know? I knew I wasn’t gonna like the guy as soon as I met him, but it was more money than I’d seen all year. Couldn’t really pass that down." "What was the case?" "It wasn’t a case, just a contract formation." "And how much was this greaseball paying you?" "Thirty." Roy’s eyes widened and his jaw creaked open. "Thirty thousand for a contract?! Jesus, who hit this guy on the head?" "That’s what I thought. He did seem a bit desperate, come to think of it, but eh. I’m not going to turn down such a good sum for so little work." Floyd shrugged. His soggy shirt was knotting and pulling his armpit hair; he pulled the material loose before it started to hurt. "Should’ve been a piece of cake, but shit, the way he sealed that contract? Guy’s a fuckin’ con artist, man. Guy should be in jail." Aware that the incident was obviously troubling Floyd, Roy watched his gaze turn to the wooden floor below. Gently tapping his words against the table, Roy waited to see if there was more to come from his friend. There wasn’t. "Well I won’t intrude and ask what it was, that ain’t my business. Must be something severe to get a guy like you all cloudy, though." "I can deal with arrogance, that ain’t a problem, but shit, we’re supposed to uphold the law. I can’t abide this. I can’t, but the guy paid so well." Floyd sighed a deflated sigh and returned his gaze to Roy’s craggy features. "Thing is, the contract was with a dying man." "He didn’t kill him, did he?" "No, of course not." Floyd recoiled. "Nothing that severe, it’s just things like this become a little trickier when there’s death involved." "You’ve gotta turn this guy in, Floyd." "The but pay—" "Fuck the pay," Roy snarled. "I’m not going to pry, but whatever happened has obviously taken a toll on you. If you don’t report whatever it is to the police then you’re just as morally bankrupt as this client of yours. You’re a lawyer, Floyd: it’s your duty." Floyd was wrenched. He know that Roy was right, and if the offender did end-up in jail then Floyd knew he’d have no problem with it, but it was a dilemma nonetheless. He couldn’t just take this man’s money and send him to the slammer, no matter how despicable the act had been. It was all or nothing, and thirty grand was a lot of money to throw away. "You believe in karma, Floyd?" Roy continued, but didn’t give his compatriot enough time to answer. "Everything balances itself out. We do a heck of a lot of dirty work in this business. No matter how good our intentions are, sometimes we end-up hosing the good guy. Opportunities to do good don’t appear often in this dirty world, so when they do you’ve just gotta grab ‘em." Floyd nodded slowly. "You’re right, Roy. You’re right." "I like to think that those four decades in the job weren’t wasted." Roy extinguished his cigarette in an almost-full ashtray. "It’s inevitable that you’re gonna do a lot of wrong in a position like this, Floyd. All you can do is try to find a balance." * * * * * Andy stepped from the church doors and watched a slow procession of melancholy faces followed him into a gunmetal-coloured afternoon. It was all black suits and ties; tears and streaks of eye-liner. An old man sobbed into a tissue, and a baby clung close to his grieving mother's torso, completely unaware of the sorrow surrounding him. Andy groaned; whoever said that funeral's were a "celebration of life" was full of shit. He stood with his brother and watched Rueben's brothers slide the coffin into the hearse. A fierce gust of wind blew dust around their feet as the first drops of rain dribbled from the sky. Andy pulled his jacket closed and stuffed his hands into the pockets, looking downward as the black-clad group clumped together. "You're doing fine, brother," Sidney said, gently patting Andy's shoulder. "Just fine." Andy said nothing; he noticed Cherise and the kids walking through the door, and that occupied all his attention. She'd kept it together. Though newly-widowed, there were no tears on her cheeks and now swelling around her eyes. She's sat-up straight in throughout the ceremony; not saying a word, but not cracking. Ninety pairs of beady eyes focused on her as she lead her a fatherless family down the stairs. "Pretty impressive, huh? Thought people'd still be scrapin' her off the floor right now," Sid said. "She's stronger than she looks." He was just about to walk across and talk to her, Andy, when he caught something in his peripheral vision. A slight flash across the dim churchyard, and an unmistakable click from not too far away. "What the..." he said, spinning around on his feet, scanning through tombstones and dying trees. There they were. Two of 'em, crouched behind a moss-covered headstone. One with a camera, the other with a notebook. Andy's shoulders dropped. "You're fucking kidding me," he grumbled. Sidney turned to face the same direction as his brother, but Andy was already marching over. Not used to their subjects descending upon them, the vultures panicked. The cameraman tried frantically to stuff his camera back into its pouch and the other one tugged at his colleague's coat, trying to coax him away. It was too late, though. Murray, brows furrowed, took the man's camera and through it against the back of the headstone. CRRRACK was the noise it made as glass shattered and plastic snapped. "What the fuck are you doing! That's a $300 camera!" The photographer shrieked. A scrawny runt of a man sporting greasy brown hair and what looked like one of Johnny Depp's more embarrassing facial hair experiments, his voice was as piercing as his slight frame suggested. "What the fuck are you doing?" Andy growled. "This is a funeral, shithead. Do you have no shame?" "Hey man, I'm just doing my job." He raised his hands. Took a step back. "Didn't you hear me? This is a funeral. Would you want some little junkie-looking piece of shit hiding in the bushes and taking pictures if it was someone you loved in that hearse?". Nostrils flaring, Andy stood towards the paparazzi and clenched a fist. His partner had long since bailed, and his face had become a picture of intimidation. "What the hell is this?" a scowling Sid barked as he walked briskly towards the scene. "Little fucker thought it'd be a good idea to get some shots," Andy said, turning back to the runt. "You want to photograph me and premières and public appearances, go right ahead. When I'm out shopping? Fine. When I'm out at night? Be my guest. But this? This is out of fucking line." The rodent-like "reporter" calculated his chances of escape Andy Murray's giant strides. "Please, man. C'mon, I didn't mean no harm..." "Who's paying you?" "I-... I can't say." Andy groaned. Suddenly he took the man by his t-shirt and pulled him closer. "I said 'who's paying you'?" "Hood," the man croaked. "John Hood." Andy relinquished his grip almost immediately. The man dropped to the floor and crawled backwards, but knew the threat of physical violence had subsided. Andy's angry gaze met Sid's eyes. "Fucking Hood, I knew it. Can't even leave me alone when I'm trying to bury my agent." Shaking his head, Andy pulled out his wallet. "How much for the camera?" Astonishment hit the man. "What?" "You heard me." "Ummmm," mumbled the rodent as he slowly rose to his feet. "Three hundred." Andy counted out a few notes and tossed them at the paparazzi feet. "Here's four, now get the fuck out of here." "What did you give him money for?" Sid asked as they turned back to the crowd. "He's not the problem: the person who paid him to be here is." "You're too fucking charitable, man. I'd have let him leave with a black-eye, nothing else." A set of eyes followed them as they walked back to the clearing. Cherise's. * * * * * Floyd Holland’s nerves were jangling as he pulled-up by the sidewalk and killed the engine. He sat in the car for a few minutes, giving himself some alone time that, in hindsight, probably wasn’t such a good idea. He’d been stewing over what he was going to do for weeks, and spurred on by a pep-talk from the man he considered his legal consigliere, he was finally ready to do what was necessary. There were two steps, and the first was the telephone call. He left his car and dropped a couple of quarters through the coin slot. Thirty seconds later he was filing an incident report. * * * * * "I'm sorry, Cherise," Andy said as softly as he could. "Truly." She shook her head as she handed their youngest son to her balding father. "You don't need to apologise, Andy. It's not your fault." "It is. If I wasn't here then they wouldn't have been here either," Andy said shamefully. "Maybe I shouldn't have come." "Come on, don't be ridiculous," she said almost scornfully. Sid, not wanting to be a nuisance, latched onto a familiar face and shuffled away towards the pack. "That's crazy talk. You were his closest client, and maybe even one of his closest friends by the end of it. You had to be here. To be honest, I was kind of expecting it anyway. Pretty relentless, aren't they?" Andy nodded, finally lifting his gaze. Rain was drizzling from the sky, but it wasn't yet heavy enough to be anything more than a minor inconvenience. "All they want is money and a story. They don't care about anything else." She shrugged. "The price of celebrity." "Maybe, yeah, but there's a line. You don't just ruin a man's funeral 'cause someone's going to give you a couple grand for some photos of a famous guy looking glum. I'll sort it out." "Do you know where they came from?" "Yeah, my 'agent'." Cherise's heart sunk lower than it already was. She'd been the one to agree to sell her husband's agency to John Hood in principal, but her fragile mental state only weakened further now she could see the damage it had done. "Then it's my fault, Andy," Cherise said, stifling a sniffle, and not for the first time that day. "I landed you in this mess in the first place. I should've taken the other offer: I'm sorry." "You weren't to know this would happen, Cherise. You hadn't heard about me and John." He had to place a hand under her chin to get her to meet his eyes again. "Hey, come on. This is Rueben's funeral, don't let a prick like Hood get in the way of that." She nodded. A single tear leaked from the corner of her eye, but she soon dabbed it away with the tissue from her coat's breast pocket. "You're right, Andy, I just didn't want this to happen." "Bastards will be bastards, nothing we can do about that." Andy paused. "You did a fantastic job organising the ceremony, though. I'm amazed you've managed to hold everything together on your own." "Well I can't grieve forever," she said through a sad smile. "I haven't quite made piece with it yet, but I will. And thanks, Andy. I'm sure he would've been proud." "He would've: I have absolutely no doubt about it." He took her right hand in his own, then gently placed his left hand over the top. ""Listen, though. I'm not going to be able to stick around for the wake. I'd love to, but my schedule's crammed. I'm sorry." Suddenly a sharp, throbbing pain return to Andy's temple. His eyes clenched and his mouth winced. For a second or two, Andy felt himself lose balance. "Jesus, Andy. Are you okay?" a concerned Cherise asked. Andy soon regained control of his posture, but the pain remained. "Yeah, just a migraine I think. Nothing to worry about." "Here, I've got some pills in my bag." Andy raised a hand to protest but Cherise was already rummaging around inside her handbag. She pulled out a bottle of pills and handed two to Andy. "Here." "Thanks," Andy said, with little choice but to take them. He threw them down his throat and swallowed immediately. "I've got to go. Hope the rest of the day goes as smoothly as the ceremony." She nodded. "Thanks for coming." "It was my honour, Cherise," he said, releasing her hand. "Thank you." They parted with a wave and Andy dragged his shorter brother away from the crowd. Sid's shorter legs struggled to keep-up with Andy's tree trunks as they walked away. "Come on," Andy said. "We're going to sort this shit out once and for all." * * * * * John was feeling good about himself. The morning had flown-by without incident and, having successfully tracked down the location of Rueben Dallas' funeral and dispatched a pest in Andy Murray's direction, he'd taken the one thing that had been getting on his nerves that morning and turned it on it's head. Having rewarded himself with an extended lunch break, Hood sat down at his desk still chewing a mouthful of sandwich. He shook his mouse to kill the screen saver and got back to work. The man's loud appearance and louder personality meant there was always at least one or two sets of eyes peering through the office's glass walls, but if ever there was a man who didn't mind people staring at him, it was John Hood. Washing down the last of his lunch with a mouthful of Sprite, John found himself distracted by an unmistakably large creature hovering around his assistant's desk. He looked-up and Kate was pointing to the office door, Floyd Holland standing in front of her desk. John let go of his mouse and leaned back as his lawyer stepped inside the office and closed the door behind him. "Jabba, you slimy bastard!". A grinning Hood put his hands behind his head. "To what do I owe this visit? If you've come to ask about being paid in weight-loss supplements again then I'm afraid you're out of luck!" Floyd's saggy features remained expressionless; he'd been exposed to so many of John's insults recently that he'd almost become desensitized to them. A response was what John wanted, and Floyd would not give him one. "What's wrong, Flabby? Not talking to me today?" "Hello, John," Floyd's faltering voice said. The sweat spreading down his back made him thankful for the cream jacket he wore. I, uhh. I." The Dallas Group's new proprietor leaned forward expectantly. "You what?" Floyd swallowed. "I came to tell you something." "Yes, okay. And what exactly have you come to tell me? The deal's done, Floyd. Enjoy your money." Reaching inside his jacket, Floyd pulled out a crumpled brown envelope and slid it across John's varnished desk. Andy Murray's agent's face twisted with confusion as he thumped the envelope open and tipped it upside down. Three cheques, each for ten-thousand dollars, fell onto the table. "You still haven't cashed these things? They can't have bounced, Floyd, there's more than enough money in the accou--" "I haven't cashed them because I don't want them!" Floyd yelped with such intensity that he almost felt ashamed of the attention he'd drawn to himself. John was taken a-back. "What do you mean you don't want them?" "I mean exactly what I said. I don't want your dirty money." "There's nothing dirty about it! Rueben's wife made the agreement, Rueben signed the contract. What's the problem?" "You know what you did, John. You know fine what you did." "What, so you've got a conscious all of a sudden?" John scoffed; he couldn't believe what he was hearing. He slid the cheques into his hand and rose to his feet. "Take your thirty grand and be merry, Fatboy. I'm not going to say it again." Floyd looked John dead in the eye, but he did not take the cheques. Instead he used his hand to push John's away. He shook his head. "No, John. It ain't right." "Well fuck you then, Floyd. Fuck you and the miserable piece of shit that you call a life." Full of venom, Hood took all three cheques and tore them down the middle. They glided to the floor like snowflakes. His bank account had effectively become thirty-thousand dollars bigger, but nobody disrespected John Hood like this. Nobody. "Good work, Lardy: you just waved goodbye to the biggest paycheque you're ever gonna make. How does it feel?" Good, Floyd wanted to say. Good, but not as good as it's going to feel when they get here. Instead, though, the lawyer remained silent. Knowing that saying nothing would irritate Hood more than responding to him, Floyd turned towards the door and slowly paced his way out of the office. He stopped just short of leaving, though, and looked over his shoulder. "I'm sorry, John," he said without meaning. "Yeah, you fucking will be. Get the fuck out of here you fat piece of shit." John Hood's last volley of abuse sailed clear over Floyd's head as he walked towards the elevators. Two men in uniform stepped from the sliding doors, heading for the first employee they could find and asking where they could find John Hood's office. * * * * * The first thing he saw when he pulled up outside the office block was the last thing he expected. John Hood, his agent, handcuffed and being led through the revolving doors by a couple of police officers. He threw his door open with great haste and slammed it without locking. Marching over to the trio as John as bundled into the back of a squad car, Andy was aghast. "What the hell?" he said to himself. The first policeman disappeared inside the car, and Andy tapped the other's shoulder before he could do the same. "Hey, what's going on?" Andy asked. "We're taking Mr. Hood in for some questioning," was the reply. "'Questioning'?! What for?!". Andy had known for almost a decade that John Hood was not to be trusted, but not to this extent. "Coercion and forcing the formation of a contract with undue force." Andy's facial muscles sagged. "I don't understand." "You know Rueben Dallas?", said the cop, leaning closer. "Guy went to see him in hospital, took his withered hand, and forced him to sign. Now step aside, sir. We have to get moving." Shock. It hit him like a brick to the small of the back. A hundred questions filled his head, but the police car was already halfway down the street when he started seeking answers. "Fucking hell," he said to himself, having to sit down on the building's steps to stop himself falling over. "Fucking. Hell." "Andy!" Sid shouted, sprinting across to his seated brother. "Hell was that about? I just saw Hoo--..." "That bastard. That utter bastard." Before Sidney could get another word in Andy had his phone out of his pocket and was dialling Cherise's number. She answered as politely as a grieving widow could, and he rose to his feet. "Cherise," he said, "why didn't you tell me?" "Tell you about what, Andy?" she asked, obviously confused." "About John and Rueben. About the way the contract was signed." "Oh, that." Her tone dimmed. "I don't know how it happened, Andy, and I don't think I want to. All I know is that I wasn't around when it happened, and that there was no way Rueben would have had the strength to ink his signature on his own." "How could you let this happen?!" Andy barked. "How could you let John march in and force his hand like that? And without telling me." "The deal was already done. What difference does it make?" "A huge fucking difference! That's against the goddamn law, Cherise. John Hood is going to jail!" "How DARE you raise your voice to me today?!" Cherise suddenly bit back. "I just buried my husband, Andy! What's gotten into you?" A sinking feeling hit Andy. "I'm sorry, Cherise. I didn't mean to--" "You know what? I don't need to hear this today," Cherise interjected. "Call me when you've cooled down." And then the line went dead. "Hello?" Andy called into it. "Hello?!" Nothing. A rush of blood hit Andy's head and, with gritted teeth, he suddenly threw the phone down against the concrete. Tiny plastic shards flew like shrapnel as the cell hit the ground. "Would you calm yourself down!?" Sid called, but it went in one ear and out the other. "They fucked me, Sid," Andy said. His balance was beginning to waver. "Everybody fucked me." Then it returned. Throb. Throb. Throb. Unrelenting, incessant, intolerable. Little bursts of pain, but worse than before and growing more intense with every heartbeat. Andy's brain started to swell and his eyes fluttered. He barely heard his brother call his name. "Andy?!" Sway left. Throb. Stumble forward. Throb. Eyelids heavy, senses failing. Throb. Hit the ground. "ANDY!" Black.
