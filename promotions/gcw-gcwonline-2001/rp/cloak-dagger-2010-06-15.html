Cloak & Dagger

15 June 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06/15/10 A besuited Floyd Holland waddled a laboured path from his Escalade, through the parking lot, and into the coffee shop. He pulled his sleeve up and glanced at his timepiece. 7:55am: five minutes early, and knowing John's punctuality (or lack there of), plenty of time to grab a coffee and scan the newspaper tucked under his arm. A few minutes later and he was sat at a table with a hazelnut latte and a muffin (he could never resist Caribou Coffee's double chocolate chip variety) before him. Wetting his plump lips, Floyd struggled to pull his too-tight jacket from his bloated frame and hat to stand up to finish the job. Soon he'd sat down down again, but found himself interrupted just as he was about to sink his yellowing teeth into the chocolatey delight. “Put it down, Fatboy,” John Hood said as he strode brazenly into the shop. It wasn't hard to spot Floyd with his 50+ inch waistline and prawn-pink skin tone, and it took Hood just a fraction of a second. John pulled a chair from beneath his lawyer's table and sat down. “What's that, you're fifth of the day?” he said, using the table's shiny surface to adjust his mid-length black hair. “Hey, I've lost five pounds this month,” Floyd protested as he sat the muffin back on its plate. Floyd's was the voice of a child: soft and high-pitched, one that belied his immense girth and sixty years on Earth. “I thought I told you about that, anyway?” “About what?” Hood asked as he pulled a completely unnecessary pair of shades from his head and slid them in his pocket. “The fat stuff. I'm your lawyer: show me some respect.” “With the amount of money I'm paying you, I'll talk to you however I want.” Floyd sighed his concession. He often questioned why he put up with John's constant stream of jocularity and abuse, but he paid as well as anyone he'd ever worked for. John cut to the chase. “So, you brought 'em?” Floyd nodded. He unfolded his newspaper and pulled a white envelope from the middle. John took the envelope, pulled it open, and ran his eyes across the papers without really taking anything in. “This is it? Five sheets?” “Uh-huh. The deeds aren't too complicated; most of what we'd normally include was in the sale contract, anyway. All you gotta do is sign.” “Where?” “Last page.” Hood's bony fingers quickly flicked to the last sheet. “Got a pen?” Wordless, Floyd took a cheap ballpoint and handed it to John. Floyd wondered if John was aware of how ridiculous he looked wearing a maroon suit in a coffeehouse usually frequented by poor college students and the recently graduated. It didn't take long for John to edge his Hancock across the bottom of the page, and soon he'd returned the pen to Floyd's pudgy hand. “That it?” “Yeah, that's it,” Floyd replied. Floyd's monotonous, emotionless tone drew a curious glance from John. “What, no 'congratulations'? No pat on the back?” Floyd's giant body seemed to expand as he took a deep breath in before shrinking back to his normal size with a sigh. He may have been of a profession occupied by slime and vermin, but Floyd had his moral fibre. “John, I don't like this. Not one bit.” “The deal got done, didn't it? I got my company and you got paid. Everyone's happy.” “I'm not happy, John.” Suddenly there was more bite to Floyd's tone. “What you did in the hospital--” “What did I do in the hospital?” John interrupted. “Please, tell me.” The scowl was burning a hole through Floyd. “You know exactly what you did. You took a dying man by the hand and forced him to sign his company over, and you've not shown a shred of remorse since. This isn't what I signed-up for.” “I did what I had to do, Floyd,” Hood said as he folded the deeds and put them back in their envelope. “I didn't force Dallas to sign anything; he agreed to the sale beforehand, remember?” “His wife agreed to the sale.” “Right. Rueben Dallas has a month left to live. He was in no state to deal with the sale of his company before his passing, so his wife helped him out. Likewise, his accelerated vegetation made it impossible for him to ink that contract on his own, so I helped him out. What's the difference?” The lawyer said nothing; just sat there stewing. He'd always known John Hood is a ruthless businessman and a fierce competitor, but never had he seen such cruelty from the man he'd had a professional relationship with for the best part of a decade. “Now,” Hood said, “you still want that bonus? Because there are plenty of things I could do with that extra money, you know.” Floyd had stood helplessly in the hospital at Rueben Dallas' door and watched John literally force Rueben's hand. He'd watched him take advantage of a frail, dying man in one of the most heinous acts he'd ever witnessed, and yet when Hood threatened to withdraw his bonus the only thing Floyd could do was sit and not. He was appalled with himself. “Good,” said Hood, standing up and stuffing the deeds into his pocket with little regard. “Keep that mouth shut in the future, Floyd. I'll call you when I need you.” John was already gone by the time Floyd had opened his eyes. Deflated, he looked down at the muffin and pushed it away. He wasn't hungry anymore. * * * * * “How the hell did you get a job with this guy anyway?” asked Scott Thomas as two steel gates slowly crept open in-front of his modest Ford's hood. “Dude's, like, a megastar.” “I told you – he knows my uncle,” Kathryn replied. There was a broad smile across his face but her stomach was buzzing with butterflies, such were her mixed emotions at starting her new job. Most of the others in her class were stocking shelves at Wal-Mart or serving drinks in a run-down bar to put themselves through college, but she was about to start working with one of North America's most recognisable faces. “I wish I had an uncle that cool,” Scott said. The gates eventually opened and the car was soon crawling along coarse gravel. “Think he could get me front row GCW tickets?” “Scott!” she said, slapping his arm softly. “Hey, no harm in asking.” Both of their eyes widened as the house came into full view. It only stood at two storeys and it wasn't quite as gigantic as some of the monstrosities they'd seen on Cribs, but the red-brick building was quite a sight for two Floridian college kids more used to dorms and bungalows. A large semi-circular porch jutted out from the building's front and the sun hid behind its arched roof, casting a shadow over the BMW and Mercedes-Benz parked out front. There was greenery everywhere, too; trees around the side, potted plants on the porch, and perfectly-manicured grass at either side. Pleasant. “Christ,” Scott said, shaking his head. “How come you get to work in a place like this and I'm stuck in KFC?” Kathryn smiled at her jealous boyfriend. “Connections, Scotty! Connections. Besides, you're going back to Gainesville on Friday, so no more chicken burgers for you.” “Guess so,” Scott said, still admiring his surroundings. He always knew that there were some grand homes hidden behind Centerville Road's dense shrubbery, but this was the first he'd seen in-person. “Nervous?” “God yes,” Kathryn said as she gathered her handbag and notepad and set them on her lap. “It's pretty daunting, but I'm not here to get all misty-eyed. I've got work to do.” “You'll be fine.” Scott leaned over and pecked her on the cheek. “I'll pick you up at around four, yeah?” “Perfect.” Kathryn opened the door and slid her legs out. “Wish me luck.” “Good luck.” Scott smiled and parted from his girlfriend with a wave. For her part, Kathryn closed the door and walked up the porch's steps. Scott watched her far enough to see her knock at the door before he drove off down the driveway and back to normality. Kathryn felt a knot tightening in her throat with every second that passed. There were voices inside (once was definitely his), but nobody was coming to the door. Growing nervy, Kathryn eventually assumed that nobody had heard her and she balled her fist again. Just when she was about to knock, however, the lock clicked and the door suddenly flew open. Andy Murray stood in the doorframe and ushered her inside. “Come in, come in.” Before she'd even had a chance to say “hello,” Kathryn Toomes stepped inside Andy Murray's lavish him as he strode back towards a boy who she assumed was his son. She watched Murray take a packed lunchbox from his kitchen table before handing it to the gaunt, spectacle-wearing child. “Now, what time does study club finish again?” she heard him ask faintly. “Five.” “Alright, I'll pick you up by the gates.” As attentive to his son as a lioness is to its cub, Andy straightened his son's blazer and looked him in the eye. “You tired, son? You've got bags under your eyes the size of tennis balls.” The kid slowly nodded. “Uncle Cayle kept me up again.” Murray paused. “I'll have a word for him. But hey, hurry on before you miss the bus.” The gangly kid crammed his lunchbox into his rucksack before shuffling away from his father. “Later, Dad,” he said, passing Kathryn without as much as a glance. “Have a good day, Marv.” Soon he was out the door and Kathryn found herself gazing at the surroundings. The great room's high ceiling extended through both of the building's floors and the white walls and cream carpets only seemed to enhance the room's vastness. Kathryn estimated that she could fit her entire parents' house inside this one room alone. “Ummm, Kathryn?” Andy Murray asked as he approached her. It caught Kathryn off-guard and she took a second or two before she could turn to look at him. The former GCW World Champion was a handsome man even when he clearly hadn't made much of an effort. Unkempt bedhair sprung from his scalp and he was dressed unspectacularly in a pair of sweatpants and a plain white t, yet his stubbly jaw-line, perfect white teeth and gleaming blue eyes more than made up for these discrepancies. Kathryn found himself staring at his chest; he was over a foot taller than her 5'6” and this was the first time she'd met someone of his fame and stature. Andy tried to brush her shyness away by extending a hand. “Nice to meet you.” She coyly put her tiny hand in his giant paw and accepted the shake. “You too,” she said, looking up nervously. “Sorry about the lack of organisation – sometimes it's a struggle to get the little man off to school when he'd gladly curl into a ball and sleep the day away instead,” Andy said, letting her hand go. “But hey, you're a student; you're probably used to a bit of rabble, right?” Kathryn's mind tried to form a witty retort, but all she could muster was a “yes”. She cast her eyes around the room again. “You have a beautiful home.” “Thanks. I just wish I could spend a little more time in it! Just got back from Australia and I'll be jetting off to Hong Kong in a few days, but I'm sure you don't want to listen to me moaning all morning. You ready to work?” She nodded. “Good. I can't tell you the trouble I had trying to get my layabout brother trying to do this stuff...” Andy lead Kathryn towards the kitchen table where he'd already set-up a laptop, a telephone, and the morning's mail. She sat down gingerly as he pulled out a chair. “Thanks,” she said. “No problem. Now...” Andy's voiced trailed-off as he turned the computer on and sat down beside his new assistant. “I'm not gonna lie; this is pretty boring. Not the most taxing job in the world but it'll drive you insane if you don't take a little break every now and then, so feel free to dive into the fridge if you get bored. Uncle Steve tells me you've already done a few clerical jobs here and there?” “Sort of,” Kathryn said, flicking strands of blonde hair from her bronzed skin. “I temped in an office last summer and I've been working in a mail-room.” She smiled. “I know all about the tedium of filing documents.” “Then I'm sure you'll blitz through this in no time.” * * * * * John Hood could barely contain his excitement as the elevator neared the top floor. He had the lift to himself and the temptation to break into song and dance was almost overcoming him. He managed to compose himself long enough for the buzzer to ding and the doors to slide open. He stepped into his new surroundings with closed eyes. Taking in two lungfuls of purified air, John eventually opened his eyes and gazed around. People had always told him stories about the Dallas Group's offices and how pleasing their aesthetics were, but they really exceeded his expectations. A floor of shiny grey marble and white walls housed mahogany-panelled booths and glass-walled offices. Natural light poured in through giant floor-to-ceiling windows as agents and admin staff buzzed went about their day-to-day duties, with only a few noticing John's arrival at first. A mile-wide grin spread across John's tanned features as he strolled through his new offices like a chihuahua with a ten-inch penis. More and more of Ruben Dallas' former staff began to pay attention to the brash man in the wine-coloured jacket as he slid through, passing those who would soon be his lackeys. “You there with the Daffy Duck tie,” Hood said, pointing towards a man sitting in one of the booths. “What's your name?” “Steven.” “Steven, that tie is appalling. Clear your desk; you're outta here.” Hood continued on his way when a balding man in a grey suit caught his eye. His smile stretched further as he pointed at the man, who looked as if he was praying for a hole to open up beneath and swallow him hole. “Terry! How long has it been, five years?” “Uhhhh--” “Never mind, you're fired!” Terry hung his head; he new what was coming as soon as his former employer had strolled into the room. John walked towards a glass door marked “Rueben Dallas” and, with no hesitation, yanked down the small plaque. It landed on the floor with an echoing clatter as John turned to face his audience. “Ladies, gentlemen: there's a new sheriff in town!” Happier than a cat who'd just discovered a way to lick it's own arse, John Hood stepped inside his new office and dropped his body on one of the room's two leather couches. Ahead of him was a spectacular view across the city, and beside him was a large quarter-circle desk that housed a telephone and a computer. The shelves at the other side of the room were empty and the walls were bare, but John would soon fill them up. He didn't stay on the sofa for long. Soon he was up and walking towards the desk, unable to resist the urge to clench his fists and scream “YES!” like his favourite team had just won the Superbowl. All he'd ever wanted was finally his; he'd not felt such a thrill since his wedding day, and the fun was just getting started. Hood strolled back outside his office and looked around the room. “Which one of you is my PA?” “Me, sir,” said a bespectacled brunette sitting behind a desk to his right. “Phone Andy Murray and tell him to get his ass here. Pronto.” * * * * * It had been three hours and finally Kathryn was easing into her surroundings. Now Andy Murray's home seemed far less daunting and the job wasn't quite as soul-destroying as Andy had made it out to be. Sure, Kathryn had essentially become Andy Murray's unofficial secretary, but it was a job she'd done before and sifting through Andy's mail was a lot more interesting than going through Target's. It didn't take her long to work through the mail and email, and soon she was scheduled appointments and organising appearances like she'd been doing it all her life. As easy as she was finding the job, Kathryn estimated that it would be quite some time before she got used to Andy's presence. As friendly and forthcoming as she was, Murray remained an intimidating character. His fame was immense and, judging by his living conditions, so was his fortune. He was a worldwide wrestling superstar who everybody knew the name of, and there she was, a second-year college student from Tallahassee, sitting at his dining table. It was a lot to take-in. Taking a breather, Kathryn slid her cardigan down her shoulders and set it on the back of her chair. The phone rang for only the fourth time since her arrival and it startled her. She picked it up, cleared her throat, and pulled it to her ear. “Hello, Andy Murray's office,” she answered professionally. “Oh, hello,” said the female voice on the other end. Whoever it was clearly hadn't expected a woman to answer the phone. “I'm calling from Rueben's offices; we'd like Andy to come-in and see us immediately, if possible.” Kathryn heard a voice barking in the background on the other end. “It's urgent.” “Okay, I'll let him know.” “Thanks. He knows where to go.” Kathryn parted her lips to utter “goodbye” but the line went dead before she had a chance. Shrugging, Kathryn stood up from the table and put the phone down and headed towards a room at the back of the house. There were voices inside, and on closer inspection, Kathryn could tell that they weren't exactly exchange pleasantries. Curiosity got the better of her and she leaned an ear to the door. Andy was almost roaring at the person he was in the room with, but whoever had phoned did say it was urgent. Kathryn's knock was answered within a couple of seconds. The voices ceased and Andy, red-eyed, pulled the door open with a forced smile. “What's up, Kath?” “Ummm, there was somebody on the phone for you. Someone from the Dallas Group?” “Huh.” Curiosity crept across Scottish features as Kathryn noticed the man standing behind Andy. “That's weird. What did they want?” With a shorn-head and tattooed arms, this man wasn't quite as refined as Andy and was nowhere near as tall. It didn't take a great deal of deduction for Kathryn to realise that they'd been arguing. Kathryn's train of thought returned. “They want you to head over there.” “Did they mention why?” “Nope.” She shook her head. “Just said it was urgent.” Andy responded with a slow nod. “Alright, thanks.” As Kathryn walked away she heard Andy spit “we'll finish this when we get back” at the other man. She returned to her seated position as Andy (now showered, shaved, and dressed more formally in a white shirt and blue jeans, swept by her. “Are you okay on your own for a little while? My brother's here but he won't bother you.” She nodded, smiling. “I got it.” “Good.” Andy walked across to the table and picked up a loose piece of paper. He took a pen and began to jot something down. “This is my cell; call it if anything important comes up.” He left without saying goodbye. Kathryn picked up Great Britain's most successful wrestler's cell phone number and gazed at it, smiling. * * * * * It was a route Andy hadn't travelled in months, but it didn't take long for Andy to travel the short distance from his house to his agent's former offices. The first he did when he reached the top floor was to walk past concerned, glaring eyes towards Rueben's old office. “Samantha,” he said, addressing the brown-haired desk-worker in front of him. “What's going on?” She looked at him from beneath a heavy brow and he knew instantly that something was wrong. “Sam?” “I think you should go inside, Andy,” she said, pointing her pen towards the door. Looking through the glass, Andy could see somebody sat with their feet on the desk, but he couldn't quite make out who it was. Squinting curiously as he edged closer and closer to the door, Andy eventually put his hand on the panel and pushed his way inside. The character behind the desk leaned back and Andy felt his stomach knot as their eyes met. “Andy fucking Murray,” John Hood said, grinning. “So nice of you to drop by!” “What the hell are you doing, John?” Andy growled, stepping towards his former Omega Championship Wrestling colleague with both fists clenched. “No wonder you've got no friends left if that's how you go around talking to people.” “It's been eight years since I cared about your approval.” Surprised, Hood raised his brows. “Oh, you don't care about my approval? That's petty Goddamn rich, considering everything that you did to me. Don't you remember Cleveland, Andy? Because I sure as hell do.” “I fucking told you this when you followed me to NC-17!” Andy snapped, his decibel garnering the attention of just about everyone on the other side of the glass. “How many times do I have to bash this into your thick fucking skull, John? NOTHING happened in Cleveland. Nothing!” “That's funny, Andy,” John said nonchalantly. “Alison's been telling me the exact same thing every day for the past eight years. It's not hard to tell when a person's lying, you know. She gets all earnest and apologetic like she's trying to win my approval back. You? You work yourself up; you get all angry and start shouting and swearing. I can read you like a book, Andy.” Murray tried desperately to calm himself, knowing that further outbursts would only play into John's hands. Nobody had quite worked their way under Andy's skin like John Hood had. Not Chris Bagwell, not Jay Terror, not LeStatt Knight – nobody. Normally Hood was the type of moron that Andy would try his best to ignore, but doing so was tough. This was the second time in 2010 that he and Hood had “bumped into each other,” and the nature of their dispute was so personal that it wouldn't go away until one of them held up their hands and surrendered. That wouldn't be Andy. “Get your feet off Rueben's desk,” Andy said, calmer but still firm. “It's not Rueben's desk any more.” Something wasn't right. Andy had seen John Hood maybe once or twice between his resignation as an OCW colour commentator and the start of 2010, yet now he'd ran into his former workmate twice in quick succession. John must have hunted him down – he must have. His anger may have been built on a misunderstanding, but it had lingered for eight years and was showing no signs of wavering. Still standing, Andy's eyes never left John. He knew what Hood thought of him and he knew what Hood would do to his career if given half a chance. He also knew that Rueben Dallas, his agent, had but few weeks left in the land of the living, that he had put his agency up for sale, and that John Hood could only be sitting behind his desk for one reason. Andy had a dreadful feeling in the pit of his stomach. “This isn't a convenience, is it?” Andy asked. “You being here.” “It most certainly isn't.” “You-... you bought it, didn't you? You bought the agency?” A pause. Hood was torturing him, teasing him. Then, eventually: “yes, Andy. I bought the agency.” Andy's shoulders suddenly felt two tonnes heavier. A thousand thoughts ran through his head, yet none of them stuck but recollections of the three-year contract renewal he'd sign with the Dallas Group at the turn of the year. Andy Murray's career had fallen into John Hood's hands. “Awww, cheer up!” John said, rising from his chair. Andy sank into the sofa and held his head in his hands as John walked towards him, shoes click-clacking against the marble floor. “Just think of all the fun we're going to have!” “Fuck you.” Hood ignored him. “Just think, Andy; your career's in my hands now. When I say 'jump', you jump. You do what I say now, Andy, and that's the way it's gonna be. And the best bit? 20% of your GCW salary goes straight into my bank account!” John leaned forward as Andy, face flush with red anger, looked up. “I got you at last, motherfucker. You're MINE.” “This is bullshit.” “Oh, is it?” Hood reached inside his jacket and produced Floyd's envelope. “The deeds are in there, Andy. You can read them if you like.” Andy didn't need to. Questions began to fly through his head faster than he could answer. What possessed Rueben Dallas to sign his company over to a man of Hood's reputation? Why would he knowingly sell his company to a man who surely wanted nothing but failure for Andy's career? And what happened next? Was Andy really at John's mercy? Was there a way out? Andy didn't know. All he could do was rise to his feet, turn, and storm out of the office. John chose not to call after him; instead he sat there, rubbing his hands together, watching Andy head for the elevator. * * * * * The last thing Andy wanted to hear when he got home were excited voices hooting and hollering but the pool, and when he heard them he almost flipped. Looking through from the lounge, he could see several figures wandering around and his brother leaning against the frame of the French doors, quite relaxed. Andy was at breaking point after his run-in with John Hood. Sudden;y his career was in the last pair of hands that he wanted it to be in and his future was far from certain. Hood would have little impact on his on-air GCW career, but he could do just about anything he wanted with Andy when the cameras were off. All Andy wanted to do was the chance to let off some steam, but that was an opportunity that wasn't about to present itself. There stood his brother; beer in-hand, not a care in the world. Still not looking for a job, still not looking for a way to repay Andy over two year's worth of rent-free living, still pissing his twenty-seven year life up a wall. Andy had had enough. He stomped up the stairs and threw open Cayle's bedroom door. Pulling a suitcase from under the bed, it didn't to empty the drawers and stuff their contents into the case. Soon he'd delved into the cupboard and packed-in his brother's shirts, before closing the case and hauling it down the stairs. He stormed through the great room and out to the swimming pool. His scowling face and towering presence caught everyone's attention. “Party's over. Everyone, outta here.” Seven or eight confused faces looked up at him. “NOW.” They didn't need to be told a second time. Though there were protests, Cayle's friends filed one-by-one from the pool area and through the house. Eventually they were all gone, and Andy was left standing face-to-face with his younger brother for the second time that day. “Hell did you do that for, Andy? We weren't doing any harm.” “What did I tell you before I left for Australia?” “Ne--” “WHAT did I tell you?!” Cayle hung his head. “That if I hadn't started looking for a job before you got back then I'd have to find a new place to live.” “There's your suitcase.” Andy stepped aside. Cayle was aghast. “Oh come o--” “No!” Andy shouted away his brother's protests. “I meant it. Every word.” “Bu--” “No if's, no but's. Get the hell out of my house.” Andy grabbed the suitcase and forced it into his sibling's hand. He pushed Cayle towards the door, pulled it open for him, and shoved him out onto the porch without any hesitation. Cayle lost his footing but managed to regain enough balance to stay standing. “Someday, Cayle, you'll thank me for this.” And with a slam of the door Cayle was gone. Andy's troubles were far from over, but he needed to cool off. He walked through to the kitchen, took a glass from the drying rack, and filled it with cold water. He gulped down two big mouthfuls before he noticed a blurry figure through the water. Kathryn. “Hey, ummm. See you tomorrow?” she asked. Andy set the glass down. “Kathryn. I thought you'd have been gone by now.” “My boyfriend said he'd pick me up at four; that's him outside now.” Andy nodded. Sighed. Finally feeling his pulse begin to slow, he walked towards her. “I'm sorry you had to hear that, Kath.” “No, it's okay. Don't worry about it.” “It's not okay, not at all. You came here to work; you shouldn't be subjected to shit like this.” He kept going until he was standing only a few feet away. Andy tried to smile. “Tomorrow'll be quieter, I promise.” A car's horn sounded outside just as she was about to respond. “That's my ride. Same time tomorrow?” Andy nodded and Kathryn left. When she'd gone out the door Andy took his half-finished glass and hurled it across the room. As the glass shattered, Andy groaned: he couldn't even hire a new assistant without something going wrong.
