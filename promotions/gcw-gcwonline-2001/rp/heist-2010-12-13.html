Date: 12/13/10 "Ninety seconds!" The patrons and staff of the United Central Bank in downtown Los Angeles hugged the cold marble floors, faces pointed downward, filled with fear and uncertainty of the situation. Two men in all black, sporting black Kevlar vests, black face masks and M-4 Carbine assault rifles in hand, hovered above the shivering. The one by the door, Eddie Cardenal, intermittently peered at his watch and out the shade-drawn front door at the passersby that early Thursday morning. Frankie Faulkner kept the barrel of his rifle pointed at the unwilling participants as he passed over them. A whimper would draw his attention, the gun floating overhead like a vacuum passing over a carpet. Frankie looked over at Eddie, who returned the look. "Seventy-five!" Sweat poured down Barry Goldstein's brow, onto his cheap polyester suit and vest below. Kev shoved Barry forward towards the vault. "Open it up," Kev belted out. Barry fumbled over his words. "The vault...the vault's on a time lock. I can't--" Triumph did not take kindly to his stalling, spinning him around and shoving the rifle into the bridge of his nose. "Open it up now." Barry closed his eyes, and Triumph pressed forward with the rifle, deep enough to leave an impression. "Now." Barry closed his eyes. "Oh...okay." He re-opened them. "Okay. I'll open the safe." He imagined seeing his daughter Sasha, playing volleyball at UCLA. She'd have just turned nineteen, embroiled in the midst of her sophomore year. Maybe she'd have chosen a path in law. She could call dad for some tips. He'd have liked that. He imagined going to her games in high school, maybe watching her win a championship or two. He'd hug her at her graduation, when tears would stream down her face, and he'd tell her how proud he was of her. He imagined and felt at ease, the events that would never come to pass. He drank more that night, and dulled the senses further. He retired to bed, and laid next to his wife. He stared at the ceiling, the fan wobbling back and forth above him. Laying next to her was nothing short of agonizing. He never imagined in his wildest dreams that the loss of their only child would drive such a wedge between them, and the gap merely widened with each passing day. He figured they had said a hundred words to one another these past seven years. He often thought about leaving her. When he had that affair with Ford a few years ago, he had told Amy. But she merely shrugged and said "it's okay." Still, he feared what she might do on her own. He couldn't sleep, and when though he thought of his wife, there was still something that dominated everything else inside his head. It was the job. These robbers, these bastards that were spitting in his face, spitting on everything he had spent his life to build. Caldera spotted Triumph in the back row and approached slowly, his hand at his side, ready for what he might have planned. He had hoped that Triumph wouldn't start a firefight in such a place. Hoped. When he took his seat behind the man Faulkner had told him was the absolution of his burdens, his head began to go light. He thought of what he had been put through, what he had put others through. "I remember my dad taking me to these old theaters, and we'd watch them on the big screen. True Grit. The Searchers. The Duke, in all his glory." "I loved those days, you know?" Triumph's voice seemed to trail off for a second before returning just as face. "And when we were done, he'd always say that it was all about the good man. The righteous man, doing the right thing at all times." Triumph smiled. "My dad, he was the good man. Worked for the city most of his life. Sanitation. Street sweeping. The unwanted and unimpressive. That was him." Triumph paused. "Then he died, and everyone forgot about him. Everyone just stopped caring. And they threw him away." If today was gonna be the day. The last day. The end." "I wonder." Triumph bit his lower lip. "I wonder if that's how he felt every day. Triumph let out a piercing laugh, one that interrupted the sermon. When all eyes turned from his attention grabbing, Triumph let out a deep breath. "Always did despise folks that beat women." "But not women who kill," Caldera retorted. Triumph took pause. He took a deep breath, as if trying to find the courage to say something, anything. Instead, he rose up from his seat. "See you around, cowboy," he said to Caldera as he passed him by. Caldera sat for a moment, closing his eyes. He rose his hand to his chest, and passed over his forehead, down to his lower torso, crossed to his right pec and over to his left. When he finished, he cupped his hands over his face, shook his head and sat in silence. ****** Triumph made his way past the stage with the strippers to a back booth at Jumbo's Clown Room and slid in next to Kev, who sat nursing a gin and tonic. You're grasping at straws, Steve," Yale smugly stated. "Just like always." "Fuck you, Dave!" Caldera wagged his finger at him. "You and the rest of your snot-nosed trust fund fucks." Director Bryan forced himself into the argument. "Agent Caldera!" "I don't care what this asshole thinks," Caldera belted out. "But if you won't do anything about this, I will." "Agent Caldera," Bryan begged, but it fell on deaf ears. Caldera shoved the chair out from under him and picked up speed out the door. Yale and Bryan shot each other uneasy glances. ****** "Ninety seconds," Eddie shouted out inside the Union Bank downtown. Alexis passed by the hostages, all face down. When he saw Triumph, hands on his forehead, his friend dead in his arms, Kev knew then it was over. ****** In an FBI town car, Caldera and Sanchez made the drive down the 10 towards the 405 and the drive to Huntington Beach.
