A Change of Season

14 March 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/14/10 Stirred conscious by March's golden morning, Murray kicked away the sheets and slid from the bed's edge to his feet. Spring was taking over the world outside. Daffodils and daisies were starting to push through the soil and shades of green were returning to bare branches. Bird's songs filled the air, and a gentle breeze carried scents of pollen and blossom through the open window. Snowy crowns remained atop the peaks of distant mountains, but the sun was shining and the mercury rising. Mother Earth was coming to life again. If only Andy's change of season had been as graceful as Denver's. * * * * * Nothing could stop Rueben Dallas three weeks ago. Seventeen years of hard graft, starting in the Neal Hamil Agency's mail room and concluding as head of one of Houston's most profitable boutique agencies, had paid off. The seven-day weeks he'd worked while cutting his teeth were a thing of the past, and his increasingly-illustrious list of clients was the envy of even some of the nation's biggest groups. The Dallas Group had been the subject of numerous bids from larger companies in the past, but they'd all been rebuffed. Rueben could have walked away from the business with enough money in his pocket to live off for the rest of his life, but selling was out of the equation. The Dallas Group was his. He'd built it from the ground up, and watched it's escalation from humble beginnings as little more than a desk and a telephone sitting in his dining room's corner. Such was his dedication to getting his firm off the ground that he'd sold his house and lived on his sister's couch for sixteen months, just to fund the venture. He'd poured years of blood, sweat, and tears into his creation. The last thing he wanted was for it to be absorbed into the American capitalist machine, but cancer had forced his hand. With but a few months left to live, there was nothing for Rueben Dallas to do but sell-up and secure his family's future. With the money they'd accumulate, the Dallas family would never have the worry about their bank balance again, even with their only earner gone. He'd left the sale in his wife's hands. Though Cherise was no businesswoman, Rueben's health had deteriorated rapidly. Even lifting a glass of water to his lips had become a challenge, so negotiating his company's sale was out of the question. All Rueben could do was lay in his tomb of over-starched sheets and a stiff mattress, praying his wife and lawyer could seal the deal alone. Cherise sat at his bedside, watching him sleep. Chemotherapy was a savage beast; for something that was supposed to ease the pain, it sure did a good job of draining the life from Rueben. Thin wisps of black were all that remained of his once bushy eyebrows, and the spark in his eyes that she'd fallen in love with would never return. Thought she knew he was dying, something inside Cherise kept her clinging on to the faintest of hopes. Three months was nowhere near enough time to prepare for the death of a man she'd spent her entire adult life with. She'd tried to come to terms with what was happening, but she wouldn't accept that Rueben was slipping from life's mortal coil until it happened. In that hospital room, surrounded by medical equipment and "get well soon cards," Rueben Dallas' thirty-nine years on Earth would come to an end. Leaving a wife without a husband, and three children without a father, he would cease to exist. Only then would it hit her. Cherise heard the private room's door opening. Two female nurses, dressed identically in regulation green scrubs, entered the room without a knock and joined her at Dallas. "Mrs. Dallas, can you give us a moment?" asked the shorter of the two in a soft, Southern tongue. "We need to change the I.V." Stifling a sniffle, Cherise stood and took her handbag from the floor. She'd stay in Rueben's room all-day if she had the choice, but there was no point in getting in the way of those trying to ease her husband's pain. Voiceless, she left the room and sat down in the deserted waiting area adjacent. The room was pleasant enough with a fish tank at one end and a potted plant at the other. Well-furnished, too, with a central coffee table surrounded by padded chairs. It almost justified the obscene sums they'd spent on healthcare over the years. After a few moments of slow, heavy breaths, Cherise opened her handbag and pulled out two A4 envelopes, both addressed to her husband. Enclosed within were two offers for the Dallas Group for her consideration, one significantly larger than the other. Pulling documents from both envelopes and setting them on her knees, Cherise glanced her bloodshot eyes over them again. Two bids, one for $2.8m, another for $3.3m. It should've been a simple choice; she and the children could comfortably survive on either, but that extra $500k would make a markable different. With it, the kids could educate themselves at Yale or Harvard, and she'd be able to provide them with everything they could ever want. Without it, they'd still enjoy a standard of living far higher than the average American citizen, but 500 grand is 500 grand. Unfortunately, it was far from an easy decision. Though there was quite a gulf between the sums, other considerations complicated matters. The lower of the two offers came from a man who'd worked with Rueben for the best part of a decade. He'd become the highest-paid of all Rueben's agents, and had justified his salary with a tireless work ethic and a knack for inking some of the brightest lights in American sports to long-term Dallas Group deals. With the ambition of a man half his age, and an unparalleled knowledge of the companies internal workings, he was probably the best man to lead the Group once it's founder was gone. His bond with Rueben was so strong that he may have expected his offer to be accepted with little deliberation, and the offer was certainly a fair one. Cherise didn't know how he might react if knocked-back, but questioned whether the possibility of losing his trust and respect was worth $500k. If only the second offer had come from such a favourable source. Though she had never met the second offerer, she knew that Rueben spoke only ill of him. "Someone not to be trusted," he said. A man who's business ethics seemingly sat on the opposite end of the spectrum from Rueben's. Was this really the kind of person Rueben would want to leave his company too? Probably not, but his financial clout was unparalleled. Cherise stuffed the documents back inside their envelopes. She had a choice to make. * * * * * There had been many occasions during Andy's career that he had questioned whether the sacrifices he'd made were really worth it, and his time spent alone in that Denver hotel room was one of them. He found himself in a position that most of his colleagues would kill to occupy. Days after becoming the longest-reigning GCW World Heavyweight Champion of all-time, The Scottish King of Cool took-on Vivica J. Valentine in his first ever NC-17 main event. On the biggest stage in the business, in front of a global audience of millions, Andy emerged victorious. He legitimized his newly-acquired record by defeating the previous holder, ensuring that his reign would surely go down as one of the most impressive in GCW history. On a professional level, he was soaring higher than ever before; but victory had come at a cost. Vivica J. Valentine, a woman he'd truly fallen for, had gone from one of the most popular competitors in the business to vilified harlot in the space of a few weeks, and all because of the strap of gold and leather that lay atop his unopened suitcase. The fans had lost a hero, Andy had lost a partner. He'd struggled for years to balance romance and wrestling. His relationship with Jane collapsed twice because of his heavy schedule, and his continued dedication to GCW eventually drove Kayla from him too. So when he found himself romantically entwined with someone who would never leave him because of the business, he was delighted. Vivica's road schedule was almost identical to Andy's; realistically, they could spend every day of the week together. The sole reason for the collapse of his previous relationships was gone. Everything seemed perfect. But it wasn't. In a moment of reflection beneath the shower's warm jet, Andy finally recognised that it was doomed from day one. Vivica J. Valentine was one of the most competitive wrestlers in the game. Her entire career revolved around gunning for the GCW World Championship; without it she was incomplete, but while there was always going to be some tension when she won contendership to his belt, Andy never imagined things would have escalated so quickly. But they did, and there he was; alone again. Vivica J. Valentine found herself so consumed by her hunger for the belt that she stuck a knife between Andy's shoulder blades and twisted. Her true colours had shone through. The girl that Andy Murray fell in love with died, replaced with a bitter, jealous harlot, willing to sacrifice even her own happiness for gold and glory. After showering, Andy dried himself down and pulled some casual clothes over his lofty frame. Picking up his belt, he examined it carefully in the sunlight, and rubbed away a smudged edge with his t-shirt. Andy and the belt had been through a lot together, and his issues with Valentine had been some of the most tumultuous times of the career. Getting involved with Vivica turned out to be one of the biggest professional mistakes he'd ever made, but he wouldn't let it happen again. He left what little love he had left for The Bulletproof Blonde lying in a wrestling ring inside the Cowboys Stadium, and he wouldn't be going back for it. The King had moved on. The past few months had been difficult, but he'd emerged from them a better man and a stronger competitor. He'd learned enough from Vivica J. Valentine to know that he'd never let anybody betray him like that again, but had the means justified the end? He still wasn't sure. Regardless, he couldn't dwell. LeStatt Knight and WorldWide 127 were just around the corner, and The Admiral of Awesome would have to be at the top of his game to overcome GCW's icon for a third time. He knew nought of what else may await him ahead of Rampage, but there was little point speculating. If LeStatt Knight didn't occupy 100% of his focus, Andy was in trouble. He'd worry about the future when 127 was done with. Putting the belt back in his suitcase and sealing it with a tiny padlock, Andy grabbed an already prepared gym back from his bed and slung it over his shoulder. Picking up his wallet and keycard, he left his room and took the stairs down to reception where he left his key with the Hispanic lady behind the desk. He stepped out into a mild afternoon and looked around for a cab but the taxi rank was empty. Screw it, he thought as he began to walk in the Pepsi Center's general direction. Denver's a pleasant walking city anyway. * * * * * Cayle Murray shuffled down the stairs of his brother's Floridian home shortly after 2pm. Armed with an empty glass and two paracetamol, the groggy Scot headed for the kitchen and poured some orange juice to aid the pills' passage down his gullet. Hopefully they'd do something about the hangover. Returning to the fridge, Cayle rummaged for nourishment. Though the remains of last night's chilli caught his eye, there was no way his churning stomach would be able to handle its heat. He settled instead for the untouched deli sandwich that someone had forgotten about. Taking a plate from the drying rack and setting the sub roll on it, he walked around the breakfast bar and pulled a stool out. His body wheezed involuntarily as he sat down and unwrapped his breakfast. Cayle felt like dirt. The older he got, the harder he found to keep up the pace. With a mouth drier than a raisined grape and a headache that pounded like a bass drum, he was in the throes of the worst morning after he could remember. The light pouring in from the window above the sink was irritating in the extreme, but Cayle couldn't shake his lethargy long enough to get up and draw the blind. Beer number twelve was a bad idea. All he wanted to do was curl up like a hedgehog and spend the rest of the day under his duvet, but he couldn't. Andy had left him responsibilities, and after living under his brother's roof free of charge for so long, Cayle knew he was indebted to his brother. If he failed to fulfil his duties then he'd only disappoint a man who'd given him another chance in life after his second incarceration. Or even worse, Andy might not pay him. Taking the first bite of his sandwich, Cayle looked around the building's expansive open-plan living area. The day was growing later and the stack of mail by the front door was large than he'd seen before. He was running out of time if he wanted to sift through the pile by nightfall, and then there were the emails, telephone messages, faxes... Closing his eyes and messaging his temples, Cayle groaned. * * * * * It had been one of Andy's pre-show ritual for as long as he could remember. The morning after arriving in a host city, The King would track down the show's location and familiarise himself with the settings. Though he'd never let it show on camera, pre-show butterflies were still a concern for him even after a decade at the top. Getting to know his surroundings was just one thing he could to to calm his nerves before the broadcast, and even though he'd wrestled in the Pepsi Center many times before, Andy saw no reason to break the habit. Though he had to walk, it took him less than thirty minutes to reach his destination, and soon he arrived at the foot of the Denver Nuggets' home. An imposing circular building of white and terracotta, The Can wasn't the most grandiose arena Andy had ever performed in. But surrounded as it was by empty parking lots and sparse roads, it dominated its surroundings. Andy couldn't but stop and admire its splendour as he stopped in front of the building's glass-fronted western wing. Pulling his eyes away from a "MURRAY, KNIGHT: ROUND FOUR!" billboard, Andy adjusted his bag strap and stepped inside the glassy foyer. Tens of thousands of rabid wrestling fans would funnel through this area in just a few nights' time, but the lobby Andy walked into was completely deserted, save for a few area employees who scuttled back and forth across its marble floor. He'd do what he usually did. First, he'd scope the backstage area and become re-acquainted with it's layout. He'd work his way through the locker-rooms and engage in idle-chit-chat without whoever was hanging around, before heading out to the ring. Passing beneath the Megatron, Andy would walk down to the ring, before hopping a barrier and getting a fan's eye view. Then, when finished, he'd find the nearest gym and pump iron until his limbs were screaming at him to stop. Simple. Or so he thought. "Well, well, well." It was a familiar voice, but one Andy hadn't heard in years, and it's source was walking straight towards him. "Andy Murray, the champ himself. What a pleasant surprise!" Andy grimaced as a tanned man in a wine-red coloured suit swaggered towards him. Though he couldn't have stood any taller than 6ft, he walked towards the 6'7" GCW Champion with the confidence of a chihuahua with a ten inch penis. "What, you got nothing to say to an old friend?" the approaching man said, stopping a few feet from Murray. "C'mon, Andy! After all we've been through." Andy said nothing immediately. He'd have been quite happy if he never had to look at John Hood's greasy mug ever again for as long as he roamed the Earth, but there he was, face-to-face with a grinning former employee. Instead of acknowledging his words, Andy glanced at John's suit jacket. "I see your fashion sense hasn't improved." "Pfffft. Five years since we've seen each other and that's the best quip you've got? Poor form, Andy; you've lost your touch." "What are you doing here, John?" Andy asked in a voice laced with impatience. He knew that this wasn't to be a pleasant exchange. "This is my town, remember?" Hood replied, removing a pair of thin Ray Bans from his head and sliding them inside his breast pocket. "Besides, when I heard GCW was in town, I just had to arrange a little sit-down with the new boss. Making contacts, y'see." Nodding, Andy took a moment to size-up the man. Truthfully, he'd aged well. His straight, black hair was showing no signs of receding despite the fact that his wage was on the wrong side of forty, and his olive complexion was as smooth as Andy had remembered. "Good for you," said Andy. "Now if you'll kindly get out of my way..." Moving to squeeze past Hood, Andy found his path impeded by John's sidestep. "Whoa, whoa. You too Hollywood to shoot the shit with an old friend these days?" "What makes you think I want to stand here and 'shoot the shit' with you, of all people?" Andy furrows his brow. "Step aside, John." "Oh, that's fucking rich, coming from you," Hood responded, abandoning his smug façade for the first time. "Obviously you don't remember what happened back in 2001 anymore, huh?" "That was a long time ago." "So you're trying to brush it under the mat now? That's classic Andy Murray. If only the world knew just how big a piece of shit you really area." Closing his eyes, exhaling slowly, Andy tried to calm himself. The last thing he wanted was a clash with someone as volatile as the former Omega Championship Wrestling commentator, especially with an important World Title defence ahead of him. "How many times are you gonna hit me with this 'guilt trip' shit? How many times do I have to say 'sorry' before it's finally good enough for you? Just change the record." "Thing like that just doesn't go away with time, Andy," John said. "You have any idea what I had to go through 'cause of what you did?" "Yes, you've told me a thousand times." "... and still you brush it off like it was nothing." "You took her back though, didn't you?" Hood nodded. "Still together to this day." "Well then." Again, Andy attempted to try and move past the man who'd called more Andy Murray matches at the start of the century than anyone else. Again, Hood would not let him by. "Out of my way." "No." "I'm not gonna ask again." "Ohhh, what'chu gonna do, Andy?" Mockingly, Hood clasped his hands together and feigned a quiver. "Please don't hurt me, Andy! Please!" "You're fucking pathetic," Andy spat. "I'm not even gonna bother with another apology, 'cause frankly, you don't deserve it. Now why don't you go fuck-off back to your rink-dink agency and push a pencil round your desk or whatever the hell it is you do out there." Finally, Andy brushed the smaller man away with his forearm and strode away. John Hood wasn't quite done, though. "You can mock all you want, Andy; but I'm going home to my wife and kids tonight. Bet you wish you could say that." Andy stopped in his tracks. "I hope Ms. Valentine gave you a nice little taste of what you put me through. Later." He could hear Hood's footsteps as he turned to walk away, but Andy wouldn't respond. * * * * * The nurses had long since cleared from Rueben Dallas' room, and Cherise had stuffed the offer letters back into her handbag. Waking back through to her husband's room, she noticed through the glass that he was stirring for the first time that day, probably woken by the drip changeover. "Hey you," she said, forcing a smile through her face's heavy mask as he walked into the room. It was a smile that Rueben no longer had the strength to return. Waking around the end of the bed and returning to her seat, Cherise sat down. Though hindered by the tubes in his nose, Rueben slowly tilted his head to meet her gaze. For Cherise, watching him struggle through consciousness was even worse than watching him sleep. It was during those times that Rueben's sudden frailties became most apparent, as his ravaged body laboured to complete even the simplest of tasks. She locked her skinny digits around Rueben's fingers and clung to him. Cherise could see the whites of his eyes, but he was barely able to keep his lids from closing. She sat there saying nothing, and for a moment the only noise between them was the heart monitor's beep. "Are you feeling better today?" Cherise finally asked. Rueben's nodded response was so slow and slight that she could've been forgiven for missing it. "That's good. At least the treatment's doing something right." Rueben knew that if she knew the truth it'd only make matters worse. He could more and more of his body dying with every day that passed. Though chemotherapy had eased the pain somewhat, there was no way he'd be able to communicate his current state without causing her further grief. A simple nod would suffice. "Listen, I know you're tired, but we need to talk about the sale again. It's time to think about accepting one of these offers." Rueben let his eyes close momentarily, before managing another slow nod. Though the nurses had told him it was best to not worry about his business, the deal had to be concluded while he still had the strength to hold a pen. "Now I know you wanted Bryan to take it over, and $2.8m is good money. But this other one..." Cherise's voice trailed off. "It's $3.3m, Rueben. That's a lot of money." Again, Rueben's eyes closed. Cherise felt guilt creeping through her brain. In the brief discussions they'd had before his treatment, Rueben had made it perfectly career that Bryan was his preferred bidder. He was a family friend and a trustworthy professional who'd surely take the company in a direction that Rueben would've wanted, and Cherise was about to advise against accepting his bid. For all the faults of the other bidder, his monetary position just couldn't be matched. Lord knows what may happen with The Dallas Group in his hands, but Cherise would have little trouble cutting it out of her mind. With Rueben gone, there was no need for her to think of it any more. Why worry about what was happening to a company she no longer had any affiliation for? Besides, Rueben was hardly in any condition to ask his name. He'd never even find out. It was for the best. "Rueb, I think we should accept the second bid," she said, tears building. "I know Bryan's your friend, but it's a $500k different. Think of us. Think of the kids." Slowly, Rueben's eyelids parted. He squeezed his wife's hand. Nodded. And that was that. * * * * * Ten minutes later, a car stopped outside the Pepsi Center's Wynkoop Street entrance. John Hood hopped into the passenger's seat and buckled-up. A few seconds after pulling away from the kerb, the driver noticed John's smile. "How'd it go?" "He doesn't suspect a thing." * * * * * Andy killed his cellphone's ring with the push of a button. "What?" "Christ, you're happy today." It was his younger brother. Andy sighed. "Sorry, Cayle. Just bumped into somebody I'd rather not have bumped into. What's up?" "Not much. How's Denver?" "Real nice city, man. It's actually quite a shame that we've got a show to prepare; I could walk around this town all day." Multi-tasking had never been one of Andy's finer skills, and after almost tripping over one of the Pepsi Center's fitted seats, he opted to sit down. "You keeping on-top of things back home alright?" "That's what I wanted to talk about, actually," Cayle said. "It's... a lot of work, man. I've been going through letters for almost an hour now, and I'm only halfway done. How much of this shit do they actually expect one man to read?" Leaning back against the uncomfortable plastic, Andy chuckled. "Tell me about it. It's during times like these when an agent's true worth becomes apparent, huh?" "Shit, at least they have assistants. I'm tryin'a do all this on my own. I mean, it's not hard work, just soul-destroying." "Ask the wee man, I'm sure he'll lend a hand." "Marv? He's gone to his friend's house again." "Sid?" "Fuck knows where he is." Again, Andy laughed. "Well, you could try asking Winston. I'm not sure how good dogs are at sorting the post and checking emails, though." A pause fell between the two, before Cayle finally piped-up on the other end. "Look, bro, would you mind if I just, ummmmm, left this for when you to do? I'm getting cabin fever here, it's killing me. I ain't even started the emails yet." Andy's facial expression soured. "What?! You were practically begging me for this job." "I know, and I'm thankful for the money, but if I'd known it was gonna be so fuckin' dull." Andy heard his brother sigh. "I just don't know how many more endorsement requests I can read before I wanna chain myself to a brick and dive into the pool." "Okay, listen. I know it's not exactly a task to look forward to, but you've not had a proper job since you got out of prison. This is good for you. Besides, Rueben'll have sold the company in a few weeks and I'll have a brand new agent. Just knuckle-down and get on with it; it'll be over before you know it." "I dunno--" "How about I start charging you rent, then?" Another sigh. "Fine." "Just think about that money dropping into your bank account next week." "You're right. Sorry." Another pause, shorter this time. "How's Rueben doing, anyway." "I, uh." Andy swallowed. "I'm not sure." "You haven't been to see him yet, have you?" Cayle didn't need a reply. "You're choice, I guess." "I will, it's just difficult right now. I've had to go from Valentine to Knight real quick. Hardly had the time to sit down, let alone do anything else. I'll go visit as soon as I'm ready." "You should. He needs you." "I know." Andy stopped to look at his watch. The hour was growing late. "Uhh, look. I hate to cut this short, but I really need to hit the gym. I'll talk to you later?" "Alright. No doubt I'll still be sitting here tryin'a sort out all these goddamn letters..." "Heh. Take it easy." Andy hung-up and pulled the phone from his ear. He used his thumb to scroll through his list of contacts, stopping at the entry labelled "Hospital (Ruebs)". Maybe he should call the hospital, maybe speak to Cherise and see how he's doing. The man had represented him for a long, long time, after all. He'd become more than an agent; he was a friend, and Andy owed it to him to-- No. Enough distractions. For the first time in his career, Andy Murray was going into a match against LeStatt Knight on a completely level playing field. His preparation for the match had been scarce enough, and the last thing he needed was something like that hanging over his head. Rising to his feet, he looked out across the empty arena, a man firmly in-control of his own destiny. * * * * * Harsh whiskey nipped John Hood's tastebuds as he knocked his head back and drained his glass. "Whoa, chief!" Eric Avila said from the other side of the desk. "Take it easy, 'this is an '87 single malt; slamming it's a waste." "Fuck it," Hood said after swallowing. "Still tastes the same, doesn't it?" Avila - a smart, clean man with short blonde hair and a million dollar smile - concedes, choosing not to argue with his combative superior. Cradling his own glass in both hands, Eric's gaze turned to his pristinely polished brogues as Hood put his feet up on the cluttered desk. "You should've seen the look on his face, Eric," Hood said, smiling. "That fuckin' shithawk. You know for years I couldn't look into my wife's face without seeing that Scottish schmuck staring back at me." "I can imagine," Avila responded, unsure of how to react. "Tried to brush me off like I wasn't there, too." John shook his head. "Ah well, soon there ain't gonna be nowhere for him to hide. And the best part? Dallas ties his clients down to contracts, and Murray's isn't up for another two years. You know how long I've been waiting for a chance like this to come along?" "Nine years?" "Almost ten. Lord knows I've been patient, but now I've finally got that adultering piece of shit in my sights." "There's only so much you can do from behind a desk." "Exactly." John placed his hands behind his head and leaned back in his chair. "Thank you, Rueben Dallas, for giving me this opportunity." Suddenly a set of knuckles tapped at the office door. John pulled his feet from the desk and leaned forward as Eric cupped his glass and turned. "Come in," John barked. The door creaked open and a spectacle-wearing female stuck her head through the gap. "Oh, sorry Mr. Hood. I didn't realise you had company." "No, no. It's alright." He smiled. "You got good news for me?" She nodded her head. "The funds have been cleared, sir. All we need now is the signature." "You beauty!" Hood said triumphantly, clenching a fist. "Thanks, Dawn." With another nod, Hood's assistant closed the door behind her, leaving John and Eric alone again. Noticing his colleague's dry glass, Avila reached for the Scotch. "Want a refill?" "Nah, fuck that." Hood waved him away. "There's champagne in the cabinet."
