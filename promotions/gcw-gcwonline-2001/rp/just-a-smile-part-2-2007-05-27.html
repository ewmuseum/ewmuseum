Just a Smile: Part 2

27 May 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 27th, 2007 I can still remember the night of May 27th, 2007. It would have been hard to forget, if I had ever tried. Not easy to forget when you spend time in jail. Maybe I should clarify. I have never, and was never in jail. But on that warm summer night I sat in the county jail for nearly five hours. It’s not very long, less than a quarter of a day, but it still stood out vividly in my mind. It would the first time I’d ever see Chris, shed a tear. It all started hours before. I was watching Scott Drake, otherwise known to the wrestling world as Chris Storm, practicing in the gym. For nearly ten minutes he had been shooting on an invisible opponent in the ring. It all looked rather boring to me, but I was dedicated to following each movement. I hoped that among his actions another piece to the puzzle that lay before me would reveal itself. But all I saw was Scott sliding in the ring and occasionally popping up as if he had just caught the invisible man. After a few more minutes, Scott finally rolled out of the ring. Though now his body was drenched in sweat and he looked as if he just lost a few pounds, something that was quite possible with the workout he had been through today. That is not to say that today’s workout was a rarity. Rather in the time I have observed him, he has spent at least four days in the gym, with one of those four teetering on torturous. This was one those practices. Offering him a towel, he stared at me for a second. His eyebrow twitched, and I had the impression that he wanted to shove me over for this offense to him. That still did not stop me from trying to strike up a conversation, "so how come you don’t seem to have any hobbies?" "Hobbies?" He said the word as if it was foreign to him. "Yeah," he finally took the towel from me, wiping the sweat from his face, "things you do for fun." His head tilted back toward the ring, "that is what I do for fun." "You train?" He simply nodded, walking in the direction of the locker room. It took a moment for my mind to process this new information. How something so obviously painful could be fun for him, was foreign to me. Rather than find a piece of the puzzle, all I managed to do was discover even more pieces. Noticing his lack of presence, I hurried to the locker room to find him already half dressed. Lacing up his shoes, he reached into his gym bag and threw on his favorite t-shirt; a black Punisher shirt. It was the one with the large white skull on the front. Somehow I pictured him imagining himself to relate to the Punisher. For a moment we sat there in silence, then without so much as looking up at me, he muttered "I’m going out for a drink." Though he still stared down at his shoes as he finished the knot, I nodded my head in agreement. A strong shot of bourbon would do me right. Arriving at the bar shortly there after, Scott took a spot right up at the bar as to get a good angle of the television. Nodding for the bartender to come over, a large and scruffy guy with a full beard walked over. Scott tilted his head in the direction of the television, "can we get some wrestling on?" The bartender just turned away, placing his thick hand on the remote, and changed the channel until a wrestling program was on. It was a Monday, so the wrestling on was crap. But Scott didn’t seem to mind one bit. In fact, he wanted intensely, never letting his eyes wander even when the bartender asked him what he was having. "Cherry coke" he stated plain as day. The bartender had already reached for the beer mug when he had to stop, interrupted in his duty. Putting away the beer mug, he looked under the bar into a small refrigerator. Coming up a moment later, he looked over at Scott, "sorry, no cherry coke." Waving it off with his hand, his eyes stayed focused on the screen. "Then just a water." Scott was funny that way. He would go to a bar for a soda. "So, have you checked in on Markus?" I asked, looking over at him from my bourbon. Markus was the reason why he trained as hard as he did today. Why he felt the need to plus himself so much. Markus was Markus Stone, the other half of Banned & Exiled~! That was the name of Scott’s tag team in a promotion called Global Championship Wrestling. He was also Scott’s longest running friend in the business. Their tag team actually sprouted from their backstage antics that got the two in so much trouble when they traveled together in a previous promotion. But Markus was hurt, and Scott wasn’t exactly the best at expressing his emotions. Scott shrugged his shoulders, "dunno, haven’t checked on him yet." Like I said, he wasn’t the best at expressing his emotions. Inside, he most likely wanted to kick himself for letting his best friend get hurt, on the outside he just stared into the clear water and looked into his reflection. I didn’t say anything else on the matter. He wasn’t about to talk and I wasn’t about to try and make him. Not long after his water, and my bourbon, arrived, another man showed up and took a seat beside Scott. He didn’t seem to have any specific interest in him; rather he just looked up at the television and noticed the program on. He must have seen or overheard when Scott had asked for the channel to be changed, trying to spark up small conversation by asking "so you like wrestling, huh?" Without skipping a beat, Chris flashed him a grin, "I better, it’s my job." The man took a long second look at the person beside him. Hanging blonde tips, crystal blue eyes, and an arrogant smirk that would make even a saint swear. How did he miss it? Beside him sat the reigning GCW Tag Team Champion and one of Legacy’s brightest new signees. If that didn’t get a smile out of the man, I don’t know what would have. Suddenly he felt as if he had known Scott forever, wrapping his arm around him and giving him a friendly tug. "Well if it ain’t Mr. Big Time! I could just kick myself for not recognizing you." To little surprise, Scott was less than receptive. I chuckled in my bourbon as I overheard him mutter, "Please do". The man missed the subtle message, to only further dig himself into a hole. Releasing his arm from around Scott’s shoulder, he nudged him in the ribs to try and get a reaction. "So let me ask, how’s that hot little thing you’re seeing doing?" When Scott simply turned away, trying to ignore the man, the guy still did not catch the clue and decided to put the final nail in his coffin. Thrusting his hips out, he gave a goofy frat boy smile to Scott, "I bet you put it on her so good she walked bowlegged." Feeling the need to save the poor fellow, I leaned behind Scott and nodded my head to get his attention. "Hey, buddy, whatcha drinking?" Thinking I could redirect the dialogue away from a subject that would get his face smashed in did little good, as the man ignored me to continue to press the conversation with his newfound best friend. If I had to guess, I would imagine that he had already been enjoying several of what he was already drinking. "So did you make her scream your name?" His head slummed down, looking for a glass. There is a quick drop in the tone, "I need to get myself a good fuck." Frankly, it all happened too quickly for my eyes to follow. My ears registered what he said. My brain processed it, enough for me to laugh, and then the poor man was down on the ground with blood coming from his forehead. In that flash in the pan moment, Scott had bounced his head off the bar like it was a tennis ball. He never even blinked as he took a sip of water, as if nothing had happened, except swatting a fly. I didn’t even know where his change in tempo had come from, and I don’t think Scott cared. The bartender just stood there with his jaw hanging out the bar, trying to figure out what happened. Truth be told, even I was at a bit of a lost. It seemed random, though to Scott it made perfect sense and was justified. When the initial shock wore off, the police were finally called. My “flight or fight” system told me to "RUN!" but my feet stayed planted on the ground and my ass kept the barstool warm. Despite the boys in blue coming for him, Scott still refused to bat so much as an eyelash at the situation. He just drank from his water and kept his eye on the television as if nothing else even mattered. Out of the corner of his bottom lip though, I swear I saw a smile. The police showed up fifteen minutes later, which would have given Scott more than enough time to get away and never have been caught. But when they came through the door with their hands on their sticks, he was still in his seat watching the program. At his side, making a mental note of all this for my dissertation, I never budged myself. So the police took Scott in, with no fight, and I followed in my car to the station. What happened to the man that he had laid out? Last I saw one of the cops was trying to revive him with the smelling salts. It was funny enough that the cops were the first people to actually try to help him. All the patrons in the bar had just left the man on the ground. Either they knew and didn’t like him, or they were in fear of getting their head bounced off the bar as well. Either way, it was none of my concern. I had only one objective, to learn about the origin of the smile. The one I thought I caught a glimpse of early was a different kind of smile, one of satisfaction, not of love. As far as my records could show, the latter smile only surfaced when he was around his pregnant wife, Tessa. But that would not stop me from recording every moment I could in an attempt to try and unlock its secret. "So, are you going to bail me out?" Chris would ask occasionally from behind steel bars. Across from his cell I sat in a metal folding chair, looking in on him like one would watch a caged beast at the zoo. I shook my head, "Not my place to do. I’m just here to observe you." He had put himself in this mess; he would have to get himself out. "Then if you’re not going to bail me out, would you go get me some fucking decent food? The stuff they serve here is crap." A laugh came from down the hall, getting closer and closer one step at a time. Up walked a portly fellow in uniform, flashing an Oscar smile. An Oscar smile was one you saw on slobs, the kind of people who went home to TV dinners and daytime television and somehow still felt lucky in their lives. I hated Oscar smiles. Scott, thankfully, didn’t have an Oscar smile and most likely never would. The man approached Scott’s cell, peering in for a moment and then waiting for some reaction. Scott was supposed to acknowledge his presence, so that the two could engage in a conversation. It was the polite and social thing to do. Scott was neither polite nor social. So instead, what the man got was a grunt and Scott turned his back to use the toilet. "Uh, sir," the man started, doing his best not to stutter. "Busy. Pissing." I nearly choked on my laughter, something that did little to help the courage of this poor officer. Taking a second look at him, I noticed that he appeared to have an 8 by 10 photo in his hand. That laughter I was holding back nearly made a second escape as I realized what was going on. This man wanted an autograph. Now I knew Scott wrestled as Chris Storm on a public stage, getting national exposure on television, so it would not be irrational for someone to be a fan of his. Those in the media do tend to attain followings, no matter how they act. But to see it in person, in this kind of setting, all seemed unreal to me. Despite traveling with Scott, watching him train intensely, and craft his art; I still did not take wrestling serious. It was male soap opera disguised as a sport. Loyal to his cause, the office waited until he could tell Scott was finished. Zipping up, he turned back to notice the person was still standing there, still wanting to talk. Rolling his eyes, he leaned against the bars. "Yeah?" The man’s eyes lit up when he was spoken to, like a child at Christmas and seeing all those glistening presents left to him by Santa. He lifted the photo to Scott, showing that it was a stock picture of him in full Chris Storm attire. It must have come from one of those meet and greet conferences he had when he signed to Legacy, judging by the background. With pudgy little fingers holding on tight, he held the photo for Scott to see. "Uh, sir, Chris Storm, my son would really’really like a’uh’autograph. Please?" I was actually half surprised he was able to utter the words without fainting, personally. Sweat had already started to build around the corners of his forehead. Scott looked down at the piece of photo then at the man, then back at the photo. I was surprised to see him take the photo. The officer quickly handed over a pen, which I’m pretty sure was against regulations, and nearly exploded with excitement when Scott laid down his John Hancock. Just when the man thought his life would be complete, Scott flashed a near satanic grin. "And the name’s Scott Drake, Chris Storm is just a ring name." The man looked as if he had just learned that Santa was really just his dad, who had accidentally run over his dog not long before. Looking down at his photo, his sad eyes casted one last glance at his false hero before he turned and returned from where he came. Part of me felt sad for him, a very small part. It wasn’t long after the officer had left that another showed up. I had figured at first it must be because Scott had made his partner cry, but rather with him was Tessa. Scott perked up instantly. The officer stopped half way before he got to the cell and let Tessa go the rest of the way alone. Then, when she reached the bars, he nodded his head to her and left. Part of me felt the polite thing to do would be to let them have their moment alone. But for my dissertation, this was a critical moment. His actions and reactions would tell me leagues about his personality. From the look on Tessa’s face, it was clear that a number of emotions were running through her veins at the moment. At the moment, the most prevalent showed how mad she was. Her small fist hit the cold metal bars as she leaned in. "The bail is three thousand dollars." Her voice was weak, but was still filled with anger. "That is three thousand dollars that could be in the baby’s college fund." Scott, however, did not seem to see that as a problem. He moved in close to her, "Don’t worry, I can make the money back in no time." Her eyes refused to come up and meet his, rather choosing to look away at the grim on the wall. "That’s not the point," she muttered. "But I want to know why you felt the need to attack that man." This time it was Scott who turned his attention away. His back came up against the bars, "I had my reasons." Tessa looked my way to see if I would offer any input on their discussion, as she must have known that I was there when it happened. But as an observer, I kept my mouth shut and kept my focus on my notes. When it became evident that I wouldn’t talk, she looked back at Scott with puppy dog eyes. "Please," she begged, "just tell me why." With his back still facing her, Scott wouldn’t say anything for a solid minute. Tessa was about to leave when he finally turned around to face her. Lifting his eyes to her, his lips parted. "Did you post the bail?" His avoidance of the subject seemed like an arrow to her chest. Those puppy dog eyes looked tired and weary all of a sudden. "I can’t keep doing this Scott. I just can’t." For the first time in the day, I witnessed real emotion in Scott Drake. Reaching through the bars, his arms wrapped around her rounded and pregnant waist. Hugging her close, though the bars still separated them, he let his face embrace against her skin. Her tiny fist hit his chest, her face turned to the side. From my angle I could see the tears starting to build up, but she held them back furiously. She wouldn’t let him see her cry, not here, not like this. "You know you’re bad for me" she told him as he hugged her. It was soft, nearly silent, but I heard the words from her lips. It was enough for me to put down the pen and pull my eyes up to them. I wanted to see what Scott would do next. Tilting his head down so as to look into her face, his finger cupped her chin and pulled her face up to meet his. "I know" he said, "but you still love me." She pushed away from him, breaking their embrace. They just stood there for a moment, under that dim hanging light in the cell. The entire world had ceased to be, everything just hung on the seconds the two shared now. Even the very laws of time and space bended for them as it turned those seconds into hours of silence. "You posted the bail, right?" Tessa turned away from her love, the father of her soon-to-be child, and looked out at the hallway from which she came. One foot stepped out in front of the other. "Yeah." Then she said nothing more, simply walking down the hall and out of sight. I looked to Scott, to see what his reaction would be, but he kept a stone express. Then, out of the corner, I saw that smile. A single tear drop followed it. It was the first time I had ever seen Scott shed a single tear. I didn’t know if it was for his actions or for Tessa. She had posted the bail, all three thousand dollars of it. It was taken from their savings account, wired into a money order and dropped off at the station. A cop, the same one that wanted the autograph, would come and open his cell to release him. This time, however, the poor man never even uttered so much as a single word to his pseudo hero. He just walked him down that same lonely hall that Tessa had taken an hour before. I still didn’t understand why it took an hour for Tessa to post Scott’s bail. And frankly, it wasn’t my place to ask. The best I could think was that she wanted him to have time to think, to be alone with his actions. I didn’t follow him back to his condo; I already had more than enough for the day to put into notes. Instead, I sent the next three hours pouring over mountains of notes, organizing them into my laptop. Each piece of information needed to be dissected, examined, and looked at for a new clue to the puzzle. All I knew was that the smile just didn’t fit. It felt eerie each time I saw it, like something out of a thriller flick. But it was real. And I needed to know why.
