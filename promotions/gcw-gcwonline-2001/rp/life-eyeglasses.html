Life: Eyeglasses

Date: 9/15/89 START 2 Years Old Fondest Memory: The Eyeglasses 5:08 am It was one of those lazy mornings. I could afford many of them considering how lucky I had it. Although I remember being cranky more often than not, my new parents treated me with a ton of love. I had even forgotten that they weren’t my biological superiors. By the time I hit age two, I wasn’t as rambunctious as most other kids. I mean sure, everyone goes through the terrible twos but I just didn’t seem to have it that bad. Like I said, I was crankier than anything else. So there I was. On an early morning after a restless night, and I crawled out of my parent’s bed to go waddling throughout the house. Everything is soooo big! The cold tiles beneath my feet feel like ice! I could potentially stick to them! I just need to get to the brown carpet over there. It’s like the best carpet ever. Man was that carpet fluffy. It was rather thick, and that’s what I loved so much about it. Whenever I’d get lost or run away from my parent’s, I’d always draw recognition from the brown carpet. It became my little place to be. If baby Superman had a fortress of solitude; it was the brown carpet. It was the equivalent of having a dog. I used to lie on that carpet all the time and watch television with my family. My Dad was a successful car associate with the local Volkswagen dealership. My Mother was a part time homemaker during the day and a part time line chef at Denny’s during most nights. They never had money problems? they never hurt me? they never made me feel unwanted? they never fought, and if they did; it was always civil. Zane and Claire Fairmount were their names. We lived on Cherrington Drive. This has to be the best life ever. Who’da thunk you can bypass the terrible twos? Or at least? most of it. I hope this feeling never goes away. I love my family. I feel loved. Deserved. Deserving. And most of all; I’m glad Mom and Dad don’t hate each other because of me. Even at the young age of two years old, I used to lie there and dream about the future. What I would do when I go outside, what different places I would see and explore. So many exciting times ahead. Except for today? the day in September that my crankiness got the best of me. So like I said, I was lying on the carpet minding my own business. My Mom woke up shortly after to make breakfast. “You’re up kind of early cutie,” Mom said as she sauntered into the room. She was such a nice looking Mom. Just your prototypical Mother. Nothing extra special about her. She was just a fun gal. Her big curly brown hair complimented those typical 80s styled glasses. Her figure was quite appealing. She said that I’d always stare up at her with big blue eyes. Like blueberries that she wanted to pluck right out of my head? in a fun and loving way of course. I remember drooling at the sight of Mom in the kitchen. My Dad did too? but for the other reason. Heck, I would too if women in long t-shirts turned me on as a kid. Yum! Make me food please! I guess that’s why I got up so early. I’m cranky because I’m hungry. Gee whiz Mom, you sure know your son. She smiled and made quirky sounds as she was cooking. You could hear the sizzling of bacon and the soft sear of eggs. She knew that I went bananas for scrambled eggs. By far, my favorite thing for breakfast. My Dad got up shortly after and came down stairs dressed for work. He always looked pretty sharp. He wore a decently expensive Rolex watch, a pinstripe suit, shiny black shoes and his trademark; yellow suspenders. He would always kiss my Mom before leaving, as he did that day too. “I’m sorry hun but I really gotta run. I’ll grab something on the way to work okay?” My Dad asked. That’s fine with me. I mean? I’ll just get more breakfast! But I’ll miss you Dad. Have a good day at work. “No worries dear. Maybe just have a glass of Orange Juice before you go. Clyde and I are going to have my famous Eggwich breakfast. Then maybe today we’ll go shopping for a new little outfit. Oh” and don’t forget about tonight Zane. You promised you’d take me to the theatre tonight,” Mom reminded. My Dad adjusted his tie quickly as he followed it up with a glass of freshly squeezed OJ. And if you’re wondering how they knew my name was Clyde Fox? well, my original Father wrote my name on the tag in the snowsuit I was wearing. His chicken scratch scribble was barely legible, but it still resembled a name that read “clYd3 f0cx” in black marker. “Oh yeah. I almost forgot. Should be a lot of fun tonight. I heard that it’s supposed to be a great play. Alright, I’m off. I’ll call you on lunch,” My Dad replied as he gathered his brief case, keys, and a kiss from Mom, “You behave yourself sport. I’ll see you when I get home,” he said as he turned and pointed at me. I was all excited about the day. But then I had to go and ruin it. It was probably the single worst memory that I ever did to those two. Just as my Mom finished cooking, she grabbed some plates, set the table, and came and picked me up. She was about to place me down in my chair but I started to cry. No! I wanna be held by you still! Don’t put me down! I want to see all the food you made from your height. I wuv you Mom. You’re the bestest Mom a baby like me could ever ask for. My Mom tried to juggle me in one arm, and the food in the other. She almost dropped everything, but her chef instincts kicked in as she made it to the table with everything intact. Hmmmmm. Wait a second. Those don’t look like Eggwiches! In fact? those don’t even look like eggs at all! I began crying like there was no tomorrow at the sight of the partially burnt eggs. I mean? I expected perfection. And at that age, just about anything set me off. “What is it sweetie?” My Mom consoled, “Is it about the eggs” I know baby, I’m sorry. They got a little overcooked. Mommy is trying out a new pan spray. It’s because everyone at work is using it too. I just need to get the hang of how long to cook things with it, that’s all,” Mom explained. I didn’t bother listening. I was filling up with anger. The sadness that my breakfast was murdered slowly turned into hatred. My Mom still had me in her arms as she walked back over to turn the stove off. I remember the feeling that jumped into my body at that moment. I stopped crying. I don’t think you know who I am bitch. I am CLYDE MOTHER FUCKING FOX! I get what I want, when I want, and it better be perfect? OR ELSE! I head butted my Mom in the face. Shattering the right lens of her glasses into her eye. The whole other lens popped out of its holder and fell into the pan with bacon grease sitting in it. The lens splashed the grease up, searing my Mom’s arm that was holding me. She dropped me out of reaction. I smacked the floor hard but I wasn’t really hurt at all. I remember looking up at my Mom and seeing droplets of blood falling down to me. Like sparse raindrops tumbling off of a leaf, the droplets bounced off my forehead. She grabbed her left arm as steam was wafting off of it. All I could think to do was to crawl for the brown carpet. My safe haven. I darted there as fast as I could. I didn’t mean to do it. But my aggression got the best of me. That event taught me about my temper. I don’t really have one? but when I get pushed around or find something going on that I don’t like, I end up lashing out in a big energy filled event. Once I was on the brown carpet, all I could do was curl up and cry. My parents didn’t end up going to the play that night. My Dad had to come home right away and take her to the hospital. I never really got in trouble for it. My Mom thought it was more of an accident than anything else. And at two years old, a rational person would think the same way. But I knew what I had done. And it scarred me for life. Not the fact about what I was capable to do as much as? it was a waste of eggs. They went cold. END
