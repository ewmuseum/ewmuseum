Biting The Hand That Feeds You

29 August 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/29/09 The present ... It wasn't exactly the debut that Cade Carson had been hoping for. He'd visualized his match against Dan Black countless times, his mind playing out every second of the contest, from the opening bell to the moment the referee raised the winner's hand in victory. In his mind's version, he'd come out on top. But in reality, that wasn't what had happened. He'd lost, his professional record already blemished after just one bout, his dreams of making an instant name for himself shattered into oblivion. He'd put up a valiant effort, but was out-wrestled and, perhaps, out-classed by the former Television champion. Black's ring savvy proved too much to overcome for the newcomer to professional wrestling, and while Cade couldn't be faulted for his effort, drive or determination, in the end it just wasn't nearly enough. Since that night, Cade had spent his time wondering where it had all gone wrong. It wasn't as if Black had dominated him from bell to bell. Cade had his moments during their match in which he looked strong, even capable of pulling off the upset. Anyone in attendance would attest to the fact that, for portions of the match, Cade had managed to gain the momentum. But no matter what he threw at Black, no matter how much of an advantage he seemed to have at a given moment, he just couldn't put Black away. It was as if it was destiny that had chosen Black right from the start, and Cade's actions would ultimately prove futile. A part of Cade resented the fact that no matter how well he'd performed that night, God had already decried that he was going to lose. God, he thought bitterly. When has he ever been there for me? Every time I needed his strength, he seems to abandon me. Has he forsaken me? And if so, maybe I should do the same to him. Almost as soon as the blasphemous words entered his mind, he regretted thinking them, regretted giving voice to his doubts. He glanced around nervously, as if at any moment he would be struck down for his sacrilegious thoughts. It wasn't God that had failed to defeat Dan Black. It was Cade. The only person he should blame was himself. Maybe Father Michea is right. Maybe my taint is so strong that it'll take years for me to repent for what I've done. He needed to talk to someone. He had no answers to the questions he asked of himself. Perhaps he was too close to the situation to see the obvious. Perhaps an outside perspective would have the clarity that seemed to elude him. Father Michea will give me the answers I seek. He's never let me down before. But then a troubling thought crept its way into his thoughts. What if Father Michea couldn't help him, couldn't give Cade the peace of mind that he sought? What then? ~ ~ ~ Cade paused at the entrance foyer of the church, just before the back row of the two sections of benches, his fists clenching at his sides, his jaw muscles twitching. It was mid-afternoon on a weekday, so there were no masses scheduled at this time. Currently, the entire audience chamber was empty, so after bending on one knee and making the sign of the cross, he made his way down the aisle of seats, toward the front podium, and then continued to the left of it, toward a doorway that lead to the offices situated at the back. As he approached the door, he noticed it was closed. Reaching for the door handle, he heard faint noises from the other side. Leaning closer to the door, he listened carefully. The door managed to muffle the words that were being spoken, but he was certain he recognized the familiar voice of his boyhood mentor Father Michea, as well as that of a teenage boy he didn't know. Cade cringed inwardly. Without realizing it, his heart rate increased and his palms became sweaty. He swallowed the lump rising in his throat, then raised a shaky hand to the door. The first knock was tentative, his knuckle barely making contact with the wood. The second and third knock were more forceful. He moved away from the door and waited. A few moments later, he heard footsteps approaching the door, then forced a neutral expression onto his face, as it swung open. Standing before him was a boy younger than he'd expected, perhaps barely in his teens, wearing the same look Cade often saw in his own reflection when he'd look into the mirror. "C...Can I help you?" the boy asked. He was wearing a pair of brown shorts and a blue and red striped rugby shirt. The shirt seemed disheveled, as if the boy had put it on in a hurry, and his medium-short brown hair was mussed. His cheeks were flushed pinkish-red. A sudden anger rose within Cade. He blinked it back, shaking his head to clear his thoughts. "I'm here to see Father Michea," Cade said, his voice cracking slightly. "Oh, uh ..." the boy glanced back nervously. When he looked back at Cade, Cade could have sworn he saw an imploring look in his eyes. "I don't think he's here right ..." "It's okay, Brian," a familiar voice called out from inside the office. "You can let him in." The boy did as he was told, moving aside to allow Cade into the office. Sitting behind an antique wood desk, his hands resting comfortably in front of him, was Father Michea. Dressed in a pair of black pants, black dress shirt, he was in the process of buttoning up the final button on his shirt. His thinning hair seemed almost too perfect, with not a hair out of place, as if it had been combed in the last minute. His cheeks had a rosy hue to them. "Cade!" Father Michea exclaimed, his face lighting up. "To what do I owe this surprise?" Cade glanced at the boy, standing quietly to one side, his eyes staring down at the carpeted floor, then over at Father Michea. "I needed to talk," Cade said. "But if you're busy ..." "Don't be silly," the priest waved his hand dismissively. "Brian and I were done here, weren't we, Brian?" The boy nodded mutely in response. "Run along, then," Father Michea instructed the boy. Glancing momentarily at Cade, the boy then lowered his eyes and made his way silently to the office exit. Just as he was walking through the door, Father Michea called after him. "Remember what we'd talked about today, son," Father Michea counseled. The boy paused at the doorway, turning to look back at Father Michea. "I expect to see some improvement when we next meet in, say, a week's time?" The boy nodded, his eyes drifting to the floor once more. His shoulders seemed to slump, and his voice was barely above a whisper. "Yes, Father." Father Michea smiled. "Dismissed." After the boy had left, Father Michea got up from his seat and made his way to join Cade by the entrance to his office. He wrapped his arms around Cade in a fatherly embrace, clapping him on the back. Cade returned the embrace, albeit reluctantly and with much less enthusiasm. Father Michea pushed him back to arms length, looking him straight in the eye. "I really am glad to see you, Cade. To be honest, I was going to give you a call this evening. I was worried about you." Cade frowned. "Worried? Why?" Father Michea paused as if searching for the most diplomatic way of answering. He maintained his warm smile, but Cade could sense the older man's mind working a mile a minute, trying to decide on the right words. Instead of answering, however, he moved toward the exit to the office and gestured for Cade to follow him. "I've got a few tasks to attend to before I go home for the evening. Come, we can talk while I work." Cade wanted to press him further about what he'd meant, but before he had a chance Father Michea was already out the office door, heading toward the closest wall of the main audience chamber. Cade followed, his anxiety building. Father Michea stopped before a long table, covered with a white cloth. Two rows of candles sat, an even number in front and behind. He reached into his pocket and retrieved a box of matches, flipping open the box. Snapping one off, he struck the match, setting it aflame, then began to touch the tip of the lighted match to the candles in the back row, starting on the left. Cade watched silently as the elderly priest went about his work, his hands wringing together in front of him nervously, waiting for Father Michea to speak. He was rewarded a couple of candles in. "I saw you on television the other night," Father Michea said over his shoulder, as he continued along the row of candles, lighting each one as he passed. "That little interview you had before your match, it was ... interesting." Cade cringed. He wasn't sure how he came across to the audience, worried that he'd went overboard. His mentor's vague comment did little to ease his worry. It wasn't that Cade was necessarily acting, but rather what the audience saw was an ... exaggerated version of who Cade Carson was, a glorified version of his beliefs. He'd been told by those who trained him that professional wrestling was one part athleticism, and one part showmanship. What Cade gathered that meant was that not only must a wrestler be talented inside the ring, but must also be able to market himself outside of it, in order for him to be someone a company could build around. It was a lesson he was determined to heed. Maybe Cade still needed a lot of work on the showmanship part. "That bad, huh?" Cade asked with barely concealed trepidation. Father Michea turned from what he was doing, to look directly at Cade. He smiled, in that same fatherly way that both made Cade feel warm inside, and just a bit uneasy as well. "Of course not. I just hadn't realized how much my teachings influenced you," Father Michea said with just a hint of teasing. Cade chuckled. "Well, I guess we can officially write off the entire evening as a complete failure then, huh." Father Michea frowned at that. "How so?" Cade lowered his eyes to the ground as he answered. "Not only did I lose my match, but I came across like a complete fool as well." He snorted bitterly. "Not exactly how I envisioned my debut." "And how did you envision it would go?" Cade paused, wrestling for an answer. "I don't know," he said tentatively. "I guess ... well, just not like that." "I see," the priest said, almost absentmindedly. Cade waited for Father Michea to expand on his comment, but when he didn't Cade lifted his eyes from the ground to the priest. He scowled as he noted that the older man was now in the process of arranging the flower display beside the speaking podium. A twinge of irritation struck Cade then, annoyed that he appeared to only half be paying attention to their conversation. "Should I come back another time?" Cade asked, a little more testily than he'd intended. Father Michea turned to him, a small yellow flower that Cade didn't know the name of held in his hand. "Of course not," the priest said kindly, either oblivious or ignoring Cade's ire. "Come, I know what will put your mind at ease." Placing the flower with the rest of them in an ornamental vase, Father Michea lead Cade down the steps of the alter, to the side of the church where a small shed-like structure sat, with two doors sitting side by side, known as a confessional. It was where the masses came to confess their sins and look for absolution, or as Cade often referred to it, a Catholic's get-out-of-jail-free pass. Commit sins all week, show up on Sunday and ask for forgiveness, rinse and repeat. As Father Michea entered the door on the left, Cade made his way to the door on the right. He wasn't even sure why he bothered with this ritual. He'd never felt absolved after any of his confessions to Father Michea. Instead, it just made him realize just how ... imperfect a life he lead. Inside the confessional booth, they were separated by a mesh window, which obscured the appearance of the person on the other side. To Cade, it was like looking at Father Michea through a glass window covered in dirt and grime. Father Michea sat on one side, his left side closest to the mesh window, while Cade knelt with his elbows resting on the ledge in front of him, hands clasped together. "You may begin whenever you are ready, my child," Father Michea prompted. "Bless me father, for I have sinned. It has been two weeks since my last confession ..." ~ ~ ~ After Cade had professed his sins, ones he was aware of and ones that he'd only realized he'd committed after Father Michea had pointed them out to him, he'd relayed to his mentor what had brought him here today, and what he'd hoped speaking with Father Michea could do for him. "Cade, you know I can't solve all your problems for you," Father Michea said kindly, but with a hint of admonishment. "I know. It's just ..." "Just what?" the priest asked. Cade paused, fighting for the words. "I don't know where I went wrong," said finally, giving voice to what had been troubling him ever since his match. "I did everything I possibly could do to prepare," he continued. "I trained hard, I exercised, I kept the advice of my trainers in mind while doing both. I even secluded myself in my room two days prior to the match, so that I could clear my mind without any needless distractions. And yet ..." He didn't finish his sentence, the memory of the loss still as fresh as an open wound. He lowered his gaze to stare at his clenched hands, waiting for Father Michea's stern voice to lecture him, to tell him what Cade believed he deserved to hear -- that he was a failure. When Father Michea finally responded, his words shocked Cade into stunned silence. "I absolve you of your sins, my son." Cade's head shot up, his features pulled back in a frown of confusion. "What? You ... You forgive me?" "No, it is not me who forgives you," Father Michea corrected. "The Lord does. I am simply his messenger, and through me, he has accepted your request for absolution and granted it. Your sins have been forgiven." Cade knelt in the confessional, dumbstruck. He came here looking for actual advice from a man who he held in high regard. Instead, he received the same token respite that anyone else who had given up ten minutes of their day to come to confession would have received. For a reason he couldn't quite understand, he felt cheated. Father Michea must have noticed Cade's lack of a reaction to his statement, because he leaned forward close to the mesh window that separated them and said, "Is God's forgiveness not enough for you, Cade?" Cade shook himself from his stunned silence. "No, it's not that. It's just ..." "Are you above the rest of us, Cade? Are you better than anyone else seeking God's forgiveness?" Father Michea's tone was becoming harsh, condescending even. "No ..." "Then, what, Cade? You came here today to confess your sins, and because of your repentance, the Lord saw fit to grant you absolution." The priest let out a snort of disdain. "And you reward his generosity by, what? Demanding more from me? Demanding more from him? How arrogant of you." "Of course not. I just ..." "Perhaps that is why you lost your match," Father Michea went on as if conducting one of his sermons. "Perhaps your hubris is so great that you truly believe yourself to be the Chosen One, that a gift from God himself still isn't enough for you. You want more. You crave more. And in doing so, you shun the one who watches over you, and who keeps you safe." Cade's next protest was barely above a whisper. "No, that's not it." Father Michea made a tsk-ing sound, and Cade could visualize him shaking his head on the other side of the mesh screen. "Maybe I was wrong earlier. Maybe you are a failure." Father Michea paused, as if giving Cade time to let the last word sink in. When he spoke once more, the anger in his voice had disappeared, replaced by a weary, almost disappointed tone. "Go, now. Think on what you've done here today. Maybe once you've proven you're truly remorseful, you'll be welcome again in God's home." "I'm ... sorry," Cade said, his voice soft, trembling. But Father Michea had already exited his side of the confessional booth, leaving Cade's apology to fall on deaf ears. He knelt in the darkness for a few minutes, his eyes closed, breathing steadily through his nose. He felt alone. The sensation reminded him of the day he'd been notified that his parents had gone missing, assumed killed in a plane crash but never confirmed, when Cade was just a child. He reached out instinctively to the one hand that had always been there for him, through thick and thin. His eyes opened, his breathing got heavy, as he came to the realization that that hand was no longer there for him either. You brought this upon yourself, the voice inside his head taunted. Good job making us look bad, genius. It was then that he remembered he was never truly alone. But that realization didn't bring the slightest bit of comfort to him.
