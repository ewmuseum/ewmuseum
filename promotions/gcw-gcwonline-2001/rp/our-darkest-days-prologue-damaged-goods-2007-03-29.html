Our Darkest Days, Prologue: Damaged Goods

29 March 2007

Date: 29/03/07 (written 11/01/08) “The Scottish King of Cool: Reborn in 2008?” By Scott Coleman of the Pro Wrestling Journal (http://www.pwjournal.com), written 3rd January 2008. Andy Murray is pretty damn cool. Unfortunately, if you looked up the meaning of the word “cool” in the dictionary (and we’re not talking about “moderately cold,” okay?) you wouldn’t find a picture of Andy Murray because dictionaries don’t have pictures, you stupid bloody idiot. Instead you’d find the definition of a word that suits the Scotsman like nobody else; “great,” “fine,” “excellent,” “highly skilled,” “socially adept,” you name it. According to those around him, Andy Murray is so cool that he was once asked by Will Smith to play him in a movie of his life; a role which Andy turned down only because Mr. Smith wanted Martin Lawrence to star alongside him. Can’t really blame him though, can you? Who the hell wants to co-star with Martin Lawrence. Another common rumour doing the rounds is that the real reason Eminem hasn’t released anything recently is because he knows his rhymes will never stand up to Murray’s phat mad wicked rhyming skills, yo. The factual accuracy of the above statements is most certainly questionable; what is not questionable, however, is that Andy Murray is a pretty awesome dude on the whole. The thirty year-old Aberdeen native has carved out a nice little niche for himself in the wrestling business; he’s toured the world, competed against some of the very best that the business has to offer and he’s held championship belts just about everywhere that he has been. From his initial rise to prominence in 2001 as an employee of OCW and PCW, to reaching a whole new audience in 2005 as the Frontier champion of the now-defunct Atlantic Wrestling Club and right up to his short, ill-fated run as an employee of FUSE, Andy Murray’s natural charisma has always set him aside from his peers. His in-ring ability may be somewhat average when compared with some of today’s modern technicians, but what he lacks in technique and innovation he more than makes up for by being one of the most consummate professionals in the business. And yet, in spite of what looks like a relatively impressive curriculum vitae, chances are you’ve never even heard of Andy Murray. There are a good few reasons for this, the main one being that in spite of having been in the business for over a decade (eight years as a full-time professional) it could be said that Murray has yet to reach his peak as a performer. His career has progressed in a very staccato, stop/start manner ever since he sustained a horrendous back injury whilst competing under an open contract in the upstart New Legends of Wrestling promotion back in 2003. It is this very injury that has come back to haunt him on numerous occasions; forcing him to take twenty four months time out from the business, forcing him to leave the AWC on two separate occasions, and forcing him to drop his FUSE Fatal Faction championship (co-held with recently incarcerated brother Cayle) on his first defence. If it weren’t for said injury then it’s entirely possible that when your typical wrestling fan, when they heard the name “Andy Murray,” would straight away be reminded of a sublime talent good enough for the Main Event of just about any promotion worth its salt instead of the uninterested shrug of the shoulders that you’d be met with, in reality, if you brought up the name in conversation. Injuries aside, the self-styled Scottish King of Cool has had an incredibly turbulent year at a personal level. I don’t want to go into too much detail and stray too far from the topic at hand, but some of the things that this man has gone through this year regarding certain family members would have driven a lesser man to depression, and perhaps beyond. So given the Scottish grappler’s recent troubles, it may have come as a surprise to some eagle-eyed wrestling fans that the name “Andy Murray,” recently appeared on the Global Championship Wrestling roster. I know that I, for one, was pretty shocked when I sat down to watch NC-17, only to see the Highland Hatchetman make a long overdue return to our television screens. To speak the truth, I figured that if we were ever to see him compete again it would be at a much lower level. But no, at the age of thirty, and at a point in his career where many now view the injury-prone superstar as being past it and over the hill, Mr. Murray has just been thrown the biggest opportunity he could ever have wished for. Surely hiring someone with such a long injury record would be an extreme risk, especially for a promotion with a global audience like GCW? Well, think again. It is my understanding that there’s a “get out” clause somewhere in Murray’s contract that would allow Steven Caldera to dispose of his services if and when his troublesome back injuries should ever resurface (which, lets be honest, is pretty likely). A smart decision on behalf of the GCW, no doubt; if his back decides to stop working properly again, they can simply pull the plug. But what if, just this once, the injury doesn’t recur? What if the Scotsman is finally able to string together to kind of run that he has always promised to, but not yet really been able to produce? What if, ladies and gentleman, the Scottish King of Cool is finally able to shake off the unwanted tag of “perennial underdog?” Then, folks, GCW will be on to a winner. ---- March 29th, 2007, Aberdeen, Scotland. Scotland’s third largest city wasn’t exactly the most inviting of places at the best of times, let alone on a bitterly cold March evening. By all accounts Aberdeen should shine; the granite structures that accounted for the vast majority of the city centre’s buildings should sparkle in the sunshine, or even with the reflections of car headlights. Europe’s oil capital should be vibrant and full of life, with local businesses thriving in the prosperity the oil industry’s money brought to the place. Unfortunately, things don’t work out like that. Many of the city’s famous granite buildings have gone unwashed for many years; their dull grey colour only adding to the overwhelming sense of monotony an outsider feels when they first step into the city. They called it “The Silver City,” silver hardly seems appropriate, especially on a gloomy, rainy day (of which this part of the world has many) when being in a city so devoid of colour and brightness could have disastrous effects on the morale of those not used to the city’s fairly depressing aura. Many who have travelled the world often refer to Aberdeen as “the Seattle of the British Isles,” a tag that would prove hard even for the proudest of Aberdonians to argue with. There are certain types of people that just belong in places like Aberdeen. With its numerous suburbs and nearby towns, The Silver City was commuter heaven (or hell, if you prefer). It’s not something you’d notice from a weekend break in the city, but if you spend enough time in Aberdeen you’ll witness the grim faces of the hoards upon hoards of faceless workers shuffling off to their nine-to-five jobs in the morning, and then home again early evening. Every day, like clockwork, the same old faces plodding along with their identical daily routines. Aberdeen was not necessarily a bad place to live; it just wasn’t a very exciting one. It would be quite easy for someone to settle down into an inconsequential job typing the same details into the same computer system day after day; if that sort of lifestyle appealed to you, and you’re quite happy to plod along in the rat race for the rest of your existence then the Silver City is as good a place to live as any. Andrew Hugh Murray wasn’t one of these people. Sure, he felt a certain sense of pride towards his birthplace, the city where he had spent the first seventeen years of his life, but unless you’ve got a real desire to work in the oil industry Aberdeen is no place for someone with the ambition of The Scottish King of Cool. He spent only a couple of weeks a year in the Silver City usually, but following his release from FUSE he had elected to come over for a bit longer than usual. He figured it’d be a good idea to have the necessary surgery in Aberdeen, around his closest family and his oldest friends, hence why he has spent the last two months away from his usual Tallahassee residence. Andy sat upright in his bed, a slightly out-of-date Dell laptop computer resting on his lap. He tapped away gently at the keys, pausing regularly to take a sip from the cup of tea sitting on his bedside table. His flat, situated in the one area of the Silver City that could be described as “picturesque,” the University region of Aberdeen known as “Old Aberdeen,” was quite a modest affair for a man of his relative wealth, but it was small, warm and homely. His bedroom was decorated mainly in a deep red colour making it seem more like a reception room than a bedroom, with the varnished, pine furniture a fine contrast to the maroon-coloured walls. Pausing for a few moments, Andy skims his eyes across the screen back and forth a couple of times, before beginning to type once again, dictating the words he types. “Dear Extreme Keith, if that is indeed your real name,” he began in his soft, North-Eastern Scottish accent, slowing the pace of his speech down so that it coincided with that of his typing, “while I am eternally grateful for your congratulations regarding my “promising tennis career,” it is with great regret that I inform you that I am not actually a tennis player, but in fact a professional wrestler. If you want to get in contact with that tennis prick, I suggest stalking, camping outside his house, whatever method suits you the most. If you see him, tell him I still think he is a little runt. Regards, Andy Murray? the wrestler, not the tennis one?” There were not many things that got the Scottish King of Cool riled, but being mistaken for a certain professional tennis player with whom he shared his name and nationality was one of them. Cases of mistaken identity were now almost a daily occurrence for Andy, but while it did get on his nerves it also amused him somewhat. “How could anyone be so stupid?” he muttered under his breath whilst shaking his head. He did have a point; The Scottish King of Cool was at least seven inches taller and nine years older than his namesake, and of course, faaaaaaar better-looking. Plus, who can forget the Scottish King of Cool’s overwhelming aura of infinite coolness? Cases of being mistaken for professional tennis players aside, this was all Andy had been physically able to do for the past six days. Since he had the fifth back operation of his career, Andy had been practically bed-ridden in an empty flat with only his computer for company. Of course, numerous well-wishers had popped by for brief spells, just to see how the big guy was doing after yet another gruelling operation, but other than that it was just him, alone. 2007, despite being only three months old, had already been a very busy month for Murrr. He had, of course, entered FUSE with his younger brother Cayle as his tag team partner, before again being forced out of the business after another injury flare-up. He wasn’t bitter that the promotion had released him from his contract so soon after offering him it in the first place; he knew that from a business standpoint it wouldn’t make any sense whatsoever to keep paying a competitor who’d signed a twelve month contract but would possibly be out for the rest of the year. No, he’d been there before, running his own promotion, he knew exactly where they were coming from. One thing he wasn’t too happy about was the company releasing his brother from his contract also; Cayle had been showing plenty of promise during his brief stint with the company, and he certainly had no injury problems of his own, so why not keep him on as a singles worker? Who knows, maybe management didn’t see enough star power in the young wrestler to keep him on his own; he was certainly a more versatile in-ring performer than his brother, but he lacked Andy’s charisma and had a reputation for having a poor attitude backstage. But that was all in the past, there was nothing he could do about it, and in Andy’s mind there was no point in worrying about it; it was done and dusted. ---- March 29th, 2007, Tallahassee, Florida. It was early afternoon on another glorious Floridian afternoon as Cayle Murray grabbed the telephone receiver from where it lay, on a worktop surface in the large, rectangular kitchen inside Andy Murray’s large American residence. Cayle had lived with Andy ever since he was released from jail after serving time for a DUI charge that he to this day claims his innocence for. The twenty-four year old lacked the maturity of Andy and their older brother Sidney, but was no longer the troubled tearaway of old. Spending time behind bars had taught him a lesson and turned him into a better man, although it still took a lot of convincing ? on Andy’s part - for the FUSE management to even consider giving him a contract. Cayle thumbed the number into the phone, before brushing strands of his shoulder-length dark brown hair behind his ear. As the phone began to ring he held it up to his ear; just a few moments later the ringing stopped. “Hey bro, what’s up?” Cayle chirped in a voice that wouldn’t sound out of place on Dawson’s Creek or Smallville, in spite of his Scottish roots. “Not much ratboy, hope you’re keeping the place in order over there,” the groggy voice of Andy Murray responded from the other side of the Atlantic. “You best not have let the notorious G-R-O-B out of his cage after midnight again,” he chuckled, referring to a member over the Murray household that we would soon surely come into contact with. “Nah, of course not,” sniggered the younger of the Murray siblings, “I wouldn’t make the mistake of letting that nasty-ass green motherfucker loose again, not after last time. How’s the back doing?” “Bloody awful, man!” Andy responded in a cheery tone of voice that was probably x juxtaposition to the pain he was experiencing. “You know me though, I’ll be as right as rain in no time” ain’t no tiny little injury going to derail the Scottish King of Cool! Anyway, what are you after man? You called me last night too?” Cayle brought the receiver in closer to his head as if to prevent anyone from listening in. “There’s something weird going on here Andy, it’s Jane?” he said at muted volume. Jane was Andy Murray’s wife; an English immigrant who he had met in Florida and married while he was still a teenager. She was a stunning attractive woman with deep hazel eyes, shoulder-length dark brown hair and the archetypical hourglass physique. Not only that but she was an incredibly smart young woman and had an air of sophistication about her that Andy found irresistible. Even Andy himself would admit that he was probably punching above his weight in the relationship. Shortly after the marriage, Jane gave birth to Andy’s son, Marvin, and by all accounts it looked like the three of them would go on to enjoy a long and happy life together. If only things were as simple as that. As the years passed the couple grew further and further apart, until they barely spoke to each other on the rare occasions that they did see each other. It was Jane’s prerogative that Andy’s budding career as a professional wrestler was putting far too much strain on the relationship, and by 2003 they were divorced in particularly messy circumstances, with Jane ending up with prime custody of Marvin and Andy’s access to his son restricted to weekends. For years the two seemed to loathe each other; and the occasions when they came into contact would often end in arguments, right in front of their young son. It was a miserable situation, and a major psychological weight on Andy’s mind. Not many men marry the same woman twice, but that’s exactly what Andy Murray did on the first of March, 2007. Throughout the year 2006 the couple had found ways to push aside their differences and peacefully coexist, and by the end of the year they had officially began seeing each other again. And despite the concerns of Andy’s friends and relatives, the rest, as they say, is history. “What about Jane?” Andy croaked down the phone line, the interest level in his voice increasing. “Well, there’s the dude in a suit here, I’m not sure what’s going on. They’re in the living room, the guys got a bunch of papers laid out on the coffee table, it’s strange?” “Eh, I’m sure it’s nothing,” said Andy, without even the slightest hint of suspicion creeping into his mind. Jane was in Scotland with Andy up until the night before the operation when she returned to Florida; she said she didn’t want to see her husband bed-ridden and in agony again. One would normally assume that at times like this a wife would want to help take care of her husband and help nurture him back to full fitness, but Andy thought nothing of it. “What do you think they’re doing?” Cayle enquired, seemingly more worried than his brother. “I don’t know buddy, but I’m sure it’s nothing to worry about. Seriously, don’t lose any sleep over it.” “I suppose you’re right,” responded the younger Murray brother, still somewhat distrustful. “Sorry for bothering you, bro?” “That’s alright man, nothing of it. I’ll be home in a couple of weeks, see you then ratboy, take care?” “Yeah, bye,” was all that Cayle could muster as he hung the phone up. What on Earth was Jane up to? Cayle was going to get to the bottom of it.
