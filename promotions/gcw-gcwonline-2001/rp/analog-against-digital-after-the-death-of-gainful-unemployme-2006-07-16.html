Analog against Digital, after the death of gainful unemployment.

16 July 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 16, 2006 Dawn’s breaking on the other side of a wall. I’m not too amped up about it, at least not enough to go hunt it down. All oranges and reds and purples smearing across the sky; nothing to get excited about. After a burnt night, I mean, there’s not much to get excited about. I can’t just sit around all day and celebrate failure anymore than I could raise a toast to ineptitude. So I’ll just sit around the house for a few hours and mope while I’m still the only mobile human There’s trees felled like dominos surrounding me, cracked or leaning or hanging or uprooted, disappearing behind a wall of broken branches and limbs. It’s, well, it’s not the prettiest sight even when glistening in the chill morning air. Which is just not as electric as it was yesterday. It’s steady, years away from the intense personal tumbling of a storm coming or going. Almost silent. The world has become a deadly still. The vast nothing that suggests only death as an option for change whispers in my ear: "you’re all just worm food anyways." No birds chirp to quiet the thought, no lightning cracks to rush it from my head. The world, I fear, has grown content with this peace. I need to wash it from me with tar and slime and depravity, I need to run until exhaustion, I need to press my back into the wall and humbly submit my resignation if I’m this goddamned needy all of a sudden. Ah, Jordan. Will you never learn? I’ve learned plenty: why the sky is blue, where they make balloons, why bad things happen to good people’and more. Just an unimportant more to go with the unimportants given, and we’re on to the first cigarette of the sunlight day. That makes it, uh, the thirty-somethingth of the day I tend to go with, where it starts when you wake up and ends when you finally sleep. Relativity. This thirty hours up, five down, ten up, fifteen down schedule has to be killing me. I feel more than oncoming cancer and kidney failure for once, I mean, and it just has to be something I’m doing to myself. At least it doesn’t feel genetic, and I can’t think of too many hereditary issues that go from plain discomfort to all-out malaise in the drop of an ash. So, poor sleep. I miss it so. Grievances made, I’ve got a day to go seize. Might hold it for ransom, not really sure what to do with it other than let it slip sand through spread fingers. Another thing a guy’s gotta figure out mid-coitus I guess. So, I’m just cheery enough to think about putting cigarettes out on furniture I paid for and I’m wondering which outlook I should go with of the two available to me: looking at life as things dying or as things dead. I feel digital so I go with dead and, well, we’re off. Not to Vansterdam. Once I hit B.C., I do not plan on coherency. I do not plan on validity. I plan on getting back home and finding out how I did in my fiftieth debut. I hear Vegas money’s printed with my face on it at this point. Okay. I expect. The people I knew that cared about that sort of shit are all swine, and I’ve found the smell of pork almost impossible to get out of good clothes. The smell is worse than the general odor of humanity by a power of twenty three, if we’re getting technical. To a gas station. Now that’s how to start a day, with a half hour of driving before you reach a point where you can start getting curious about where things should go. It feels white-bred, suburban, aimless, impotent. None of those sit well with me, so if I turn the music up loud enough I should be able to choke my questions out. Well, least I can’t hear myself anymore. I did lie about moping up the house to burn a few hours, I’ll admit. Now, on to the world. I never realize how quickly it can move past me until I really step on the gas. To be rooted, trees can maintain an immense speed under the right circumstances and are even prone to blur into nothing but an endless top-green bottom-brown rectangle if allowed. A funeral process, maybe; or just a century long march into infinity. How could anyone say we’re harming the environment? Every highway a verdant corridor, ever pitch and swerve gently tunneling into the very heart of nature. Why, those petunias in the dividers are certainly producing oxygen at a tremendous rate. What’s a rain forest? And the hell is ozone? Other than a gas. Less of a genuine concern than the dead light art; less of a problem than the lack of any real sense of society. My gut still longs for empty nights in low-income Oakland apartments, swallowed up in corduroy bean bags and feeding only nose and lungs. Like I’d be better off in a circle of good friends’ketamine, amphetamine, dopamine’and poets erupted from the chests of bankers, with some glassy-eyed trim of an indeterminate age collapsed on the couch as a subject of intense debate: "where then, exactly, would you have us draw the line in this matter?" "way beyond her if she’ll wake up." "there are men with truncheons because of lepers like you, Jordan." Brandon’s voice hangs in the Impala with me. It’s become harsh over the years, questioning why I’d ever lose my fucking mind to the point of letting myself into the world with these riot gear stormstroopers. All my fault, though. No reasons to bitch, regret, or entertain different ways things could’ve gone; I mean, who could complain about the high life? Every goddamned rock star in America. I mean, uh, greasehead with a band. Rock is dead. Viva rock. Sundown, ya better take care, if I find you been creeping “round my backstairs. Gordon, why ya gotta insight that pussy paranoia? It’s always chicks, I mean; a pretty one’ll tear your little head up with just sixty degrees of sway on her hips. Full displacement? She’ll break your heart, too. Not that I’ve got any excuse for thinking like a teenage player-in-progress when I’m nearly a decade out of that. Just, well, can’t pretend we get smarter as we get older, that we catch on more to universal truths with each generation. Just figure out the way we wanna see things, the way we want the world to work, and run with all that. It’s not maturing; it’s shifting what you’ve got wrong and what you’ve got right. It’s all rock and roll’s fault, these three or four generations that never expected to make it to thirty. We all went scared. We all fell prone to the floor, desperately clawed our way under desks or curled up turtle with our asses to the hall and the crowns of our heads against blue cinderblock walls. How many millions that thought they’d be superstars ended up in tight suits with their undershirts buttoned all the way to a fully shaved neck? We knew how to rock, we knew how to drink, snort, smoke, shoot, and fuck. How many anthemic sprees are dedicated to driving a Passat to work? Less than you’d expect. So we played it analog, we played it gray, we made it all work and now’like those couple modern generations before us’we’re trying to keep from going stiff. Most of us’ll be clean of everything but alcohol, maybe coke, by thirty. You can only be around so much starch before the clothes really do start to make you who you are. Overnight, it turned out we’d spent an entire life as bad people and only noticed it when we went straight. And everyone that’s a teenager now? Fuck, sorry for those kids. I mean, yeah, I was kind of one of those little loaded pierced-up dyed-long-hair fuckheads when I was growing up, but at least the music was something. The stupids don’t even get that benefit anymore. Think suicide rates would go up if suddenly everything artistic was at least well executed? I don’t see it. World’s ugly. Not just in comparison; it shifted subtle from blue to gray, so well hidden that no one was able to back out until it was too late. Like there was no in-between. Like it was an on/off switch flipped while the whole world blinked. "Had to tell her pops, yo, stop cockblockin? B." My phone, oh. Lowly people. Normal people. Average people. Not a drug dealer, a real friend, or a lover. Won’t be answering this one, fuck. I’m adrift. Mi cabeza no estoy ocupado. Ahora. But my face, fuck me. Mirrors like sneaking up on me, pointing out a red splotch on either cheek like a fist fight with the poison ivy monster that lives in the woods. Or at least that’s what I’m telling people. I don’t get it, personally; two open wounds that want to be scabs just don’t seem that normal of a problem to have. From acne to ebola, apparently. Maybe I’ll go to sleep, not wake up until they slap together some cure. And the common cold. That’d be nice too. And down the road we go. So, there’s this broken down car. It’s, I don’t know, from one of the only decades in recent memory about as bad as this one, and worse on aesthetics. A clunky red box’breathing a plume of thick white smoke that towers barely high enough to dissipate entirely’sitting on the edge about a quarter mile away suddenly seems like a good diversion from the current schedule of looking like there’s something to do. I can’t count people from here, just point out indefinite figures that appear to be moving towards the road. Waving, maybe, something. Surrender. I’ll lay waste to the males of the tribe and claim their women as just plunder. They should not be here’maybe in some parallel dimension wandered far from this one, where I don’t rise every morning to mark my territory for a hundred miles in every direction. But no, they do not belong here. "Hey!" Shit. Stop, reverse, stop again. It’s a college age’maybe’guy, short and doughy and it’s a small hatchback with a stern waif of a girl in the passenger’s seat. I’ll have time to make her out later. Now, there’s him mainly in the way and definitely sucking the attention up. Sweat slips down from curly black hair that twitches and frizzes erratic in the summer air; it collects in pools that stain a gray shirt in a wide circle around the neck. Leaning into the window, he rests his arms on the green shirt of surrender. "Hey, man. Could you help us out?" "Do I look like a fuckin? dealer?" "Uh." "I mean, what’s wrong. That." Rivulets run down his cheeks, almost jowls, to collect in a heavy drop that looms at the center of a rounded chin. God, passenger’s seat. How I will pity you. "I think my water pump’s busted. I just need a lift into town." There is no window with him there. "Ask something reasonable. Like, to use a phone. Damn." He looks down, notices some scab or mosquito bite on his arm and lets it distract him. Heavy fingers pull, pick, stretch, squeeze. "Shit, that’d work too. I guess." I dig in my pocket for a pack of cigarettes to keep up the rhythm we’ve established so far. It gets lit, yeah. I contemplate it. "Yeah, lemme find it." Another intermission. Pockets generally are too tight. It evades until it’s tired, then gives in without anymore of a fight. I hand it to him and he steps out of the way. The girl, finally, is sallow and damp. Her hair, shitty and broken, hangs in a black bob. She glances over with a face of shadow and slit eyes that catch mine just briefly before they break to go back to the ever interesting road. She droops like wax. Gone, again. "Thanks, brother. I owe you one." Watch the cigarette burn. "One what?" He turns around, uncomfortable. "Just a saying, that’s all." "Well, damn." Hear it sizzle. "Fuckin? kids these days, can’t say what they mean. Or mean what they, uh, say." He laughs, as big of a soft laugh as word usage will allow. "Sorry. Well, God bless you, man." Stare directly into the cigarette, like some distant flickering star. "Yeah." Now, Jordan, now, look wistful. Think of something deep and meaningful, or at least get that knowing glimmer in your damned eye. Establish your status as either sage or psychotic. Now, turn back to the kid. "You even know what beauty was?" "Huh?" I am not an artist. All I can draw are blank stares. "Eh, shit. Never should’ve thought you’d get it. Hah. Well, fuck. Take it easy." "Yeah. You too." He hands me my phone, slick with people grease. I wipe across my sleeve, hoping for the driest section. He still lingers. "What?" "I’ll, uh, be back in a second." He moves again, slow and dedicated strides to the driver’s window of his car. I can’t make out words. I don’t really care about them. I flip through the radio, luck on a station playing out Biggie and turn it up. There’s a certain charm in the censored in "heys" or scratches every line; a certain dumb faith in editing out "HIV." We all know there’s no middle ground between the clean and the dirty, and that if we try our hardest to pretend to only see the clean then all the dirt will just disappear. Tapping at the window. I look up to the hollow eyes of the girl as she slowly drips into nothingness. I roll the window down. Her shirt, a sleeveless light blue cotton thing, hangs loose and empty. It also doesn’t just leave empty as an assumption. Inside my head, I’m smacking my forehead for fear that the awkward’s about to turn around on me. She arches her back to guarantee it before she opens her chapped lips. "Hey. My brother and I needed a little money and." Her wilting voice dies out entirely, eyes slipping down my chest and stopping at my belt buckle. Maybe even the zipper. She’s almost cute, maybe twenty pounds away from me considering it. Men with truncheons. A very bad, very dirty world. "You think you could help us out?" "I look rich?" "Just a few bucks, baby." And I’m wondering if my life is just some misogynistic tale made to discredit all those pesky women, or if maybe I’m right in this whole dead world concept. "I really need it." She rolls the "really," lets it spiral off her tongue like some overripe cherry. "I’m sure you could think of something." "Fucking. How old are you?" "Old enough for that." I hide a laugh. Maybe it’s her fault she’s stupid. Maybe it’s my fault for saying "fucking" like a transitive verb turned noun. The guy, maybe even her brother, leans against his car, staring at us like an unfinished statue. I roll up the window between me and him, look back up to his yellow sister of hard angles. Bent over to ninety degrees, she sways left to right in the breeze with extended hips. I’m thinking back to when I was her age, definitely under eighteen, and all the girls like her in one way or another. And of debasing them. They were whores to me. In my maturity, my quaint understanding of reality, I’ve come to understand them as my lot, as skanks and junkies and the rest of society’s hedonistic underpinnings. Us, them. She runs her tongue over her lips; it’s disproportionately fat, writhing like a slug. Guy walks over. Maybe curious. Maybe violent. "You know, a day like this, I mean, I didn’t have much planned." I let each word rest, slowly barge out of my mouth. "Yeah?" "And any diversion’s a diversion, y’know? It’s just, well." I sigh. "There’s definitely better out there." She backs away, not even insulted, and silently nods. Her eyes go back to shadow. Definitely a robot. I crank, accelerate. He stands in the center of the lane as long as he exists in my rearview’gyrates his arms wildly to the fairy as she passes him to the car’then turns to stare at me with his once waving shirt hanging low to the ground. It can’t be the fame. It can’t be wanting a clean world. It’s sure not this maturity thing. Right? Whatever. I’ve got a job, a modest income, and a big and tall bank account. A home, a few pets, my own shower. That’s maturity enough. Maybe I just don’t feel like being around my people anymore than I have to. Maybe I get covered with enough grime rolling around with pain pill addicts, adrenaline junkies, attention whores, and drama bombs. Or, well, got and will get. Can’t call myself a wrestler “til I’m actually doing it. Probably shouldn’t think about it “til then either. Might jinx myself. Bet that bitch knew how to lick a popsicle, though. What noble sacrifices I make in the great name of maintaining the integrity of my chosen profession. Why’s I gots to make so many excuses? Now, about that gasoline.
