An Experiment, Part I

20 November 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 20, 2008 An Experiment Part I: Curiosity, Not Good for Rats *November 20, 2008* Last week’s incident has brought a very deep curiosity upon me. I have always known that I possess an ability to go above and beyond the moral codes most people find themselves compelled to follow, but I had never seen it come so clearly into fruition as I did at that little convenience store... The black ink dries almost instantly with all the extra heating inside the coffee shop. Images of last week flood the already convoluted mind of Face: The clerk, wary of Face as soon as he walked in the door; The robber, leaving the clerk speechless; The black pistol, pointed in his face; The corpse, burning to a crisp at his feet... The images bring an unseen grin to his face as he sits at his table, isolated in the corner of the shop. Business is all but dead; no customers to be seen, letting easy rock fill the air. The grin of Face fades away. He hates easy rock ? its soft sound and comforting lyrics breed unnecessary hope. “Then again, the more hope they have, the more I can rip from their hearts...” Face mutters to himself, allowing his unseen grin to re-emerge. Face looks up from the book he was writing in to find the girl at the cashier peering towards him uneasily. She’s young ? no more than 15. She quickly looks in the opposite direction as soon as the two black crosses that constitute Face’s “eyes” glance back. A chuckle escapes from under Face’s mask; he’s amused with the fear he so purposely brings forth. After a few more moments, he directs his attention back to his writing. Fear...It is always interesting to see the affects of it...especially on someone trying to utilize it themselves. The man thought his gun and his empty threats were enough to pull the wool over my eyes...and it would certainly have worked on most people, but that’s the nature of the beast. If you can’t back up fear with substance, it is empty and useless in the event someone just as versed in the art comes along. Survival of the fittest applies, as always. Of course, that brings the question... “Excuse me.” The pen stops abruptly with the interruption. Face looks up to find the manager of the coffee shop staring down at him with deep cerulean eyes. The manager is thick and worn; the years have sculpted him into a granite wall ? firm and unafraid of anything thrown against him. He has a stern aura about him, but an inherent kindness holds its hand. Face is delighted at this discovery. The manager is clearly not sharing that same emotion. “I’m going to have to ask you to leave, sir.” Face tilts his head, studying his opponent. His stance is as firm as his words. Those deep blue eyes show no give. An evil grin curls up beneath the mask of Face; this manager is exactly the kind of person he was looking for. Face decides to play the innocent card, his normally harsh voice smoothening to a tolerable level. “What have I done to deserve such a thing” All I’ve done since I’ve gotten here is sit and write, minding my own business.” The manager doesn’t buy it. “You’re scaring my employees with that outfit of yours,” the manager says with a firm, but controlled tone, folding his arms over his chest. “I’m doing you a favour by not calling the police. Don’t expect that favour to last much longer if you don’t leave my shop.” Face drops the innocent act like a bad habit and lets his true voice ring ? or, rather, growl. “Your employees, you say” Hmmm?” inquires Face knowingly. He looks around the shop, finding the manager, the girl at the cashier and himself to be the only people there. “You mean your...lovely daughter over there?” The manager remains composed as Face continues to dig his mental claws into the manager’s mind. “Please leave sir, or I will call the cops and have you arrested.” Face completely ignores what the manager just said as he continues to question him. “You must be a family man” Hmmm? Hiring your daughter to work in your little coffee shop here...Isn’t that nice. Tell me, Mr...”” The frustration continues to build within the manager, but he doesn’t let it show. He won’t let this freak in front of him get the best of him. “My name is not important.” “No, of course not...you’re a man who puts others before himself.” Face pockets his pen and shuts his book, getting up from his seat and standing directly in front of the manager. The manager’s daughter looks over at the confrontation from behind the counter. “It’s what everyone should do,” replies the manager, in an attempt to impose values and morals on a man with none whatsoever. That works about as well as a cell phone in a subway...when the cell phone has no actual power source. Despite this, Face actually shows some manners, albeit manners doused with sarcasm and set aflame with insincerity. “Thank you for the favour...I’ll be seeing you again.” “No.” The manager doesn’t flinch, even with Face being at such a close proximity. “You won’t be.” With that, he unfolds his arms and points to the exit. Face continues to stare into the manager’s eyes for a few moments, glancing at the manager’s left pant pocket, where some keys are dangling. He then turns and brushes past him. He opens the shop’s door, but turns back before he exits, looking back to the manager, who hasn’t taken his eyes off him just yet. “You know, you should watch your daughter’s eyes...she’s quite the looker.” A hoarse cackle follows and Face leaves the shop. The manager’s face glows a slight red as he looks over to his daughter at the cash register, his fatherly love for her clear as day. She’s visibly creeped out, but also glad the masked man is gone. Her father shares the same emotions, although he wouldn’t allow himself to show fear with such ease... *Later that night...* The lock clicks shut and the manager removes the key from its home. He stows the key away in his coat before walking away from his coffee shop, his daughter waiting patiently for him against the wall to his left. There’s a chill in the air, causing her to shiver slightly. She links onto her father’s arm for some extra warmth, reflecting on the unique day at the shop. She can’t help but shake the incident that happened earlier from her mind. “Do you think that creep will be back again?” The daughter looks up to her dad with her big blue eyes for an answer as the two cross the street. “No. I won’t let him back in and if he comes back while I’m not there, and he won’t leave, call the cops.” His tone is dead serious; he doesn’t want Face around his shop or his daughter ever again. The two reach their car and the manager goes around to the driver’s door, unlocking the car’s doors. He enters into the driver’s seat and his daughter sits with him in the front, looking forward to getting home after the eventful night. The key is placed in the ignition and the manager turns it. The car sputters and sputters, but doesn’t start. A second try receives the same result. He sighs, his daughter crossing her arms impatiently. “Wait in the car,” says the manager as he exits the car and shuts his door. His daughter isn’t exactly going to go anywhere, but he says it anyway. He opens the back door and reaches to the floor of the car, picking up a flashlight. He then heads around to the hood, closing the back door behind him. His daughter watches on as he opens the hood, flicking on his flashlight. He leans in and looks around, trying to figure out why his car has suddenly decided to stop working. The answer becomes obvious: the engine’s starter is no longer there. Confused, the manager scratches his head, wondering how it could have possibly disappeared. That answer becomes obvious as well: the hood crashing down on his head with an applied force. His daughter screams in terror as she sees the man responsible: Face. With her father stunned and stumbling backwards, Face takes a few rapid steps and leaps into the air, driving his knee straight into the manager’s jaw. The manager crumples instantly, the lens of the flashlight he was holding cracking as it falls to the hard pavement below. The girl screams uncontrollably and tears begin to stream down her cheeks as Face looks down on his now unconscious opponent. Satisfied, he turns his attention to the man’s daughter, an evil chuckle escaping the confines of his terrifying mask. The girl quickly lunges at the driver-side door and presses a button on it, all of the car doors locking instantly afterwards. Unfortunately, this only serves to amuse Face for a moment before he walks up to the girl’s door. She shakes her head, pleading for Face not to do anything. It doesn’t work. She turns away and hides her face in her hands as one of Face’s shoes is projected through her window, spraying glass into the car...
