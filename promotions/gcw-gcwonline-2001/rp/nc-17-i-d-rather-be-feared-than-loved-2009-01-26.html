NC-17: I'd Rather Be Feared than Loved

26 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: January 26, 2009 Four Months Ago... Hours have passed since we first got on the road. I feel like my mind is in a million different places -- but what's really new there. Last night was the best night of my life. Ironically, it was also one of the worst nights of my life. I've finally been reunited with the one true love of my life and at the same time, I lost everyone else that was near and dear to my heart. Since we began our car ride, Rebecca has filled me in on all the events over the last several years. She's told me how Abigail was the mastermind behind the escape but couldn't do anything without the financial backing of Timmy. She told me how they approached Shane after he hired me in Ultimate Championship Wrestling and wished to keep him in the loop until it was 'time' for them to let me know Rebecca was alive. She told me how Shane actually had written into my contract an additional salary bonus to be sent to them in order to help them get by. Part of me feels bad for walking away from the people who have kept Rebecca alive for all these years, but at the same time they've killed me. They've watched me fall apart day in and day out. My physical condition is diminishing. I made Rebecca a promise back at her ranch in Odessa. I told her that I'd leave the wrestling business for good and we could run away together. What I didn't tell her was that I also invested a great deal of money into that stock option her father had died waiting for. I had faith in him. And he was right. The company name was Yahoo and the company exploded from 1998 to 2004. My earnings were beyond my wildest dreams and were the only reason I was able to survive for so many years with my drinking habits without having to worry about a job. I was going to finish what her father couldn't. I was going to pay off the final debts her family owed to whatever family it may have been. I'm done with the drugs. Done with the alcohol. Done with the wrestling. Rebecca and I have been through so much. Despite the fact that we may not have been together for a long time, we still grew up together. We grew up in each other's thoughts. Growing up is never easy. You hold on to things that were. You wonder what's to come. But last night, I think we knew it was time to let go of what had been, and look ahead to what would be. Other days. New days. Days to come. And my new day begins in just a few more hours. I'm going to make the call that will change my destiny for once and for all. In just a few more hours, once the offices open... I'll be putting in my letter of resignation and after Dangerous Games, Chris Bagwell will be no more in the wrestling industry. So let it be known... ..and let it be sworn. ____________________________________________ "The more sand has escaped from the hourglass of our life, the clearer we should see through it." ____________________________________________ Chapter I : Right Now "It's been so long," she said in a soft whisper as she brushed her hand down along the ridge of my face. "Since I've seen your face." Her fingertips reached my chin and trickled down along my throat to my chest. She proceeded to press her head against my pectoral and listened to the soft, steady beat of my heart. It'd been years since Rebecca and I were able to hold one another like that. I wrapped my arms around her body and pulled her tightly. I hadn't the slightest intension to ever let her go. Deep down, I did have a heart; and it hungered for her. "Chris," she said tilting her head up. "Do you really think you're going to leave?" It was a question she'd surely pondered for the last four months. I'd said that I'd walk out of the wrestling business and the two of us would run away together, disappearing into the rest of civilization. I even went so far as to pick up the phone and start dialing the phone number to the Ohio headquarters. But I hung it up. I couldn't bring myself to finish dialing Steven's number and I just couldn't figure out what it was. Rebecca didn't mind, though. She knew that it was a stupid idea to just up-and-walk-out so suddenly. One thing is for sure -- we'd certainly be hurting financially. I blew way too much of my money on material items with limited life spans. Apparently, three hundred and twenty dollars in a strip club only gets you thirty minutes and heavy petting. Coincidentally, shaving ten of those minutes off also saves you three hundred dollars. Who would've thought? But I could never tell her that. I just blamed my lack of money on alcohol. I had less shame telling her I was an alcoholic than telling her I overpayed at a strip club one too many times. I suppose that defines my priorities in life somewhat clearly. "I made you a promise," I reassured her. "And I intend on keeping it." I wanted her to be at peace with herself. I wanted her to know that she really did mean everything to me and I was absolutely prepared to give up the rest of my life in order to satisfy hers. But she wasn't as greedy as I. "You know, you really don't have to," she told me. I wasn't entirely sure how I should take it. Women were always playing mind games. Was she really legit in saying that she wanted me to stay in the business? Or was she playing that game when a girl says something and means entirely something else? It reminded me exactly why I hated women. But I did love her. As much as I hated that word; as much as I thought it was such a pussy word -- I used it. It's just one of those moments, as a guy, where you have to put your pride aside. And that was one thing I never so much as thought about doing. EVER. "I want to give you a great life, Rebecca." I tried to appeal to her a little bit more and, maybe, try to convince myself that it was what I really wanted. "I don't want you to have to travel all over the place all the time and get bothered by all the media." "Yeah, that all sounds great.." she said with a hint of hesitation. "..for the future." And that was when Rebecca completely and entirely sabotaged every image I had of what she wished for. I couldn't help but look at her cluelessly. Without even seeing the expression on my face, she knew I was lost. She lifted her head from my chest and looked me in the eye. She had a smirk dancing across her face. "I've spent the last six years sheltered and hidden from the world," she said with disdain. "The last thing I want to do is spend the next six doing the same thing." "So," I mumbled, "what exactly are you suggesting?" "I don't want you leaving just yet. I'm not worried about the future; I don't want to even begin to think about that. That's how we always thought and look what happened. We sat there and watched life slip right through our figners. Now that part of our life is gone -- I can see the rest of it a little more clearly. So lets stop thinking about later and lets think about now," she shouted. "Right now!" ____________________________________________ "Since it is difficult to join them together, it is safer to be feared than to be loved when one of the two must be lacking." ____________________________________________ Chapter II : Just Dance She made her ambitions very clear. To hell with the sitting around like a pawn waiting for life to come and roll you right over. We were about to do something. I never saw Rebecca wanting to get involved with any of this stuff. It blew my mind. But I wasn't about to complain. We finally left the hotel we'd been staying in for some time and hit the open road. It was midday by this point so my stomach started screaming. We swung into a convenience store in downtown Phoenix and went looking for a healthy snack. I sat in the car for a moment when we pulled up and just watched Rebecca strut into the store's front door. Her body was wrapped in a form-fitting dress that touched down two inches past her knees. She was raised a perfect Christian girl, so her new change of heart about the business really came as a shock to me. I know what they say about those Catholic girls, but unfortunately Rebecca didn't fit into any of those stereotypes. But holy shit if she did. I'd be one lucky guy. I stepped through the front door into the store to hear Rebecca ordering from the deli counter, "Yeah, give me a large cheesesteak with American cheese, onions, ketchup, oregano... I don't know! Give me the works!" So much for healthy? I crept up behind her and slipped my arm around her waist. She clasped my hand with hers, cocked her head to the side, and laid her lips against mine. "Whoa," I said. "Where'd that come from?!" She responded only with a smile and a glimmer in her eye. The worker behind the counter slid her sandwich over to her and looked my way, "Anything for you, sir?" I shrugged and asked him for a simple Italian styled submarine sandwich. After looking at me like I had four eyes, I think he figured out what I was implying. Okay, so Rebecca helped that along a little, "Salami, Ham, and Provolone on a long roll, slick! Come on, we don't have all day!" I was in awe as to what suddenly came over her. She was like a new girl -- entirely new. But I was digging it. Especially when she grabbed a twenty-four ounce beer from the beverage cooler. I can't remember the last time I saw the girl drink a sip of alcohol, let alone a beer. And she must not've cared about my alleged "drinking problem" I lied to her about because she promptly pulled one out for me, too. "Beer?" I asked her. "You? And beer?" All she had to do was wink and she walked right on over to the checkout counter. "You're all set," the deli guy shouted to me, interrupting my concentration on Rebecca's firm little ass. As I swiped my sandwich from his station with my eyes still locked on Rebecca, I couldn't help but get a very familiar, particular feeling swarming through my body. No, not that. I felt that urge to get back inside the ring and really do some damage. I felt like I wanted the spotlight again. It couldn't have come at a more inopportune time. I had just talked myself out of wanting to stay in the business. But it was something about the way Rebecca was acting. She waved me over to the counter to put my stuff in a bag and finish paying the clerk. "Do you have a scissor or a boxcutter or something back there that I can borrow?" she asked the register clerk. He nodded and shuffled through a drawer for a scissor. And then she did what I definitely wasn't expecting to see from her. She started cutting up the bottom of her dress so instead of it slipping past her knee, it barely passed her ass. "Can you get the back for me, babe?" She asked me with intimate intent. And, naturally, I complied. "What's making you do all this," I had to inquire. "You know when you're out at a club and you want to have a good time. So with the music pumping and everyone around you moving to the beat, you're faced with a decision. You worry that if you're too wild, people are going to look at you weird. But on the other hand, you're worried that if you're not wild enough on the floor, people are going to knock you out of the way." "You're starting to lose me, Rebecca." "Dancing. When you're dancing. If you think about what you're doing, you're going to talk yourself out of it. You're going to think you look stupid. That's how I feel about life right now. I always talk myself out of everything and I tend to regret it. I don't want to live like that. The only way you'll actually enjoy yourself is to just go out there and do it. Don't think about it..." And so she said with such sincerity... "...just dance." ____________________________________________ "If an injury has to be done to a man, it should be so severe that his vengeance need not be feared." ____________________________________________ Chapter III : Live Your Life We drove for a few more hours before finally reaching our destination. As fate would have it, we pulled into yet another hotel parking lot just a few minutes from the University of Phoenix Stadium in Phoenix, Arizona. In a matter of a day, Rebecca and I had went from agreeing to wanting to leave the business to agreeing that this business is the backbone of our lives together. At first, I really wanted out. But I think I wanted out because I thought it would make my relationship stronger; and that was something I'd craved for so long. I tried to hide my emotions for six years by sexually harassing other women and ruining the lives of other couples. Take Andy Murray, for example. Despite the fact that he and Jane were already divorced, I had no business dipping my nose in their affairs. But I did -- and did so because I didn't want anyone else to be happy if I wasn't happy myself. I don't really know what I'd have done if the roles were reversed. Probably would have snapped. Although...it never really did take much to set me off. But today - today was a new day. After today, I don't think I could ever think of having sex with a co-worker's ex-wife and boast about it on national television. Wow, it sounds a lot worse when I say it like that. "So what's the story with this Vivica," she asked with a hint of concern. During our drive, I had tried to catch Rebecca up on what exactly was going on within the company. I wanted her to be perfectly aware of who I was and what I had gotten myself involved with over the last year. I wanted no surprises to jump out at her. But Vivica J. Valentine was a completely different story. My history with her was unlike any of the rest. The history between Vivica and I dates back to my first night in the company, where I pretty much verbally assaulted the fact that she was a woman...and successful. "Ahhh, Vivica J. Valentine," I spoke in soft words. "Vivica is the poster girl of the company, if you will. She's stolen the hearts of all the boys and obtained the envy of all the girls. She's the company's shining star." "And she's your next obsticle to the top?" It was as if my face was a book and she'd just been reading along with the words I spoke. She knew what I was going to say before I'd even get the chance to say it. "I wouldn't call her so much an obsticle as I would a bump in the road," I reassured. Rebecca smiled and rolled her eyes at me. "There's that cocky, confident attitude you seemed to lose when we were reunited. I fell in love with that, you know?" Cocky? She loved cocky? And all along, I thought people considered it to be obnoxious. But shit, if she liked it...that was something I could really get used to. 'Cocky' was the name of my Siamese twin. "Since when do you like it when I'm cocky? I thought you hated that," I checked with her. I figured it was possible she was playing another one of those girl games on me when she says something and means the opposite. "You're cute when you're cocky. And you've got a lot of drive -- you seem to do amazing things. This soft, mushy Chris isn't the Chris I fell in love with so it certainly isn't the Chris I want to see hanging around all the time." She winked at me and I couldn't help but get that feeling again; that hunger for the spotlight. "You know, I find it kind of ironic that you're praising my tendancy to be disrespectful and, quite frankly, encouraging it more so with your own sudden change in attitude." I didn't want to sound too brash when speaking to her, but I felt compelled to say something. "Lets just say," she started, "I finally want to be on the opposite side of the unjust and anarchy that tends to terrorize this world. I'm tired of always being the victim." Her words were so sincere. "Besides," she went on. "I think it would be fun to be by your side as you stand at the top." She had given me her final blessing. The last thing I really needed to hear to know the my decision was made. For the last twelve months, I've manipulated my way to the top of company but never capitalized. I've been given opportunities but let them slip by. I started a movement...and let it come to a screeching hault. "Then let's do it," I told her. "Its time you really got a chance to live your life." And so it was said and then it was sworn... NC-17. Valentine. TORN.
