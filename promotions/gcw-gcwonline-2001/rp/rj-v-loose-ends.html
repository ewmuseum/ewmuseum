Date: Last week .:: ::: ::. CANTO XIII One ear o' th' harvest thresh'd, And its grain safely stor'd, sweet charity Invites me with the other to like toil. Undisclosed Location Lia Ambrosi pulled the hood of her jacket tighter around her face. The snow was swirling in an unforgiving whirlwind around her, and though it wasn't anything she wasn't used to, having grown up in and around Prague, it was still annoying’and a far cry from her current home in the Las Vegas dessert. This was where she'd tracked ZC to, after calling in a favor... 'Hello?' 'Ian...' There was dead silence on the other end of the phone. 'Hello?' 'I'm still here, Mrs. Ambrosi. I'm waiting for you to state your business.' Lia's eyes squeezed together in some semblance of a wince at the tone. It stabbed through the earpiece. 'Ian, I need something from you... something small, I know better than to ask for any favors right now.' Silence again. Ian cleared his throat. 'You know, I'm sure, that I can't provide assistance on any sort of work you're doing. You're still suspended as an officer pending a time that the Council can address your case.' The official line. Should have figured she'd get it at some point. 'I'm aware of that. I'm not asking for state secrets here, I'm asking for a very small piece of information that will guide me where I need to go. It's not even related to anything you guys are interested in, just something you have on file from when you were poking around Zenith's interests.' There was a long, gusty sigh from the other end of the phone. 'You're aware what sort of trouble I could get in if anyone finds out that I've told you something from our secure files when you were suspended?' The guilt trip. His voice was rough, but wearing. He didn't want to have an argument over it. 'You don't have to tell me anything, Ian, I just thought I'd ask.' 'And then I'd have the guilt of knowing that you needed something, and I turned you away.' Guilt wasn't normally something that seemed to bother Ian Childress, but his loyalty to his operatives, suspended or not, was anything but fickle. 'Tell me what it is.' He had finally relented. Trying to sound tough, but he'd probably tell. 'I just need to know the location you had for that facility of his...' 'Nova Scotia. Halifax.' He said the words like he just knew them off the top of his head. Considering what a receptacle he was for loose information, she wouldn't have found it surprising if he did. The building was white. It didn't stand out against the snow, nor was the metal silver ZC logo obvious against its side unless you looked pretty hard. It was New Year's Day, which meant that the facility was scarcely staffed. Ian had succumbed to even more guilt lately and sent her a floorplan, as best as they had been able to make it out. She didn't know how the Order of the Hammer had gotten it, but she didn't ask questions. She just hoped it was right’the mission was going to be hard enough as it was, and all she was really having to do was confirm that the cells were, in fact, where she thought they were. That was why she was about to climb a tree at the moment, something which she did not normally make the habit of doing, since she wasn't ten years old anymore. She gripped the lowest branch with her right hand, the strong hand, and pulled hard. But her fingers were too damn cold right now to grip well, and she found herself knees-first in the snow. “Dammit...” It slipped out before she could prevent herself, and she clapped a hand over her mouth, remembering Ian's one brief note that there were listening and surveillance devices all over the compound. Second try. She grabbed the bottom branch and, just as she was about to slip off again, swung both legs upward into the lowest branches. They grew into one another, forming a sturdy, though not comfortable, seat. Her eyes scanned the next branches up to see if there was another limb that went farther out than the one she'd selected. No such luck. Moments passed, tense moments in which Lia Ambrosi displayed her total lack of coordination in the outdoor sport of tree-climbing. Lucky thing she'd never wanted to be a lumberjack. Inch by strenuous inch, she propelled herself, finally to the farthest point that would support her weight. Now, it was time to have a look around. From her coat pocket, she produced a small electronic scope, leftover gear from when she had joined the Order. They were finally useful now. Nothing at first... just a facility, and some grey windows she couldn't really see through. She adjusted the knob, twirling it to the right for precision and finally, got something at least somewhat remarkable. Grey windows, hatched across with black metal. She could make out shapes, most of the dim unless they were closer to the window. A long row was visible, a hallway, probably. She shifted to the side slightly, trying to get a better look. She could see maybe one cell, or two. They looked like cells from this distance. She squinted, as though that would help. One figure came into the viewfinder. A guard, more than likely, no more and no less, and really, it was a significant amount less, since seeing guards didn't do anything other than tell her that Christian Zenith had suckered some poor saps into working on New Year's Day. Shifted. Saw one prisoner, or someone who at least looked thin and gaunt enough to be a prisoner. But he couldn't have been Markus’not unless Zenith's 'scientists,' such as they were, were keeping nutrients from him. She wouldn't have put it past them. She tried to hone in a little closer, but the scope seemed to be pushed to its limits even now. There was no point in trying to figure this out, but she had to-- Aha. The prisoner raised his head from where he sat, with black hair shrouding his face, and she saw it. The thin line of Markus' gaunt nose, and the hollows of his eyes, right before he dropped his head again. Bingo. She grabbed onto the edge of the branch as tightly as she could and flipped herself backwards out of the tree, into the snow. It was unfortunate that she'd just left a Lia-sized indentation in the thick, powdery pileup, but it would just have to do. None of these bumpkins was smart enough to think it had been anything but a bear, or a large bobcat, if they even noticed at all. Cell phone reception was shit out here, so there was no point in trying to call Vivica now, even to let her know that there was a confirmed sighting, that things could move ahead now. Some time, Vivica would have to learn patience, though Lia had to admit, she'd pretty much been a saint throughout this entire ordeal. She couldn't have guaranteed the same for her own behavior had Virgil (or Dawn for that matter) gone missing. Time to get the hell out of the snow. Lia couldn't say she was sad to leave it behind. As soon as she was out of range of Zenith's radio towers, she picked up the small CB that was strapped to her hip. “Virgil... ready to get out of here?” ... “It's fucking cold, of course I am.” If nothing, he was a creature of habit. It made Lia smile. “Give me fifteen minutes, I'll be back on the path. Find me?” “Si.” The radio clicked dead in her hand and she slid it into its holster once again. Things might not have been right just yet, but the plan was coming together. There was at least something to be said for not sitting around and expecting the rest of the world to do your work for you. .:: ::: ::. CANTO XIV Whoso laments, that we must doff this garb Of frail mortality, thenceforth to live Immortally above, he hath not seen The sweet refreshing, of that heav'nly shower. The jolts of the jeep as it headed down the rutted, muddy road weren't enough to keep Lia Ambrosi awake. Virgil was the better driver of the two, and so he'd taken the role of keeping the truck not stolen while she'd gone to the ZC facility. Lia had been up for almost two days straight now, trying to get things straight in her head, memorizing layouts and going over details in her head. Virgil felt bad for her in a way, but there was no point in pitying a creature like her, because she only would have scorned the emotion, and perhaps rightfully so. He was trying to take it easy, but the next jolt came with a force that made Lia open her eyes, and Virgil curse under his breath. “S'alright,” Lia said softly, yawning and forgetting to cover her mouth. “The roads up here suck...” It was perhaps the biggest understatement she had ever made. It was also the last statement she'd make before, even still fighting it, her eyes closed, and she drifted slowly off into sleep. It was deeper than the road warranted, but her body simply refused to take anymore. It was unfortunate for her body that her mind didn't agree. There was Phoenix again, hovering patiently as he waited for her to resume her trip. She'd all but forgotten about her trip in recent weeks. She wished it had stayed that way. But there he was, above what looked like thick cloud cover. She looked down to discover that she was walking on it. Her immediate instinct was to panic, but there was no point’Phoenix's soft chuckle came in her mind. “Don't fear simply because you don't understand... trust.” It had always been a dodgy word with Lia, if only because hers had been tossed underneath the proverbial bus wheels so many times. She took a step forward, and Phoenix gestured with the tips of his wings, urging her. She was beginning to feel like a wayward, stubborn sheep, far from the pen. Suddenly, in front of her, there was no longer a pleasantly gold-shimmering blankness, but a figure. Its features remained slightly hazy, being that it was obscured by a silver miasma that swam and pulsated, mingling with the already gold light. The figure was dressed in a dark cowl, some of its edges raggedy and torn. Its face was not obscured, but the details indistinct, as though viewed through a translucent but cloudy veil of linen. “I am Magica.” Proclamations of this sort were seeming to become a regularity. Lia wondered if she'd ever be able to introduce herself to anyone again without making one. But she only nodded, seeming to proffer the respect that was asked. “You have had much contact with me throughout your years,” the figure went on, “As I have succored you in your failings, as I have helped you implement some of your greatest triumphs. But I am no saint, not as some of these creatures you have seen.” “Then why are you here?” The question came quickly, starkly, so much so that even Phoenix seemed surprised. Magica only chuckled, the hem of her (its?) robe undulating in a breeze that did not come from the immediate surroundings. “I am here because I am a lesson teacher. Because it is my job to play the part of Artifice in many of the little events of a lifetime. I am the one who reminds your kind that they can only control so much of their fate, no matter how strong they profess to be.” Nobody liked to hear this kind of stuff’this was the life lesson that nobody wanted to learn. That no matter how hard you worked, your fate was basically out of your hands. That was what she was basically being told, wasn't it? That all that work was for nothing? As was usual, her thoughts seemed to be vocalized, though she hadn't felt the vibrations in her throat to indicate that she was doing so. Magica smiled, or seemed to, the edges of an indistinct line on its face turning upwards. “It is not settling to one such as you who bears your strengths as well as your failures on your own shoulders, and has gotten by doing so for many, many eons. You motivate yourself to act by claiming control... but in truth, it is only one of many motivators. Some people have come to accept that life is without control, that one must learn the lessons one is asked to learn’whether about dignity, submissiveness, honesty... or hubris.” That last word seemed to be especially pointed, especially poignant to a woman who had just recently lost something that she had worked very hard for. “Acceptance, Lia, that is the secret. It is the lot of all those who truly find peace. Accept what you are, and that you cannot direct the flow of your own fate without severely upsetting the balance of the wheel...” Anathema. The words did not hold to her psyche, because will was the only thing that made a difference. Strong will. Steel will. “Acceptance...” The word burrowed once more into her psyche, severely disturbing the wall she was attempting to build to shut it out. In the long bench seat of the jeep, Lia stirred, an expression of consternation on her face. She seemed for a moment like she might wake, and Virgil's eyes left the road for longer than they were accustomed. But she only shifted, her face straightening once more into more placid lines, the curve of her lips turned slightly downward. .:: ::: ::. CANTO XV I turn'd me to Beatrice; and she heard Ere I had spoken, smiling, an assent, That to my will gave wings They were home. After two days of a long road trip, they'd made it back to their Desert Castle without a scratch, and only a few minor annoyances. Lia didn't seem any more rested than she had before, and that upset her husband, twisting wires in the pit of his stomach into a nervous knot. “To bed?” he asked, pointing up the stairs towards the master bedroom. Secretly, he hoped she'd agree, because he was bone-tired himself. Lia, giving him only a wry smile, shook her head. “Unfortunately not. I've still got a few loose ends that I need to tie up while I'm awake. Any case, sleep hasn't been the most restful thing to ever happen to me lately...” That last stream of words trailed off into nothingness, as things often did when Lia was trying to play them off to be less troublesome than they actually were. Virgil, who already had one foot on the landing, turned and followed her into the living room. He'd expected her, at least, to make one phone call, and that's what she was doing now. Her tiny cell phone, which she despised, was held up to her ear, and she was pacing as she waited, nervous energy ticcing under every patch of skin. “Yes, Vivica?” Her Czech accent was very clear on this call, making her tag team partner's name sound like 'vee-vee-kah. “I've got some good news for you... Call me when you get this message, we'll need to talk about a plan.” A message like that would practically bait Vivica to call Lia back. She'd been waiting on pins and needles ever since she found out Lia had a lead, even though at least part of that time, she'd been furious at Lia for not trusting her. Lia smiled to herself as she set her phone down on the end table. That story would have a happy ending, at least’for she was confident that they'd have no trouble getting Markus back from a facility out in the middle of nowhere with very few people guarding it. She wasn't going to go all the way back out there only to leave without him. Virgil flopped down on the leather couch, only barely managing to still make the effort look graceful. He probably would have been displeased with himself, had any one else seen the gesture. “She'll be happy,” he said, deadpan almost. Exhaustion now found its way through his bones, making him sag with weight that he didn't think the couch could continue supporting. He felt glued to it, stapled to it, melted to it. Lia only nodded. “Naturally... this has been a long time coming for her. They'll have a happy reunion, he'll be ecstatic for her... all will be right with the world.” Rarely was it that simple. Virgil, Lord Sterescu, knew better than to buy the line, too. He arched a brow at his wife. “So it's that easy, is it?” “Why wouldn't it be?” Now it was the coy game’pretend like nothing is bothering you so that you can continue to stew in it on your own. If there were ever a classic Lia Ambrosi move, that was it. “Lia...” Virgil said, sighing, “If you don't tell me the truth of it, I'll have Rune down from his room to open up a can of that awful Swedish fish.” She looked at him askance, her hands on her hips. “You wouldn't... he almost made everyone in the house throw up the last time.” Virgil nodded sagely. “I would... especially if my wife refused to tell me the truth about something.” It was his way of joking. Everyone always assumed that Virgil was deadpan, straight-man, not a bone of humor in him. Nothing could have been farther from the truth’it was just learning to read him that was the problem. Virgil hated letting people read him. He was looking down at his fingernails now, which he prided on keeping obscenely long, to the point where he seemed to make people uncomfortable with them. Given that that seemed to be one of his goals in life, they probably pleased him very much. He glanced up at her. “Still stings, eh?” She didn't have to ask him what he meant. Her hands crossed protectively over her chest, as if by doing so, she could prevent him from looking into her heart, and seeing the truth of it. “...Yes,” she said, with a short sigh, “It still bothers me.” Virgil shrugged slightly, looking up to catch her gaze, “It's better her than someone else, isn't it?” “Of course it is,” Lia said, nodding as she looked away from him. Her eyes were far away now, remembering times in past promotions when she'd been cast off from the throne, just like that, and sent plummeting down into oblivion. Paranoid instinct told her she was on the verge of it again. “It's just all the other possibilities are...” “Unconscionable?” “Yes, there's that. Among other things.” A question was coming. It was one that Lia had dreaded to hear all these months that she'd spent in GCW alone, the place vacated by all her former comrades... except for Vivica. “Why do you still bother?” He was staring at her again, but the gaze was unwavering, and quiet. He was not challenging her, though he expected honesty out of her, as he always did. “There's my competitive pride... and... it pleases me.” The latter maybe wasn't always true. It was something that Virgil could easily seize on. And he did. He sighed, smiling slightly. “And if my wife would only leave those pleasures behind, I could find a thousand other ways to keep her diverted, occupied, and happy. If she'd only leave those obligations behind and give me half the chance.” Lia smiled too, and for a moment, it seemed like Virgil had won a minor victory, like perhaps he'd nearly convinced her that staying on in GCW wasn't the right thing for her to do, even if it had been the right thing for him. He was quickly disabused of that notion. “I'm giving it half a chance right now. Plainly, there's still something compelling about my presence, or I wouldn't be in the position I am right now... if I can scrape up a handful of wins, I'll be back where I was... and in the very least, I'll be given some competition in the process. If that doesn't play out...” Virgil raised a brow. “What's the point?” Lia didn't answer the question for a moment. She might have been acknowledging that there wasn't one. But whether she was acknowledging that or not, she was pulling open her bag, strapping on her training gloves, shucking off her bulkier outdoor shirt in favor of a tank top, which was what she customarily wore when she trained. She smiled, but this time, it was a grim one. “GCW has wanted to get rid of me for a long time... they think I'm irrelevant.” She didn't need to make any sweeping statements. She bent to pick up her coat from the ground, and slide it back on the back of the chair. She was already moving out of the room. Virgil tilted his head, shook it ironically. “Do not go gentle into that good night,” he quipped. He enjoyed quoting poetry, even relatively new poetry. “Rage, rage against the dying of the light,” she finished, her voice fading, the last flash of her fingertips coming round the edge of the door as she left him alone. .:: ::: ::.
