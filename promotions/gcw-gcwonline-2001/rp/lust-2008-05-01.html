Lust

1 May 2008

Date: 5/1 It was an 8 x 10 press photo. Just like the one he had. Just like the one every member of the GCW roster had. He himself had sat before stacks of five-hundred of them, signing every last one over the course of marathon autograph sessions. This one, though, seemed to have Seymour Almasy’s undivided attention. A petite, female figure stared back at him from the glossy photograph, clad in a blue and white halter top, with matching stretchy pants. Also of note was the GCW United States Championship, wrapped snugly around her waist. Yes, like seemingly everyone else of note in Global Championship Wrestling, Seymour Almasy was fixated, in some way, shape, or form, with Miss Windsor. “You gonna stop staring at that picture, or should I get you a package of Kleenex and some lotion and leave you alone for ten minutes?” Even as Seymour’s head jerked up in the direction of the offending smartass, he knew that the dry, sarcastic tone of the comment meant that it could be only one man, his out-of-the-ring assistant, Jonathan Calver. “I think I’ll pass on that for now, Calv,” Almasy retorted, “but if you find LeStatt Knight running around, you might want to get him the tissues and lotion, stat.” “I’ll keep that in mind,” came the reply. “But seriously, you’re staring at that picture like you want to fuck her or kill her.” “The answer is probably somewhere in between,” Seymour replied, placing the photo down beside him, remembering for the first time in several minutes that he was, in fact, on a couch in a lobby of a Sacramento-area Marriott. Jonathan Calver sighed audibly, taking a sip of the scotch that he held in his hand. Being Seymour Almasy’s attendant often meant alcohol was necessary to cope with the seemingly endless array of random situations that could come up. “You’re probably going to want to lean closer to the “kill” end this week, especially since her belt’s on the line in the gauntlet match you’re booked for.” Almasy gives no indication that he is actually listening to Calver’s opinion, instead letting his head fall into his hands. “This would be much easier if LeStatt Knight wasn’t in her head, telling her the same things that I’m trying to tell her.” “So, you’re pissed off that he’s mindfucking her more successfully than you are, is that it?” Jonathan shrugged, smirking at his charge. “Because God knows you’re not tormenting the girl for a nice purpose. Neither is LeStatt.” “Actually, you might be surprised on that last bit. Though, I will agree with you on LeStatt. Yevon knows he wants in the girl’s pants badly.” Jonathan shook his head. “He probably wants to use her to stay on top professionally. The guy’s involved”.though, to be fair, that doesn’t stop professional wrestlers very often.” The reminder of his own personal discretions made Seymour twitch noticeably. “Maybe he wants both. Damned if I know, though. Everyone wants SOMETHING from her.” “You make it sound like you don’t, Seymour. I mean, fuck, you’ve put the girl through Hell. You’ve been in her face every week, challenging everything she knows, and why” What do you want? I don’t understand at all. ? Almasy fell silent at that. “Truth be told, Jon,” he began, “I don’t know what I want from her. But I do know what everyone else wants from her, at least partially. Mortality wants her title. Knight wants her body, or to use her for his own personal gain. Storm wants her to be his wife. But in the end’they all really want the same exact thing.” “Humor me then, oh wise RPG master. What do they all want?” “They all want her as she is. They want her to be Tessa Windsor of the present, not the Tessa Windsor of the future. Knight tells her she wants to be World Champion. He’ll train with her. But when it comes right down to it’given a choice between her and the title, what would he pick?” “The title, obviously,” Calver replied. “Are you saying you wouldn’t pick the title?” “I’m not saying that at all,” Seymour stated, picking up the promotional picture once again. Again, he stared at it longingly, though Calver could not tell if it was the title or its holder that Seymour gazed so intently at. “What I am saying, however, is that I’d tell her that upfront in front of the world. Now that you’ve been questioning me for the past Yevon knows how long, can I please have my room key” I’ve been in these tights all night long, and I could go for a long, hot shower.” “Don’t forget your picture,” Calver offers, a grin playing about the older man’s lips even as he hands over the key to room 312. Seymour snatches it out of Jonathan’s hands, and shakes his head. “I won’t,” Seymour promised, mockingly. “I guess in the end the deal with me and Tessa is that, outta everyone she talks to, I’m the only one who’d do her the courtesy of stabbing her in the front. Kinda ironic considering we’re not on great terms, isn’t it?” The Standard-Bearer turns to go, picture clutched in one hand, key clutched in the other. “Seymour,” he heard, over his shoulder as he left. “Seriously now, if Tessa came up to you right now and wanted to go to bed with you, you’d tell her no?” Almasy’s reply, as he left, was about what one would expect. “Of course I’d sleep with her. What do you think I am, dead?” ========================== Truth be told, Seymour Almasy spent too much time in toy stores. Not only did they tend to have video games at better prices than dedicated game stores often did, but they also had that very visible sign of a wrestler’s fame: action figures. And it was in the action figure aisle that Almasy found himself, admiring the latest work of the Mattel toy company. The new WorldWide V line had been released, and Seymour found Vivica Valentine, Dorian Ryan, the especially hot Banned & Exiled~! double pack, and himself looking back at him from the pegs. “Not too bad,” Seymour observed to himself, looking at his own action figure image. “Damn. Really have changed quite a bit since the last one of these. That one had my long white hair and drawn on marker tattoos.” Cue Seymour shuddering. “I’ll have to pick one of these up,” he said, pulling his figure from the peg. “Damned cheapskates don’t give us freebees.” He was just about to leave when he spotted’it. “It” was on one of the bottom shelves, next to the official GCW ring (which happened to come with a free Shaman action figure, because, let’s face it, who’d actually BUY a Shaman figure otherwise?). Wrapped in a cardboard case, it was the spitting image of what Seymour lusted after at this very moment more than anything? Just made out of plastic. The official GCW United States Championship children’s replica belt , available at a fine purveyor of toys near you for a mere $14.99, called to Seymour. The mere sight of it was enough to call to mind the very recent memory of Almasy being in possession (but not the holder) of the real deal. Why, if he closed his eyes, he could remember coming out at Rampage with the United States strap over his shoulder. The real thing weighed ten’maybe twelve pounds, but it had been a pleasant weight on Seymour’s shoulder. The feel of the leather belt itself against his skin was, dare he think such a ludicrous thing, almost sexual in intensity. The belt meant being the center of attention. It meant fans grudgingly or willingly recognizing you as being someone. It meant they booed or cheered you that little bit more. It meant hearing the ring announcer using words like “reigning”, “defending”, and “new”. The routine was comfortably familiar to Seymour, a man who had held championships in promotions all over the world. He had been a tag team champion, a World champion, a North American champion, a Cruiserweight champion, an Intercontinental champion, even a Scorpion Fighting Champion. But he had never been a United States champion. The more he thought about it, the more he realized the severity of his situation. He hadn’t been a champion since..2006, his last run as PTC Global Champion. That was years ago. Years. Gold was addicting. Along with being a tournament whore, Almasy was noted by those in the business as being a title whore as well, the sort of athlete who derived meaning from his championships. Like Tiger Woods and his obsession with majors, merely winning matches wasn’t enough for the Standard Bearer. He needed golden performances to be truly happy. This was only his third chance to be a champion in GCW. One was a successful bid for tag gold. One was a victory in a Cruiserweight title match, but not by the requisite pinfall or submission. He would not be denied this time. Not even with three other challengers and a champion on the hot streak of her life. Placing his action figure on top of the belt, Seymour left the aisle, heading off towards the cashier. His smile was somewhat sheepish, as he knew that the second the cashier rang up the toy belt and put it in a bag said title would be removed from its cardboard packaging and placed over his shoulder. He would be a grown man with a toy title over his shoulder. In public. Most CHILDREN wouldn’t wear their replica title in public. Seymour Almasy, though, was a child at heart. He always, to an extent, had been, no matter how serious he became, or how far he strove to separate himself from his roots. And so there would be a plastic belt on his shoulder as he left the store and headed to his car. People would point and laugh at him. But he’d long since stopped giving a damn about what most other people thought. Even so, he’d done so far, far too late. ================== It was past ten o’clock the night before a title match. For Seymour Almasy, that usually meant that he would be in bed, dreaming of the glory to come the next day after a good workout and planning session on how to defeat his opposition. Tonight, though, he was awake, the dim light of the hotel room’s desk lamp illuminating the small pad of paper that he was scribbling at. “I still don’t get why you’re still awake,” Jonathan Calver chimed in, the other man clad in pajamas. Almasy made an entirely impolite face at Mr. Calver by way of reply. “Shouldn’t you be in your own goddamned room getting some sleep?” “And,” Calver retorted,” Shouldn’t you be in bed getting ready for a United States Championship match? It’s not like you to be up late scribbling on a piece of paper with barely any light in the room.” “No, but what exactly is “like me” has changed quite a bit in the past year, Jon. You know that as well as anyone.” Seymour’s eyes were bleary, yet focused. It was clear that there was a method to his madness, a purpose to be here, scribbling away instead of in a warm bed. Jonathan Calver looked at his charge and sighed. “Everything I’ve suggested to you for the past few weeks you’ve turned down. I wanted to get you a manager, and you turned me down?” “I’m getting myself into something big, Jon. I don’t want anyone else involved. Not a manager who’d be thrown into something over his or her head. Not you. Not anyone.” “Damn right you’re getting yourself into something big,” Calver screeched, “it’s a GCW UNITED STATES TITLE MATCH!” “I KNOW THAT!” Sitting bolt upright in his chair, Almasy glared into Calver’s eyes, anger radiating from his pupils. Jonathan stumbled back several steps, looking at Seymour with a dropped-open mouth. “Look,” Seymour began, “once I finish this, we can do shit your way. We can bring in a manager for me, I can go out to the middle of the ring and proclaim my greatness, I’ll do all of it. But you know what” Right now I’m got three things on my plate. I’ve got the GCW United States Championship, which, contrary to what you’re thinking right now, I want more than anything I’ve wanted in a long time. I have this situation with Tessa Windsor, which I have no idea how the Hell to solve. And I’ve got one more thing to put on my plate, which is why I’m scribbling out a plan.” Before Calver could come up with a response, Almasy was on his feet, taking slow steps towards he former Vice President of Talent Relations. “Get out, Jonathan. There are things in my life and my career that I have to take care of on my own. This is one of them.” The steely tone of Almasy’s voice convinced Jonathan that this was a battle he didn’t want to fight. With no further words, merely a nod of his head, Jonathan Calver was out the door, closing it behind him, en route to get some shut-eye. That left Mr. Almasy alone with himself and his scribbling. Silently, he reached into his pocket, unearthing his wallet. Inside were credit cards of various description, about $30 in cash, and some business cards. Most importantly, though, was the photo tucked underneath a plastic cover on the left side of the wallet. The face that stared back at him was angelic, at least to his eyes. The woman’s shoulder-length brown hair was familiar to him from hundreds, no, thousands of hours spent cuddled up with her, his fingers twining softly through it Seymour had looked at this particular picture of Laura Marie Winters more times than he could count. It was only this time, though, that he noticed something in it that he hadn’t before. Her eyes were alive, as they always seemed to be. Alive with teasing. Alive with a challenge, as it were. The same expression she’d always had when they were away from prying eyes. She always would tell him that he needed to stand up for himself. Be his own man. Not take shit from people just because they expected it. And every time he would nod at her and promise not to let it happen again’and every single time nothing would change. Bless her heart, she always put up with it. It was an entirely different Seymour that looked at her picture today, however. It had cost him more than he liked to think about at times, but Jason Seymour Wilson was his own man now. He didn’t take crap from people. He refused to be considered an afterthought, even in a match with four other competitors. Neither the rising star Andy Murray, the enigmatic Brad Jackson, the brash Phil Atken, or the bubbly Tessa Windsor would stop a Seymour Almasy with a clear path and ambition. Yes, Seymour did have a lot on his plate. And yes, it would be difficult to manage all of it. But Seymour Almasy could handle it. Leaving the scribbling on the desk for the time being, Seymour walked over to the bed, wallet still in hand. In a way, he supposed that Jonathan Calver was right. He would have to deal with this title match first, win the championship, and raise it over his head before he could deal with everything else. For once, he would sleep peacefully tonight. Laura would be so very proud of him.
