God has a plan...

24 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/24/09 The sky opens up on the city of Clifton, Nj. To the effect of a congested street in New York, men and women in business attire are running down the sidewalks with umbrellas encasing them in a world of seclusion. Those of who were caught by surprise have briefcases and the newspapers of the day over their heads, trying to reach the same subliminal state. Mystified condensation flows out in rhythmic breaths behind a wall of water running off of the cathedral above. The dry keeper of the threshold adjusts his monk-like hood and steps through the membrane between his world and the wet one beyond. This world is nothing more than a limbo between heaven and hell. Zacharia walks like a mercenary walking into a sure job with a heavy payout. He turns the corner; the general public running past him trying to get into a place of warmth and comfort not even taking notice to the man walking next to them. Sometimes you don’t expect what is to happen next, nor do you want to accept it as the truth? Zacharia makes his way into downtown Clifton, full of busy shops and busier people. Sidewalk venders are not as apparent as in the bigger New York City, but a modest attempt of a city for their markets. Zacharia walks towards the newsstand for something to read, some sort of escape from his reality in this vessel operated under the conditions of divine intervention. “but everyone wants to live with strife and struggle, it is the fantasy all want to live. A crowd of blue collars in their rain coats and umbrellas all gather under the outreached arm of cloth to shield all in need of shelter from the elements. Newspapers are drawn at different points reading the gossip of the city and state and the financial recession that is inevitable to avoid. Zacharia makes his way into the store to the book section. “but not me. I just want to escape, but HE won’t allow me to up and go. Or will he” Zacharia catches a hold of a dozen eyes watching his every move. Normally the attention does not bother him in the slightest bit, but the eyes were more than the usual suspicion. He looks over his shoulder to even see the clerk staring. Looking over to the window, a television catches his attention with a woman as beautiful as a Chinese porcelain doll as the news anchor, speaking nothing more than blurbs and mumbles around the crowd. A “Top Story” banner floats onto the screen next to the woman and a distinguishable chalk outline on asphalt with police tape makes the story obvious without hearing anything. The image fades into the blue background and cross fades with an image of a man in a hood. Once the image comes into its own, he drops the book in his hand. Zacharia’s eyes focus and protrude from his sockets, jaw dropping like a cartoon character. He swivels his head at a neck breaking speed to a newspaper next to him where on the front cover the headline news is, “LOCAL RELIGIOUS LEADER & ENTERTAINER MURDERED.” Without reading much more, he focuses on the picture in the center of the column, which is the picture of him from the GCW roster. He makes his way through the crowd and out the door. The crowd outside has yet to catch onto the dilemma of the walking deceased in their presence. He slowly makes his way down the street and cuts through an alley to get to the main street. Zacharia slips next to a newsstand on the strip and looks over his shoulder next to him, reading the article: GCW and local community is devastated to see a religious figure and sports entertainer slain at The Church of our Father in Clifton, NJ. The president of Global Championship Wrestling, Steve Caldera did not return calls, but a few of his friends from the organization made a point to talk to reporters? Zacharia notices people starting to rubberneck in his direction. He looks at the picture and notices the hood; his trademark. He begins to sprint down the street, catching the attention of the general public around him. As he runs, he struggles through his hoodie and sheds his skin of it. A tattered t-shirt remains below, full of holes and worn out strips. He tosses it into the nearest trashcan and pivots like a basketball player on the court to fake out anyone in pursuit. He tracks around in a large circle and then disturbs the subtleties of the park with his frantic dash for the church. Upon return to his threshold, he sees mourners with candles lined up on the steps of his church. His hair drips like sweat after a rigorous workout down his face from the rain. His shirt, the little protection it provides, now takes on the properties of shrink-wrap, showing every ripple and curve of muscle that lies beneath. For moments that seem like hours, he contemplates how to handle the men and women in front of the church. It was not a Sunday, yet the frequent visitors to his place of worship were all there, praying for the man that stands no more than two-hundred yards away from them. He decides to take action. He makes his way into the crowd and parts a path through the sea of people like Moses, angry at the intrusion until they realize whom the imposter is. “Listen to me everyone, there was some sort of mistake in the paper. I am going to get everything straightened out, but for the time being, I need everyone to clear away from the church before the press see the crowd and intrude on our place of worship. Again, there was a mistake in the paper, and everything is fine!” Several people look upon him in disgust, others fall to their knees, praying and thanking God for the mistake. Zacharia does not hesitate in his breaking off from the mini news conference and revolts against the mourners trying to grab him in awe and misery. He finally makes it to the door when someone grabs his shirt. Out of surprise and anger, he flips around and grabs the arm of the perpetrator and nearly flips him over when he realizes the man is wearing a brown UPS shirt. The box truck in front of the church makes the reassurance. Zacharia vaguely apologizes and grabs a box out of the man’s arm. Upon entering the church, Zacharia throws the box against the wall and paces in the foyer, desperately trying to makes sense of the situation. The news broadcast and the pictures of himself keep flashing like a montage of a film in front of his eyes. He throws his hands into the air and lets out a groan of frustration. Prayer would do nothing but serve as a meditation to calm down, but that is not what he wants, he wants to know how this came about, and how someone could make a mistake like this. The newspaper said that GCW knew about it. Chris would know something, possibly know more than he does at the moment. He has no way of getting in touch with him except coming down to the church, but what are the chances that he is in Newark, now? First, getting arrested for being wrongfully accused of killing someone, now being written up in the paper for dying. Zacharia needs answers and needs them before more confusion erupts. Zacharia goes for the door when he steps on the package that he threw against the wall. About to kick it out of annoyance, he catches the handwritten label instead of the typed up one. More personal, but who the hell would send him a package? He picks it up and tosses it up in his hands, noticing it had no weight to it at all. Zacharia rips off the tape going down the center of the seam and claws through the crumbled newspaper only to find an index card with immaculate handwriting on it. Read the return and you shall know. Zacharia flips over the flap of the box and takes closer notice to the label in the same handwriting. Reading Obituary 225 Star Ledger Clifton, NJ 07013 Zacharia stares perplexed at the message and walks to the altar without much realization that he is actually walking. He mulls over the address and finds himself sitting on the steps at the back of the church, still holding the box in his hands. He puts the box down at his feet, the side that was resting against his body soaked through, and takes out the newspaper that was in the box. He flattens it out and looks up at the top of the page. Wednesday February 25th, 2009 Confused by the date, he looks down the page, which is the obituary section of the paper. This is tomorrow’s paper? Zacharia opens the paper and sees a section cut out from the center of the third page. He drops the paper, watching it fall in slow motion to his eye, and picks up the box again. Afraid to look at the label again, he takes his time on flattening out the seam to read the return address. He realizes that the enigma is nothing more than instructions. Tomorrow, he needs to find himself in the obituary section of the paper? *** Zacharia stands, once more at the threshold of the doorway into the city, but in a skeptic mindset. He contemplates making his way into the streets for the paper, or calling someone to bring it to him. The clock on his wrist strikes nine am with such force that he feels his arm move to the right enough to take notice. The abrupt stop of the little hand on his watch resonates like a tuning fork through his arm and into his head. A constant ringing develops and the anxiousness of the time around his wrist makes its way into his head, forcing him to take a step forward into the city streets, once again out of the membrane of his world and out into another. He quickly makes his way into the park and wastes no time on the designated path and runs through the trees and towards the opening of the fence that leads into the main street. He notices a paper on the bus stop bench next to the gate and slowly creeps up on it as if it is a temperamental animal guarding its den. He picks up the paper quickly and unfolds it at the crease and glances at the top. In excitement but nervousness, he sees that it is today’s paper and he flips through to the obituaries. He freezes once he sees his eyes staring up from the paper. If he didn’t know any better, he would have thought that the image was animated, but he knows better, and it is an inanimate version of himself. Zacharia reads every word intently. It starts off about how he was a great man and honors him for his religious studies and how he gave back to the community through the spreading of the word of God. It then touches briefly on his career at GCW. What really makes him analyze is when it talks about how he died, which he thought was very unorthodox for an obituary? Zacharia Taylor was faced with a moment of struggle when an intrude went through the back door of the church and attacked the priest around 11am. Police reported that there was a struggle in the center of the church, several pews being knocked over and damaged. After a struggle, Father Taylor was shot three times in the chest and once in the arm, reported dead on scene. Police are looking for any information on the intruder? Zacharia cannot believe what he just read. An intruder in the church? He looks to the bottom and sees the author’s name is “Caller”. He looks down at his watch and notices that an hour went by already. He was so caught up in the newspaper that he didn’t realize the passing of time. He looks around for anyone looking at him, but the street was empty. He decides it is best to get to the church and make sure it is secure, but then the thought of running comes to mind, but then who is to say that this is even true? Zacharia thinks of the message from the morning before and decides to make his way back to the church to see if there is another message of some sort. As he passes by the payphone he regularly uses, he contemplates calling Chris, but the idea quickly flees from his mind as he looks up and sees a UPS truck parked down the street in front of the church. He starts at a slow jog, and quickly begins to spring up to the church as the truck pulls away. Once the courtyard and side door are in view from the street, he slows down and looks around inquisitively but then takes off down the sidewalk and up to the door where a small brown package remains in the aftermath of the delivery. Zacharia picks up the package, noticing there is no return address this time, and opens up the church doors and closes them behind him to open up the package in solitude. He tears open the tape from the center seem and notices it is packaged in the very same manner in which the other one was, in crumpled newspaper. He gently puts the paper to the side, knowing that it will have some sort of clue on it, and looks at what remains in the box’a phone. He stares at it and flips over the box’s flaps to make sure there was no return address. He picks up the phone and is about to open it but then something catches his attention on the paper. His name is printed at the top of one of the pages that is lying open. He opens it up and sees a biography of himself printed across the pages. He is in awe as he sees the division between his personal life and GCW. Before he can get too far into it, a movement catches his eye in the back of the church. He stops and looks around, but nothing is there, he shakes his head and laughs. He looks down at his watch and it reads 11:40am. RING RING RING’RING RING RING’RING RING RING? Zacharia looks at the ringing phone dancing a lyrical ballad in the box. He leans over into the box and picks it up, scared to answer. He looks at the calling screen and the name of the person calling is “Caller”, presumably the same person who wrote the paper’s articles and obituary. He raises the phone to his ear: “Listen very precisely to what I say, Taylor. You have eight minutes before your killer comes through the back door. He is going to walk through the courtyard and make his way in through the back door. You need to walk past him, he has to see you in close quarters. You then need to run. I will call you after you start running. Go to the left and around the corner towards the liquor store.” “Who are you?” “Not important?” “Who is the killer?” “Christian Thomas.” Zacharia pauses and the voice picks up the slack. “Yes, Thomas is going to kill you. You will understand. Get past him and start running.” The phone clicks off and the phone’s screen goes blank. Zacharia looks around the church and looks down at his watch, reading 11:42. Zacharia lips the words “six minutes” and runs over to a window that overlooks the church’s courtyard. Christian hasn’t made his way into the courtyard yet, but how is Christian the killer? There must be something wrong. How will he get past someone with a gun? Not to mention someone that? The phone vibrates in his hand and shows an envelope flying. Zacharia opens it up and sees the text message that says: “make your way to the back of the church.” Zacharia looks around but doesn’t see anyone. How does this person know everything? He grabs the newspaper and folds it up and puts it into his pocket. On his way to the back of the church, he picks up a zip up sweatshirt and starts to put it on as he sees someone walking through the courtyard. When a person is faced with something they don’t understand, that is where the raw emotion and instinct comes into play? Zacharia takes a deep breath and makes his way by the back door. It open as he gets within feet of it and a hand holding a gun passes through the threshold of the outside world. Zacharia charges the door and slams the door back onto the arm. A bullet discharges from the chamber and rests in the door jam. A yell of pain emits from the other side of the door and the gun drops to the ground. Zacharia opens the door and rushes the man on the other side. Christian falls backwards as Zacharia pushes into him and they lock eyes. Christian looks down at the phone in his hand and falls off the steps. Zacharia doesn’t wait a wink and charges down the courtyard and turns the corner. The phone begins to ring as the liquor store comes into view. “Taylor, get into the cab in front of the liquor store. Tell the driver to drop you off in front of Saint John’s Hospital in Patterson. You will make it there in 12 minutes, but Christian will catch up.” Zacharia, trying to maintain his composure, tries to put his thoughts together. “How do you know all of this?” “Astute question, my friend, but do exactly as I tell you. I do mean exactly, or I will not be able to help you escape from Christian.” “Who are you?” “Keep running!” Zacharia hangs up the phone and looks over his shoulder to see Christian in tow, holding his arm. Zacharia gets to the cab and jumps inside and yells the directions to the driver. The driver speeds away, driving past a smiling Christian. Zacharia takes a deep breath and looks at the phone for more instruction. He has a feeling he should take out the newspaper, so he does, and he begins to read. Nothing is written out of the ordinary, but to his surprise, he sees the mention of the Mercenary of God and the Modern Testament. He looks at the phone once more before reading the GCW career section The phone rings as the cab pulls up to the hospital. “buckle your seatbelt and grab onto the handle above the door.” Zacharia complies. He grabs the handle as a Clifton Police car slides into the emergency lane and slams into the cab on the driver’s side. Zacharia unbuckles himself and gets out of the car. Christian Thomas is in the driver seat of the police car. “Run to the West Wing of the hospital and gain access to the roof. Take the stairwell near radiology.” The phone clicks and Zacharia puts the phone in his pocket. He runs into the hospital as doctors and nurses run out to the cab and police car. Zacharia doesn’t look behind him as he enters the hospital. A nurse tries to slow him down but he pushes past her. As he runs he looks above each flame doors for the radiology department. He sees that it is an arrow in red, so he follows the red line on the ground winding through the halls and under doors. He reaches to his head and brings it to his face, seeing blood running down his fingers. He thinks to stop, but continues to run to the advisement of the Caller. The red stops as he enters radiology and spots a small sign that says “Stairs. Roof Access” next to a door. He pushes in the door and starts to run up the stairs, fatigue starting to take over his legs. On the third floor, he hears the door below him slam and clicking of steps and the rattling of the railing as someone runs up the stairs. He looks over the edge and gets a glimpse of Christian’s face. He hurries up the remainder floors and makes his way to the roof. The phone starts its lyrical ballad once more. “Double time! You stopped on the steps. He is catching up! Jump onto the building to your left and run across the roof. Stay on the phone!” Zacharia sprints for the edge of the building, seeing that there is a ten foot gap between the hospital and the other building, but the other building was lower. Zacharia is running with the phone to his ear and nearly reaches the end of the building when the Caller’s voice cries out. “JUMP!” Zacharia jumps and it feels like minutes before he reaches the other building. He hits and rolls onto his back, sliding to a stop with his feet in front of him. Taylor holds his head once more, and pulls the phone back to his ear. “Get up and keep running, he is coming.” He looks behind him as the voice trails off, seeing Christian leave his feet on the top of the building. Zacharia runs for the other building in front of him, which is at the same level as the one he is on, but the gap is a matter of feet. The voice in his ear yells to drop, so Zacharia does as a sound much like thunder rumbles behind him. The Caller tells him to get up and run in a straight line. He obliges and continues to run, not looking behind him. He makes it onto the other building as he hears gunshots behind him. He tries to keep running in a straight line, adrenaline and panic trying to balance out. The Caller starts to become audible once more? “Listen very carefully. The highway in front of you, you are going to jump onto it.” “What” Are you out of your?” “Taylor, have I guided you wrong yet” Once you jump onto the next building with the billboard on it, you are to run in a diagonal to the corner of the building, that is where it is the closest to the highway. Run as fast as you can, and jump.” “Ok?” “Hang up” Zacharia puts the phone in his pocket and continues to run. He makes his way to the gap between the last two buildings and jumps it with ease like the one previous. He pivots and starts to run with all his energy and strength to the corner of the building. There is traffic on the highway, cars moving at no more than ten miles an hour. Everything around him goes quite. Sparks fly out of the air conditioner in front of him from a bullet zooming over his shoulder. Zacharia begins to hear his heart pound and his breath as it escapes his pursed lips. He reaches the corner of the building and jumps. If God has a plan for everyone, I hope that mine doesn’t involve Interstate 20 and the oncoming black van? Zacharia clears the fence barrier, and his flailing legs and arms go untouched. As if in slow motion, Zacharia heads towards the black van, which’s door is sliding open. A man stands next to the open door, his eyes slowly blinking. Once Zacharia makes his way into the threshold of the van, everything speeds back up and he slams into a safety net set up across the back of the van. Zacharia’s hand is bent backwards int the net, cracking his thumb backwards and wrist in an awkward way which falls to the side abnormally. He settles and falls onto his side, looking up at the building he jumped from in extreme relief and pain. He sees Christian standing at the edge of the building smiling. Before the billboard can obstruct his view of his presumed killer, Christian waves. The billboard cuts in their way, and the traffic breaks, making the billboard go by quickly, but Christian is no longer there. The man beside him closes the door. “Zacharia Taylor, I am the Caller?” Zacharia gets a good look at the man, and he falls to his side in shock. ??but? you can’t be! You’re dead, Father Eman?” Before Zacharia could finish his sentence, a tractor-trailer jackknifes trying to avoid a car slamming on its breaks. The van that Zacharia is in slides and veers around the trailer, but is then hit in the rear, causing it to change direction, heading right over the guardrail and off of the overpass, landing on the highway below. The van is crushed and beaten in. Zacharia stirs in the van and tries to move his battered body, but he is wedged between the window’s crushed frame. He looks out into the highway as an incoming car heads straight for him at sixty miles an hour. Zacharia can do nothing more than blink as the car hits. God does have a plan for everyone, and mine? Zacharia pulls his hood on over his head and stands up, cracking his neck to the left and right. He takes the US title belt off of the chair next to him and heads for the door. “has yet to be written. The door slowly creaks closed behind the US Champion on his way to his match.
