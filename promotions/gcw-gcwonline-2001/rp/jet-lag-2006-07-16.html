Jet Lag

16 July 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 16th July 14th 15:48 Thornwood Estate The mailbox at Thornwood estate was generally considered somewhat suspicious. The local residents (what few there were) often questioned the fact that there was never mail pouring forth from it, yet for some reason nobody had ever seen the owner of the pleasant house. Rumors often circulated throughout the town nearby that it had been bought, sold, that the owner had died, moved out in the middle of the night with all of his things, et cetera. It stood as a monument to the unknown, the cool stone columns at the front of the house supporting an unsolvable mystery. Virgil Ambrosi, the owner of said estate, peered curiously out of the window at the sound of a larger automobile crackling and crunching down the dirt road that led around his long, iron-fenced yard. Upon inspection, he found it to be an unknown vehicle. Slightly puzzled by its box-like shape and red-blue racing stripe pattern, he watched it as it pulled up to the mailbox, stopped for a moment, and drove off down the road, kicking up a cloud of sandy-colored dust in its wake. The shape in the window that could be seen from the outside disappeared once again, depriving the locals of another juicy rumor. The sun glared at him fiercely, as though remembering that it was it's divine duty to incinerate this being and everything like him in a burst of flame and ash. However, through his sunglasses, Virgil's very existence continued to mock the sun for now, as he stepped down the sandstone stairs and onto the gravel path that led to the front gate. He had often thought unproductively about how pleasant the gravel was to have instead of a paved driveway. Aside from the satisfying crunch as his feet fell upon it, it acted as a baffle to anyone who might come looking for him. After all, he'd never shown a taste for anything but the pure opulence of old European style, which often included paved driveways. He imagined a carload of Cathayans coming for him, and looking at the somewhat tall grass, gravel driveway, and aged wrought iron fencing, and speeding off to his neighbor's house five miles away. -creak- The hinges of the mailbox protested direly at their sudden usage, after eons of disuse. Virgil pulled a long envelope out of the mailbox, and began the hike back to the house. The heat was stifling, and he felt as though he could do no reading when standing in it. Everything around him seemed to droop and hang with the weight of the humidity, and he had no desire to join them. As the door shut lazily behind him, he slit the letter open with his fingernail, after noticing the postmark, 'Bilbao, Espa’a'. The envelope fell to the floor, as Virgil pored over the letter, finding himself terribly excited about what he assumed it contained. 13-06-2006 Banco de Espa’a Bilbao Se’or Herratxa, It pleases me to notify you that the property you inquired for is indeed in our possession. There is only one previous owner on record, and we acquired it after his recent death. It is fortunate that your letter came in such good time, as we were considering leasing the land to the local government. However, you did indeed have sufficient funds to bid higher than they, and they refused to pay any more for what was described as 'a dilapidated ruin'. We now have the title on file under your name, and you may request a copy or view it yourself if you like. It has certainly been a pleasure to deal with you, se’or Herratxa, and we hope to have business with you again. Sincerely, Umberto Garcia y Puente Banco de Espa’a He held the letter in his hand for a moment, staring at it with unfocused eyes, after he'd read it several times over. It was such a short letter, and didn't even address him, instead one of his pseudonyms, but it somehow seemed that the entire world had been packed into that small amount of words, every drop of ink containing entire cities, still buzzing with life. It slipped from his hand, and drifted slowly to the black-bordered white marble floor. He slowly walked away from it, and back up the stairway. Now, there was much to be done. - - - - - - - - - - July 15th A worn castle Around fifty miles outside Barcelona There wasn't much distraction to be had here. An occasional bird chirped and warbled in the distance, but there was little to no noise of cars or motorcycles speeding past, none of the flash and havoc that Virgil often associated with nearby Barcelona. He was surrounded by slightly crumbled walls, though the interior of the castle, while thoroughly pillaged, was still partially intact. What wasn't, of course, could be rebuilt if he wanted it to be. There were now trees growing through bare patches in the floor, twisting and winding their way toward the sky, which in some places was barely visible for the trees that had been there for ages. The flow of memories that had washed through his brain some weeks ago had left him dazed, completely confused about his existence. There were still patches of fog in his mind, but he remembered the most important of things, at least. Nothing could have been more valuable to him than to know what his life had been like, and specifically, who he had spent his life with. Somehow, it gave him a bizarre sense of deus ex machina to know now that Lia Vlaszy, his companion for some time, had actually been his wife, though hundreds of years prior. The likelihood of something like that happening was astronomical, after all, and of all people, it happened to him... it only further cemented his belief that there was no god, that horrible things did not necessarily happen to 'horrible creatures'. Even the smallest of things had been coming back to him ever since he'd stepped foot in the fallen remains of his father's castle, which had been his after his father's death. Every brick held some vision of her, in various slices of time, mapping out the entirety of their life together. He remembered their daughter, Elena, running through the hallways despite the insistence of the servants that she not do so, from a very young age. For the moment, he decided not to think about what happened to her. He had remembered other children, but their memory was not so potent as the one of the first, the one that was taken from him. He had no regret whatsoever for buying the land. Even if just to preserve it as it currently was, it was worth everything he could spend on it. Someday, however, he'd bring Lia here. Certainly, if there was so much here for him, there had to be just as much for her. A pair of shadows flew overhead, finches of some kind, darting off toward some common destination, riding the light wind that had come up. His footsteps caused the small animals nearby to run in terror, as the dried leaves of seasons past crunched under his feet. His eyes followed them carefully, as they flitted through the tall trees that had grown around fallen stones, growing over the mortar that had once held them together. They came alight on a cornered piece of wood jutting out from the ground. It was rotted thoroughly, but years of standing in the same place had practically petrified it. Virgil took a step toward it, and tilted his head slightly, as hazy thoughts began to slide forth from his subconscious. “Hmm... that chapel,” he mused aloud, taking one more tentative step toward it, before deciding that he had best let it stay undisturbed for now. After all, there wouldn't be much time from now until he was back again, but next time, not alone. No, next time, she would be there with him. The thought was enough to stir the newfound possibility for excitement that he'd rediscovered recently, but for the moment, he knew it was time to leave. The plane back to Canada would not wait for him. - - - - - - - - - - A few hours later... Abord an airplane bound for Vancouver Virgil had always wondered what it was about airplanes that made them complete aesthetic vacuums. There was nothing to do except sit or look out the windows, but on trans-Atlantic flights, the only thing to be seen was the endless expanse of swirling blue water, crashing and squirming about perpetually, and he saw little use in staring for that. Naturally, deprived of all else, he was forced to consider the present, something he'd found himself rather loath to do lately. It was an unfortunate circumstance at the moment, as far as he was concerned, that wrestling compromised such a large part of his concern. Certainly his time could be better spent elsewhere, but it still served some purpose of entertainment, he supposed. After all, things could be worse. He could have chosen to amuse himself as an investment banker, or an accountant. However, at least the two of them could have been easily terminated when the whim grew stale. There was nothing to do but stand, and walk out. He had often considered doing so with wrestling. There was little to keep him there, wasn't there? The comrades and 'friends' he had made were not all that important. Save one notable exception, he could do without them. Certainly, they had their uses, and their company was not altogether unbearable, but they only served to remind him of exactly the sort of situation in which he was currently trapped. “So why not walk away?” he thought, staring calmly at the air conditioning vents above his head. It seemed a fair question. If there was little keeping him there save the alliances he'd forged, and the thought of disappointing Lia by withdrawing from their campaign prematurely. He'd had the intention of removing Ehren Moore from wrestling, but Moore accomplished that feat himself. In honesty, Virgil had found himself somewhat stimulated by his conflicts with the 'most impressive physical specimen in G.C.W.', and now that there was to be no more, he felt as though he'd lost his purpose. There would be others, yes, but he found he had no patience to wait for them to come along. For now, all there was to do was to defend the completely legally acquired belt sitting in his luggage. Take one opponent per week, cut them to ribbons, move on... repeat, repeat, repeat, until one comes along that manages to catch him unaware, and remove him from the spotlight. However, the idea he had decided to impose upon the 'division' he'd spawned by acquiring the Hardcore Title did lend some interest. However, that announcement would come later. Everything would have to come later, considering where he was currently trapped. He leaned back in the fairly uncomfortable chair, and continued staring at the ceiling, for lack of a better thing to do. Thoughts of wrestling were quelled by the thought that he would just stay where he was and make the most of it, and quickly supplanted by the idea of revisiting his recent land purchase. - - - - - - - - - - Early morning, July 16th Backstage Drones slogged back and forth through the hallways, a mix between those who had been working all night, and the equally worthless morning risers. They filtered past Virgil carefully, ferrying bits and pieces of the arena setup back and forth, back and forth, and Virgil began to wonder if the majority of them were even conscious of what they were doing, as they seemed to be repeating themselves, over and over. It wasn't exactly the most pleasing thing to watch, especially after recently leaving a cramped airplane, a similarly monotonous environment. He began to hastily beat a path toward the locker room shared by the members of Dies Irae. Being the break of day, he knew he could count on finding only one person. Conveniently, she was exactly the person he wanted to find. The door squeaked in protest as Virgil shoved it open, and his eyes quickly adjusted to the dim lighting. There was only a small lamp casting a dim glow over the room, and coloring the pale skin of Lia Vlaszy a startling amber. She seemed awake, but only barely. “Lia...” Her eyes opened quickly, as apparently she hadn't been completely conscious of her surroundings. However, her visage softened considerably upon noticing it was him. The revelation dealt to them nearly a month ago now had altered their interactions in a somewhat strange way. Affection was now altogether common, where before even between the two of them, there had almost seemed to be a businesslike manner to their conversations. “I missed you,” she said, sitting up straight on the sofa she had been using as a prop to keep herself awake. He reached down and took her hand, sitting down next to her. “Well, I have plans to take you with me next time. There's something I want you to see.” He cracked a smile, a movement that still felt strange, awkward, as though someone were reaching up and rearranging parts of his face to make the proper expression. She raised an eyebrow shakily, as her eyelids continued to droop. “You're leaving me in suspense, then, I take it?” He nodded, standing from the couch, and gently persuading her to follow. He had no plans to lay surprises on her right now. After all, she was half asleep, and he'd much rather prefer to bring her directly to the secret he was currently hiding. “I'm afraid so... but not for long. I promise.” With that, his own resolve to stay awake began to wane significantly, and rather than simply collapse in a heap on the couch next to her, he led her toward the door, with the intention of staying awake long enough to reach their hotel room. There was little time to waste before they'd have to be out again, as preparations for WorldWide began in earnest, and he had no plans to leave them grasping for energy when it came time to leave for Spain.
