Nothing. Happens.

5 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/5 I've been sitting here, for over an hour now, and I've been waiting for something to happen. I've been waiting for one answer to any question, and yet any one answer to any question just leads to more questions and more than one answer. And nothing happens. I've been trying not to look at my glass of beer. It disgusts me. And because it disgusts me, I can't turn away from it. Nothing stops me. Nothing forces me to look at it. I am completely free to look away from it. Just like I am completely free to do anything wtihin the realm of physics. I could stand up. I could leave this place and go see the world and live a life of no regrets. Or I could go home, invest all of my money, sit on my ass and live off of interest for the rest of my life. I can urinate on the waitress. I can kill someone. I could kill myself. I could go out and make a life out of ruining people's lives. Or I could sit here...and stare at my beer. And be disgusted with my own freedom. As soon as I arrived back at GCW, the wave of freedoms hit me. I came to the realization that very few things can stop me from doing anything I want. Although I suppose that's something I've known for a while now. And this disgust hasn't really struck all at once like I would like to believe, but rather has been growing in me, like a deadly nephritis. Fear and anticipation of things I can see coming, a future that I need not predict, and once it's here I know I loathe. A calm acceptance and a calm hatred rolled into one, and in victory or defeat it only subsides at best. Yes. It felt good to beat Seymour Almasy. It felt good to beat him again. That's right. People conveniently forget the joke of a tag team match against Seymour and Michael Stevens. And that's okay, because it makes this one feel even better. 'Hey Johnny. It's been a while, hasn't it?' Half buzzed and with a tangible malaise, I look away briefly from my humanly concocted High Life that brings me the fascade of happiness and see my friend Amos. It's been a while, Amos. 'It's been a while, Amos.' 'It's been a while, Johnny. How's life?' 'Peachy. The world is my oyster.' In a sense, the world is too much my oyster. That's exactly the problem. For the first time I look at Amos. He's not dressed like Amos. Let me prelude this with pointing out that Amos is something beyond 'ahead of the times.' He's always had his ear to the ground without having his ear to the ground. He never cared about the way things were, but he was a sort of 'social surfer.' Always riding the wave just because that's who he is. And now he's dressed in a suit and tie. Fresh hair cut smells like success as he's got a briefcase and I barely know him anymore. Maybe that case holds his past life within its cold grasp. 'Dude...what's with the suit and tie?' He breathes in. He knows I'm not going to like this. Something...is about to happen. 'Conformity is the new rebellion, Johnny.' ... 'Come again.' 'It's the way of the world and how it works, Johnny. Everybody rebels. Everybody goes against what society says. But what if society says what everybody does and rebelling against rebellion is the only answer? So? What am I going to do? In a society that says that I need to have loose morals and complete freedom and do what I want? Well, I'm gonna get a nine to five! And buy a house! And have a white picket fence and go home at the end of the night and fuck my wife!' He's looking at me and nodding and trying to catch my approval with a net of confidence. And he knows just like I do that something almost happened. Almost but didn't. And I hate him for it. He's going down a new road to the same end. A different path to an all-too-familiar destination. He's rebelling for the sake of rebelling, for the sake of being different. His identity is found in doing what no one else does. And if getting a big time job and a wife and all that is what he calls rebelling, then it's only a question of how long before the rest of society follows suit and rebellion sings a different tune. 'I see.' Amos and I sit there. I before a match, rearing and ready after one victory and feeling a bit hopeful. He on the threshold of a new chapter in his life. And yet all I can see is things repeating. And nothing happening.
