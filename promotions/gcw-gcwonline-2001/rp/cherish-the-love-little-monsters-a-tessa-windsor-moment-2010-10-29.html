Cherish the Love: Little Monsters (A Tessa Windsor moment)

29 October 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/29/10 “Oh my god, Aislinn! Put that down!” Tessa’s voice was shrill and little Aislinn looked up from her patting and pawing in the tiny litter pan to face her with wide blue eyes. It was the look of knowing she must be in trouble from her mother’s tone, but with no hint of comprehension why. Tessa strode forward and scooped up her youngest as Tempest peered around the corner and chuckled at them. “Oh, I’m sure it’s clean. The ferrets haven’t had time to pee in there yet,” she said, looking at the pan and then back up at Tessa, whose purposeful scoop had joggled Aislinn into a fearful sort of mewling even though Tessa was smiling. She stroked the child’s dark hair and shushed her as she tried to keep her voice even. “Sweetie, the ferrets go potty in there. It’s not a sandbox.” Aislinn looked unconvinced and between her petulance at being pulled away from a perfectly good mess-making opportunity and Tessa’s too tight grip, she started to cry. “Oh honey, it’s ok.” Tessa tried to sound soothing and bounced the toddler in her arms a little, but the rescue came in the tiny, furry form of Emil, Tempest’s white ferret, wearing a bright, multicolored clown collar. He hopped up on the arm of the sofa and stood on his back legs peering at them and Aislinn instantly burst into laughter at the sight of him. Tessa looked to Tempest who nodded in encouragement. Slowly Tessa set the little girl down as Emil came closer and sniffed at her hair and face, eliciting a wave of giggles. “Ooo, can I see?” Evan, Tessa’s oldest boy poked his head around the corner and watched with interest. One of the quieter of Tessa’s children, Evan was fascinated by the animals, seeing as they had no pets of their own. Too much time spent away, moving, on the road, precluded such things as the family dog. And if Tessa had to be responsible for one more living thing, she wasn’t sure she could handle it. Five kids was enough. Scratch that, four. And Chris Storm. They didn’t ask for Uncle Marcus so much now. The addition of Auntie Tempest had, if not taken his place, at least provided a raucous enough distraction to keep them from dwelling on who was missing. Tessa hated herself for thinking it, but if Aislinn forgot him entirely it would probably be for the the best. And while the others were too old to forget, she hoped they would retain only the good memories of him. It was like a death in the family, only worse because the man still lived, somewhere. He might come back into their life like some specter of the past, just when things were going well. “Ok, so what’s it gonna be, zombies or ghosts?” Tempest asked, waving a couple of DVDs. She made ghost noises as the kids all gathered around and much discussion followed on what movie they would like to see. “Girl, you can’t be serious,” Tessa said, looking askance at Tempest. “ Aislinn’s way too young for anything truly scary.” “Oh don’t worry! I brought Godzilla and the classic Night of the Living Dead, which I doubt she’d understand. And it isn’t overly gory. I also have The Others. Seriously creepy, but again, not gory. I doubt she’d follow it enough to be scared. But, we can always start with The Corpse Bride too.” Tempest grinned, at least as full of mischief as the children most of the time, but she had chosen well. It amazed Tessa sometimes that she seemed able to think of things from a child’s perspective, but apply adult reason. “Aw, that’s a little kids movie! Did you bring anything scarier for us for after Linny goes to bed?” Pushing the envelope as always was Chris. His father’s namesake, he lived up to his biology smartly and in this case, he knew well that Auntie Tempest would indulge him. She eyed Chris with folded arms and said, “Maaaaybe. But I’ll need help with the popcorn before I divulge any truly scary movies that I may or may not have brought.” Suddenly she had volunteers and Tessa watched with great amusement as the boys piled into the kitchen with her and even Crista, the quiet oldest girl of the bunch went along. At fourteen she was struggling in the nether land between childhood and the true teens, rebelling, but oh so quietly. It was Tessa’s hope that with role models like Tempest around she could learn to be a strong and independent woman someday. And she hoped that she was herself such a role model. She was never sure though. Watching the kids hovering around the kitchen, chattering, all smiles, it was easy to assume things were perfect. “I don’t know how you do it, but thanks.” Tessa smiled at her friend, as they retrieved bowls for popcorn and cups for drinks. “For what?” Tempest replied. “This. For getting this together and even thinking of the right movies.” She smiled and swapped sides of the hotel’s kitchenette, to get the paper towels she knew they would need in abundance. “I swear I can’t even get a day planned out without something going wrong. Let alone an evening with the right movies, everyone here at the same time, and even animals.” She stood back suddenly as Aislinn raced by and right through the kitchen, in hot pursuit of Fernando, the darker ferret wearing a little jacket of shiny red fabric. Squealing with delight, she disappeared as quickly as she’d appeared around a corner and Tessa started to call after her, but caught the look from Tempest. “It’s fine Tess,” she said quietly. Don’t worry so much.” Tessa let out a breath slowly between her teeth, now alone in the tiny room with Tempest. “You sure? I don’t want the kids hurting your ferrets. I know they’re special and all, but…” “They’re super smart stunt ferrets, Tess. Even your kids will never catch them unless they want to be caught.” Tempest grinned. “Besides, it’s good for them. I can’t leave them in a cage. They need stimulus. The kids are great playmates for them.” Tessa nodded a little absently, her gaze following the children’s movements around the hotel suite. Nothing was too loud or seemed to be happening too fast, but she couldn’t always tell. “Tessa.” Tempest’s voice made her snap back to the present. Tempest smiled and shook her head very slightly. “It’s ok, they’re fine. Relax.” Tessa nodded and quietly said, “I’m a good mother, you know.” “I know that.” Tempest smiled at her more openly. “I only offered to help because I can help, not because I think otherwise.” She patted Tessa on the shoulder and nodded her head toward the living room where debate on the movie was continuing and they joined the kids with snacks in hand. As they settled in and the opening previews played, popcorn was passed around and Crista turned to look at the two women. “Don’t you guys have to fight this week?” she asked, plainly and without guile. She seemed intrigued by the relationship between her mother and Tempest, and how either of them could manage with the violence it must entail. “Yup,” Tessa sighed and looked at Tempest fondly, if a little sadly. The match was a guaranteed ratings winner, the best thing that could happen on a show in which people like Jay Terror and Vivica Valentine were gumming up the airwaves with ruthless contempt for everyone’s health, happiness and future but their own. But it was the worst thing for each of them going into Dangerous Games. To test their friendship in the ring was one thing, but they each had an injury to mind. And mind they did. Tessa didn’t want to crank on Tempest’s bad neck any more than she imagined Tempest wanted to twist her bum knee. The whole thing stank of a set up, and Jay Terror’s inherited influence. “Oh don’t worry about it,” Tempest said with a laugh as she launched a piece of popcorn into the air and caught in her mouth. “We’ll go out and tear it up and remind everyone just why we are the awesomest chicks the wrestling world has to offer, and then we’ll share ice packs and get ready for Dangerous Games. It’ll be fun.” Tessa smiled. It would be fun. ~ Aislinn stared in wide eyed wonder at the screen as Godzilla let loose with that screeching nuclear reactive roar. The ferrets were curled up on the laps of Evan and Crista respectively, and dozed, causing the occasional snicker as their feet fluttered, running in some far away dream. The monster stomped through the middle of Tokyo, demolishing the peaceful lives of those beneath his feet. Just as the music reached a fever pitch and the suspense of the moment hung in the air, portending further destruction, a knock resounded at the door. Everyone jumped and then dissolved in laughter at their own skittishness. “Wasn’t expecting anyone.” Tessa got up with a shrug and went to the door. The children were well accustomed to life in hotels and didn’t have any real expectations about a knock at the door. But Tempest was intrigued and kept peeking back into the little hallway from the door. But as it opened, it was Aislinn who spotted the newcomer first. “Daddy!” And so the stampede began. The children practically tripped over each other, save Crista, who meandered that way, but seemed embarrassed by all the fuss. “Chris? What are you doing here?” Tessa asked, confused, even as her husband engulfed her in a hug and scooped up Aislinn. “What do you mean, ’What am I doing here?’ My family’s here.” He looked at her as if the question were out of left field and looked around. “Listen, I wanted to talk to you about this match. It’s…” About that time, Tempest arrived and Chris Storm just stared at her for a moment before starting his sentence again. “Ok. I’d say something about who you let into your house, but I suppose I’ll just get an argument.” Tempest smiled wanly and shrugged. “I’m an opponent, not an enemy. I should think you’d have noticed that by now.” Chris stared with that sort of look that was reserved for those rare times when he actually held back what he had to say because of the kids, or because he didn’t want to have to deal with Tessa after saying it in front of the kids. “I’ve noticed,” he said. “I’ve also noticed you take care of business when you want to and not much of anyone gets in your way.” “Chris…” Tessa began in a tone that distinctly said, “Back off.” And as she stood with her hands on her hips, a hush fell over the kids. Tempest seemed to take the hint and clapped her hands together. “Ok! We’re going to watch Godzilla tromp all over Tokyo and leave your mom and dad alone to talk,” she announced with a nod from Tessa. After a moment of stunned silence from the kids, she threw in, “Last one on the couch fetches more popcorn!” It worked like a charm. There was a run for the sofa and a light thump as each body propelled itself onto it. Tempest grinned at the couple and headed back over to the sofa herself as Tessa stepped around the corner of the suite’s little hallway with Chris. “Not exactly standing up for you now, is she?” Chris said, looking back furtively. “You should be careful. Just because she’s your friend now…. Well, you remember where friends got you in the past, right?” Tessa folded her arms and eyed Chris with more than a touch of annoyance. “I know real friends know when to let each other fight their own fights. Tempest has some appreciation for her own space. She doesn’t butt into mine.” “Oh ok. She’s just here butting into mine now that I’m here to spend time with my family.” Chris adopted a sullen expression that Tessa was all too familiar with. He wasn’t getting his way. “Oh well I can’t imagine how that would have happened. Just because you haven’t been around in weeks and I haven’t heard from you since before the last show.” She shook her head. “So I made plans with a friend.” “And those plans are now more important than seeing your husband?” Chris shook his head and glanced toward the other room, still trying to keep his voice down. “Look I know you’re not all that happy with me right now. But I’m here. And I want to try and help you. I know you’re under a lot of stress and I don’t want to you to… well I don’t want you to rely on anything you shouldn’t to get by and…” “Is this about the freaking pills?” Tessa hissed, still trying to keep the volume below that of the destruction of Tokyo in the next room. “Cuz I swear to God…” “Come of it Tessa. I’ve been worried sick about you with Jay Terror trying put you out of action and Dangerous Games Coming. Things aren’t getting any easier on you.” “Ah, so you’re so concerned that it just now occurred to you to come back from training Mini-Marcus.” Storm started to object, but Tessa simply raised her hand and continued, “f you were even slightly worried that I had a drug problem, you should’ve been here every minute instead of leaving me with your kids. But obviously that was a too much trouble. For your information, Tempest is helping me out on that and helping keep the kids safe. Besides referring me to her physical therapist and showing me all her non-drug ways of dealing with it, she’s got the pills now.” “Ok , so you have an enabler.” “I have someone I’m accountable to who gives a shit.” Silence hovered as the two stared at each other. Their volume had risen slightly and their eyes strayed toward the living room, but no movement betrayed any intrusion from there. Tessa took a deep breath. “It makes perfect sense, Chris. Tempest doesn’t have kids to get into her stuff by accident and she doesn’t mind bringing me one at all hours if I really need one. We’re always on the road together anyway.” “I was on the road with you. And I’m here now. Look, you don’t need to surround yourself with people you can’t be sure of, especially not now.” Storm tried to pace, but the hotel’s short hallways was too small for much of that. “So send her home and let’s spend some time as a family for once.” “No.” It was what she always wanted. He had to have misheard that. “What?” Chris Storm just stared at Tessa a moment. “It must be nice,” she said. “What do you mean?” Chris sounded lost. “To come around once in a blue moon, and be welcomed like hero. To get nothing but the best days, the fun, the ice cream and cake part of love and family, but never the work. Never the diapers, sicknesses, nightmares that need quieting, fights that need breaking up and tough questions like ‘When’s Daddy coming home?’ at bedtime.” Tessa sighed and added, “Come by tomorrow. The kids are enjoying their fun with me and Tempest. “ “But tomorrow I have to …” “Tomorrow you still have to be here for your kids and me,” Tessa said softly. She reached out and squeezed his hand before turning to the living room and leaving him to decide on his own what to do next.
