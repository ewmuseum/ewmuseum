Learning to Accept, Ch. I

11 July 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07/11/10 Saturday, July 3rd, 2010 "We cannot change anything until we learn to accept it" - Carl Jung Have you ever woken up one morning only to find yourself questioning what you'd done the night before? At the time it seemed like a great idea but, looking back, it was completely and entirely the wrong choice. Imagine waking up one morning and having that feeling - except not about the last few nights... ...but the last ten years. That’s how I felt when I woke up this morning. When I opened my eyes, I felt a feeling of emptiness unlike anything I'd really ever felt before. The last several months had been particularly difficult for me, getting over the breakup with Rebecca and struggling to find a new job, so I'd become accustomed to feeling a bit off. But this morning was different. Significantly different. It was more than just feeling lost and alone - it was far greater than regret. I had felt as though the last decade of my life was nothing more than an absolute waste. Kind of ironic, wouldn't you say, as the last ten years were responsible for all my monetary wealth. But as they say, money can't buy happiness. And it doesn't. I'd felt like somewhere along the line, I missed the wagon; like there was some sort of Chariot to Happiness chugging along that I should've climbed aboard. To make it worse, it was the eve of our country's Independence and arguably one of the most significant days in American history. The Fourth of July was traditionally a time to rejoice and to celebrate life. Yet for me, it was nothing more than another excuse to drink get drunk. While I didn't usually admit it, I'd developed a drinking problem. I found myself coming up with literally any excuse possible to pour myself a glass of hard alcohol. It's raining out? Might as well drink. The sun is shining? Hey, let's drink. Shit, I even found myself toasting the sunset every night. I wanted to change. I wanted a real life...a happy life. I just wanted to escape the loop I'd be driving around in since the day I got behind the wheel. But the only way I knew how to ignite that change was by pouring a drink. As miserable as I had felt, I couldn't bring myself to sit in the house all night by myself - especially on the eve of the Fourth. I brought myself to throw on a pair of ragged old jeans and a stained white t-shirt. I looked anything but a stud. But I didn't need to look good. I was headed to the number one ranked dive bar in the state of New Jersey. A sad claim to fame, yes, but still a true one. By the time the sun fell, I found myself sitting at a bar stool in Jack n' Bills in Seaside Heights. And yes, I was toasting the sunset. "Haven't seen you in a while," the bartender said with a smile on his face. It didn't immediately click who he was and I portrayed that in the way I stared at him. Recognizing my uncertainty, he answered before I could ask, "Jordan. We met damn near a year ago." I smirked and nodded my head, acknowledging that I knew him. Truth is - I still had no clue. "Cuervo Black and Cola, right?" And that's when it hit me. It'd been almost a year to the day since I'd last been inside Jack n' Bills and for good reason. The last time I'd been here was after my falling out with Zacharia Taylor and - coincidentally - before my final encounter with Jordan White. That's what made me so uneasy about the guy at the time - his shared similarities with White. "You've got a good memory," I noted. "But the last time I was here, I recall that mix not ending up well." Jordan chuckled, understanding my reference to the fight I had gotten in with a couple of punks before I left the bar. "Well you won't have to worry about those kids around here anymore," he said. "Half of them were locked up a few months ago." "For doing what," I inquired. "Gang busts in Asbury Park," he muttered as he pulled the Cuervo Black up from underneath the bar. "Apparently there was a ton of trafficking going on under the Asbury Park Boardwalk and the State Police finally got wind of it. Not before long, you saw a lot of regulars disappearing." I couldn't help but laugh and shake my head, "Regulars?" "You don't get named the number one dive bar in the state because you've got a bunch of wealthy suits as your clientele," he joked. Jordan slid a Cuervo Black and Cola in front of me, despite my wishes against it. The bar was hardly crowded yet, so he pulled a stool up and decided he'd keep me company. "Thanks," I said politely. "You bet, bud." I took a sip from the glass and my eyes cringed. The taste of dark tequila racing down my throat was just about the last thing I wanted to feel. But beyond the sting, it tasted pretty good. "So what's bothering you," Jordan asked in more of a statement? I raised an eyebrow, "a guy can't have a drink?" "Not at this place, he can't. If you're in here, you've got something fucked up going on in your place. You're pregnant, you got your bitch pregnant, you're broke, somebody died, or life just plain sucks." "Well," I said with hesitation. What is it about bartenders that make us feel comfortable enough to trust them with our entire life story? Have you ever wondered that? "I guess I'd go with the last one." I took another sip of my Cuervo Black, awaiting a witty response. But Jordan wasn't the type to give witty responses. As a matter of fact he sympathized. "Sometimes life gets the best of you," he said in a comforting tone. "Fact is, you've just got to change it." It was a phrase I've heard a thousand times before. 'If you're not happy with your life, then change it.' It was incredibly easy to say, but I couldn't quite figure out how to actually go about doing it. "How," I shouted unintentionally. "How am I supposed to change it? I've tried time and again to turn things around but I always end up back in the same direction." I grew flustered, "I just don't think you can change it. This is the life I was dealt." "Exactly," Jordan commented enthusiastically. I rolled my eyes up at him, wondering if he'd even heard my mini-rant, "Exactly?" "We cannot change anything until we learn to accept it," he said. "This is absolutely the life you were dealt and you can absolutely transform it into whatever way you please. The key isn't wanting change, it's creating change." I stared at him with a blank face, "that really doesn't make any sense." I took another swig of my drink, polishing it off. Without my even asking, Jordan begins making me another. "Forget about your strengths in life," he went on. "Think about all of your weaknesses." I raised my eyebrows at him. "We all have weaknesses," he added. "Okay," I confirmed. "Changing your life is about embracing your weaknesses. It's about accepting them and accepting that they are part of you. Once you're able to do that, you'll be able to figure out a way in which your weaknesses can become strengths," he rambled as he slid me another Cuervo Black and Cola. "You're in a psychological battle with yourself." I nodded my head. I didn't say a word because, quite frankly, I had no idea what to say. As hard as it was for me to try and find a weakness in myself, I realized that what Jordan had said was absolutely correct. A chain is only as strong as its weakest link. "I just need to find my weakest link," I thought out-loud. Jordan smiled. "You've got it, bud. A chain is only as strong as its weakest link," he said as if he'd read my mind. I wasn't exactly sure what to do next, so I stood up and pounded my drink. I slammed the glass to the table and exclaimed, "My drinking has become a weakness. However in my drinking, I've learned that I have the ability to be persistent. It's shown me that I have a strong passion for something...just the wrong thing." Jordan just continued to wear his smile and nod his head. I reached for my wallet to pay him for the two drinks, "What do I owe you?" Jordan waved his hand. "Nothing," he said. "It's all covered." "No seriously," I argued. And believe me, I'm not normally one to argue about free drinks. Jordan pointed his finger across the bar at a young man who was seated with an attractive young girl. "See that guy?" I nodded curiously. "He told me the minute you walked through the door that anything you drank was on him." I looked across the bar at the kid and again hadn't the slightest clue who he was. In doing so, I caught his eye and he gave me a subtle wave. I bowed my head in response. "Who is that?" I asked. "Good decisions always ultimately pay off," Jordan answered. "He's the same kid you helped the last time you were in here." I smiled and suddenly remembered everything. This kid was being picked on by a couple of punks for looking at a girl they were with (and treating like shit). They tried shoving him around until I stepped in and stopped them. It was completely out of character for me to get involved in a stranger's business, but I couldn't stand by and watch this kid get pummeled three-on-one. As fate would have it, there he was - seated with that very same girl. It was in that moment I realized that I did have a heart. I did care. Most importantly, I could change my life. "My life," I whispered to Jordan, "is finally beginning to change for the better." He looked me in the eyes and dipped his head. "It's already begun." __________ Sunday, July 4th, 2010 "The measure of a man is what he does with power" - Pittacus It was the first time in a long time that I woke up and I felt refreshed. I'd become so used to waking up depressed and alone that to be in such a good mood felt SurReal. I couldn't decide if I felt so good because I wasn't hung-over or because I was eager to turn my life around. Either way, my run-in with Jordan opened my eyes a lot. He might've just been talking out of his ass, but the things he said really gave me a new perspective on life and the way we live it. I honestly couldn't wait to begin my complete transformation. Coincidentally, my transformation was to begin on the Fourth of July. Perhaps I'm looking too deep into it, but I felt as though that was symbolic. A century ago, my forefathers declared their independence from an entity that had done nothing but strangle the life from them. They sacrificed their lives to break free - and they succeeded. It was no different for me today. For so long, I'd suffered from an entity I could not yet identify. It's virtually killed me. Yet today, I'm free. I've broken away from its grasps. Today, I declare my own independence. Nightfall came quickly and before I knew it, I was sitting along the New Jersey shoreline with the sand crevassed between my toes. As the sun tucked itself away for the night, my hands were empty - I wasn't toasting to the sunset. The first fireworks began almost immediately upon dusk. As I stared off into the sky to watch beams of light explode, I had the opportunity to think clearly about the last several months and my whereabouts. When news broke that I joined the World Wrestling Alliance, many people were beside themselves. They couldn't fathom why a fame monster would want to throw himself in the mix with an independent organization that had a small following and virtually no television time (outside the occasional pay-per-view event). At the time, my intention was to stay under the radar. I wanted to be out of the spotlight for a little while, while I pieced my life back together. But keeping myself out of the spotlight was like taking a cube of dry ice, tossing it into a 16 oz. plastic soda bottle, mixing it with hot water, and sealing it up. It was only a matter of time before the cap blew off. The World Wrestling Alliance was filled with guys of heart and talent - but it didn't have my heart. It couldn't capture my heart. I was a lost puppy who merely found a new home and just didn't fit in. I remember calling Johnny right around the time I heard he was taking over GCW. At first I thought it was all a work, but once I heard the paperwork was actually filed - I was all over it. Johnny and Steven were two entirely different men who dealt with talent in two entirely different ways. Where Steven had the health of his company in mind, Johnny seemed to always have the health of his superstars in mind. Once I finally got a hold of Johnny, I spilled my guts to him. I asked him what I needed to do to get my shit back together. I asked him where I went wrong to end up here - in the minors. Johnny didn't have all the answers. As a matter of fact, he didn't have many answers at all. But what he did say stuck with me. I didn't quite understand it then, because I didn't have a clear head. Nevertheless, it all started to make sense. Johnny said to me to me that in my final days in GCW, Steven Caldera was testing me. By hiring me as the head dog in The Establishment, he was running me through the final leg of his gauntlet. He admits that Caldera had every intension to let his company ride on the shoulders of Chris Bagwell. Caldera had said to Johnny that, "the measure of a man is what he does with power." And, in my case, the power got to my head. Chris Bagwell broke. As the fireworks continued to explode, I couldn't help but think of Jordan's words. As I left the bar last night, he told me that my change had already begun. And he was right - it began the day I recognized my first weakness. It began when I agreed to rejoin GCW and start from the bottom-up. I just got back up a helluva lot faster than anyone had expected.
