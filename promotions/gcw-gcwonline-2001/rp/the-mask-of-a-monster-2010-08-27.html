The Mask of a Monster

27 August 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/27/10 I. Dominic’s heart pounds inside of his chest, threatening to tear through his body. Sweat pours from his body, especially from his face and hands. His eyes are open wider than normal, unblinking. Not even when the sweat drips down into them does he blink, he hardly acknowledges the burning sensation. His mouth is dry and he can taste a hint of the bile that is trying to force its way up his throat. His hands adjust as he tries to get a better grip on his weapon, his right hand slides back over the trigger and his left tightens around the hand guards on the barrel of the rifle. Wall takes a tentative step forward, his eyes locked on what has happened in front of him. With each step, Dominic’s world seems to lose more focus. What was once reality now seems like a distant memory or a movie. With each step he felt more and more as if he were becoming lost inside of himself. Like he was transforming into something else entirely. By the time he reached his destination, only a few feet ahead, he couldn’t hear anything besides his thumping heartbeat. His eyes look down, admiring his work. The man was sprawled out face down in the sand. Blood poured from the wounds to his chest and head, making the sand around him into some kind of crimson mud puddle. Dominic points the muzzle of his rifle towards the downed man, probably dead but just in case, before he slowly kneels down next to him. Once down on one knee, Dominic removes one hand from his own weapon and reaches down to grab the pistol once held by the attacker and tosses it out of reach. He looks over the body and sees no signs of life, no breathing, no crying, no shivers. He’s dead. Dominic killed him. The thought was somewhat sobering, momentarily taking his mind to a place it couldn’t afford to be in the place that he currently was. This thought brought him back to reality. At least this reality. He goes back to work, searching the body for weapons, chargers, detonators, anything. He finds something. Something more damaging to him than any weapon would’ve been. A wallet. Dominic pulls the man’s wallet from his back pocket and unfolds it. Immediately the bile comes back but this time it doesn’t hold back and rushes to the surface and projects from Dominic’s mouth, covering the shoulders and back of the head of the dead man. It takes a few moments for everything to come out and Dominic to compose himself again. He takes in a few deep breaths and shakes his head. Inside the wallet was only one thing. A Picture. Kids. This man’s children. The man was not in the pictures with the kids but he just KNEW they were his. He realizes now that he never got a good look at him, the man he killed that is. He considers turning him over to look him in those blank, emotionless eyes but he reconsiders. He doesn’t want to. He can’t do it. He’ll just call him something, anything. Perhaps, The Man with no Face. Instead he folds the picture up and places it inside one of his cargo pockets. Then suddenly, his body became weak and he crumbles to the sand in a seated position. It is not long before the tears come. It is not long after that when another thought comes, this won’t be the last time he will have to do this. He will be asked to leave more children without parents …. Or parents without children. What he can’t understand is why he even cares. They are evil. They want to kill him. A real soldier wouldn’t give a shit! Then another realization, one much more frightening hits him like a ton of bricks. Maybe, just maybe… He liked it. II. “Wake the hell up.” Roberto’s voice forces Dominic awake, somehow he had fallen asleep while waiting for his turn in the sparring ring. His breathing is erratic, sweat beads have formed on his forehead and Roberto quickly notices that there are tears sitting in the corners of Dominic’s eyes, holding on for dear life so that they don’t spill down his cheeks. Of course Roberto is concerned but he chooses not to address it now. “Is this how much you give a shit about winning and losing, Dom?” It takes a little time for Dominic to fully recover from the dream. Roberto’s words reach him but they sound more like the words of an adult from the Charlie Brown cartoons. He takes in a deep breath and closes his eyes to wipe the slate clean, to slow his beating heart. He shakes his head and opens his eyes to the image of Roberto holding out his hand to help Dominic up. Roberto forcefully yanks Wall up to his feet. “Get your shit together,” Knight shakes his head in disapproval. “You‘re up Let‘s see if we can‘t knock some of that sleepiness out of you.” Dominic nods and moves to the ring where he slides in and stands with his back against the red corner. A young kid, younger than Dominic’s 23 years, slides in front and wraps Dominic’s hands before slipping on a pair of black MMA gloves. Dominic really doesn’t feel like doing this. His mind is still focused on his dream, his nerves are rattled. It is only by instinct that he walks to the center of the ring to meet his sparring partner. To him, it feels like this could be the dream. What if he is still hovering over his faceless enemy and tormenter? Is this all some illusion? He checks an attempted leg kick. It’d make sense to him, that this wasn’t real. He has felt more like he is on the right path now than he has in years. Roberto has been good to him, steady work has now come and he seems to be getting better at it. With enough focus and dedication, the sky is the limit. He blocks a punch. But no matter how many steps forward that Dominic appears to take, there is this hand that comes out and yanks him backwards. He can’t explain it, it just happens. He would love nothing more than to rid of himself of all this anger inside like a snake shedding its dead skin. Dominic is a good kid, mostly. He stuffs a takedown and gets back up to his feet. It is times like these when Dominic misses the guidance of his foster mother the most. She would know what to say and how to make things right. She was his strongest link to humanity. He let her down though. Wasn’t there for her when she needed him like she was always there for him. He let her slip away … he was a murderer before the faceless one. The anger builds. He strikes his partner to the body with a powerful kick. Maybe there is no fighting it. Maybe this is what he was always meant to be. Violent. Brutal. A Killer. It could’ve been just laying dormant inside of him and once it got the opportunity to show its face, it do so in a very fantastic way. Can you fight destiny? Shouldn’t someone just give in? Another kick sends Dominic’s partner to the mat, Dom hovers over him. People could fear him, he knows he can make it happen. He could be relentless in his pursuit of blood. He could make people cry in agony. He could pound them into submission until they wouldn’t even recognize their own reflection in the mirror anymore. He could smile while doing it. He could laugh, hysterically even. The sight of the crimson liquid gushing from wounds could excite him. He could dominate. His thoughts are interrupted by a team of people tackling him to the mat. It realizes how tired his right arm is. He looks to his left and then to his right, finding the worried gaze of Roberto Abitua. Dominic is having trouble breathing. He looks at his sparring partner. Bloodied, broken, unconscious He didn’t even know what he was doing. He didn’t know who he was. III. The hot water crashes down onto his head when he turns the shower on. Dominic feels sick to his stomach and in the back of his throat he can taste the vomit from his dream. He has been fighting since he left the Army to escape the person that he was forced to turn into. There are days, hours, moments when it feels like that other person has disappeared completely, melting away into the ground at his feet and never to be heard from again. It is always a false sense of security. One dream, one event to trigger a bad memory and all of those emotions flood in as if a dam has burst inside of Dominic’s mind. It makes him feel cold and detached and it gives him the feeling like he could do anything to anyone and not feel remorse. He hardly knows what he is doing until it is too late as if his body goes into autopilot. He becomes a weapon. He is beginning to figure out that once you turn from a someone to a something, you never fully get that out of your system. A normal person cannot appreciate nor understand. The day you take another person’s life is the moment that you cease to exist as you were. The ads promoting the Army don’t have that anywhere though, not even in the fine print. Maybe Dominic will not be able to keep it hidden forever, the monster lurking inside of him, but he won’t stop fighting it. He can’t, can he? What would he become here? In the real world? Becoming a monster while in a war zone is similar to wild, vicious animals running through the jungle with no civilization around. Being a monster here? No, the world has enough evil in it without me adding to it. No matter how far the mask tries to slip away he knows he has to continue to put it back in its place, hide the scaly skin and sharp fangs that is hidden underneath it all. A shower helps. The hot, scolding water refreshes him. Dominic closes his eyes and lets it wash over him and he can feel some of the hate and blood flow down his bare skin and to the shower floor, creating a puddle at his feet before storming down the drain. Finally he is able to get his breathing back to normal, his heart rate drops to a normal level. The mask of humanity slides back into place and the world doesn’t seem as distorted. Dominic collects his thoughts. Roberto will want to know what was wrong. He’d want to apologize to the poor sap he just mangled. He has some healing to do. When Dominic feels settled enough he turns the water off and steps out of the shower. He snatches the towel hanging from a hook near him and wraps it around his waist as he heads to the sink. His heart rate picks up slightly when he thinks about seeing his reflection in the mirror above the sink. To avoid it he lowers his head and goes right for the knobs to turn the cold water on and cups his hands beneath the running water and drinks it. He takes a couple gulps before shutting the water down. He swallows hard and moves his head up, greeting his reflection. It doesn’t look like a monster. No scales, no fangs, no horns. Looks could be deceiving though. “I’ll beat this. I’m better than this.” Dominic hears his response in the form of a low growl from somewhere deep inside of him. Or maybe it was a chuckle. No matter, he’ll fight it until it kills him. This leads him to another thought. What did the Man with No Face see before he died? The Monster … or the Man? Is there even a difference?
