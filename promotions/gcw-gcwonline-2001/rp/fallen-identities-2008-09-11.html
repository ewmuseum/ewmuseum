Fallen Identities

11 September 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 11, 2008 "Death does not have a final say on identities, Only if a body is taken and when." The rain from outside tumbling against the tall cathedral roof of the church sends an echoing reverb of light strikes throughout the church. The continuous sound puts Taylor into a trans, propelling him into a deeper state of thought. The church was dark and desolate, flickering with the candlelight from the prayer candles and the fixtures on the walls. The shadow of Taylor’s body dances on the wall next to a portrait of Jesus Christ kneeling down praying next to his own tomb. In what seems like days, only twelve hours has passed since the police have left. The repeated knocks of passer-bys and other pastors has disrupted his sleep, so he decided to stay awake and ponder the possibilities of Jake Fenning as being an actual possibility. The police won’t know the identity of the attacker by way of DNA sample for quite some time, having to send he sample out to a lab. The murder isn’t high profile by their standards, so the priority of the blood sample is somewhere down around a fender bender on the other side of town. This buys Taylor more time to find out the source of the picture as well as the true identity of the killer, being Jake Fenning or not. Taylor takes out the Modern Testament from the pew in front of him and flips through to the chapter on virtue and the afterlife. The image at the start of the chapter is in black and white like the rest of the book, but the artist rendering of what will happen to the soul is not he stereotypical drawing you would find in a religious text. The body is gruesomely disfigured, looking as if the body was severely beaten, and the soul is rising up out of the chest not with a smile, but with a disturbing look of disappointment. Most images Taylor has seen around other religious texts show the body lifeless, but unharmed, and the soul in an uplifting spirit traveling through rays of light into the gates of Heaven. Zacharia lays the book on the space next to him, giving way to his gaze of where Father Emanuel lay in his last moments alive. The blood stained carpet in the center aisle plays with his imagination, placing a more disfigured Emanuel where his body once laid. Zacharia shakes his head to try and clear the image from his mind, but it only makes it worse by adding an animated Father Emanuel in a mystified essence walking towards him. The figure looks to be unmarked, unlike the body, yet it is extremely disappointed. His lip is hung, his eyes baggy and cruel, and his face drooping with sadness. Zacharia stands up and yells inaudible words at the figure, but the figure walks closer to Taylor. “Leave me alone! What do you want of me?” The ghostly figure lifts his hand and points, ever so emotionless, towards Zacharia’s pocket. He looks down and reaches the picture out of his pocket, staring at the image of a once happy family with a potentially jealous murderer behind them. Zacharia looks up from the picture and falls backwards after seeing the image of Father Emanuel within inches of his face. He crawls backwards towards the wall as the figure pursuits. “I will find him! I will get him, just please, leave me in peace Father!” Father Emanuel stops, slowly gliding backwards for a few seconds, and then stops. Zacharia gets to his feet and looks at the picture for brief second once more. He looks up at the figure and immediately feels saddened, but also angry by the fact that we was not able to save his mentor. Zacharia started to shed tears down is cheeks for the first time in years. The ghastly figure nods its head and then full on charges Zacharia, causing him to let out a blood curling scream. Zacharia opens his eyes wide, expecting to see Father Emanuel over him, but instead he sees the tall ceiling of the priest’s quarters. He slowly reaches off his chest and onto the bed that he is laying on. He pans his head right and left very slowly, expecting anything and everything, but only sees the walls and habitance of the quarters. He sits up, looking all around him. Reaching down to his naked chest, Zacharia grabs the cross around his neck and silently says a prayer. In is silence, he asks God for forgiveness for not being able to get to Father Emanuel sooner, and for failing to subdue the attacker. He prays for the power to withstand the wound on his leg and to find the murderer. He ends the prayer with the sign of the cross. He stands in the center of the room, looking around at all of the different items littering he walls and floor. He starts to clean up the room, taking down articles that he finds insignificant, putting away religious symbols that he cannot make peace with, and turns the room into something a little more simple, but relative to himself. What he is used to is sleeping on a bus or plane going to a new venue, or sleeping on a park bench, he was not used to waking up in a bed in private room. As he moves things around, he starts to think about when he used to spend most of his life inside the church and sleeping behind the alter late at night. He thinks about how simple things were back then until he moveshis notebook onto the desk, revealing the picture laying on the floor. Out of anger he wants to tear it to shreds, but out of sympathy of his parents, he wants to cherish it long hours at a time. This is all he has left of his parents, and he only received it but several hours prior to this very moment. Staring into the eyes of he innocent Zachary Taylor, he loses control and starts to cry uncontrollably. He falls to the chair in front of the desk and sulks, holding onto the picture. He is unsure whether it was seeing himself or seeing his parents which triggered the outburst, but he does know that everyone in the world that has ever cared about him is now dead. In this moment of sorrow, the image of Chris Bagwell comes to mind and he punches the desk at the very thought of that monster. Even though he was a monster, he still thinks how it would be nice to be consoled in some way by someone who would be there for him. He stands up and wipes the tears away from his eyes. He clears his throat of sorrow and grabs his hoodie off of the chair, grabs a handful of cash out of his bag, takes the notebook off of his desk and goes out the door. He throws he money in his back pocket and puts on the hoodie, putting the hood over his head, casting his face in slight shadow. Zacharia goes out the front wooden door and turns around to lock it. He stands for a moment in the archway of the entrance and looks out into the rain cascading down into the streets of Clifton. A couple cars fly past, splashing massive amounts of water onto the sidewalk. Zacharia is about to walk out into the rain, but a police car pulls up to the church and an officer gets out, signaling for him to stay under the protection of the archway. The police officer is one of the men that he spoke to the night before about Father Emanuel’s death. “Can I help you officer?” “I was just swinging by to make sure everything was ok since we left last night.” Zacharia looks at him and sees his genuine concern, so he replies with a smile. “Everything alright?” “Yes, sir. It has just been a long night and I am trying to clear my head.” “I understand. We are going to be sending a car past here every now and again to make sure the man doesn’t come by again. If you would like to be moved into a?” “Thank you, sir, but I think that I will be alright. I don’t want to go out and start a whole new life, I am very content on staying here. I highly doubt that he is stupid enough to come back to the scene of a crime again.” As these final words pass through his mouth, his hand tightens around the notebook which contained the picture inside. No, he wouldn’t come back, but he is not hesitating sending a messenger over. “Alright, Mr. Taylor. Even though I agree with what you say, we are still going to be sending a car over anyway just to be sure. The last thing that we want is more harm to be caused to you.” With the officer’s kind words, he was gone just like that. He ran off to his car and jumped in before becoming soaked. Taylor looks over both sides of the street, not seeing a taxi, he steps out into the rain. He puts the notebook under his sweatshirt and heads down the block. He walks aimlessly down the street, until a taxi pulls up next to him and the driver yells for him to get in. He responds with the motion of getting into the back of the car and shakes off the wetness and cold on his body. He looks up into the rear-view mirror and stares into the eyes of the driver, nodding to acknowledge the courtesy that he provided. “Where are you headed buddy” Free of charge if its somewhere close. It is the least I can do for a man stuck in the rain.” Zacharia ponders the question for a few minutes. He really did not know where he was going, he didn’t put any thought in it at all. He was looking for a cab, but never thought of where he wanted to take the ride to. He fingers the notebook under his clothing and thinks of a destination. “Can you take me to Oak Ridge Road” 125 OakRidge?” “Sure can my man, but that is quite some hike. You were going to walk all the way there?” “This should cover it.” Zacharia throws a fifty into the driver’s hands. The driver turned around and began to drive. “Thank you sir, it will more than cover it. I will give you the change once we get there.” “No, thank you sir, I really appreciate your troubles of picking me up. Just take me where I need to go, and if I owe you more, let me know.” The driver turns off the “On Duty” light above the cab and continues down the road, turning on occasion. Taylor slouches down in the seat and closes his eyes, imagining Oak Ridge Road when he was a child, but nothing comes to his mind except the image of the burnt down houses surrounding him as he cried in the ashes of his destroyed house. He lived at 150 Oak Ridge from the time he was born until the time of his parents? death. He never went on that side of the city since his long walk away from his home. As the car drives down the main and side roads, he finds it amazing that, as a child, he had once traveled these many miles across town to get to the church. Time was suspended, slowed, and hastened that night as he walked the streets of Clifton, but he never imagined that he had traveled this far. Nothing even seemed remotely familiar until the cab turned onto Van Houton Road. He recalls calling it Van Houston when he was younger, his father always playfully correcting him. He knows they were close because he does not remember much, but he does remember driving Van Houton many times. The car turns onto Clifton Ave and then makes a quick turn onto Saint James Place, which turns into Oak Ridge. The taxi traveled slowly, watching his GPS on the dashboard, showing that the house was just up the way. Several of the houses seemed familiar, but he wasn’t very sure if they were houses that he used to go to or not, not until he saw very large white Victorian style house on a bend. The house is very clean and very much so perfect from the lawn to the condition of the house. The striking image of the house disturbs Zacharia, but he had no idea why. About another 30 seconds up the road and the cab stops in front of five identical two story houses, one boastfully showing that it was 150. The house is nothing even remotely close to the house that once stood there with the same number on the front porch. The only thing that remains the same as it did those many years is the tree that is off to the right side of the property. He can still see the carving of his name into the bark that is facing the street. Zacharia holds up one finger to the driver to tell him to wait, and gets out of the car. The rain has slowed to a very slow drizzle, making it respectable conditions to be able to stand without any covering. Zacharia takes off his hood and walks up to the tree with a hand outstretched, anticipating the smooth surface of the carved bark. The rugged letters “Z.T.” still remain in the gigantic tree. The identity that is in this tree died with the house behind it, but the history still remains. He reaches out along he side of the tree, feeling for more carvings, but feels nothing. He takes two steps back and looks down the street both ways, noticing that the five houses which were identical were all much newer than any other house along the street. He turns around and looks directly across the street at the tall yellow house in which he took shelter so many years ago. He walked over to the house and rang the bell. “Hello” Who’s there?” “Hi, ma’am, I was just wondering if I could ask you a few questions.” “I am sorry, but I do not talk to solicitors or traveling religious representatives.” “No, ma’am, I am here to ask you a few questions about who used to live across the street.” At the mention of this, the door opens up and an old women came out, fixed in position by a small cane. The women had not changed a bit since the last time Zacharia saw her. She clearly id not recognize him, but that was all for the better. “How do you do, ma’am” I wanted to know if you remember who lived across the street, before these tall houses were built.” “Well, young sir, I remember in fact. It was such a tragedy, it was. See, there was a terrible fire that started in that house, the one just there. The fire was so well fueled, well, I think you can imagine what happen. It took all five houses with them in the dead of the night, yes it did.” “That is tragic, ma’am, but what about the people who lived there?” “Oh, poor souls. Out of all five houses, six lives were claimed. Three of which were in the house right across the street at 150, yes, poor souls indeed.” “You say three perished?” “Yes, sir. The child fell out of the window in a panic, dying on impact they say, but he was brought to my house, poor soul. He was slightly burnt, but the fall must have killed him, God rest. The EMT’s must have taken him out with the other hurt children, because when the morning came, he was no longer there.” Zacharia shakes his head as if he were truly saddened by the story, but it is out of he misfortune that nobody ever clarified what happen to him in the morning. They would have noticed him not there, but out of all the confusion with the fire, they must have thought that the child was with the burn victims and perished n the fire. This women was clearly not asked what happen to the young Zachary. “You say there were others killed, who else was among them?” “Well, his parents of course were killed, God rest, but their neighbors were killed on both sides, killing both couples, God rest. It was a tragic day, yes it was.” “That truly is sad, ma’am. Thank you for recalling these tragic memories, but I do have one last question.” “Sure young man, what is it that I can help you with?” The women’s face perked up, probably expecting something a little less grim, but in fact, Taylor wanted to now about the Fenning’s. “I recall hearing bout a family that was really close to those which lived on this street, I was hoping that maybe you could help me with finding them. The name was Fenning.” “Oh dear, they live right down the street, great people they were. Tragic as well though.” ? Tragic? How so?” “Oh, my, we have been plagued with many sad events on this street, God rest them all. Their son was driving around town one day, when he was hit by a car, pushing him off the overpass and onto the highway. Oh, poor soul. That Jacob was such a sweet boy.” Hearing his name sent chills down his back. He was right, the man in the picture was Jake, but how could he have received the picture the other night, if he were dead? “Ma’am, that truly is sad, but when did this happen?” “Oh, it must have been about five years ago” The poor family was devastated, especially after losing the Taylor’s. They were their best friends! The Eulogy at the funeral was written by Mrs. Fenning. I never remember hearing such a heartfelt speech.” Zacharia wanted to fall to his knees. Hearing someone talk of his family makes him weak in the knees, but hearing about the funeral makes him extremely sad. He looks over his shoulder at what used to be his house once again, and then thanks the women for her time. She smiles, nods, then walks off and closes the door. Zacharia grabs hold of the banister on the porch and supports himself from falling. Jake is dead. His family is dead. Now Father Emanuel lies dead. If Jake did kill Father Emanuel, there must have been a mistake with Jake’s accident, just like there was with Zacharia’s presumed death. If Jake really did want to be like him, he must have found out that he was still alive, and faked his death. This seems so impossible, but who else would have given him he picture? It seems more unlikely that Jake is the killer now, but the issue of he picture troubled him. Zacharia gets back into the cab and asks to go to the library. The cab takes a matter of minutes and then lets Zacharia out. He turns towards the driver and throws him another twenty. “Thank you very much sir for waiting for me. I am going to spend some time in here, so there is no need to wait. Thank you very much.” Zacharia turns towards the library and enters through the door. He immediately goes up to the receptionist and asks where the periodicals were and the archived newspapers were. She points him in the right direction and he starts to walk towards a computer below the sign of archives. He starts by typing in he name Fenning, but far too many results came up, ranging from best kept lawn to donations. He types in Jake Fenning, but nothing came up. He tries Jacob Fenning, and three articles are listed. He opens the first one entitled, “Fire in Clifton, Leaving 7 Dead”. There was a statement in there from the Fennings, one from Jacob Fenning saying “I am going to miss my best friend.” Zacharia starts to get chocked up, but then he says to himself that this was a past life. The second article was listed as, “High School Grad Accomplishes More,” which, after reading, talked about how Jacob Fenning “reached the stars and then grabbed the sun in his academic and athletic triumphs.” Images of a different life flash in front of Zacharia’s eyes of him and Jake running track and graduating on top of their class, but again, that is a past life. The last article as entitled, “Car Accident Kills Local Teenager” which went into detail bout how the accident happen: “A local teenager, graduating from Clifton High, was killed in a tragic car accident in the early hours of the night last night. After running stop sign, the car was plowed on the driver side and sent off the overpass onto Route 46. Upon impact of he highway, the car was struck by several cars, bursting the car into flames. The body of 21 year old Jacob Fenning was burnt beyond recognition, but was said to have perished upon impact of the first car on the side street. The car was registered under Sean Fenning of Clifton. After interviewed, he said that the car was his son’s. He also stated that his son said he was going out for a drive to clear his head, but never returned home after several hours?” Zacharia read this, disturbed, but also convinced that Jake perished in the car accident. There is no way to get into a car accident like that and fake it. Angry at the fact that he now no longer has any leads, Zacharia leaves the library and flags down the closest cab and asks to be brought back to the church. He pulls out his keys to unlock the door to the church, but realizes that the door is already open. He is not used to the practice of locking the door after he leaves. As he goes into the church, he locks the door behind him. The click of the lock resonates through the church, amplified by the cathedral ceilings. The walk to the back of the building is a long, quiet walk, but nothing is more disturbing than looking down, seeing that deep red stain in the center isle. He takes the notebook and the rest of the money out of his pocket and puts them in his left hand, as he opens the door to the priest quarters. He places his belongings on the desk and walks off to the bathroom. Upon return he sits in his desk chair and puts his head down into his hands. His concentration is disturbed by the shifting to the side of him. Before he can lift his head all the way erect, a newspaper drops down in front of him. The sudden disturbance throws him off his chair and onto the ground. A tall gentlemen stands tall in front of him with a smile on his face. He was about five foot eight and of average build and weight. He stood there wearing a black button up shirt, half open, with an undershirt exposed. His pants were black pinstriped, with a black belt and black shoes. The only contrast to his very dark appearance was his light brown hair. He reaches out a hand to help Zacharia off the ground, but Zacharia moves backwards avoiding his hand like the plague. “Zacharia, take my hand and assistance to help you up.” “I’What’How?” “Very articulate, son, now take my hand and get to your feet.” Zacharia takes his hand and gets to his feet. The man has a very firm grip, much more of a rough hand than he is used to. His hands remind him of? his own. “Who are you?” “Ah, much more articulate. Father Emanuel only brings out the best in his students.” “You know Father Emanuel” But how?” “Another fine question, Zacharia. I was his student.” The sarcasm in his voice was starting to annoy Zacharia very much so. He got on the defensive and got past the fact that he was just surprised to see someone in the church. “You didn’t answer my question before, who are you?” The man smiles and walks over to the desk, grabs pen and the newspaper. The main headline is “Christianity Under Fire, Leaving a Pastor Dead,” and the subtitle is “The old cathedral church off of Saint Thomas Blv lays in tragic dismay after an armed robber takes the money and life of its pastor, Father Emanuel.” The man with the pen circles two words on the paper and gives it to Zacharia. “Christian Thomas?” The man smiles and nods. Christian from Christianity and Thomas from Saint Thomas Blv. “You sure are quick, Zacharia. But what you misunderstand is my intention?” “Well, then explain yourself Mr. Thomas.” “Father, actually. I am a priest.” “I don’t care who you are, but you better leave or tell me your intentions.” “My intentions are to take what my mentor left. I have come to help you and run the church.” “I do not need your help?” “You need me to find a Jacob Fenning I think?”
