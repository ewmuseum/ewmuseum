The Blood-dimmed Tide is Loose

14 October 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/14/10 Things fall apart; the centre cannot hold; Mere anarchy is loosed upon the world, The blood-dimmed tide is loosed, and everywhere The ceremony of innocence is drowned. - W. B. Yeates. One The strain ignited a spark in the tinderbox of a stressed synapse. It fizzled through the wiry weave of easily excitable neurons, burning branching bridges of axons. Your brimming brain cells bristled with electricity, waving to one another with wavering membrane potential. Amidst the frenzy of illuminating impulses, an undiscovered, benign tumor sat heavy, like a cerebral black hole around which the galaxy of twinkling synaptic stars spiraled. As the blood vessels in your eyes began to burst and a thin trickle of sweet crimson ran from your left nostril, fraying dendrites lashed around the squirming Rasa as it tried to retreat deeper into your diseased brain. Restrained, and with the spiking current of a million neurons triggering paralyzing its malevolent grip, you had rendered Rasa helpless; for as long as you could remain conscious. “We need to talk.” Two Draped in a powder-blue prison jumpsuit, Russell Greer was in the process of licking his thin lips when correctional officer Snelling gave him a hefty shove to the back. Teeth nicking tongue, a stinging stab-wound trickled blood into Greer’s mouth; his cuffs rattled when a hand went to instinctively pat his lips dry. “Forgot about those, did we, champ?” Snelling snorted. “Don’t you fret, though… we’re releasing you into your natural habitat any minute now.” Russell Greer’s fists clenched, stretching pale, scarred skin across his knuckles; skin scarred by the teeth of his late wife. Bloody smile painted across his razor-featured, rodent-like face, Russell looked up at the hollering galleries as he - un-spoilt meat - was paraded in front of his fellow in-mates. “Betcha wish you’d just asked wifey twice, huh?” Three The salty air blowing off the pacific tickled the tip of your nose. Every revitalizing lungful purified your blood while the tender kiss of the setting sun blushed your cheeks with color. You closed your eyes; a blissful smile settling naturally across your lips. Over the soft purr of the Camero’s engine, distant chitter-chatter of seabirds pulled your mind from the car, against the foamy wash of the waves, and out onto the open ocean. For a few seconds, the sturdy cushion of the car’s passenger seat became the fluid massage of the warm waters licking at the peachy horizon. Your mind wandered to calmer places, nowadays. Floating up on the breeze rather than swilling down into the sewers. “Mommy!” hooked your ear and hastily reeled you back to the shore. “Daddy! Daddy, look!” Her voice brimmed with an excitement which the world cruelly stole away as its fare from youth to adulthood; the boatman must be paid. No explanations to dispel the raw magic of the world surrounding her, no words yet to describe what it was that she saw through innocent eyes. “You’re not looking!” she whined, nose pressed down upon the top of her half-open window. “Why aren’t you looking?” The pleading, frantic horn drowned out her last words as the superheated air between tire and asphalt released a pent-up, piercing scream. As the thundering semi ploughed into the flank of the Camero, your tired eyes burst open. “Fuck.” The clumps of grass clogging up your wiper blades smeared heavy swathes of rain-sodden mud down the windshield. The rickety steering column winced with a strained squeal as you fought the wheel, bones shuddering to the point of splinter through shot suspension. Your headlights merely gave opaque substance to the blanket of dirty rain and grass which lashed across the your path. As you came to a concussive halt, the fraying seatbelt’s cerated weave hacked though your shoulder as your body, a slave to turbulent momentum, was propelled forward. Breathless, chewing on vacuum, your head flopped back against its rest. Your bones ached through to their marrow, and your stiffened neck’s muscles felt as if they were being torn violently apart, fiber by fiber. Your eyes strained to peer through the dirt-smeared windshield to the tree-trunk buffer beyond, their lids sparing them the dry ache by bringing the shutters down on the outside world. The salty lick of the ocean’s tide tickled between your toes as the baking sun erupted sweat from your every pore. Four How long do you really think you can keep this up? Rasa lashed out, clawing at the inside of your skull and carving deep trenches through which the poison of its infectious words could flow. I’d give it a minute - minute-and-a-half, tops - before those bloodshot eyes burst in your skull or a blood vessel in this primitive little brain of yours goes pop. “Hurt, much?” It unleashes a hollow cackle which echoes through your psyche. When you pass out, I’m pouring a kettle of boiling water over your hand; I just wanted you to know that. Or maybe I’ll just throw you down a flight of stairs, hurl you through a window, then walk you in front of a bus. Oh, and just so you know? No. It doe- “I need your help.“ Your voice plummeted past icy and settled in the dead tone of absolute zero. “I need to stop fighting you; at least until I get one thing done.” Interesting, Rasa hissed; the flicking of its serpent’s tongue tickled. Through the lasso of dendrites and rapidly firing synapses, Rasa saw the shadow of benign, mutated tissue. Very interesting. “Russell Greer.” His name has to be peeled from your tongue so deep are the vitriolic barbs attached to those three syllables. Oh, I liiiiiked him, Rasa goaded. Your skull felt as if it were about to splinter and implode as you tightened the synaptic, electrified bonds around Rasa. Despite its protests, you heard it stream within your diseased brain. Syrupy droplets of blood began to drip off your left earlobe. “April; that fucking piece of shit killed her - beat her to death - and you helped him.” The strain forced your words out through grinding teeth, their roots pulsing a dull ache throughout your gums and into your jaw. “I was meant to protect her, and I owe it to April to make his last moments as violent as hers.” So very noble, it scoffed, repulsed by the sickly taste of human emotion. But what’s in it for me, I wonder? “Ending the miserable life of Russell Greer is the only thing I have left to do.” Your voice spoke with the emptiness such a statement demanded. “Once the last breath is beaten out of him, I won’t put up a fight… I’m too tired. You can have this body.” Hmmmmmm, A maddening buzz besieged your senses as Rasa pondered from within its dendrite manacles. And if I decide I’m having too much fun as things are, and just want to keep it casual? You answered by pressing the barrel of the Beretta M9 up into your soft palate. Through the haziness of the buzzing electrical impulses encircling him, Rasa peered back into a fading memory of a bullet being fed to the pistol’s hungry chamber. I think we have a deal. Five You looked down on the city as a swollen, nuclear sunset engulfed the horizon. The shadows carved features deeper into your scarred face as a floaty, evening breeze purged the stuffy, smoggy heat from the air. Your eyes winced as they peered between the silhouetted skyscrapers, senses overloaded by the blinding, terracotta plasma. By the time blotches of fluorescent color had begun to dance around behind your eyelids, you finally felt awake. Behind you, heavy footsteps ground gravel into dust as Paulo Cacia approached. All rounded edges and flat features, his bloated face was immune to twilight shadow. Around him clung a grey, pinstriped suit jacket, its buttons holding on by the last, fraying thread. As he stepped into the dying light, lavish jewellery - chains, pocket watch, earrings and rings - lit him up like a Tron suit. The colossal, gristly meatball of a man needlessly adjusted his tie, while beneath his tight-fitting jacket, a small reservoir of cold sweat collected in the small of his flabby back. “Do you know who I am?” you asked, turning to face him. It was not a face he remembered, but rather a mask of bruised and scarred, dull and lifeless skin stretched across your once familiar, fresh features. Paulo Cacia was nervous around you five years ago; today he was terrified. “Y-… yes,” he mumbled, permanently hoarse voice almost blown away on the breeze. “You, um, you look…” His nervy words hung off of his lips like a stray, phlegmy strand of saliva. A probing arch of your pierced eyebrows prompted him to suck it back up through his plump lips like a piece of spaghetti, and spit it out again. “You, er… look different.” “I grew up,” you bluntly told him. “But, I assure you, it’s still me.” Paula swallowed, his Adam’s apple bobbing underneath the stubbly folds of his jowly jaw. “Five years ago, I was told that I could come to you and ask for one favor,” you told Paulo Cacia, as if he needed reminding. “So, Paulo… I need a favor.” A lone bead of sweat broke ranks from his clammy forehead and trickled down into a bushy, graying eyebrow. “Hey, now, look…” he evaded, stretched jacket riding up as he raised his hands defensively. “That was a long time ago, kid. Things have changed a little bit, ya see? Now, I mean no disrespect, but… your… well, he ain’t around anymore, ya see?” “Oh, I see, Paulo,” you answered with piercing politeness. “I saw when I visited his grave for the last time. He is, I can confirm, very, very dead. But his word isn’t dead. I’m not dead. And, at this precise moment in time, you’re not dead. Ya see?” “What does this one favor entail?” Paulo enquired, bitterly. He asked himself what was it that you could possibly want. A good plastic surgeon to put the right side of your face back together just a tad more neatly? A dealer to be gently persuaded to waive the payment for whatever it was that cast the shadows across your eyes and dried your skin out? A corrupt landlord dangled out of his own splintering and cracked window? Small change. “Not to question the legitimacy of your businesses.” A mischievous grin cracked your lips open. “But I’m going to assume you have a prison warder or two on the payroll?” Six “Gyaaaaah-ah-ah-ahhh!” erupted from Russell Greer’s diaphragm as his splintered shin bone stabbed out through mangled flesh. As he collapsed, powder-blue-trousered pistons drove heavy boot soles down into his body, crumbling his skeleton. A breathless whimper escaped Russell’s mouth before a heel kicked down, cracking his jaw out of alignment. The ball that he’d curled up into unraveled from its core as bones broke, their shards carving apart muscle and tendon. He held a bloody, limp hand up in a plea for mercy, only for his lingers to be wrenched back and broken with a queasy, muffled snap. As he choked on a cocktail of blood and vomit, poured over shattered teeth, his hand dropped limp onto the cold floor where the scars of April’s teeth were trampled and ground under steel-toe-capped boot. As Russell Greer’s dying breath seeped out over grotesquely split lips, the stuffing of his pillow was forced down his throat before the door was bolted shut behind his executioners. With the death of one evil man, something far darker was about to be birthed. Seven In the end, it was easy. You simply… let go. Let go of sanity, let go of humanity, let got of emotion. To suppress Rasa was to endure a thousand migraines a second as your brain superheated and steamed in its own skull. To succumb to Rasa was to bathe in warm honey. You treaded heavy, sticky water beneath your own skin; unable to reach the surface, unable to sink into oblivion. Ha… ha… hahaha… the eruption of wicked laughter no longer echoed around your skull, but instead skipped around in the distance. Ha… HAHA… HAHAHAHAHAHAHAHAHA!!!
