Control

16 March 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/16/07 Twilight had fallen over the suburb of Burnaby BC, part of the greater Vancouver area, the rush and bustle of the day already giving way to the frenetic and break neck pace of the popular night life. Neon signs came to life all over town, street lamps flickering on to accompany them as people put away their suits and ties, got out their casuals or club clothes for a night on the town. From the balcony of her condominium, Dawn McFarlane watched in silent pleasure as the dullness of the day disappeared entirely, the last of the quiet rays of sunlight vanishing over the horizon to be replaced with the man made miniature suns that lit up the night. Some nights it seemed as though a thousand stars came to earth just for Vancouver, lending a portion of their glow to light the night for a race that had become increasingly unwilling to face the dark of night. The rigors of this week and the week previous wore heavy on Dawn's shoulders, her muscles and bones still aching from the nearly continuous beating she'd received since first meeting Joseph ?Just call me Joe? Depent. Wrapped in a thick blanket she sipped slowly at a steaming mug of tea, small whisps of steam being carried off into the cool spring air. For many in British Columbia it was still cold, but the Ontario native was enjoying the relative warmth of a spring that came early...far earlier then she was used to back home. ?Home...? She murmured, legs dangling between the railing bars of her balcony, hanging out over the sheer drop that would take her violently to the street below. Despite weeks of trying to grow accustomed to her new surroundings, the Steel Rose still found herself referring to the closed estate in Ontario as home, unable to forget the familiar comfort of it's sturdy walls...no matter how stifling it had become. ?At least I'm not wondering why I'm out here anymore.? She smirked, that wry half smile tugging at the corner of her mouth. That particular puzzle had been laid out for her by the man Ian Childress had personally recommended... ?...Joe Depente, pleased to meet you.? The real Joe Depente was an impressive specimen, easily three or four inches taller then Dawn herself, wide across the shoulders and with biceps that strained the sleeves of the black mock turtleneck he wore. His smile was a slash of perfect white across his dark face, hair only just starting to grey at his temples and peppered through his hair. Dawn stopped short in front of him, emerald eyes roiling with anger, hands clenched into fists at her sides. She didn't take his hand, didn't so much as look at it, but that didn't seem to deter the smile from his face as he let it drop slowly back down to his side. Sheepishly he rubbed the back of his neck, looking very much the picture of a child caught with his hand in the cookie jar. ?I think it's safe to say, by the way you're looking at me with those beautiful eyes, that if we weren't in a public place you'd be seeing about feeding me those gloves you're wearing.? ?Good guess.? Dawn ground out between her teeth, ?Even in public it's awfully tempting. Joe.? Nodding sagely he sat back down in his chair, neatly folding the newspaper he'd been reading and setting it to one side. With a large hand he gestured to the table. ?Would you like to have a seat? It'll make the explaining easier if you're not towering above me on those amazonian legs of yours.? She didn't smile and he sighed, whistling out between his teeth. ?Ian said you had a temper on you, told me to watch for it...but if you ain't going to at least listen to what I have to say then you might as well turn yourself around and walk back out that door, save us both the time and trouble.? His attempt to take control didn't go over well, and the steely glint to his hazel eyes didn't seem to deter the angry stare of hers. For a long moment nothing was said between them, the silent battle of wills passing through their gaze in a matter of heartbeats. Slowly Joe shook his head, breaking the contest without acquiescing defeat. ?Suit yourself, stand if that's how you want it.? Leaning back in the chair he took a sip of dark liquid from the mug before him, eyes traveling up and down Dawn's rigid form with the practiced ease of one used to evaluating at a glance. Leaning forward he set both elbows on the table, steepling his fingers together and gazing at her over them. ?What you went through at my gym was a necessary evil. Ian and I go way back, I've been helping him train people for years...and I've turned away easily three times the number of people I've helped. That little test is a way for me to gauge four things.? He held up four fingers, ticking them off as he went over each point. ?First, it lets me know if you even have the skills to be learning from me. Without a solid grounding in the basics I'd have more luck teaching a train how to fly. Second, it tells me if you've got the right skills to be learning from me. Just because you can hold your own in a tussle doesn't mean you'll learn anything from someone trying to teach you more then just brawling. Thirdly, it gives me an idea of what kind of prejudices you've got coming into things, whether or not you're going to be open to the training I provide or if you've got too much pride.? ?And the fourth?? She asked, intrigued despite herself. ?Finally it tells me if you've got the common sense to hold yourself back from seriously hurting someone. My people were holding back there and so were you, which means you ain't gonna take what I teach you and land yourself in jail with it. You may not have much of it, but you've got control.? She'd bristled then, snapped some angry retort that had sounded hollow even at the time. ?Control.? She snorted, shifting so the dull ache in her back and sides was less pronounced. ?Who the fuck was I kidding?? Her cheeks still burned at the thought of it, the first time they'd dragged out the pads so she could show him what she could do...and had almost showed herself right out of the gym and back to going nowhere. The Depente Gym had undergone a miraculous transformation while she was gone, the old pads stripped away to reveal brand new flooring with well maintained impact padding. The rusting machines were gone, gleaming steel monsters in their place, the most high tech and high end equipment that money could buy. And the people. Whereas it had been empty before, the gym was now full of both patrons and trainers, all running through the various steps of whatever exercise routine had been chosen for them. Everyone took a moment to glance over the newcomer as she came through the door, some challenging, some curious and some amused, the latter obviously having been aware of Dawn's test. ?You don't kid around when you make a test.? Dawn said over her shoulder to Joe, ?Shit I wouldn't even think this was the same place.? ?That's part of step three.? He rumbled, stepping past her. ?You follow a suit into a gym like that one and most people will just turn around again and walk out. Too high and mighty to think about bein' somewhere that doesn't kiss their ass.? ?I thought about it.? She admitted, falling into step behind him. ?You'd be an idiot if you didn't.? Came the blunt retort. ?Everyone thinks about it, you can't help it, your brain's tellin' you that this isn't some place you want to be. But if you can overcome that part of your brain...well, it's your first step.? ?Overcoming my brain?? She sounded incredulous, even to herself. ?I'll get to that in time, first things first. Put on those pads and show me what you can do. I've seen those long legs work in the ring, show me what they can do to someone outside of it.? Most of the padding was for him, but firm shin and knee guards slid easily into place over her lower legs. Tying her longish hair away from her face in a foxtail, Dawn brought both hands up and stepped towards him. ?Kempo?? He said, eyeing her appraisingly. ?Yeah, how'd you...? ?Once you learn the basics of any style it's hard to miss. Not many kicks in Kempo though, show me something with just your legs. We're gonna start from the ground up with you and that means your feet.? Swapping to something more appropriate, Dawn launched towards him, mixing sweeping Tae Kwon Do kicks with the shorter, sharper strikes of Muey Thai. Each strike met a pad, never slipping past his guard to touch his unprotected flesh. A deep frown worked it's way onto his face and on one kick he stepped into her and shoved hard, depositing her on her ass. ?Do I look like a child to you? Or some kind of glass dummy? Quit hitting these pads and hit me or I ain't never gonna know what you've got.? Suppressing a flash of irritation she kipped up to her feet, moving immediately to the attack again. Between kicks she tossed in knee lifts and strikes, each blow still meeting a pad, but with much more force and impact. Joe started to grin as the sweat ran down her neck and sides, glistening on the skin of her exposed midriff. ?You tired already? We can take a break if you like, maybe lie down for a little nap or something. Help you get some strength back in those legs so you ain't hittin' like my grandma.? At that she did growl, slapping a stiff kick into his arm pad, driving forward with each step, raining kicks and knee strikes onto him. He blocked or slipped away from each attack, sometimes stepping out of range of a sweeping kick, sometimes rapping her on the back of her calf with a pad. ?You were the world champion with those weak ass kicks? What's GCW stand for? Geriatric Championship Wrestlin'? I've trained six year old girls who kick harder 'n you, at least they could fuckin' well hit me.? ?I might be doing better if you'd stop badgering me.? She snarled, snapping off a right, then left circle kick, following through with an inside crescent kick. ?Badgering? You call this badgering? Girl if you can't take a little friendly banter you might as well pack yo' bags right now. You think the men and women at GCW gonna say nice things to you? They gonna tell you how nice your hair is while they're beating you around that ring? Was Chris Storm whispering sweat nothin's in your ear when he laid yo' ass out?? Her feet were barely touching the ground between kicks now, each one smashing into padding or cutting the air like a blade. She was panting with exertion, each breath a snarl of anger as she zeroed in on her trainer, determined to smash aside his defenses, connect with bone crushing kicks. Joe took a high kick off his shoulder and she stepped in, arms looping around his neck and legs pistoning up towards his chest, slamming into the guard pads on his arms. As her left leg came down he hooked one arm under her right and the other around her waist, bodily lifting her from the mat before spinning and planting her down hard. The air whooshed painfully out of her lungs as she hit, his left hand pinning her right arm to the ground and his right hand around her throat, digging the leather of her collar into her flesh. His hazel eyes locked on hers and when he spoke it was low and soothing with a hint of command, like you'd talk to a dog that had just slipped it's leash. ?Okay, okay. Leave go Dawn, leave go. Deep breaths, in, out.? She lay on her back, gasping and panting even after he let her go, easing himself into a sitting position nearby. Wordlessly he handed her the water bottle she'd brought and she drank greedily from it, water spilling over her neck and down her collar. When she capped the bottle again she found she couldn't meet his gaze, her eyes lingering on the droplets of sweat that had pattered on the mat. ?Well...Ian was right. Somehow along the way you've lost yourself, and you're tryin' to make up for it by keepin' yourself in check all the time. You think too much.? She barked a laugh that was entirely devoid of humour. ?Think too much? I just broke like an animal on you and you're telling me I think too much?? ?Yeah.? He fired back, his own voice snapping a little. ?You think too much because when that gets stripped away from you, you've got nothin' left holdin' yourself back. Once your brain derails you lose yourself, you're like an animal and you can't help it.? She frowned, turning the water bottle over in her hands, watching as the water devoured any individual droplets clinging to the sides. ?So what do I do?? ?You start over. With me.? At that she looked up, eyes wide. ?You still want to...?? Joe laughed, leaning forward and tapping her on the breastbone. ?Dawn I seen a lot of men and women like you. First through basic for the Marine Corps, and now through this gym. Somewhere in there you got all this anger that's swirling around, eatin' you from the inside out. So you push it down, you lock it up, you toss it away...but it don't go away. It just keeps growin', getting worse and worse, and when you lose that careful control for even a minute...boom.? He spread his hands out like an explosion, expression serious. ?We're emotional creatures Dawn, but without some kind of control we're little better then animals...and it's the same thing if we have too much control. Then when you're forced to think with your heart and your gut alone...you can't do it. You choke, you lose control.? Dawn nodded slowly, riveted by his words, eyes following the expressive motions of his hands as he spoke. ?So we figure out what the problem is, where it all comes from.? Joe pushed himself up off the mat, offering her a hand. ?We teach you how to handle it when you have to, and how to use what you've got to your advantage.? Accepting his hand, Dawn let herself be hauled to his feet. ?So how do we do that?? ?You let me worry about that.? He replied, thumping her on the arm. ?Go home, get some rest and come back tomorrow. This is gonna take a lot of work...? Setting her empty teacup down on the patio, Dawn pushed to her feet and leaned out over the railing into the wind. Burnaby wasn't home yet, and Joe was a long way from her friends in KB: Dies Irae...but out here she had a chance to connect with the person she most wanted to, to find the person she'd lost somewhere along the way. Herself.
