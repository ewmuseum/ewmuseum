Suffering's End

20 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/20/06 Mid-December of 2005 Outside the Hell's Mouth bar The wind kicked up sand in the Nevada desert, tossing it in great sprays over the dead plants and unshakeable cacti that populated the area, a strangely flat stretch of land surrounded by distant buttes and mesas, giving the impression that the road running past the tiny bar called Hell's Mouth began and ended within sight, coming from nowhere only to end up in the same place. Virgil's patience had been wearing thin, very thin, over the last few days, having been forced to wait for Dawn McFarlane to arrive from Japan, likely with ex-vampire hunter Alexis Key in tow. In truth, Virgil approved of neither of them being anywhere near here, but realized that were he not to allow them even the option of showing up, he'd never hear the end of it. It was, after all, Dawn's choice to involve herself in this likely dangerous matter, and anything that happened to her would have been her own fault for being stubborn. A small, insignificant speck appeared through the hazy distortions caused by the heat on the asphalt, swirling and shifting back and forth like the same mirage of water on the dips in the road. It wasn't quite so bad through Virgil's thick sunglasses, and he could make out the shape of an older transport van hurtling down the highway at a likely illegal speed, which pleased him somewhat. At least Dawn was making up for the inconvenience of an overseas flight taking more than a day by at least making haste on the road. It approached quickly, and Virgil leaned against his motorcycle, watching as the van pulled quietly to a stop next to him. The front doors opened, and he was soon approached by Dawn McFarlane, who greeted him somewhat tersely, and Alexis, who ran up to Virgil somewhat excitedly, wrapping her arms around his waist, although somewhat hesitantly, as though she might burst into flames upon contact. Virgil looked down at her momentarily, somewhat puzzled, before tentatively putting an arm behind her back. 'Hello, child.' Alexis backed away, looking somewhat sheepishly back and forth between Virgil and Dawn, who half-glared at each other for a moment. 'Dawn.' Her expression didn't change much. 'Virgil.' A rather large black man slid out of the van, looking around the area, sunglasses covering his eyes as well. He walked confidently up to the trio, nodding at Virgil, who regarded him with casual curiosity. 'Who are you?' With the grace of a confident businessman... of Asian origin, he bowed. 'Michi Noketsuna.' Virgil raised an eyebrow, looking over his very black form, more than a little puzzled. However, questions such as this weren't important enough to be asked now. 'Virgil Ambrosi. Might I ask your purpose in being here?' He nodded politely. 'To drive this van.' Virgil glanced at Dawn, who nodded. 'Don't worry about him.' Virgil leaned back against his bike for a moment, slipping together bits and pieces of the strategy involved in this likely dangerous expedition. He'd had plenty of time to figure out exactly how he was planning on handling Dominik, the ghoulish bartender who couldn't lie to save his life, perhaps quite literally, and it was appearing as though there would be no better time to start. After all, morning would soon be giving way to afternoon, and the night was no time to be dealing with dangerous situations. 'The three of you can handle making certain that nobody enters while I'm inside, I assume.' Alexis and Michi nodded, but Dawn's brow furrowed, something that Virgil had particularly dreaded seeing. The indignant expression unique to Dawn's face had become, over his acquaintance with her, a cause of consternation and anger, something which often gave him the idea of clawing her face directly off. 'I'm not waiting out here while you go in there and rescue her.' Virgil raised an eyebrow. 'You obviously enjoy the idea of being killed, then.' Her eyes narrowed. 'I don't care, I'm not letting you go in there by yourself.' Virgil's face quickly soured into a glare that matched hers in intensity. 'I didn't give you the option. I will go in, and take care of the ghoul, and the three of you will wait for me.' This wasn't the end, by any means, to Dawn's opposition. 'I already said I'm not waiting out here! Are you deaf?' At this point, Virgil was beginning to become apathetic about Dawn's safety, and he was considering letting her go in by herself at the moment. After all, that was what she wanted, wasn't it? 'Then wait until I get back. Security will force at least one of us, preferably more, to make certain that we won't be caught in some sort of ambush.' 'Oh!' Alexis walked over to the van, and opened the back doors, out of which tromped a pair of very large, monstrous dogs, Lia's pets, Damek and Jirina. 'I forgot they were in there... don't want them to get overheated.' The dogs scampered over to Virgil and bounced excitedly on their front paws, grunting and lolling their tongues out of their dangerously-fanged maws. Virgil scratched both of their heads, and smiled, but Dawn cut the reunion short. 'There. Damek and Jirina can wait outside with Michi. Is that alright with you, Michi?' Michi nodded. 'I'm not certain who I'd be looking for inside.' Alexis blinked. 'Wait, so I get to go, too?' Virgil made a cutting gesture with his hand. 'Absolutely not.' Dawn resumed glaring at Virgil. 'She's coming with us. You know as well as I do what kind of training she's had. She's entirely capable of handling herself in this kind of situation.' Alexis stepped quickly back to the van again, and Virgil followed her this time, laying eyes on the impressive array of artillery littering the back of the van, oddly enough the same place the dogs had been sitting. Alexis picked up a large rifle-shaped firearm, with a clip of wooden stakes hanging out of the bottom. She looked through the scope, and adjusted it slightly. 'I'll be okay, Virgil.' Virgil shook his head. 'I can't be around to save your life whenever the situation necessitates it, child.' Alexis beamed a smile at him. 'I know, but you forget, I had to try and kill Lia once, when I was still with the Den... I don't see how this could be much worse. I promise I wont get in the way.' Virgil glared at Dawn, who had a triumphant look on her face that made him want to pull one of the doors off of the van, but he resisted the urge. 'Neither of you are coming with me while I deal with Dominik.' Dawn's expression, once again, soured. 'Why?' Virgil started toward the door of the bar, smiling slightly. 'My plan doesn't necessitate extra people.' ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The heavy wooden door of the Hell's Mouth swung open, and Dominik looked up from the bar, and the glasses that he was perpetually cleaning, despite the fact that patrons in these parts were few and far between, to make a generous estimate. He smiled warmly at Virgil, someone he'd recognized from a previous encounter, as someone who was a threat. A threat to the plans of his master, whatever those might be, and possibly a threat to him. But, he knew well that Virgil had been searching in the desert for some time, and obviously, his search would have led him past the bar once again, likely just in search of a rest. And, as Virgil sat down upon one of the rickety bar stools, his assumption appeared correct. 'Some water, please. I'll gladly pay for it.' Dominik nodded. 'Sure thing.' Virgil concentrated on the illusion he had been crafting in his mind for hours, waiting for the right moment to project it, which happened to be now. Quickly, threads of color coalesced into an uncertain form, gradually hardening and defining themselves into the form of an extremely large, winged demon, known to the Tzimice, who often took their form, as a Zulo. It stomped toward the bar, as Dominik rose with a glass of spring water, which he immediately dropped, shattering the glass. 'I...' The Zulo slammed its bony hands on the top of the bar, and Dominik backed into the wall, knocking a couple of bottles of whiskey from their shelves. 'You... It understands that your master hides one of its children below this pathetic establishment without its approval.' Dominik swallowed a lump in his throat, and managed to stammer out the words, as Virgil pretended to calmly back away from the Zulo, in order to keep his illusion from being compromised. 'Who... who are you?' 'It calls itself Tzimice. You are familiar with it, no doubt.' It leaned over the bar, bearing rows of serrated fangs in a terrible smile, and Dominik slipped in the water he spilled, falling to the floor, his back still against the wall. With a mighty swipe, an entire section of the bar was removed and slid across the room. It took another step closer. 'It will make this request once more. Where is its child being held?' Dominik held up a shaky finger, pointing to an area underneath the bar with what appeared to be breaks where a trapdoor was set. Before he could realize that the massive Zulo wouldn't even be able to fit through the trapdoor, his head was snapped to the side by the heel of Virgil's boot slamming into his jaw, knocking out a molar, and sending Dominik to the floor, holding his jaw. A punch to the back of the head left him unconscious, as a trickle of blood dripped from his mouth. Virgil looked over at the Zulo, which had been motionless after he'd stopped concentrating on it, like a grotesque statue in some eccentric millionaire's collection. However, he had little use for such a hideous thing now, and it quickly burst into particles of light and color, before fading from sight completely, leaving the bar as serene as it had been, with the minor exception of part of the bar itself laying halfway across the room. Virgil slung Dominik over his back, and carried him into the midday sun. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Inside the tunnels underneath the Hell's Mouth bar The tunnels branched off in several different ways before them, their cool stony surface encrusted with lichens and mushrooms of various sorts, looking for all intents and purposes like a natural cave, one perhaps untouched by modern explorers. However, Virgil was quite aware that what lay down the tunnels was likely not simply darkness, nor only Lia. There would be resistance on the way to her, and when he, or they, perhaps, as Alexis and Dawn stood at his sides, found her, her captor would likely not be so willing to part with her. Alexis peered down one of the tunnels, holding her stake-loaded rifle up just in case. 'There are lights in the tunnels... we should be able to see without any kind of help.' Virgil nodded. 'Good. However, that likely puts stealth out of consideration...' Dawn made a consternated face, and turned toward Virgil a little. 'Should we split up and check the tunnels separately?' Virgil shook his head, not looking directly at her, something he'd grown quite practiced at doing. 'No. I assume you've never had to meet with Tzimice creations aside from Lia's hounds?' Dawn blinked at him. 'No.' A bit of silence hung in the air for a moment, as a scratching sound emanated from a passage to the center of them. 'You would not like to meet one alone. Even a mewling wretch of a szlachta can be deadly, given the chance. We will stay together.' 'But...' He held up a hand, cutting her off, before drawing his pistol. 'When Lia asks what happened to you, I'd prefer not to explain that you're scattered in pieces throughout this cave.' Alexis' eyes widened a little bit. She'd never seen a szlachta, and, in fact, had no clue what such a thing could possibly be. However, to hear Virgil proclaiming death and destruction like this was rare enough that she wouldn't be suggesting wandering through these narrow passageways by herself. No, she'd hang back and shoot at vampires, were there any. Virgil could probably have done this by himself after all... ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The tunnels all converged at a single point, much like they did at the entrance to the network of caves, which the three well-armed amateur spelunkers were rather pleased to find. The smooth rock passages terminated in a set of matching carved staircases, painstakingly cut out of rock into a spiral, which twisted downward even further. Upon reaching the bottom of the staircase, Virgil halted suddenly, peering down what had become a sterile-looking corridor, much like one that could be found in a research facility of some kind, albeit a rather decrepit one. 'Wow.' Alexis' quiet statement surprised Virgil, who had been inspecting the corridor to the best of his ability before impulsively stepping down it, likely setting off some manner of trap. A quick glare turned her silent again, and Virgil began to lead them down the hallway. A large wooden door began to loom in front of them, seeming to grow into a ridiculously ornamented wooden door that stretched nearly eight feet. Virgil paused for a moment, glancing back at his two companions. Dawn held a pistol in each hand, somewhat shakily, causing Virgil to wonder if she'd actually had any kind of training whatsoever in handling them. Alexis seemed much better prepared, crouched down slightly, with the barrel of her rifle pointed past him, ready to make the first attack if necessary. Now was as good as any other time... Virgil shoved the massive door open, and it creaked loudly, before slamming into the wall of a well-appointed laboratory, filled with various containers and apparatus that Virgil had little clue or care as to the purpose of. His eyes scanned the large room quickly, immediately being drawn to the table near the center of the room, with a very familiar body strapped to it. He stepped toward it, mouthing silent words of disbelief, relief... she was unharmed, he could tell, but she couldn't stay strapped to the table like that... He found a quick burst of strength, and was able to tear open the metal bonds holding her wrists, before slashing the leather ones around her body. As they fell to the floor uselessly, a raspy voice echoed throughout the laboratory. 'Intruders? In my domain?' A reasonably tall, gaunt form had appeared through a small doorway to the left of the entrance, and as the white light of the room fell upon him, Alexis knew what he was as quickly as Virgil. Virgil sneered at the terrible yet strangely beautiful visage of the being across the room from him. 'You assail one of your own?' The vampire opened its mouth to speak, but before the first word could come out, Alexis wasted no time, knowing there was no use in negotiating at this point. One thing she had learned well from her time with the Den; when invading an unknown vampire's territory, there will be no avoiding a confrontation. She lifted the barrel of her rifle, and pulled the trigger. An ashwood stake blasted across the room, barely missing Virgil's arm, and lodged itself in the chest of the Tzimice, the impact knocking him back a step. However, much to Alexis' horror, he pulled the stake from his chest, and threw it across the room, the wooden skittering noise echoing loudly. 'You insolent little whelp!' It charged across the room, directly toward Alexis, yelling in frenzied Polish. Dawn reacted quickly, firing rounds from both pistols in the general direction of the charging vampire, seeming to slow it down for at least a couple of paces. Alexis loaded another stake into the chamber, and raised her rifle to fire, when the Tzimice bridged the distance between them, knocking the gun from her hand, and swinging a powerful arm in her direction, knocking her several feet to the side, sending her sliding across the floor, and into a wall. Dawn tossed her empty pistols to the side, and draws the short kodachi she'd carried for an emergency. She thrust forward with the blade, but her much quicker target dodged to the side, and raked its claws down her chest, before shoving her away, thinking her quite dead, as he'd felt her body armor give way easily under his attack. He began to smirk at the work he'd done, when several tiny points of flame erupt from the enraged vampire's back. It whirled around to face Virgil, who still faced him with a half-clip of explosive rounds. No longer concerned with the only standing mortal in the room, the Tzimice stalked toward Virgil, taking another pair of rounds to the chest. Large spikes of bone slid out from its arms, and Virgil tossed his gun to the side, realizing it would be of little use at close range. It brought its hands down doubled in the direction of Virgil's head, but he ducked to the side, raking the Tzimice with his sharpened fingernails, and directing a powerful kick to its back, sending it reeling. However, it swung around with a spiked arm, jabbing Virgil in the side, and pulling back, opening him up reasonably severely. Virgil doubled over from the sudden pain of the wound, and the Tzimice recognized its advantage, coming at Virgil once again with spikes ready. Virgil fell back, and used the momentum of the fiend against it, tossing it over at high velocity, sending it skidding across the floor, into a strange rod rising from the floor. A small door slid open near where Dawn had climbed back to her feet, her body armor nearly slashed through, and the Tzimice rose as well, slightly in front of her. No coward by any means, she leapt forward with her kodachi, slicing a deep furrow down the fiend's back, causing him to howl in pain. However, he quickly regained his composure, and turned to face her, yellow eyes glowering down into hers. He raised a spined arm, prepared to slice her open, when Virgil crashed into her shoulder-first, knocking her into the wall. The spikes found their mark, though in Virgil's powerful shoulder, rather than Dawn's more fragile mortal body. He dropped to a knee, and the fiend smiled wickedly, raising the arm once again, before his face twisted into a pained grimace, and he froze, as another stake found its mark in the other side of his chest, where his heart obviously was. Seizing his moment to strike back, Virgil slipped an ornate dagger of some age from his waist, and buried it deep in the front of the fiend's chest, meeting the stake from Alexis' rifle somewhere in the middle. Paralyzed, the Tzimice dropped to its knees, face still twisted in agony. Alexis darted over to Virgil, who climbed back to his feet, though still bleeding from the wounds he'd received by way of the Tzimice's body modifications. 'Virgil, are you okay?' Virgil turned toward her, and nodded. She looked over to Dawn, and noticed a tiny stream of light in the stairwell that had been revealed, leading upward. Not the cold, artificial white light of the laboratory, more like... 'Is that sunlight?' Virgil's eyes whipped over to the stairwell, where he immediately noticed the small shaft of light as well. Never had he been so glad to see the sun he'd feared for centuries. 'Help me with this thing.' He lifted the fiend onto one shoulder, which prompted ineffective squirming from the Tzimice, whose legs were still free. Alexis grabbed them, and they started toward the stairwell. With every step, the squirming became a little more frantic, and the the dimly reflected light grew a little brighter. Obviously, the Tzimice hadn't designed this passage to be used during the daylight hours, as the smell of slowly searing flesh began to fill the tiny area. Alexis felt bile rise in her throat in response to the vile odor of the smoking Tzimice. 'We don't have far to go.' At last, the top of the stairs was near, and only a few feet remained until they were standing back in the desert. Virgil turned his head back to Alexis. 'Let go of him and go downstairs.' She nodded, and dropped the fiend's legs, darting down the stairwell quickly, knowing from experience what was about to ensue, as Virgil climbed over the threshold with the Tzimice on his shoulders. He threw it off as the sun hit both of them, the immediate burst of flame searing his coat, and part of his face. He stepped back quickly, as the flames grew more intense, consuming the ancient finery the fiend had been wearing, and eventually its skin and bones as well, until a smoldering, black pile of ash lay in the hot sun. Virgil stared at it for a few seconds, a silent remembrance of all the suffering the Tzimice had indirectly caused him, his invisible nemesis for months. He turned back toward the passage, not even dignifying the remains with foul sentiment. Nothing else mattered now, as he practically flew down the stairs, and back into the cold laboratory, seeing the woman he'd been so frantically searching after for so long. It was finally over, he thought, as he walked right past Alexis, who was tending to Dawn at the moment, and collapsing to his knees at the unconscious Lia's side. It was finally over. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ February 20th, 2006 Mifune Dojo Virgil stared interestedly down at the short-legged table in front of him. A tao painting adorned the top, something he'd become rather familiar with during his visits to China. While not traditionally very Japanese, it still seemed to fit well with the dojo's overall theme. He had to admit, despite still not liking Dawn very much, that she had secured them reasonably pleasant lodgings for GCW's excursion to Japan. He pored over the hanji on the painting, trying to read the handwritten strokes. The mountains red, large Swallows cross the evening sun slowly. From the end of a dynasty, Virgil surmised. A setting sun was never the best of imagery to have attributed to one's reign. His silent thoughts were interrupted as one of the doors to the living room slid open, and the tall, muscular form of Michi Noketsuna stepped silently into the room. 'Lia-sama is on the balcony, Virgil-sama. She mentioned to me that she'd like to speak with you.' Virgil looked up indifferently at Michi, perhaps one of the more promising looks he could give someone he wasn't particularly familiar with... but then, Michi was an exception. He'd first met Virgil on a rather important day, after all. Had he not been there, perhaps things would have gone differently, perhaps Dominik, rather than staying under Michi's gun, would have called for assistance of some kind... regardless, it had given him a quick trip into Virgil's trust, along with Dawn and Alexis, though the latter was perhaps his favorite of the mortals. He realized that there was little to complain about, these days. Everything had finally come together, and things were acceptably normal. Now all that was left was to enjoy his new hobby, and the newfound peace that he welcomed so... at least until the next crusade came along.
