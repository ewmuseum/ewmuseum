Date: 08/30/10 “No.” “Troy, don’t be ridiculous,” Miyuki said, as evenly as she could manage under the circumstances. “I’m hardly going to whisk her out of bed for a rigorous training session or off of press interviews. You do realize I am her friend, right?” She stood before the closed door of the apartment loft that Tempest and Troy shared, sun streaming through the skylight above. And Troy stood firmly in the doorway, between her and the way in to Tempest. “I realize you’re her friend.” Troy nodded. His arms remained folded across his chest and something along his upper tricep twitched in agitated contrast to the calm of his voice. “I also realize there’s a big pay per view coming up and you’re her manager. And I don’t give a shit. You’re not seeing her. She’s resting. You’ll see her when she asks to see you.” “But…” “No. You can console her and tell her how much ass she’s going to kick and how she’s going to be back on top when she can turn her head again without that excruciating look on her face. Til then I have no interest in you filling her head up with the sort of bullshit that your business calls ‘hope’.” With that the door closed, softly, but firmly. Miyuki glared at the smooth brushed steel adorned with washes of bright primary colors. “That man is going to be the death of her career. And someday I’m going to see him right out of her life,” she said under her breath. “Funny, he said something like that about you.” Miyuki wheeled around to see Frank Valleta grinning at her from the stairwell in his best dusty jeans, gray LCW tee shirt, and work boots. He sidled up to her, as she fumed and said, “No visitors I take it?” “None,” she said irritably, folding her arms across her neat crimson silk chemise. She took a step back from Frank’s dusty six foot six bulk. “As if I’m somehow the enemy.” She turned and glared at Frank as if he might be the enemy. “I have stood by Tempest through things he can’t even imagine! And he has the audacity to accuse me of being a bad influence, as if I want to take her away from him and throw her in harm’s way.” Her shoulders rose and fell with her agitated breathing and Frank nodded very slightly. “Well, you can’t blame him for being a little paranoid. Her getting locked up in Hong Kong, getting out by means you guys still don’t seem to want to talk about and now getting nearly choked to death by that asshole Frost doesn’t do much for his confidence that the sport is good for her. Can you blame him?” As she opened her mouth, Frank raised his hand to stop the flow of objection before it started. “I know your intentions are good. But he loves her just as much as you do.” Miyuki eyed at Frank curiously as he took off his weather-beaten ball cap and scratched his head, his brow knit with the effort of having to now explain such a seemingly simple statement. “I don’t mean the same way, just that you two are thick as thieves and he doesn’t understand everything you’ve been through, sure. But he loves her enough to care what happens to her completely beyond what she does. You two are friends with a purpose. You move as one and think as one when it comes to wrestling and her career and what she ought to do next.” Miyuki’s head moved very slightly backwards, a tiny motion of her thin neck and an arching of her perfect Japanese brows that acceded his point. “She needs me now. If I don’t know what condition she’s in, how am I to help her?” Frank smirked and Miyuki rolled her eyes awaiting an answer she would doubtless not like. “Remember what you told him in Hong Kong?” he asked. She nodded and sighed with resignation. “Well, there it is. ‘Trust Tempest.’ It’s your turn. She’ll let you know what she needs. Now…” he clapped his hands together causing a tiny dust storm which Miyuki fanned away from her face. “How about a beer? I’m buyin’.” Miyuki stared up at the towering, grinning, dusty, truck of a man before her and her mouth opened and shut, working on a perfectly measured response. “Another time, perhaps,” she finally said and made her way gracefully down the stairs. Frank turned and watched her go, with a huge shrug and said, “What? I don’t smell that bad?” but that didn’t stop him from checking. ~ After the voices in the hall died down and Frank’s size 13’s retreated down the stairs with a mighty roar, Troy Rivers walked back into the loft and peered over the edge of the huge red sofa that basically formed what they called their living room. Tempest lay upon it, floating in a sea of brightly colored cushions. A few were very strategically placed, beneath her neck and on either side of her head for just the right support. Her eyelids fluttered, but otherwise she was still, off in a dream laden sleep. Dreams. He wished once more that he could fathom why hers must be so dangerous to theirs. But his musing was interrupted by another knock at the door. “Oh my god, go away,” he mumbled quietly as he walked away from Tempest’s resting place and back to the door. He opened it without really looking and said, “Guys, look really it’s…” He stopped and stared a moment. In the doorway was a thin Asian man about five feet eight inches tall. He wore a suit, but not a very refined suit. It was the forgettable sort, a suit that one would not really be able to recall or describe later if asked. “Um, hi… can I help you?” Troy asked, suddenly thrown well off his game. “Yes, Mr. Rivers. I was wondering if I might inquire as how Tempest is doing and if I might speak with her.” The man’s voice was calm and pleasant and his smile reassuring, but Troy just stared for a moment before speaking. “And you’re… who?” A moment passed. The man’s smile didn’t falter, but he flicker of calculation taking place was unmistakable. “My name is Wei. I’m with the Hong Kong Police department. I spoke with Tempest while…” His words were interrupted by the loud echo in the four story hallway as Troy closed the door firmly behind him. A startled flutter of wings above the skylight cast shadows over his face as his gaze zeroed in on Wei penetratingly. “She’s fine. Now what’s this about?” “Well, I would like to speak with her if I may and…” “You may not. In regards to what?” “Mr. Rivers, you’re a reasonable man and if I may say, I believe you are concerned for her well being. Maybe even more than she is.” Troy’s eyes narrowed. “I am. But I haven’t got much evidence that suggests you are. So remind why I would let you anywhere near her.” Wei smiled an obsequious smile and said, “You do not think Tempest can make a simple decision of whom she may speak with one her own?” Troy eyed Wei carefully and then said, in a tone more calm than was natural, “I don’t think I like your tone, Mr. Wei. I think you’d better leave.” He started to turn, slowly, not taking his eyes off the other man, but Wei shook his head. “That is not what I meant. Believe me, I intend no harm to her. I just wish to speak with her.” “And why should I believe that? Last time you just wanted to speak with her, you locked her up. Now if you don’t get off my landing, I’m going to call the locals because I’m pretty sure you’re a few thousand miles out of your jurisdiction here.” “Precisely, which is why, you must surely know that all I intend is to talk.” Troy sighed. “Fine, talk. But you can talk to me for the moment.” Wei nodded, eyeing Troy with curiosity as he spoke in a careful tone. “Then she is injured. I understand. I will remain in town and if you will ask her if she will speak to me, that will be sufficient. Believe me, the danger she faces will be worth her while to discuss with me.” He produced a slip of paper and handed it to Troy. “She is surrounded by enemies, whether she knows it or not.” “Oh I’m pretty sure she knows it,” Troy said, eyeing the paper, his eyes not leaving Wei for long. “Do you think I’m referring to wrestling? No, I don’t mean this little circus she plays in. Your…” Wei paused and looked at Troy and small, thin-lipped smile came over his face. “Well, are you actually married to her, Mr. Rivers?” “No.” Troy shook his head. “No, we’ve been together nearly three years now, but we’re not married.” “Ah, then the divorce isn’t final, still? There must be some mix up, a clerical error.” Wei smiled, eying him carefully. Troy’s gaze hardened, even as a hint of a smile played across his face. “Nice try. It’s final. And our plans aren’t your business.” Wei nodded, inclining his head in a slight bow. “I understand. I will look forward to Ms. Tyrell’s call. Good day.” Troy watched him traipse down the steps until he heard the door close behind him at the bottom before stepping back inside the apartment and closing the door firmly. He flipped the lock and grabbed his phone and pressed a familiar speed dial. “Hey Frankie… yeah, well she’s still out of it. No, she’s really not ready for people to be over, but listen, something’s come up. How good are you with security systems?” ~ “It’s creepy man. Never needed this kind of thing around here before,” Frank Valletta said as he clipped off a wire end and sought just the right connector. He looked at Troy Rivers who was quite noticeably not looking at him, but rather down the street. In the later afternoon it was uncrowded, with a bit of lazy traffic moving by. “Just trust me, ok?” Troy said. He returned his gaze to the bracket he was mounting and motioned for the wire lead that Frank had made up. “Oh I do. But that doesn’t make it less creepy. Wei came all the way here? Uh, no pun intended.” Frank grinned, but it seemed lost on Troy, who snapped the leads in place with a sharp click of the connector pliers. “Are you going to tell her?” Troy sighed and stopped working a moment. “I’ll have to. I’ve talked to her a little bit about Hong Kong, but I can’t really guess why he’s here or just how big a deal that is. I just don’t want to take any chances you know? Especially not now, while she’s hurt.” “Oh I agree.” Frank nodded vigorously as he placed a tiny sensor into a clamp on the side of a camera lens. His meaty hand deftly turned a tiny screwdriver to tighten the clamp and he worked with a delicacy that belied his size. “I guess I just didn’t really want to think of her life being this complicated, ya know? I guess I didn’t want to believe the danger was for real.” Troy nodded. “Neither did I. Believe me, neither did I.” ~ Troy came into what passed for the living room in the converted warehouse that he and Tempest called home and stopped short. He stared at the television’s large screen and squinted in disgust. “What in the world are you watching?” “Real Yeti Stories,” Tempest said sleepily. Her eyelids drooped and she shuffled under her blanket, as she remained nested in the rainbow of pillows on the sofa. Troy looked again and snickered. “Tempest, that’s Real Housewives of New Jersey.” He smirked down at his beloved as she looked up at him from beneath a tousled mop of black hair. Her eyes narrowed as she stared at the TV screen. “Oh… so it is. With all that flying fur and screeching I thought it was a yeti fight to the death. I guess I missed that it was inside a five star restaurant.” Tempest yawned and stretched carefully. Troy shook his head and laughed. “It’s a good thing you don’t get hurt bad enough to need drugs very often because your brain on drugs is a very scary place.” He sat down beside her ignored the continued high pitched voices from the television. Tempest seemed dazed, still only half aware of her surroundings. He put his arms around her carefully. “You feeling a little better?” “Oh yes. Just a little disoriented. But lookit… I can turn my head now.” She did so,a nd to the right worked well enough. But to the left she didn’t get far before seemingly hitting some internal stop. She winced and said, “Ok, maybe not quite there yet.” “It’s ok. Just take it easy.” “What day is it?” she asked, blearily. “Cuz you know I don’t have time to take it easy.” She sighed and watched Emil, her pale ferret chase his own tail around in circles on the floor. “Already I can practically hear them. ‘Tempest lost the TV title and Frost is now walking around with both.’ ‘Tempest is all washed up.’ ‘Tempest loses momentum heading into Dangerous Games.’ There’s no rest for the weary, and even less for the genuinely broken.” “Hey,” Troy said “Ok, so you lost the title. But you lost it in a hardcore match. And last time I heard you talk about it, you hated hardcore. What did you call those guys?” “Um… bumbling ogres with no sense of art?” She rubbed her face, recalling the words with some effort. Troy nodded. ”Yeah that was it. So congratulations, you’re not a murderous ogre who just chokes the life out of people and cheats to win by making sure there’s no ref around to see it.” Tempest sat up and stared at troy for a moment. “You watched the match. Like, all the way through.” He shrugged. “Not my favorite one by far, but yeah. “Aw, that’s still way more than you usually do.” Tempest smiled and lay back, resting her head carefully on Troy’s lap. “Yeah well, doing the best I can.” He stroked her hair as Tempest relaxed, sinking into the sofa. Her eyes blinked dreamily and almost lazily she said, “Why didn’t you tell me you had a sister?” His hand froze over her head and he stared a moment. Nearby a tiny bell rang from inside a toy as one of the ferrets batted it around. “Well… I didn’t really think to tell you because… what was I going to say? ‘Oh yeah, I had a sister. She’s dead now, so you won’t be meeting her.’ Just didn’t seem to lead anywhere worth going, ya know?” Tempest sat up with some difficulty and hugged him. “I’m sorry,” she said softly. “Ah it’s all right. But I could ask you the same thing.” Tempest peered at him curiously, the fog of painkillers still heavy in her expression. “Why didn’t you tell me you’d been married and divorced?” “Oh that,” Tempest said, trying to summon something like consistency to answer him. “Well, it was kind of the same thing. Where does that lead? It was years ago, before we even met. I had a husband, now I don’t. And you’ll never meet him, because he’s gone and pretty well forgotten.” Troy nodded. He pulled Tempest closer and they watched the two ferrets circle each other around the floor as LEDs blinked silently on the new the security system controller on the wall. ~ History. Whether it’s deep in the past, or just last week, it has a way of coming back and getting in your way when you least expect it. And as sure as you want to forget it, that’s the stuff that will just keep popping up in your face. I’ve tried to forget so much that it always surprises me when the past jumps up yet again. As if I can’t get in enough trouble in the here and now. But some sleeping dogs just won’t lie. And some we prefer to wake, and set on the attack ourselves.
