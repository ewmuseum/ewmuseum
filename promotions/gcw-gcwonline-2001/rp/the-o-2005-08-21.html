the O

21 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 21, 2005 A place far from here, where no one can ever go, tells a story about one man's life. A place far from here, not exactly a physical location, hold's a man's dreams, and thoughts. A place far from here, a man remembers his homeland of Ireland, and what he misses about it. Within the head of Coyle O'Connor, his world has completely turned upside down. Every little memory, detail, thought, they are all absent. All he can think about is how can he find total happiness again, what it was like to have a grin always on his face. For the last year, he has been standing away, slouching, secluding himself from the world. All he can think about is himself walking on a dirt road to nowhere; or paradise, just that he can't find it. Day in and day out, Coyle O'Connor wastes himself away, not a clue of what to do with himself alone in this world. You know, Coyle hasn't always been this depressed; in fact, he used to be one of the most jolliest of people ever. Being stripped from your homeland can do a lot to you though. He lost his family, his friends, his pubs, his house, his dog, his everything. It's something that doesn't happen to a lot of people, but unfortunately it happened to him. Every morning he wakes up, he thinks about what he did to go wrong; then he remembers. He can never go back to Ireland, his head has a bounty on it from the Irish mob. 'Why did I have to walk back behind that pub?' Coyle thinks of this everyday. He self-witnessed the actions of an Irish mob, and because of this, his life is threatened. It basically all comes down to his 'unluckiness.' Coyle hasn't always been a lucky person. Since the day he was born, he always was in the wrong place at the wrong time. Throughout the years, he has been blamed for crimes he did not do, breaking things he did not break, saying things he did not say; he is quite simply unlucky. His friends used to call him the 'O' because of this, and to their little party, the O meant the (bad) luck of the man, rather than the luck of the irish. No instances of being in the wrong place were ever his fault, it's just what worked out. And it all got him into a lot of trouble in the end. But nowadays, as you may or may not know, Coyle is a loner in America; imagine how tough that is, especially in Newport, RI. Actually, he probably doesn't have a single friend, but this is because he isn't making an effort. He feels something bad would happen, afterall, he is the 'O'. He just doesn't want to try anymore. However, there is one thing he does want to try, despite the fact its a hobby that luck can deeply effect. He always wanted to fight within an organization; he has always been a naturally strong man, why not give it go? And up into recently, when he got signed to the GCW, his urge to fight was higher than his urge to return home. Now he has a shot at fighting though, to release all of his anger and pain and thoughts on the person across the mat from him, legally. He sees it as a form of self-ventilation, and perhaps he is right. The GCW hired him because they saw this in him, it's something that can effect how he'll please the crowd. His eagerness to fight has lead him to train everyday for a few months. He is pretty light, only at 215lbs. then again he is 6 foot. He has about 4% body fat, which means he has some muscle to him, but without it he could still fight. Coyle is a tough kid, a crazy kid, and a flexible, athletic kid. He has always been in fights throughout his life, and because of that, this is just a walk in the park to him. However, the fact that he works out just makes him a harder menace. Coyle is ready to fight, and as he waits to be informed on who he is going to take-on for his debut match, he sits alone in the dark at his kitchen table. 'Let's see, it's 6:13, they'll probably call between now and 9:00. I mean, they have to, they have to let me know I've been scheduled, I mean, I am looking forward to it. I mean, they just have to, right? Of course.' Probably because Coyle is so anxious, is why he is talking to himself like this. For once in his life, he has been given a break, one that he doesn't know how to handle, but for once in his life, he actually isn't the peon of the story. 'Where can they be, it's about 6:24, I wonder if they'll call.' He still waits by his phone impatiently. 'Hrmm, 6:58, come on, hurry up.' A couple of moments later. '7:18, huh? HURRY THE HELL UP!' And just a few more. '7:36, sigh.' Obviously, Coyle is waiting eagerly; but why? It isn't like he's fighting tonight. Perhaps it is just to get ready, or perhaps it is for comfort, but whatever it is, it surely has him all hollered up, he is practically breathing out adrenaline. Finally, the phone rings. He picks up, and gives a faint hello. The person on the other line goes on with what they had to say. Eventually he hangs up after a while. 'God damn telemarketers...' Ouch, that pisses him off. The clock's minute hand keeps moving, 7:38, 7:39, 7:40...7:49, and then the phone rings again. Before the phone started to ring, Coyle was playing with a ball and a paddle, but as soon as he heard the phone ring again, he dropped the two items, and steered at the phone. It rang another time, and he couldn't move. One more time, and his fingers slowly got feeling in them. For the fourth time, his body leaped towards the phone, and he innocently asks hello. He stood there for a while, just doing the typical 'yep, ugh-hmm, yes,' just letting the other person talk. Eventually though, when he hung up, he stood quiet. He slowly lowered himself to his seat because he wanted to sit down, he had to, he felt like he was about to faint. He was completely dead, it appears that he didn't get the call he wanted. He closes his eyes, and let's out a rough sigh, and tilts his head downward. As he keeps lowering to the seat, he loosens his body up. Before he hits the seat, however, he jumps up real high, maybe a few feet, as anxious as a train, and as loud as a bell. 'You got it, Angel! I'm coming, my man, I'm coming, the O is coming!' Completely enthusiastic and ready for this match, Coyle walks around his house at 40mph. And can't stop for anything, one thing that he wanted so bad has actually happened, now all he has to do is win... 'Which shouldn't be so hard!' Absolutely and positively ready for this match, Coyle starts thinking. 'I can give him a Beermug, yeah, that's a good punch to start things out, and I finish him off with, hrmm... I'll call it the Clover. Yeah, that's it, the Clover, a super-duper uppercut that hits his, hrmm, jaw, no throat, no both, and I'll jump when I do it. I'll even wind up. Hell, I can do two things at once, so I'll trip him as I'm uppercutting him. That can be my ultimate move, no way you can continue on after getting hit by that.' He is so anxious that he actually just made up his moves. It's something he can't wait patiently for, and it's something he wants so bad. Perhaps, this can all be a good thing on his self-esteem, and on his life as a whole. He finally is getting something he wants for the first time, and he realizes it. But what he isn't thinking about is that he is, afterall, the 'O'. Either way, no matter what, it seems as if that won't effect him at all right now, but we can only see...
