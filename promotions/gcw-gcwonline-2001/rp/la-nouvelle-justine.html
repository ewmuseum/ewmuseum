Iberia Once Upon a Time The chill of the air was lost on her, though she was beginning to feel the cold of the snow seeping into her bones from below her thin shoes. But she couldn’t move, couldn’t motivate herself towards the energy to go home, or the energy to go farther into the woods. Strewn before her were carcasses, dozens of them. They were too small to be anything but rabbits, and she had often seen her own grandmother skin and cook rabbits in the stew, especially in the spring, when the rabbits were plentiful. This was somehow different, twisted into something shadowy and fantastical, fuzzed around the edges and incomprehensible in its horror. Her mind ran in circles, concocting stories about the blood-stained snow, the bits of fur strewn about in gory clumps and the tiny bones that had long since been snapped in half by much more powerful teeth. There was only one explanation for it, of course: the hunting store of a pack of wolves, which had slowly built up over time. She could imagine them in her head, bringing the kill back to this half-concealed clearing for the pups and adults to tear apart. The growling and yipping, the voracious appetites, the hunters tearing at their prey, ripping it apart until there was nothing left of any nourishment, and strewing the remnants on the ground to step on until they too became distant memory. On to the next meal. It was the long, protracted howl, a lonely, dreadful sort of sound, that sent the hair crawling erect on the back of her neck. It was joined by another, lower and softer, hulking in the shadows of the more plaintive cry, feral and dangerous and huge. A chorus rang out, low voices, tiny yipping pups, all ranging together, all around her’she couldn’t gauge direction, and wasn’t trained in that sort of thing anyhow. That was when she realized that the wolves were far closer than she had originally taken them to be. Another howl split the dusk, and it came to her’this was not the self-satisfied cry of a sated pack? it was a warning. Intruders in the territory. Cold fear gripped down on her, much colder, much more numbing to the reason than the snow all around her. Every tree sagged under the burden of heavy snow, cloaked in obscuring ice that made discerning direction impossible. She didn’t know what to do, only that she had to act. A last look at the unmarked graves of a dozen lapines convinced her that, stupid or not, there was only one course of action to take. Run. Adrenaline coursing through her, shooting jolts of electricity straight down to her spine, she closed her senses to the golden gleaming in the thickets rimming the clearing. They were not eyes, she told herself, and it didn’t matter if they were anyhow. A slight frightened stumble through the brambles on the other side put her out of the clearing’the path had been practically snowed over, and the gray clouds above meant that more would be coming soon. Survival instincts clenched deep within the pit of her stomach, clawing her into action. Her muscles burned with acid as they burst into action. White-knuckling her long skirts, she bolted, with no thought but finding the nearest tree that looked like it could withstand her weight, and clambering up as high as she could. Her mind raced back’three months, six months, eight months, ten. Wolves, wolves, everywhere there were wolves in memory, snapping their fangs just out of reach of her skin, held back by some unknowable force. It had been nearly two years ago that the lie had been told. She still remembered vividly the warmth of that blood covering her hands, the way it stained her small dagger all the way up to the hilt and spilled over onto the ground. And the wounds, large and small, Jaroslav only a memory of what he was’mangled, he didn’t look like such a good choice for a husband. She had run to the priest as quickly as she could, in mimic of panic, and told him the best story she could concoct: a walk in the woods, a surprise wolf attack. Jaroslav stepped in front of her to protect her, she said. He’d been struck down, but had killed the most voracious wolf with his sword, she said. They ran into the woods, but Jaroslav had already lost enough blood? Shaking, tear-stained, she’d led him to the body. And when the priest had prayed over him and asked for the blessing of his soul in purgatory, it was then that the sin had passed for righteousness. She had given him a hero’s death, but in promoting that lie, had promoted one of her own’and left the scene of her first murder unscathed. She knew these wolves now. The wolves that she ran from were those that she’d conjured in her mind to make Jaroslav into a memory. They had come back to exact the price they had paid for their guilt; she could still remember wolf pelts strung up and stinking in the town square after the incident. More blood on her hands. The thought turned into a scream just as she saw the tree, only a scant few steps away from her, and reached for it with a desperate hand, which looked clawlike and ugly in the half light. Her fingertips brushed over its rough bark, and she was about to use the strength of her fear to pull herself up, when she felt a jaw seize down on her heel? as the bones crumpled together, she knew, suddenly, what it must have felt like to be a rabbit? She opened her eyes with a cold sweat pouring down her forehead, her entire body trembling as though in a seizure. A hard pair of hands gripped around her shoulder and shook her gently. “Amore” amore, stop? it is only a dream.” The words jerked her back into an objective reality’beneath her, she felt the large expanse of counterpane that was the bed she shared with Lord Sterescu. She felt the hands, the cold metal of his wedding band pressing into her skin. But she couldn’t make sense of it’it had all been so real, the cold, the fear, the screaming, the pain? “I” I?” she stammered, trying to make it all come together in words that he could have understood, trying to make it into something that he could soothe away. There were no words forthcoming. “Shhh?” His arms wrapped around her tighter, his lips pressing softly to her forehead. He wanted always for every affair of her life to be well-settled and joyous; soothing away the demons that haunted her in sleep was no different a task than helping her at the hearth when the servants had not done a satisfactory job scrubbing the stones. This, though, was perplexingly more difficult. Her other problems were tangible; this was no more than a tenacious phantom, continuously pricking at the hidden folds of her psyche with reminders of a past that she was desperately trying to elude. Just like any night, she’d forget her dreams by the time the next three days had passed. What wouldn’t go away was the feelings that came with them, that feeling that the thread holding up her personal Sword of Damocles was dwindling to so thin a strand that the next puff of wind would cause it to fall. Squirming slightly, she stretched a hand down to her heel’she could have sworn she still felt it aching, spilling over blood. But it was whole. Slowly, the pain faded into the ether, leaving her only with the memories, and the guilt. .:: ::: ::. Prague Once Upon a Time The narrowing of her eyes was like the dimming of light peeping through a crack. It was a thoughtful look, slanted slightly to the side as she tilted her head to stare at her creation. By no means would you have called it beautiful. But what seemed to be only a collection of hastily cobbled together bits of mottled flesh and sinew had truly taken her weeks upon weeks to compile to her satisfaction. As per Vaclav’s advice, she had not put down the design on paper first, but had envisioned the end result vividly, and built slowly, with that in mind’and it had changed as she worked, just as he told her it would. This was to be her first vozhd, and would be the mark of accomplishment that would allow her to move to the next level of training. For that reason, she wanted to make sure it was perfect before she animated it, and showed it to her sire. It lay on the stone table that occupied the center of the room, lit all the way round by the hanging candelabras. Its skin was grayish, mottled by rough patches where the flesh of one individual had not quite blended into another. The whole surface was pockmarked by bulbous near-appendages that hid beneath them bulging muscles, capable of rending off the arms of anyone who might assail the castle with one clawed swipe. And there were indeed claws. At the end of its six arms, which were nearly four feet long at their longest were three-inch claws, deep gray with a metallic sort of sheen glistening on the top. The rest of the beast, still stretched inanimate, was massive. Without measuring, she gauged with her eye that it had turned out nearly fifteen feet tall. A slight smile of pride graced her face’she didn’t think that Vaclav had such high expectations for her first effort at this, and she was his favorite student. No doubt, he would be pleased. She finished grinding the concoction necessary for the last of the rite in the mortar. With a calculated gentleness, she set down the marble pestle and carried the mortar over to the clay poultice that was heating on a marble slab near the fire. With great care, she turned the bowl sideways, spilling the mash onto the clay. Delicate fingers massaged them into the top of the clay, which she carried carefully over to her vozhd. For a moment, she was almost afraid to try it, afraid to see what she had created? but that quickly passed as an undignified thought, one more suited to the mortals she had been taught to regard as lower beings than to herself. She chewed her lip for a moment, then flipped the clay, mash side down, onto the chest of the creature, where a gaping wound still slashed through the ugly flesh, waiting for the very mixture she was now applying. For a moment, it was all held breath and anticipation. She knew it would take a few moments, and any moment now, she should see its first stirrings of life. She waited a minute? two? then that turned into ten, then fifteen. Nothing. Cursing under her breath, she clenched her fists at her side. She’d done everything right, all to get to the final point of this, and? nothing! She ran the entire process, all the arduous hours, over in her head, but there was nothing she could think of that she could have left out, no mistake that she could have made’she’d followed Vaclav’s instructions to the letter, and it still hadn’t worked. She glowered at the creature, though she knew it wasn’t the creature’s fault. She glowered at the spare stacks of bones laying to its side that she had chosen not to use for appearance’s sake, and glowered at the fleshy pile of refuse in the corner, the offal of the bodies she had used to compile the animal. She hated all of them at the moment. But she knew it was no use hating them, and with a head heavy with the anticipation of Lord Ionescu’s chastising, she skulked out of the room and up the twirling stone staircase that led to his private chambers. When she tapped on the door and heard his half-annoyed sounding response, she knew this was going to be a long afternoon. “Master Ionescu,” she said softly, bowing her head, “I humbly request your assistance in my laboratory.” He raised his eyebrows at her ever-so-slightly. “My help, Lia” I thought you were aware that the rules of this trial require you to finish the vozhd on your own.” “It is finished,” she said, “only, I went to animate it, following all the instructions that you gave me, and” nothing happened.” Vaclav sighed, standing up from his table. She could see the disappointment written in his features, and that was the worst part of it all. She felt like she was being crushed under a heavy stone weight. “I will come and look at your mess, childe, but it will be your own responsibility to fix it, is that clear?” Silently she nodded, and then found herself following him back down the stairs at a brisk clip to the laboratory level. As soon as they walked into her station, he paused in the doorway, surveying what she had done. The slight smile that passed over his face was at least reassuring. “Let us see,” he murmured to himself, stepping immediately over to the table. For a few moments, it was all hanging in silence, him simply nodding his head as he checked things over, murmuring various noises of approval under his breath. Finally, he leaned over the creature’s chest and sniffed delicately. And sniffed again. “Ahh?” he said, pointing over towards the table, “Did you remember your wormwood?” She stamped her foot. “Pah! I had everything else right!” He smiled. “Every detail must be perfect, or the entire project will fall back into its component parts” remake your poultice, and try again.” He started towards the door, then turned back to her seriously again. “And, when you have the animation complete, don’t forget to remove it and quickly seal the wound.” She nodded, her mind already sinking back into contemplation of her task, so quickly absorbed that she hardly heard the door close as he left. With a frown, she pulled off the first poultice, and flung it into the pile of refuse along with the rest of what she hadn’t used. A soft, but not aggravated, sigh passed her lips as she went back to work. Her eyes passed over the creature once more, almost lovingly despite its vile appearance, even the vile stench that had begun to emanate from it in the past few days. This was all part of the task, a sacrifice made towards the end of something more beautiful? sort of. The herbs in their correct quantities nearly flew into the mortar, with the speed at which she worked. She’d memorized them and recited them in her head many times over so that this was nearly automatic, instead of something she had to think about. Her eyes caught a glimpse of a face, or what used to be one, sheltered in the crook of one of the vozhd’s powerful elbows. It was sort of an engineering flaw’extra flesh didn’t just produce itself, and so she had used a face, removed of its eyes, of course. In a way, she liked the way it looked, a smooth, whole patch of skin amid a jumbled together mess where the other pieces were no more than an inch in width. It gave character, she thought to herself with a smile. For a moment, as she kneaded another poultice back into shape, she wondered what it would be like to be this creature of her creation’a hundred different component parts, a hundred different voices inside, each one howling for dominance. It was no wonder that the vozhd had no sentient thoughts, and were consumed by their rage from the moment they were created. It was for that reason, after all, that they made the perfect war ghouls. For a moment, she almost felt sorry for her creation? only, not sorry enough to stop. With a half-smile, she carried the complete poultice back over to the table, and stretched her hands out over the creature, bearing the compound like it was a crown. “Better luck this time, I hope?” .:: ::: ::. Seattle, Washington April 10, 1994 “Lia and Beckham” the two of you will stay here, and guard our rear flank, from outside. Under no circumstances do I want you in the building unless I personally call you in as emergency backup. Beckham? you are to guard Lia’s life as you would your own.” Beckham slid out his Luger and nodded curtly. This was an assignment he was used to’because he was the fiercest in the pack, and thus often the least easily restrained, when the mission required delicate stealth, he would often find himself outside, guarding Lia, their medic, and making sure that no enemies could slip in from the backside and surround the others. The rest of Morningstar Regiment filed in slowly, counting off thirty seconds between entry with Dante heading off the line. In three minutes, all of them were inside. Now, it was only a waiting game, until someone got hurt, until Dante called for backup, or until they went back to their lair unscathed’the least likely of the three scenarios in this particular case. Unit Lodestone of the Camarilla was inside, holding a meeting on how best to stabilize the territory of Seattle and make it into a Camarilla stronghold from which to engage the Anarch Free State of California. The problem with that was? Seattle was very nearly Sabbat turf. The Camarilla had been kicked out three months ago, and anarchy had reigned since then’but an anarchy so controlled that the only conclusion could have been that the Sabbat was at its machinations, and about to install a new Archbishop over the city. Unit Lodestone was a prestigious unit that had come under fire from Camarilla leadership lately’they were full of young upstarts whose talents varied from organizational skills to battle prowess. They’d been sent out to organize Seattle in order to get them out of the elders? hair, and hopefully to quell their discontent enough that they could be depended upon to not break away and form a rogue unit. After tonight, they’d have no chance of that. Because Morningstar Regiment’s orders were shoot to kill. Of course, for Lia and Beckham, it was a waiting game now. Beckham seemed to be amusing himself by picking out a clot of gore from his incisor with a toothpick’one of the many he always carried around for idle moments such as this. Lia quickly found that rather distasteful, and turned away from him, absorbed in her own thoughts, which at this moment were willing the team to come out unscathed. A sharp blast from inside caused both of them to snap to attention. It was followed by a round of gunfire, and a few words, which Lia could not clearly make out, even with her acute hearing. “Sounds like the shit’s hit the fan,” Beckham noted, with his usual inverse-of-eloquence. “Yes,” she rejoined, waiting on pins and needles for a moment. More gunfire, and then Lia’s radio leapt to life. “Lia,” came Dante’s heavy Spanish voice, “Kyle’s been hit by a trap. He can’t continue on with his arm in the state that it is, so I’m sending him out to you, and the rest of us will go on as planned.” It snapped off after that, and she didn’t dare respond to him, for fear of making more noise than was necessary as they crept ever closer to the tiny, concealed room that Unit Lodestone was using to meet. As it was, it was probably going to be a firefight in there, now that Lodestone was aware of an infiltration, and she didn’t want to risk making them more obvious targets. Beckham’s nostrils flared. “I smell blood” plenty of it, coming this way.” Lia winced. “I hope I have what I need to take care of it?” There was no time to worry about it, however, because moments later, the backdoor burst open. Beckham trained his Luger on it, making sure that this wasn’t some pre-emptive prelude to Kyle’s appearance, but Kyle Nahuatl was indeed the only one to stumble out the door. His shooting arm, his right arm, was clenched close to his body, and Lia could see a blood-stain soaking upward. His face was racked with pain, his gait that of an animal limping and trailing an injured limb along the ground’an animal on its last legs. She resisted her urge to scoff. Until she saw it, that is. “Ah!” Kyle groaned as she pulled his arm away from his side to get a closer look. Whatever was in the trap had done a fine job of mangling his arm, shooting shrapnel up and down it in a pattern that would have made a land-mine designer a mint. She snapped into action, the gore having no effect on her for the moment; if she didn’t? act fast, there was a distinct possibility that Kyle wouldn’t have use of his shooting arm ever again, and it was the arm that he needed to disarm traps anyway’which was probably what he had been doing when the trap went off. Lia flipped open her case, and pulled out the pair of tweezers she had imagined when she’d first seen the wound, larger, but still precise enough to pull the small pieces out. Gripping his forearm near the joint with the elbow, she flipped the tweezers to a working position in her right hand, and pulled out the first piece of shrapnel, the biggest piece she could see. “SHIT!” Kyle jerked his arm nearly hard enough to pull it out of her hand, had she not clamped down on her grip just as he did it. Red tears surged upwards to his eyelids, but the look that she gave him caused a cold-cocked reaction in his body that pulled them back in as he was reduced to growling ferally. She pulled out another piece, and then another with deft jerks. She knew it hurt him, but there were only two choices here, and leaving the jagged scraps of metal inside his skin certainly presented the worst of the two. She turned his arm palms up and found what looked to be the biggest piece yet’and it would have to be worked out slowly because of its proximity to his veins to boot. She clamped down on it and slowly started to work it loose. “AAAAHHHH!” “Damn,” Beckham said, leaning over to look closer, “Can’t you do anything to lessen the pain?” “I left my pharmacist’s license at home,” she said, in a quiet, even tone that indicated she really had no time for such trivial quibbling at the moment. She was halfway through the large piece, and stopped in the middle of pulling it out to give Beckham something of a dirty, though completely emotionless, look. Kyle’s squirming could be held back no longer, as she and Beckham faced off, and the tweezers slipped slightly, serving to jam the metal back into its former hole. “FUCK!” “Shut up!” she snapped, ice in her eyes as she turned back to him, “Shut the hell up, unless you want to blow our cover!” “Man, can’t you just leave it in there” The skin’ll grow back over it, right?” She gave Beckham a weary look. “Yes, it’s that simple, that’s why I’m putting him through this unnecessarily. If I leave this in, his arm may get infected, and he may lose it for good. I don’t think that’s what he wants, or what the Regiment needs, so I suggest you go back to your post and let me finish my work.” There was no questioning her’she was the pack priest, and other than Dante, her orders came second to no one. She turned back to Kyle, who was trembling with the effort not to make a sound, his eyes shining bright with pain. “Isn’t there” anything for pain?” he asked, evidently either not having heard hear earlier response to Beckham, or perhaps to senseless with the pain to remember her answer. “Maybe so,” she said, pausing to flip open the case at her waist, the little clip that held extra bullets of varying kinds, depending on the mission. She slipped out a silver one, mainly used for lupine missions, and showed it to him. “What’s that for?” The confusion had distracted him from his pain for a moment, and she slipped it between his lips, smiling at him with a grim, cold sort of look. “Bite.” .:: ::: ::. The Present Words from the Accused And so it is that I stand on trial, before a tribunal that has been convened in my honor, forced to stand in front of the masses and justify why I speak to whom I speak to, why I prefer to wear black instead of yellow, why I prefer to sleep only six hours a night instead of the recommended eight, why I blink my eyes the way that I do. Any number of inane and ridiculous accusations have been leveled at me, but you see, this is a show trial, where my words mean nothing, and will be selectively perused and picked to pieces by those who have a certain something they want to glean from them. Allow me to speak plainly, if I may. I stand accused of many things, the first among them the charge that I am a false representation of what I call “my kind,” that I am only smoke and mirrors, an act to desperately cover up a tiny and fragile person who desperately wants to escape a lackluster reality. I assert, as I have always asserted, that such charges that I “live in a fantasy world” or that I am “only faking” or that “it’s all a publicity stunt” are utterly and completely false. But I will make no move to prove the doubters wrong. Perhaps that seems strange? you may say to yourself, “if she really wants people to believe her, then she should prove herself in a public capacity so that no one would be able to question her again.” This, too, is a fallacy, a lie that you are telling yourself because you wish to play witness to another PTC spectacle, which I will not indulge you by becoming. Were I to prove the truth of my existence, it would not be enough. There would still be those of you to say that you wanted more, those of you who called it all a magic act, or Hollywood special effects. Not to mention, publicly proving that those who doubt what I am are idiots would undoubtedly call a great deal of attention to me? not all of it good, as there are still those of my kind who adhere to the ridiculous codes of law that say we must pretend to be what we are not among those whose tiny worlds might otherwise be upset by knowing of our existence. What I am saying, in essence, is that I cannot force you to listen to something that you have already closed your ears to. And thus, whatever I am, and whatever I will become will have to remain a mystery. You will accept what I am, or you will not, and whichever you choose to do is not any of my concern. I live as I live’so long as I know the truth of my own state, what does it matter whether others see it? I might suggest also, perhaps to the consternation of this “just” and “honorable” court, that this preoccupation with what I am and what I do is covering something much, much deeper. Despite what you may think of me, I am not entirely ignorant of psychological machinations’it is a long-held notion that those who must spend so much time focused on the faults of others do so in order to mask a great fault within themselves. Additionally, for anyone to suggest that it is “normal” to spend so much time obsessing over an opponent, and what she does and says so that any little flaw or inconsistency may be picked apart? well, who’s to call what I do abnormal? I, at least, go along living my own life as I must without concern as to whether or not others are living their lives the same as I live mine. Is it not the greatest act of hubris to assume that one individual has the market cornered on truth or normality. In fact, I might suggest that it is pathological to think that way’sociopathic, even. Aside from all this rhetoric of persecuting the “other,” which really, is all too common in human history, there are other things which I must address? I do not so much mind being this “other,” as it is of very little concern to me what others think of me? but surely, I should be expected to speak in my own defense. My reason for even stepping in the ring has been questioned. I find this dishonorable from an opponent, even more dishonorable from someone who seems to have nothing to do with his time but hold a grudge. Allow me to let you in on a little secret: the real reason I am here is because I find this hobby to be pleasantly diverting. And not even diverting so much as it is simply pleasant. I consider myself something of a scientist, you see’of course, if you buy into the notion that my entire past life has been fabricated, this won’t make any sense to you, but as I have said, I already knew this jury was stacked against me from the beginning. Anyway, as I was saying? it’s all a matter of pain, and I don’t mean my own. Pain is an interesting motivator’it is the stimulus that produces all aversion reactions. If something causes us pain, we avoid it, it is that elementary, that simple. But, there are those of us who seem to find pain not so much undesirable as we find it a challenge? and it is these very beings that I am interested in. Those who endure pain far beyond the normal limits of what those not in this profession would consider acceptable. You might say? that I am interested in helping them find those limits. Everyone needs someone to test them, correct? In any case, I would like to point out that this is a two-fold argument. I have given my reasons for stepping into the ring week to week in the previous paragraph. Yet, there is still a question lingering in this instance, one which I find very important to the matter at hand. Is everyone who signs a contract to PTC subjected to this sort of rigorous scrutiny as to their motives? Is everyone expected to stand on a table and shout to the top of their lungs why, exactly, they find it necessary to step into a ring and compete every week? Furthermore, the question is, aside from a few obsessed nutcases, who would care about such things? Would the PTC fans really listen earnestly as each one of us took a soapbox in order to justify every single action we have ever taken in our careers? Would they care, or would they fall asleep? Or change the channel? Or would they simply find it ridiculous that such things were called into question in the first place? My point is? when it comes down to the hard truth of the matter, no one cares what individual motivations we may have. No one case what our lives are like outside of the ring. From a fan’s standpoint, it doesn’t matter what we do with ourselves otherwise, so long as we come into the ring and make them feel like they didn’t waste their money on a ticket. From a career wrestler’s standpoint, what matters only is that there is a warm body across the ring. It doesn’t matter what that person’s past is, doesn’t matter how they got where they are today, doesn’t matter where they grew up, or what they think about the current state of world politics. What matters is the physical. Action-to-action, finisher-to-finisher, hand-to-hand. How you stack up. Whether you can face someone without fear in your eyes, no matter the advantage he has over you. What you can bring to the ring that your opponent does not expect. These are things that matter. The bottom line, the point that I wish to illustrate, is that it does not matter one single bit whether or not you think I have a reason to be standing across from you, in opposition to you. I am still there, when you are finished with your histrionic fit-pitching. The fact that you dislike me because of what I represent seems to be the only reason for you to question me so deeply, and so voraciously. I dislike anyone who would do so just as much’perhaps this honest recitation will trigger many others like it? Perhaps we will all be inundated in a flood of people rushing to justify themselves in the same manner, because it is evidently important to do so before you can justify yourself as a big-name player in the wider world of PTC. Well? Have I earned my wings yet? I suspect not. I suspect that you will all continue in your criticism, you will continue ridiculing me both behind and in front of my back, that you will continue hurling the same old tired insults about my supposed delusions, and your supposed superiority. I pray you will understand if I choose simply not to listen. Condemn me if you must, but understand that I am of the school that asks one to live by one’s own choices. Guilty of what you accuse me of or not, I will continue to live by my own merits, as I will hang by them. I will not change, I will not repent. .:: ::: ::. “Miserable creatures, thrown for a moment on the surface of this pile of mud, is it decreed that one half of the flock should be the persecutor of the other” Is it for you, mankind, to pronounce on what is good and what is evil?” .:: ::: ::. -The quotes at the beginning and end of this roleplay belong to Donatien Alphonse Fran’ois, le Marquis de Sade.
