The Devil's in the details.

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Today Johnny-boy Borealis. What can I say about him? 'You're a real son of a bitch, you know that?' 'Yeah, well...good to see you too.' 'What are you doing in Europe?' Straight shooter, he is. Always one to the point. Me? I take my time. Let him bake. Light up a cigarette and suck it in, ash to ash, and let him wait. Let him fester. The curiosity swelling. Why am I in Europe? 'I came to see how your progress is going with your little...assignment.' Johnny-boy Borealis' jaw drops. He's T-minus a couple hours to fighting a match that he doesn't even know why he's fighting, and he doesn't even understand that I need him for something mroe important. Something more important than his assignment of bringing down the Baptist Church for that matter. Little bastard can't see two steps in front of him in regards to where he's gotta go and how he's gonna get there. He only sees the inconvenience. 'Don. I have a match tonight. Your little 'get rid of annoying Baptists' isn't exactly on the top of my mind.' Light up another cigarette. The first one was a one-inhale extinguish. That was for affect. This one...this one and the following ones are just because I'm addicted to it. Simple. Put. Nothing else to say. There is no pleasure anymore. Once there was, back when I first discovered tobacco centuries ago. But it's worn off since then. After dying of cancer about six times, you tend to not like cigarettes much anymore. They don't have any taste of sorts. They don't even bring comfort, since comfort shall not come. Simply put, they have a hold on me. Me, for as powerful as I am, and for all that I am. Addicted. Simple. I'm sure if there were past lives for anybody else, they'd have the same afflictions. Alcoholics were alcoholics in past lives. Tyrants were tyrants. Smokers were smokers. Rapists were rapists. Johnny-boy Borealis was a fighter. I notice this because I've gotten to know quite a few people. I have been around for a while, you know. And what I've noticed, inevitably, is that there are several kinds of gladiators, fighters, and competitors out there. There are those that do it for the glory. Those that do it for the money. There are those that do it because they're good at it and they're not good at anything else. And then there are those that are just plain addicted to it. No other excuse. Nothing else that they can blame it on. They get up every fucking day and train all day long, and they eat strict diets and they hate everyone around them, and they have nothing to show for it but calloused knuckles and a constant number of injuries. Why do they fight? It's got a hold on them. They're addicted to it. 'Well...I was also kinda bored. And the ex has been pestering me again.' 'Well, come in. Grab a cold one.' Johnny-boy ushers me into his hotel room. Throws me a beer. It's not Genny, but it'll do. 'Here. It's the good shit. Not that fuckin Genny that you drink.' 'I like Genny.' 'It's piss water.' 'I like piss water.' Johnny-boy's becoming a nihilist. Slowly. He denies it with every pore of his body. But denying it only speeds up the process. Already I can see his shoulders sag a little more. His physique isn't what it was six months ago, and his smile is practically non-existant. Watching his matches, even when he wins, even when he tears through someone, the flare is leaving. He's coming to believe that there isn't a point. So why does he continue to fight and strive for beating his opponents? Addiction. 'You made a mess of Luke Hanson, by the way.' 'Luke Hanson made a mess of Luke Hanson. I just cleaned it up.' 'And now you're going after LeStatt Knight. Should be interesting.' 'If you give a flying fuck, it outta be interesting. Which means that LeStatt is going to be interested.' 'You?' 'I give a flying fuck. I just...' He'll deny it. He'll say that he's just not feeling one hundred percent. But that he'll get up and charged when the bell rings and he'll be that bright boy that went out to make a mark on the world so long ago. He'll say that one of these days he'll be coming back to officially being 'Johnny Borealis, American Hero.' But I know what he really means behind it all. He just doesn't get why he should bother. But he's going to bother anyway. It's like punching a brick wall. You know it's not going to do anything- 'I'm just going to go and do it.' 'Attaboy, Johnny-boy.' That's it, right there. I see him. I saw him do it. He looked down at his finger for the millionth time, and he certainly hasn't forgotten. 'You thinking about Kate again, Johnny-boy?' '...yeah.' 'That's probably great to do going into a match.' 'Probably.' 'Get all emo and sad. So that you're nice and tenderized when LeStatt Knight runs over your ass.' 'Well...that's my choice.' Truth is he isn't thinking much about Kate. Even when he's thinking about Kate, he isn't thinking about Kate. I've seen the look he's got on his face on countless other faces. Good friends that I've come to know that are long since dead or dying. Warriors from the ancient times. Heretics from the dark ages. Explorers, scientists, rulers, and lovers. They all have that same face. They think they're thinking about the girl. They're really thinking about someone before the girl. Johnny-boy thinks about his poor mommy. And if I still had emotions, I'd feel something for him. Because fact of the matter is that Johnny-boy isn't my servant. He's not my anti-Christ. He's simply my friend. Nothing more. Nothing deeper. No philosophical or historical context. Take away all the spiritual bullshit that you're taught. He's my friend. Has been his whole life. 'You know, Johnny-boy...I was coming up here to this very apartment building when I was approached outside. This kid comes up to me, right outside the door in this European shithole, and he comes right up, not knowing who I am or nothin, comes right up and wants to know if I want a job. Now, you and I both know I don't need no fuckin job from some scum of the Earth little shit in a hamlet in fuckin Europe. But my curiosity's piqued. This kid must be like twelve or somethin, and he's coming up to me and offering me a job. And he don't have the slightest clue who I am. So I'm figuring, okay. Maybe he just hasn't that much exposure to my usual influence on the world, and he doesn't realize who he's talking to. So I says to him, lemme know what kind of job it is. Kid beats around the bushes for a minute and eventually says that he's looking for an amateur hitman. A hitman! A fucking hitman, and he's recuiting on the street.' 'You took a hitman job?' 'What's the point of that, Johnny-boy? Killing people? Please. Boredom City. No, so I turn him down and all, right? And he offers some more money. I turn him down again, and he offers more. Now, I ended up turning him down altogether, and he flips me off and goes off to peddle drugs or something. But I got hit with an interesting notion that I hadn't thought of till now.' 'And what's that?' His half open eyes don't fool me. His interest is piqued as well. 'I realized that there was a little voice telling me to take the job. A little voice. A little devil. On me. Not that there was an angel. There was more of another devil, this one more bored. But I still had a temptation. Me.' 'So?' 'This kid was a walking temptation. I had the urge. And the urge made me think. We're all devils, Johnny-boy. Sure, maybe only a couple of us don't have the pleasure of fully dying. Maybe a couple of us have any sort of power. But we're all the devil.' 'If your argument holds, then we're all angels as well.' 'And that's the paradox I'm hit with.' Johnny-boy Borealis. America's fucking hero. Sitting there, wondering what I'm saying and sucking it in like a sponge. Two minutes ago, he wanted me gone. He was busy getting ready for a match. Now? Now he's pondering a simple thought of what I said. 'So does that mean anything?' 'For every person out there, it's the way you look at them that shows what they are. Your personal viewpoint determines whether anybody in your life is a devil or an angel. You. You sit there and you see that girl that you left as an angel, and that leaving her made you a devil. I see it as the opposite. You moved on and she's trying to drag you down. That kid was an angel, trying to offer me a job. But he was a devil, cuz I didn't want it. And the more and more you disect it, the more I realize that I, the devil, am not as much a devil as just one of many.' 'You could say the real devil then, is in the details of it all.' Johnny-boy Borealis. Thinks he gets it. He has no idea. I send him off on some bastardized errant, and he hops to because I'm his friend. Angel or devil? I don't even know. And that, more than anything, is what makes me wonder about it.
