The Day After...The Morning Before

24 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/24/05 I could still hear her screams. Chilling. They haunted me even as the sunlight traced the lengths of my face? ? Yeah, last night was good. I mean GOOD. I think she was a blonde. Though, after all the barolo, she could’ve been a heffer and I still would’ve been game. You gotta get your rocks off somehow. I let my eyes slowly adjust and looked at the alarm clock. 11 a.m. Those comforting silk sheets made me feel like spending the day in bed, but I couldn’t shake the nagging feeling that I had something to do. Thankfully, my female companion hadn’t stuck around to share her feelings this morning. Women may have to fake orgasms, but faking interest takes a lot more effort. What the hell do I have to do today? It was starting to irritate me. My memory is usually a little more reliable but I was beginning to regret not writing this phantom task down. I didn’t even know whether it was important. At this point it probably didn’t make a difference. The thing that baffled me the most was the fact that, for the first time in months, everything seemed to be laid out in front of me. My life felt the most organized it’s ever been. I knew what I was doing this week and I knew who my opponent was at NC-17. The only aspect that hadn’t been decided was the type of match itself. However, that wasn’t what was troubling me. Hell, it’s better than not knowing when you’re going to get your next paycheck. I finally sat up, digging my toes into the plush blue carpet beneath them. Surprisingly, the room stood in good condition. Clearly the party hadn’t been as wild as my ego led me to believe. Maybe that was a good thing. I finally gathered up enough energy to make my way to the window and get a better view of the outside world. It was an unseasonably gorgeous day. I was half-expecting to hear the drone of lawnmowers and the shrieks of children playing in their yards. Alas, the reality of fall turning into winter was becoming more and more apparent. Of course, the chance of snow was minimal, but the rain clouds were slowly gathering in the distance, preparing for an onslaught of nasty weather. The gathering storm. I mused. It was so obviously symbolic that I almost didn’t notice it. WorldWide was directly in font of me, and NC-17 loomed in the near future. I was looking forward to it. I’d missed the first NC-17 due to injury. The second edition had seen one of the most brutal matches of my career. There was only one difference between that match and the upcoming clash with The Marauder, that one had been for a belt’this one was much more personal. *BOOM!* The thunder nearly shook the house. Just like that, a promising day had been turned dreary. I hated the rain. It wasn’t the rain as much as it was the pale gray landscape that the rain brought with it. Some people found that shade of gray to be depressing. I simply became lethargic whenever the overcast was upon me. Now what? I thought bitterly. Flying, even if it were first-class, wouldn’t be very enjoyable in this weather. I had to be at the airport in a little over two hours. The rest of the roster would’ve arrived in Philadelphia yesterday, but I had’business to take care of. Besides, it’s not like they were paying me to spend the extra day there. I headed towards the bathroom. Hopefully, I’d have time to get a light workout in’and accomplish that other nagging errand I couldn’t remember’before I left for the airport. That first warm blast of water from the showerhead hit me like a hot meal. I let the water run down the back of my neck to knock off the rest of the morning chills. The hangover that was dominating the back of my head soon began to retreat. Focus. I stepped out of the shower and grabbed a towel. The mirror had fogged over so I wiped it with one end of the towel. I stared at my reflection for a while, hoping it would jog my memory. Haircut? No, I had done that last weekend. It looked pretty damn good too. Do I have to buy something? That seemed to ring some bells but really led me no further. I forced myself to push the disturbance from my mind. There were more important issues to focus on. Issues such as, what kind of match I would devise if Marauder lost tonight. It didn’t matter. He doesn’t stand a chance. No, Marauder didn’t stand a chance’but there had been rumors. You KNOW he won’t go there. Or did I? Of course not. He doesn’t trust himself to take it to the next level. He’s weak. Maybe, but I had to be ready for anything. There’s no time for doubt now. I breathed in heavily. No, now was not the time for doubt. I’d gotten far enough without it. I dressed quickly and grabbed my duffel bag. As I headed to the door, my mind was continuing to run in circles. Focus. I checked the clock again. 11:45 a.m. There was still time to go to the gym.
