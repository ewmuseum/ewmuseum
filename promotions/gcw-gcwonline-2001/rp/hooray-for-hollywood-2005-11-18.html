Hooray for Hollywood

18 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/18/05 '...and this is going to be the bar set, that over there is going to be the interior of the ship, and that set over there, which is still under construction is going to be the parking structure where Lex the Marauder gets run over after he and The Smuggler have their fistfight.' I nodded and marvelled at how fast the sets had come together for my movie The Smuggler. Most of the set was covered in green screen to which later the cool sci-fi backdrops would be digitally added. Then I met with The Rock, Uma Thurman, James Marsten and the newest stars to sign to be in the movie. Tyler Mane who had played Sabretooth in the X-Men movie, Steve Buscemi who had been in more movies than I could count and Jessica Beil who had been in the remake of the Texas Chainsaw Massacre and Blade: Trinity. The Rock and I had been trading Eddie Gurrero stories when Tyler, Steve and Jessica walked in and my jaw dropped. As beautiful as she was on-screen, nothing compared to seeing her live and in sho nuff living color. 'I'm a married man, I'm a married man, I'm a happily married man.' I muttered under my breath. 'You and me both.' The Rock said. 'Jeff...hit me.' I held my hand out and Jeff smacked the back of my hand as hard as he could. It was a joke between us that streched back to our SWO days. 'Thank you.' I said shaking the sting off my hand. 'You're taller than I thought.' I said to Tyler who laughed and looked down at me, not something many people can do. 'I get that a lot.' he said, 'People forget that I used to do what you do.' 'And you're a lot less creepy in person.' Jeff said to Steve. 'I don't get that a lot,' he said, 'In fact most people tell me just the opposite.' 'Well, when you've been around guys like Bryan Mayhem, Jason O'Neil, Toxic and LeStatt Knight, like we have, there's not a lot left to creep you out.' Jeff said. 'But you have played some great creeps,' I added, 'Especially Garland Green in Con Air. I love the line about singing 'Sweet Home Alabama' when you're talking about irony.' 'So where's the big man?' The Rock asked, refering to the director, the one and only Steven Speilberg. As if on cue a production weasel came up to us, 'Mr. Speilberg just got out of a production meeting and is on his way.' he said and scurried off to talk to a cameraman. A few minutes later I finally got to meet Mr. Speilberg himself. 'So here's the man of the hour.' he said walking up to me and shaking my hand. 'Who me? Nah, I'm just a guy who strung a few thousand words together that happened to make a semi-coherent plot.' I said, shrugging it off. 'Well compared to some of the crap that's been churned out, it's probably not what people expected out of a wrestler.' he said. 'One thing we wrestlers are good at it's acting.' The Rock said. 'Really?' I said, 'Cause Hulk Hogan and Roddy Piper have made some crappy movies. Trust me, I've seen Mr. Nanny, and those are 90 minutes of my life I'll never get back.' 'And let's not even get into Hell Comes To Frogtown.' Jeff added. I shivered, 'At least you-' I indicated the Rock, '-can actually act.' 'Speaking of which,' Mr. Speilberg (I couldn't bring myself to call him Steven) said, 'Let's get you guys into makeup for the scene.' I had taken the liberty of writing myself a part as a bartender in the bar scene in where the Smuggler meets Wild (played by Jeff), and gave myself two lines. 'Yeah, whaddya want?' and 'Hey! Keep your tentacles on the other side of the bar!' I went into makeup and came out looking like an extra from the mini-series 'V'. The prostetics were making my face sweat, and then there was a lot of sitting around as Jeff had to get into the full body suit for Wild. Then the mechanics for the face didn't work, and that took an hour to fix, and then we finally got to film the scene. I had to swat at nothing for the second line I had. The tentacles in question would be digitally added later. Then I got out of makeup. Taking prostetics off hurts. You know how it is when you take a band-aid off? Either you do it fast or slow but either way it hurts? Same thing for these. It felt like my face had gotten ripped off when they finally pulled them off my face, little by little, and I was sweating like a pig. 'I think after this, I'll stick to wrestling.' I said after getting all the fake scales peeled off, 'Not quite as painful.' I will admit to being somewhat awed by the whoe movie making experience though, and it did amaze me how much patience these guys had for doing the same scene over and over again from five different angles. Not to mention having to compose themselves when someone flubbed a line, or if the lighting wasn't right, or if something didn't look right, making sure that things in the background didn't change. 'I don't know how you guys do it.' I said later in the commisary as we broke for lunch. 'I don't think I could stand to have to do the same thing over and over and over again.' 'I don't know how you can do what you do,' James Marsten said, 'Getting beat up night after night after night. I hear you guys are always in pain.' 'Well, it's not quite that bad,' The Rock said. 'Yeah,' Jeff added, 'There was one point in my career where I was convinced that my body was just used to the abuse that I heaped on it night after night. But really, we just learn to block out the pain.' 'Maybe you do,' I said, 'But I still wake up with aches and pains. My back still hurts from that buried alive match at Devil's Night. And my jaw is still sore from that fight I had with Borealis backstage last night. For a little SOB, he hits hard.' 'Well, you'll get your chance to take it out on him at NC-17.' Jeff said. I nodded, I didn't like the fact that Eva was getting a chance to compete in the match, but at this point there was nothing I could do about it. I'd beaten her before, and I could beat her again. Of course I'd have to beat her and Johnny to become the US champion, but I didn't doubt myself for a second. I never did, if you did that, you already lost. Shooting went well into the night, and I had retired back to my hotel around 11. I'd already made arrangments to fly to Philly tomorrow and concentrate on the next WorldWide where I had to face Typhoid Mary. And after that, onto NC-17, and my second GCW title. The words Mike Savige, GCW United States Champion had a nice ring to them.
