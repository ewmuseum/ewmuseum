Date: 03/11/11 The Golden Touch, Part III: Chris Bagwell "Because at the end of the day, it doesn't really matter how many tournaments you won or titles you've held. At the end of the day, all that matters is your love for the game...and the legacy you left with it." ---- As I laid there and looked into her eyes, I saw shooting stars shimmering in a clear-lit sky. The sunlight trickled through my window and set upon her face, bringing a soft glow against her rosy cheek. She smiled, looking back at me. "Why are you staring," whispering curiously? My lips rolled back, returning to her a smile. "You," I said with conviction. "You're just..." Her eyebrows rose in anxious anticipation and my heart had skipped beat. Truth was - I had never felt so passionately for someone. I was nervous, eager, and excited all at once. I knew not how to act. "You're..." I choked on my words, and felt like a fool. But her eyes were comforting. They told me a story of a girl who had waited all her life for a guy to come along and treat her right. They soothed me. They let me know that she didn't mind waiting just a few minutes longer to hear what I had to say. Every second felt like days. My heart beat faster. It beat harder. And then I dug down deep to say what I really felt inside. The only word that could describe the emotions racing through my body and engulfing my mind. "...amazing." She blushed, with all sincerity, and kissed me on the lips. She didn't need to say anything. That moment said enough. ___________________________________ The air was brisk and tantalizing, but that was nothing out of the ordinary for a January night in Salem Massachusetts. Just as quickly as I pulled into her driveway, Jenny bolted for her front door. "Come on, Chris," she shouted. "My mother will crucify us if we're late!" It was the first time I'd be Jenny's parents, so showing up late for dinner wasn't an option. I scurried from the car, up the driveway, nearly forgetting my keys on the driver's seat. "I'm coming, I'm coming." Jenny and I met through mutual friends and the chemistry we shared was something you'd find in a science lab. She was peanut butter and I was jelly - we became great friends, inseparable. Although we were nothing close to being a couple, Jenny insisted on bringing me home and introducing me to her parents. She'd always pinned it as though she just wanted her parents to meet the guy who'd been looking out for her. I'd traditionally done well with parents, so I had no qualms with that. "And here's the class of 1997 now!" Jenny's mother was standing in the kitchen in her red apron, spatula in hand, as we walked through the door. She'd been entertaining family members, trying to plan for Jenny's high school graduation party in a few short weeks. "Hiii mom," Jenny said enthusiastically. "Hiii Nona, Auntie Sue! This is my friend Chris," as she pointed in my direction. My cheeks reddened - in part because I was cold, in part because I was nervous. "Hey! It's nice to meet you all." I was greeted with smiles and waves and the obligatory awkward comment, "So you're the boy whose been keeping our Jenny out late on the weekends?" Her grandmother was half kidding-half serious in her question, and I really hadn't the slightest clue how to respond. But Jenny came to my rescue, "NONA! It's not like that, kay? Thanks!" Recognizing that the conversation wasn't going anywhere pleasant, Mrs. Paris quickly changed the subject, "I hear you're planning to play football for Rutgers University next year, Chris? "Yes, that's the plan right now. I was offered a partial scholarship already, but I'm waiting to hear back on whether or not they're going to offer me a full ride." "That's exciting," she said with approval. "Definitely is. I'm really looking forward to the opportunity," I added. "A football player," interjected a deeper, stern voice. At first I thought Auntie Sue was actually in the process of becoming Uncle Sue. Much to my relief, however, I turned my head to Mr. Paris entering the room. "You've definitely got the build of an athlete. What position? Wide receiver?" "Cornerback, sir," I said with authority. "Excellent," he replied with approval. "Pardon me for my manners. I'm Mr. Paris. Jenny's father." Mr. Paris extended his hand while holding intimidating eye contact. I answered with a shake, nervous that he'd break every one of my fingers with the firmness of his grip. "Pleasure to meet you, Mr. Paris." "No need to be formal, Chris. Just call me Pete. You've got a firm handshake there, too. That's a sign of confidence. I like it." Surprised, I nodded, "Thanks Mr. P-err-- Pete!" He winked, acknowledging how incredibly nervous I was despite how much I considered myself 'good' with this type of thing. Mrs. Paris took back control of the conversation, waiving us in for dinner. "Why don't we get seated and have a bite to eat," she said. Naturally, we all complied and filtered into the dining room like a steer of cattle. And that's when we first met. "Chris, this is my sister Rebecca," Jenny said in what sounded to me like a faint echo. It had been the first time I'd ever seen Rebecca. We locked eyes, holding a deep gaze. "Hey," I muttered softly. Her cheeks reddened as a smile crept upon her face. "Hi," she replied. "It's nice to meet you." ___________________________________ "Fuck." There were few feelings worse than having a three hundred pound Native American laying on top of you while some jerkoff in a striped shirt slaps his hand against canvas for a three count. Very few. And this was one of them. Watching Rebecca lying next to Johnny Borealis sent chills down my spine - and not the kind of chills you get when you're excited, mesmerized, or pleasantly surprised. These were the kind of chills you get when you can't believe your eyes - the ones that make you question whether or not you're truly living in reality. It hurt. There are no witty remarks or half-ass comebacks that I could recite that would make that blow less painful. It was a Hiroshima or Nagasaki in my life. It was 9-11 or Katrina. "It stings. It stings badly." I sat across from my long time friend, Shane, over a glass bottle of red wine. The sound of ice cubes clinking against the glass was hardly enough to change my focus. I was fixated on the thought of Johnny and Rebecca. "I know how you feel, Chris. We've all had that one who got away." "It's not like that," I argued. "You know better than anyone what Rebecca meant to me. We weren't just some kids who gave dating a shot. Our relationship is...was stronger than most marriages." "It's just that first one, man. That first guy you see her with is always going to sting the most because that's your realization that she's moving on." "And it had to be on national television?" He shrugged with hardly any comforting justification for what Johnny did. "It's the nature of the business, I guess. You get a jerkoff like Johnny Borealis who thinks he's on top of the world and so he's going to do something that gets people talking." I sat in silence, twirling a lone cube around my nearly empty glass. In a strange way, it sort of symbolized my emotions. I was alone and with every second passed, I was deteriorating. Unless I changed my environment, at this rate it wouldn't be long until I was left as nothing. A puddle of my former self. "In all fairness, Chris... what about Jane?" I knew where he was going and it was a subject I had often tried to erase from my mind. "What about her?" "Well that's a stupid question. You know what I'm asking you." "That was different," I tried to argue. Ineffectively, I might add. "Jane and Andy had a son together, Chris. It really isn't all that different." I stared blankly across the room, "Yeah, I guess." I hated to say it but the old cliché 'what goes around comes around' was becoming eerily true. And fuck, there was nothing I hated more than hearing little seventeen year old girls running around talking about karma-this and karma-that. "I understand your devotion for Rebecca and why this all upsets you. You spent years looking for her on a hunch that she MIGHT be alive. You hardly had any proof. But if that's the case, why'd you sleep with Jane in the first place?" He began to pry. I wasn't really sure if he was still trying to justify Johnny's actions or if he was trying to legitimately help he conquer this. Either way, I really didn't want to be a part of this. I wanted everyone just to tell me I was right and be done with it. I wanted to snap Johnny's neck in two. But that just wasn't an option. "I didn't even know Rebecca was alive, Shane." "Then why'd you search so fuckin' hard? Why'd you spend so many nights awake on a whim?" "I had hope." "And I commend you for that. But if you're going to dedicate your life to a lost love, shouldn't that've influenced your decision into a one night stand with another woman? I mean, come on Chris." He was right. He was exactly what I didn't want him to be. Right. Right about it all. "I think you let go of Rebecca a long time ago. It's just that now, with timing and her choice in rebounds, it's just finally starting to nip you in the ass," Shane added. Johnny Borealis pulled a very Chris Bagwell-like move. But in doing so, he was inadvertently opening my eyes. The problem was I still just couldn't quite see clearly. ___________________________________ Initially, Jenny didn't take too kindly to my blossoming relationship with her sister Rebecca. It wasn't so much that she didn't like the type of guy I was - because she did - but more so she was upset that she had lost the most important man on her life (modesty has never really been my strong point). I think secretly, Jenny had always hoped that the two of us would get together and things would be perfect. But we were far too dissimilar for a relationship between us to have worked. It didn't take long for her to grow accustomed to sharing her time with me with her sister Rebecca. She had even told me once that "I was the greatest thing to ever come into Rebecca's life." It meant a lot to me - to get her approval, that is. With Jenny's blessing, I finally gathered the courage to ask Rebecca on a real date. At eighteen years old, your options were quite limited. To that end, we decided it best to just see a movie. "Titanic," she said enthusiastically. "Two for the Titanic." I rolled my eyes at the thought of having to spend three hours in a theater to see Leonardo DiCaprio pretend to be tough. He always came across to me as one of those people who you see and just want to knock in the face. "I can't believe I'm letting you con me into this," I teased. "You owe me...BIG!" But her smile was enough of a payment. She, like every other teenage girl in the late 90's, had idolized DiCaprio. Well, I guess it wasn't so much an idolization as it was a flagrant crush. We cozied up in the back of the theater and spent most of the movie whispering back and forth to each other. In the end, the movie wasn't all that bad. I got to see Kate Winslet nude and DiCaprio died. After the movie, we headed back to Rebecca's house. "Thanks for tonight," she whispered before kissing my cheek. We then locked eyes and within a moment, locked lips. "I had a great time." "I'm glad you came," I said with a smile. She got out of the car and walked to her front door. Like any gentleman would, I waited there to make sure she got in okay. In doing so, I took notice to something on her porch. Because it was dark and from where I was, I couldn't quite make it out, but it looked as though it might've been a dead fish with some sort of note attached to it. Tired, I dismissed it and let it be. "OH MY GOD!" Rebecca suddenly screamed and bat her arms frantically. I sprinted from my car and up to her porch. When I got to the door, I nearly threw up on myself. Rebecca, in shock, dropped to the floor. Not knowing what to do, I fell beside her, wrapping my arms around her body and holding her close as she broke out hysterically crying. In front of her laid the bodies of her mother and father, both with their hands tied behind their backs. They looked as if someone had dumped a bucket of blood all over them. Her mother is on her side with six bullet holes in her chest. Her eyes are only half shut and so every time Rebecca glances up, she feels like her mother is still staring at her and screaming for help. Her father is laying face down on the flood, blood still spewing from his skull. It appears as if he'd been shot in the back of the head. And if the bullet didn't kill him, the pool of blood certainly drowned him. Rebecca began shaking violently. "HEEEEEELLLLPPP!" I screamed as loud as I could, "WILL SOMEBODY PLEASE HELPPPPPP?" Her neighbors heard our screams and raced over, appalled at what they saw. "Where's Jenny?" one of the neighbors asked. I shook my head. I didn't see her anywhere, but I was also too afraid to move. "Son," he asked again, "let me take her to my house until the police arrive. See if you can find Jenny or get a hold of her right away." I nodded my head with acknowledgement and pulled myself up to my feet. Rebecca held my arm with a firm grip at first, nearly refusing to let me go. "I'll be right back," I whispered to her in her ear. I walked up the main staircase towards Jenny's room slowly, trying not to make a sound. I was nervous that whoever had done this might've still been in the house. With every step, I grew increasingly light-headed. Once reaching her room, I pushed the door open slightly and scanned around. There was no sign of struggle and not so much as a dash of blood anywhere. As I continued into the room, I noticed Jenny curled up in a ball in the corner. "Jenny! Thank God you're alri.." I took hold of Jenny's shoulder and her body fell limp to the floor. There was a gaping slash across her throat and the blood had all but drained out onto her shirt. I fell to the floor. My mind went blank. Twenty four hours ago, I was working up the nerve to ask Rebecca on a date. Now, I was all that she had left. ___________________________________ It felt creepy and somewhat stalker-ish. But I had to see her. I had to give myself an opportunity to clear my mind. I sat outside Rebecca's house for over an hour waiting for her to come home when her car finally pulled into the driveway. I was so intent on her not noticing me that I actually rented a car. Her hair was pulled back and her sunglasses covered most of her face, but it was still enough for me to appreciate. I remember the first time we kissed, sitting in my car just after we left the movie theater. I remember seeing her again for the first time in 6 years - the feeling that I got in my gut. I remember poking fun at her because she couldn't snap her fingers and spending hours upon hours trying to show her how. I remember the look on her face when she finally did it. I remember her smiles. I remember her laugh. I remember what her tears felt like as they hit my arm. I remember how tightly she held on to me when her parents were killed. She never wanted me to leave her alone. I remember my promise to her that I'd always be there. And then I remembered my promise to Jenny. That I'd make sure Rebecca was forever happy and would be with a man nothing short of perfect. That man wasn't me anymore. I wasn't the same kid who had his future lined up with a full sports scholarship at a reputable University. I wasn't the same kid who had a family to go home to. I was different. I had changed so drastically from who I was that I lost sight of what was most important to me: her happiness. Rebecca lifted the last bag of groceries from her trunk when I started the car. "Thanks for the memories," I said softly and pulled away. ___________________________________ It was almost like they were wearing a concrete coated glove and slapped me across the face with it. I was baffled and beside myself that two men that were supposed to be my friends would dare do something like this to me. I'm not entirely sure who I should blame first... or even blame more. Perhaps they were equally guilty. Perhaps one masterminded the whole experiment. Or perhaps they hadn't the slightest clue. Timmy was the one who organized this rally. He's the one who set me up with this charity event to speak with the specially educated kids. It's neither something I wanted to do nor something would I have ever dreamed about doing. To be perfectly honest, I was more than happy living my life knowing that every sole on this earth hated my guts. It fueled me to be better. It pushed me to work harder at achieving my ultimate goals. What satisfaction do you gain from something when everyone around you is constantly rooting for you and saying that you can do it? You go in with the mindset that you can, in fact, do it. And where's that leave you? A sore winner. Not because you're going to rub it into someone's face after you achieve what you wanted but because you won't know how to lose. You'll come crashing down and go into a deep depression. And all because everyone sat there and told you that you could for so long. I knew the feeling all too well. It's how I launched my career. Everyone sat there and boasted about how I was going to be the next big thing. Then I fucked up once and couldn't handle the heat. Next thing I know, I lose my stardom and I lose the one true love of my life. So it’s a simple deal. I stopped depending on people. I made them want to hate me and I was only happy when they did. And they do. It was completely unnecessary to do this stupid down syndrome fair and Timmy knew that. He was just trying to change an unchangeable man. A while after the whole escapade, we all ended up back at some shady hotel. Leave it to Timmy to book us in a 3 star resort. I wasn't necessarily livid about it -- I had a bed to sleep in. However, I would've much preferred something nicer. Although I'm sure that was asking too much. I sat outside that night and stared off into the distance. Well, it wasn't so much the distance but more like at the convenience store across the street. The people of Odessa moved so much slower than those of New Jersey. I felt almost as if I were in a completely different country. In a strange way, it was kind of soothing. An older model Honda Civic whipped into the lot and parked, completely uncharacteristic of the Odessa drivers. My first instinct was that it was an out-of-towner, but the license plate said differently. An attractive younger female stepped out of the car and pranced into the convenience store, strutting her stuff. An old Chris Bagwell would've walked right in after her to try and take her back to my room. After all, it was merely across the street. But just the fact that that I knew Rebecca was out there somewhere made me want to wait. I never knew when she'd pop back up again. I mean, realistically what where the chances she'd be in Odessa, Texas? It was a shot in the dark but wasn't worth the risk. So instead, I sat there and just entertained the idea of seducing that young lady. It would keep me occupied at least until she left the store. I imagined myself walking up behind her while she was trying to decide on which carton of milk would go best with her cookies. I'd come up from behind her, brushing my hand along her side to slip by her in the aisle. Naturally, I'd whisper my suggestion in her ear in a seductive tone. One thing would lead to another and I'd have her back here, slipping her clothes off of her body before finally engaging in the closing act. And boy, oh boy, do I have a lot of frustration I could vent into her. It'd certainly be the time of her life. I saw her silhouette at the counter and eagerly anticipated her exit from the double glass doors. "Abigail?" I slipped and said it aloud. She looked up at me and her eyes lit up like a deer in headlights as she slipped back into her car and ripped it out of the lot. It couldn't be... she was dead. Abby was dead. But then again, so was Rebecca! I raced towards the street from the hotel porch to try and catch a glimpse of where she was headed. I made it just in time to catch her making a left down a road about a half mile up. It was far enough in the distance that it realistically could've been anyone making the left. I could've completely missed her. But I still had a hunch. Shane stepped outside after seeing me take off. "You alright, Chris?" He always looked over me. "Shane! Abigail! I just saw Abigail! Rebecca HAS to be here, Shane. She and Abby disappeared at the same time. I'VE GOT TO FIND HER!" He froze up. He didn't know what to say. It's almost as if he knew something more and wanted to spill his heart to me... but he didn't. In my mind, there was no other option. This was something that needed to be done. My life had culminated in this very moment. I raced into the hotel room, ravaging through everyone's stuff to find the keys to Timmy's car. I pulled open dresser drawers, tore through suitcases, and even started flipping bed mattresses. You'd think I was some crazed, psychotic killer with how I was acting. And perhaps a part of me was. Shane came to the door looking to plead with me. "Chris," he said softly. "Think about what you're doing right now." His reasoning was dull. "I'm going to finish a chapter of my life that's been left unwritten for too many years, Shane." I snapped back at him. He knew I wanted to get out of there. He knew there was little he could say. But he persisted, "Can you even be sure that was really even Abigail? It could've been someone that looked similar to her." It was a valid suggestion. I mean, after all, I'd probably be pretty livid if I found this chick and it turned out to be a complete stranger. Hell, I'm sure she'd probably have a shit-fit if she found out what I'd done to get to her. "THE HELL WITH YOU, THEN!" I screamed at him. "You pulled me along and said you've got something to tell me about Rebecca. And then you give me nothing. Not a God damn thing. I'm sick and tired of the games, Shane. This isn't some Goddamn business you're fooling around with. It's my fucking life!" With that I launched the mattress across the room. The corner of the mattress clipped one of the lamps and sent it toppling over. Luckily it landed in a pile of sheets and didn't bust all over the floor. I looked up at Shane with a fiery passion in my eyes. I was growing increasingly frustrated that I couldn't find these damn keys. And then he dangled them in the air. "Here," he said, tossing them at me. "Go find out the truth." I think he thought I'd feel some sort of guilt and maybe change my mind... make a more rational decision. But the rationality in my mind was to find out just what the fuck was up. So I did just that. I ploughed past him and charged towards Timmy's car. I spun the tires the whole way out of the parking lot and took off. As I was leaving, I could've sworn I caught a glimpse of Timmy walking around the hotel property on his phone. I don't know if he had the slightest indication as to what was going on. I really didn't care. As I sped down the road following what I thought was the correct path, I took some time to think what I was doing. I had to reinforce myself in saying that I was right. The hell with my friends. They've never done a thing for me. I swung a left at the same road I saw Abby's car turn down earlier. At least I think it was the same road. I traveled along the road for a little over two miles until catching a glimpse of a disheveled street sign jutting into the road. The sign led to a dirt road. Ironically, the road was called 'Williams Street'. That was Abigail's last name. I felt like I was in some sort of horror flick. The coincidence was way too freaky. But if I knew one thing about Abigail, it’s that she'd be the one who would leave clues to herself. She'd never abandon her life completely. At least I didn't think she would. I hung a right down the dirt road. There were car tracks all over the road so I guess there was no way to really tell whether or not someone had been down them recently. But it wasn't even a mile up the road when I saw the car. That very same car from the convenience store. I pulled Timmy's car right up behind it and slammed it into park. As I approached the front door, I felt nervous. What if this was all a big mistake? I knocked. But there was no answer. I'd gotten this far... and for what? Nothing? I twiddled with the handle and the door opened up. I think it was my pure curiosity that pulled me into the house. It was only a small, one-story ranch. There wasn't much room to hide, if Abigail and Rebecca were indeed there. I searched around, looking for something... ...anything. Anything to let me know that they were there or that they'd been living there. Even a picture of a complete stranger would have been enough to curbside my presumptions. I found myself wandering into one of the bedrooms. There was still no sign of anybody in the house. God forbid I did get caught -- I was surely going to get arrested. The aroma inside the bedroom was that of a familiar one. It may have been my head playing games on me, but I felt like I knew that scent. The room was a mess. Drawers were ripped open as if someone had already been in here, tearing the place apart. Someone like me -- someone looking for answers. I approached the nightstand next to the bed. I once heard that women always kept their most prized possessions in the night stand next to their bed. It kept them close to their heart when they slept. It could've been a complete crock of shit, but I was willing to try anything. I slid open the drawer and looked inside. There lay a picture frame and inside a photograph...naturally. I lifted the frame from the drawer and my heart sunk. There was my answer. It was a picture of a boy and a girl. They held one another in each other's arms and locked lips. It was the kind of kiss people do when they're in love. You know. You could tell by their posture and the way their lips touched that throughout that kiss the rest of the world was just a blur. They blanked out everything in that kiss and they only saw each other. Perhaps the most comforting thing about that picture... ...was that it was of me. And Rebecca. I pressed it to my heart and closed my eyes. "Hi Chris..." I thought I'd been dreaming. I turned around and there she stood before me. My heart beat faster. It beat harder. "Rebecca.." ___________________________________ "I've let her go," I said with relief. I found myself again sitting across from Shane, sharing a bottle of red wine. He was more than accommodating of me, particularly with it being two o'clock in the morning. "What makes you so sure," he asked. Truth was, I really didn't have an answer for him. "I've just got ... I don't know. I've got a feeling. I feel like I was able to get some closure today. A kind of closure I've been searching for, for the last six years." Shane just nod his head. He knew I was speaking from my heart. He knew I was sincere. "You know, it's amazing," he said. "Everyone you've ever come in contact with - they've turned to gold. Whether it be measured in championship belts, success with business ventures, or finding peace in their life. Everyone, but one." "But one?" "Yes, one. Some may call you self-centered and egotistic, but you've given all your love, your passion, and your success away to everybody else. You've made it damn certain that every person around you has always led a better life. You truly have the Golden Touch, Chris." "Thanks, but uhh... What are you trying to say here?" I was stumped. Alicia Silverstone in Clueless. "I'm saying that if you've truly moved on and let her go, then that Golden Touch has finally affected you." I smirked and shook my head, "Ha." There was nothing more to be said. I was finally free. For the first time, I was able to see beyond measuring success by title belts or awards. Because at the end of the day, it doesn't really matter how many tournaments you won or titles you've held. At the end of the day, all that matters is your love for the game...and the legacy you left with it. And I was finally ready for my legacy to truly begin.
