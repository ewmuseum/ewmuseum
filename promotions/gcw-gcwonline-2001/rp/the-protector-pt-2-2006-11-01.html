The Protector Pt 2

1 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 1 2006 Humanity. Though it might not seem it at any particular moment in time, it is one of the most vital senses to grasp. Yet, coincidentally, it is one of the simplest entities to lose sight of. Especially, when competing in the ostentatious platform that is professional wrestling. Now, whether you irradiate an arena with your mere presence, sauntering to the ring under the cheers of the adoring masses, or possess the keen ability to deteriorate the gathering applause into hoards of death-wishing threats, it can happen to you. Whether you are stepping into the biggest match of your career or merely being enticed by the fan’s frantic screams for you to rip your opponent’s head from his shoulders, it can happen to you. Whether you, like Shadow, are in line for one of the most sought-after prizes in the industry, or like me, are fighting to scratch your way from the anonymity of an industry that still looks at you like a rookie, a mere soldier in a battle that you have just recently come to understand, a pawn carefully making his way across the board to his unrealized potential? oh you better believe it happens to you. This event being, a loss of reality, or temporary note of insanity as it were. It occurs when you do more than start to believe in the hype that surrounds your name. It occurs when you become the moniker you have thrust into the fan’s faces on so many occasions, when you forget that behind the wrestling attire, the flashy entrances, the crafty moves, and arrogant smile, you are simply human; weak, frail, and? mortal. No better than the man standing across the ring from you, no better than those you loath,, and no better than those you would try to protect One way or another every man, woman, and child finds a way to rise above their inhumanity and reclaim their sense of veracity. It just takes a particular instance of meditative pondering; a situation in which a person is left nothing but time to contemplate on his own physical or mental frailties. For most, including myself, this occurs the instant I step foot into a hospital. Whether I wake up in a cast, paying my respects to someone on the inside, or suffering the effects of my own delusions of invincibility; the result is that same. From the exaggeratedly considerate flowers of well wishes breeding the main kinds of self pity, the I.V. drip that is made to accompany you through your adventures from the bed to the lavatory, or the fact that when sauntering around the halls, you are paid the degradation of having half your rear side poking through the back of a dangerously thin hospital gown. This is when reality begins to set in, this is when the world no longer seems to revolve around your ever whimsical stride, this is when you consider the notion that maybe, just maybe, you weren’t as unbeatable as you had come to believe. Even the proudest of men realizes that the moment he regains consciousness, arousing to the methodical chirps of a respirator and sterilized air, he has failed himself. To others, poking their heads in and offering hands of sympathy, this might seem a place of solace, a place of tranquility, and a place of repose. But, to the man resting, bed laden, and struggling to digest the sickly excuses for nourishment referred to as hospital food, this is a reformatory, an ever clear reminder of his debility, his reality check. ~~~ “I don’t know what to tell you, Mr. Conway.” Brack Conway was a man past his prime. The fans knew it, I knew it, but he refused to accept it. In his mind there was still so much more that he had to offer the Professional Wrestling World; Either as a competitor, or as a manager. He was a wealth of knowledge to an industry that had forgotten the wrestling of old. I was going to be his rush to fame? the push back into the spotlight where he knew he and I belonged. He stared, with those cold, icy, blue eyes, following his thoughts through the hospital window; vacantly drifting over the scenery as if fighting a silent war within himself. The short faded spots of hair cleverly wavering to form a bald spot on the tip of his head. He gently patted his right knee, weakened over the years of bounding through the Wrestling ring without a care in the world. He adjusted himself in his seat, keeping his sore back in place, as his eyes slowly trailed toward the doctor, Ms. Okiko, in the room with him. This was far from the man the rest of the wrestling world and I had come to know. He had let himself go. Even the wash board abs he had prided himself on in the past, had faded to a keg of a gut that he uselessly tried to hide under his medium sized polo shirt. His bones were weary and his knees ached when it was about to rain. He was old, but refused to admit it. He was still worth something to someone. Though, it hurt him to see that person in the hospital bed. It hurt him to see me lying motionless. Since the first time that he ever laid eyes on me he saw a fighter; A man willing push himself towards his goals no matter how outlandish those goals seemed to be. If I had told him that I wanted the stars, he would laugh. But, somewhere, deep down, he could never find it within himself to doubt that someday I would get them, grasping them in my tired hands to show him. I should be, at this very moment, fighting with everything I had. I should be in pain. I should be showing the fight of my life in my face. I should be writhing in agony of my injuries. But, my motionless body looked tranquil and serene to him. It looked at peace, and it scared the hell out of him. “That’s not exactly the answer I was looking for, Dr. Okiko!” Brack said running a quivering hand along the rail at my bedside. “I want to hear something substantial. I want to here that he is going to be alright, or that you have at least the slightest clue of what is wrong with him.” Dr. Okiko stumbled on her words for a moment as she peered into the concerned eyes of my manager. It was hard for her to put a finger on just what kind of attachment the old man had to me. Yet, how could she? So many experiences in my life were a mystery, even to myself! He had an attachment; more than someone should! She could see the unwarranted guilt writhing through out his entire figure. He felt responsible. But, for what? “I’m sorry but?” “I’M SORRY, IS NO LONGER AN OPTION, DOC! WHAT IS WRONG WITH, JULIO!” Her lower jaw puffed out for a moment as she struggled to hold her tongue and remain professional about the ordeal. Brack, wasn’t a likable guy to begin with. He was loud, obnoxious, and rude to his friends. He let words slip through his mouth like there was no connection from his mouth to his brain, and never apologized if he offended someone. He said what he felt, without regard for who it hurt or who he was dealing with? and he acted that way with his friends She knew he was upset; it was a given. But, his attitude was something that Dr. Okiko was not accustomed to dealing with. When most people sat at their friend’s bedside she dealt with a somber tone. Brack wanted me to be alright so bad that it had driven him into fits of rage. She struggled to figure out why he was so desperate to see me through this. Like he had someone to answer to! Was he concerned or afraid? “For all intents and purposes, Mr. Conway, there is nothing wrong with Julio.” She whispered doing her best to keep her anger at bay. “He was extremely fit to begin with and the injuries you described were minimal at best to a man of his physique.” “His injuries don’t have anything to do with what you see before you.” She tapped her pen across the file as she shook her head in disbelief. “His damage is not physical. His brain activity is off the charts. Julio is, for lack of a better term, mentally overwrought!” Brack took a breath; letting the estranged quiver that ran through his arm fade. “What exactly does that mean?” “It means that what ever Julio is dealing with, we can’t help him.” She folded my file and placed it in a slide at my bed as she looked to my brain activity on a monitor. “It means that what ever battle Julio is fighting” It is a battle he will have to win with himself!” Brack’s head finally gave way as he buried his face into his hands. “Whatever you are going through, kid, I’m here for you”
