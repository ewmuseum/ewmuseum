Face-Off

22 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/22/10 February 19, 2009... Flick. The lighter head opens up with a quick snap of the wrist. Thoughts run wildly through the mind of a tormented man as he recalls his ugliest – and, unfortunately, most common – nightmare. "I bring them in the bunker...and so begins the ritual," a raspy voice proclaims in frustration. "The blood flows down some blurred face..." David stops to think if it matters whose face it is: A corrupt businessman that steals millions from his flock of mindless sheep? An asshole convict that pushes hard drugs on vulnerable teenagers? An innocent mother? A cute little girl with pigtails? "It doesn't matter whose face it is," he confesses in a depressingly accepting tone. He's gone through this process before – many times – but that question always seems to come up in his mind, despite his knowledge of the answer. Fwoosh. The flame jumps up and dances with the pulse of the slight draft in the room. "Some beg. Some threaten. Some whimper." David pauses, shaking his head. He runs his free hand along a seem of the dark red couch he's lying on. "Eventually, they all scream... as I torture them... as I make them torture each other." He lifts his free hand up to look at it – in all its warped, scarred glory. "As I kill them in cold blood." Crack. Out goes the flame. He lowers his hand back to his side. The psychiatrist continues to scribble down notes for a few moments longer, before turning his full gaze back to his patient. "Unfortunately, Mr. Spencer, I feel these nightmares may continue to haunt you for the foreseeable future. Just continue to remember that they are, in fact, dreams and not at all a representation of reality." The psychiatrist puts down his book on the top of the drawers beside him, placing his reading glasses on top of the book afterward. He clasps his hands together, leaning forward and placing his forearms on his thighs. "As you know, David, this is our last sessions together before-" "Before you release me back into the real world...out of the crazy bin," finishes David, letting out a short, harsh cough afterward. The psychiatrist nods and continues in a reassuring tone, leaning back into his chair. "You will be supervised, but your situation will no longer be under our control. It's a bit unprecedented and a bit of a risk, but we feel that you can manage with the progress you've made and the success of your medication." He reaches into one of the drawers beside him, pulling out a paper bag full of the aforementioned medication. He quickly checks through it to make sure the right set of pills is inside. Satisfied, he looks back over at David, an eyebrow raised. "It is my understanding that you are going to go back to your previous career as a professional wrestler," says the doctor, with a note of intrigue in the key of concern. "We won't intervene with your decision to do so. However, your actions outside of your profession will be monitored closely and, if you begin to deviate from your current healthy path, we will have to bring you back." The doctor ends his last sentence on a very serious tone. David gets up from the couch he was laying on, pocketing his lighter in his pair of blue jeans and taking the bag of pills from the doctor. He opens up a backpack that was sitting beside the couch and places the bag within it, closing it up afterward and throwing it over his shoulder. "I understand, doc," says David, extending his scarred hand to the doctor. "I thank you all for letting me do the only thing I really know how to do." The doctor shakes David's hand and smiles as his patient heads out of the office for the final time. As David is just about to close the door, the psychiatrist throws out his final inquiry. "Will you be competing under your former persona?" David stops in his tracks. He pulls down his backpack from his shoulder and opens it up, rummaging through it. After a few moments, he pulls out a face he knows too well. Without looking back at the psychiatrist, he gives a definitive answer. "Face is dead." With that, the wrestler formerly known as Face exits the office. He heads down the hallway, past the receptionist and out the front door, staring into his old crossed eyes the entire time. As soon as David gets outside, he steps to the side of the building and pulls out his lighter from his pocket. Without hesitation, he proceeds to light the demented, clown-like visage on fire. He lets it burn and melt a little bit in his hands before tossing it to the sidewalk, where it continues to burn and smoulder, letting off dark black fumes. After one last long glance at the man he used to be, David heads off in the other direction – to a fresh start of an old life.
