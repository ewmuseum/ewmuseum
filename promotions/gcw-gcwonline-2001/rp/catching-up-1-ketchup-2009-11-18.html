Catching Up (1): Ketchup

18 November 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/18/09 (takes place in August) August 18th, 2009 Chris Storm glared down at the small glowing screen of his phone with a dead stare. He was in deadlock. His thumb tapped down, scrolling through the numbers that occupied the digital memory. He stopped, his thumb hovering over the buttons by less than an inch. He wanted to call, but he really didn’t want to call. He wanted to put the phone back into his pocket and forget the entire thing. Why call after all? What would he even say? He didn’t know, so therefore by his logic he shouldn’t call. But he still wanted to call. No, he didn’t. If anything he just wanted to hear her voice, but that was meek when done over the phone. It wasn’t her voice, but some poor audio copy meant to represent her words. Calling her would do absolutely nothing, if he wanted to hear her voice he shouldn’t call. He pressed send. Damn it, why was he calling? Of all the self-inflecting ideas he has had in his life, this was right up there with the worst of them. If he was lucky her phone would be either turned off, on mute, or just buried at the bottom of her purse where she would never find it. “Hello?” Ah shit, she answered, and before he could think of anything to say. If he had half a brain he would tell her he dialed the wrong number and just hang up. Too bad Chris Storm was notorious for his lack of thinking skills, “oh, hey Tess, uh…just calling to see what’s up?” And likely to also say ‘I want to tap that ass harder than fucking rock.’ In that moment uncharacteristic of silence from Chris, Tessa was more than likely wondering what is going on. After all, he hadn’t talked to her in weeks, nearly months. To just call her like this? What was going through that screwed up mind of his? “Chris?” She questioned. He wagered her mouth was agape by this point, and had he actually put money on it, he would have struck it big. “Hey.” He sheepishly responded. If she had seen him pacing the same four steps around his living room at this moment, chances are her lips would have curled up ever so slightly before letting a subtle chuckle trickle out. Her response bore little more confidence, “hey.” The muttering small talk bounced back and forth between the two like a volleyball as they tried to grasp at any form of conversation. Tessa struck first, “you called about something?” And with that she batted the ball back in his direction. Put on the spot as he was, Chris did his best to muster back the strength to knock the proverbial ball back into her court, “do you…want to maybe get a drink or something today?” A great wall of silence spiked the ball straight back into his court. Quickly he dove to save himself from defeat, “I just figured maybe you needed to get out.” As flattered as she was that he took at least a minute of his time to think about someone other than himself, her suspicions were rightfully high, “what you really mean is ‘you need someone else to back you up now’?” Again he dove to suavely save himself, “as much as I deserve that, I’m pretty sure that’s not what you really want to say.” “You’re right about that.” The momentary pause served its purpose well in misleading his inner thoughts. “There’s so much more I’d love to say, but it’d make me look bad in the process for being the glutton that took every piece of bait you’ve ever thrown me. I’m sure this times no exception.” What the hell could he say to that that wouldn’t make him look like an asshole? “I deserved that as well,” was the best response he had to offer, “but considering everything that’s going on right now, the last thing I want to do is burn what’s left of the bridge between us. “ Despite his best efforts, the response he got in return headed in the complete opposite direction of what he wanted, “if that’s the case, I’d be more than happy to drop the kids off.” Beating around the bush was failing him miserably. It was time to be little bit frank with his on again off again, “I’d love to see them, but things just aren’t the same.” Either she was not getting the point, or she just refused to acknowledge it, “they’re your kids. They’re growing up. Of course it’s not going to be the same.” “I meant without you.” Her jaw would have hit the floor if that sentence had not been beaten to death years ago. “I can’t.” “Why not?” “It’ll just end the way it always does.” The thing he hated the most about calling her is moments like this she had a good point about the past. Denial would not change the fact that the two were constantly finding reasons to casually intermingle, which usually only lead to more drama. Chris scrambled for yet another one of those weak casual excuses, “not even to celebrate?” He should have seen from miles away the response she quickly shot back, “what? Getting fired?” Thankfully he managed to stumble across the ace hidden up his sleeve by merely looking up at his calendar. Thank god he had some charm to muster up behind it, “the fact that two years ago, despite all the shit I’ve put your through all the past, you put aside your career and delivered one of the biggest blessings in my life.” That time the only thing burnt was Tessa’s escape route. Of course she had to cave. She questioned, though it came out much more like a plea, “no tricks?” “No tricks.” He replied back, though he had no idea how much it’d actually convince her to such. On his side of the phone he kept his fingers crossed, hoping he would hear those words he was dying for. There was a soft and defeated sigh barely audible over the line before Tessa confessed to her defeat, “fine, I’ll bring the kids over and maybe we can go out and do something.” Chris smiled, as rare of an event as that was for him to truly smile, “great, thanks Tessa.” Tessa was not nearly as happy as she incoherently muttered, “see you in a few hours.” Boy was she in for a hellacious drive. ---- I caught a chill and it's still frozen on my skin I think about why I'm alone, by myself No one else to explain How far do I go? No one knows If the end is so much better, why don't we just live forever? Don't tell me I'm the last one in line Don't tell me I'm too late this time ---- Five hours is an awfully damn long drive when it is spent enclosed within the tight space of a fully loaded five passenger vehicle. Add to it the reluctance of reaching your final destination, four exhausted kids, and the occasional kicking and screaming of a bored two year old and you’d know exactly why Tessa Windsor stood in front of Chris’ door with said two year old in hand, relentlessly pounding at the front door. “Do not tell me….” She began to fuss under her breath as she placed Aislinn, the aforementioned two year old, on her feet to give her arms a moments rest. Both her, and the small pack of children beside her, were visually exhausted, hungry, and more ready to settle in, though it was mostly the kids on that last part. Before she had a chance to finish her sentence the door opened wide enough to grant entry for them. Chris stood behind the door, having barely managed to get the door open in the first place, his arms tied up with several different items that made it clear he had just come out of the kitchen. “Sorry,” he managed to squeeze out as he turned to one side to allow them past him, “I was in the process trying to finish things up before you got here” he explained, raising his arms to show off the items cradled in them as if to further prove his point. Resisting any sort of eye contact Tessa answered back quietly with little sympathy, “don’t worry about it. I would have let myself in eventually anyways.” Aislinn was much less resistant to her father. Desperate for attention she yanked at the bottom of Chris’ black tank top that complemented his sculpted figure well, “daddy!” Quite unknowingly, Aislinn led her father into the perfect pickup line that seemed perfect to break the ice with. Bright blue eyes widened to their fullest, he purposely swung his head in Tessa’s direction and heckled, “well at least I’m loved by someone.” Tessa refused to bite. After driving five hours alone with four kids, a guilt trip was the last thing she was looking for, “please don’t go there.” Chris knew better than to try and win her back with guilt and quickly shut his mouth. Taking his seat at the head of the table with Aislinn bouncing on his knee, he looked back at Tessa, who was sat at the opposite end. There he waited for the chance for her eyes to meet his to see his sincerity but she seemed to be doing everything she could to avoid any form of eye contact with him. After several minutes of awkward silence he managed to get the slightest bit of contact her eyes. Desperate to keep them within his grasp, he spoke up, “it really is good to see you again,” he told her softly. He knew he meant it, but he could only hope that his tone conveyed that to Tessa as well as possible. It managed to curl her strawberry colored lips the slightest bit upward. She only surrendered so much as the corner of her eye when she finally responded, “you’ll grow tired of me in about a week, I’m sure of that.” He assumed she was kidding, “giving yourself a bit of credit, aren’t you?” Thankfully she was. “Always,” her lips curled the same distance as before, then she realized her vulnerability level had multiplied by ten, “that’s how I always get myself into this mess, isn’t it?” “Yeah, but isn’t it a great mess to be in caught in?” He asked as he placed Aislinn into her booster seat. He quickly sought another exit before the topic went south, “now let me go get the food before it gets cold.” And with that he excused himself from his seat and left out into the kitchen area to grab the various dishes for them, leaving Tessa alone with the kids momentarily. Left alone with the kids, Tessa’s eyes darted around the table as if god would bestow upon them the answer to every question wandering around in the back of her mind. Of course, Tessa was let down again. The only thing she was left wondering now was how after nearly seven years of this cycle she’d yet to figure out how to escape, or at least move on to the next level. Luckily for Tessa, the kids were always around to ease the moments as awkward as this one. “I’m hungry,” groaned the younger Chris. Despite the fact that he was Storm’s eight year old child from a previous affair, Tessa had pretty much taken him in as her own. Thus, he got scolded by her as such. “Your dad’s getting it, just be patient.” “I hope it’s not spaghetti again.” Evan, their adopted ten year old, chimed in. Then of course, Christa, Tessa’s niece turned daughter, following the passing of her father, had to chime in being that she was fourteen and all. “It’s not Chris’ fault that’s all mom knows how to make.” And then there was Aislinn, who found the way gravity overtook her sippy cup to be absolutely enthralling. It would have been much easier to ignore had she not managed to knock the stopper loose, thus spilling chocolate milk all over the brand new blue and white lace trimmed dress Tessa had just bought for her. Rather than kick up the fuss the kids were likely expecting by now, Tessa merely buried her head deep within her palms. It was times like these where a chair shot to the head seemed welcoming. So welcoming that Tessa couldn’t avoid the topic any longer. The minute Chris was within eyeshot range again she burst, “So, I’ve been fired. Can I ask what your excuse is for not showing up?” Poking his head around the corner to look in on her, Chris replied back stumped, “what do you mean?” Using what napkins had been placed out on the table, Tessa tried to clean off Aislinn’s dress as best she could. “I mean, why haven’t you been appearing in GCW? I was fired, but I haven’t heard anything about you.” Finally resurfacing, Chris brought as his sacrifice a whole chicken rotisserie style on it. He placed it on the center of the table and wiped his hands clean. “Oh,” he muttered as he looked down, “I don’t know. When Markus and I went our separate ways, it just wasn’t as much fun, so I just stopped doing it.” It managed to get a chuckle out of Tessa, who almost couldn’t believe her ears, “I never took you as the type to need someone by your side. Guess I was wrong there.” Storm blinked twice, taken a bit back by her laugher, “You think I actually cared about all that nonsense when I signed? Please Tess. What else could I possibly achieve?” “I guess nothing; you just happened to break that lil tag team record along the way to nothing.” Chris shrugged her compliment off like it was dirt on his shoulder, “we really weren’t trying to break any records. If anything we got lucky because of a weak division. I’m sure they could have given the janitors the belts and they would have broken the record. “ Tessa smiled and laughed; something that warmed Chris to his core. “Me and Markus only had one goal in mind; to have some fun. And we did. I can live with that. I think we all knew it wouldn’t last forever anyways.” As much as she hated to admit it, he was right. It couldn’t have lasted forever, as much as they may have wanted it to. The reality of it was, in the game of wrestling, there was no such thing as friends til the end, BFF, or any other of those catchy friendship lines. It still would not change the fact that anytime the topic came up, Chris failed to hide the obvious betrayal felt on his end. “That’s what you tell yourself anyways.” Tessa interjected, as if she were his own personal therapist. Then again, Tessa could have easily written a thesis on the topic given how many times she’d been dumped on her ass and left for dead. “You can’t tell me you didn’t see it coming Tessa.” Sincere as can be she shrugged, her baby blue eyes wandering away to hide their naivety. This time it was Chris who buried his head into his palms. Sometimes he wondered if she had the capacity to spot an oncoming freight train headed straight for her. Course, as kind natured as Tessa was, she had to make excuses for their long time friend, “He’s just confused Chris. We’ve all been there at one time or another. Whether you like to admit or not, you cast a large shadow. He’s probably just overwhelmed.” “He’s been overwhelmed since we first met” Chris fired back at her, “it never gave him any reason to do what he did before. I don’t see why it had to now.” He shook his head in obvious disappointment, “should have seen it coming.” In his attempt to change the subject, Chris grabbed the large carving knife on the table and drove it straight into the breast of the roasted chicken. At times like this it was best to leave Chris to his thoughts, which is exactly what Tessa headed. Wasting little time, she used the kids as a good reason to head to the fridge. She figured anyone of the four of them would be looking for some sort of condiment, so why not grab one of everything? Course there was not much to offer. “Someone doesn’t like to eat,” she subtly teased in attempts to lighten the mood, “I think I’ve seen more food in a homeless mans pocket.” Chris turned his head to look at her as he answered, “I’m sure Arizona would take offense to that.” Their attempts at light humor did little to ease the heavy tension in the room. Instantly Tessa started to think to herself just how big of a mistake it was coming here. No doubt Chris was lambasting himself for every inviting her over if it was just going to be like this. While scattering the condiments across the table Tessa took a moment to acknowledge Chris attempt with a half smile before heading back to her seat, ketchup in hand. “Ketchup?” Chris questioned, staring straight at the bottle as if he expected it to jump to life and bite him, “Who dips a rotisserie chicken in ketchup?” Far from amused by his question, Tessa answered, “Your daughter.” If you went by his answer, you would have thought Chris had no clue where kids came from, “my daughter?” Once again Tessa found herself burying her head deep into her palms, “yes, Aislinn.” Glancing over at Aislinn, Chris of course had to pass the blame, albeit jokingly, for the train wreck taking place in front of him, “you shame your mother.” Lord it was going to be a really long night for all of them. ---- Out here, nothings clear Except the moment I decided to move on and I ignited Disappear into the fear You know there ain't no comin' back When you're still carrying the past You can't erase, separate Cigarette in my hand, Hope you all understand I won't be the last one in line I finally figured out what's mine ---- Men; they’re a woman’s biggest weakness. And even though Tessa Windsor could grapple with the strongest of them and still somehow come out on top, when it came to matters outside of the ring, Tessa Windsor was absolutely helpless. Case in point: Chris Storm. Any sensible woman would not wish upon a star for a man with an attitude problem. Any sensible woman would not let a man walk in and out of her life at his own discretion. Any sensible woman would not fall for a man like Chris Storm. But of course, it has already been well established that Tessa was far from the sensible type. Instead of spending her time at the comfort of her own home where she called the shots, Tessa found herself in New York City curled up on the leather sofa inside her on again off again love interest’s immaculately clean condo. And speaking of the devil himself, Chris Storm entered the room with no more than a towel wrapped around his waist. His body glistened, more than enough evidence that he had just gotten out of the shower. Tessa turned her head, gazing at him for a moment and taking in his well sculpted body. “Go put on some clothes,” she finally responded quite bluntly. He stared down at the piece of white egyptian cotton covering his lower half and shrugged off Tessa’s suggestion. “Why?” He asked just as bluntly as she had, “this is my place.” “And YOUR kids are here. Now go put some clothes on.” This time Tessa applied much more force to her voice. Chris grumbled, surrendering with a sour face, but not without chiming in his dirty little thoughts first, “you’re only punishing yourself.” He hardly got the reaction he was expecting, “I’ve already punished myself enough by being here.” Still, from the next room over he chimed back, “you don’t have to play tough you know. You would have come here regardless of the situation.” He had her there. Instead of answering, Tessa pulled the tan colored bed sheet further over her body. Surely he wouldn’t come back in if he thought she was calling it a night. Sadly, she couldn’t have been more wrong. Within moments Chris had reemerged in pajama pants, still sans shirt. She could have easily dealt with the lack of shirt had he not chosen to plant himself at the end of the couch, right beside her feet. “There was something I did want to talk to you about though,” she mentioned quite sheepishly, aware that there really was no escape, “while I was here.” Her tone was more than enough to turn Chris’ head in her direction. Tessa noticed him look her way from the edge of her eye and used that as confirmation to continue with her thoughts, “I was thinking about fighting my termination.” Chris shrugged his shoulders without giving the thought so much as a moment to linger in his head, “fine, whatever, no skin off my back if you want to go back to that.” While he pretended to be all calm, cool, and collected about the whole ordeal, Tessa quickly picked up on the agitation buried within his tone. “That?” She had never once heard him refer to the business as simply just ‘that’. It was only in a rare moment that Tessa saw Chris so distant from the business that made him a star. When he was tearing up the tag scene with Markus winning over thousands of fans he would have never referred to GCW as just ‘that’, “What do you mean by ‘that’?” Realizing he’d been caught in a moment of tension, he quickly corrected himself to make it sound as if he were merely trying to encourage Tessa, “back to GCW. Go on and fight your termination. I’m sure you can find some way back in.” “You didn’t answer my question.” “Sure I did. I know what you’re talking about.” “Chris, come on!” Already Storm was roping her back in to his control, though this time quite inadvertent, “Just tell me what’s wrong.” He let out a sigh, hoping that she would cave as she had earlier in the evening and let it go. But he knew her, and just how stubborn she was when it came to his wellbeing, “just forget about it, nothing’s wrong.” Tessa tilted her head to the side in attempts to catch his eyes as she said that, so that she could gauge how sincere his answer was. He failed quite miserably which only left Tessa with more questions, “how am I supposed to believe nothing is wrong with an answer like that?” “What’s bothering me is none of your concern anyways.” He tried his damndest to convince her. It probably would have been a lot more convincing if his head didn’t drop towards the ground after he answered every time. “Somehow I doubt that.” Tessa’s persistence paid off for once. Finally he rose from the couch to look his former fiancé dead in the eyes when he questioned, “you aren’t going to drop this, are you?” Tessa abruptly shook her head to inform him that there was no chance that he could get her to just leave it be; at least not for now. Chris dropped another sigh which prompted Tessa to scoot over on the sofa and allow him room to come back to spill his mind. “Come on now Chris! Just sit down and tell me what’s wrong.” This time it was Storm who found himself helpless. As much as of kryptonite as he was to Tessa, she just as easily drained away at his defenses. Plopping his massive self down beside Tessa on the couch, he buried himself deep into the cushions before divulging his inner most thoughts, “I’m just tired of it Tess. I’m tired of all the backstabbing, tired of manipulation, tired of trying to be something special. I’ve chased away everything good I’ve ever had going for what? Caldera’s approval? For money? Guess what I’ve got now Tessa? Absolutely nothing! Markus is convinced I’m the reason why he’s never made it big, I’m disposable without Markus to draw in ratings, and you’ve come to hate me because I trusted both of them. Why would anyone want to go back to that mess? That’s all this business does Tessa, it chews you up and spits you out!” That was certainly much more of an answer than Tessa was expecting. Realizing this, Storm quickly hushed and let his head fall straight onto Tessa’s shoulder. Clearly he was a lot more vulnerable to heartache and deception than those who were close to him would chose to believe. His voice dropped down to a defeated sigh whose tone walked the very fine line between defeat and absolute heartache, “I can’t do this anymore Tessa. It’s not worth it.” Tessa had been there for him countless times in moments of distress, but never once had she seen the self proclaimed Rock Superstar simply walk away without so much as a glance from his problems. It left her with little to say. Why would she even want to take a chance of making matters worse when it was obvious what kind of pain Chris was in? Assuming her natural role of caregiver, Tessa slid her fingers through Chris’ hair, which had returned to its natural black shade during Chris’ absence from the business. So many times they’d gone through moments like this before, but usually it was her falling apart. Things seemed so much more complicated when she was the one who had to fix them. The only thing Tessa could think to offer was a suggestion that sounded so much better in her head, “I could talk to Markus for you.” It got little response from Chris, who seemed to simply want to erase Tessa’s mind once all was said and done. Here he was, a man known for his success in the wrestling industry, falling apart before a woman who he’d hurt more times than he cared to count. It really sucked to feel so damn vulnerable, really really sucked. “No.” “It won’t hurt anything.” “No.” “Please just let me try?” It was growing near impossible for Chris to resist gazing over into Tessa’s baby blues. She seemed so desperate to make things right again, the way they always wanted it to be. She was so full of hope, it made his heart shatter into microscopic pieces. The last thing he wanted was to let her down again, but at the same time, he hated taking a chance of putting her in harm’s way. He was torn, and he knew if he so much caught of glimpse of her pleading for a chance to make things better for him, and for their family, he’d be absolutely helpless, and helpless was intolerable. There was only one thing he could think of that would help the situation, at least for him. “Can we just talk about it tomorrow at the gym? I don’t want to spend all of our time with this stuff now with the kids around. If they wake up and come down here, I don’t need to see me like this.” His statement did at least one of two things. First, it hopefully put the conversation on the backburner, and second, if the first bit failed, he at least dropped the hint that he hoped she would stay another night there with him. “Tomorrow then.” She conceded. It wasn’t the easiest thing for her to do at a time like this, but pressing the issue further would only drive a wedge between her and the man she trying to win back. Her lips pressed up against the side of his face to reassure Chris that there would indeed be a tomorrow. It was great to feel wanted, but even with her there coddling him, Chris could not help but be curious about the future. “Tessa?” “Yeah?” “I’m sorry.” She had absolutely no clue what he was apologizing for. Instead of addressing his apology with words she dropped her head down into his lap so that he could look down at her and see for himself that there was no need to worry, “we’ll talk about it tomorrow.” Her eyes sealed themselves shut as she rolled onto her side and whispered, “good night.” Chris’ eyes wasted little time in following suit.
