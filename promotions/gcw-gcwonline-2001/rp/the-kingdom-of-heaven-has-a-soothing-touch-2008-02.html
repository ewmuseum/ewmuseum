The Kingdom of Heaven has a Soothing Touch

February 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Begining of February "One reason why God created time was so we can put our failures and mistakes in the past." The heavens opened up to the impenetrable darkness of the night’s sky, showering the Earth with the irresistible glow of the stars and moon. The silhouetting moonlight penetrated the high-top windows, casting a disfiguring light upon the cross above the alter. The dancing flames upon the red candles set that rhythm to the silent dance of the silhouette of Jesus Christ on the wall, giving life to the forever rising savior of stone and tomb. The epic battle of color among the distinct glows of heaven and Earth cast a discoloring shadow upon the man sitting in the front pew, glancing at his ripped open hands and battered body. The gothic cathedral is empty, all except the healer and the one in need of healing. Resting upon the pew as if a crutch to the ever falling world around him, he bows his head in disgrace; a feeling not readily accepted in His house, nor to himself. The mixture of blood and tears dried up upon his godly praying hands, faltering the pigment of color of his skin. Blood, this can’t be his blood! Every drip of blood from the never-ending river of death is just the byproduct of weakness and susceptibility to the darkness every human soul possesses. This drives the wailing tears from the back of his eyes to flow as that very river, burning red arcs into his eyelids. He struggles with himself to look up upon his savior, but cannot come to terms with the tender wounds plaguing his body. The brown hoodie casts his body into a more shameful shade of darkness, torn and dirty from the embarrassing escape from inside the ring. The mistakes ensued will be overcome, must be overcome. It is not an option, or choice, but fate to overcome all failures. From the day he lifted the cross from the burning rubble of what he called his home a new path of stars were aligned. The God given purpose bestowed upon him has given him the burden of loss, but this loss is much more personal and to the heart than those of the past. Failure to do his Lord’s bidding is not just to the mental stability of man, but to the heart of him as well. It burns into his heart as an image would on a television screen, never able to rid himself of the lasting effects of his mistakes. Zacharia looks up hesitantly, tears profusely running down his blood plastered cheeks, splash upon the torn cloth and polished wood of his metaphorical crutch. His lip trembles at the sight of his Master, too weak to articulate words to express his forgiveness. Why should he articulate? He knows all that is to come and what has passed. It is not to tell of what has happened, it is more of a recognition of failure, and that no more disciplinary actions should follow to express the disappointment of failure. The loss to a man far lesser in power has overcome a mercenary of the Almighty, and the iron fist did not tip the scales in the passionate favor of he who stands for judgement. Is the righteousness not supposed to reign down from the heavens through the conduit He has chosen? Why has this man been able to go where all other men have failed before? Never has blood ran like the river of weakness from so many wounds on his body. Hands are mere instruments of power, weapons of man to do their/His bidding. Scars lay across his hands like water laces the cracked Earth. The forever disfiguring patch of skin that lays across his chest burns at the thought of the beating he has endured on this God forsaken night. The painful memory that has been inscribed upon his heart bursts through his body and into his weakened state of mind, projecting into his eyes the memory from the past as if his eyes were projecting a film strip from within. A narrating voice started from within: "On that day, the heavens were truly testing me. A premature rainfall cast shadowy imagery all around me, giving me the sense of being watched at all angles. The requiem was premature, adding more gloom to the thought of what lied ahead. The sudden feeling of the past crept up through my chest and down into my fingers, crossing over the smooth patch of skin that was burned years ago from the cross that now lay cool and pristine around my battered neck. As I walked cautiously through the alley by the liquor store, I heard voices resonating from behind the building, casting a warm chill over the erect hairs on the back of my neck. My hands took to my cross routinely and brought the chilled gold to my lips. Passing around the corner lead me into a realm of a darkness I had never stepped upon before. The rain cast rippling blows upon pools of crimson blood, falling to the depths of the lakes and then resurfacing, pushing the puddles ever further beyond. A steel oil drum laid on its side with roaring flames escaping the barrel with pieces of wood sticking out, slightly turned by the soot emitted from the barrel’s inhabitance. Next to the barrel lay what was once the clerk from the store, blood profusely pouring out form his skull and other extremities. Standing over the lifeless corpse was a man shrouded in a black hoodie, holding a lead pipe in his right hand. I froze, looking down at another innocent life taken by the hands of an incompetent man, looking only to better himself with some extra cash; Shameful. I reached up to the zipper on my jacket and undid it, letting the metal identify itself with its sound of friction, sending a signal to my opponent that I was not going to let him take another life on this Godforsaken night. Rolling my shoulders out of each side of the jacket, I released myself from the burden of a restraint and put my hands up, ready to release him of his burdens as well. I now knew that my hands would break through the staff of this song and add a new note with the soul of the perpetrator. I looked into the heavens and closed my eyes for a matter of minuscule seconds, then looked once more at the man standing over the innocent body of the clerk. I rushed him, with no mercy left for him, striking him across the jaw with all of my might and fell with him onto the dumpster. My eyes were enchanted by the amount of blood that splattered from his mouth onto the dripping black top of the structure we now rested upon. I hit him once more, but then felt the hardened tip of his lead pipe go into my ribs, pushing me off of him and open for a strike from his instrument of death. I pushed my arms against my face in time stop the pipe from hitting my face, but my knuckles suffered a blow, bursting immediately all over. We now stood toe to toe, both bleeding out darkness from our bodies. I felt a rage build up inside of me and I released it with another charging action, this time avoiding the pipe all together and bringing my knee into his now bent over body. I thrust my elbow into the back of his head, adding to the momentum from the strike, and pushed him to the ground. I immediately thrust the heel of my enchanted leg into the back of his head, seeing new puddles of crimson flowing from underneath. Awestruck at the actions in an invigorating way, I froze, basking in my own glory, only to fall to a blow from my opponents weakening force. I spun around as I fell onto the flaming barrel. The red hot edge of the barrel scorched my chest, slanting across to my abdomen. Hearing my skin blister and crawl with the immense heat, I shot around and rolled onto my back. My chest lay in ruin, raging pains plagued my mind and cluttered my thoughts. The teardrops from the heavens only made my chest pain even more. I screamed out looking for solitude to deal with my pain, but I was not granted sustenance on this cursed night. I saw my opponent slowly rise from the drenched ground, his face disfigured from a broken nose and rushing blood. He started to laugh. How can such hate produce such reckless power? I looked up, cowering from my pain as he neared. As he came towards my fluttering feet, I looked to my right and saw the barrel that lay me in ruin, but there was ever more detail put upon my eyes on the wood. I looked up as my opponent neared within striking distance, waiting for him to be in reach. I grabbed the wood out from the burning barrel and pushed it upwards into the demon’s chest. I can still hear the burning of his flesh as the decaying board’s byproducts pushed forward to conquer his skin. Out of either panic or failure of strength, he fell to the ground, smacking his head upon a keg that lay behind him, snapping open the back of his head, finishing his life. I remember looking into his open eyes and seeing nothing left, wishing he would only feel more pain at my hands. The fire burnt on through his flesh and up the erect board, reaching for the heavens that granted him this judgement. I turned around and looked back at the clerk that had to pay for such reckless hate. I felt sympathy for him, now completely drenched in his own blood. I walked over to him to look into his eyes, I only saw pain and sadness inside. I reached over, and closed his eyes and fell to my knees. I crossed his arms as if in prayer and said a prayer with him. “May my Father grant you passage into his gates of paradise and never let you know the word of pain ever again. Grant my request Father, and guide this poor soul into your bosom. This child died at the hands of a demon this evening. In Nomeni Patri Et Fili Spiritus Sancti.” I took my jacket and put it over his body, covering him from the drenching tears of his new family. I looked up towards the heavens, focused on the power of the Almighty, and headed back out to get back to the church where Father Emanuel could tend to the wound which now lay dormant, but ever so painful upon his ruined chest." Recalling the story from what seems like so long ago, intensified the pain he felt in his hands and poisoned his mind of more pains and sorrows. Zacharia starts to shake his head and tenses up in fury of the recollection, looking long and hard at his master’s son who also holds the look of anguish. The nail in his hands causes him to look down at his own; tears come to his eyes ever more at the thought of more pain and suffering. He slowly comes to stand, his knees shaking, and walks out of the pew. He steps up to the alter and falls to his knees, his arms falling limp to his sides. Raising his left hand in a struggle against his aching body, he makes the sign of the cross, pulls the cross out from under his hoodie, and then kisses it. He then clasps his hands together as if this is his last chance at prayer. "Father, please mend my wounds with your Almighty powers. Grant me the sanctuary of your house tonight and put grant sleep upon my eyes. Let me succumb to your will and let me drift into an everlasting sleep until I can get my strength back. Grant me these needs my Lord. In Nomeni Patri et Fili Spiritus Sancti." He raised from his knees and walked up to the cross to kiss the feet of His son and walked towards the isle. Once he reached the entrance to the isle, he turned around and knelt down to bless himself once more. Walking up the isle he reached the basin of holy water. Zacharia reached for the bottom of the bowl with his hands and let the water rush over the wounds of his hands. He begins to make the sign of the cross on his forehead, the water beading up and dripping down his face. The candlelight made it seem as if a darkness was falling over his figure in the basin, waging a battle of light and dark over his face and body, accenting the features of his face. He started to close his eyes as more tears began to flow, but he wipes them away before they can make any voyage down his cheeks. He steps back into the corner near the confession booth, and makes himself comfortable, for he is spending the night in the eyes of God tonight. When he went down to his backside he cringed in pain as his abdomen ached and swelled. He feels as though this was his punishment for not being ready for such an epic struggle in the ring. Next time, next time he will be ready, he just wait... ********* A blazing ray of light blasted through the morning clouds and down into the house of the Almighty. The church lit up as if the radiant glow from every candle was amplified tenfold and the moonlight became heightened. The church now looked a lot more comforting, reflecting the dreams of triumph that Zacharia had in the hours of the night. He pushed the hoodie off of his head and rubbed his eyes with a constricted strength, the morning holding onto his muscles with sleep. The faint sound of foot lay startled him slightly, throwing the hood back over his head and using the wall as leverage to push his awkward body into an erect position against the corner. A door closed and a shadow glided into the mouth of the church. The clatter of beads led Zacharia to believe that it was Father Emanuel coming in to open the church and prepare for the school children that will filter in around eight am. "Father. Do not be scared, it is only I, Zacharia." With his assuring voice, the father only stutters for a second, then continues to walk on his path following the head isle. "My son, where are you? I cannot see where your voice has come from. Show yourself so you do not startle this man of faith." Father Emanuel stops, his shadow following the orders of his body. Zacharia pushes his concealing hoodie back over his head and straightened up as if being inspected by a teacher for approval. He steps into view of the father with both is hands slightly raised out, calling upon his religious mentor to his presence. Father Emanuel bows down his head in greeting and raises his left hand to put it upon Zacharia’s head. He bows his head once more and mumbles a prayer to himself and finishes it with a solid Amen. He folds his hands in prayer and heads towards the alter, Zacharia in pursuit. After they reach the first step leading up to the alter, they both kneel down and do the sign of the cross to themselves. Each with their eyes closed, hands folded, and lips fluttering up and down mumbling a pray to themselves. Each bless themselves once more and stand up and walk up to the front pew and sit in silence. Father Emanuel has been a father figure since the moment Zacharia stepped into his church fifteen years ago. He was a tall, but stocky man in his forties. He took on being a priest when he was twenty, after losing both his parents as victims of a bank robbery, thinking it was the best way to find a reason "why" they were taken away from him. He felt that if he could take in religion and devote his life to God, than he could possibly make the world a better place. After learning more about religion and the injustice of the world, he turned to the interpretation of the bible to find answers. After biblical studies for years, he came across vengeance as the only possible answer to crime. It was not in his power or position to deal out this punishment, but it wasn’t until Zacharia stumbled upon his church one night that he knew there was someone that could. After teaching Zacharia about the injustice and what could be done to right the wrong, Zacharia took to this idea as the "right" way to handle these issues. He became to feel that God sent him the cross and split the pavement leading to his church for this to become true. He began to call their studies of the bible and human nature as the study of the "Modern Testament." Zacharia rises from the pew and walks out into the isle, facing Father Emanuel. The movement broke the trans of prayer and he looks over at Zacharia. "My son, what’s the matter? Why do you look so down and confused? Did something happen last night?" He looks puzzled, nervous. "Father, last night I failed. I fought someone last night and lost." Zacharia unzipped his hoodie and dropped it to the ground. Several bruises and cuts were revealed on his body. Dried blood still remains on his chest and hands where the fight from the previous night left him battered. He turns out his hands to show the battered knuckles to the priest. "Zacharia, who did you fight? I would hope that it was for good reason. God forbids such losses by His men! You have been battered before, but never have you looked so down upon yourself, especially after spending a night of comfort inside His house. Please tell me how this happen at once!" Father Emanuel sits on the edge of his pew, using the one in front of him as a crutch as Zacharia had done the night previous. He listens intently as the story unfolds. "It was against a man, Clyde Fox. He is not a man that is right in the head, father. He talks to imaginary creatures from a television show, as if they are with him right then and there. He acts out these ridiculous themes in his head, twisting what people say to go along with his scheme. I never have met such a possessed individual in my life. Not only does he interact with these imaginative creatures, but he draws power from them. Not a minute goes by where he doesn’t think that they are there and doing his bidding. I was pitted against him in a fight, but I could not summon the power to beat him. It was not God’s will on that night for justice to prevail!" The priest looks into Zacharia’s eyes, bewildered by what was just said. He is able to tell that what he says is true, seeing the proof of the loss upon his body. Each bruise and cut seemed to intensify by the notion that this was God’s will for the loss. Was he not worthy on that night to beat Clyde Fox? For such a man so evil, neither teacher nor student can figure out why He would not let this accomplishment be seen. Father Emanuel rises from his crutch, walking towards his student, and puts a hand on his shoulder. "Zacharia, this was a will of the Almighty, not a failure of your own. You know that His will outweighs yours. You should also know, my son, that it is not always about the pain you behold, it’s the pain you inflict." With these words, Father Emanuel walks up the isle and into the church office. Zacharia stands looking at his hands, rotating them in the light. Even though only a night went by, it seems as if the holy water cleansing helped accelerate the healing process. He turns towards the alter and kneels down. He says a prayer to himself and blesses himself saying: "In Nomeni Patri Et Fili Spiritus Sancti." He looks one last time at Jesus on the cross and knows what to do, get a rematch. ***** Walking outside of the church, the sun hit him strong, forcing his eyes shut to readjust. Zacharia pulls his hoodie back on once he glances over his right shoulder and sees two people walking towards him. Leaving it unzipped, but putting the hood over his head, he walks down the street, going to call Bagwell. Chris Bagwell, his mentor and a father figure, was at the match the previous night, fighting in it himself. After the loss to Clyde Fox, Zacharia walked into the back alley and started to talk, not knowing exactly where he was going. He jumped in a cab and eventually got home. The failure of the match was such a personal loss, he just did not know how to deal with such a physical and mental loss. "The only pain that matters is the pain you inflict." How could this be true? Deformation of the body and the spilling of blood are all forms of weakness and darkness, so how could it not matter? Father Emanuel has never put him in the wrong direction, and has cared for him as a son... Maybe it was time to get to this phone. As he turns the corner, he sees a very dirty looking man hitting a younger man, knocking him to the ground. As he fell, the dirty man starts to kick the back of his head into the wall of the building. Zacharia looks up into the sky, closing his eyes for a moment, and made for the man attacking. Something did not seem right about this man, not only because he was fighting a seemingly innocent man, but also for the way he carried himself with anger. This was a target for vengeance, and also a way for him to release his anger through action. When he reached the two men, he reaches out and grabs the man’s shoulder, immediately grabbing a fist that was thrown by his opponent. He starts to squeeze his fist, gritting his teeth, and starts to push his hand down. "You will not live another day to beat down on the innocent and take from them everything that is not yours to take." After this, the dirty man throws a knee, trying to strike Zacharia in the stomach, but he is far too quick for him. Bending back the arm that is extended to the man’s shoulder, he strikes him in the jaw with his elbow. The man stumbles back due to the hit and the unbalanced stance, half-tripping into the building. Zacharia throws a punch into the man’s gut, making him keel over in pain. A knee comes flying up into his jaw, pieces of a few teeth and the tip of his tongue plummeted to the ground. Screaming in absolute pain, Zacharia leans back, caught up in the punishment, then punches forward, but slips upon delivery. He strikes him in the throat, bones and skin crushing under the extreme force of his fist. The man falls limp to the ground, next to his former victim. Zacharia stares at the man laying motionless, then to the other man unconscious to the world. He bends over the lifeless corpse and says his prayer, finishing with "In Nomeni Patri Et Fili Spiritus Sancti." He stands up and heads back on course. Another block down, a phone booth comes into view. This is the closest public phone to the church, where he spends a lot, if not more of his time. He feels that this is more private than the church phone, where Father Emanuel may hear him talking to Chris Bagwell, someone who he doesn’t necessarily agree with. He looked into Bagwell’s background when he found out about him, and immediately expressed that it may have not been the best choice to have him as his mentor on the streets. Since this conversation, Bagwell was never brought up again, even though it is known that they still see each other. Bagwell helps organize the fights and handles a lot of the politics of the fighting realm. He needed to talk about last nights fight, and about getting a rematch against Clyde Fox. Every justification points to the vengeance Father Emanuel talks about. We steps into the phone booth and looks down to see a Star Ledger newspaper on the small counter. The Sports section was facing up, showing all the local sports and international. Bagwell always told him not to look at the newspaper after a fight, especially the day after a fight. Zacharia started to flip through the sections of the paper when a man knocks on the door, making him jump and the paper fall to the ground. He holds up his hand to the phone and reaches into his pocket to get out some quarters. The stare at the man made him back off the booth and sit on the bench on the side of the street. He dials Bagwell’s number and listens to the tones ringing. "Hello? Who’s there?" "Bagwell, it’s... it’s..." "Taylor? What’s up man? What happen to you last night? I was lookin for you in the locker room, but you never showed. Where did ya go?" "I...lost... Chris, I lost..." "Yeah, I heard man. I am sorry to hear that. Look, hes a good opponent, he’s got some skills in the ring, but come on man, you know you can beat him." "Look, I know. I talked to the Father, and he told me to shake it off. I need to get a rematch out of this. I’m beat and I can’t go out like this Chris, I can’t." "I can see if I can set somethin up for you Taylor, but it might take a couple weeks. Matches like this get hype, but they take some time to plan. Maybe we should think about how we can go about this, because I know that you can..." The words start to fade out into the background noise of the city. When Chris started to doubt if the match can be scheduled so soon, Zacharia looked down and saw one of the sports pages sprawled out across the bottom of the booth. Clyde Fox’s face was staring back at him, holding a belt in his hand. All time seemed to freeze and every ounce of anger seethed through the wounds on his body. "Television Champion" how appropriate. A man possessed, thinking he has tv characters following him around, and he is the "Television Champion". A fist clenches at his side, and the sadness turns into uncontrollable rage. The match starts to play in his head. "Why didn’t I win, how could he have beaten me? I dominated the beginning of the match, but he got me." Zacharia stands tall to his opponent, pinning him against the ropes, unleashing strike after strike, but then gets clobbered by a destructive close line, and then a dreadful elbow drop. The pain seared through his head, but he was able to get back up after breaking out of a pin. He starts to throw more strikes, and eventually gets him in his signature move, but Clyde still summoned his strength to push through. Going for another move, it was reversed into a full nelson, leaving the world in a spinning frenzy and muffled substance. The next couple minutes are a dizzy blur, until the announcement of Clyde as the victor. He then got up and stumbled into the back alley, where is humiliating venture home would start. Bagwell comes into the picture yelling at Zacharia. "Taylor, are you there? Taylor, pick up the phone!" "Sorry, what was that?" "What the hell were you doin? Where did you go?" "I, I saw something.” "Get your head in the game son, didn’t you hear me? We are flying to Japan." Zacharia hangs up the phone, looks down the street to flag down a cab, then heads off to Bagwell’s apartment.
