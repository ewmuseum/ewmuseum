Not again

25 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/25/09 I hadn’t seen Max since Greg was killed. I knew I should have thanked him for getting me out of jail, but I was bone tired. Between getting arrested on the most important night of my career and getting stabbed in the back by Axel; I felt entitled to a break from the craziness that is Max. But, truth be told, I was also scared out of my mind. As far as I could see my life was about to regain normalcy. Greg was dead, and my entire goal had been to get Brian out of his clutches. I didn’t want Max to tell me that this crazy adventure wasn’t over, that I wasn’t about to get my life back on track. But I knew I at least had to thank him for bailing me out, not to mention make sure that Brian got into the right kind of foster family. His building looked wildly different from the way it was when I first saw it. I don’t know if it was caused by the steady income that I had provided Max, or whether he just decided to upgrade his place but it was starting to look professional. Before he had the space for three offices and used about half of one. Now there was an actual secretary in the lobby, and to my immense surprise there were actually people waiting for their appointments. I hadn’t been aware that a detective’s office could be so busy, especially Max’s. I nervously walked up to the secretary that was fending off two complaining customers with expert efficiency. As a rule I generally hate talking to secretaries, their artificial politeness combined with the contempt hidden in their eyes makes speaking with them a wholly unpleasant experience. I swallowed my distaste, and walked up to her hoping to bully her into letting me see Max immediately. “Excuse me,” I said in an uncharacteristically small voice, “I would like to see Max please.” I winced as I realized I had never even learned his last name. If you think that my’I suppose we can call it fear of secretaries seems out of place, well it is. Let’s just say it stems from when I was in second grade, and is the result of a very mean, very bitter secretary who enjoyed terrorizing a small boy waiting for his dad. “Max’max” she murmured as she concentrated. I saw her eyebrows drop down in confusion, and then suddenly clear up as she figured out who I was talking about. “Oh you mean detective Max! Yes he can see you now.” I avoided raising an eyebrow at the dual facts that the secretary did not know Max’s last name either, and that she was letting me bypass all these people. But I wasn’t about to complain, so I quickly headed for his door before anyone could object. Strangely Max’s office did not live up to the professionalism of the lobby. I would call it Spartan; he had no windows, and one rundown desk, though he also had a life-sized Indiana Jones model in the corner of his office. I suppose that was what passed as his version of decoration. I sat down in the wooden chair as Max looked up with a surprised face. His face split in a wide smile as he saw me, though for some reason the expression unnerved me. “Hi Max,” I said with a forced smile. “I guess you’ve gotten a bunch of new clients now?” “A bunch of new clients,” he repeated mystified. “Oh, funny thing about that. I was running behind on the rent on this place, so I rented out the other two offices to a couple of lawyers. Not only do they pay me for the empty offices I had, but they even spruced the placed up, makes my customers feel more at home. “Max,” I said slowly, “just how many clients besides me do you have anyway?” “Ten,” he replied instantly. I blinked surprised at the number and Max immediately recanted. “Alright, I have six,” he amended a blush rising to his cheeks. I continued to look at him as the real answer finally became clear in my mind. I fought to keep pity out of my expression, even though the state of affairs had to be hard for him. “Since your case is done,” he said with a scowl, “I am back to zero.” “I don’t get it,” I replied sympathetically. “I admit I want to kill you half the time, but you’re good at what you do. I would have thought that you could have a pretty consistent source of work.” “Dude, we live in the Big Apple remember?” he asked me sarcastically. “All the best guys have a bunch of crazy equipment, contacts with the police, and are known by every big payer in the city. I’d be willing to take on the cheaper cases, but those are generally taken by local, neighborhood detectives which I am not. Hell, before you walked through my door my fridge was nearly out of food. Why do you think I glutted you as bad as I did?” “You glutted me?” “I charged you three times my regular rate.” “Wonderful,” I sighed though I did not really blame him. I knew what it was like to live on a tight budget with an unsteady income. I paused for a second trying to think of something to say, but I couldn’t come up with anything. I had no idea how to run a detective agency, and it was not like I could make up work for Max. “I’m’sorry,” I finally said when I could think of nothing else. We sat there for another minute, both of us too uncomfortable to break the silence. “So,” I said when the silence finally became unbearable, “what happened to Brian?” Max started then shook himself back to awareness. “Oh, yea the kid,” he murmured. “Well, a family wants to adopt him and I ran a background check on them. From what I can see they’re all clean, mostly seem to want a brother for their daughter. Moreover, for the kid’s own safety, the judge decided to not make the news about his trust fund open to the public, including possible foster families, until he’s of age. So they probably genuinely want to take him in.” I leaned back closing my eyes in relief. This whole, hellish ordeal was finally over with, and I could begin regaining my life. No more invading shady strip clubs, no more craziness, no more Max. I opened my eyes at that last thought and studied Max, noting the slightly downcast feeling he was exuding. “Also,” I continued awkwardly, “I want to thank you for bailing me out. Tell me how much it cost and I’ll reimburse you.” He looked up in surprise. “Bailed you out,” he repeated you slowly, “sorry dude but it wasn’t me. I never even got to jail.” “What do you mean you never got to the jail?” “Well, remember that stripper that I met a few weeks ago” Well, we met up while I was heading there. We got to talking, reminiscing about the good times we had, and next thing I know I wake up in an alleyway completely naked, wallet gone but feeling oddly satisfied.” I would have loved to know how he expected me to respond to that. Of course my first reaction was to strangle him for leaving me in jail while he performed some unnatural act with a stripper. But I suspected that would be more trouble than it was worth and decided to go with the old expression, alls well that ends well. “Alright, alright,” I replied. “I guess Caldera got wind of it and sent down one of his lawyers or something.” At least that was what I hope happened, I really did not want to have to deal with the mystery of who got me out of jail. But as I stared at Max, who was glumly realizing his last paycheck was about to walk out the door, I decided to throw him a bone.” “I suppose we could discuss the possibilities over a beer,” I suggested hesitatingly. He gave me a grateful grin, and headed for the door. “I’m driving.” Take a minute to imagine something. You are in the passenger seat of a rundown Volkswagen, happily arguing over who is going to win in the BJ Penn vs George St. Pierre fight with the driver. On the way to the bar you pass your apartment building? only to realize that the entire building is on fire. The fire trucks circled the building as I looked on in numb disbelief. I have always prided myself on not being overly sentimental, and thought that I could easily pack up and move if the occasion occurred. But now I was faced with the reality of having lost most of my possessions, and I had no idea where I was going to spend the night on top of that. Because of the shock it took me awhile to realize just how bad the fire was. In my building the sprinklers turned on as soon as the fire alarm went off, but it seemed that they were not powerful enough in this case. I flinched as fire shot out another window, while the firemen bravely tried to put it out. “This isn’t right,” Max snarled. I looked at him wondering where the anger came from. Sure it sucked that I was now homeless, but I hadn’t even asked him whether I could spend a couple of nights at his place. “There is no way in hell,” he continued, “that this fire was accidental.” I blinked, but before I could fully absorb the implications of his words he sped off. We drove in silence, Max in a cold fury, and me in shocked disbelief. I could have lived with an accidental fire rendering me homeless. But, callous as it sounds, I was hoping to hell that this was just the act of a budding young arsonist. The last thing I needed to hear was that someone had deliberately set fire to my home, for God knows what reason. I told myself that I was being paranoid, hell I lived in an apartment the fire could have been meant for any number of people. Somehow, that theory seemed a bit coincidental for it to hold much weight. Max abruptly parked in an empty space while I slowly looked around trying to figure out where his house was. “We have to go in there,” he said pointing towards a bar. I rolled my eyes, ready to tell him that drinking myself into a stupor was not going to make me feel better. “I have a theory as to why someone would have tried to kill you,” Max explained beating me to the punch. “I can’t go in there myself; there was an incident not to long ago involving the owner’s wife and her secret exhibitionist streak. But luckily, its simple enough that I can figure it out even if you do the legwork.” I looked at Max skeptically while wondering what else he had done with the owner’s wife. “Max,” I replied unhappily, “you do remember that I am not exactly equipped with a bunch of detective skills right” Beating up people kind of sums up what I can do in this situation.” “Relax would you?” Max demanded. “I am not asking you to do rocket science here. Go into the bar; find the girl sitting alone in the first table on the right. Sit down next to here, and ask how much for a night. Then come back and tell me what she said.” “I am NOT going to do research for you on the hooker’s prices!” “You WILL if you want to know who tried to kill you!” I let out a shout of pure rage and frustration, but Max seemed to be unfazed by it. I contemplated kicking out his windshield in pure spite, but that would make the eventual drive to his house a cold one. Finally I sighed realizing that I was trapped. I knew Max well enough to know that he was more than willing to sit out here all night until I did what he wanted, and it wasn’t like I had anywhere else to be. Slamming the car door on my way out I stalked my way towards the bar. Hopefully the experience would not be too painless. I would go in, ask the price, tell her that she was too pricey, then go back and strangle Max for making me do this. The girl was right where Max said she would be. I will admit that upon seeing her I was tempted to punish Max by letting him wait an hour while I actually hired the girl. She wore a tight black tank top that left little to the imagination, and black leather pants that showed off the shape of her legs nicely. Basically imagine a playboy model dressed as a hooker and you get the general idea. Nervously I approached and sat down next to her. She arched an eyebrow, daring me to make a move. Now I am no shy, blushing teenager. I know how to ask out a woman; but I’ve never directly asked a woman how much it would cost for her to sleep with me. Ok I have, but before they were the ones to bring up the subject. “So babe,” I said trying to sound confident, “how much for a night.” She looked at me for a moment, and then slapped me across the face. The sound was loud enough to be heard two tables over, which consequently began a series of jeers directed towards me. I ignored those idiots, as well as my stinging cheek, and stiffly got up and headed back to the car. “She slapped me and then I left,” I told Max angrily. “What kind of hooker does that?” “Oh she’s not a hooker,” Max replied. “She works for the bar. She sits there and looks hot; you wouldn’t believe how many drinks guys will buy her. Its one of the reasons this bar does so well.” I began to strangle the air, envisioning Max’s neck between my hands. I assume he noticed because he hurriedly continued, “but the guys who are shot down tend to be approached by another, slightly less attractive woman. She is a hooker, and I don’t like the fact that you never even saw her.” “Why?” “I’ll tell you back at my place. We probably shouldn’t say out in the open for now.” He began to drive before I could say anything. I gritted my teeth in frustration but if someone was trying to kill us, I supposed that staying out in the open was not the smartest of ideas. I leaned my head against the window and tried not to get nauseated. I had been so fucking close to living a nice, safe, (relatively) normal life again, and then this shit happens. For that matter I didn’t even know if I was actually a target, maybe this was all in the head of a detective in need of some cash. But honestly I was too tired to argue with him; so tired in fact that I fell asleep. I had a short confusing dream. Both Max and I were running in the ruins of a city. It was like one of those apocalypse movies, everything ruined and decaying. Yet it seemed like we had a purpose in our movements, a goal that gave us hope for the future. Then Max fell’or was tripped. He called out to me, but I shut my eyes and continued to run while his screams echoed in my head. “Dan’Dan’DAN!” I awoke with a start as Max shook me. I waved him off and stepped out of the car, only to stumble as a wave of dizziness abruptly overtook me. I waited until it subsided before following Max into his’ok I can not force myself to call it a house. The best I can do is label it as a shelter. It was a pathetically small’building with a about three rooms. I honestly would not have been surprised if Max had told me it was really just an apartment that had somehow gotten separated from the apartment building. His three windows were dirty as hell, and his couch was infested with at least three different types of mold. To my surprise there was one thing in Max’s house that actually looked spotless, a collection of DVDs that were carefully placed on rack. Of course, upon further inspection I realized they were all porno movies of a?.shall we say varied variety? “Um Max,” I began firmly, “before anything else I would like to know if you expect me to sleep with the greenish thing on what used to be your couch?” “Well I suppose we could ?” “I’ll sleep on the fucking couch. Now would you mind telling me why you think that my life continues to be a bad action movie?” Max threw me a bottle of coke before answering. “Dude think about it. Greg must have found out that someone stole that flash drive from him, and the information on it wasn’t exactly kosher. Not to mention that an operation of his size needs more than on boss, he must have had at least one partner in the loop.” “Alright,” I concurred before gulping down some coke. “That still doesn’t explain why his alleged partner would be targeting us, its not like they know who took the flash drive. And you still haven’t told me why I had to get my face slapped so hard that I can still feel the sting!” “Oh stop being such a girl,” Max joked but quickly turned serious. “I would bet anything that they have at least one contact in the police, which means that they knew you were the prime suspect for Greg’s murder. So they snuck inside your house, found the drive, then decided to get rid of you.” “Then why did they set my apartment building on fire, when I wasn’t even in it?” Max shrugged. “They are pimps not murderers. They probably hired a hitman who botched the job, which is why the entire building went up in flames. As for the girl, well honestly that had nothing to do with you. That hooker I was talking about asked me to help her get away from her pimp, so I planted some incriminating evidence tying him to a major felony. I assume that since she wasn’t there today, it worked. Thanks for all your help.” “So all you have so far is speculation,” I growled. “Besides, even if you’re completely right, we are still screwed. We don’t even know who we’re fighting, much less how to fight him or her.” Suddenly Max grinned at me. “It might not matter. They destroyed the evidence, you have no proof, and you’re basically no threat to them. As long as you lay low for awhile they’ll probably forget all about you. I’m the one who is up the creek without a fucking paddle ?” I never heard the window break and crash onto the floor. Rather I felt the air rush past me as the bullet just missed my shoulder. I saw Max fall down to the floor with a dull thud. I had the presence of mind to realize that Max was probably hurt. It took me a tad longer to realize the shooter now had a clear shot at me.
