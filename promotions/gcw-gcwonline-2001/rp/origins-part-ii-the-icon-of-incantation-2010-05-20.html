He hadn’t been sipping it as vigorously as he intended and the ice had all but melted away. Compensating for this, he took a wild gulp. It’s probably unbecoming of a professor, he thought, shifting his eyes back and forth between his fellow passengers. But no one seemed to care. Dressed in his cargo jeans and a faded green shirt, he didn’t look the part, anyway. To them, he was just a another traveler, someone adventurous enough –or stupid enough- to go to Iraq. Coach class. He paused from his alcoholic indulgence for a moment to rub the foggy lenses of his wide-rimmed glasses. This was the worst way to travel, he realized. But the price was right. And considering what his intentions in the region were, the university anthropology department probably would not allow him to write it off as a research expense. It was more of a private matter, really. Private, even though the knowledge he sought would have repercussions for the entire human race. Hmm. Maybe he would get reimbursed, after all. After finishing off his glass, he turned to Rachael and smiled. “Studying, dear?” he asked, but more for the sake of conversation than out of actual curiosity. She nodded. The beautiful young Samoan graduate student turned to meet his gaze, flicking her head in a quick motion that flung her straight black hair around her shoulders. “No, not really,” she said, showing her impeccably white teeth in a friendly smile. She held up a book and explained, “Just figured I’d read up a little on the region. I’ve never been to the Middle East before.” “Ah,” the professor replied, “doing your homework even when you needn’t be,” he laughed. “Well, if it means anything to you, we won’t be going out much,” he explained. “In fact, we’ll probably be stuck in the lab most of the time. Not a lot of time for sightseeing, dear.” She nodded her head in affirmation. “I know, professor-” “Call me Arpad,” he interrupted. Rachael smiled. “I know…Arpad. But I want to know a little bit about their culture, even if we won’t have time to order falafels, attend a mosque, or as I was hoping, smoke hash oil,” she joked. “There’s always time for hash oil,” Arpad joked. “As soon as we save the world, that is.” He looked out the window briefly and chuckled, not letting the full weight of his sentence sink in and sour their light mood. “I just hope this guy’s for real,” said Rachael. She flipped open her phone and looked at an image of PAZUZU, THE DARKNESS! “Otherwise, we’re fucked.” Professor Arpad Younan nodded his head in agreement before motioning over toward a stewardess, intent on getting another drink. He was a man of few words and only hung around long enough to hold their meetings and then he was off back into the desert. Where he was from, no one knew. Where the hologram had just been was now a massive opening to a tunnel that was well over 300 meters in diameter at its widest point.
