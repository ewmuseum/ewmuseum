Dos - Wake Up, San Diego

3 September 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 3rd 2009 Fresh off a debut victory in GCW, M3 and his associates are busy preparing for his first ever pay-per-view event, Warpath. For the unknown luchador, his appearance on the show will mark the accomplishment of a life’s ambition, with a victory setting him on the path to another �" holding a title in an American wrestling organisation. His win over Cyren at WorldWide 117 was a positive start to his GCW adventure, not to mention a good taster of the style he will have to deal with north of the border. His brief time in the ring left him bruised and battered, the high-impact moves dished out by Cyren a far cry from the more acrobatic, high-flying style he was so used to. Come Warpath he was expecting more of the same and was already preparing himself for it. Unfortunately, Jorge Ramos was more interested in the bright lights and glamour that holding the TV title would entail and as such, was focused more on getting his man on as many local news shows as possible, just so the media were familiar with M3 before the glory days arrived… *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ ‘Good match, great victory!’ M3 responds to this with a noncommittal shrug, him being the strong, silent type. ‘Now we have much to do! Very busy time!’ As he throws on his jacket, which looks downright bizarre next to his brightly coloured mask, M3 looks anything but busy. ‘Joo got interviews all week, everybody will know you!’ Even through the mask, we can see the raised eyebrow. Apparently Jorge can too. ‘No, no, es bueno. Joo are on ‘Wake Up, San Diego’ mañana!’ ‘Con Ron Burgandy?!’ ‘No, Senor Burgandy es no there no more.’ Another shrug from the Unknown Luchador, this time one of disappointment. ‘I know, es malo. Joo better get joor sleep esta noche, big day!’ M3 holds his hands up in the international gesture for ‘I know, don’t worry’ as he makes his way out of the apartment, leaving an excited Jorge to enjoy an evening of the finest American-made television. *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ ‘Hurry, hurry! Monito, joo are ready, si?’ The midget wrestler nods to Jorge from his perch in the makeshift living-room ring. M3 is nowhere to be seen at this stage, a fact that is causing Jorge’s blood pressure to skyrocket. ‘Taxi is outside, we gonna be late! No good!’ Jorge, it has to be said, is looking exceedingly sharp, or at least he would be if he was in the 80s. The wide-lapel, pastel blue suit looks like it came from Crockett’s wardrobe down in Miami and the gold jewellery gives him the appearance of a dodgy bookmaker. Still, he has at least got a look that people will remember…if he ever makes it to the interview. ‘Come on, rápidamente!’ It’s at this point that M3 emerges from the bathroom, in full wrestling gear. Impressive, yes. Practical, no. ‘Dios mio! What joo doing?! Es only a pequeño cab, jool no fit in!’ Another shrug, this one saying ‘Well, it’s too late to change now’. Amazing the range of emotions that can be expressed by this simple gesture. ‘Okay, we go now, before tengo el infarto.’ With this the eccentric trio made their way out of the apartment on their way to the glitz and glamour of local television. *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ As they pulled up at the address Jorge had been given, both M3 and Super Monito turned to him, as if to say ‘This is the big TV show?’. ‘Si, es definitely the place!’ The cab driver, having endured 26 long minutes of these three clowns, was keen to get rid of them. ‘That’ll be $38.60.’ Jorge paused halfway out of the cab, reaching into his pocket and pulling out some bills along with a lot of loose change. ‘We call $35, eh?’ The look the driver gave him wasn’t one of amusement. ‘It’s $38.60.’ ‘Sure, but we call it $35 this time. Es bueno!’ ‘Look pal, don’t mess me around!’ ‘Okay, here is money! Chill ese!’ Having been paid in full, the cab takes off, leaving Jorge spluttering in a cloud of burnt rubber and exhaust fumes and with a grand total of 78 cents on him. ‘That’s it, no lunch hoy!’ Walking up the steps to the entrance, Jorge feels as if he is walking the red carpet at the Oscars. Turning to his associates, he says some final words. ‘Monito, joo behave today! We no want any screw-ups! Es muy importante!’ Then they are inside and the adventure really begins. *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ ‘Larry Hodge, nice to meet you Mr Ramos.’ The producer was everything that Jorge had expected - suave, well-presented, mid-fifties. He looked like he’d stepped off a movie set, at least in Jorge’s mind. The reality was that he was a slightly overweight, average looking man who had been in the industry for 30 years yet hadn’t progressed beyond producing a local morning show. Not exactly Harvey Weinstein. ‘Es good to meet you too.’ Jorge is utterly sincere and slightly starstruck. Growing up, he never imagined that he would make it to America, let alone get on television. Yet here he was, only minutes away from being on the air. ‘I guess this must be Mr M3 and Super Mario then?’ The midget shrieks loudly at this, earning himself a sharp look from Jorge, ‘Super Monito, si.’ ‘My mistake. Nice to meet you both. Now, onto the important business. The interview will last around 7 minutes, with our anchors Ryan Scott and Abigail Carter, speaking with you about how you came to be here, what you and your associates are involved in at the moment et cetera. Then we’ll throw it over to M3 to show us what he can do.’ This confused Jorge. He thought he’d just come for a sit down interview, never had there been mention of any actual wrestling. ‘How you mean ‘show what he can do’? You want him to wrestle?’ Hodge looks bemused, as if wondering how his comment could be construed in any other way. ‘Umm…yes. I spoke with Mr M3 just yesterday to discuss it.’ Jorge looks at the Unknown Luchador with utter bewilderment on his face. ‘Que?’ M3, who is standing impassively with his arms folded, just nods. Turning back to Hodge, Ramos attempts to get his bearings. ‘He no habla inglés, how you speak?!’ ‘We have a lovely Puerto Rican lady who works here, she translated for us. We have the ring all set up and got a young guy from a local wrestling school in to provide token opposition. I think it will look really good on screen.’ Ramos nods at Hodge, before looking to M3, then back to Hodge. ‘Si, it will look fantástico.’ Then they are in the studio and the real fun begins. *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ With only minutes until the show goes live on-air, the studio is a hive of activity. Gaffers are busy ensuring the lights and cameras are in the right place, runners are ferrying scripts and coffee around the place and the talent is getting the finishing touches applied to their make-up. Jorge is loving the whole experience, the big lights, the constant activity, the woman moving round him and fixing his hair. This was the big time, after this appearance, they’d all be stars. M3 was over in the ring, loosening up and trying to explain to his opposition, using only his hands, what was going to happen when the cameras started rolling. ‘We’re on in 30 seconds, anchors to positions!’ The piercing voice of the production assistant breaks Jorge’s train of thought and the aforementioned make-up lady is suddenly nowhere to be seen. It’s time. *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ Eighteen minutes into the show and Jorge is in full flow. So far both Ryan and Abigail (‘Call me Abi!’) seem to be enjoying the interview, giving him plenty of time to get his points across and helping him out with his English if he needs a prompt. There is a slight element of laughing at, rather than with Mr Ramos, but he is blind to this fact and is having a great time, ‘So Jorge, your main man is called M3, the Mysterious Masked Mexican. Now you gotta let us know �" who is he really? I mean, I can’t imagine he was given that name by his parents! And that mask, I mean, it’s a bit over-the-top isn’t it?!’ Their laughs are well-practiced and obviously fake, yet Jorge carries on and responds, oblivious. ‘His name es secret. His mask es hiding secret identity. Es very serious thing Mr Scott.’ The solemnity of Jorge’s tone makes Ryan pause, a slight note of uncertainty creeping into his voice. ‘That’s…very interesting. We’re going to throw it over to the man himself now for a little demonstration of his skills that you can catch each and every week on GCW WorldWide. Trust me folks, this is impressive!’ The camera pans out from the anchors to show the other side of the studio where a small wrestling ring has been set up, artfully missing out the mess of wires, cables and technical equipment that would ruin the illusion of this being a vast, grand studio. In the ring is M3 and the local talent, a 6’4” streak of piss that weighs in just under two bills soaking wet. He has the anti-tan that only those with Irish heritage have and when standing next to our Unknown Luchador, looks like he has just been taken off hunger strike. Still, he’s just there as cannon fodder, shouldn’t be an issue. Except there is a problem. You see, this kid isn’t viewing this as a minor gig, no. He sees this as his ticket to the big time, to getting noticed in the wrestling industry. So far from taking it easy and letting M3 showcase his skills, he’s going to be awkward, fiery and downright aggressive. The phrase ‘Shit for brains’ springs to mind. From off-camera we hear Abigail’s voice, slightly too enthusiastic and precise, over-practiced in her delivery. ‘Take it away guys!’ With that, our local worker runs at M3 and lands a hard forearm to the temple that rocks the luchador, following it up with several knees to the gut and a running knee to the head. Jorge’s voice can be heard in the background. ‘What es he doin’?! Es not a match, es exhibition!’ For his part, M3 is doing remarkably well to keep his cool, merely trying to absorb the blows and not doing any damage back. If he has to, he’ll just make this guy look good and give him a receipt when the cameras aren’t rolling. Unfortunately, the enhancement talent thinks he is actually controlling the luchador and as a result tries to press his advantage. More punches, kicks and forearms and then…he makes his major error. He goes for the mask. The mask, the most sacred item to any luchador, the symbol of history and status and respect that every wrestler who wears it holds so dear. The one inviolable thing. As soon as the guy tried to untie the laces of the mask, M3 exploded, obviously having learnt a few lessons from his match the week before. He fights back with fury, landing blow after blow, then a body slam and repeated stomps. The anchors are at a loss. ‘Uh…what’s happening? Are we not going to commercial now?’ All hell is breaking loose in the ring and things take another turn when two other guys get up from their seats off-screen and make a beeline for the Unknown Luchador. Apparently, Joe Jobber brought some friends. ‘Don’t worry pal, we’re coming.’ Jorge is going spare. ‘No, no, no! Es bad! Stop camera, stop camera! Dios mio! Que pesadilla!’ The sun-starved punk’s back-up has arrived now and M3 is looking around for a chair, a ladder, a bat, anything to defend himself with. Instead, he gets help from a surprising source. Super Monito. As the reinforcements climb into the ring, the pint-sized superstar pops out from under the ring and pulls the legs of one out from underneath them, causing them to eat some apron. Things get even better for the Mexican contingent when Super Monito grabs a 2x4 from under the ring and tosses it into M3. Suddenly the tough guy isn’t so full of it. ‘Okay man, no need to get angry, we’ll just be going now. Come on buddy, think we should be leaving now.’ He grabs the human milk bottle and drags him out of the ring before quickly making his way out of the studio. M3 and Super Monito stand tall (well, M3 does) in the centre of the ringas the camera pans back to the anchors and Jorge. ‘…Uh…umm…that was just a taster…’ ‘…Yeah…GCW…live pay-per-view…’ Jorge has recovered totally now and is beaming from ear to ear, ecstatic over how his guys looked. ‘Si, es real good GCW! Warpath pay-for-view con Andy Murray, Trash Bag Johnny y M3!’ The open-mouthed anchors stare blankly at the camera as the show goes to commercial. Jorge turns to them and gives the thumbs-up. ‘Es muy bueno, no?’
