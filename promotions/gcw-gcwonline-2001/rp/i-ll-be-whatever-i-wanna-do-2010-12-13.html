I'll be whatever I wanna do.

13 December 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/13/10 "Come on, Dad, three more reps. Don't be a wuss about it." My daughter. "You don't need to talk trash, honey. Just spot the bar." "If you've got breath to talk back, you've got breath to gimme three more reps." I won't lie. I get a kick out of her. She's got her mother's intellect, wit, lexicon, and cunning. But she definitely takes after her father when it comes to instigating and pushing buttons. What can I say? I'm proud of what I've created. At first the gym told me I wasn't allowed to bring my ten year old daughter in with me. I'm honestly not normally one to abuse my celebrity status nor the depth of my wallet. But, upon informing the desk jockey in the back where my business was liable to go the tune quickly changed. The fact that I purchased a five year membership for my (not even close to) eighteen year old daughter helped to smooth things over. One self-appointed savior of the human race decided to get a bunch in her panties and convey the error of my ways about ten minutes into the workout. This isn't really the safest environment for a little girl her size. Don't you think your daughter deserves to be safe? I politely informed her that the world isn't safe, that I know more about safety in a gym than she ever would, and that I promised I wouldn't lecture her on how to raise her kids. She told me I was irresponsible...like I didn't fucking know. Luckily I didn't have to say anything back because my ten year old daughter promptly told her where to go. "One more! Don't be a nancy." My little miracle. A long exhale accompanies the barbell as I rack it and sit up. My diminutive angel holds out a towel for me to clean off the sweat, her face in a half smirk as she is taking her role as my trainer very seriously. It takes a moment to dry off the back of my neck...more of an issue of the length of my hair than the intensity of my workout. I'm not stupid. I'm not going to bust my ass with a ten year old spotting me. "You do good work, Superman, but you can do better." "Superman, eh? I'm not sure, pumpkin, but that name might be copyrighted." "I still like it better than Johnny Borealis, Daddy." Finished cleaning off the bench, I take a swig of coconut water before glancing each way. Most of the gym members know who I am and know that if they want autographs that I'll sign in the parking lot. It's unofficial gym policy that there be a celebrity bubble, usually between one and two machines thickness, surrounding certain "privileged patrons." On one hand I don't like being pretentious. On the other hand it gets people excited, gets them worked up, even gets them polarized about the issue. Who the hell does he think he is? Well...to be fair, he isn't just some regular jackoff hitting the weights for a few hours every week. Still, there's no need to parade my name around. Nobody's heard her though, or they haven't shown it if they have. "You don't like my ring name, Sarah?" "It sounds silly." "Well that's part of the point, honey-" I've been lifting weights for a couple of hours, but lifting my daughter up to sit on my lap is somehow different. She's physically lighter, but there's an effort, a care put into it that puts it in a different category from pumping an iron bar over and over and over. Pulling my daughter onto my lap rejuvenates my muscles but taxes my emotion, and perhaps it's because I know this is the only time we'll spend together for at least a week. My only day with Sarah and I take her to the gym. Sometimes I wonder if I even know what priorities are. But I take the moment to share a bit of my real self with her. "-If people think I'm a goofball, they don't take me seriously. And if they don't take me seriously, they don't see me coming. And if they don't see me coming, that's bad news for them. And it all starts in a name." That's all bullshit and I know it. Nobody of GCW's caliber is fool enough to take opponents lightly. "Keith says that how people see you is important. It's who you are." Keith is Kate's choice for a surrogate father when I'm not around. He is pretty much everything I'm not, and that's probably why she's convinced herself she loves him. "Babygirl...'who you are' is something people spend their whole lives trying to figure out. It's not as clear cut as what others see of you. And if it is, you've probably failed as a human being." Her little ten year old brain tries to wrap itself around the logic of a cynic. Sometimes I don't know if I feel worse for my daughter or myself, but luckily before I can have a chance to bounce that thought back in forth in my head- "JOHN!" -Sarah's mother derails the train for me. Kate storms over and body builders dive out of her way. She's probably been building up a full head of steam the entire drive from her house, and she's convinced herself up and down how much of a jackass I am. Don't let those little boy eyes look at you and don't even let him open his fucking mouth were probably her mantra as she weaved in and out of the congested roads in an effort to catch me in the act. It doesn't take a genius to figure out that the bitch my daughter told off earlier probably took it upon herself to inform that little girl's mother just what Johnny Borealis is doing. "What the Hell is wrong with you?" "Hi Kate." "Hi Mommy!" I can't help but smirk. I really, really know I shouldn't. There's just something about watching Kate come to a dead halt and take a breath, lowering her voice and making an effort not to yell at her precious daughter, all while still concentrating on staying mad at me. "Hi Sarah. I love you. I need to speak with your father for a moment." Sarah stares at Kate for a moment, not sure what that statement means. Nothing would piss off Kate more than if I got Sarah to step away while being the good guy. "Babygirl, could you go get me another coconut water from the counter? Here's a dollar." "Okay Dad! But don't bitch out while I'm gone." Sarah snatches the dollar bill from my hand and bounds off with a ten year old's excitement. She's been given an important task. She has no idea how important; Kate needs to hurry up and scold me before she pops a blood vessel. There's a visible effort to control her burn as she ignites her words all over me. "Don't...bitch out? Is this what you call parenting, John?" It's all strategy when I take the weights off the barbell and take my time putting them away. If I treat this scenario like it's a big deal, it will be a big deal. Right now it's just an angry lady yapping at a man on an endorphine high. "Hey, she didn't learn it from me, Kate. Is that the first time you've heard her swear?" "Well it better damn well be the last time." Again, I can't help but smile. Angry people make asinine statements. "She's ten, hun. This is the prologue to The Hobbit of swearing. In three years you'll be knee deep in The Fellowship of the Ring with ring wraiths made out of the word 'motherfuck' and 'cunt.' It's a fact of life." Did that make sense? It didn't have to. I may or may not have just finished LoTR last week... "And you think it's alright to encourage it? You think that's a good thing, John?" The last of the weights is on its rack and I finally turn to look her square in the eye. The fact that Kate is still wearing her business suit and heels tells me she hasn't had a chance to get home and change her clothes after another fine day of lawyering. She's riled up, but she's still in courtroom mode, which means she's planning on being vicious and remorseless in her attacks, planning to back me into a corner and make me damn myself with a mispoken defense. Her immediate dismissal of her daughter only reinforces the fact that she's not in Mommy Mode yet, and the clench in her jaw tells me she is ready to tear my throat out. My God she is hot. "She didn't learn how to swear from me." I don't know that for certain. "Really? Not from her father who takes her to a gym at ten years old?" Probably. "Nope." "Well if she ever used that kind of language in the Academy, I'd be the first to hear about it. So, pray tell, where did she learn how to talk like a sailor?" I relish the moment. I won't lie, I was curious myself the first time Sarah told me to "sack up." Needless to say, I found the moment funnier than Kate would have. But it wasn't until she told me where she heard it that I had to laugh at the irony. Now I'm smiling like the cat that ate the canary. "You know that thing that I do that you say that you hate? Your perfect boyfriend lets our daughter watch it on TV." Kate's entire fascade drops in an instant. The rug has been pulled out from under her, and it's just so perfect because for once in my life it's not my fault. For months now I've had to hear subtle jabs about how fucking great Keith is. Keith works in an office. Keith dresses in a suit. Keith is home every night by six. Keith is nice to Johnny while Johnny is a douchebag. Keith doesn't beat the shit out of other human beings for a living. It's a small victory, but knowing that Keith is the direct cause for Sarah learning how to curse really, really feels good. I say it's ironic because Kate and I discussed it and neither of us felt that Sarah should be watching GCW. We didn't think she should see Superman get dropped on his head, Kate didn't think she needed to see the violence, and Kate also didn't think Sarah should hear her father swear as much as I do. I conceded...but I wasn't the one who let her watch behind Kate's back. Sometimes in life there are these perfect moments. Kate standing there, trying to process that for once Johnny did the right thing and Keith fucked up, is one such moment. "Personally, sweet cheeks, I couldn't care less. It's a fact of the world. People curse. But I was more than happy to play your game, and your perfect boy-toy done goofed, and God damn does it feel good. It really does." Of course, she's not out of it yet. She had a purpose for storming in here before she heard her daughter utter the heart-stopping b word! "Fine. But you're the one who brought her to a gym, John." So? "So?" "Can your brain process how small her body is? Can you comprehend how easily she can get hurt? Or what if...what if there's someone here who has bad intentions. You just sent her off out of your sight in a room full of strangers. My God, is there one moment in your life that you think about someone other than yourself?" By Sarah's age I was already sneaking in to R rated movies and disappearing for hours on end. I'm not claiming to be any sort of bad boy, just a normal boy. A healthy kid. Someone who was raised in a time where neighbors weren't scary, Halloween was for trick-or-treating, and sometimes you just fucking scraped your knee. Kate is one of the new breed of parents. Every day she hears about how the world is trying to kill and maim her precious little snowflake. Kate doesn't late Sarah go out on October 31 even though there has never been a documented case of razor blades or poison in the Halloween candy. She doesn't let her daughter meet the neighbors because even if Kate has known the people for ten years...you never know! The most important thing to Kate is keeping her daughter in a safe little bubble, away from the ravenous monsters outside the house, so that her little girl can stay alive. I just want my daughter to know what living fucking means. "What if a car slams into you, Kate? What if a plane crashed into your house? What if what if what if. Does it ever hurt being so paranoid?" We've had this argument a few times before. We've had this argument a few times before our daughter was even born. Back before I left this girl ten years ago, back when we were in love, we used to fight about this shit all the time. I used to be energized and challenged by her stances and her willingness to defend them. As time went by, though, her arguments became dulled and redundant, and so too did the discussion. She didn't have the passion for the subject at hand, and she had grown tired of hearing my reasoning and my logic. It was a sad day when fighting with Kate became boring. I see it one of two ways. You can push against the corruption of aging, or you can be flexible and mold it. Sarah has started swearing...if we push against it, it becomes the forbidden fruit. If we work with it, if we simply redirect the outlet that the vice gives her, it loses appeal. Opposing it outright creates a rebel, an oulaw, not unlike GCW's current World Champ. Working with it, however, would molt our daughter into a strong, capable, relatable human being. As Kate's gearing up for another assault on my character, I catch a view of Sarah skipping back in our direction with a refreshing drink in her hand. A quick nod tells Kate to cool her jets before we start cursing each other out and tearing each other down in front of our daughter. She has time for one last thrust of her words. "It wouldn't kill you to take at least one thing in your life seriously, John." It might. Sarah is oblivious to the tension as she pops up next to her mother and extends the drink to me. I take it with a thank you and smile at her before a sip quenches some fire. Kate has no idea the things I take seriously. "Little girl...your mother and I were talking, and we don't know if it's a good idea for you to watch me on TV. It might have to wait until you're a couple years older." Sarah is part scared, part betrayed, and part shocked. Being allowed to watch GCW was a special secret between her and Keith, and telling me about it was a special secret between her and I. Now Keith might get in trouble and she won't be able to see her dad and worst of all...she won't have that one little thing that her mom won't let her have. A tiny dose of rebellion is important for a kid. Her mouth starts to form a protest as she turns from me to Kate to me to Kate. But she knows that when the fun-loving parent lays down the discipline, it must be for real. She buries her head into her chest and mutters to appease us. "Okay..." I look up at Kate with an is that what you wanted look and she flashes me her patented I can't believe you and the world keeps spinning. A shrill beep from my phone takes my attention for a split second. Kate looked like she might want to say something to make sure I know that Sarah knows that Mom is in charge too, but I hold up a finger and check my text message. It's this week's matches for WorldWide. In the immortal words of Will Smith, shit just got real. "Kate...can we, um...can we continue what we were talking about later on tonight? I can swing by with some...dinner or something. For everybody. Keith included." "What's wrong, Johnny?" She let it slip. Johnny. She only lets that slip when she isn't concentrating on showing me that she doesn't care about me. I feel kind of bad. In her mind it must be something horrible. Someone might have died or I might have been fired or there might be a catastrophe in text message form in the palm of my hand. There are so few things that grind me to a halt, so few things that, in her words, I take seriously. A tiny shake of the head and a laid back smile try to tell her that nothing's wrong, life is peachy, and everything is just swell. I hardly believe it myself. "Just some news from work. Nothing big." Right. Nothing big. "Daddy's probably got to defend his title, Mom. Take some names and kick some asses." Sarah goes sheepish as the last word slips out almost accidentally. Kate doesn't notice. "You sure, John? You don't look...I mean...you're okay?" For the sake of needing something to do, I find a dumbell in my hand. I was pretty much done with working out, but my mind is racing a little too fast to just sit and have a conversation right now. If my critics are right about one thing, I'm not a good enough actor on my own. Luckily the natural cringe of muscles as I curl the weight take some of the edge off my face. Kate knows something's up, but she also knows not to ask when I say not to ask. It wasn't supposed to happen like this. "It's all good...really...how about you guys...head out...I'll stop by later...and make some of my...kung pao tofu." The straining pauses are hiatuses from thought where my autopilot can fill in words. In reality, a conversation might be difficult and I need the two most incredible women I've ever known to leave me alone with my thoughts as they speedball through around synapses. "Alright John. I'll tell Keith to pick something up for him on his way home. He's not a fan of tofu." Like I give a rat's ass. "Bye Daddy! I love you." Sarah darts in to give me a hug and kiss, and the dumbell almost clocks her in the face. It takes every ounce of available concentration to pull the weight in toward me and miss her, and Kate's hand on her shoulder helps significantly. Those adult teeth just came in, we don't need her losing them so quickly. I don't know if Sarah caught the look in my eye mid-lift or if she was surprised by almost getting fifty pounds of metal in the jaw, but there is definite hurt in her eyes for the briefest of seconds. Maybe she can't believe she almost got hurt so close to her dad. Maybe she can't believe the grimace on the face and the fire in the eye from exerting myself. Not with the damn weight, but with the news. Jay Terror? Right now? God damn it. "Come on, sweetie. Daddy will stop by later." The venom is gone from Kate's voice as she tugs our daughter away. Looking up, all I see is heartbreak. It's the first time in her entire life I haven't picked her up and hugged her and held her for about five minutes when she's saying goodbye. Try as I might, I can't stop myself. I need to do this next rep. And the next one. And the next one. Like a prisoner watching behind the glass, I have to watch Johnathan Fitzwillis act like some animal while his daughter just tries to say goodbye. What if there's a car accident? What if a meteor hits their car? What if the world ends? What if my daughter dies of heartbreak. It's not until the two of them round the corner that I lose my ability to contain it. What it is, I don't know...frustration, anger, anxiety, excitement. All I know is fifty pounds of metal crashes onto the mat about a milimeter from my foot, and the noise from the weight rolling onto the concrete is deafening. The patrons of the gym give a start and turn to see a grown man hurrying to the bathroom in a haze. It's like someone just turned on a fucking fog machine in my brain. Analyzing the situation results in a half coherent mess, and none of it is at all good. That's the only thing I'm sure of. Who knows what the crowd in the gym thinks as I'm staggering to the bathroom like a kid that just downed his first bottle of tequila. These are the same people that scoffed or laughed or shrugged when they saw my ten year old daughter spotting for me a few moments ago. A few moments separate a cute father/daughter bonding trip from a display of shame. All any of them can wonder is what's wrong with that guy as I almost knock over a rack of weights on my way to the restroom. It's not like I got punched in the gut, it's just that I can't seem to connect the wires to get the engine to start. Jay Terror. Now. I didn't do what I needed to do. It should've happened before, it should've happened later. It should've fucking happened two years ago is when it should've happened, Johnny. God damn it. The everyday jackoff gym members stare and part way to keep up the celebrity bubble for Johnny Borealis as I push open the door to the men's room and lean on the sink. They listen for sounds of vomiting or a tantrum or some sort of gossip piece to come from the bathroom so that they can be the first to say yeah I knew he was a train wreck. They're already making snide remarks about knowing how to stretch or knowing what to eat or taking bets on whether or not I've done drugs or this or that. Sarah's words from five minutes ago suddenly echo in my head like a gong. "Keith says that how people see you is important. It's who you are." Fuck Keith. Fuck those people. They have no idea what they're seeing, so let them see what they want. If Kate could manage to read my mind, she'd understand exactly what I take seriously. A handful of cold water on my face clears up some thoughts. Brings things down to Earth for a moment so that I can wade through the shit and the muck and maybe come up with something. As soon as my feet touched the floor at Dangerous Games, I knew I'd blown it. The kicker was, as usual, it was all my fault. Having overcome a wardrobe malfunction, having overcome Chris Storm's temper tantrum, having overcome other peoples' size and strength, the same thing bit me that bites me time and time again. I got in my own damn way. I have no way of knowing if I would have won Dangerous Games had I kept my head screwed on straight. Not knowing doesn't stop me from rearing back and giving Johnny a good kick in the ribs, though. I'd let myself get caught up in what? A cheap on the spot competition with Chris Bagwell over the number of eliminations. In reality, there is only one number of eliminations that matters...one or zero. How many times did I get eliminated? Andy Murray walked out with the prize of prizes. Sure, some people believe that the World Title is the end-all be-all of being a wrestler. Fuck that. There are plenty of world champs that aren't even remembered. If I were to walk in there at WorldWide and talk about how I practically ended Michael Stevens' career, there are a good number of people who have been there for years that would give me a blank look and ask "Who?" No. The chance to take the World Title at center stage at the biggest event of the year...the prestige of surviving a thirty person battle royale...the endorphines that rush to the brain as one basks in that unbelievably, painfully perfect moment...that is the greatest moment that I think could be achieved. Andy Murray earned it. He earned every second of it, and I'm not about to bitch about any of it. I don't know if I would have lasted any longer if I didn't make a big deal out of showing up Bagwell, I don't know if I would have even lasted as long as I did. What I do know is that the choice I made, the path I chose, led me to cause my own God damn elimination. My heart sank through the concrete when I looked up in that ring and didn't see myself. "Sometimes things don't work out like they're planned, Johnny." The face in the mirror knows what he's talking about. I've trusted, absolutely one hundred percent trusted, one person and one person alone in over ten years of professional wrestling. Only one man has proven that he will do what he says time and time again. And on April 2, 2008, on my fucking birthday, I came home from a video shoot and I watched Jay Terror wrap a steel chair around his head. I remember knowing at that moment, as soon as Jay cracked Jordan White's skull with the metal chair, that I was going to come back to GCW. And I was going to get my hands around Jay's smoke stained throat. I wish it was for vengeance. I wish I wanted to right the wrong. I wish beyond wishing that I wanted to beat the living shit out of Jay Terror for some noble, righteous reason. Instead I wanted to hurt him simply because of jealousy. Jordan trusted Jay the way he never trusted me...Jordan trusted Jay because he and Jay went through hell together. I'm so fucking fickle I even find myself hard to believe. But there was Jordan, literally turning his back on his best friend and letting his guard down because he absolutely trusted him. That should have been me. Jordan should have trusted me. Sometimes things don't work out like they're planned. Again, like Dangerous Games, it was my own damn fault. And again, yet again, I spent two years of my life wondering a couple hundred what if's. And my chance to meet Jay on the center stage, to earn my spot legitimately, with no bullshit and no party crashing and no talk of conspiracy...and I fucked it up for myself by getting into a dick sizing contest with Bagwell. But now... "There are a couple things in my life I take very seriously, Kate." Telling Kate to her face would have been as pointless as saying that to the bathroom mirror. She's absolutely convinced, the way that most people are, that I treat life as a sitcom. And I'm going to walk in to WorldWide for a standard match against the man who ignited the flame in my gut, and most are going to forget about it in a month or two. The way that they've forgotten about Jordan...the way that they've forgotten about Michael Stevens. Each of us are forgotten eventually. "Keith says that how people see you is important. It's who you are." Keith's wrong. It is what we remember that makes us who we are. It is what we can look back upon those perfect coincidences of our lives that make us who we are. I've been handed my chance to do exactly what I've been yearning to do, and in true form it is exactly opposite how I wanted to do it. Titles aren't on the line, we're not the main event, we're not embroiled in a blood feud. Jay is going to take me seriously, but only because he takes everyone seriously. He's not going to come down to that ring with a distinct purpose unless I go out of my way to give it to him. This match doesn't mean shit. Except to me. I guess there are moments in life when the universe says, "How much does it really mean to you?" For two and a half years, I have wanted to make Jay hurt because I hurt myself. I've wanted it for myself. I've wanted to hurt the man that crushed Jordan White, and I've wanted to do it for no other reason than the fact that Jordan White never trusted me enough to let his guard down. I never got that chance to choke, I never got that choice to make, and I might possibly hate Jay for it. People might think I envy that bastard for his World Title or for his infamy, but the harsh truth is I deserve what he pissed away. The trust of a friend. Hands braced on porcelain, I can feel my cognitive thought dropping back into place. For a brief moment, my memories carried me through time itself, looking back at the man that made the choice that caused my want to come back. Jay chose for himself, and immediately I chose too. I chose to never let my chance at the spotlight slip away again. Sometimes things don't work out like they're planned. Fuck it. I've waited too long to complain about the particulars.
