Date: July of this year ---- July of this year. Villefranche-sur-Mer, France. Early evening. ---- A picturesque evening if ever one had seen one. A warm summer's evening was descending upon Villefranche-sur-Mer, one of the last harbours remaining on the French Riviera yet to be bespoiled by the wrectched and all-consuming hand of the tourism industry, and the waves of riff-raff and hoi-polloi it would inevitably drag along in its wake. A beautiful harbour with a tiny fishing community, it was as untouched and unspoilt a location as one could ever hope to find, and the perfect place for the DeVilliers' annual yacht party. As the sun dipped gracefully below the horizon much like a well-trained butler bowing majestically out of his masters' room, its last few rays stretched out as if to wave goodnight to the sky, now an artist's mixing palette of mauve, of crimson and of orange. Were it not for the queasy-looking guest lurking anxiously by the lavatory door, looking rather green of face and wobbly of knee, one would have to pinch oneself to assure that this wasn't heaven. Our intrepid protagonist, a certain Thomas James LeStrange, stood elegantly on the deck of the DeVilliers' yacht, supping from a glass of brandy, carefully the young Constance DeVilliers, in conversation with a plain-looking young man and clearly rather agonised by the whole affair. The dying light of the day only served to enhance his handsome features; his strong, masculine jaw, softened by his delicate nose and mouth, made enigmatic by his intense and engaging brown eyes. His hair, of a wonderfully rich chestnut colour, was worn forward in a side parting this evening, down towards his forehead before being masterfully swept towards his ear. As always, his attire was impeccable; black tie, as the occasion demanded, with a low-cut waistcoat and beautifully polished black shoes. Constance DeVilliers herself was no less a vision of beauty, resplendent in a fine gown. Thom (as he had implored people to address him), having watched her squirm for several minutes before the considerably less-than-satisfactory chit-chat he appeared to be offering (something his old rugby team-mates would have referred to as "smelly chat") moved over to the pair and greeted Constance, hoping to save her from the frightful bore beside her and cure the situation with a little of his own effervescent conversation. As he kissed her hand, Thom observed that his eau de toilette, Lacoste Red, appeared to be exercising somewhat of an intoxicating effect upon her, for every time she inhaled he saw her eyelids flutter and a brief smile flicker across her striking features. "Thom, you must meet this chap here, he tells me he was at Bedford School around the same time as you were." Constance motioned towards the chap next to her, whose expression had turned somewhat sour. Thom reached for a handshake, which the chap grudgingly returned. "A pleasure old chap. Did you enjoy your time at Bedford? Cracking place wasn't it? Never a dull moment, every chap game for a laugh or a lark. I remember in my final year we pulled a cracking joke at this dullard Mark Smith's expense. He was this cretinous, weedy little thing, used to be the butt of everyone's jokes. Easy target, don'tcha know. Anyway, we placed a false notice in with the usual notices just before Final Assembly of our last year. Sure enough, assembly runs as usual, we sing the School hymn "Domus Pater Harperiae", and so on, we have a prayer, the sports cups are given out and the Headmaster announces next year's Head Boy, and sure enough, he gets to the notices. There's the usual guff about returning CCF equipment, signing out with our Tutors and so on, and would you believe it, he reads ours out without realising what he was saying." Thom paused briefly, savouring the expression of suspense and delight that was playing itself out over Constance DeVilliers' features. "And what did it say?!" She implored, barely able to contain herself. "It said, 'Mark Smith has lost an infant's size protective cup. The Junior School has been searched but there is no sign of it. If anyone knows of its whereabouts, please contact Mark or his tutor, Dr. Young!'" At this, Constance exploded into mirth. Satisfied that his anecdote had hit the right spot, Thom glanced over at the dour young man at his side, who remained stonyfaced and unamused. "Not a fan of those sorts of japes, eh? Fair enough old chap, each to their own." There was a long pause in which Thom could hear an audible grinding of teeth. Eventually the sourpuss deigned to speak. "I am Mark Smith." There was another, considerably more awkward silence, which was punctuated with Constance's unsuccessful attempts to stifle her laughter. At one point, Thom was sure he saw her extract a handkerchief from her handbag and dab tears from the corner of her eyes as she continued to guffaw. Mark angrily excused himself and sauntered off, red-faced, to find a drink. Constance turned to face Thom, smiling, her eyes thankful. "Thank you very much," she said, "I'd been trying to get rid of him for about fifteen minutes. It seems all that was required was one of your anecdotes and off he scurried!" She chuckled at her own polite insult. Thom smiled back. "You are quite welcome. I'm not sure if that was an insult or a compliment, but I shall take it as the latter!" "It's quite genial to find a chap you genuinely enjoyed his schooldays. I fear people take it frightfully seriously these days, no one seems to be able to enjoy their childhood any more. Mind you, youth of today. All this under-age drinking and such..." "I'm afraid, my dear Constance, that we are hardly in any position to judge. T'would be hypocritical of us to criticise the young for their substance-related indiscretions given the place of my first meeting with your father - in one of those lovely little places those charming law-enforcement chaps sent us when we'd over-indulged a tad in some naughty substances. Apparently a chap can indulge in a few snifters of the old Bolivian marching powder in the comfort of his own home, but when he challenges a chum to see if he can urinate all the way across the Thames, he's gone a bit too far." "Is that really where you met Daddy? I haven't heard that story!" Thom smiled confidently, whistfully taking another sip of his brandy whilst looking out at the view of Villefrance-sur-Mer from the yacht. Along the Promenade des Mariniers the quaint little seafood restaurants were beginning to open, and behind them the labyrinthine steep streets of the Old Town were quiet. He took another sip of his brandy before beginning his tale. "Well, we both happened to be at a party in New York, as we'd both decided to reside there for a while. Both being the only two English chaps there, we naturally began talking, and embibed a fair amount of port between the two of us. Later, he mentioned he'd purchased a reasonable quantity of a certain white powder for the occasion, and asked if I'd like to partake. At the time I was rather in the habit of taking the stuff, and indeed had gotten myself into a few scrapes with the local constabulary for my various acts of drug-induced tomfoolery, and naturally replied in the affirmative. Next thing you know we've been arrested and charged with assault for accosting some foreign johnny off the street called Tom’s, and convincing him his name was 'Mr. Pippy' and that he was to be our butler and lackey for the evening. Our respective lawyers being the clever cads that they are, managed to get Mr. Pippy to drop the charges and convinced the boys at the NYPD that a brief stint in one of those rehabiliation 'maisons' would be the just ticket to put us straight, and then we'd be on the next plane home." "So next thing you know, we're in this rather seamy clinic, where we can't go out for an evening stroll, and we couldn't get an evening scotch for love nor money. We're sitting in a room full of sofas for a spot of what they called "group therapy", surrounded by a rather varied collection of young bucks. They've all rather taken to us, probably fascination with the way the other half lives, you know, these urchins spend their lives traipsing around with mullets and course fabric calling each other "dude" or some other preposterous term of salutation, and in strides a chap with a finely-tailored Jermyn Street suit and a pocket handkerchief. Hardly surprising they'd take an interest. Anyhow, they all seemed to take a liking to us, particularly our accents, and decided to call us both "London". Not an unflattering sobriquet, but quite how one can distil the diverse metropolis of London into one pithy little nickname is beyond me." "So we're all sitting on rather comfy sofas for a group session, in which we'd all sit around and bemoan the horrors of substance abuse for an hour before returning to daydreams about Golden Brown or pearly-white smoke, and this particular session they decide that each of us should take a stuffed toy to be our mascot for the day. I had my eye on a rather handsome-looking koala bear, but this rather burly chap named LaRoi purloins it before I have the opportunity. I didn't raise a fuss - he was considerably larger than myself, and given his apparent other-side-of-the-tracks upbringing, I naturally assumed he knew all sorts of kung-fu-kicky and would promptly dispense with me, despite my own stature and ability to roughhouse, before I knew what hit me. So it reaches his turn, and as he begins to regale us with a rather jolly little story about how he had briefly toyed with the idea of a career as a rent-boy for sailors to subsidise his wicked crack habit, he picks up his mascot koala bear and gives it a squeeze. Sure enough, it turns out to be one of the musical toys which sings a song when you squeeze its belly, and as he diverts us with tales of the horrors of addiction, this koala begins to sing "B-I-N-G-O, and Bingo was his name-oh," and he's trying desperately to stuff it down the side of the sofa whilst retaining his composure. It was a jolly good wheeze..." Once again Constance dissolved into laughter, and Thom good great pleasure in watching her amusement, not because of the satisfaction of seeing another anecdote produce the desired effect (although he couldn't deny, it was a factor), but simply to observe her beauty. As she laughed she threw her head back, and Thom watched as her delicate chest rose and fell with every peal of her mirth. He noticed too that as she laughed she had been fingering a beautiful diamond necklace. "I must say, Constance, that is a truly charming necklace." Constance smiled, squinting her eyes inquisitively at Thom. "I wasn't aware you were a connoisseur of antique jewellery, Thom." "Oh, but yes, my dear, I am extremely keen on the subject! And if I may say so myself, I believe my knowledge to be second-to-none... Or at least, second-to-very-few..." "Oh Thom, in which case you simply must come to my boudoir and have a quick look at some pearls that belonged to my great-grandmother. I shouldn't mind your expert opinion!" With that, Constance placed her dainty white hand firmly upon Thom's, and guided him through the assembled guests towards the interior of the yacht. Pausing briefly to finish the remainder of his brandy and place the glass on the empties table, Thom nearly felt his arm unhinge itself from his shoulder in a desperate bid for freedom, so forcefully was Constance DeVilliers pulling his hand. Barely managing to catch his footing, he followed her into the bowels of the yacht to examine her pearls. Once in her private chamber, Constance extracted a small safebox from a bag. With a key Thom had not seen appear, she opened it, and took out a glimmering pearl necklace, cradling it gently in her palms and handing it to Thom, anxious to see what his response might be. Thom surveyed it carefully, caressing it in a gentle fashion with his fingertips. "Oh yes, Constance, these are fabulous. Your great-grandmother's, did you say?" "Yes... A friend of mine said they could fetch several thousand pounts at auction." Constance ended the sentence with a slight inflection, as if to imply a question, to see if her friend's judgement matched that of Thom. He shook his head in disagreement. "Less?" asked Constance, somewhat anxiously. "Much more, by my judgement. Constance, might I take this to an acquaintance of mine, Rupert Thackwray the Marquis of Kidderminster? He is a noted amateur jewellery assessor, and is considered the foremost brain on these matters. He may be able to offer a more exact and reliable valuation..." "But of course, Thom." And with that, the conversation continued, taking in all of their favourite pieces of jewellery, the most noted jewellers, and to whom belonged the most noteworthy collections. Much later, they would return to the deck of the yacht and rejoin the party. Following the festivites, Thom would later bid Constance adieu with a kiss on the cheek, before returning to shore for a fine seafood breakfast repast at one of the small eateries on the Promenade des Mariniers. ---- The following day. The East India Club, St. James' Square, London. Lunchtime. ---- Thom LeStrange reclined back into his chair, savouring the taste of a fine single malt whisky and the smell of pipe tobacco that permeated a snooker room of the East India Club, a select private school Gentleman's club in St. James' Square. As he watched a fellow club member attempt a tricky long pot, he extracted Constance DeVillier's pearls from the pocket of his overcoat. As he examined them again, a smug grin etched its way across his devilishly handsome features, before looking up towards the other man in the roon. "Thackwray old chap, come and take a look at these..." The man who had previously been playing snooker returned his cue to the rack, collected his half-empty martini glass from the drinks table and strode over to his companion. He extracted a pair of pince-nez glasses from the pocket of his sports jacket, and peered at the pearls Thom held in his hands. "Nice... Very nice, LeStrange... Did you pull the old scam again? Compliment her necklace and pretend to be a connoisseur and all?" He saw Thom nod wryly, and added, "You cunning little lounge-lizard!" Thom and Rupert chuckled a quite devious and roguish laugh as LeStrange returned the jewels to the pocket of his jacket. -End.
