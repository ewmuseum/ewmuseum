I don't even like penguins

12 May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/12/09 It was another normal day in the world of two men. Two men that knew what it meant to be successful in the wrestling world, yet lived relatively normal lives outside of the ring. Relatively normal until today, that is. Darren Slade, the self proclaimed King of Kaos, stared at the busy intersection, catching a glimpse of a very attractive young woman walking her dog. “Well, I guess this walk wasn’t so useless after all, Grandpa.” Tom, better known as “Legend Inc. Tom Lexian, allowed a grin to slide across his face, “Yeah, go ahead and impress her with your degree. You’re a doctor, right?” He pauses and laughs hysterically. “Oh wait, a doctor of…EXTREME!” “Oh god,” Darren replied, “Would you give it a rest already? I’m tired of your shit about my na…” Tom holds up his hand, stopping Darren from replying and shows him Bobby the cactus which he had been holding the entire time. “Please, no arguing in front of Bobby. Besides, you’re not exactly Casanova yourself.” “True, but at least women feel safe with me,” Darren shot back, as he nodded to the young woman. Tom snorted, taking another lick of his ice cream cone that he had picked up a few blocks before. “Women are like cones of ice cream, Darren. They make you feel…I don’t know, younger and more alive. They give you that boost of energy. You know what I mean?” Darren huffed. “I’m already young, grandpa. Besides, I’m not old enough to need to get tied down like you.” A smile crossed Tom’s face. Slade reminded him of a younger version of himself, but with a bit of a meanstreak. An edge. This would be useful, but he changed the subject quickly. “Don’t you think we need a manager?” Darren looked at Tom for a second, then away, “A manager? Are you not taking your meds or something? You ARE my manager.” Tom appeared perplexed as he thought about the question. “On paper I am your manager, but in reality we’re partners. And we’ve already got Bobby as our team mascot…” A loud sigh erupts from Darren’s mouth as he stops Tom mid-sentence. “Dude, are you always playing this character of yours? We’re not at the office, stop being so…so…” He finally shouts, “LAME!” “Oh, I’ve tried,” Tom remarked. “But it doesn’t work. My nature is to be lame, and it works. Besides, you need a ying to your yang. Can’t be brooding and “oh so serious” all the time.” The last part was said with a “Joker-esque” voice. “We need a team symbol.” Ignoring the Batman reference, Darren snorted, “A symbol? Why would we need that? We’ve got New Wave stamping their logo on everything I touch anyways.” “Batman had the bat signal. Superman had his trademark suit,” Tom began to explain. “Hell, even the Thundercats had those two little cats running around behind them. We need something like that too. So when people see it, they’ll say, “Hold on! Look at that! Dr Extreme must be here!”” Tom seemed proud of his revelation and eagerly waited for Darren’s response. Darren sighed, and then smacked Tom in the back of the head, “We’re wrestlers... not cartoons, Grandpa.” Tom looked upset, but it soon subsided to acceptance. “Fine, forget the whole idea. I guess we weren’t meant for GREATNESS.” The duo continued walking down the block, coming upon a local zoo at the end of the block. Tom stopped for a moment, examining a sign posted on the wall. “Penguins for sale?” Tom observed with some interest. Darren looked at the sign, and then at Tom with a worried facial expression, “Please tell me that you’re joking...” “Tom looks at the sign again. “Let’s check it out.” Darren sighed, knowing he wouldn’t win this. “No, I’m putting my foot down. No way. No fucking way.” Tom pulled out his cell phone and started dialing a number while Darren watched on. A worried face finally emerged. “Who are you calling? Is that Stevens’ number? Jesus Christ don’t call that bastard...fine, fine... ONLY to check it out. Are you happy now?” Tom does a quiet fist pump and hurriedly makes his way into the zoo, followed lethargically by Darren who is now carrying Bobby. People walk by staring at the muscular man and stunningly looking cacti, and Darren amazingly didn’t trip as he walked with his hand to the ground. A few monkeys run across the floor past him, causing him to step carefully as he watches a boa constrictor slither its way into another hole around the corner. Somewhat annoyed, Darren stopped for a second, but he thought to himself that there was no turning back now. A horny looking koala rubbed against his leg, and he quickly -and reluctantly- continued to follow Tom, who was far ahead of him, “Grandpa, wait up!” But Tom wasn’t listening. He was already at the front desk speaking with a man that bared a remarkable resemblance to… “CROIKEY!” The zoo keeper exclaimed in a heavy Australian accent. “You just stumbled upon the greatest deal in all of San Francisco! These penguins are AMAZING animals and they come at the low, low, LOW price of only ten dollars a penguin!” Darren heard the zoo keeper, and tried to yell across the room to the desk, “TOM! YOU BETTER NOT!!” Tom shoved ten dollars in the zoo keepers face and extended his hands to accept his grand, new prize. “One penguin please!” Darren saw the exchange of money, “NO! DAMMIT TOM!” “Relax Darren,” Tom replied, giving his oh-so famous smile that had captured the hearts and minds of millions…err, well, maybe 1 or 2 fans. “I’ve got everything in control.” Suddenly, a black, furry little penguin jumped into Tom”s arms, pecking away at Tom’s chest. “Everything under control huh?” Darren muttered. “GAH!” Tom exclaimed. He turned to the zoo keeper for help. “Excuse me, Mr. Zoo Keeper, but why does he keep trying to…uhm, peck me?” The zoo keeper just smiled. “Aww, he just likes ya, mate! That there is the friendliest penguin this side of the Bay Area!” The penguin leapt out of Tom’s arms now, tired of pecking, and avoided the outstretched grasp of Darren, who tried to capture him. The penguin ran off as fast as his little penguin feet could take him, scurrying across the floor and …. WHAM! Tom looked at Darren. “Did he just run into that fish tank?” Darren nodded, “He did. He’s either the clumsiest non-flight bird in the world or…he’s a dumbass like you are.” He sighed. “In both cases I”m screwed.” The zoo keeper rushed over and picked up the penguin off of the ground, brushing the dust off of his feathers. “CROIKEY! He’s alright, mate. He just has problems seeing objects sometimes.” “Is there anything we could do to help his vision?” Tom asked looking concerned for his non-flying bird friend. Darren looked at the zoo keeper, then at Tom, “You sure the damn thing even has vision?” “He might need prescription glasses,” The zoo keeper replied with a bit of a shrug. Tom looked exasperated. “Glasses for penguins? Where do we take him? Lens Crafters for Birds?” Darren looked puzzled and perplexed. He started to realize that he was living in a sitcom. “Tom... where are you going to keep him? And what’re you going to feed him?” “Well…” Tom began to say, unsure of the answer himself. “…he can stay in the hotel with us. And we’ll feed him fish. Yeah, that’s it. I mean, penguins like fish, right? They live near the ocean.” “... I thought he had to live in a cold environment?” Darren questioned. “We’ll stick him in the freezer when we aren’t playing with him,” Tom answered simply. Darren slapped his forehead. He measured the distance between himself and Tom and quickly determined that he couldn’t pull off a neck breaker from where he was on Tom. Plan A was quickly dashed. Tom reached out and grabbed his penguin back, thanking the zoo keeper for his help. He turned back to Darren with a broad smile. “So what do you think we should name the little bugger?” Before he could answer, the penguin leapt out of Tom’s arms again, running out of the store against Tom’s shouts to return. But as he made his way out of the store… BOOM! … ERR! The penguin ran smack dab into a parking meter, knocking itself out cold. A woman getting into her car next to the parking meter saw the entire ordeal and proceeded to faint, falling down next to the unconscious penguin. * * * * KA BOOM! “Lord Jesus in the heavens above!” Darren exclaimed, watching the little penguin crash over a small vase. The glass particles littered the floor as the furry little bird waddled into the bathroom where more collisions were inevitable. He grabbed a broom and tried to shove most of the exposed glass into a corner. “I swear, I’m going to kill Tom for getting that little piece of...” CRASH! “My mirror!” Darren shouted in horror, as the shards of the former full length mirror appeared from the doorway. “That’s it, no more Mr. Nice Guy.” He took the broom in his hands and stalked into the bathroom, finding the little penguin sitting cutely on the toilet. It looked up at Mike and simply made the sound, “HONK HONK!” “Don’t try to stop me,” Darren said to the penguin. “I know you may be some innocent little bird, but the fact of the matter is...” He paused while he said it, “...YOU”RE LIKE THE DEVIL IN A PENGUINS BODY! DYYYYYYARRRRGH!” He lifted the broom over his head to slam it on to the poor bird, whose eyes have widened in absolute fear. He is paralyzed in shock and cannot move... “...NO!” Tom Lexian grabbed the broom from behind and takes it away from Darren, who spun around in fury. “Tom, what are you doing?” He growled angrily. “Stopping you from making a big mistake,” Lexian replied, placing the broom behind his back. “Now, calm down.” As his back was turned, the little penguin scurried underneath Darren’s legs and past Lexian. Darren desperately tried to stomp the cute bird into a pancake. STAMP! STAMP! STOMP! STOMP! “DYYYYYYYYYYARGH!” Darren cried out in frustration. He sat down at the toilet seat and just stared at the wall. Lexian just shook his head, dropping the broom to the ground. “Darren, it’s over.” “You’re right,” He answered back. “My career...it’s over. I mean, look at us. We’re fighting over a penguin. I’m a fighter, man. I once took a guy from the top rope and suplexed him on his head onto a table.” He stared at the shattered glass on the floor, seeing only a fragment of his former self. “Now I’m half expecting an Acme anvil to land on my hand.” “Look, I know the little guy can be a handful...” “It’s not only that,” Darren interrupted. “I mean, what am I doing with myself? I blew my chance against Fisher. Now we’re in a tag match with some guy named Lucas Quinton. I haven’t talked to the guy at all yet. All I know is that he likes puzzles. Shit, maybe I’ll get him a Rubix cube or something.” Tom shrugged. He knew that Darren, who was hoping for a big opening splash, would be subject criticism. A loss to Fisher, while nothing to be ashamed of, brought them further down the rung of success then they had anticipated. “I don’t know what to tell you, Darren. But I do know that we can go out there and do what we do best.” Darren looked up and smirked. “Grandpa, you somehow turned an ass kicker into a penguin babysitter. Now you expect me to go out there and destroy teams on a whim?” Lexian smiled. “You’ll never lose your flavor and touch for the dramatics. Besides, dropping December Revolution on their heads will make you feel better.” Lexian reached out his hand and shook Darren’s, who let himself laugh. CRASH! BOOM! BOOM! Lexian looked into the living room and returned with a concerned face. “Say, did you really like that picture of Billy Joel on your wall?” Darren slapped himself. “I say we call him Boomer,” Lexian said nodding his head. “It fits perfectly.” Yeah, just perfectly.
