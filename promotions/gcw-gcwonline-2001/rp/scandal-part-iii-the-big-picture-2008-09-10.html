SCANDAL PART III: THE BIG PICTURE

10 September 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/10/2008 PREVIOUSLY? It began with a business proposition - They wanted McKail to purposefully lose a match at a minor independent booking and in return he would earn a very healthy payday. It was conceived by a misconception - An aging ex-pro wrestler named Howson, who claimed to have once been friends with McKail’s late father, assumed he could be easily bought just as his father had been all those years ago. Like father, like son. It escalated with an act of defiance - McKail refused to be bought and was not in the least intimidated by Howson, his threats and his alleged “connected business associates”. McKail made sure to win the match in style. It was enforced by violence - For his insolence, Howson had his hired muscle beat McKail to a bloodied pulp. It began anew by strength - McKail would not break and Howson was forced to pursue other avenues of forcible persuasion. It continues? AFWA WRESTLEVANIA NIGHT TWO BACKSTAGE MEDICAL BAY "What the hell happened to you?" The medic asked him. "You seemed fine after the match yesterday..." Sitting on the medical bench, McKail simply glared at the him in silence as he wrapped the bandages carefully around his waist and chest. "Your mother rolled over too fast in bed last night," the medic’s assistant quickly responded. He was much younger than the medic and quite obviously felt uncomfortable with the silence. He didn’t know any better but to fill the silence with humour - or something like it. "Like a damned rock slider." McKail ignored him, whereas the medic allowed a chuckle. "Very funny, Steve. Why don’t you go get a soda while I finish up here?" The assistant glared at him for a while, seemingly a little perplexed by the strange request, but after a time he simply nodded and wandered off into the corridor. McKail rolled his eyes and cursed under his breath. He’d seen enough doctors in his time to know when a sermon was headed his way, usually preceded by faked interest in his well being and useless solutions that helped nobody but fulfilled the man’s job requirement and conscience. The medic raised both eyebrows. "Not much of a talker, huh?" McKail reached inside his jeans and extracted his cigarettes and lighter. "Cut the chit chat and just patch me up, doc." "Okay," he replied, "but you can’t smoke in here." Looking him directly in the eye all the time, McKail placed the cigarette his mouth and purposefully lit it. The medic shook his head, a little pissed off that authority had just shat on so openly. McKail took a drag of the cigarette, but at least made sure to blow the smoke away from the medic’s face. He didn’t feel like being a complete asshole. Yet. "Don’t worry about it, doc," McKail advised, "sooner I’m out of here, the sooner the cigarette’s out of here." The medic cracked a grin. "Very clever, but you do realise if I don’t give you the green light, you don’t get to go out there and work tonight." McKail shook his head. "Don’t be a pussy." The medic shook his head. "Seriously Jacob, what the hell happened to you?" "I fell," McKail told him. He took another drag of his cigarette and looked away. "Can you hurry it up a little? I wanna hit catering before my match." "You fell?" The medic replied. "You seriously expect me to believe that?" "I don’t much care what you believe." The medic eyed him warily as he taped up the last strand of the bandage. "You know, you’re not medically fit to wrestle tonight." McKail shrugged. "What you saying, doc?" The medic shot him a toothy grin. "I could stop you from going out if I wanted." "But you’re not?" The medic shook his head and handed McKail back his t-shirt. "No." McKail grabbed it immediately and slipped it on, whilst simultaneously standing from the bench. "And why is that?" The medic shrugged. "Because you can obviously handle it." McKail half nodded still a little confused, but headed for the exit anyway. "Oh and," the medic began, forcing McKail to turn and face him, "Mr. Howson says “have a good one?". McKail threw his cigarette aside and lunged forwards and grabbed the medic by the collar, eyes wild with anger despite the pain from the exertion making it difficult for him to even breath. "What the hell is that supposed to mean? How do you know, Howson?" The medic grinned, disturbingly pleased to see he managed to get under the skin of the ignorant bastard McKail was gloriously proud of being. "Not so silent now, huh?" McKail shook his head. "Don’t fuck with me, doc. I’m not in the mood." "No," he replied, "I imagine you’re not, but then you never are, are you?" He allowed the rhetoric question to hang in the air for awhile before conceding. "I was sent to make sure you passed your physical. My assistants not the sharpest knife in the draw, but even he could see you’re in no fit state for much of anything tonight." "Why?" McKail questioned. "Why does Howson want me in the ring tonight? Does he expect me to throw the match? Because I ain’t doing it!" The medic shook his head. "I don’t know and I don’t care. I didn’t ask and he didn’t tell me." McKail grunted angrily, but relinquished his grip and without another word left, wondering just what in the hell was going on. AFWA WRESTLEVANIA NIGHT TWO MAIN EVENT JACOB MCKAIL VS LANCE MICHAELS McKail inhaled deeply, trying to regulate his breathing to calm himself. The business with Howson was still playing on his mind and the confrontation with the crooked medic had only made things worse. Now Howson had him guessing what he was going to do next and McKail didn’t like it one bit. But stood in one corner of the ring before a couple of thousand fans who were chanting his name (mostly because he was recognisable face from the television, more than actually liking him), he realised he needed to forget it. He needed no baggage coming into this one but the thing of it was, every time he breathed in and out the pain from his ribs would shoot up and down his body forcing him to remember the whole sordid affair all over again. He winced in agony and grit his teeth. It’ll all be over soon? The ref finished making a few final checks to his opponent and then called for the bell. McKail eyed his opponent warily as he emerged from his corner, staring at his pretty-boy face and long blonde hair and wanted to destroy it entirely. He immediately chastise himself and shook such thoughts away. Calm down, McKail. You don’t want to give this pretty boy any advantages? As Michaels hovered around the centre of the ring, McKail pushed himself free of the turnbuckle and joined him, trying to ignore his opponent’s cocky grin. McKail wasn’t much for smack talk, he preferred to let his actions speak volumes, but he had no problems with his opponents letting spill their bullshit. It helped him focus most times. "Jacob McKail," Michaels said, in between chewing his gum, "this isn’t GCW, man. I won’t let you off easy like those folks will if you’re too damned drunk to fight." Rumours of McKail’s addiction were widespread. Was even something of a scandal a while back, but he’d learned to manage it better since then. Still, it was nothing every smart mark who knew the wrestling business didn’t know and it was hardly likely to get a rise out of McKail. He’d heard it all before. "Not talkin?, eh? Don’t blame ya one bit. I’d be intimidated too, if I had to fight me." Michael’s laughed a little and McKail could see the white gum placed at the back of his mouth just resting there. McKail hoped he would just choke on it and put them both out of their misery. "You think it’s just a matter of bad luck you haven’t been given any decent, straight cut title shots in the GCW yet?" Michael’s continued, still wearing that same damned cocky grin. "Be fair, Jake. They can’t just let any old drunk carry one of their titles. They’d never live it down." McKail rolled his eyes. He was better wrestler than Michael’s would ever be and despite his relative success in AFWA, McKail could tell just by looking at the fool that he would never get close to the big leagues. Hell, he didn’t figure GCW wouldn’t give the idiot a tryout, never mind a spot on their roster. "If Mary could see you now, what do you think she’d say to you?" The question hung in the air for a time and McKail found himself questioning whether he’d even heard the fuckwit correctly it was so leftfield. "What did you say?" "Mary," Michaels began, "think she’d be proud of you, man?" He grinned that same damned toothy grin, already knowing he’d get a reaction. "I don’t." What the fuck...” McKail lunged forward and grabbed Michael’s by the throat, before he could even react. "How the fuck do you know about, Mary?" McKail squeezed harder and realised he didn’t really want any answers even if Michael’s were to provide them. He just wanted to hurt somebody and Michael’s had apparently volunteered himself. "What the fuck is this?" But Michaels didn’t answer him, he only grinned. If this clown had been sent to push all of McKail’s buttons, it was working. Fuckin? Howson’It had to be. "Tell me how you know about Mary or I’ll break you in two," McKail told him, making sure his point was both clear and concise. He decreased the pressure on his throat slightly, allowing him sufficient oxygen and flexibility to respond. And respond he did. "Fuck you!" He yelled, his grin widening. And with it, he kicked McKail in the ribs, effectively breaking the vice clamped around his throat. Pain shot through McKail’s entire body, almost enough to force him to blackout. Almost. His anger simply wouldn’t allow him to momentarily slip out of consciousness. Not now. Not after this. Michaels went to follow up his advantage with a powerful shot to the jaw, but McKail blocked it and twisted Michaels arm around, stopping just short of popping his elbow out of it’s socket. Instincts were taking over and his fury was backing them up. McKail took a second to savour the agony plastered across his victims face, before announcing, "Fucking with me comes with a price, kid!" With that McKail pounced, jamming his hand - fingers first - directly into Michaels throat and then hurling the startled carcass down into the turnbuckle. With Michaels sat up in the corner, clutching his throat in agony, McKail set about him, dropping boot after boot upon his chest, face and groin, never relenting and never once stopping for breath. After a few minutes and eerie silence washed over the crowd, now their concerns were placed firmly with Michael’s who was no longer even able to attempt to block the boots raining down upon him - unconsciousness had already taken him, but it didn’t to matter to McKail. He didn’t care anymore. Not about answers to his question, not about the once faithful audience applauding him and certainly not about Michaels well being. It wasn’t long before the ref attempted to interceded, wedging himself in between McKail and the turnbuckle, attempting to shove the frenzied bigger man away without much success. Ultimately, such interference annoyed McKail to the point where he briefly ceased his merciless attack just to push the ref away from him a single arm, forcing the official to tumble backwards and fall on his ass. Now angered himself, as the ref rose back to his feet again, he signalled strenuously for the bell for all the good it did him or Michaels. McKail ignored it. He’d been disqualified and he didn’t even care. The audience had flat out turned against him by now and already begun to yell “McKail sucks!” amongst other things, but McKail cared even less about that. Ultimately, it took the better part of the backstage staff and few local law enforcement officers to prise him away from his victim and they might not have succeed, if not for his injured ribs. AFWA WRESTLEVANIA NIGHT TWO BACKSTAGE JACOB MCKAIL’S LOCKER ROOM McKail placed on a bench in his locker room, his hands strapped firmly behind his back whilst they decided what to do with him. That manic look of furious dementia had yet to fade from his eyes and as a result nobody wanted to get near him. He’d half heard somebody tell him that charges were being brought against him and that he could face some serious jail time, but that was background noise. His anger had refused to subside, instead it changed focus. Michaels was just a puppet they’d put in the firing line. He was their mouthpiece and by all intents and purposes, they were more than happy to place him in harms way. Probably paid him well to take it too, the question was would it even be enough to cover his hospital bills? Judging by the state of Michaels? face when the medics carted him away from the ring, McKail doubted it. Deep down, he realised he should feel at least a little compassion for his demolished victim. But he didn’t. He didn’t much care about anyone or anything these days, much less some asshole looking to push his buttons. The man pulling the strings behind all of this could only be Howson, that much was clear even his state of mindless rage. If this was what Howson had meant by “getting to him via other means” then the old bastard had succeeded and McKail had walked right into it. Howson had McKail right where he wanted him and if he whispered the right lies into the right ears, then his entire life could be ruined. "Well, well," the familiar voice began, "what do we have here?" McKail didn’t need to look up to know it was Howson standing there. "Seems like yer’ve got yerself into a little trouble, boy," the elderly man continued. He allowed the comment a little time to hang in the air, in the meantime sitting on the same bench as McKail, making sure he’s a safe distance away, despite the metal restraints. "Don’t wanna talk, eh? Can’t say I blame ya. But don’t look so glum, boy. We’ll see ta it that Michael’s drops tha charges against ya." McKail kept his head down and his mouth shut, but he knew his heavy breathing gave the game away; Howson was getting to him and they both knew it. But he couldn’t do anything about it. McKail felt lost, useless, humiliated and entirely played. Yet, looking back over the course of events, he knew if everything were played out again, he wouldn’t change a damned thing. His late wife, Mary, had always said he was stubborn. Takes one to know one, he’d tell her right back. Her memory calmed him some and as long as he had that, Howson and his alleged “business associates” would never truly defeat him. At least, that’s what he told himself. "What? Still nothin? ta say? No thanks?" He chuckled to himself. "Even though you ain’t sayin? it, I bet you’re wonderin? why we’re helpin? ya out, eh? I mean, yer cost us all that money last night with that fiasco an all." But McKail already knew the answer. Everything clicked right on into place and the big picture was finally revealed to him. He cursed under his breath, making sure the old bastard sitting next to him neither saw nor heard it. "My associates and I made damned sure our clients knew precisely how tha match was gonna end," Howson explained. "We advised them ta back tha disqualification. Yer made us a whole heap of money of this one boy and don’t say we don’t pay well fer it." With that, he retrieved a bundled of cash from the inside of his jacket pocket and threw at McKail. "Yer work fer us now, remember that." He turned to leave. "We’ll be in touch." Outside, McKail heard him stop by one of police officers ordered to stand guard at his door. "All charges are bein? dropped. Give me five minutes, then let him go." McKail wanted to scream, to rip the shackles around his wrists apart even it meant tearing the flesh away from the bone to do so; he wanted to run after Howson and do to him exactly what he’d done to Lance Michaels not fifteen minutes before. But he did none of those things. He kept it all inside. They’d won this one, the last thing he wanted to give them now was even more satisfaction at his expense. Bottom line was, he didn’t sell his soul to the devil and become the man Mary would would’ve hated. At least he had that and for now, it was enough. To Be Continued...
