Coming Of Age

16 June 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 16, 2009 There was a when she was a little girl that these hallways seemed so massive and intimidating. The hustle and bustles of the nation's largest airport is a nightmare for some, but had become somewhat of a second home for Vivica J. Valentine. It seemed like every person at luggage claim, every ticket salesperson, every security guard had her known on a first name basis at this point. The same people who were once arguing with her over bag specifications when she first started traveling for Zenith Corporation in her late teens were now asking her how her career was going and if they could get autographs for their children at home, who of course were always her "biggest fans." Truth be told almost everyone watching GCW television had become her biggest fan. There was something about the short, athletic blonde girl from Highland Park. She had that special something in the business they called "it". No one knew exactly what "it" was or how to get "it", but most people knew it when they saw it. They could see it by the lines of people who usually follow her towards her flight for the next episode of WorldWide. It also appeared in the form of a crowd, surrounding whatever restaurant inside she decided to eat at. As intruding as it had become into her personal life, she had learned reluctantly to embrace her masses of fanatics. That is what made this particular trip so odd... yet somewhat comforting for the woman known around the world as The Fearless Phenom. Due to a slight scheduling conflict (thanks to her travel agent) she'd found herself boarding a 3am flight to New York City. The familiar faces she'd gotten to know were still there, smiling through the obvious sleep deprivation to make her feel at ease... but they were surprised when she'd approach them alone. No fanfare, no large crowds, no screaming kids asking for autographs... just Vivica and her bags. It reminded her herself of when she was a little girl and would occasionally accompany her dad on a long distance trip just to get some time with her. These days the corridors didn't seem so frightening anymore. After making her way through baggage claim, The Fearless Phenom approached a chair and flopped down on the rather uncomfortable cloth-covered plastic chair. Leaning back she tried to crack her back and closed her eyes, dropping her carry-on down to the tile floor next to her. With a deep sigh she let out months worth stress and anxiety. It'd been a rough few weeks, after all. The hectic lifestyle, constant traveling, and relentless attention hardly gave her a moment to herself. A smile crossed her face as she stretched out her arms and legs with a yawn, her body practically screaming out in joy for a moment of rest. Many people didn't realize the pain and hardship she went though, not even some of her closest friends. Her adamant straight-edge life style had swayed her away from dependance on the vices that helped many other wrestlers make it through the day. Whether it was painkillers or alcohol, all Valentine had was her own determination, her unyielding desire to entertain, and her adamant passion for her business. As she'd said many times it was what she always wanted as a little girl, ignoring all the snickers and giggles that'd followed her to the top. Her eyes started to grow heavy, causing her to lean back in her seat a little bit in a more comfortable position. Closing her eyes, a smirk crossed her face as she enjoyed the silence. It was almost like she was at her own private little spa in the mid... "Excuse me." The voice jolted her out of her tranquil state and she looked over, a bit startled by the tiny voice that'd nipped out her nap before it even began. Standing in front of her was a little girl. Her blonde hair up in pigtails with purple ribbons, wearing a matching purple dress with white frills. She couldn't have been any older than six. "It must be a fan." That was her first instinct, smiling back at the little girl while pulling a sharpie out of her right side cargo pocket. One that she'd always kept handy for just such occasions. It was almost as needed as her cellphone these days. She didn't even hesitate looking back at the girl, still a bit groggy from her almost nap. "Hey hun, what's your name?" Valentine looked back at the little girl. "My name is Sierra." She said, looking almost half asleep herself, "Can I sit next to you ma'am? That security guard over there said that you wouldn't mind." Vivica's eyes narrowed on a large black man, wearing a black security shirt who waved when she'd made eye contact. His name was Devon. She'd met him on more than one occasion. One time when she'd first started in GCW, he'd even held a heckler back as she made her way onto her flight with the rest of Dies Irae. The hecklers rarely show themselves anymore. Although she was never quite sure if it was because they were scared, or there were just so few of them. "I don't mind." Vivica said, keeping on her friendly smile as she patted the chair. "Sit down, I don't bite that hard. I promise." "Thank you." She couldn't help but notice what a gracious little girl she was. Her own mother would've loved to have Sierra growing up instead of Vivica. As Sierra sat down and glared eagerly at the window, waiting for the plane to arrive something had become shockingly apparent to Miss Valentine. This little girl didn't have the slightest clue who she was. She looked so lonely though, it was no wonder Devon had sent her over. "Where are you parents at?" Vivica asked, no longer able to hold in the questions that'd been flying around her head from the little girl. "My mom is at home sleeping." Sierra answered, "She has to work at 8. She couldn't make it with me." "I'm sorry to hear that, what about your dad?" "He lives in New York." She answered, "He works for a big business and he's really famous. So he doesn't really have time for me. But he told me when I turned six that I could fly out and visit him." "So you're excited huh?" "Yes... kinda." Sierra's gracious smile suddenly turned to self-doubt. She didn't really know how she felt. "Kind of?" Vivica asked, "What's wrong?" "I have a brother and a sister in New York." Sierra said, "Their names are Jason and Samantha. They're 3 and 7. I've never met them before." "Why wouldn't they like you?" Vivica asked. Sierra answered only the form of a shrug. She still wouldn't make eye contact, staring right through the window. Still waiting for the plane to land. "You know..." Vivica couldn't help but reach out to the little girl, it'd become an instinct for her... even though she grew up without any brothers or sisters, and barely any parents for that matter, "...it's okay to be scared, sometimes." "My mom kept telling me to stop worrying about it." "Well, you want me to tell you a secret?" Valentine smiled looking at the little girl, who slowly moved her head to look back at her for the first time. "I'm scared sometimes too." "You?" Sierra questioned, "Big girls don't get scared." "You'd be surprised." Vivica shook her head as she spoke, "Especially when I was a little girl." "Really?" Sierra asked, now fully into the conversation, "What did you used to be scared of?" ---- I was the runt of the litter I was the weakling I was the last to discover I was the ugly duckling We're all the same on the inside Though we're always up against anybody's outside And it's hard to live up to strangers beautiful color Some people always finish last Still I ended up a badass I was never on the team I could never seem to click But in the end I guess I just wasn't into it We're all the same on the inside Though we're always up against anybody's outside And it's hard to live up to strangers beautiful color Some people always finish last Still I ended up a badass The best I advice I ever got was Don't be somebody you're not And it's hard to measure up But I think I'm enough -- "Badass" by Kacy Crowley ---- Well one time, when I was about your age there were some other kids at school who didn't like me. Tock The clock counts down to the world falling down, or at least that's what it seemed like to the blonde little girl. Staring up at the clock, she watched in uncomfortable silence. Her cerulean blue eyes moving along with the second hand inching closer and closer to 5pm. With her arms crossed over a hot pink glittery t-shirt and a pair of miniature stone-washed jeans, the lump in her throat grew wider and wider as time passed. Her parents sat on each side of her in the same silence, knowing full well the disciplinary action she'd recieve once they got home. It was only a matter of time. The tranquil and empty classroom was a far cry from the usual chaotic mess that is a 2nd grade class. For everyone one child paying attention, another two are drawing, talking, or trying to pass notes and play a game. Although she didn't quite get a long with her classmates, the little girl who'd grow up to be GCW World Heavyweight Champion would gladly go back to being picked on by her peers in exchange for not having to go through the tongue lashing she had in store. 5:00 PM Right on time the door swung open. Enter Miss Donald, stage right. "Hello, Mister and Misses Valentine, I'm glad you could manage to make it here today for this parent-teacher conference. I hear you two are very busy." The teacher said from under her black glasses. She had reddish hair that was up in a bun, and wore a darkblue dress with a light blue floral print on it. Jack and his wife Marie got up out of their seats next to their daughter to greet her elementary school teacher. "Oh, it's no trouble at all really." Jack stated casually. He was a smooth talker, a lawyer by trade, Vivica's father by genetics alone. He was always gone away on business, never having time for his only child. Jack learned of his child's problems and accolades mostly through three minute long-distance phone calls from a nagging mother. "I'm glad you could have us." Marie followed. A thriving socialite in the Chicago area, Marie had a very different outlook on Vivica. She was an over-possessive mother, striving to make her only daughter a spitting image of herself. Feeling as if Vivica's behavior and attitude was a direct reflection of her parenting skills, the daughter Valentine had to be prim and proper like the prototypical lady. The only profession exceptable in the Valentine household for Vivica was only achieved through a masters degree and numerous internships. To say thus far in her life Vivica J. Valentine was a disappointment to both of her parents would be a huge understatement. "Take a seat." The middle-aged teacher said. Leaving Jack and Marie to take seats next to their daughter, who thus far has decided to keep her mouth shut for what would be one of the very few times she's decided to do so in her lifetime. "I'm sure you'd like to know why I called you here today." "I've had some contact with some of the other students parents, so I have some idea." Marie stated before looking down at Vivica, whose eyes were fixated squarely on the floor. Arms still crossed in a shamed manner. She wanted no part of the conversation. "Yes, your daughter Vivica here has had an issue playing nice with the other kids in class. So far this week she's had three visits to the principles's office and we've broken up two fights with her involved in it with two different classmates. One boy and one girl." Miss Donald explains. "They started it." Little Vivica chirped in for the first time. "It doesn't matter who started it Vivica." Her father interrupted, "It's not nice to hit other children. You don't like being hit do you?" "No." Vivica said looked back down at her shoes. "I'm under he impression that your daughter has anger issues." Her teacher spoke, "I'd suggest counciling, unless you two have other methods you'd like to take in handling the situation. See Vivica is the smallest child in my class, and sometimes ends up being walked all over or picked on by her classmates. Instead of turning the other cheek so to speak, she had a tendancy to lash out." "I don't know where she gets it from." Marie is quick to wash her hands of any blame for Vivica's actions. "Well, judging by the fights we've broken up... I'm no doctor or psychologist, but I'd think it has something to do with professional wrestling." "We don't allow that trash in our home." Jack said, getting up, "It was a pleasure meeting you today. You seem to be a great teacher." "Thank you." Miss Donald said, "The pleasure is all mine." Vivica stands up with her parents reluctantly, ashmed and embarassed to be there. Why did she have to go creepy classes. It made her feel like such an outcast. Like she was so different from everyone else. All she ever wanted to be was average. Just to fit in and be like everyone else without being picked on all the time. Vivica paused thinking back to the moment, "You can't go through your life pretending to be somebody that you're not. With a big grin the stewardess walked over towards Sierra and Vivica. "My name is Stephanie, I'm going to escort you to New York City. Are you excited Sierra?" "I am now!" The girl exclaimed looking back at Vivica. The stewardess looked at The Fearless Phenom as well, having met a couple of times. Grabbing ahold of Stephanie's hand, Sierra jumped down off of her seat while Stephanie looked back and mouthed two words. "Thank You." Vivica simply waved back at them with a smile of her own as they made their way onto the airplane first. Picking her carry-on bag up off the floor Vivica realized she'd even learned a little bit about herself that day. Even if her parents didn't approve of what she'd become, she was very happy living her life. She wouldn't have it any other way. Spending the rest of her life never being able to speak to her parents again was a small price she had to pay for having what, in her mind, is the greatest job in the entire world. Soon she'd even have the chance to prove again why she's the best she is at what she does... by winning the GCW World Tag Team Championships. Even if her parents did come back again when she won the title and disappeared again when she lost it... it didn't matter anymore. Because in the end... she was happy with who she was. That was the most important thing of all.
