Call me a sinner and I'll call you a liar

9 October 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 9, 2008 It only took ten minutes for the fire to consume the apartment complex. Slowly, silently, it crept its way up the brick crumbling floorboards and ceilings during its ascent to its ultimate destination. The families mostly escaped, minus a family dog and a girl's doll collection. As the pumping water cannons forced the flames into submission, news trucks and anchormen spilled onto the streets asking everyone and anyone they could get their hands on about what had happened. Some said kitchen fire, most said they didn't know. It wasn't the answer they wanted to hear. As the cameras turned on to send the final feed to the studio, it became a consensus that Jay Terror had been burned alive and killed during a horrific fire that struck his apartment complex. Naturally, watching the news and hearing his name, Jay leaned closer on his couch and opened his ears wide enough to take in the "facts". It was ten a.m. apparently when the flame started. A former fan of Jay's managed to light and toss a flaming bottle of gasoline at the complex creating a raging inferno that killed him. Even the world title they added. There was no word from family or friends, only dead silence. That's when the phone started to ring. He flipped it open and looked at the id. Caroline. For a second, his heart stopped. Caroline? Of all people, Caroline is calling him? She must have seen. Must have. He accepted the call and drew the phone to his ear. A faint 'Hey' escaped his lips first. 'Jay?' She questioned. 'Caroline?' 'Jay, I just saw the news. I thought -' 'I know. Strange world, huh? Coulda' even fooled me.' 'So you're ok? Like alive, no burns, nothing?' 'You could say that, yeah.' He paused looking for the right words. He could feel a conversation shift but he couldn't think of the best turning point. He managed. 'You're ok?' 'Why wouldn't I?' 'I don't know. Just asking. You know? It's cordial.' She was lying through the microphone and making it far too obvious to sense. Something about the way she said it, maybe the informality of it, gave her away. 'Where are you?' 'My hotel. Nowhere near Philly.' 'Then where?' 'Aberdeen. Still. I haven't managed to get myself going just yet. Been thinking about it though. Where are you?' A pause, she was processing. 'Philly. Been home for a little while.' 'Not following Jordan?' He knew it was a shot but it was one line that needed to be crossed. The words brought a lengthy silence, one of awkward pause and empty air. 'I'm sorry.' 'I was never following him you know. That wasn't my intention.' 'You don't have to have intentions Caroline.' He could feel his footing getting worse and worse as he trekked into the conversation. Still, he managed to walk up the mountain with very little injury dealt. 'I just figured that, well - you know.' 'That we fucked?' 'It was on my mind. Yeah. I mean, do you blame me?' 'Yes. You really think I would just go and do that? Jay. You know me.' That's where he cursed under his breath. 'I didn't know. It's Jordan.' 'That means nothing. He's nothing but good Jay. Remember? You guys used to talk. You used to fight for the same cause.' He leaned back on the couch, an eye roll. 'But his cause - no. Forget it. Not now.' Another pause followed, this one lasted longer than the previous few. Caroline's voice broke it as it burst through in its overall peaceful nature. 'When are you coming home?' 'I was thinking today. Seeing as my place is burned, I figured I needed to make an appearance. You know, to show them I guess.' 'Can we meet?' 'Where?' 'Doesn't matter. Call when you're home.' 'Yeah, sure.' He heard the line disconnect on her end and he snapped the phone shut. It landed on the couch cushion beside him and he splayed his arms over the head of the leather. A deep breath filled his body and he released it steadily. Across the way was his world title, beside that a bottle of Jack Daniels. The mix signified his life. It was him in a snapshot. His first and third love stood with a gap between showing a patch of empty space. Space for a woman. Space for a woman he had lost. Scratching his head, he turned back to the news. Useless babble about Barrack Obama. He turned it off. Pulling a cigarette loose from a box in his pocket, he lit it and took in a heavy inhale. Ecstasy. And then another phone call. The id read Steven Caldera. 'Steve, hey. What's going on?' 'Good, just checking.' 'You see the news too?' 'I didn't think you made it out of Aberdeen. Good thing too, huh? Don't think of it too hard.' 'Not too hard, no. I was planning on leaving soon though. Maybe an hour or so. The plane ok to take?' 'Are you heading back into Philadelphia?' 'I wanted to stop in, yeah. I needed to see the damage, assess the situation. You know?' 'It makes sense but not now. We can't have you back around there now. You have Dread this week, remember? We can't have any distractions in your way before this big match. I mean, Dangerous Games is right there. Jordan is already booked. You have to make it there.' Another eye roll from the champion, 'I know. Don't worry. It won't hurt me at all. I'm focused. I'm ready.' 'You don't know Dread though. I've worked with him; I've seen what he's capable of. It's not going to be the easiest win you've ever picked up.' 'Then why him?' 'The fans needed to see something good. Don't get me wrong. It's doable, nothing you aren't capable of. Just saying, thinking of your home and all of that is something we should avoid until Dangerous Games is over.' 'It'll be fine Steve. I can make my own decisions.' 'That's what LeStatt and White both said too. Look at them now.' Jay stood from the couch, made his way into the kitchen. The Jack Daniels was calling his name. After a swig, he chased it with another cigarette. 'I see them. But look at me Steve. See where I am after this gauntlet?' 'Facing someone you shouldn't have to?' 'Proving I'm the best there ever was. So I have to beat White, it's more the reason to send him packing his stuff and sitting it on the shelf. All I need is one more win.' 'You better be right. About the plane, I'll book you a ticket. Check your messages. I have stuff to do. Good luck in Philadelphia.' 'Thanks.' He ended the call this time with a simple click. Another shot of whiskey poured down his throat before he finished his second cigarette. The couch felt the same as it had before when he laid down again. His eyes shut and his mind turned on hibernate. Dreams filled. __________ There was something to be said for tenacity. You know, White and those types. They gave effort, they overcame. It's not everyday that a person can overcome pressure and take their skills to the next level. It takes a certain kind of drive; it takes a certain kind of man to do it. White, well he was that kind of man. Angry, passionate, looking for redemption. He had all the key factors. He was a man to be feared. Fear though, was not a word that rested in Jay Terror's vocabulary. Instead, he wholeheartedly welcomed the challenge of Jordan White and Andy Murray, much like he would do so with Jason Dread. Why? Because Jay was a fighter. A revolutionary. He knew things that others didn't and he performed at a level above the rest. He was a man of men and a true force to fear. Dread? What's it matter? Terror is the only thing Dread has in his future. __________ It was raining in Philadelphia when he touched down; thick heavy drops reminded him that he was no where near the cool air of Scotland. As he loaded his belongings into his limousine, he instructed the driver and pulled a cigarette out. The 27 spat smoke into the tiny confines and Jay portioned through the lingering bits and drags he took through the filter. He dialed a number in his phone and leaned casually in the seat. Caroline answered. 'I made it.' 'Fancy.' 'You ok?' 'Just fine. You know. Just fine.' What was fine anyways? Okay but not so much? 'Something happen?' She ignored, 'Where are you?' Jay slightly laughed, 'In the car. You mind a diner?' Her response came quickly, almost rapid fire. 'No. Not at all. Which one?' 'I was thinking Mom's place maybe? Or perhaps Gerald’s? Unless, you know -' 'No, it's fine. Mom's is good. Been a while since I've been there anyways. Meeting when exactly?' 'Ten minutes fresh with you?' 'Fresh for sure.' He hung up and shoved the phone away. Another drag from his cigarette brought about another gust of smoke from within his mouth. The driver shut the center window divide and Jay watched him cough in the front. The limo did a slight swerve but nothing overly noticeable. Terror tossed his legs onto the seat cushion beside him and laid horizontally across the entire way. His head rested against the cold damp window. A moment passed where his mind jogged along the track of life making stops every few seconds to look in on a certain memory. There was childhood. There is always childhood. This particular moment was him and his dad. They wrestled before dinner. Jay was smiling and so was his dad. Now, though, he was not smiling. His memory pushed forward, this time skipping beats to his GCW debut. Daniel Franco was in his mind. Jayce Bradley outdid him knocking him out of Rampage contentions. He bounced back though, the Television title followed. Caroline came to mind now. Blonde hair, blue eyes, typical woman. But not so typical perhaps? Mild drug use and alcohol abuse came to mind; times that he himself forgot existed. Then came a kiss. The moment froze. Here, it stuck with him drawing the corners of his lips upward to form a smile. The smile stuck, it held strong for a few minutes. Then the mind fast forwarded again. The smile dissolved and replaced itself with a picture of Jordan White. Apathy struck his face, painted him to be a man of no sound reasoning. It showed him to be a warrior, a man to conquer. Jay nodded and felt the car coming to a halt. It pulled around Mom's diner and the driver found Jay's door to open it. Terror stepped out, winked at him, and walked inside. It was cool; they obviously hadn't realized the rain outside. Terror undid his jacket and found a booth by a window. He waited. 'Good day sir.' A voice came from behind, it was the waiter. 'What can I get for you today?' 'No, not yet. I'm waiting on someone.' 'Of course. Alright then.' He sealed his lips and stared for a second. It was a long second. 'Have I seen you somewhere before?' 'Could have. I've been seen by a couple people around here.' He tried to avoid the inevitable but it wasn't going to happen. The waiter continued. 'Wait a second. You're the one they said was dead. That wrestler guy. Go figure!' 'Heh, yeah. Strange world.' 'The world hate you enough to want to try and burn you alive?' 'Pardon?' 'I mean the story. About the fan.' The rumors would never end. Perhaps an upset fan truly did try and destroy Jay's life by lighting his home on fire. Maybe he really was a prick that people didn't care for in the least sense. Jay didn't know nor did he care what others thought of him. It didn't change the way he worked. The person he was. 'I've heard crazier talk on the news for sure. Who knows.' 'I mean, why do people hate you?' 'You mind giving me some space?' 'I'm sorry.' He knew the rule about customers. The waiter had been warned on many occasions to avoid confrontations with the people he served. Still, he found that there was always at least one thing unsettling about others conversations. It could be politics and he would have the urge to petition Obama. It could be food and he knew a better recipe. In terms of Jay, he found the gossip almost too much to let sit. Jay was unphased. He took out a cigarette and tapped his feet waiting for Caroline. She burst through the door at the end of his first cigarette and looked around the diner for Jay. Spotting him, she walked his way. Apprehension was obvious. She seemed stiff, unsure how to act or what to do. Jay just nodded and put the butt of his 27 into the ashtray. 'Hey.' 'Hey.' Jay sat for a second unsure where to go until, 'You look good.' 'Thanks, you're not bad. Unshaven I see.' 'I figured it might be nice to see where it takes me. Maybe a beard, who knows. You hungry?' 'Hungry enough, yeah.' Before Jay had to motion for him, the waiter was back with his pen and flip book ready. 'What can I get for you too? Drinks first? Food yet?' 'Water's fine,' she said. 'Yeah, water works. We'll check the menu.' 'Fair enough. It'll be over in a minute.' Caroline checked out the menu, Jay check her out. She seemed disheveled, unsure of herself. The lack of confidence made Jay question. 'You working again?' 'No, not now. How come?' 'You seem tense. Stressed maybe. I don't know. What's wrong.' 'Just awkward Jay. You know. It's been weird seeing you. Talking to you. I thought you were dead and here you are. Alive as can be, still Jay Terror.' 'Would you rather me dead then?' She sighed, pushed the menu to the center of the table. 'No Jay. No. I'm just saying, things were rocky. They were shady. There was that girl, there was Jordan. People came between us and sides were taken. You know?' 'I gotcha.' He nodded, the waters came back. He sipped his. 'Give us another minute,' he said looking up at the waiter. A nod was the perfect answer. 'Caroline, I'm sorry for what went down. You know how it is though. I mean, Jordan. Just Jordan. If it were anyone but him then it'd be fine. But Jordan. Of all people, Jordan.' 'It was platonic. Platonic as all hell. And calling us fine would be a lie. You're still Jay Terror. You're not Talley. You're not the guy that cared about my feelings. Jordan was just an easy cop out for the inevitable.' 'Inevitable? Caroline? Really?' He was frustrated but a breath cooled him down. 'I don't understand.' 'It's you Jay. It's always been you. You either love someone or you love something. In this case, that title got your kisses.' 'I can't have both?' 'Not in this world Jay.' 'This world allows anything Caroline. It's open to suggestions and I'm making one. Why not? I can love two things and still be on course. I can still distribute it easily.' She shook her head, found a cigarette from her purse. 'No Jay, no you can't. I've witnessed it firsthand.' Her cigarette ignited his urge. He fetched one and lit it. Their smoke mingled. 'Then what? Why are we meeting?' 'You asked.' 'No Caroline, you did. On the phone. Why?' She paused, he was right. It took a second to gain momentum but after the build, her words rolled freely. 'I wanted to see who you were these days. I figured maybe the gold hadn't gotten to your head after you finally got it. Maybe it was just a phase you needed to complete. Now though, now I know I was wrong. You're Jay Terror. The Outlaw. The Established One. You're not the guy from the wi-fi cafe or the guy who managed to avoid sleeping with me for a month's time because we "Weren't ready".' It was a mouthful and he digested it in pieces to absorb each part. 'I don't know Caroline.' 'And that's all I needed to see. I'm going to go.' 'And breakfast?' 'Not hungry.' She stood and looked into Jay's eyes. They were deep and hazel and she managed to stare endlessly. 'Don't go.' 'Good luck out there in the real world. Maybe someday things will work out for you. You can have everything that you ever wanted to in life. You know? Love and gold. Yeah. Thanks for the water.' She turned away waiting for Jay to grab her by the shoulder. He remained sitting though looking as helpless as ever. What could be done to change her mind? To make her realize? She had decided and that was that. She continued to walk out until she disappeared through the doors of the diner. Jay watched her get in the car and drive off down the street. The waiter came back. 'Ready?' 'I'll get the check.' 'Check?' He turned, looked at him. 'You heard me.' __________ He would have a conversation with Steven Caldera again. This time it was face to face in his office. Confidence was low in his eyes and with a spark of momentum from the overlord, Jay was back on track. His blood was pumping. His mind was settled. Caroline was a blur in his focused state. The only thing on his mind was destroying Jason Dread and moving forward to the next phase of his career. Ending and crushing the dreams of Jordan White. Why? It was his fault. All of it.
