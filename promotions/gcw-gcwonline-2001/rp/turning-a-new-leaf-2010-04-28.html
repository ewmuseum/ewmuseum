Turning a New Leaf

28 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/28/10 Didn't matter what her name was. At the end of the night, she wasn't Tessa Windsor and she wouldn't take anything he truly cared about. Even when her nystagmus was peaking, he didn't stop to ask questions. There were better things on his mind -- people he'd let down, himself even, and her eyes were the last thing he wanted to worry about. At the end of it all, he pulled the comforter around his body, dropped off the bed, and grabbed his box of cigarettes. It was ritual to close the event with tobacco and it was one ritual that he actually didn't mind. Pulling the Honolulu glass door open, he took a glance back at the dazed local. She wore a closed-eyed smile and he could hear her purring as her brain continued to feed on the spiked serotonin. He shook his head and closed the door. His Bic lighter had a worn wheel and spun quickly, lighting the tip of his cigarette with an easy breath. He took the first drag and stared over the ocean. Some lights were lit, the necessary ones perhaps, while the rest of the skyline left room for interpretation. Unfortunately, Jay Terror didn't have the mind or the spirit for creativity. He was a broken soul sipping on his first cigarette, and he'd continue to be a broken soul through the entirety of it. It was all he knew. Lighting a second, he could feel the wind pick up. It was chilling but nothing like the look on Tessa's face. It was a damp memory, the kind that found its way into dreams and nightmares as readily as ocean water. Honestly though, it wasn't the look that bothered him most. On top of it all, he knew that it wasn't he who he was trying to please. There was another. A promise had been made to be the best. A ceremony had proclaimed that he would stop at nothing to attain it. With his words slowly fading, Jay realized that preservation only came in attainment. If he were going to walk in the shoes Steven Caldera left behind, he'd have to do so with a smile on his face. Guidance or not, it was time to take charge of his destiny and put the right foot forward. --- In Maggie McVay's time with Jay, she hadn't reaped a reward as sweet as Hawaii. Finally, feeling the rays of light against her pale skin, she grinned as she skimmed the pages of Cosmopolitan magazine. Her body was nestled into the contours of a lounging chair and there was a virgin daiquiri on the table beside her. She wanted to thank Jay personally for the invitation but that would have to wait for his sobriety. Oddly though, it wasn't Jay that occupied her mind. Instead, she focused on the secrets to a good sex life and how to find that right partner -- an article by Mary Widener. As she licked her finger to turn the page though, she was stopped mid-sentence by a charming voice. "Could I get you a drink?" She pulled the magazine down and squinted behind her sunglasses. The voice belonged to a man, a tall man, who wore only a pair of swim trunks. "I have one actually," she said, nodding toward the daiquiri. "Let me try again," he grinned. "Do you need some company?" "I'm reading currently." She didn't know why she played hard to get, but there wasn't much she could do to stop it. "And what are you reading?" "About healthy diets," she lied. "It's really interesting." "I bet it is. If it means anything, you look healthy enough. I mean, you don't look bad." "Thank you." "Where are you from?" "Everywhere really. I've been traveling for a while now seeing a little bit of everything." "That sounds nice. Where will you go next?" "I'm not sure. Maybe England." "I heard England is nice around this time of year." "So did I." She pulled the magazine back into focus and finally finished the sentence. The line ended on the phrase 'perfect orgasm every time'. "I don't usually do this. It's just; I had to see your face. Your body was too much of a tease." "Well, thank you. I appreciate that." "I wanted to see if you were up for lunch maybe. You can bring your drink." "I don't know if I can. I'm busy today." "Busy reading about health?" "I'm here on business. I have to meet with some people in an hour." "An hour is plenty of time to eat lunch. I promise." "I already ate though." "Trust me; this place will make you hungry again. They serve the best fish tacos in Honolulu." Maggie laid the magazine across her lap and looked at her watch. Honestly, she had two hours before she had to leave, but an hour was enough time to find out the man's true intentions. She glanced back into his slate grey eyes and smiled, "OK, let's go." --- Trying to recollect how she got from the bar to the mattress, Michelle, the local, bit her nails. She'd only been awake for five minutes, but already she didn't like the signs. Her clothes were scattered, a collage of tasteless threads that, when put together, would hardly fill a dress. It wasn't until her eyes found the ripped wrapper on the night stand though that she started to tear up. Deep down, she was ashamed of what she'd done. It wasn't what she had come to Hawaii to do, nor was it what she'd told her mother she was doing the previous evening. Distrust rolled within the wet beads as well as a growing fear. They were safe and she was happy about it. Who they were was the foggy part of the puzzle. His clues had been cleaned minus the duffle in the corner. She slowly crawled out from the comforter, depression gliding like vultures above her head, and made her way across the white hotel carpet. It was a red bandana that caught her attention and it was that red bandana that she peeled back when unzipping the bag. His wallet was gone, most likely in his back pocket, as well as any other defining marks. The jeans she found were old, tattered and dirty, which mostly matched the shirts. Her tears came in greater waves in the seconds she shoved through the belongings. They were focused, a cry for help even, and when she started to zip the bag closed again, she felt that they'd never end. A flyer told her otherwise. The sheet of paper was tucked just behind the duffle and she pulled it loose. It was a Shockwave advertisement and her eyes immediately were drawn to the red cloth in the corner of it. Her heart sank, her tears came again, greater than before, and she dropped the flyer. She knew who he was at first glance -- there was no denying those yellow teeth. Michelle shook her head, cursed the Ecstasy, and used a heavy sigh. Although it may not be true, she decided then and there that consent had gone out the door. It was the scapegoat she needed to clean her conscience, and it was one that she was willing to use. --- James Bryan was combing his hair when Jay Terror walked in. It was in the bathroom and Bryan looked away from the mirror to see who entered. He and Jay shared a glance. "You dressed up?" "Some, yeah. Turning a new leaf." "Yeah, what's that? There a turn in the works?" "Like they'd believe it anyways." "You are pretty well branded, true. So what. You got a suit though, that's good. This just for Shockwave?" "Honest, I'm turning a new leaf. It's time to get serious about things." Bryan nodded and took the comb back to his hair. He smiled to check his teeth. "That's good to hear. I was afraid you were starting to slip some." "I think I was. I mean, I just kinda' lost my way I guess. The people. The expectations. All of it kinda' built on top of each other." "Understandable. If you're back then welcome back. It'll be nice to see the real Jay Terror back in the ring." "I hope so," said Terror, who approached the sink casually. He checked his own hair before sliding onto the porcelain counter. "How do you think I should bring it up?" "Cut to the point. You've always been a head first kind of guy and I think the people like that about you. Maybe you've said it before, but I think they didn't believe it. I heard that if you say something enough times that people start to trust it." "Makes sense. Will the others be there? Tessa, Fox, Storm?" "Nah, just Fox. Tessa and Chris had family matters to attend to. Could be a strong driving piece for the two of you. Either way, should be good media attention. The recent draw from Johnny has brought a whole new set of reporters to the table. Doing this show in Hawaii helped too." "Johnny's doing good things." "Sure is. I mean, Steven was a genius, but Johnny knows how to give the people exactly what they want." "Just wish we got along better. Wish we saw eye to eye." "The title shots?" Jay nodded and followed with a sigh. "It's just been so long waiting. I came back with the mindset that I'd be wearing it within a month. Him showing up was supposed to be the icing on the cake." "Win this match. Hell, beat Tessa Windsor. If she punches your card one more time then there's no way you're going for that belt yet." "Funny." "Just saying. The records may not speak clear all the time, but this time it's starting to matter. She has you beat on all occasions." "I know it." "You need the comb?" Jay looked at the flimsy black hair prop for a moment before turning it down. "Nah, I like to go natural with these things." "Of course." The door opened again and a bell hop entered. He looked at the two of them, smiled briefly, and disappeared into a stall. James Bryan turned on the faucet and washed his hands. "How're you gonna' start?" "Figured I'd sit back and wait for the questions like usual. That's how we usually run it." "Right, but didn't you turn over a new leaf?" "I didn't really prepare a speech for it. I figured the confidence would be enough." Bryan wiped his hands on a paper towel and Jay slid off. JB led the way outside of the men's room and Jay followed. "Usually it works. Getting these people to realize it though takes more than that. They want you to speak up, be vocal about your opinions more, and don't stop until you have to. It's all about commanding the microphone." "Just figured I did good enough on it." "Not bad, no. Like I said, start them off. Give them something to feed off of. I know they brought questions but make them toss them aside." "I like your style." "I do this for a living. You're not the first guy looking for some advice. Besides, a good word with Steven never hurts." "Like you need it." "You never know." Jay grinned as they stopped outside of the conference room. Terror clawed at his pack of cigarettes and pulled the box into sight. He opened the lid and offered one to the GCW announcer. "Yeah?" "No, I need to get this room ready. Do me a favor and go outside though. They really don't like you smoking inside." "Nowhere ever does." "Please?" "Right," Jay replied, shoving the pack away. He tucked one stick into his ear and found his way toward the back double doors. James Bryan disappeared into the conference room. --- Maggie McVay wiped her mouth with the cloth napkin and dropped it onto her plate in defeat. The fish tacos were in fact the best she'd ever had, yet she was happy to be finished with them. The man, who went by Lyle, had managed to change her virgin beverages into alcoholic ones. She was three deep and he was asking for a fourth upon the waiter's passing. "I really must be going. The business." "But I just ordered you a drink. You can't go yet." "I know. It's important though." "What do you do anyways? I guess I never asked where you were going." Maggie flicked her eyebrows realizing the same. They'd talked about health, thanks to her original lie, and talked about the beaches of Hawaii. They talked about how he came from Chicago and was visiting a friend for a month away from his law firm. In fact, she thought that it was then that she mentioned working with Jay. Whether it was a clever ploy or a genuine question though, she couldn't deny the umbrella drink that was settling on their table. "Thank you." "Of course." "I'm kinda' an agent I guess you could say. I mean, I do the same things like take phone calls and help book flights." "Anyone in particular?" "You ever heard of Jay Terror?" "Never actually." "Really," she asked before taking a swig from the drink. She ignored the straw to finish faster. "He's a professional wrestler for GCW." "That's why. I stopped watching GCW back when Rich Rollins left. He was something." "He was. Jay actually retired him." "You don't say. That's a shame." "Well it was good for his career. Sure made him a household name." "He must be good then. How long have you been his agent?" "A few months. He took me in when I needed a helping hand. He's good like that." "And good in other ways?" "Sorry," she coughed. Lyle grinned realizing he had overstepped his boundaries. "I didn't mean that. I just figured it was common for wrestlers and agents to be intimate on the road. Seemed like protocol to me." "Must have missed that memo," she said drinking her daiquiri faster. The strawberry was a perfect blend and easy to handle in larger doses. "Well I'm sorry. Just a misunderstanding." "Of course." "You doing anything after you see him? Maybe we could watch the sunset or something." "Something romantic you mean?" "Well if you consider the sunset romantic then sure. You seem like a classy girl who likes charming things. I figured that it was a decent cliché at least." "I don't like clichés so much." "An outside the box thinker?" "You could say that." She looked down at her watch realizing it was time for her to go. She finished the end of the drink in a hefty gulp and slid the glass to the center of the table. "I really must be going. Jay will wonder where I am." She stood and grabbed her handbag. "Thank you again for the drinks and lunch though. It was really great." "Sunset tonight?" "Maybe." "Take my number. Call me if you have the time." Maggie grinned and pulled out her phone. She took his number and saved it. "Again, thank you." "Of course, Maggie." She placed her phone back into the bag and turned back toward the hotel. Her footsteps were mild, the kind that tried not to seem rushed. There were stone slabs and she walked across them until she finally reached the rear of the hotel. As if waiting for her, Jay Terror stood against the wall with the final three drags of a cigarette in hand. "Where have you been?" "Been around. Had lunch and a few drinks." "Sounds better than my morning." "She leave you early?" "Was in a coma. I swear, some of these girls do too much before they go out." "Was she like that last night." "She was on E. She was wilder than I've seen a girl get in some time. It was one of those where you finish and she's so overcome that --" Maggie cut him off, "Jay." "Right, sorry." She grabbed the cigarette for its last decent pull and stomped it out on the stone under her sandal. "You ready to go inside?" "Was just waiting for you." "How sweet." Terror opened the door and Maggie followed him into the air conditioned hall. The conference room was two doors down and they entered together. Upon arrival, Maggie merged with the reporters while Jay turned onto the stage where a chair beside James Bryan was waiting. He took a seat. "Ready," Bryan asked. "Let's do this." JB stood and made his way to the microphone. He didn't carry a speech in his hand, nor did he need one. He was a gifted speaker, the kind that understood how to grab people's attention. "Thanks for coming tonight, everyone. We've got a lot of you stuffed in this place but bare with us. As you can see, we've got Clyde Fox, Jay Terror, Donny Diamond, Bishop Steele, and Slash all with us tonight. They'll all be open for questions at some point. I wanted to kick this thing off in backwards order though. I figured we'd start with a member of the gold star match -- the established one himself, Jay Terror. Thank you again." Bryan relinquished his spot behind the podium and turned to Jay. He joined in with the clapping as Terror stood from his chair wearing a newly pressed suit. Fitting the microphone to his height, he placed his arms on the face of the podium. "Again, thanks for coming." Hands went up as soon as his lips opened but Terror called them down. "I wanted to start tonight off with a few words actually. We'll get to the questions in a few." The hands slowly lowered and Maggie smiled. "It's come to my attention that a lot of people have worried about me and my future with GCW. Lately, it seems that I've fallen through on my promises and I've been unable to secure a solid victory. The last week on WorldWide could be the reason for a lot of doubt." Jay cleared his throat. "Nonetheless, I'm here at Shockwave facing much of the same challenge that I've dealt with in the past. I've seen all of these superstars in the ring with mixed results. You may have heard me say this before, but at Shockwave, I plan on showing you all what I'm truly capable of. For a long time now, I've been growing in Steven Caldera's shadow. I've been pruned and placed properly to become the largest star GCW has come to know. In this growing process, I realized that I relied on my fertilizer more heavily than I should have. Instead of doing things for myself, I've done them for others." He stood taller on the podium to make his point. "Not anymore. It's time that people realize that I'm more than my accolades say of me. It's time for me to grab that title belt and wear it for the world to see. You may have lost faith in what I can do, but for those that know what I'm capable of, don't forget it. People have told me all week that Shockwave is a turning point. It's another chance to shine and take the reins of the company. I'm not letting this pass me by again. After this match, I will have a win over Tessa Windsor, I will prove that Chris Storm shouldn't be here, and I will make Clyde Fox feel inferior. It's in my nature to be ruthless and recently, I've found out just how to show it off." Jay and Maggie locked eyes for a moment. Her smile was wide giving him the reaction he needed. He slouched down again. "Now, any questions?" As the hands came up, Jay grinned at the prospect. Unfortunately, reporters weren't the only ones looking for answers. Two police officers entered the conference room with circling eyes. They focused on Jay and began to climb the stage. In their entrance, voices rose, yet Terror remained silent as they approached him. "Jay Terror?" "That's me," he said, away from the microphone. James Bryan was on his feet. "Could you come with us, please?" "On what charges?" "There was a reported rape on one Michelle Genny last evening. We would like to ask you some questions." "Michelle Genny?" "We received a call from your hotel room this morning from her. Could you please step outside?" James Bryan stepped in with confused eyes. He nudged Jay. "Go ahead, clear the air. I'll take care of the room." Terror nodded and followed the officers out. When he said he meant to turn over a new leaf, he never expected it to come in the legal manner. Before passing into the hall, his and Maggie's eyes met again. Her interest was peaked but her trust was secure. She knew one thing: as slimy as Jay Terror could be, he was honest when it mattered.
