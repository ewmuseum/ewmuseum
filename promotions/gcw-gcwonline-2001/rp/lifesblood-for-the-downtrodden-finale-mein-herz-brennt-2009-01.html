Date: January Mein Herz Brennt Lifesblood for the Downtrodden, The Finale “Consider what you think justice requires, and decide accordingly. But never give your reasons; for your judgment will probably be right, but your reasons will certainly be wrong.” - Lord Mansfield. Prologue: All I Had I Gave He was twitchy, Andy; hands clamped to the wheel of a stationary vehicle with a white-knuckled iron grip, he was hunched forward in an awkward position that his posture would soon be cursing him for. Sitting at the end of the road, his car was nestled under a canopy of leafless trees at the side of an eastern Tallahassee road with sporadic back-and-forth traffic passing-by intermittently. Focusing on nothing at all, Andy had his gaze fixed straight ahead; cold, blue eyes squinting under a furrowed brow. Dressed in a black lumberjack’s shirt over a long sleeved white tee, the Scot loosened one of his hands and began to tap the tips of his fingernails on the dashboard slowly, allowing his large frame to slide back in a plush leather seat that he couldn’t find comfortable in this frame of mind. A deep, unsatisfied sigh gathered steam in his cheeks and bellowed from between his lips as he ran a hand against his sandpaper-rough unshaven chin. He was trashed and scattered, completely, and his head was as stable as an elephant on a tightrope. For what seemed like the hundredth time in his thirty-one years on God’s green earth, women had brought him to the point where he was struggling to function as the all-conquering wrestling demigod that Steven Caldera’s Global Championship Wrestling had turned him into. Andy was sick, he was tired. Unable to focus, unable to give Jay Terror and NC-17 so much as a second thought and sporting a demeanour that would make LeStatt Knight seem like Mother Teresa, Andy was so far from GCW’s impossibly charismatic crowd pleaser that he was beginning to forget what being The Scottish King of Cool felt like. It was driving him up the wall, and that’s why he was taking the steps towards the inevitable remedy. For far too long the women in his life had had an unintentional taint on his professional career and Andy knew that he could go on like this no longer. Kayla and Jane. The girlfriend and the ex-wife. Andy knew that the two of them could no longer function in his life. For every time he looked in Kayla’s eyes he saw his former spouse’s devilish glint staring back at him, and every time he crossed the threshold of Jane’s house he could think of nought but the Midwestern farmer’s daughter sitting alone in his Floridian mansion wondering what had happened to the men she fell in love with. No, this could go no further. For as long as he lived in the same city with the same two women, Andy Murray would never find the solace and peace of mind that he so yearned for. He was done sitting on this. Finished. With the biggest show in GCW history looming, for the moment he’d cast wrestling to the side; forget about it. Foolish, perhaps, but some things are bigger than professional wrestling. Andy Murray needed to piece together his fragmented personal life before anything else; Jay Terror would have to wait for another day. And so, on what would surely prove to be one of the defining days of his recent history, Andy turned the ignition and pulled the Mercedes onto the road, setting off to act out a plan that he could not afford to fail. I: Euphoria Minus One Placing her dainty hand on the faucet, Kayla Jarosz turned on the hot water and hastily rinsed the glass with little care for making sure the thing was actually getting cleaned. Gazing out across the almost endless lawn of Andy Murray’s back garden, she barely noticed the temperature of the water rising until it burned the back of her hand. “Ahhhh, shit!” she yelped, recoiling immediately as the surprisingly hot H2O made the executive decision to make its presence felt. The slippery glass flew from her hand but landed conveniently in the drying rack as she shook her hand wildly. She turned off the hot water and twisting the blue faucet, but while she waited for the water to turn cold she realised that it didn’t hurt enough to cause her more than a couple of seconds worth of discomfort. She sighed; disgruntled and malcontent with all around her. Life in the Murray household was becoming harder with each passing day and the appeal of being a professional sportsman’s other half was waning. Kayla couldn’t wrap her head around it. For sixteen months she’d lived like a queen; she thought she’d found the man she’d share the rest of her life with. Andy Murray was the last of the good guys, she thought. A man who put his family first and refused to let adversity and disappointment get in the way of those close to him. Things were perfect; like a fairytale, almost. For sixteen months at least, life couldn’t have been any better. Things were bound to go wrong at some point, the pessimist in her said, especially after such a long period of bliss. But the change in Andy was remarkable. He was barely recognisable from the man she’d met in a Texan bar almost two years ago. He was twisting, contorting to a form that was almost subhuman in his coldness, and living with it was becoming a nightmare. The distance between them was such that Kayla couldn’t remember the last time she and Andy had actually held a proper conversation, let alone gone out of their way to spend some time together. She leaned back against the kitchen worktop, gazing out across the lavish but tastefully decorated great room with an emotionless look on her face. The place was a palace, there was no doubt about it, but she was sharing it with a man who was slowly slipping away from her. It wasn’t worth her time trying to figure out what was going on inside her man’s head; she’d come to realise that by now. The possibilities were, frankly, endless, and with Andy seemingly unwilling to utter more than a couple of words to her it didn’t look like she was going to find out any time soon either. It couldn’t be his career, surely? So Andy might have taken a bit of a knock in confidence during his battles with LeStatt Knight but those days were behind him, and Jay Terror, for all his attributes, was never going to match the intense psychological warfare of the GCW hall of famer. Besides, Andy and GCW were getting along just fine; she couldn’t remember the last time he’d lost a match, and he was about to take part in one of three main events of what could end up being the biggest wrestling show ever broadcast in North America. There was Jane, of course. She knew all about the history, all about the ups and the downs, and she knew that Jane and Andy had done their best to bury the hatchet since the whole Chris Bagwell mess spiralled out of control on national television. The ill-fated lawsuit that followed caused Andy more than his fair share of strife, Kayla knew that, but surely if the two of them were getting along it would have a positive affect on the man? It was beyond her, completely. She’d wracked her brain for too long looking for an answer that was never going to come and she vowed for the hundredth time not to waste another second on it. Whatever Andy’s reasons, though, she needed to do something. Nobody could go on living like this forever, and Kayla was beginning to get sick of it. Leaving a man who’d given her so much was drastic, perhaps, but something had to change. Besides, how could you reason with a man unwilling to communicate? Kayla tossed the idea around her head a couple of times before her trance was shattered. “Hey Kay,” a young, chirpy voice called across from the other side of the room. Kayla raised her head as the young Marvin Murray walked into the room and sat himself down on the black leather couch in front of the television. In a matter of milliseconds a British sports channel flickered into life on the LCD screen as Andy’s son made himself comfortable. “Morning Marvin,” she said, trying her best to sound cheerful. At least there was one Murray in the house she could still speak to, even if he wasn’t yet a teenager. “You want me to rustle up some breakfast?” “Nah, I’m good.” “Alright,” she replied at a decibel so low Marvin probably didn’t hear it. With the palms of her hands Kayla pushed herself away from the worktop, slowly pacing away from the mahogany-panelled kitchenette. She watched Marvin zoned in on sports headlines that, to her, may as well have been in another language. He was big, the kid; maybe not quite as tall as his father was at that age, but he still had a couple of inches on all the kids at his school. With his short black hair and bold blue eyes, Marvin Murray was growing to be the spitting image of his father; very much his father’s son. At a loose end, she sat down on Andy’s favourite leather armchair. Leaning forward, hands pushed together between her knees, she watched a faceless soccer player jabbering gibberish into a reporter’s microphone. “So who’s this guy, huh” What’s he up to?” she asked, trying to share the kid’s interest. “Jones. He’s going to Tottenham, I think,” replied the young Murray without turning his glance from the television. “Oh.” Kayla nodded; she didn’t know who this “Jones” fellow was or what “Tottenham” was supposed to be, but she persisted anyway. “Is he any good?” she enquired, immediately ruing her lame choice of question. “Yeah, he’s alright. The team he plays for at the moment is pretty crappy, though.” Again she nodded, unsure how exactly to relate to a twelve year old kid in full-on “soccer fan” mode. She stayed there, though; watching the studio feed with feigned interest. “Where’s my Dad?” Marvin suddenly asked, the words leaving his mouth and hitting her like a bolt from a crossbow. “I thought he wasn’t leaving for NC-17 until Friday?” Kayla looked across noticing that Marvin was doing the same. She looked him in the eye, unsure of her response. “I-... uh,” she stuttered. “I wish I knew.” “Oh.” He was disappointed; Kayla could see it in the way his facial expression sunk. Perhaps she was not the only one that Andy’s slump in enthusiasm was taking a toll on. Marvin’s gaze wavered; he peered down at the floor and then up at the ceiling. Kayla wanted to talk to him; she wanted to know if Marvin was feeling the same way that she was, but before she could put the sentence together in her head he’d spoken again. “I wish you two would start getting along again...” Kayla swallowed hard. She sighed; the words seemed to carry more weight coming from a child’s perspective. “Me too.” “You-...” he paused, thinking thoughts of a child who’d been through one parental implosion too many. “You’re not going to split up, are you?” Kayla Jarosz couldn’t give him an answer. She didn’t know. II: Down in a Hole Clusters and Hops, 707 North Monroe Street. Not exactly the most upmarket eatery in Florida’s capital city, but a more than ample setting for a spot of midweek lunch. Jane Murray sat away from most of the patrons on a lonely table by the window, carefully examining the menu. Dressed in an informal white top and a knee-length skirt with her shoulder-length brown locks tucked neatly behind her ears, she felt as good as she looked on what was turning out to be another pleasant winter’s day in downtown Tallahassee. The atmosphere was pleasant; pockets of tourists, working professionals and the elderly filled three quarters of the dining room, and waiting stuff floated around the place exuding a Southern charm typical of the city. The faint din of simple, inoffensive roots music seeped from a sound system sitting just underneath the chitter-chatter of the other customers, subtly injecting a little soul into the afternoon ambience. Jane was stuck on whether to order the chicken or the pork. Not exactly typical lunchtime fare, but if this was to be the last time she ever stepped foot in this restaurant she wanted to make it worth the while. She lowered the menu and took a look around the place; she’d miss it, definitely. Jane and Andy had spent so much time in this place during their courtship that they were practically on first-name terms with ever waiter and waitress in the building, and she thought it fitting that she should spend her final afternoon here dining peacefully with a man she had truly never fallen out of love with. She saw him coming the moment he walked through the door. In his checked shirt and loose-fitting jeans, Andy Murray scanned the dining room with great care before eventually spotting his waving ex-wife at the other end of the room. Jane allowed a smile to stretch across her features as Andy trod towards her, managing to crack a half-smile of his own. “Hey stranger,” she grinned as Andy pulled back a seat and sat down. In truth, he didn’t look great; he hadn’t combed his hair and there was at least a couple of days worth of stubble sitting on his jaw. He wasn’t exactly dressed like a millionaire either, but she understood. This wouldn’t be easy for him; what the two of them were about to do. “Thought you weren’t gonna show for a moment there...” “As if.” Andy winked, hanging his limbs loose in the chair as he grabbed a copy of the menu for himself. Jane watched him carefully, savouring their every moment together. The fact that this man was on the verge of taking her to court just three short months ago now seemed almost inconceivable to her. Andy noticed her gazed and returned it, smiling. He’d told himself time and time again that he was an idiot for doing this, a fool. After all, this was a woman recruited to GCW by a man who, at the time, was the greatest rival he’d ever had in the industry to tear his career to pieces. She tossed Andy to the wolves of the mass media following the dissolution of their marriage, she wanted to see him burn. And yet here they were; sitting in a restaurant on a Wednesday afternoon, hours away from a radical step that would change their lives forever. For all Jane’s faults she was a good woman essentially, Andy thought. She’d been manipulated, taken advantage of by Chris Bagwell. She was angry, bitter following a messy divorce and she wanted to strike back at him. He could understand that, and yeah, they’d had their problems, but when Andy sat down to think about it the same realisation cropped up time and time again; despite the strife, despite the conflict, the only time in his adult life that Andy Murray had been truly happy was when he and Jane were together. “You sure you want to do this?” Andy asked. Stupid question. He already knew the answer. “Of course,” she responded. “There’s nothing left for me in this city, nothing at all. Dallas is a good city, once we get Marvin into a school and get a place of our own, we’ll both have forgotten all about Tallahassee.” Andy nodded, swallowing hard. “What about you” I mean, this can’t be easy,” she said, trying her best to sound sincere, “you’ve got Kayla to worry about, too.” “Yeah, well. She’s just going to have to deal with it,” Andy replied, not looking at Jane. His voice cold. Jane reclined in her chair, satisfied. Andy had some doubts, of course he did, but as soon as the two of them escaped she knew everything was going to be all right. She leaned across the table, wrapping her skinny fingers around Andy’s giant paw. “I still can’t believe we’re doing this. You and me, all the shit we’ve been through. I mean, we’ve got enough material here for at least two seasons of Dawson’s Creek.” Andy looked at her. He nodded in agreement. “But despite all that’s happened, despite everything, Andy. I just want you to know that I never stopped loving you. I never gave up on us and you’ve no idea how grateful I am for this chance. This is a new start for us; let’s make amends for all those mistakes, hmmm?” And Jane looked up, gazing into his eyes with those big, round olive orbs he’d fallen for so many times. His smile dimmed with a twinge of regret, but he knew she was genuine. She had to be. This was all she had left. “So, uh. The sale of the house gone through yet?” “Not yet,” she said, loosening her grip on Andy’s hand, “shouldn’t be any longer than a couple of days until the buyer’s funds go through.” “And the plane ticket” You still got it?” “Yeah, it’s in my purse. I still don’t know why we’ve gotta travel separately, though...” “Jane, trust me on this,” Andy said in his best “don’t argue with this” voice. “It’s the best way. Last thing we want is for people to cordon on to what’s going on here, and I know the chances are slim, but that’s not a risk I want to take. You arrive, you head to the hotel this evening and me and the little man will be right behind you, I promise.” Jane exhaled, satisfied. Content. Andy’s word was all that she needed, and as she began once again to go over the menu choices on the first day of the rest of their lives she was already thinking about their future with a childlike optimism and renewed buoyancy. If only it were that simple for the man sitting across the table from her. III: Tact, Or Lack There Of “Ha, eat that you hairy motherfucker!” a bare-chested Cayle Murray goaded as he sprang away from the basketball net in jubilation. “S’like the sixth time in a row I’ve rounded your ass. Sure you ain’t thinking about giving up, old-timer?” Sid Murray picked the orange ball from the concrete beneath his feet and watched as his shaven-headed sibling teased him with a mocking wink. The thirty-three year old shook his head, “Yeah yeah, kiddo. Bet’cha can’t do it one more time,” he said, forcefully throwing the ball at his brother’s chest. Cayle caught the ball with ease. Holding it against his side, he raised one hand above his brow, shielding his gaze from the sun’s infernal glow. Even in winter, when most of the country were tucked up in their fluffy gloves and woollen hats, Florida sweltered. “Alright beardy, let’s go!” The youngest of John Murray’s sons knelt prone, bouncing the ball with one hand as his long-haired, bandana-wearing brother cracked his knuckles. Cayle eased towards Sid, turning his back as his elder sibling tried to nab the ball from him. Jostling for space, Cayle tried to twist around his brother and round him for another shot at the hoop, but a rogue swipe from Sid’s hand sent the ball flying from his clutches. The Murray brothers watched helplessly as it bounced once off the concrete and then skimmed across the surface of Andy’s swimming pool. “Awww, look what you’ve done now man! Thing’s stuck right in the middle,” Cayle said, playfully punching brother on the shoulder. “Shit, C. Warnin” ya though, I’m not good with all this sappy crap...” Walking away, Cayle grabbed the towel he’d slung over one of the garden chairs and dabbed the perspiration away from his torso before pulling a discarded white t-shirt over his frame. He gingerly slid the door open as his brother retreated towards the pool, looking to recover the basketball from its watery prison. Stepping on eggshells, Cayle almost felt like a thief on the prowl as he slid the door behind him and paced across the kitchen tiles. He weighted his footsteps deliberately so that Kayla would hear him before she saw him, but it didn’t seem to be working. The raven-haired girl at the table seemed too transfixed on whatever speck of dust on the table was interesting enough to captivate her full attention to even considering offering Cayle a glance. Feeling awkward, Cayle stood just a few feet from the table. Swallowing, standing straight, he raised his hand to his mouth and cleared his throat. Finally she looked up. “Uh, hey.” She nodded ever so subtly; the movement so small that calling it a “nod” might not be completely accurate, but she didn’t say anything to him. Damn, Cayle told himself, this ain’t gonna be easy. “So, uh. I’m alright.” But her voice was flat, uninterested. Cayle knew that she wasn’t speaking the truth, and he knew that he was going to have to delve deeper; a task that the runt of the Murray litter had never exactly excelled at. He gritted his teeth and pulled up a seat, though. He’d try his best. “Are you sure?” Immediately he wanted to slap himself in the forehead for such a knuckleheaded retort. She’d already slammed the front door behind her before he could finish. Cars and buses pulled up by the sidewalk dropping hordes of travellers off as bodies scurried in and out of the entrance. Pulling up the sunshades she’d hung over her collar and putting them on, she picked up her two suitcases and looked out towards a city she may very well have seen the last of. “Nice knowin” ya, Tallahassee,” she smiled. With a heavy heart she pulled her cases from the ground and walked slowly towards a new life, safe in the knowledge that in just a few short hours she’d be safe in the arms of a man she’d once called “husband”. Forever. V: Weight If only things were so simple for Andy Murray. Not for the first time he found himself sitting alone in his car, nought to keep him company save for the voice on the radio and the guilt hanging round his neck. Sitting in a parking lot outside a vacant strip mall, he rolled up his sleeves and wiped the sweat from his brow before leaning over and turning up the air conditioning. He had so many things to do and so little time to accomplish them. If he was really going to go through with this then things needed to be immaculate, there was no doubt about it. Somebody was going to get hurt in all this mess, that was pretty much unavoidable, but Andy didn’t let that dwell on his mind for too long. This was a big step; a giant step, even, and while some may have called him crazy if they found out, Andy Murray didn’t care. He was sucking the poison from his life once and for all, and come hell or high water, he was going to do it. But he couldn’t afford to dwell on things; time wasn’t a resource he could afford to waste and he had to keep the ball rolling. He pulled his cell from his pocket and quickly punched in a number. “Oh, hello,” he began as a voice on the other end picked up. “Its Mr. Murray here again, I’m just calling to make sure that our transactions still on track” Yes, of course I can hold.” He waited patiently as the person on the other end tried best to answer his query. Across the road he watched a young couple trot passed, ducking and diving through the swarm of pedestrians that were out in force this afternoon. “Oh, yeah?” he retorted as the voice on the other end returned. “Good. Excellent. Two days” That’s perfect. Yeah, that’s right; 330 Pine Forest Drive. Thanks a lot.” Snapping the flip phone shut and sliding it back into his pocket, Andy thought of Jane and how she’d be buckling up in the plane at that very moment. He wished he had the time to recall the good times, the great memories, but there was still the small matter of Kayla Jarosz to worry about. He knew the girl was suffering and it was tearing him to pieces, and if she’d grown to resent him then he knew he had no reason to blame him. After all, he’d hardly given her the time of day recently. She didn’t deserve it, really. She was innocent, just a victim caught up in the seemingly endless cycle of turmoil and disruption that is the personal life of Andy Murray. Andy thought that if there were any way he could save her from this ? any way at all ? he’d do it, no matter the cost. Andy started up the engine but didn’t pull away from the lot immediately. Instead he paused, pondering his next move carefully. For over a year, Kayla Jarosz had tried her best to bring him nothing but joy and support. She’d been there through thick and thin; she’d given him her all. No, Andy couldn’t leave it like this. She deserved better. Knowing well the next stop on his list, Andy pulled out of the parking lot. VI: Holding Something Having never felt more detached from the rest of the world in all her life, the prettiest girl in the park sat alone, using the sleeve of her sweatshirt to wipe salty tears from the corner of her eye. Eyes red and puffed from the tears, Kayla sat upright on the bench hours after arriving and the rest of the world completely passed her by. Lycra-clad joggers dashed by at an unhealthy pace, a group of college boys tossed a pigskin back and forth on the lawn, a family sat by the fountain enjoying a picnic on a warm winter’s afternoon. And here she was, sobbing. Wondering how long she could continue living like this. She’d long given up on trying to call Andy; even if he did have his phone on he’d probably reject the call anyway. He was shutting her out of his world almost, and she was left wondering what she’d done to deserve such a fate. Still, conflicted as she may have been, she wasn’t quite ready to give up on Andy Murray just yet. A figure ? plump and obtuse ? suddenly slouched down beside her on the bench, catching her off-guard. Caucasian and female, the comically round individual wore a scabby pair of black pants and a tattered grey jacket. The hood was drawn over her head and her craggy, weathered facial features stuck out through a small, clothe porthole.
