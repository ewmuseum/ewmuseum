Let's all wake up the slumbering beasts that time forgot, sure

undated

Date: Let's say 8/27. Let's lie! 'Hey Trey, what's up man?' 'Not much, J. Just practicing fire safety.' 'Sounds interesting. Mind sharing?' 'No problem!' Trey pulls a lighter from his pocket and catches his pants leg on fire. It spreads up like, well, fire and quickly encompasses his body, except for a Cheshire smile. Jordan stands on in shock before Trey falls to the ground and rolls. The flames die out after thirty seconds and Trey stands, brushing the ash off of himself. 'Wow, that was amazing. How'd you do it?' 'Simple. Stop, drop, and roll.' Okay. No? Well. Only rats leave a sinking ship. The captain must stay on until it sleeps on the bed of the ocean. Trey, Trey kicks as hard as he can and thrusts himself from the water--paddling like only the possessed can. Tension locks up into his shoulder blades and pops muscles all down the line. The roars keep rising from below, the echoes enough to rattle his ribcage as the nitrogen in his blood bubbles and releases. His head swims in a dirty soup of nostalgia, animosity, content, and every other emotion named by the English language, maybe a few more. Except schadenfreude. He can't understand everyone in the world, but anyone could empathize with the nasty mess he's built up around his life. Two decades of acceptance crack away from his frame and tear away soft tissue with them, straining an already overly-confused narrative almost to its breaking point. Still he fights upwards, desperate for a clear glimpse of the silver moon. Heart-set and mind-content, he breaks. Sitting on the coast of Metro Beach, wondering what was going to happen. His mind wandered ahead, away from his high school drama, his failing amateur wrestling string, his growing apathy, and to a long story that he slowly built. He'd already put down his guitar in frustration a hundred times and refused to bother with lifting it again on each; he wiped it away from the future he drew out in the sand. The jacket he wore to his father's funeral lay beside him, softly fluttering in the breeze, and his dreadlocks already fell to his shoulders. The lake was endless, in time and scale, and he let it flow by his first half-thought dream. He's thought about wrestling and it made him laugh. Yeah, fake. He tried to use it as consolation and instead found it an almost convincing argument. He had charisma, he had the rockstar look. Nothing said that he had any limits that day. There was no clicking, the hand of fate still bided its time. On that same shore, he laughed at himself even harder and ate a Swiss Cake roll. The camera crews watched him stare out into the water, having sectioned off a quarter of a beach to film promotional footage. It was a shitty gimmick, and he knew it, and he didn't care. The fans cheered almost as loudly as if he'd just bought them all hookers. It was something new, if not something good, but it passed. Christ, he couldn't wrestle. They had him train constantly but he was still breaking his back to make ends meet. The fame made up for it, as fame often does, and brought swarms of women to him. He'd lean back and enjoy the exposure. Writhing through the water like tar, he calls the feeling in his veins decompression for a minute before finally settling on the bends. His muscles churn acid and his stomach throws out bile and--somehow--he's sweating madly. The saltwater in his nose stings with every inhalation. His lack of comprehension is a blessing, it keeps him alive in his struggle. He tries to scream and lets out an empty yelp; Hell roars out below and he takes refuge in the refuse. For all the strain he drags his body through, he finds himself treading water and barely gaining. He names that frustration, would accept it if not for the tapping that keeps him moving infinitely for a minor gain. 'Trey.' He's not telling his name to anymore specters--they never let up. Passion fills his soul because it has to, and he looks down to see a black-green eruption heading directly for him. Its edges blur in the depths, becoming an extension of the shadowy floor as it ascends. Trey swims lateral, and the laws of physics send him flying backwards as the water breaks away from the creature. He struggles and, a moment before failure, latches himself to a scale on the hide. It jerks to the east, headed towards the coast at speeds that rip Trey's jacket from him, and gives no sign of caring for its unexpected passenger. It takes effort he'd kept hidden away in a lazy sack of tired conventions to hang on, grasping and regrasping onto flesh that leaves his hands slimy and smelling of death. It takes more effort to not vomit in the wake of the filth torpedo. The moon grows, his head swells and threatens to burst, his muscles want to die. It's progress. Voices still call out, growing louder and louder and more annoying in their repetition. Straightening out his will, he begins to climb along the rough flesh. His fingers wrap around a knob and pull him up and onto a leg. What? Not buying it? It stops. The sudden halt sends Trey screaming forwards into the sloping sands of the beach. Ribs definitely aching, he climbs out and falls to the sand. Mucus and saliva string from his mouth, and he coughs up salt-water with every twitch his stomach makes. The name still comes in a faint whisper from over dunes, but it drowns itself out in the rising tumult in his own head. Fear, loathing, paranoia, disgust. He feels an anger with no hope of catharsis. He met her a few weeks before graduation. Or, he talked to her then. He'd known her name since fourth grade, and she'd know his since third. It was Felicity Moore, and she asked him why he took so long. He gave her a shrug and smiled. 'I like your hair,' she said. A month later, they were stuck penniless in a condo at Myrtle Beach with a week left to gather the resources needed to escape. They laughed about it and smoked all hours of the day, sleeping minutes at a time before coming back around for another round of, well, whatever came up. 'What are you doing now?' 'What you mean?' 'Now that we're out of school. We're adults now--all grown up and ready to get on with our lives. What's your's?' 'You know, I think I wanna wrestle.' 'Hah. You sucked in school. Why the change?' 'It's money, I guess. Lemme live easy.' They didn't talk about it again until Trey happened to luck out in the most convenient of ways. He went to an opening fed with a head full of smarm and a heart of confidence. They fell in love with him and he was hired immediately. Then they had to talk about it. She didn't raise her voice when she found out, just smiled and laughed. 'You're serious, aren't you?' 'Yeah.' 'Christ. Well, I'm proud of you.' 'Thanks.' The next morning, he woke up early and pulled away from her sleeping arms to call the manager at his shitty register job in a record shop. A 'fuck you, I quit' worked, and he didn't worry about his resume looking worse for it. He made coffee and rubbed a scrape on his knee. His ankles throbbed like always, having spent some months dedicated to his goal by living a life like an urban Tarzan in his free-time; scaling fences to leap from a balcony and catch a well-placed rail a few stores below, dropping again and catching his feet awkwardly against a wall and running down it to a dumpster. It was his one good run, and he looked to it constantly. Pure luck. Most of the time, he skinned his knees or slammed his ribs or failed in some other, equally painful way. 'When you gonna break the bad news to me, handsome?' He turned and smiled to her caramel body and wavy frosted hair. A loose robe draped over her shoulders. 'Now's as good as ever.' She poured two cups of coffee and sat at the table, Trey taking a spot across from her. 'This is gotta end, y'know?' 'Yeah, I do.' She stopped to drink. 'When're you leaving?' 'Noon.' 'I'll be at work.' 'Shyeah.' He stirred sugar and cream until it matched her skin and drank heavily. 'I'm gonna miss you, girl.' 'You too. Just, just keep in touch.' Rolling to his back, he sees water pouring from the seams of the great best, its yellow eyes glowing like suns and towering stories above him. He trembles and pushes up on his elbows. Leathery wings stretch to block the stars from the sky, and two mighty arms rise, sending waves crashing high on the beach. Trey fights the undertow to go stoic, epic. Of Gilgameshian proportions. Or, as close as possible while half-alive. He breathes deeply. A dog darted in front of his red and decrepit Acura, and the brakes weren't enough to stop. He felt its body hit on the grill before rolling up the hood to crash into the windshield. In the moments that followed, he whipped to the edge of the road and stepped out of the car. It had been a black lab, body sleek and lean. Blood streamed down, formed in pools on the windshield wipers and drained down from the edges. He lifted its body, surprised by the weight, and carried it in his arms towards the nearest house. Thoughts hadn't formed when he rang the doorbell, they waited until a child opened the door. She was a little blonde girl, no older than ten, and he ended up remembering her smile for its sheer innocence. It lasted a split-second, before she understood the scene before her and disappeared screaming into the house. Moments later a balding man with glasses, khakis, and a buttoned blue shirt stepped out. 'I'm sorry,' he stammered, 'it just darted out, shit, I didn't-' The man looked it over and smiled. 'I should thank you. That stray's attacked our dogs for weeks now. The hell with it, I say.' Trey struggles to push up to his feet, a taste of copper on his tongue. He spits blood onto the sand, and gets a knee up. Enough to return his stare to the hellish eyes. Nothing happens. Birds don't fly. Cars don't start. People don't love. Hate doesn't kill. He takes a breath. 'Bitches leave.' A rumble begins. It comes in shockwaves and becomes a ravenous laughter that sends the ocean forward to claim further and further towards the world with each repeat. Lashing tentacles around its mouth swing and curl and move the winds to form crushing storms. The sky howls and lightning strikes and thunder roars and hell awaits. The wings push out fully from the ocean and flap once, knocking Trey to his ass and rolling him down the back of the dune into a small pool forming. They flap again and a wave crushes in on him, and a third time lifts the bulk fully from the water. It hovers in the air, corpulent and shining luminescent, gaining with each mighty beat. Trey stares up, struck dumb in terror. The wings drown out the cracking monsoon, drown out thoughts, drown out hope. He breathes and blinks and breathes again, and a roar reaches down from the heavens to claim him. Her name was Michelle. She was beautiful (thin, dark brown hair and eyes, a light tan, and grand tetons if not too impressive an ass) and stupid and a grade higher than him. They mixed well enough to count as a relationship for one week, which was all Trey'd ever planned on. The last night together, he met at her house and drove until they found a quiet spot by a small lake. He parked and she turned the radio to the Clear Channel rock station. Brick was playing. 'I love this song,' she said. 'It's just so sad, like, I don't even know.' He feigned for time. It was enough for her to lean in and lock her lips into his. He worked with a careful eye, following her lead without a delay. The tongues came next, and she pounced into his lap as he stretched out along the front seat. His left knee bent hard right, stuck against the floorboard at a bad angle and getting worse. Her hands went up his shirt first. But, Brick? It ended. The DJ whined and whimpered and shilled in background to Trey's feigned professionalism. They'd moved on to sucking and nibbling and she slinked down his chest towards his belt as the next song started. It came in with a high and quick guitar riff, drum beating a steady cadence in the background before the cymbals kicked. And then, David Lee Roth. 'Oh yeah-hah!' Trey, infinitely amused like teenagers usually are, held back a laugh at some imagined sublime quality of the moment. 'Jump back, what's that sound? Here she comes, full blast and top down.' He had to stop himself again when the first shouts of 'PANAMA!' came around. The next verse he spent grinning and humming along, mouthing out what words he knew. Michelle stopped and looked up to him with a grin before going back to work. When the chance returned, he caught himself singing along, voice louder with each line until the chorus. Choices. He didn't scream out 'Panama', because he wanted to actually get laid. He dumped her when he dropped her off later on, too easy of a conquest for a guy to keep around. She took it in stride. 'Nah,' he says. 'Not ready for that trip yet.' The roar dies. Fish suffocate, flapping by Trey's outstretched hands. He stares on as the magnificent terror rises through the clouds. It becomes a shadow, then becomes nothing. Contented for a moment, he stands and knocks the sand from his clothes. The moon hangs low over the ocean and the clouds break to let the dead stars' message back through. He laughs and reaches for a cigarette to no reward. Hell, that's life. Can't spend it frozen in awe, he figures, and begins climbing the hill. His nostrils throb and his eyes burn, so he shuts the first and breathes through his mouth. The blind walk's as safe as it gets, monsters perching somewhere and waiting for the perfect moment. He turns. 'I made the first move. Bring it to my court, kid.' Owning up.
