Wizards and Ruining Lives

16 July 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07/16/11 "I can’t believe you haven’t read the books, Dad." Before I had a daughter, I wouldn’t have thought of this as a getaway. The last place to find nineteen year old John would’ve been standing in line with a couple hundred snot-nosed kids, dressed up fat chicks, and overzealous fans of a fantasy series. At midnight. Of course if nineteen year old John dropped sixty bucks on a date, he’d have been expecting some serious action that night. Ten years later I’m happy to buy my daughter as much candy and popcorn and bootleg Harry Potter glasses and all the bullshit I can get my hands on, so long as it’s time spent with her. Not to mention time away from the rest of my life. "Well honey, you’ve really done an excellent job telling me all about them," I have to speak up louder than usual, else the crowd will drown out my words, "And I’ve seen all the movies plenty of times." "Yeah, but they leave so much stuff out. The books are so much better." "I bet they are," shifting my weight to hold on to the soda, two boxes of Sno-Caps, fat-drizzled popcorn, and the ever-prized movie tickets takes some serious effort, "But I don’t have a whole lot of time to read novels, sweetums." "I mean, they left out the whole plot where Hermione tries to free the house elves." "...I know, right! I couldn’t believe it when I saw that," I lie to my own child. Whatever. Don’t pretend you wouldn’t. The food’s for her. Well, not all at once, obviously. But for as much as I rail against corn syrup and saturated fats and filling your body with empty calories, Sarah looks at me with those eyes and I can’t say no to her. Fuck it. Let her eat whatever she wants. Maybe if I let her get away with murder she’ll keep loving me, despite my schedule or the choices I’ve made. Despite the fact that I missed the first six years of her life entirely. "Mom used to read them to me," she says, making sure to say ‘Mom’ instead of ‘Mommy’...her little sign of growing up before my eyes, "Even Keith read them to me sometimes." Lump in my throat. Kate’s boyfriend and I had been at an impasse for a long time, vying for the affection of a ten year old girl like a couple of dogs fighting over a scrap of meat. All for the wrong reasons, of course. See, Sarah is the key to winning Kate. If Kate’s daughter prefers one of us, that could go a long way toward swaying the mother one way or the other. At least this was the mentality of two selfish little boys, resulting in a game of tug of war which culminated a couple weeks ago in a pretty nasty way. I’m at the theatre with Sarah. I’ll have plenty of time to kick myself in the ass later. "Hey kiddo," try my best to sound like this is coming out of left field, like this hasn’t been eating away at me for an eternity, "I’m sorry I’ve been away so much lately." "It’s okay." "No. It’s not. It’s not okay, Sarah," I hold out the popcorn and she takes a bit, munches on it while I plead my case, "I’ve had my priorities a little screwed up, girl. Had my heart in the right place, I think, but I didn’t know how to do what I was needing to do." "What’s that mean?" She’s hard to read...smart for her age but more innocent than you’d expect. I can’t tell if she knows what it means or if she’s asking to make sure I know. "Well..." trying to formulate regret and the recognition of my failure as a father into words isn’t exactly easy, "I’ve tried a little too hard to make money, to be successful, to help take care of you. I think maybe I should have been here, spending time with you...taking you to more movies for example." Sarah grabs another handful of popcorn and turns back around, facing the front in brimming anticipation of the opening of the theatre doors. "It’s okay Dad," she hones in on the mention of movies, "The book is always better anyway." "If you want me to read them to you, Sarah, I’d be glad to." She shrugs, almost oblivious to what she’s shrugging off. "I can read them myself now, Dad. That’s okay." Fuck. ‘That’s okay.’ Might as well be two more nails in the coffin of father/daughter bonding. She’s still at that incredible age where she doesn’t recognize a moment of awkward silence, but the discomfort is too much for me to take. For shit’s sake I’ve got to talk about something, even if it’s debating the advantages and disadvantages of the books versus the movies. "Well...I like the movies just fine. The one with the werewolf was good." "Dad, it’s ‘The Prisoner of Azkaban,’ not ‘the one with the werewolf,’" she sighs, probably hoping someone didn’t hear me, "And the book was a million times better. The book’s always better than the movie." "You know I make movies, right sweetheart?" I’m half kidding, of course. I don’t kid myself into thinking I’m Oscar material. She knows, and the embarrassment leaves her face. "I saw your new commercial. You were really good." "No need to patronize, hun," a quick ruffling of her hair makes her laugh, "I’m sure my portrayal of a guy sitting at a restaurant table isn’t going to shake the acting world." She laughs a bit. It’s a relief to know I can still make her laugh. She’s coming up on her teenage years a little too fast, the two of us connect on so little, and I’m making sure to hold on to what I can while it’s here. Keith pointed it out to me in a confrontation...my life is going to drive her away. Just as I’m becoming comfortable sharing a moment’s silence with my daughter... "I saw Shockwave too," she says without looking at me. She’s ten years old and not allowed to watch what Daddy does for a living, so she’s anticipating a scolding, "Keith was watching it and I watched you from the other room." It’s like she’s talking to the priest on the other side of the screen in a confessional, "I’m sorry you didn’t win." I know I’m supposed to yell at her or discipline her or something. I’m sure Kate has some punishment or another set in place for when she breaks the rules...I don’t know. Whatever I’m supposed to do, she looks like she’s expecting the worst, like she’s somehow disappointed me. In the end there’s a little girl who decided to confess her sin, the watching of the forbidden fruit, for the greater good of making me feel comforted. "It’s okay, Sarah," my arm around her shoulder tells her there’s no reason to be afraid or ashamed, "Sometimes I lose matches. Happens." She turns, looks up, and smiles. The theatre doors open and the herd of expectant people go rushing in. Sarah is locked on me, and I have to take her hand and bring her in to see this movie. There’s something more important in this world than winning a match or being a star. While there’s a good chance I’ve realized that truth too late, I’ve still realized it. I can still enjoy these precious days before she pushes me away. ---- ---- Two hours of goddamn wizards and fucking lightning and shit later... Sarah’s ten years old. It’s two thirty in the morning, she’s loaded up with sugar, we just watched her favorite movie, and she’s bouncing around in the car going a mile a minute. In other words, I can already tell you I’m in deep shit when I drop her off at Kate’s. "I thought the snake wasn’t going to scare me, but they made the snake scary enough that it made me jump. Did you see me jump, Dad? I thought I wasn’t going to, but when it snapped out of nowhere I jumped even though I knew the snake was coming." "Yep." Pulling in the driveway. Something clicked with those words before we entered the theatre, and it’s like my daughter shredded three or four years during course of the movie. Sarah’s regressed back to the first time she saw the first Potter movie and she’s clinging to me and hanging off every word and anticipating my take on every idea rattling around in her head. Ever since she divulged her secret of watching Shockwave, father/daughter bonding has been resurrected. Ahh the healing art of owning up. "And then. Harry. And Hermione. They were running and then the battle was just absolutely crazy. I didn’t think it was going to be that crazy, Dad." "Bet it didn’t look like that in the book, huh," complete with a smirk. Regardless of how much sugar and excitement is coursing through her veins, my daughter is quite capable of firing an incredulous look at me, as if to say nice try, Dad. "It was awesome, Dad. Not better than the book, but it was awesome." Throw it in park, nod with content. Awesome is the word, that’s for sure. Maybe it’s knowing that I don’t have a long time left where she’s going to be my little girl and I’ve started holding even the most mundane moments precious. It seems like everything she says inspires awe. "Yeah," I unbuckle, taking a gander at the front door. The light’s on and they’re probably none too happy that I’m bringing her home this late. Whatever, it’s worth getting yelled at, "It was awesome, wasn’t it?" She nods over-energetically and hops out of the car. Walking up to the door she grabs my hand, swings it a couple times and skips a couple steps, completely oblivious to the silhouette in the doorway that catches my eye. It’s not Kate-shaped, which tells me that this could get nasty in a hurry. The front door opens before we get up the steps, and Keith’s haggard face is what greets me. A couple months ago I would have given the world to see the man so dejected, so beaten down. The once-pristine smirk and charming bone structure has been replaced with two weeks of no sleep and the unmistakable look of despair. He’s not exactly happy to see me. I don’t blame him, after what I did. ---- ---- What I did. Two weeks ago shit hit the fan. First I absolutely crushed my now-ex-girlfriend and cut her loose. Hit a bottle of whiskey for the first time in three years and woke up with one of the most brutal hangovers in forever. Then, with Kate, the love of my life, checking in on me, comforting me, and telling me I’m not a bad person for dumping some chick, I remembered that I’d given the order to fuck up Keith’s life something fierce. That long story short, I hired a private investigator to dig up dirt on Keith...the same P.I. that he hired to shit on my life. The man found stuff alright, including a huge number of fraudulent files and portfolio shammashamma’s...I don’t know, I’m not a business man. All I know is Kate’s "Mister Perfect" boyfriend was in charge of a bunch of people’s money, and that money was missing. I didn’t even have the investigator check if Keith knew about it, if he had any idea that he was helping run a ponzi scheme. Fuck it. I was drunk, I was sick of losing Kate bit by bit to a suit-and-tie wearing son of a bitch. Pull the trigger, let ‘er rip. See, it’s shit like this why I went three years without having a drink. Without inhibitions my impulsive decisions get downright nasty. I ended up remembering giving the order to expose everything, to tell the P.I. to deliver the report to a news station, right in the middle of Kate trying to comfort me. She was like an angel, looking to help me during an awful time, at the exact same time when the investigator I hired was effectively ending Keith’s career. I’d made my mind up to atone for my sins. I didn’t know how I was going to do it, but I was going to stop the story from breaking. I was going to throw myself on the sword, declare that I’d faked everything, give myself up and refuse to beg for mercy. I swore I’d let them throw the book at me to spare the person I counted as my rival...all because I was better. Johnathan Fitzwillis should be better than I’d been. From birth to that moment I’d been given every chance under the sun, every luxury a person could ask for. And when I threw it all away time and time again I would only find another opportunity to squander. The only reason I was stuck on getting back at Keith was because of my own God damn choices. He provided for the woman I love, the daughter I would do anything for. He was able to give them the life I never could, and it was a perfect, hungover moment of clarity when I realized I had to undo all the damage I’d spent so much time, money, and effort creating. All I had to do was throw my entire fucking life away. Would’ve happened too. If that fucking cop didn’t pull me over. I was about three blocks from Keith’s office, ready to put it in to action, when the lights flashed behind me and my heart sunk in to my stomach. Hungover and speeding is no way to go through life, and just like that I’d shot myself in the foot trying to do too much. The story broke. The company crucified Keith. I failed. ---- ---- What I should have been. Keith’s drunk. Or drinking. He eyes me with a tired, defeated gaze. Less than a month ago the man was the epitome of success, an alpha male dealing his way through one hundred thousand dollar business negotiation at a time, all the way to the top. Now he’s little more than a bum, leaning against the door frame, never having had to pick himself up out of the mud before. He’s always been on top of the world and the bastard is having a hell of a time dealing with being at the bottom. "John," I almost miss when he called me Johnny-boy. At least that was in a world where I didn’t ruin someone’s entire life, "You guys are home a little late." Sarah gives him a hug that he somehow manages to reciprocate. That’s something that would have burned me down to the core a short time ago. But Keith’s decrepit state, his utter hopelessness, removes his threat. She’s an amazing child, not knowing how to judge someone just because of their status in life. Sarah’s inside and running to tell Kate all about the movie, and it’s just me and him on the porch. Keith eyes me sideways for a heartbeat, then pushes off the doorway and fishes in his shirt pocket for a cigarette. Lighting it, he offers one to me, probably knowing beforehand that I’d reject it. Maybe it’s the humbling of a man, or maybe he just sees what is and isn’t important in this life, but there is no more animosity emanating from Keith. Maybe he’s just got more on his mind...trying to figure out how to get his life back. "Good movie?" he has a seat on the porch steps and stares into the darkness, not really giving a shit about the movie. Sitting down, it’s the first time I realize I’m tired. It’s almost three in the morning and I spent two of the last three hours staring at a giant movie screen in the middle of a gaggle of screaming teenagers. If I wasn’t so fucking tired maybe I’d have to sense to go inside and see Kate, the person I actually give two shits about. The thought hits me. Maybe I don’t feel like I deserve to. I did a real shitty deed, and maybe I need to square away what happened to the man that was my arch-rival. After all, he doesn’t know it was me. I never made it down to stop the story from hitting the news. I was taking a breathalyzer test while the reporter contacted Keith’s firm, and I was saying my alphabet backwards while he was getting shit-canned. It probably took all of twenty minutes for his corporation to cut ties, especially if they were the ones to set up those fraudulent accounts. Now the man’s being sued by everyone that knows how to sue someone. He’s out of a job, blacklisted, and near as I can tell has no idea what caused it all. Just another statistic of the economy, just another victim of Wall Street. My guilt could eat me alive. "Yeah," I finally bleat out, "They did a good job. I guess. I never read the books." He snorts a laugh. "You know something, John," he takes a big drag of his cigarette, "I never did either. And here I was thinking Kate was the only thing we had in common." I’d like to say I could appreciate the humor. I’ll just tack it up to him being depressed and more than a little tipsy. Taking one look at Keith I can’t help but wonder what monster possessed me to the point where I’d ruin all he has ever worked for. Distaste and rivalry aside, the man had only ever tried to get me to stay away from his girl. In his eyes I was the obsessed ex-boyfriend, allowed to stick around because Sarah had a fondness for me. He was civil, even a fan of my sport, and I was the one that spat in his eye as often as I could. Because he put together the girl that I had left so long ago, and that was somehow a sin. Enough was enough and he hired a private investigator, dug up some dirt, and tried to use it as leverage to get me to distance myself from his woman. The girl he loves...the girl who loves him. He had decided he was done being polite, and while he could have used what he found to hurt my career and my entire life, he only used it as leverage to get me to back off. A strong man would have realized this. Instead I took it like a kid getting pushed in the sandbox. "You know man," Keith pats me on the leg, feeling all buddy/buddy for the first time ever, "I don’t see why you and I gotta be fighting over Kate. And Sarah." Running his hand through his hair, Keith turns and leans on the railing, taking a look at me and relaxing a bit, "I got all bristly. But can you blame me?" Yes. "No." "I mean I made some serious cash, you know? An’ I had my name and my reputation," he stops himself from rambling, "An’ here you come along. An’...do you know what’s that’s like, man?" I’ve never seen what I look like coming along, no. "I mean you’re Johnny fucking Borealis," partially slurred, my name tumbles off his tongue, "You’re fuckin’...body of a twenty year old, right? God damn perfect hair...fuckin’ abs and everything. I mean...you’re a specimen! And whaddayou think an alpha dog’s gonna do if you come sniffing?" "Keith. Don’t, man. It’s not-" "I got defensive. Me," he pokes himself in his own chest a little too hard, "I mean I did you some wrong, man. I should’ve just talked to you about me and Kate and you and all that without bringing in some guy to fuckin’....take pictures and follow you and shit." I take it back. Dude’s not tipsy, he is pretty sloshed. He’s reached the ‘apologetic bro’ phase. "Keith, don’t-" "An’ I got all up in your grill. An’ I’m sorry," one of his hands flops on my shoulder. I can hear Kate and Sarah talking inside, but she hasn’t come out yet. Probably figures Keith and I have some conversing to do, "I know you din’ take it well when you broke it off with yer other girlfriend-" "Sarah." "Yeah. The one with the same name as Sarah." "...that would be correct." "I know you been hurtin’. Can’t be easy. Wouldn’t know...never broken a heart before, man," he takes another drag and tries to slow down his thoughts to something more coherent, "I just know that...I’m fuckin’ out of a job, man. Out of a life. Got fuckin’ screwed cuz some guy wanted to stick my name on some paper, saw me as a scapegoat." No. Don’t. Don’t you dare, John. "Sucks, man." The truth is putting cracks in the concrete, seeking to burst out from hiding. If I can just make it till Kate comes out I’ll be alright. I’ve just gotta hold out until my daughter shuts her yap about the movie, then Kate will come out and usher Keith inside and I can go home without having to say a God damn word. "An’ it just...just made me realize...there’s no reason to hate, man. No reason. An’ Kate...fuck, she is incredible. Just wrapped her arms around me and held me. Told me I’m not a bad person." Some synapse fires off in my head, back to just before I remembered I had condemned Keith. Kate told me I wasn’t a bad person. She sure has a way with words. "I don’t fuckin’ know, man," he snuffs out the smoke and uses the railing to stand up, "I’m just past us being dicks to each other. We each got bigger shit going on, right? Don’t need us two fighting while maybe we could be taking care of that little girl in there, right?" Pressure valves are popping. My lips are straining to hold back the words. "It’s all good, John," he smiles. Keith’s entire life is in shambles, and the man manages to smile at me, "You’re a good man. Good to finally be square with you." Through one of the worse times I can imagine, at his most vulnerable, Keith smiles at the man that he had been feuding with. He is stronger than I have ever been, able to withstand such a blow to his pride and use it to become someone better. I tried my best to stop my mistake from bearing fruit. Even if that traffic cop hadn’t pulled me over, I didn’t have a plan. I was going to go in there and give myself up, tell them I had faked everything, and thrown my life away to save someone I couldn’t stand. I had all the guts in the world to prevent disaster from happening. But owning up to the disaster that already destroyed his life...do I have what it takes to stand up to that? My daughter could admit her sin to me, however small it may be compared. In a time where I need a reason to bleed, a chance to choke, Sarah is the example I need to follow. The most difficult choices in life are also the simplest. In this moment, all I must do is tell the truth. "Keith," a shaky breath does its best to prepare me for what I’ve got coming to me, "...it was me." Still a bit of a smile, his face goes a bit confused. Eyebrows furrow, he has no idea what that means. "What was you?" Last chance to back out. Make something up. Just let him be and move on and make up for your mistake by taking care of things, John. "I hired an investigator...the same one you had tail me," the words are tough to get out, so I just charge through them without further hesitation, "He found the accounts. I exposed them." Silence. In one brief moment, I can see Kate over his shoulder. Our mutual guardian angel, coming out to see her two boys playing so nicely. My daughter just got done telling her how great our night was, and she still has a pleased look on her face when Keith raises his fist. Instinct wants to kick in, but I’m using every neuron I have to suppress it. He deserves this more than any person who has ever punched me. He’s not trained to fight but it doesn’t matter as his fist connects square on the bridge of my nose. Instead of ending, this night just got started.
