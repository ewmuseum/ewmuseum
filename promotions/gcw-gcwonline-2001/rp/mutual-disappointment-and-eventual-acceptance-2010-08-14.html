Mutual Disappointment (and Eventual Acceptance)

14 August 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/14/10 08.08.2010, 9:00 AM Courtyard by Marriott, Room 391 San Diego, CA I had discovered over the years that pain was something I rarely felt in its worst forms during a wrestling match, or even in the immediate afterglow thereof. Adrenaline was a more powerful drug than caffeine or even nicotine in that way. Even after taking Triumph Frost's Sympathy for the Devil, the pain wasn't agonizing. It had knocked me temporarily unconscious, more than enough for him to acquire a three count, but I had managed to leave the ring under my own power, shower, and then conduct my first ever on camera interview. Of course, what all of this meant was that I woke up the next morning barely able to leave my bed. As I lay there groaning in agony, I realized yet again that GCW was different. Being thrown around by guys closer to my size was one thing. Being tossed around by "The Future" was another entirely. "Ugh," I whimpered, trying to pry myself out of bed long enough to get to the bathroom and take a small handful of Advil to get my protesting body working for me once again. "I feel like I got hit by a bull." Very gingerly, I walked over to the bathroom, using the walls and beds to brace myself as I went. By the time I got inside, the sight that greeted me in the mirror was truly pathetic. My face was contorted in a grimace, small flecks of the Bhava facepaint that I hadn't fully removed still clinging to my skin. "So much for glory," I sighed, reaching into my travel kit, and unearthing my bottle of Advil from underneath a razor, shaving cream, and a tube of toothpaste. I poured four Advil out from it, and threw them all into my mouth, quickly filling the nearby water cup to wash them all down. Being in agony after a victory still sucked, so being in agony after coming up on the short end of the stick was one of those things that worked against the desire to get up in the morning. As I staggered out of the bathroom, I caught an envelope on the floor out of the corner of my eye. Apparently, it had been slid under the door, and it bore my name, so it was presumably for me. "Checkout's not till tomorrow," I grimaced, seeing a potential verbal battle in my future. Bending down to pick the envelope up was more of a struggle than I liked to admit, but once I had, I realized that this wasn't from the hotel. It was, instead, from my employer. "We are pleased to inform you..." I read, skipping over what looked to me like jargon to try and get to the important parts. What I ended up seeing were the words "too confusing," "giving you a mouthpiece," and "training partner." Needless to say, I was displeased for a wide variety of reasons. "They couldn't have called me," I asked aloud. Thankfully, I didn't get an answer. Maybe they really did think I spoke solely in circles and Zen-influenced logic. Or maybe I wasn't worth a phone call when they could just have an underling slide the note under my door. Whatever the case, they had at least notified me. I shot a sideways, longing glance at my bed, but it looked as if it was time to greet the world and see who GCW had found to be my mouthpiece and spare the world from another rambling Bhava promo. To say I wasn't offended that they'd seen one week of my stuff and were already muzzling me would have been a lie, but this was America and GCW. This was my dream. If they wanted me to be the Amazing Cocksucker, I'd probably have done it. Maybe. If they paid for all the mouthwash I'd need. Given Triumph Frost's reaction to my white mist, it was quite possible he thought I already WAS the Amazing Cocksucker. I'd been told that being different was okay when I'd been signed. Apparently, being TOO different was bad. At the end of the day, though, I was a professional, and that meant getting dressed, having my complimentary continental breakfast, and making tracks to the nearby Starbucks to see if this "mouthpiece" thing was going to be as bad as I thought it would. After two plus years of toiling in Canada and Japan, the success of my career was about to be forever tied to someone I'd never met. * * * 08.08.2010, 9:00 AM GCW Training Academy Houston, TX It was the call that I had been waiting for my entire tenure in GCW's Houston training academy. After receiving it, I had all but done a jig in my apartment. It would all be worth it, the time spent busting my ass under the hot Texan sun. All of the injuries sustained in training. Every last bit of it was worth it, because I was being called up to Global Championship Wrestling. As I sat across a desk from one of the academy's higher-ups, though, I was listening to my dream being torpedoed and turned into something else entirely. "We'd like to call you up to the main roster as a valet for one of our newcomers," he explained, as the expression on my face shifted from elated to crestfallen. Apparently it was an obvious enough shift that he noticed. I never was very good at hiding my emotions. "I know you want to be on the main roster, Autumn. This doesn't mean that'll never happen. But right now, this is how we can best use you. You'll be on WorldWide every week. We'll sign you to a proper talent deal befitting your new status. You can work out with everyone when the roster trains, and we'll fly you back here when needed. There's a lot you can learn being on the road with the roster." "If you're so confident in me," I asked, "why aren't I being called up to wrestle?" "Because we've just signed a whole slew of guys," he replied. "We hired Bhava, Dominic Wall, and Rediker in the past week and a half or so." Great, so I was going to be the valet for someone who'd taken one of the spots I could have fit into. As much respect as I had for LeStatt Knight's achievements, I was sort of miffed that his recommendation had given someone outside of the GCW system what I considered to be my spot. At this rate, I was expecting them to cram me into one of the World Champion's patented half-shirts. Not that I had a problem with showing off in moderation, but that thing was just...tacky. I loved Tessa's work though. Her work, and Vivica J. Valentine's, and Tempest's... Going back a couple of years, there were also Eva Van Lorne, Lia Ambrosi, and Dawn McFarlane, the first female GCW World Champion. All of them had blazed a trail that I wanted desperately to follow. I had watched hours upon hours of their matches after training, trying to take elements of their games to improve my own. My reminiscing on GCW's outstanding female competitors was rudely interrupted by being informed of more details of my assignment. "You'll be working with Gerard Roy. He plays Bhava." I blinked at Mr. Reynolds. That was enough to get him to elaborate. "He just made his debut on WorldWide 135. We signed him away from a Japanese independent company, WORLD WARRIORS. Before that, he competed in Canada. Kind of strange guy, he's a Zen Buddhist and wears face paint. First thing he ever did in a GCW ring was spit white mist in Triumph Frost's face. Frost was not happy about being, well, frosted." Before I could contemplate Bhava any further, I cringed at the positively horrible mental image my superior had just given me. Ugh. With that done, there was a slightly embarrassed twinge to my cheeks. I watched WorldWide every week, but I was slightly embarrassed to realize that I hadn't quite gotten around to it yet this particular week. "Give the show a looksie when you get a chance, Autumn," he said, as if I hadn't already figured that out. "He likes to give rambling, hard to understand interviews. It's supposedly Zen, but our focus groups don't seem to get what the fuck he's saying. The decision has been made to give him a mouthpiece, someone to speak with and for him, and you're the best talker we have down here." I was flattered, but really, I'd never thought much about my ability to speak. It was part of wrestling, but never had been one of my major focuses. Wrestling made me think of technical acumen, death-defying aerial moves, and spine-crunching suplexes. Rarely, except maybe when the masters were at work, did I think of a microphone. "We also chose you because you speak Japanese and have spent time across the Pacific," he continued. "He's lived in maritime Canada and Japan his whole life. He doesn't know anyone here at all. To be honest, we'd like you to be his manager both in and out of the ring. You will find it to be valuable experience. We have high hopes for you, Autumn, and when a spot opens up on the main roster, it'll be yours. Consider this on the job training in life outside the ring as a professional wrestler. We've had lots of highly touted prospects come in and flame out because they couldn't handle everything that happened outside the squared circle." He seemed sincere, at the very least. "Alright," I said, "I get the point. I'll do it." "Excellent! We're going to put you on a plane to San Diego to meet up with him tomorrow morning, then we'll fly you back here to tie up some loose ends and send you on to Phoenix for WorldWide 136. Congratulations, Autumn. I know this isn't what you expected, but I have a feeling it's going to work out well for everyone involved." I shook his extended hand. I was still miffed, but he'd said enough right things that I couldn't be too pissed. As long as this was a speed bump on my way to being a GCW superstar, I could deal with being a valet for a while. He was right, too, in that I imagined that there probably WAS a lot for me to learn travelling the roads. Still, something was bothering me. "I'm...not going to have to prance around in a bra and panties, am I?" He chuckled in reply. "Absolutely not. It is Steven Caldera's policy to never do such things. Female competitors, as you well know, have been a vital part of GCW history and present. I actually think you'll appreciate what we have in mind for you. From all of my experiences with you, you enjoy tradition. The role we have in mind for you is both classy and classic." When he showed me the outfit an hour or so later, I couldn't help but agree. From there, the next ten or so hours were a whirlwind of official photoshoots, paperwork, and a flight from Houston to Texas to come face to face with destiny. After two and a half years, my wrestling fate was about to be connected to a man in white facepaint who liked to meditate in the ring. * * * 08.08.2010, 8:00 PM Starbucks San Diego, California Whether in Canada, Japan, or the United States, there was always one constant: Starbucks. Sure, I much preferred Tim Horton's, but Starbucks ran a close enough second that it wasn't totally disappointing to run into one. This Starbucks, however, was going to do more for me than let me purchase an overpriced espresso beverage. It was going to be the place where I met my new professional partner. I'd have been lying if I said I wasn't looking forward to meeting my new manager. Being alone in a foreign country, no matter how much there was to do, was not the most appealing proposition in the world. Japan had been very new to me, but Mark and I had a bond from the beginning. In time, I'd gotten to know lots of others. I hoped that would be the same here, but for now, I was very much trying to keep my head down in the locker room. I nursed a grande cafe mocha at a table in the corner, watching people as they came inside. The letter I'd received had been beyond vague. I didn't even have a gender to go by, so every time someone new entered, I tried to figure out what idea they'd be bringing to the table. An older, heavy set man had brought me from Japan to do his evil bidding. When he walked over to another table and hugged what was presumably his wife, I had to find someone else to ponder over. Sipping at my drink, I had to stifle a chuckle at how ludicrously I was acting. Without friends to talk to, or knowledge of the cities I was in, my mind was more or less on my career 24/7. The door opened again, and this time, the woman standing in the doorway seemed to be scanning the establishment. Sure, she could have been here on a blind date, for all I knew, but it was a hopeful sign. Hell, in some ways, it was a blind date: two people coming together with little knowledge of one another in hopes of making things work out. In this case, though, it was a professional arrangement. As I was trying to figure out what sort of approach that blonde girl in the doorway would bring to a managerial career, I had the shit scared out of me. "Are you Gerard," I heard a voice ask, from somewhere behind me. I spun around in my chair fast enough that I almost knocked myself over. One of her hands went over her mouth to stifle a giggle, and I felt like an idiot. Already, before I'd ever said anything. That was a new record for me. "Yes, I am," I managed, rising from my seat to extend a hand. As I did so, I realized that the woman who was most likely to be my manager was probably about as tall as I was. Red hair flowed down to her shoulders, and she looked at me with green eyes, partially obscured by a pair of glasses. She shook my hand firmly enough that I wondered if I'd ever get it back. "Nice to meet you," she replied, pumping my hand a few times before letting it go. "I'm Autumn. GCW assigned me to be your manager, both in and out of the ring. Sorry for being late. I was here a while ago, but thought I'd have enough time to pick up a drink and I didn't see you." She held out her other hand, which held what looked to be an iced latte of some sort. "No problem at all," I replied. "You're more than on time." I mulled over what she'd said for a moment, and then asked the only question I had. "Out of the ring," I asked, quirking an eyebrow at her. She nodded. "Think of me as your real manager also, to an extent. They thought you might need some help navigating airports and making travel arrangements, that sort of thing. And on the shows, they want me to speak for you." I tried my best to smile at her. Apparently, my best fake smile wasn't good enough to fool her. "You don't need to pretend to be happy about this, Gerard. I understand, believe me. I'm in GCW's training academy," she explained. "I was hoping they'd be calling me up to debut in the ring, but I'll be doing this instead." Well, then. There was...something vaguely comforting about us both harboring some form of resentment towards this assignment. "I suppose we should then move forward and do the best we can to make this work for both of us," I offered, to which she smiled and nodded. "Exactly what I was about to suggest, Mr. Roy," Autumn said. "From what I've been told, you and I can teach each other a lot." From all indications I'd been given, she was supposed to teach me how to talk, while I was supposed to teach her some more things for the ring. I was also pretty sure that neither of us thought we particularly needed help in either department. "Ah, yes, that does remind me. The letter I received from GCW officials told me that we'll be travelling and training together. I must admit to being curious to see what GCW teaches its most prized prospects." "And I am curious to see the talents of a man who has competed in Japan first-hand," she replied. "I spent a year in Japan during college, before I decided to become a wrestler, and the action I saw over there was incredible." I offered a somewhat embarrassed shrug in reply. "They hit harder and have press conferences. There are lots of differences, yes, but what people never focus on are all of the similarities. I consider our sport a language that those of all nations can speak. We have different dialects, of course, but we all speak the same tongue." "I've never quite thought of it that way," she admitted, turning the idea over in her head. "I like the analogy. At any rate...I've heard you face Donny Diamond and David Spencer on WorldWide this week." "Yeah," I replied, shaking my head. "Can't seem to catch a break with the matchmakers at all." "They have high hopes for you," Autumn told me, nodding once. "They wouldn't be testing you so much if they weren't." "Here's hoping you're right. Starting off 0-2 looks pretty bad no matter my competition." Making the adjustment from being a sizeable fish in a small pond to a tiny fish in the GCW ocean was proving more difficult than I'd hoped. In Canada and Japan, I was actually known as one of the better talkers. Here, I was deemed "in need of help" by GCW management. "I watched your match on the flight from Houston to here," she said. "You looked good. Triumph's a tough opponent. I would question your ring strategy, though. Are you trying to throw people off?" "That would be the idea, yes," I nodded. "Didn't seem to work so well against Triumph though." "Sometimes, trying to outthink people, you end up outthinking yourself," she offered, and for a moment, I wondered which one of us was the Zen Buddhist. "You're probably right," I had to admit. "I'm going to tone it down a bit this week, see if that brings me any more success. But enough of me being a weirdo. What exactly do they want you doing? You gave me the basics, but..." She pulled out her phone, and pushed several buttons in rapid succession to bring up a photograph on it. "Here, take a look. This is what they have me wearing. Basically, they want me to be an old-school female manager." I took the phone from her and looked down at the photo. I had to admit that the look they had her going for was quite nice. Her long red hair was curled and hung down to her shoulders, and she wore a silver and black dress that showed just enough to arouse curiosity (and probably other things) while still remaining elegant. Still, as beautiful as she was, I couldn't help but feel that something was missing. "What's wrong," she asked, and I took a deep breath in. This was going to be an interesting relationship, particularly if she could read me this well and I'd barely even met her. "Forgive me for suggesting this if it offends you, Autumn, but I think the outfit needs a little something. I think you need to wear facepaint that matches Bhava's." Her face froze for a moment, and then one of her hands rose to stroke her chin as she mulled the idea over. "Any particular reason, other than symmetry," she asked. It was my turn to think for a bit. "It gives you more mystery, I think. Bhava having a beautiful redheaded valet leads to lots of questions. Where did we meet? What's your purpose in being here? All of that sort of stuff. The facepaint ties the two of us together more." A secondary side benefit suddenly flashed to mind. "Besides, if I fail miserably, you wearing the facepaint means you're not attached to that failure." "Such confidence you have, Gerard." She put her hands on her hips. "If we're going to be working together, I'm going to have to work on that." "Such a deadpan you have, Autumn," I replied, mirroring her pose. "If we're going to be working together, I'm going to have to get used to that." We both cracked up at the same time. I couldn't speak for her, but I was starting to feel better about this arrangement, our bruised egos aside. "Well, we should probably get the basics out of the way," I reasoned. "You know, the 'where are you froms' and all of that." "We'll have plenty of time to tell each other our life stories once we're traveling together," she said. "For now, though, I've got to head back to the airport. I'm staying at the academy in Houston for a few more days, and then I'll fly to Phoenix the day of the show to meet up with you. From there, we're attached at the hip." "There are worse fates," I chuckled, and she shook her head. "Don't make me smack you. Would be a terrible start to a working relationship." "It would at that," I agreed, and extended my hand. "Well then, Autumn Nevergavemealastname, shall I see you at WorldWide?" Once again, my hand was nearly crushed. "You shall, Gerard Roy. And I won't be giving you a last name. Mine is embarrassing." "You would deny me the chance to patronizingly call you Miss Lastname?" She smirked, and nodded. "I quite like being the only one able to patronizingly refer to you in this relationship. Perhaps I will tell you on the road someday when we are both truly bored." "Then," I said, smirking right back to her, "I will eagerly await that day. Until then, I will return to my room and meditate for a bit before my flight." One of her eyebrows arched. "Your faith isn't only on camera, Mister Roy," she asked, a twinkle in her eye. Somehow, I figured I'd have to get used to being called that. "I am indeed a Zen Buddhist, Miss Lastname. What you see on camera both is and is not an exaggerated version of my faith." At her confused expression, I grinned. "Allow me to put it as simply as I can. For me, the question "what is the sound of one hand clapping" has an actual answer." "Oh," she asked, seemingly genuinely impressed. "Would you mind enlightening me?" As suddenly as it appeared, my grin faded to ashes. A little voice in the back of my mind told me that what I was about to say was probably also a decent analogy for where I was in my GCW career. "I will enlighten you, Autumn Lastname, once I have the knowledge to enlighten myself."
