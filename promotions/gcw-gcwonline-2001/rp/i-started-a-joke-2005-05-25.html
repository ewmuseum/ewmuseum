I started a joke

25 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 25th, 2005 'You got a kid, J?' 'No; why would I?' 'Figured you'd have fucked up enough.' 'Good call, but I've been lucky. Why?' 'Just wonderin'.' 'What, you got some chick pregnant?' He took his time for once and finished off another glass of wine before he replied, his voice low and soft. UnTreymanlike. 'I think so.' 'Think?' 'This girl I used to chill with back in Detroit, last I saw she was pregnant and people were sayin' it was my kid. I dunno though, I ain't stick around long.' 'Didn't wanna find out?' 'Nah, I do. They just weren't gon' accept that their little boy Trey had grown up this ugly; my mom and my girl, I mean.' 'Harsh.' 'Yeah, s'like there's this big hole missin' right where my heart is, sayin' that ain't nothin' in my life goin' right.' 'Nothing?' 'Nothin' that matters. Shyeah, I'm rich and all that shit, b'what's it matter if I'm not happy?' 'Means a lot in a capitalist economy.' 'Shit, that don't matter an' you know it. Ain't about the economy, it's about my life, thinkin' I might really be too fucked up to go home.' 'If you're ever fucked up to go home, then maybe you didn't know what home meant in the first place.' The air was sticky humid and the diner was empty save for us two and the workers that still hung around while everyone else was out relaxing. I could feel the drunk coming on and it wasn't boding well for me, especially not mixed with the suffocating heat. Too dark inside, the windows blocked from the sun by a dense line of thin trees that extended up with no regards to holding proportion with their girth, the stench of grease had filled my nostrils and made me long for the sweet smell of gasoline and pollution waiting for me back home. I finally paid and we left without another word, Trey hanging his head and letting the shadows hide what was left of his face. Christ yeah, I felt worse about myself than I was used to. He had put the burden down on my neck and expected a better answer than the one he got--he expected me to break from my normal tactics and give him some hope about his family, about his life, about his overall place in the world. Angst took his soul when I didn't have the answer he wanted, and I let him keep up the silent loner act for a few minutes before getting back to the root of the matter. 'You sure you're up to this?' 'Yeah.' 'Heh.' 'What, you don't think I am?' 'I just don't know if you're ready for it.' 'I'm not, but if that's what's up, then I guess I gotta.' 'If you're worried, the Italian government's had an open season on Nazi sympathizers since the government was restored, World War 2 era law that never got changed.' 'Really?' 'Yeah. You'll be fine on that front, and if you can make it out of the hotel before they realize someone across the street's been shot, you won't even have to go through the motions of court. They'll just write it off as another greedhead bastard slain for his own sins.' 'Shit.' 'It's an easy call. I'd pull the trigger for you, but Caldera asked me not to. Kindly asked.' 'Yo, Jordan.' 'What?' 'Cut the bullshit.' God damnit, I thought. He knew about the game and was just going to ruin it instead of turning it against me. I missed a turn in defeat and started to head back to the hotel we'd stayed at. 'Why they really want this guy dead? Ain't 'cause he's helping Commies--' 'Nazis.' '--Nazis leave the country, and that ambassador story ain't reason to drop a guy either. What's up?' At that point, I thought maybe he was playing me. My half-thought story had been full of glaring plot holes and cheap jokes that I don't remember, so it wasn't a stretch to think that he'd caught on. I needed something good, and I needed it to look good. Summoning all the acting ability of a typical Generation X drop-out, I dropped my jaw to my chest and breathed deeply. 'The world is full of very bad people that do very bad things, Trey. Some people, and some of the stuff they'll do, will fade away from the public mind and never even come up again after they're dead. Some people, and the things they do, will never escape your memory. Hitler, Mengele, Stalin, Mussolini, Dahmer, Ted Bundy, Albert Fish, those fuckers and their deeds are here to stay, to keep kids up at night wondering about heaven and hell, and if life is really just. He's one of those fuckers, the really evil ones that get caught doing it. I want you rested up for your big match, so I won't tell you what he did, but I'll tell you that there's a mother somewhere that will be made a thousand times happier by what you're about to do.' Trey went silent again, not even showing that normal glint of fear that comes up in his eyes when I'm driving. The car bounced and rattled about on cobblestone alleys, and traffic broke in waves to accept the crazed American that had read up on the Visigoths. I turned the stereo up, a CD with loud music that encouraged abandon, cutting the Porsche through another tight alley. Cats leapt back up to their perches to be safely out of my reach and hissed as we bobbled past. I lost control when the right front crashed hard off the curb, but a clearly frightened women in some little black box of a car slammed on brakes and gave me a clear path to the hotel. Then, trying to look serious even as his body was still slinging around in the car's cabin from my last lucky incident, Trey finally spoke up. 'When in Rome.' We laughed about it, short shallow laughs that were for completely different reasons, and I pulled up. 'Just go to the desk clerk, tell them you've got a reservation as Metzger on the fifth floor. Don't take any shit from them.' 'So, this is gonna make me the hot commodity?' 'Only if you want title reigns and lots of money.' ''Preciate it, man.' 'Thank Caldera. He picked you.' Trey got out of the car and made it half-way to the lobby before he heard me calling his name. 'You bastard, don't forget the briefcase. The trunk's hanging open.' 'Fuck,' he calls as he races back to me, 'didn't mean to do that.' 'Can't afford mistakes in this line of business. I'll be parked over by those bushes when you get out.' 'Word.' Who the fuck says 'word'? Just another bad habit to break, I thought, and pulled around the corner to wait. The Porsche idled off smoothly and I took to considering getting one of my own, a little red beast with quick reflexes and a tight grip on the road--something to take the brunt of carrying Trey's emotional baggage away from the Impala when I get back home. From my vantage point I could see half of Trey talking to a desk clerk's shoulder. If I could read shoulders I'd know what they were talking about, but failing that means I have to take a good guess, and it comes back funny to me. I lit a Newport and when I looked up, Trey had vanished from my impaired line of sight and the clerk was holding a phone to his ear--no longer hidden behind an overgrown bush in the lobby. A few minutes of that quiet wait passed. Shit, had they bought his story? It'd be an even greater pay-off to see him running back out to the car, damning me for either making the whole damned thing up or for losing the gun and ruining his chances at a title shot. Two more cigarettes passed before I knew something was up, and another passed before it became clear that I was going to have to do something, if I didn't want to risk losing the chance to actually have a day outside of a cheap joke on a too-trusting fool. Once again, I'd been too brilliant for my own good and would have to clean up the mess. The contingency plan of me walking in and explaining everything to Trey went into action, and I walked in with my heart set on explaining everything to Trey and just getting the fuck out of there with my pride hanging limp. Too easy. I scanned the lobby and saw no sign of Trey, not to say that it's hard to spot a black guy with bright red hair in Italy. He'd show up on an aerial map of the country, which meant that I should've had no problem eying him out in the sparse crowd that congregated under the shimmering chandelier. Most of the people seemed to be the middle-class American tourist type, clad in khaki shorts and button-up Hawaiian shirts and found in every prosperous nation, and were probably there for the vacation of their dreams. They'd picked the right spot, as I finally caught on to the lobby's duel purpose as a massive ballroom, draped in crimson and gold and framed by massive staircases that ran behind some central curtain, probably to an elevator or an overpriced bar. No one was at the desk; I approached it with apprehension and rang the bell twice quickly to get a clerk's attention. I cut him off before he could talk, 'you seen a black guy with red hair?' He raised a hand to me and shuffled off towards a girl with hair that hung to her shoulders in tight black curls and a body deserving of a better face than the one it had been given. 'Would you like assistance?' 'Yeah, looking for a friend of mine--black guy, red hair, was wearing a black tanktop and, uh, jeans, I think.' She lowered her head and locked a stare straight on me, thinking I didn't notice the little button she'd trailed her finger over. 'He caused a disturbance and was taken by security.' '...what?' I didn't have to wait long for an answer. There was the crash of something, metal against metal, and the triumphant roar of Trey Tremendous kicking open a steel door behind the desk. He burst out of the room they'd thrown him in, with security just footsteps behind, and screamed something at me as he leapt over the desk and ran towards the entryway. I tried to follow but a guard thicker than he was tall rammed his shoulder into my chest and sent me back awkwardly. I regained my balance to see Trey apprehended again, grabbed in the parking lot by security that had been waiting for him. They brought him back in, kicking and screaming, staring dead at me. 'Jordan, do somethin' man.' I thought quick and put my hand around the can of Mace in my pocket. The security guards around Trey eyed me as I crossed the room, a problem I fixed with a nice spray in the eyes of the first that came at me. He dropped with a scream and clawed at his face, causing one to back away and another to grab my wrist and restrain it behind my back, pulling the can away from me. There was a crowd forming as Trey kicked at the guards holding them until they loosened their grips enough to turn the game around. 'Run!' 'What about you, J?' My shoulder burned like hell, wrenching against itself in the socket and sending waves of pain through my body. I once wrestled, so I'll call it a hammerlock and say that I knew more than a few ways out of it, and chose the dumbest one--popping myself straight up and spinning my body to level a solid elbow into the guard's temple. I heard something between a tear and a pop, but put it in the back of my head as I ran my ass off out of the lobby and to the car, Trey patiently waiting in his seat. 'What the fuck, man? What the fuck?' I cranked the car and cut out of the parking lot, ignoring both Trey and the stabbing pain running the length of my left arm and chest, spreading from that one shoulder. The car resisted my plea for performance with the shrieking pitch of tires squealing, a waste of rubber at every turn or stop--Zeus forbid I should've ever stopped in that mess. No, I never considered the option. 'Jordan, what the fuck?' I tried to keep the car in third gear, steering it with my knees whenever I had to shift. Right about then, Trey noticed that my left arm was sort of limp at my side and that maybe, just maybe, I hadn't exactly pulled things off like I wanted to. 'Man... your arm alright?' I drove harder and the pain stung louder and louder--ringing in my ears like some chaotic symphony of warning bells overlapping a nuclear siren--and the world kept getting further and further away. Trey's voice was in the distance, as intelligible as if he'd been whispering to me across the Grand Canyon, and I was picturing better things than this. He tells me the last thing I did was laugh, before passing out in the hotel's parking lot. I don't ask anymore questions of him and he doesn't ask anymore of me. 'It was gonna be a funny joke, Trey.' 'Heh, it was funny.' I look at my arm draped out in the cast in front of me, really understand what shattered means for once in my life. Trey looks me over with his one good eye and laughs. 'Hey, Jordan?' 'What?' The beeping of the heart monitor in the corner's really annoying at this point. There's still much to be done before Thursday. Trey stifles another laugh and raises his hand to me as he cuts out of the room. A moment of silence passes, intensely alone and sterile and itching from the cheap hospital sheets, and then Trey comes back in, pushing a wheelchair. He stops it beside of the bed and gestures. 'Let's get the fuck out of here.' That's the best idea you've ever had, Trey.
