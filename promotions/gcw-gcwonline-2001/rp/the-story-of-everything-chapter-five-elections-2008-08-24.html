The Story Of Everything, Chapter Five: Elections

24 August 2008
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8.24.08 (8.23.08 if you keep heading west) I walked into the Stopplegangers headquarters, obviously on another Jenkem related adventure. It was a sad, drab place, but I guess as far as offices go, it was alright. There were plants and posters and an American flag decorating the room. It smelled like those fancy soaps taste, and in some nondescript way, standing in the room brought about a certain feeling of washing hands; not my own hands, but hands in general. The secretary at the desk paid me little attention. There was something really French about her, French Canadian maybe. She typed loudly, and she smelled like a gathering of berries. I could tell she liked to chew on her pencils; through them even. I looked at her nameplate, which read Marie Carmande. "Excuse me, uh, Marie. I'm here to see Mark David." "Do you have an appointment?" "No," I admitted. "Is that going to be a problem?" "What's your name? I can tell him you're here." "Just tell him Johnny. He'll know who that is." "Alright, sir. Take a seat, and I'll let you know when Mark David is ready to meet with you." I felt like she was giving me the run around, so I remained standing and just wandered around the office as Marie talked in code to the intercom. I knew that the offer to sit was just a diversion tactic. Once you sit down, you run the risk of getting immersed in the gloriously alluring world of sitting. I sat for a good three months in some type of waiting room before. I don't remember what I was waiting for, but there were a lot of magazines about, and from time to time, they'd bring in more magazines, faster than I could keep up. "So, how'd you end up here?" I asked. "I responded to an ad in the classifieds." Marie went back to typing, punching the keys as if she had a personal vendetta against them. "I mean how'd you die?" She ignored me, and I continued pacing around, looking at the posters and reading their inspirational slogans. I was getting bored. This was a waste of a Jenkem trip. Marie sensed my boredom and tried to quell it. My pacing was probably making her nervous. "I have an informational brochure if you'd like to read it." "Sure, why not?" She handed me the pamphlet, which featured liberal use of red, white and blue. The title read "John Adams for 32nd Ward Alderman." Despite my initial assumptions, I looked above the text and examined a picture of this John Adams character. I had half expected to see a man in a powdered wig, but instead, I saw a clean-cut young man who bore too much of a resemblance to me. There was something off about him, like there was nothing behind his eyes or his painted smile. Then I started coughing heavily. "Do you need a glass of water?" asked Marie, doing her best to sound like she was doing her best to sound concerned. "Who is this guy?" I managed before starting to cough again. I threw the brochure back on Marie's desk and turned away from it, trying to regain my breath. "That's John Adams. Mark David and Stopplegangers are backing him for the upcoming election as the 32nd Ward Alderman." "How come he looks like me?" Marie examined the picture and then took a close look at me. "I don't see it. He looks nothing like you." Her phone lit up, and she answered it, divulging little more information than affirmations to whoever was on the other end. "Mark David is ready to see you." I walked into Mark David's office and shut the door behind me. He was sitting behind a desk looking crisply burnt as per usual, but he attempted to divert attention from his hideously charred face with his dapper dress. "Mark David." "Johnny!" he replied, extending his arms in welcome. "You have my money?" I reached into my pocket and unearthed a rubber-banded wad of bills. I tossed it onto the desk and Mark David grabbed it. "What do you need this money for, anyway?" I sat down across the desk from Mark David and pulled out a cigarette. "Do you mind?" He slid an ashtray over to me. "I need it to fund the election." "What election? I thought this was an anti-doppleganger faction." I sparked up my cigarette and puffed on it. "And who the hell is this John Adams guy?" "He's actually your homunculus, and he's running for 32nd Ward Alderman. I'm his campaign manager." "Well, I hope you don't mind, but I'm going to kill your candidate." "Actually, I do mind. I mind very much." Mark David glared at me and started tapping his fingers on his desk. "Besides, how are you going to kill him? If you see him in person, you'll die first." "I'll have you know that I'm an excellent blindfolded swordsman." Mark David laughed a bit. "Well, don't kill him. Just avoid him. I've got a lot riding on this." "How do you know my homunculus, anyhow?" "I tracked him down after I bumped into you at the airport. I figured he'd be the perfect host for my political agenda. Once he's elected Alderman, I'll work him up the political ladder. The problem is, I only have limited contact with him with me being dead and all, so I'll need a go-between." I assumed he was referring to me, but I failed to see how the logistics of the situation would iron themselves out. "How's that going to work without me being able to survive seeing him?" "Johnny, he's a homunculus. All he needs is a loud, booming voice to give him direction. I'll have Marie give you his telephone number on the way out." I mulled it over while dragging from my cigarette. "I'm not sold. I'd still prefer blindfolding myself and slicing him up." "Look," Mark David reasoned. "There will be incentive enough for you. I'll let you have control of as much political direction as you'd like so long as it doesn't interfere with my agenda. It may not seem like much now, but with twenty or thirty years of experience, who knows how much power we'll have?" "Do you really think that a homunculus is going to make it all the way to the Capitol?" Mark David started laughing. "Man, Johnny -- you still got it. You don't know it, but you got it!" "But I don't get it," I responded. "Why couldn't I have just run for office?" "Look at you, Johnny. You're a mess. You can't have a beard in politics." He held up the brochure, and I started coughing uncontrollably again. "Now look at John Adams. He's the American Dream. Look at that strong, hairless jaw and that expensive haircut. That's what it takes, you understand?" I couldn't respond. I was coughing too heavily. Mark David noticed, came to a realization and hid the brochure. "I almost forgot. If you see your doppleganger's picture, you'll start coughing or sneezing, and if you see him on video, you'll probably start to vomit, but you won't die unless you actually see him face to face." "Good to know," I said, managing to catch my breath. "But just to be clear, this whole Stopplegangers thing is just a front for your campaign? I thought dopplegangers were a big problem here." "They are, and I assure you that Stopplegangers is legit. I just figure that it's better to rehabilitate a homunculus than to hunt one down. You should know that one of my key tenets is abolishing capital punishment. After all, a large percentage of them haven't even done anything yet, and for all I know, you could be his homunculus. " "That's an interestingly asinine theory, but you know that I can't guarantee that I won't kill him." "Just," Mark David searched for the right words. "Just don't kill him, alright?" "That all?" I asked, putting out my cigarette and rising from my seat. "Just about," replied Mark David. "Just get the phone number from Marie and call John Adams to get an update on how he's doing. Take some aspirin, too, because you'll get a pretty bad headache when you talk to him. I assume you've probably got something stronger than aspirin, too. If so, use it." "Yeah, I think I can get my hands on something." "I'm also going to have Marie give you an address. Go there. I've got a stockpile of five-gallon jugs that I'm going to need. They might come in handy if you ever need them, too." "What's in them?" "Whatever you do, don't open the jugs until I tell you what they're for." "Yeah," I shrugged. "What's in them?" "Insurance." *** I sat on the curb outside the Stopplegangers headquarters, which was on the corner of Quentin and Palatine. The building looked impressively unremarkable, and there were no signs or markers to designate what it was. From the outside, it looked like it had been long abandoned or perhaps never even completed. I unfolded the paper in my pocket and read the number and address with my phone in the other hand. My hands were bobbing up and down slightly, like counterbalanced weights on a scale. I flipped open my phone and entered in a number: [detail withheld]. It started to ring. "Hello?" "Doctor Wayne, it's Johnny. I need you to pick me up." "I can't right now, Johnny. I'm in the middle of something." "It's important." "It's never important, Johnny," insulted my doctor and attorney, Wayne Gunn. "This time I'm serious." Wayne paused, and I could hear him sigh. "Where the hell are you?" "Quentin and Palatine." "Where the fuck is that?" "I don't know," I admitted. "How do you not know? You had to get there somehow." "I suppose so," I said. It made sense. "Look, can't you just look at a map or something?" "Fine, Johnny, but you owe me big time." "What else is new?" I joked, wearing a grin so loud I think Wayne could hear it. "At least you still got a rich sense of humor," he chided back with defeated sarcasm. "Hey, I'm going to need you to bring a refill on my prescription." "Which one? The white nurse?" "White nurse," I confirmed. We hung up our phones, and I resumed sitting there on the curb, thinking about John Adams and the upcoming election for 32nd Ward Alderman. I was sinking a lot of money into the campaign of a man I planned on assassinating. It would be akin to blindfolding myself and slicing up a big pile of money with a samurai sword at this point. On the other hand, this investment was gambling with my own safety, but if you understood the numbers like I understood the numbers, gambling wasn't always such a bad idea. It all boiled down to the probability of the denizens of the 32nd ward to support a clean-shaven man over another man who was likely to be just as clean-shaven. It was a coin toss, but I checked my pockets, and I had no coins. "Just my luck," I muttered. I waited and hoped that some willing wayfarer would be kind enough to borrow me a coin. After a fruitless few minutes, I heard footsteps approaching. Some gent was approaching, out for a saunter, no doubt, with his companion. Surely he would love to amuse her with an exciting game of chance. I hopped up to my feet from my spot on the curb as they were passing and stood in their path. "Excuse me," the gentleman said, trying to shoulder his way past me while shielding the lady behind him. I stepped to my side so that the couple could not so easily evade me. "No, excuse me," I said, playing my disinterest in aggression. "Could I bother you for a quarter?" "Sorry, buddy. I don't have any change on me, or I'd give it to you," the jerk lied. "Can you move aside? We've got to get through." "Sir, you can have the coin right back," I assured him. "I just need it for thirty seconds. Then you and your lovely lady can be on your way." He grumbled and reached into his pocket, fishing around and eventually procuring a quarter and placing it in my hand. I grabbed it at the edges by the thumb and forefinger and waved it back and forth before their eyes in a display of fine showmanship. "This coin represents the situation I'm in. I'm faced with a risk, which if I pursue, leaves me with a fifty percent chance of an unprecedented power I've never before held and a fifty percent chance of death. If I choose not to flip this coin, there is no risk at all, but neither is there any reward." I swung the coin more dramatically, making sure their eyes were transfixed upon it and the crisis it represented and would ultimately resolve. "There is also a fifty percent chance that this coin will land on one side and a fifty percent chance that it lands on the other. There is no skill involved in guessing which side of the coin will land facing up," I paused, stopped and stood back pushing my arms out to the sides to create a clear area. "I'm going to call it in the air." I flipped the coin up in the air, and it somersaulted and tumbled as it reached its peak. "Heads!" I yelled, slashing my hand in front of my throat to intensify the theatrics of the whole thing. The coin crashed off the ground and rolled, which I found rather symbolic, before gyrating to a stillness. I bent down over the coin and peered at it. "What is it?" the man asked as I peeled the ground off of the sidewalk and put it into his outstretched hands. "It was tails," I said. The both of them stared at me blankly, mired in a haze of confusion and quite possibly, just a hint of disinterest. "Then it's settled," I declared. "I'm going to risk it." "I thought you called the wrong side," said the only slightly bewildered gentleman. "Doesn't that mean you shouldn't go for it." "The way I see it, especially if you play it by the numbers," I explained. "If I got a fifty-fifty chance, I can't lose twice." *** "Can you hold this for a second?" I said, holding out a folded piece of paper to Doctor Wayne Gunn, attorney-at-law. "I need to tie off my arm." Wayne snatched the piece of paper from my hand as he piloted his sports car down the road. He unfolded the piece of paper and read it over quickly. "This is where you want me to drop you off?" "Yeah," I sputtered out, trying to hold the disposable rubber tourniquet tight between my teeth. "This is in the middle of fucking nowhere." "I need to pick up a bunch of five-gallon jugs." "What's in them?" Wayne asked, and I suddenly felt like I was in some kind of strange loop where everything cycles around against itself. I felt like this sequence of events that had been unfolding since I had called Wayne to pick me up had happened in some unordered fashion sometime before. "Hold on," I grunted, pushing down on the plunger to adequately medicate myself. I removed the needle from my arm, capped it and shoved the syringe down back into my pocket. "I have no idea what's in them, but I'm going to find out. Mark David told me not to open them, but I don't trust him." "Isn't that the guy who set himself on fire by the freeway?" "Yep." "Well, if he's dead, does it really matter what he told you?" I failed to see his point but wanted to argue it no further. "Sure, why not?" I sunk down in my seat and watched the clouds turn into shapes and do things. "What's this phone number here?" Wayne asked. "[detail withheld]?" "That's the homunculus form of my doppleganger. His name is John Adams, and he's running for office. I'm supposed to call him." "I'm not even going to ask." "I better call him now, before all this morphine wears off." It was the reasonable thing to do. I opened my phone. "What was that number again?" "[detail withheld]," repeated Wayne. "Hold on, you're going too fast." "[detail withheld]," Wayne said, only slightly slower. "I wasn't ready. Just keep repeating the number until I tell you." "[detail withheld], [detail withheld], 312-" "Alright, I got it." It was ringing. "Hello?" His voice sounded like an agitated bell, ringing horribly, even with the morphine coursing through my veins. But it was tolerable. "Is this John Adams, candidate for 32nd Ward Alderman?" "Yes, who is this?" A thousand different pushpins forced into my skull. "Just call me Johnny Dough. I'm the guy who's secretly funding your whole campaign." "What do you want from me?" A spray of rapid-fire sped centrally and spread out from itself. "I'm just checking in. Mark David wanted me to see how you were doing." "You know Mark David?" An infinite field of war drums or detonators. "Yeah," I furrowed. "Don't you?" "Only through various contacts. I've never actually met him." Pandora's box opened pandemonium like a mouth choking with sound. I didn't know what to make of it. "So you're doing alright in the polls and such?" "I don't think they do any pre-polling on the local level." A storm of electrons around a nucleus amplified by a megaphone being amplified by a megaphone by another megaphone. "So you're saying it's a fifty-fifty shot?" "Technically, no, but theoretically," he trailed off at 781 miles per hour. "I like those chances," I said. "I'll tell Mark David that things are going as expected, and I'll contact you if he has any further instruction." "Well, I appreciate your support." It echoed like a hammer, and I had to hang up the phone. "We're here," Wayne said, and the Pavlovian response triggered in me, though the calamity was beginning to settle in my head. I took a look around, and there was nothing, save for a big, worn-down, sea foam green garage with rusting white doors. "You want to go inside?" I asked, stepping out of my door. "Doesn't seem like much else to do," said Wayne, already almost out of the door himself. He followed me to the door on the side of the garage, and it was unlocked. We stepped through the doorframe into the preserved garage, which seemed like it hadn't been entered in years, kept up only by its dusty guardians. The entirety of the ground was bunched with these five-gallon jugs. There were hundreds of them, and they were all marked with large, stamped text that read "Undead Sauce." "I can't fit all of this shit in my car," Wayne barked. I ignored him, and opened up the lid to one of the nearby containers. "What is it?" Wayne wondered of me. I dipped my fingers in and stuck them in my mouth to examine the taste. "It's hot sauce," I informed. I could feel some good waves vibrating inside me, inciting pleasure. "It's the best hot sauce I've ever tasted. Wayne, you have to try this." Wayne shrugged and stepped over to the container. He dipped his fingers in and licked them clean. "That is pretty good," he admitted, and then he dipped in his entire hand and began to suck the sauce off as it dripped down his forearm. I dropped to my knees and started cupping some more of the hot sauce into my own mouth. Wayne stopped and started laughing maniacally, and I saw the unattended hot sauce still pouring down his wrist. I stood up, sauce caked into my beard and began licking the sauce from Wayne's neglected, sauce-covered arm, but soon I too began laughing uncontrollably. I tried to talk as I laughed, and we were both stumbling backwards around in semi-circles. "How ridiculous!" through laughter. "Two grown men!" through more laughter. "And hot sauce!" riotous laughter! "In a garage with nothing for miles!" Wayne backed against a wall and fell down. He propped himself up against the wall. "It's like I can see everything I can see and everything I can't see instead all at once." I too was having difficulty with my footing, and soon enough, I was lying down on my side, right in the middle of the garage. I could see the intersection of realities. "It's like being alive twice!" I shouted. I also encountered this strange ability, whereas before, when I could control my faculties, I could locate myself at a particular point within the three dimensions of my comprehension. Now, it seemed as if there were plenty more to deal with, and I noted that in any one of those specific dimensions I could navigate before, I could place myself into two positions and interpolate my presence between those two points in each dimension across all three dimensions. "What the hell is going on?" launched Doctor Wayne, who was looking none too medical, unsuccessfully trying to contain laughter and regain movement on the other side of the garage. "Where the hell am I?" He was freaking out. I had to calm him down. "Everywhere, man," I said. "You're everywhere." It didn't work, and then he became unresponsive. "Hey, man," I called out. "You alright?" Then I felt my limbs get numb, and the numbness grew inward. I remember knowing that I was going to be fine, and since the same thing happened to Doctor Wayne, he would be fine, too. I don't remember much else, though, honestly.
