The Kids

Date: Timeless We focus all too often on the wrestlers. Sure, there’s a reason for that. After all, this is wrestling. Men and women step into the ring; bleed, sweat, and cry for their craft. But they do it in front of an audience. An audience that watches them loyally, on television, and live in person. That audience is composed of smarks and marks alike. People who cheer for the good guys, and people who cheer for the bad guys. To the smarks, what is occurring in Global Championship Wrestling is cool. A mostly female stable has come in and practically backslapped the entire federation, taking most of the championships and looking damn good while they do it. Plus, for the ladies, Virgil has a damn fine ass. But there’s another breed of fans who watches on in horror as Shadow makes short work of another victim. Cries as Vivica Valentine defends the US Title. Shudders at the way Dawn McFarlane makes Seymour Almasy subservient to her (and not in the way he’d like, either). The kids. And for them, goddamnit, it’s still real. ----------------------------------------------------------------- “I don’t believe it,” ten year old John said to his best friend in the whole wide world, Steve, as he left Worldwide #44, parents walking a few steps ahead. “How could this happen?” Steve looked equally despondant as he trudged alongside John, the events of the show running through his head. “There’s no way they should be able to beat Seymour and Eva!” John had a reply ready. “It’s so not fair,” he groused, sighing. “They had that Ambrosia guy refereeing! Why would GCW make such an obviously unfair match?” John was, perhaps predictably, clutching his GCW Seymour Almasy action figure. Steve held a Michael Stevens figure. It was a dark time for heroes in GCW, it seemed. One didn’t need to look at the young men to determine that, though. One could see it in the look on Seymour Almasy’s face, as the GCW star was trudging through the parking lot, no doubt heading for his rental car. Steve caught John’s attention with a sharp jab to the ribs. Soon enough, John knew what his friend was looking at. “He looks so sad,” John observed. His parents had looked back, seeing the professional wrestler, and stopped, so as to allow the kids the time to observe their hero. “Of course he’s sad,” Steve replied. “They humiliated him! And he lost to Shadow again!” “She must be his kryptonite,” John said. “I guess that’s what that guy next to us meant by that sign saying “Seymour Fears Boobies”.” That caught Seymour’s attention enough to make him chuckle. It was a small chuckle, but a chuckle nonetheless. “I do not,” he explained, looking down at the boys. “I’ve just been a little off lately.” His boots kicked at the pavement. He was frustrated, but trying not to let it show. Putting on a good face for the kids was more than his job, it was one of the few things that he prided himself on. “Do you need our help,” John offered, looking in all sincerity at the Final Fantasy. “There’s four of them, and only one of you. We can help! We’ve been watching wrestling for forever, and we know the moves!” Steve nodded as well, as the two youngsters grinned. Seymour knew they were serious, that they would go to battle with him. That was the thing he liked about fans. As much as people said they were fickle, they could also be very loyal indeed. “As much as I could use the help,” Seymour explained, “I’ll have to decline. They’re mean people. They wouldn’t hesitate to beat up kids.” “I know they’re mean,” John replied. “But you need help! Why is no one helping you?” That was the $64,000 question, really. Seymour didn’t know. He’d figured that GCW would line up behind him, or at least alongside him, to fight off the threat. Instead, everyone was involved in their own petty bullshit. LeStatt Knight was fighting Bryan Mayhem. The Guild was playing D&D and making baseless accusations at the only guy who actually WAS doing shit. Even Eva VanLorne was treating him with kid gloves. John and Steve were looking at him, asking for an answer. But Seymour Almasy’s cupboard of replies’was bare.
