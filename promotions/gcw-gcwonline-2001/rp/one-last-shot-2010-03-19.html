One Last Shot

19 March 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/19/10 "We are what we think. All that we are arises With our thoughts. With our thoughts, We make our world." - Buddha "Stick with me, kid. Doctor's are here. You are going to be alright." Doctor's? I remember feeling fear like never before as soon as I heard that. What happened to me? Where are my parents? Whatever hindered my sight moves away, the outline becomes full picture. A fireman is kneeling beside me, his face stained from charcoal. I want to talk, I want to scream. But instead I just stare. Even though I feel like I've been perfectly still, I must have moved somehow as the fireman's reacts immediately to me with a smile on his face. "He's alive!" He shouts with joy, and I lean up with my hands on the cement ground. The chaos around me must have caused me not to notice what was happening. But now I remember. My house is burning. Where are my parents? ---- "It is dissociative identity disorder, caused by a major event in his childhood where he lost his parents in a house fire. The memories and feelings go into the subconscious and are experienced later in the form of a separate personality. Dissociative identity disorder frequently co-occurs with other psychiatric diagnoses, such as anxiety disorders (especially post-traumatic stress disorder-PTSD), mood disorders, somatoform disorders, eating disorders, as well as sleep problems and sexual dysfunction. The symptoms of the disorder have a considerable overlap with borderline personality disorder." Jason sat and listened closely to the Doctor. He knew it was to good to be true. He saw Joey Harris training and was floored with his fluid motion in the ring, his ability to stay multiple steps in front of his opponent and the swift ease it took to put on submission holds that savvy veterans can not do. He moved gracefully in the ring, and had deceptive speed. And even more - he was charismatic. His very countenance shows a confidence that can not be taught. He carries himself well, the fans will surely connect with him. He is only 26, about to enter his prime and it was until the drive home that Jason considered the obvious question: Why wasn't he signed yet? "DID is a controversial diagnosis and condition," the Doctor continued, "with much of the literature on DID being generated and published in North America, to the extent that it was regarded as a phenomenon confined to that continent. Even within North American psychiatrists there is a lack of consensus regarding the validity of DID. Practitioners who do accept DID as a valid disorder have produced an extensive literature with some of the more recent papers originating outside North America. Criticism of the diagnosis continues, with Piper and Merskey describing it as a culture-bound and often iatrogenic condition which they believe is in decline. There is considerable controversy over the validity of the multiple personality profile as a diagnosis." Jason faked that he was writing some notes on his pad, but instead he was too desperate to worry about the findings. Jason was a wrestler himself, he was nearing the pinnacle of the business until everything fell apart with one awkward landing from the turnbuckle. Shattered his knee in four seperate places, tore apart his ACL and MCL. "Are you listening?" The Doctor said in an annoyed tone, "you asked me opinion?" "Right," Jason responded, "I need your opinion. Can he compete?" "He may be a great wrestler on the day you saw him. But as historical documents prove, one day he could be Joey Harris and the next day he could be this other personality. It's dangerous." The Doctor responded, shaking his head, "I would not recommend it." Jason dropped his head back in exhaustion. He is just a scout now, someone who finds talent for GCW in a business that is dying at best. He needed to find a break, something that would cause the executives to look closely at his understanding of the business. One superstar away from a big payday. He hasn't seen a paycheck in over two months. "I..." Jason stuttered with the next words, "how dangerous can he really be? Some say this... DID is not even legit." The Doctor stared at Jason, realizing that nothing he said could convince him otherwise. "Jason, you can not seriously be considering putting this man in the ring. With thousands of people within close proximity to this young man is asking for trouble. He may be fine for a day, a week, a month or even a Year. But if he snaps." Jason waited at the edge of his chair for the next comment. The Doctor's pause lasted for what seemed to be an eternity. If what? What will happen? "If he snaps, it is documented that he can be a danger to himself and others," the Doctor continued, "we are talking lives in danger." Jason looked up to the ceiling, and shook his head. This was his luck, as soon as a break was about to come there was something that would bring it down. He thought about his wife, working 50 hour weeks and taking care of their two children while he chases this wrestling dream. He thought of his parents, who told him never to step foot in this business but he went against their wishes. If only he could take it all back, Jason thought. "Tom," Jason said, "I need this man. You know I do." The Doctor, Tom, knew what he was asking. He was asking for his signature that shows that this Joey Harris is capable mentally to work in a high intensity enviroment such as GCW. Thomas would normally never consider breaching medical ethics such as this, but Jason was his little brother. "Please?" Jason sat and stared at Tom who began to pace behind his desk. Jason couldn't believe he just asked for his brother to do this. Now he is putting his own brother's career at risk. Maybe what his parents said was right, maybe Jason is selfish. "Fine. But you listen to me brother," Tom said sternly, "if you see any of these signs. Even a hint towards some of these signs that you must pull him out of the ring. Away from the public. You fire his DID ass if he even shows hints of these." Jason leaned over to look like he was listening, but instead he went back to dreaming up the possibilities. He was soon going to introduce Joey Harris to GCW. Jason may have just gotten the break he was working for. ---- Joey is sitting on his mattress at 7:13 in the morning on Friday, March 19th and looks across his one bedroom apartment. As soon as the contract was finalized and this talent scout, Jason, actually came through with his promises - Joey decided to get his life in order. Scattered across his "less than stellar" apartment, are boxes from Ikea. New furniture he paid with his credit card that he was planning on getting set up before going to sleep. He made it through the "furniture in boxes" half-way until he crashed on his mattress and fell asleep. It was the first peaceful sleep that Joey had in a few months atleast. The thought of finally doing something with his life, and getting a shot at what was his "boyhood dream" was enough to keep his mind settled for an entire night. He had been promised great things before, talent scouts come and go in this business all promising green pastures. Titles, legends, thousands of fans, book contracts, merchandise sales, and the whole nine yards. Then, as fate would have it, his past would creep up and they would run away scared. Joey would end up back in his apartment, with a bottle of Scotch and an evening full of nightmares. The voices are silent now. He feels like the real Joey Harris. Or atleast the one that he enjoys the most. Now he just has to travel by bus to Salt Lake City and get himself training and preparing for what could be the first match that actually means something for his future. A three-way bout against another prospect in T.A Giles and a man who has had disappointing results as of late, Bishop Steele. He pours a glass of milk in his kitchen and takes a sip. Milk. Not alcohol. He had thrown out every trace of alcoholic beverage from his apartment, in order to quiet the demons and get himself in right shape to compete. This was the turning point in his life, finally he would make his Mom and Dad proud. He considered calling his foster parents, the one's who had actually kept him. When he was eleven years old, a sweet "old" couple had taken him in to a farm outside of Seattle, Washington. The man, Preston, was a former professional wrestler and Joey spent hours in the barn in the backyard going through rigorous training with Preston. In hindsight, it would appear that this couple decided to adpot a child for the sake of Preston having a chance at re-living his glory days and seeing a young man follow in his footsteps. Joey remembers the only time his mind being clear was when he stepped foot in the makeshift wrestling ring and took a shit-kicking from a 60-year old man. When Preston felt Joey was ready for tryouts was when Joey once again disappointed the one who seemed to have loved him. He lost control of himself and ended up blowing three seperate opportunities. He had the in-ring ability, and the charisma. He did not have the sanity. Feeling that he had disappointed Preston too many times, Joey left the house and went back to Seattle. He had no money, no education, and presumably no future. Just a set of wrestling skills that he was not confident he would ever use again. This time in GCW, he will not disappoint. Joey was going to hit the gym this morning, and start getting his cardiovascular system ready for what will be a challenge. A three-way match. A brutal way to start anywhere. He kenw he would have to have "eyes on the back of his head", that at any point the second opponent could blindside him. He would use the corners and ropes to his advantage, and keep both Giles and Steele in front of him at all times. He would stay low to the ground to not leave his head exposed to often, he can't take too many shots to the head. He knows that Giles is a high-flyer, high-intensity wrestler who will try to impress the masses with his speed. If he can slow Giles down somehow, take him to the mat and force him to try to out-manuever Harris' arsenal, than he would surely succeed. Joey was fairly certain he could take out Giles. It was Steele he was worried about. Would he be sore from the last show? Would the devastating loss sap his energy and intensity? How would he react about being booked against two green rookies? Would he take the opportunity to pummel us and show he can still dominate? Or would he sulk back and give up? Surely if Harris would end up going blow-for-blow with Steele he would not last long. This is it, Joey thought. One last chance to make something out of nothing. One last opportunity to feel as though his Mom and Dad are smiling from above. Just stay in control.
