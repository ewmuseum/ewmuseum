I am human garbage, and I know this. The biggest piece of shit in the industry. Over-rated. Unwanted. Pretty much every sentiment you could imagine in the negative spectrum, mostly unwarranted. Have I done anything to deserve that sort of treatment? The way I see it, no. No fucking way. I've been told that more times than I can count. It's as familiar to me as lacing these wrestling boots and taping my hands. I'm well aware of how hated I am between these walls. I know that not a single one of you would piss on me if I was on fire. That's fine. I accept that mantle with pride. Must be doing something right to get to you this way. So, go for it. Call me a bastard. Funny, really. It's true. I was an illegitimate child- a son that my father never really wanted. He just wanted to get his dick sucked by some Amsterdam whore. I guess life's a little fucked up like that. You never really get what you want, and you certainly never get what you truly deserve. Take me for example. Is this what I deserve? Another tag match against my favorite people for a little tune up. Oh, horray? Bryan Mayhem and Shaman, part eleven. It never gets old. I joined The Establishment, for what reason? For more invisible man clusterfucks? That's why I attacked Jason Dread. That's why I asked for this match at NC-17 via my actions. He's a fuckin' legend around here. Common sense says that I'll be something of note if I flatten him. No, fuck that. No if. When. I don't deal in possibilities... it's all about the REALITY. I am better. Shit, I'm better than half the goddamn roster. Not that you fucking morons out there realize that. Maybe I need to pull a Bagwell, and start beating the hell outta random people until you get the point. Nah, it's been done. I'm not another Bagwell. I'm not another Jason Dread. I'm Brad Jackson. The Machine. My legacy was forged in places like PWX among the hardcore elite, in places like PCW where I almost lost my life- and again in HIWF, where I thought I'd finally found my place, only to be stabbed in the back so hard I thought I would never recover. In places like that, I was an untouchable god. I was the fuckin' king. World Champion. Feared and revered for what I could do in the ring. And here? People forget my name. People don't even flinch when I come at them. The paradigm shift is killing me. The inevitable question remains, though: have I done anything remarkable here? How do I quantify the things I've pulled off in the past without sounding like another random ego- without sounding way too much like another Andy Murray clone? Easy. I don't. Instead I flash a smile, and shake my head. The past is passed. Whatever. Here? Fuck, I've done nothing but endure one joke after another. One massive failure after another. Scrapes with the likes of Thom LeStrange and James von Doom, only to have them vanish without a trace. I feel like a black hole, like tangling with me drops you into the abyss of obscurity. Fuck. Now why can't that happen to Shaman, and his buddies? Ah, right. Because the universe is cosmically aligned against my wishes. I forgot. My bad. ---- Saturday, January 10, 2009 Oceanside, CA Jackson lifted the glass of warm Scotch to his lips. It burned down his throat like paint thinner, making his cheeks pucker and his eyes squint. "Good shit," he muttered with a shake of his head, and then took another torturous swig. It filled his body with artificial warmth, and for a while made him forget the ache in his muscles. Training too hard, sleeping too little. Old habits died hard after all, despite the holidays. The Rust Bucket. Old school nostalgia. Like lemmings playing follow the leader, all eyes at the bar were riveted on the television mounted over the colored bottles glistening in front of the nicotine-stained mirror. Jackson sat in the midst of the early evening crew, cloaked in shadows at the end of the bar. Sipping on the fine Scotch on ice, he was managing to blend with the motley crew of blue collar men and women who'd stopped off to wet their whistles after work. Few stumbled over the threshold, pushed by the frenzied wind that swept in off the water in this lower part of town. He'd always felt at home here, even going so far as to take a job as a bouncer here back in 1998 after the stint in Tampa hadn't worked out. That job had lasted three weeks. Tampa, there was a memory. SFCW- shitty little independent promotion, working matches in what was nothing more than a run-down high school gym. Escape from that was more like liberation. Anything was better. He'd figured one palm tree-lined coast was as good as another. Just a little nostalgia to ring in the new year. Why the hell not? The bar was glossy wood, reflecting back the muted amber glow of the lights overhead, holding up a half dozen sets of elbows of men who've worked too hard for every dollar they made. With his heavily lined face, and those world weary eyes, Jackson fit in perfectly. From the kitchen, the enticing aromas of grilled onions and burgers seeped through the swinging door, making his stomach rumble. The sizzle of the fat fryer filled the silence between the slurps and thumps that surrounded him. Nobody looked up as he reached over the bar. Nobody gave a shit. The jukebox played in the corner, hollow and lonesome. Johnny Cash, singing a song that Trent Reznor had penned, never once dreaming the power that dark little ditty would have. His knee bounced against the worn wood, boot heel hooked against the rung of the high stool. With a crooked smile, he plucked one of the sticky menus from the low ledge, looking it over. The woman came up, cold and silent, and they made brief eye contact. He pointed at something, and she nodded. Telepathy. "Another?" She reached for the empty glass. Jackson nodded, pretending not to notice her eyes on his body, measuring him beneath the leather jacket, and the plain black t-shirt. She topped up the glass over fresh ice, and set it down atop a fresh coaster, flashing him a sly smile. There was something predatory in her eyes. "Not interested," he mumbled, shaking his head. His voice was so low she didn't hear him over the din. He nodded, tilting the glass in a mock salute. Deep down he felt not only some strange stirring of unease, but anger as well. He lifted the glass slowly to his lips as if to take a sip, instead taking a deep breath. The exhale became a sigh. The cup made its way to his lips again. Better. He reached inside his cold leather jacket, pulling out the mangled pack of Camels, setting it on the counter. Without a word, she pushed a lone ashtray in his direction. He lit it with the no-nonsense flair of a lifetime smoker, blowing the smoke towards the nicotine stained ceiling. The waitress swabbed the bar, her eyes drifting to the television screen, and a reporter standing on the steps of some building. Talking head with a microphone. "Fuck." He muttered, feeling an instinctive need to retreat further into the corner. The waitress turned her head towards him, a sympathetic smile on her lips. "You're not from around here, are you?" Jackson grunted, "accent give me away?" She dipped her head, giving him that look that all bartenders knew. Maybe there was a school for that somewhere. "No. I've seen your face before, somewhere." "Here it comes," he thought, maintaining that tight-lipped smile that was almost a grimace. "Maybe I've just got one of those faces," he offered. "A face like yours?" She giggled, "I'd remember?" she snapped her fingers, "holy crow, I've seen you on TV? That wrestler?" "Wrestler?" A greasy blue collar worker nudged his companion further down the bar. "Heh, you hear that?" "Who the fuck watches that shit these days? Buncha sweaty fags prancing around in eyeliner and tights. Fuck naw, I don't watch that shit." The first man looked sidelong at Jackson, at the scars on his knuckles, visible as he gripped the half-full glass of scotch, clearly reconsidering his position on the whole wrestling thing. He grunted and turned back to the TV. Jackson cringed inwardly, avoiding looking, despite the feel of the eyes on him. Instead he turned, looking out into the bar. A siren with dark hair hanging down her back chalked up a cue at the lone pool table, pursing a set of perfect lips to blow off the excess dust. She leaned over the table, lining up her shot. A hint of perfect cleavage showed beneath the scoop neck baby doll shirt she wore, and her jeans rode so low that a hint of skin and the tease of a tattoo peeked above her waistband. She missed the shot, tossing her head back as she laughed. Such a fucking shark. She was well into a game of cat and mouse with man playing against her. She leaned against the table as he lined up his shot, those captivating sea-green eyes lifting to fasten on his across the room. The charge was electric, passing between them, and as she stretched against the table, lifting one leg to make a perfect bank shot, the jeans dipped lower. Blue and pink swirls rose up, cradling her tailbone, widening visibly before dipping suggestively below the denim. Some men would have called it a tramp stamp, but never on her. Somehow, she was above a label that low class. It gave men a hard-on just trying to think about what naughty splay of colors might be bordering that perfect ass. Jackson took the last drink of the aged Glenlivet and slid it to the edge of the bar as he heard the men speculate. "Is that a butterfly on her ass?" No. It wasn't. The butterfly was right below the curve of her hip, midway between her belly button, and her- "Nah, man. It's one of those tribal things. Celtic or something." Wrong again. The creeps shared some raucous laughter but none of them had the first clue what lay beneath the clothes that she wore. Sometimes the swirls looked like waves, crashing on some unseen shore at sunset. Sometimes they looked like what the wind must be, elemental and strangely beautiful. Another drink appeared, and he took a long pull of that, sucking in the ice cubes and cracking them between his teeth. He smoked the Camel to nothing more than a smear of ash, nearly jumping out of his skin as the waitress spoke behind him. "Why the long face, sugar?" Jackson grunted, casting a sidelong glance at the Wurlitzer as though expecting it to conjure the song that would answer the question so he wouldn't have to. It didn't, instead switching to Elton John, singing about Daniel leaving on a plane. "Blues," he grunted, fixing those dark eyes on her and stealing the breath from her lungs. He had the most intense stare she'd ever seen, like he could look right through her, and see everything. "Don't everyone got the blues these days? Holidays've come an' gone. They'll do that to ya." The food appeared in the pass-thru with a muted ding, and she fetched it, setting the grease laden plate in front of him. Silver dollar fries, and a toasted Reuben, the corned beef thick and fatty around the edges, the bread soggy with sauerkraut. "Mustard," he said, keeping the single word conversation rolling as he lifted the edge of the rye. "Dijon or yella?" She asked, her hand hovering above the bottles. "Surprise me." She handed him the bottle of yellow, watching while he tapped it against his palm before flipping open the top. He squeezed it out, forming a figure eight on the melted Swiss cheese. Infinity. "You're the strong silent type, aintcha?" She leaned in close, cracking her gum. Beneath the makeup, he could see she may have been passably decent looking, once upon a time. "Some." He shrugged, taking a bite of the massive sandwich. It was decent, but he didn't really taste it. It slid down his throat, could have been sawdust for all it was worth. He didn't elaborate more, singular focus on the food. She walked towards him, pushing through the mass of sexually depraved laborers, fighting off their wolf whistles and hungry leers with a bitchy sneer, and a toss of that mess of raven hair. With a smile on her lips, she stopped behind him, smelling the scent of his cologne, mixed with smoke and booze. Raw and masculine. "Get anything for me?" She purred, slipping past him to steal one of the round fried potatoes. She popped it in her mouth, and chewed slowly, her eyes fastened on him. "Nope." He shrugged, taking another bite of the sandwich. "Back off, get your own sandwich." She set down her drink, laughing softly to herself as she turned her back to him. He growled low under his breath, shaking his head as his gaze slid to watch her. She leaned against the bar, snapping her fingers to get the attention of the barmaid. "Hey! HEY! Chickie-poo! Bring me something fattening," she said with a grin, "something so fucking sickening that it'll take my arteries weeks to clear the blockage." Half of them were thinking they'd maybe want to watch that. The others were wondering just what in the fuck that gray haired guy at the end of the bar had that they didn't. Kitty laughed, leaning against Jackson, clinging to his arm. "Fuck," he groaned, his voice low enough that only she could hear, "I wish you wouldn't do that. Trying to maintain a low profile." She lifted her head slowly, meeting his dagger glare with a smile stretching across her lips. It was faster than the drawing of a gun, one of those moments that are over so quick that they never actually happened; but if you're lucky enough to survive them, you carry the scars for life. Fuck, he thought to himself, just when I thought I was finally over it, she does this shit all over again. A flash of those eyes, slipping past his guard, a moment of pure vulnerability. "What's your angle, Kitty?" Her hand dropped to his lap, and she stroked the middle seam of his jeans, smirking at what she felt there, rising to the occasion. He sighed as the waitress leaned in to whisk away his empty plate, giving him a perfect view of her ample bust and the ruby red smile on her lips. But he ignored her, took his drink and slung it back. He didn't even notice her walk off. This bothered him, and thoughts that were best kept to himself were starting to float to the top in a sea of whiskey. "Fuck," he growled, shaking his head as he shifted in his seat, trying to squirm away from the things she was doing with that concealed hand. She gave him another lingering squeeze through the denim. The shots had gone down too easy. He was well on his way to getting wasted. Not good. "Last year, NC-17? was a fuckin' joke. Plastic title belt, fuckin' humiliation. Showing up on that wannabe rebel's doorstep, and begging for another chance. Love, what a trip. She dipped her head, remembering it. "Yeah. I know." It was nice that they understood each other. Maybe couldn't stand each other for more than a few hours at a time, but it was still nice that she got where he was coming from. "I want to win this. I need to. I fuckin' need to." "Little Engine That Could," she immediately corrected, drawing a sour look from him. "What-the-fuck-ever," he growled, "like it matters? It's not that easy, babe. Shit, I'll get on the phone and give Survivor a call, get them to write you something inspirational." "Bite me," He glowered at her, and she didn't even flinch. She smiled, laying a hand on his arm. Guys like Trashcan Man Johnny get the accolades and I get the leftover shit. I've been here before. I DO NOT WANT TO REPEAT THIS. I cannot, and I will not allow doubts to rip apart my world again. What are you going to do to me this time? I walk in shadows. I crave the dark. Nothing gets to me in this moment as I knocked on the closed bathroom door. I could smell the fruity steam of her bubble bath. I knocked on the door, told her I was going to go to the bar around the corner for a drink. I lied. Instead I picked up my bag, and I hit the door running. Dusty trail behind me, and I never looked back. Phoenix here I come. Alone, and without any baggage. It's a new year. About time I realize that and move on.
