Orchestra of Wolves, Part 2: Goliaths Disarm Their Davids

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Today ?You are the embodiment of the information you choose to accept and act upon. To change your circumstances you need to change your thinking and subsequent actions.? - Adlin Sinclair. ?Sure ye don't wan' a hand? Looks like ye've gotta lot 'a shite that needs organisin',? quizzed a gruff and unmistakably Scottish voice from beneath a bread that would make Alan Moore green with envy. Dressed in a white t-shirt adorned with the logo of an obscure Canadian metal band across the chest and a pair of loose-fitting black sweatpants, the eldest Murray brother ran one of his calloused hands through the unkempt dirty brown mass of hair that hung from his scalp like the branches of a weeping willow. Andy arched his back and dropped a hefty box of documents to the floorboards with a sigh of relief. Standing upright, he shook his tense arms loose and took a deep breath, mentally cursing his decision not to hire a crew of deliverymen. ?Come on, mate. Don't try to tell me you came all the way across town to help me organise a thousand sheets of paper into neat little files,? he said, flashing his elder brother a trademark Andy Murray smirk. ?You just want to toss baby brother around that brand spanking new ring for a little while, don't 'cha?? Holding his hands out to his side, chuckling, Sid immediately abandoned all pretence. ?What can I say? He's a fun fucker t' beat up.? ?Why don't you come say that to my face, Sasquatch?? The playfully cocky tones of the runt of the litter echoed across the furnished expanse of Andy Murray's gym. Turning their heads simultaneously, Andy and Sid watched as Cayle pushed his weight forward against the top rope of the wrestling ring. Clad in a black vest and a pair of jeans, the shaven-headed twenty six year old pointed at Sidney and flashed him with a smug leer he'd no doubt picked up from Andy. ?Why ye so eager t' be put on yer ass like a little bitch again, eh?? Sidney looked towards Andy and shrugged before making his way across the varnished floorboards. As he hopped up onto the apron Cayle retreated by a couple of steps, reflecting images of Rich Rollins, Twizzy, The Jhub and other industry greats in his inner cortex as he prepared for a routine sparring session in the middle of a ring Andy had only gotten round to assembling a few days prior. Stepping into the ring, Sid was quick to stomp both feet down hard on the apron. A dull thud accompanied each impact; the ring was solid and the boards beneath the canvas had little bounce to them. He made a mental note involving slackening the springs below as he leaned back against the plain blue ropes. Watching from the other end of the room as his brothers exchanged harmless trash talk with one another before locking up in the middle of the ring, Andy scanned his eyes across the facility he'd almost single-handedly put together. On the 20th of July 2008, Andy Murray defeated Chris Bagwell in what was the first truly massive victory of his young Global Championship Wrestling career. Days later he purchased an old, dusty building just a couple of blocks south of downtown Tallahassee and set about finally putting something back into the sport that had provided him with a lifestyle usually reserved for the Brad Pitts and Justin Timberlakes of the world. Work on the gym was slow and it's development was often rough and turgid. His newfound ?golden boy? status in GCW meant that his free time was at a premium and the little time he spent not on the road he dedicated to his bride to be and rebuilding his relationship with a son he'd almost completely alienated after the very public disintegration of his ex-wife's sanity. Either way, he managed to sneak in a couple of days here and there and piece-by-piece the place began to take shape. Soon enough he'd be able to swing open the doors and try to help mould the careers of those less experienced in the game than he, but there was still work to be done. A building that was once little more than an empty space of dirt and dust had slowly, steadily, been transformed to a perfectly acceptable gym with a clean, crisp sense of design. The kind of brilliant sunshine that you only get in Florida poured in through the massive windows on either side of the room amplifying the brightness and reflecting rays of light against the pure white walls and polished surface of the varnished floor. Sets of weights and various pieces of exercise equipment were dotted around the room and six blue mats pilfered from the ringside area of a GCW show sat on the floor at the far end. The ring in which his two siblings were engaged in friendly competition was, of course, the centrepiece. Black trim ran around the grey apron and the blue ropes were strung tightly to black ring posts and turnbuckles. It might have taken him more than two days to set the thing up and cost him the best part of two grand to get it there in the first place, but if if upped the game of just one would-be pro-wrestler then it would have proved its worth tenfold. Refusing to pick up the excessively large cardboard box again for fear of straining himself, Andy, with great effort, used his foot to push the container against the door of the room he'd designated his ?office?. Immediately the door swung open, bouncing back against the doorstep and harmlessly hitting Andy's shoulder as he shuffled into the room with the box at his feet. The office was a small, simple room housing a desk, two chairs, a filing cabinet and a cornucopia of unsorted documents and personal effects. The solitary window looked out across a small park popular with local dog-walkers, providing a pleasant retreat away from the blood, sweat and tears that would surely be expended in the main hall soon enough. Wearing a GCW tee and some khakis, Andy grimaced at the thought of the hours of coma-inducing paper-pushing that awaited him. There must've been ten ? maybe twelve ? boxes of the stuff and he thought that he would rather go out for a romantic meal with Chris Bagwell and Thom LeStrange than sift through it all. Andy's mind began to wander, as if he were subconsciously going out of his way to avoid doing the work. He cast his gaze across the room and finally decided to start with the one box that he might actually enjoy raking through. Walking across the room towards a box that was crudely marked ?some old bullshit,? Andy picked wrapped his arms around the stiff, brown cardboard and heaved it up onto the desk. Ignoring the sharp, prickly burning in his arms he unceremoniously tore the binding tape away. The tape ? which had taken half of the flap with it ? flew threw the air as Andy tossed it over his shoulder and began to rummage around. Passing by a couple of framed pictures ? one of his deceased parents and one of his son Marvin and fianc?e Kayla ? and resting them on the desk, Andy's hand eventually came to rest on a cool, metallic surface. Looking down, he fumbled beneath oodles of sentimental newspaper trimmings until he found something he could grip. Then, with all the gentleness and grace of a baby elephant, he pulled the bronze and leather strap from the box. It had been quite a while since he'd last held the Online Championship Wrestling World Heavyweight title belt in his hands and he'd forgotten just how heavy the damn thing was. Blemished with scratches and fingerprints and with the leather beginning to crumble through wear and tear, his first ever World Championship wasn't exactly in prime condition but Andy could not have cared less. He'd captured the belt on the very same night that the company announced it was to cease business, and to the present day it was the only World Championship he'd captured in a career that had spanned more than ten years. It would make the perfect centrepiece. Taking the belt towards the wall opposite his desk, Andy located a picture hook and used it to suspend the title. It swung precariously, it's weight threatening to tear the hook from the wall with even the most subtle of movements, so Andy pushed the palm of his hand against it to make it stop. Not an ideal method of attachment, he thought, but one that would suffice for now. Stepping back, he watched his own image reflected against the blemished gold surface. It was enough to make him almost bleary-eyed with nostalgia. Those were simpler times; easier times. Sure, OCW was never going to be anywhere near GCW when it came to size and influence and his paycheck wasn't quite as fat back then, but at least he didn't have to deal with the demented presence of LeStatt Knight threatening to rip and tear at his psyche until there was nought left but rust and bones. So absorbed was he in fantastical thoughts of the past that he didn't even notice the heavy thump of approaching footsteps. Slow and uneven, punctuated by the soft clink of rubber on wood, they came to a halt soon. ?Heh. Eleven years and that's still all you've got to show for it.? Andy jumped; startled, he missed a breath. ?Jesus fu-?... he stammered as he regained his composure. Even after eight years, though, the shrill South London accent was as familiar as ever. ?You scared the hell out of me.? Regaining a steady breathing pattern, Andy saw the battered and bruised wreck of a man struggling for comfort on a single crutch. His skin tough, leathery and blotched with sporadic liver spots and his thin, white hairline biding a hasty southward retreat, the fifty-something bore all the scars of a long and hard existence. A White shirt hung over his bulky frame and his arms were a patchwork of fading tattoos on saggy parts of his tanned skin where muscles once bulged. ?You look like shit,? Andy blurted unintentionally. That was how Andy greeted him. No ?how are you??, no ?how's it going??, ?how's the family??. None of that. Just, you look like shit. Charming. ---- Buzzing around the outside of the ring like a hummingbird, Walter Rhodes was in his element. Gliding around the ring on his tiptoes and slapping his palms down on the rough canvas of the apron, he barked orders at the two apprentices that were duelling inside the squared circle. ?Drop your hands, Andy. That's it! Now watch your elbows... lift, pull back...? The lanky, black-haired grappler had his smaller compadre locked in a double underhook. With an almighty heath he pulled back, pulling his opponent's feet from the ground and gliding him over head with some kind of suplex variation. He hit the mat like a sack of potatoes, Andy's opponent; a lot of people will try to tell you that professional wrestling is ?fake,? but there's no way you can have your back driven into a sprung mat of tough, unforgiving canvas by a near 270lbs man and not have it hurt like hell. Walter watched as the twenty-three year old Murray turned on his heel. His opponent was winded; breathing heavily, trying to shake the cobwebs from his head and yet still down. ?Good! Great! Now put an end to it,? said Rhodes as he clenched a tight fist and slammed it down into the apron. ?You offer a guy this amount of time in an actual match and he's gonna be up again in a couple of seconds.? Acknowledging his trainer with a subtle nod, Andy took one giant stride towards his blonde opponent and contemplated his next move. Walter tightened his fist, whitening the knuckles. The kid was special. Standing somewhere between six and seven feet and with a build loved by any of the country's top-tier wrestling promotions, he had the perfect combination of looks, charisma, and raw, natural talent. Never before had he worked with a student that was so quick to learn ? even Andy's elder brother, far superior in the technical aspect of wrestling to his sibling, hadn't caught on this fast. He was going places; Walter knew it, the other students knew it. In fact, Walter often wondered if Andy Murray himself was the only one blissfully unaware of the potentially glorious career that awaited him on the North American circuit. His character ? his on-air persona ? may have been as bold and brash as they come, but behind the scenes you couldn't come across a more modest human being. No matter how many times he'd tried to tell him, Walter just couldn't get it into the Scotsman's skull that he was a man with the world at his feet and that he could be well on the way to international megastardom if he managed to remain free of injury. It was both his greatest blessing and a potentially catastrophic curse. His unassuming nature and generally pleasant manner made him an immediately likeable character to have in the locker-room, but Walter couldn't help but wonder if he'd ever even begin to come close to his mammoth potential if his head wasn't in the right place. Making friends backstage is nice, sure, but it doesn't put food on the table, and it would have killed Rhodes to have to watch the young Scot toil on the toilet independent scene purely because he lacked the competitive edge needed to get ahead in the business. In the ring, Andy had the teetering cruiserweight back on his feet. A quick whisper in the ear confirmed that the blonde kid was doing alright (he didn't want to injure him, after all), so he threw his head underneath his armpit, grabbed a handful of the kid's tights and drove his head into the canvas with a crisp DDT. Laying there on the mat, the blonde rolled onto his back and his eyes flickered like an old lightbulb. ?Alright, good! Now, hit him with an elbow drop!? barked Walter from ringside. Andy wasn't so keen to respond; his opponent was lying on the mat, perhaps mildly concussed. What was the use in inflicting further punishment on the poor fellow. ?Well go on, don't just stand there!? The sweating Scot turned his head to the side, vibrant blue eyes meeting with Walter's cold, olive orbs. Rhodes could read the doubt on Andy's face. This wasn't the first time he'd tried to drive Murray towards a somewhat excessive beatdown and he felt remorse for the poor kid he was inflicting the damage upon, but if Andy Murray was ever to become a true wrestling superstar he needed to learn to push himself above and beyond the limit. ?Come on!? roared Walter, his face flush with crimson passion. ?FINISH IT!? Andy looked once at Walter and then once at the stirring wrestler that lay below his towering frame. For a moment he genuinely contemplated Walter's suggestion, but he was caught in two minds. Trusting his gut over his head, Andy, without warning, stepped over the kid's body and pulled his large body between the top two ropes. He hopped down from the ring, boots slapping down on the gym's wooden floor as he grabbed a towel from the turnbuckle and set off towards the locker-room. ?Andy, get the fuck back here!? Walter Rhodes shouted across the room, distracting a couple of students who were hard at work on the rowing machines. Without as much as a glance Andy disappeared into the locker-rooms with his head hung low. ?Oh for fuck's sake,? the thoroughly irritated trainer seethed as he hobbled towards the door on his one good leg. ---- ?Yeah, and it sure is a pleasant ray of sunshine to see you too,? came the response. The battle-damaged Englishman smiled a wry grin of broken yellow teeth as Andy approached and embraced a man who'd been instrumental in the development of his formative years as a professional wrestler. ?It's good to see you, mate? Andy said, smiling. ?I, uhh. I didn't think you'd come.? ?Hell, was a while even I didn't think I'd come. Know we didn't exactly part on amicable terms.? Hobbling his beaten-down form across the room, he slowly lowered himself onto Andy's plush leather office chair without asking. ?Was just passing through the state anyway, an' I couldn't concoct a good enough reason to stay away.? The craggy old man suddenly leaned forward, allowing a hoarse, guttural cough to burst from his throat like the snarl of a wolf. Andy paused, glancing at the sickly corpse-in-waiting sat before him. Even he was finding it hard to believe that this crutch-reliant old-timer was once known as the toughest gymhall drill sergeant in the southern states. ?Got something to drink? My throat's as rough as a badger's arse,? Walter Rhodes croaked as he carefully set his crutch against the desk. The former wrestler looked up at The Scottish King of Cool who was still quite shocked that Rhodes had made the journey to Tallahassee in the first place. ?Ohhh, uhhh. No,? Andy began, regaining focus. ?Sorry. Still trying to get the place set up all nice. Besides, you know I don't drink.? ?Fuck, of course you don't. It's almost been a decade, though. Memory ain't what it used to be.? Shifting a few discarded sheets of paper from the other chair, Andy wiped some dust from it's leather surface and perched his 275lbs frame on the edge. ?You don't exactly look like a picture of physical health either.? ?Guess that's just what happens when you hit the grindstone too hard,? Walter rued with a shake of his head. His body was a terrible advertisement for his chosen career; he'd never been the most mobile guy in the world, but Andy had never quite seen him in such a state. ?The leg got worse. Got to the point where I could barely walk on my own accord anymore, and of course, me being me, I didn't call the doc,? he paused again, swallowing. ?The human body don't like gathering dust. You leave it sitting around motionless for too long and eventually it stops working.? If Andy Murray were a more pessimistic individual he might have sought parallels between Walter's present condition and his own future, given Andy's far-reaching injury history. ?You taking anything for the pain?? Andy asked. ?Kidding me? I wouldn't be able to roll outta bed it if weren't for the pills.? Walter's eyes diverted from Andy's for a moment and he looked around the room and then through to the main hall where the other Murray siblings were still training. ?So, you into the trainin' business now?? he asked, diverting the topic of conversation away from something he didn't want to dwell on. Andy smiled. ?Looks like it.? ?Funny. Thought that was the kinda thing a guy did when he was thinking of winding things down a little.? ?Heh, there's not much danger of me slowing down any time soon. Besides, Sid's gonna help out when I'm not around. We'll do fine.? Once again Walter looked around the building as if he were intentionally avoiding eye contact with Andy. ?It's a tough game, you know. Training kids. You start off thinking you're putting something back into the game, but then the students start getting cocky; they grow too big for their boots, especially in this game. You tell yourself you're doing them a favour but nobody thanks you for it. Nobody ever does.? Suddenly he shot his eyes back at Andy accusingly, releasing a thinly-veiled barb that had been brewing in him for eight years. It made Andy feel uncomfortable and he visibly twitched in his chair. ?I'll figure that out by myself,? he said, trying to any potential conflict. ?You still in the game?? Walter threw Andy the kind of look you'd give to a three-headed man. ?Are you going blind or something, Andy? I can hardly fucking walk, let alone keep up with a bunch of twenty year olds. No, age has taken this old dog from his pen I'm afraid.? Solemnly accepting the obvious answer with a nod of the head, Andy did his best to deflect the harsh tones of his former trainer. During the three years he'd spent under Walter Rhodes' tutelage he'd learned that the man had some of the thickest skin in the business, but he was having a hard time trying to decide whether the old man's vitriol was genuine or a facetious. With the way things fell apart between the two it would be perfectly logical to assume that some resent could still have been harboured, but the fact that Rhodes had even came to the gym in the first place suggested that his bitterness hadn't consumed him entirely. ?You're doing alright for yourself though, aren't you?? Rhodes began again. ?Seems like I can't open a magazine or log on to the internet without some journalist putting you over. Keep your nerve and you might even have another one of those to wrap 'round your waist in the near future,? he said, jabbing his thumb towards the OCW belt. ?That's what they tell me. I suppose the trouble lies in the guy who currently calls the belt his own.? In his head he tried to figure out a way to deflect the conversation away from him. He was far from the timid twenty-three year old greenhorn that Walter Rhodes had known during the formative years of his career but being sharing air with one of his most influential figures seemed to bring him back eight years. ?I want to apologise for what happened, back then. The way we parted. Believe me when I say that it wasn't what I'd intended.? ?Hmph. You want to apologise now? What stopped you from picking up the phone eight years ago, Andy?? Walter paused, waving away the growing concern on Andy's face. ?Relax. I'm fucking with you. That was a long time ago, better off just forgetting about the whole thing. Besides, I wouldn't have come here if I was petty enough to hold onto an eight year grudge.? The response brought Andy some warmth and he managed to crack half a smile. ?Either way, the least you deserve is a 'sorry'.? ?Fuck it. S'just one of those things that happens along the road. Besides, maybe everything worked out for the best; you've done well enough for yourself without having to put up with me shouting and bawling in your ear every five minutes...? Walter watched Andy clasp his hands together. It was a strange sight, seeing one of the most extrovert personalities that Global Championship Wrestling had ever seen sitting before him devoid of all braggadocio and bravado. Andy felt humbled in the presence of a man he'd once called his ?mentor?. He'd almost forgot what it felt like. He still held a great deal of respect for Walter Rhodes, even if the Englishman refused to acknowledge it. ?So, what am I doing here, Andy?? Walter quizzed. ?Huh?? Andy looked up, startled. ?In the email you said you had something you wanted to talk about, and I know for a fact that you didn't drag me all the way down here just to offer an apology.? Nervously, Andy shifted his weight across the chair and tapped his fingers against it's plastic armrest. There was an elephant in the room and he could deny it's existence no longer. Sitting himself upright, he took a deep breath and parted his lips to speak. ---- ?Andy! What the FUCK are you doing!?? Walter Rhodes stormed into the modest breezeblock locker-room with a mouthful of piss and vinegar and a stomach of blood and thunder. Slamming the hefty wooden door behind him, he struggled to keep his cool as Andy sat down on a bench and buried his perspiring head in a white towel. ?When I tell you to do something, you fucking do it, okay?!? Walter leaned forward, waggling a furious finger in Andy Murray's face. ?Scared you were gonna hurt the guy, you fucking pussy?? The fact that Andy was seemingly paying him no attention whatsoever only aggravated Walter's temper as the wild-eyed Englishman spat a rant that had been brewing for quite some time. ?You're never going to get anywhere in this business unless you get passed this 'moral code' bullshit you've got drilled into you. How many bloody times do I have to repeat myself?!? Still Andy Murray would pay him no attention, instead deciding that mopping the sweat from his brow was higher on his list of priorities. ?What, you can't even look at me now?? Nothing, still. Walter let the words hang in the air for a couple of seconds. ?Fucking look me in the eye when I'm talking to you!? he screamed, grabbing Andy by the head and leaving him with no choice but to look. Furiously, Andy swiped Walter's hands away, his 6'7? towering over Rhodes' 6'1? with menace. He scowled, Andy, but soon turned away, shaking his head, suppressing the almost overwhelming urge to slap Walter Rhodes across the face. ?Sometimes I wonder if you even want to get ahead in this game. You're never going to make it out of the indies if you can't even put one of your training buddies to sleep, for fuck's sake.? ?'Put him to sleep'?? Andy growled, eyes lowered to the floor. ?This isn't Primetime Championship Wrestling you maniac. I thought I was training to be a wrestler, not a beast.? ?There's more to wrestling than cutting a bunch of flashy promos and being super-nice to everyone, Andy. Do you have any idea how many men have made it to the top of the ladder without having to leave some of their morality behind along the way? You can count 'em on one hand,? he said, drawing closer to Andy. Normally he wouldn't get so worked up over an individual pupil but it was because he saw so much in Andy Murray that he found himself in this situation. Walter knew that in a business as cut-throat as pro-wrestling it was impossible for a man to get ahead as a complete goody two-shoes. Andy Murray was too timid, too nice. Having him beat on one of his fellow students was an excessive way of doing it, but Walter had to draw that ruthlessness from him somehow. ?Professional wrestling ain't a sport that nice guys take part in, Andy,? barked Rhodes, sounding more aggressive than he had intended. ?Fuck it. Who am I kidding? I've said it to you so many times before, what are the chances of it making a difference now? I don't know why I bother.? For the first time, Andy looked up at Walter on his own accord, his nostrils flaring. ?I've had enough of this,? he mumbled. ?YOU'VE had enough?!? Rhodes suddenly flared. ?Are you kidding me? YOU have had enough? You came here asking, BEGGING me to take you on, to show you the ropes, okay? It wasn't the other way around. I try to tell you what you need to to do better yourself, and you odn't fucking listen to a word I say, and YOU'RE the one that's had enough? Give me a fucking break.? ?I told you before; I came here to learn how to wrestle, not to learn how to hurt people.? ?You can't out-wrestle every fucking opponent you step in the ring with, Andy! It's not enough being the best wrestler in the world if you don't know how to take it to the next level. You think a guy like Meanstreak or Ivan Stanislav gives a flying fuck about how many holds they know?!? Again Andy lowered his head. ?I am NOTHING like those guys.? Walter huffed; very heavily, very audibly. His frustration boiling over, he stepped up to Andy who again had no choice but to look him in the eye. ?No; those guys are successful, and you're a fucking no-name indy-level HACK! And that's all you're ever going to be until you straighten your shit out and learn that professional wrestling isn't all clouds and sunshine!? Andy furrows his brow and flashes Rhodes with a smouldering glare. ?Awww, what's wrong, did I hurt your fucking feelings? Grow a pair, for fuck's sake. Before I-? And then it happened; Walter's speech interrupted with a suddenly crack of four knuckles across the side of his cheek. Andy Murray lost control of himself; he'd had enough of hearing this man tell him an uncomfortable truth. He saw the red mist and he lashed out. Walter Rhodes backed into the wall from the force of the blow, clutching the side of his face tightly. Slowly the warm, rusty taste of blood crept between his teeth and seeped through to his tongue. Moving his index finger to his lips, he pulled back his hand and saw a small red smear emerge on his fingertip. He panicked, Andy; not knowing what to do. His heart rate intensifying, he backed into the wall as Walter looked up scornfully. ?Get the fuck out of my gym.? ---- ?I could use some help.? Walter blinked; his face contorting with confusion. ?Come again?? ?You heard it.? ?Yeah, I...? Walter paused, his ageing mind struggling to come to terms with what was quite possibly the last thing he'd expect to come out of Andy Murray's mouth. ?You. You want my help?? Only nodding in response, Andy pushed himself up from his chair and sighed. He may very well have laughed had he considered the absurdity of the situation for himself. His former trainer stared at Murray as he wandered across to the other side of the room. The list he saw Andy Murray, the Scot had just slammed his fist into his cheek, and here he was now asking for help as he had done almost eleven years prior. ?Andy, what are you talking about?? Walter asked, still sitting in the chair. ?When you left the gym I thought we both came to an agreement that we couldn't work together, but that ain't even important here. Fact is, I just can't see what a guy like you can learn from an old, beaten-up piece of meat like me.? Still without words, Andy stood in the corner, watching as Walter Rhodes reached out and grabbed his crutch. Pushing down on the handle and placing the wooden aid under his armpit, it took a mighty push for the Englishman to reach his vertical base and immediately a barb of white hot pain shot up his bad leg. ?I can't get in that ring no more, Andy. Look at me; I couldn't even climb up the steps...? ?I don't need you to.? ?What then?? Walter quizzed, hobbling closer to the Scot. ?From what I see, you're just about the hottest thing going in GCW right now. You're about the last person on the planet who needs career advice from a guy like me. All you've gotta do to keep reaching the top is keep doing what you're doing.? ?If I keep doing what I'm doing then I'm never going to beat him.? Andy turned his head round, his gaze cold and steely. ?Look; eight years ago you told me that I'd never hit the top if I couldn't find a way around the morality that I've forced upon myself, and up until about five months ago, I still didn't believe you. I've reached the verge of genuine immortality as Mr. Popular, yeah. But Dangerous Games taught me something.? Walter sighed. ?I was wrong, Andy. That was supposed to be a motivational technique, nothing else, but the fact that you've made it this far without having to sacrifice a drop of that precious morality goes to show that it was-? ?You weren't wrong, Walt,? Andy interrupted. ?I was, and it's taken eight years and a brush with the devil to come to terms with that.? Rhodes knew exactly what Andy was talking about. Despite the way in which their pupil/teacher relationship had spun out of control he had tried his best to keep track of his former student's GCW career. He knew all about Andy Murray's battles with LeStatt Knight. ?I'm not going to lie to you, Walt. I think I can beat him,? Andy began, his tone stern. ?Take all his psychological bullshit out of the equation and we're pretty equal. At Shockwave, if there's a way I can stop him weaving his black magic then I can do this. I know I can...? ?Then what do you need me for?? Walter quizzed. ?I need the extra push. At WorldWide 100 he almost drove me to an act so ghastly I could never have lived with myself. He lost that night, sure, but he won the war. He had me in his pocket at Dangerous Games.? Briefly, Andy looked down at the floor. ?If I am to ever become a World Champion again I can't afford to let that happen.? Walter's nod was slow but understanding. A student of the sport even with his life slowly drawing to a close, Walter Rhodes knew all about the cerebral impact that a war with LeStatt Knight brought. He could help Andy Murray, he knew it, but doubt lingered yet. ?I don't know, Andy. I haven't done this in a long time.? ?Walt, look. I don't need a mentor, I don't need anything full-on. I just need a favour. I'm the golden child; every day I spend on the road I'm surrounded by gophers and lackeys who do nought but blow smoke up my ass and spoil me with praise and I'm fucking sick of it. You're the only person I've met with the balls to tell me when I'm fucking up, Walt. I need that again, if only 'til I'm done tangling with LeStatt Knight. Please.? Letting a soft smile spread across his dry, cracked lips, Walter Rhodes was flattered that a man in Andy's position would even consider him a valuable teacher at this advanced stage in his life. The truth was that for every ounce of respect that Andy Murray had for Walter Rhodes, the trainer returned it tenfold, regardless of their sketchy history. ?So, you need someone to help with that last giant leap?? he asked Andy without waiting for a response. ?Promise not to smack me in the jaw this time?? Andy grinned. ?It was the cheek, actually.? The two of them shared a chuckle. The pain returned to Walter's leg all of a sudden, as if his body was screaming for him to decline Andy's request. Walter Rhodes had waited eight years to perfect the wrestling acumen of his most promising student. In spite of his aches, pains and poor health, the lure of perfecting the Scottish King of Cool was too much for a man in his position to refuse. ?Let's win some gold.?
