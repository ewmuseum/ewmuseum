Break Stuff, Part 3: My Heart

27 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/27/10 I promised when I first started back in wrestling that what Troy didn’t know wouldn’t hurt him. But like so many of my promises, it simply wasn’t to be. He wasn’t supposed to know. How did I ever think that was going to work out? I’m not sure now. It seems pretty nuts. I think somewhere in my mixed up little brain I thought coming back would be a quick vacation that wouldn’t stick. I would get back in the ring and find all the reasons I left waiting for me. Who was I kidding? I found everything I loved waiting for me all over again. He’s doing his best though, sitting there trying to digest all of this, trying to understand. God, I love him for trying and not just fleeing as he has every right to do. But he’s running his hands through his dark brown hair, nearly black like mine, and trying to keep his voice even. And I know he’d give anything if I’d just be that same girl he met a couple years ago, that seamstress who took meticulous pride in getting the sequins perfect on the dancers’ outfits and was just as happy as he was with a job well done and a cool drink in hand at the end of the day. She was goofy and fun and offbeat, but still just a girl. But I’m not. I’m Tempest. I’m still that girl, his girl. But I’m also the girl who’s going to Shockwave to beat a man named Sean O’Neill into submission. And Troy can neither fathom why I’d want to, nor what I’ll risk to do it. He looks at SurReal’s anger and pities him. He looks at my pride and wishes I could back down. He doesn’t see that it’s exactly the same pride I put into sewing a seam or holding a friend’s hand or anything else I do. I just wish I didn’t still need this icepack on my head. I’m sure I’d be more convincing while trying to have this conversation without it. Fuckin’ Jackhammer… “You still dizzy?” he asks. He’s pacing. “All my life, baby.” I chuckle. I give him that ‘nothing-gets-me-down’ smile. He isn’t buying. He’s worried. It’s annoying really. I can sell most anyone else on that smile. “That was after one move.” He stops and just stares at me, those dark blue green eyes staring past all the bravado. “What happens if he hits you with it twice? You really want more of the same?” “What happens if I do the same to him or worse? He just caught me off guard. You know, being in the middle of my match and all.” That had some bite. I roll my eyes. I’m not used to having to defend myself verbally after taking a beating too. I’m trying not to take it as an attack, because I know it isn’t. I want him on my side. I want him to understand. But it’s hard to convince him that the thing he thinks is most wrong with the world is right when I’m the one doing it. “And it’s all going to go perfectly this time?” He says it softer this time and takes a seat facing me beside our kitchen table. Whether he’s challenging my grand scheme or looking for reassurance I can’t quite tell, maybe a little of both. “Well he won’t have the chance to sneak up on me. And facing me head on isn’t as easy as it looks, just because he’s bigger and stronger.” He’s giving me that look now. “Not meaning to doubt you or anything, but why not? You said he was good. And you said he was bigger and stronger. What part of this is supposed to reassure me you’re not signed up for your next trip to the hospital here?” Hopefully a huff speaks volumes to him before I answer. “Because I’m better, that’s why! I’m faster, I’m more agile, and I’m way more adaptable. I’ve been turning guys as good as him on their heads for years. And way bigger too!” He looks at me doubtfully, like he wants to believe. He stands up again and paces to the counter. “How does that work? I mean… the bigger they are the harder they fall?” “Something like that. I have to use his weight against him, turn him ways he didn’t mean to go, reverse his momentum. I’m good at it though. And in this case, I’m looking for a submission. So if I can lock in something that hurts enough, well that’ll be all she wrote.” I love that idea. It makes me grin like that cat that swallowed the canary. But Troy just shakes his head. “I don’t know if I can watch.” “Then don’t!” The explosion is like a firecracker instead of a bomb going off, but the snap in my voice is there to be heard. “But you should. And you should cheer for me. I’m going to teach him a lesson.” “You really believe that?” He just looks at me, unsure. He’s not taking the bait into a fight, but neither is he backing off. “See that’s why I’m so opposed to violence to begin with; no one learns anything from it. It just begets more of the same. You used to believe that too. Or maybe you didn’t. I don’t know. What should I believe about you?” He doesn’t say it as an accusation, but the sting is there. What’s worse is that it’s true. He takes a seat again, having scooted closer. He takes my hand and looks at me with those eyes. Yeah, those eyes, the ones I can’t look away from. “Do you really think you’re gonna change this guy’s mind about anything? That it’s going to do anything but escalate until one of you hurts the other really badly? I only meant that whether it’s on the big screen or hiding out in the back alleys or huge armies against each other makes no difference. It never really ends. And I may not be able to right the whole world, but I can try and look out for you. You’ve…” He hesitates. We both know where he’s going. But only I know that it’s coming home sooner than he thinks. “Rachel, haven’t you seen enough to be done with it?” I have to think about this one a minute because the truth is so much more frightening than he knows. What he’s seen on TV is the kindergarten version of my life. And explaining that in a way that doesn’t make matters worse is tricky. “I’ve faced worse. Way worse, people who weren’t just looking to prove a point, or see their name in lights, but people who meant business for real and didn’t want to do anything out there on camera for the world to see. People who think that little stage we play on is the most dangerous thing going have no idea.” He nods slowly, trying to take this in without being freaked by it. It’s a brand of courage too quiet for a TV audience, too sublime for our brash business. That’s probably why I admire it. It’s my turn for hand holding. “It’s an I Quit match. He’ll give up. And he’ll leave me alone. And what’s more, I’ll forgive him. I don’t hate him as it is. But I’m not gonna let him walk all over me. You should know by now that whatever I do, I do with all my heart. This is no different. And that it’s violent…” He watches me more intently than I’d like. “Well, things are different this when you’re competing. This time I see what’s coming. This time I choose my fights. It makes all the difference.” He nods. “You choose this fight then?” “You know I do. And I’ll do what it takes to win it. The one thing that never breaks is my heart.” ~~ “It was heartbreaking even then to find out all that stuff, so I’m a little surprised you want to pursue it, Ms. Tyrell. Not to mention the fact that you’ve taken your life in your hands by re-emerging. I have to ask: What possessed you to go back into wrestling? What makes you think it’s safe now? There still have to be people out there who want you dead. Weren’t you in witness protection?” That’s Kraig Carter, my private investigator, on the phone. He’s also a professional worrier. He had the dubious duty about six years ago of finding out who killed my father. He also had to find out who my father was. And my name. All I had was “Tempest” before that day when we sat down over coffee and flipped open the big brown folder that gave me back what so many take for granted. “Tempest” and the breath in my lungs was all I escaped that world of violence and terror with. I was seven when he was killed. “I left protection about two years ago. If you check with your sources, I think you’ll find that after the initial explosion of violence five years ago, after I first disappeared, those that made it out of that alive slowly but surely killed each other off. There is no one left to care about me. Or rather, the last person that did showed up floating face down in the Ma Wat River two years ago. So… now I get my life back.” It’s not that I’m cold or callous to the lives that have been destroyed in my wake. But make no mistake: I have no sympathy for those who were all to ready to destroy me. They set themselves on that path, and all the sympathy and sweetness in the world doesn’t stop me from hanging onto one thing: survival. If it comes down to me or you… you can bet it will be me. I did my five years in hiding. I’m done hiding now. “Right…” Carter recovers himself. I can hear the tapping of keys in the background as he takes notes. “Well, finding these people shouldn’t be all that hard now that I know who your father was. Tracking him down was Hell and his killer… well, certainly the most dangerous task I’ve taken on. Tracking down his family should be a walk in the park. I just wonder whether you’re going to find them… receptive to you.” He’s not the only one who wonders. My fitful childhood memories include just enough reference to his parents to know that they weren’t speaking. I don’t know if they even know I exist. But it’s one of those things I simply have to find out. It may hurt me to hear or see the truth, but I have already faced the worst of it. That’s what no one seems to understand. If my heart didn’t snap in two when my father fell dead at my feet, it’s not going to now. “They may not be. But I still have to know. They have to know that they have a daughter, a granddaughter, a niece.” I dangle a chew toy for my ferret Emil and shift the phone to my other ear. “Even if they choose not to care, they should know.” ~~ Scouting an opponent is not just an exercise for the brilliant technical wrestler who would have you believe that anyone with sparkles in their hair is unworthy of the name. Oh yes, it’s an advanced art of viewing video footage, examining records, and eating Cheetos. I get lots of help with that last part with the two ferrets and one dog in the house. So as I watch SurReal pummel a hapless Lori Equinox on my big LCD screen, I turn away from the predictable bits and look at my laptop, shiny and purple as you might expect. On the screen are the salient files from Squared Circle Scene. Remember my good friend Darren Stearns? Yup, editor in chief of Squared Circle Scene for years and years. He’s the world’s greatest search engine when you want to know something about a wrestler. I read his email… knee injury, not really a weak point anymore. Back injury… major source of paranoia. Hmm, something to place with. I click through some more of the highlights. They’re very nice. I’ve never said the man couldn’t wrestle. I could have given him the respect he deserves. But he didn’t choose to ask for it. How sad for my dear Mr. O’Neill… For you, life began the day you first wrestled. Whether it was in a ring, or a on a mat in a gym, Greco-Roman or some adapted style matters not. But my life does not begin there. No, not by a long shot. Nor even does my “career.” For that you must dig so much deeper. And there are no video clips, no record books, and if you could find the “fans” they might not even be willing to admit to it. No, you think life began for me the first time I strutted out to a ring in New York on the arm of… what was that idiot’s name? Or maybe it was the first time I hit a moonsault from a rickety ring post onto someone else whose future wasn’t so bright. When I was signed to a television fed? Perhaps the first title I held, a “mere” women’s championship? The first “big” title? The first World? So you really don’t know. You don’t know where I’m from and what I’ve come through to get this far. You don’t know what it’s like to scale that mountain, nor to have your hopes dashed. And you have no idea what it is to lose everything you love in a split second. And what you don’t know will hurt you. But my heart is not so simple, and hungers for much more than the love I have been so fortunate to find, the family I lost and long for, and even the simple satisfaction that my successes have brought me. You have something I need as well, all embodied in those two little words: “I Quit.”
