He doesn't like Mummy when she shakes.

Date: WorldWide 108 "I dunno if it’s such a great idea to get in the ring this week, Craig. You’re pretty banged up," says Doctor Noller, with one of those sympathetic looks over the top of his glasses as he always does when he delivers news he thought you didn’t want to hear. I shake my head. Pat at the end of the bandage to make sure the little metallic clip thingy was not going to poke into my rib cage. The sweat of worry illuminates on Doc Noller’s forehead thanks to the fluorescent bulbs in the room. He’s been my family GP ever since I was little and I can only ever remember seeing and am not interested in changing my ways. He was kind and caring, full of worldly wisdom and fatherly advice. He was almost like an uncle. That stuck things in your body. Not in the creepy way. "No can do, companiero. See, the trick is I don’t get in the ring this week and I take a step backward. People’ll think I let some schmuck bullies get the best of me and ain’t nobody gettin' the best of this guy here, yeah?" Doc sighs, face-palms himself and runs the hand up over his nose and through his hair as if he were trying to bury some bad news in his hair. His hair had seen better days and each time he runs his hand through his comb over he has to smoothen it down again to maintain his professional composure and doctoral image. "What, Doc? Spit it out now. Don’t hold back. I been seein? you since I was knee high to a grasshopper and all that shit. Don’t go holdin? back on me now." He makes that "um" and "arr" sound that people make when they are trying to figure out how to say something. The tug-of-war going on his brain as he manifests the words into the order he thinks they should come out in but doesn’t quite want to say it. I can see by the look in his eye that he does not want to disappoint me. His internal war with emotion and profession draws me to push my ass onto the edge of his little gurney, trying to grasp the information from him with my body language. "Doc?" "I think you might’ve fractured a rib." "Think? Doc. C’mon. You don’t think. You know. Have I or haven’t I?" Gives me that look. That look that says he knows I do not want to hear the news. Nothing needs to be said when you see that look. "Fuck!" I curse, rising to my feet and moving to the window, like a diva on a daytime TV soap opera. Push on the sorest spot of my ribs in test of my own pain threshold. Feel myself wince from the slight pressure. "Chance of it breakin??" Shrugs his shoulder at me, less elegantly than a doctor should. "Dunno, Craig. Might. Might not. I cannot predict the future. I’m just instilled with the gift of retrospect," he says amicably, as if it was his own little personal joke. "Oh, puh-lease. Save me the philosophical psychobabble, Doc. So, what you’re saying is you cannot say that it WILL happen." "Well, yes bu-" "So you cannot make the assurance that if I step into the ring I WILL break my ribs?" I ask again, making sure he understands that I am seeing his loophole and raising him, trying desperately to jump through it like a trained poodle. Doctor Noller sighs, palms his face, sighs again and runs a hand through his hair as if would release the mounting stress he were feeling for my health. Pats it down once more. "Craig. If you break a rib it could puncture one of your lungs. A punctured lung is a very serious condition. Even moving about in the ring, it’s going to cause you grief. It’s going to infringe upon your breathing and stamina. There is a very likely chance that you could break your ribs if you chose to compete." "But you’re not stopping me." "Based on the condition, I cannot recommend nor could I stand in the way of you competing." Nod in agreement, my mind already made up. The silence stands between us like the Great Wall of China, with Doc Noller’s facial expression like a kid trying to jump to see over. I stand up, tug on my Raptor Bandit t-shirt, the pride and joy that I bought on drmcninja.com. Shrug at my old friend. "I’m only hearin? premises and allegations, Doc. No offence, but you don’t got nothin? rock solid for me. I can’t take the advice on things that could happen, broseph. Should I not go walkin? down the street in the case that a rogue bus comes beltin? down the sidewalk and tries to bust me up coz the bus driver had one too many methamphetamines to get through his tragic bus driver life?" Blinking as if trying to finite the image in which I so graciously instilled into his mind’s eye, Doc Noller finally blinks away the images to give me that un-doctorly shrug. "Well? I guess not." Shrug and throw my hands up for the Doc in his defeat. I let them slap against my thighs with a little extra slap, just to punctuate my point. "Case closed, Doc. I’m goin? to 108 and wrestlin?. And that’s that." Slap my hands together as if I were dusting them off. "And that’s the facts." Grab my leather jacket and tug on the sleeves, hold my hand out for Doctor Noller to give me a high five, which is probably the single most awkward occurrence in my life as he limply slaps hands with me. Battered and bruised, I was facing Dan Black. Take my exit from the doctor’s room and head off down the hall when I can hear the feint sounds of Nirvana’s "Smells Like Teen Spirit". My first urge is to rock my head back and forth to the hypnotic, yet chaotic, beat before I realise it is the ringtone of my cell. Plucking it from the inside pocket of my badass leather jacket, I flip it open and accept the call. "CJ here." "Daddy?" My daughter, Leslie, was on the other end of the line. My heart lit up and fluttered because being away from them for so much of the time dragged my heart down deep. Hearing her voice was like hearing an angel singing a duet of "Spoonman" with Chris Cornell. "Darlin?! Oh my little mamacita, how you doin? baby girl?" There’s an awkward pause. I can hear Alex crying in the background. "What’s wrong, darlin?” Where’s ya mama?" "Mummy felled over and there’s ouchies in her ear." "Ouchies in her ear? What’s that mean?" I begin to feel the warm wash of panic as my stomach feels like it just dropped out the back of my ass. "What’s that mean, sweetcat? You mean blood, darlin?” Is it red water? Is it kinda like red water?" I can hear a swishing noise against the phone. "Leslie, are you noddin?, sweetcat? Coz Daddy can’t hear you when you’re noddin?, baby girl." "Yeah. I nodded, Daddy. There’s wed water, Daddy." Jesus H. Fuck! I think. Desperately spinning on my heels and bursting back into Doctor Noller’s office. Catch him off-guard and he gets the fright of his life. Clutches at his chest as his face contorts to that anger of being surprised as he stands up, ready to rile against the intrusion. "Holy mackerel, Craig Jonah Woods! You scared me half to dea-" I blurt the information at him, covering the mouthpiece on the phone. "Morag’s bleeding from her ears, Doc. Leslie’s on the line. Call an ambulance to the apartment." "Shit! I’m on it, Craig." Remove my hand and put the phone back into an audible position to speak to Leslie. "Leslie? You there, honey bee?" "Yeah, Daddy. Alex is cwyin? but. He doesn’t wike Mummy when she shakes." Back to the doctor. "SHE’S FUCKING FITTING, NOLLER! GET THAT FUCKING AMBULANCE THERE A-SAP!"
