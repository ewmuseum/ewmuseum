An Off Week For Everyone Involved

Date: The week of January 16th, 2008 Nothing matched that purity of being; he could admit that much. The clarity of wanting without having or having tried; he remembered her then: pulling up beside of him at the light where their paths diverged after school, her jaw wide and face open in the ridiculous smile of mock surprise as her arm twitched an ecstatic wave’not her spouting racism during his life's most disappointing 3am. It tasted something like regret at the back of his throat; he'd traded in longing after a dream for going after a person and found both clouded for his efforts. He took no comfort in wallowing in the angst that followed; he just took out a lawn chair and watched his fantasies crumble away against the night sky. It all flowed into one solid memory painted in the blue and gold of his old school, inseparable from his selfimage. He tried not to think about himself much. He was a passenger and a visitor and only truly comfortable as such; content enough on his personal shade of passive activity to ride it out wherever it went. It had taken him back to that high school with a head full of ganja and just enough clout as an alumni to have the front office overlook the stank. Still as twitchy as ever under fluorescents in those long hallways of dark cinderblock, he held his breath and cursed that he'd have to deal with a class change again, cursed his flight and cursed his taxi on the following inhale. "Think Condoms Make Sex Safe?" "Drug Free School Zone." "Keep In Mind You Can't Rewind." "You Never Even Read These When You Were Here, Frank." He shook his head and didn't break stride. Fair enough; the poster was right. He'd written his learnings off as a casualty after graduation, as necessity during college. Sometime after that muck, he lost them altogether; save for how to harmonize against the ringing bells. He'd learned their freqency in his time there, matched their pitch and forced his brain into modulating between an understanding of one or the other and wavering consistent between both. Of all the rote memorization over the years, it was the one that had stuck with power and it hung in the back of his throat with a twitching whine’buried beneath the ambient of a thousand people flowing into his hallway and crowding blind. He moved his yellow visitor pass from his jacket and clipped it onto the neck of his shirt. He thought of Wooderson eternal and shared the statement, if not the understanding gathered. They didn’t change, they were forever: vacant, dope eyed, buried in makeup and dedicated to the worst. He couldn't stomach himself in those days; not in retrospect at any rate and he found it increasingly difficult to divorce himself in the moment from the variations on himself that preceded. Besides; lines like that tended to lose their meaning once crossed, regardless of any backpedaling and justifying afterwards. He qit thinking about it, qit trying to acknowledge his moral code as anything solid and kept moving. Muscle memory; the same memory that made him perk up when he recognized the dealers in the same formations at the same corners they'd always been, the same that left him on the furthest side of the hall possible from anyone that looked like him. Sideways then, he took odd angles at crowds and slipped, juking, through the mass until he found the marred/scarred double doors leading outside and dove, headlong, into the chill. He couldn't see his breath; it struck him, staggered him, left him sucking his lungs full and exhaling sharp in the hopes of one plume. Nothing. He zipped his hoodie and dug deeper into it. Crossing campus, sloping downhill on an asphalt walkway between winterdead oaks and maples, withered and blackened by the gray world. High school again. No recess. He dropped down the steps that eventually lead inside; the low flung brick building itself buried in dirt up to the bottom of the window frames. Sandwiched by retainer walls, waiting by the locked door. He scanned the acrylic painting that blocked the window from inside, ran a fingertip along the glass following thick black lines that separated shade from shade. He understood it as: at random. He understood it as: pretty. He understood it as: meaningless high school art that came before him and had decent odds of outliving him. He decided to knock. A muffled voice inside kept him at bay as he counted clouds, tapping his foot off beat (to any beat) until a small man with wireframe glasses and a ring of thick, wiry gray hair swung the door in. The glow inside reflected off his scalp, sheened into an almost halo as his bearded jaw fell open. "Frank." He popped a thumb under his visitor's pass, propping it up for the man. "Return of the prodigal son." "Hah. Cant say I was expecting you here." "Chick I left in Texas probably wasnt either but, hell. Hows it goin?" "Same as it ever was." The man backed into the room, Frank followed and closed the door behind him. "Yourself?" "Copasetic, copasetic." Wrists still buried in his pockets, he heel toed his way past a wall covered by a beaten set of handbuilt cabinets, paused at the first of the collapsible desks that helped turn the room from a studio space into a cluttered mess. He inhaled paint thinner fumes, he exhaled calm. "See things havent really changed that much." "One of us is still getting paid too much." "Orbison?" "Costello." The man wrinkled his nose with a smirk. "So your tastes have changed some, I see." "Not my tastes, really. More, uh, more that my area of experience got bigger." "See? Getting old isnt that bad." "Easy to say when youre holdin up as well as you are. Huh. Shit." He picked up an unpainted/unglazed hunk of clay that he didn't immediately recognize as anything; it was still warm from the firing. "You gonna stay on til they make you leave?" "Well, after what they did to my hair, I have to stay to retirement. It wouldnt be the same if I didnt get revenge." "True, true. How many years you got left anyways?" The man rubbed his head with both hands, shifting his gray irises to the folds of his eyelids. "Three years, with good behavior. Theyve already got my replacement lined up, if she takes the job." "Yeah? She any good?" "Shes wonderful, but you remember her. Used to be Leslie Dawkins." "Oh. Shit." "Didnt think youd be too excited about it. She still brings you up now and then." "So you know I dropped out?" "Hah. Yeah. Thats fine, I did the same thing my first time through, and I wasnt half the talent you were. You could finish it anytime, no problem." He paused, tilted his head to the side and fixed his glasses. "If you wanted to, that is. Youre doing well for yourself, I hear." "Well enough. Cant say Im enjoyin it like I enjoyed paintin but, shit. I bought a new car, already. Better than workin at a museum." "Zoe? If that girl wouldve qit fucking up." A shrug. "Nothing an art teacher can do about it." "Best class I ever took, if nothin else. You did your job, did it damn good." "Yeah. I like to think so." "How, uh. Hows Leslie doin?" "Hah. You still smoke too, dont you?" "All them old habits die hard." "They do. Shes fine. I could get her for you, if you wanted. Shes in class, but I could cover for her, at least until lunch." "No, no. Its cool." "Are you sure? Shed love to see you, I know." "I wouldnt. Just. Let it be." "Frank. Its not like she ruined your life." "No, she didnt. But I aint tryin to push it." Paul Rosen hung his chin to his collarbone, head swaying leftrightleft. "You always overestimated yourself just a little." "Nah, nah. Its not like that. Its. What, shes married, got a kid now?" Paul nodded to him. "I would. Man. You dont even know. I would wreck all of that shit for another shot at her, and I couldnt even stand chick last time I saw her. Aint sayin I got a shot, just that Im famous and I dont wanna risk it. Yknow?" "If I didnt know you, that would be the worst thing Id ever heard. But, yeah. I understand what youre saying. Its almost noble. If it werent so goddamned selfabsorbed, of course." "Yeah, well. Either way. Like to think shed appreciate it. Dont like to think about it, though." "So long as you dont actually believe all of that bullshit." "Heh. I dont. Not really." The sole of his shoe sqeaked against the tile floor as he kicked faint at the radiator. "Sounds good though, doesnt it?" "Not the worst thing Ive heard, no. A little too. A little too Hollywood, though." "Ouch. Cold." "Well. Im not generally the person people see for reassurance and hugs." "Shit, Im in the wrong place. Already criticize self enough, yknow?" "No such thing as enough criticism, Frank." "Wheres her class?" "Hm? Oh. Hah. Down the hall. Its the old ROTC room." "Yall dont have that shit no more?" "We do. They meet in the old gym now." "Fuckers. Gunner still there too?" "Yep." And Jordan slapped his thigh. "God damn. Hope he remembers me." "Well. I wouldnt guarantee it, but. He does." "True, true. So, uh. Aint even gonna ask why I wanted to know her room?" "You dont seem like youre going to kill her. Im not too worried." "Hm. Yeah. Yeah. I dont want her dead. Shit. Hows classes?" Paul shot a glare to his side, out the window and up the hill. He lowered his head and turned back to Frank. "Theyre a bunch of fuckups. Ive got one good student right now, this junior that moved here from Missouri last semester’theres a lot of Lucian Freud in her style and shes just miles ahead all the other assholes and slackasses that take my classes. There are all these idiot athletes taking Art 1 for easy credit, and none of them could tell shit from Seawright if both were on a table, so. Fuck them. Its about the same as it ever was. Hah. Not to mention the people signing up because Leslies the youngest female weve got teaching here." Frank adjusted his knit cap and cleared his throat. "You have any of that students stuff?" "Hm? Melissa? No, no. I wish I did, shes spectacular. She hasnt started in on anything this semester." "If I told you to hold onto somethin for me, would you?" "If it was okay with her, sure." "Do that, then. I should be back this way before too long." "Alright. So, not to pry but. Whyd you come here anyway?" "I. Shit, I dunno. Nothin wrong with keepin in touch." "Of course not. Theres just a difference between keeping in touch and coming from, whered you say? Texas?" "Well. Aight. Paul. Heres the thing. The other day, man. The other day I turned 24 and you know what I did? Me and a chick Im not even really fuckin got drunk in a hotel room and watched shit TV until we passed out, and. Shit. Hard to look at that and say Im not wastin my life." "Compared to what?" "I dunno. Potential?" "Youre already fucked if youre trying to compare against potential. Might as well compare it against how bad things couldve gone for you." Frank’s eyes rolled up, he cupped his hand over his mouth. "Huh. Yeah, yeah. Its stupid either way. I just, shit. I need somethin like focus. Never known too many people that could get that much outta me." "I hope Im not one of them." "Maybe. Shit, shit if I know. Just the first person came to mind that mightve gave a damn about my head bein too full up on stupid shit. I mean, damn. Got a hell of a month ahead me, not tryin to be too distracted." "Thats fair enough. What exactlys eating you?" "Heh. Think Im doin better now." "I dont have to directly involve myself in your problems, and I still help solve them? I went into the wrong career." "Really damn did." He nods: curt, qick, sharp. "Were a dyin breed, arent we?" "No, I dont think so. I wouldnt even know what breed it was, at least." "Hah. Not big fish in a small pond, nah. Hm, uh. Fish that care in a pond that doesnt." "Id say the pond cares. It just might not care the same way we do." "I should talk to her, shouldnt I?" "Yes. You should. My offer still stands." "I can wait for her class to let out." "Theyve got lunch in, well. Halfway through the period, so fifteen minutes or so. If she doesnt let them out early." "Then I should start waitin?" "Probably." "That case, shit. Was good seein you, Paul." "You too. Youre welcome here anytime. Well, anytime that Im not in class." "Ill be back. Wont be long." "Bring some of your work when you do." "I." Frank stopped dead. A faint smirk crept across his face. "Alright. I can do that’have a good one." "You too." Frank turned from Paul and dragged feet to the back of the room to leave through another painted door, into a hallway lit by the pale sun outside. To the opposite end, he stopped at a drink machine and bought a Gatorade without difficulty. He planted against the wall, clock across and caged in. He counted cinderblocks until he realized he was using a constant repeater, a "1, 2, 3, 4, 1, 2" under his breath. He took his cap off and folded it in half. He rolled it longways and shoved it in his back pocket. He scratched his chin, slid up to run his finger through the groove of scar tissue parallel to his hairline. And he thought. He wandered, skipped through time nonlinear. A head cradled against his collarbone. A hand wrapped in his. Being the accessory in something that looked like infidelity and felt even better. They came in shards, buried beneath his years and just breaking the surface. "Same goddamn chicks. Repeatin forever." He smacked his fist against the wall and closed his eyes. He sat his drink on the ground and qit thinking. He qit counting the seconds when he realized how far off he'd gotten from the ticking above. "My god. Frank?" Her hair was still red. He thought she'd gotten thicker; maybe. Maybe it was the sweater, maybe it was that he'd never imagined her in things that weren't tshirts or sundresses. "Yeah. Hey, Leslie." He didn't fake surprise for trying to stifle a smile. She, on tiptoes, wrapped her arms around his neck and supported her weight against him. He reciprocated. "Hows things?" "Theyre good." "Lisa?" "Shes already painting. Fingerpainting, but. Its something." "Thats cute." He let her down, replaced hands to pockets. "Did you ever find out?" "Dont talk about that. I dont want to talk about that." "Okay. What do you want to talk about?" "Something nice. Its been years, jesus. How the hell are you?" "Im alright. Been better." "Youre famous. How has it ever been better?" "Its been better." "Frank." She tugged at his arm with both hands. "Are you hungry?" "If you wanna grab somthin, sure." "Thats not what I asked." "Then. Nah. Im good." "Boy, have you ever been hungry?" "Not really." "I did love a cheap date." He cringed. She might've expected her money back. "You gonna get anything?" "Id rather catch up. I think." "Not sure?" "God, no. Ive never been sure about you." "Always were smarter than the rest." "Dont give me that, Frank. We were stupid as hell." "Was fun, though." "Yeah." She wrapped her arms around her stomach. "I guess." "Can I ask you one thing, Frank, and we wont ever bring it up again?" "Yeah. Of course." "If, and this wont happen, no way at all. But, if I found out about her. Would you want to know?" He nodded. "And if. If. You know. If, would anything change?" "I dont think so." "Are you free tonight?" "Nah. My girls expectin me. Been awhile since I was around and. Shit. I owe it to her. Yknow?" "Frank? Did you ever get that beach house?" "Nah. You get yours?" "No." He slid his arm out from under her shoulders, sitting up at the edge of the bed with the sheets wrapped twisting at his waist. He pulled his pack of cigarettes from the nightstand in the window glow; he lit one and dropped the box where he'd found it. He, for the third time that day, thought of smashing his apartment and disappearing back into the night that continually returned him home. He didn't move at that, just kicked his bare feet against the carpet and smoked as much as he burned. He rolled his shoulder and looked back at her, leaning to wrap her tight under the sheets. The light lied her hair red, her skin fading pink. Her eyes flitted open. "Frank? Are you leaving?" "Go back to sleep, Ash. Im not goin anywhere."
