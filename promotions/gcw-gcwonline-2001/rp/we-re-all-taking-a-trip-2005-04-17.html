Date: 4/17/05 So yeah, turns out I've got a 17-year-old sister, and she's the only sibling I've got that's connected to my mom. There's a long backstory involved that doesn't matter at all, so I'll just gloss over it and lay out enough details to set the mood. Her situation was the same as mine, and I understand my parents a little better in seeing that my mom has a tendency to fuck people and then abandon the kids. She didn't know if mom was still alive, but she told me that mom's name was Maria, and that she was Spanish--as in, from Spain. The aunt that raised me wasn't an aunt; rather a friend that my mom had picked up and pawned me off on when the time came for her to get scared about raising a kid. She'd stuck around with my sister long enough to let her know about me, then tasted the fear a little after the girl hit 11 years old. She left and no one's really said anything useful about her since. See, by the time I got to that point, my sister was in tears and still hadn't told me her name. I finally recognized who was on her shirt then, too: it was a faded and cracked picture of me a few federations ago, my hair long and purple and my eyes bright red. I was smiling on it, so I blame that for the confusion. As fucking ridiculous as it all seems, it was all too true. The names, the places; she even the poem my mom wrote on the back of the photo of her, me, and my aunt that used to be on the mantel at the old house. 'What's your name?' She looked up to me, wiping away the murky stains on her cheeks. 'Gloria White.' And honestly, I didn't know what to do. My genetics were apparently to blame for my sojourns, and my mom might even still be alive and easy to find. But yeah, what the hell's that when faced up to a sister I'd never heard of? She went on to tell me about how she'd idolized me for years, followed in my footsteps and always trying to live up to what she thought I'd respect. I asked her why she didn't try to get in touch while I was still in America, but she couldn't give me a good excuse other than that she'd finally thought the time was right. It was then that she pressed a rolled up sandwich bag in my hand and smiled at me, bright and happy and beautiful. I was confused, six hours ago when that was all present tense. I'd asked her what was in the bag and she laughed and told me to enjoy. A quick look explained away everything, and here we are now. Gloria, who I've been calling Glory out of slack for awhile now, is laughing behind me. There's nothing but silence and stupidity now, shared between the group unequally.
