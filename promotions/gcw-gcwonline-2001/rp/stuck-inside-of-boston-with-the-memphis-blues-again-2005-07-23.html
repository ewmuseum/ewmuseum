Stuck Inside of Boston With the Memphis Blues Again

23 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 23, 2005 My hands shook, twitched in the orange glow of my cigarette when I pulled and cast monstrous shadows on the wall of beasts with steamrollers in their gaping maws, rumbling, unsteadily. I looked over my shoulder at only the back wall of the restroom and kept at pushing my motor skills far enough to roll. Herb slipped from the ends of the White Owl and onto the sticky-drenched floor as I fumbled, stumbled to finish. In my head were a thousand voices that gave up being dim, a sick symphonic reminder of my entire life. Fear, loathing, paranoia, angst, once introduced the system, will never subside to the other, greater, humors. So my fingers slipped and twitched and someone knocked on the bathroom door and I licked a seal and flushed the toilet with my foot--cigarette spiraling into something a little worse than oblivion. 'Sorry,' I said. 'Almost done.' A gruff voice barked across the door and stomped away to give me one more moment of silence before I burst forth with an exaggerated nod and got out of the fluorescent gas station without even a glance around. Trey waited in the car, chugging on a tall can in a brown bag, and laughing maniacally. I lit another Newport and got in. 'What was up with that?' 'Shouldn't have taken that long.' 'I know, man. That was like, ten minutes. Not your style.' 'No light.' 'Damn.' 'Yeah.' I fumbled around in my pocket for a key and, finding it, cranked the car for a speedy escape. We tore past the pumps and quickly hit the road, empty as a zombie flick. 'Why'd you roll in there anyway?' I shrugged and let it slide. Pressed buttons until a CD started playing, pressed buttons until it stopped and left the radio to Trey and the spoils to me. 'Keep the can down,' I told him as I pulled the blunt from the pocket of my Levi jacket and lit it up, surely unaware of the irony. 'You're a crazy motherfucker sometimes, y'know?' I nodded to him and took a deep hit, let my lungs fill and eventually collapse. 'I don't know how you even got me this job, J. What kinda connections you got?' 'At least two or three.' He laughed and I rested the blunt on the ashtray as a trade for the cool goddess of Menthol. 'One of them's not a drug dealer.' 'Shyeah, shyeah. Play it down, you know you're rollin' like hell.' 'Haven't found any around here.' 'Not X, jackass. Y'know what I mean.' 'Sure.' 'Man, there anythin' to go see? Movies, an' shit.' 'Hollywood got nuked.' 'Damn.' 'The time was right, I think. Rebuild from the ashes of fallen glory. Create new classics to someday rape. Could've been worse.' 'Shyeah, could've nuked us.' 'No one wants to nuke us, Trey.' 'I dunno. I've caught some evil-ass looks backstage, man. Don't know what the hell Knight's up to, dude could be planning some crazy revenge.' 'That was a long time ago and, fuck, Knight doesn't deal in nuclear weapons. I think we can guarantee that much.' 'What, you keepin' track of the black market?' 'Nah, build them in the trunk of the car. People'll pay out the ass for one, you know.' 'Shit, nice deal.' 'Yeah.' I got quiet and drove, taking roads that felt right and not really wondering where I'd gotten to until I started recognizing it. I'd come to know the city over the last few weeks, for the cost of several thousand dollars a day and no real concern as to my well-being or my sanity. Trey'd become a local figure and basked in the glory whenever he went out, coming back in moments before dawn smelling of liquor and with his wrist cramped up from autographs. I'd laugh, and Tulz would laugh, and Trey would grumble and pass out on a sofa, and I'd hit him with whatever was handy until he went back to his room. I'd become known as the guy that tried to bum a cigarette from everyone that talked to him. It got me free smokes and tended to keep people away. 'Why you carryin' the piece everywhere now?' 'In case.' 'In case a' what, zombies?' 'In case Lexicon tries to leave Mexico.' 'Dude.' 'Don't. It's not gonna make sense.' 'S'I can quit tryin'?' 'Definitely.' 'Nice. Not that I'm gonna just let the hand cannon slide.' 'Never expected you would.' 'Seriously, ain't just Lexicon that's got you like this. You even been sleepin'?' 'I'm just a little wired. It'll pass.' Trey sniffed hard. I looked over to catch the grin plastered across his dreary face. 'Like I can find blow in Boston.' 'Connections.' 'Don't exist.' 'I've never been that stupid, y'know. Somethin's got your head, man, an' it isn't pretty. Seriously, J, what's up?' 'Your show, kid, not mine.' The world shifted into hollow, warm hues that clicked at the back of my neck like a loaded gun. Somewhere above me the sun was fighting like a stone-cold motherfucker to break through a line of clouds and finding hard resistance--or maybe I was more wasted than I thought. I remembered sleep, at the time, but never the before it and rarely much after it. Maybe I was just in the clutch of Boston, to the point that I caught myself using the accent occasionally. No use in resisting; they had me. 'We need to get out of here,' slipped from my brain to my mouth without my will behind it. A warning from some greater force, the singular presence of the city focused directly onto my pineal gland and screaming, crying, begging for me to seek freedom from its looming walls and foreboding inhabitants. 'Shit, gotta stick around for the Games, man.' 'You meeting contractual obligations isn't half as important as me getting this goddamned stench out of my soul.' 'I told you, man! Place smells like fish shit.' 'That's just the harbor. Rest of the place reeks of, Christ, 1998. Can't you smell it? Khakis and striped polo shirts, too much hair gel, something. It's hell.' 'You sure they let you keep a concealed weapon?' 'It's concealed.' 'You're gonna get me arrested just for bein' the black guy. Cops'll think I kidnapped your scrawny ass.' 'America's moved on to racial tolerance. Now we just judge people based on money.' 'Nice tolerance.' 'You're telling me. If I wasn't rich as shit, I'd be dead five times over.' 'Can't die more than once, man.' 'I'm good.' I jerked the car left, throwing it across a protected turn in the last moment before it dropped out to red, and accelerated hard and fast. The music was too quiet so I turned it up, MF Doom slurring his gravelly voice over jazz, and drowned myself in it. I tattooed a reveille on the steering wheel with my thumb, half-conscious impatience that rose up from the soles of my feet. 'We need something to do.' Trey shrugged and started to say something until his cell phone rang, rattling change in his pocket. 'Hold up,' and I turned the music down and slowed down for no good reason. An undercover patrol, still dressed in his uniform and with the give-away camera on his dashboard, pulled out of a road on my right and into the lane next to me. He cast a quick stare to the Impala; at Trey plastered up against the window with his drink down and his phone stuck to his ear; at me, surrounded by dense smoke that swirled away from the air conditioner vents, and completely uncomfortable in my surroundings. 'Shyeah. I got you.' Trey wrapped his hand around the bottle of the neck. 'No.' 'Huh?' He put his phone to his lap and looked over, making an 'oh' sound and picking his call back up. 'Sorry about that, man. But yeah, I can make it.' The leech traveled alongside us and watched from the corner of his eye, waiting on any wrong move. My license plate didn't fit in, my car didn't fit in, and I was just completely fucking out of place. 'Shyeah.' I rolled my window down and hung my arm out, half-burnt blunt halfway to the asphalt below and contemplating its existence, I guess. 'No problem, yeah. Sounds good.' Pig eyes still burned holes in my facade, tracing out a thousand punishments in my head if I was dumb enough to make the wrong move. I could either lose or just not play the game, slide away from the chance of failure with a flourish and luck. 'Trey.' 'Hold up,' he said into the phone. 'What's up, J?' 'Drop your bottle in the floor. He can almost see in.' He did what I asked and went back to his call with an 'Alright, man. Later,' that held no great significance. 'Guess what.' 'We're in hell?' 'Nah, there's a show before DG.' 'The shit you say.' 'Hah, nah man. F'real. S'pposed to be just a fun card, few matches an' shit for the fuck of it. Dunno if I got a match or not, but I gotta show up in case.' 'How can you not know if you've got a match?' I pulled the blunt in from the window and hit it with all my gusto, dragging long and hard in a valiant attempt at quick, controlled arson. 'Random matches, man. Surprise the fans, alla that.' 'Yeah, yeah. Boston have open container laws?' 'Dunno.' 'Shit. At least I'm straight.' 'Shit, if straight means stoned.' 'They can't prove a god damned thing.' One last lung-crushing hit and I tossed what was left as close to in the line of a tire as possible, tumbling end over end to the street. 'Too paranoid, J. Dude pulled off a minute ago.' I looked around and cursed myself as Trey cackled. 'See, man? Nothin' to worry about.'
