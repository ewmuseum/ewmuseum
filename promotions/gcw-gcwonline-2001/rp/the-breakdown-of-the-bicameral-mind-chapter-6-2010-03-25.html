The Breakdown of the Bicameral Mind: Chapter 6

25 March 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/25/10 “I love you,” he said, the emotion dripping from his words slowly. “I loved you from the moment I set eyes on you.” The hairs on his arms stood on end as he felt her breath on his face. The vulnerability of the moment scared him. It terrified him. He knew he had to do this if he wanted to be happy. The walls that protected him slowly crumbled away. A foundation that had taken years of hard work to build was falling away around the two of them. Their lips came together and then their heads and eventually their bodies as they embraced each other. Soon enough they were one. Their bodies had melded into each other. The moon and the stars were the only witnesses. After a while, they lay on the piece of cloth that separated them from the cold hard earth, looking up at the sky. Their skins still glistened with a mixture of rain and sweat. The cucubanos shone their brightly green lights all around them, outshining the brightest stars. He tried catching one with his bare hands in order to put it inside one of the glasses they had used earlier, but he was unsuccessful. She laughed and that laugh was music. The notes floated through the air as if stuck in that place for the rest of eternity. Not only her laughter, but the slow trickle of the river that ran right besides their camping spot and the sound of their hearts thumping against their chests like a drum rang true. Either of them could not have come up with a more perfect moment than then and there. Their imaginations couldn’t possibly top what they had experienced. She was the first to get up. In retrospect, he would come to think of that as a sign of things to come. Nothing is as perfect as it seemed. It was rotten on the inside. The day after, she received the news. They argued. “Leaving for so long with your tour and my schedule will separate us for too long. It will ruin us,” seemed to be his argument. “You have fulfilled your dream. You are doing what you love day in and day out. Don’t you think I deserve the chance to do whatever it is that makes me happy?” seemed to be her retort. They knew they were both right. Neither of them was willing to make the necessary sacrifices to salvage the relationship. She left two days later, but part of her stayed in that perfect night. He could never recover from the blow. Instead of walls, he locked himself inside a vault, never to be seen again. What everyone saw was a casing that used to hold something valuable. ----- The tears streamed down her face as she recollected the scenes from back when they were somewhat happy. Nothing in her life had ever come close to replacing that night as the best night in her life. In fact, ever since that night, she found it way too hard to look forward to dates of that sort. When she finally was able to start dating again, nothing was ever good enough, because nobody was really good enough in her heart. The distance between them was too much to hold their relationship together. Dr. Matheson looked at her intensely trying to figure out why exactly she had become so attached to Antonio because his doctor’s mind couldn’t help it, but more importantly he tried to figure out how he could use her to his full advantage. The first phase of his experiment had gone off without a hitch and now this second part would be a little bit trickier but if successful, it would make his theory work. “Have you thought about what I asked you to do? I know it is hard, Ms. Rodríguez, but we need to do something quickly or he will probably be lost forever. I am confident that your deep relationship with him will help to bring him back to reality. I am sure you don’t want him to do what he did to you again? Or worse, actually kill someone,” Dr. Matheson said in a certain tone of voice that was specifically designed to make her feel bad. The accusation hung in the air for a moment before Verónica decided to answer. Her brow was furrowed as she weighed the pros and cons of what might happen in her head. All the while, she impulsively touched her ring finger where the engagement ring had once been. It felt heavy today, as if it where there. “I know he would have done the same thing for me had our roles been reversed. I abandoned him once, I can’t do it again,” she said emotionally. The smirk on Dr. Matheson’s face was like a ghost. It was there for a moment for anyone that looked hard enough for it to see. It was all falling in to place. The final piece of his puzzle had been set and it was now just a matter of placing them in a room together where the sparks and he hoped the blood would fly. “You are a brave woman, Verónica. The decision you made here today was not an easy one, I know. But in the end, it will be for the best. You have no idea how much good it will do him.” As he said those hollow words of encouragement, he put his hand softly on top of Verónica’s. She wouldn’t remember at that moment, but when recalling it, she couldn’t help but feel a burning sensation where that hand had touched her. ----- You walked through the lonely streets of Old San Juan. It felt good to be back home and to breathe the salt air of the ocean. The old city was practically empty. A couple of bars were open at this hour and on this day where the citizens of San Juan could drown their sorrows away with the poison of their choice. It was almost always rum. You couldn’t help but feel like a spirit as you walked through those cobblestone streets. It was hard not to remember the old salsa song from the old salsa great Hector Lavoe as you walked through those streets. “Calle Luna, Calle Sol” was a classic that described the rough streets of the old city. It was a strange city. There were skeletons and ghosts and all kinds of creepy crawlies lurking through the night. Shadows watched silently as unknowing people put their very souls in danger. It was a dangerous city. You felt at home though. Not only because you had walked those streets countless times before, but because you were one of them. You were a shadow that lurks in the darkness, ready to strike. The architecture of that place helped to enhance the eeriness of the place. The streets were built in a way that they took advantage of the trade winds. Thus, at any spot in the old city, you could hear and feel the faint whisper of the wind. You walked and walked through places that you had some history in. You saw those places with new eyes. The time you flew your first kite somewhere over the Morro fort; the time you said ‘te amo,’ for the first time and the first time you were rejected. The memories flooded your body and mind in a torrent of winds and salt water. A tsunami of memory that no security measure could ever have stopped. That’s when you saw him. A demon of those damned old streets that raped and pillaged like the pirates and corsairs of old. The vagrant held a man hostage with a knife to his back. His eyes filled with green, he shoved his hand down the pockets of his captive as you watched in silence. After stripping his captive of his valuables, the drifter wasn’t done. A violent grin shot across his face as he cocked his arm back to take a stab at the man he held prisoner. That’s when something inside you clicked. You knew something had to be done and that it was you who had to do it. You quickly sprang into action and before long you were on the floor, one hand full of cloth and the other closed into a fist. You began to punch without mercy. The bone and flesh and blood combined in a deadly fusion of violence and hate. The vagrant looked on in shock. His eyes were wide as he held the wallet in one hand and the knife on the other. The blood rage was fulfilled before long and you looked up at the man with the knife in his hand. Your eyes filled him with such fear that he dropped both wallet and knife and ran away as quickly as he could. You stood up and looked at your bloody hands. There was something about spilt blood that you couldn’t help but enjoy. The man on the floor coughed blood and teeth into the floor and looked up at you questioningly. You didn’t try to explain though. You moved on slowly understanding that the man deserved no explanation. The man was nothing more than useless tissue that wasted precious air. As you left, you couldn’t help but look back at the man lying in a pool of his own blood and feel disappointed in yourself. As much as you wanted it to not be true, you felt that tug somewhere inside you that made you stop. You couldn’t get the job done. You were weak. You were still not perfect. ----- Dr. Andrew Matheson sat in his office contemplating his plan. Everything seemed to be going perfectly and it was kind of worrying him. He expected this whole crazy idea to not run as smoothly and the first few days he would have probably said it was insane. It no longer seemed insane to him. It was perfectly plausible that he might be on the verge of creating the next rung on the ladder of human evolution. Striping away a person from all sentimental emotions and instilling a mindset in which that person will do what is needed in order to get to where he wants no matter how seedy or evil it might seem is just the first few steps. Destroying those preconceptions of what is supposedly ‘good’ or ‘evil’ is an important part of this whole plan concocted by Dr. Matheson. What is good and evil anyways but human constructs based on dogma from millennia ago. His mind would wander through all these existential quandaries that would soon enough be obsolete thanks to his experiments. He was close to it. So close. There was a knock on the door to his office that took him away from his day dreams. “Come on in,” he said loudly so the person outside could hear. “What news do you have for me?” The young doctor had a smile on his face as he entered his elder’s office. “Sir, I have some very promising news for you,” he said a she sat down in front of Dr. Matheson’s desk. “Before I tell you the good news, I took care of the bodies Test Subject #1 left when he left this place and no one will be asking any questions.” “Good job. The subject showed incredible potential in the way he destroyed the two people he thought were guilty of this whole thing. I look forward to seeing what he can do next,” Dr. Matheson said with something like a creepy glee on his voice. “We already have in our hands what he can do next. The person we deployed to track the subject saw him basically attack an innocent man that had just been mugged and almost killed him,” the young doctor said, just as excited. “Apparently he just rushed at him and started to assault him. The homeless man that had mugged the man was so freaked out by what he saw that he dropped both knife and wallet on the floor.” Some of the excitement had drained from Dr. Matheson’s eyes and posture as he heard the account of what happened. “This is not as good as I had hoped,” he said as he scratched his chin in contemplation. “Sir, he attacked an innocent man because his brain is working exactly like we wanted it to. His brain told him that the man who got mugged deserved it because he was weak enough to allow it,” the young doctor said trying to cheer his boss with what he thought were great news. “Yes, but he left him alive. By that reasoning, he should have killed the man. We need to put him in front of something else that might trigger that desire to kill.” The young doctor wondered what they could do. “We could put him in front of his ex-fiancée. That might do it.” “Not yet, John. It is too soon. He would never kill her. But eventually they will meet and he will finish her off. Once that is done, the experiment will be a success.” They could only hope. “Send in Test Subject #2 and let’s see what happens.” “Eve?” “Yes. I want to see what her interaction with him is. It might just paint a clearer picture.” On the tenth day, man created Adam and Eve and they saw it was good; and Adam and Eve went on to wreak havoc and destruction to humanity until man’s will was done and humanity’s will was broken. He could already read the words written about him and his boss in the new and improved bible that would be written when humanity was changed forever. They would be gods. Or at least he could day dream for now.
