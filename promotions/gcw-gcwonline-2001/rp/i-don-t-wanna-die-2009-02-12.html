I don't wanna die

12 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02/12/09 I instinctively drop down as another shot sails overhead. I can feel my heart beating wildly in my chest, and my body threatens to freeze up with fear. You can’t fight a fucking gun, no matter how strong or fast you are. Sure, some people say that you can just disarm the gun but those asses probably never had a hit man shooting at them! I look over at Max who is clutching his left arm with his right. But he is making his way to his knees, but is careful to stay below the window. “We have to get the hell out of here,” he hisses to me. “There’s an exit in the back, but we have to cause a distraction. Our only chance is to get to a car and drive the hell out of here.” Cause a distraction? This is seriously his plan? How the fuck does he expect me to cause a distraction without getting my head blown off? “Max,” I question as another problem pops into my head, “what if there’s another guy waiting for us in the back?” “Then we are both going to die,” he answers bluntly. I can feel myself beginning to panic, but I stop when I see that a gun has magically appeared in Max’s hand. For some reason the firearm is sending cold shivers down my spine, but I decide to ignore them. After we make it out of this shit I will have all the time to freak out as I want, but if I lose it now I’m dead. “Why don’t you just shoot him?” I ask Max, who answers me with a withering glance. “Dude, I don’t exactly use this thing on a regular basis,” he responds dryly. “Matter of fact I haven’t used it since I bought it two years ago. I don’t know if it will still fire, and I definitely don’t know how to aim. So I think I am going to stick with the make a distraction plan.” I nod my agreement and finally regain control of myself. Now there is a clear plan that I can focus on, and luckily it’s not too complicated. Max is going to fire those bullets, at the shooter, we are going to run for the door in the back and hope we don’t get shot in the process. Max lifts the gun and pulls the trigger. The second I hear the gun go off I charge to the back of the house, faintly aware that Max is right behind me. What in the hell? Why is the back door’oh fuck. I slam into the second hitman with all the force that a man my size can summon running at full speed. The collision barely affects me, I stumble but quickly regain my footing and continue running towards the car. With a quick glance behind me, I see that Max is close behind casually trampling the would-be assassin on the way. It’s funny, I know that most killers these days don’t need to be body builders, but I didn’t even see a gun in that guy’s hand. I am panting by the time we reach Max’s car. It was not a very long distance away but I am not exactly a runner under the best of circumstances. Miraculously, despite his injury Max does not fumble with the keys as he opens the car door, and I dive into the passenger seat yelling at him to “hurry the fuck up!” We speed off breaking numerous traffic laws in the process. I don’t know whether Max is finally starting to register the pain of his wound, or if the adrenaline is starting to wear off but his driving is frighteningly erratic. Thirty seconds on the road and he actually knocks of the side mirror of a blue Chevrolet with his own, so now we are driving with only one side mirror. Wonderful. But at least we’ve managed to shake our attacker, judging by the lack of shots coming our way. I look over to Max whose steering the wheel with one hand, with a pissed off expression on his face, and with blood beginning to stain his left sleeve. “We should get you to a hospital,” I finally say and I am rewarded with a glare. “Look, I didn’t exactly go to medical school,” I continue coolly, “but I am pretty damn sure its not good to leave a bullet in your arm.” He refuses to answer me; instead he puts his attention back on the road. But I stare at him until he finally opens his mouth. “There’s a place where I can get this treated and we can hideout for a while,” he says tilting his head towards his injured arm. “Only problem is that they probably won’t be, what’s the word’ecstatic to see us.” I cringe, aware that for Max a statement like that could mean anything. “What the hell did you do Max?” I ask hoping that the answer won’t be too bad. “Nothing much,” he answers nonchalantly. “Oh I suppose that it wasn’t the greatest idea to get completely wasted and accidentally climb into bed with a homophobe. But its not like we had sex or anything, and he was the one to initiate the spooning. Anyway, when he woke up he freaked and started shooting up the place for some reason. Took five guys to bring him down, and ended with thousands of dollars in damage. I still don’t understand why people decided to blame me.” I close my eyes and slowly count to ten. One’I really want to kill Max. Two’I still want to kill him. Three’homicidal feeling is not going away. Four?.hey that girl on the sidewalk is hot. I am shaken from my count when I realize that Max is beginning to park while the hot girl glares at him. He looks over to her and gives a sheepish grin, one that doesn’t seem to make her feelings toward him any less hostile. I slowly get out of the car, ready to dive for cover in case the girl decides to pull out a gun and start shooting at Max. In my experience he brings out that urge in people. The girl hisses. I do not mean figuratively. I mean she literally hisses, as if she is some sort of tiger. Max chuckles and gives the girl a genuinely warm smile in return. “No matter how much you want it,” he admonishes the girl, “you are never going to become a lioness Jacqueline.” “My name is-? “Your nickname is Lin,” Max says firmly. “Mom would be rolling over in her grave if she knew what you wanted to call yourself. Hell it made you sound like a stripper, the trashy one who gets on her knees for ten bucks.” Holy crap Max has a sister! I must make sure not to even think about how hot she is, because trying to get laid with Max’s sister is going to open up a whole mess of problems that I frankly don’t need to deal with. Besides, it would probably be a good idea for me to focus on finding out who is trying to kill me and why. “Look Lin,” Max continues and the weariness in his voice is noticeable, “we ran into some real trouble and we need a place to hide out. I know things are really comfortable between me and the guys right now, but I need your help.” Lin looks at Max and I can see real concern in her eyes. That doesn’t surprise me; despite his propensity for making people want to kill him Max does have his own brand of charisma. “You know the deal Max,” Lin replies, “most of us would help you in a heartbeat, but you gave Pete the excuse he needed to freeze you out. You know as well as I do that if Pete says something goes no one has the balls to argue with him.” I turn to Max hoping for an explanation, and with a sigh he provides it. “Lin and the guys she works with are wannabe detectives. Basically they run errands for people too lazy to do it themselves, and then pretend that they hold the city in the palm of their hands. Pete is the ringleader of their little group, and they are too scared of him to do anything he says not to.” “So these are the people you want to depend on for help?” I ask him incredulously. “Believe it or not,” he answers while Lin transfers her glare to me, “they do have a pretty secretive hideaway. Probably because no one has ever bothered to find em, so we’ll be safe there. Plus Lin knows how to take a bullet out of my arm’which by the way is really starting to sting.” “Alright, alright,” I concede defeated. “What the fuck do we have to do to get this Pete to let us hideout with your sister and everyone else?” “You have to kick his ass,” Lin answers flatly. Then she grins, and her sparkling green eyes hold enough promise that I am forced to remind myself that she is Max’s sister. I shrug and motion for her to lead me to him. But I doubt that I shall have to many problems with the man. How dangerous can the leader of a bunch of wannabe detectives be? -20 minutes later- Pete is fucking huge. I am no lightweight, but Pete makes me look like I need to eat protein. But while he may be broader, taller, and stronger, I have more class. I mean come on, a skull in the place of her is just cheesy. But allow me to note that he is a great deal taller, a great deal broader, and freakishly stronger. Basically he’s overdosing on steroids, which is why the fist he slams into my stomach causes me to double over. I am trying to force my hands up in a semblance of a defensive position, but before I can do so Pete takes me down with a good old fashioned tackle. Imagine getting hit by a bus going at a hundred miles an hour’that’s almost as bad I feel right now. It’s not just the physical punishment Pete is dishing out, though I am certainly going to be ridiculously sore in the morning. It’s the psychological aspect I am having a problem with. I am very used to being able to destroy my opponents with brute force, to use my strength to manhandle them all over the place. To have that same thing done to me is a disconcerting experience. The tackle has positioned Pete’s torso between my legs and I instinctively close guard. Unfortunately its not doing me a great deal of good as Pete is so fucking big that my legs are becoming fatigued just from the effort it takes to keep them locked. Meanwhile Pete is letting his fists loose on my face, and my arms are barely able to keep his blows at check. Just when I am about to consider calling it a day he slows down. I sneak a peak and realize that Pete’s conditioning is sub par, probably as a result of his size. He’s panting, his head is down and he’s slowly trying to break my legs apart with one arm. “Wait a minute; he’s sliding his arm under my feet trying to break my legs apart. Pete! You stupid, unskilled, oblivious son of a bitch! With a reaction honed by consistent practice I shift my hips and lock Pete into a triangle guard. For someone who’s never seen MMA, a triangle guard involves locking your arms around your opponent’s neck and arm, so that one arm is isolated outside of the legs. It sounds fairly innocuous, but this position is deadly enough to choke anyone out. I don’t even have to adjust the choke to make it tighter. Pete’s massive bulk is working against him, all I have to do is push down on his head to make the choke tighter, and he is soon knocked out. I let him go instantly and stand back up, but admittedly out of spite I give him a good kick in the head as he begins to stir. I walk over to the couch and collapse in it, while the room watches me in silence. For the secret hideaway of a bunch of would-be tough guys the place is pretty nice if sparse. It’s neat enough that you do not risk breaking your neck every time you step somewhere, and the furniture is decent if rare. The couch I am sitting on is oddly comfortable, though that could be the blows to the head talking. I watch as Zach, one of Lin’s cohorts, works on getting the bullet out of Max’s suffering arm. His wound has turned a pretty shade of green and purple which is probably not good; now Zach is pouring a clear liquid into the injury that is causing Max to yelp in pain. I feel a hand tugging on my own; I allow it to lead me as I am too exhausted to object. Not from just the fight, I have recovered from that easily enough. But the day’s events have taken their toll; I am so mentally tired that I am zoning out at this point. We walk into a dimly lit room and I am pushed onto the bed. I can make out Lin’s features like her raven black hair, tanned skin, and ridiculously fit body. Every working brain cell I have is screaming at me to make her stop. But my worn-out brain is no match for the sensation of her lips on my neck, my chest, and lower. I have abandoned all pretense of uncertainty. The consequences will probably be damnable but my ability to be worried about them has been stripped from me. I smile down at Lin, who responds by naughtily commenting, “The Mob wants Max dead.” My eyes pop open and I hurriedly get off the bed. I need space to think, and I can do it with Lin’s head at my crotch. “What the fuck do you mean,” I demand angrily, “that the Mob wants Max dead” The Mob wants important people that can hurt them dead; Max doesn’t fit the fucking bill!” “Because Max is an idiot that’s why!” Lin bursts out with tears coming to her eyes. “The moron slept with the Bosses daughter and got her pregnant. He didn’t know who she was at the time, so he told her that he’d be a father and pay child support but wasn’t interested in being in a relationship. Turned out the bitch was insane, she killed herself and her dad blames Max.” I blink at the long winded explanation. I wasn’t surprised that Max would be enough of an idiot to get a girl pregnant, but getting the daughter of a Mob Boss pregnant was a new level of stupid. “Alright,” I reply while pacing around the bedroom, “but then why was the shooter shooting at me?” Lin shrugs, “I guess you were just at the wrong place at the wrong time. But listen, I want you to help Max out of this. He’s had to hide out here before, it’s like the Mob made some sort of game out of this. They try to kill him, he hides and they look for him. After a month they stop and he can stop laying low, but a couple of months later they start hunting him again. The last time they followed him to our old hangout and shot up the place while we ran for it. That’s when Peter banned Max from coming here.” I gap at her wondering if she understands just watch she is asking. She wants me, the guy whose ass was nearly kicked by a another guy with a skull tattoo on his forehead, to help a poor detective with creepy sexual habits to fight the mob. “I don’t think so,” I reply slowly as I make my way towards the door. “I have had enough people trying to kill me for a lifetime. I am sorry that Max is in the shit he’s in but there is nothing I can do for him. In fact you should tell him to leave town, its probably the safest thing for him.” “Unless they decide to kill him for trying to leave the city,” she points out, and I am having a hard time finding an argument against that. “Besides, that still leaves the question of who tried to kill you with that bomb, and you probably can’t afford to higher another detective right now. So think of it more like a mutual arrangement to help each other out of a major jam.” My mouth works furiously as I try to counter her argument, but it’s pretty solid. If Max leaves the country I have nowhere to stay. I can’t pay rent and higher a new detective, and if whoever planted the bomb in my apartment wants me dead I am pretty much screwed. “Fine,” I say defiantly, “but if we get shot at again I am using Max as my human shield.” Lin laughs at me as I stalk out the door but I wasn’t joking. If we get killed I refuse to be the first one to go down.
