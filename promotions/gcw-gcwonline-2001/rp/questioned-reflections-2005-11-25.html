Questioned Reflections

25 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/25/05 You’d think being stuck in the back of a luxurious limousine, for a drive from New York to Philadelphia, could hardly be considered a bad thing. Yet there I was, alone, sitting on rich Italian leather, with only bad thoughts pacing through my mind. What the hell just happened? Not more than two hours ago I was exactly where I wanted to be. I was in a luxury box, enjoying the torment of two of my foes. Then it all spiraled out of control on me. How? Perhaps you might consider it a bet that had gone awry. Even though I had played coy for the last month, everyone knew the match at NC-17 would be inevitable. I’d had the advantage for the last two months. I had the mental edge. Then, in an instant, it was shattered and momentum shifted. How? No matter which direction I took my mind in, the question was waiting around every dark corner. There was no avoiding it. The only thing I could to do was confront it. Had I crossed the line this time? That much was obvious. However, it didn’t make sense. I’d been crossing the line for the past twenty-two years. And more recently, I’d been crossing it on a weekly basis. How? How had it ended up like this? No, the war was not over, but I couldn’t believe I’d just lost one of the key battles. Momentum was huge, and now there was no way I could get it back before Sunday. “GET UP! GET UP YOU SON OF A BITCH!” The words still echoed in my ears. I had received a beating there was no doubt about that. And the bastard had humiliated me too! In front of thousands of people, he had degraded me! Turned me into a stumbling oaf! How? I took a deep breath and tried to calm down. The mini bar opposite of me looked very inviting. The rich mahogany surrounding the liquor only made the premium scotch that much more enticing. I quickly dropped a couple ice cubes into a glass and poured three fingers. It helped. I glanced out the window. We were coming up to the New Jersey turnpike and I could see the Manhattan skyline in the distance. As much as I hated to admit it, that first glimpse of New York always sent a shot of adrenaline rushing through my bloodstream. It’s the lights. I thought fondly. But that wasn’t the only reason. This was the home of Madison Square Garden. That point wasn’t lost on me. Hell, I won my World title here. What the hell is wrong with you? I quickly snapped out of my daydream. What was I doing? Reminiscing like some sixty-year-old man. No, there were much more pressing issues at hand. I hadn’t earned this pleasant reflection period yet. While I sat there, trying to figure out my plan for Sunday, the driver came over the intercom. “The Sheraton, sir?” He asked. I pressed the black knob a few inches away from me. “Of course! Where the hell else would you take me, Motel 6?” I hissed back at him. He had asked an innocent enough question, but I wasn’t in the mood to placate to that old fool. And besides, he knew exactly where we were going. I stayed at the Sheraton in Times Square every time I was in New York. That senile bastard had interrupted my train of thought. My mind began to drift again. I remembered my first trip to the Big Apple. It had started off so well, but had ended with me being scraped off the hood of Rich Rollins? car. It had been my first match. Tonight’s events had brought the memories back in a flurry. Both nights I’d left the arena with the same feelings boiling in my gut. But you came back. I always came back. No matter how many people questioned my ethics, none of them dared to question my determination. Did that make me a good guy? I thought about this for a moment. The answer, of course, was a resounding “no”. Though that would be the same answer if I asked myself whether I really gave a damn or not. I didn’t get into this to become anybody’s hero. I thought bitterly. At least I had the integrity to admit I was in this business for myself. Everyone was, whether they admitted it or not. Some just took different roads to get there. What’s the plan for Sunday? That little miserable thought refused to be kept down for long. It came charging into the front of my mind, rebounded, and made a return trip. Of course I had no simple answer to shut it up with. WHAT’S THE PLAN FOR SUNDAY?! It screamed at me. I had no idea. It wasn’t mere revenge I was looking for. This was a chance for redemption. I’d been humiliated, and nothing but a complete and indisputable victory would do. There was nothing more I could do at this point. He was entering my environment, my jungle. The odds were on my side. I just had to play them.
