Made In America

9 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/9/05 I sat down comfortably in a red, white and blue striped booth. Apparently this was a patriotic diner. Perhaps they served freedom fries with their beef dip. What would they rename the au jus though? I made a mental note to ask the beefy waitress when she took my order. The diner smelled like stale tobacco but there was a sign that clearly stated smoking would not be tolerated. A scrawny line cook in the back looked like he was in no position to be making such demands. What would he do if one of those Hell’s Angels types in the corner suddenly decided to light up? There would only be one position to take at that point. Fear. Jesus. I thought. What if they involve the police? This was, after all, California. For every bleeding-heart liberal from Hollywood, there was a biker and a blue-collar man. I looked up to see what the special was. The sign read Tuna melt for $2.99, but anyone with any common sense would stay the hell away from that. And there was another problem with that. The sign made no mention of fries whatsoever. What if they’ve boycotted fries altogether? But I dismissed the thought as ludicrous. The waitress waddled wearily over to my booth. “Can I help you?” She asked tiredly. Help me? You can’t even help yourself. I quickly decided against offering this opinion to her. “Yes’I will have the beef dip with french fries.” She nodded and jotted down the order. Clearly this establishment hadn’t reached the point of being completely unreasonable. I hadn’t gone far enough South for that. Either that or she just wasn’t in the mood to argue with a 300-pound man. “Anything to drink with that?” Beer. I thought momentarily. Checking my watch, however, I noticed it was only noon and decided otherwise. “Just water.” I responded after an unusually long moment of indecision. She smiled politely and waddled back from whence she came. It had already been a week since the last WorldWide but the feeling of utter satisfaction hadn’t dissipated. Everything had gone just right. Any night you could humiliate a man and convince a woman she came onto you, while she was under the influence of what many would consider an impressive cocktail of sedatives, could be chalked up as a big W. How can a man possibly top something like that? Well maybe Lestatt Knight, of all people, had come up with the right answer. It would make sense, he was the only who could possibly match me when it came to the disturbingly twisted gene. The difference was that Lestatt could be considered a victim of extraordinarily terrible situations. I was born this way. What can I say? Nobody gets me. The waitress brought over my water. I almost lost my appetite at the sight of it. I can’t say whether it was the brownish tint or the sludge like texture of it that brought my stomach up to my throat. “Excuse me.” I called after the waitress. She hurried back as fast as her celluloid legs would carry her. “Yes?” What do you mean, “yes?” LOOK AT THIS SHIT!!! However, I didn’t even want to think about what they’d do to my food if I complained, so I kept my comments to myself. “Would I be able to get a bottle of Coke?” I asked. She smiled again and headed off in the direction of a toothless man across the room. It was Lestatt’s birthday this week. Which, to most twenty-six year old vampires, isn’t cause for a spectacular event, but who am I to judge? I’ve never been fully aware of their mythic customs anyway. Nevertheless, the champ felt like celebrating by taking over full control of WorldWide. I wasn’t sure which bozo in the board room came up with this idea, but I was pretty sure that person would be unemployed next week, and not because of the Bush administration’s economic policies. Lestatt, in a rare showing of competency, managed to put together a solid show. It included a classic six-person tag match with the champ overseeing everything as the ref. Much to the delight of every stockholder in Caldera Enterprises, he made sure to include me in that match. And, I’m sure to the delight of Marauder; I’m facing three other men, including Marauder. Big deal. If that poor fool can even walk to the ring, he’s going to have bigger problems than a banged up knee. What’s that smell? I wondered as the aroma of burnt toast and poorly cooked meat lingered in the air. The answer came quickly, as the waitress plopped down an extremely unappealing “beef dip” in front of me. Something’s missing. I thought with a glance at the plate in front of me. “Something wrong?” The waitress asked insincerely. I had forgotten she was still standing there. “Where’s the au jus?” I asked, puzzled. “We don’t serve French food here. It’s our way of saying “thanks” to the troops.” She responded with a tone of patriotism. I wasn’t entirely sure which part of her statement made was more ridiculous, so I sat there, completely dumbfounded. Obviously she wasn’t hanging on my response, and she went back to the toothless man. Shaking my head in disbelief, I dropped a ten on the table and left.
