Date: 6/23/2074 86 Years Old Death Saturday, June 23rd, 2074 12:30 am All good things must come to an end. Despite what we think, that saying will always stay true. No matter how hard we fight to etch our names in stone for the future generations to remember; they will always forget. Not a singular event will be remembered; but it only takes one to define a life. Herein lies the final chapter of Clyde Fox’s life. He’s eaten his last skittle… drank his final milkshake… and flung his last macaroni noodle. It’s time to face something that every life deals with eventually. This is the death of Clyde Fox… The gym was pretty well vacated. There were the obvious tilted seats and a random arrangement of garbage spread on the floor. All signs of a good night. It was a nice escape for most people. The fluorescent lighting flickered as a huge shadow walked over. It was none other than the amazing “Russian Rumbler” himself coming over to give a big thank you to his comrade. “Good show man,” Yaroslav Boris congratulated in his thick Russian accent as he patted a gentleman’s shoulder with his weathered hand. He had the hands of what could have been mistaken for a war veteran. Very large, cut up bucket hands on the end of tree trunk like arms. Comparable to that of a 20th century comic book relic known as Colossus. His broad shoulders seemed to swallow every room he’d enter. The bigger the room, the wider the guy seemed to get. Forget bowling ball biceps, Boris had thick car tires stacked under his shirt. Nicest Russian wrestler ever though. He wouldn’t hurt a fly even though he could bend steel rods with his bare hands. A career laborer, Boris has experienced an astonishing amount of jobs. However his favourite is also his passion. It has always remained the same; good ole wrastlin! “In my country,” Boris started, “They would have thrown rose petals in the air for the kind of performance we put on tonight you know?” Boris rubbed his hands together as his comic book chunkiness served as the focal point. His square jaw was even more visible under the lights. His dark stubble poked through in spots as his dark brown eyes accented his face. The old man grumbled as he turned from his brown trench coat and replied. “You were great rookie,” The old man said as his voice scratched away. By no means was Boris a rookie. But once you get a certain age, everyone below you is a tyke. The guy’s still a modern day beast in the ring. Boris was unique to say the least… not like those streaky P-O-M W impersonators. Real wrestlers hate those Proto-Omni-Machine Wrestlers. Running most places out of business these days. It’s truly become a sad time in the early 70’s. But with solid guys like Boris still plugging away on the independent circuit, there’ll always be a hope for authentic wrestling. “Really? Well I hope I did well enough for you,” Boris implied, “I think we work well together. Maybe next time we can fight through the crowd.” Boris laughed as he put his dukes up, playfully challenging the old man. “Depends,” The old man started, “I’m running low on shelter points and I know my next credit pulse isn’t coming in until the wave after next.” 2074 hasn’t been all that kind. It really started back in 2070 when wrestling wasn’t the only thing getting the curtain call. Just about everything is on the fringe. The economy hasn’t been this bad since the dip that started in 2008 and ended with the bottoming out crash of 2013. The glory years have long since passed. The big fat robotics corporations now own the car companies too. Producing over stock on nearly every model of car, which out populates the people. They create demand by investing in robot driven cars and sell them off to the smaller dealers. Oil hasn’t existed on the planet in over 30 years… clothing has been relegated to a handout policy only… and food as well as money has dwindled in both availability and value. “That’s too bad my friend,” Boris sympathized, “I’m sure things will cheer up. When I was working in the coalmines, I had very little money but I made it work. I’m confident you can too! Besides… you taught me everything I know. I won’t let anything bad happen to you.” Everything has changed. And what hasn’t is what most people know. The only thing is, everything is about to change anyways. Governments are fortifying and cracking down on rebellious behaivour. The world is being forced to unite under such harsh circumstances. The new idea of salvation depends on blowing everything up to restart anew. “Thanks,” The old man said dispassionately, “But you don’t have to stick around here if you don’t want to. I’m actually pretty nimble for my age you know.” The old man completely turned around and pulled his matching brown fedora off of his head. His hair was scraggly and long. His stubble was much to thick to even be labeled as such. His ruffled look showed wisdom but most of all, his age. Clyde Fox; age 86. “Heh,” Boris remarked, “I know how fast you are little one. But I would never abandon a man who brought me to such a beautiful country. I still don’t know how to pronounce it correctly.” Old Fox carefully placed his Fedora back on as he gathered his belongings behind him. A few custodians enter the gym and begin sweeping as both Boris and Old Fox exit with their duffle bags. “Canada. You’re in Canada my friend,” The voice of Old Fox grew with a little energy, “It won’t matter soon enough though. Not with all that convergence conspiracy and crap going on.” “HEY!” A voice rang from within the small gym. Fox and Boris stop and turn to notice a custodian waving them over. They walk back in interest of what the custodian has summoned them for. “Is there a problem?” Boris inquired. “No, not really,” The older, scruffy custodian said, “I was just wondering if this mess you guys call an event was worth it. I mean come on guys… it’s twenty freaking seventy four! No one cares about wrestling anymore! Not to mention it’s the fifth show I’ve cleaned for you guys since coming to this gym and not once have I gotten a gratuity!” Boris rubbed his thick chin as he pondered what to do. Old Fox is quick to step in. “Sorry but we’re having enough trouble staying afloat ourselves. If you want, you don’t have to clean it up… we can do it,” Fox directed. The custodian motions over to his partner as his replies. “Nah that’s alright. I need whatever wage I can get. Oh… umm I think someone left this here,” He said as he picks up a musky looking leather jacket off a nearby chair. “Thanks,” Fox says as he takes it, “I’ll put it in the lost and found.” “You two take care now. Wouldn’t want anything happening to the talent now would we?” The custodian half joked in a cocky voice. Old Fox slung the jacket over his shoulder as him and Boris finally exit. And that’s what it has all come down to. What seemed like a disgruntled employee really embodied a microcosm of society’s failure. Families would have babies just in hopes that they had a daughter that they could sell to the rich CEOs… meager job holders were always hungry for more work or riots would breakout… it seemed like as Fox aged, the whole world around him did as well. Deteriorating with every step the old man took. Ready to die side by side. Cla-cling! The dog tags slapped against each other and Boris’ chest as they walked down the hallway and towards the front doors of the facility. The first tag said “Faith” on it and the second read “Respect.” Two rules that Boris bent over backwards to enforce in his life. Deciding to be different from the flow of complainers that has become known as today’s society. Boris gets to the door first and holds it open for Old Fox to trek through. “Sooooo,” Boris stated, “It’s Friday night in beautiful Kanaday and let me guess… you have nothing planned, no?” Fox throws his arms up in comedic style as a rare smile breaks over his mouth. “You read me like a bookie book rookie,” Old Fox joked. “Hmmmmm, well… I promised Ivana I’d take her to the pods tonight. You’re always welcome to come comrade,” Boris invited. Old Fox stood there, back against the wall and in exhaustion. “Thanks Boris but I’m going to have to pass this time. I don’t want to cause a scene like I did last time… nearly cost you guys your spot at the pod,” Fox reflected. The pods are one of the few places that hold some entertainment value in the world. It’s equal to the bar and club scene of the early parts of this century. The pods were made to cut down on violence and shootings at clubs because communities were getting scared. Picture a giant common area surrounded by reserved pockets, lounges… or as they were commonly referred to by, ‘pods.’ “Nonsense comrade. That waitress deserved to be pinched in her beee-hind,” Laughed Boris. The two shared in a moment’s fun just as a tricked out F Supreme Mega GTO Ford Crusher pulls up in the parking lot. “That must be Ivana,” Boris said with excitement, “Well if you don’t want to come to the pods, then let Ivana and I at least take you home safely, no?” Old Fox rubbed the back of his neck as he conjured up what to say. “Thanks, but I’m alright, really. Ruby should be here in a few minutes anyways. She’s coming right from Rachel’s jirotation recital anyways,” Fox replied. Jirotation has come a long way since its conception in late 2044. It’s a kid’s sport that mixes both musical ability with ones own body movements… hence the name jirotation. Every school has their own jiro coordinator and every once in a while they’ll hold recitals to see who advances to the next level of jirotation. “Are you sure?” Boris asked, “It’s suppose rain pretty hard my friend.” Old Fox nods profusely as Boris accepts his master’s wishes. “Alright, alright. Well have a safe night and I’ll give you a hi-me tomorrow,” Boris promised as he walked out from the marquee and into the spitting rain before jumping into the vehicle. A loud, pumping bass can be heard from within the car as it zooms off into the distance leaving Old Fox by himself. “There goes one of the finest friends ever,” Fox mumbled, “Going to the pods… ha. I’m too old for that place.” The rain starts to crash down with more ferocity as Old Fox stands under the shelter of the building. The rain makes a sweet pitter-patter noise once it hits objects all around the building. It’s so peaceful, yet it’s still a dangerous storm all at the same time. How can something that can hold so much power to destroy, be so soothing to the soul at the same time. The rain calmed Fox’s inner being if for at least a brief moment. But that was more than enough for him seeing that any quick escape from his life of pain is welcomed. “Urgh,” Fox grunted as he pulled out a cigarette from his trench coat pocket. Flick, flick, foom. His lighter finally sparked up as he lit the smoke in his mouth. After taking a puff, Old Fox hacked away as he never did fully adjust to the smoking life. His body has been battered, broken and torn all for the entertainment of his lifelong friends… the fans. If you asked him if he’d do it any differently he’d laugh at the very idea. Three spinal disc surgeries, two skull cracks, and a prosthetic leg later and Fox was still living it up in the ring. The time alone always allowed Fox to think about things like that. “Oww,” Fox groans as he reached down his right leg in pain. Completely cut his right leg off from below the knee down during a table saw match in 2067. He vowed never to do anything as stupid as that ever again. Fox’s body had become its own museum of scars. It’s own sanctuary of fond and brutal memories. Something he’d cling on for the duration of his elderly life cycle seeing that there was nothing else worth living for in this world unless you were beyond loaded financially. “Hey!” A voice echoed from around the corner. The smoke rose from Fox’s cigarette slowly as he pulled his head around the corner. Sssssssssssssss! Fox jolted back quickly as his cigarette was struck from his mouth. He tries to control his breathing as he realized that he peeked his head out from the overpass too far and his smoke was hit by the rain. “Dammit,” Fox moaned, “That was my last rette.” The cigarette lies on the ground, engulfed by raindrops, ruining it completely. Not being as stealthy this time, Fox walks around the corner and into the rain. The pitter-patter carries with Fox as raindrops splash off his fedora. The shadows are deep as he walks down the narrow alley between the school gym and the massive yard shed. One light pans downward giving very little overall glow. The light is accompanied by a side door that slowly creeps to a close. “Heto?” Old Fox grumbled. Click, clack, click, clack. The sounds of footsteps slice through the water as a dark shadowy figure steps in. It’s a man by Fox’s deciphering skills. Wearing coveralls similar to the custodians in the gym. His sleeves were rolled up just above his triceps but very little could be discerned about the rest of the man. His shoulder caught most of the light which gave Fox a vague idea of things. “Did you call for me?” Old Fox questioned. The figure folds his arms as a small red dot near his face lights up. It was an already lit cigarette. Smoke rose up and fought the rain like today’s society against depression. A losing struggle as the rain easily overpowered everything in its path. The man took one final puff before discarding the smoke by his side. “Yeah,” The man’s scratchy voice echoed, “I did.” Fox takes a deep breath as he stares down the faceless man. “Well I’m here. Can I help you or something? My ride will be here momentarily,” Old Fox advised. “Heh,” The man started, “That won’t be an issue. This will be quick.” The man stepped forward one pace as Fox compensates with a step back. “What do you want? Who are you?” Old Fox inquired. “I know a lot more about you than anyone else,” The man stated, “I’ve known about you since you hit the big time. Everything.” The man motions with hands but it’s too hard to see exactly what he’s doing. “Let me enlighten you,” He said, “Clyde Fox, born August 1st, 1987 to a set of fucked up parents. You were lost, then found, and then lost again yadda, yadda, yadda. End up training with Daddy to try and continue the family business. You get a lucky break in 2007 and sign a deal. I know everything that happened to you that made you what you are.” The man takes a breath and continues. “Everything. Your struggle to the top, your World Championship reign, your issues. Even when you left GCW in 2039,” The man turned, “It was simply called ‘The Incident.’ But that didn’t stop you. I even wonder if that ‘Miracle of 2055’ was real or just a stunt you pulled off as a quick money grab. Yeah… I’ll believe that a retard for life just genetically matured to his proper age and wrote a book about it. It’s astonishing you’re still alive today. What? How old are you?” “86,” Old Fox cautioned, “But my body feels hundreds of years old.” “But yet you speak, look and act like you’re just brushing into the sixties to me. How do you do it? Let me guess,” The man played, “You’re probably getting doma ray treatment at the clinic aren’t you!? You probably have all the money in the world yet you put on this charade for pity! It’s people like you that make me sick! You fuckers are the reason why the world is like it is today!” Obviously delusional, the man’s voice becomes consumed with rage. Old Fox quickly staggers backwards in fear. He hadn’t ever meant to create a backlash with his life events, they just sort of happened. It just seemed like when something great in Fox’s life happened, something equally evil was brewing just to create a balance. Fox’s scarred body held more than just physical reminders; he had emotional ones too. “I… look,” Old Fox tried to reason, “You need help my friend. You don’t understand. If you are a fan than you are my friend. Let me help you.” The man stepped backwards as he was caught by more of the light. He ran his fingers through his grey hair in frustration. This man was clearly suffering from some sort of psychotic episode. Becoming more and more familiar with his surroundings, Old Fox readied himself just in case of an impending attack. “Help? Friend?” The man laughed, “I’m far from both. I was… past tense… WAS your biggest fan throughout the years. Then I noticed how much of a fucking sellout you are! I thought you were truly special. But just like this disgusting world, all humans are flawed. You are just the epitome of it all… and it’s my job to set you free.” The man stops rubbing his head as he glares over at Fox. “You. You washed up sonuvabitch! This is destiny! This moment. We were both meant to be here at this exact second in time!” The man conveyed. The man puts his palms to the sky and embraces the rain. The rain has drenched both men in its grasp as if consuming them into their own world. It was strictly between Fox and his nemesis. Finally, Fox could face everything that ever dragged him down… too bad it was at this point of his life. The leather jacket on Fox’s shoulder had been ruined by the rain. After noticing how soaking wet he really was, Old Fox throws the leather jacket to the ground. “My destiny has long been determined,” Fox replied, “I’ve lived long enough not to be afraid of anything anymore.” “Good!” The man screamed as the rain poured down as hard as ever creating even more noise, “Then it’s time to set you free once and for all. The world will be a better place without Clyde Evan Fox.” The man reaches down to what is assumed his pocket. He pulls out a shiny white object. It glistens in both the light and rain. The man smirks evilly as he points the object at Old Fox. “Any last words before I blow your brains out?” The man asked as he turns his body into the light revealing a gun in his hand. Old Fox puts his arms up as if being stuck up in an old western shooter. The rain pounds down around and on him as he stares at the man from under the visor of his soaked fedora. “Do what you will do anyways. I have nothing to say. I will forever be remembered by my friends… my family. I know who I am and I forever will be Clyde Fox,” Fox defied. The man’s hand shook as he jittered in laughter. “Ha! That’s where you’re wrong old man! You won’t be remembered. No one will be! Not in this world; not in any! Society doesn’t care. No one does! You’re death is nothing more than an execution of a resource sucking time bomb. It’s become survival of the fittest and I intend on living by weeding out the twerps like you!” The man screamed. Twerp. Twerp? Did that guy just call me a twerp? I’d expect something more timely like viral discharge, immune system hazard, omni prescent crutch or flagrantly gay washed up wrestler celebrity wannabe but twerp will work I guess. Heh… in all my years of… well in all my years, I’ve never been called a twerp by someone. Ah well, I guess I can scratch that one off the list of names I’ve been called. Good timing too seeing that this deranged psycho seems set on carrying out his threat. The man shakes but this time in intensity as he stares down his prey. FLASH! An odd light from a couple hundred yards back catches both men’s attention. Seizing the opportunity, Fox rushes the man in hopes to disarm him. Old Fox is quick to grab the gun but the man struggles with him, finally kicking him back. “You’ve said your piece. Now your time has come. FUCKING DIE CLYDE FOX!” The man cried. Everything stopped. The rain. The screaming. The noise. Everything. The man stood there with his mouth still open like a fierce tiger lunging towards its prey. The gun pointed straight at Fox who was frozen falling backwards. The man’s arm was projected jet straight from his body. His grip on the handgun was unparallel in strength. Each raindrop stood still in its place. Nothing even needed to happen. The past had all led up to this point and the future was imminent. This was just a necessary event in time. Slower than a snail, the man’s finger loomed over the trigger. The raindrops started to snake their way down ever so slightly moving only fractions at a time. The man pulled his finger down on the trigger, as like a time bomb ticking toward detonation, the only sound that could be heard was the clicking of the action. The hammer launched backwards in a huge roar. Then it lunged forward sending slow moving sparks everywhere. Kaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaa-Ommmmmmmmmmmmmm. The barrel rocked back and forth in slow motion, sending waves of rain debris and an empty bullet shell to the side as the recoil quaked the arm of the man. A single bullet. One solitary bullet was sent piercing through the air destroying everything in its path. Old Fox looked at it as it was streaming towards him in what seemed like an eternity. Death was coming and it was riding a gallant silver chalice in the form of a chain and ball of doom. Piano music please. Piano music would be nice to accompany this. I always imagined dying to piano music. A big symphony or something. I wonder what it feels like to be shot. I don’t think I could ever imagine it, yet I’m milliseconds away from experiencing it yet it might never get here. I don’t think my Mom would like me getting shot bless her heart. In what takes years to build up can be wiped away from the world in seconds. Life is truly the strangest thing I’ve ever got to experience. The friends, the family, the fun times. Life. My life… As if Fox embraced his impending doom instead of rejecting it; he smiled. Clyde Fox smiled. Year 1 was quite a trip. Getting caught in a snowbank and being found by foster parents wasn’t exactly an ideal start to life. At least they were normal people though. I needed that. Year 2. Heh. It was not my fault that I hit Mom’s glasses into the pan. I was dropped that day. It was both the first and last time I was ever in the kitchen during infancy. Year 7. I was a baseball all-star. Hitting home runs and leading my team in the ultimate team sport. I sure was fantastic at it. Year 9. Bedwetting still haunted me. I’m glad I finally grew out of it when I turned 28. Year 16. Got my first woody and it was in class too! Good thing I didn’t have to solve that math problem. Year 20. GCW. My first real home. Gosh, do I ever miss everyone. Year 86. The end to my story. The end to my life. …BANG! Just like that, everything flew back to normal speed. The gun went off fast and furiously as Fox’s chest absorbed the shot. The thunderous pounding of the rain poured down quicker than before as smoke exuded from the tip of the gun. Old Fox twitched and shrieked as he fell to his knees bleeding. The man walked into the light. His face was as clear as day to Old Fox. Fox looked up at his assailant as it drew all the breath right out of him. “Ahh… you!” Fox weeped, “Fr�"friend!” Old Fox’s eyes slowly closed as he fell from his knees to his side, splashing the accumulated water around him. Suddenly flashing lights and police sirens flood the scene as the man’s head moves on a swivel. He then turns and says a final word before running off. “Friend. I’m not your friend… anymore,” The man finalizes. The body of Clyde Fox lies on its side as blood trickles from the chest and mixes in with the water. A body that had given so much for so long to so many has finally stepped its last step, blinked its last time, and taken its final breath. Sadly, and profoundly, the body of Clyde Fox was on the ground dead. Old Fox’s body, once full of life was now indeed… lifeless. THE END
