Second Chances.

11 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/11 Alarms blare. Signal flares shoot up and off and out and my heart's pounding out my throat to the point where I can taste copper on my uvula. Tinted orange with a hint of blood, liquor and iron filtered through panic. Fast mind, fast find, always gets you out of a bind. Twain palms clench together and squeeze water out from under the skin and nails drive into each until little crescent moons appear, four per hand, and signal the end of a long, long night. I've never been more awake than now. I've never been more alert than I need to be right now. More ready. More conscious of what's going to happen, what's come to pass. And with those bone white hands reopened, peppered with pink and red marks of tight fists that are now simply memories, they're trembling because they're afraid of what's coming. Like a train down a tunnel, and there's nowhere to run or hide. Just a bright white light of truth barreling down on top of them. And me. 'Johnny? Oh my God Johnny, how are you?' Arms around my neck. An embrace that could tighten and squeeze the life out of me in an instant, instant gratification. Instantly cinch shut and then it'd be over, done, and skipped like a crack in the disk, a crack in the system that I could slip through. Be there, looking at Jesus, and say, 'Aren't I the lucky one? I got out of it and it wasn't even my fault.' And Jesus could say, 'What do you mean? You dug your own hole.' And I'd say, 'Yeah, but she's the one that killed me before I had to fess up and see anything through.' And Jesus would say, 'Wish I had that on my side. I had to see it through.' And I'd go, 'That's the difference between you and me, Christ. I'd rather die out of spite than suffer and lose my style. Lose what I'm all about. You can't drag me through the mud if I sink down in it and disappear first. Johnny Borealis, the only man that could disappear before his body rotted and started to reek. And now they built a statue to me already.' And then Jesus would look at me long and hard and say, 'Pride. That's the seventh circle of Hell, I believe.' He'd pull a lever that he's got next to him and wave goodbye all sad and move on to the next guy in line. And I'd go down still a hero. But it's just a gentle hug. It won't kill me or stop what's happening. 'Johnny...aren't you going to say hi?' If I was smart, I wouldn't. '...hi?' 'Oh come on, Johnny. Don't say you don't remember me. ... ...Sarah!' Furrow the brow. Each ridge and wrinkle a secret message sent to her via radio wave telling her that this is not the time nor place and please please please turn around and leave because a big part of me wants this to end right now. But she's too happy, too excited to see the man that fell in love with her so long ago and she's not about to walk out of his life like she did back then. Like I should have done to her. Maybe if I hurt her, she'd hate me. But instead, she hurt me. And I still love her. And she's found out she loves me. And Kate's going to be overjoyed by this. 'Sarah! Of course I remember you. How could I forget?' It'd be like forgetting what a sunset looks like. Forgetting what it feels like to be cold. Or how to cause someone pain. It's not something you forget, for better or for worse, because there's no true good or bad about it. Nothing that you can staple to a sheet and hand to someone and say, 'There it is! I remember it!' No. I remember Sarah with a nod of my head and a quick exhale and a secret wish that I could go back eight years and stop myself from ruining two lives: mine and hers. And risking how many more? Kate's? My daughter's? It's time for a change, Johnny. A change from all this kind of crap. 'I was in the area because I have tickets to the show tomorrow night.' 'Show?' Twin blue eyes burn through me with their ice. Pain and pleasure melt away. 'Yeah. You know...tomorrow night.' '...' 'You're in it.' 'Oh! The, uh...the-the the match. Yeah. I, uh...the match.' Johnathan Fitzwillis. Johnny Borealis. Wrestler extraordinare. Main eventer. An idol, a god, a role model. A good guy and a bad guy and a foe and a friend and a tag partner and a loner. Get it in your head, Johnny. Tone down the alarm. You're not at home. Kate's not here to see this. Your past can die in the past and you can look Sarah in the eye and say 'Hello.' Hello, woman I fell in love with a long time ago. Hello, woman who had the same name as my mother. Hello, woman I connect with my mom. Woman I wish I could live in. Live in her bones. Live inside of her. Breathe and know only her as she floods my system with her ambrosia and phermones. Hello, woman whose name I called out in my sleep, over and over again. Hello, woman whose name I told Kate was for my mother. That I was having dreams of losing my mother. That I was calling out, 'Sarah. Sarah!' because I was holding my mother by a thread over a pit of death. When it wasn't that. It was holding my first love by her body over Johnathan Fitzwillis' wants and desires. Hello...woman that Kate named my daughter after. Thinking she was naming her after my mother, because she thought I missed and loved my mother so much that I called out her name in my sleep. I wasn't. I didn't. I was calling out your name, Sarah. ... I'm so fucked. 'Jesus, Johnny. How long's it been?' 'Eight years. Five months. Probably about a week and some change. Six or seven hours.' Her laugh is pure pain. Regret. What. Could have. Been. 'So you're going up against the two best, huh?' 'The...two best?' 'Michael Stevens. Seymour Almasy. Jesus, Johnny, don't you even check who you're going up against.' 'Well, of course I do. Yeah. Yes. I do. But, uhm...' Snap to, Johnny. Million dollar smile. Smile in the eyes. Smile behind them, let her know who you are. You're not that kid that she met on the beach. You're not that child that poured his heart out to her. You're none of those things that don't matter at the end of the day or at the end of a life when she looks back and lets out that golf ball sized sphere of white hot regret. You're Johnny fucking Borealis. Let her know it! 'It's two of the best...going against the best, Sarah.' Eyes me. Up and down. Lips pursed and eyes narrowed, she knows that I know. She wants me. But she also knows how much I want her. I can only wonder how much she knows beyond. 'One's the Global Champ. Took the belt from the greatest champion GCW's ever seen in the bloodiest match GCW's ever seen. Undisputed. The other's had a better record and a better showing at every showing than most people can claim after an entire lifetime of hard work. And...who's your partner?' 'Scott Taylor.' 'And...who is he?' 'He's a descent, well rounded, accomplished wrestler.' 'Uh huh. And what, exactly, has he accomplished?' Quick smile. Reach out and put your hand on her shoulder and pretend it doesn't burn. Doesn't burn all the way down to that secret area where things count and matters matter and the past comes back to bite you. Pretend you don't want to recoil and go hide under the bed, pull the sheets tighter to block out the light and wish the night away that you could go back. Go back and get what you could have had. What could have been. What's gone. Dead. Over with. 'The real question you should ask yourself...is what is Johnny about to accomplish?' 'And what's that?' '...I'm taking over the whole world, Sarah.' 'The whole world?' 'By storm. The age of sin is in. And I am everybody's-' 'Guilty pleasure. I know, Johnny. You think I don't know your interviews? Do you think I haven't memorized your bio? Bookmarked your website? Even that I, a mid twenties woman, have a poster of you on my wall and think about running my finger down that perfect chest and across those perfect abs and taking your perfect body and-' 'Whoa! That's really very-' Finger to the lips. She doesn't even need to 'shush' me. Just a purse of her lips and with her finger to mine it's kinda like she's kissing me quiet. Quiet after years of wishing, years of questioning. Don't even wonder about it, John. Because it's here. Right in front of you. 'Sarah...if you know anything about me...you know this can't happen.' 'What can't happen Johnny?' 'Kate-' 'Isn't your wife. Isn't your girlfriend.' 'Sarah-' 'Yes?' 'No. Sarah. My little girl.' 'What? You can't have sex because you're a father? An unattached father who has no restrictions and no reason not to go for the gold?' 'Go for the gold?' 'Yeah! Shit, Johnny...I see you after all these years. I take the time to find you, to see where and how you are. And all you can tell me, after all this time and work...is that you can't see a great friend that you almost spent your life with. Because you have a daughter.' Go for the gold... It's so easy to decide to go for it, isn't it? Be a success. Be the champ. Fuck the girl. Blaze of glory. And then you don't even realize it when it's looking at you, right in the face. Ice blue eyes have you frozen in your spot and a jaw's working to find out where it is, hanging so low you could trip over it. There is no noise. There is no time. There is only choice. In the ring, it's a world of rules and guidlines. Ones that are followed, ones that can be broken, and ones that always hold true. And one that has held true for all of time is that you don't get the gold, you don't get the blaze of glory, without reaching up and taking it when it comes your way. When you look around where you are and it's not good enough, when it's never good enough, and you can make as many half assed promises and resolutions for the dawning of the new era as you want, but that's not what matters. Because there's only one that needs to be made. And there's only one that counts. Johnny Borealis' list of resolutions: 1.) Just fucking get the job done. '...why don't you close the door, Sarah? I've got the night off before tomorrow's match, and I figure we could spend it getting to know each other. A decade's a long time to catch up.' 'Especially when so much of it has been spent waiting for one thing. One person.' She's let the seas calm. Alarms die back down and the fog's starting to clear. Looks like all the Argonots are going to make it back in to the harbor this day. 'If you spent so much time thinking about me, why'd you take off in the first place?' I can remember clear as cold water that summer day eight years ago. Boy falls in love with girl. Maybe a little too hard, maybe a little too fast. But it's tragic when you find out that you're soul mates with someone, especially in under a day. And in a matter of hours, I found out all I needed to know about her. And she was perfect me for me...where I knew everything about her, but she always kept me guessing. Relates to this sport, where you can know your opponent and know your ring, that's like knowing your partner that you dance the deadly dance with...but they always surprise you. It's always something new. And then she left. Just like that. Just like I did to Kate... Maybe that's why I love her. Because she did what I did, only she thought of it first. You know...it makes me think. Why can I remember that one day so well, when there are thousands I remember nothing about? So many islands lost in the mist. So many times where nothing happened. I merely existed. And all the smiles and all the tears shed in that sea of nothing don't matter. Those moments mean nothing. Just the big ones count, I guess. 'It was too much, Johnny. It was all so much at one time and finding out so much about a person and having them find out so much about you, it was...horrifying. Because I knew that there was nothing I could hide from you.' Couldn't hide. 'And now, Sarah, you've come back.' Couldn't run. But I guess you can't do either. Not from the big moments. The ones that stick out and the ones you remember. The door is shut and the mind is open. A book for her to read. Complete with incompletions, holes in the plot and bad character analyses. And it's unfinished, and she's aware. Dangerous as it is, she's ready to do it anyway. Story of my life.
