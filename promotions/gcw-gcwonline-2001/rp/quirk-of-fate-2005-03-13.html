Quirk of Fate

13 March 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/13/05 Somehow, Japan had more people than anywhere else in the world. That, of course, wasn’t a fact, but wandering the streets of the country, one could be easily persuaded of its truth. Especially one who had spent much time in it. Taking a day off from preparing for the biggest night of his life, Seymour Almasy walked the city streets of Sapporo, Japan, in search of adventure and excitement. That, or a cosplay convention. He hadn’t found the latter yet, so perhaps the former would come. For now, though, all that seemed likely was Seymour’s being crushed by the throng. “Ugh, hey!” he called out, futilely, as someone damn near stampeded over him from behind. Somehow, leaving the security of the hotel and room service for the streets of the city now seemed like an extremely poor idea. In truth, he had no idea WHY he’d decided on Sapporo for his excursion. He’d been to most of Japan’s major cities: Tokyo, Osaka, Kobe, even seen Hiroshima and Nagasaki, but there was something about Sapporo he liked that he couldn’t quite place. Maybe it was that noodle vendor he particularly enjoyed. Or the cuisine in general. Whatever it was, it had drawn him to the city today. Sighing, Seymour begin trying to work across the grain, towards a side street that seemed to be empty. “Come on, come on?” he whispered, trying to will himself there, so he could take a break from the throng of people and catch his breath. Finally. Free space. Seymour took a few great gulps of oxygen, adjusting the black leather trench coat that he wore. Not typical attire for Almasy, but it was good for going out when he didn’t want to be noticed. With his hood up, covering his long, white hair, he was far less recognizable. “Hmmm, doesn’t look like I’m going to get back in the queue,” he observed, watching as the crush of humanity only seemed to intensify. Shooting a glance over his shoulder down the side-street, he quirked his eyebrow. There, a few hundred yards away, was a wooden gate. Inscribed on it were Japanese characters, which Seymour did his best to translate. “A wrestling academy, eh” Hmmm’wonder if they’d let me in for a bit. It is rather cold out here?” A slight dusting of snow on this side street seemed to confirm that. It appeared to be the road less traveled, as only a few rows of footprints marred the snow, as opposed to the icky slush that characterized the sidewalk. Wordlessly, he gathered himself and walked for the gate. Standing there was a young man, presumably there to stand guard. “Who are you,” he asked as Almasy approached, in fluent Japanese. Seymour himself could not speak the language in anywhere near as refined a manner, but he did his best. “A simple traveler, of your profession,” he replied. “May I come in” It is rather cold outside?” “One moment, please.” With that, the young man turned, entering the gate, and jogging across what appeared to be a courtyard. Seymour simply waited, eyes looking through the now opened gate as the student disappeared behind another door. “Wonder why I’ve never seen this place before.” It was a fair thought, considering Seymour had walked this path a good number of times and never noticed this side street. A wrestling academy was a good thing to know the location of. It meant lots of opponents to test one’s skills against. The sort of thing that he needed before a test like Rampage. A few moments later, a figure returned through the door. This man, though, was not the boy from earlier. Clad in traditional attire, he had to be over fifty years old. “In great shape, though,” Seymour observed, watching him approach. His body moved with purpose, arms not flapping at his sides, but rather being held up, strong and firm. Closer and closer he approached, until he exited the gate, and snapped off a crisp bow, which Seymour graciously returned. “Welcome to my academy,” the other man said, gesturing behind him. “I am Toshiro Mifune. You are?” “A traveler,” Seymour repeated, remembering his reply to the presumed student. “Of your profession.” Mifune nodded, but looked closely at the trench coat wearing Almasy. Looking into his eyes, Toshiro held that gaze for a few moments, before nodding a second time. “Welcome then, traveler. My student told me that you would like to come in?” The Final Fantasy nodded. “If it wouldn’t be too much trouble, of course, sensei.” “I shall show you around, then.” With that, Mifune stepped forward, leading Seymour through the academy’s courtyard. “What style do you practice,” Mifune asked, as Seymour walked in step with him. That question gave Seymour pause. “It’s sort of difficult to explain,” he said, trying to figure out exactly what the hell it was that he DID use. “Aerial tactics. Submissions. Mainly those two’I’m not exactly the biggest guy in the world.” Mifune-san chuckled at that. “No, I suppose not. ?” he mused, opening another door for Seymour, leading into the academy proper. Toshiro led Seymour down another hallway, which ended in a large gymnasium sort of room. Front and center was a wrestling ring, with several students working out in and around the ring. “Care to step into the ring?” “Huh?” The question clearly caught Seymour off guard. Had he had his gear and such, he would have been more willing. Then there was the fact that he rather liked being anonymous? “If you need anything, it can be provided for you.” Seymour looked back to Mifune, and the calm, level look in his eyes. Seymour Almasy knew a challenge when he heard one. “A pair of tights would help,” he admitted, “considering I don’t have mine with me. Which of the students do you want me to step into the ring with?” “Students,” he asked, patting the Final Fantasy on the shoulder. “When did I ever say anything about students?” “You mean,” Seymour asked in response, looking at the owner of the academy in disbelief. “Do not underestimate me because of my age, Mr. Almasy.” So he knew. “I guess my disguise didn’t work so well then, huh,” Seymour said, sighing, pulling his hood back and letting long, white hair flow out behind him. Mifune shook his head. “You’re one of the more recognizeable figures in the sport. My condolences on your recent misfortunes.” “Thank you.” “So, you don’t recognize my name,” Mifune-san mused. “Interesting?” “Should I,” Seymour replied, getting a rather sinking feeling that he actually should. He looked at the larger, older man again, trying to place him without success. “Perhaps,” was Mifune’s immediate response. “Though, I think I know a way to jog your memory. Go into the locker room and change. We will have tights for you in a few seconds, and you will face some of my students.” “What about you,” he asked, not quite so willing to give up on the fact that he’d been essentially challenged. “If you still wish to face me afterwards,” Toshiro Mifune said, approaching the ring to talk to a few specific pupils, “I shall be more than happy to oblige.” ----------------------------------------- As promised, no sooner had he stepped into the locker room than a student had arrived, with a pair of simple black tights. Bowing and accepting them, Seymour retreated for a corner, taking a deep breath. “Haven’t worn anything like THESE in ages,” Seymour said, stripping out of his trench coat and other clothing. “Not since before I made it in UWF.” Seymour was somewhat used to his flashy attire. Thus, looking down at himself after he put the trunks on, he couldn’t help but feel a bit’naked. “Ugh,” he sighed, “black is SO not my color.” Still, he had some training to get in. Leaving his attire in a pile on the bench, he began to stretch, limbering himself up, all the while his mind reverted to the state of wrestling. Fun and games time was over. Serious work was at hand. The Rampage tournament had turned out to be an ordeal. Beating Kannon to qualify, losing to Shadow and Dawn for the right to be #1 seed, retaining his spot against three of GCW’s best, and pulling out a hard-fought win over LeStatt Knight had drained Almasy. And considering that if he wanted to win he’d have to go through Dawn and Shadow or Rollins, drained was a very bad thing to be. Judging himself ready to go, he stepped back out into the gym, only to find all eyes were on him. He felt self-conscious, somehow, in spite of the fact that all of the trainees were dressed in similar gear. Maybe it was his hair that made him stand out. Maybe it was the fact that he was a gaijin, an outsider. Or maybe he was just nervous. “There’s your opponent,” Mifune-san said, nodding firmly. Standing in the ring was a young man who looked roughly six feet tall, lean yet strong. The Final Fantasy smiled, nodded, and slid under the bottom rope. “Begin!” Before Seymour could quite react, the other man had shot behind Almasy in a go-behind, and was attempting some sort of suplex. Almasy responded with a quick elbow to the face, before bouncing off the far ropes. Seymour crouched as he ran, throwing his body into a tight spiral, launching into the spinning roundhouse he called the Jecht Shot. Caught. “Wh--? Seymour was stunned. He’d never had his kick caught by a TRAINEE before! Before Almasy had a chance to be more shocked, he had been promptly taken over by a Dragon Screw leg whip. Shooting a glance over his shoulder, Seymour looked at Toshiro. “Seem familiar,” the older man asked. The Final Fantasy simply grimaced. He had to admit it did. But from where? No time to worry about that now though. Not with the trainee approaching him, seemingly poised to drop him on his head. Up Seymour went, as his foe crossed his arms behind his ba-- “NO!” An anguished, tortured wail tore forth from Seymour’s throat. Only one other person had ever attempted such a move on him. And combined with that improbable counter from earlier? Seymour’s body went into seeming convulsions, struggling against his attacker, finally managing to wrench a wrist free, and yank himself away. “What’s wrong,” the academy’s proprietor asked, knowing even as he looked at the GCW star that the point had been gotten across. “I’ve taken three of those in one match before,” he said, voice shaky. “They had to fit me for a neck brace afterwards. The Japanese Ocean Suplex and I are not good friends.” Mifune simply nodding, waiting for the question that was likely inevitable. “Mifune-san, why does your student’s style resemble that of my opponent this Sunday?” Ah, there it was. Time to come clean. “Because, Mr. Almasy, I am the man who trained her.” Seymour looked at the surrogate Dawn in the ring, who nodded, and then back to Mifune. A somewhat defeated sigh came from deep within him, as he looked at the academy’s trainer. ??that means she’s probably here. Which, in turn, means I’d better get out of here before she finds me and I get decapitated.” “She’s not here. None of them are. If they were here right now, I would not have invited you in.” Well, that was a relief. Mifune wasn’t a sadist. “I can see that you are surprised,” he said, in sort of an obvious statement. “Come, we will leave the ring for a bit. Care for some tea?” Seymour could only nod dumbly. ----------------------------------------------------------- A half hour later, the Final Fantasy and the owner of the academy sat on a couch somewhere in one of the establishment’s living areas, nursing cups of tea. Seymour chuckled to himself at something, finally choosing to call the older man out on it. “Mifune’s School of Wrestling Excellence,” he asked, slightly dubious. “Someone’s confident.” “Someone can back it up,” came the reply, and even at the other man’s age, it was difficult for Seymour to doubt that fact. “Enough about that. Why are you here” I figured you knew that I taught Dawn, or that the others were her--? “Others,” Seymour moaned, heart dropping to somewhere around the vicinity of his spleen. “You mean Lia and Virgil and Vivica?” “Yes,” Toshiro said, with a nod. “They’re out as well. I am, err, aware of the issues that you have with them, and with Dawn.” Really, Seymour wasn’t especially surprised. Dawn had to talk to SOMEONE about all of this, and with her teacher here? “Advice for you, Mifune-san. If you ever want to try and woo a female, dressing up in black latex is NOT the best approach.” Toshiro remained quiet at that, but nodded respectfully. “I will keep that in mind.” A few moments passed, before Toshiro and Seymour looked at one another, saying the same thing at practically the same time. “She really wants this match.” They blinked, and chuckled a bit, as Seymour yielded the floor to his elder. “She’s been watching the tape of last time. Again and again and again. Trying to figure out where it went wrong.” Seymour shuddered. “I got lucky, essentially. She dropped me on my head a half-dozen times, but I saw one chance and took it. I was about as shocked as she was when the official’s hand hit the third time.” “Dawn hates to lose. She’ll be coming at you much harder this time. There’s so much at stake.” “Tell me about it,” Seymour said. “I’d figured that part out on my own. Since there’s a surrogate Dawn around here, would I be correct in assuming there’s a surrogate me that Dawn trains with?” “Oh, yes. A rather nice young man, he is.” “Can I meet him,” came the reply from the Final Fantasy. “I’m afraid not now. He’s currently recovering in the hospital wing.” Super. This was not shaping up to be one of the more fun nights of Seymour’s career, but then again, he hadn’t expected it to be. Even if he got by Dawn, Shadow or Rollins was awaiting him. But for once, Seymour Almasy wasn’t looking that far ahead. “I’m focused on her,” he said, suddenly. “Of course you are,” Mifune-san replied, a small smile forming at the corners of his mouth. “Rather’intently focused, from what she’s told me.” “I MEAN on the match,” Seymour fired back, eyes suddenly going intense. “Lately, I’ve been all looking ahead to this, and looking ahead to that, and feeling sorry for myself, but the ring is the one thing I can control. I can’t control whether it’s Shadow or Rollins. I can control how things go against Dawn.” Mifune merely nodded as Seymour continued talking himself through everything, both men pausing for a sip of tea. “She’s good. Very good. But I can’t let that bother me. I have to fight my match. My battle?” Suddenly, though, the prospect of her dropping him on his head even more times when she found out that he was here was not pleasant at all. “Err, can you please not tell Dawn that I was here?” Toshiro let out another small chuckle, and took another sip of his tea. “I’m afraid I can’t do that. Besides, even if I didn’t, one of the students would.” “Drat. Well, it was worth a shot?” The Final Fantasy was resigned to his fate. Rampage would be either the most glorious night of his life, or one of its most ignominious. If he could beat Dawn, he would have a chance at the GCW World Championship. If he could not, it would be another in the string of disappointments that had become his life. ? Shadow and Rich Rollins were possibilities. Dawn was a guarantee. “You regret much, do you not?” The words of Toshiro Mifune jarred Seymour from his dark thoughts, and forced him into a still more obscured portion of his mind. Of course he regretted. Had he just told Laura that he would marry her a few seconds earlier, she never would have-- “Yes,” he replied, without hesitation. Wordlessly, the master rose, beckoning Seymour to do the same. On limp legs, Almasy stood, looking into the intense eyes of his elder. “Come. It is time for our contest. When we are finished, I will give you the same advice that I gave Dawn.” The battle raged for a half hour that day, with neither the grizzled veteran nor the young high-flyer reigning victorious. The advice that followed was probably meant to follow a Toshiro victory, but it resonated nonetheless. “With each defeat comes an invaluable lesson. Only by learning our limits, do we learn how to surpass them. Do not regret, that only dulls the importance of the lesson.” Good advice, Seymour thought. Good advice for someone who wasn’t partially responsible for a loved one’s death. Good advice for someone who had failed to avenge her death. Good advice for Dawn McFarlane. For Seymour Almasy? Advice that, no matter how logical, how well-thought out, or how erudite, he simply could not follow. For until the world finds a way to bring back the dead, Seymour will regret. And he will, in the only way he knows how, in the Battle Arena? He will atone.
