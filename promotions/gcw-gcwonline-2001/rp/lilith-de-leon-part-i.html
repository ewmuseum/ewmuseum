Lilith de Leon (Part I)

Date: 4/15/11-4/18/11 Monday, April 18, 2011 2:07 AM Eastern Daylight Time The InterContinental Harbor Court Baltimore, Maryland His name was Jordan Clarke, and he worked the night shift at the front desk of the InterContinental Harbor Court Hotel in downtown Baltimore. It was a position Jordan had volunteered for – and his friends never let him forget it. They might have missed him during their weekend benders, but his shift differential brought him up to just over eighteen bucks an hour, and you can buy a lot of beer for eighteen dollars an hour. Besides, the job was easy when the weather cooperated. The hotel was maybe a fifteen-minute drive from Baltimore-Washington International Airport, but virtually no commercial flights came in after midnight unless a blizzard delayed them, so he didn't have to deal with crowds. In fact, he spent most nights surfing the web or watching DVDs on his laptop, save the occasional call for something easy like extra pillows, and he'd grown complacent. Jordan would even say he'd come to like it, that he looked forward to the peace and quiet he found most nights. Tonight wasn't going to be one of those nights, as the elevator chimed just after two in the morning, and the shouting started before the doors were fully open, a rather animated (and underdressed) teenage girl shrugging off the staying hand on her elbow. "Nein!" she chirped – it was the kind of voice Jordan heard everyday. Entitled. Spoiled. It wasn't usually in German, though. "Dieses ist nicht--" "You can't expect them to fix it at two in the--" the man at her side protested feebly, reaching out his hand again as the blonde stalked away. "--morning," he sighed, stepping quickly to follow. The blonde's steps were short, sharp stabs into the hardwood floor. Jordan tapped the spacebar to pause his movie, levering his laptop shut to favor the oncoming girl with his brightest smile. "How can I help you?" She looked no more nineteen, which meant Jordan figured she was sixteen, but he hoped she was eighteen, with a head full of curly, straw-blond and brilliant blue eyes that were wide and deep – the kind someone more poetic than him might compare to a clear night sky. Jordan was too busy staring at her legs to notice, however, bared by bright red boy shorts, the kind she might wear to bed. "Vhat ist thist?" she demanded as she approached the desk. Her accent was so thick that it was nearly incomprehensible. "You promised..." She trailed off with a frustrated sigh, glancing over her shoulder to the man. "Sie sagten nichtraucher!" The man nodded, raising a staying hand. "Relax, fraulein. Just..." He sighed again. "Just stand over there and let me... let me see what I can do. I'll fix this." "Sie besser! Meine mutter..." "I know," he said, gently guiding her away by the shoulders. "Just... just give me a second. Okay?" The girl nodded, pulling a diaphanous white coverall tight across her t-shirt. He didn't think he'd watched for long, but the sandy-haired man snapped his fingers, and he looked away, his cheeks coloring from shame. "Sorry..." The man cleared his throat, sneaking a look over his shoulder. "Girl's a handful and a half, but her mother pays well, you know." He squinted. "It's Jordan, right? That what your name tag says?" "Yes, sir." Jordan smiled again. "Forgive me, but I don't speak – was that German?" "Oh... oh!" The man was handsome enough, at least as far as those things went, with sandy hair, green eyes, and a ready smile. "Sorry. I forget, you know?" His smile stretched. "Name's Martin Tucker, but everybody just calls me Marty. Annika doesn't really speak the language, so let's see if we can work this out 'tween us two." "Of course, sir. I'm just not sure what help I can be at this hour." Again, he glanced over his shoulder at the girl, but this time, he leaned both forearms atop the desk, his hands clasped together. "Look, Jordan. When Mrs. Gottlieb checked in, there were some, ah, arrangements made for her daughter's room." "Arrangements?" Jordan's smile thinned. "Sir, none of those are guaran--" "I know, I know..." The man waved his hand again. "It's just that Annika is really... well, she's pretty fuckin' allergic to cigarette smoke, so your manager swore he'd get her a nonsmoking room, y'know?" "I understand, but it's a Friday night, sir, and we're at full--" The sandy-haired man rapped his knuckles against the desk. "I said I know that, sir, but you've gotta understand..." "We can move your sister in the morning if something opens up." "Sister? Nah, man, she's not my--" He broke off with a shake of his head. "Look, if she's gonna be stuck up there, is there anything you can do? Maybe send a cleaner up there to give it a bit of spit-polish, get the smell out?" Jordan pursed his lips, his eyebrows knitting in. "I'm sure we can--" "And what is going on here?" demanded the woman walking through the front door of the hotel. She was tall (taller still in those heels), with deep, mahogany brown hair that barely brushed the tops of her shoulders and brilliant blue eyes that glittered behind the lenses of thin, officious eyeglasses. Her brow rose sharply as she stalked across the lobby. "I asked you a question, young lady." "Ahh..." Annika looked from the newcomer to the two men at the front desk. "E-Es tut mir leid, ich verstehe nicht..." The woman walked (swayed, Jordan thought) across the lobby, her brow coming up again. "In English," she demanded. "Your father and I taught you better than that, and you vill never learn if you do not use it." Had she had that accent earlier? Jordan wasn't sure. "Now, hang on a minute," Marty cut in. "We're trying to solve a serious issue here..." She cuffed him across the mouth, eyes suddenly gone wide. "Show a little respect!" she demanded, wheeling on the teen. "As for you, young lady," she growled. "What is the meaning of that outfit? Do you intend to bring shame to our family?" "Mutter!" Annika gasped. "Nein, ich konnte nicht--" It only took a single step forward from her mother for the girl to switch back to English. "I vas unable to sleep. Martin... he help me to breathe." "It's true, Mrs. Gottlieb." Martin looked back to Jordan, raising his brow imploringly. "This man here was about to arrange for housekeeping to clear the smell, since they don't have another room for Annika to use." Mrs. Gottlieb nodded perfunctorily, folding her arms. "A female cleaner," she demanded, shooting Jordan a side-glance. "I will not have my daughter be... be ogled any longer." She gestured toward the elevator bank. "In fact, go wait over there while I speak with the kind gentleman." "But Mother..." "Go." Gottlieb leaned on the word ever so slightly, dragging her eyes to Martin. "You vill watch over her – be certain there are no other attacks." For a moment, Jordan thought Tucker would argue, but he relented with a single, sharp nod. "Ma'am," he said sharply, stepping past. The teen followed after him, coughing delicately into her fist. Mrs. Gottlieb watched the two of them go, scowling as Martin leaned close to Annika to murmur something that went unheard. The girl looked back, lightly touching her hand against his arm, so gently that only three fingers connected. "They are sleeping together, I think," Gottlieb said off-handedly, slipping off her glasses to clean the lenses. "They think I do not know, that I cannot see what is right in front of me." She glanced back, a certain impishness creeping into her smile. "Worse, they think I cannot hear." Jordan cleared his throat, suddenly bashful at her open appraisal. "Let me get someone from housekeeping up to your room." "A woman," Gottlieb repeated. "Preferably someone attractive to steal that man's heart." He smiled slightly, swallowing a chuckle. "What room are you in again?" "Ah... you know, I do not know. It is on the third floor, I believe." She laughed lightly. "We live in so many hotels, I think they have all blurred together. Just have your woman meet us. Either Martin or I can guide her from zhere." "Of course, ma'am." He picked up the house phone to place a brief call. When he was finished, he didn't have to look up to know that she was watching him. "I wonder if I am really so old that you have to 'ma'am' me." She smiled at him again, touching the temple of her glasses to her lower lip. "Please, you vill call me Katja, ja?" She met his eyes again, and he got lost in her gaze somewhere. "Tell me, Jordan... do you work alone all night?" "I...." He felt himself flush. "Yes. Yes, I do." "Good." She slipped the glasses back on. "Perhaps later, if they wake me up..." "Frau Gottlieb!" Martin called. "The cleaner is here!" Katja looked back quickly, and Jordan could swear she was blushing, too. "Yes, well..." She cleared her throat, tapping her hands on the desk. "I should..." "Of course." Jordan smiled again. "I'm sure Imelda will be able to get the room clean enough that your daughter gets a decent night's sleep." "Thank you so much..." She turned slowly, reluctantly, and he watched her join the others. It'd be several hours before he even thought to check to see if there was a "Katja Gottlieb" registered at all. * * * Imelda Orozco stifled a yawn, keeping her eyes focused on the numbers above the elevator as the older woman sauntered up to join them. She tried not to roll her eyes as Mrs. Gottlieb looked back over her shoulder, catching Jordan staring. Typical. He couldn't go one night without trying to pick up a little strange on the side? "What are--" Annika began in a huff, but the man at her side (too old for her, Imelda thought) touched his hand to her wrist and she fell quiet. "What she means is..." he began. "We, uh, that is to say we thought you wouldn't be here. Had a meeting, didn't you?" "We ended, ah, how you say, prematurely," Gottlieb said, suppressing a smirk. "He was called away." The chime sounded, and Martin put his hand against the door to keep it open while Imelda pushed the cart inside. "Thank you," she said through a tight smile. It took everything she had not to glance at her watch. How many more hours of this did she have? The two women followed her in, leaving the man to go in last. "Three, right?" he asked. "Ja," Annika said, clasping her hands behind her back. "Drei." "I love your hair like that, you know," her mother said. "You look hot." "Yeah. You look great, too." Imelda coughed gently, turning her head down and away. The older woman nudged the teen, who nodded slightly, turning. "I vant to... I must thank you for coming out here at such a ridiculous hour to help me." "No problem," the maid said. "It's my job." "Nein, nein..." She took one of Imelda's hands in both of her own. "It is not... not so simple, I think. I am... ah, wery grateful." Her mother put her hand on the girl's shoulder. "Don't smother the poor woman. It's still just a hotel room." "Tt." The girl scoffed, turning as the elevator slowed to a halt. "I am just being polite." She and Martin both stepped out of the cab, holding the doors open. Imelda turned to walk backwards, pulling the cart out with her, just as she had a hundred times previously. Still, she stumbled as she entered the hallway, her eyes losing focus. The cart was the only thing that held her up, and that damn wobbly wheel sent her careening to the side. Martin stepped in to her side, one hand on her back. "Careful there..." he said. "You okay, lovely?" She was dimly aware she was sweating, and put the back of her hand to her forehead. Something there itched, and she turned it over to stare at the off-color tab affixed to her skin, dumbfounded. "What is..." She pushed him away. "What did you do t--" Imelda staggered, dropping briefly to a knee as she gave Martin another shove, harder this time – enough to force him back against the wall. She tore off the strip and tried to run, but the teen blocked her path. "Hang on," she said, accent gone. "Just relax..." Imelda's eyes widened in panic, and she surprised the girl with a fist to the midsection, knocking her aside to make a drunken, shambling break for it. She never saw the silhouette break free from the icemaker's ill-lit alcove – but to be fair, most of them did. "H-help..." Imelda panted, her tongue thick and cottony. "Somebody--" The black-clad figure dove for the maid's legs, sending them both tumbling awkwardly to the carpet. Imelda got up to her hands and knees, kicking our frantically. "No!" Her assailant skittered aside, pouncing atop her. One slender arm snaked around the woman's neck, hand clamped to her mouth. Imelda struggled fiercely, but she only managed to tear off the other woman's Nationals cap before the combination of the chokehold and the sedative put her out. Alexandra Pierce flowed lithely to her feet, snatching up her cap. "What the hell happened?" she snapped. "You were supposed to--" Pierce broke off, both brows coming up as light footsteps carried her back to the elevator. "Mom, wait!" Annika Gottlieb – Quinn Gregory – exclaimed, raising a staying hand. "She actually--" But her mother wasn't listening, stepping into the cab in a light crouch. She found the woman who'd called herself Katja Gottlieb leaning casually against the railing. "You," Alex growled, lunging forward to press the other woman up against the wall, forearm to her throat. "I told you – I fucking told you..." The woman smiled, and it wasn't anything like the simmering promise of a grin she'd given to Jordan downstairs. This one was sharper, a triangular Cheshire's cat's grin. It was then that Alex felt it, the small prick of a knife against her ribcage. The blade probably wasn't long, just an inch or two, but this close and at this woman's hands, it wouldn't have to be. Drusilla Devonshire – the woman who had quite literally defined the term "psychotic ex-girlfriend" for Pierce over the last two-plus years – simply lifted a defiant eyebrow, setting her jaw. She knew the same thing Alex did: either of them could end it right there, at the tip of Dru's knife or through a sharp jerk of Alex's forearm. She knew it because Alex had trained her to know it. "Hello, sweetie," she said. "I thought we might have a chat..." ---- Three days earlier, in a strip mall in San Leandro, California... From the street, it wasn't much to look at, just a small office sandwiched between Mr. Li's Laundromat and a GNC supplement store in a particularly boring part of a very dull neighborhood. There was no neon sign, just a glazed window that read, "API" and gave a phone number. The windows were usually shuttered, the lights normally off, but the rent got paid on the first of the month like clockwork, so the leasing company didn't complain. Several of the GNC employees could honestly claim to have never met API's owner, and, if pressed, the titular Mr. Li might relay stories of the dark-haired girl who came by every so often to check the mail and do some tidying. Today was one of those rare visits, as Quinn Gregory meandered up the walk, her hands in her pockets and white wires trailing from her ears to the iPod clipped at her waist. The girl was tall, perhaps five-ten or five-eleven, with dark hair and cat's-eye glasses that were a bit more grown-up than the ones she used to wear (which got stepped on during a PRIME show on her birthday). Quinn would admit she wasn't paying all that much attention as she approached the office, lost in her own world. Her mother was still away on PRIME's Asian tour and wouldn't be back in the States until after the show in Beijing later that evening, and Amy was in the middle of a marathon press junket calling into DC-area drive-time radio shows to try to drum up the weekend's crowd at the National Mall. So Quinn had only stopped home long enough to drop off her bag and change from her school uniform before she took the short walk up to the office. On a good day, she could make it in ten minutes, but it was a clear, beautiful late afternoon and she'd stretched it out to a good twenty, turning the corner just as the sunset began to spill across the sky, all pink and purple. Maybe it was the picturesque sunset or perhaps she'd just grown complacent, but the teen was momentarily dumbstruck to actually see someone standing outside the office at all. One time, she'd scared the postman so badly that he'd dropped all the junk mail he'd been about to deliver. (She couldn't say she was broken up about that fact.) This was different, however -- it was a tall, tear-stricken woman built like a beanpole. Her mascara was smudged, and she scrubbed forlornly at her cheek as she stared at the door. Her brow furrowed, and another fat tear dropped from her eyelashes. Quinn's hands came up out of her pockets as she watched, half-turning away. She had no idea what she could say or do or even if she should do anything. She felt frozen, a deer in headlights, and was ashamed to find herself stepping backwards. All she'd have to do was make it back around that corner and she wouldn't have to deal with it. Maybe the woman would leave a message or something. "Mom?" She didn't even see the young boy half-leaning out of the window of the late model sedan parked outside. He was a small, effeminate thing with a high-pitched, whiny voice. "Are we done yet?" The woman turned away from the door, as if to shush the kid, and in so doing, she saw Quinn before the girl could make her escape. Embarrassed, the woman turned her face away, wiping irritably at her face. "Uhm," she said. "I'm sorry... do you know the person who uses this office?" "Uh... a little, yeah." "Is... is he out of business?" "What? No! Why would you--" "It's just that I've called a couple of times this week, and no one has returned a call back. I thought maybe I'd come by and catch him if he was at the office..." She trailed off, sniffling. "Sorry, it's just... I haven't had anyone to talk to about this. I don't mean to burden you with... with..." "It's fine, don't worry about it." Gregory approached again, her hand digging in her pocket. "So what did you need to see..." She almost said Mom. "...to see Alex about, anyway?" "I, uh..." She sucked at the corner of her lip, probably a nervous habit. "Wait, do you, like, work for him?" Quinn tugged a keychain out free. "Yeah, kinda. I was just coming to check in. I haven't been able to come by all week, my, uhh... my other job really packed on the hours, sorry." "And Mister--" She cleared her throat. "Mr. Pierce?" "It's Ms. Pierce, actually. Alexandra, not Alexander." "Oh, my gosh." The woman covered her mouth with her hand. "I didn't even think--" Gregory slid the key into the lock. "It's cool. She's out of town on business now, so, uh, I mean..." She pushed the door open. "Why don't you come in and tell me what's bothering you?" Still the woman lingered at the threshold. "I don't know. If-if Ms. Pierce is going to be out of town for a while, I don't want to get Dominic's hopes up." "Dominic is your son out there?" The woman looked over her shoulder, her hand lingering on her throat. "Yeah, he's..." It was the first time she'd smiled since Quinn first saw her. "He's pretty great." "He can come in, if you want. We have an Xbox and some games or whatever." "...at a private investigator's office?" The girl grinned. "It's a long story." She extended a hand. "Come on, come in. I'm Quinn. Quinn Gregory." The handshake was brief, but firm. "Lilith De Leon." She gestured back to the car. "Dominic! Come on." The car door thumped open and then closed, the boy's gangly steps carrying him to the two women. This close, Quinn pegged him as maybe thirteen or fourteen. "Go play Xbox while I talk to the nice lady. And be quiet, please..." He slipped between his mother and the teen, drawing up short at the entertainment set-up. "Whoa!" he exclaimed. "They have everything!" This earned Quinn another dubious look, but the girl raised a hand. "One of... of Alex's friends is a big-time gamer, and she outfitted the area herself." "Another investigator?" "Uhh... I might call her an investor. She's an associate of Alex's." "Just like you are?" The tone was sharp, scolding -- a mother who worried. "Forgive me, it's just... you don't seem much older than Dominic." "I'm eighteen," Quinn lied. "Just working here for the summer before I go off to school." "I see," the woman said, affixing her with the mom-eye. "Going to become a private investigator, too?" The teen didn't flinch away. "Something like that, maybe. If you'd like, we can step into the interior office and you can tell me how we can help you." "I... I really don't know. I mean, when Helena suggested I call, she didn't... she didn't say it was like this." "Helena Maddox?" Gregory's eyes climbed. "Wow, I haven't heard that name since... " She pushed open the door to the interior office, rethinking her response. If everything else had thrown the woman off, then "since she came after my mother for murder" would probably be a deal-breaker. Even if she could clarify that Alex was never actually charged with the crime. "I think it's been a year or so," she said instead. "She said you should come here?" Quinn's mother had taught her daughter a number of difficult lessons, and one of them was how terrible a tool social pressure can be. When the girl went into the inner office, Lilith wasn't given much of choice but to go after her. What else was she going to do, stand out there alone? "Yeah, she said... she said that the kind of help I need isn't something that she can help with, but that API sometimes, uh... sometimes bends the rules a little." "What kind of help are we talking about here?" The teen gestured to the chair opposite her mother's desk, circling around. "I mean, if you're looking for someone to rob a bank or something, I think you maybe went to the wrong place." "No, no..." Lilith sat down, crossing her legs, left over right. "Nothing like that... it's just..." She sighed, looking away as tears came back to her eyes. "Sorry." Quinn watched intently, a few quick keystrokes turning on the video camera secreted in the bookcase. "Take your time." "I was married." Lilith glanced to the door. "Dominic's father, Xavier... he was a lawyer. I mean, he was just a junior partner, and he didn't deserve to--" She choked on a sob, snatching a tissue from the box that sat on the desk. "He died. Just a couple of weeks ago, he died of a heart attack, and..." Again, she looked away. "And I don't know. It's stupid. I shouldn't even be here. I'll just..." She scooted the chair back. "Just tell your... your boss that I'm sorry I--" "Sit down." Gregory didn't have the steel in her voice that her mother did -- not yet at least -- but she still managed to channel enough to freeze the woman in place. "Please, Mrs. De Leon. If it's important enough that you came all the way out here, you should at least finish the story. I can't promise that Ms. Pierce will be able to help, but... please. If not for you, then do it for your kid." She sat down again, perched at the edge of the chair like she was still preparing to rabbit. "I think he was murdered. I can't prove it. I don't know for sure. But I think that... that he found out something that he wasn't supposed to. They say his heart just gave out, and his doctors never said anything like that was a danger." "So you're looking for someone to prove your husband was murdered?" "No. I... I mean, yes, but that's not why I went to Helena." She was quiet for a long moment, to the point that Quinn thought she might have to prompt her to speak again. "About a week ago... about a week ago, a man was waiting in my driveway when I came home from picking him up at school. He..." She set her jaw and met the girl's sea-green gaze. "He said that Xavier had changed his will." A creep of anger hit her words. "He said that my husband had decided to... to give most of his money to one of their clients. That he was... was moved by the assistance they brought to the community." Like she imagined her mother would have, Gregory took notes during the meeting. "And what's the name of this client?" "I have the card here." De Leon pulled her purse onto her lap. "They said that Xavier had asked them to take care of Dominic and I." Again, the anger tightened her jaw, sharpening her words. "That account... my husband was supposed to take care of us. Here it is." She slid a small, lily-white rectangle across the antique desk. "I don't even know what this 'Polyphemus Group' does." "Just to clarify: what are you asking us to do?" "I don't know." She sat back. "There's no way that my husband changed his will. No way. I asked to see it, but they said it was locked away and that they'd get it for me." "And?" "And that was the last time I heard from them. It's been a week. They won't return my calls. They won't let me in to see anyone. They have some conference next week that they've all been preparing for this week. I just... I have to know." "So you want us to steal your husband's will so you can see if it was faked?" The woman nodded. "I know it's silly. But Dominic is... what if he gets sick? What if something happens? I haven't worked in almost seven years. I'm just a housewife. I can't--we'll lose our house." "I understand," Quinn said quietly. "What I need you to do from here is for you to..." She slipped a form out of her mother's drawer, studiously ignoring the pearl-handled .45 in its holster next to it. "To fill this out. It's just some basic contact information in the event my m--ployer takes the case." She cleared her throat as she plucked a clicky pen from the ceramic mug on the desk. She turned the mug – which featured a photo of Amy Campbell, Quinn Gregory, and Alex's tiny orange tabby cat all wearing fake mustaches, with the caption "MASTER OF DISGUISE" – to keep the picture out of Lilith's view. "And, uh... it's an average of seventy-five dollars an hour or five hundred and fifty dollars a day, depending on the complexity of the case and the number of outside contractors she'll need to hire." Lilith nodded, her pen moving in careful, even strokes. "That's not a problem. I've... we had a joint account before--" Wetness dotted the form. "It's not a problem." "Great." The girl stood. "I'll be out front if you need me. Take all the time you need, okay?" This time, the woman didn't even nod, her lips pinched in consideration, head tilted slightly to the side in thought. Less than ten minutes later, Lilith emerged from the room, the paper folded in half and held in both hands. She gestured to Dominic (who hadn't even looked away from his video game when Quinn came back into the front room), saying, "Come on, baby, time to go." "Let me finish this game, Mom." "Okay, but then we're going." She set the paper down on the receptionist's desk, where the girl was jotting down a phone number from the machine. "I just wanted to... to thank you. Even if Ms. Pierce doesn't take my case, it felt good to get that all out." Quinn gave her a genuine smile. "Not a problem at all," she said. "Believe me, I'm glad I could help." The tow-headed boy dropped his controller and tromped over, earning a tousling of his hair by his mother. "Thanks again," Lilith said. "No problem. We'll be in touch after I've had a chance to run this up the flagpole." * * * That chance came just a few minutes later, as Quinn contacted her mother via Skype, some six thousand miles away (and seven hours earlier). She played the video of the interview over the connection, and was even able to upload the raw file to a secure FTP server so it could be viewed from anywhere. After she watched it from the comfort of her hotel room in Beijing on her iPad, Alexandra Pierce frowned in thought. She flipped back to the videoconference with her daughter, who was doing her level best to seem like she wasn't interested in her mother's decision. "That's not a good look," Quinn said. "Have you tried to call Helena?" Alex asked. "Uh, yeah. The number you have for her cell is bad, and the people at Stryker Security said she was out of town. I didn't leave a message. Didn't think that was a good idea." Alex nodded fractionally. "Okay, we'll leave that stone unturned for now. I want you to get in touch with Donnie. Have him look into this woman and her husband -- financials, criminal records, known associates, the works." "Uh... Donnie?" Quinn asked doubtful. "Isn't he... unreliable?" "I was going to go with 'the only choice I have'. Donnie's a lush and a louse and a layabout, sure, but I need to know if what she's saying is legitimate." "What about the, uh... don't we usually research the bad guys?" "I assume you've already been to the Polyphemus website." "I, uhhh..." Quinn clicked closed a web browser. "I might have looked." Pierce's smile sharpened. "It's okay, I did, too. That conference Mrs. De Leon mentioned is in Baltimore next week." "Baltimore as in... a short drive from Washington, where the GCW show is this weekend?" A light sparked in Quinn's eyes. "Tell me you're considering something." There was a long pause on the other line, her mother so motionless that the teen thought the signal might have died. "If we can get something workable together before the show, yes. You and Donnie can fly out to DC and update me there when I land." "So what do I do until then?" "Come up with a plan." Alex leaned towards the camera. "And don't tell Amy what you're doing, okay? I don't want her to spend the night yelling at me if I decide not to go through it. I may squash this after I've had some time to think about it, so don't get your hopes up. Now I've got to go -- I want to get some work in before I get in the ring with Chainz." "Yeah." Quinn nodded, lifting a hand to sweep the hair behind her ear; it was a nervous gesture that they both shared. "Be careful out there. Guy's a creep." "You don't have to tell me that. You be careful, too. I love you. And tell Amy I said, 'Twenty-eight hours'." The teen didn't really have dimples, but they weren't really necessary for that particular smile. "I can't tell if it's scary or adorable that you keep track." "Let's go with adorable, okay?" "Sure. See you tomorrow night. Good luck tonight." The connection went quiet, and Quinn locked up the office tight. She placed the call to Donnie Bell on the way home, setting into motion another round of research and preparations, but one that she thankfully didn't have to take part in. All Quinn had to do was pack, and it had been far too long since she'd been to a wrestling show. ---- A little over three days later, immediately following said wrestling show... The aftermath of a show like WorldWide 148 that was the real killer. The National Mall might have looked good on television, but the cleanup was murder. There was trash and tents and scaffolding and folding chairs and the ring and more left to be torn up and moved aside; in several places, they'd had to lay down plywood to protect the lawn from permanent harm. All that meant that just after midnight on show-day, most of the lights were still on, a crew of close to a hundred dismantling the essentials – the things you wouldn't leave alone overnight in downtown Washington DC. Some of the workers were still hanging around, chatting. Friendships in the wrestling business are fleeting – oftentimes, they only saw each other once every two or three weeks, and a good number of those encounters involved phrases like "the fists were flying" or even "and then she punched him in the balls". Professional wrestlers are acquaintances that share the same risks, bound together by effort and sacrifices no one outside the business can really understand. That didn't mean they had to like each other. So after a show, amidst autograph requests and fans who might travel hundreds of miles to see them for two minutes, most discussions devolved into a kind of guarded, "hey, how's your mother?" small-talk. Of course, most of those discussions don't involve seventeen-year-old girls. Quinn Gregory leaned against a white production truck, scratching at her forearm. "So... wait, no meds at all?" A rough estimate would put it at about the hundredth question the girl had asked in the last five minutes, but her subject was used to them. "Not even aspirin." Vivica J. Valentine, the most decorated woman in GCW history, smiled indulgently. "I know, I know – it sounds crazy, right?" "Are you kidding? I chew on Advil like they're M&M's. It sounds like you're from another planet." Vivica's hair was always messy, but it was still damp from her post-match shower, and if she minded that she was on babysitter duty, it never showed. "See, but that sounds just as strange to me. It's not like I get a headache and think, 'Gosh, I'd really like to take some Tylenol, but I can't because I'm straight-edge'. I just don't think about it at all." "Wow." Quinn slipped off her glasses, rubbing the edge of her t-shirt across the lens. "Sorry if I'm like, 'Your entire lifestyle is strange and foreign to me' or whatever, it's just... not what I was expecting." "What were you expecting?" "I don't know." The girl shrugged, squinting to peer through the glasses before she put them on. She had the kind of stare that made people ask if she had an old soul, deep and fathomless and wise beyond her seventeen years. "I mean, that guy I've seen out there is always claiming straight-edge means you're better than me, so I guess I expected less... humility, I guess?" Valentine grinned, glancing away briefly to wave at one of the production assistants. "Thanks, I'll try to take that as a compliment and not that you thought I was going to be a stuck-up bitch or something. No, he... that's a very twisted viewpoint of the way most of us are. I mean, I could say the same thing – I've watched your mom and Amy on television and I've seen them say and do all kinds of terrible things. Does that mean they're terrible people, or that lesbian couples shouldn't be allowed to have a kid?" "Depends on who you're asking." Gregory stared glumly off in the distance. "I'm sure there're a lot of people who already think that." She smiled; it was when that expression touched her lips, small and wry and faintly shouting "I know something you don't know", that she most resembled her mother. "Speak of the devil and the devils, they doth appear." Amy Campbell and Alexandra Pierce didn't walk arm-in-arm or even hand-in-hand, but there was still no question they were a couple. They were too casual about their closeness to be anything else, as if the thought of "personal space" didn't really occur to them. As usual, they were in the middle of a conversation – and, as usual, it was Campbell who did most of the talking. "You don't have to take my word for it," Amy was saying. "Ask anybody; they'll tell you that remix is an atrocity." "I'm not doubting you, believe me," Alex said. "I'm just saying that sales don't lie." "But sales aren't music, or else Diddy would be one of the best musicians of our generation, and I'm not sure I want to live in a world where that's the case." She jogged ahead a few steps. "Here, let someone else tell you. Viv! Vivica!" The blonde's brow quirked slightly. "That's me." "The remix of Rihanna's S&M that Britney Spears did vocals for – worst thing of all time, right? Apparently Alex likes it. And yes, you can stay politely quiet about it, but now we all know she's experiencing some loss of hearing. Old age is a filthy, ugly thing." "I didn't say that!" Pierce protested. "I'm just saying that there are plenty of people who'll buy it." "That's basically the same thing. There are plenty of people who'll buy Wonder Bread. That doesn't make it gourmet cuisine." "Well," Valentine hedged. "I haven't heard it, but once you include Britney Spears, it's already overproduced garbage." Amy nodded feverishly. "See? This is what I'm saying!" Campbell reached for the iPod clipped to her hip. "I have it saved here. You have to hear how bad it really is." "You bought it?" Vivica shook her head. "You realize that makes you part of the problem, right?" "We – well, I – try to tell her that every day," Quinn offered. The comment earned her an obscene gesture from Campbell, but a wry smile from her mother. "Thanks for keeping an eye on her," Alex said. "It's no problem." Viv leaned her shoulder against the truck. "I can only imagine what it'd be like to bring a kid into a place like this." "Mm." It was a quiet, noncommittal sound, the verbal equivalent of a shrug. "She'd tell you it was worth it, given how rarely we see each other, but sometimes I'm not so sure." "I heard about your schedule. Did you really fly in from China last night?" Pierce smiled ruefully. "Yeah, and I go back next Friday. It's been... it's been brutal." Amy snorted. "'Brutal' doesn't even begin to describe it," she grumbled, looking up from the iPod's screen. "Okay, here's--" The rapid-fire, staccato bleating of a car horn interrupted them, beep-beepa-beeeeeep. "Who the fuck is blaring a fucking horn?" Amy grumbled, glaring over at the classic, '68 Mustang as the driver's side window rolled down. Donovan Bell shared his widest grin (and, for him, that was saying something). "Ladies, your chariot awaits," he smarmed. "Next stop: paradise." "You know this guy?" Vivica asked, brow lifting. "Y-yeah," Amy stammered, uncertain exactly how she should introduce Bell. "He's..." Bell leaped in with something approaching grace. "Donovan Bell," he said with a cheeky grin and a hodgepodge of a British accent. "Donnie to my friends, which is everybody, 'cause everybody likes me. I hope that everybody includes you, lovely, because you are fit, if I may say." He looked quickly to Alex and then Amy. "'Less you two've gone an' added her to your daisy chain – which'd be terribly unfair." "Donnie." Alex leaned on his name only slightly, but it was enough to shut him up. "Sorry..." She flashed a brief, strained smile to Valentine. "Donnie works for my publicist, and he was nice enough to fly in from London to help negotiate merchandising rights between GCW and PRIME." The lie fell from her lips without the slightest hesitation. "Right... what she said." Donnie tossed in his trademark grin, which was, whatever else could be said about him, actually quite charming. "I'm here to publicitize Ms. Pierce's new line of... of belt buckles." "Belt buckles?" For her part, Vivica seemed amused by the outgoing Brit. "I'd never considered--" "Yeah." Bell nodded. "We fink they'll be a hit with the little tweeny girls. We'll put a spider or a medusa head right inna middle, an'--" "Al?" Amy cut in. "I thought your meeting was in Oakland?" "I asked him to fly in while I was here. That way, I could spend my time at home with you." Alexandra Pierce may have been an experience liar, but her lover was not nearly as adept, and strain drove her voice higher. "So you scheduled your meeting at 12:30 at night?" "Actually..." Quinn stepped in, tucking a lock of midnight-black hair behind an ear. "I asked Donnie to drop by. I've never been to Washington, and I figure the landmarks are probably pretty all lit up." Her mother's lips twisted with regret. "I know we'd all talked about getting something to eat, but I figured maybe--" "No, no..." Valentine raised her hands. "You guys go. Far be it from me to stand in the way if some good sightseeing..." "Great..." Alex circled around to the passenger side door, tugging it open and pushing down her seat to allow Quinn to slip into the backseat. "Then we'd better go. Amy, I'll see you back at the--" "How about you see me around the corner here for a sec first, Alexandra?" When Amy said it, both Pierces knew it sounded far more like a suggestion than it actually was. She waved a hand at Valentine. "And text me later or something, we'll get together if I'm still around and she bails on me." "Hey, no problem..." Vivica ducked low so she could see the girl in the backseat. "It was great meeting you!" "You too! Keep kicking ass!" As the blonde stepped away, Alex sighed. She leaned against the doorframe to peer into the car. "Don't leave without me." "Wouldn't dream'a it," Bell said, his hands turning the steering wheel into a drum. "We'll be waitin'." Quinn turned over her wrist to glance at the face of her watch. "Don't take too long, Mom. The Gottlieb thing only works if it's the middle of the night." "I know. It'll be just a second." She shut the door behind her. "Keep the engine off." As Quinn's mother trailed Amy down a narrow alley between two half-deconstructed tents, the teen slumped back into the seat, her arms folded. "I've got a tenner that says Campbell ends up comin' wiffus," Donnie said, watching the girl in the rearview. Quinn snorted. "Not taking that wager." Ahead of them, Amy stopped, looking back with no small amount of irritation. She jammed her hands into her pockets to give them something to do. "I can explain," Alex broached. "Explain?" The question came out louder than Campbell intended, and she leaned in closer. "You can explain? I get that you live two lives or whatever, but you were a hair away from having to tell Valentine just what it is you do when the cameras aren't rolling, and now you can explain?" "She wasn't supposed to be there. Donnie jumped the gun. Maybe he thought she was part of the group? I don't know. The point is--" "The point is that he shouldn't be here," Amy scolded, "And don't think I don't know when you're lying to me, Alex." Pierce shifted uncomfortably under the weight of that glare. She hated her two worlds colliding every bit as much as Amy did. "It's not that simple." "It never is with you. So tell me the truth." "I'm trying to protect you. What I'm doing is... not strictly legal." "You're protecting me, but involving Quinn? Seriously?" Amy snarled. "Throwing your daughter into something 'not strictly legal', all the while trying to sneak around me. Fucking bravo, Al. Brilliant thinking." "Do you speak German?" "What the fuck does that--" "Quinn does. She's taking it this year in school." "So?" "So I need someone else in there for this to even have a chance of success. And you may noticed I don't have the resources I used to. I'm making do." Amy was unmoved. "I speak French. If you needed someone else, I could've done it." "You don't even know what 'it' is. Believe me, if I thought it was something you could get away with, I'd have you in there with me in a heartbeat. It kind of has to be her." "Doesn't explain why you're keeping secrets from--" "I wasn't. I was keeping secrets from Vivica. You said tonight that you're sick of people labeling you, and I figured that would stop when they got to know you. The last thing I wanted was to drag you off on one of my stupid missions – that I know you hate – instead of letting you spend time with people who might actually be normal." "Wrap it in whatever excuse you want – you tried to get away without me finding out about this. And on top of that, you didn't think it was important enough to tell me you were taking our--" Just as Campbell's words threatened to spill over the edge of her commitment issues, she corrected herself. "--taking your daughter on a fucking 'mission'. That's not okay." Alex sighed sharply, her response almost a growl. "She volunteered." "Of course she did. You're her mother and she thinks you're James Bond or something. So she wants to spend some time with her kickass spy mom by being a kickass spy herself. Who wouldn't want to join up for one of your crazy sch--" "She volunteered because unlike you, she actually asked what I was doing before she jumped down my goddamn throat." Alex looked around, making sure their row hadn't attracted the wrong kind of attention. "If you want to talk about this, fine. But if you want to yell at me because I didn't include you in something I knew – I knew – you wouldn't want to do anyway, then you might as well catch up to Valentine, because you might have guessed I'm on kind of a tight schedule here and I really don't have time to rehash this with you." "I want to yell because... no, you know what? Forget it. I get it," All of Amy's anger suddenly boiled over, but it became something much tamer, much smaller and somehow still more overwhelming. She swallowed and looked to the ground. "That life put me in bed with Kathryn Shaw. It's nearly killed me a hundred times. It's hurt you more, but it... it lost me a father, so don't resent me for hating it." As tears came to her lower lids, Campbell meekly waved a hand. "I guess I'll see you at the hotel or on the plane tomorrow. Don't let Quinn get too caught up." "Amy, wait..." "No, Al, you're right. I'd just slow you down." She jammed her hands into her pockets, half-turning away. "I do trust you. Just... just come home in one piece, okay? All three of you." The little redhead disappeared into the labyrinth of dismantled tents, leaving Alex to turn away and swat ineffectually at some nearby scaffolding. The attempt drew a scathing look and an under-breath comment from a passing worker, but he wouldn't meet her snapping glare. Pierce stalked briskly back to Donnie's car, and her protege lifted his brows as she neared. "Looks like y'shoulda bet me after all, girlie," he said. Quinn sat up, her forehead wrinkling in concern. "Did something happen?" "Drive," Alex instructed. Donnie yanked the Mustang into reverse, kicking up pebbles as he backed up. "Little Red ain't coming?" "She's going to the hotel." "We could swing by an' get 'er..." "We don't have time. Drive." "But Mom..." Quinn implored. Alex turned so she could look at both of them. "We don't have any fucking time, okay? Just drive. I'll... I'll fix it later." She glanced down at her phone, but its face dark, before she shook her head, tucking it away. "Do you have my bag?" The girl leaned to the other side of the seat, lowering her voice. "Are you okay?" she asked. "Just pass me the bag." Alex's smile was tight. "I'll be fine." Donnie was singing softly to himself as he eased onto the street. "Down a long and lonesome highway... east of Omaha..." ---- About two hours later, as tensions rose in an elevator in downtown Baltimore... Alexandra Pierce tightened her grip, pushing against Drusilla's Adam's apple, her eyes narrowed and lips thin. "I told you what would happen if I saw you again." "I know, but can we pretend you didn't?" Dru chuckled to hide her cough. "I come in peace, Alexandra. Really, I do." "I don't believe you." "You probably shouldn't, but what else are you going to do, crush my... ahem, crush my windpipe?" Pierce stepped in closer, lowering her voice to a growl. "You think I won't?" "Mom!" Quinn raised a hand, but Donnie kept her from entering the elevator with a hand on the arm. "Mom, don't--" Devonshire dug the tip of her blade into Alex's side, not far enough to cause any serious damage, but enough to remind Alex it existed. "I think..." She leaned up, speaking in a playful whisper. "I think you want to. Do you remember the sound it makes? The hollowed-out crinkle, like someone biting into a potato chip? My eyes will go wide, jaw working as I gasp for air... will you hold me up and watch me suffocate, or will you leave me here to flop on the floor?" "Dru..." Alex said warningly. "I think you miss it." She curled her fingers over Pierce's shoulder. "Do you remember the way it feels? The power? I think you're straining against the walls of that prison you built out of your heart. I think you're aching for the chance to have that kind of power again. So kill me. But then you'd better kill the maid, too. She'll wake up soon, and she'll raise an alarm. And Donnie? You should see the way he was looking at your little girl..." "Shut up!" Alex stepped back, delivering a single, snapping backhand to Devonshire's jaw. Her ex stumbled to the carpet, the tiny switchblade spilled from her hand. "Mom!" Quinn shoved free of Donnie's grip, stepping into the car to grab hold of Alex's arm before she could bend for the knife. "Mom, stop... she could have – if she'd wanted to do something, she could have before we got up here." "I could have," Drusilla concurred with a bloodstained grin. "I could have slit Quinny's throat and you never would have even known I was here. Maybe next time I won't be so--whoof!" All the air rushed out of Devonshire's lungs as Gregory spun into a low kick to her midsection, clumsy but effective. "You are not fucking helping," the girl said, jabbing a finger down. "I don't fucking care what crazy deal there is between you two or what she wants." She glanced back over her shoulder, lowering her voice. "There's a family – a woman and a child – that I met and that are counting on me to make sure that they can afford to live, okay? So you can have your bullshit dramatic rooftop showdown later." Her mother's fists were still balled, her nostrils still flared. Her brow furrowed, a sigh slowly escaping from her nose. "She's right." "I am?" Quinn's brows climbed. "I mean... I am, yeah." "The De Leons are counting on us. On me." She combed her fingers through her hair. "Let's go." Alex turned. "Donnie, make sure she stays." Bell cracked his knuckles, leaning his shoulder against the wall. "A'right. I'll keep an eye on--" "I can help." Devonshire knuckled her lip. "What makes you think I need your help? Quinn and I can hand--" "No, you can't." She lifted a hand as she used the rail to get to her feet. "Look, she was impressive downstairs, I'm not going to deny it. But this isn't some front desk clerk you're talking about now, and speaking German won't get you in that room." Quinn's lips twisted. "It's two o'clock in the morning – what exactly do you expect to be up there?" "That's why I'm here," Drusilla said. "I'm pretty sure you're walking into a trap."
