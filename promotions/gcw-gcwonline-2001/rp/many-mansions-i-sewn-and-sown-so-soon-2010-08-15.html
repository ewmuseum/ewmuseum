Date: 15 August 2010 Lionel I guess it all started with the dressmaker's dummy. Well I cant think of no better place to start, could you? I aint been out to daddy's house in years, what with him living down in Tullahoma now with that new woman, but I might could bet you the farm or the bottom dollar or what else people these days wager that that dressmaker's dummy is still settin somewhere in a bedroom closet somewhere. I dont know where else it would be, truly -- it been in some kind of closet long as I known it. Some well-intentioned wedding guest give it to our mama thinkin she could sew, but let me tell you bout mama: she wasnt no traditional woman of any sort, she didnt cook or couldnt either one, she could tar a roof better than most men do, she could tell you the difference between purlines and girts. She'd make you feel like a right idjit just for askin bout it. She owned three dresses her whole life: her Sunday best when she was a little girl and her Sunday best when she grew out of that one, and her wedding dress. She said she didnt need no dresses save for churchgoing. Dale It was Lloyd used it at all. If it was up to Mary Earl the dummy would have stayed hid away. Taught his own self all right, Lord knows it wasnt me or Mary Earl taught him it. If you ask me he spent too much time with the thing. I dont know what to make of a boy prefers to spend his time with a doll all day, I still dont. I figured he'd turn out to be one of them queers you hear bout these days, one of them queer folks. Mr Durard, for a long time people called him the best in town -- but when there aint no one else around that does what you do people dont have much else to call you. You cant finish second in a one-man race. He had this shop on Bailey, it wasnt big. Big ol glass windows tall as a man. He rented out some kid went to Vanderbilt for drawing or what have you. I know it was one of them Bowe kids lived on Chapel Road, Clyde or Fenton either one. There was always a surplus of Bowes. They kept mostly shut up down there in the house, and folks in a small place like ours dont like such quiet. But Mr Durard didnt mind it. He rented out Clyde or Fenton either one to paint his windows. The Bowe kid ended up painting up some flowers and lattice, I dont know. Wedding stuff, like. Veils and flowers and them long flowy dresses, but I couldnt tell you why. Mr Durard didnt make wedding dresses. He said he tried a long while back, but it didnt work then either. He said, Every wedding dress I ever made fell out of fashion the week before someone wanted one. I figure he learned the hard way what I known for as long as I recall. That is, Blountville aint for marrying. I aint never in my life seen a wedding here at any of the churches. I dont know a single wedded couple got married here, my own parents included. That sort is just imported over from someplace else. I couldnt say why. Some towns is just like that. Blountville is like that. Little Blue Ridge town aint no one ever heard of or heard from. It didnt care for Mr Durard, it didnt care for his fancy painted dress shop neither. It wasnt the people didnt like Mr Durard -- though that might be as close to the gospel truth there is. Blountville itself held a grudge gainst that man. He got Clyde or Fenton either one to paint his name smack dab in the middle of the window. These big ol loop-de-loop letters in white. G. Myron Durard they said. Right under it he made the Bowe kid write Modiste. Hand to God I think that last word did him in for good. Modiste aint a word you write in your window here, it just aint. Pretty soon them windows had these long carkey scratches on em real good, real deep in there. The paint got chipped out here and there til the point where them flowers looked like a nest of tent caterpillars got into em. Rain washed away more and more of it, it just looked sorry. You could tell he didnt know how to handle nothing. He was looking for people who done it. That was the wrong way to go about it. My daddy would tell you, Bad things dont stop til there's no good left in a thing. It sort of just hollows the good out to the marrow. If no one here is in agreement, they dont know Mr Durard. Didnt know him. I seen a kid one summer shit on his doorstep during business hours. There aint no remedy for that. It wasnt no one-time thing neither: as soon as Mr Durard figured there cant be no way someone’s going to shit on his doorstep again, there it was. Big ol steaming lump of shit. There was other things too. Like his store got hit by lightning. It caught fire in the backroom, it wasnt bad. Burned away all the wedding dresses he never sold but that was it. You should have seen him scooping up the ash, the lace. He couldnt make sense of it nor nothing else. The fire put itself out, there wasnt no other damage. Then three storms later it hit again for the hell of it, I guess. Or it was like punishment for questioning the first time. And all this time he thought business was good. You could call that my fault. You just dont write Modiste on your window. I had made dresses for some ladies at church. Well. They started spreading the Good Word round and pretty soon I got Mr Durard's loyalest clientele lined up looking for a new gown, new sheath, new little black dress. I was sixteen at the time, seventeen -- I didnt know about different brands of these things. I was this kid knew how to make a dressmaker’s doll look good. They say money talks though, and I reckon it did. It told all them ladies, Go head and show me what you want. Dale There was this one time over yonder in Memphis I was in St Mary's Cathedral. It belongs to them Episcopals but it aint that bad. It has them high vaulted ceilings and big ol lights coming down from em. There aint no dark to be found there or I aint never seen none. I met a man had been to Alaska a time or two, he said to me there are days there when the sun dont settle down hardly at all. Blink your eyes and you missed the nighttime for the day. Larry he told me his name was, and ol Larry might tell you I'm right when I tell you that church aint far from Alaska. All them lights. All that quiet. I imagine Alaska is real quiet. There aint nothing there to disturb it. The church had that quiet and the house up there in Blountville had it too. When it was quiet there the house was full of it, it was more fuller of quiet than all the others I been in or lived at. It might could be something Mary Earl did to it, I dont know. This one day it was real quiet. There werent no wind blowing or nothing, no leaves shook up in the little tulip poplars or nothing. Just that quiet. The brand of it that feels real high up, like it's hung over you. I had found the ol rawhide belt I wore those days and I took it up in my hand. And Abraham stretched forth his hand and took the knife to slay his son, Genesis 22:10. For it was my christian duty to save his eternal soul, I'm talking bout Lloyd now. I wasnt then nor now dumb enough to think you can just scare the queer out of someone like it was the hiccups or something. You have to put the fear of God into them. I couldnt just say to him, Son I dont want you to be no dicksucker, could you put away your doll. That wouldnt do nothing. Wish in one hand, shit in the other -- see which one fills up first. That's bout the sum of things, my granddaddy used to say. I had to put the fear of God into Lloyd. Lionel I couldnt tell you what nobody thought about it, Lloyd making dresses. A lot of folks looked at him cockeyed and walked round him and out of his way. Probably they figured he was one of them queers or touched in the head, I couldnt tell you what all. But I can say he wasnt neither. Lloyd Shaw aint a dumb man: Lord knows I thought he was though, and daddy thought he was, and mama swore he was up and down. She told me, Lionel I swear it’s all I got not to drown him in the river like a blind pup. You got to understand something. It’s one of life's sad facts that Blountville dont hardly have pretty girls of its own breed. Boys here learn double-quick about sacrifice and exception -- that is, you might not want to take home that girl uglier than homemade soap, except you dont have no other option. I know that too. I was seeing this girl at the time. Her name was Addie. Might as well have set fire to her. I didnt figure ugly to have an ugly sister til I met that autopsy: her teeth looked like railroad tracks, I had to shake my pillow down pret near every time she laid her head on it, her toenails were things most workingmen would use as garden shears, I cant tell you what all was wrong with her. Ol Percy Goose, he was the banker bout sixty if he was a day, he never touched his brakes. Drove his little car straight into the hitch and ass-end of Sam John Luce's Ford at the one stoplight in town. The sound of it was awful, the metal crumpling like it did and Sam John hollering every word his mama raised him not to. Lloyd didnt hear a thing: he didnt see us stop to look, he kept on and tripped over a fire hydrant and knocked out two bottom teeth. I didnt know you could spread a gallon of milk like he did over the street. Lloyd After a while I was putting in twelve hours a day doing them dresses. I had them women go down to G. Myron Durard, Modiste, and pick out something off his racks.
