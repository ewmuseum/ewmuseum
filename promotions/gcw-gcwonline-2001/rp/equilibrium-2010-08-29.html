Equilibrium

29 August 2010

Date: 08/29/10 I chill out in some other punk's locker room after the match, drinking a Stella and looking at my bounty as some geriatric fuck fixes my forehead up but good. He's an old dude, wrinkly, wheezy, reeks of beef stew. Obviously he's not the best doctor money can buy. His hand trembles as he works and AAAAAAAHHH!!! "Fuck!" I push the old fucker away and glare the glare that shames all other glares. "Mind your fuckin' craft, man!" He raises his arms, as if it ain't his fault, and then goes back to his sloppy work. He finishes the hatchet job and before the adhesive on the bandage can even think about drying, I shove him away and tell him to fuck off. He picks up his leather bag and stumbles out of the room. For a moment, I'm alone. And I enjoy the fuckin' moment. I see Alexis stalk up to me. Splotches of blood seep through the bandages on her face. She sports a noticeable bruise on her cheek. And I can tell she sure as hell is not proud. She's anything but. I state the stupidly obvious greeting. "What's up, babe?" Her voice hoarse, vocal chords strained. She almost hollers out. "What's up?!? I look like Tila fucking Tequila!" I can't do anything but smile. She's pissed beyond belief. She should be. I would be. She marches right up, a soldier on a mission, gets right in my face. I'm drenched in sweat and blood and I'm fuckin' enjoying this beyond belief. She slaps me. Hard. I shake it off. "That all you got?" She slaps again. Harder. "That it?" Again. "C'mon." Again. "C'mon!" Her nail cuts my cheek. "Hit me!" The palm clenches. "Fuckin' hit me!" The swing comes faster. "Hit me, you fucking cunt!" The fist smacks into my jaw. It's raw. I know a tooth got knocked loose. I know it wasn't 'cause of her. I rip it out anyway. Blood dribbles from my lip to the floor. I smile. "About fucking time," I say, and flick the piece of calcium to the ground. And she storms off. I spit more blood and know that I will have to take all the shit that'll end all shit. But that's fine. She's worth it. This is worth it. Finally. All of this is worth it. ~~~~~~ On the flight home to San Francisco on the Gulfstream, I sip a Nagasaki Airburst and ponder about things. A lot of things. He's fast asleep in his chair, the rum and Coke in his hand dribbling out onto the beige carpet. I see the flight attendant gather up a towel and we share a look. She understands, and I understand, and she scrubs away at the stain. The car ride home is just as civil. Civil, in that his eyes are shut and the noises he makes echo an epileptic swinging around a chainsaw. I do my best to ignore it. I text my BFFs Lacie and Carly and tell them to meet me at the house later on today for drinks and convo about all the things we've been doing. It'll be nice to hang out with real people for a change. When we pull up in the driveway, he barrels into the house without saying a thing and leaves our bags for me and the driver to sort out. The driver's nice enough to oblige to bring everything into the grand foyer and I quadruple the tip I was gonna give him and we're both happy. The driver leaves and I call out for Triumph. I walk around the house, and it's not long before I realize he might not be around. When I walk out into the driveway and see one of the cars missing, I know it to be true. Good. I lock the front doors and Lacie texts back that her and Carly'll swing by in two hours, and I figure I've got some time to unwind. I make my way up the stairs to the second floor, the stairs that have always been way too damn long, and I undress in the bedroom. When I pass by the vanity mirror, I stop dead cold. I touch my face. The cuts. The bruises. The aftermath. He beat Tempest, a girl I admire the hell out of. I don't blame her. And he got what he wanted. But I wonder... I almost blame myself, really. I've been pushing him, prodding him, preparing him for his moment. "You're gonna make him pay," I've yelled. "You're going to embarrass this asshole," I've bellowed. "Show these motherfuckers what the Future is all about!" ...And then I feel his grip. I see Tempest floating in like a missile that'll never go off course. I know I'm the bait, and I want to say something, anything, convince him this isn't the way. We all know it's too late for that, though. I brace for impact, but it's like an oncoming car. You can swerve and yell and pray, but it won't stop and you won't stop. Almost. Hhhhhhhh... I take off the rest and I'm naked when I turn on the faucets of the shower and find that perfect balance between hot and cold. Anything made of glass fogs up in the search, and when I discover equilibrium I jump in. The droplets pelt my body and soak my hair. My mind races all over. Of him running amok in Tokyo, me ripping him out of jail. Of his escape to god-knows-where, to do god-knows-what with god-knows-who. Of Phoenix and...and...I replay the moments of that hour over and over again in my head. The pain. The avoided. The captivity. The victory. The celebration. The afterward. I remember it all and I want to cry and I want to lash out and I want to just run away. I get scared. Scared of what this all means. If this is just the precursor, or if this was the exception. But most of all, I'm scared of how this has made me feel. As the spray of the water hits my skin like millions of tiny little needles, I'm overwhelmed by the feeling. It overpowers. I nearly drop to my knees. Is this how he feels all the time? Is this how it is for him, every single moment that he's staring down the murderer's row? When he peers into the eyes of Mayhem or Rasa or Tempest? Is it...? I go lightheaded. I almost feel like throwing up. I turn off the faucets, and wrap myself in a silk robe, and the feeling still lingers. Me, Lacie and Carly chill out on the patio in the back of my house with tall glasses of Long Island Ice Teas. These two are my best friends. The best I have left, anyway. I haven't seen them in ages. I missed 'em so much. We toast our drinks with clinks and talk shit about the old whores in 'Sex and the City' and share a quick laugh. "A fucking first class plane ticket?" Lacie nearly chokes on her tea as Carly goes in for the kill. "Those sows'd be lucky to get a taxi ride for a HJ from the old bitch." "Yeah," I say, stirring the straw in my drink. "But you paid to see the damn thing." Carly pulls out no stops, wagging her finger my direction. "First off, I didn't pay to see that shit." Her finger wags no longer, instead gripping with her thumb to position the straw strategically. "And secondly, Lance brought me to the premiere." She leans forward, and takes her much-needed sip. Lacie bursts out in laughter. "Lance? He's gayer than eight guys blowing nine guys." "Well I'm not fucking him," Carly chortles. "Besides." Her eyebrows raise. "I'm pretty sure he blew Tommy-boy in the bathroom." Our jaws drop. Lacie asks the obvious. "What would Katie think?" And we all can't contain ourselves, and howl like it went outta style two years ago. "You didn't hear that from me," Carly says through the laughter. "No shit, dyke," I joke. This feels good. I forgot how much I missed things like this. We all take a moment to breathe. That's when Carly decides to lean over and brush the back of her hand against the hack cover-up job I did on my battle scars. "Falling down stairs now, are we?" I brush my hair over the bruise and turn away. "You know it's not like that." "I know," Carly chides. "I saw it." I brush the hair back, and I'm full-on embarrassed. "You...saw it?" Carly tilts her head to the side. "Honey, if you did hardcore pornography, I'd be cheering you on as I pinked and stinked." Lacie spits up her drink on the table, and I smile and shake my head. "You're such a bitch, you know that?" Carly maintains her pride. "Since the day my 'rents fucked in the backseat of a Thunderbird." Things sort of slow down. Everything goes calm. Carly slides her hand across the table, and Lacie follows suit. I clutch both, hold on for dear life. "Thank you," I tell them. The thoughts persist, and I want to cry again. "Thank you." "You aren't getting rid of us," Lacie assures. "We're like herpes," Carly chimes in. "We ain't ever going away." My tight grip pushes capillaries away. I don't wanna let go. I wanna hold on forever. My drink ripples. It ripples again. An earthquake? We rip away, and start to question it out loud. Is this the big one? No, it's getting closer. Bigger. What the hell is it? Lacie almost screams. "It's coming from the front!" We run into the house, through the hallways, past all the empty rooms, heels clicking on marble floors. The rumbling gets louder and louder when we make it to the grand foyer. It's like a jackhammer, rattling windows and fixtures. I push through the front doors. We stand in the driveway, looking for the culprit, when Carly grabs my shoulder and points to her right. I see the shock in her eyes, and I turn my head... I can see Carly and Lacie out of my peripheral, uttering sighs of disbelief, mouths agape. The bastard, he said if he won, "I'm totally doing this shit." I told him he was crazy. He just told me to wait and see. But I can't believe he would... Christ. He did it. He bought a fucking... ****** I shove open the hatch and pop out and this bastard growls like a fuckin' tiger. I've got my bottle of Maker's and my Patton-era battle helmet on, and I am fuckin' amped! "Wassup, bitches?" I belt out to the girls, and I'm euphoric. But they ain't. Hands on hips, pouty lips, slivers of eyes. They ain't amused. Tough crowd. They don't say anything. Nobody says anything. But I can see Lexi, and she's a powderkeg in a mine shaft, and ol' Gus went and lit the gunpowder fuse. It's only a matter of time. Sure enough, she's a ball of fire. "You bought an M1A2 Abrams tank with TUSK equipment?!?" "Yeah," I reply. Wait, what? Everything stops cold fuckin' dead. We all look at her, me and her two shithead friends. She's confident as all get out. "I watch the History channel, okay?" "Well, Ms. Ermey," I say. "Whatcha think?" She shakes her head. "Where did you even get this damn thing?" "Arnie's havin' an 'everything-must-go' sale. And, baby..." I take a gulp from the bottle, and wipe my mouth afterwards. "Everything done went!" Lacie's been trembling since the start. She always was a weak bitch. "Can you even own this thing?" I fish out the paper from my pocket, and flick it over to Lexi. She unfolds it, and I relish watching her eyes go as big as saucers. "This...is this...?" I take another drag from the bottle. "Signed, sealed, and Governator delivered." Lexi stares at the paper for a moment before folding it back up. Her eyes burn with blue fire. "Can we talk about this?" "Ain't nothing to say, babe," I tell her. "I mean..." She pauses uncomfortably. Her voice nearly turns to a whisper. "In private." I'm kinda weary. Maybe it's her friends. Maybe it's the Maker's. But I'm not feeling up to it. "Later, babe," I say. "Later." And it's not the answer she wants to hear. Then Carly starts flapping her yap. Christ, she's insufferable. "You know you're gonna get your Terrence Howard punk-ass tossed in jail." So I chuckle. "Shit, Carly, didn't see you there. They ever find that cure for anal warts?" Carly scrunches up her nose and gives me the finger. People still do that these days? Huh. Lacie gets her two cents in next. She's useless, though, so I don't even pay attention to her pleas. Hell, I'm pretty sure I interrupt her. I know I do. "Fuck! Will the lot of you just let me enjoy this?" They squawk about, spitting all kinds of colorful phrases, when Lexi tells them both she needs a moment. They whisper reassurances or whatever in her ear, and cap it off with hate stares thrown my way. I grab my sack and let them know what's what. Fuck those bitches. When they're out of earshot, she turns into my mother. "Can you get down from there?" I take a long drag from the bottle and keep my focus. "Nah. I'm good here." She closes her eyes and takes in a deep breath. Her eyes open. "Please?" I climb out and take a seat near the gun barrel. She walks up to me, before stopping. "Wait," she ponders. "How'd you learn to drive that thing?" "I didn't," I say as I rap my fist on the side. "Mikael did." Mikael pops his arm from the inside with a thumb's up. "That's my motherfucker." She rubs her temples with her index fingers. "You can't keep it." I jump off the tank. "Like hell I can't." I walk towards the house. She catches up just as fast. "There's nowhere to put it! It's an eyesore! It's..." "Blah blah blah blah blah." I try to get rid of her, speed up my stride, but there's no quit in her. She snakes in front of me and stops me dead in my tracks. "It's illegal, Tri!" I grab the paper out of her hand and unfurl it. "The Terminator says otherwise." "I don't care..." She rips the paper out of my hand and throws it to the ground. "What that damn thing says!" She's breathing heavy. She's still pissed over all that shit? Why can't she just let it go? I did. Then everything turns quiet. Our eyes meet. It's almost too obvious. It's all over her face. "Just..." She pauses and looks away. I can hear the frustration build. I can see her start to turn. "Just be ready for dinner tonight." And she walks away. Just like that. ...Huh. ~~~~~~ The candles flicker on the table at Gary Danko. I sip from my glass of red Chateau Latour and take a bite of my horseradish crusted salmon medallion. He picks away at his braised veal breast and keeps his head down. We've barely spoken a word to each other the entire night. He's been on his best behavior tonight. He didn't hassle the maitre'd for a cheeseburger with bacon and chili fries. He's only asked for more water. He actually put on a tie. I know he's trying to act right. I just wonder if I should buy into the act. When our meals have finished, the busboy snatches up our plates and the waiter asks if we want to stay for dessert. I oblige, order the warm Louisiana butter cake with roasted peaches, Huckleberry compote and vanilla ice cream. He declines and just asks for more water. The waiter leaves us, and Triumph fidgets about. He's uncomfortable in these adult settings. This lifestyle has never really been his thing. For me, I might as well take advantage while I can. You never know what tomorrow will bring. But him... Some days, I wonder if he'd just be happier on his own, out of the way from the rest of the world. Where no one can bother him. Where no one even knows he exists. Where he's just another nobody in a nothing world, doing what they can to get by. But he's not. He's a rich kid with a privileged life that he never seems to have wanted, a booming career that he doesn't care about, and a beautiful wife that he treats like shit. And I intend to get all of this out in the open right now. "I'm sick and tired of all this," I say as I swirl around the cusp of my glass with my finger. I've got his full attention. He tilts his head to the side like a confused puppy. He rubs at his eye. "What are you on about now?" "I get that you think you're this awesome bad-ass straight out of an action movie." I stop swirling the glass. "But you're nothing more than a selfish punk kid, and I'm tired of being your lapdog." He stares at me, and then laughs, incredulously. "Is this about the fuckin' match? I told you, it just happened..." "No," I interrupt. I've never been this sure of myself around him. "I'm talking about all of it." He clicks his tongue, and then goes coy when the waiter brings my dessert around. When the waiter walks off, Triumph leans forward on the table. "Look, I don't know what you want me to tell you..." "How about that you're sorry? That I'm not cannon fodder or some hot piece to show off like some goddamned trophy. That you'll actually care about all of this." I stick the knife in and twist. "That you'll actually care about me." He pulls back, stunned. He's speechless for a moment. The words don't come immediately. "You think..." He's lost. Confused. "You think I don't care about you?" "Well." I need to know. "Do you?" He swallows hard. He bites his lip and gazes down at the table. The gears in his head must be spinning miles per second. He can't even look at me. "How can you even think that?" "Tell me," I ask. "When you run about and get drunk, start fights, go missing for days on end. Do ever think that I'm up, crying, wondering where the hell you are? If the cops picked you up? Or if you're fucking around on me?" His eyes shoot back to me. "Or maybe that you're dead?" He rubs the back of his neck. "I..." "No, of course you don't." I don't let up. "But I am. Every single time. I'm sick to fuckin' death, thinking the worst every time. And when you stroll back through those doors, and you don't care, it...just..." I stop. I don't want to cry. But I'm about to. He stammers. "I...don't know what to say." "You don't say anything," I assert. "I'm not going to be your victim anymore. You're not going to treat me like you do anymore. Understand?" He knows I mean business, and he nods. I take a bite of my dessert. He just watches. ****** When we get home, she storms right upstairs and doesn't say shit. I amble through the house, a bit dazed. It's a lot to take in, when the person that you love with all your heart and soul questions that very love. I feel like I've been gutshot. I need a drink, so I make my way to the kitchen. I open the refrigerator, but it's empty. I start rifling through the counters and drawers, but they're empty too. Fuck. She really did dump it all out. I continue my journey out to the back patio, and put a cigarette in my mouth. I'm taken in by the moon dancing off of the Pacific. But it's not right. It's just not complete. It might never be again. Instead I stare, alone. I never realized all this bothered her so much. I shoulda known. She cares about shit like this. What people think, how people act. She wants to be a princess, proud and tall, adored by everyone. But not me. We're not gonna be prom king and queen. I'm not gonna sign some shithead brat's program. Save for the Brit, I don't wanna be best friends or drinkin' buddies with these fuckin' louts. But she wants it all. I don't know why. There's a thing about people. They just end up letting you down. I stub my cigarette out. I wonder how much wrong I've really done. I need to clear my head. ~~~~~~ I hear loud bangs ring out in the backyard and rush to see what he's done now. I see him on the patio, and make it just in time to see him fire a few rounds from a Beretta handgun into a pile of junk. I almost don't want to ask. Which is exactly what I do when I walk over. "Whatcha doin', Daniel Plainview?" He tosses the Beretta into a box filled with history's greatest death dealers. "I got a box of Heston's shit, too. Free of charge." He fishes out a revolver and looks at it. "That man sure loved his guns." He aims and fires three times. I notice the tank not far off. At least he moved it off the front lawn. "You know," I tell him, as I take a seat next to him. "The neighbors are gonna bitch." "Right." He points to his right. "Would that be the gay couple that lives a half-mile that way?" His finger moves to the left. "Or the old lady with the blue hair that's a mile that way?" I smile. "Point." He lines up the sight of the revolver and fires another three times into the debris. He takes another shot, but gets an empty casing. "Shit," he mutters, and he dumps the shells onto the patio. The brass clangs on the marble. He doesn't refill the gun right away. Instead he spins the chamber. "Randomness," I say aloud. I pique his interest. "Hmm?" "The revolver is the agent for chaos." I reach over and extend my hand, and he gives over the revolver and the bullets. I hold one up, and slide it into the chamber. "If you put one in, and give it a spin, you've got five chances to survive and one to not." I slide in another bullet. "You add another, your survival rate goes to one-third." Another bullet. "Then one half." I add the rest, and spin the chamber "Eventually..." I jam it shut. "The chances simply run out." I hand him back the gun. He holds it in his hand. He stares at it. He doesn't move for a few moments. When he speaks, I hear pain in his voice. It's...different. "For what it's worth..." I nod, and he goes quiet. There's something that lingers about. Embarrassment. Longing. Regret. Whatever it is, he breaks it. "You wanna shoot it?" I hesitate. "Shoot...what?" His grin creeps until it covers his entire face. ****** KABOOM!!! "WOOOOOOOO-HOOOOO!!!!!!" She pops her head up out of the hole and squeals with delight. I smile. I'm glad. She climbs out of the hole and jumps off the side of the monstrosity, brushing herself off. "Fuck, did I ever need that!" I laugh. "Feels good, don't it?" It's then that she stops, and her glee turns to worry. She clutches my shirt. "Wait, was that a live round? I fired it and...and..." She turns away, and covers her mouth. "Oh God!" "Relax." I grab her from behind, rubbing her shoulders. I can feel her trembling. "They're empty shells." Her trembling subsides. "They just put on a helluva show." She swings around, and shoves into my shoulder. "Will you warn me next time?!?" I laugh, and I bring her in close. I don't think about Japan. I don't think about Kansas. I don't think about Phoenix. I don't care about Vegas. Just her, in my arms, head against my chest, purring. The world feels right again. Things are better for now.
