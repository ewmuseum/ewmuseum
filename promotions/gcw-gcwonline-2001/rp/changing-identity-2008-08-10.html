Changing Identity

10 August 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 10th, 2008 <c>The greatest changing moment of one's life is death.</c> Father Emanuel walks down the isle, looking down at his lifeless body for a brief second, then takes the microphone at the bloody and broken podium. "Vita, Perturbatio, Nex, Rerovatio. Life, Passion, Death, Rebirth. Life is the God almighty's gift to all his creatures in exhistance. With Exhistance comes emotion and passion, distinguished from likes and dislikes, love and hate. Passion gives the created a strong desire to survive. Death ends existence, but rebirth continues the legacy of the soul in an everlasting way." A younger Zacharia Taylor touches his knee to the ground and blesses himself right next to the living, breathing, Taylor sitting on the second step of the alter, then kneels before Father Emanuel's side. "But Father, how is it that the soul lives on? Reincarnation does not exist nor is it ever mentioned in our faith." "The rebirth that I speak of does not necessarily mean a physical existence, but more of a spiritual one." Taylor hangs his head in the airy, harmonic words of his mentor... "So, what you mean is a spiritual destination that the physical cannot explore. Such as Heaven and Hell or a parallel existence where the soul.." "No, my son. What I mean is a metaphoric death and rebirth. It changes in the mind and heart. A passion for something changes the mind forever, and changes the very principles that people live by." "One part of them die..." "Exactly Zacharia. When you turned towards God and faith drove you to me, left homeless and unguided, your passion for God, justice, and pure morality suppressed your old beliefs and your soul went through a rebirth. Through this rebirth, you changed identities." The coroners lift Father Emanuel's body onto a gurney and raise it into the air. The sudden stop once the makeshift bed is erect tosses his arm to the side. His hand falls in slow motion as the conversation continues... "Father, forgive me. I have another question. What about when you said, 'continues the legacy of the soul in an everlasting way?'" "That is the rebirth I was talking about. It is a rebirth unlike a reincarnation, but a new beginning, or understanding. The legacy of the soul isn't only what the outside thinks of the person, or their soul, it is also what the person thinks of themselves. Through this rebirth, they are changed, their morals and passions have changed, so it guides them in a different direction of life, or in other cases, death." The echoes of Father Emanuel's sermon still rings in his ears... ---- It was only a matter of hours from when the police responded was everything quiet again in the church. The guy that shot Father Emanuel was still at large, and there was nothing that could be done except make up a sketch and send it through the database at the station. Nothing besides go out and find him... Taylor fought with the idea of going after him once the guy ran, but he was hurt, so he couldn't pursuit the murderer. The blood that was on the ground of the church was sampled, also being run through the database for a DNA analysis. Taylor wanted to find him before the cops had a chance to tell him the identity of the man. It wasn't like it was too dark to see the man, it was just that in the moment of seeing his mentor, his second father die in front of him, and then belting towards the man with fists didn't leave too much room for observations. The fact that the matter happen in a sacred place, the police were all over it, but what was the true intention of killing Father Emanuel? He did not how any sign of struggle. Why would the man fire his gun before he even saw the Father? Was he trying to get the attention of whoever was in the church? These ideas weren't mentioned to the police, not only because they seemed odd, but because any information that would give away the man's identity more than a poorly drawn character of the intruder was foolish. This would not be the first murderer that Taylor would be dealing with... Taylor rises from the pew that he took refuge in as the police were all over the church. Everything was so quiet, yet so loud. As he looks around he sees everything unfold in fast motion. Everything is fast except when the gun was to the back of his head. The painstaking, adrenaline pushing click from the handgun bounces off all sensors in his brain as he reaches this event. Taylor can see himself swing his arm back against the arm of the assailant, but then the slam of the front door breaks his concentration and the image is lost. A man comes stumbling into the isle of pews and falls to his knees. He stands up once again and sees Taylor walking towards him, causing him to fall to his knees again. "I'm..I'm..I'm sorry sir. Sorry to disturb you, but he told me to give this to you..." The man hands over an envelope and then runs out of the church with newly acquired agility. Taylor opens up the envelope and sees a picture and a piece of paper inside. He first takes out the picture, but then drops it, along with the paper. The picture glides back and forth like a feather gliding on the gentle supports of air. When the picture hits against the ground, Taylor stares blankly at the ground. He snaps to and charges towards the doors and swings them open with all his might, slamming them against the inner walls of the church. "Come back! Where did you get this? Who gave this to you?" The delivery boy was already way gone. He served his purpose, and he got out of there as soon as possible. Taylor walks back over to the paper on the ground and picks it up, leaving the picture on the ground. He turns over the paper and reads, "Don't shoot the messenger" on the back. As he looks up from the paper, he sees the stained rug where Father Emanuel spent his last moments alive. He then looks over his shoulder at the still open doors of the church. Which Messenger... Taylor turns around again to go shut the doors of the church. Just before he completely shuts the doors, he looks up and down the streets...wishful thinking. Taylor bends down and picks up the picture and puts it into his pocket without looking at it again. He then heads to the back of the church to go into the priest's quarters, Taylor's new home. He takes off his shirt and grabs a towel out of his gym bag, then walks down the hall into the bathroom where a much needed shower awaits. The shower seems like an altered time, seeming very slow. Each droplet of water from the shower head seemed to leave a lasting impression on the skin. Each droplet can be seen before it hits his body. The elongated time gives Taylor more time to think, without disruption. Who was that guy? Who did he get the message from? Did the killer give him the message? Where did he get that picture? While thinking about these questions, Taylor starts to play back what happen in his head. The guy puts the gun to the back of his head... He was a lot taller than I am... Taylor opens his eyes and swings his arm backwards to hit the hand with the gun... He was built. His arm was massive... The gun goes off, the bullet striking one of the nearby pews... He was white, with a foolish grin on his face... He takes a swing at the intruder and strikes him across the face... He was unshaven, having a strong 5 o'clock shadow... The man takes action and hits Taylor strong with the butt of the gun to the top of Taylor's head... He had a chain around his neck with a cross... The man mumbles something in anger... He knew my name... Taylor punches the man's knee as he pulls the trigger, shooting Taylor in the back of the leg... I only remember glass breaking and the man running away... Who is he? Taylor turns off the water faucet and grabs the towel. The side of his leg was grazed by the bullet, going in and out of his calf, but it was the cuts on his hands that were bothering him from the hot water. He did not remember why his hands were so cut up. The only thing that comes to mind is the crashing glass from his boggled mind. His hands received a few butterfly stitches across the back of his hand, but nothing too serious. He walks into the priest's quarters and walks up to the bed to lay down. He unknowingly starts to rub his chest where his reminder of a scar pulsates under the dead skin. Failure. Father Emanuel is dead, and not one prayer has been spoken from his mouth since the death. Was there a point? Why would Father Emanuel be taken, and why did the killer chose him? These are questions that he can never answer. He does not understand the senseless killing everyday. He was put in purpose to stop this, and to even out the balance of good and evil. His hands were the tools of a preacher, the tools of God. Why did his mentor have to die? Regardless of how out of touch he has been in the last couple months with him, it does not matter. Once a father, always a father in the eyes of a child. He brought him up, and all he can say is that he possibly died because someone was trying to get at him. This is the second time a father of his has been killed in protection of Taylor. First the fire, now the bullet. Both reminders of how evil life can be. Taylor looks over at the light and reaches to turn it off. His eye fixated on the picture that was given to him by the messenger. Taylor turns off the lights. In Taylor's mind, he goes over the picture, but in a dream state. The picture falls slowly to the ground, the glare from the lights passing over Taylor's face in the picture. The Taylor that is seen is not the one dreaming, but the one that is dead, the child that was left in the ashes of his house. The picture is from when he was at a BBQ with his family around the age of five. In the background of the picture is a young man staring at the camera with a very disgruntled face. The look of despise on his face is not for the camera, but for the subjects in the picture. The child's name is Jake Fenning. The child of Zacharia's father's co-worker. The Fennings were always at every party, every BBQ, and every gathering imaginable. Jake was always told to be more like Zacharia. He was always told he can learn something from Zacharia. The newspaper about the fire had several quotes from the Fennings, saying how great of a loss it was to lose the whole Taylor family, especially their greatly gifted son, Zachary Taylor...
