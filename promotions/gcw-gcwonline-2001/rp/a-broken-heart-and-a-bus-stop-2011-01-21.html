A Broken Heart and a Bus Stop

21 January 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/21/11 “What’s the policy on smoking,” asked a delicately dressed Jay Terror. His hands covered the edge of the hostess’s stand and caressed the wood as he stared into her slate-colored eyes. She was young, a college student studying at Rosemont or maybe the University of the Arts, and generously smiled at the long-haired customer. “We don’t allow it,” she informed, “but you may smoke outside away from the door if you’d like.” Terror scratched small ticks into the wood with his fingernails. “That’s a shame,” he replied before changing his tone. “There’ll be two of us tonight.” The hostess, Lauren by label, nodded as she had with every customer and fumbled in the stand for a pair of menus. There was a seating chart on the top of the podium and she double checked it before stepping out into sight. She was petite, further confirming his guess on her student status, and wore a tight black dress. “Follow me,” she said. They weaved past a divider and moved toward the wall where a line of plush leather couches repeated for a total of fifteen tables. Between each couch was a heavy mahogany table that wore a white table cloth and lit candles. Jay took his seat according to her command and slid to the wall where San Giorgio Maggiore at Dusk was hanging. He took a look at the blues, yellows, and reds before turning back to Lauren who was holding the menus rather intently. “I’m sorry,” he said as he reached for the menus. “Are you waiting on a woman tonight,” she asked, intentions completely harmless. “You know it.” “I’ll be sure to point her your way then. Have a great dinner,” she added before leaving. Jay couldn’t help but watch her walk away. It was something about her hips and the natural sway she seemed to utilize that kept him mesmerized. Disappearing behind the wall though, he turned his attention to the ceiling and lighting in general. It was dim, for the mood most likely, and Jay laughed to himself slightly as he realized where he was. He then noticed what he was wearing. For the first time since his press conference at Worldwide 141, he had put on a suit, a well-fitting suit at that, which had been complimented by a crisp white button-down shirt. He’d even found a pair of cuff links in his jacket pocket, perhaps a present from Steven Caldera or James Bryan, and used them to fasten his sleeves neatly. He had found an honest shoe shiner a few streets away and glancing under the table, he was still able to appreciate the glow coming off the face of the leather. To top it off, he’d even seen a barber earlier in the day to trim the frayed ends of his hair, something that he hadn’t done in nearly four months. Jay pulled his sleeve up and took a glance at his new Citizen Eco Drive watch, a gift for beating Vivica Valentine in a ladder match, and realized that he was extremely early for his date. In fact, he was nearly twenty minutes early. He tucked the watch away and opened the menu across his lap. The selection was broad, most of it marked at market value, and he simply scanned the pictures with their captions. Honestly, his focus was far from eating and more toward his guest. Distracting him from lingering on her though was the wrestling ring. Never far from his mind, Jay remembered the phone call he’d received earlier in the day. James Bryan laughed as he spoke and ended up admitting that Garbage Bag Johnny had managed to pull off something admirable. He mentioned how every new guy in GCW was both the luckiest and unluckiest person on the roster. When he finally told him who he was facing, Terror nearly snapped his cell phone shut. Seven rookies would line up around the ring and would each have the chance to walk away with the world title. Not only that, but they would have the opportunity to headline NC-17 in one of the most impressive ways possible. Jay huffed to himself as he thought on it, the taste was still sour in his mouth. What he couldn’t understand was how Johnny could effortlessly ruin everything for Jay. Instead of allowing him to fully focus on improving for Andy Murray, he was continually throwing obnoxious road blocks in his path. Murray on the other hand remained unscathed, matchless since his win at Dangerous Games. Terror looked back down at the menu hoping it would distract him. It didn’t. Instead, he was happily surprised to see Maggie McVay walking his way in a heavy winter jacket. “Maggie,” he said, “you’re early.” He slid out and helped her out of her coat. “What’s that make you?” “I guess I didn’t notice,” he said. Maggie slid in, and Jay tucked her coat on the seat beside him. “Just glad you found the place OK.” “I was surprised you chose it, yeah. I knew of it though,” she admitted. “You order yet?” “No, not yet. Was waiting for you. I feel like you always pick the better things.” “I just pick what I like.” “I guess I just like what you like then,” he stated. Maggie looked at him and at his ensemble. “You clean up well Mr. Talley.” “Don’t call me that,” he retorted. “I mean. Yeah. This is a nice place.” “John and I thought of coming here a few weeks ago. Ended up not doing it. I’m glad we’re here today though.” Jay looked deeper into the menu at the mention of John, but found that he was paying even less attention to the items. Their waiter approached and smiled much like the rest of the staff had. He had a practiced grin, one from years of serving which likely awarded him with a generous payout at the end of each meal. “Hello. My name is Lucas. Can I interest you in drinks this evening?” “I’ll just stick with water,” Maggie said. “I’ll do the same,” Jay agreed. Lucas turned away and his hair hardly moved due to the amount of product he had applied. Maggie looked at Jay, something that she had been doing all along, and spoke up. “I have something to tell you.” “I have to tell you something too.” “Go ahead,” she pushed. “No, you.” “No really. It’s OK.” Jay shrugged and picked at the napkin on the table. He was trying to unravel a string as he began to speak. “I talked with the doctor. He said that there is good reason to believe that I can overcome this mess. He seemed very confident about it too. Said that I have to be careful though.” “Well of course you have to be careful.” She sighed, “if it were up to me, you’d be home instead of wasting your lungs wrestling. These title match scrambles aren’t going to help any.” Her sudden blurb of care showed Jay how she really felt about his condition and about his occupation. She smiled and reached her hand across the table. Jay comforted hers with his. “I’m glad to hear it though. I really am.” Jay smiled, “You know it’s not my idea to defend the title like that. Not against them. Not against anyone really.” “If you had the power that you keep chasing, I’m sure you’d hold that belt until they told you that you had no choice.” “I’ll get that power. I got one night at least.” “I saw,” she said. “You know when that’s happening?” “Whenever I feel up to it I guess. I figure I’ll know when the time’s right.” “Yeah,” she agreed. “You’re always pretty good with that.” Jay rubbed his finger on hers thankful to see that she wasn’t wearing a ring. “So what did you have to tell me?” She sighed for a second and looked at Jay, “Well,” she started. Before she could continue though, a man approached the table wearing a leather jacket and khaki pants. He looked at their hands on top of one another and smiled. “Am I interrupting something,” he asked. Jay looked at Maggie with confusion. “I was trying to tell you that I decided to bring John along tonight,” she explained. “I wanted you to meet him.” Distrust continued to look at Maggie with a new distrust swimming in his eyes and then turned to John. He was a well built man with blond hair and a dusty goatee. “I just thought,” he said before stopping. Terror pulled his hand away from Maggie’s, thankful still about the ring, and forced a smile. “That’s fine,” he finally added. John took a seat next to Maggie and kissed her on the temple before extending his hand across the table to Jay. “I’m John. I’ve heard a lot about you, Jay.” Dressed the part, Jay shook his hand courteously. “Likewise.” Maggie’s eyes read Jay in a moment of silence. Lucas, the waiter, returned with a tray of waters. “I’m sorry, I was under the impression that there were only two of you,” he said. He placed the waters in front of both Jay and Maggie. “What can I get you,” he asked John. “I’ll have a water as well.” Lucas nodded but Jay got his attention before he could turn away. “I’ll have a glass of Maker’s Mark.” Lucas seemed somewhat surprised to hear it but took the order regardless. Jay turned back to the table. “What should we get, Maggie?” “I heard the duck is fantastic,” John chimed in. “A colleague of mine had it a few weeks ago and couldn’t stop talking about it at the office.” “Where do you work,” Jay asked. “With the McGowan investors on Market Street. You know, general brokerage and investments of that type.” “And you like it,” Jay continued to ask. “Love it. Keeps me around the Philadelphia area and Maggie.” Jay turned back to Maggie. “What should we get, Maggie?” She looked back down to the menu, regret possibly lingering on her shoulders, and shrugged. “To be honest, I was thinking just a salad. Maybe chicken of some kind.” “Not hungry,” he questioned. John placed his arm around her smugly and nodded as if he were being questioned. “We had a big lunch today. There’s a place down the road that serves some of the best sandwiches in the city. I’ll give you an address if you’re planning on being in town for a few more days.” “I’m not too hungry myself,” Jay said. “Had a long day.” “Jay heard good news today from the doctor,” Maggie added. She hoped to pick up the conversation and lift the mood, but it seemed to do the opposite considering the look on Jay’s face. John, not one to read faces, put his hand up for a high five. “That’s great, Jay. She told me about it and we were both worried for you.” Terror didn’t acknowledge the high five nor did he care to talk on the subject longer. As he had before, Lucas returned to save an awkward silence with a glass of water and a healthy portion of Maker’s Mark whiskey for Jay. Terror took the glass off the tray for him and immediately embraced the sinister liquid. “Are you all ready to order,” the waiter asked around. “Are you,” asked Maggie to John. “Well ladies first please,” he sad cordially. She looked at Jay before stuttering on the menu. “I’ll have the roasted chicken,” she managed to say. “Can I start with the wedge salad though?” “Certainly,” Lucas affirmed while jotting it down. John looked at Jay and Terror took the hint. Taking another large swig from his glass, he shrugged. “I’ll get what she got.” John waited for him to finish writing. “And I’ll have the duck with apple dressing please.” Lucas scribbled the last of it and nodded happily. “Thank you. They’ll be out shortly.” The air was stagnant again after he left, a dead silence so frightening that John felt the need to fix the issue. In the time, Jay managed to finish a decent portion of his drink. “Well,” John started, “I heard you’re a champion or something with that wrestling. That right, Jay?” Jay took a long look at Maggie telling her everything he needed to. He grabbed his drink and took the last of it before a condensation ring could rightfully form on the table cloth. “I need a cigarette,” he said to the two of them. Without waiting, he stood from his seat and placed Maggie’s coat in his place. “I’ll be back in a few,” he promised. Terror walked across the floor and back around the wall toward the hostess. She smiled at him. “Cigarette time,” she questioned. “How old are you,” he asked back. “Just seventeen. My dad owns the place and said that I had to work a few nights here. How come?” He swore under his breath and looked back to the table. Maggie and John were getting comfortable with each other. She was laughing at something. “Take this for my table. Tell them that something came up.” He placed a hundred dollar bill on the podium. “Tell Lucas he can keep it. You can have some as well.” “Is everything OK?” “Just lost my appetite is all.” She nodded sympathetically and watched Jay pull his box of Marlboro’s from his pocket. He pulled a cigarette from the box and lit it just before meeting the harsh winter wind. Outside, snow had began to fall again. It had been starting and stopping for three days beforehand, but it hadn’t started to stick until he began down Chestnut Street. Each step scrubbed the shine off his shoes and in a sense, he felt himself rid of the evening in general. Before he could forget it entirely though, a woman on a bus bench caught his eye. “When’s the next bus supposed to come,” he asked. Her neck spun around in the cheetah print scarf she wore and her eyes found Jay. “Probably not for another five minutes,” she responded politely. Jay moved closer to her and the bench until he finally took the seat beside her. She wore a long black jacket and black boots that rose to her knees underneath. “Mind if I wait with you?” “Not at all,” she replied. Her words were broken up with shivering but Jay was able to tell that she had a true Philadelphia accent. “How long have you been waiting?” “Just five minutes,” she answered. Terror took another pull from his cigarette. “I bet you watched the first snow flake fall then tonight, huh?” “Not quite.” “Where’re you coming from,” he asked. “Nowhere special,” she replied emptily. “Oh yeah,” he started. “Me either.” “Rough night?” “Something like that. You?” “Something like that,” she responded somewhat sadly. Terror looked at the woman whom he still didn’t know by name, and offered her a cigarette of her own. She took one and thanked him politely. Jay allowed her to light hers with the end of his. “So what happened,” he wondered. She took her first drag and coughed. “Well,” she said before coughing again. “I had an interview. I don’t think I did so well.” “This late?” “It’s a night job,” she said slowly. Terror shrugged it off and placed his arms across the head of the bench. “I got my heart broken,” he replied. “That doesn’t make me feel better.” “I figured it might help some.” “I’m sorry to hear that. Was she beautiful?” Jay blew a plume of smoke into the snow flakes above. “One of the most beautiful that I know.” The bus came soon after that, a glowing beacon of warmth plastered with advertisements for Worldwide 144. The girl stood from the bench and Terror followed suit. Although she was only halfway done her cigarette, she smoked as much as she could to show her kind nature. “You getting on,” she asked. Terror watched behind the bus as headlights started to show on the pavement. A long stretch limousine pulled up. “You wanna’ go for a ride,” he asked back. She noticed the limousine and the cigarette that was burning between her lips still. “That’s yours?” “Yeah. I’ll take you anywhere.” She shrugged, threw the semi-smoked cigarette into the snow, and started behind Jay. “Thank you,” she stated. “No. Thank you.”
