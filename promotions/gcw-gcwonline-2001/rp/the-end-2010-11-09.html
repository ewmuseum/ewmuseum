The End

9 November 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/09/10 Even from fifty yards their forms stood-out, their black outfits like ink blots on the church's white wall. They huddled close as an Autumn gust blew dying auburn leaves and dust around their feet and onto the uncovered casket. Their number surprised him at first, but the deceased's was hardly a life worth celebrating. In his last few months on Earth the man in the ground's greed had torched the few bridges he had left. An almost decade-long quest for revenge came to a head when he forced a dead man's signature and alienated his own family. Perhaps it was just that only six had turned-up to his funeral. The man in the car waited patiently, knowing no good would come if he decided to show his face. The absurdity of the situation was not lost on him. There he was, wreath in hand, camping outside the funeral of a man who'd hated him with every fibre of his being. Doubt hung over his head live a graven spirit. He'd first considered turning around as soon as he saw the church doors slam shut, and gain when he'd seen the widow and her children plod sombrely to where the pine box lay. ”No,” he told himself, gripping his hands tighter round the wheel to ease his shaking fingers. ”This has to be done.” The grey sky grew blacker and blacker until forked lightning streaked across the crowds with a deafening crackle. A dense pitter-patter struck the car's roof as heavy rain started to pore and the mourners dashed for the safety of the church. The Father hobbled behind them and slammed the double doors shut with a bang. That's when the driver open his door and, ignoring the weather, walked towards the grave plot. Eventually he reached the hole. Loose dirt had started to gather on the casket's varnished surface and the water was quickly turning it to mud. He thought it a shame to see such expensive wood soiled, but it would soon be buried anyway. He closed his eyes for a moment. Rain water streamed down from his sodden hair and washed his pale cheeks clean. Soon he was wiping drops from his eyes and kneeling down before the gravestone. He laid the wreath down, stood up again, and turned to leave before being noticed. Through raging winds and pelting rain, Andy Murray walked away from John Hood for the very last time. * * * * * He gets to the arena just after 10am and there's already tens of them there – hundreds, maybe. While some put their hoods up and keep a low profile, desperate not to be spotted, Andy smiles, waves, and makes himself as conspicuous as possible. This is one of his favourite parts of the job. It takes him hours to get through the line. T-shirts get signed, photos get taken, and memories are made. Those he comes into contact with wear a smile that wont leave their faces all-day, but he leaves wearing one wider than any of the fans. It's the same every week: hordes and hordes of fans line-up outside the building before showtime, looking to catch a glimpse of their favourite wrestlers. If there's one constant in professional wrestling then it's their presence, and Andy Murray is always happy to oblige. His presence provides relief to the Triumph Frosts and Bryan Mayhems of the world. They can slink by unnoticed as The Scottish King of Cool draws the crowds in and caters to their every whim. Most wrestlers will stop-by for a while before heading inside quickly, but not Murray. Even on the biggest night of his career he finds meeting fans more rewarding than putting an extra couple of hours in at the gym. It's lunchtime before he's finished, and they whoop, chant, and holler his name as he waves and heads for the building's entrance. Some call it pandering, some call him an ass-kisser, but Andy Murray couldn't care less. He's in the presence of those who'll never turn their back on him, and that's all that matters. * * * * * Kathryn felt her grip tightening involuntarily as her stress levels rose. “Uhhh, no. I'm sorry, sir, but like I said: Mr. Murray is not available on those dates. How many ti--?” She groaned audibly as she found herself interrupted again. There was only one thing for it. Click went the line as she slammed the telephone receiver down and clenched a fist. “Fuckin' prick,” she whispered. “Stressful day?” Andy's voice caught her off-guard. Immediately she sat upright in her chair and struggled to compose herself. “Uhhh, you could say that,” she said, forcing a smile. Munching on an apple, Murray walked into what was once his dining room and smiled back at his assistant. She sat behind a large light-ash desk housing a mountain of paperwork and a powered-down laptop with her glasses on her nose and her blonde hair slightly out of place. “I'm surprised you're handling it so well, honestly,” he said. “You should've heard some of the rants Rueben went on when people rubbed him the wrong way. Nicest guy I ever met, but Christ. It's a wonder he never sound-proofed his office.” She laughed. “I don't think I'm quite at that stage yet.” “No, definitely not. I don't know how you manage to keep up with all this paperwork, you know. I only did it for a couple of weeks, but I swear I've never done anything so stressful.” “It's not so bad.” Kathryn shrugged. “Pretty mundane, yeah, and you have to deal with the odd prick every now and then, but I've had worse jobs. Much worse.” She paused for a moment, wondering whether what she was about to say was appropriate. “Besides, having 'PA to Andy Murray' on your resume is pretty cool.” Luckily for her, Andy chuckled. “Can't do you any harm, I guess.” He took a few further steps forward and sat down at the other side of the desk. Kathryn felt her muscles tense: she was a lot more at-ease with Andy than she was when she first started the job, but his imposing size and undeniable charisma still intimidated her a bit. “Listen, there's something I wanted to ask you,” he said. “Mmmm?”. Kathryn's eyebrows raised. “You think you can balance this with your university commitments? I know I said I'd only need your help for a couple of months, but, well, you're doing a much better job of it than I did, and I'd hate to have to go and find somebody else.” He quickly changed his tone. “Don't think this is something you have to do. If you don't think you can handle this and college then that's cool, I'll think of something. The job's yours for as long as you want it, though.” Though she paused, there was only one answer on the tip of Kathryn's tongue. The job was hardly taxing, and she got to spend twenty hours of her week in a home she could only dream of affording, working for a man who's affability was matched only by his generous pay packages. It was a no-brainer. “I'd love to. Things'll get pretty busy as the semester goes on but I'll cope.” “Are you sure? I don't want to force you into a commitment you can't keep.” “I can keep it.” She smiled. “I promise.” “That's a massive help, Kath,” he said, rising to his full 6'7”. “After this Hood fiasco I think I'm gonna steer clear of agents for a while, so having you keeping up with the paper trail here is gonna save me a lot of stress. Thanks.” She parted her lips to speak but found herself interrupted by the phone's ringing. Andy waved away her apologetic glance and left the room, smiling. The clouds were clearing. Everything was coming together. * * * * * Four hours until the show starts and Andy Murray is walking. While his colleagues pump iron and throw taped fists at heavy bags, The King clears his head. He follows the same match-day procedures he's been adhering to since day one: he doesn't go anywhere near a gym, doesn't worry or fret, doesn't let himself stress. Just eats a healthy lunch, stretches his legs around the arena's labyrinth of corridors, and lets his mind travel wherever it wants to. Today, though, he takes a slight diversion. He's only ever competed in one Dangerous Games match before, but Andy knows the drill. Before she show goes on the air must find out what number he's drawn, and the signs plastered at every intersection make his journey a short one. Eventually he comes to an open and enters with a polite knock. Joey Andrews looks up from a tombola machine and nods. “Murr. How goes it?” “Good, sir, good,” Andy says, approaching the ring announcer curiously. “Hell are you doing back here? I thought the boss usually did this job.” “Johnny's busy so I offered to chip-in. Besides, it's a few extra dollars and there are still a few hours before the bookies stop taking bets...” “Damn, man.” Andy shook his head. “I saw what happened to you on the blackjack tables at Shaman's casino. When are you gonna learn?” Joey shakes his head ruefully. He straights his grey suit jacket and looks back up at the Dangerous Games hopeful. “Lost a lot of money that night, man, but things are gonna be different tonight! I've got a good feeling about this, I'm tellin' ya.” Not wanting to prod at the announcer's gambling addiction but still curious, Andy folds his arms across his chest and tilts his head back. “Oh yeah? Who've you got?” “Fuck if I know.” Andrews shrugs. “I'll do it on instinct; make my pick as soon as I get there. So, uhhhh. You come for your number.” “I have indeed.” “Alrighty then. You just missed Bagwell, actually.” “Oh yeah? How'd he do?” Joey shakes his head. “Didn't look too pleased with what he got.” “Ah well, fingers crossed for me then.” “Yeap.” Joey nods, firing the tombola machine into action with the push of a button. “Yeap.” Tens of tiny white balls fly around the cage as it spins round and round. Eventually, after a few revolutions, one of them drops from the cage and rolls down into a cup below. Andy takes the cup, removes the ball, and cups it in his hands. After a few seconds of staring, Andy looks back up at Joey Andrews. “Well, should I be betting Scottish tonight or what?” Andrews asks. Andy just grins. “Go with your instincts, Joey. See you later.” * * * * * It took him seconds to navigate the familiar surroundings of the Dallas Group's Houston office and soon he'd reached his destination. “Can you get Steve for me?” Andy asked the girl who'd once been Rueben's assistance before walking into his former agent's empty glass-walled office and gazing out across the city. “Andy, how the hell are you doing?” Moments later a voice bounced against the marble floor and took Murray's attention. He turned to meet Steve Callis with a firm handshake. “Never better, sir. How are you?” “Not too bad, all things considered.” His smile slowly faded. “It's been a rough couple of months here as you can imagine, but we're all doing fine. Guess with John dying we're just all wondering what's next.” “Maybe I can help you with that.” Andy pulled a manilla-coloured envelope from under his arm and handed it to the blonde-haired sports agent. “Take a look.” “What are these?” Steve asked, brows raised. “Peel it open and find out.” Steve tucked his thumb behind the seal and separated paper from gum. Soon he had several sheets of paper removed and the envelope tossed aside. He examined them carefully, his eyes scanning down the front page. “What are these, the deeds?” “Exactly.” Andy nodded. “They're a little crumpled, I'm sorry. Somebody broke into my hotel room last week but one of the porters found them lying outside. I guess they were looking for something more important.” He took a few seconds to digest the information, Steve, before narrowing his gaze. “Andy, what's going on here? Why are you showing me these?” “Because they're yours.” Steve blinked. “What?” “I know you bid for the Group at the same time as Hood, and I know you were a couple of hundred grand short.” Andy paused. “You know I posted John's bail, right? I did it so that he could sign the deeds over to me, so that Rueben's name would no longer be tarnished by his dirty hands. The company's yours.” A stubbly jaw almost dropped. “Jesus, Andy. I don't know what to say. How much money do--?” Andy raised a hand. “Don't worry about the money. I didn't pay a penny for these and neither will you. You're a good agent, Steve, and I know that if Cherise had much of a choice in the matter than she'd much rather have sold to you than Hood. It's only right that you're the man to take his company forward. I sure as hell don't have the time to do it.” “Wow, I. Thanks. Thank you so much.” “I know you'll do a good job with it. But listen, I don't wanna pour cold water on this immediately, but I should let you know this too.” Andy took a deep breath. “I'm leaving the Group, Steve. Effectively immediately.” “Oh.” “I'm sorry to drop that on you so soon, but it's time I'm making a few changes in my life and with Rueben and Hood both gone, I think it's time to move on.” Andy's apprehension was soon relieved by Steve's slow nod. “It's time I started taking control of my own destiny again.” Silence hung between the two but it did not last long. Steve Callis' smile returned. “It's alright, Andy. I understand completely.” His disappointment vanished almost immediately: the high from finally controlling the Dallas Group would take a long time to fade away. Steve extended a hand, and again they shook. “Good luck, Andy, and thank you. Thank you so much.” “Just do the old guy proud, Steve. That's all I ask of you.” * * * * * The photographs line the wall of every dressing room he occupies. The last thing he does before changing into his wrestling gear is to take them up and put them on the wall. There's an order to it, and he takes his time. The first shows a young man shaking hands with Steven Caldera, full of glee having just signed his GCW contract. In the second he's standing victorious over Chris Bagwell after the Rampage Tournament finals, and in the third he's beaten and bloodied, but still holding the GCW World Championship proudly above his head. In the fourth he's suit-clad and accepting his Wrestling of the Year award, and the fifth photograph, the most recent one, is of the GCW record books: his name, the GCW World Championship, and 269 days. Five pictures, one for each of his proudest moments. It's been a while since he's had a new photo to hang, but tonight he finally has one. One last photograph: a photograph for Dangerous Games 2010. He hangs it with the rest and admires the view. Two sets of crossed fingers stare back at him from the wall. * * * * * Sometimes Andy wondered how things would've been if he'd followed the same career path as his friends. Marriage had failed him twice and he was sure he and Jane would still be together had it not been for the wrestling business, but that was just the price he'd paid. Wrestling had brought him fame and fortune, but was the money and the glory worth coming home to a faceless hotel room after a hard day's graft? Andy was desperate not to become as insufferable as those in the business who constantly complained about the lifestyle, but it was difficult sometimes. He spooned penne pasta onto two plates and looked across the room. Replicas of his GCW and Just Wrestling belts sat proudly in the trophy cabinet. Had they been worth it? In truth, Andy wasn't sure if he'd ever figure it out. He ladled thick, red sauce onto each plate and carried them over to the table. Marvin, his soon, took his plate gratefully and tucked-in before his father had even sat down. All Andy knew was that moments like this made him realise just how lucky he was. * * * * * He can't stay still. Too much blood in his veins, too much fire in his belly, to much spring in his step. Andy bursts from his locker-room door with his sequinned robe on and makes great strides towards the gorilla position. The crowd are fired-up. It's one of the biggest nights of the wrestling calender and he hears their every “ooh” and “aah” as Dangerous Games unfolds in front of them. When he bashes a fist into his palm he can't remember the last time he was so fired-up. This is unquestionably the biggest night of his career. The biggest night of his life. He's focused on nothing but this since the night LeStatt took his title, and now it was time. Time to go out. Time to perform. Time to re-establish “The Scottish King of Cool” as GCW's top dog. He passes technicians who clapped and called their support but did not acknowledge them. His mind was one-track now. The calm of earlier is long gone, replaced by DG-induced tunnel vision, and he is ready. “This is it,” he tells himself as he reaches the curtains. He hears the fans counting down from ten and the butterflies in his stomach flutter harder. “Seven, six, five,” they shout. “Four. Three! TWO! ONE!” Buzzer. Clutch, “Mob Goes Wild”. He puts his hands between the curtains and bursts out, a ball of energy. They're blowing the roof off the building, and it's been a long time since he felt like this. Knight may have taken his title, Valentine may have stolen his pride, and Frost may have nudged him from the spotlight, but tonight one thing is undeniable. The King is back.
