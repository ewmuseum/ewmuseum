Tiny Fox, Huge Forest

4 August 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/4/09 If you were running a relay race, would you ever stop and question whether or not your teammates are doing the best they can? Even for just a split second, if they hesitated, paused, or slowed down at any time, what do you think would happen? Or are you really the one to blame for a lack of effort because, after all, those thoughts are running through your mind, detracting from your performance. Life is like a relay race. Broken up into parts. Not necessarily all carried through by you all of the time. This is most prevalent in childhood. However, almost everybody grows out of this stage and takes responsibility for their own actions. They don’t continually depend on others. Or be deceived by others. Almost. --WorldWide 114-- FOX “CHEW ON THIS, ESTABLISHED-MINT!” He screamed. BRYAN “What the hell is this!?” His voice echoed. The air was sliced as Clyde pulled his arm back like a Major League Baseball pitcher going from the stretch. His elbow hit it’s maximum flex point and everything just stopped. His mouth was wide open and spit was protruding from his tongue. He had the eyes of a fierce Murloc poised to strike down its prey. For once in his life, Clyde Fox felt pure hatred. But it would all be taken away. The things Clyde stood for, the lessons he had learned. They were all about to be royally flushed down the toilet. Before Fox could lunge forward and decimate Bagwell’s face, an all too familiar feel wrapped around his bent elbow. He was suddenly turning and didn’t know what was happening. WHACK! He caught of glimpse of who it was. Goddamn Garbage Bag Johnny. That putrid piece of trash. Clyde knew then, in that moment, that Johnny wasn’t really a fan of ‘Nintendo’ but instead of a rival company, ‘Microsoft.’ Clyde had been baited once more. He could only mouth the words ‘Mother-fudger’ as the torque of Johnny’s punch sent him reeling into the arms of Chris Bagwell. The final straw had been drawn. Clyde’s trust would be forever changed. WAKE UP! Clyde could see Bagwell. He could feel his authoritative grasp. The invincible Chris Bagwell was about to give Clyde the biggest wake up call of his career. Not quite dead, not yet anyways. Into the air they went and down they came. THUD! Welcome to the big leagues small fry… small Fox. BRYAN “The Torn Identity!” He screamed. Hope you like the view from the mat. Staring upwards… bright lights… ambient noises… no sense of anything tangible. Double… or rather quadruple vision. The Establishment… meet Clyde Fox. --Saturday, August 1st, 2009-- The candles weren’t even lit yet. The only thing shiny in the room was the tears forming across Fox’s lower eyelids. Happy 22nd Birthday. Fox sat in a room that would be better described of as a cube. Four close walls and nothing more. A chair was near a table with a vanilla cake shaped like a Poke Ball on it. Two candles were saddled atop the fluffy icing representing the number twenty-two. I have to change this. He didn’t manage very much thought. But it was better than nothing. He was sadly alone in a room far away from anyone who cared about. And even that number is still pathetically small. FOX “Happy Birfday to meeee,” Fox sang crying, “Happy Birfday to meeee. Happy Birfday dear Clyyyyyde. Happy Birfday to… me.” The sobs overtook Fox as he slowly grabbed some matches from his pocket and let the candles. The flames stood tall, as Clyde could not. Every friend he’s ever had has broken his trust. Taylor, Bagwell, Johnny. A trio of screw jobs. But what was more confusing to Clyde was whether or not he deserved it or screwed it up himself. FOX “And many moooore,” He sobbed. A drip of hot wax slid down the side of a candle. Clyde watched it collide with the base of the cake. A cake he would not eat. Candles he would not blowout. A birthday worth forgetting. Friendships that should have never been. Fox clasped his hands together and slowly leaned into them. He began to cry uncontrollably… alone, in his dark little room.
