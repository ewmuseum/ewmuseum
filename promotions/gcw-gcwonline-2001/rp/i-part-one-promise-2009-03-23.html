I (Part One - Promise)

23 March 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03.23.09 It’s almost midnight in Verona. I should be in bed but I have a half-eaten pack of Godiva chocolate in my lap still waiting to be finished and the caffeine is doing its job. I find myself unable to get to bed no matter how heavy my eyelids are getting. Instead I set on my carpeted floor with my back leaned against the foot of the bed as I stare at the TV. I have the remote control in my hand and the volume is kept low so my parents won’t hear as I flip through channels. For now, I’m just passing the time. It’s almost midnight in Verona. My mama sleeps alone in the bedroom down the hall while my father is probably stretched out on the living room couch. He doesn’t want to go to bed, either, but the thing keeping him awake is a desire unlike mine. My mind knows what will be coming next but I’m too nervous to move. I hear his voice; I know that it’s nothing above a whisper but it’s loud enough that there’s no way I can go without noticing it. "Rosa," he calls. I’m not sure if it’s because of how loud he said it or how unmistakable his American accent is to me by now, but his voice makes my ears ring. Right now Rosaline is my name and I wish it wasn’t, but it is. I’m eighteen years old, too young and afraid to think that I could fight back. The hands on my pink-rimmed clock on the wall above my television unite and point straight up toward the ceiling. He’s probably tired of waiting and I can hear his feet padding along the floor. I have to think quickly, because this isn’t the first time he’s called for me in the dead of night. I know what he wants and as much as I want to fight back I know that’s impossible. I couldn’t possibly hit anyone now. Lucky for me, I have another plan. I buy myself some time and open my closet door so the doorknob wedges my bedroom door shut. Luke breaks in but once he does, he’s sure that I’m gone. My bedroom window is wide open and my bed covers are draped out of it, tied to the nearest bedpost. Mama is awake now and she enters the room behind her husband. She sounds drowsy as she asks him if he knows where I went. It doesn’t occur to her to ask what he’s doing in my room in the first place. I’m not sure if she just wants to go back to sleep or if she’s ignorant of it all. She asks if they should call the police but Luke insists he’ll take care of everything. It’s the easier thing to do. He convinces her to go back to sleep and she is too groggy to put up a fuss. While she leaves, he sits down over the edge of my bed. I’m afraid that he won’t leave until he finds out that I’m hiding underneath the mattress he’s sitting on. I don’t know why I didn’t actually leave; I guess I thought that they would care enough to go looking for me. Instead, it’s an hour past midnight and Luke gives up. I hear the springs in the mattress squeak as he stands up and leaves the room, closing the door behind him. I wait until papa’s footsteps stop before I crawl out from under the bed. Checking my clock, I’m relieved at the position of the clock hands; I’m just in time. It’s one in the morning in Verona but the night is still young in America. There, my cousin, Michael Mercer is having his first heavyweight title match in his career in the SAWF. I’ve only heard of him from family and I’ve never met him in person, but he’s still family and I would never miss the opportunity to support him. I find myself inspired by his performances more often than not. If only I could be that tough. I unwrap the rest of my chocolate bar and nibble on it. I find it difficult not to cheer but I know I’ll be caught if I do. Still, I grin as I watch the card for the evening unfold, waiting for the time where eventually it will be Mikey’s turn to compete. I’m feeling proud that I am related to such a successful celebrity. I begin to wonder how great it would be if I were ever able to compete with him; I know in my head and my heart that I am too small and weak but it doesn’t stop me from thinking about being there to support my family. There’s no way it can be any worse than staying here. I begin to let my mind wander and think about my ring name. Rosaline Mercer doesn’t have as good a ring to it as Michael Mercer does. I need something with a bit more pop to it. I rattle off the names in my head one by one, mulling them all over. Michaela. Nope, that’s too similar. Mia. Not as close to Michael, but loses a bit of that catchy tone to it. I move on to the next name. Marianne. Nah, that one's too bland. Mindy. I’m not so sure about it. It sounds almost too childish to be taken seriously; very weak girly, although a catchy name none the less. Still, it’s awfully unintimidating. At the same time? it fits me like a glove. Mindy Mercer. Yeah- come to think of it, that doesn’t sound so bad at all. I’ve even worked on my New York accent so I can really sound like him, too. It’s difficult, but not too much considering Luke sounds close enough to him. Just one thing he’s useful for, at least. It’s nearly three, and Michael is walking out to the ring. As tired as I am, I have to hold back my excitement by stuffing the last couple pieces of rich dark chocolate into my mouth and chewing vigorously on them. I smile and hug my knees close to my chest as I watch the thirty-two inch screen and watch my cousin’s match unfold. I can feel the butterflies in my stomach; I can’t help but feel nervous for Michael. It’s his third heavyweight title match in his career, after losing his first two. I really think the third time is the charm tonight. He’s doing so well? “but his fortune doesn’t rub off on me when I am so far away. I freeze when I hear the crack of the bedroom door snapping open; I’m afraid in his anger Luke might have broken the lock on my door. Luke grabs me by my black hair and drags me to my feet. One hand covers my mouth as he kicks my door shut. I’m positive mama couldn’t sleep through that, but she doesn’t make a sound; like every moment similar to this one, there isn’t anyone to save me. I’m in there by myself and I wonder how mama can let that happen. He hits me, beats me black and blue, but it feels good compared to what I had expected. He wants me to cry, to hit him back. Each slap and punch and bite and choke leaves its own painful sensation and bruise, but I don’t cry. I smile through it, because I know soon he’ll give up and leave my room. Over Luke's grunting and the thud of his fist against my chest, I hear Michael’s name. He’s being awarded the one thing he wanted his entire career. I laugh in my father’s face. As brief as it lasts, I enjoy the furious look in his eyes. I almost feel like I’m being announced the winner as well. Bloody and beaten but I’m a winner, immune to being broken even as he tries punching the grin off of my face. Despite the immense pain, I continue to laugh. ~ It’s four in the morning in New York City. I wake up from my dream and I cry. I don’t realize I’m in Markus? house until he’s awake from his chair across the room, quickly tending to me. "Are you alright?" he asks, "You went out like a light in the car. Last time I give you a drink," he jokes comfortingly. Like always, I smile through the tears. "I woke up from a good dream," I lie. He doesn’t need me giving him further reason to worry. I’m just wishing my nightmare could’ve been the way the reality of it had turned out. I promise myself I’ll never cry for dad again. I’ll win this time, though. And the next. And the next. And I won’t stop. I go to the ring every night by myself, but I know this is different. Now I know I can fight back. Now I will fight back.
