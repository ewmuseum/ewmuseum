Date: 07/06/10 "Wasn't until he beat Vivica that I bought it. He was new and finding his face still; joining Caldera was just another rock he looked under. Taking that belt from her like that though -- damn. There was no denying the devil that had been created." -- Former Jay Terror Fan --- There was nothing peaceful about his walk much like there was nothing peaceful about Japan. The people, the traffic, the people trafficking -- all of it was as insufferable as it comes, hence the decision to set-up camp in the homeland. Instead of resting in the suite beside Clyde Fox, Jay Terror took the airplane from Beijing straight to Philadelphia. There were reasons, sure. There's always a reason behind maddening eyes. These reasons however, were hardly new. Privacy was a factor, naturally, as was home-sickness. Still, no reason was grander than Maggie's reopening of the McVay gym. The cobwebs had been cleared, the aroma had been replaced, and the bags and weights all stood with a new shine - a gift from Jay himself. There was potential for the small training facility and Maggie was all smiles as she opened the front doors that Steve, her late husband, had formerly installed. There wasn't a crowd, nor was there a commotion, but what Maggie saw on opening day was a recuperated dream. Her life's passion and true desire had finally made its return to reality for one final run. With her hands on her hips, she grinned watching Kent Skelly smack the speed bag. "You sure cleaned it up nice," said a stranger. However, the stranger wasn't as mysterious as he believed. The cigarette rasp, the Philadelphia twang, and his breath itself all killed the surprise. She spun but left the smile on the other side. "You're supposed to be in Tokyo right now getting ready for your match." Terror smirked, "Who says?" "Me." "How compelling. I'll get onto the next flight." "You're doing this again, aren't you?" "Doing what," he inquired. "You're quitting. Maybe thinking of quitting. You're doing something stupid hoping I'll talk you up." "Do I do that a lot?" "More then you think." He shrugged, "Not today at least. I'm here for you, here for the place, here to get away from all those people." "Which ones?" "Asians, reporters, those people I have to fight. Way I see it, out of sight and out of mind." Maggie shook her head and leaned into him. She embraced his back with a comforting rub and Jay returned the gesture with a hug of his own. "Thanks for coming. You like everything? I didn't know if the weights looked right this way, and everything was so heavy. I was afraid that people would see it and leave." "Looks like you've managed to avoid that so far, huh?" He pointed to the speed bag monster. "Guy looks like he's right at home." "We'll need more than one renewed membership to catch-up to the pennies we spent on restoring things. The rats tore just about everything to pieces." "Forget costs, Mags. I got some extra cash lying around that I've been waiting to spend." "What about the alcohol and cigarette cost?" "Covered. I may not be winning but between endorsements and my reputation, I've got sponsors practically handing me the stuff." "Speaking of winning -- a knife?" Her face twists with the air of perplexity hanging between them. Jay rolls his eyes and runs his hand through his hair. "Maybe I got out of hand. It's just been a tough few weeks." "Tough's no reason to stab a referee. You want the title that bad or is it just a win you're after now?" "Very funny." "Just saying, keep it low key. Hawaii was a mess in itself." Jay agrees. They're in the office now, Jay on a desk and Maggie in the accompanying chair. Terror's pulled loose a cigarette and Maggie's shut her eyes. It's early still, nine in the morning early, and she yawns to clear the air. Terror responds, "Late night?" "Couldn't get comfortable. You know how it is. The memories this place brings up are almost unbearable at times. I figure I'll get used to it, but last night was no hay ride." "Well if it means anything, I know he'd be proud. Steve that is. He may have run the place, but we all knew why he did it. Seeing that glow in your eyes again is something that's been missing lately." "Jay Terror, dare I say that you sound comforting." "Don't let it out of the office." "Of course." "So you think I got a shot at this match?" "See, there you go," she said triumphantly. "What's that?" "The lifting your ego up thing. I swear Jay, every time you get ready to wrestle I'm sitting behind you rubbing your neck. Are you just nervous or do you like to hear my opinion?" "I like to hear your opinion." She shook her head knowing that he'd never admit to nerves. "Well you know what it is. It's the same every time you wrestle. You're good, one of the best, but you're an idiot. You could beat every person on that roster if you wanted to, but for some reason you think too hard and blow it." "I was thrown off with Bagwell." "I didn't mean Bagwell. I meant against Murray, against Windsor, against anyone that's beaten you. Stop thinking and just do." "Do I really do this a lot? I never noticed." "All the time." "Does it make you mad?" "The only thing that has me mad is that you're not training right now. Get out there on a bag and start wailing into it. I'm sure Kent will even hold one for you. Just do something." "Thanks." "No, thank you. Glad you came to see the place today." "It's those foreigners. Out of sight, out of mind." Maggie laughed and Jay slid off the desk. He made his way onto the newly installed rubber floor and found the second speed bag. Kent was stretching instead of punching, but Jay liked to pretend they were hitting head to head. His strikes were fast, they were heavy, and they were better, in his opinion, than anything Skelly could muster. Maggie watched from the window in the office while combing through old records. It was how it used to be. --- "You know this will change everything, right?" The voice belonged to Steven Caldera and he sat behind his desk. Jay Terror was the man on the other side, the man who had to make the decision. He played with his bandana under the desk thinking that fiddling would help. He tore strings lose from the edges, frayed the piece of Rich Rollins memorabilia even more. In the end, it only multiplied the nerves. "I still don't think so. I have support. People like me." Steven Caldera smiled in his fatherly way. "People like the good guy, Jay. If you go through with this tonight then you're throwing it in their faces. You're telling each and every one of them that you don't need them to succeed. It's a slap in the face but one that grabs my attention." "And you promise that I'll be great?" "I promise that you'll be a champion." "What about Jordan?" "Jordan White isn't like us. He had his time with me and it wasn't for him. If he's the reason for holding back, I'd seriously think about things, Jay." "I am thinking. It's just not easy. I like it when they cheer me on. I like to be helped along when I fall into a bad spot. It's encouraging." "And gold isn't?" Steven Caldera had heard the complaint before from Jordan, LeStatt, and Octavian even. Each one had their reasons not to join, but each one bought into the bigger picture. With Caldera, prospering was certain. It was the image he needed to place in Terror's mind. "This is a chance to be the best there ever was. You'll be a champion, a hall of famer, an icon. People may not cheer for you but deep down they know you're going to win. They'll love to hate you and at the end of the day, it's still love." "Couldn't I get the title my way though? Couldn't we change the face of The Establishment?" "There's a formula in place that shouldn't be messed with. Believe me, there's going to be a new face, but it's not going to be how we do business. It's going to be that bandana and your eyes." He was still tugging on the strings. "And you'll be out there tonight? You'll come out and let the world know?" "That's the easy part. I need you to send a message to Jordan White. You need to send a message to Vivica Valentine and GCW. Win tonight and do it in commanding fashion." "Did LeStatt have this much trouble?" "He was never sold on impressing people. He wanted to be the greatest. No if you want an answer, but he's just that kind of person." He took a deep breath and exhaled slowly. The bandana came up from below the desk and he tied it around his neck again. Grabbing a pen, he nodded. "Where do I sign?" Caldera grinned and slid the piece of paper across the desk to him. He guided his new son to the dotted line and leaned back in anticipation. It was the night of April second, 2008 that Jay Terror signed his soul to the devil. However, most called it Rampage 2008. --- Even at thirty-seven, Cary Kolat walked with the same swagger he carried down the halls of Jefferson-Morgan High School. Stepping out of his car, he was as intimidating as he was on the wrestling mats. There was no denying his presence whether you knew about his perfect high school record or not. Fortunately, many didn't know the story behind his career. They didn't watch him in his time with Penn State and they didn't remember his performance with the United States wrestling team at the 2000 Olympics. To most, he was a fit guy, the kind that frequented the gym in an attempt to mimic his younger form. To himself though, he was getting through each day one rep at a time. He was outside of the new gym still when he slid his sunglasses onto his forehead. The sign above was new and read 'McVay's' in thick red letters. He nodded; knowing that he'd made it, and continued through the open doors. Even with four Pennsylvania State Championships around his waist, he felt nervous entering the gym. It was a new facility, a much needed transfer from his old one. Beyond that, he'd heard of the names training inside. They were up and comers, high school guys on the same path that he had traveled in his prime. Cary reached into his pocket as he stood inside and grabbed a crumpled piece of paper. It was a classified ad. The instructions lead him toward the front corner office and on his way he took the time to marvel at the machinery. There were weights for all sizes, bags for ten men, and rings for boxing, wrestling, and cage fighting. They had it all within the small establishment encouraging him even more to seal the deal. Approaching the office, he knocked on the door frame. Maggie McVay was seated at her desk licking the back of stamps. "Hi, I called about -- " Before he could finish, Maggie stood from her seat. She rushed toward the Olympian and grabbed his hand to shake it. "Cary Kolat, I sure as hell never thought this day would come. Hey." "Ha. Hey. You know who I am?" "I graduated Lock Haven a few years ago. Your pictures are all over the place. You're a legend, Cary." "Yeah, I guess I did well there. What a coincidence." "I'd say," she said following it with a gulp. "I'll be honest; this gym is standing because of you. My husband got into wrestling because of the NCAA championships you won at the Haven. It trickled down to this." "I'm flattered. I'd love to meet him." Maggie took a second to remember Steve, but only a second. "Unfortunately, he passed on. I've reopened in his memory though." "I'm sorry to hear that." She put on a grim smile and looked down at the crumpled newspaper in his hand. She was dying for a transition into a new topic and found it with the classified ad. "So you saw the ad? You're actually interested in teaching people?" "It's been my passion, yeah. I asked around and people said that you had some talent waiting for some encouraging words. I'd like to see if I could help out." "You don't know how much that'd mean to me. Our numbers are low now, but the people we have are some of the best boxers and wrestlers in Pennsylvania." "That's impressive. It's a good state for them." "Well a person who's done as much for the state as you have is just what we need here." Cary smiled at the compliments. Even at his age, he enjoyed remembering the things he'd done. "You think I could have a look around?" "Of course." She took the newspaper ad and tossed it into the waste basket. "I'll show you around." Maggie led the way out of the office. She was a helpful guide, speaking proudly of what she had built. First were the machines for biceps, triceps, leg presses, and bench presses. Then came the treadmills, the ellipticals, and step machines. She moved onto the medicine balls, bungees, and free weights. A little further came the first ring. It was meant for boxing and wrestling yet no one was inside. Maggie continued by showing off the punching bags, speed bags, and kick dummies. In the corner she showed off their newest addition, a full caged ring for the growing MMA trend. She left a side note that Jay Talley had paid the expenses. When asked who Jay Talley was, she laughed, and promised to introduce them promptly. As the tour came to an end, he found his familiar playground. They were wrestling floor mats, circle and all. He felt them with his palms and grinned wide. "You have it all, Maggie." "It took time and hard work, but I'm proud of what's here." "As you should be. I'd love to help out. I think I could do well for those trying to learn." "I'd love to have you here. It'd mean a lot." They ventured back to the office to sign the correct paperwork. All the while, Maggie wore a Cheshire over his shoulder. It was finally her week. --- Losing at Warpath wasn't how he said it would go. He promised greatness, promised that things would be easy. Between Valentine and White though, it was the furthest point from a sure thing. They had heart, they had more bite than their bark predicted, and they out-wrestled him. Instead of wearing the title belt, he sat backstage with Tyson DeBough. Neither one spoke about the incident or what would be done to fix it. Instead, they stared into their lockers waiting for their father to lead them. A television was on and they watched Steven Caldera walk down the steel walkway. Tyson spoke first. "Did you know about this?" "Nope." "You think he's gonna cut us loose?" "Because we lost?" Tyson gulped. His mind was more focused on Jay's loss than his own, but he ended up agreeing nonetheless. "Yeah. He's looking for winners. We didn't come through on the promise we made." "I don't think he'd do it." "Then what?" "Just listen." They listened. It was Vivica's time to celebrate her defense. It was her chance to say that she had beaten The Establishment and beaten Jordan White. Steven Caldera wasn't ready to accept that though. He was a harsh critic, an angry father. When it came down to it, he shocked the world by putting his foot down once and for all. Instead of balloons dropping and horns blowing, the name Jay Terror came from his lips in the form of a rematch. The crowd was stunned, silent even, and grabbed their hair at the announcement. Jay had been granted another opportunity. "Look at that! You got yourself a huge match, Jay." "Yeah," he replied. It was a shocked response. "You alright?" "Just taken back. I didn't think - I just didn't know." "Well you better get suited up. This is your chance to redeem yourself." "Yeah." Later that night, Steven Caldera would meet with the two of them. Tyson DeBough found his orders were to deny Garbage Bag Johnny of another win. When Caldera turned to Jay though, there was a different conversation. "Don't screw up" he said. Jay swallowed and accepted the challenge. He'd bought his opportunity and it was then that he realized why he'd signed up for it all. This time, he wouldn't lose his chance. He wouldn't fall again. --- It was a Wednesday morning when Jay Terror left for his Philadelphia home and a Wednesday afternoon that he returned to McVay's. Entering through the doors, he immediately found a change. The gym had come alive with activity contrary to the prior days. Moreover, a circle of people formed around the American wrestling mats. He approached, finding Maggie's waving hand calling for him. "Don't ever let him take your back," came the voice on the mat. It belonged to a man and Jay stepped onto his toes to see who. Unfortunately, he had no idea. Maggie grabbed him around the waist and spoke into his ear. "How about this? You see this crowd?" "This a demo?" "Do you know who that is?" Jay shook his head, "Never seen him." "I'm not surprised. That's Cary Kolat. The guy's a wrestling legend here in Pennsylvania. He went one-hundred and thirty-seven and oh in high school. Ended up winning two NCAA championships and medals at the Olympics. He's a big deal." "I'd say. When'd he come in?" "Just after you left. I changed the message on the sign out front mentioning he was here and these people showed up. Amazing how well he draws a crowd." "Heh." The Olympian grabbed his opponent and grabbed him around the back much like he told the others not to. He shoved him into the mat and wrangled his arm in a wrenching manner. He released after a second of showing off. "I'm going to be teaching a class here for the next few weeks. If any of you would like to join then find Maggie," he pointed to Maggie who raised her hand. "She'll be able to help you out." "Teaching a class," asked Jay. "I think it's brilliant. He loves to teach and just walked in off our wanted ad." "Aren't we lucky." Maggie couldn't tell if he was being sarcastic or not. It didn't matter though as Cary was approaching them. He talked to people on the way through them but his eyes were locked on Jay. "Cary, I'd like you to meet the guy I was telling you about. This is Jay Talley." He offered his paw and Jay grabbed it firmly. Kolat had the handshake of a gorilla. "Pleasure to meet you. I heard you were the benefactor to this place." "That's me." "A fan of working out or just a friend with a deep pocket?" "He wrestles," interjected Maggie. Cary started to grin but she added more to her statement. "He's a professional wrestler for Global Championship Wrestling. He wrestles under the name Jay Terror." "Never heard of you. Then again, I can't say I watch very much." "I don't blame you. It's all a big act anyways." "Stop that," Maggie stammered. "He's talking about the backstage segments and animosity. The wrestling is as real as the stuff you're used to, Cary." Cary seemed interested. "Mind showing me a thing or two then? I've never really wrestled like that." "No offense but I think we should stick to what we know." "I can take a punch. Go on then. I wanna' see some stuff." Terror shook his head and eyed up Maggie. She was thrilled with the prospect of them working together. Jay looked again at Cary who was bent over with his knees ready. "Just don't break my arm. I have a fight coming up." Whether Cary heard or not, he didn't show it. He stayed bent down and ready to block. Terror cracked his knuckles and moved in to grapple with him. To his surprise, Kolat passed to his back and chopped him onto the mat. It was almost identical to the expo he'd walked in on. Kolat spun and grabbed Jay around the neck. Terror tapped on the mat. "You ever have wrestling training?" Huffing, "Never. I taught myself everything." "I'd be glad to help you." "Let's just try that again." They reset positions and Terror went for a kick to the shin. With reflexes beyond the range of a thirty-seven year old, he grabbed Jay's leg and dropped his weight across his stomach. Jay tried to wiggle free but found his arms wrapped around his back. Kolat, using his strength, whipped Jay overhead for a vicious suplex. Cary stood again and helped Jay back to his feet. "I really think you could benefit from some help, Jay." "I'm just getting warmed up." "One more time?" "Yeah." They circled and this time, Kolat was on the attack. He went for Jay's feet but Jay had other plans. He stomped on his neck and jumped onto his back. Kolat felt the shoe imprints but stood anyways with Jay on his back. He fell, forcing all his weight onto Terror, and wriggled free from Jay's hands. Kolat changed positions and grabbed him around the waist. He rolled him on the mat into a pin forcing Terror to quit again. Out of the twenty-nine times they wrestled, Jay Terror lost twenty-eight times. In his one victory, he managed to poke Kolat in the eyes. Maggie's voice chimed in as they were getting ready to go for a thirtieth time. "I think you guys proved your point. Take a breather and get some water." They listened and followed Maggie to the office. Kolat continued to speak with Jay. "Look Jay, I think it'd be good to learn how to defend against stuff like that. There's gotta be some guys in your wrestling that have trained like me." Jay could think of a few. "What would we do?" "Just defense stuff. I'll teach you how to manipulate your weight, seem bigger, and block the takedown." "I fight in a few days." "I won't tire you out." "Fine." Maggie was at the front with a beaming smile. Whether Jay knew it or not, it was the one hole in his game that needed to be patched up. Remembering their conversation from the day before, she turned and looked at Jay. "I think you can win this, Jay." Jay shrugged. His apathy was a tell-tale sign that he agreed. --- As Josh Brigg's hand hit the mat for the third time, Jay Terror knew he'd done it. Rolling backwards, he shut his eyes and smiled wider than he ever had before. The World Heavyweight championship came to his possession soon after and Terror pulled it as close to his person as he could. He wanted to hold it forever, never let go. The crowd was shocked, some were even appalled, but he didn't care. Just as Steven Caldera had promised, he stood atop of the roster as the champion he was meant to be. Stepping over Valentine, he climbed the turnbuckle. Tears wanted to crawl out from their ducts, but he denied them. He stood proud instead, showing off his championship to one audience member at a time. People say that you can live in a moment for a lifetime. During his time on the turnbuckle, a lifetime wasn't long enough. He'd found his one true love and shared his warmth with it for what felt like weeks. In the end, nothing comes between love. Once you get a taste, you do everything in your power to keep it up. Jay Terror was ready for another taste.
