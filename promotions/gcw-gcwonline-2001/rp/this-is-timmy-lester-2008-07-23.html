This Is Timmy Lester

23 July 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/23/08 “Jesus Christ, Lester, where the hell were you?” Garrett Michaels was fuming. Timmy Lester was not to be found all week leading up to WorldWide 94, and since Michaels was the one who hired Lester, well, he takes those kinds of unexplained leaves personally. “You missed out on all the promotional stuff, you looked like absolute shit out there...” “Yeah, no shit. You’re not the person who just had an ass in his face.” “You embarrassed me out there, you’ve been embarrassing me all week. This isn’t like your movie career, you’re not the big shot...” “Yeah, about that, that’s the reason why I couldn’t show up. I’ve been trying to tell you that. I’m still trying to get out of my movie career, and I’ve been running around like a chicken without my fucking head on so I could cut those ties and give you and Steve...” “Mr. Caldera to you...” Tim squinted and shook his head. “You don’t know me all that well, do you?” “Apparently not.” Michaels turned around to leave the locker room. “Wait up, just listen.” Michaels turned around. “All right, I’ll bite.” Lester inhaled deeply. “Look, I’m not gonna lie to you. I’m dealing with a bunch of spiteful dicks, and it was my fault for miscalculating the length at which they’d go to make my life miserable. It won’t happen again.” Michaels sighed. “I hope not. Because it’d be a shame for this company to miss out on you because you can’t get your shit together.” ---- Miami in the summertime is hotter than hell. Scratch that. Hotter than hell and a lot more humid. Nothing like the breezy California coasts, but fortunately for Tim, he was only stopping by for a short visit. “Bruce!” Bruce Campbell looked up from his script to find his old friend heading towards him. “Tim, hey! I guess you didn’t get beaten to death in that boardroom.” “No, but it got a little hairy at times. Normally, those types don’t scare me, but I’m pretty sure at least one of them was strapping.” “Yeah, tough times nowadays.” “You got out at a good time. I’m sure cable TV is a little less seedy.” “Yeah, it is. And I don’t have to worry about not getting paid either.” “I’m trying to make that transition. Trying being the key word.” Cambpell’s expression went from jovial to serious instantly. “What’d they do?” “Exactly what you said they were going to do, Bruce. I told “em I wasn’t going to do the reshoots because I had stuff for GCW to do. Then things got testy, and well, let’s just say I raised my voice a little and used some colorful language.” “Classic Timmy Lester.” “Yeah. And in classic movie exec style, they boned me, Bruce. Bad.” “You mean...” “Yeah. They told me since I was outright refusing to comply with studio demands that they were well within their rights to tack another film on the deal.” Campbell furrowed his brow and put his hand to his chin. “Yeah, I figured they’d try to put one over on you. Spiteful fuckers, the lot of “em.” “What am I gonna do, Bruce” I don’t want this life anymore, but if these fuckers keep doing this to me, I’m gonna lose my shot with GCW.” Campbell nodded. “You’ll figure something out. You always do.” Someone started to yell something in the distance. “Tim, I gotta run. I’ll meet you at Barton G’s tonight, all right?” “Yeah Bruce, thanks.” Tim walked away from the Burn Notice set and hopped in his car. As the ignition turned the car over, his radio started playing “Big Bottom” by Spinal Tap. ---- The door flew open to a smoking suite at Toronto’s airport Marriot. In barreled Tim, tossing his duffel bags on one of the queen beds and plopping on the other one. It’d been a turbulent flight, and customs weren’t exactly kind to him. But he finally got to his room and planned on relaxing a night before getting started with the pay-per-view’s promotional work in the morning. He grabbed the remote and flicked on the TV. Wrestling with Shadows was on, and a light bulb went off in Tim’s head. It was almost like something was speaking to him. He grabbed his cellphone and hit 15 on his contacts list. “Hey Phil.” “Lester” Shit, you never call me Phil. You must want something.” “Yeah, yeah, I should have figured you wouldn’t say hello. Caller ID is your best friend.” “Save it, Lester. What do you want?” “I have an idea for a movie that’ll make you rich.” “Really” How altruistic of you... oh wait, you hate my guts. I’m guessing you need me to make it.” “So cynical. But yeah, that’s it in a nutshell.” “Ha, it’s not cynical if it’s true. Lay it on me.” Tim cleared his throat. “How do you feel about going legit?” “Legit... is this an intervention?” “No, if it was, I wouldn’t be involved, Napoleon.” “There’s the Lester I know and despise.” “Har har. But hear me out here. How would you like to do... a documentary?” “A documentary” What the fuck, are you trying to bankrupt me?” “If I was trying to bankrupt you, I’d be selling you on a script for Gay Robot 2: Son of Gay Robot.” “But documentaries are so fucking... boring.” “To you they are because you’re an ADD-addled dope dealer with a fetish for manly looking hookers.” “Fuck you, Lester, all right?” “Hey, I’m trying to make you money here. I mean, look at it from this angle. People like reality television, right” They especially love it when it features someone washed up or only marginally famous doing something that they’re not exactly known for doing.” “What does this have to do with anything?” “Shut up and listen, Napoleon. I was just getting to that part. All reality television is is a documentary serialized for weekly consumption. What we’re going to give them is a two-hour snippet of reality TV in the theaters or at the very least, straight to their DVD players, featuring their favorite B-movie actor branching out and trying valiantly to come back to wrestling.” There was an awkward pause. “So, what you’re saying is, I help you by making you a household name AND releasing you from your studio contract without having the hassle of going back and forth between your road dates and Hollywood. Yeah, I ain’t doing you any favors.” “And why not, Napoleon” After all the shit I did for you, you’re not gonna pay me back?” “What?” “Don’t play stupid with me. Remember the dead hooker in Modesto that I helped you bury” Or how about when the studio bigwigs caught you with all that pot and I said it was mine? Or need I bring up the time that I kicked that...” “All right, all right, you made your fucking point. I’ll pitch it to the execs. You fucking... “Thanks Phil.” Tim pressed “End” on his phone and went back to watching the television. ---- Fast forward to a couple of days later. Tim sat in his locker room, lacing up his boots, even though the pay-per-view was still a few days off. It was time to promote Warpath. He looked around at the camera crew surrounding him, nodding. “I’m ready when you guys are.” “Not so fast.” Tim looked behind him. It was Michaels. “Oh, hey, am I in trouble with the boss again,” Tim sarcastically asked the CEI higher-up. “No, I’m just checking in and I wanted to thank you for making the extra effort to get here early...” Michaels trailed off as he noticed that the camera crew in Tim’s locker room wasn’t a GCW one. “This isn’t one of our camera crews.” “I know. They’re here for the documentary we’re shooting.” “Docu... what the hell’s going on” What are you...” “Relax, Garrett, relax. Nothing funny’s going on here. Remember when I told you that the guys I work for, well, soon to be used-to-work for, were spiteful dicks?” “Yes, I do.” “Well, turns out that while they may be spiteful, they’re definitely not mindful of the consequences of said spite. When they told me that I was going to have to do another film to fulfill my contract, well, they never specified what kind of film. So, I suggested that we do a documentary on my wrestling comeback.” Michaels gave Lester a puzzled look. “This isn’t going to be one of those expos’s on the seedy side of the business, is it” Because...” “Relax, relax, I’m not about to bite the hand that will be potentially feeding me for years to come. If anything, I want to paint you guys and the business in general in a very positive light. I know you guys took a PR hit recently with the whole PTC dissolution thing. I was thinking maybe this could give you some of that positive image back.” Michaels rubbed his chin. “Y’know what. What the hell. I guess there’s no harm. And I’m guessing that with this, you won’t be having to fly back and forth and you can put your full energies into being a GCW superstar?” “You bet your sweet ass.” “Good then, carry on. I’ll let Mr. Caldera know the good news. This should make his recuperation that much better.” Michaels left the room, just as the director on site said “Action!” Tim smiled for the camera as he started talking about how electric the pay-per-view air was. He knew his ticket was punched.
