Pines of the Past: 3

4 September 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/04/11 I 12:23 AM Tony glanced over at William, "This it?" "Think so...n' looks like he's up." He looked onward at the small, brick house. At a window, facing their vehicle, stood a shadowy figure. His eyes appeared a bright white, such as pearls in a peaceful slumber, confined by a shell, a house in this case. It was an awfully eerie looking place. A thicket of brush enveloped the landscape, clusters of weeds bunched together like strongholds, acting as fortresses for critters and insects alike. Grape vines slithered their way around tree branches and strangled them without hesitation. Competition was everywhere, with plants striving to be the tallest, striving to feel the sun's heat, and striving to live in a place so unfamiliar, but it was unfamiliar only to the gazing onlookers who still did not budge, and William suddenly realized that his eyes were no longer meeting those of the stranger, but directly on a shadow looming in the darkness just beyond the east portion of the house. "You see that," his voice cracked, and the words moved softly across the car. His eyes expanded like a balloon, growing larger with each puff of air, the sheer thrill sent his whole body through a downward spiral of fear branching from the utter unknown. "The guy?" "No, I saw something...over there." He pointed toward the side of the house, which was a very dark spot. They could barely make out the English ivy that crept upward along the house, digging into the bricks and mortar like fangs into flesh. A Rhododendron contorted its limbs into tentacles of the night, waving rapidly in the oncoming breeze. A bed of leaves rustled about, somersaulting into the humid air and becoming crippled against the cold links of a fence which enclosed the area. As all of this unfolded before their eyes, Tony simply lost track of what he was suppose to be looking for. Everything was so dual, and nothing stood out more so than the next illusion, or object, but either way it was becoming quite difficult to decipher the shadows from their creators. "Are you sure you're not just seein' shadows, 'cause that's what I'm seein." Tony kept his cool, as nothing had really happened yet. He slowly glanced over at William and studied his behavior, feeling a sense of discomfort now, and slowly feeling more distant from everything. His mind raced back to the old man, sketching into his memory the eyes which felt so damningly cold, and soon after vanishing without even a fare warning, or goodbye. He felt alone, and William wouldn't take his eyes off the shadows, the ones which pulsated in and out, exploding and imploding into diverging shapes of the unknown. "Does he know you," Tony hesitated to speak, but the words rolled off his tongue as if undaunted. "He'll know who my father is," William responded after a brief moment, releasing his attention at last from the indistinct region bearing the likes of dismal entity's of other worlds. It seemed a bit outrageous, so he thought no more of it. II 12:36 AM It wasn't long after that the two made their way to the doorstep. The walk seemed long, and each step was harder than the last. Tony made sure not to step into any holes, or anything of the sort, seeing as the yard was a complete booby trap. Branches of many sizes and lengths rested at the base of trees, ones with great girth and bark of deep furrows rippling up and down the trunk. William led the way, stepping over obstacles with ease, and keeping his peripheral vision at hand. Once at the door, they looked to one another but nothing was said. Tony of course made no attempt to knock, with his mindset being that this was William's trip, not his, and he was just along for the ride. Suddenly, a brisk wind shot through swiftly and percolated into their blood stream, activating some urgency within and causing William to knock rather loudly, without any recognition of the time. At this point, such facts had no meaning, but rather more importantly was that they were here and saw that the man's presence was indeed up and out of rest. His hand, tightly bound into a ball, crashed into the wooden door a few more times. Tony paid no attention, for his eyes began to tick back and forth, scanning the floor boards beneath his boots. It was the gaps between the porch floorboards, however, that rattled Tony's spine. Little specks of light grew bright, and then dimmed down. He wondered if somehow a few lighting bugs found their way under, and confidently confirmed this query and thought no more about the matter. Just as his eyes returned to the oak door, it cracked open and a raspy little voice cried out. "You can't have it!" The voice tore a hole into them both and quickly caused them to take a few steps back. Tony almost fell backwards, but caught himself upon the railing of the steps just before doing so. "Excuse me?" William replied, his face now twisted into a look of confusion. He then introduced himself to see how that would be perceived. "My names William Curr, and this is Tony Zichichi," he gestured over to Tony, who kept his distance further back on the porch, as William had taken a few steps forward as he spoke. The door remained cracked, and enough to reveal a wrinkled face with haggard teeth, stained and worn. His chin protruded outward with a prominent cleft, while sagging a bit with a few hairs spiking outward with a grayish gloss to them. "I don't know you," he hissed, "Why are you here?" "I just wanted to ask you some questions," William said in return, trying to somehow gain the man's trust. "Questions," the voice rose louder, yet the door still did not budge any further. His dry lips, with an accumulation of white crust, moved yet again, but with less roar. "What kind of questions?" "I guess I should ask what your name is." "Why are you here?" Spit flew from the pink and white tongue, flipping in and out like a serpent. "I'm seeking a Daniel Shy." "If you must know," he huffed in anger, "I'm indeed Daniel Shy." A friendlier tone emerged. "I've been looking for you." William smiled but it wasn't long until a voice rained down on his happiness. "Now off you go, I have no time for this nonsense. I'm not interested, how many times must I repeat myself?" William's eyebrow rose and he quickly returned with a question, "Doesn't the name Curr ring any bells?" He was beginning to grow upset, and had one last word, "It's my home, I will not sell this home!" The words exploded with rage, and the door exploded as well, right in William's face. "Damnit," he yelled, thrusting his head downward in rage and turning to look at Tony. "Well, that blew up in your face. Can we go?" "We're this close," he replied, "And I'm not leavin' 'till I get some answers." He spoke with diligence, and all Tony could do was shake his head and turn away. III 12:50 AM William approached the door to try once more. Tony was getting a bit uneasy, and began to pace around. He once again began to see the flickering lights from beneath the porch, but abruptly they stopped. He squinted his eyes and watched the cracks for a moment, hoping to witness them occur again and possibly get a better idea as to what they could be. Nothing. He took his attention away and considered it settled, as he wanted nothing more to do with the unknown. His blinking eyes rose to find Williams hand extended outward. His closed fist almost moved in slow motion, as Tony perceived it and watched on with his jaw dropping like a sack of bricks. In the corner of his eye, a figure stumped into play and he quickly jerked his head to the side of the porch, where he peered outward into a dense mat of fog which bled together with the darkness of night to create a barrier to the naked eye. A rough, greasy, patch of fur caught his eye, and he barely had time to seize the moment as it jolted back into the shadows. "Wouldn't you go on already?" The voice, hissing with hate and gurgling all at once, startled Tony, who jerked his head once again and in the direction of the voice. William stood before the scrawny man, who now hesitated no longer and opened the door to reveal himself whole to those invading his property. William studied the man, piecing together the memories of the past, patching together a face that he once fish eyed a long time ago. "Can you just tell me if you once knew of a Jack Curr?" Daniel's lip curled, and his large eyes rolled about in some sort of deep thought. His greasy, grey hair was in need of a good comb. His clothing, which consisted of a pair of slacks and a stained white t-shirt, smelled of mold and urine. He returned his scrutiny towards William and lashed his tongue outward, sending onward a few bold spoken words. "So you ain't those Natural Gas bastards, comin' to buy my land to frack away?" "Not at all, I just have some questions, questions regarding Jack Curr." "I worked for the man, 'n what ties might you have to Jack, just curious." He took a breath and continued. "Been dead now a few years." "You don't have to tell me...I'm his son." Daniel's head nodded a bit and an odd groan left his throat, engrossing the two men before him. "Errh, I see." His hand, with long slender fingers, stroked the brittle surface of his face, massaging his caved in cheeks and slowly running down to his pointed chin. His bug eyes twitched back and forth, examining out and beyond the extent of his property, and then back towards the two. Tony still did not budge, but still kept note of flickering lights rooted beneath. "I don't know nothin'," his temperament was once again strained of balance, relapsing back into a state of age induced stubbornness. A loud blast echoed throughout the nights sky, followed by a violent zigzag of lighting, illuminating everything for a concise second and erecting the hairs on the necks of William and Tony, but not Daniel. The wind began to pick up and William fixed his eyes solemnly onto Daniel's. "Can we come in?" "And what in the hell for," Daniel grilled. His bristly brows angled downward as he grasped tightly onto the door, almost as if ready to slam it once again. He did not, however, and instead parted his dry lips, "I wasn't expecting anyone." "I'm really not trying to cause any harm, just came to visit my father's Grave, 'bout two hours North of here. I figured you could share some memories, seeing as I didn't see him a whole lot or anything." The sky went ablaze, yet another devious bolt of lightning shot downward in the starless background. It exploded just as they caught a glimpse of its blinding, brilliant figure. Tony, for the first time, stepped forward and made his presence known. "Looks like it's gonna pick up, can't drive in this. We're not from round here, so a cup a coffee with some out-a-towners wouldn't hurt, now would it?" "If ya pull back down and outta my drive, turn left and head on your way, you'll come to a truck stop 'bout 10 miles out. That's all I can help you folks with, now get on." "You're makin' a big mistake, pal," Tony jawed. "Shut your mouth, Tony, "William said in frustration, and then turned his attention back to Daniel, who gave Tony a good, hard stare down. "Look, I saw some shit when I was here last, some shit that I can't explain." "Stuff, what type a stuff?" "Two bodies, up at one of the sites. I remember my dad taking me there and we fell some trees, got my hands dirty for the first time with him. Never heard a word about it, like they vanished into thin air. It always made me wonder, but my relationship with my dad wasn't open to such a subject, I was scared." Daniel bellowed a rough snicker and flattened the ol' brows allowing some humor to set in. "There's stuff round here you'll never know unless you live here. So you say you're Jack's son. Alright, but don't touch a thing!" He patrolled his eyes on the two men before turning his back, and stepping into the dimly lit house. The two men looked at one another, and then back on the doorway. They walked in single file, with William leading the way.
