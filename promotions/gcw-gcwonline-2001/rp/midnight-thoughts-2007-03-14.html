Date: March 14, 2007 Sometimes, I stare up at my ceiling and I wonder if anyone even likes me. It may sound like an odd question, my brain understands this. It’s too hot in Miami for that though. Instead, I spend my time staring at the fan, trying to mentally manipulate it to go faster. Down time is what leads me to wandering thoughts, those helpless little things that carry me away into a place I don’t quite like. Tessa tells me it’s because I don’t “like myself”, whatever that means. I like myself. Hell, I love myself. It never makes sense to me, but she repeats it every time I tell her about what I think. I hate this, trying to see something in the dark. Things move, things that shouldn’t move. My brain tells me that it isn’t real, just a trick of the mind, but damn my eyes don’t seem to listen very well. Finally my eyes start to get settled and I can make out the difference between the chair beside the door and the stack of unpacked boxes by the closet. Tessa hasn’t seemed to notice my movement yet, that or she is just too tired to care. My legs rest on the floor, waiting for the remainder of my body to follow along. Every muscle in my body has to work together to pull myself up from the bed. The business is hell on you, physically; don’t ever let anyone tell you otherwise. They want to claim it’s fake and everything is planned, but nothing will ever remove that bitter sting you feel when your bones fall to the stiff canvas and bounce off as if they had just hit unforgiving concrete. If you aren’t training right, it can knock the senses right out of you. I’ll make a mental note to go easier in the gym tomorrow. Working for two different companies means I can cut back on the gym, because nothing is as good of a work-out like an actual match. Maybe I’ll just work on my cardio, something low impact. I should see that doctor Markus was talking about, the one that gives him his pills for his knee. Each step to the bathroom seems further than it has in months. You never really realize just how far away something is until you try to approach it as quietly as you can. Or maybe my mind is playing another one of those tricks on me and I’m actually making good time. Fuck if I know, my head is too screwed up right now. I had to catch myself, sitting down on the toilet, to stop the groan that was trying to escape from my lips. It’s a sad state of affairs when sitting down just to whack it causes you pain. Both hands tightly clinched on the sides of the bowl, her head hangs low with her nose nearly touching the disgusting waters. It’s not much, but it is all I can do to help. Many a man would say he would easily take it upon himself, her suffering, if he could. But not me, I couldn’t last. Maybe that’s why people don’t like me. An oddity comes to me, a thought that I’m not sure why it came dawned. When I step in the ring against Pleasure and Pain, will it be like looking into a mirror? If you were to ask anyone among our circle which lover’s tag team was the best, many would correctly answer the Schmoopies. Yet in all this time, I can not recall ever having to face off against another lover’s pair. What would it be like, having to face that kind of unity? It’s strange really; I don’t know why I should even be thinking of this. Not when my soon to be wife is hunched over a toilet bowl, emptying the contents of our chicken salad dinner. Looking at her, she almost seems like a stranger to me now. Not because of any personal differences, but rather the physical ones. She uses to have such a body, one that made me hard as a rock every time I saw her. People can say what they will about who we are, and what we are like, but the sex at the beginning of our relationship was unlike anything else. I have many times looked in a dictionary, trying to find a word that could possibly describe it. Scanning the book front to back, I didn’t find a single word in the English language that could properly express what we had or the things we did. They were beautiful, chaotic, and full of emotions; so many emotions that I don’t even know all of their names. Now though? She has changed, and doesn’t have a defined shape anymore. Day by day her belly grows bigger, changing. Yet the sickness affects her so, she looks pale and tired. It is an odd combination that leaves her shapeless, changing too often to be ridged down with anything well defined. Without as much as a moment’s notice, she is up and moving back to the bed. Her body moves as if it was on autopilot. Was the entire thing to a dream to her? Had she really not noticed me standing there beside her, or was she too tired to let me know? A feeling of shame washes over me. Here she is, carrying my child, and I am selfishly thinking only about myself and whether or not she notices me. I hate it, I really do. At times I do hate myself, though I even hate to admit it. I’m lucky though. Through the bottom cracks of the window I can see the sun starting to rise. I’ll catch a quick nap down at the gym, where I also go when I have a long night. The owner, Bernie, has an office in the back that uses to be for the other owner. But now that he bought out the other guy’s half, he has no use for it anymore. He’s let me throw in some pillows and blankets, along with a sweet flat screen TV. I can catch a few winks or watch some videos to do film study. I’ll go ahead and head into the kitchen, to fix the lunches for the kids while I make breakfast. Pulling my pants up, I continue to stare out the window. Again, for the oddest of reasons, I think back to Pleasure and Pain. Are they like Tessa and I? Do they have that same passion we had for the business not so long ago? As their competition I can only hope not. But as a spectator, and as a man who loves the sport, I can only hope so.
