Hate The Vultures, But Fear Their Actions.

8 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/8/2006. World Wide #50 Backstage At The Pacific Coliseum. Vancouver, British Columbia. Jacob McKail stood at the very rear of GCW locker room, glaring at the massive white board pinned to the far wall, hope that the night’s card might’ve changed since the last time he read it, in a e-mail sent to him last week. No such luck and he never expected there to be. It wasn’t official GCW policy, but the card was pretty much etched in stone from the very moment the booker conceived it unless, of course, something major came up in between conceiving the idea and the beginning of the event. Predictably, McKail’s bitching and whining couldn’t be forced into that category. Absently, he fished inside his pocket and extracted a packet if cigarettes, took one out and lit it with his silver lighter. He took a single drag of the cancerous stick and already felt himself calm down. “Dude!” some voice inevitably sounded up. McKail didn’t even both turning to face its source. “You can’t smoke in here!” McKail chuckled, in between drags. “Like a give a fuck!” “You’re an ass,” the voice whined, “do you know that?” “Yeah,” McKail told him, “I’ve been told that before.” Whoever the hell it was grumbled under his breath and stormed off and left McKail with a grin sliced into his face. Hell, one of the main reasons he smoked because it pissed people off, which in turn cheered him up a little and with the damn three way match later on that night, he needed cheering up. The three way dance. Almost as bad as the battle royal. No structure. No hope. No fair shot. These things were born to cause chaos. McKail couldn’t think of anything worse than actually losing a match without the opponent actually having to defeat you. They were the matches favoured only by the vultures of the wrestling industry - and he hated vultures. McKail threw the unfinished cigarette at the closest wall to him and lingered there for a few more minutes, hoping that someone else would make his day and complain about his “filthy habit”. But nobody did, so he left. 3 days later. He opened the e-mail as soon as plopped into his mail box. This weeks card. And, thankfully, it didn’t involve any fucking three way dances. He could make all the excuses in the world for why he lost the match, and he had, but none of them meant anything to him because McKail knew he was superior to them both. Yet he lost. He wasn’t pinned, but that didn’t matter. He lost. He should’ve been quicker in breaking up the damned pin. He should’ve been there? He shook the thought from his mind. He didn’t it now. He didn’t the negativity crawling back into his mind to cast seeds of doubt that he might never be able to un-sew. Focus. Focus on the revenge taken out against that mystery attack last week, the one that cost him the damned Television title after going through so much? Focus on this weeks match up. To further get his pound of flesh, facing this mystery man head on. This week, he had nothing to complain about. But he’d make damned sure he complained anyhow.
