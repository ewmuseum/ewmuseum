Not Entirely True

11 October 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/11/08 "Everyone that wrestles is crazy, Frank. Sjust, how it goes. Doesnt matter if they were crazy to start with or if wrestling made em that way. Theyre just, theyre crazy. You, me, Jordan, his little brother Casey? Crazy as shit." Frank hung a smirk and stared across the table at Trey Miller. He took another drink and he shook his head. "What? Youre gonna act like you dont believe me? Alright then." "Nah, nah. Noticed that same shit myself. But, damn. You gotta draw some lines, man--like, shit. Maybe Sage is a little out of it, but he aint as out of it as like, Twizzy was, or GBJ is, or, hell. Nowhere near as bad off as fuckin Casey." "Maybe not, but that doesnt mean hes any less crazy. Come the fuck on, I mean. How insanes a person gotta be before you start docking em credit for it? Like, I dunno. You got an acceptable level of crazy youll accept outta people?" Frank nodded. "Yeah, shit. Either that or look down on everybody I work with for bein fuckin nuts." Leaning back, then, cast eyes over the railing and to negative space between windows, amber and purple and faded blue and some lingering in white or similar enough to not be distinguishable; dropping stories, skipping floors by five, by ten, by sheer omission of the distance between. The golden arc of a street lamp. A coupe stopped under a red light. A trunk, out of sight, that rattled against the strain of bass that didn't make it to his ears. "How are you gonna look down on em? Youre crazy too." "Not that crazy." "But crazy, right?" Frank's head dropped. Rolled. He took another drink. "Maybe. I could be." "See. Its, if you stay around those weird ass wrestler types long enough? Youre gonna end up just like em, so howre you gonna hate them for bein crazy?, is what Im saying. I got out, you know, and Im still fucking insane. Like, you better start thinking bout retirement if you want to come out like, halfnormal," and he laughed, his teeth a crescent. And Frank swept pupils to Trey. "Sage didnt tell me to find you so we could just sit around an bullshit." "And youre done wasting your night on washed up Trey Miller. Thats just cold--got no respect for your elders." "How old are you?" "Doesnt matter. Whats Scratch want?" "Hah. Got a block on just callin him Sage?" "Like to call people for what they are. Or think I do, whatever. Jordan, Sage, Satan himself; we know who Im talking about. Sitmatter what name were usin to invoke him?" There was a silence in the city. A waitresses' voice hung in the air. "Whyd he send you?" "Whyd he send me, or whyd someone get sent?" "Whats he want?" Frank rolled his cigarette between his fingers, read the logo on backwards rotation. "Me here so I can check up on your ass, then that I hop a flight back to the UK two days after I even fuckin got here like yeah, jetlag aint no thing." "Hah." "Yeah. And, you know? I think, by now, Im gonna be able to tell him whats up with you, one way or the other." Trey finished his vodka. "Shouldve known I was being interviewed." "Studied. I wasnt askin questions. I was listenin." He was standing as he talked, drawing his wallet. "Im payin. Have a good one." "Youve got some serious fucking problems, dude. I dont even know you, but you got problems." "Yeah. An Im gettin real tired of em." A bill floated to the table. Trey dropped his brows, cut eyes to Frank; another bill followed. "Shyeah, I was keeping track on how much I drank. I know how fucked up I am." Frank had his back to Trey, on stumble past a grove of wrought iron tables and the affluent 18-33 demographic. He threw a hand up in a demonic facsimile of a wave and he was gone, into an elevator. He shoved hands in the pockets of his jacket and buried his chin between its open collar. He was looking rough again, he was avoiding the face of his reflection in the metal accents. He ran his vision in laps through the curves of fake woodgrain and he continued to descend. He sucked the hairs of his moustache into his mouth, ran them back and forth in his teeth. He thought about trimming and he was already putting it off until some nebulous date of "before it matters" when he reached ground level. He was shrugging the world off and he was out of the lobby and into the streets before he opened his eyes in any useful manner. He looked up over his shoulder to the line of the roof against the night sky and he didn't see Trey and he headed for his car in the subterranean yellow of a parking garage. He fell into his seat, phone already dialing. It rang until Sage answered; "whats good, young buck?" "Treys an asshole." "Shit. So you actually got in touch with him." "Yeah. Bout the twentieth time he called you Jordan instead of Sage, I just had to get my ass outta there. Like, I dunno. Brick walls buttin heads." "I never really saw eye to eye with him, myself. It didnt run me off, either, though." "Yeah, but no way was he this bad back in the day. I mean, its like, he was namedroppin all the way up til he started just talkin shit about the job, an. Damn. I just wasnt in the mood for all that shit." "About your job?" "Yeah. Just, yknow. The usual shtick from ol wrestlers, bout how its bad for your body, bad for your brain." "Hes right, you know." "Yeah, but its my job and I dont wanna hear that shit. Like people tellin you to quit smokin coz its bad for you, yknow? Actin like Im too dumb tove figured that shit myself." "Drinking?" "Bad for me too, but I aint that big on it." "No, I mean. You been drinking?" "Oh. Yeah, yeah. Was bout to hitch a cab, or somethin. I dunno yet." "Huh. Alright. Other than being an asshole, what can you say about him?" "Shit. He aint that crazy, he dont, like, appear to want you dead or anythin. Talked some trash on Torm, so they aint all buddy buddy or whatever. I mean, shit. What you wanna hear? He wasnt like, beggin me to get him a job wichu or anythin." "But you did put the offer out there?" "Yeeeeah. Well." He put his head on his steering wheel. "Fuck. Okay. Ill get up there, do that, an hit you back. Aight?" "Heh. Alright. Do that." And Frank hung up and lit another cigarette from the one smoking in his ashtray. He slammed the accelerator to the floorboard, pantomimed driving and laughed when he fell back into his seat. He tussled with his hair and he snuffed his cigarette early. Car door open. Head hung, shaking. He stayed in his seat and he pulled out his phone. He dialed; Trey answered. "What, think I fucked you on the bill?" "I aint even thought about it. Look, man, Trey, dude. Sage wanted me to, to offer you a job doin, somethin, or somethin. I dont really know--look, to me, at least, its just fuckin ridiculous that I gotta act as middleman between two grown up motherfuckers and. Just call the motherfucker, aight? Ask what the fuck he wants, and get it done with." Trey paused. "You have his number?" "Yeah." And Frank gave it up; he felt the matter was complete and dragged his feet back inside. He rolled details of the conversation over and over inside of his skull and he thought, again, and wondered which came first: the insanity or the job. He wanted to meter himself and was thinking of ways to avoid violence until he was facing Terror; then his phone rang. It was Zoe and he came up with several reasons he wouldn't have been able to answer at that time, all fair excuses and most of them centered around the notion that he was, for all intents and purposes, supposed to have been located in the United Kingdom and was therefore: subject to a different time zone than her, distanced from his phone, training, showering, dealing with the press. The list continued until voicemail picked her up and it was then that he felt safeguarded against the subject should she ever stab it at him in the future. Resentment, he guessed. He wanted to talk to Leslie again before he lost his mind; he'd addressed the problem and was trying to cope in between flights and matches and junkets and training and a world still foreign in spite of the fact he could count his time with it in years instead of months. Not many years; he still had an out and the time for it. It wasn't that he hated all his peers, and it wasn't even that he didn't enjoy--to some degrees--what he did in the ring. He was changing, was all, and he didn't even have a serious problem with that; he just worried about who he'd become. He'd lost the moral qualms that used to at least disturb him when he'd considered using brutality in the name of job advancement and he knew, again, that the only difference between him and Terror was placement; considering again, he decided that Jamison Talley had more patience in regards to Caroline, but that was tangential and--even with heavy eyebrows and a brain that didn't work entirely as advertised--he knew it didn't really matter. He sneezed, cracked his forehead against the steering wheel, and felt the first drain of blood. "Little time to heal. Cmon. Sall Im askin for." He shook his head and the trickle diverted to either side; he wiped it away and dug in his glove box for a napkin, a towel, some useless scrap of paper; there was nothing that fit properly so he took his BMW owner's handbook and, cracking its spine for the first time, ripped out enough pages to cover his forehead until coagulation kicked in. He put his car into accessory and relit the cigarette he'd cast aside. He closed his eyes and he entertained visions of production delays, of scheduling hold ups, of just managing an extra few hours for himself to recover. He let the window down to keep smoke from clouding, stagnating, and he let out a cough, shallow, that dislodged nothing. He, face beaten by steering wheels, dreading the inevitable, reasoning and reasoning his peace of mind away, was Jordan White, the most successful current iteration of a name that once deserved an addendum like "The Hate Machine." He knew he wouldn't live up to that kind of legacy, but he felt his subconscious had tried to form him into a similar mold; he felt like, at best, a Bone Machine traipsing blind, indulging shark nature whenever he caught a whiff of gore. No social capabilities. No great insights. No connection between the overworked head and the uncontrolled body. And someone was tapping at his window. He rolled it down with one hand, killed volume with the other. The instructions as how to properly refill power steering fluid fell to the seat, crumpled and red. "Hey, you alright in there, bro?" Parking lot attendant, vested, goateed. Some guy Frank had walked past twice. Frank rolled eyes to him. "Yeah. Just. Lil staggered sall." "Whoa. Waitaminute, man. Youre, youre. Youre fucking Jordan White, arent you?" "I, uh. I wrestle as Jordan White, yeah." "Holy shit. I knew I recognized you before, but I didnt place you until I saw you all busted up. Holy shit, bro. Whats up?" "Im fuckin trashed and, ungggggh, uhunno?" He gestured with that, a hyperbolic shrug, palms up and smirk uneasy, confused, lost. "Shit. You want like, a photo or somethin, man? Im not really like. Talkin to fans mode. Yknow?" The attendant shook his head, laughed. "Well, no offense, but Im not really that big a Jordan White fan anyways. Its just, I dont exactly run into a celebrity everyday. Holy shit." "Yknow. I kinda thought I was about to catch a drunk drivin charge when I heard you." "Well, if you try to drive out of here, Im going to have to call you in. Nothing personal." Frank nodded, rolled his hand. "Oh yeah. Of course. Nobody wants to see me, like, try to drive righ now. Jus be, yknow. Bad idea." "You want me to call you a cab? Its the, its protocol." Frank Black closed his eyes. Very tightly. He opened them and the kid was still there. He was tired, Frank was, of many things. Red was nearing the top of his list; he grabbed the boy by his scruff and slammed his throat across the door. He fell across the concrete and there was a sound of gargling and Frank stepped out. "Mother fucker," other epithets. He stumbled, some, and kicked the boy in his ribs. There was no motion from the body and the gesture felt empty. He slurred something and it echoed down the rows. He kicked another man's car and he was starting to consider urinating across it when an SUV pulled to the till and got its ticket. He started off, legs fumbling around a rhythm as he fell across the black hood, slamming fists into it and shouting at the driver. The man's eyes went wide and his wife turned to him; they found reverse and smashed backwards through the gate. It ripped their bumper off. Frank threw his shoe at their windshield as they hit the street and he didn't see them again but he went after his shoe and he pulled it back on, staggering, finding that rhythm. Awkwardly jabbing his foot into his shoe on every step. "Cunts." A blue sedan crept by and he assumed they were staring; he pulled a loose brick from a low wall and sent it tumbling through their windshield. He approached. A man stepped out and Frank slammed the door shut across his shins. The man screamed in his seat and his two passengers stepped out from the far side. "The fuck?" Frank grabbed the brick from the dash and held it above his head. They circled, surrounded him with threats; he switched feet, faced them down on a constant rotation. One reached in and Frank took a step back, brought the brick down against the base of the man's skull as the other grabbed him from behind. He struggled and crouched beside the exploded skull, left the standing man holding his jacket like a cat with a lizard's tail and he turned on the balls of his feet and punched the man in his groin. Frank was standing and he looked down the hill at traffic as it came to a standstill. He touched his forehead and dragged the fingers across the man's face, left a red smear. There was little struggling and Frank pulled the car door open and the second man's ankles were hanging limp, free and he started to scream again as Frank moved his friend's head into the aperture between door and car; he swung it closed several times and there was a crack and it finally shut. Another body slumped to the ground and Frank started down the street, wiping his mouth. The front row of drivers tried to reverse and there was the sound of metal scratching and plastic cracking and horns honking and the head of the pack just accelerated past Frank while drivers and passengers alike deserted several other cars. He called someone an asshole and a man in the middle, gridlocked, responded and came at him with a tire iron. Frank mumbled the death koans he had learned as an introduction to his career until they drowned out the noise and all was silent and the man swung at Frank's head and Frank ducked, very low, and tackled at the man's ankles. The crown of his head as the initial impact point against the asphalt. Frank rolled him onto his back and knelt over him and the man put his arms up and Frank headbutted him in the bridge of his nose until the man wasn't fighting anymore. He locked his hands around an unshaven adam's apple and locked them tight and pulled, and pulled. Blood welled around his fingers. He spat and he growled and he put his foot at the man's sternum and leaned back. Someone grabbed his shoulder and pulled him away; the man's throat gave and Frank swiveled, shoving whatever meat he held in the interloper's face. A police siren wailed from behind, from the peak of the hill. Frank kicked the intruder to the ground and he stomped their neck and moved on. Men in black suits aimed guns at him and spoke through a loudspeaker. He never blinked. They came to him and he retreated and he maintained his distance as the threats increased for every inch the front line took towards the remnants of his temper tantrum. He backpedalled into an abandoned car and crouched, ran around it and weaved his way through the mass of vehicles and most of them were idling and he had his pick of a truck with an unobstructed exit. The police fired and missed the truck mostly and him entirely. He took a left and he careened headlong into a river of flashing highbeams and swerving motorists. He went around the block, stood at the peak of the hill and flicked his brights at the police officers returning to the cars below; they froze and he put both feet on the accelerator and rose from his seat. The engine roared and it hiccuped as it set off. One cop dove to Frank's left and he swerved into her, caught her lower spine beneath the driver's side tire and left the ground as his bumper sandwiched another's torso with a precinct cruiser. The man made little noise; there was the roar of twisting metal and the car rolled onto its side and the truck was jackknifed into the air on top of it, tailgate and rear of the bed demolished. They careened down the hill, showered sparks and tumbled until the car beneath gave and flipped onto its roof. The truck was spinning wheels, riding axle to axle headlong into the vacant automobiles below. Frank's stomach slammed into the steering wheel and his head dislodged the safety glass. The sled came to an abrupt halt and the truck didn't; it hung a crescent in the air and, with some modicum of grace, landed on its side and straddling a wagon and a coupe. Frank coughed and something thick came out; he pushed his door open and fell to the concrete, rolling from beneath the suspended truck and pulling to his feet on some silver trunk. He coughed again and it was blood splattering across some silver trunk. He pushed at his lower ribs and he shook his head. The officers were coming down the hill without discretion. Running, screaming. He lit another cigarette and the filter was red and something other than smoke filled his lungs as he dragged and he moved beneath the truck and found the officer's legs, tangled in the truck's grill and twisted bumper, all in a mass of red and redder. Frank reached beneath a slop of hanging intestines and wrestled the pistol from its holster as a warning shot or just a missed shot collided with the truck's gas tank. He ducked and another one planted itself quietly into the half of a corpse. He took the safety off and stood, letting off three rounds into one officer's torso before taking cover. A barrage pitterpattered against steel and plastic. He stuck his head up and emptied the clip as he retreated down, down past the flowing gasoline and into the confusion of cars. He ripped his shirt off and dragged it along the truck's leaking stream and opened a car's gas cap and, pushing the stopper aside with his finger, forcefed it as far as he could. He took his lighter and he held it beneath a dry section of cloth as the police joined him in the sea of autos. It flared up and he fanned the fledging flame until it spread and he fell further away, sparking his lighter along the trickle of gasoline. It blazed back to the truck and someone screamed but it was outpacing any escape. Frank ran too, then. He turned and he ran and he heard the roar and he felt the shockwave and he lost his balance. There were lights closing in from the other three directions. He sat down in the intersection and listened to the world going up behind him with his legs crossed and his neck leaned back, palms up to accept whatever his heavens would offer him as the heat radiated across his bare and bruised back.
