Begin Again (Part Two)

Date: 02/11/12 Act I – "Dr. Kasidy Williams Drake Comeback Tour" Chapter Two – "Begin Again (Part II)" Written by [name withheld] I slowly put on the jeans Cassie had thrown me unsure of what else to do. My mind raced as I stood up from my sitting position on the bed to button the jeans. Prometheus Serum had been linked to mental side effects, but I hadn't touched the stuff in over five years. Was this some new late-onset effect? Was I still dreaming a dream within another? Or was it possible that this was all somehow real? The bathroom was directly next to the bedroom of my apartment and so I could see Cassie, still in a yellow t-shirt and black panties standing in front of the mirror over the sink brushing her teeth. In spite of the impossibility of it all, I couldn't help but stare for a moment. "Eyes on the prize mister," she mumbled as best she could through the foam of the toothpaste. Then she spit it out. "Dressed. In the car in ten minutes. Your flight leaves at eight and this time I'd like it very much if we can take a nice ride to the airport without getting another speeding ticket on my car. You know how the Parking Authority is." She turned and looked me directly in the eyes sternly after a moment taking my silence for defiance. She was trying to look serious, but after years of dreaming of her face, I couldn't help but crack a little smile as I marveled at her glimmering emerald eyes. It wasn't real. It couldn't be real. I'd seen her die. Been at the funeral. Visited her grave at every opportunity for seven years now. Still, in that moment as our eyes met for the first time in an eternity and my smirk prompted one of her own and I saw her dimples again... in that moment, I couldn't be bothered with what was real. It was good enough. "Right," I said bowing my head in compliance. "I just need my... my..." Almost reading my mind Cassie chuckled and pointed to the ironing board in the far corner of the room whereupon lay a freshly pressed light-blue button-down shirt. Something in my heart leapt at this most simple of things and I wanted to cry. But we all know how dreams sometimes abruptly end once we realize that they're dreams and so, holding it together, I went over to where she'd indicated and picked up the shirt. It was still warm. "Cassie, I have to ask you a question and it's going to sound strange," I said with a sigh. "What is it babe?" she responded nonchalantly as she came back in to the room to retrieve her own clothing. "If it's about last night, it's okay, I wanted to catch The Voice before ten anyway." I cocked an eyebrow. "Um, no, it's not that," I stammered. "I just um forgot... who am I wrestling for again? What company?" Cassie looked up at me instantly concerned. "What do you mean babe? GCW." she said matter-of-factly. "How could you forget something like that? You've been talking about this comeback tour nonsense for months now. You just got your wrestling licence reinstated yesterday down at City Hall. Are you okay?" "Yeah, I-I just..." I responded stuttering badly. "You know me, I'd loose my head if it weren't attached to it. I've been in and out of so many companies over the years, sometimes they all just run together in my mind. Lots of initials to memorize." Cassie nodded as I finished the top button of my shirt still resolved to let this all play out for as long as it could. I certainly didn't feel like I was in wrestling shape anymore than I did before this weird fantasy began unfolding. If it was a dream, it certainly seemed like my brain was being lazy with the details. I stopped pondering things for a moment once I saw Cassie's face again. She looked worried and her eyes seemed sad. She wasn't convinced by my lie. "Maybe we should go see Dr. Rubio again," she said quietly. "You've been acting strange all morning. Maybe it's your HAS. The doctor did say that mental symptoms might eventually manifest themselves." My heart sank a bit. Whatever this was—dream, hallucination, whatever—that one thing had not changed. I still had HAS here in this strange world. Hyperactive Adrenal Syndrome. The disease was one recently discovered in individuals who had taken the Prometheus Serum when it was still on the market. Since the serum manipulates the adrenal glands to produce chemically-altered adrenaline to give its users increased physical ability over time, sufferers of HAS are stricken with an endocrine system that is burned out. The altered adrenaline, what we called hyper-adrenaline in the lab, also had the potential to burn out a person's nervous system up to and including brain damage. "Those sorts of symptoms wouldn't come on for some time," I said as reassuringly as I could as I walked up to her and held her hands in mind softly. "I've just had a lot on my mind baby. Besides I'm sure whoever those jobbers are, they're not worth the recognition of the Demoticon." I chuckled and she couldn't help but crack a smile. "I thought you were done with the Smiley gimmick," she pointed out. "I haven't heard you call yourself the Demoticon for a long time." I shrugged, "It sold more t-shirts than the 'DKD' ones in Global." Another one of those wonderful laughs of hers. I'd missed them so much. She gave me a small peck on the lips and I almost died. "Well, I'll be ready in a minute, why don't you go warm up the car," she said. "I packed the tapes from your opponents. Eddie Whiskey of course and Xander Seale. Respectable guys in their own right, but I'm sure they're no match for my baby." Another heavenly peck on the cheek and I was out the door with my jacket and luggage being wheeled alongside me. As I got onto the elevator, I marveled. Fore most,I marveled at how, even though I'd pretty much decided that this was all a dream, that I was so desperate for it to be real. Also, I marveled at how real it felt--whatever it was. I was unsure of why I had lied to a character in a dream. Mental side effects present themselves in those with HAS almost immediately. And then it dawned on me. Rather, I allowed myself to be conscious of the other horrifying alternative for the first time. Maybe the reality had been but a dream and the dream a reality. Perhaps Cassidy DeMoore had never died and, in fact, I was suffering from some sort of bizarre dementia. Funny, how even at death's door the human mind cannot help but be lost in the wonder of possibility. As I stepped out of the apartment's front doors and turned of the alarm on my red Accord before climbing into the driver seat, I felt numb and cold in the pit of my stomach. Somehow, up until that point, in spite of having been diagnosed with a fal disease, I hadn't really been quite as disturbed as I was now and the sudden possibility that my mind—the only thing that had sustained me throughout all of the bad times—was possibly fading away. If this was true, my once great mind was melting like that of an Alzheimer's patient, only faster. If this was true, there was no way I'd be aware of it consciously. The last thing that I should be doing was wrestle... "It's not a dream," a voice that was not my own suddenly answered from beside me. "It's not a hallucination. It's not a trick." I turned around and my eyes met the figure from before who'd told me absurdly that he was my creator. That he was giving me another chance. I was hallucinating again. "You're not hallucinating, as I said," the man said as he pulled a pack of Newports from his pocket, withdrawing one and lighting it. "You don't mind, do you? You smoke too right? Ever since you murdered Cassidy DeMoore?" I simply gaped at him as he took a deep drag from the white cylinder and blew out the smoke audibly and with great satisfaction. Of course, if this was a hallucination created by my mind, my mind would know such a dark secret of mine but— "Stop rationalizing," the man said as he offered me a second cigarette he pulled from the box. "Here, you forgot yours." Furrowing my brow I quickly patted down my pockets. Surely enough, my ever-present pack of Mavericks was nowhere to be found. Deeply disturbed, I reluctantly took the cigarette from him. I put it between my lips and he lit it it for me. I rolled down the window and took a few drags and focused on the sensation of the smoke burning my lungs to distract me from the fact that I felt as though I might faint at this point. "Believe me or don't believe me," he said with a laugh. "Wrestling needs Kasidy Williams Drake in it. This is where you truly belong. I've even managed to get you an easy match against two easy opponents. I mean, how many tough guys do you know named Xander?" I flicked the ash from my cigarette. "Tell me what's going on," I demanded, but when I turned to look at him, there was no one there except for Cassidy who looked at me clearly confused as she opened the passenger-side door. "Since when do you smoke?" she demanded looking disgusted. "I've never seen you smoke before." I quickly tossed the cigarette out of the window and cleared my throat to buy myself a moment to think. "It's uh, for a gimmick I'm thinking trying during my comeback tour at GCW," I stammered. "They want to shoot some vignettes of me for the pay-per-view commercial and they think it'll make me look tough if I'm smoking a cigarette. I don't know—management." I laughed nervously. Cassidy's confused expression did not change. "Um, but yeah, I'm trying to get used to it so I don't hack up a lung during the shoot," I continued to lie as she opened the door and reluctantly got in scrunching up her nose. "I guess I shouldn't have done it here in the car. Sorry baby." Cassie raised an eyebrow. "You are like the worst liar on the planet," she laughed. "Whatever, just keep the windows rolled down. Smells like you had more than one—you'd better be careful with those things." I nodded my head and forced a s mile although my heart was beating at about a million miles a minute. I started the car and looked straight ahead as I pulled out of the parking space. I couldn't stand to look at her. "Sorry baby," I lamented aloud about more than I was letting on. "It won't happen again." She just laughed. "So are we doing dinner after the match?" she asked ass turned off of our street. "There's this wonderful steakhouse in Kansas I was reading about yesterday. Excellent wine selection." "Ah," was all that I could say at that point. "In all honesty though babe," she sighed. "You know how I feel about you doing this comeback tour. Your body is in no condition to be getting suplexed through tables and the like. Whatever happens out there, just be safe baby. You don't have anything to prove." I winced at her last remark. It was too glaring to let pass. "What's that supposed to mean?" I said with a snort. "Why would I think I had anything to prove to these youngsters? Back in the day man, I would have schooled these jobbers like it wasn't anything. Hit them with the Punchline and make them tap out to the Smile With Style. No question. These youngings don't know who they are dealing with." A forced laugh. She didn't buy it. "Hey, you remember that time I beat—" "—Jason Hughes in the Ringmaster Tournament in Core?" Cassie interrupted with a smirk. "Yes baby, you've been talking about it for seven years now. No one even knows who Jason Hughes is anymore baby, it's 2012. And you're not Smiley anymore, you're not in your twenties anymore, and we joke about them, but these guys you're going up against are hungry and fresh. You need to protect yourself when you get into the ring with them. If either of those guys mess up my baby's face I'll have to kill someone." She laughed at her own joke and sighed. I used to love when she did that. "I'm sure it'll be fine," I said. "Just like riding a bike." "Uh-huh," Cassidy responded flatly. "And what about the Drakewerx situation. "Opening statements are just a few weeks away. Shouldn't you be at the office instead of running off getting your ass kicked in front of millions on international TV?" Damn. The Class Action Suit. That hadn't changed either. The families of fourteen athletes killed from taking my serum seeking me to divest me of the few scraps I had left and the pharmaceutical giant I had created. I secretly had hoped I'd pass away from my illness long before that could be sorted out in court. Not that the families of those who died for the sake of my research don't deserve the closure and justice or whatever but dying men often have little regard for selflessness. "We'll see who kicks whose ass," I said defiantly. "And the lawsuit thing? You're still worried about that? I've got people working on that as we speak. It'll blow over just like that. Let's just enjoy Kansas while we're there and enjoy seeing your man shock the world and dismantling these fools in the ring. Just wait and see." She pondered for a moment and then nodded, her beautiful red hair shining in the sunlight coming through the window. "Okay baby," she relented. "I guess I just worry about you too much sometimes. I trust you." See? I'm not as bad at this lying thing as she said. "I protested, but in the end I guess I'm kind of happy you decided to do this," she admitted. "We don't know how much time we have left with one another, and naturally I want you to spend it all with me, but I respect that you're doing this. I know you've been wanting to get into the ring again for a long time and although I still think it's dangerous in your condition, I want you to know that I support you." "Thanks," I said with a nod. "That means a lot to me. And I want to spend every waking moment with you that I can. I'm glad you're coming with me to the show." "I wouldn't miss it for the world." Was it a dream? A hallucination? Was I loosing my mind? Who cares. "Me neither," I said in jubilant response. For whatever reason it had happened and however it had happened, I didn't care. Looks it was time for DKD to make a comeback tour. Eddie Whiskey? Xander Searle? God have mercy on their pitiful souls. [OOC Note: If there's an RP limit, I want to use this one for judging. If not, then doubly-awesome.]
