The End of Heartache

undated

Date: 06/06/06 and /07 June 6th, 2006 Above a wooded area, near Baltimore The wind sheared sharply across the wings of a jet-black bird, who soared majestically through the sky, not paying particular attention to what lay below him. He'd already eaten earlier in the night, though he couldn't remember exactly what, or much else of any note. His dark eyes narrowed as he glided downward on a convenient thermal current, dried out a little painfully by the increased speed. He knew little, despite being a crow of above-average intelligence, save what he'd been painstakingly trained to do, which was to ferret out information, and report it. He had a name, too, but he didn't know it aside from when he heard it used. Then, he knew he was being addressed, and then almost always by his friend, or the other thing of his friend that stayed around him often. Tall pines whizzed past underneath, and he briefly entertained the thought of alighting upon one of the branches, and sleeping for a while, but he knew this was important. The thing his friend had asked him to look at was running away from the water, being followed by lots of other things, and that was what he'd been sent to look at, so no trees. It replayed in his somewhat small mind, as he tried to pick out the fuzzy details and make them not-so-fuzzy anymore, because his friend needed to know those things most of the time. It didn't take him remarkably long to remember, because he paid attention to what he was supposed to. Suddenly, a crosswind whipped underneath him from behind, and he held his wings out, relaxing into its gentle goading toward the south. He knew where to go, and it felt like he'd always known where to go. He didn't know much, but he knew that, that it was like it used to be when he'd fly north and south all the time, when it got too hot or cold. The trees were familiar, despite being unfamiliar. He knew where his friend was at all times. But, he knew he wouldn't be able to make it the rest of the way to wherever that was without eating, and he thought he saw something moving in a tree beneath him. He held his wings stiff, and whirled downward in a circular swooping pattern, and the treetops rushed up to meet him. He crashed through the canopy with a loud crackling, likely disturbing anything nearby, but it didn't matter to him very much, as he knew there wouldn't be anything big that could kill him all the way up here. He scratched at the roughly-barked branch under his feet, poking it with his claws. It was a good branch, one that he could easily get his feet around, to hold securely, but also one that was very sturdy, and didn't shake too much when he moved. His head tilted to the side, curiously taking in his surroundings, and he noticed a bunch of tasty-looking aphids crawling up and down the side of the tree. As he hopped toward them, mouth opened wide in anticipation of a few tasty morsels, the memory of what he'd been sent to see came rushing back, like he'd wanted it to. - - - - - - - - - - Baltimore, Maryland Maryland Inn 06-06-06 The great white bird with the spinning beak came to the long flat land that my friend had told me to sit on and watch tonight. I wanted to leave when I heard it, because it sounded hungry, and I've heard of some of us that will eat others, but I didn't want to disappoint my friend, because he always feeds me and talks to me nicely. It didn't hop like I do, but it made a lot of noise and kept its wings straight as it slid across the ground. I guess that ground's really slippy, and that's why my friend told me to sit in the trees. He's a good friend. The great bird got quieter and quieter, until it really wasn't making a whole lot of noise at all, but it kept moving on the slippy ground into a big nest that didn't look like a very good one to me, but it was very shiny so maybe we have something in common, but it looked more like it all came from the same place, and that's not how you build a good nest. Then, the strangest thing happened! When the big bird was still and completely quiet, its stomach opened wide! I was scared, and I couldn't help but hop down the branch that I'd been sitting on, moving toward the trunk of the nice-smelling pine tree. I didn't know what was happening, because even the weird ones of us like the big big ones that fly around at sea don't do things like that... but then, I saw something else that was very strange. A thing walked right out of it! It was one of those things kind of like my friend and his friend that talks to me too, but a different kind of thing. I don't know what the difference is, because they look like the same things, but my friend says they're not, and that's why I'm here watching some thing or other. Oh! That must've be the thing my friend sent me to watch! That was a big thing, though. It's got such a great bird with it, what could I do to make sure it's okay if such a huge bird can't? But, that bird can't build a nest right, so maybe it can't fight either. I hope I don't have to fight, though, I don't like doing that very much. What's that big thing doing? Ah! It closed the big bird's stomach! Oh, that's good, though, it was probably getting cold and its food might get out. The thing did something weird after that, too. It took this weird looking vine thing, that didn't look like one I could pull apart, and put a shiny tube thing into the side of the bird... I don't know what for, but its beak moves so much, I guess that's how it eats. It was probably tired from spinning so much. What happened next? Oh, that's right, the thing next to the bird pulled the shiny thing out after a few minutes, I guess, and put it somewhere else. What a strange bird. Then, there was a weird noise out somewhere in the trees, that I couldn't quite figure out. It sounded like another bird as big as the one that thing had, but different some how, like there were a lot of them, talking one after the other. But, it was a weird sound, like a chop chop chop, and I don't know what bird sounds like that, maybe one from up north, there's weird ones up there. I wasn't supposed to look, though, I knew I was supposed to keep watching that thing do something. And it did something after that, too, so I didn't have to keep listening to the other birds. It got into this funny looking thing with four feet that looked like the ones the great bird had (but they're all round, and that's probably why they slide like that), and then I think the thing got eaten by it! But it was riding in the stomach of the big bird, too, so maybe that thing can do stuff like that. I think this was what my friend wanted me to tell him, though, so I flew off then, because I didn't think I could fly fast enough to keep up with the long thing with four feet. I knew where my friend was, because he told me, and I flew off that way. I was hungry, but I knew I had to get there as fast as I could. 06/06//06 (wherever the hotel is) The rain fell lightly for the moment, and made a lulling patter sound against the windows of the hotel room Virgil Ambrosi had taken out, though his companion, Lia Vlaszy, had already been asleep for a couple of hours, and so didn't particularly need help. Virgil had been sleeping fitfully for the last half hour, though he hadn't had quite so viciously long a day and night as his companion. His eyes lazily opened once again, and he'd lost count of exactly how many times it had happened in the last hour. He attempted to keep reasonably still, so as not to wake Lia, though he knew that doing so would be akin to raising a golem from a pile of stones at the moment. -tap- Virgil's eyes darted to the side, puzzled by the sudden noise. He assumed it was probably the plastic end of the cord to the Venetian blinds tapping against the window. -tap tap- Virgil rolled over in bed, and stared at the window suspiciously. The air conditioner wasn't currently on, which puzzled him. How could the blind cord be tapping against the window with nothing to move it? -scratch scratch tap tap tap tap- He slid delicately out from under the covers, managing not to cause a great disturbance in the bed, but there was quite a disturbance in his mind at the moment. He felt a slight rush of adrenaline as he stepped closer to the window, and he pulled up the blinds in a swift motion, imagining he'd be confronted with some idiot thief, or something of the like. Instead, he found himself staring into a the beady little black eyes of a very wet crow, who tilted its head up to peer at him. 'Ah, Mobius.' He pushed the window open slowly, and the crow now identified as one of his retainers, and above-average bird he'd named Mobius, hopped through the window, and fluttered to the floor, briefly fretting with his damp wings, picking at them with his beak and making strange noises. Virgil's connection with Mobius was mostly telepathic, which was rather convenient at the moment. Lia would not be awakened, and there would be no irritated pounding on the door from the hotel proprietor. Mobius glanced up at Virgil once again, and proceeded to flap his wings slowly, and tilt his head to the side, apparently gesturing to Virgil as he recounted what he'd seen. 'The thing you wanted me to look at is at the place you told me to go to.' Virgil nodded, sitting on the floor, so as not to intimidate the smallish crow. 'Where is he now?' 'I don't know, friend, the thing went away in a thing with four feet. He went away from the moon, but it was low.' Virgil scratched his chin. He supposed that meant to the west, but it was difficult to tell what Mobius was attempting to point out with his somewhat limited vocabulary. 'Was he alone?' 'Yes, friend. There was nothing with him other than a very big bird, that I've never seen before.' Virgil smiled, amused by Mobius' interpretation of technology. 'Good... you've done well again, Mobius.' Mobius hopped in place, a difficult to interpret gesture, but one which Virgil supposed meant he was pleased. At least, that's what Virgil assumed. However, it soon didn't matter, as Mobius quickly flapped out of the window, and back into the rainy night, presumably to find some manner of food. Virgil slid the window closed, and watched for a moment as the hazy black shape quickly faded from sight. He yawned, and turned toward the bed again, which was now seeming more inviting than it had been. The mental effort of attempting to communicate with a crow was not a trivial one, but it served the purpose of remedying Virgil's sleeplessness fairly well. He paused for a moment, staring at the woman stretched out luxuriously in front of him. She seemed to be resting peacefully, though occasionally, she would twitch or make a strange noise, what he assumed to be her dreaming. With a smile, he climbed back into bed with her, and was soon in a similar state of unconsciousness. - - - - - - - - - - 06-06-06 Between Baltimore and Washington, D.C. The inconspicuous Oldsmobile puttered in protest as Rune pushed the gas pedal in further, attempting to get all he could out of the 'junker' he'd asked Virgil to provide him with upon his landing. As something clattered, causing a looming sense of disaster in the cabin, Rune raised an eyebrow, wondering if he should have specified a bit more precisely what kind of 'junker' he needed. Maybe an old Nova, at least... -clang, clank- He glanced down at the temperature meter on his console, and, noting it still to be standing straight up in the 'normal' position, decided that whatever happened to be rattling loose on the car wasn't anything of particular importance. With that in mind, he put on a bit more gas, pushing the speedometer over ninety-five. -thunk thunk thunk thunk thunk- The front end seemed to vibrate, as though one of the wheels was out of alignment, but it didn't particularly faze Rune's sense of urgency. He shot over a bridge, giving a brief look at the rather wide river beneath him, and didn't notice the Maryland Highway Patrol car sitting behind the large road sign at the end of it. Almost immediately, the surrounding area was lit up in a brilliant shade of blue, and Rune looked behind him, then in front of him, and realized that he was the only car on the road, with the exception of the trooper. 'God damnit.' He fumbled for his wallet, hoping that the excuse 'I didn't see the lights immediately, officer' would suffice for an explanation. Thankfully, he'd remembered to bring his 'pilot's license' and 'driver's license' from 'Massachusetts' along with him. With a sigh, and another mumbled oath, he pulled over to the side of the road, his heart pounding from a mix of adrenaline and irritation. Minutes seemed like hours as the lights shimmered and alternated behind him, but eventually, he saw the moonlight reflect off of a polished black boot in his rearview mirror. In a couple of moments, he found himself staring into the narrowed eyes of his pursuer. 'You were... driving very fast back there, you know.' Rune's eyebrow raised slightly. He'd never have imagined that as xenophobic as he'd always thought Americans to be, that a Chinese immigrant (and apparently a recent one) would make it to a police force outside San Francisco. Or, at least, Rune assumed as much, from his thick accent and slightly out-of-place intonation. 'Yes... I wasn't paying attention to my speedometer, I thought I saw something floating in the river.' The officer continued where he left off, scribbling in an almost illegible script. Rune stole a couple of glances at the ticket, especially at the exceedingly large amount of two hundred dollars for a speeding ticket, and he rolled his eyes obviously, before his eyes came to rest on the woods, where an errant motion caught his eye. 'Oh, shit...' He dove across the bench seat, as a crack sounded through the desolate stretch of highway. He felt flecks of the padding from the seat he'd just been resting against fall against his back, and almost like an instinct that had always been there, his foot stomped on the gas pedal. The tires spun wildly, and the 'trooper' jumped out of the way as it skidded back onto the interstate, causing an oncoming car to skid into the emergency lane, its horn blaring and its tires screeching. When Rune finally regained upright state, he noticed that the windscreen was practically unusable for the most part, though a small little sliver on the driver's side was just enough to see through; That is, until the next three bullets came through the back window, and blew it out completely. 'Shit!' He fumbled for the pistol at his side, and clicked the safety off. There were no lights in the rearview, but he knew exactly who was shooting at him; the false state trooper held his arm out of his side window, attempting to take aim on the bumpy highway. However, Rune quickly made it more difficult for him, whipping his arm around, and firing a few blind shots in the general direction of his rear. He heard tires squeal, and noticed the nearly-new Crown Victoria dropping back significantly. Obviously, he thought, that wasn't one of the bloodsucker ones, or he would have been dead by now. However, he wouldn't be able to hold out like this forever. Next to where his gun had been sitting, his cell phone sat plugged into the cigarette lighter. He scrambled for it, and flipped it open, holding down the first speed dial. -ring. ring. ring. ring.- 'Yes?' Rune's voice shook with his adrenaline rush, and he realized for a moment how terrified he actually was, before burying it under his usual machismo. There was no use in fear, after all. 'Virgil... I think I might need some of that help you offered me.' 'Oh? Did things not go so smoothly as they should have?' Rune glanced at the windscreen, which only existed as a memory for the most part, and smirked. 'No, not really... I have no windshield or back window, and I came half a second from having no head, and there's a guy behind me dressed like a state trooper taking potshots at me.' 'Where are you?' Rune peered ahead at a road sign, that whizzed by much more quickly than he would have assumed this car had the ability to go. 'I-95, close to... Calverton?' 'Keep driving, you're about eight miles out of Washington. I'll be there in a few minutes, just try and keep them back as long as you can.' Rune nodded, as a couple of cracks sounded behind him, and his mirror lit up with the flash of muzzle fire. 'Okay. I don't know how long I'm going to be able to do that, most of my ammo's in some walls or people in Norway.' 'Don't worry about it. Just drive that car as fast as it'll go, and take the ramp onto 495 south when you get there.' Rune nodded once again, not realizing the futility of the gesture immediately. 'Alright. I'll do that... thank you.' 'Don't thank me yet. Call me if anything else happens.' And, before Rune could say anything else, the line went dead. Almost simultaneously, another bullet whizzed through the interior of the car, popping a large hole in the roof. Not entirely sure what the consequence would be, Rune floored the accelerator, and found the engine remarkably responsive. He saw the headlights behind him shrink a little bit, and began to think that maybe, possibly, he could survive this. - - - - - - - - - - 06/06/06 College Park, District of Columbia Virgil's 'borrowed' Corvette C6 skidded across the median, as he noticed a particularly familiar Oldsmobile Eighty-Eight sitting upside down on the side of the road. The tires left the road for a moment, and the C6 screeched to a halt halfway on the shoulder, halfway in the grass. The door swung open, and Virgil stepped out of the car, immediately drawing his pistol. 'Virgil!' Virgil's head snapped to the side, and he noticed Rune crouched behind the beleaguered Oldsmobile, clutching his own pistol like it was the last thing he had in the world. Immediately, and without much regard to his own well-being, he strode forward, toward his familiar pilot. Without much warning, Virgil's shoulder snapped back as a slug grazed the top of him, and he shot a glare in the general direction that it had come from, before bridging the rest of the distance between he and Rune. 'Watch out, one of them has some kind of explosive rounds... that's why this damned car's upside down.' Virgil glanced curiously at the back end of the car, which he noticed was in an oddly angled position, certainly not the position the manufacturer had intended, and not the condition Virgil had purchased it in. 'How did you manage to survive that?' He turned his eyes down to Rune, and for the first time, noticed completely how badly he'd been hurt. His shirt was torn nearly all the way up the side, with several bloody lacerations and less severe cuts running up it. His face, though smiling with a somewhat morbid humor, carried burn marks, and various minor cuts as well... 'Good god, Rune, you look horrible.' Rune looked up at him with one eye, the other one having swelled shut after his head had been smashed against the door frame when the car flipped. 'I don't know if I can get up... I took one in the leg when I tried to run for the woods. I don't know how bad it is, but it hurts like hell.' Virgil nodded, but their conversation was cut off by a cacophony of gunfire, which hammered the other side of the Oldsmobile, sending tiny clouds of dust into the air. Virgil stood up, leveling his pistol at the only car he could see, firing off six rounds into the cabin of the car, shattering the windscreen, and sending shards of one of the lights atop the car flying onto the empty highway. Several shots came in response, and Virgil ducked back behind the car, as tiny divots shot up from the ground, little microcosms of what real war must have been like. 'Do you know how many of them there are?' Rune shook his head, mournfully eying the empty clip of his gun. 'I don't know... I think I shot two of them, but I don't know how many of them there are out there. Didn't you get one with all that noise?' Virgil peered over the top of the car, scanning the no-man's-land behind it. 'If there was one in the car, then yes... otherwise, I don't know. I don't see anyone right now.' Someone standing directly in Virgil's line of sight had the poor judgment to move, and Virgil fired off a pair of remarkably accurate shots, and between the echoes of the shots over the flat highway, he heard something heavy crunch on the dry leaves of the woods, and he allowed himself a brief smile, before another volley of fire came his way. He ducked back once again, keeping his eyes over the top of the car, to follow the muzzle-fire. Between their shots, he began returning fire, and the flashes started to come from only his side, save one more. 'Fuck!' Virgil turned his head back toward Rune, who had apparently been shot again, if Virgil were to guess. He emptied his clip in the direction of the last shot, and dropped his pistol, practically falling on top of Rune. 'Mierda... are you alright?' Rune coughed, and mumbled something Virgil couldn't quite understand. 'What?' 'Huh?' 'Are you alright?' Virgil's voice escalated in panic with every syllable, until he sounded nothing like the confident, overly indifferent being he usually was. 'Rune!' He coughed again, and reached up to rub his mouth with the back of his hand, exposing the large bloody hole in his chest. He spit on the ground, and Virgil didn't have to think especially hard to realize that it was more blood than saliva. 'I think I'm done, Virgil.' Virgil shook his head violently, his eyes narrowing in anger- not directed at Rune, but at the collection of what he assumed were corpses around them. 'No, don't say that...' Rune shook his head, smiling up at Virgil, with a slight laugh that Virgil wasn't entirely certain how to interpret. As suddenly as the smile had come, Rune's eyes closed, and Virgil felt his body go limp in his hands. 'No... Rune! Rune! Don't fucking die!' Mixed degrees of pain and grief ran through him simultaneously, and several impulses fought for dominance in his mind, before the one he'd followed the absolute least won out, and he reached up, pressing his fangs against his wrist, slitting it open, and holding it up to Rune's mouth, tilting his heavy head backward. Every synapse in his brain fired toward the hope that it would work, that the one man Virgil could honestly call his friend could be revived somehow. He felt the slightest pull from Rune's lips, and drew his wrist away. The underside of the Oldsmobile sizzled with a crackling noise that didn't make Virgil particularly comfortable, and he scooped Rune into his arms, a considerably more difficult task than he'd imagined, and carried him toward the Corvette, which had somehow escaped non-cosmetic damage. No bullets began flying toward them, the second Virgil stepped out from behind the flipped Eighty-Eight, and Virgil reached into the Corvette, managing to more or less secure Rune in the passenger seat, before climbing in himself, and turning the ignition. The engine misfired, then roared to life, and Virgil's adept hand threw the gearshift into reverse, squealing onto the highway, and squealing similarly forward, as blue lights began to flash over the horizon, several miles back. - - - - - - - - - - 06/07/06 Maryland Inn Tension hung heavily in the air, as Lia stood upright, looking down at Rune with a difficult to interpret expression on her face. She'd spent nearly the last hour attempting to close the wounds that Rune had incurred during the battle. With all her efforts, he looked remarkably similar to the man he'd been before the battle, though he still hadn't regained consciousness. She glanced up at Virgil, who hovered over the bed in a similar way, and he shook his head slightly, pushing some of the hair out of his eyes. 'I suppose we've done everything in our power for him...' Lia only nodded, and she sat down on the small couch across from the bed, her eyes firmly fixed on the floor. Virgil joined her shortly, keeping his eyes fixed on his fallen friend, who had yet to show any kind of life. There wasn't much to say between the two of them now... conversation would seem empty, and forced, the kind of talk families had while they sat outside intensive care rooms, while family members slowly wasted away on the other side of the wall. 'Ugh...' Their heads snapped up in simultaneous motion, as the large lifeless form on the bed began to twist and stretch, as though awaking from a protracted sleep. Virgil glanced over to Lia, and a silent decision was made between them to let him awake on his own, instead of hovering over him. He quickly did so, very slowly pushing himself up to a sitting position. He shook his head, staring at the bed as though nothing stranger could be presented to him. 'What the hell?' He looked up, out of the window, at the blinking lights of Washington, D.C. outside, before turning to the other side, and locking eyes with his inhuman compatriots. 'Eh... Virgil... Lia? What's going on? How did I get here?' Virgil looked upon him somewhat cautiously. This would take some delicate maneuvering, as most probably wouldn't take too kindly to flat honesty in this case, as 'oh, by the way, you're no longer a human' tended to cause some measure of alarm. 'You were injured very severely outside College Park... I don't know if you remember.' Rune nodded, reaching down for his stomach, which growled audibly. 'Damn... why the hell am I so hungry? Eh... yeah, I remember. I got shot, after the car got flipped, but I've got it in my head that I got shot somewhere else, too.' Virgil nodded, and Lia stood from the cough, stepping over to the side of him, to get a better look at how his chest had healed. Rune didn't seem to notice her clinical gaze quite as much as he usually would. 'You were shot in the chest by the last of your assailants to survive, yes.' He blinked in shock, and immediately noticed that it didn't make very much difference in how his eyes felt. He leaned over slightly, continuing to hold his growling stomach. 'How the hell did I survive something like that?' Virgil stood up from the couch, and pushed some more of his hair behind his shoulders, sighing, and glancing out the window, to avoid making contact with Rune's confused eyes for a moment. 'There was only one way to save you... you had bled so much from your wounds that your body was nearly exhausted of blood completely... I had to give you some of my own.' Rune glanced down at his stomach, and back up to Virgil, pieces starting to slide together in his mind. He felt a gradual sense of anger boiling up inside of him. 'Would that have anything to do with the thought of food making me sick, but still being hungry as hell?' Virgil nodded, somewhat somberly. 'I didn't want you to die.' His bright blue eyes widened, and he tossed the sheets off of his legs, turning to face Virgil on the bed. 'So you decided to turn me into a fucking vampire?' Rune leaped up from the bed, and took a step away from Virgil and Lia, ambling somewhat shakily toward the mirror, and staring at his reflection, which looked remarkably sickly, despite still bearing his familiar work-honed musculature. He whirled around, to find Virgil answering him through gritted teeth. 'Yes.' And with that admission, despite already practically having the knowledge, his anger finally boiled over, and he took a dramatic step toward Virgil, still clutching at his stomach. Lia leaned over the desk, rifling through her bag, doing something neither Virgil nor Rune could see for certain. 'So now I'm going to live fucking forever remembering the bullshit that Heidrun put me through? I get to think about never seeing my daughter again for eternity? That's just fucking wonderful!' An accusing finger pointed directly at the furious Rune, coming from an equally agitated Virgil now. His voice practically boomed, syllables spat out with enough venom to choke a hundred snakes. 'Ridiculous! At least you are still in possession of your memories! Some of us have not such a luxury as you! I would take the memories of a thousand lifetimes of horrible misery instead of the blank expanse I currently have! Perhaps you would have been happier had I let you die on the side of a highway?' Lia shot him a look best interpreted as a visual cease-and-desist order, as she whipped a corked bottle from her bag, and crept up next to Rune, uncorking the bottle, and holding it up to his mouth. Practically at the point of frenzy, he drank without convincing, and quickly found himself under the influence of a powerful sedative in addition. Virgil managed to guide him back toward the bed, but was unable to cover him, as he was once again little more than dead weight, in a state of hibernation. Virgil shook his head regretfully, suddenly overcome with a sense of remorse, more for what he'd said than what he'd done. She watched him carefully, her eyes strangely backlit this evening. It was partly the situation with Rune, but only partly. She glanced toward him, pity in her eyes. 'He will sleep for a few hours. It should give his wounds time enough to heal a bit more properly.' Virgil nodded, resting a hand on the back of his neck, and turning toward her. 'Good... I cannot imagine it does him any service to be in the state he was.' Her eyes would grant him no mercy this evening. 'Do you have time to speak with me now? There is nothing more to be done here, at least not for the moment.' He smiled, nodding once more. He'd almost forgotten her request from earlier, among the events that had transpired since, but now, there was nothing left but time, it seemed. 'Of course.' She nodded, not returning his smile- not yet, at least. 'Your choice where we go... sofa, bed, balcony...' He glanced toward the large window across the room from them, where the night seemed much more inviting, all of a sudden. 'We can spend some time outside... it's rather pleasant tonight.' She practically glided over the carpet to the balcony, pushing the door to it open and leaving it open with the intention of having a good view of Rune on the bed in case he required anything. Once she was out, the night breeze cooled her, threading through her hair like gentle fingers, but she felt none of it. Her attention was only on him, as she leaned against the balcony railing to watch his every move. The humidity of the night wasn't quite as stifling as it had been when he'd left earlier in the night, and he was rather thankful for that, as he set his hands on the rail, gazing out over the city, before turning his head to face Lia, who he noticed seemed even more serious than usual. 'Now that we finally have a moment with no disaster, what had you to tell me, amore?' She swallowed, finding her throat suddenly quite dry at the prospect of talking to him about this. No matter how many times she had rolled the words around in her mind while he was gone, they all seemed inadequate now. She took a deep breath, and pushed the first of it out. 'Have you had anything strange... with your memory lately?' He leaned forward slightly, rubbing the side of his hand against his chin, and attempting to remember anything of the sort. 'Aside from a few scattered dreams that seem exceedingly familiar, I don't suppose I could say so... though I've come to accept them as too familiar to be my mind's fabrications.' She nodded and turned to him, her hand gripping the iron railing. Nearly face to face with him, the adrenaline was starting to flow through her in an unstoppable rush, bursting forth from its dam. 'What were your wedding vows?' His brow furrowed, and he looked at her with a slightly confused look, puzzled as to why she would be asking. 'I don't remember them...' Her face contorted in slight annoyance. 'No, no, no... Don't think, just say, repeat them as you said them, please, just... try it.' Her jaw clenched, teeth grinding hard against one another in desperation. He nodded, and closed his eyes, attempting to dredge up something from what he generally considered to be a foggy nothingness with no end and no beginning. '... time has relented upon me...' Suddenly, words began spilling forth from places in his mind he never recalled having. '...and brought you to me from your distant homeland, and I can say little that could express my truest admiration and love for you. Never have my eyes fallen upon a creature so beautiful and I know for certain that they never shall again. I have awaited this day for what feels to be centuries, and I swear to you an oath of honor, that I shall protect you from all harm, shelter you from all evils, and remain true to you as my only love in existence...' He opened his eyes, and stared somewhat blankly at her. 'That's all I can remember... I think there was more.' She jerked as if shot through by an electric current, her eyes wild and feral. 'And your wife's?' Her chest was tight with anticipation. Virgil closed his eyes again, attempting once more to conjure words from the past, less certain this time that it could be done properly. 'My Lord Virgiliu… I have scarcely been under your roof more than a month, and yet these halls are already so much more than stone to me. They are a home to me, and you have endeavored to make them so with ceaseless effort-hospitality that I dare not expect from the closest family friend, and yet am all the more thankful to receive when it comes from the man who is to be my husband.' She picked up where he stopped, her voice shaking with barely restrained emotion. '...I am honored by your oath, and to your swearing it, swear one of my own that I will uphold the dignity of your family name by taking it as my own, that I will make your house into a home for you, a sight always welcome to your eyes after an absence, that I will be as true to you as you to me, yearning for your presence no matter how far your duties may take you. My gift to you, this ring, is from the house of my father, and signifies not only our two families unifying as one, but of my gift to you of myself. You are all that I am now, and all I shall be...' Anxiety clamped down on her muscles, and she found that she couldn't move, no matter how much she was torn right now, to throw her arms around him, to leave the room before finding out that it was a cruel joke... it was not her choice. She was held in place by an unseen hand that would decide her fate, as soon as he opened his eyes. His eyes practically shot open, and turned toward her immediately. Briefly, very briefly, it crossed his mind that this was some kind of trickery, but reason quickly trumped the thought, and left him simply confused, positively dumbfounded. His mouth was open, begging a flow of words forth, but none would come, regardless of his bidding. There were no words that could properly describe the turmoil now swirling and contorting within him, but the realization that came with it made the experience worthwhile by an immeasurable count. 'Lia... I...' Tears threatened, and she fought them, but didn't much care whether she won or not. 'What was your daughter's name?' Suddenly, he barely had to think about the answer. 'Her name was Elena.' She closed her eyes. 'And she died after a case of wormwood poisoning, intended for you, from a band of spiteful Roma...' The mention of the Roma, recalling immediately the vivid dreams he'd had last week, set the gears of realization in motion, and he took a step toward her. 'There's no possible way you could know all of this, unless... you... were my wife?' The words seemed far away, as though spoken in a vacuum, but he felt something swell in him that had felt dead for centuries. He felt as though of all the pain he'd been dealt over his life as an immortal, the most severe wound had just been miraculously closed. She couldn't find any words with which to answers him as she slowly opened her eyes, red-rimmed and rent with emotion. Her fingers gripped tighter around the iron railing as though reality was threatening to shred itself apart for her, but the only response she could give him was that look. Something seemed to build inside him, as he began to recall all of these things that had previously been hidden from him... years of time spent with her in Iberia, which seemed like such a far off, inaccessible place, and which still felt rather distant, if only because it had been so long since he'd had the slightest memory of what it was like. His eyes were stung with impending tears, but even they relented, apparently finding their own presence inappropriate at what should be a moment of joy. His muscles came to life with a renewed vigor, shaking off the jaded miasma of centuries of nihilism and strife, and with passion he'd never known before, his arms once again wrapped tightly around Lia, though now, he was able to put to words exactly what it was that felt perfect when he did so. Everything he felt for her lost its logic, its basis in reason, and suddenly the deepest of emotions were painfully obvious to him, as though they'd been in plain sight for eons. With them came the threatened tears, which instead of the isolated incidents they'd been for as long as he could remember, now there was a constant stream, unaccompanied by anything else remotely associated with sadness or pain. Her fingers gripped his back, her head laid on his shoulder like it had always belonged there, had always been there. In a sense, perhaps it had.
