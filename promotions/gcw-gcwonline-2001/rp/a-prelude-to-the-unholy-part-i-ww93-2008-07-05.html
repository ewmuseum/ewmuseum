A Prelude to the Unholy- Part I (WW93)

5 July 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 5, 2008 Have you ever had one of those days where everything that you see, hear or do brings up an old feeling or memory? Well, for me, today was one of those days. It started off just like every other morning of my life starts-- Awaking with a jerk from a sound slumber due to that damned alarm clock radio. The horrid sounds of Emo Rock quintet, Hawthorne Heights, resonated in the air of my tiny bedroom. "Oh, fuck me...!", I mumbled, slightly annoyed at the sounds that were emanating from the noisebox. As usual, I couldn't help but think that today was going to be the worst day of my life. Rolling out of my bed, I struggled to get to my feet, not wanting to face the day. Being the impatient person I am, I couldn't take anymore of the dreadful 'music' that was polluting the air in the room, so I snatched the clock out of the wall and threw it against the wall, smashing it. I never liked the thing anyway, it was a gift from my brother, Ace. He never did know how to shop for presents. Having spent a late night out at the church praying to Lord Tioneb, I was still quite drowsy, and my eyes burned from the sun. Stumbling through the room, I managed to find my pants. Upon pulling them on, the phone rang. In quite the hurry, I staggered into the kitchen and drowsily picked up the phone. "H-Hello?", I stammered, as I picked up the phone. --- On that fateful day, ten years ago, it all started with a phone call. I remember it quite vividly... I was sitting in the living room of my parents' home in Hollywood, Florida, and I was watching a documentary about the Holocaust when the phone rang. "Hello?", I said in a rather chipper tone, not expecting what was to come. "Yes, this is Dr. Hastings. I've got some horrible news. There was an accident, and well, your parents didn't make it.", said the voice on the other end of the line. My muscles tensed, and my legs buckled, causing me to drop to my knees. I couldn't speak, only sob. My parents were dead, and my heart was shattered into a million pieces. My older brother, Ace, came home from work to find me curled up in the fetal position on the floor. I remember him rushing over and kneeling beside me. "Axel...! Axel...! What's wrong?", he asked, and all I could do was point to their picture and whisper the word, "Dead." Much like myself, he was shocked, but he was able to remain strong. Or maybe he wasn't strong, but was acting that way for my sake. I don't know, and I guess I never will. Finally, the day that they laid our parents into the ground came. I remember being angry at God, wondering why he would take our parents away from us. "God has a reason for everything.", people would say. What kind of supposibly benevolent, loving God would take the lives of someone's parents? Especially when that someone still needs them? I ended up going into a deep depression which in itself contained several attempted suicides. Each one was a bigger failure than the last. Eventually, Ace had me committed to the local insane asylum. I spent two years inside a padded cell, during which time I was training myself and building my body with little things such as pushups and crunches. By the time I was released, I had developed quite the physique, and was in top physical condition. I returned home to my brother, to see he had gained quite the physique as well. That's when we decided to become professional wrestlers. However, an accident during training ended his career before it even started. --- I had been a lifelong fan of professional wrestling, and grew up watching all the legends of today. There was just something about it that was so alluring, and I couldn't stay away from it. Eventually, the Omega Wrestling Alliance Academy opened up, and its head trainer was none other than the legendary TyeDye Guy himself, Karl Zarn. When I first went to him to see about some training, he seemed like a nice enough guy. However, once training started, all of that changed. The first day of training, he had me working on my bumps, and made me learn how to properly get up from the mat. Every time I'd put my hand on the mat to help myself get to a vertical base, he'd stomp my fingers and berate me. "You pitiful excuse for a worthless sack of shit! Don't fuckin' put your grubby mitts on the mat!", he'd exclaim as he stomped my hands into oblivion. I can't tell you how much I hated him at that point in time. We'd begin training each morning at nine o' clock sharp, and end somewhere around three in the afternoon. Each day, the training got more and more strenuous, and he became meaner and meaner. I recall this one instance where he actually whipped me with a leather strap because I botched a Side Russian Legsweep. Granted, I hadn't been one to cry in over two years, seeing as how I cried all my tears out, so to speak, after Mom and Dad passed. However, with the lashings I was receiving, I couldn't help but cry. The pain was absolutely excruciating, and the leather was tearing into my flesh. "P.. Please, stop. I'll do it right this time, Mr. Zarn! I swear it!", I pleaded with him, hoping he'd stop beating me like a pack mule. The torture went on and on, and every time I'd make a mistake, he'd insult and beat me. Finally, I reached my boiling point, and I snapped. As he took one last swing at me, I caught his arm, and if looks could kill, he would have been dead at that very moment. "I don't think so...", I said, twisting his arm half-over, stepping into position and snapping him backwards to the mat with a perfectly executed Side Russian Legsweep. "IS THAT GOOD ENOUGH FOR YOU?! I'M NOT SO WORTHLESS NOW, AM I?!", I screamed, as I knelt over him, punching away at the top of his head and at his face. After I had finished giving back some of the punishment I had received over the months of training, he simply looked up at me and smiled. He stated quite matter-of-factly, "Congratulations, Axel. You've graduated." I felt as if I had achieved my goal of becoming a wrestler already, but in reality, I knew that there was a long road ahead of me, and the worst was still to come.
