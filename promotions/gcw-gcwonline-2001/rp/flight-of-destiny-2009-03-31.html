Flight of Destiny

31 March 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 31st “Look, kid. Life ain’t about choices. It’s about stickin” with the choice you make and stayin? true to who you are, all the same.” Uncle Truck takes a deep drag off of his half-spent Marlboro Red. The setting sun highlights his silver sunglasses as the young man next to him takes a drag off of his. UT looks down to the kid, spotting the Verizon Wireless logo on his nametag and the letters “P E T E” underneath it. He exhales. “And if workin” for the man and wearin some suit is your gig then... shit. No judgin? here partner. I wish I had a cell phone.” Pete lets his airway open and exhales, nodding in agreement. “Yeah...you’re right, man.” He looks off the stoop to the city skyline. “You know what” For one scary lookin? guy, you’ve got some good stuff to say. Thanks for listening.” Uncle Truck grins. “No sweat. Just uh...don’t tell anybody, alright” I’ve got this rep as this mega bad-ass who loves to eat babies for pre-snack snacks, ya know?” The two share a chuckle. Uncle Truck stops and stares back to Pete. “Seriously. I can tear your arms off. Easily.” Speechless, Pete stares in awe at the man he had incorrectly assumed was a gentle friend. The door slams against the wall behind them, interrupting the conversation and the threat. “Truck! There you are.” Friar comes stumbling onto the platform of the old warehouse. In his arm’s he is carrying two duffle bags, multiple articles of clothing, and a baseball bat. “For the love of Christ, man! Hurry yourself. We have a flight in forty minutes.” Throwing the items down, Pete hops to his feet and backs away from the two rather colorful characters. Uncle Truck stands, extending his entire six-foot ten-inch frame to the ceiling of the shipping platform. He glances to Pete, raising his eyebrows. “Sorry, pal. Looks like this is my cue to head on outta here.” Truck extends his hand down to tussle Pete’s hair. Friar looks to the young man. “Pete, is it?” Pete nods to the masked man who seems to make him a bit uneasy. “Well, if you’re a friend of Uncle Truck’s... then I’m sure you have some sort of criminal record I should be worried about. Get lost before I call the cops.” UT slaps his partner’s shoulder. “No, no. Pete’s a good kid. Works and helps Mom at home. Ain’t that right, Pete” Tell him.” Pete gulps down all fear and nods to the big biker. “Yeah. I work down in the Plaza at the Verizon store. Right now I’m taking classes at Community College; art classes right now. After that, who knows?” Friar laughs. “I’m just toying with you, Pete. Not a lot of people understand how sarcastic I can be.” He extends a hand to the young upstart. “you can do anything you wish, young man. Just stick with it and remember... God is with you every step of the way. He’s with us.” Pete stares with Friar, who holds the handshakes to explain his ways, and then glances to Uncle Truck. The big man nods and smiles. Pete does the same. “Certainly, sir. Thank you.” “Now, be gone. We have the Lord’s bidding to do.” Friar picks up some of the clothes and begins shoveling them into the bags. Uncle Truck does the same and the two sling them over their shoulders, simultaneously almost, and head for the doorway. Friar stops, turning back for the missing Louisville Slugger. Doing so, he locks eyes with a confused and concerned Pete. “Why do you need the bat?” Pete waits in silence on the platform for a few seconds. The duo both stand tall and stalwart at the questioning of their purpose. Friar takes a deep breath. “Our mission is not some fairy tale or comic book story. It has real people, real evils, and even real perils. We must be prepared to do what we must in order to accomplish this mission.” “Plus...” Uncle Truck grips the bat and pulls it up. The piece of lumber looks fitting in his grizzly mitts. “Sometimes doin” the Lord’s work gets messy.” Friar grins under his mask and takes off to the door. Uncle Truck taps Pete on the shoulder with the end of the bat. He winks to the young man. Before Pete, the guy who stepped out behind the Plaza warehouse for a smoke during break, notices... “Holy Hell...” Pete thinks to himself. “So much for quitting .”
