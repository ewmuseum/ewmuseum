Warm

15 July 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07/15/11 Five weeks ago. That kid was gone. It was just us now. I hadn’t seen her for awhile. A long while. She hadn’t seen me for the same. It was what it was. Thank God people don’t live around us for miles. I wake up to see her on the balcony in a bathrobe, staring out into the infinite abyss. It pains me to just get out of bed, but I do. I have to. I walk up and wrap my arms around her waist, and kiss her neck. I cannot stop kissing her. I will not stop kissing her. I miss her so much. She misses me, too. I forgot what this felt like. It’s…nice. She tilts her head back. I can feel her warm breath on my neck. I hold her tight. Hold her close. I never want to let her go. Three days ago. Lexi thought it might be a good idea for me to do some self-promotion publicity for the title match, so she booked me on some bullshit satellite radio show. It’s a sports show, she thinks, on a channel owned by some rapper asswipe. Her voice doesn’t exactly exude confidence in the whole affair. I honestly can’t tell if she thinks this is a good idea or if she’s just punishing me for something I did to her recently. It might be a little of both. I think about blowing it off, but under the circumstances, I figure it best not to piss her off any more so than usual. I roll up to the studio and walk in like I could give less of a fuck, which is funny, because I couldn’t. I ignore everything the nosepick of a show director says. He’s sporting some dime-store navy blue dress shirt and musical-themed tie, telling me the specific points that they’re going to hit on or some such nonsense, and I just zone out. The hydrocodone I popped on the ride over here only adds to my disinterest. Why am I doing this? Inside, I find myself stuck between ass-rape Hell and mouth-rape Hell. On my left is a white boy with an oversized black T-shirt and even more oversized jewelry who constantly insists of referring to me as his “son” for some reason, which is only made worse by the fact he can’t be more than five years older than me. On my right is a Hispanic girl who speaks so fast during banal questions that she might as well not be speaking at all. They start right away even when it’s clearly obvious I haven’t cared from the get-go. “Yo, that main event is siiiick,” spits the mouth-breather whose name I never even bothered to learn. I return and give him dead silence. He fills the dead air with more blathering. “Yo, VJV is a down-ass chick, knowumsayin’?” I stare blankly in his general direction. I blink twice, just to send it home. He gets anxious, and his twat of a co-host finds time to jump in. “Whas’ it like gettin’ yo’ ass beat by a girl?” I snap my head toward this bitch. She doesn’t stop. “You beat Valentine in a triple threat, right?” I nod. The bitch continues. “You beat her and four other cats in a match, right?” I nod again. “But one-on-one, Valentine choked yo’ ass out!” My eyes turn to slivers. I fight every instance to grab this idiot ‘round the neck and show exactly what I do to disrespectful bitches. Suddenly an image of Lexi shoots into my head, and I breathe smoke instead of fire. And so asks the most overconfident dumbass bitch I’ve ever met in my life, “What about dat?” I respond by saying I beat tits twice, and that you can say and do and bring up all the meaningless bullshit you want. But I still beat tits twice, and that’s twice more than tits can say about me. And then they hoop and holler like all that means something. But then it takes an odd turn. Something I’m not thinking of. Not expecting. I let the white boy have a brain wave, and he washes a tidal wave right over my face. “What ‘bout yo’ girl,” chuckles the king of all cartoonish wannabe-gangster white boys. His grin is about as obnoxious as the next thing that comes out of his mouth. “You and yo’ bitch.” He takes a long pause. He should savor it, because it’s about to be the last pause he’ll ever take. “What’s with that, yo?” I don’t hesitate. I don’t pause for dramatic effect. I don’t even care that we’re on the air. “You talk about my wife again, and I’ll pull your tiny-ass head out of your outstretched asshole.” I lean right in, make sure his personal space is an afterthought. He snaps back. I guess that means he gets the message. There’s that dead air again. This time, I fix it. I’ve got a question for these fine interviewers, I say. They’re all ears. So when I ask how many dumbass upper-middle-class pieces of shit it takes to set black culture back fifty years and respond to my own question with “two”, all I get is confused looks from the zoo crew and daggers from the production team. They dump to a station break and the interview is obviously over. Musta done something good in a previous life. I’m not about to wait for a security escort. I’ll make my exit on my own, thank you very much. Two weeks ago. She’s secretive with her purse as she tries to sneak up the stairs. I’ve got a bottle of Moet in my hand and a curious disposition. “What’s up, girl,” I ask her, and she freezes and clutches her Gucci bag close to her side. “N-nothing,” she blurts out, like she’s holding a Three Musketeers bar she stole from the local grocer. “Nothing at all.” My right eyebrow piques. “Oh yeah?” She shows off those pearly whites and nods like a Japanese manga character. "Totally!" And then she hurries up the stairs. I wait a few moments before following her, which is probably a mistake. When she acts all shady, there’s something up. I don’t think she’s ever really truly trusted me. Probably the biggest reason we’re still together. I take short steps up the stairs. No long stride. No sense of urgency. No rush. I make it to the top in time to spot her ducking into the bathroom far down the hallway. It’s not a bathroom either of us usually uses. Curious. I knock on the door, and she shrieks. “What?!?” “You okay in there?” I ask. “Don’t want you to fall in or anything.” She finds no humor in my jest. “Go away!” I’m her husband. I’m supposed to care, even when I don’t. So I play the part. “Really, babe,” I assure her. “If something’s wrong--“ “Tri, I love you,” she says amidst a grunt and a stream. “But go the fuck away, will you?” I’m a bit drunk, not afraid to admit. So I press. “Hey--“ “Please,” she insists. “It’s…girl stuff, okay?” And with that, I let it go. I know I shouldn’t. I know I should keep pressing. But I let it go. I have faith in her. I hope she has faith in me. Christ, my shoulder is on fire… What is wrong with me? Yesterday. She wants to talk, but I don’t think I’ve said a word since yesterday to anyone. We jetted to LA. She’s got a thing in the Hollywood hills tonight, some fashionista party with the well-to-do hipsters and leeches that cover this town and suck it dry. I skip it and hit a hole-in-the-wall dive on Vine. Inside, it’s black with red lighting and smells like piss, and no one gives me another look. I like the place. It’s like having a drink in the waiting room to Hell. One more nightcap before an eternity of fire. I get another text that she wants to talk. I text back that I don’t think I’m up to it. Lot on my mind. She doesn’t respond, so I pay it no mind. My reflection glares back at me from the mirror on the wall behind the bartender. My eyes meet my own, and I try to peer into myself. See what makes me tick. Why am I doing all this? What does any of this mean? Do I even really care? I finally look away, none the wiser, so I down the whiskey and hit the door. Outside, the air is lukewarm and unforgiving. I buy a pack of smokes from the newsstand up the street. The contents of emptied bars pass me by, and one slams right into me right after I light up. He apologizes, and then promptly vomits on his friend’s shoes. I laugh. First time I’ve done that in awhile. Even this late, walking down Hollywood towards Highland amidst a sea of strangers, it doesn’t even really feel like the dead of night. Above me hovers a thick, dirty air that’s more orange than black, and there isn’t a single star in the sky. As I walk about, I try not to think about any of it, and I fail miserably. Lexi finally texts me to ask where I am just as a bum asks me for a smoke. I oblige him, and respond to Lexi: ‘see you soon.’ But I’m not going back to the hotel. I decide to get one more drink at the Roosevelt with the sophisticated crowd. There’s some socialite girls clustered on a couch being clocked by some dudes straight off the set from a teen drama. I order a Glenlivet neat from the bar and cut their viewing pleasure short. The girls sure know how to talk, but I prefer the Zero 7 soundtrack to whatever they’re on about. One girl casually throws out how she’d like to take a swim. Another says poolside’s closed. The pool is indeed closed, but only to the public. I’m not the public. Their underwear leaves little to the imagination. They splash about, pissing off just about everyone within earshot. They invite me to join them for a two A.M. dip, but I decline and drink from the bottle of Glenlivet I snagged from the bar before last call. I watch them frolic and cackle, and I can’t shake the thoughts. I’m exhausted. I hurt all over. I haven’t had a good night’s sleep in forever. I’m not sure I even want to bother going to Columbus. I haven’t the slightest fuckin’ idea what I’m gonna do about Lexi. I have no idea what I’m doing anymore. A few days ago. I hate hospitals. The smell, the crappy lighting, the food, the decor. All of it annoys me. When I arrive, she’s in room 302, sitting on the bed in a gown. I have no idea why she wanted me to be here for this appointment of hers. I’m already late as it is. It took me long enough just to walk through the door, but now… Once she sees me, she gives me an uneasy smile. I ask her if she’s alright, but she says she’s fine. She won’t say why she’s here, and I haven’t mustered up the courage to ask. Sure, she’s been sick, but…visit-to-the-doctor sick? I’m nervous. She’s nervous. I hate being here. The doctor finally walks in the door, a petite Korean lady, and she’s startled by the sight of me. Can’t blame her. I’m practically death warmed over. She looks down at her chart. “You’re…the husband, I presume?” I nod, and she makes her way over to Lexi’s side. The chart goes onto the counter behind her, and her disposition becomes sunny. “Well,” she says, drawing a deep breath. “Let me be the first to say congratulations!” Congratu…huh? I stammer a bit. “What for?” “You’re five weeks along,” the doctor says, looking right at Lexi, and I near drop to the floor. Instead I stagger back a bit. Not enough for the doctor to notice, but I can see it in Lexi’s eyes she didn’t miss a thing. The doctor talks about…something. I can’t hear her. It’s all white noise. Static. She might as well have been silent. She says she’ll give us a minute to talk. Lexi grabs my wrist and my head goes light. She practically drags me onto the bed with her. Her eyes light up. Her lips quiver as she speaks. “So?” She’s waiting for me to tell her something about this momentous occasion in our young lives. I want to say something, tell her where my head’s at. I’m happy? Completely ecstatic? Scared out of my fucking head? I desperately want to tell her how I feel. But I can’t. Because I’m not sure I feel anything. I kiss her on the forehead and bring her close to my chest. I can feel the tears stream down her face and staining my shirt. She tells me she’s the happiest she’s ever been. I know how I feel right now. Numb. An hour ago. Murray ran away. Lexi’s in bed, eating a bucket of ice cream and watching the E! channel. I sit outside on the patio nursing a Heineken. My body’s still sore. Everything hurts. I don’t know why. Not even getting fucked up by that goddamned clown hurt this bad. I pop another hydrocodone, but it doesn’t help. Nothing helps. Murray left. The sun makes its descent, and I wash down the pill with the drink. That bright orange ball of fire, cresting on the horizon, falling down behind the sea. Falling down. Falling down. Falling down. Murray, you coward. This is not how I wanted this. Not against her. Not this way. Not like this. But he ran away, and here I am. Left with his sloppy seconds. Stuck with the second string. Not good enough to get his shot. Here one day, and…poof. Gone the next. Murray, you sonuva bitch. No one wants me to win. Not a single one in the bunch. They all want me to fail. To fall flat on my face. To fumble it all away. Get swept back into the middle of the fold, just another anonymous shithead with nothing interesting to say. They’re afraid of what I am, but they’re more afraid of what I might do if I win. Of what I might become. And they should be. Murray, you motherfucker. I’ve walked through that building in Dallas. I’ve seen the faces, the ghosts on the wall. Every time I walk past a picture or some iconic image of a day time does everything to forget, I want to vomit. It’s all bullshit to me. Phantoms. Specters. Memories time wants to forget, but these people fight to keep them alive. I’m not what they think I could be. I don’t want to cement a legacy. I do not want to build my foundation. I don’t want to become another legend or goddamned Hall of Famer. No. Murray, you goddamned bastard. I want to burn it all to the ground. I will tear apart the very history of this stupid fucking title. I will ruin this company. Tarnish it. Tear it asunder. Octavian. The Jhub. LeStatt Knight. Andy fucking Murray. Every single one of ‘em. All of it. Every single piece I’ll rip out, brick by brick. I will choke the very life from this place. Watch it gasp for air. Listen to its cries for mercy, and ignore them all. I will rip the still-beating heart out of this company with my bare goddamned hands, throw it to the ground, and stomp it into dust. I will leave nothing behind. And in its place, I will start over. Refresh this garbage and begin anew. And in my wake of destruction, all that will be left is me. Not the goth cunt with a stick up her ass. Not space monkey. Not that clown bastard, the firecrotches, leather-jacket tough-guy, the shitbird Z-lister, or that bandana-wearing sonuva bitch. Not the lot of ‘em. Not any of ‘em. Only me. Just me. The sun sets and I take another sip. Just me. I lay back and absorb the moment. Just me. I close my eyes, and think about the future. Just me. My future. Me. Now. I stumble upstairs and make my way to the bedroom. I’m a bit tipsy, but not too tipsy. But I use the wall to keep me vertical all the same. Never hurts to be cautious. She’s asleep, with a book about raising your kid right open on her chest. I’m about to climb into bed with her, but I stop. I hesitate. From this angle, in this light, she’s a goddamned princess. An angel from above. I don’t want to do this anymore. She is the woman that I love. That I’d kill for. That I’d die for. I never want to leave her side. I want to be with her, every step of the way. I close my eyes, and I can see it right now. She has the baby bump, and I’m practically carrying her everywhere. She’s having violent mood swings, screaming at the top of her lungs one moment, and crying hysterically the next. I don’t need any of it. The hospital scene is a mess. Alternating demands for drugs and me to be closer than ever to her. She’ll clutch my hand so hard it’ll leave marks forever, blood vessels busted beyond repair. We’re up all night, changing diapers and feeding the little monster. We bend to its every whim. Cater to its every demand. But we love it like nothing over. Savor every moment. In my mind, he’s my son. A strong, smart, damn fine looking son from the best genetic stock available. We take him to the park on a sweltering summer day. It’s hot outside, damn near unbearable, and he’s as swift as the wind. He runs rampant like a monster through a city. And when I spot Lexi out of the corner of my eye, she’s cradling our infant daughter. Her smile instantly makes me smile. It’s hot and we’re having fun and life is good. Why am I still doing this? When I open my eyes, it all goes away. She’s no longer sleeping. She stares right at me. She asks if everything’s okay. I lie, tell her everything’s fine. I crawl into bed next to her, and she lays her head on my chest. She purrs. I tell her everything’s fine again. I hold her close. Hold her tight. I don’t ever want to let her go.
