Know your roots

16 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/16/05 'You aren't the first one of your bloodline to be my friend, Johnny-boy.' ---- Johnathan Fitzwillis was fathered from Ian Fitzwillis. Ian was fathered from Carey Fitzwillis, who was fathered from Michael Fitzwillis, who was the son of Keegan Fitzwillis, who was the bastard son of Willis. That was his only name, because his wife only referred to him as, 'Damn that fekkin Willis fer leavin me!' Anyway, Willis was married to Maggie Cooley, who was the daughter of Ian Cooley, son of Brendan Cooley, and the line of sons goes on for a long time. A long time all the way back to when your family wasn't even from Ireland. They were from Scotland. And ironically, his name was Johnathan of Marr. We called him Johnny. And he was my friend. 'Thom, what exactly are you tryin to tell me?' 'I'm tryin to tell ya that there ain't no reason to go be stupid, Johnny-boy.' He shined up his helmet. His protective helmet. Johnathan took care of his helmet, making sure it could shine with brilliance, that you could see him coming from a long way away. You'd know it was Johnathan coming down the road if he was wearing his helmet. It kept everything out. Everything. 'I'm not being stupid, Thom. I'm doing right what needs to be done. I'm not doing nothin more, and I won't settle for nothin less.' And Johnathan of Marr liked to ignore advice. He walked over to a shelf. Picked up his sword. He always saved the sword for last. His eyes ran down the blade as the blade pointed up. Shined almost as much as his helmet. Both were given to him by his father, given to him by his father, and the process goes back a long way. He held the sword at his side and held his helmet in his other arm. With his armor made from animal hides and his boots, everybody always said he had the finest boots, he looked ready to take on the world. And I'll admit. I talked him into it. 'Johnathan...this is somethin for someone other than you. A brave man. Not a brave man, but an unattached man. Whose bravery can shine through. Whose bravado will allow him to live on forever, living through eternity, because of his actions. Because of the great deed that was accomplished for his friends and family. You have a family. Stay here and protect your family. Let someone else be brave, Johnny.' You could tell already from the look he gave me that he would have none of that. 'Thom...did ya come by here today to talk me outta doin what I've waited my entire life to do?' 'You've waited too long, Johnny. You can't turn back time.' 'Damn it, Thom!' I remember that look he gave when his hand slammed down on the table. Pissed. No other way to describe it. He wasn't frustrated that I knew what was going on inside of his head. He wasn't aggravated that I was trying hard, and succeeding, at pushing his buttons while looking like I wasn't. He wasn't anything but pissed, because he knew he had to do something and he knew it was a stupid thing to do. But, the choices we make... The choices we make are everything. And Johnny, Johnathan of Marr, knew that. He knew that he was given two bad choices, and he had to choose one of the two. He could either sit where he was, doing nothing, and defend his house that was soon to be obliterated. Or he could go out and sacrifice himself, forgoing everything that he had built. Sit and wait for the storm or go out and try to stop it, and those were his only choices. So he put his helmet on his head. His protective helmet. Nothing could get through that thing. No steel, no fists, no wood, no rock, no wind, no breath, no words, no reason, no love, no hate, no thoughts. Not in or out. It was his own bubble, his own protective shell from everything that the world could ever possibly throw at him. That helmet went on, and Johnathan of Marr went on whatever path he was last set on. And nothing could change that. 'You're right, Thom. I can't turn back time. You're always right about that shit, you know?' Glanced a look at me. He always had young eyes, no matter his age. 'Suppose that's why I keep an unshaved rat like you around, eh?' 'And what am I to tell your wife when she finds out that you've gone off to die?' 'You don't tell her anything cuz you don't know anything.' 'Bullshit in a horse, Johnny-boy. She knows damn well when I'm lyin to her. And I'll not have it again where she hits me with her big spoon.' 'You mean the one she cleans the clothes with?' 'Nay. I mean the huge fekkin one she cleans up the cow shit with.' Johnathan tried to look at me and not laugh. And it lasted a few seconds, longer than most. He'd been practicing. But I guess there was something about picturing me getting slapped upside the head with a medieval pooper scooper that entertained him. Putting his hand on my shoulder, he smiled again. I must have found some way through that helmet after all. Though I didn't change his mind. 'You tell my wife that I'm gonna be just fine, Thom. And stay bein my friend.' 'I ain't going nowhere, Johnny-boy. I died a few too many times in a few too many painful ways ta wanna go with you. But I'm not about to leave my friend's soon to be widow out to dry. Figure maybe she'll need some takin care of when you're cold in the ground.' 'You touch her and I swear the devil himself will find a new meanin fer fear.' And he made me laugh. He made me laugh, Johnny. You know how hard that is? To make me genuinely laugh. I think it's gotta be something about the people I choose as friends. Maybe I'm drawn toward somebody with such an audacity that it surprises even me at times. And as much as I've experienced it, and as used to it as I should be, it catches me off guard. You do it too. All my friends have managed to do it once or twice; actually make me laugh. Perhaps, Johnny. Perhaps you're right. Maybe it's me affecting them. But then again, maybe it's their affect on me. Maybe every single person on the face of the planet has the ability to actually make me laugh. Maybe I just have to be around them long enough to forget what pain is first. I don't really know. What I do know is that I remember Johnathan of Marr stepping outside, with his leather adornments and his family's helmet and sword and it was one of those days that really make you miss living them. The kind of days that seem like a dream, or a memory that you never actually lived. Autumn. A warm autumn day in Scotland, which was one of the rarest things in the world. And the leaves were all golden and brown and red, and the sun was getting ready to set. So light was streaking sideways onto us as we exited the hut. With Johnny's wife collecting this or that from the other huts nearby or from the woods, she would have any clue what idiocy he was up to until he was already dead. But in that moment, the sun shone on him and he was alive. And I don't get the chance to compare many ancestors with their descendants. You two are the only people I've really gotten to know in my lifetimes here that I know are directly related to each other, in more or less a straight line. Parents to parents, sons to sons. And I can say, right now, that you honestly do remind me of him. 'A gorgeous day, Thom.' 'Aye. Gorgeous. Ye won't see many more like 'em.' 'And why's that, Thom?' He knew the answer, just as you know the answer, Jack. Johnathan of Marr was no fool. He knew why. 'The evils of men, Johnny-boy. One day they'll be spread from sea to sea, and land to land. And you one such as you or I will look at it and forget what it means to live in a day like today. To be free. The sun won't shine quite so golden, the fruit won't be as sweet. They'll block the sky with dirt, they'll create a scourge in the soil with their waste, and they'll forget what it means to have freedom to live in a world of their own. And it all starts with the mistake of one man, Johnny-boy. Just one.' He knows. 'I'm going, Thom.' 'I know.' 'And I want you to get my wife out of here. Take her where she'll be safe. At least somewhere where she'll be safer. Do that as my friend.' 'Aye.' Without another word, Johnathan of Marr went off. His protective helmet on his head. His sword in his hand. His armor on his body. He went with the fullest of conviction towards fate. The local lord, a Scottish lord...not an Enlish lord, but a lord of Johnathan's own country had a vendetta against him. Johnathan...a simple hunter. He had gained a hearty reward for the capturing of a well known criminal in Scotland. And his prize was that this Scottish lord, afraid of Johnathan finding out his lordship's own shortcomings and bringing him before the English king, decided that a quick elimination of a possible future threat was the safest bet. And so, after the first group of assassins fell under the blade of Johnathan of Marr, he decided to bring the fight right to the gates. Self-sacrifice has always been melancholy in its rewards, Johnny-boy. On one hand, your mother was brought with her unborn child to Ireland. She escaped Scotland before much of the violence erupted there. Instead, in Ireland, she found a much slower cooking violence of everyday-ness. But she grew strong. As did her children. As you are too. ---- 'Cute story. What does it really have to do with me though?' 'Simple, Jack. You're my friend. And as a friend, I felt it was needed to tell you what being a real target is like. Your ancestor knew. And he didn't sit back and wait to die. He went out there, full force, and met his death.' 'So he did die trying to kill them.' 'Jack. It was one man. Going against a Scottish lord with an estate and a small army. And I won't lie to you and say that he fought valiantly and he took down twenty people with him. He killed two guards and injured a third before they riddled him with arrows from a far distance. But, since Johnathan of Marr was dead, they didn't bother hunting down his wife and unborn child like animals. Doing so would only kill them, it wouldn't hurt him. And with their leaving of Scotland, they didn't have a name or reputation to injure.' 'So he saved them.' 'He saved them. He did nothing for himself. And look where that got him.'
