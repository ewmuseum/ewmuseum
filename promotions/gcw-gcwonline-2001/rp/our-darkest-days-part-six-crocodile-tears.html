Date: Various CUE: Dear Lover by Social Distortion 25th JUNE 2007 WINGATE INN TALLAHASSEE, FLORIDA 10:15pm “Would sir like another drink?” the barman asked in a voice so soft and eloquent that it might have been English. Andy responded only with a slow nod; it had been at least the fourth time that the bartender had asked him that question this evening, so he saw no point in wasting his breath. He watched the barman pick up his small, used crystal glass and gently place it in a nearby sink. He was short and stout and clad in the hotel’s bar staff uniform which consisted of a pair of black leather shoes, freshly-pressed black pinstripe trousers and a wine red coloured waistcoat over the top of a pristine white shirt. With his quickly-receding brown hair and well-maintained beard, the man reminded Andy of his father. “Thanks,” the Scotsman grumbled as the barman handed him another double measure of Glenfiddich Rare Collection single malt. After spending a couple of seconds swirling the forty-year-old scotch around the ice cubes, Andy knocked back a good amount of it. By now he was beginning to get used to the burning sensation at the back of his mouth every time he sipped on his preferred poison, but at the start of the week it was a very different story. It had been almost two years since alcohol had last passed his lips. In fact, the only times he really decided to partake in an alcoholic beverage were when he had something to celebrate or reason to feel sorry for himself? and right now, Andy Murray was all out of things to celebrate. It was the seventh consecutive night that he had spent in the bar of the Wingate Inn. During that time not one other guest in the hotel, aside from the bar staff, had made the effort to spark up a conversation with him. He’d spent every night of the past week alone with nothing more than a barstool and a glass of whiskey for company. He wasn’t surprised by this; after all, would you choose to sit next to a 6’7? man with stubble on his face and an all-round unhappy demeanour, especially when there were plenty of other free seats? Didn’t think so. He liked this bar. It was reasonably quiet; there were just enough hotel guests in the room to create a pleasant atmosphere, but nowhere near enough to call the place “busy”. The walls were decorated with rich mahogany, and the dark red carpets matched the colour of the waistcoats worn by the bar staff. Everything just fit together so perfectly. It was the perfect place for him to get away from the world? the perfect place for him to spend some time alone and gather his thoughts, and the chance of anybody sticking their nose in was minimal. And with his wife’s betrayals now confirmed, thinking was something that Andy had done a lot of over the past few nights. When Wesley told him, he let his anger take hold of him. He clad himself in some black, thief-like attire, grabbed a weapon, drove over to the home of the man that she had been seeing behind his back and, for lack of better terms, beaten the living shit out of him. He didn’t hit the guy hard or long enough to cause any permanent damage, just enough to send him a message. On all fronts it seemed like Andy’s fortunes ? both professional and domestic ? were on the rise until that snivelling little prick showed up and decided to ruin everything. Benjamin Grunwald had driven a wedge between Andy and Jane so deep that it looked like they may never recover. But hold on a second? Andy Murray had forced his way into another man’s home and physically assaulted the guy. He was very, very lucky not to find himself behind bars with his younger brother Cayle right now. Benjamin Grunwald probably had a family out there somewhere ? who knows, he may even have had kids of his own ? and Andy had taken the liberty to attack him and, more than likely, put the fear of death into the man. Andy pondered his own son, Marvin, and how he may react to discovering his own father laying in a bloody heap on the floor, small teardrops trickling from the corners of his eyes? ? he felt guilty for all of five seconds. Of course he was justified; this man was sleeping with his wife! If he ever came face-to-face with Benjamin Grunwald again, it could only come too soon. He didn’t want to end up in the slammer like his younger sibling, and he may yet go on to regret his actions, but any punishment handed out would be worth it. But Mr. Grunwald wasn’t the only guilty party here; it takes two sides to form a full equation, after all. Jane had let him down. The thought of her spending most of her time outside the house in this guy’s bed sickened Andy to the pit of his stomach. When he’d first learned about it he was at home in Aberdeen, recuperating from a potentially career-saving back operation. When Cayle told him about it on the phone he dismissed it right off the bat; after all, the two had only just been re-married a couple of months ago. His dismissal seemed like the logical thing to do; if only he knew then what he knows now. The first time the marriage dissolved wasn’t pretty, and Andy got the impression that any forthcoming separation would be even messier. The first time around Andy’s hectic touring schedule as a professional wrestler proved to be far too much for Jane to handle, and by the end of the marriage the two could hardly stand being in the same room as each other. He felt guilty about it; he blamed himself for months? in Jane’s eyes, he had chosen his career over his family. But Andy wasn’t about to give up the one thing he’d worked towards his entire life at the drop of a hat. This time, things were different. There was another man; it wasn’t Andy’s fault, there was nothing he could do about it. His feelings of guilt from all those years ago were now replaced with a vehement hostility, the flames of which would not be doused until he had settled the score with both Jane and Grunwald. Andy let out a sight and polished off the remnants of the whiskey, even taking one of the ice cubes into his mouth. He demolished the ice with his teeth and swallowed the bits that didn’t melt as his mind began to drift away from the negativity that hung over his head like a storm cloud.
