Path to the Grail

13 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 13, 2005 Eva dreamt. She dreamed a dream of deep meaning and obscured mysteries..mysteries, that when unraveled would leave her wanting for more..... The castle reared high above streamers of mist shrouding the landscape in a shifting cloak of opacity. Floating down from a pale blue sky, early morning sunlight, bright as fool’s gold softened the brooding, melancholic mass of the castle hooding the severe gaze of its intrusive impacting presence on the quiet green of the countryside. The castle walls built of squared, massive blocks of stone affording mute testimony to the skills of ancient hands. Even so, the bleak forbidding look of the castle glared like an anomaly in all that quiet of meadow and mist, soft and clinging as any spider’s web might be. A solitary raven perched atop the castle keep, shone like burnished jet against the pale, almost blue-white winter sky. The sun shone cold, a distant golden ball shedding frigid rays that filled the mist with hazy, opalescent light as though it burned within, igniting empty incandescent fires shimmering and shifting through the mist like an ethereal rainbow. Yet, for all the magic of that enchanted morning which smote the woman’s vision with awe, fear gripped her gut in harsh, tightening hands; tapping away inside her chest, soaring through her like some wild, untamed melody. That, she thought with increasing worry, is not good, it is not very good at all. But then she had never laid claim to being a brave man, just a bold one. She began to soundlessly repeat over and over as fast as she could, a steady, calming mantra to bolster her faltering courage. She cannot say it worked but she forced herself on towards the castle anyway. The castle loomed above the mist. The blocky, stupendous mass outlined against the sky dominating the surrounding meadows and woodlands with authoritative investiture. Yet for all its revered, if not legendary sacral existence in myth, legend and romance it was real enough, and yet in her mind’s eye it seemed to cast oppressive gloom. The sheer mass of the ancient castle was intimidating in its very presence. If not downright menacing, she thought with a nervous gulp of frosty air shivering down his throat. She had given up eight years of her life to get here. Eight years of search and discover. Enduring the ridicule and scorn of academics and mythologists. Pontificating endlessly on what they claimed to know, whilst at the same time disclaiming that which they studied might actually, once upon a time, have been true or that some remnant might still exist in this brave new world of ours. Here she was confronting the evidential conclusion of her search and feeling none too sure that she should be here at all. It was a bit like meeting ones god, and she was the sacerdotal wanderer standing awe struck in the presence of divinity. In honest notion it wasn’t like that at all. Truth to tell, she was damn near scared to death. Really, how was it possible for her to even be here when for centuries so many expended their lives searching for this place but never found its location perishing for the quest. Now here she stood wishing she were some place else. One might think her fear to be irrational but she can tell you, it was real enough, right down to the shiver in her soul. After all, this place might very well be sacred but that did mean it was safe. She trembled in mingle fear and morbid anticipation as the chilled air wracked her spine. The castle was illuminated in a strange light that presented a bleak forbidding visage, hoary with age. Her feet were leaden and cold to the bone. Tendrils of opal hued mist coiled about her legs causing her heartbeat to quicken. The frosty air hushed and stilled as though it waited for the preordained moment when the first stone of that vast cathedral like castle was laid. Who built it? Maybe god did or at the very least divinely inspired entities. The odd thing was, during her early years of research she came across a mysterious obscure passage in a two thousand-year-old manuscript she found while moldering away in the library of the Monastery. It suggested in an oblique way that originally the castle had nothing much to do with god or the sacred and that it was in fact built to the glory of something else entirely, something spiritual, but as to what, who could say? Then again there are numerous spiritualities so she guessed. Vague the script simply eluded to some undisclosed aspect of the state itself, not the condition one could understand, but the state claiming to view it as a condition is a grave mistake indeed. It might very well be the reason why those who vociferously claim otherwise display lamentable lack of progression in spiritual matters. It smacks of absolutism an error commonly made. 'Keep moving,' she urged herself, 'before you turn and run away.' Not that she would, but it was tempting. She walked on, her mouth suddenly dry and for all she wore clothes a plenty her flesh grew cold and clammy as she followed a barely discernable trail winding through woodland and meadow. Abruptly the trail petered out and she found herself standing in the shadow of the castle gates, breath stolen by the fear squeezing at laboring lungs. They were huge, weather stained and thick, reinforced with rusting bands of iron riveted in horizontal strips across the width of formidable timbers inset in the great stones of the castle itself. Somewhat bewildered she stared at the massive portal in morbid fascination and wondered how she had actually gotten here, when no more than a heartbeat ago she was still a good distance from the castle. How was it possible for her, between one footstep and the next, to be distant then near? She simply had no idea, having no actual recollection of the travel in those moments, feet having not logged the distance. It simply wasn’t possible, but obviously it was. She looked around at tree and meadow, covered with thinning wisps of mist. All was quiet and still. Amber radiance streamed down. The frosty ground was a hard and unyielding, patch of ice and frozen puddles, gleaming like gray blind eyes. Bare trees stood in the mist, skeletal limbs ugly in their thrusting nakedness. The castle loomed above her, somber and aloof and of its sacred nature, of its rumored shrine like quality she could detect nothing at all. There was only the cold indifference of hard, obdurate stone. She shivered as chill hands walked her spine. The frosty air nipped her ears. Her breath billowed out in clouds of white vapor. The moment was unsavory filled with doubt and fear of the unknown. She had it to do, her told herself; now she that she was here she had it to do. If she were to turn and walk away she’d have wasted every minute of the past eight years of her life. 'Do it', she goaded herself. 'Just bloody well do it. Otherwise why bother journeying here in the first place. Stalked by vagabonds and thieves, nameless haunts who harm simply because they can.” The castle was the end of her pilgrimage. She tried to will herself to do it with no thought given to consequence. It was foolish she knew. She took a deep breath bolstering her flagging courage as best she could as she rapped her knuckles smartly against the huge wooden door. Silence. Trying again she used her fist to pound against the door. Her pummeling setting up a hollow, booming sound within the keep that seemed to her ears to reverberate with a symphony of sound out of all proportion. She wish she had never found this place, she thought somewhat grimly as tendrils of fear tightened in her belly, and a sudden premonition of horrors to come all but shriveled her resolve to remain. Then suddenly one half of the castle’s massive portal slowly swung open and to her complete and utter astonishment she found herself staring into a pair of luminous, deep violet eyes set in a pale, hauntingly beautiful face, whose visage hinted at mystery. He stood taller than she, a slim, fragile looking man with his head cocked to one side as he gravely studied her from head to toe with a ghost of a smile hovering about his lips. His eyes, she thought to herself, are the kind that haunt the mind, round and deep, luminous in their depth of violet against his pale, translucent flesh. A thick mane of sun spun colored hair tumbled about his shoulders, the color of pale gold she thought idly. He stepped back into the shadow cast by the open door; his gleaming metallic armor catching her eye. She was not by nature an easily enthrall woman but she could not help but stare at his broad masculine shoulders wondering what it would be like to touch them. His ethereal smile deepened. Tearing her eyes away she drank him in. The very armor he wore was cause enough for wonder in itself. The luminescent material patterned in strange eye baffling designs of vibrant silvers and golds hinting here and there of sparkles which shimmered through. The neck and the hemline of his under shirt were bordered with a Celtic design picked out in twinning bands of deep green and silver. The long sleeves were caught at his wrists in what looked like thin enameled silverand gold circlets. The enamel work gleamed in a deep rich violet within the shadowed light. The effect was stunning, almost bemusing to such a one as she. She coveted the beauty of it all. His lips though full were oddly wan as though bloodless beneath chiseled cheekbones. That along with the rest of him lent him an exotic otherworldly look of fragile containment, as though he held to this earth with tenuous grasp. His eyes, she thought with an inward sigh, knowing the look of those globes against the sheer beauty of his face would haunt her mind forever. Oddly, it was then that she realized her fear had slipped away and she felt kind of warm and safe inside. It was a spiritual thing she later came to know, a sort of projection of inner calm and warmth one may allow another to borrow or bask in for a while. It was like a stream of unseen light encoded within self, as one conceives self to be in the encoded diametric of the entire strange continuum. The aspects of physics she later came to learn, were often postulated in temples devoted to the worship and study of Light Theology and the recognition that spirituality is a state and not a condition. Unruly thoughts jostled and tumbled inside her head, unlocked thoughts, undreamed ideas and elusive concepts flooding cranial paths. It seemed as though she knew when she faced towards him, that he was an illusion, his being not actually there, though the idea that he stood there remained, along with a sense of hidden energy. The concept was too much, too high, wide and finely tuned for her to take in for long. She began to tremble as he spoke. Her vision cleared. She again felt warm and safe. To her he was still standing there, in front of her. He was no longer standing in the shadow cast by the door. The sun had swung along its upward arc gently bathing him in sunlight from it near above position. She had been standing there for hours! It was almost noon, but she had no recollection of time having passed. It wasn’t possible, she thought, it just wasn’t possible, but again, seemingly it was. “I wish I’d never come here. I wish I hadn’t spent eight years of my life searching for this place” she whispered softly to herself. Eva awoke then with a startle, the words still edging past tired lips as she stared at the ceiling wide eyed and in panic. What kind of dream was this? She had no fascination with medieval things and strange myths. Was there something she was missing...a hidden message that would help her rise to the top in the GCW? There was only one way to find out. Eva shifted, turning to her side, that head resting snug within goosedown pillow....and she began to drift asleep once more...
