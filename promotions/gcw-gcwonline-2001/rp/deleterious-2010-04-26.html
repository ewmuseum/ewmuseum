Deleterious

26 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/26/10 8:32 P.M. Los Angeles, California. Staples Center. Backstage. In the inner workings of the Staples Center, right outside the locker room door shared by the Clippers and the Lakers, just moments removed from his debut and subsequent defeat, Triumph Frost storms to the wall, with Alexis Frost following closely by. A few overweight teamsters eat hot dogs and drink cheap beer nearby, and give Triumph a quick look before going back to their chat. Triumph can barely contain himself, as he kicks over a few equipment boxes, which gets the teamsters’ attention again, and causes Alexis visible discomfort. He slaps his hand hard against the wall, and breathes heavily. She rubs his back, an attempt at comforting Triumph after his loss. “It’s okay, baby,” she reassures him. “It happens to everyone.” She continues to rub her hand along his back, until… Laughter. Loud laughter. His hands on his knees, he cackles like a madman. She steps away, as he shoots his head up in the air. “Fuck!” He barks out” That fucker was…fast!” “Umm…” She stammers and struggles to find something to say. He finally turns to Alexis. “I want some chicken,” he tells her. “You want some chicken?” Alexis is at a loss for words. 9:35 P.M. Los Angeles, California. Zankou Chicken, corner of Sunset & Normandie. Sitting in the restaurant, Triumph, in black Nike sport pants, black Nike Hyperdunk sneakers, and a white-with-black-design zipped-up Hurley hoodie, devours his half chicken plate. Alexis sits, almost looking a bit embarrassed, as Triumph raises his hand high in the air, calling for the young Armenian girl behind the counter, his mouth full. “Muhr Hhmmms phlsss…” he mutters, spitting food as the young girl looks at him, disgustedly. He continues on like a pig, while Alexis fidgets. He finally pauses from his gluttony and gives her the side-eye. “Whuh.” Chicken spittle flutters from his lips. She closes her eyes and shakes her head. “You look like an idiot.” “H’m fuggin’ hnngry!” The words garble together as he shovels food into his mouth. He grabs his soda and slurps it until the only noise is air upon ice. He slams the drink downm satisfaction in his eyes as he releases an elated grunt. “I mean, I really did work tonight, you know.” “What is this?” Alexis remains amazed at what has transpired before her. Triumph puts his fork down mid-lift. “What’s what?” “What happened to ‘I can’t be beat! No muthafucka’s gonna keep me down!’ You’re eating like Rosie O’Donnell at his nephew’s birthday party.” He ponders for a moment before chuckling. “Oh yeah, huh.” He lifts the food up to his mouth, and chews exaggeratedly. Swallowing it down, he puts the fork down. “I didn’t expect that fucker to be Barry Allen.” Confusion washes over her face. “Huh?” He sighs. “Barry Allen? Silver Age Flash?” He lowers his head, shaking it side to side. He rises up slowly. “Do you not listen to a fucking thing I say?” Her incredulity shines. “About comics?” He clicks his tongue and pauses. “Fair enough.” Alexis looks down at her phone. “So much for your plan,” she scoffs, as she raises her head and gives him the condescending look only a woman could provide. Triumph raises his eyebrow, and a smirk crosses his face. He breaks off a piece of pita bread and dips it in the Hummus. He sneers, holding up the piece “Joke’s on you,” he says. “I didn’t have a plan.” He pops the bread into his mouth. Alexis’ holier-than-thou moment squashes in an instant. “What?” “C’mon,” he chuckles. “When have you ever known me to plan anything?” She nods knowingly, but still shakes her head in disbelief afterwards. Rolling her eyes and sighing, she asks, “Then…?” “Well,” he remarks as he digs his white plastic fork into a hunk of poultry. “I was bored one night last week, so I popped in that reference DVD thing you gave me. Boring shit, I tell you.” He pulls off a chunk and devours it. “So, I just said ‘fuck it’, and copied that one guy. You know the guy. White hair, old, wrinkly leathery skin, bitch tits. Wears a Liberace robe and screams a lot?” Alexis tilts her head to the side. “Ric Flair?” “Probably,” he responds. Her voice stinks with complete disbelief. “You copied…the Nature Boy?” “What the fuck kinda name is that, anyways?” Nature Boy,” he snorts. “That is S-T-U-P-I-fuckin’-D.” She breaks out into laughter. The scattered patrons stop and watch her, as Triumph stares daggers through her. She slowly calms down, barely containing herself. “Well, I didn’t have any idea what the fuck I was doing.” His voice rises, holding the fork up. “Six months ago I’m bare-knuckled, beating the shit outta ultimate fighter wannabes and boxing scrubs, not having some midget straddle my face to do god-knows-what with my lifeless corpse.” He grabs another forkful and shakes his head from side to side. “I’m outta my element here.” “I was wondering why you used the Figure Four,” she tells him, her voice still peppered with comedy. She wipes a tear from her eye, and slowly returns to seriousness. “So then all of the specialty training that I had you go through to get you to at least fit in the proper way…?” “Oh, I remember all that stuff,” he says. “But I only really wanna do the cool shit I know how to do. The stuff that can break bones or send folks to the hospital, that’s how I’m gonna make my mark. Not by dancing around like a jackass.” She nods, and puckers her lips. “Okay,” she says, sheepishly. “Okay.” The silence ensues for a few ticks. He eats, while she looks down at her phone. She re-focuses her attention on Triumph. “So what now?” He finally finishes up the last of his food, wiping his hands with a napkin. He looks right at Alexis, and, in between chews, replies. “We go home.” 8:45 P.M. Pacific Heights, San Francisco, California. Triumph Frost’s House. In a sprawling dressing-type room, Alexis sits in a flowing and low-cut Dolce black evening gown at a elegant ivory desk with gold trim everywhere, and a plethora of beauty products scattered about in front of her that’d make Liz Taylor jealous. “All The People” by Money Mark fills the air, as she carefully applies eyeliner, paying close attention to the mirror resting on top of the desk as to not make any mistakes. She unfurls her ruby-red lipstick and crosses it upon her lips, one swift dash and then another. She closes it up and puts it down, and begins looking for something. She opens up the drawers and searches through them, but still not finding what she’s looking for. “Hey, babe,” she calls out. “Have you seen my earrings?” From inside a large walk-in closet, Triumph responds. “Dunno. Check the dressers or something.” She heads over to the corner of the room, her bare feet gliding across the carpet, where two oak dressers converge against one another. She goes through the drawers, and stops for a moment. At the top of one of the drawers, she finds an old framed picture. The glass is cracked, zig-zagging over the top and obscuring the faces. She pulls it out from its encasing. The picture is of Triumph’s mother and father, and another Japanese man. All three smile, as his mother holds the infant Triumph in her arms. Triumph walks in, nearly fully-dressed in a tuxedo. He fidgets with his bowtie. “Can you help me with this goddamned thing? I can never figure it--” She freezes, as he notices her with the picture in her hand. She stammers as he stalks forward towards her. “I…I didn’t--” Triumph rips the picture out of her hand. As she trembles, he half-smiles, and kisses her on the forehead. He turns to walk away. “Who’s the other man in that photo,” she asks him. “No one,” he says, without hesitation. He heads over to the mirror and checks himself out one last time, brushing his short blonde hair with his hand. “Just finish getting dressed.” He leaves the room. She slides the drawer back into place. 10:05 P.M. San Francisco, California. IwaCorp Tower. 47th Floor. On the top floor of IwaCorp towers, an elegant party rages on. It is a garish party, one that resembles the excess of the elite, replete with an orchestra band playing Wagner’s greatest hits, waiters and waitresses touting around trays of drink and hors d'oeuvres, ice statues of Greek gods and goddesses, and enough stuffy white people to fill a Republican orgy. The floor itself is a testament to the corporate dream, with water fountains, exotic flora, ivory columns, and windows looking over the sparking lights of the city down below. The gold elevator doors part, as out comes Triumph and Alexis. Alexis seems at first a bit captivated by the grace of the area, a kid watching his first baseball game or reading his first Playboy. Triumph, meanwhile, just stumbles forward, obviously a bit drunk. She turns to him. “This is unbelievable,” she gasps. He moans. “Yeah. These things make me feel like a real Nazi.” She elbows him in the side, as he grunts slightly. She grabs his arm and they walk through the room, getting a few looks from the much older partygoers. “I kinda feel like a princess,” she gushes. “Everyone’s looking at us and stuff.” “That’s ‘cause they think we’re assholes,” he remarks. She stops him and gives him a doe-eyed look. “Why would they think that?” “Because they’re assholes.” He grabs a champagne glass off a passing girl. His voice raises. “They’re just a buncha snobbish assholes. The whole fuckin’ lot!” She tries to quiet him down. “You’re drunk already,” her voice nearly a whisper. “Maybe we should just go.” “Fuck that,” he bellows. “They invited me.” He points out to the people around him. “Don’t forget I used to own this shithole.” Suddenly, an older Japanese man strides up to the two. He’s the man in the picture, with more wrinkles this time around. Triumph notices him, closes his eyes and sighs. The man is Kenji Iwamura, Triumph’s uncle and the new CEO and primary stockholder of IwaCorp. Kenji feigns some pleasure at the sight of Triumph. “Well,” Kenji says, extending his hand. “It’s good to see you after all these years, Triumph.” Triumph looks at the hand, and back at Kenji, with “are-you-shitting-me” splashed all over his face. Kenji retracts his hand. “I must admit,” Kenji says. “I was quite surprised that you took me up on my invitation to come tonight.” “Really,” Triumph groans. “That’s ironic. ‘Cause I was curious to see how a backstabbing piece of shit like you was even let past security.” Kenji turns bemused. He puts his hand on Triumph’s shoulder. “Maybe we can talk in private--“ Triumph knocks his hand away. “Get you’re fucking hand off me!” The party’s attention has almost fully turned to the two. “You’re not my fuckin’ father.” Kenji’s reaches eruption. With a cold and complete determination, Kenji grabs Triumph by the collar and leads him away towards a large oak door. He opens it and throws Triumph through, and slams it behind him. 10:22 P.M. San Francisco, California. IwaCorp Tower. 47th Floor. CEO’s office. Inside the massive office that overlooks the bay and the Golden Gate Bridge, Triumph immediately loosens his tie and tosses it on the desk. He walks over to the sidebar, and tosses ice into the glass. He pops open a nearby bottle of Maker’s Mark and fills it. He sits on a crimson couch nearby, draping his feet on a coffee table in front. Kenji watches him intently. “The more things change,” Kenji claims, as he walks over to the sidebar himself. Triumph swishes the ice around in his drink. “They made you head honcho so quickly, I see.” Triumph eyes the drink, as he takes a sip. “Who’d you have to blow to get this gig?” “My accountant,” Kenji replies. “He’s the one that let me spend my money to take a controlling interest in this place. You know, the one you sold without conferring with your family first.” “Ehh,” Triumph grunts. “The name means nothing to me.” “I see that. Mr. Frost.” Kenji smirks, as Triumph pauses to stare for a moment, then goes back to the drink. Kenji sits down across from Triumph in a matching crimson sofa chair, and takes a sip, never taking his eyes off Triumph. “I heard about your new line of work.” “Gotta do something to pass the time,” Triumph says. “But…that?” Kenji keeps his eyes on Triumph, who just shrugs in return. Kenji takes another sip. “I got to watch your debut.” “Prick got the best of me,” Triumph snorts. “But I’ll get him back.” He downs the drink, chewing the ice afterwards. “Is that humility I detect,” Kenji queries. Triumph leans forward. “Just what is it you want from me?” “An explanation,” Kenji counters. “I think I deserve one.” Triumph puts his glass down on the table and leans back onto the sofa. “Okay, here’s your explanation. My father was an adulterous piece of dogshit that fucked around on my mom, and now he’s dead.” “You shouldn’t speak so callously of your father,” Kenji warns. “Well,” Triumph groans. “He’s being eaten by worms and ass-raped in Hell. I think he’s got bigger things on his rotting mind than what his only son thinks of him.” Kenji shakes his head, displeased. “Have you no honor?” “Honor?!?” Triumph shoots up from his chair. “How about not cheating on mom? How about not crushing everyone in your way to get to the top? How about just bein’ a fuckin’ man?” Kenji chuckles, incensing Triumph further. “What’s so funny?” Kenji stands up and walks towards Triumph. “You remind me of him.” Triumph turns away. “I’m nothing like that bastard.” Kenji sighs, and pours himself another drink. He takes a long sip, and harshly swallows it down. “Your father was not...perfect, by any stretch of the word. Yes, he drank, and yes, he was unfaithful. He made mistakes, and he was ruthless in business.” Kenji swings around towards Triumph, whose back is still to him. “But above all, your father still believed in family. He loved your mother, and he cherished you.” Swinging around, Triumph has had enough. “What are you getting at?” “Does family mean absolutely nothing to you?” Kenji calls out. “Is your hatred for your father so great that you would ignore everyone else, and renounce your roots?” Triumph looks down at the ground, and slowly rises his head. He stares right through Kenji. “I didn’t call you because you tried to fuck my mom at dad’s funeral.” Kenji stops, nearly turning white. “Amy…told you that.” Triumph nods, as Kenji turns away ashamed. He slams his fist on the bar. “So now you know,” Triumph states. “Turns out you’re more like my old man than I could ever hope to be.” Silence permeates the room. Finally, Kenji gains the courage to break it. “I don’t deny, I’ve made mistakes in my life. I’ve let a lot of people down over the years.” He turns back to Triumph, solemn. “But…I only wanted the company back so I can make a difference. So I could be proud of a legacy that has too-long been tarnished. I only...” Kenji’s voice trails off. “Well,” Triumph puts his hand on Kenji’s shoulder. “You got what you wanted.” He slides his hand off the shoulder and heads to the door. Kenji attempts to muster up something to say, but nothing comes out. The oak door slams behind Triumph. 11:15 P.M. San Francisco, California. Triumph’s Limousine. In the back of the limousine, Triumph slumps in his chair. Alexis rests along his chest. Eric Clapton’s “Wonderful Tonight” plays from the driver’s window at a soft tone. They drive in silence, as Triumph’s eyes close. “You okay, babe?” She asks him, her voice bouncing off his chest. He doesn’t say anything at first. She “hmm’s”, awaiting his response, as his eyes flutter underneath the lids. “Yeah, girl,” he finally tells her. “I’m good.” She nestles up closer onto his chest, as the limousine drives off into the cold, crisp night.
