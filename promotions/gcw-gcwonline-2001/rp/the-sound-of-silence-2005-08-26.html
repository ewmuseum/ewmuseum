The Sound of Silence

26 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8-26-05 “Your anger is a gift.” - Zach de la Rocha ~<>~<>~<>~ Coors Amphitheater, San Diego, California? From stage to skyline the ground was an ocean of bobbing heads. Seventeen thousand people had gathered in the Chula Vista amphitheater, swaying and dancing with the cool sound that poured from Coldplay’s onstage aural assault. With the rear stands pulled down, the white choral shell of the stage opened out to a field that was filled to the horizon with countless bodies, all moving to the seductively powerful chords of “Yellow” with hands splayed to the heavens and minds tuned into the marijuana haze that permeated the open air arena. Random displays of carefree flamboyance dotted the crowd; small circles opened amidst the masses as naturally as a sinking sunset, filled quickly by women with oversized hoola-hoops and men jostling one another around with reckless abandon. Some called it “moshing.” Rich Rollins preferred to label it the stupidity of youth. Lost in a sea of stoned college-somethings and high school hipsters, Rich “The Renegade” Rollins carefully picked his way through the tight assemblage step by agonizing step. A distinct herbal scent hung in the air like sour wine, doing little to mask the ever present aroma of sweating bodies and the occasional ecstasy-induced vomit that he did well to shimmy around. An ominous overcast sky hung itself across the Orange County landscape, threatening to release its payload upon the hapless ravers and soak the soil until it ran black with mud. At least it wouldn’t smell so god-awful, The Renegade mused. The world danced and vibrated around him as if in slow motion. The ambiance was indeed surreal; it had been some time since Rich Rollins had found himself in a concert atmosphere. He had once seen the Stones perform on the very same stage that Coldplay now occupied, and to be honest, he had enjoyed the Stones more. At least they were a hell of a lot better than Hoobastank. But none of that was on Rollins? mind, not now. He wasn’t about to let his sudden contact high dissuade him so easily. His goal was simple. He was out to find the Devil. ~<>~<>~<>~ Earlier? “Funny, I would have thought that GCW’s inside man would have been more, well” not you.” Standing with his arms firmly crossed against his broad chest, Rich Rollins eyed his contact wryly as the man shuffled uncomfortably under the larger wrestler’s imposing gaze. With an exasperated sigh The Renegade finally fished out his wallet, opening the pocket. “So what’s the damage, bucko?” Tossing a surreptitious glance over one shoulder, Kevin Jacobs wipes his brow as the folded corners of a dozen Benjamins swam through his pupils. With a greedy smack of his lips he grinned. “Hit me with a cool two large, and we’re in business.” Jacob’s oily smile conjured up a decidedly dark scowl on Rollins? face. “I’ll hit you with two, all right.” The Renegade replied with more self control than he felt himself capable of. “And just so we’re on the same page, I’m talkin” about fists, not charitable donations. Take the damn money.” Tossing two hundreds at the GCW interview man, Rollins shook the frustration from his head as the scraggly reporter dove to the floor in pursuit of his bounty. Catching the man’s chin with one steel-toed boot, Rollins frowned patiently as he lifted Jacob’s gaze to his. “Who is Trey’s juice man, Jacobs?” The tone in Rollin’s voice left no room for good humor. “I need to know where he gets it, and from who. Start talkin”.” Stuffing the bills into a wad in his front pocket, Jacobs sucks at his teeth for a second as he considers Rollins? no-nonsense demeanor. A firm steel-toed nudge into his trachea loosens his lips in a hurry. “Some Californian kingpin, although nobody on the roster actually gets their shit from the guy. It’s always a bunch of hood rats and third-party folks, depending on what city we’re in. I pick it all up in a rear parking garage and make my rounds through the locker rooms.” It was a garbled response, too hurried and breathy to be a blatant lie. It shouldn’t have been possible for Rollins? frown to deepen, but it did. “You’re still not giving me a name,” he warned dangerously. “It’s the Devil.” Once again Rollins? frown began to dig deep furrows in his forehead. “I already met him.” His boot went further into Jacob’s throat. “No, you don’t understand,” Jacobs blurted. “The White Devil, it’s what they call him on the streets. It’s got something to do with his way of handling lousy debts, a circumstance I’ve never really wanted to investigate too intimately.” Interesting, indeed. Kevin Jacob’s eyes were wide, too wide to hide any sort of underhanded deception. Besides, Rollins didn’t really think the man capable of bullshitting his way through a pile of manure, let alone straight to his face. With a satisfied grunt The Renegade finally let his weight off of the interviewer’s throat. Jacobs gulped audibly, caressing his neck with tender care as he rose to his feet. “Where could I find this White Devil?” Rollins inquired with none-too-subtle implication. Jacobs shrugged. “The guy lives in Orange County, and that’s all I know. Try hitting some venues around “Frisco, that’s likely his biggest market.” He paused, almost thoughtfully. “Why the hell you looking for a guy like that, anyway?” His intended purpose finally fulfilled, The Renegade was already on his way down the hall as he casually tossed over his shoulder: “I’ve got a headache, and I’m too cheap for a shrink.” Jacobs just shook his head as he watched him leave. ~<>~<>~<>~ Back in Orange County? Rollins had been wrong. The rain only made the stench stick. And it stuck to everything. Chris Martin was just warming up the second verse to “Amsterdam” when a fantastic bolt of lightening split the clouds open like a gushing wound, eliciting a rousing ovation from the rollicking ravers. The Coors Amphitheater became a sopping slosh-pit before the band could hit the bridge. Rollins’s head began to pound, and he knew what would come next if he didn’t wrap it up quick. As if some greater power had heard his plea, a thrashing rain-soaked youth began tossing himself randomly into those nearest him, quickly opening up a circle around him. Others quickly joined in; as The Renegade watched on with morbid amusement the circle grew considerably, filling up with half a dozen like-minded rowdy moshers. It was a good an opportunity as he was going to get. Whipping his wet hair out of his eyes, Rich Rollins stepped into the thrashing mass. Had any one of the youthful delinquents been even remotely sober, they would have taken one look at The Renegade and steered clear. As it happened, sobriety was most definitely not an “in” concept at a concert. Within seconds the first adrenaline-fueled hooligan shoved into Rollins? chest, and without skipping a beat Rollins shoved back. The boy - no older than eighteen, by Rollins? estimation - stumbled backward into the wall of concert goers who were doing their best to ignore the mayhem. They shoved the boy back into the circle violently, where he was promptly taken off his feet by another shirtless moshing maniac. Guys began hurling themselves into the concert-goers just to provoke more participants, but Rollins was more interested in getting the current crowd’s attention. When a second bare-chested buffoon tried ramming into The Renegade with his shoulder, Rollins simply snagged a handful of the kid’s long hair and jerked his head to the sky. Wheeling his prey around, The Renegade pulled them both into the center of the pit, which was now a massive mud pot. Another angry youth took his chances and charged Rollins from behind, but a swift boot to the gut cut short his aspirations rather quickly. A few of the moshers began to take notice, and their faces fell from the doped-out pallor of delinquent mischief and into something much more primal. Much more primal. It was then that somebody began swinging their wallet chain. The impact caught Rollins square across the jaw, and he could taste the alkaline tang of hot blood in his mouth even before he hit the ground. The kid with the long hair had managed to wiggle away; in his place were three different faces, and each one of them had The Renegade dead in their sights. The first two charged drunkenly, one pulling his wet shirt off his back along the way. The third - the bastard with the chained wallet - started swinging his weapon above his head like a lasso, which incensed more than a few of the innocent bystanders who were merely trying to enjoy their forty bucks. The first two reached Rollins first, but he was already waiting. The first kid - the one now sans an Abercrombie tee-shirt - slung his wet apparel like a whip, trying to blister The Renegade’s skin. Rollins caught the shirt in his fist on the second attempt and yanked forcibly, pulling the raver directly into the path of the second charger. Both of them fell into the mud after the collision, and he would be lying if Rollins said he felt guilty. The swinging chain struck Rollins again, this time on the nape of his neck. A searing pain shot up and down his spine as he stumbled forward, but his footing held. Turning sharply he took off at a dead sprint, hurtling a passing mosher en route to his wallet-touting antagonist. The spry little bastard tried to evade him with another swipe of his deadly lasso, but Rollins caught him dead center and planted him in the mud. He felt a sick kind of satisfaction as he heard the kid’s lungs gasp for air. Yanking the chain from the raver’s grip, Rollins chucked it somewhere into the crowd, instantly pulling the breathless youth to his knees. Locking the kid’s right arm behind his own head, The Renegade applied a basic hammerlock that effectively held the boy in place. By now some of the others had stopped shoving each other, curious as to what the commotion was all about. With a defiant glare Rich Rollins roared into the rain: “I’m lookin” for the Devil. Who’s got his number?” A dozen stoned faces blinked at him, unsure of how to respond. So he forced a startled yelp from the boy at his mercy, wrenching his arm further down his back. The sound of eerie silence met The Renegade’s ears. Coldplay had stopped playing. People started staring. Nobody started talking. And his headache was starting to pound. “No?” he inquired, pushing the boy’s arm to the breaking point. “I’d hate to turn this little shithead into a visual aid-?” “You mean de White Devil, esse?” someone piped up. Rollins tilted his head ever so slightly, trying to find his addressor. He was an older guy, and by older it meant he was obviously out of high school. An olive skinned Mexican with a bad goatee and worse teeth flashed Rollins a yellow grin, blinking the rain out of his eyes as he nodded once from the relative security of the mosh pit’s boundary. “Finally, somebody who speaks fuckin” English.” Rollins tossed the boy into the mud as if his prisoner had never existed, taking a few imposing steps toward the Mexican in the white tank top. The remaining thrashers continued their drunken brawl, but a noticeable bubble of space followed The Renegade as he walked through the thick of it. The silence passed as Coldplay launched into “The Scientist,” causing the entire fifteen thousand strong to scream with approval. “You want to see de Devil” Why?” the Mexican hollered bluntly against the roar of the crowd. Rollins allowed a slender smirk to cross his lips as he approached the man, absently balling his hands into fists. “I’ve got a wicked headache, and I was hopin” your esse would have the right kind of medicine.” The Mexican grinned, leaning into Rollins? ear as he came into arm’s length. “De Devil gots everything you need, vato. Come, I show him to you.” They slipped into the crowd as two silent pythons stalking the pampas, leaving the kids to the whims of their own senseless brutality. ~<>~<>~<>~ Minutes later? He should have known it was an ambush. He’d run his mouth like he was John fucking Wayne, and this was what he got for it. Rich Rollins could still feel the goose egg on the back of his skull, inches above where he was already lacerated from that brat’s swinging wallet. He had followed the Mexican into the men’s restroom, which had been conspicuously empty at the time of their arrival. When the lights had gone out, The Renegade knew he had made a catastrophic error. He felt the warm slickness of his blood as he reached for the back of his head, trying to pick himself up off the grimy floor. Countless pairs of rain-drenched sneakers had tromped an unpleasant gray matter across the white tile, a gray matter that now wore itself on Rollins? cheek. On top of it all, his headache had become a raging war zone in his mind. It was then that he heard the laughter. That damnable, damnable laughter. “Look at you now, Mr. Champion.” He placed the voice instantly, and Rollins didn’t need to turn around to know that a man who didn’t exist was standing right behind him. “If you can get jumped by a drugged-out wetback, what possible chance do you have against Borealis and Trey?” Dave Olson heckled from somewhere behind him, somewhere in the depths of his mind. It was his headache. It was his curse. He had been drugged during the Infinite Gauntlet, and he didn’t know by who. But he knew that his only hope of erasing the hallucinations in his head was to find the chemical that caused it, track it to its roots. The laughter continued, echoing through the canyons of his psyche. It was then that Rich Rollins looked at the restroom wall, where a message had been left just for him: Better luck next time, esse. Sinking to a new low that would have scared the rational side of his consciousness, Rollins submitted to the voices in his head. And he laughed right along with them.
