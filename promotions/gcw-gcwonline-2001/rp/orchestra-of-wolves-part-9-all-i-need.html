Orchestra of Wolves, Part 9: All I Need

undated

Date: Today He swallowed the last of his wine and leaned back in the sturdy wicker chair as the night's cool breeze kissed his bare arms and face. It was a quiet night in northern Florida, with only chirping crickets and the faint hum of the garden's lighting system to accompany their own noises. Gently tapping his fingers against his empty glass, Andy looked at his blonde companion. “This is going to take some getting used to.” She washed down the last of her salad with a mouthful of her own beverage (non-alcoholic, of course) and smiled. There she was, sitting on five thousand dollars worth of decking outside the GCW World Heavyweight Champion's million dollar mansion. It was nearly 10pm and the first week in November, yet still warm enough for her to go sleeveless, and she sat before a table topped with enough food to feed twice their number. A feast for two, painstakingly slaved over by the man on the table's other side for hours before. Her eyes met his pearly blues when she'd finished admiring her surroundings. “I dunno,” she said. “I might just be able to live with it.” Andy felt his heart flutter as he returned her grin. If someone had told him two weeks prior that he'd soon be calling Vivica J. Valentine his girlfriend, he'd have laughed in that person's face. It was something he'd never even considered; the thought of dating his long-time GCW ally hadn't so much as crossed his mind until the night before Dangerous Games, yet now that it had all came together it felt like the most natural thing in the world. “Again, I'm really sorry about the OJ,” Andy said, nodding towards Valentine's half-full glass of Tropicana. “Totally forgot about the whole straight-edge thing.” “It's oka--” “Store's open 24/7 you know,” Andy, always keen to satisfy, interjected. “Wouldn't take five minutes to hop in the car and pick-up something a little less... ummm, breakfast-ish. I was just so bus--” It took the activation of his sense of touch to stop him. “Murr, it's fine,” she said, leaning forward and curling her dainty fingers around his own. “Honestly. How can I complain about the drink when you've gone to all this trouble with the food? I don't even wanna know how much all of this cost...” her gaze fell downwards to the emptied soupbowls and dirty plates. “Yeah, well. I think it was worth it. And please, you don't have to call me 'Murr' anymore.” “Sorry, force of habit.” Vivica paused to brush a rogue strand of red hair from her eye. “Nobody's ever done anything like this for me, all this time and effort. “And this place,” she said, looking around the house's red-brick exterior one more time, “it's all just so much more than I could've ever asked for.” He leaned forward, gently rubbing his thumb into the back of her tiny mitt. “Stop it. Beneath that stubborn exterior, you're one of the sweetest girls I've ever met. Don't try and tell me that every you've been with hasn't tried to give you the world.” Andy was wrong - totally wrong - but Vivica wasn't going to sour the mood by recounting her the dismal months she spent by Christian Zenith's side. Instead, she let the moment consume her as she enjoyed the spoils of the one good thing that Dangerous Games had brought her. “I can't believe how well you've taken this. When I told you I lov--... well, I thought you'd never wanna speak to me again.” “What can I say? I'm an impressionable guy,” Andy beamed. “Honestly though? I ain't gonna lie, it hit me like a tonne of bricks. Was just about the last thing in the world I expected to hear from your lips, and maybe I wasn't so sure about what I was doing at DG, but I sure as hell am now. And you know, these past few months haven't exactly been the easiest for me,” he continued, pouring himself another glass, “and some people tried to tell me to stay away from relationships for a little while, after what happened with Kay, but what do they know? This is a risk worth taking. Still, I gotta ask you something.” “Anything.” “How long's it been?” Valentine's face loosened as confusion nipped her inner cortex. “What do you mean?” “You said that you loved me. That kind of thing doesn't exactly spring-up overnight.” “Ohhh,” she said, pondering. It had been a while, longer than Andy could ever have known, and she wasn’t sure if she wanted to divulge. Not yet, at least. “A while.” “And how long is ‘a while’?” “Just a while.” He got it. It had only been a couple of weeks, and it had all moved so fast that she wasn’t quite ready to explain absolutely everything. “What about you?” she quizzed, leaning forward with her elbows on the steel table. It caught him off-guard. “Huh?” “Well, you made your mind up pretty quickly. Usually when someone’s friend says they’re in love with them, it takes a little longer than twenty-four hours for ‘em to get their head straight. Must’ve been something there, otherwise we wouldn’t be doing this right now.” “Heh. My head was about as straight as Garbage Bag Johnny’s mental state at DG, V,” Andy said, smiling. He leaned closer and again took her hand. “I dunno, maybe there was something and I just didn’t realise. Maybe all it needed was a spark to light the flame. I guess I have been flirting with you a heck of a lot on WorldWide…” “Tell me about it; I barely get a moment’s peace these days without you trying to wind me up.” “That’s a bad thing?” “Of course not.” He squeezed Vivica’s hand delicately as he turned to gaze out across a garden enveloped by dusk. The small lanterns that lit-up the deck only spread light across a small portion of Andy’s tidy lawn, with nought else but the swimming pool’s luminescent blue shine to pierce the darkness. Quite romantic. Andy looked back at the myriad of cutlery and crockery on the table. “It’s getting late. Wanna go inside, get some dessert?” Vivica shook her head. “No,” she said tenderly, “let’s stay out here a little longer.” Slowly they moved together until their lips met over the table. Andy felt a warmth in his stomach that he thought he’d lost forever as he savoured the moment like it was his last. * * * * * Andy Murray in... All I Need * * * * * Andy heard her faint footsteps padding quietly behind him as he dropped two slices of seeded bread into the toaster. He looked up and smiled. “Morning.” With a hefty GCW holdall slung over her shoulder, Vivica shuffled along to the great room's kitchen area and returned his smile with a nod. Clothed modestly in a black Atreyu tee and some jeans, the Dangerous Games runner-up stopped just short of the Scot. “Leaving so soon?” Andy asked, pouring some fruit juice into a glass. “Sure you don't want some breakfast?” “I gotta head-up home before WorldWide, got a few things to take care of. Besides, I think I've robbed you of enough food over the past twenty-four hours.” “Alright,” Andy leaned down the full 1'3” height different and pecked her on the cheek. “Need a ride to the airport?” “I already called for a cab. Are you always this--” “-- generous?” “Well, I was gonna say 'clingy', but I guess that'll do,” Valentine said mockingly, folding her arms and chuckling at The King. “Pfffft.” Andy shrugged. “Not my fault you're the most stubborn woman in the world when it comes to GCW; gotta make the most of it just now, 'cause I know on WorldWide it's gonna be another one of those 'awww shit, V's on a tear!' type deals, isn't it?” Without allowing his chicanery the chance to marinate, Murray upped his dukes and playfully punched Valentine on the shoulder. “God, you're so annoying.” “I know.” The shrill parp of a car's horn sounded from outside, immediately catching both of their attentions. “Gotta go,” Valentine said, pushing herself on her tiptoes to hug the 6'7” GCW World Champion. “See you at the show.” Andy retracted, nodded. “Alright. Just promise me you ain't gonna get to beat-up over the whole Jay Terror thing? It's not healthy to let a parasite like that kill your buzz, no matter how fucked-up that shit was.” Valentine's nod and smile were only half-convincing. “I'll try. See ya.” She turned around to make the short journey to Andy's front door, before before she could get there Valentine found herself overtaken by Murray's long strides. Clamping his hand around the door handle, Murray pulled it open and a cool waft of morning air quickly swooshed through the vestibule. “Let me get that for you.” “I can open doors for myself, you know.” “What can I say? I'm clingy.” Vivica laughed. “Later.” “Later.” He watched her as she stepped down from the porch and tossed her bag into the cab's trunk without the driver's assistance. Vivica slid into the backseat and Andy returned her wave as the car slowly began to creep down the gravel driveway and out of his line of sight. Damn, my life is brilliant, he thought, stepping back inside the house, closing the door behind him. As Andy went though his to-do list for the day, he couldn't shake the feeling that he was forgetting something. He walked back through to the kitchen just as his toast had popped out of the toaster. Turning to retrieve some butter from the fridge, Andy pulled the door open and saw a small, tinfoil package laying on the refrigerator's bottom shelf. Marvin's sandwiches. A lightbulb went off in his head. Shit. Looked down at his watched, breathed a sight of relief. 8:30. Still plenty time to get him to school, he thought. Retrieving his son's sandwiches from the shiny white depths of a refrigerator that was far bigger than necessary, Andy quickly stepped passed the dining area, through the lounge, and hopped up onto the staircase. “Time for school, little man,” he hollared as he made it onto the top landing and walked towards Marvin's door with a smile on his face. It felt good to be doing something nice for his kid, no matter how small the gesture. Balling a fist, Andy wrapped his knuckles against the door's white surface. No response came from within, so he gently twist the doorknob and strode in. “Time to go, c'mon,” he said as he entered. Quickly, he scanned his eyes over his new surroundings. Unmade bed, open wardrobe, droves of discarded DVD cases and clothes from previous days. Typical twelve year old boy's bedroom, but no sign of it's occupant. He stepped into the room and walked around the single bed, just in case Marvin was hiding somewhere, but no sign of him. Andy noticed that the bedside table where he usually gathered his schoolbooks and pencil case was all but cleared. Releasing that he'd failed his son again, Murray sighed. He knew he shouldn't have gotten rid of that god damn dog. * * * * * Marvin was sweating by the time he'd reached the school's gates. The morning sun was school and unforgiving, and there was nary a cloud in the sky to break-up it's incessant glow. He'd been walking for almost three miles with a heavy sack of books slung over his shoulder and an ill-fitting, uncomfortable school uniform clinging to his long, wiry frame. All he wanted to do was get inside the building and collapse. The black-haired juvenile passed through the red-brick gates and immediately attracted the same level of attention he always did. Height-wise, he was at least one or two inches above the next tallest kid in his year - a gene he'd inherited from his father, no doubt - and his scrawny, gangly appearance didn't exactly endear him to the school's army of young future jocks and meatheads. But it wasn't his appearance that garnered him such recognition, no; it was the fact that his father had become one of the most recognisable faces in American sport. He could usually tell what kind of day it was going to be by the nature of the first cat-call that met him on his arrival. This morning's “hey, butthead! Tell your Dad his girlfriend's a bitch!” didn't exactly bode well. Closing his eyes, groaning, Marvin felt the presence of a bulky adversary draw closer to him. He opened his eyes and there he was. Danny Ford - an ugly little shithead with a shorn head and a squashed button nose that looked like someone had taken a sledgehammer to it - flanked by two faceless gonns that Marv couldn't recall the name of. Dealing with Ford wasn't the most pleasant of tasks at the best of times, but Marvin was in no mood for his shit-talk. “What's 'a matter, four-eyes? Cat got your tongue,” the pre-pubescent bully pried, his words punctuated by a smattering of sniggers from his loyal slaves. Still Marvin refused to look Danny in the eyes. Things hadn't been so hot for Marvin since Kayla had left. With his dad on the road all the time he found himself more often than not completely alone in a home big enough to house ten. Sometimes his uncles were around, sure, but he'd lost count of the amount of times he'd had to make his own dinner or send himself off to school over the past couple of months, and things were beginning to wear thin. Winston was the last straw, though. At least, when he was there, he had a companion, someone to talk to while he was all alone in his father's mansion. But no, dad had to go and get rid of him; something about having to get rid of everything that reminded him of Kayla so he could move-on. Typical grown-up mumbo-jumbo. “Come on, bitch. Say something,” Danny said, softly shoving Marvin in the shoulder. A bulldog's grin planted on his unattractive features, the twelve year-old repeated the action with a little more force. “Come on.” He was mocking him, goading him as he'd done so many times before, but Marvin was fed up. Fuck it, he thought, and summoned strength he never knew he had to plant four knuckles right under Danny Ford's jaw. The kid hit the deck instantly, and Marvin staggered backwards, surprised by his own actions. He stepped backwards as Ford's wingmen hit the deck, tending to their fallen leader. Everything around him seemed to freeze as the dreadful repercussions of decking the grade's bully began to fill his head. That trance was quickly broken as the playground monitor's whistle sounded and a heavy hand clasped onto his shoulder. “Crap”. * * * * * “Outta the way,” Sid Murray growled as he bashed his palm into the middle of his steering wheel. Slowly, the crowd of camera-wielding shithawks began to disperse, but not quite as quickly as the eldest of the Murray clan would have liked. He revved his engine menacingly, clearing a few more of the paparazzi from his fender as his car crawled towards Andy's driveway. “Fuckin' assholes.” There must've been at least fifty of them, but Sid wasn't counting. He was no stranger to the army of press vultures that descended upon Andy's house whenever something big was going down, but he'd never seen anything like this before. His brother's stock was at a record high; he was dating one of the most desirable women in the North American content, and perhaps the only competitor who would legitimately challenge him for the title of GCW's most popular wrestler, and then there was the small matter of defeating another hall-of-famer at Dangerous Games. Everyone wanted a piece of The Scottish King of Cool, but that didn't excuse the group of dirthounds that had completely surrounded his home. Peering into Sid's car window, trying to look for someone more important than he, they were like maggots around a goat's carcass, pressing their palms against the windows but keeping a safe enough distance so as not to get run over. Eventually he made it through, and as the large, black gates closed automatically behind him, he thought that it may be the sweetest sight he'd see all day. Glad to be away from the paps, the bearded Murray patriarch pulled his old Chrysler up beside his brother's shiny Mercedes and stepped into his home away from home. Immediately he saw his brother, sitting at the dining table wearing some eighties hair metal t-shirt, finishing off the morning paper. “Oh, shit, Chewbacca!” Andy said, looking up from the sports page. “Wasn't expecting to see you today. What's up? Life on Kashyyyk getting you down?” “Hey, if I'm Chewie, you're totally Yoda,” Sidney said, pointing at his brother. “Because I'm wise and full of knowledge?” “No, because you're ugly and full of shit.” “Touché.” Sid strode through the room, his thick brown hair bouncing off his shoulder blade with every step, much like a lion's mane. He took a seat at the table and immediately grabbed the coffee pot, helping himself to a cup as he passed his gaze across the empty room. “Mind if I take a cup of Joe?” he said, despite the fact that he'd already taken his first sip. “Yes, put that back immediately.” “Piss off.” Andy smiled. His greetings with his elder brother were always a little unorthodox, but he wouldn't have had the banter any other way. Besides, him and Cayle had done a grand job of keeping the house in tip-top shape while he was away for Dangerous Games - the last thing he could do would be to begrudge him of a cup of coffee. “Quiet in here today,” Sid observed. “Vivica's gone?” Andy nodded. “She had to go back up to Illinois before WorldWide. I think she's pretty bummed about Dangerous Games.” “Pfft, I would be too. That Terror's a sneaky little bastard for sure,” Sid responded, taking another sip. “Gotta be careful around little fuckers like that. But shit, man. You're the guy that's gotta face him at NC-17. Again. Gotta keep your eyes open this week.” “Heh, Terrible Jay's the last thing on my mind at the moment, mate. Gotta put up with Taylor and Digi this week, that ain't no small challenge.” “Oh, indeed,” Sid responded, drawing out the “indeed” as if he were The Wire's Omar Little. He took another look around the room, in-particular noticing Marvin's usual perch by the television. “Young'in's at school?” “Yeah,” Andy's tone suddenly faded. “Must've slipped out early; when I went upstairs to give him a lift he was already gone.” “Heh, 'least he actually made it today.” Andy's facial muscles tightened with confusion. He looked at Sid curiously as an “oh shit” feeling descended upon the eldest of the Murray family. “Uhhhhhh.” “What are you talking about? He never misses school.” Sid sighed. He didn't want to give up any more information, but he'd slipped-up and there was no backtracking. He had too much respect for his brother to be anything less than truthful, even if his skills as a single parent remained suspect. “I caught him skipping a day while you were in Miami. Just one, though.” It didn't matter to Andy that it was “just one”; the news hit him for six. “What? Why?!” “I dun--” “Christ, man,” Andy interrupted, shaking his head. “I go away for four god damn days and this happens. Can't even trust him to go to school no more.” “Hey, be easy on the kid,” Sid said. “This whole Kayla thing ain't exactly easy on him, you ain't been the only one feelin' it. Besides, s'just one day, and I sent him in to catch the afternoon session. Just a few hours he missed, really.” “Hours turn to days, days turn to weeks. Fuck's sake.” His frustration evident, Andy's hand curled into a fist and he bashed it softly against the table. He'd noticed his son's behaviour had become increasingly erratic since Kay had departed from his life, but this was unacceptable. “I'll have a word with him when he gets home.” “Uhhh. I don't mean to pry, man, and far be it for someone like me to give parenting advice, but don't you think you should maybe do somethin' a little more endearing than give the boy an ear-bashing?” Sid paused. “I mean, you ain't exactly done a lot of father/son stuff lately.” “I'm on the road, what can I do?” Andy shrugged, ruefully. “You could've not gotten rid of the pup, for a start.” Andy sighed; this was eventually going to crop up, but that didn't make it any simpler. “I had to do that.” “Did you really have to?” Sid queried. “I get the whole 'movin' on' thing, but seriously you got V now. How much harm would it have done to keep the furball around? Kid's at a loose end without the pup to toss a ball for.” The King said nothing, just sat there. Contemplating. Sid shrugged. “Just sayin'.” * * * * * “Marvin Murray! Just because your father is a professional wrestler doesn't mean you can go around hitting other children in the face.” Mrs. Keynes was fuming. The dog-faced headmistresses leaned forward in her chair, scowling at Andy's son as if he'd just committed a terrorist atrocity. He'd seen her striding down the corridor like a woman on a mission before, hordes of terrified children scurrying out of her way should they incur her terrible wrath, but never before had he been the front of her fury. Marvin would've felt move comfortable in a Dutch oven. “I'm sorry, Master Murray, but we cannot tolerate that sort of behaviour from anyone, even you,” she continued without even giving Marvin a chance to speak up for himself. “The school nurse has had to send young Danny to the hospital for check-ups, they think he might have a slight concussion. Your Dad might get away with doing that on television, but you cannot do that here!” For the first time, Marvin dragged his gaze up from his shoes. His “I'm sorry,” came out as little more than a whimper. “Sorry isn't good enough, I'm afraid. I have no choice but to send you home for the rest of the day, after that, we might be looking at a suspension. This is serious business, Marvin.” Dread washed over him. A suspension, great. Just what he needed, just what his father needed. “Now run along to the office and we'll arrange a way to get you home. Someone will be in-contact later in the day when we figure out what we're going to do with you.” Slowly Marvin rose from his seat and trudged his heels across the hardwood floor. He tried to utter another apology, but the spectacle-wearing rector had shooed him out of the room before the words could come out. * * * * * “You're right man,” Andy said to his brother. “Maybe I should take him to the next show. Vancouver, could be a fun trip.” “Yeah, and what was it you were just saying about missing school.” “Good point.” Andy gently tapped his fingers against the dining table's mahogany surface. Across from him, Sid slid the paper towards himself and pulled it open at the middle pages. Things weren't terribly peachy between him and Marvin, but they'd been through tougher times and got through them fine. But still, he'd barely spoken to his son in the past two months, and that wasn't a healthy position for any parent to be in. Since she'd been gone, Andy had found his son more willing to spend time at his friend's houses than at home. He could hardly blame him, though; when Andy wasn't around, all that was left for Marvin were Andy's brothers, and they weren't exactly the most obvious father figures in the world. For the first time in quite some time, Andy put his career to the back of his mind for a second. Professional wrestling had cost him three relationships, he wasn't about to let it do the same with his son. He knew what needed to be done. He rose from his chair quickly and walked over to grab his keys from the kitchen worktop. Sid looked up, distracted. “Where you goin'?” “I'm going to get Winston.” And then he was gone. * * * * * Marvin Murray made his way through the Tallahassee brush to the back gate that the press pack round the house's front knew nothing of. It wasn't exactly the most accessible of entrances to the property and he'd had to traverse an uneven path littered with fallen branches and dying leaves to get there, but it sure as hell beat trying to stave off the wave of paparazzi that wouldn't hesitate to stick a camera and a dictaphone in Marvin's face. He brushed off the tree debris from his school uniform and closed the gate as he stepped onto the lawn. It was getting late in the afternoon now, and the sun had sunk low behind the Murrays' family home, but it was still warm enough for him to feel uncomfortable. Lack of tolerance for the merciless Floridian heat; just another trait he'd picked-up from his father, and an unfortunate one at that. Dad would probably notice that he was arriving home about fifteen minutes earlier than usual, but Marvin didn't care. He'd been moping around downtown all-day trying to avoid having to come back early and explain everything to Andy, but there was only so much skulking he could do. He set of on the long walk back home, deliberately the scenic route for fear of running into his father along the way. Dad wouldn't understand, not a chance. If he told him that'd flattened a kid at school then Marvin wouldn't hear the end of it. Sure, he would surely find out when the school called about the suspension, but Marvin didn't care. It was a brief respite, even if he was only prolonging the inevitable. The young Murray walked around the outside of the uncovered swimming pool and slid in through the French doors with a heavy heart. The house was probably empty, and he probably had another boring evening alone ahead of him, but god did that air-conditioning feel good when it kissed his moist skin. Tired, Marvin slung his school bag to the floor and sat himself down in the house's second dining area. He leaned forward on the table with his elbows and pulled his thin glasses from his head; he felt like giving up. It took a gentle, familiar pitter-patter on the tiled floor to awaken him. Drawing closer and closer, too quick for him to react. Suddenly he felt tiny claws digging through his thin school trousers and a frenzied panting that he thought he'd heard the last of. A tiny dachshund pup was clinging tightly to his leg, wagging its tail so frantically that it might have fallen off. “Winston!” he chirped excitedly, scooping the chocolate-coloured dog into his arms and kissing it on the forehead. He stood up, struggling as the little dog tried to climb up his chest to lick his face. “Good boy, good boy.” A shadow loomed from the doorway. A proud father leaning against the wall, grinning. Not knowing how he'd be feeling at the puppy not still been at the pound. “Dad,” Marvin said as he tried to fight off Winston's hyperactivity. “You got him back!” “Never should've let him go, son,” Andy said as Marvin brought the dog towards him. Andy rubbed his thumb behind Winston's ear, softly. For the first time in longer than he could remember, he felt a thrill greater than any pinfall could ever bring him. “How about we take him for a walk, huh?” “That'd be awesome.” “I'll get my shoes.” Buoyant, beaming, Andy strode through to the great room with a lion's pride. Something caught his attention almost instantly. A song playing out, not loud, not quiet. Shrill and tinny, coming from a tiny speaker across the room. Andy recognised his son's ringtone and walked-up to the coffee table to retrieve it. He tried not to look, but it was hard not to when he say the name flashing on the screen. Mom. Beneath it was a number he recognised instantly. Jane's? He sank.
