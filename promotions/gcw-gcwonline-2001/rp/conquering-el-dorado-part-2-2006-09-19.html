Conquering El Dorado Part 2

19 September 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 19, 2006 CHAPTER ONE: Invisible Wounds It was just minutes to eleven at night as I leaned against a wall in the Rose Garden Arena with my hand clutching the knob of the exit. I pulled my black hood over my head, and took a breath of fresh air. I did my best to push out the throbbing pain coursing over my ribs after two hard fought victories over Rufio and the Jackson Brothers. It hurt; and not the injuries to say the least. Rufio wasn’t anywhere near the experience that I felt I should be and I still felt like I would be pissing blood after the beating at his hands. Even, with a tag team victory, I couldn’t help the feeling that the Jackson’s hadn’t come prepared to fight while the dauntingly clear reminder of what it meant to fail against a team that did, Fiery Passion, remained in my ribs. “You ready to get out of here?” beckoned the baby blues of my manager from a crack on the other side of the exit door. I nodded, taking in another sigh of exhaustion, and pressed through the door out into the lions den. “La Sombra, congratulations on your victorious night.” “Mr. De Hoyas, with injuries sustained tonight, how do you think your chances of winning Dangerous Games will fair?” “Julio, is there a reason you are leaving the arena before the card is over and do you think this will affect your place in Ascension?” “What’s wrong rookie, can’t hack it?” The reporters swarmed my path like locus in a blinding sea of camera flashes and Portland Oregon’s luminous nightlife backdrop. Each one shared a commonality in their dauntingly idiotic comments and my annoyance for their disregard of the clearly marked barriers, separating their standing area and the passageway for GCW Superstars to make it safely to their already waiting vehicles. They shoved, bumped, and subtly clawed at one another in the hopes that raising their voice or shoving a microphone into my chest would afford them an interview or words that Brack had already guaranteed them wouldn’t happen. My goal of weaving past the sea of obstructions to a rental awaiting my presence, was beginning to look damn near impossible. I could feel the blood thirsty media closing in around me, their breath, beating across my back, begging me to turn around and lose my proper bearing. I had joked in the locker room about the actual matches paling in comparison to the gauntlet of journalists we swam through to get to the arena for each show. Though most wrestlers from Mexico, like me, were accustomed to the Icon-like pedestal that the country held them to, it was still a bit intimidating when confronted with American reporters who had a “higher than thou” attitude and were used to a very physical approach of getting an interview. Though, for a man like me, who had his own tribulations to trouble myself with, this gauntlet represented an entirely new monster and irritated me to the breaking point. Brack did his best to clear a path for me, fighting off the endless hoard of reporters with each step he took; trying to smile his way through slight slaps and kicks! All it took was an accidental nudge in the ribs for me to hit the ground like a wet sack of potatoes, and for Brack’s usually soft baby blues to turn to an icy cold gaze as he snapped his head back. “ENOUGH!!” The sound of his voice surprised even me. Jaws hit the ground as Brack parted the flock of reporters like the red sea and plodded his way to me. Like a mother to his cub, I could almost hear his snorts of breath form into growls as he helped me back to my feet, daring someone to look at me cross-eyed. Brack could see the lack of patience in me and was unwilling to let me lash out. Conway opened the passenger door as I gingerly slid into my seat with a sneer on my face and glaring a hole through the reporters, sliding a pair of Oakley’s over my worn eyes. A sigh escaped me as I tossed some golden locks from my face threw a fist into the dash. “I don’t know how much longer I can do this, Brack.” I whispered with the stench of failure erecting from my voice. “How much more do I have to do to prove to these people who I am and what I stand for?” Brack keyed the ignition, and wiped a thin layer of sweat from his brow as his worried glance passed from my bandaged midsection to my hardened face. He didn’t see the torn luchador he had taken in so many years ago; he saw a man with blind ambition and a loathsome gaze in his eyes. “Prove to them?” My heated manager managed to reply as pulled the vehicle into drive and adjusted himself in the rearview mirror. ?” or prove to yourself?” What an ignorant thing to say. Of course I knew who I was? Right? “Just get me to the airport, Brack. I want to go home.” “I don’t suppose we will be visiting your family out there will we” How long has it been? nine, ten years?” “Just drive, Brack” CHAPTER TWO: Insomniac’s Candyland Six hours later? Mexico City, Mexico. Home; it was always weird to arrive in a country that I had roots stemming back to an Aztecan era, and still feel like an alien. There was no adoring crowd waiting to greet my coming, no camera crew wanting to catch a glimpse of the Fallen Luchador making his presence into the country that has done all it could to forget him, and no family members waiting to hand out the proverbial pat on the back for a successful night; Just that sickening desperation that twisted my stomach inside out! At times the city, hanging over my shoulders, provided the necessary motivation for me to strive to remove myself from the shadow that my history here had represented. Returning home used to be what reminded me to put all that much more into my goals. But, this time, I couldn’t help but sneer back at my past. I was tired of running from it, tired of the nightmares, and tired of the unattainable goals that I forced myself into. ? And for what? Who was I fighting for if not for myself? Brack found a quaint little motel in a town on the outskirts of Mexico City and took a room near the office as I took the one closest to the icemaker; no doubt I was going to be needing it. I hit my motel room like a rock. Yet, after spending a half an hour laying on the couch, staring into space, I couldn’t? I didn’t want to fall asleep. After squandering most of the night and morning in front of television, among a plethora of my past matches leading up to the current position of my career with a glass of vodka in my hand, I lunged into the insomniac’s candy land; The lovely randomness that is infomercials! I spent the time, in my mind, looking over the events of the past two months in search of this grand and noble reason that I thought I was worthy of stepping into the ring again. I just wanted my people to believe in me. Just want the world to believe I was better than the inexperienced Luchador who snapped his brother’s neck in half. The infomercials were presenting nothing of interest and I wasn’t one of the impulse shoppers that has kept these shows in business. I was simply annoyed at the speaker’s fake enthusiasm and the hideous jewelry they tried to pass off as a steal for the absurd prices they asked. But, it wasn’t the speakers I needed to direct my attention to. “I am La Sombra.? I said in a desperate attempt to convince myself, taking a shot of liquid courage in the form of a bottle of Vodka. I had spent so long running from my past, that I had lost sight of who I was. I hated everything that I had been raised to believe, everything I had become, and everything I felt I was forced to be. So much? that, years ago, I had dyed my hair blond and dubbed the mask of La Sombra. Anything I could do convince myself that the man staring back at me in the mirror wasn’t Julio De Hoyas, wasn’t a disgrace, and wasn’t a scorn on Lucha Libre. “I am” La Sombra!” I repeated with conviction. But, I still refused to accept it. I wanted to blame my brother for refusing to get back up, I wanted to blame my father for the years of Nazi-like training under the iron clad belief that Lucha Libre was the end all, and I wanted to blame my family? I wanted to blame the whole of Mexico for raising Lucha Libre to the icon-like pedestal that it sat! But, the hard facts pointed at only one man. ~?There’s more to life than wrestling??~ Ciel’s words echoed through my head as I took another shot, doing the best I could to make whispers of the words bounding through my head. He couldn’t understand why it was so important to me, couldn’t understand that wrestling was who I was and who I was raised to be; It was just another paycheck to him? just another way to pay the bills. He, as well as a large chunk of the Ascension members would never understand that this was more than a job to me. It was a history, a tradition, my Olympics. Half of Ascension didn’t believe in what they were fighting for, didn’t believe in anything more than their united hate for the people standing on the other side of the fence. It wasn’t about the whole. It was about their own personal agendas. More than anything I wanting GCW to succeed, and I wanted to be part of the reason it did! Knight understood. But he was from Mexico so perhaps it was the United States that had turned this sport into a joke. ? or perhaps I was just deluded? ~?You've got to fumble through the dark before you can see the light Julio...just don't fumble for too long, or you might never see it.?~ Dawn McFarlane’s words pierced my head as I struggled to make sense of it all. Too many thoughts! I wanted to sleep. But, I knew all too well what happened every time I closed my eyes. There was no peace within my dreams; just a broken past. And the whispers of a curse. So damn tired? I was tired of running, tired of feeling the past’s icy cold fingertips on my shoulder, and tired of letting it control me. It was time to face my demons.
