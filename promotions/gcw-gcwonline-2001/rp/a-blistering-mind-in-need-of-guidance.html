A Blistering Mind In Need Of Guidance

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: January 5, 1993 "True Judgement can only be attained by the Lord"-Zacharia Taylor Walking without any certain direction, the boy walked confidently down the street, cross in hand, head held high. Every time the wind blew or the trees would sway, the image of his room that would soon catch ablaze would bombard his mind. A garbage can fell over off the curb, and the sound of it bouncing from curb to asphalt made him think of his fire enraged father who tried to rescue him. He closed his eyes and pushed on, trying not to look at the homes on each side of the street, still and unharmed by fire and loss. The cross felt so heavy in his hand. Why was he alive and his family dead? Why did he survive the fall, not being able to reunite with his parents? Why did he find the cross? He had so many questions, but had no answers. There was a sudden understanding of purpose when he pulled the cross into his flesh, burning and blistering with every long second of religious intent. The purpose drove him away from the ruins of his home, not letting him look back, and led him down several streets. A car drove past him, nearly hitting his hand with the shiny chrome mirror. He looked down at his hand and stopped, staring at the burns. This is the first time the spell was broken of looking ahead and going wherever his feet led him. His hand was still bright red with white blisters in the shape of a cross. He stood there pushing the cross over the blisters, as if the burn was a grotesque shadow of the former. He was dumbfounded. Zacharia had no idea that his hand was so badly burned, and as to how was even more of a mystery. Had the cross burned him when he picked it up from the rubble of the house? Was the cross in the fire and the cool gold surface be only a disguise for its heat? Ultimately, where did the cross come from? More puzzling questions started to hurt his head. The reality was starting to set in. He was lost, mentally and physically, and in pain on all levels. His hand now started to swelter in the sunlight, making him wrap up his hand in his shirt. He looked around and found himself in the middle of the street, but why would he walk in the street if the sidewalk was such a safer and smarter place to be. He had almost been hit by a car, yet that was the last of his worries. He walked over to the nearest tree to get out of the sunlight. As he started to cradle his hand as to nurse his wounds, he noticed that he was still holding the golden cross in the other hand. He started to make a fist around the cross and started to feel anger, but that was immediately overcome by sadness and disappointment. He wanted to throw the cross, but it is all he has left to remember his house, family, life. He wanted to believe that it was his father’s, but all he could think about was the flames on his back and then the falling down the stairs. There was no happiness of his past life, it went up in flames with the house. Zacharia walked to the corner of the intersection ahead, tears profusely pouring from his eyes. He felt like he didn’t deserve to live anymore, that his life was taken from him. His eyes were no longer brown, they were black. There was no more love, it was all sorrow and hate. Was there a God that would do this to his family? His father always talked about their faith and devotion to God, how he was loving and forgiving, but vengeful on those that break his laws. This he understood to be in reference to all those on the streets or the news that you see who are always angry and violent. His father pointed out something on the news about someone killing ten people in a mall, but then was compelled to kill himself after. His father said that God make him put the gun to his own head before he pulled the trigger. Him killing himself would send him to hell, not heaven, and nobody would condemn themselves to hell. If God is vengeful and kills those who betray the laws he created, did that mean that his parents were evil? Why is it that there are so many innocent people that die every day for no reason? There are now so many questions and there are no answers. Furthermore, there aren’t people to get answers from. His father answered everything for him, and if his father didn’t know the answer, he would ask his mother. It is hard to believe that his teachers, his parents are gone. A simple fire, fed by the ever merciless wind carried death across the whole block, killing all the misfortunate on its warpath. Making his way to the end of the street he came up to the quiet intersection and looked both ways as if he was going to cross, but couldn’t. He took one step off of the curb and onto the street but was frozen in his step. He couldn’t muster the strength to take another step. Everything hit him all at once. He is alone, and has too many questions for only an eight-year-old to be left with. He looked to the heavens, only to see the weather match his indecision and fear; it was getting very dark and cloudy. Tears walloped his eyes, swelling his eyes to be in dimensions that restrict 20/20 vision. The tears were enough, but the kamikaze raindrops triggered the breakdown. He reached for his eyes but his knees went weak and started to shake. He started to kneel down on the one knee but the other succumbed to the pressure and fell forward. He smacked his knee against the asphalt so violently it ripped open his jeans and spilt blood onto the untainted rocked ground. The cross fell from his hands and bounced onto the ground, landing in a small pothole which was now filling with the rain water. Even though he had been sobbing for a couple minutes before the breakdown, he had just started to heave and lose control of his extremities. His hands were doing things that he had no control over; reaching for his face, than touching his bleeding knee, than up to his face once more, but than back towards the ground. The opaque clouds were darkening the sky, but now it was completely covering the sun. The day was as dark as knight, as if there was a full lunar eclipse in an overcast sky. The smoke from the many blocks away was being blown in the other direction, the whole city was now cloaked in darkness. If it wasn’t for the loud crack of thunder, this moment of entrancement in one’s own emotion would never have ended. The sudden burst of noise made Zacharia shift in position and the pain jolted from his knee all the way through to his head. His eyes started to strain as they focused on the ground ahead of him. As the more in focus his hands got, the more pain erupted behind his eyes. He wiped all his senses clean, trying to get rid of the inconvenient byproducts of involuntary crying. With the help of the natural shower nature tends to give the Earth from time to time, he was as clean as he was when he left his house. He started to shake his head to clear his sinuses when ne noticed the cross in the tiny hole. The logistics of the intersection were not put to the test and altered like they should have been by the engineers of the city. The blood that claimed new residence outside of his body sifted through the rocks in the asphalt and into the pothole where the cross now lay. The way that it was propped up against the outer edges of the hole, it was right side up as if it were placed their in some kind of art gallery, completely surrounded in blood. When the next bolt of the fury of God struck the Earth, the cross was illuminated clear as it had been in his hand ten minutes prior. The cross spiked anger and remorse once again inside. He reached down into the pothole, covering his hand in his own cocktail of nature’s finest and blood from his veins. The cross was now dripping a concoction of holy water from the blessed cross that now trembles in his hand. The water was soothing to his burnt and blistered hand. Whether it was from the cold rain or the fact that the blessed water ran over his hand continuously. The anger overcame the sorrow, making him clench the cross with all his might and look to the heavens. Squinting and twitching at the every touch of rain upon his eyes, he tried to make a stand against Mother Nature and look through the darkened sky into the Almighty’s eyes. He reached towards the heavens and started to beat his fist against the rain as if he were attacking an opponent traveling through each droplet. He opened his mouth but nothing came out at the will of his throat. The crying made it very difficult to open up and express through words. He cleared his throat and tried again. “What is it that you want from me” Was it not enough to take my family? He said You only take the evil and bad, so what did they do to deserve this? Kill me my Lord or give me direction! I didn’t deserve this. I didn’t...deserve...” The sorrow overcame the anger once more, submitting him to tears once again, but did not leave his body unfunctional. He took both his fists and took them from the suspension in air to the hard asphalt. Pounding his fists against the ground, cross in hand, felt so much better than yelling to a being that tends not to answer when called upon or challenged. The last time he pounded his fists, the Earth below him split, he went momentarily blind, and the crashing of thunder resonated throughout the world. Out of shock from the cracked asphalt around him, or merely the fact that lighting struck the telephone pole right beside him, he sat there on his knees as frozen as a statue. When the lightning struck the pole, the transformer blew up and knocked out all the power in the surrounding houses on the block. For all he knew, the whole city was out. The next flash across the sky illuminated the street and revealed a large crack that went through the intersection and down the street to the left. Was it that God works in mysterious ways, or was this only another set up for failure or death? Slowly, he rose from his domain of rock and crimson and started to hobble across the street following the broken ground. His knee was still aching from the fall, but it did not restrict his determination to find this mysterious end to an aimless travel across town. Time has passed since midnight prior, but time was endless and impatient, it would soon be night once again, and he was getting tired and hungry for shelter. Turning the corner led Zacharia into a neighborhood that was very unfamiliar to him, looking like a more suburban neighborhood than what he was used to. Commercial buildings and offices were on the one side of him, and business storefronts were on the other. In the distance was a black rail fence which was signified by a white sign that said “Park”. He checked upon the crack every moment he could when the lightning cracked the clouds and allowed for more light to be emitted. When he crossed the street he turned back to where he stood when the phenomenon started, having doubts whether he should continue. As he turned and looked at the telephone pole, it split in two and fell to where he once kneeled. He was not surprised by what just happened after the day that he has had, so he turned back around, looked for the crack, and continued on his journey. When he crossed to the closest building, he looked down the alley to see what would await him. When he stepped into the alley, he heard something on the roof, but because of how dark it was, nothing could be seen. When he reached the end, he looked to the right and there was a tall church with a high bell tower. And steeple. Each window was illuminated by what seemed to be candlelight. When the lightning cracked again he saw a bird fly overhead to the church and landed on a landing above the front doors. He looked towards the sky once more as thunder pounded away at his ear drums. He took this as a “yes”. He walked forward confidently and slightly relieved for he had finally found a place to get shelter. It was not ironic that all those “signs” led him towards a church. The storm seemed to have passed to a more distant realm as he reached the steps of the church. The doors were as large as two men and each knocker was in the shape of an eagle’s head. He reached up and slammed down the large metal ring and awaited judgement from behind the doors.
