Lather, rinse, recondition

6 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/6/05 I told him to talk to me like he'd talk to the police, because I wanted to hear a story and I wanted it to be sober. No confused back talking, I wanted him to think it out beforehand--to at least know what it was about if not every detail. Fuck the details, I say--it's whatever makes the story better that really matters. So I smoked a blunt and waited, my stomach starting to twist in a bad mix of jet lag and cheap booze. Nicotine dropped into my system like a two-ton rock, and I wanted the story more than ever. I needed to lean back and relax and maybe figure out what sort of life I'd helped destroy. General good guy stuff. He hadn't known what it should be about first, so I told him to entertain me. Just take a wild fucking shot and see if he hit anything that made me think it mattered. It was all part of the greater plan to fix Trey's brain. He understood, I think, or he played along. That's what matters. So, he thought and pondered and he considered and he wondered and he eyed my smoking like a bastard so I threatened to beat him to death with his own shoe. Communication broke down again after that, letting me get back to my waiting and him to his thinking. He asked me a question, 'How old of a story you want?' 'I don't wanna be involved with it. Too many beatings down that avenue.' He laughed and tried to play the insult down. The spark of creativity had finally struck him, it seemed, and I interrupted to prepare him a glass of water and ask--one more time--if he was sure he wanted me to try and fix whatever Casey'd done. He took a swig and committed himself to it again, so I let him start with his story. It's impossible for me to know how much of it was true, and it's also impossible for me to know how much of it are lies that I exaggerate from not remembering what all he told me, but I like to think I get a better story out of it this way. So, without further bullshitting, here goes. Trey left the country for the first time when he was 14. His dad still lived with him and the rest of his immediate family then, before he got laid off from an executive position in some law firm in the area. It was a few years until things were going to go bad for him, so Trey wasn't of any mind other than enjoying the trip. They'd ridden for four hours from Detroit to Niagara Falls, a reluctant Trey and his younger brother Calvin playing with GI Joes in the backseat of the minivan to pass the time while their dad got miserably lost. His youngest sibling, Andrea, had been young enough to stay in a safety seat the whole time, save for when their mom was feeding her. So they got lost and, of course, found their way back onto the main road and eventually even headed in the right direction. Four hours turned to eight and the sun died out to allow the pinprick sky control. The van was steaming and lurching, pistons missing every few firings for a lack of gas, when they pulled into a gas station on the outskirts of the city. It gave Trey the time to piss and, feeling some inexplicable sense of freedom, buy his first condom from one of the vending machines always placed right above the pisser. He remembered it well, glow-in-the-dark and too thin for him to think it could actually protect anyone from anything. After examining it in the flickering fluorescent aura that left him feeling as dirty as the bathroom for awhile, there came a knock at the door and the whining of his little brother, so he shoved the holy experience in his wallet and made his way out. As he grabbed a soda, he was careful to give a smirk to a blonde in the gas station to let her know that while he'd just bought a condom, he didn't care if she knew about it or not--and he wanted her to know that. And yeah, that's an editorial comment. Trey, in talking about the girl, wandered off far enough that I was sure she'd play a later part in his story. I couldn't understand that he'd allow so much fond description to a person whose importance appeared to be 'stand-in in background of Jordan's friend's story,' and I was quite disappointed when he reached the end and I realized that it'd just been him hearkening back to a decade of masturbatory imagery when he spoke of her body as though it were an old friend. Knowing this now, I'll skip his over-drawn portrait and move ahead to the next part of his tale that seemed important to me. It was the next thing he got to that wasn't awkward as hell, for that matter. When they'd gotten to the hotel rooms, Trey, having realized that he and Calvin had a room to themselves, set to ordering pizza and creating a tiny chaos in the room. It wasn't rock star destruction, it was that generally sneaky kind of distraction that gets a grin instead of a pat on the back. They ripped drawers from the cabinets and flipped them over to scrawl out the best profanities anyone knows at that age on the bottoms, before putting them back and writing hidden message on the toilet paper rolls and carefully wrapping the seal of safety back around them. I found Shaun of the Dead on as I idly flipped through the channels, yet Trey still had to ask what it was that had taken my attention. 'Drowning out the filler in your tale.' 'Shit, man; it was your idea. You don't gotta flip like that.' 'Can't blame me for wanting a conflict.' That got him thinking again, quiet and introspective with his chin tucked tight against his chest and a little dribble of water running down from his lip. His forehead alternated between drawing itself up into tight furrows and going slack as his jaw dropped down--bad confusion. I tossed a throw pillow from the couch to Trey's chest, and he snapped to and brought himself back up by taking another drink of the water before he continued. The trance had clearly allowed him to figure out what was worth sharing, and he started it with the scenery. Some big indoor pool with low-lighting and fake marble inlaid to the walls had had those wavy honeycombs of turquoise light playing up on the ceiling and Trey simultaneously. He'd been fucking around in the pool for hours to enjoy the wealth that he'd found in his family's idea, which was taking the evening to see the city, when this girl came in; or floated in if I were to actually tell Trey's story straight from the one I'd heard. That'd be the easy way out, and it'd be a lie. I know him well enough to know that he'd downplay every little detail of the girl with words like 'sublime,' and 'angelic.' I could tell that the water he was drinking was very good, as he focused on the way the lights played off of her body more than he focused on her body itself. I still take it to be the ultimate dream for a 14 year old with a condom in his wall, and that's a 14 year old girl that's already sprouted tits and ass without suffering the torments of being Queen of the Lardites. 'Was she cute?' He didn't answer with a yes or no, instead taking the blank space to the side to give me an essay form answer that highlighted a thousand little details that he was making up on the spot. Her skin wasn't the hazel-gold he painted it as, and her eyes weren't the dream-filled blue saucers he'd wanted me to think they were, but he had his heart in inventing all those little nuances so I let him slide and just accepted that she was apparently 'cute.' If not 'angelic,' I mean. After his spiel he turned to me and snickered, as if he were letting me in on a secret. It signaled the end of the story, and left me to try to fill in the blanks that'd actually give it a resolution other than implied sex. And, well, a conflict to resolve. I was relieved as much as I was disappointed, having learned the most valuable thing I could've hoped to gleam from the encounter. Trey's life was such a bore before I showed up that I can't feel bad about fucking around with his brain. After his laugh he slipped back off into the trance that the mixture of acid and psilocybin had drawn out on him after a few days of selective conditioning a year back. Full access, blank slate, Tabula Rasa. Hell, that means blank slate, so I'm just a jackass. The point is, he was open to suggestions and easier to program than a drunk sorority chi--wait. Nothing's easier to program than a drunk sorority chick. He was still stoned with the might of Zeus, so I began to pick apart and figure out how to make this bastard fool-proof. There was only one sure way, of course. 'CASEY BAD' The rolled newspaper slapped across his face and shocked him enough that he tumbled onto the floor and became victim of another newspaper to the back of the head. 'DON'T LISTEN TO CASEY!' Another thwack, and he repeated to me: 'Casey bad, don't listen to Casey.' Now, 'JORDAN GOOD!' 'Jordan good.' I didn't strike again, as passing the blunt makes the idea of Jordan much happier than the now newspaper-associated Casey. 'Do you remember 'Croatoa'?' He nodded and I continued. 'There is no more 'Croatoa,' and there is no more 'Axel Foley.' From now one, when you go all bug-eyed psychopath, it's because I, and only I, say the word 'synergy,' and when you turn off it's because I, and only I, say the phrase 'twat hat.'' He nodded again and took another deep hit from the blunt. I helped him up and collapsed on the couch to wait out his trip and see if it'd worked as well this time around. Now, present tense--I think it may have.
