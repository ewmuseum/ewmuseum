Cheating at Solitaire

14 March 2012
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/14/12 I don’t know where to begin. Have I ever, really? Don’t answer that. I’ve been doing this for far too long, or maybe it’s just the shots to the head that are making my memory a little funny. Aside from that, I do not feel like that man that deserves this. Any of it. I’ve set to stand on my own and be the man I thought I should be. A stronger man than my father. The type of man that would not fall in line behind a woman like my mother. The man that would not be cuckolded by the bitch that said she loved me. But what did that leave? It feels more than a little hollow to define myself as not this, or not that. What would I point to as the man you see every World Wide, however increasing infrequent those are getting? Grady would probably open his mouth first. “What are you talking about, retirement!?” It’s fair to say the news did not sit well with him. The nest egg going bust and all. Any ‘victims’ of his scams would hardly believe the emotion I could draw from his face with just a few simple words. “We just signed that Trojan deal!” I sit at the other side of the table, saying nothing. I’m content to nod along, watching his reactions all the way. The passersby seem to enjoy the show, too. “What is it? Health concerns? You? You’ve injured more people in the ring than Bryan Mayhem has bragged about killing.” That was true. I had a list of thirteen men. Thirteen miserable men who had the miserable fortune to run in me. Since meeting with me, they had been laid up in hospitals, released into the tender care of loved ones and promptly out of the federation. It’s not something I take particular pride in, but I’ll let it speak for itself. “Is it Hardon? I mean Palmer? Listen, I know he’s been showing up drunk to shows,” he continued. He stopped when I shook my head. Twice. I’m sure Grady couldn’t tell if it was just condemning the behaviour, or he’d missed my Battleship again. A5, miss. Oh yeah, Hardon’s act isn’t so much an act. I’ve know the kid since high school, and about the only thing that has changed in him is that he doesn’t have to used fake ID’s to get his booze. Not that it stops him from fucking around and using fakes anyways. “Did,” he let the word linger, second guessing saying, “did Goldstein walk in on you in the shower?” “No. God, no. Grady, where the fuck is your mind?” He finally got me to talk again. Probably not the tone he wanted to hear, but I’ve been in this man’s company too long. He’s like a honeybadger, but it’s a buck he’s chasing. Always the next dollar. “I... I heard he has been off doing those direct-to-DVD films with Murrr Jr. That will mess with a man, change him. I caught him not staring at Sally Ford’s breasts last card.” Just more of shaking my head. Maybe a few sips of the Diet Pepsi to wash the taste out of my mouth. And while I am on it, why don’t Pizza Shops sell beer? I mean, fuck. “Okay, so if it isn’t health, and it isn’t Palmer and you’ve kept yourself away from Barry Goldstein,” gasp, “what? Why do you want to give up the E-list celebrity status? The modest paycheques? The adoration of dozens of stupid women and young boys?” He always did have a funny way of pitching things. “Do I need a reason?” Okay, I shouldn’t have asked that one. Grady’s damn near ready to have a heart attack. “I just don’t. I don’t know. It just doesn’t do it for me.” “Oh, whah! Poor baby. My job isn’t fun. How many people do you think actually enjoy the shit work they do? No one, that’s who! They all just accept it, knuckle under and get blitzed the minute they are off the clock. Why can’t you just be like everyone else?” “Because we aren’t everyone else, Grady. You are a career scam artist. I’m a psychopath liable to break any day now.” “Again, and I hate to disagree with you here, but that is just like everyone else.” “Fine, you know what I mean.” I sink my teeth into another bite of St. Louis’ best. It’s gone cold, but frankly wasn’t all that good warm. I’ll leave it for the flies to choke on. “Give me Dangerous Games? Just give me that. We can talk about this again when you are not on the rag, and maybe you’ll be headlining NC-17. That’s a payday neither of us could give up!” “Sure. Dangerous Games,” I say, all the while thankful that the table we sit at is hiding the obvious chubby Grady is getting talking paydays. --- I know my time is coming. Everyone’s is. Now it was all a question about getting out ahead of it, or going down with the ship. I know this should be a more meaningful decision, but I am ready to flip a coin and let fate make the call. Heads. Tails.
