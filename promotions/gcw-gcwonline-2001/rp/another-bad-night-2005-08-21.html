Another bad night.

21 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 21 Staring up at the sky, I let the afternoon sun wash over my face. There’s a cool breeze whipping through my hair, and I find myself smiling despite everything else. The city itself is vivid and alive, human chatterboxes buzzing all around me. Pinstriped business suits and cell phones are everywhere, no doubt signifying that this is a city of communication - albeit poor or otherwise. The City of Angels, inappropriately named, as everyone knows. I’m shielded by my dark shades as my face stays solid but my eyes dance wildly. I’m surveying the land, marking the exact spot where the shit will hit the fan. Normally on the day of a job, my mood is somber and grave. Today, however, I’m morbidly chipper. Call me Rei. Basking in happiness while the hour is at hand ... But not for me. I’m the messenger. ~Scene Cut~ I spill into the Argyle Hotel and some of the cool breeze clings to my coat-tails. I observe several people in the lobby and several more leaving, out for another action filled day in the land of the living. I remove my hat, slipping it underneath my fingers that are clinging unbelievably tightly to an ashy gray briefcase as if it were holding all the skeletons in my closet. Not all. Just one really big one. I smile as I approach the counter and slip my sunglasses in my coat pocket. 'Hello, sir, how are you on this fine and sunny afternoon?' His tone is shallow and fake. The implication of a man who has no respect for his job. I could cast an even bigger thunderstorm on his parade, but I’ve got more pressing matters to attend to. 'As fine as one could be, I suppose. Reservation for Charles Williamson?' I look away as the clerk begins scanning the computer system. I remember the phone call earlier, how it awoke me from a peaceful sleep. My serenity shattered. I should be used to it, by now. The job is becoming routine. Phone call. Directions. Half payment up front, the rest when I’m done. It’s not the details that are mind-numbing; it’s the awful timing on the part of those who beckon. I can’t really complain, but I will anyway. 'I’ll be right back, Mr. Williamson.' I watch him walk somewhere in the back and stand simply waiting. My gaze eventually falls to the briefcase, and I sit it beside me on the floor. Jeanette’s heavier than I thought, especially in smaller pieces. I’m nervous about sitting the briefcase on the floor, but the weight was beginning to stoop my shoulders. I notice someone coming behind me and slide the briefcase directly in front of me. 'Here you are, Mr. Williamson.' A green keycard with 'Argyle' printed in fancy cursive now rests in my palm. 'Take the elevator up to the twelfth floor and yours is the last one down the hallway to your left.' Good. Last time, they mixed up rooms somehow and the result was less than satisfying. 'Have a pleasant stay. Also, don’t forget about the restaurant and cocktail bar if you get hungry.' I make a note of that and it is quickly an afterthought. I wave and pick up the briefcase, making my way to the nearest elevator. An attractive blond and an elder man occupy the available one, so I put on my gentle smile and slide inside. I find myself wondering what they would think if I opened the briefcase. ~Scene Cut~ Down the hallway, to the left. I pass several housekeepers, cleaning carts and dirty towels along the way. One in particular smells of alcohol and urine, almost turning my stomach. Once I reach the end, I notice the door slightly ajar. I place the briefcase carefully and quietly on the ground, backing away from the doorway and pushing the door open slowly. Seeing no one, I step inside. The room is cold and it takes a moment for me to adjust to the drastic temperature change. The bed is still made nice and neat. Trash can is empty. Window is shut and the blinds are closed. 'Excuse m-' I whip my body around so fast that I nearly sling myself to the floor. A pretty little housekeeper named 'Anne' jumps in surprise. I force a laugh as she smiles. 'Forgive me, sir. I didn’t know anyone was checked into this room, yet. I was just tidying up.' Her smile is so cute it makes me sick. 'Perfectly alright. The fault is mine, anyway; I should’ve knocked.' We shake hands and I escort her out of the room and out of my life. I hang a 'Do Not Disturb' sign on the outside handle and bring my briefcase in before I close the door and lock it, tossing my hat on the bed. I entertain the thought of an evening with 'Anne', but my business head takes control and the smile fades. My date is with Jeanette tonight. I am Rei. All work and no play. ~Scene Cut~ I take the longest drag I’ve ever taken from a cigarette and grin. Jeanette. My new baby. A Tango-51 Winchester Supreme .308 Caliber Sniper Rifle, with a McMillan Fiberglass stock and a suppressor for noise reduction. It weighs only 10.8 pounds and measures at 112.5 centimeters. Black and gray in color, she’s a beauty words can’t really describe. It takes me a minute to assemble and 35 seconds to take her down. I’ve got it timed to perfection. No mistakes in this line of work. I can take the shot and be down in the lobby in a minute and a half. Two minutes gets me in my car and ten more seconds gets me on the road in traffic. Or at least, that’s the schematics. There’s no room for error, as I said. If I make a mistake, I might as well jump from the window. Anyway, the protagonist of this skit. I grab the manila envelope from the briefcase and empty it on the card table beside me, spilling out various photos and notes containing information. I pick up a photo and stare it, letting the acrid smoke from my cigarette coil around my face, framing it. Walter Garrison is his name. A powerful man by most standards, he has dealings in oil and petroleum among other things. Not really important. Even though I normally like to have as much information as I can about a hit, this one’s line of work is about as interesting as making love to a brick wall. I sigh and glance at my watch, stubbing my cigarette out in an old dingy ashtray. In five minutes, he gets a kiss from Jeanette. ~Scene Cut~ Right on time. I watch him step from his limousine, gray hat resting atop his head. He spent a lot of time in the sun like me, I note by his darkened hands, and continue to watch as several bell-boys from the hotel go to his aid. Through the scope, I also notice the exchange of several hundred dollar bills amongst Walter and the bell-boys. A man who knows how to take care of his people. I admire that. I exhale strongly, somewhat agitated by the fact that his face is hidden by his angle and the hat. Glancing at my watch again, I estimate that I have less than a minute to make the kill before he disappears inside the hotel. I focus. Pedestrians are everywhere. Lucky for me, Jeanette knows how to whisper. Lady luck. Time - less than 30 seconds, now. I’m starting to perspire, beads of it running into my eyes, stinging them. I wipe it away and take post, again. He’s still not at the right angle. In order to make this shot, I need to see his face. Special demands want it directly through his only good eye - the left one. 20 seconds, now. I remind myself that there’s no pressure, and continue to wait. No mistakes. 12 seconds. I wipe my eyes again. Finally he turns, and I line up the shot. ... My eyes grow wide and I back away from the rifle, horrified. Less than 10 seconds now. I force myself back to the scope and look again, wanting to throw up. Something is terribly wrong. My watch beeps insanely as I continue to stare at Walter Garrison, only it’s not really Walter Garrison. It’s myself. Looking right back at me. Mind trip. ~End ~ I inhale violently and throw myself in a sitting position. I struggle with the covers before kicking them aside, taking a moment to adjust to the darkness all around me. My back is soaked with sweat, as is the bedspread beneath me. The pillows are in the corner, no doubt thrown there during ? What? A dream? A nightmare. Everything felt real. The gun. Walter Garrison. Anne. It was only a dream. Eerie mind trip played out by the twisted psyche of Rei Tiu. I’m going to make sure to keep that one out of the GCW mental evaluation. My dark blue boxers are the only article of clothing on my body. I can’t dress for bed. Shirts are a no go. Pants would simply end up tangled in the sheets. I thrash in my sleep, which is also why I’m still single. Ha. I wish that was the only reason. The clock on the bed stand beside me is beeping, I notice. It’s 3:34 A.M., and of course, I’m wide awake. Not surprising. I’m a candidate for insomniac of the year. Call me Rei. Watching the Trinity Broadcast Network to fall asleep. I swing my legs over the side of the bed and press my fingers to my temples. Headaches are almost a daily part of my life. Wake up with a headache. Shower with a headache. Take eight-hundred millogram Ibuprofen, headache gone. When I get comfortable, headache often shows its ugly visage, again. Anyway, I’m still thinking about the dream. When dreams start dancing around the thin line of what you know is real and what isn’t, that’s when it becomes scary. I’ve gone to therapy for these type dreams and for what seems to be an overactive imagination and the conclusion was unsatisfying. Psychiatrist: You’re very creative. An artist, Mr. Tiu. Nothing wrong with having an active imagination - Could prove to be quite lucrative. Yeah, right. Maybe to someone who didn’t get their jollies off by beating up sweaty men who are bigger than they are. I’d rather have a big hairy man grab me by my ass and play rag doll. This lucrative imagination of mine is good for nothing more than sleepless nights and stained bed sheets. The room is spinning as I stand to my feet. Much like a room in a carnival funhouse, I can hear laughing clowns and beeping noises. I credit that to the movies I watch. Killer Klowns from Outer space was the last thing on. B grade films at its worst. I listen to the hum of the air conditioner I as walk down the hallway. The steady drip of the water pipes. The pleasant bubbling sound of the fish tank in the living room as I pass by. I glance at my glow-in-the-dark poster of Death beckoning to me and smile. Garage sales can turn up some interesting things. I am Rei. Bargain shopper extraordinaire. My computer’s on, frozen on a gray background with a red 'floating' Celtic rose as the centerpiece. It’s amateur digital art, but it’s alluring. In the kitchen, my answering machine exclaims through a constant series of blinking red lights that I have messages. I already know from who. [Enter photograph] Emerald green eyes and raven black hair that much resembles my own - part of it, anyway. Rebecca Hendley is her name, and I've been friends with her since I've lived in California - over six years. She’s the only person who can see right through me ... And the only girl I know who ends a message with 'Peace.' [/Exit photograph] An introduction, even for me it seems. She’s gorgeous, I think to myself. I’ve been avoiding her phone calls. She’s probably pissed by now and I know why. I break promises more often than I break a sweat. Something I’m proud of? Of course not. We live entirely different lifestyles, Rebecca and I. She falls off her horse and is on to something else within a flutter of her beautiful lashes. I’ve fallen from grace so many times that I need an extra hand or foot to hope to keep count. It’s bitter irony that wrestling is my life and often my undoing. Rebecca calls me an insane, poetic genius - that I’m too smart for my own good and that I’m ruling in my own personal hell. She’s right. About my personal hell, at least. It was taking a turn for the worst, and I was starting to conquer hers as well. Ugh. A nasty situation I care not to think about right now. I hesitantly press the play button and wait for my inevitable mental anguish. BEEP Rei, man, how’s it going? I haven’t talked to you since the other day when you told me to keep my eyes glued to my television ... Jacob. A friend’s friend. I trust him more than I trust myself. I called him to tell him the news of my GCW ordeal. Unlike Rebecca, he’d be thrilled. They’re exact opposites in almost every single way. That’s awesome. It’s a big organization, too. I’m happy for you and I’m looking forward to seeing you again. We’ll get everyone together and celebrate. I hate parties. I know you hate parties, but you should know that we don’t care. I smile. Alright, I’m out. Talk to you later. And for Christ’s sake, get some sleep. BEEP End of messages. And I frown. Another mind game from Rebecca. Not calling because she’s a sadist and I’m suffering because I’m her masochist love subject. Call me Rei. Admitting I have a problem. Anyway, it’s back to what I consider stability in my life, now. Days filled with musings and ponderings for all to know. Nights filled with regrets and sorrows for all to know. Stepping back into a lifestyle that can make and break a man. I’ve always called it high-priced, organized violence. It’s exactly the same as when I first started a few years ago, except I’ve got a better understanding. The industry demands everything, and I give everything ? and a little more. My life in one sentence? Paid to humiliate, or be humiliated. Too simple for some people, yet too complex for others. It means nothing in the real world, which I’m content with. I prefer sulking in the backdrop. I am Rei. An overpaid bully influencing your kids. Enough of that. On with the show, they say. I glance out the window, at the night sky pressing firmly against the glass. I breathe against the panes and watch them fog. My mind runs back through some of the thoughts. Rebecca. GCW. Jacob. Jeanette. The dream. Shaking my head of the groggy cobwebs, I decide on a glass of iced water. I take it with me to the nearby dining table and slump languidly into the wooden chair. I raise my glass in salute of GCW and drink small sips, alleviating my thirst. I think about Annihilation, GCW’s scheduled super PPV of sorts, and the total anarchy that was the announcement of the Cruiserweight division’s return. I can’t even remember most of it, just that I’m not the only one whose ears liked the sound of it. But I can’t think about this now. I’m irritated that sleep is playing hide and go seek, again. I hate that game. I always lose. I should be sleeping hard. Near comatose. But dreaming. Instead, I’m sitting in my underwear sipping water like it’s wine. There’s probably a joke to be had at my expense, but I don’t feel like putting forth the effort. Call me Rei. Taking myself seriously is only a part-time job. I glance at the clock in the kitchen and groan as I realize only ten minutes have passed. I’m trying to waste time, hoping to make my lids drag. Instead, I’m prolonging it. 'I, Lucifer.' I say this aloud. The book on the table. An interesting read, I nod to myself. The cover is red and has a unique depiction above the title. Flip it one way, it’s a walking man. The other and it’s a silhouette of Satan. It's not an ambigram, as it's two seperate images. It's still interesting. But that’s it. Bath time. In a flash, with energy I didn’t know I had, I’m up and with the book, into the bathroom. I turn the faucet on completely hot and let the steam soak into my system. Immediate relaxation. I inhale slowly and exhale even more slowly. No more thoughts. No more anything. I close my eyes and welcome the calm obscurity. It won’t be long before insanity makes its nest.
