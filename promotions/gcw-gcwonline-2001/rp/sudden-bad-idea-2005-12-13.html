Sudden. Bad Idea.

13 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Dec 13, 2005 So, exactly how long you think someone can ride their life out on a single fuck-up without realizing their mistake? Like, not those little mistakes people make that just make them stupid people--like living a life of inside jokes, jumping from whim to whim with the abandon of a foster child--but when a good person stumbles into something that works for them without ever feeling right. Let's ask Trey. En media res, I'm sorry, so there's catching up to do--old friends that we are. We all know the players in this farce by now, and if not, saying that I'm still Jordan White won't mean much. I'm expecting you to have kept up though; none of this is going to matter one iota if you haven't. Not to imply it'll actually be an iota's worth if you're a faithful follower, it'll just matter. A little. Here goes. Remember Anarch? Lexicon's lifeblood until it was finally wretched from his greedy mitts and handed over to me. I'm more than used to the silver platters of sheer fortune at this point, so it might be a little more important to someone else. What mattered wasn't the company, but the control it afforded me. Christ, man, I could've been a king if I'd have wanted. I just didn't feel like it, already had my taste of the vainglorious (I own a city populated by a former cult, I saved either the President or his clone from Mexican terrorists, I caused a riot at an open-air event just by saying the right things) and was ready to move on to something a little better than the same old boring static of random thrill. Hell, I was ready for a life of drugs, sex, loud noise and escapism. Still am. Still aiming for it. So far, it's made me do something very unJordanlike in actually formulating a complex plan to help someone out. My values, ethics, morals are schewed but strong, and I'm not trying to pretend to be the good guy here. That'd take a lot more effort than I've got locked away right now--hospital visits always drain the life from my marrow and leave me huddled in a corner, begging for a quick bullet to fix things right up--so I'll just give the backstory and move on to the Here-and-Now. Trey made it three, maybe four years in this bad deal rut because some asshole had to prove to the world that fame, fortune, and success don't guarantee bliss, and even I was too stupid to catch on until a couple months back when it decided to become the root force of every conversation. Now, as good of a person as he really is, there's no way that he'd have been able to break his occupational malaise without finding a new job, and even that was almost impossible because of his slack. It was a task I had to pick up since I was the one that dragged him back into things. Just to make sure I don't come across as some scarred-gimmickys Bond villain, let's make it clear that the plan's worked and I'm just trying to take stock in if I was doing the Right thing or just off on my own sick bent. Lately, I've noticed a slight divergence between my views and whatever universal truths exist, so I've started trying to account for it. Back on track, my head's never been on quite right--always lopsided or tilted or twisted--so I went for the most dire plan I could figure out. Something absolute, unquestionable, and a deal where my intentions would show through in the end result, if just a little bit clouded. In retrospect I'm an evil genius of the highest caliber, a real king-hell son of a bitch, but I can manage. See, I know people; usually very bad people that engage in various nefarious schemes in their free time from their careers of nefarious scheming. In the years of traveling, it's impossible not to meet enough that you've got the right people for any job. Most'll never come up as useful outside of a place to crash or a few bucks to bum (haha, that's really me right there), but then there's those outside bets that end up paying off. As a bad man, doubtlessly, I knew just the lot that needed to be swindled in step one of the plan. I knew them through Sobias, this group of tough thugs of some indeterminate Euro-heritage that liked playing rough in spots no one else wanted. On an off bet, they were gamblers and proud of it, more than eager to welcome a high-roller (guy on television) like me into their fine establishment to wager a few dollars. Note: Read few dollars as major portions of Anarch's bank account. It took them a few weeks to notice my spree, turning in massive dividends on money put against a wrestler I happened to manage. Huh, what a shock. This is, of course, highly illegal and immoral in all circles. More so if it's the circle you're swindling money out of, and even more so if they're the low balling type prone to paranoia and too small time of an outfit to allow a little grace. Still playing the part of a real bloofthirsty operation, the logical step is to start gunning for someone to try and recoup the losses, as he has indeed drained much of your resources. And when Jordan White slips off the face of the earth, as he is prone to do, you go after the next best thing, the obvious partner in the operation. Poor Trey. Poor, stupid Trey. And if that's not how it happened, I'll swear I just hired someone to shoot him with that as a story if it ever came up. You know, the dreaded moment of Trey grasping my shoulder and locking eyes: 'J'accuse,' except italicized. Frightful, I'd stab him in the throat with whatever was nearby and disappear for eternity--if I didn't have that story/truth to give him. Have to account if someone asks a straight question; only lying when it's a really good one that just has to get said. Still, poor Trey. All the stupid calls he's made over the years, from even starting this dumb dumb dumb job to ending up as guilty as Patty Hearst. Stockholm. Cruel words, evil names. Bad comparisons. Fear. 'Just an excitable boy,' no time for justification or arguing or anything but explaining to that eternal chorus at the back of my head exactly how things got the way they did--because someone should know. Remember. Have the whole truth available in their head when they want it. So, Trey gets shot. It's not much: either just a warning or just a knee shot, depending on who you ask. Something to pass along to Jordan, since Trey's so likely to just pull a nine out and blast my ass any given time. Something to pass along to me. A bullet. Not heavy enough to make me obsess over mortality; hell, it's the way things were supposed to go. So just wake up, you son of a bitch. Twitch those eyelids. Quit pretending to be asleep, you've heard every fucking word of this--just like last time you didn't need to know things, or the time before. Of all the lackluster bullshit, man, this whole deal of you always knowing the utmost secrecies for no apparent reason is getting the most tired. Don't have to hide out. I know you've been listening. I know you. Son of a bitch. Twitch those eyelids. Look up. Play stupid. That a boy. 'Jordan?' 'Yeah.' 'Damn, still bumming around this sick place. What's up?' 'You're still on a hospital bed.' 'Shyeah, heh. What's new?' 'Not much, apparently. Still taking it like a fucking warrior.' 'Just life. Heal myself up an' find something to do.' 'Hah. Wrestling?' 'Bum knee, J. Shit, thought you'd've noticed by now.' Stifle the smirk, Jordan. 'Point. So, what's it like?' 'What?' 'Freedom.' 'Dunno how this shit's supposed to be freedom. Ass all hanging out the back of this gown and shit, damn. Ain't cool.' 'This is the most freedom you've had in years. Just think about it--you're out of responsibilities, of requirements, restraints.' 'Man, some of us ain't that up on unemployment.' 'Yeah, huh? Maybe should be, all that reconsidering you've been doing lately. Trapped in a contract though, weren't you?' 'Trapped in bein' rich as hell? I could dig it.' 'Doing what you hate, what frustrates you, what you can't ever quite pull off by yourself.' 'I gotta kick your ass when I get outta here.' 'Good luck. I'm a mite tougher than Eva or Borealis, and we know how those to tend to go with you.' 'Hah, still a badass.' 'Yeah?' 'S'just a bullet. Y'know I could tear arms off an' beat you to death with 'em.' 'Only if I told you to. You remember the word, don't you?' 'Jordan, fuck man. Chill out. You're an asshole, not usually this much of one. What's up?' 'Alright, want it solid?' 'Shyeah.' 'How would you take it if I told you that I'd orchestrated all the crazy shit you've gone through since, well, like a year or so now?' 'I'd think you were crazy.' 'Well, I won't tell you that.' 'Shit. Thanks anyway.' 'Mean that?' 'Maybe.' 'Good enough. I've gotta get out of here pretty soon.' 'Don't worry about those bitch made wannabes that shot me; if they'd meant something I'd be dead. Besides, no way they're making it through security.' 'Not out of this little ass here you've got, out of the big here. Out of the public eye, out of the country, out of civilization; whatever. Escape.' 'Ain't scared, are you?' 'Not of them.' 'Then what?' 'What's happened. Can't be something I'm not, whole goofy manager routine's got to go. Spent too many years being a fuck-up that took himself too seriously, and the only way I'm fixing that's escaping the people that define me. Not part of society? Hell, no one's out there on the fringe to think of me in some befucked way, to categorize me and keep me in a hole. Might reinvent myself, who knows.' 'Ain't like you, J.' 'Tried and true's failed. Time for a new approach.' 'Damn. Comin' back?' 'Who knows?' 'Smooth. What about Anarch, man?' 'Fuck Anarch. Fuck Lexicon, Eva, Borealis and anyone else that thinks I'll owe them anything for a good cut-and-run.' 'Got in over your head, huh?' 'Haha, yeah, guess so. Guess I thought I could run a company without everyone involved being a money-hungry whore. I'm cute like a puppy sometimes.' 'Whatever you say, man.' His voice, dropped, comes out quick and breaks the flow of the conversation. Fear. Paranoia. 'Look, don't worry. I've got you covered.' 'What you mean?' 'You made me a lot of money, Trey, and more than that, you've been a good friend. I owe you something for all those times you got to be the victim.' 'You've helped me out before, man. Got me my last job, shit.' 'Yeah, with chains attached. This time, it's free and clear. All yours.' 'You've apologized enough, man. S'been even for awhile.' 'You're too fucking forgiving, you know? Never tried to milk me, never tried to sucker up for the big breaks when you could've just expected me to use all of Anarch to flaunt your name. Deserve at least some sacrifice out of Jordan, at least once in his twisted little life.' 'Well... what?' 'I'm rich. There's a scrap of paper in the table by the bed at your house--' '--my house?' 'The one in Hawaii.' 'S'mine? Damn, thanks man.' 'Yeah, but look. This scrap of paper has a couple very important numbers on it. You call the one that looks like a telephone number, and tell them the other one.' 'And...?' 'You're set for life. Hell, maybe for more life than you could ever hope of living.' 'So, a lot of cash?' I nod. He grinds his teeth, thinking with eyes fixed at some nothing-point on the wall. The clock ticks. 'Yeah. I can accept that.' 'Always reliable.' 'But, shit, what if I need to get up with you? I mean, like, Sobias know where you gonna be?' 'Fuck Sobias. He used to be a bastard friend of mine, now he's just a bastard. Life.' 'Shit. Dude was shifty yeah, but what about Danielle?' 'Sobias won.' 'Damn.' 'Yeah, look. You wanna find me, it's going to take time and effort and money, all of which you'll have. Just use your head and it'll be a lot easier.' 'Tulz?' 'She'd know--but do you know where she is?' 'Good ponit.' 'Yeah, thought so myself.' 'So, yeah, use my head? Try to outthink Jordan White.' 'No, gotta get rid of that. Jordan White's been ruined.' 'Shyeah?' 'All my fault, but sometimes it's just easier to cut losses, back down, and ease out before things get worse.' 'That what you doing now?' 'On all fronts. Too young to be this tired of fighting uphill battles. Need a break.' 'Not that you ain't too addicted to those fights to let 'em go or nothing.' 'Nothing's permanent.' 'The exile or the fightin'?' 'Cycles, Trey. Way things have been going this whole time. We'll meet again, no matter what either of us do to get away. That, yeah, that you can rely on. So, some things are permanent.' 'Yo, Jordan.' 'Yeah?' 'Entropy.' I laugh, can't deny or hide it. 'Maybe. We'll see.' 'How you gonna try and pull this big ghost act anyway? Know damned well you've got something else you wanna say to the world, that's gonna keep your ass bummin' around for another twenty years.' 'Maybe I'm going to lose my voice.' 'Shyeah, right. Maybe big bad Jordan finally lost. Got broken. Gave up.' I nod to him, dip sunglasses down to look at the clock. 'Patronizin' motherfucker, don't gotta play along with me an' shit. Y'know that didn't happen, J. You're a badass, from point one. Badasses don't lose, man. Badasses don't give up.' 'Badasses know when it's time to call it quits. Take the girl, take the money, run.' 'There's a girl?' His face softens. He gets the picture. 'Beside the point, Trey. It's the stupids that kick around too long, ruin themselves by trying to play the role of what used to come naturally. Once your life's an act, you've got to stop and think.' 'Losin' it, man. You're always gonna be Jordan White, no matter what you do. S'on your parents, s'on your aunt, s'on every teacher, friend, bitch, or enemy you've had along the way. S'on you for every straight, crooked, or inane shit you've ever pulled.' He chuckles, trying to keep it to himself. 'Shit, give it a shot. Try an' break the way your life works, rebuild it how you wanna see it. Been runnin' as the same motherfucker your whole life, you ain't gonna kill that without killin' the body too.' 'Not the type.' 'Exactly. Just remember, you're always Jordan White.' A good time for both of us to laugh and neither of us to worry why. It's a long walk outside. The air's cold, breath hangs in clouds that break across my face. Can't think--done is done. My part in everything's over, time to let nature take control and watch how things go. As much as I want to give Trey that permanent out from the cycle, that three-pound brick of self-realization, I can't let myself go that far. I can't change his head for him; I can't end someone else's story. Doesn't matter how fitting an ending I give them, how well-constructed and complete their overall bit in life seems to go, I can't force it on them. Let people be people, take count afterwards. Understand the repercussions from a safe distance--never getting close enough to remember the taste off a stray breeze carrying the smell--and then figure out if anyone really wins. It's the best I can do for him. Time to think is a great gift to throw someone, but after that it's on them to make it worthwhile. Just smile. Remember, names change. People rarely do. These are the facts of life. When the room spins out of your control, just walk away. Too cold. Should've worn another jacket/smoked another cigarette/set myself on fire. Too late now. No second-guessing. Stare up at the window-wall. Laugh. If things haven't worked out for the best, at least they've worked out. There's some peace in that, if little comfort. A concession to losing my head whenever I did, a concession to trying to keep it up all this time without a hint of the right environment, a concession to the hungry masses for playing along. I hope you like nachos & cheese and stale popcorn, masses; we're all out of hot dogs. One more thing that maybe matters, a concession to finding a way to make things work again, whether it's exactly like they used to or rerouted and held together by duct tape. It doesn't matter. Things have been done. My memory's shit, no use trying to analyze how things were back there. Just look ahead, light another cigarette. A few spots down and across the lane, headlights flash on. Fear. Paranoia. Whatever.
