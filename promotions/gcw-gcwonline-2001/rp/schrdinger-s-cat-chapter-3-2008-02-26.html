Schr’dinger's Cat: Chapter 3

26 February 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 26th, 2008 The pain was almost unbearable. Antonio instinctively checked his forehead for blood but instead found a small bump. He tried to get up, but there was almost no strength left inside his body. He looked around and saw that a very nice room surrounded him. The bed he was in was comfortable. Antonio was weary. After he had been attacked and knocked out, he was put in such a nice room. That was not the way he had gone about it. He swung his legs over to the side of the bed and planted them on the ground. His head was still groggy from the blow to his skull. There was a knock on the door and Antonio sprang to his feet, ready to attack if it was needed. The door was opened and a woman stuck her head inside. She looked very familiar to him. “Antonio” How are you feeling?” she asked with genuine concern in her voice. She opened the door completely and entered the room. A lock of her jet black hair fell on her face and she had to flick it back into place behind her ear. That was a movement he had scene a lot of times. His great friend Sheila stood before him. “Sheila?” he said as he received her in his arms. “I can’t believe it is you. I thought you were dead.” She sobbed for a while in his arms. Antonio didn’t know what to do. In a way, it was still his friend, but in another way? she might be a completely different person. He understood that people in this universe were not the same as in his own. Seeing Burton kill that person in cold blood made it evident. “I am not the Antonio from this universe,” he tried to explain to her after she had stopped crying. She looked up to him with wet eyes and suddenly stood back. As if she had been hugging a porcupine. There was a look in her eyes of wild confusion. It was as if he had stabbed her in the back. “Are you working for him now?” she asked wildly reaching for her gun. She aimed it at Antonio’s head. “Are you?” “No. I just arrived here yesterday and a man was waiting for me. He took me to see Burton-? “Don’t call him that. He stopped being Burton a long time ago” right when he killed you and made enemies of all of us,” she said with venomous spite. “He took me to see the grave of the Antonio who lived in this universe. I really don’t know what is going on around here. I just want to go home,” he said, the frustration clearly showing in his voice. Sheila lowered her gun when she saw how vulnerable he seemed. It had been years since she had seen Antonio and a lot of feeling were welling up inside of her. When he was around, they had fallen in love. Right before he was murdered by the traitor, by “The Raven”. They stood there looking at each other for a few minutes before either said anything or even moved. “We will be eating dinner in a few minutes. I’ll leave you here to get ready,” she said before she turned away and went to the door. “Wait” where am I?” he asked before she could walk out the door. For some reason he felt a sort of relief that he was at least seeing faces he recognized. If he had found himself in the middle of a war and knew none of the players, he would probably be dead by now. “In your house,” she said pointing to a picture hanging by the wall that he had not seen. It hit him faster than the blow to the head he had suffered earlier in the day. He recognized himself in the picture. The woman standing besides him was one he had thought about for years. He grabbed the picture and fell to his knees. He passed his hand over the picture were Justine stood, smiling, happy. The tears fell slowly from his eyes. For years he had dreamed of living a happy life with the woman he had passionately loved. Justine was his first love. He had never been able to marry her and to raise their unborn son because she was killed by the mistakes in Antonio’s past. Those ghosts from his past took away the most precious thing he had ever had. In between Antonio and Justine in the picture was a young boy of no more than six years of age. With tears falling on top of the picture, he couldn’t help but laugh. Somewhere and sometime he finally found out that he would have been happy with Justine if only they’d have gotten their chance. He wiped the tears from his face and put the picture in the pockets of his black Dockers. All the fears he had and all the situations he had entertained had suddenly vanished when he saw that picture. He had to find out where Justine and his son were. He walked through the door to a table full of people. There were women and children and a few men. He thought he recognized a few faces but he wasn’t sure. “Where is she?” he asked in the direction of Sheila. There was a new found determination in his voice and she could hear the steely rasp with which Antonio spoke when he had gotten something inside his head. She wished they could stop talking and she could kiss him even though he was not the same man she had fallen for, not really. “We don’t care to know where that bitch is. She’s probably sucking that idiot’s dick right now,” she said spitefully. There was obviously a lot of hatred in her voice and it was like Antonio had walked straight into a wall. “Don’t speak of her like that! She was supposed to be my wife!” He screamed at everyone in the table. Some of the kids seemed scared as they saw a man they knew for a fact to have been dead walking and screaming in front of them. Sheila sighed. She remembered that he didn’t know her as she had been in this universe. What he knew was what he had experienced in his own. “She betrayed you. She betrayed all of us. A long time ago, you, Burton and Justine went on a routine mission to grab supplies for the group’s survival. Before you knew it, Burton and Justine betrayed you,” she said as she waited for his skeptic response. It was nowhere to be found. Antonio put the picture back inside his pocket and slowly walked towards the empty chair reserved for him. He sat down and had a sip of water. “So, how do we kill that traitor?” --- The bubbling sound of the Jacuzzi’s massage system was almost a stress reliever. After all the hard work he had gone through during the day, to relax for a while inside his most prized possession was exactly what he needed. Killing and threatening people was tiring. What he hated most of all was food distribution. Their food stocks were running low and everyone he worked for had to eat. They were all expecting him to come up with a new plan. Burton slowly stepped into the Jacuzzi and when he was finally down, his muscles relaxed visibly. A sigh of relief was released. “Are you comfortable?” a voice asked from somewhere Burton couldn’t see. He knew the voice perfectly though. “As comfortable as I can be these days,” he replied with a smile. “Your presence though, just made things a lot better.” Justine sat down by the Jacuzzi wearing only a robe. Her jet black hair was untied and it fell over her shoulder, almost touching the water. She gave Burton a mischievous smile and took the robe off. She slowly got inside. “Where is he now?” she asked changing the mood of the scene a bit. “He is at the old house with all of our former friends. The fools really think that they can do something with that man, a shadow of his former self. This plan will work out flawlessly. He will guide them on a mission to see the kid and kill me, which will fail horribly and in one fell swoop, we will eliminate their leader. Sheila doesn’t stand a chance.” Burton moved closer towards Justine. His mind quickly drifted towards other things. “How are you so sure this will work?” “A surprise will be waiting for them when they least expect it. I was able to acquire the services of one of them. They will be betrayed once again by one of their own. Then, after that, I will forever be rid of the thorn at my side.” She moved towards him with a lioness? determination and kissed him passionately. “All this talk is getting me hot,” she said as she stood up. The curves of her body glistening with the residuals of the water. She sat on his lap and kissed him one more time. --- The night seemed darker than he had ever seen it before. It was as if the stars had been stolen from their positions in the sky. He had tried to look for something that made him feel like home but it was too hard. Not even in his old house with some of the people he knew back in his normal life could make him feel comfortable. Not even the darkness. He sighed loudly as he thought of everything he had learned the past few days. It was too much to process. If there was a chance he could see Justine once more though, he had to take it. There were so many things that had been left unsaid it ached him inside. “Still not used to all of this?” said Sheila from behind him. She walked to where Antonio stood and stared out into nothingness as well. This whole ordeal had not been easy for her either. She had suffered the death of Antonio just like all of the people that were at the table earlier in that day. To see him again had been startling. “I don’t think I’ll ever be,” Antonio answered in a laconic manner. “This is just all too strange for me.” “It is strange to all of us. For what is worth though, I am glad that you are here. You have no idea how much I missed you,” she told him truthfully. “And to know that there are two of you is probably the best thing ever,” he laughed. “I never thought the universe could hold two of you at the same time. It seems like a tall order.” She smirked. “Idiot.” She really smiled for the first time in years. It was invigorating to know that there was still happiness to be had in this harsh and callous world. They both stared back into the gloom and stood there quietly. In the sky, Antonio finally saw one small spot of light. It felt as if it was battling the surrounding shadows in a battle to the death and losing, but it was there. A small glimmer of hope in a vastness of dark. It was exactly how he felt. He had been lucky that he had found Sheila and all these people and now he found out that he could still be able to speak to the one person he never thought he’d see again. He would grasp unto that star. He knew that it might seem small to him and to anyone that saw it light years away, but he knew that it was a ball of fire of great proportions. Something so small to someone that wants it to, might mean something powerful.
