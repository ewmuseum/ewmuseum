Break Stuff, Part 2: The Mirror

9 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/09/10 The First Shard: Northwick Park Hospital, London March 21, 2010, the Vernal Equinox Post Dual Halo “Careful there, coming through. Mind the gurney! What was your name again, Miss?” “Miyuki.” “And you’re with her, then? Are you…?” “I’m her manager. " “Right, and the big chap? Move aside please!” “That’s Frank.” “Bodyguard?” “In a manner of speaking.” “I can guard her body if you need me to, like now.” “Look, I don’t yet know what we’ve got here. Make way! You there, hold that door for moment and then get some security to the ER. Miyuki… Look, I was hoping for some family. Is there anyone...?” “She doesn’t have any family, Dr. …” “McMillan.” “I think you’ll find that I am entitled to represent her just as much as a family member. Now, can she hear us?” “Yes, I should think so.” “Then maybe we shouldn’t talk about her like she isn’t here.” “Right, just trying to get some things settled. Here we are. Nurse, if you please, get that that curtain and get those damn photographers out of here! How in the world did they get this far? Where is security?” “Frank? Will you please go be security?” “You bet! I’m bitchin’ security. Watch me secure this baby old school style.” “Try not break anyone.” “Thank you. He’s…” “Very useful. And also very close to Tempest.” “Is that just a stage name? Is there something else I should call her?” “You do not yet know her; it would be rude to call her anything else.” “Right… Tempest? Listen to me, love. I know you’re in a lot of pain. Can you tell me where? Or show me? Can you hear me?” “There? What does that mean?” “Not a lot yet. You said it was originally her back she complained about, yes?” “Yes, we thought it was just a hard landing, a bruise, something muscular. But she did take a very long, hard fall.” “How far?” “Which time?” “Christ… wrestlers." “She took a fall sixteen feet with another woman in tow and hit the mat hard. But that was after being punched, kicked, and pummeled. And then she got more of the same. I can’t begin to guess when she would have gotten this particular injury.” “This is her dream then, is it? To be beaten pillar to post like this?” “It’s not as bad as it seems. Anyway, what do you think?” “Could be several things, but her kidneys are around there and vulnerable to that kind of impact. Never mind anyone punching or kicking her. Tempest? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?" “She isn’t.” “What? How do you…?” “Take my word for it and stop wasting time.” “Right, then. Any drug allergies? Past injuries?” “No allergies, and more injuries than I can possibly catalog for you.” “Ok… well, this needs to come off, can you give me a hand?” …… “Tempest! No no, stop it! It’s Miyuki! It’s ok. Calm down. You’re safe. This man is a doctor. He’s trying to help you. Now let go of his throat. That’s it… that’s it. You’re safe, it’s ok.” “God damn… she’s quick.” “Very.” “Right, carefully then. Tempest? I’m sorry, love. Now, I just need to take this off to see where you’re hurt. Miyuki’s here and you’ve got curtains all around you, so no one’s going to see you, ok? I apologize if I offended you.” “That’s better. She’ll be ok now. Won’t you, Tempest? It’s all right. Just pull your arm up a little… there.” “Ok, and if we just turn her very carefully.” “Yes, I’ve got her.” “My god… that scar. It looks old, but does she… does she even have a kidney on that side anymore?” ~ I once believed the Devil was my father too. After all, he told me he was. He told me he would take care of me. He told me a lot of things. I often wonder if any of them were true. ~ The Second Shard: Coming Home Tempest smiled and thanked the stewardess as she handed her a steaming mug, brimming with cream and emitting a mouthwatering aroma of bittersweet chocolate. Tempest blew onto it and the steam started to crawl up her wide mirrored sunglasses, the rimless sort that make anyone look like a celebrity. That Tempest was one in certain circles, was merely a coincidence. Lane Stevens and James von Doom had, at various times, pounded on her face, leaving her black and blue. But the shades were certainly more glamorous looking that. She turned to Miyuki, and the other woman’s reflection shone in the lenses, one brow slightly raised with interest. “Did you find him?” Tempest asked quietly. “Yes,” Miyuki said. “Are you sure you want to talk about this now? I know you’re tired.” Tempest shook her head very slightly as she sipped the hot cocoa and let her head fall back against that airline seat. “Yes,” she said. “I need to know. Does he know anything?” Miyuki shifted slightly, turning in the wider first class seat, to look at Tempest. “Not yet. As you may recall he wasn’t looking for family before, at least not extended family,” she said and watched Tempest’s face. Behind her reflective shades, and unmoving expression, whatever was happening wasn’t apparent to Miyuki. She continued, “He kept all of the files from before in meticulous order. He remarked that it had been one of his most challenging cases, and among the most dangerous.” “It should be safer this time,” Tempest said and raised the mug of cocoa to her lips. “True,” Miyuki said with a small nod, smoothing her skirt absently. “Although, after all this time… Tempest, why do you want to try and find your father’s family now? It’s been so long and you said yourself that from what you recall, he had left them or they had disowned him or something. Are you sure this is a question to which you really want the answer?” “When we have the answer, I’ll let you know.” If her eyes closed it wasn’t obvious, but Tempest’s head did not move again until touchdown in the U.S. ~ What does it mean to be alone in the world? I have always been surrounded by people. It’s just that after a very short time, I had to start trying to figure out which ones were on my side and which ones were out to get me. I was never very good at it. ~ The Third Shard Home Alone? The ringtone whistled lightly with the sounds of Japanese flutes and Miyuki snatched it from her handbag hurriedly, before ducking out into the sunlight on the breezy balcony. She glanced over her shoulder back into the loft, for any sign of movement behind the painted wooden screen, black lacquer with a delicately ornate Chinese dragon coiling around its center. It separated Tempest’s “bedroom” from the rest of the living area, but from her nest of red and black pillows and satiny comforters, there was no sign of movement, none that concerned her for the moment at least. “Hello?” she said into the phone quietly. She shook her head at her own absurdity. Tempest was not only behind the glass doors and twenty five feet away in her bed, but hurt, tired, and drugged. “Yeah, hello? Is this Miyuki?” “Ah yes, Mr. Rivers. I’m glad you received my message,” Miyuki said evenly and smoothed her silken black hair back as if this were a personal meeting in which she must look her best. “Yeah. Hey, just call me Troy. I know we haven’t met in person, but it’s cool. Listen what’s this all about?” “It’s about your girlfriend,” Miyuki said. “She needs you back.” “Wait… why am I getting this call from you? Is she hurt? What’s happened?” Miyuki closed her eyes and held the phone very slightly away from her head as Troy’s voice rose with panic. “She is. Relax, she’ll certainly recover,” she said, trying to relay the fact, but not to sound overly comforting. “But she still needs you. I realize what you’re doing is important, but so is she. And she is particularly vulnerable right now.” “What do you mean? Look, she respects and values what I’m doing down here. This is hardly some party I’m off at…” “Listen to me and listen good, “ Miyuki spat, planting her hand on the railing and looking down at the city street below as she tried to keep her voice calm and her dark eyes narrowed. “Tempest doesn’t do ‘alone’ well. And when things are going badly, she does ‘alone’ even worse. I don’t know why you were foolish enough to leave her alone for this long in the first place, but if you value her I suggest you come home and quickly.” “Now wait just a second here. Are you saying what I think you are? Cuz I don’t think I like your tone. You can insult me all you like, but I don’t believe for one moment that Tempest would…” “You don’t know Tempest nearly as well as you think,” Miyuki broke in. Her tone was calm, even, devoid of malice or any hint of crowing at the man on the other end of the line. “Remind me… How long did you know her without having a clue that she had spent the better part of her life as a wrestler?” The silence weighed heavily across the line, free of static, but not of charge. “Two years,” said Troy from more than a thousand miles away. “Then you see my point?” asked Miyuki rhetorically. “Just because you know her intimately doesn’t mean you really know her. I do know her. And I know that she needs you to be here right now for her. You can be noble later. I’m sure the world will still need saving.” She closed her phone with a soft click, not waiting for a reply. It was rude, terribly so. She didn’t like behaving this way. But the urgency of her own cause moved her. She folded her arms around her in spite of the warmth outside on the balcony and moved along it to where she could just see where Tempest lay. The dog was curled up at her feet and the ferrets dangled their feet lazily over the headboard where they snoozed. Tempest’s hands twitched now and then and she shifted restlessly, her tousled mop of black hair shifting and shuffling like a lion’s mane among the grasses of the savannah. The only reply needed from Troy was his presence, the sooner the better. ~ Sleep. I’ve always had a hard time relaxing. And sometimes when I close my eyes, I still see them all around me; those that would hurt me. I don’t always remember their faces. But sometimes they’re as plain as day and as fresh as yesterday’s news. ~ The Fourth Shard Coming Home to Roost “…And for the record woman, I leave when I decide I’m finished.” Tempest shook her head watching those words leave SurReal’s mouth once again, just before the fire extinguisher flew across the large LCD screen and into the back of his head as she knew, inevitably that it would. She giggled and nodded to Darren Stearns, now back to his day job so to speak as the editor of Squared Circle Scene and said, “You mean that ‘alarming change in attitude’?” Darren nodded vigorously, even though he was smiling across the living space. A small fire burned in a raised fire pit and the cool night air drifted in through the open windows. “That would be the one,” he said, raising a mug of dark brown ale in her direction. “Not that I’ve never seen you throw down before, but that was a bit sudden.” Tempest looked taken aback as she sipped her own yellow-orange concoction, and raised it opposite her other hand in a wide shrug. “What was I supposed to do, Darren? Smile and toss him a lollipop?” she asked, still giggling, full of her own mischief at the scene. Frank Valleta laughed and pointed at the screen. “Oh! Down like a sack of potatoes. Irish potatoes, get it?” He downed what little was left in his glass and set it down with a clatter that made Miyuki jump slightly, sitting opposite him. They were all lounging on the long L shaped couch that both formed a viewing area and surrounded the fire pit, raised on its iron legs above the floor. It emitted enough light to warm the room, but not so much as to overpower what was on the screen. “And now,” Frank went on, extending his hands in Tempest’s direction. “Our girl gets a shot to take back that title and shut up that corned beef smellin’ mother…” “You should show a little more respect. He could wrestle circles around you,” Miyuki broke in, eyeing the younger man across the distance over her wine glass, with her arms partially folded. “Aaaaaand, much like Tempest could have done so while you were in diapers most likely,” added Darren, although he smiled and his tone was chiding. Frank threw up his hands and flopped backward onto the sofa, causing a couple of pillows to pop up under his bulk and Emil, the white ferret, to skitter away from his hiding place. “Jeez! You guys! Tempest, where do you, of all people, find these serious people? Look, this is wrestling. If I got any future in it, I gotta start mouthing off about guys I can’t possibly handle sometime.” He shrugged, with a grin that spoke volumes of good natured self effacement. “Might as well start now!” Tempest burst out laughing and said, “You know sometimes people ask me, ‘Tempest, why in the world is that big lug your protégé? Of all people?’ That’s why!” She giggled and high-fived Frank while Miyuki looked on silently and Darren chuckled, shaking his head. “I suppose that’s one way to look at it. But tell me something Tempest,” he said, looking slightly more dour. “Why the big change? I mean, you can tackle anybody in the ring. I’ve certainly seen it. And I’ve seen you prank the living hell out of guys like him. But attacks are… I dunno, new for you.” He shrugged slightly and looked at her with a cautious curiosity playing across his plump and slightly creased face. “Dude! Were you watching the same tape I was?” Frank interjected. “He put a hand on her. Never mind just being an asshole, world’s full of ‘em. But he put his hand on her! You don’t let that go. Nobody does.” Tempest smiled at her young apprentice and then her eyes flickered toward Darren. “That’s a good standard answer for your readers,” she said. “Honestly? I didn’t put that much thought into it. I saw the fire extinguisher and made it fly. He should be glad that’s all I found.” She sipped her drink and smiled slightly into the glass, its rim distorting her image from Darren’s perspective as he nodded back at her, contemplating her answer. Then all at once the door flew open and two solid thuds announced the arrival of someone else and the landing of two very heavy bags onto the loft’s old wooden floor. They all jumped, but none higher than Tempest, whose eyes lit up like tiny firecrackers as she saw first who had come through the door. “Troy!” Her jubilant squeal was equaled only by the hang time as she launched herself over the back of the couch and into the waiting arms of the long absent boyfriend who had walked into range. He caught her, a little unsteadily. Flying acrobatics were still uncommon to him. If the kiss was anything less than one of the most perfect, most passionate, most pure since the invention of the kiss, by the standards set forth in the Princess Bride, it was not for lack of effort. And it was certainly cause for a round of applause from those gathered. Miyuki smiled with particular amusement. “Ok… wow. Come up for air guys,” said Frank, laughing at the two. Finally they broke apart, but only a little, as Tempest clung to Troy like a second skin and he smiled down at her. “I heard you got hurt, but you look awfully healthy to me now,” he said, ruffling her hair and looking into her face. His eyes followed the pattern of fading bruises across her nose and cheekbones and his smile faltered ever so slightly. “Reports of my demise are, as usual, premature!” she said with a grin. “It’s nothing. And I predict a rapid and miraculous recovery now.” Troy nodded and hugged her close once again, staring over her head at Miyuki who seemed suddenly very uncomfortable in all of this. “Baby, I’m a little jet lagged,” Troy said, eyeing Tempest with an unsteady smile. “Any chance I could get you to make me some of that fantastic espresso? I’m afraid I might blow the machine up the way I am right now. Hmm?” He kissed her forehead lightly and her cheek, squeezing her carefully, a bit of caution still left in his approach. “Oh all right,” she said and giggled. “It’d be better if you didn’t fall asleep right away. I’m guessing we can get you over that jet lag.” If anyone else was in the room, Tempest seemed to have forgotten, but recalled long enough to nod back at them. “Ok, be right back.” After she nearly bounced her way off to the kitchen area, Troy covered his eyes with one hand and rubbed them blearily. “Aw man, it’s good to have you back,” Frank said, stepping up to shake Troy’s hand. Troy shook it and smiled at him, a little thinly, but no less sincerely. “We can really use you back on the theater project too. It’s been a shambles since you left.” “Yeah well that’s all going to have to wait,” Troy said pointedly. For the first time, Darren and Frank seemed to catch something in his tone that Miyuki already knew was coming. “I asked her to go in there so she wouldn’t become involved in me throwing you out of our home. So please don’t make a fuss about it and just go.” “I understand your concerns, “Miyuki said, in her most diplomatic tone. “But you do not own Tempest herself and…” “Nor do you,” Troy broke in taking a step closer to her and thus coming between Darren and Frank as well. “Now I don’t know what all of you know, or think you know about Rachel. But let me tell you something - I love her. And I loved her when she was ‘nobody’ as far as you were concerned. I loved her when she was just a seamstress and if she stops this madness tomorrow I still will.” He glanced around among the three stunned faces and continued, “So if you’re jerkin’ me around to build her up toward her championship fight or the next pay per view or whatever it is that you think is so important, consider this: None of those things are what make her important to me. She simply is. Now I think it’s time you were going.” There was no further discussion as the three filed out of the loft quietly. A moment later Tempest trotted back into the room with a tiny cup and saucer in hand and looked around in confusion. “Oh… where’d everybody go?” she asked, peering up at Troy, her blue eyes wide with curiosity. He smiled sheepishly. “I sent them home,” he said, with a shrug that gave no trace of the authority with which he had emptied the loft. “I dunno… you really want them around? Now?” She laughed and handed him the cup. “No, I suppose not.” She stood back and smiled, just content that he was home, and kept her hands to herself for the moment. After all, he was going to need to finish that esperesso. ~ Happiness. I knew it so briefly, but it became the beacon that shone through everything that came after. It was the light of home for me. And when I could find it, when I could summon the tiniest reason for it, I would grab onto it for dear life. Some things never change. ~ The Fifth Shard On the Outside Looking In “I’m sorry about the other night. I didn’t really mean to throw you out with those others,” Troy said, peeling off his cap and mopping his brow as he sat opposite Frank on a stack of lumber. Frank shrugged and tossed a few loose wire ends back into his tool bag as he said, “It’s ok. I get what you’re saying. You had a life with Temp… I mean, Rachel before any of this and it’s been invaded. Hell, it’s my fault it got invaded to begin with.” “No,” Troy said, shaking his head before taking a swig of water from a tall metal bottle. “No, it’s her fault. But you know, I’m past blaming anyone. It’s no one’s fault. She didn’t try to deceive me because she’s a bad person.” Frank nodded, “She’s as good they come in the business really.” Troy hissed slightly between his teeth and said, “It’s the business that bothers me. All these wrestling people around her that I don’t know and I can’t get a handle on their motives. Who the hell is this Miyuki? And who is she to question my place with Tempest? Like I’m just there to keep her prize pony happy so she run fast and be a champ?” Frank shook his head, with a sudden consternation and said, “Nah man it’s not like that. Miyuki’s been close to her for years. She’ probably the one person Tempest could trust above all the others through some serious shit. Look, I know you’re not getting off to a great start with her, but she really does have Tempest’s best interests at heart.” “And I don’t?” Troy said, looking up at the larger man. “Just because I’m not part of that world, because I see violence as stupid, which makes me an outsider to that world, suddenly I don’t have her best interests at heart?” “No! Dude, that’s not what I said, and you know it,” Frank said, firmly, but with a note of disappointment. “And I’m one of those ‘wrestling people’ too. We’re not all out to make a buck off each other. There’s a lot of love for what we do and that’s what keeps us going.” Troy shook his head and said, “I know. I know. I’m sorry. And I’m not really lumping you in with those other guys.” “Why not?” said Frank with a sudden grin. “I don’t get to be a wrestling guy just cuz I won’t wear spandex?” Troy laughed and said, “No, it’s because you’re still here, wiring circuit breakers by day. You still have a job and a life I can recognize as normal. And even making the mistake of blurting out Tempest’s wrestling name and outing her like that. It was a perfectly human error. Those things I understand. What motivates people to willfully commit violence, as a sport, and then… just shrug it off, I don’t get.” He shook his head and Frank just watched him for a moment. The silence was a gulf between them he didn’t know how to fill for a moment. Love of wrestling, as far as he was concerned, was something that if you had to explain it, you wouldn't understand the explanation. “Look, she’s been through some stuff. I know that,” Troy said quietly. “She has a scar that… well, you can’t see it most of the time, but I certainly have. We don’t talk about it. She seems like she doesn’t really want to remember and I don’t want to make her. But she doesn’t talk about growing up in Hong Kong. No pictures, no friends from school, no favorite places to eat or shop or hang out.” He shifted his weight forward as Frank watched him curiously. “All I know about it is that scar, and that her father was murdered when she was seven. Mom… she barely even knew. So I can put a few things together and guess she survived some pretty nasty shit. But… that doesn’t make it all make sense to me. Why would she choose to fight and face violence when she clearly doesn’t have to anymore?” He looked at Frank, genuinely perplexed and for the first time the younger man saw that there was something he might understand better than his friend. “Listen,” he said,” when they trot out that word ‘survivor’ that’s just some trumped up PC bullshit for ‘victim’.” Troy sat up straighter and stared back at Frank as the bigger man said, “And no one wants to stay a victim forever.” ~ I’ve been fighting as long as I can remember. At first it was for survival. Then it was for fun. Now, I tell myself it’s to pick up all the broken pieces. But really… do I simply not know that there is any other way?
