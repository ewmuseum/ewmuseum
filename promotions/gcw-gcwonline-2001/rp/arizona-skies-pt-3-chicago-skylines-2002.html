Arizona Skies Pt. 3 (Chicago Skylines)

2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Sometime in 2002 They called him Arizona because that was the only name he would give them. In most businesses, that sort of thing would keep anyone from being hired. But this was the wrestling business, and things operated differently for them. That is why someone like Arizona could sit behind the desk of Jason Chae, the owner of a promising wrestling promotion, and not get tossed out on his ear. It certainly did not hurt any that at his side was one of WIW’s most brightest stars, Scott Drake, otherwise known as Chris Storm. "I’m telling you Chae, just ink this guy a deal." Scott told him, leaning forward in his chair. "I could use him for what you have planned." Jason Chae tilted himself back, allowing himself to think on the situation. His eyes shifted over toward Arizona, who stood in the corner and did his best to try and soak it all in. As a professional bum, he hadn’t spent much time in plush offices. He just allowed Scott to do all the talking, as it seemed he was more than experienced. Oddly enough, Scott had been in exactly the same position not too many years ago, having his mentor make a sales pitch to Chae on why he should sign Drake to a contract. It looked as if Chae was seriously considering the proposal. Every few minutes Chae would nod his head silently and tap his pen against his lips. The entire sales pitch lasted for half an hour and by the conclusion Jason Chae had not said a single word. Scott’s last words resonated with Arizona, "come on Chae, you know if I work with this guy that he’ll be something special. Just give me the chance." Not since his high school football days had anyone told him he would be anything special. After all this time, he had given up on himself as well. To hear someone else, a person he barely knew, willing to throw that kind of support behind him really meant something. Allowing a few more minutes to pass in silence, Jason Chae nodded and leaned forward in his plush leather. "Fine, I’ll have someone draw up a contract and send it your way for approval." Arizona’s heart felt like it stopped for a moment. It had actually worked. Scott agreed to the terms and reached across the desk to shake his hand. Lifting him, Scott nodded toward Arizona to follow and the two exited the office. Once in the hall, Arizona had to stop Scott to take him. He had just got out on a limb with his boss and put faith in a man who was down on his luck and in need of some help. "Hey man, thanks for the pitch in there." Scott Drake’s eyes were cold as he sized up Arizona, turning and cutting him off with his body. "Woah now," Scott said, "don’t start sucking my dick just yet. Don’t forget, I got you this best I need a human punching bag. You do that and I’ll see we keep you around as long as I need you to be a meat shield. But don’t think I’m going to bat here for you." "But?" "I did this for me, not you. I’m not a fan of getting my ass stomped by four men every time I step out of my locker room." There was a silence as the words sunk in for Arizona. He had just been put in his place, rather harshly, by a man he had just threw his trust into. It was not an easy thing to swallow. "Fine" he muttered as his faded blue eyes looked down at the carpeted floor. Scott Drake didn’t even wait for his head to lift back up before he started to walk away. Only by hearing the heels scrap against the carpet did Arizona even realize he was leaving. Like a sulking child, he hurried after his partner. The two reached the exit doors of the large Chicago skyscraper, the bustling city unfolding before them behind the panes of glass. Watching Scott reach for the door, Arizona cleared his throat to grab the attention. When that didn’t work he took a much more direct approach, "so what do we do now?" "We?" Scott asked, turning his head slightly back as to look at Arizona, "what do you mean “we??" He pressed his hand against the glass door and shoved it open, "I am heading back to my place. You wait for me to contact you and tell you what to do." "Oh." The word floated in the air until it came crashing down by the sound of the glass door shutting. Arizona watched him leave, wondering where he was going to stay, what he was going to do, and how he was going to eat. Reaching into his pocket all he found was a half empty unmarked pill bottle and thirty-seven cents. Popping the cap with his thumb, Arizona took a small handful of the pills and tossed them back in his mouth. "Well shit" he uttered to no one but himself, walking through the door and out into the city that earned its nickname, "the Windy City". Arizona looked in each direction, trying to figure which would be the best to travel in. There was a Chinese place, looked about two blocks away. They were always known for holding leftovers for far too long, and you had to watch for bugs, but they never cared about people picking their leftovers like some did. Arizona looked in the other direction, already able to smell a hint of fried chicken being carried in the wind. There must have been a fry shack down that way, not too far. They usually threw out food regular, but those spots were often hot spots for criminal activity. Arizona knew better than to hang round those places, trouble always seem to find people at the wrong time. He continued to scan the cityscape, looking for his best choice. Noticing the famous symbol used around the world, Arizona headed over to a small private clinic just a few blocks down from the left. They had everything Arizona could want; plenty of medicines, food often thrown out from either lunches or in house patients, and security never paid much attention to anything going on outside the clinic. Walking around the building, Arizona found what he was looking for, the dumpster. Checking it out as carefully as if it was a Picasso painting, he checked for any locks or other warning signs. Spotting a padlock, he grimaced. He had two choices, he could break it and hope to find something good before security came, or leave it be and avoid any trouble. Under normal circumstances Arizona prided himself on being very quick in his dumpster diving, even with the bad knee, a trait that had helped him survive as many nights on the streets as he’s had. But he knew he could not throw away his opportunity he had with this. No dumpster was worth it. Dropping down to rest, he looked to see if he could spot anything else of interest. Rain slowly trickled down, escaping from the dark clouds above head. Arizona looked up, realizing he would need shelter soon before the sky gave out. Giving up on his search for good, he rolled over and hid behind the dumpster, hoping to avoid as much of the rain as possible. As it came down harder and faster, he wondered what Scott Drake was doing at the moment. Did he live comfortable? A nice roof over his head maybe, one where he didn’t have to worry on nights like this. Was the refrigerator well stocked with more than enough food for any one man. Maybe, Arizona thought, he even had a woman he could come home to and cuddle up next to when he got cold. That would be nice. He tilted his head upwards again to look at the heavens, wondering why he had been dealt this hand. "I sure do miss those Arizona skies" he grumbled as he laid his head down onto the concrete.
