Thanks for the Heads Up

6 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/06/06 McKail’s career is back on track, or so the gossip mill suggests, hell, he didn’t even realise it was derailed. He’d lost one match out of a dozen, you’d think he’d was just some kind of jobber, desperate for his first win. Apparently, by defeating the former Primetime Championship Wrestling superstar, Nerezza, last week on World Wide these sources of “inside” the wrestling industry have foreseen a bright future for him. Well thanks for the fucking heads up, McKail thought to himself, throwing the wrestling magazine in the nearest trash can he could find. They have no idea what they’re talking about most of the time, they’re just smart marks reading into what they see on television, regurgitating onto paper whatever their contrived, if not deluded, minds can conjure. Every time he read these kind of rags, he always found himself getting angry at the smallest thin, hell, he didn’t even know why he bought these damned things anymore. Now that his career was “back on track”, to quote the pundits, he wanted more, he wanted to push the envelope further and he wanted to sight his sights on something higher. He knew the loss of his television title to Danielle Northsky was a minor set back to his success rate within the GCW, but the victory against Nerezza at least won back some of his standing and at Warpath this week, he figured he’d get the rest and change. McKail had overheard some of the guys in the locker room and some of the technical staff talk, when they thought he wasn’t around anymore, about the decision to offer the open challenge for the gauntlet match to decide who was going to take a shot at Northsky’s television title at Warpath and he smiled almost immediately. They couldn’t understand his logic to enter into such an event, but he couldn’t see the logic not to do the match. When he’d first started within the GCW, his first match was a newcomer gauntlet match, which he dominated. From that point on, things really started to happen for him. This number one contender match at Warpath was virtually the same thing and he intended to repeat the process. Defeating all those other hopefuls and then going onto defeat Danielle Northsky for her television title would firmly cement his standing in GCW forever more. Danielle Northsky. GCW Television champion. McKail still had trouble getting his head around that phrase, never mind putting it into words. It seemed like for the longest time, even though it could’ve only been a little over a month, he considered it to be his title. Putting someone else’s name on it seemed so foreign to him. It seemed wrong, dirty maybe. He shook the thought off, without too much difficulty. After all, at Warpath that will all change and the title will come home, the person it truly belongs to.
