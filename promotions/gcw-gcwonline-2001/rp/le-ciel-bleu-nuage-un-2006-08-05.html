Le Ciel Bleu: Nuage Un

5 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 5, 2006 Does anyone actually retire? So many people leave the ring, but is it intended to be a permanent absence or just like Jamie Foxx in Collateral, how something temporary suddenly turns into 10 or 12 years. To think on how many of the old guard, those who were 'IT' when I first came into PTC back when it was a lower interfederation organization under the umbrella of the then-strong Primetime Championship Wrestling. You have people like Doug Troy and Frisco, Jeremy Howard and Alexander Miyagawa, the White Mexican and El Diablo... all gone now. But with the exception of Howard and Miyagawa, they never said that they were retiring. Actually, maybe Frisco was arrested... can't remember. Back in the day... man, if 2001-2002 doesn't seem like an eternity in the past... Ivan Stanislav was one of the main players in PCW and then in Old School Wrestling. Alan Kriegman was an evil genius who was doing his best to tear up both places, especially OSW. Boda had managed to come into his own, transforming from a 6'10' habitual loser to the flagship champion in more than one federation (Jon Kano, unfortunately for him, hit his peak in PCW in 2000 and only fell since then). Xavier Kannon started off as an upstart in Primetime Wrestling Academy before quickly rising through the ranks in NTICW and then in PCW, eventually taking over. Hessian was a walking war crime everywhere he went, delighting in the hardcore environments that were prevalent at the turn of the century, allowing him to craft his violence poetry on national television. More closely connected to me, going down the line in PWA you'd have your Drake 'Fallen Angel' Maxwell, who started strong in NTICW and was in the main event before coming to PCW and fading out after failing to best Howard. Ken Kaze got progressively sillier over the years, drifting out of PTC altogether for a little while taking in places such as Practically-Insane Wrestling and the Hardcore Wrestling Organization, then came back to PC and he was in... alright, where he was escapes me. Even The HIT (my PWA instructor for all of a week before they realized they had nothing new to teach me) had fallen out of the business because of injuries. Ivan Stanislav was around before me, and he recently came back... he's in PRIME now. Originally he'd resumed his years-past feud with Tyler Nelson, but Ty's gone so now Ivan's just around terrorizing the masses. Clyde is over in UWF along with Chris Storm (those who preceded me and followed me in PCW, respectively). I caught a brief glimpse of Carl Ca$h over in HSW... then again he was never around in PTC or WrestleOnline very long in the past that I saw much of him. Paddy O'Shea and Red Rock are about the only ones who were coming into PTC at the same time as me who are still around, and they're in AWC. Plus, they had a lengthy absence towards the start, making their return later on. Still, to think of it, I look at Primetime Central now, it having taken the fore over the now defunct WrestleOnline (as The Phantom Booker who created PCW and the organization itself had a bit of a mental breakdown or something, subsequently dropped off of the earth). WO is dead, and with it I struggle to find all those names that I once knew. Not just a handful; in only three years, an dozens, hundreds of people have disappeared from view. Main-eventers and curtain-jerkers alike, those who closed gates and those who nearly had to buy a ticket to get in, everyone's gone. The community has changed so much from the last time that I was here. I started in PTC back in 2001. Came from Japan having trained in the junior heavyweight style (the most I've weigh professionally has been 245 though I've been billed as more) and working in small garbage indies as were available for quick work back then. Drafted to Eternal Wrestling (out of Primetime Wrestling Academy) by Nicholas Jaxx to his little upstart promotion that hardly saw roster-attendance higher than 11. I worked alongside Young Tiger and a little later Six-Finger Cisco against the likes of people like Gideon and Duzza... I think the latter's managing over in LoC. EW didn't really see much action, it was always the 'third' in the hierarchy, after CCW and before NTICW (with PCW, by design, on top). Jaxx destroyed the EW product and left PTC, but I stayed, along with a few others. NTICW closed around the same time, so there was a massive influx of the best of both feds (far fewer from EW) taken into PCW as the roster ballooned to about 65. From EW there was myself, Shane Wethers, Bobby Stormbringer, Six Finger Cisco, and Young Tiger. Within a matter of a couple of weeks, Stormbringer and I were the only ones left, and even BS had to leave shortly after that to just leave me. NTICW, on the other hand, gave up Xavier Kannon, Rush Mason, Hessian, Drake Maxwell, and many more. PCW got the better end of the deal on that one in terms of future-marketable stars. But then OSW opened right up, and many of them were taken over there along with a few of PCW's top talents. From January 2002 through April 2003, I was in PCW, quietly working my way up. PTC continued to evolve as it was coming out from under the thumb of Phantom Booker, who created another higher-tier organization altogether. (WrestleOnline... for those who didn't know, PB was notorious for starting something and then moving elsewhere. The Johnny Appleseed of wrestling at the turn of the century.) But PTC was always right there, never out of sight. After PCW closed, I went right back to PTC, joining into Rush Pro Wrestling, but that closed, too. I really can't say much about my time in PCW and RPW. Not that there's nothing to say, just that I don't want to make this a total exercise in name-dropping. And there's no need to list my resume as I've already got the job. But from the summer of 2004 onward, I went from being fairly-steadily employed to having been out of the wrestling spotlight altogether. Is it for lack of trying? Yes. The reason I'm wrestling again is partly for the same reason that I took a break from it. As I sat tacking away at the keyboard, it was with incredible timing that the voice of a teenage girl drifted into the upstairs computer room from the kitchen below. TANIA: Hey, Sky! I need help with something! I smirked, tapping CTRL and S before pushing the black rolling chair away from the desk. Standing to my full height of 77 inches, arms outstretched inside the sparsely-furnished 'media center', figured that a break from this journal entry would do me some good. Tania's voice is coincedentally appropriate on multiple levels. The computer could be left on... I've got a good antivirus program and firewall installed. I only use this one for e-mail and research anyway. The kids have their own tricked-out system to the side, a glorious affront to the RIAA and MPAA that's got 300 gigs of video and music downloaded on separate accounts for each of the girls, and that's a fraction of their total. I'm not even sure how Evan hooked all of it up and won't attempt to understand how. He's the resident Computer God (ten self-awarded points for the Black Sabbath reference), it's best not to question that which I could not hope to comprehend. 'Be right there,' I called below, and left my inner thoughts (which have never really been private, I'll tell whoever asks... they just don't), for posterity, though I do intend to come back to it later. ---- I live in Fort Lauderdale, a big house on the Intracoastal just off of Federal Highway near A1A. The front facade is rather tall and painted in a Mediterranean yellow (as is inside of the house, save for the Greek-styled blue kitchen). The garage holds 3 cars comfortably, there's 6 bedrooms, 2 bathrooms and 3 half-baths. The Florida Room (that's just what I call the general entertainment area) has tropical floral-embossed white furniture arranged to allow 12 people to watch the large plasma television affixed to the wall, eating at the two flow tables if they so desire. The kitchen is bigger than many people's bedrooms, the custom-designed cooking range in the middle with seven stovetop eyes of various sizes. The countertop area extending around it gives room for maybe 6 husky people to eat, and there's a separate elliptical table setting off by the window, opposite the big stainless steel refrigerator. It's right by the window, which allows a view to the addendum indoor pool outside (though a flick of the switch and the shutters retract to make it an outdoor covered area). We were outside pool covered because because it's too hot outside for us to be under the light of the sun. I've got on a breathable blue Hawaiian shirt and some khaki Bermuda shorts (and unlike most people who wear them, my legs look good enough that it doesn't constitute a fashion crime). Closed-toe sandals with heel straps complete the look; I am 'in the zone.' And Diet Snapple peach iced tea in my hand, I'm reclining in the outdoor adjustable lounge chair, listening to Tanya explain what she needs from me. TANIA: How do you get a girl to like you? Oh, Christ. I was hoping it would be something easy. If you were there you'd see a look of incredulity upon my face as I respond, 'You're actually asking me?' I took a sip of my drink, because it stopped me from adding the words, 'what the hell were you thinking?' It wouldn't be appropriate, especially since I was the parent here. TANIA: Who else am I going to ask? You're the father-figure here... figure. Trust when I say that I had no idea how to answer her. Well, at least the ideal answer; I could have given her something but it may not have been satisfactory. This wasn't quite the thing that I signed up for when I took custody of the girls, but then again I couldn't have known at the time that Tania would grow up to be bicurious. Rubbing my goateed chin with my left hand (as I tend to do; not sure where I picked up the force of habit... it's not exactly a nervous reaction to anything), I told her in all honesty 'Well, what do you expect me to say? If I knew how to get women's attention I wouldn't be single.' Well, it's not like I'm totally clueless. Though I've been out of the dating game for a while yet (... was I ever in it? Haven't been on a date in three or four years, I don't think), I still surmise that acting respectable and respectful is the way to go. Then again, who knows... Tania, meanwhile, crossed her arms over the denim overalls. She'd strategically picked a thin-strapped white shirt, the straps of which were covered by the overalls. It looked like she wasn't wearing a shirt. I wondered if it was something she'd already decided to get the attention of this mystery girl. She'd definitely get male attention in the meantime. TANIA: You must've dated somebody. Wasn't there that girl on PCW? Sandy something? Wong, though really PCW was more of a work than other federations. Sandy Wong was a former interviewer that came to me with the idea of pretending to be an item... didn't really go anywhere, but she got a few on-screen segments and a couple trips to Japan out of it. We didn't really date, though; it was just a promo thing. Not only that, in PCW I portrayed a blind man and was billed at 6'8' and 287 pounds at heaviest. The action itself was still real; just everyone was highly stylized since that's the only way we could get the job. On the upside, they paid exorbitantly, which allowed me to pay down on the house and cars. But yeah, I haven't done the dating thing in a while. With Tania expecting something from me, I had to play the father figure, as she said. Hell if I knew how I could help the situation, but I had to try. 'That wasn't a real relationship, doesn't count. But really, why are you asking me? You just assume I'll be okay with you being attracted to a girl?' TANIA: Do you have a problem with it? 'No, but I wish you'd just start from the beginning with me. You ask me for advice on something by starting at Point C, meanwhile I'm still back at Point A because I didn't know you were going to bring this up. You can't jump ahead of me and expect a great line right off the bat.' This is why I'm going back to professional wrestling -- a match is easy. Parenting? Guh. Bill Cosby, Paul Reiser, Al Roker, and John Trent have utterly failed me. Failed, I say. My 16-year-old charge lightly claps her hands in front of her mouth, as she tends to do when she needs to start over or go back to something previously mentioned in a conversation. TANIA: Okay, what do you need to know? Left hand through my straight black hair, I gathered a breath... good delay. 'First of all, let's backtrack for a moment,' I said, trying to wrap my head around this thing. Maybe a reset could help me to get some perspective on it. I don't want to leave her hanging. 'Any one female probably likes something different from the next. Maybe I'm giving too much credit in thinking that the average teenage girl is distinctive from the next, but we'll try it this way. Do you know this girl personally?' She nodded. 'Would I even know her among your close friends to suggest something in particular?' There were only a handful of friends she really had, Tania is the kind of person who doesn't make many acquaintances, but cherishes those she has. Must have developed a special attachment to one of them. TANIA: ... Zha. Ouch. Her. Wulong Zha, better known back in the year 2003 as Xiao Suarez, was a tag team with her friend Saadra Mehta aka Tanya Isama in Action! Wrestling and The Squared Circle. A legally-emancipated minor, she was 14 at the time (billed at 16, a huge leap there) who was a legal immigrant but with no family in the United States. She voiced her desire to be an independent worker, as she'd already received an education equal to that of the Florida public school system (we suck at that sort of thing). Passed the GED, she was technically an adult so she could do adult things (though she'd still be treated as a minor if it came to crimes against her). Both Zha and Saadra were wrestling students of mine; and they both lived in the household with me. I've got room enough, and they help out with the kids. But yikes; what was I supposed to say to that? I can't give her too much information because I don't want to think of the very real possibility of my adoptive daughter having sex with another girl her age, my tenant. Then again I suppose no father really wants to think about a daughter having sex in the first place. Except for the sickos, which I'm definitely not. Honestly. Really. 'Um...' I couldn't believe this was happening. If I gave some sort of satisfactory answer to this I need to get an award. Where was a hurricane when I needed it? I'd have welcomed the distraction. 'Is she...?' I'd never really thought much about it, but Zha never even attempted to date. I figured it was due to her being too young for older men, but too mature for younger boys. She did watch too many rap videos, though, and most of the time there's just well-endowed and scantily-clad women dancing around. Thought that maybe she was just watching her favorite artist... was she watching the dancers instead? TANIA: Yeah, she said once when I was talking to her a few months ago. Never came up in conversation with me, but then again I'm not really the kind of talker that people easily open up to. I listen almost too much. Sometimes people want a dialogue and not a monologue... besides, as the only male in the house, I accepted early on in the living arrangement that there were things under the roof that I just didn't hear or know about, so long as certain rules were followed. Rule #1: Sweets are evil, and will not be allowed in the house. Rule #2: Sweets outside the house, go nuts. Rule #3: Be extra nice to Tanya, Saadra, and Zha between the 13th and 15th of the month. Rule #4: If Saadra if Saadra intends to bring a man here to spend the evening, she's restricted to the poolhouse. Rule #5: Never never never never give Ines anything with peanuts in it, she's deathly allergic. So, among other secrets, if Zha was bicurious or full-up lesbian, it was news to me. Then again, it really didn't matter to me since she could do as she pleased so long as she kept the noise down at night, paid her rent, and didn't swear in front of the little ones. Wait... Damn, I need to pick my words better. 'Well, you know her,' I said, arms outstretched in that gesture like a Paisan going 'Come on!' 'You don't know how to get her attention? You've got to have an idea what she likes. If it's girls, you've already got the eliminating criteria out of the way.' I knew of Zha's hobbies, but her taste in potential dates was something that flew completely over my head. I wished Ines or Rafaela needed my help for something; I'd have loved nothing more than an excuse to ditch this, as I felt inadequate to help Tania at all. The message here was to ask and ye shall receive. While fumbling for something else to say, Zha stepped outside with a bottle of Zephyrhills. She was woefully overdressed for the day with her yellow zip-up sweatshirt and dusty gray jeans. Then again she'd been in the house all day and the girls were blasting the A/C. I really didn't know what to tell Tania other than to just be open and honest with Zha (who apparently didn't date, at least to my knowledge... not like Saadra, who can't seem to stay in for more than one night in a week). Jumping up from my seat, I praised whichever deity sent Zha outside, as I was relieved of this fatherly conversation and in my inability to handle it. If only those 'How to be a Dad?' books covered the hard stuff.... Sweeping my right arm (Snapple bottle empty as by now I'd spent a good deal delaying my answers to Tania) toward the Chinese-Jamaican immigrant, I invited her to sit down. 'Zha! I'm glad you're here. Listen, I was just talking with Tania, and she's got a problem that I'm not sure I can help her with.' In the meanwhile, my 15-year-old daughter buried her face into her hands. She probably wanted to spare embarassment, but then why did she come to me? Saadra's better for keeping it fully-confidential; I readily passed things off to those I felt were better qualified to handle it than I was. Though I am a professional wrestler (even during my extended absence I worked in the Florida and Georgia independents), I've never pretended to know everything. 'I think,' continuing as I ushered Zha into my seat beside Tania, 'that this would best be discussed as a girl-to-girl thing. I hate to push this on you, but it's really pressing on Tania and Saadra isn't here at the moment.' I never knew where that woman went during the day. At least she occasionally came back with groceries. Tania looked like she could die right there, her long curly brown hair was all that I could see of her. Zha nodded to me, then turned her attention completely toward my daughter. It's still a little weird saying that... she's only 12 years younger than me. And I don't want to seem like one of those buckwild ghetto teens who couldn't keep it in my pants until after puberty was over. What can I say? Circumstances. Heading back into the house, I dumped the glass bottle into the recycling bin beside the door. We're all about the environmental consciousness in this household. Rafaela wouldn't let us have it any other way. I swore that girl channeled Lisa Simpson one night and we were stuck with it now. On that note, continuing with the chain of coincedence, the middle child Rafaela (looking much like Tania would as a younger girl of 11, except that her hair is straight and she's got blue eyes instead of brown) walked into the kitchen from the Florida Room, where I could still hear the tail end of the credits for Cartoon Network's Miguzi programming block. RAFAELA: Can I ask you something? I needed a quick booster shot after dealing with Tania, so I said, 'Of course.' From outside I heard a loud voice. ZHA: WHAT? REALLY? And here I thought they'd be discreet about it between themselves. Back to the middle one. RAFAELA: I think there's a boy I like. What should I do? Sweet merciful molasses, a softball. I could so handle this, but she'd just asked me to step up to the plate. I had to ask for the wind-up and the pitch. 'You know this boy? What does he like?' RAFAELA: He likes all the things that I like. Including boys. ....... Oh, Christ. ---- I'd killed my fatherly self-esteem enough for one day. About the only thing I'd done correctly was to fix little Ines a turkey sandwich and some strawberries. The children were all taken care of at the moment, and it was to hot to go out. Lacking anything else to do, Rafaela and Ines watched cartoons while Tania and Zha sorted out exactly each one felt about the other (I really hoped that would come back to haunt me, or them). I trolloped back upstairs three at a time, not for being in a hurry but simply another force of habit from my younger days as a man with responsibility to myself alone. Had to make sure that Ines and Rafaela didn't try to copy me, their balance, strength, and leg length wasn't near mine. Back upstairs, with the house full save for Saadra's usual absence, I turned left at the top and the closest door on the left (across from one of the full bathrooms) was that same media room I was in earlier. The screen-saver was that of one of Zha and Saadra's matches, their win at the first supercard for the Texas indy -- now defunct -- called The Squared Circle. It was their first tag team championship, won in a gauntlet match. Zha didn't use the computer, so I knew that Saadra put it there. She didn't talk much about their time in Action! or tSC, so to see that there was unexpected. Maybe she wants some of that glory back, just like me. But I also have another reason... The reason that I wrestle again is not just for the want for competition. That's a good part of it, to be certain. The independent level has some rising stars, but it can't be denied that if someone were qualified for the big show then they're jump at the chance to be there, and likewise on the promoter's side they'd jump at the chance to bring said wrestler in. A good wrestler is one who can make money for the promotion, and honestly few I were facing could do that. As a result, even though I'd invested wisely from the money I made off of PCW, little of it is liquid. The house and cars are paid off, that's not a problem. My regular job as a wrestling instructor wasn't paying enough. My training methods work, but they're too demanding and unorthodox for most people. I don't train anybody off of the street; they've got to know how to defend themselves, and be in shape, before they come to me. I tax and strengthen their minds so that they will have the proper mentality to deal with whatever comes their way. Of course, every kid with a decent dropkick thinks they're hot stuff, and so I don't get many people coming through. Along with that, though I command a high fee for indy appearances, it's not enough and only certain promotions can pay. 'Relatively-high' fee, I should say. Not broke yet, and there's money in savings accounts I could tap into, but I don't want to do that. It's set aside, and not for me. That reason why I wrestle again? Coming back into full-time status after three years away? It feels longer, and shorter, than that, but it's been my time away from the road. And I'm coming back now because back in January of 2003, my friend Xavier 'Juan' Garcia passed away. We were friends in wrestling, though I'd gone overseas to train while he stayed local because he had children at an early age by his girlfriend. Xavier and Guadalupe both passed away, and who was left to take care of the girls, at the time 3 (Ines), 7(Rafaela), and 12 (Tania)? They had family in the area, but they lived in Miami Gardens. For those who are outside of South Florida, I'll have to explain that one. You see, there's Miami, the glamorous section, that you see on television and in movies. One of the fashion capitals, skin city, celebrities go there all the time. A really upscale, trendy place to live; that's Miami. Just north is Miami Gardens... the contrast is so great it's like comparing a rich gated community in California to the slums of New Jersey. Not a good area for raising children. I got the girls because I lived in a safer area and had more money with which to raise them. And I'd seen them more than some of their relatives who'd otherwise have received custody had I not been request by last will and testament. Those girls are not of my own blood, but Xavier was a good friend and I've sworn to him that I'll do everything in my power to give them the best life that I can. So I don't want to tap into savings because that's cash I've set aside for the three of them once they become adults. They can use it to follow their dreams, or do whatever they want. I, in the meantime, need to bolster my personal savings (it's not like I don't have to spend money). And honestly, yes, living in a house full of females does get a little grating sometimes, but I wouldn't have it any other way. I need to get back on the road because I need to make a living once again. I need some time away from the house. I need to do what I must, for my own good. The girls are taken care of... it's me time, now. On a personal note, Hope Clarkson couldn't have missed the mark further on me. I don't know what sorta of sadism to which she prescribes, that's her own business. I didn't ask about it, I don't want to know about it. But this whole 'hearing people scream' thing is weird. I've always been an excellent submission wrestler; from the beginning, I've had submissions in my arsenal and I've used them. But I don't go for the overkill; I'd prefer a fight to be over as soon as possible, and I know from experience that submissions are the best way to do that. I'm not seeking to injure anyone, and I don't get my jollies from hearing the throes of pain out of my opponents. But I am good at what I do. And honestly, why would I want to waste 15 minutes trying to pound someone so much that they'll stop kicking out if I can end a match in a third of that time (if not sooner) with a quick joint lock? I would've thought that professional wrestling has gotten past the point of trying to address opponents. People are usually wrong when they do it. I suppose some old habits are hard to break, no matter where they are. At least she didn't try to overhype me coming back after a three-year hiatus as a name wrestler... Nope, she did that, too. Shooting blanks on all accounts, I see. She's lucky I'm not the type to discount a person's wrestling ability just because they don't seem to have a clue when attempting to say something about me. But not like she'll have to worry about what I think at all. This is just between me and the computer screen...... The save, the close, the shutdown. I pushed myself away from the computer. GCW was the federation that offered me the most money; I wouldn't think anything less of myself for making pay a strong part of the negotiations. I've got a family to feed, after all. They can't eat pride, honor, virtue, or a championship belt. The return, as Ciel (simply 'Sky' in French), was almost at hand. I was excited to get back into things after so long away. But I wasn't anxious... the affairs of household kept me distracted well enough. *END*
