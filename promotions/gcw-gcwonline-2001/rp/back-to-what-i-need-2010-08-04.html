Date: 08/04/10 “You know Dan,” Dave says with a theatrical sigh, “this was supposed to be a working dinner, with an emphasis on the word working.” I flash a grin at the man who basically runs the three night clubs that I’ve opened since leaving the wrestling world and take another bite of sweet and sour chicken. “Come on Dave,” I answer around the mouthful, “I told you the deal when I asked you to come work for me. I let you run stuff the way you want, and in return you make me a ridculous amount of money while I do nothing at all. I feel like making me work during dinner goes against that agreement.” Dave glares at me with an interesting mix of amusement, contempt and resignation. Dave is one of those weird people who actually enjoy working, and he can’t ever seem to understand the pleasure I get from reaping the benefits of his efforts with no work on my part. “Well if you must know,” Dave replies as his glare softens, “I invited you out here as a favor to you.” “What are you talking about?” I demand uneasily. Dave has a familiar expression on his face, the one that says he’s about to say things that I really don’t want to hear. Unfortunately, they tend to also be words that I really do need to hear, whether I want to admit it or not. “Tell me if this is an accurate representation of your week. Monday, you spent the day scratching your ass and then got roaring drunk. Tuesday you got roaring drunk and then scratched your ass. Wednesday you actually mixed it up; you went to a bar, got drunk and got into a fight. Thursday you went to a bar, picked up one of the few girls who remembers or cares that you were the United States Champion and fucked her in the bathroom. Then on Friday all the drinking finally caught up to you and so you spent the day in the fetal position wondering if having sex with that girl will make your penis shrivel up and fall off.” The laugh that has been building up in the back of my throat slowly dies out as our eyes meet. There’s no humor in Dave’s face, just an expression of intense concern. The problem is that I don’t know what brought this whole thing on. It’s true that despite the obvious exaggeration my week was kind of boring and full of alcohol, but hell I drank more in college. Then again I don’t think it’s possible for me to drink more than I had in college. “What’s your point man? There are people who go through their whole lives who wish that they could where I’m at!” “Which is where?” Dave barks at me loudly enough that some of the other restaurant patrons to look over at our table. Dave reddens and quietly continues. “Think about it. Before you entered GCW you were pretty much broke. But when you entered GCW, you did so under Bagwell’s tutelage. You went from having absolutely nothing, to a world of woman, cars, and expensive restaurants without a single minute to adjust. Even after you and Bagwell parted ways, you managed to keep the lifestyle through one way or another. But you can’t live like you had anymore, and you’re bored.” I firmly force myself from snarling out an angry response. Everything in my core is screaming out that Dave doesn’t know what the fuck he’s talking about; that my life is actually exactly what I want it to be. But I know that isn’t true despite the money that my nightclubs are bringing in. The semi-celebrity status that I had enjoyed whilst in GCW has all but faded, and I wasn’t making enough money to make up the difference. “So what are you saying? You think that I should pick up the phone and go back to wrestling?” “Don’t be a moron,” Dave replies disgustedly. “You were self-destructing in that ring and you know it. I bristle at the implication. “I don’t know if I would call winning the United States Championship self-destructing. And I could have won it back if I decided to stay in the business.” Dave sighs; apparently I’ve failed to see the point. That tends to happen a lot with these deeps talks that I have with him. He may be a short, balding, and chubby man but he’s way smarter than I ever could be. “Your problem is that you’re single minded like no one else I’ve ever seen. Back in GCW you’re entire life revolved around keeping that title. You were ready to sacrifice your friends, your family, and your integrity all to keep…what? A piece of jewelry that probably cost less than a grand to make if that. On-“ “It’s not about the jewelry.” I cut him off as I lean back in my chair and close my eyes. For one moment, for one single blessed moment, I’m back in the ring. I can feel the roar of the crowd wash over me, sense their hate, and under it a sense of adulation. Then just as quickly as it came it leaves, and I’m back in this Italian restaurant with just a one man audience. “You have no idea what it feels like to hear tens of thousands of people screaming your name.” I open my eyes and look directly at David. Strangely he looks frozen, almost as if he’s in a state of shock or fear. But I ignore it, too wrapped up in this opportunity to finally articulate the demons that have been chasing me for months. “It doesn’t matter if they consider you their greatest hero, or if you’re the man that they hate the most. It’s like a thrill ride, hearing them scream with every hit you take, with every holy shit moment you deliver. But when you’re a champion-“ I stop for a second as a familiar ache enters my heart. Some people think that the moment that you realize you’ve lost the title is the worst that a wrestler can experience. I can tell them with no doubt in my mind that they’re wrong. It’s the hour or so after you’ve lost it that is the worst. Once the news has finally sunk in, and you realize that everything you’ve lost in order to gain the championship has been rendered meaningless. That love you let slip away, the friend that you’ve stabbed in the back, those endless days and nights you spend in the gym instead of living your life, their loss has been rendered futile in the span of three short seconds. But that’s not the worst of it. “But when you’re a champion,” I repeat, “it’s a drug. You can’t wait for the moment that you’re music hits the ring and the crowd comes alive. Ask anyone who’s ever won a belt and they’ll tell you exactly, the same thing. It wasn’t a depression that I went through when the doctors told me that I would be out for months, it was a withdrawal. “Then why didn’t you go back?” Dave asks confusedly. “You could have, once you healed up. Hell, the guys over at GCw were practically breaking down your door a full month before you were even cleared. They were all set to see the hell you would raise to try to get your title back, but instead you just turned them down and decided to open a bunch of night clubs. Why?” I stare at him and my jaw works furiously. I know the real answer; I’ve always known it but I never had the balls to say it aloud. I still don’t, so instead I abruptly stand, throw a fifty down on the table, and head out as Dave calls out for me to stop. **** There’s a seedy looking bar not too far down the block, I’m almost tempted to ignore it purely because I know I’ve become used to high quality alcohol that this place will almost certainly not have. But for some reason a night of heavy drinking that I know will lead to a crippling hangover seems strangely appealing, and thus I hurry into the bar. It’s even worse off than I imagined it. Out of the ten tables in the place, only two of them aren’t obviously broke in one way or another. The area is dimly lit, but I can see vomit in various parts of the floor, and the man that I assume to be the bartender is lying face-down on the floor snoring .There’s one man sitting at the bar, nursing a drink that is either beer or just straight up piss. He’s an older man, about mid-forties though he might be even older. He’s got gray stubble coming out of his cheeks, and the dark circles underneath his eyes suggest that he either hasn’t been sleeping in awhile, or he’s been drinking for awhile, or both. He turns when I come and gives me a wave, with a mental shrug I go to sit next to him. “Yuri,” he grunts in between sips. “Dan.” “So Dan, what are you in for?” I stare at him blankly. He sees the expression on my face and snorts into his glass. “No one comes to this hellish, piss poor excuse of a bar by choice. Look at me for instance; I usually drink hard liquor not this shit. I spent twenty years in Russia for the love of God, by all rights I should be burning this place down for giving alcohol a bad name. But the police have decided that I’m responsible for the coma my girlfriend is in and I expect to be carted off to jail very shortly. The only reason that I’m not there right now is that my sister blew the detective to buy me one more day of freedom. I asked her to sleep with him, but she’s saving herself for marriage.” “Is it your fault?” “I suppose, but it was an accident and she asked for it.” I stare at him icily. I have issues with men who like to beat up defenseless woman and the nonchalant way in which he said it has me on edge. Perhaps what I’m really looking for is nothing more than a good fight, and I can’t think of anyone I’d rather bunch than the asshole that’s sitting before me. It appears that he senses my sudden hostility, for all of a sudden he’s making soothing gestures. “I meant that she literally asked for it. It was one of the more uncomfortable fetishes that I’ve dealt with, and back in Russia I dated a girl who would not so much as give me a hand job unless I dressed up like Stalin.” I burst out laughing. But it’s not from humor, rather it’s the type of laughter that stems from a man who wonders how the hell he’s ended up in the fucked up position that he’s in. I guess that Jared recognizes what’s going on for he doesn’t react other than to calmly sip his beer. Slowly my laughter turns in a quiet, choked sob that I manage to quiet after a few minutes. “By all accounts I have no right to complain about my life,“ I whisper “but only one thing has ever made me feel really, truly alive and I don’t think that I can ever do it again. The only thing is, if I can’t do what I used to do…I’m not sure how to live anymore. I lived for my job, and now it has slipped through my fingers.” “Wrong!” Jared shouts into the empty room. The complacent look in his eye is gone, replace by a crazed intensity. “I will not go quietly rot for the rest of my life because that crazy bitch couldn’t take a punch! If my life is over, than that means that I can still have the ultimate high!” “Look, “I answer tiredly. “While I’d be lying if I said I believed in living a drug free life, I’m not about to go out by overdosing in this dump. I’d prefer to die while having sex with twin strippers or something like that.” “That’s now what I’m talking about,” he hisses as he pulls out a gun from the back of his jeans. I know I should start calming him down, or try to take the gun away from him or something…but for some reason I’m curious as to what this has to do with the ultimate high he’s talking about. If he just wants to know what it’s like to kill someone I don’t plan on being the willing corpse, but the fact that he hasn’t shot me immediately probably means that murderer isn’t on his agenda…I hope. “There’s a game that originated in my country,” he says eagerly, “known as Russian Roulette. Six shots, only a single bullet between all of them. Never knowing if pulling that trigger will make you die…can you think of any better way to end it. Much better than allowing the police to drag me off. Here…try it.” My hand takes the weapon almost on its own. The logical part of my brain begs me to stop, tells me that my life is still so good that I’d be an idiot to partake in this insanity. But somehow the barrel of the gun has found its way into my mouth and my finger is caressing the trigger. The really pathetic part is the true reason that I’m doing all this. Dave asked me why I didn’t go back to GCW if I missed it so much; well the fact is that I’m scared. Scared that my loss to Vivica J Valentine was only the first step down a long and vicious slope from which I can never recover. I can take the hate of the crowd…fuck some days I almost get off on it. But it’ll kill me to walk out to that ring to the sound of polite applause .Or worse yet, cheering brought on by pity. Cause it will happen if I don’t know when to stop, one day I’ll just be another over the hill wrestler who couldn’t move on with his life. It’s better to…to…wait a minute. Seriously this shit is the best that I can come up with? I’m fucking Dan Black for crying out loud, and I’m debating shooting myself in a bar that should be condemned? Just because I lost my United States title I’m ready to give everything up, just so I won’t have to risk losing it all at a later date. That has to be the worst fucking logic that I ever heard! So what if I listen to the radio one day and hear them talk about how “Dan Black should retire already.” And so what if I end up going online one day, and read that I should make way for the younger talent? With a gasp I tear the gun from my mouth and throw it onto the bar. “You can go ahead and throw your life away if you want Yuri,” I tell him coldly, “but if you think I’m willing to risk my life in a place like this you’re crazy. And if killing yourself is really the best idea that you can come up with; at least treat yourself to some quality vodka before you do.” I can feel him glaring a hole into my back as I head out of the bar. I ignore it and focus on flagging down a cab; I’d rather get out of this place before I get shot by a random bullet. As I step into the car I hear a gunshot from the general direction of the bar. I guess Yuri didn’t feel like drinking that vodka after all. I can’t stop looking at my phone. I know that I should probably think about this decision more in the safety and comfort of my own apartment, but it feels right. More importantly, if I stop to think about it, I may talk myself out of it. No, I have to go with the way my gut feels now without any hesitation. Quickly I begin to punch the numbers into my phone. For some reason my heart is pounding wildly; and I almost hope to be directed to voicemail. But no, Dave’s frantic voice is jabbering in my ear. “Dan where the hell have you been? I’ve been calling you for hours!” “Shock therapy Dave,” I answer with a forced smirk. I can’t show any sign of weakness or he’ll pounce on it until I change my mind. This has to be an order, not a suggestion. “Any I’m on my way home now. While I head there, call Caldera and get me a kickass contract for my return to the GCW. When I get back I’ll call you and we’ll talk about the best way to shoot me straight into title contention. “Ok.” I stare at my phone in surprise. “That’s it? I was expecting a big long argument about why going back into wrestling would destroy my life and how I should try to learn to golf or some shit like that.” “Well while you were gone I got some news. Some huge night club franchise that stems five cities is trying to muscle into NYC, almost directly where our three clubs are at. We may need the exposure GCW will give you to compete. You know, play on your name and all that good stuff. Listen, I’m gonna go and negotiate your contract, call me when you’re home.” It’s nice to know that if nothing else I can always count on Dave’s concern for the good of whatever business venture he’s running to trump any and all concern for my wellbeing.
