Better Off Dead: Part I

16 August 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/16/10 Tequila tainted his tongue as he reached outward for a box of cigarettes. His blind swatting turned up no results, leading Jay to rightfully turn and take in a day he wasn't ready to. He often found it cruel that a day could begin when a night was forgotten, but his qualms weren't enough to reinstate lost time. Nevertheless, he fluttered his eye lids and grabbed hold of his best time-slowing option. He lit one of the two cigarettes that remained and exhaled with closed, bliss-filled, eyes. There was harmony in the room behind closed eyes, the kind that was meant for dreams and death. Harmony, though brilliant, is not perfect when shared however. He could feel the sheets tug and guessed a body was sharing his space. Opening his eyes, Jay took notice of a wide-eyed girl, the kind that seemed to have been waiting for him to wake. Her smooth complexion, brunette lochs, and the pierced portion of her nose all pointed to Caroline Connors, a former lover and recently engaged beauty. Although the tequilla had erased the night, he was able to put two and two together. She smiled at him, surely pleased to see him awake, and touched his side under the comforter. "I was afraid you'd drank too much last night." "Enough to not wake up?" She nodded retaining her smile. "I didn't want to worry. It just crossed my mind." Jay flicked his left brow and took another sip from his cigarette. He glanced at the far wall. "You - uh. You have a good night?" "Was OK. You?" "Heh. Mind filling me in some? I'm tasting tequila." She slid out from the sheets, exposing her uncovered breasts, and placed her back against the headboard. "It's the last time you're having it too. You passed out right before we could finish." "So neither of us finished?" "I wouldn't say that," she replied. Secretly, Jay believed she was trying to reignite the flame. "Oh. Where are you supposed to be?" "I'm away on business. I'm a journalist now. Getting details about landmarks. The Painted Desert. The Grand Canyon. That sort of stuff." "And I bet he believes every second of it," Jay added, thinking of her fiancée's reaction. She blushed and took the smile off the table. For a second, Jay could see doubt in her eyes, the actual moment of realization, and he smoked a longer drag to enjoy it. "I needed to see you again. I just. I just figured that we couldn't end things on the note we struck at the bistro." He laughed, "you sure picked a new note. That's for sure." "Yeah." For a second, they sat in silence. Jay continued to tug on the filter of his cigarette while Caroline found shelter by playing with her hair. The tension was common for other adulterous couples the morning after, but Caroline wasn't used to the part. Jay turned toward her again. "Are you back because I lost to Shipley?" "No. I got the message. Jay Terror doesn't like wrestling. Or wrestling talk rather." "Good. Yeah. I'd rather not. Just last time. Yeah. You get it." "Yeah." "Then why'd you follow me?" "You called me. After the show, the one against Tessa Windsor, you called me and told me to come to Arizona." Jay looked baffled. "I did?" Caroline reassured him, "yeah you did. You said you needed some company. Said you wanted me to come down for a few days to hang around." Although it wouldn't surprise him, Jay guessed that the tequila hadn't blanked his memory that far back. It had been two days since the lights turned out, and in that time he'd spent one night drinking tequilla and the other doing something he couldn't remember. He smoked again, hoping the toxins would jog his memory. "I guess I did." "Are you OK?" "Just hungover. Yeah." "I mean, you seem upset. Scared maybe?" He shrugged, "Not scared. No. Just been out of it these past few days I guess. Been a long stretch of shit shows and shit towns." "Do you want me to leave?" "No, it's OK. Just need another cigarette maybe." He smashed the face of his filter into an ashtray on the table and pulled loose his last stick. He lit it with a lighter he'd kept from Triumph Frost and placed his head firmly against the headboard. Both Caroline and himself looked at the dormant television, imagining different things most likely, and wondering where to go from there. They felt dirty and lost, both needing something to rinse them free of lost nights and guilty sex. "Do you wanna' do something today? Go somewhere maybe? I don't know." "Like where," she asked. "I don't know." She looked at the side of him that she could see and grinned. "Let's get breakfast and pull ourselves together. We'll figure something out." "I can do that." --- Daniel Franco marched off the plane to his own cadence. His pace was brisk, much different than the rest of the herd, seeing as his sole goal was to recapture his own personal bubble. He'd spent nearly six hours in the air and it was only after they landed that he felt most confined. Unlike most passengers, Franco had developed a system which allowed him to travel safely without fear of others. He'd typically establish the first spot in line, which of course meant an earlier arrival, and would make his way to his seat before his neighbors could appear. Seeing that he'd already allowed his sleeping pills to begin their work, Daniel was free to sit and sleep before ever noticing he had company. For five years he had been doing it and for five years, he used the stewardess, making her final sweep, to wake him. In a sense, it was a golden move. Still, no pill or simple trick could help him maneuver comfortably through the human traffic within the building itself. Nonetheless, he watched the floor and checked his watch from time to time. Everything was a distraction if you used them correctly, he'd say. Above all though, Franco survived his trip to the Phoenix Sky Harbor airport through his own mental dilemma. Even while asleep, his mind wrestled with the one name he'd harvested and pruned. Franco, although now a business man, was more than that to a few pupils. He was wrestling-savvy -- a man with his head on straight when it came to the ring, and he helped launch countless nobodies into headline names. Of all his students though, one never left his mind. He could remember the days that Jamison Talley first appeared in his Concord gym. His drunk fists were the most entertaining and scary things he'd seen since tangling with a man known as the body snatcher. Still, Franco found a cure for his alcoholism, limited his cigarettes, and became the father that Jay always wanted. Daniel cared for his progress and showed it when he moved him across the nation to Philadelphia. Talley's name spread like wildfire and he soon became a true independent star on the underground scene. Before long, Franco had managed to contact none other than Steven Caldera and through negotiations, they were able to come to a deal. Jamison Talley was no longer a small-time guy. He'd become Jay Terror. He'd become Franco's prize pumpkin at the big fair. Still, it wasn't the rise that gave him trouble. His mind was stuck on how the two had split. Jay, although always thick-headed, became more than himself. He was an ego with arms and legs and Franco knew that training and practicing would be thrown out the door. Still, Daniel held on for the ride and tried to help him until finally, Jay bucked him straight off the horse and into the dirt. In his defense, Franco promised never to see Jay again let alone work with him. Their time together, although exciting, wasn't worth the lack of respect and second-hand smoke. Then again, Daniel Franco never expected a phone call begging for guidance. The voice belonged to a young woman, one who claimed to be a good friend of Jay's. She explained that Jay had fallen off the track and become something that he couldn't even handle. She believed that the only way to save his talent was to bring him back to his roots and recreate his success when he first entered GCW's doors. Franco was the man for the job undoubtedly. Now, as he grabbed his bag from the baggage claim, he scanned the audience until a sign with his name on it appeared on the horizon. Behind it stood Maggie McVay, Jay Terror's last saving grace. --- The hotel's continental breakfast featured a wide array of fruit along with generous portions of eggs and bacon. It was nearly closing time when Jay and Caroline arrived leading to obvious eye-rolling from the patrons behind the buffet. Jay could sense the hostility but in the truest sense, he simply didn't care. He grabbed a plate for Caroline and then for himself and began to load it with whatever he could. When it came down to it, the remains were between them and the trash. He grabbed a Danish with his bare hands, clearly ignoring the tongs, and took a bite to rinse away the tequila. "Ugh, apple," he said over his shoulder. Caroline huffed, "imagine if it were lime." "Better than apple." "You made that obvious last night too. Apparently lime's your favorite fruit." "It's up there." They shuffled to a table in the corner with two glasses of orange juice. "You think of anything to do," asked Caroline. Jay chewed through a well done stick of bacon and crunched it into oblivion. "You said the Grand Canyon's here?" "I don't want to see the Grand Canyon." "No?" "It's just a bunch of rocks. And it's hot out." For a second Jay remembered how hard it could be to cope with her. She'd grown to be a princess with his money and as it stood, nothing seemed to have changed. For all he knew, her fiancée was even richer than he was when heat became the sole reason for not doing something. "Well that's all I got." "What about a movie or something?" "I'm not seeing a movie," he said between bites of eggs. "Sounds too much like a date." "What do you think last night was?" "Smoothing things over. Remember?" Each of them smiled. Between them, Caroline was more anxious of what she'd done. Still, she couldn't help but admire who she'd done it with. There was a spark somewhere between them, a reason for their initial companionship. It was that spark that made their actions almost OK. Jay on the other hand knew what he'd done was wrong. There was no reasoning it and no pretending. In the end, he knew he'd be bit in the ass, but he was ready to accept full responsibility for it. "Well don't you have to train too?" "I don't need training. It's only Bryan Mayhem." "Like he's not a big deal." "He's big. Not a big deal." "He'll twist you into a pretzel if you aren't ready for him." "I'm ready," he reassured. Deep down, he wanted to stop talking about wrestling and about Mayhem. He felt that she wasn't meant to tell him what to do. In the same breath though, he felt comfortable around her. They shared something new. With that in mind, he allowed her to press further. "Well will you have another shot at facing Tessa?" "I better. If not, I'm not sure how long I'll be sticking around." "Lights turning off was bullshit. You'll get your chance though." "Yeah." Jay ate a slice of cantaloupe and watched the employees break down the bar. They made it apparent that it was time to leave, but Jay ignored them. "How long are you staying out here?" "I figure I'll leave after the show. I told him I'd be back in a few days." "Who is he by the way?" "Just someone I met. Great guy." "He have a name?" Caroline paused and stumbled for a moment, "George. His name's George." "Good name. Good enough at least." "Yeah." There was more silence as the fun of their situation wore off. The disguise was lost and now they were left with a name between them. He was real and so were their actions. Jay finished his fruit and tapped his pocket remembering that he'd run out of cigarettes. Caroline dropped her fork on her plate announcing that she'd had enough. "Mind if we go to the store before anything? I need some cigarettes." "You and your cigarettes." --- "I hope you weren't busy," Maggie said. She was focusing on the road while navigating a map of downtown Phoenix. "I just really think that you're the right person for all of this. He won't listen to me or any of the guys that I know." Daniel Franco tapped his briefcase nervously, "I had nothing else going on. I just hope I can help at all. He seems a bit out of reach." "And that's the problem," she replied. "He's in his own world believing that he's the best there is. When it comes down to it though, he hasn't won a match in the last few weeks. I know he's bottling it up and I figure he's bound to explode if we don't try and help him soon." "Fair enough. What have you tried?" "Very little. I tried to have a friend teach him wrestling. Old school wrestling. He couldn't keep up and decided it'd be best to forget about it. I tried to get on his level and share a few cigarettes. Make him happy, you know? Didn't do any good." "Maybe he's just had some tough draws." "Sure. But nothing he couldn't beat." Franco nodded. What made Jay one of his favorite pupils was the fact that he made his own decisions. Although Daniel had helped him conceptualize a final goal, Jay was always able to blaze his own path. He was creative and witty when the situation called for it. Although sometimes it was a trying mechanism, he enjoyed watching his flair take the stage. "I have a bad feeling that seeing me may make things worse you know." "Really?" "Call it intuition or just knowing what he's like. Either way, I'm sure he'll see this as us calling him weak. To him, getting help makes him feel like he can't do it." "Obviously." "And us doing this might be the last straw before he truly snaps." Franco paused and sighed. "He may hit rock bottom here." "Maybe I made a mistake." "Well," he continued. "I'm not sure about that. I've seen him hit the floor before. He's resilient and bound to bounce back very quickly. In the end, this may be the thing that truly wakes him up. The only problem is if he does end up winning against Mayhem. One win is all he needs to keep riding this horse he's on. Maybe we can throw him off enough to lose focus." "I'm not sure I like that idea," Maggie replied, glancing over at Franco and his black framed glasses. "I know it sounds terrible but it's smart. Jay's different than most people. He thrives on his victories and ignores his losses. Enough losses and he'd likely start to realize that he needs to change." "It just sounds risky. He's ready to walk away he says." "He always has been," Franco ensured. "Jay's been threatening retirement since we worked together years ago. It's how he is. I'm not sure if he needs an ultimatum to fight or what, but I wouldn't take it seriously." "We're almost here." Franco glanced out the window at a tall Marriott hotel two blocks away. He pulled his glasses off his face and wiped them on the front of his tie. "He doesn't know I'm coming, does he?" "He doesn't even know I'm coming. I brought cigarettes though." "The calm before the storm." "Hopefully no storm. Hopefully." Maggie pulled the car into the parking lot next to the hotel. Both her and Daniel exited. Franco carried his briefcase at his side as they entered the hotel lobby. They found the front desk with relative ease and Maggie placed her elbows on the counter. "Hi. Do you know if Jamison Talley is in?" "Actually," said the clerk. "He and a friend just left. I believe they went to the store across the street though." "A female friend?" "Yes m'am." "Thank you," Maggie replied. She turned to Franco and smiled. "Just like Jay to have a woman at his side." "I could have guessed. Excuse me though. I'm gonna' call my fiancée. It's funny. She's also in Phoenix for a few days. I figured I may need to see her with how things are likely to go today." "Of course." Franco walked off and dialed into his phone. After three rings, he left a voicemail. "Hey you. Get this, I'm in Phoenix as well. Checking in on an old friend of ours. Call me back later and maybe we can meet up. I love you." --- Caroline checked her phone and ignored a call. She tucked it back into her handbag and stood behind Jay as he grabbed two boxes of cigarettes from the clerk. "Who was that?" "No one," Caroline replied. "Right. Mind if we go back to the hotel? I forgot Frost's lighter and figured it's right there." "Not at all."
