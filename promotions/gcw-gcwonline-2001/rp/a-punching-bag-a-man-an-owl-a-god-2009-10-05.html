A Punching Bag, A Man, An Owl, A God

5 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 5, 2009 The light in the gym was dim but it wasn't necessary to show the brute force Jeff 'Koz' Kozak applied to the heavy bag before him. First with his fists, then hard with his shins - as if he had a personal vendetta with the chained object or worse, the man behind it. Steve forced his shoulder into the backside observing Newton's laws in action as he pushed back with an equal and opposite force. Still, he found it hard to maintain posture with each swing. Koz's strength grew as he went and by the time he was ready to stop, he landed five final blows that nearly sent Steve onto his back. Now drenched in his body's cooling liquid, Koz stepped back reaching for his water bottle. "You know, I think I'm ready." Steve grabbed a bottle of his own and fixed his shirt, "I'd say. S'like getting hit by a truck repeatedly back there. That night work helping out some?" "Not sure what it is. Might just be the adrenaline kicking in." "Oh yeah," he inquired while taking a seat. "You getting nervous?" "Can't say so." Taking a towel to his forehead, Koz also found a seat. "Just, you know the feeling you get before the big one? The one where you know you're in the best shape of your life and you know you're ready to do great things?" "Honestly, no. But that comes with experience." "I mean, business wise even. About to close that big deal and you're on top of your game. Doesn't matter what anyone says, it's already sealed in your mind." The thought of business deals clouded his mind momentarily as he back tracked to the man in the office earlier in the day. He wanted to tell Koz, he wanted to make sure that he knew what was in store for not only the gym but himself. Still, he couldn't. Not now. "I gotcha. I've felt it." "That's where I am. I'm just in a zone. I'm training harder and thinking smarter than ever before and it's paying off. I found power from god knows where and I'm using it in new ways that I didn't even know possible." "That's good news. Great news even. Just keep it up." With that said, Maggie McVay found her way behind Steven placing her hands on his shoulders. She offered a gentle grip of his blades before taking a seat beside him. "I think you might be sweatier than Koz, Steve." Koz smiled, "Might as well work both of us while we're at it, you know?" "Hey, you act like I need some work!" She patted his stomach jokingly, "Oh yeah, you're real firm down there." "Yeah yeah." Changing the subject, "How you feeling Koz?" "Actually, just got done telling Steve about it. Plain and simple, I'm ready." She looked back at Steve, "You tell him?" For a second Steve winced thinking she was going to ruin things. Thinking back to earlier though, he realized there was no cause for alarm. "About the lights?" He asked hoping he was right. "The lights?" "Yeah," she started. "Steve said he was letting you train at night." "Oh right. What about it?" "The lights are being left on and well, it's showing in the bills." He looked at her with a confused eye, "Are you sure it's me? I mean, I always make sure I get everything. Even the bathroom lights." Maggie shot Steve a satisfied look before raising her brow at Koz, "I don't know who else it would be. You're the last one in at night for all we know." "And for all I know, I make sure things are off. I'm sure of it." "Fair enough," interjected Steve. "I guess we'll just have to start checking the cameras a bit better. That's all." Mags nodded as Steve stood. "You mind stretching him out Mags? I'm going to the office to get a few things sorted out. Have a call or two to make." "Surely," said her with a grin. Steve kissed her forehead before turning his back and marching to the cubicle in the corner. His pace was slow, like a man on a mission who didn't mind taking the extra minute to execute it. Mags watched as he disappeared behind the door and then turned back to Koz who was already on his feet pulling his leg upwards stretching his thigh. Koz wasn't the biggest guy in the gym, clearly. What he had going for him was better than size however. In essence, Jeff Kozak was a system. He had a physique, sure. He even had the muscles to flex in case he found the need. What separated Koz from the rest of the gym was his decision making. At times, it seemed as if he was able to slow down everything to the point that conscious thinking was easily attainable. He could duck under punches, refrain from throwing a caught leg kick, and even avoid dropping to the ground for a submission. He simply knew what the outcome of his actions were before doing them. Those skills were ones people couldn't learn; they were what a person was born with. As his story goes, he's always been able to predict the outcome. Even from a young age, he was able to reason things out. Into the present, it's the reason that it's hard to find fights for him. People know his story and understand how he works. When it comes down to it, fighting Koz is an automatic loss. Especially in the shape he was in. Mags looked up his leg and over his abs to his face. He had gentle features that were easy on the eyes. Koz didn't carry fight scars or punched in ears. He was a pristine babyface that the women seemed to love. She grinned, "How's your mother?" Koz switched legs, "She's doing better. The doctor says that she's been happier than usual and making progress. Just a tough situation, you know?" "Of course." She felt for him. If they had one true bond, it had to do with their father's and their untimely deaths. Both chose suicide as an option and both chose to do so within a three month span. For Mags, her mother wasn't around to feel the depression that Koz's mom did. Still, Maggie knew how much weight it placed on his shoulders. "When's the last time you saw her?" "Last week I went in - Thursday actually. It's just weird seeing someone like that so out of it - almost zombie like." "I could imagine. I don't know how you cope so well honestly. I know I'd be locked in my room still." "I have my days, we all do. I guess I just come here and forget about everything. I'm able to blank my mind and find happiness out of hitting the bag. I can come here and feel as if I'm happy enough for the both of us and that makes me feel like I'm doing my part. It's hard to explain." Maggie could feel a tear in the pit of her eye but refrained from using it. "That's true courage. It's how I know you'll win this fight. You just have a different drive than everyone else. There're so many people wrapped up in winning that they forget what they're fighting for. You, you know exactly who you are and what you want. It's a priceless trait." "Thanks." He took a seat on the mat again, this time stretching his left leg forward and reaching for his toes. "Is everything OK with Steve?" "Why do you ask?" "It's just, I don't know. Something seems out of place with him. I can just tell." Mags scratched her head acting as if she didn't know about the dilemma. "He hasn't mentioned anything. I bet he's just fretting about the bills or something. You know how he can get." "I'm being honest you know. About the lights, I always make sure they're off." "I believe you. It's just a slight cause for alarm if it's not you. It could be eating at him a bit. The last thing we need is a break-in right now." "That's the last thing ever." "Right, of course." Koz laid on his back and Maggie moved forward grabbing his thigh with both of her hands. She pushed gently forward toward his head until finding the right position. "Maggie, do you think people are born good or bad?" Taken back by the question, she couldn't help but crumple her face. "What do you mean?" "There's a theory that people are either born good or bad." "Like Batman and the Joker?" Allowing his leg to slowly drop, Koz rested a minute before Maggie took the other thigh and pushed it up. "More or less on a real spectrum. I mean, there are some people that are born to be firefighters and there are some that are meant to set fires. Do you think that it's a trait that you can't change and are destined to do or are you able to refrain and choose the opposite side?" "I don't know, I never thought about it. Why do you ask?" "Not sure really. I guess it's just been on my mind for a while. When you sit back and observe life, sometimes you are able to break it down into small pieces that you can digest and decipher. I was in a coffee shop the other day and I saw a man who was late for work. He ran to the counter, demanded his order and continued to berate the barista saying that they moved too slow. It's those little fires that prove whether you're good or bad in life." She released his other leg and had him roll to his stomach. Mounting his back, she pressed on both sides of his spine realigning it with the skills she picked up in school. "You know what, you're one fascinating person Jeff. Really. I don't see the world like you but I really wish I did." "Try taking a second to focus on someone. Ignore everything but them. You'll start to realize things." "Ok." Her hands crept along his back as she relaxed him, "to answer you're question, I think that they are born with it. There's only so much a person can do to change themselves before one asks himself if there's a point." "You see, I don't know. I can't tell if its that way or people make decisions on how they want to be." "Maybe it depends on the person." "Maybe." She finished the massage and helped him to his feet. He bowed to her slightly to show his thanks. "Back to the house then?" "I'm going for a walk actually. The park has a way of centering me I think." "I could see that." "Tell Steve I said goodbye?" "Of course. Take it easy." "Oh, and good luck with the lights. I'll keep my eyes peeled." "Thank you." With that, Kozak walked off toward his locker leaving Maggie standing alone in the center of the gym. Her mind felt as if it had been enlightened by a new idea, something she wanted to learn more about. Once again, that was Koz for you. *** The Past The sky was a beautiful portrait with streaks of red dashed along lines of blue. Flecks of purple filled in gaps as hints of yellow formed circles in the clouds. A slight cross section of magenta existed between spaces and in the center of it all was a dying sun dropping slowly over the edge of the horizon. Steve and Maggie laid across the hood of their Volkswagen watching the parade of color as it marched back and forth in magnificent patterns. Their hands clenched together as their eyes followed a plane looping in and out of the canvas. "This is perfect," said Maggie. "I figured you'd appreciate it." Steve smiled slightly but kept his eye sight above them. He hadn't taken in the scene long enough to look away. "You came here when you were younger?" "A few times, yeah. Not for this though, mostly for the owls in the trees. There's a remarkable number of them that nest here apparently. I guess I was always curious about them." "Owls? Since when did you like owls?" "I told you," he said. "When I was younger. It started with a white owl I first saw outside of my bedroom. It was the most beautiful creature I had ever seen and I saw him five times in that same week. My mother didn't believe me but I knew what it was. I saw it." Mags turned to her side and looked at his unshaven face. The stubbles were growing as she looked at him and by the time they left she knew his facial hair would be lethal in close distances. "Well I believe you. Have you seen any by our home?" "Actually, no. I haven't seen any for nearly fifteen years." "That's some time." "I know. I guess coming here, I figured we'd spot one maybe." She turned to her stomach now and faced into the forest behind them. The trees were thick and leafy shadowing the inside dwellers from view. She scanned the branches still checking for anything to make Steve happy. He continued to stare at the sky. "We should adopt an owl." "Adopt one?" "Sure. It'd be our pet, something for you to see and play with. You know?" "You can't keep an owl like that. Owls need open skies, they need freedom. The minute you coup up an owl is the same minute that you regret it." She faced him again, "You've tried?" He looked away from the horizon finally and dug into her eyes with his, "Nope. It's just a truth that I know. They're majestic creatures of the night, not lamps or couches." Mags turned back to the sky, "Well I tried." He gripped her hand tighter and began to slide down the hood of the car toward the grass. She came with due to his pull and they met by the headlights. Using his other hand he grabbed around her waist and began to move her body with each touch on her hips. Her head bent forward and laid on his shoulder. "I appreciate it. I love you Mags." Her eyes were on the sky as a swooping creature came across her line of sight. It's cylindrical body glided above them extending its wings fully. She smiled at the sight of the owl but refrained from telling Steve. Deep down, she knew that he didn't need to see another. It was her whom he wanted to see one. *** Back to the Future Steve paced the floor of the office with the door locked and windows blinded. His hand sat uncomfortably on his hip as his other scratched his head. Anger swarmed his green eyes clouding them to a muddy grey. Every bone in his body was upset with what was going to happen. The fact of the matter was that there was nothing he could do to stop the annihilation of McVay's. He thought about fighting it and bringing it to the courts. If anything, it would postpone the destruction and allow the members to accept the loss and find a new place to train. Fighting it meant exposure though, unwanted exposure at that. He didn't want to be the front page nuisance. Ditching both of those options left him at a crossroads though. How could he salvage a good thing when bad people had the ball in their court? He was far out of his league when it came to legal handlings and knew nothing of the actions he could take to force control in his hands. Even a buyout was out of the question considering the size of the gym's membership. In the end, he took his hand from his hip and had both meet in his hair. pulling slightly, he hoped he could find a bead of sense through it all. Nothing arose. If anything were to be done, it would have to be done soon. As he sat again, he shut his eyes and prayed. He hadn't talked to god in ages but the time called for desperate circumstances. If he couldn't do nothing, perhaps the man above could.
