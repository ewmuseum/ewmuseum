'The measure of a man is the way he bears up under misfortune.' - Plutarch

Date: Ummm, November. Tuscan, Arizona - 2nd August, 2009 “What. The. Fuck.” I wailed, bashing two balled fists into the canvas and glaring at the referee. That was when I saw his foot draped across the bottom rope. The crowd roared but for their hero, not me. Prickly red heat spread beneath my skin. Anger. Got up and dragged him to the corner. Bastard tried to break loose but the Hex Breaker had taken a toll. Clenched a closed fist. Stupid junkie fuck; should've known he was beaten. Threw a punch. No hesitation. Bone shatters on impact. Then jeers. Vitriol from the front few rows as crimson spilled from his crushed snout. It took a noise I'd not had aimed at me in years to realise I'd overstepped the mark. Andy Murray was the good guy, the guy who never plays dirty, but not that night. There I was, stood in his ring – before his crowd – with his blood on my hands. Referee got in my face but “disqualify” was all I heard. Tried to reason with the guy, tried to plead my case, but he wasn't getting a word of it 'til the Touch of Red knocked me to the deck. Covered; leg hooked. One, two, three. Ding, ding, ding. Failure. * * * * * Tallahassee, Florida - 1st November, 2009 “That the last of them?” I ask, pushing another jam-packed cardboard box to the back of the boot. I stand-up, and my aching back prays for a “yes” as I straighten it. Luckily for her she nods the response I was looking for; she'd have been carrying the next one. “That’s all.” Drop the hood; it closes with a thud and the impact judders along the car's dodgy suspension. I look at her; she's brushing a few rogue strands of glossy brown hair from her eyes as the autumn gale howls. She’s looking healthier than she has for months, though dishevelled by the weather. There's colour in those cheeks again, a spark in her eyes, and the weight she’d lost to stress and dejection has found its way back I try to lift her spirit; “thanks for coming.” She's hardly said a word since arriving, and what she’s said has been about as vibrant and full of life as a Transylvanian graveyard. Given the circumstances, I can’t blame her. “It’s good we can do this face-to-face. I know it would’ve been easier to use some firm.” “Well, we didn't exactly part on the best terms. I didn’t want to end two great years with an argument in a coffee shop.” “You’re right.” She looks up; it's the first time we've made eye contact since I told her I never wanted to see her again, and it feels good. But there's no way I'll ever be able to trust her again, not after what happened. “It was great for so long, Kay. But you know I can't ever forgive you.” She swallowed. Nodded slowly. “You don't have to tell me that. I know it was wrong – of course it was wrong – and if there was any way I could go back and erase this, believe me, I would. But I can't. I did it, it happened. And it kills me that all I’ve got left are ‘sorrys’, but—“ Kayla stops when I place my hand on her shoulder; she'll only make her stupor worse if she continues. “Kay, it's fine. It's been three months, I just needed some closure.” We draw close to one another and embrace; not of affection, but of resignation. Slowly we come apart again and she walks round to the door, feet crunching on the dusty gravel below. “Take care of yourself.” A fractured smile draws itself across her olive features as she pulls the car door open. “Goodbye, Andy.” She puts a foot into the car and slides into the driver's seat. As she grabs the door handle I know I'll never see her again. “I don't blame you for what happened,” I bellow over the roaring wind. “You shouldn’t either.” I know she hears every word as she hesitates before closing the door. But close it she does, and soon the engine's growling and the car's crawling down the driveway and out of my life, taking Kayla Jarosz and the baby that never was with it. My eyes follow the car’s path until it disappears. The wind blows a cluster of dead leaves around my feet, the season's change having long since stripped them from the trees and turned their greens to golden browns. * * * * * Berkeley, California - 26th August, 2009 Dude was all over us. Outta the starting blocks like a greyhound, a guy who I hadn't given a minute's notice had hit the ring and was throwing punches like his life depended on it. Hard to fight back, guy was fucking relentless. But I was driven. I knew all it would take was one big move on this new guy, and then I'd have my conqueror alone. DDT. Down went Kingsley, then I saw him across the ring. Our eyes met before we clashed and my heart started to thump. Couldn't let him beat me again. Just couldn't. Attack. Attack. Dukes up, defend. Comeback. Bulldog to the mat. Damnit, foiled again. It's alright, though. Not a big move, I can get up, I can recover. Tried to sit up and that's it hit me. Leg drop. I'd been rolled out of the ring and the bell had struck by the time I'd realised what'd happened. Stood on my own two feet outside as he and Kingsley went at it inside the ring, mocking me. The feeling I got when I broke the junkie's nose comes back to me as I turned and kicked a dent into the ringsteps. Immediately a flock of security guards appeared to usher me backstage, but I was already on my way. No way I was going to stay and watch one of those clowns hold my title in the air. * * * * * Miami, Florida - 3rd November, 2009 There's no way that Jason O'Neil's feeling this apprehensive. Ten minutes 'til my fourth GCW World Title defence and I'm a wreck. Sweat's sliding down my forehead like someone's installed a faucet under my scalp and my heart's pounding so heart I can feel it in my throat. All I wanna do is bury my head in the sand, forget the other night even happened. Brush it under the rug so I can go out there and do what I do best, but I can't paper over the cracks. Not this time. Try to stand up but it's no good; Bambi's legs shake and quiver beneath me, too weak to sustain my 275lbs, and I crash back onto the bench, a mess. “C'mon,” I mumble to myself. “You gotta do this.” And Lord, I knew I did. I lost to Chris Bagwell last year because I couldn't shake the anger for what he'd done to my family from my head. I lost to LeStatt Knight in February because I was too scared of losing myself to his evil. I lost twice to Aaron Nothings, first time 'cause I thought I was the big-leaguer and I could just stroll through this little indy guy, second time 'cause I was so consumed by my quest for revenge that I almost forgot Terrence Kingsley was even in the same ring as me. Now I'm on the verge of losing a title I fought to capture for two years because last night, my best friend in the business told me she was in love with me. For the first time in forever I don't know what to do. I must've called her twenty times over the last forty-eight hours only to be ignored or diverted straight to voicemail every time. Tried to go to her hotel room but she'd switched buildings, and finally, when I did track her down it was too soon to her match. She had to go out and wrestle. She couldn't have changed her mind in the space of two days, surely? No. Things like that don't just come and go – this had been brewing for a while. Chances are she's just as flustered as I am. It had been the heat of the moment, after all. People says things they don't want to when they're under the microscope, it happens all the time. But she said she loved me, damnit. Why would she do that if there wasn't something behind it? I push my head back against the wall and groan. If there was a mirror in the room I'd break it; I should be getting ready with a few last-minute stretches. This should already have been dealt with, and I should be all-set to go out there and whip all ninety thousand of those fans into hysteria, just like I do every week. But no, here I am. Sitting in my ring attire with a heavier weight on my mind than anything a wrestling wring could bring. Closure, Andy, I tell myself. Closure. A knock on my door. A technician pops his head through and tells me I've eight minutes left. It takes all the effort in the world, but I get myself up. Taking the belt with me, I leave the locker-room with my heart in my throat and no clue what I'm gonna do, but it's not the wrestling ring I'm heading for; I can't wrestle a hall-of-famer like this. I've gotta find Vivica J. Valentine. * * * * * Got my shit together and headed out of the building with a storm cloud hanging over my head. I heard the commotion as soon as I reached, but it didn't slow me down 'til I hit the parking lot. “NOTH-INGS! NOTH-INGS! NOTH-INGS!” Only about twenty of them surrounded the new two-time champ, but their voices resonated around the lot's hollow shell, making their number sound five times larger. They were taunting me; I was face-to-face with one of my career's greatest failures, and it's more than I could. Reached the car. Engine grumbled into life and I drove away. I was done with being Just Wrestling's also-ran. * * * * * Tallahassee, Florida – Today “Make a list of everything that's bogging you down. Write them down. Then, go through them one-by-one. Do what you think needs to be done, then score them off.” That's what my sister told me. Something she learned on her psychology course, apparently. Usually I don't go for all that fancy mumbo-jumbo, but she was the only one of us smart enough to make it to university and I'd tried everything else. Worth a shot, right? So I made my list. Kayla and I made peace. I don't think I'll ever speak to her again, but parting on our own terms feels so much better than leaving certain things unsaid. I wish her well, truly. I called my ex-wife. Now she gets to see the boy whenever I'm on the road. It's only an extra day or two each week, but he's happier, she's happier, and it doesn't make much of a difference to me because I'm not there anyway. This week on WorldWide I shook hands with LeStatt Knight. After six months of war, he's a man I'll never trust, but have gained an immeasurable amount of respect for. And now Vivica J. Valentine's on my arm. Some say I've moved on too soon, but right now it just feels right. That's all that matters. Now I've just got one thing left on my list. One more problem to overcome and I’m free. I know I said I'd never go back there, but seeing as I can't trust the guy to show up in GCW anymore, I guess I go back to his back yard. I pick up the phone and dial Shipley's number. Thanks, sis. You're a lifesaver.
