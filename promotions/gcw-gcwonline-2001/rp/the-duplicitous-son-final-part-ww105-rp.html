The Duplicitous Son, final part. (WW105 RP)

undated

Date: 2 weeks previously. Sfinari, Crete Two weeks earlier. Late afternoon. As the sun ceaselessly followed its unchanging path from east to west, the tiny bay where we lay our scene, just outside the small town of Sfinari on the Greek island of Crete, gradually grew lighter, a silent, unspoken crescendo from the almost crepuscular gloom of morning to the resplendent brilliance of the late afternoon, when the entire bay was at last bathed in light. Even the cynical mind of Thom LeStrange found itself in no mood to point out the obvious scientific flaw in that saccharinely romantic notion (that the sun doesn’t move from east to west, but rather that it is the earth itself that rotates), as even he himself had succumbed to the beauty and tranquillity of this daily event. It was now two whole months since his supposed "return" to the GCW, if indeed one could call it that. The wrestling press, GCW fans less than sympathetic to the unpopular Englishman, and even a number of GCW employees had been quick to scoff at LeStrange’s less-than-impactful return to the squared circle. He had re-appeared with little fanfare in the Dangerous Games tournament, to which he contributed little before being eliminated, and then, at least to casual eyes, had disappeared back into the ether again. Whilst the basic elements of this analysis were indisputable, the truth was somewhat more complex and somewhat less pathetic. As it transpired, several months out of the ring under suspension, without any kind of training (near-death, drug addiction, subsequent rehabilitation and a period of sustained counselling unsurprisingly hardly allowed much free time for exercise), had left Thom with a great deal less muscle mass and fitness than he remembered having before. His spirits apparently (and understandably) buoyed by having overcome such difficulty, and anxious to return to a familiar, structured life, he had persuaded Steven Caldera and the GCW top brass to allow him to return as soon as possible. Exhausted and gasping for breath after what was hardly a great length of ring time at Dangerous Games, LeStrange had accepted that perhaps he needed a little more time. Although his suspension had officially been lifted, the agreement had been made that he would take another month or two off to focus on re-gaining his fitness and preparing himself once more for the rigours of the ring. Besides, a little longer to focus his mind and fully relegate what had happened to the past could do no harm. And so it was that Thom had relocated to a tiny bay outside Sferina, hidden away on the far Western coast of Crete. The bay in which he now resided belonged entirely to him, something made possible by two factors ? the first that it was a tiny inlet with space enough only for one large villa, and the second that his bank balance, already astonishingly healthy, had been swollen further not only by the inheritance of his parents, but by the sizeable insurance payout given to him after the disastrous fire which had destroyed Biddenham Manor, the LeStrange family home. Thom had settled into a tranquil but highly demanding routine, and was arguably in the greatest shape of his life. Upon his arrival in the GCW, he had been an undeniably athletic specimen; lean and fast but with a reserve of great natural strength and a compact frame. He had not gained any great unnecessary new bulk, for this would be detrimental to his primary asset in the ring ? explosion and powerful quick-twitch abilities. Instead, through an almost militant regime, a rigorous diet, all accompanied by some less traditional techniques, Thom had succeeded in finding an almost perfect balance between increased muscle mass and uncompromised agility. Now no part of his body was extraneous, unnecessary flesh or fat, his entire form was sinewy, dense slabs of muscle. However, mindless muscle-building had formed the very smallest part of his exercise program. By dedicating much time to practices such as yoga and pilates, Thom could now boast an almost unparalleled core strength ? invisible but deadly ? as well as his impressive outward physique. Moreover, he had spent his time in Crete pushing his stamina to limits he had never previously imagined could exist. Be it swimming for miles out to sea and back until his entire body cramped, or running up and down the craggy terrain nearby carrying the heaviest objects he could clutch until he vomited, LeStrange now believed himself capable of wrestling an entire match and hardly tiring. Apart from the most obvious advantages, one benefit of this hard-earned stamina was that it was one less preoccupation in the ring ? with little concern for fatigue, he would be better able to focus on systematically dismantling his opponent. In fact, this was the last aspect upon which his new regimen had placed emphasis: the importance of physical and mental wellbeing. By lengthy experiments with relaxation techniques, with baths of different temperatures, with essential oils, plant extracts and vitamins, with meditation and near-hypnotic treatments, Thom had succeeded in honing a method which relaxed his mind and body completely, leaving his joints supple, his muscles more elastic, and, most importantly, his mind trained on one single subject (i.e. his opponent), to the exclusion of any and all other distractions. *** The warm azure waters of the tiny bay were usually calm, still at first glance, but upon further inspection actually lilting lazily in towards the beach, miniscule waves breaking (although this was an altogether too forceful verb for the delicate lapping of the water on the sand) with a rhythmical sound that was arguably more peaceful that stillness itself. However, Thom LeStrange tore a sharp line of disturbance through the waters as he swam furiously towards land, on the return leg of his routine swim to and from the buoy which sat a good few miles out. As his powerful legs (and powerful they were, for this is where the Englishman derived his incredibly explosiveness) pounded up and down the water churned and frothed, leaving a wake a small speedboat would be proud of. His arms were a flurry of motion, tearing through a rapid circle; slicing through the water before him, and once submerged pulling his frame onwards. With clockwork regularity and precision, every three strokes his head would snap sideways out of the water, gulp a sharp breath of air, and plunge back in again. He did not relent from his furious stroke until he was physically forced to, when the water became too shallow and his hands and feet were slapping wet sand beneath him, leaving him beached. With one deep breath as his only rest, he pulled himself to his feet and strode ashore, enjoying the release of endorphins with which the completion of his exercise had rewarded him. This was his new drug. By the time he reached the stone steps back up to the villa, his feet were coated with sand, which had adhered to his sodden skin. At the top of the steps was a small shower, which he switched on, stepping under the stream and grimacing as the freezing cold water kissed his skin, knowing that it would help his muscles recover and flush out lactic acid, and that when he warmed up again, his blood flow would increase and his circulation improve. Thom twisted the tap to halt the flow of water, stepped out, and padded back towards the villa, where he would shower properly and dress. Now that he was tucked away in Crete, he had begun to dress in a fashion different to that which the GCW faithful had become accustomed. Out of the public eye, he had less of a need for bespoke suits, and despite it being January, the middle of the Mediterranean was still warm, and so he taken instead to wearing thin linen trousers, sandals, and thin, light open shirts. Today however, was different, for he was to have company. A popular industry magazine was sending a reporter out to Crete to write an article on LeStrange, to catch up with his progress a few months on from the series of tragedies which had befallen him, to give the wrestling world a glimpse of the new, healed Englishman, and most likely to try and prise out some information on a potential return. A stickler for dressing for the occasion, but mindful of the heat, Thom had decided today to try and find a sartorial compromise, and after his shower he decided to dress himself in some smart slacks, a pair of moccasins, a smart open-necked shirt, and a cravat. Once dressed and made presentable, he set about making preparations for his visitor. *** The intercom rang, stirring Thom from a moment of reverie. He crossed the open plan sitting room-cum-kitchen and responded to the call. After verifying that the caller was indeed the reporter he had been expecting, (he introduced himself as Clark Nelson), the Englishman pressed a button, and the gate at the bottom of the driveway swung open, allowing the journalist to enter. Thom then strode out onto the driveway to greet his guest as he arrived. "Mr. Nelson, welcome! I trust you found your way ok?" "Fine, the directions you sent were spot on." "Glad to hear it. Do come in and make yourself at home." The pair entered the villa, and Thom ushered the reporter to a comfortable sofa, plied him with a gin and tonic (for refreshment after his journey and to give the interview a more informal atmosphere ? to "lubricate the proceedings" as LeStrange himself would say), before seating himself in a sofa on the other side of a low coffee table. After pleasantries had been exchanged, Thom had urged the reporter to pull no punches and ask whatever questions he desired, and Nelson had prepared the tools of his trade ? notes, Dictaphone, pad and pen ? they began the meat and bones of the interview. "So, Thom, you look in much better form than when we last saw you. How do you feel?" "Much better than I have done in a long time. You all saw the state into which I allowed myself to descend, both mental and physical. I’ve been training hard, and feel fitter and stronger than I have done in my life. When I joined GCW I felt I was in good shape ? but only sufficiently ? only as fit and strong as I needed to be. Now I feel I’m at my peak, the best I’ve ever felt. But more importantly, I believe myself to be much healthier in a mental sense. I feel the entire process has given me greater self-awareness, greater clarity. I also feel more focused, more driven, more... Well, one is loathe to use the word “ruthless”, but when the cap fits..." The reporter nodded. LeStrange was renowned for being an arrogant, conceited, supercilious individual, difficult to talk to without a constant stream of self-aggrandisement. Whilst he was lauding himself now, it seemed true. A glance at the man’s physique showed that there was no arrogance in his statement that he was in peak physical fitness. Something about the way he spoke seemed that the near-condescending egotism with which he normally spoke was absent, replaced with a more measured, discerning parlance. "If you don’t mind, Thom, something the wrestling world would like more insight on is the incidents that befell you a while ago. If I am overstepping my boundaries, I fully understand." "Mr. Nelson, you are overstepping no boundary whatsoever. Part of the grief process is acceptance, and I am proud to have been able, with the benefit of time of course, to have looked back at what happened from a more detached standpoint, and really think deeply. Ask any question you desire." Clark Nelson took a deep breath ? despite the Englishman’s assurances, these were thorny issues, that needed delicate handling. True, LeStrange seem composed, genuinely prepared and able to talk on the subject, but still one didn’t like to re-open old wounds. Taking a sip of his gin and tonic, he continued. "If you could, Thom, give our readers a brief outline of what happened." "Some months ago, I was informed that both my parents had died in a tragic car accident. Understandably, I was shaken to my core by this, and used some coping methods which one would not recommend ? a highly-regimented combination of denial, alcohol and recreational drugs. However, despite this, I was just about able to continue working and functioning as a human being. Several months passed and I continued in my normal lifestyle. Well, not normal, as I was becoming wilder and wilder. What I mean is that I continued my normal routine ? I attempted to continue my life as normal, and continue working. I felt that if I continued as always, I could cling to normality. It was a form of denial. I refused to go home and deal with the usual post-death administration ? wills, sorting through belongings and so on. Our family home became nothing more than a spacious squat. Then, I was informed that my family home had fallen victim to fire. The official report stated that it had burned down accidentally ? drug users were in the practice of lighting candles, as the electricity had long since been disconnected. All of our family possessions had been locked in the cellar ? as I had not returned to deal with them, friends collected everything, placed it in the cellar, and locked them there, until I decided to sort through them. Somehow it was all untouched by the squatters ? priceless heirlooms, silver, and even worthless nostalgic items such as photos, family trees, and other such miscellanea. I believe our family butler, George, who has a special affinity towards the family, myself especially, took it upon himself to protect these items. However, they were not safe from the ravages of the fire. With my parents dead, my home and all my possessions destroyed, I felt almost as if I had lost my own identity. Although I denied what had happened, distanced myself from anything to do with my past, there was reassurance in the fact that I knew I could return when I was ready. However, this was snatched from me. From that moment, my behaviour grew increasingly erratic, and I lost control of my consumption of substances, and my mental health deteriorated. Ultimately, I overdosed on heroin and nearly died." Thom paused for breath at this convenient juncture, and for a moment there was silence as he reflected. Nelson said nothing. The honesty of LeStrange’s depiction of the events had taken him aback. The wrestling world knew Thom LeStrange as an insufferably self-important and odious man; he would ignore the negatives and emphasise the positives as much as possible, and even when there was no escaping some damning accusation, mistake or embarrassment, he would shift the blame, or make excuses. The open self-criticism he had just witnessed was almost disarming. Nelson wondered whether he should be cynical about the change. Breaking the silence, he pressed on. "And what happened then?" "What one might call an “intervention”. Friends, colleagues and superiors forced me to rehabilitate, to seek help. They did it admirably ? they knew I would not respond to advice, to suggestion, even to imploring. They had to make me do it ? blackmail me if necessary. Mr. Caldera suspended me without pay ? the best thing he could have done ? he took away the routine that fed my denial, and made me face the situation. I was allowed to return only if I passed drug and mental health tests, which I believe I am obliged to continue to take to assure I do not allow myself to slip again." "After a long process of drug rehabilitation, grief counselling, and therapy, I finally believed myself able to return. However, as was evinced at Dangerous Games, whilst my mind was in a better place, my body had taken rather a beating. And so I decided to take a little longer off, to train hard, and to fully move past what had happened. And now I am as you see me." Nelson nodded. "How do you feel about all that has happened, almost half a year down the line?" Thom paused a moment, considering his answer, cocking his head and gazing past the reporter’s head into middle distance. A moment passed, and then he brought his gaze back down to Clark Nelson’s eyes and replied. "A smorgasbord of emotions, in truth. I feel I’ve finally taken the truth of what happened in stride, and so whilst I obviously feel great pain and what happened, I’ve gained acceptance, so I can look back with a level head. I also feel a considerable shame for the way in which I reacted to the events. Lastly, I also feel some shred of pride that I have not only survived, but learnt from what happened, and I believe there is no arrogance in saying that." "Of course not." Again, Nelson was taken aback by the disarming humility and honesty that the Englishman was displaying. He narrowed his eyes, wondering if he should attempt not to be taken in by it, but it seemed genuine. Perhaps LeStrange truly had changed. If anything was going to change him, it would need to be something as cataclysmic as what had happened. "Ok, Thom, moving on, can you tell us what sort of training regimen you have been following here in Crete?" "It’s been comprehensive. Obviously I’ve been doing normal weight training, but the bulk of it has been some different approaches ? lots of cardiovascular training, and lots of work with stretching, yoga, and so on, along with some mental training techniques as well." Nelson nodded, grateful for the turn to lighter topics, and the conversation became faster and more light-hearted. "What are your thoughts about goings-on in the GCW in your absence? Have you been following?" "Well, obviously before my rehabilitation I was somewhat preoccupied with self-destruction, but since then I’ve an eye on what’s been occurring. I believe the federation is really clicking along nicely. NC-17 is going to be the pinnacle. Certain members of the roster are really making strides. It’s impressive. I’ll have my work cut out for me when I return." "And on the topic of your return... Any clues as to when that will be?" "Well, it’s not been made official yet, but I suppose I can give you the inside scoop. Worldwide 105, the week after NC-17. Singles match against Lantlas Anduril. I am leaving Crete in the next few days. I’m ready, and I cannot wait." Nelson raised his eyebrows and smiled, pleased to have this hot piece of information for his article. Hopefully it would be printed before LeStrange’s return was officially announced. He smirked before asking his next question, knowing it was a topic which was bound to merit a controversial answer, a hot topic whose undoubtedly caustic response was bound to rope in readers. "What do you make of Andy Murray’s ascension within the GCW, given both your personal history and the fact you started out at the same time? He’s become a major factor and is knocking on the door of the highest echelons?" "Well, obviously there’s no love lost between us, but one can’t argue with the run of success he’s had of late. I shall say one thing though, I am relishing the opportunity of facing him again. I hope with all my heart that at last we may face each other in the ring. He’s on top form, and I believe I’m in the best shape of my life. I believe it would be an intriguing battle." The smirk sank from Nelson’s face, replaced with an expression of bemusement. If there was one topic that was bound to awaken the sharp-tongued, acerbic LeStrange of old, it was Andy Murray. The Englishman’s response had been unpredictably level-headed, even somewhat respectful. The interview continued, with Nelson remaining taken aback for the remainder. Half an hour later, when Nelson was satisfied that he had enough material from which to write his article, he thanked Thom for his time and hospitality, and made his goodbyes. He collected his belongings, and was ushered out of the door. As he strode down the dusty gravel pathway to his rental car, he tried to take in the incredible change he’d witness in the formerly rather loathsome Englishman. As Thom closed the door, his cell phone rang in his pocket. He removed it, and looked at the name flashing on the LCD screen. George, the family butler. He flipped the phone open and raised it his ear. "George." "It’s done. All the belongings from the house are now stored safely in a warehouse outside Birmingham." "Top man. Did you fill the house with replacements before the fire?" "I did everything exactly as instructed. I made several visits to the local dump and filled the house with discarded furniture, decorations, and trinkets. The fire department believed the charred remains they found were the true family belongings." "Excellent." Wandering back through to the sitting room, Thom took his glass of gin and tonic from the low coffee table and enjoyed a long, contented sip of satisfaction. "And what of the fire? I trust you managed to make it look accidental?" "Of course. Fire department never had the slightest suspicion." "Fantastic." "How’s everything on your end? "Perfect. Everyone ate up my supposed breakdown and inspiring, if not horrible saccharine, recovery exactly as I’d hoped. And of course, you know me, never one to say no to dabbling in the odd substance or two. Gullible fools. Although I must confess, I do believe it was an Oscar-winning performance." "I’m sure it was." There was a long silence. Thom, revelling in the perfect execution of his horrific scheme; George anxious, words of preoccupation dancing on the tip of his tongue. After a protracted silence, he summoned just enough courage to spit them out. "Thom, I have to say something. I’m uneasy with what your parents." Thom was irked, and his irritation was evident in his voice. "George, quite apart from the fact I’m rewarding you more than handsomely from my inheritance, if you followed my instructions exactly there is no way neither doctor nor policeman can link their death to you, or me for that matter. We made it look like a perfect accident, of that I am sure." "It’s not that, it’s... We have blood on our hands. How can you live with that?" "George, I hardly need to remind you what they did to you. They refused to pay for the experimental treatment for your wife. You had to watch as she died a drawn-out, agonising death before your very eyes, and to rub salt in the wounds they gave you one day off, for the funeral. They killed her, and didn’t give you time to grieve. You have your rightful vengeance, and I have taken what’s mine on my own terms, by my own hand, rather than waiting to inherit it, like a pathetic, inert, unambitious little parasite. George, take your money, find a new life, and never give this another thought." There was silence on the line. George nodded slowly to himself, remembering the pain of the last eighteen months of his wife’s life. With a heavy heart he accepted what his young charge had said. "Very well." "Goodbye George." Thom rung off, and drank the remainder of his gin and tonic in one long draught. He stepped out onto the veranda and looked out over the water, the last time he would do so for quite some while, he imagined. All the pieces of his plan had finally been completed. The long days of his youth, the anger engendered by struggling to find motivation to achieve anything when his entire life was already provided for him, robbing him of any chance of the satisfaction of earning anything for himself, of accomplishing anything on his own merit, had now been vindicated. Thom LeStrange smiled at his own diabolical genius.
