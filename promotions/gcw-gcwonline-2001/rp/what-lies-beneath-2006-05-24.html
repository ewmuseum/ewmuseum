What lies beneath...

24 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 24th An exasperated sigh escaped from The Rogue Paladin as he slumped back into his leather chair, dumping the book he'd been reading from onto the floor. It crashed into a pile of many others similarly discarded. Tolkien, Salvatore, Niven, Hawthorne, Elliot, Steinbeck, even Douglas Adams; all lay in a heap that resembled one of those multi-car interstate pile ups that make the news every winter. It wasn't working. His computer sat lifeless on the desk in front of him, it's plug having been pulled hours ago. The Paladin spun around in circles staring at the multitude of literature that sat useless on his shelves. Useless because none of it called to him, none of it gave him any peace. The sick burning feeling down in his gut refused to be pushed aside. It demanded to be dealt with or unleashed, but it would not be pacified or altered. It wasn't fair. After months of work on his new study, the perfectly round room at the center of his new home, you'd think he'd be able to enjoy it. He'd spent months on the design, bartering away a signed copy of Streams of Silver to Peter Jackson for photos, schematics, and technical drawings of the Hobbit homes from the Lord of the Rings. Extracting what he could from the photos and drawings he'd made his own modifications to the design. Three concentric rings nestled into the earth at the top of a hill, the study at the very center directly beneath the apple tree that was just beginning to bear fruit. He'd spent the week before Meltdown loading box after box of books onto those shelves. The week after was spent bringing in the rest of his meager belongings to fill the remaining rooms. The worst had been trying to fit his bed through the round red door. It just wasn't fair. Sitting in the middle of his greatest creation yet, he felt no joy. The only thing he felt was an insatiable need to.... to.... Hell, if he'd known what it was, he'd have started doing it by now. Desperately restless now, The Rogue Paladin reached for the only solution he hadn't tried. His brand new Dean From Hell (Dimebag CFH replica) sat in it's stand just on the other side of his desk. He reached for his remote and activated the Randall amplifier that was connected to the wireless system he'd installed. Picking up the guitar and tossing the strap over his shoulder he pulled the pick from between the strings and strummed a few open chords, waiting for the tubes of the Randall to begin powering the recessed 4x12 cab in the bookshelf across from him. Slowly the sound built, through chimey clean notes and past a fierce overdriven growl into a roaring wall of sonic energy awash with distortion. He closed his eyes and began to practice a scale. 'Relax' he thought to himself. 'Meditate on some scales and patterns and let this sort itself out'. A reasonable plan. But whatever it was that was welling up inside of him had no intention of being 'sorted out.' It grabbed The Rogue Paladin by the hand and guided him back to the bass end of the neck. First position and simple barre chords formed with his left hand. His right no longer alternate picking the higher strings but convulsing in a syncopated palm-muting blur. Eyes still closed, he felt himself beginning to lose control. It was like some other thing had taken command of his being. In his mind, war drums pounded out a menacing tempo, driving his frenzy deeper. In his mind he could feel the blade in his hand, sweat oozing into the handle and forcing him tighten his grip as he swung out at faceless shadows that swarmed around him. Windmilling wildly he slashed again and again, feeling the bite of impact and recoil through the blade. Steel on flesh, tearing, ripping, rending. The salty warm spray of blood spatter on his face. He screamed violently, incoherently, and the drums pounded faster still. The faceless now fell around him, their cloaks of anonymity now shattered and torn as the bodies they had veiled. Almasy, Ambrosi, McFarlane, Shadow. The other wraiths now fled, their own ends crossed and their weapon now set against them. Their flight was fruitless as he ran them all to ground. His blade, his face, his very being soaked crimson to the bone in spilled blood and gore. He felt himself swing out, for real this time, and felt the guitar smash through the plasma screen that had been on his desk. He opened his eyes to see his arms smashing the guitar into the ground again and again and again. The shrieking feedback of the amplifier echoed through the room so completely that it took him more than ten seconds to realize he was screaming. <<<-tG->>> At the old house down the hill, a rising sound pulled Meatshield Trainwrecker's attention from the piece of armor he'd been polishing. Curious, he strode to the back step and looked out the screen door up at the new home of his friend and compatriot. Though dulled by distance, he could hear the frantic sounds. His pulse began to race as adrenaline surged through his veins. He could hear the raging screams and wild anger on the wind. But most of all, in his head he could hear the drums calling him to action. As the sound died out abruptly, Trainwrecker kept his eyes focused on the house up the hill. He knew well the questions that would soon be coming to the mind of his friend. Quietly he urged him to heed this advice. 'Use it Dave... Use it...'
