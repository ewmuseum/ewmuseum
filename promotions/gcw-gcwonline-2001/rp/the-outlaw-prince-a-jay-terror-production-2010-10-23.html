The Outlaw Prince (a Jay Terror production)

23 October 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/23/10 This is the story of a man who abandoned conventional morality for the acquisition of absolute power. This is the story of the Outlaw Prince. * * * ”Men are so simple and so much inclined to obey immediate needs that a deceiver will never lack victims for his deceptions.” * * * 10th May, 2007. “Three cheers for the champ!” Jay tried his best to mask his smile when he walked into his locker-room, but the gathered crowd’s hip-hip-hoorays made it impossible to resist. He came through the door with sweat-soaked hair matted to his face and limbs like concrete, but the weight on his shoulder made everything better. Terror looked around the room: it wasn’t packed, but everyone he cared about was there. Caroline, Daniel, Sage, and the handful of locals he knew from his travels. Everyone he needed but his mother. “Uh, thanks”. Jay nodded: though three shows into his GCW career he still wasn’t comfortable being the centre of attention. “Thanks a bunch.” He reached for his Zippo and ignited the unlit Marlboro dangling from his lips. A deep, cathartic drag and a puff into the air clear his head, and he sees their faces for the first time. First it’s Dan Franco, offering his hand but instinctively retracting from the smoke. “So soon after the match? Jeesh.” “S’just how I unwind,” Jay said, shrugging. “No ‘congrats’?” Daniel smiled. “Of course, I’m just messing with you. Well done, Jay. You’ve done us all proud.” “Thank you kindly.” The handshake was firm but broken quickly. Caroline bounded towards him, unable to contain her excitement any longer, and threw her arms over his glistening shoulders. Her lips carefully navigated around the cigarette and pecked his cheek. “Whoa, enthusiastic.” Jay’s face widened. “Lemme see it.” “Carefully, she’s heavy.” Caroline slid her painted nails beneath the Television Title’s leather underside and pulled it towards her. Warm fluid dripped down her fingers and she recoiled immediately, almost dropping the belt. “Eww! Sweaty!” “So am I and you still threw yourself at me. Shit.” Jay watched her wipe her hands dry and admired her reflection on the gold plates. He made a mental note to have the Jimmy Thunder nameplate changed first thing in the morning. “Pretty though, huh?” “Not as pretty as it could be.” She smiled and stepped behind Jay. Terror felt the leather sliding around his midriff, before Caroline fastened it behind his back. “There,” she said, re-entering his vision and grinning broadly. “Much better.” Jay put an arm around his beaming girlfriend’s waist and pulled her close. He was dripping with sweat and his limbs would burn like hellfire tomorrow, but he felt too good to care. Jay Terror was well on his way to becoming a household name. If only his father was there to see him. * * * ”A prince never lacks legitimate reasons to break his promise. * * * 16th April, 2008. GCW’s Outlaw, walking through the corridors just hours after knocking Jordan White’s block off, was relieved to find the catering area still open. Nutritionists told him not to eat after 8pm but his mouth was watering and his stomach rumbling. Meetings with Caldera had occupied his pre-show schedule and by the time they’d finalised their plan’s particular’s it was almost time to wrestle. Jay had gone through a long, arduous battle with his former ally on an empty stomach. He was famished. He walked into a room deserted save for a few unoccupied tables and chairs. The shutters had been drawn over the kitchen (the catering staff having long since gone home), but a spread of sandwiches and finger food remained at the top of the room. Jay made a b-line for it and quickly shovelled food onto a paper plate. A tuna-filled vol-au-vent found its way into his mouth and he closed his eyes, savouring the night’s first nourishment. “Someone’s hungry,” a shrill voice snapped. It caught Jay off-guard and sent ripples of shock through his body, but he recognised the cold tones immediately. Hooks of dread sunk into Jay as he turned. “Roberto,” he said. LeStatt Knight sat alone at a table with an empty plate sitting in front of him. Dressed casually in black and grey, Jay’s new stablemate sat forward on a cheap, plastic chair. He took a sip of mineral water and swirled it around his mouth, elongating the silence much to Jay’s discomfort. “Never knew you had it in you, kid,” Knight finally said. Still unsure how to react to a former foe’s civility, Jay said nothing: just stood there, plate full, watching LeStatt Knight watching him. “Cat got your tongue? Figured you’d have more to say given the circumstances.” For all his on-air bravado, Jay still found himself perplexed by Knight at times. Jay didn’t like it when he couldn’t figure out what made a guy tick, and he had absolutely no idea what motivated LK. Still, he moved towards his enemy-turned-ally and spoke briskly. “Think my actions said more than words ever could.” “As big a statement as it was, Jay, a lot of people are gonna want answers.” Knight’s glare intensified as his brow tightened. “You don’t have to give it to them, but you just stuck a knife in everyone’s back and twisted. I hope your skins thick enough to cope.” Jay smirked. “This your way of welcoming me to the Establishment?” “Not my place.” LeStatt’s eyes turned to the table; he sighed quietly. “It was Steven’s idea to get you involved, not mine.” He looked up again. “I can see why. You’re a tough kid, Jay: NC-17 taught me that much and you never struck me as the goody two-shoes type. I just didn’t expect it to go down like that.” Terror’s mind rewound a couple of hours and he cringed when he thought of what he’d done to Jordan. White was a man of similar moral standing. Like Jay, Jordan White wasn’t wired to appease the masses, but he’d won their support through a series of spectacular performances against reviled opponents. Jordan had never gone out of his way to make friends with anybody, but Jay had earned his trust. That meant something until two and a half hours ago. “You gotta do what you gotta do,” Jay said, shrugging. He put his paper plate down on the table and sat down. He kept his distance, though, not wanting to get too close to Knight. “You don’t sound too sure of yourself.” LeStatt was right: Jay wasn’t sure of himself. “It’s just. Eh.” Terror found himself struggling for words. Nothing came to him. Part of him still balked at what he’d agreed to, but a larger part knew that his career would surely flourish under Caldera’s wing. Steven Caldera was a cavalier, a kingmaker: Jay was better off with him than against him. “You’re worried that you’ve upset the fans,” LeStatt said matter-of-factly. “And you have, Jay. I haven’t heard those kinds of jeers in a long time. Nobody likes seeing one of their heroes sell their soul.” It would take a few weeks for Jay to come to terms with his newfound pariah status, but exposing that weakness to Knight would serve him no purpose. Talking with the Hall-of-Famer was much like a game of poker; Jay couldn’t show his hand. “I don’t wrestle to make friends. I wrestle to win.” Knight communicated his acceptance with a nod. He reclined in his creaky chair but never broke eye contact. “It’s far safer to be feared than loved, especially in this business. You’ll come to learn that. Besides, acts of good breed as much hatred as acts of evil. Valentine might have every fool in that arena chanting her name, but you could count her backstage allies on one finger.” He didn’t speak, Jay: just concentrated on swallowing a mouthful of cream cheese sandwich. Sitting across from a man who’d almost beaten him to death six months prior, the newest Establishment member was more than happy to listen to the legend. If the cameras were rolling he’d have to disguise himself beneath a veil of swagger and bravado, but backstage they could be candid. “What’s it like?” Jay asked. “Working for Caldera.” “You’ve been working for him almost a year now. You should know.” “No, I mea—“ “I know what you mean,” LK said, waving Jay away. “You’ll have to find out yourself. I’m not your mentor.” “Seems like he knows what he wants, at least.” “And he knows how to get it, too.” Knight paused and opened his folded arms. “You’ve chosen your path now. You’ve bloodied Jordan and aligned with Steve: there’s nothing you can do to change that now. It’s up to you to live with the consequences. You’ll find out what being Established is all about soon enough.” LeStatt Knight slowly rose to his full height, dusting a few crumbs from his t-shirt. Knight paced away from the contemplative Outlaw but stopped before the doorway. “And don’t worry about the fans: they have Murray to cheer for now.” A few steps later and GCW’s most accomplished grappler had gone. Jay closed his eyes. A plateful of food was the first thing he saw when he opened them again, but Jay pushed it aside. He’d lost his appetite. * * * ”The wish to acquire more is admittedly a very natural and common thing; and when men succeed in this they are always praised rather than condemned. But when they lack the ability to do so and yet want to acquire more at all costs, they deserve condemnation for their mistakes.” * * * 18th July, 2008. Caroline shuffled her final suitcase to the open door and turned to Jay. Her eyes were swollen and red, and her cheeks tear-stained. The past twelve months hadn’t changed the man physically, but he was unrecognisable beneath Rich Rollins’ bandanna and the Marlboro Reds. He hadn’t helped her pack, hadn’t helped mop her tears, and hadn’t even shown her any kind of emotion in weeks. When she told him she was leaving he just stood there and shrugged like it was nothing. Prick, she seethed. Utter, utter prick. Steven Caldera and the quest for GCW gold had consumed him entirely. Gone was the individualistic young man she’d fallen in love with, replaced by a Machiavellian beast only out for personal gain. He’d shunned Frank Black, shunned his fans, and now he was shunning Caroline. The Establishment had devoured him. Jamison Talley was no more: now there was only Jay Terror. Caroline said nothing as she picked up her bags and closed the door behind her; he didn’t deserve a “goodbye”. Jay just sat on the couch, his mind occupied only by thoughts of Vivica J. Valentine’s GCW World Championship. If Jay Terror’s turning point was the night he aligned with Steven Caldera, then Jamison Talley’s turning point was the night he let his girlfriend walk out on him. * * * ”A son can bear with equanimity the loss of his father, but the loss of his inheritance may drive him to despair.” * * * 29th October, 2008. Jay returned from the hospital with a stitched forehead and bandaged ribs. Every inhalation stung his tender ribcage as expanding lungs pushed against bruised bones and the migraine he'd been carrying would've knocked a lesser man to his knees, but he felt none of it. His pain was internal. “You lost,” he said to himself. Arms propped on the dresser, head hung before the mirror, eyes closed. He took as deep a breath as his broken body would allow and stood up straight. Tiny red lines marbled his eyes and the veins on his neck were about to burst. “You fucking lost!”. Terror screamed at nobody but himself. He clenched two fists and bashed them on the dressing table. Jordan White. Frank Black. That fucker. That laconic piece of shit took his title. HIS title. The GCW World Heavyweight Championship was Jay's. He'd defeated Vivica J. Valentine! He'd ended the longest ever GCW World Title reign for the right carry that belt. What gave a man as inconsistent as Frank Black the right to even challenge for it?! A wild fist swung at the wall, tearing wallpaper and blasting through plasterboard. Another. Another. Another until his knuckles were bloody and his wrist was speckled with grey plaster dust. The neighbours would surely have been woken but Jay didn't give a fuck. He'd lost it all – everything – and everybody in the hotel would know about it. He'd been deposed. His alliance with Caldera had failed. Everything he'd done to Jordan White and Global Championship Wrestling had backfired spectacularly. Now he was just a schmuck without a belt and Frank, the man he'd crossed to enter the title scene in the first place, was the king of the mountain. The television got it next. Jay took a share and swung it wildly at the screen. Crrrack went the glass as hundreds of tiny chips landed by his feet. He pulled the chair from the wrecked TV and flung it across the room. It hit the wall hard, creating a sizeable dent whe-- “Jay!” A voice called to him. “Jay! What the fuck?!” He hadn't even heard Steven Caldera enter, but the dressed-down GCW president was over to Jay like a shot. “The hell are you doing?!” Steven cast his eyes around the room, aghast at the damage done (not only to the room, but to his credit card balance also). “Do you have any idea how much thi--” “Fuck off Steve, I don't need a lecture.” “And look at your hands! Jesus fuck, Jay.” Jay glanced down at his hands for the first time, rubbing the bloody dust against his jeans. “Fuck it. Fuck it all. How did you get in here?” “You left the door open,” Caldera explained, lower his tone. “I'd been waiting for you to get back from the hospital when I heard an elephant in a china shop. I know it's tough losing to Jordan, but goddamn Jay, calm yourself down. It's not the end of the world.” “'Calm myself down'?!” Jay's brows raised in disbelief. “I did this 'cause you said you'd make me the biggest star in the business, and now look at me! I just lost to the guy we put in the ground in the first fucking place. Everything we've done over the past few months just blew up in our face, and you're telling me to 'calm down'?!”. “Yes, Jay.” Exhale. “Yes I am.” “Gimme one good reason why I should?” “Because I can cut you loose in a second.” Jay felt as though somebody had tossed a bucket of ice cold water over him, such was Caldera's tenor. “W-wha...” “I'm the fucking boss, remember? I can have you contract shredded right now if you want. All it takes is one phone call.” Caldera pulled his cell from his pocket and waved it threateningly at Jay, who put up his hands immediately. “No, man. You don't have to--” “Then quit acting like a fucking child,” Caldera snapped. “One big loss and you're throwing the toys out of the pram, well boo-fucking-hoo. Do you think LeStatt Knight got to where he is by trashing a hotel room every time he lost a match? Do you think Viv became the greatest GCW Champion of all-time by stropping every time she fell short?” Wordless, Jay let himself fall back on the bed. Caldera's power over him was undeniable. All he could do was sit and listen. “A lot of people believe that the true measure of a man is not how he handles himself during times of success, but how he reacts to failure. If that's the case then shit, Tessa's more of a man than you are.” Steven looked down at his crestfallen protégé and sighed. “Look, I know it's difficult to take, especially when you consider the history between you and Frank. But it's one loss, Jay. That's all. So you've lost the title, but your career's still young. You've only been doing this a couple of years and you weren't going to hold onto that thing forever. Just think how long you'll be able to keep the strap for with a few more years under your belt.” Jay gulped. “I guess.” “It's a setback – a big one, admittedly – but nothing we can't recover from. This is not how a champion reacts to defeat, Jay. Sort yourself out.” Hoping he's said enough, Caldera lets his glare linger on Terror for a few seconds longer, before turning to leave. “Come talk to me when you've calmed down, Jay. Prove to me that this whole thing wasn't just a massive mistake.” * * * ”War should be the only study of a prince. He should consider peace only as a breathing-time, which gives him leisure to contrive, and furnishes as ability to execute military plans. * * * 29th September, 2009. The apartment was quiet. It had been that way since the day Caroline had left, and it wasn't going to change any time soon. Filled only with the fruits of a GCW pay packet, there were no sentimental trinkets, no photos of loved ones, no artefacts of a happy family man. Just plasma televisions, oversized subwoofers, and plump leather couches. Very much the home of a man with no moorings. Jay stood on the veranda and gazed out across the city. The weather was turning cold and the wind nipped his bare arms, but the cigarette kept him warm. Behind him, inside, the Wrestling Channel's daily news digested spun him stories of Jason O'Neil's return and Andy Murray's latest victory. He'd been on the shelf for longer than he cared to remember. Rampage had been the last time he'd wrestled, and he'd barely thought of the business since. It was only when he turned on the television that he was reminded of a world Caldera believed still had a place for him, and it was enough to drive him outside for contemplation. Jay had done a lot of soul-searching over the past few months. Maggie McVay, a friend, had entered his life. He'd helped her combat their shared demon, alcohol, and it gave him a feeling he hadn't felt since the day he'd cracked Jordan across the skull. It felt good to be good again. Things had been difficult since losing the Title. An NC-17 loss to Murray preceded a series of disappointing performances, and soon he found himself more disenfranchised with the business that had made him a fortune. After another lacklustre showing at Rampage, Jay Terror turned and walked away from GCW, not knowing if he'd ever return. But the fire still burned. Though the few months he'd spent without GCW's burden had been pleasant, Jay still yearned for the competition. The thirst for power returned as soon as that GCW logo appeared on the television screen. He tried to forget about it, tried to blot it out, but it was no good. When he opened his eyes it was still there. There was no denying who he was. The Outlaw would ride again. Things wouldn't be as simple as just walking back in, though. He hadn't spoken to Steve since his disappearance, and the few brief conversations he'd had with James Bryan and David Yale were not wrestling-centric. But Jay was a champion. He was still one of the most prodigious talents in the industry, and, perhaps more importantly, he made Steven Caldera money. An awful lot of money. Caldera was a smart man, and although the word on the dirtsheets was that he was pondering retirement, he'd surely realise that a rejuvenated Jay Terror could bring to the table. Jay stepped back inside, closing the door behind him. A Dangerous Games 2009 commercial flashed across the television screen. Perfect, he thought. * * * ”It is not titles that honour men, but men that honour titles.” * * * 2nd September, 2010. The jeering continued long after he'd arrived backstage, pausing only for cheers as Tessa left the ring, and it brought a grin back to his face. Hatred pulsed through the walls. The stomping of feet and the shout of expletives. All of it was for him; the boos, the bile, the bad blood. The crowd's vitriol rumbled through the whole building, shaking it to its foundations, and Jay Terror loved every second. He swaggered through the hallways with the belt on his shoulder. A few distasteful glances bounced off his tangible defiance as he walked back to his locker-room. Caldera was right. Things had finally turned around. Jay Terror, GCW World Heavyweight Champion, was back in the saddle. Suddenly everything seemed worthwhile again. He entered his room and slammed the door closed. This time there was no welcoming party. No Caroline. No Daniel Franco. No Sage Black. Not even Steven Caldera. Just Jay Terror, the tar in his lungs, and the GCW World Heavyweight Championship. He wouldn't have it any other way. * All quotations by Niccolo Machiavelli, and more specifically The Prince. * LeStatt Knight used with full permission from Roberto Abitua. * For the uninitiated, Jordan White and Frank Black are one and the same.
