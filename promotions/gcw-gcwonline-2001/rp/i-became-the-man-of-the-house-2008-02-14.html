I Became the Man of the House

14 February 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 14 "All the world is full of suffering. It is also full of overcoming." Last Time At the advice of a close friend, Chris Bagwell decided it would be best to enroll himself in a couple sessions with a local psychiatrist in order to overcome his past demons and move forward with his life. He arranged a meeting with a psychiatrist who goes by the name of Erin. She's a very pretty girl with an extremely well-maintained body. One would think that her physical attraction would be a distraction to a person like Chris Bagwell. In their last meeting, Chris reminisced about the death of his father. He made it clear that it had been the very first time he'd ever spoken about the event. It was a very painful experience and had really plagued his mind every day since it had happened. Sensing that the telling of Chris' tale brought him to a healthier emotional state, Erin was determined to squeeze more from him. Perhaps Chris' psychological imperfections could actually be fixed? One can only hope to see. Psychiatrist Office, with Dr. Erin "Well Chris, I must say that I'm a bit surprised to see that you made it back for our second meeting." Erin spoke softly and understanding, and with a general concern for Chris' well-being. It was something he hadn't really felt from a person in quite some time. General concern. "I'm a bit appauled you had me pinned as that ype of person, Erin," rebutted Chris. "Well, to be particularly honest, I can't even count how many times I've had clients come and sit in for the first session and then disappear forever. It really hurts to see people holding such disregard for their healing," replied Erin, still with a very apparent feeling of concern. Chris doubted her sincerity, "You got their money. What do you care?" Taking offense to the comment, Erin disputed, "You know, Chris, if I just wanted to take people's money I wouldn't have persued this career. I'm here because I genuinely want to help people. The feeling of satisfaction you get when someone can come to you and say 'THANKS' because you really did something for them is amazing. I wouldn't trade it for anything in the world." Chris doesn't respond, but instead simply lays himself down on Erin's office couch. The soft red velvet conforms around his head as he closes his eyes and prepares himself for his journey back into his subconcious mind. "You're eager to start today, I see," says Erin. "Yeah, I figure there isn't much reason to push it off." "Well then. I'm certainly glad to hear that attitude from you." "As I knew you would be." Erin hesitates and smiles. "Last week, you touched down on an event that was really a key point in your life. It was the death of your father. You insighted me on everything from the day before until the actual death. You opened up to me with your deepest feelings and it seemed like you had a sense of uncertainty as to what would come next. Why don't you start where you left off. Your father has just passed." Chris paused with an uncomfortable silence. He opened his lips but the words wouldn't come out. And finally, they did. "I became the man of the house..." Perspective Shift. Flashback. I became the man of the house. It was a job I wasn't, by any means, ready for. But in a weird way, I knew that my father thought I was ready. He'd still be here if he had thought otherwise. It now became my time to really pull my family together and thrust them forward into the next stage. Unfortunately, that stage was the funeral. I drove back to my mother's house later that afternoon, after sitting in my shower curled up in a ball. I had the shower blasting so hot that I could have boiled noodles as I cleaned myself. But the temperature never bothered me. I was absolutely oblivious to it. There was a pain far greater running through my veins at that moment. No amount of heat could compete with what I was feeling -- regardless of the burns I might've suffered. I remember pulling onto my street and fighting back the tears. I was almost home. I had to hold out. As I came up to my mother's house, I peered around at all the cars. My street was jam-packed. I asked myself if maybe there was a party going on. Perhaps a family reunion, of some sorts? But I knew the only family reunion was that of mine. And it wasn't a reunion... It was a family mourning. Sure enough, there was an empty spot in my driveway that can only fit four. Its as if they had all saved me that spot. They knew I'd need it. They knew that I was now the man of the house and the man of the house, I guess, is entitled to a parking spot. I remember sitting in my car for what seemed like an eternity before I could gain the courage to open the door. It was my moment of truth. This next moment will further sit plastered in my memory forever. I opened the door and I stepped up on my mother's porch. I remember opening the door and there it was... There they were... My family all gleamed into the doorway, from the kitchen, and looked to me. Their faces were all red from crying and soaked with their tears of sorrow. I lowered my head, but only slightly, and I gave them a half smile. They were looking to me for support. They knew I could be strong and THEY wanted to be strong, too. One after another, they each started walking towards me as I did them. We met midway down the hallway and I was embraced with one tightening grip after another. Every single hug screamed help. They each screamed for comfort. And one after another, I bit my tongue. I held my tears. I had to be strong. I muffled through what seemed like thousands of people, but what was really only my immediate family and a few close family friends. And then...there she was. There stood the single most in pain woman in the house. There stood my mother. She walked up to me, together. She threw herself in my arms, and fell apart. I just felt her tears soak through my shirt and drape my skin. She'd lost the man she'd loved. She lost the man who she'd spent so many years of her life with, day in and day out. It was in that moment that I really got an understanding about what life really is all about. It's not about how many girls you fuck. It's not about how many beers you drink. It's not about how many touchdowns you've scored. It's not about how many degrees you earned. Life is about finding happiness. And not an instant satisfaction or a short-lived moment in glory. Its about finding a happiness that never stops feeding you a great feeling. I realized that there are so many pieces of life that we often take for granted. Every moment I was with my father, I was happy. Whether we'd be laughing along side each other or screaming at each other at the top of our lungs - my father had always made me happy. But I couldn't just credit him. It was a family effort. Each of our played our part in helping one another survive. And now he had been called to another place. He was called to a place where he could help somebody else. A place where he could play the role he played so well and help others find happiness... Eternally. And as I ran my fingers down my mother's head and brushed my palm against her hair, I knew that this was the defining moment of my life. My fate was sealed and I became the man of the house. Perspective Shift. This was an overcasting silence in the room for a few minutes. Thinking Chris was finished, Erin went on to say, "So it was just like that. New responsibility now lay on your shoulders and the pressure of that new responsibility was just too much to handle?" "Erin," Chris interrupts. "I know you deal with a lot of mental cases but, believe me, my father's death was not the reason for my breakdown. Don't get me wrong, it was one of the most trying times of my life and really set me acast a new course, but there was far more hanging in the balance." Erin nods her head in compliance, "then please do proceed." Perspective Shift. Flashback. It's been only days since I last sad goodbye to my father and we'd gotten to the point of services. Many look at the funeral services as a type of closure and a last chance to bid your farewells. Although I can definitely see where some would believe such a thing, a part of me still disputes it. Maybe funerals are just an excuse for us to cry one more time and let it all out. Call that closure, if you may. It certainly wasn't an event I wanted to bare witness to. At least not yet. The wake was just that - yet another awakening. My family traveled in from around the state and its surrounding areas. My hometown had never seen so many encompassing its roads. For all they knew, it was founder's day and the township was having a parade. As my family pulled into the lot, we filted into the waiting room of the funeral parlor. With every breathe, I inhaled the scent of flowers. But not just any flowers... those funeral flowers. Its that distinguished smell that you always relate to death or sickness. I've smelt the smell of roses and they're beautiful. It's almost as if the florists sprinkle on a special perfume called 'sadness' to set the mood. Nevertheless, the smell was imminent. As we stepped through the doors and into the open rooom... what would you call that room? I guess in a normal home, it'd be the living room but in the case of a death would it be considered the non-living room? The death room? But we made our way through the doorway and as we did, my mother's legs weakened. I'd been at her side, so she merely grasped my shoulder. I felt her emotions such through her fingertips into my pores. We stepped to the casket and there he lay. Peaceful. Resting. Dead. His lips sewn shut and his nose pinched together. His skin dabbled with some sort of makeup that I know nothing about. They made him look pretty. They made him look pleased and happy with the end result of his life. And rightfully so. I knew, deep down, they did a great job. But I was still a critic. My mother fell to one knee and rest her head aside my father's casket. She mumbled words to herself that were impossible to understand beneath her weaps. I gave her the space she needed, but only for a few moments. I had to help her to her seat as she had many hours left to go. She couldn't drain herself out just yet. As I lifted her by her arms and leaned her against my shoulder, I gradually stepped her back from the casket. "Why?" she asked repeatedly. As much as I wished to give her an answer, I knew it was a rhetoric question. She knew the answer. She knew anything I'd tell her. She just wasn't ready to accept it. But then again ... nobody was. Most of the rest of the day was nothing but repetition. I saw people I saw every single day. I saw people I hadn't seen in a few months. I saw people I hadn't seen in years. I saw old friends. I saw distant family. I saw ex girlfriends. I even saw people whom I hadn't left off on the best of terms with -- but they had the decency to show their respect. Respect is a funny thing, isn't it. A couple of hours went by and I had to step out of the room for a few minutes to catch some fresh air. The 'sadness' scent from the flowers was irritating my allergies. Or so I said. I remember walking outside and seeing my aunt standing off in the distance. She was a smoker and certainly didn't hold well to funerals. She was just like her sister ... my mother. I took particular notice to her heavy coughing, but just brushed it off as the flu. It had been going around at that point and people were popping up sick all over the place. Before walking back towards the entrance of the funeral parlor, she gave one more big and nasty cough. At its conclusion, she spit out a nice wad that was red in color. Maybe it touched her lipstick? Maybe she was chewing gum or sucking on a piece of candy? Or maybe it was blood. I'd soon find out the truth. My father's funeral was the marking of a new era in my life. I became ... the man of the house. To Be Continued...
