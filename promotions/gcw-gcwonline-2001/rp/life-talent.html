Date: 7/12/95 7 Years Old Fondest Memory: Baseball Game 4:30 pm Take me out to the ball game, take me out with the crowd? I truly didn’t care if I never got back. That’s true for nearly all my childhood friends. Kids growing up around my town frothed at the mouth to play sports. Then again, who didn’t? With all that seemingly endless energy, it was written into our genetic code to play. Some of us were multisport talents, while others struggled to grasp one simple play. It was summer of 1995 and just a few short months from my eighth birthday when I got my first taste of sports. I had always been the overly energetic kid, as you probably could have guessed by now. My parents wrestled over what to do with me. Figuratively of course. Now that I was seven, I was finally old enough to play a sport. My parents had always kind of swept the idea under the rug as a “We’ll cross that bridge when we come to it” type thing. I had also been fueling the fire to play? that didn’t help. I wanted to play anything. I didn’t have a specific interest in any particular sport? I just wanted to get my jimmies out. Right after I turned seven almost a year prior to this memory, I remember sitting down with my parents and trying to persuade them. They looked into Karate lessons, however they became fearful that I’d be too destructive and dangerous given both my past and disability. I always comforted them in the fact that if I screwed up, it’d be the end of it all and I would never play again. I was going out on a huge limb no doubt? I didn’t really want to give up something that I hadn’t even experienced before? but it gave me an out if I truly didn’t like playing sports. But you know? I wouldn’t need it. And it was clear as crystal that I’d probably end up doing something stupid. Thank Osh-Kosh my parents were too oblivious to that fact. In retrospect, sports had been my calling. I really thought that it was the perfect fit. With my energy and charisma, I’d be an instant lock with it all. T’was the metaphoric keys to a car, the flame to the fire, the condom to the man with aids. The solution to a problem that would have crippled my life if I hadn’t been allowed on the baseball field. Sportsmanship, however, was something that I would have to learn over time. My parents would carefully research the sporting options for a seven year old. Seeing that I was a Canadian boy, the first instinct was to get me into hockey. Yeah? right? with my two left feet. That was not the reason for my avoidance of a relatively goonish sport. My parents were afraid of three things: skate blades, sticks, and the other kids health. All of which I could hurt, destroy, use and abuse? not in that order. Hockey was out. My parents then pondered bout football. Or soccer to be more exact. It seemed as if we had found a winner. I really didn’t care but I showed disdain for it, like Lacrosse, because I didn’t like the name of the sport. By 1995 standards is was “Homo.” Plus the fact that my parents though I’d use my feet a little too much. Soccer was sent packing. Basketball was next? do I really need to comment on basketball? I think we all know why I wouldn’t have been good at it? I ain’t got no game yo. So my parents looked at “real” football. My Dad played it in his glory years of high school and college. He was a decent flanker who occasionally played safety. His decent speed along with his heart made up for any other shortcomings he had. Sadly, it was the fastest to get shot down because of all the risks associated. With football getting sacked, there was only one sport worthwhile left standing. Baseball. A good, clean, safe game. No real contact? just you, the ball, and the field. Complete bliss. Complete focus. A true gentleman’s game. Screw tennis, golf or bowling? my parents wanted me to have a true team experience, with the least amount of risk. Plus a sport that no Canadian kid could pursue farther than high school. Yup, baseball would let my talent flourish. We went to the sign ups in April earlier in the year and I inked a one-year deal with the North Park Sparrows. It cost 50 dollars. I was happy as a button as I got my burgundy jersey that read “Sparrows” across the breastical. I got number 22 because my Dad wore that number when he played sports. That night we had a special dinner celebration. My Mom ordered my favourite type of pizza: hot. We had the hugest party ever. I was so excited to find out what this baseball thing was all about. I remember my first practice ever. We met at our home diamond, North Park. The most northern park in my region. I was both nervous and scared, as I didn’t know anyone on the team. We started out with a quick lap around the diamond and then introductions. I was quite shy to say the least. Our coach was a nice middle-aged lady who had a son on the team. Her name was Janice Jennings? or Coach Jennings as everyone called her. Her son was one of the better players. Not the best, however. That role was deserved for number 3, Tyler Wade. He was awesome. His bleach blonde hair? his wondrous eyes? his natural ability. It all ruled. Actually? he just ruled. We had three or four good players, but that was it. Coach Jennings talked about how she had been with the team for the last four seasons and each one was worse than the last. She also said that it was her sons last year at this age level and she’d like to do a little better. North Park had a reputation for acquiring all the new players? and therefore never won more than 9 games in a 30 game season. No playoffs? no trophies? no fond memories? just blowout loss after blowout loss. With the 1994 season clearly behind everyone, North Park was looking to rebound from a 1-29 record. And as odd as things were, I’d be a key catalyst to the turnaround no one expected. Our league had 12 teams and only 6 made the playoffs, the top two getting byes. The most feared team in the league also happened to be our cross-town rival? The East Park Gladiators. They had all the glory. 9 league trophies in the past 11 seasons? only lost twice all of the 94 campaign. The undisputed juggernauts of Ottawa neighbourhood baseball. Maybe I’d want to reconsider the “never caring about getting back” part after I heard that. I wanted to be competitive? just not life or death, win or be disowned competitive. I put East Park in the back of my mind as my team gelled together. Before I knew it, it was opening night. Game 1 North Park Sparrows vs. Orton Park Bricklayers I was the starting Centerfielder for a downtrodden club that hadn’t even seen the light from the basement in years. My parents couldn’t care less. They were just so damn proud that I was out there playing sports. The only problem was that I stunk. The first batter of my first inning ever, hit a fly ball right at me. KERPLUNK! It found its way right by my glove and nailed my head! That kid easily got a home run. And I was instantly the worst player on our team. I fared no better at the plate. I struck out every time? and the last time only took two strikes! Figure that one out! Result: L 33-2. As the season wore on, so did our team. We racked up a crisp 0-14 record as everyone tried to stay positive. I was steadily improving as a combination of practice, advice from my Dad, and watching Major League Baseball helped me. Our team was in dire need of more playmakers as Cody Jennings and Tyler Wade accounted for all 20 of our RBIs. Suddenly, in Game 15, we had an awakening. Game 15 North Park Sparrows vs. Ivan Street Armadillos We were getting creamed as usual, however midway through the game something happened. Joey cranked a high fastball down the line and sent Tyler all the way home! The first guy other than Tyler or Cody to hit someone home. We were down 7-4 and it was my turn only a few batters later. The bases were loaded and I had a chance to bring us slightly closer. My coach pulled me aside and asked me to just close my eyes and swing. And that’s what I did. The first pitch thrown to me, I slapped my eyelids shut and swung for the fences. WHACK! I connected with the ball and sent it soaring. I’ll never forget opening my eyes and watching the ball disappear into a small white dot. It sailed over the fence, as everybody watching was silent. They were shocked. I did it! Holy cow! I just smacked me a homer! I can’t believe it! I stood there. Ryan came into score but I was standing in his way. I looked at him and he directed me to first base. I smiled and went on my way. I remember hearing my parents yelling “Grand Slam!” from the stands as the rest of the crowd finally reacted with cheers. For the first time all year, we had a lead? a lead we wouldn’t yield. Result: W 8-7. We had just won our first game to go 1-14 and I was the hero. My team carried me off the field? my parents bought me pizza again. Life was good. I was finally getting the hang of this whacky sport called baseball. We kept being a competitive team after that game but we just kept on falling short. We were 1-28 heading into the last game of the season. Yet another year without a playoff berth or any more wins than one. And all doubt was cast that we could win our last game as we faced off against our rivals, The East Park Gladiators who had destroyed us 49-3 in Game 4. Game 30 North Park Sparrows vs. East Park Gladiators The Gladiators wore a sharp looking yellow, white and black uniform. No one ever dared to make fun of their bumblebee resemblance or else they’d hit you with a trillion seven run mercy innings and not think twice. Their best player also happened to be my longtime neighbour, Bill Lancer. Bill wore number 9, representing how many trophies his team had won before. It was the last game before the playoffs and the Gladiators had flexed their way to a first place clinching record of 29-0. Almost perfect? almost. July 12th, 1995. The memory of my greatest baseball game ever played? Midafternoon. A crisp day it was. The green grass was so lush that you could feel it in the air. The diamond had been freshly raked. The texture of what looked like sandpaper dominated the dirt. The true essence of baseball was swirling even before the festivities of the last game of the season. Sparrows and Gladiators? David vs. Goliath. “Want your water bottle honey?” My Mom asked as I exited the car. “Yes pwease,” I sweetly settled as I pulled out my dirt coated ball bag. We had just arrived after having my favourite lunch; grilled cheese and veggies. I waddled over to our bench where Ryan and Cody were warming up. My Dad slid out of the driver seat and rolled around back to grab some lawn chairs and a book. “Excited?” My Mom asked my Dad. “I just hope this turns out okay,” My Dad worried, “I don’t want to see anyone get hurt.” As time progressed closer to the game, more and more players and fans showed up. Some even had signs that read, “30-0 Lets Go!” They were Gladiator parents of course. I remember when Bill showed up. His Dad egotistically talked to my parents about the odds we Sparrows had of beating his homemade point spread. Bill walked past me with his usual bully attitude. “Hey Smyde! How’s it going” Had a good season with the Sparrows?” He said sarcastically. “Today we’re going to play like Spartans Bill,” I cleverly shot back. He grinned as he chucked his bag down. “You better bring your A game today Smyde,” He bullied, “I’ll be behind home plate all day. Best catcher in the league, don’t ever forget it!” I cringed and volleyed a comment back. “Well see at the end of the game,” I said weakly. We didn’t get out to a good start. They had their ace pitcher, Tommy Summers in nearly all game. They jumped out to a 10-0 lead after two innings. My first at bat went horribly. Tommy toyed with me as he purposely threw two balls outside the zone. Then he beamed one down the middle that I poked to Short for an easy out. My second at bat was a little better as I faced a weaker pitcher. I still popped out but our team was building a little momentum. Each inning it seemed like we were getting more guys on base. We finally broke through after Summers uncharacteristically walked back to back batters, which set up a home run from Tyler. It was 10-3 and we only had two innings left to play. Needless to say we were shocked at how close we were playing the Gladiators. “Clyde” you’re up fourth,” Coach Jennings directed, “And Clyde” make it count.” Tommy sat out the next inning as we squirreled away a few runs. It was 10-5 as I made my third appearance. The pitcher wound up and threw? “SMYDE!” Bill yelled as I whiffed on the pitch! STRIKE ONE! I looked back surprised at Bill. The umpire did nothing. Bill threw the ball back to the pitcher as I tried to remain focused. The pitcher wound up and tossed another hard one. “SWING!” Bill again nagged me into whiffing as the umpire stayed mute. STRIKE TWO! “Excuse me Mr. Umpire sir,” I started, “Can you tell him to stop pwease?” The umpire readjusted his posture and replied. “Lets play ball guys. Just play ball,” The Ump interjected. Yes? the umpires were that scared of The Gladiators too. A record, 5 umpires were fired on the spot in a Gladiator game three years ago and each assigned umpire has been very cautious since. Alright Clyde. They’re playing hardball. Just relax. It’s just you and the ball. Just you and the ball The pitch? “SMYDE SUCKS!” Bill screamed, as I stood there frozen for the called? “STRIIIIIIIIIIIIIKE THREE! YOU’RE OUT!” The Ump signaled as my teams parental units started an uproar. I turned around to face Bill and the Umpire. I felt the tears weld up in my eyes. “FUCK YOU!” I screamed as I tossed my bat down and showed my dormant wrestling aggression. I speared Bill for yet another memorable moment as an all out tussle began. The Umpire dove in right away and pulled me off of Bill but it was too late. Mad chaos unfurled as both benches cleared and a horrific fight ensued. My Dad had that look at my Mom as if he knew something like this was going to happen. Finally both coaches came in and even though they didn’t like each other, they did a fabulous job or breaking up the brawl. Tommy held back Bill as Tyler held me back. Come on fucker. Come get some. Come on bitch. You scared? I ain’t done nothing yet. NOTHING YET? BIG TIME! After our tempers simmered down, both teams headed back to their respective places. The Umpire gave a warning to The Gladiators bench before walking over to Coach Jennings. “Number twenty-two is ejected,” He said. “What?” I yelled, “That’s no fair. Why does he get a warning and I just get kicked from the game!”” My coach sided with me and took a stand. “It’s true,” She defended, “Clyde stays in the game. Period.” The Umpire stepped back and replied. “Only if number nine of The Gladiators says its okay. He was attacked first,” The Ump instructed. And with a nod and a “Yep” from Bill, I was cleared of all charges. Nothing else needed to be said. When we locked eyes you knew it was enough. He wanted another chance at me. And by the way this game was surprisingly being played out; it was all but guaranteed. The score was 10-5 as The Gladiators took the field for their last at bats as the away team. They had a chance to put the game completely out of reach by scoring two runs. Somehow they only mustered one run. A Bill Lancer triple drove in their star pitcher, and track star, Tommy. 11-5. A 7 run mercy inning would not only end the game, not only double our wins from the season before, but ruin perfection and send The Gladiators to the playoffs with a dirty taste in their mouths. We didn’t care that they were more than likely going to run away with yet another league trophy? we had one chance to be the only team to beat them. Coach Jennings gathered us up one last time and gave a spine shivering pep talk. “Listen guys. This is it. It’s all set. For some of you guys that have been here and endured awful season after awful season, just go out there and prove that we can finish strong. To the newcomers, will you make a memory today” I mean in all my years around this team? around The Gladiators? we’ve never beaten them. Never. Not once. Everything else doesn’t matter. The fact is that they are scared. They are scared of us,” She said as I totally didn’t expect that, “Read my lips. East Park is soft. They are nobodies. And for this half inning” we’re going to be better. We’ll be the true Gladiators on the field! Let’s go!” And with that, Summers struck out the side and we lost. Haha? just joking. I remember getting up after that speech as inspired as I ever was. Did she just say what I thought she said? The Gladiators are soft? “Scared” Of us? How? No way! But? does it really matter? I mean? nothing else should matter except for right now. This moment. This half inning. It could be ours. My mind fought with itself furiously as our team began to rally. A few explosive hits from some unexpected players rang us up to 11-7. Then 11-8, and 11-9. Somehow Cody Jennings nailed a homer off a Summers fastball! And just like that it was 11-11! Truly, nothing else mattered. The faces of each Gladiator began to dwindle as they only had one out and were up against our best player in Tyler Wade. “Let’s go Tyler! You can do it!” People shouted from the stands as this truly apocalyptic event was unfolding. However Summers would rebound with a profound stand. His impeccable pitches were just unhittable as Wade went down swinging! Two were out and the raucous Gladiator parents were going wild. Next up was little Timmy Crane. Our spark plug at third base was having a sensational game. “C’mon Timmy, you can do it!” I said as everyone watched with intensity. Summers wound up and sniped a ball on the outside corner but Timmy swung at it anyways! The ball jaunted down the left field line fair as Timmy rounded first, then second and slid under the tag at third! A marvelous triple just set us up in the most clutch situation in North Park history. Who was up next though? “Hmmm,” The Coach walked over and checked the list, “Clyde. You’re up!” She said. My Mom grasped her hands together as I looked down the bench. It suddenly became sooooo long. I scooted off the bench and made the long haul down the side. I grabbed a helmet and a bat but just before I walked out there, the coach stopped me for a second. “Clyde,” She knelt down and lowered her voice to my level, “You’ve grown a lot over this year. You might not always be the best, but you have a lot of heart that’s for sure. You showed a fiery side out there last time. Do it again” but this time, channel it into something positive. Don’t let anything touch you out there? use your talent.” I nodded and walked out from the fence and stepped into the batters box. “Pffft,” Bill exclaimed, “Easy out boys. Get those t-shirts ready!” I remained calm. Not rattled. I did not gratify him with a response, for I was Mr. Clutch. STRIKE ONE! The ball came in so fast that I barely had time to track it. STRIKE TWO! What just happened!” And just like that, I stood there and took two excellently thrown pitches. The count was 0-2? tied 11-11, one man on third, 2 out. The last pitch of the season was on its way and only one thing was going through my mind. Take me out to the ball game? hmmm hmmm hmmm hmm hm hmmmmmmmm That lovely tune of “Take me out to the Ball Game” echoed through my mind. It settled me. And gave me enough strength to get through it. The ball. My bat. I swing. CRANK! I smashed it! I smashed the ball so hard that it spearheaded the dirt just one foot from home plate! I stood there in shock until? “FAIR BAAAAAALL!” The Umpire grumbled as I dropped my bat and launched myself to first base. Everything seemed to move in slow motion as Bill threw his catchers helmet off and stumbled to get the ball. I was hiking it down the line faster than a kid version of Usian Bolt. Timmy immediately raced towards home as Bill picked up the ball. Blinded by his ego to upstage me, Bill left the dirt fluttering as he ripped the ball from the ground and beamed it over to his first baseman. I stretched out with my left leg as I crash-landed into the bag just as the ball was arriving. Timmy slid home just to make sure he got there but the first base umpire had yet to determine my status until he screamed? “SAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAFE!” The Sparrows bench and fans erupted as I just beat out the throw to first base and we had won the game! The bench cleared as The Gladiators angrily threw their gloves to the ground! “We won! We won!” Everyone cheered as we were much more emphatic about our team winning than stopping a team achieve 30-0. I remember Tyler, our best player, rushing over and picking me up saying, “You’re the best!” And lifting me up on everyone’s shoulders as we went wild! 2-28? who cares! We were indeed better. That half inning was ours. Ours forever. My Dad turned to my Mom and couldn’t believe what just happened. “Wow! That’s our son!” My Dad proudly stated, “That’s our son!” The Gladiators left the field losers that day. But that wasn’t the most important thing. It was the fact that my team, The Sparrows always kept playing. No matter what. I discovered talent all right? talent that I’d use in the rest of my life. Result: W 12-11. I certainly wasn’t the best. Still aren’t. There’s still a lot I can learn. But for now, I’m taking my life, one half inning at a time. You should too. --- Good thing my Dad said figure skating was too fruity for his son or you might have just heard a story about how I nailed a slachow jump naked.
