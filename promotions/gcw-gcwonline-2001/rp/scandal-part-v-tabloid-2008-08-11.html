SCANDAL PART V: TABLOID

11 August 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/11/2008 “Fuck you,” McKail mumbled through thin lips, making sure he didn’t lose the cigarette he already had perched there. The reporter blinked and glared back at him questioningly, his Dictaphone held high in the air and his eyes wide and innocent. Must be a fuckin? newbie, McKail decided. First day on the job and hasn’t got a fuckin? clue. What the hell is this fuckwit expecting? Some sort of grandiose speech riddled with pleasantries and bullshit straight from some two-bit PR guy’s word processor or something? “What was that?” He asked. But McKail ignored him. Instead, he thrust his hands into his zipped jacket and head down, fought through the bitter November wind toward the small town diner across the way. McKail found himself entirely unsurprised to find the idiot following him, hell, he almost expected it. Thing of it was, it’d been the better part of three weeks since the alleged “scandal” had hit the presses and the local news networks and all the hassle that came with it was already wearing real thin. He’d assumed the election and all the bullshit that came with it to shake these bastards off his back, but he assumed wrong. They just sent complete bastards who didn’t know what in the hell they were doing instead. Granted, not quite as an annoyance as some of the more ruthless bastards that’d set upon him when the news initially broke, but an annoyance nonetheless. “Excuse me,” the reporter persevered, jogging to match McKail’s pace. “You didn’t answer my question.” McKail allowed him a side glance, before shaking his head in silence. “You’re ignoring me, aren’t you?” The reporter sighed. McKail rolled his head and continued to walk, taking a` lengthy drag of his cigarette in the process; somehow it helped against the cold. “Jesus Christ!” The reporter exclaimed. “Why do I always get landed with all the assholes?” McKail pretended not to hear him. No point making or getting angry, this prick wasn’t worth it. Besides, he was being an asshole, he enjoyed being an asshole and he’d be one until the day he died. Or at least, that was the dream. McKail climbed the small array of steps that lead to the diner entrance, but found himself stopped by a tug on his arm. “What the fuck?” He glanced at the hand latched onto his coat, then the arm it belonged to and finally the face of the idiot reporter, only this time he seemed a little pissed off. “Who the fuck do you think you are?” The reporter yelled. His Dictaphone wasn’t even on show anymore and that made McKail smile a little. Looks like I broke him, he thought staring blankly at the irate reporter. “Ignoring me like that! I gotta earn a living too you know! I don’t deserve this kind of treatment!” McKail rolled his eyes. Great. These fucks are reporting total fabrications about him and yet this reporter thinks he’s got a right to be pissed off about it? Yeah, right. But the reporter continued, unabashed. “Now, we are going to have this interview and you are going to manage a whole lot more than just simple monosyllabic answers you understand?” McKail simply glared at him and after a time, realising the reporter’s tirade had concluded, grabbed a hold of the hand latched onto his jacket and removed it with natural ease, before grabbing the idiot by the forehead and pushing him back, down the steps and back onto the icy parking lot. No, he thought, entering the diner with a smile, no need to get angry at all, but violence is always a great facilitator. *** McKail took a booth by the door and waited for waitress to come over, whilst finishing off the last stub of his cigarette. Out of the corner of his eye, he saw a few people glare at him and then try to whisper to others and failing miserably. In the few weeks since this disaster had unleashed itself upon his life, he’d already become good at overhearing his name in conversations between strangers regardless of distance and volume; it was only a matter of time before paranoia would set in. If it hadn’t already, that is. “Hey man,” a voice greeted. It was some guy in the next booth along, glaring over the seat at him wide eyed and giddy green plastered across his face. “You’re on TV?” McKail sighed. TV? What in the hell had the local news network latched onto now? More dead bodies from the past? Hell, there are plenty to go around. He wanted to ignore the whole damned thing, but couldn’t resist anyhow. When he saw it, he wished he had resisted. It wasn’t the local news at all. It was national. They’d picked up the story from the local network and running with it. “Fuck...” “I won’t stand for no cussin” in here, boy,” the waitress told him. She waited for an apology, but didn’t get one. She didn’t try to hide the contempt in her voice from then on. “What you havin?”” “Cheesburger,” McKail told her, glaring at the TV and then at the folk not even trying to hide staring at him any longer. “Better make that to go.” The waitress nodded and went about her business, whilst McKail buried his head in his hands. What the hell am I going to do now? This thing is national, that means I’m gonna be dogged everywhere I go. Fuck. After the news broke the last time, he went back to the AFWA show and they didn’t want to book him anymore, despite the fabricated news stories stating otherwise. Is this it? Is my career over? Can’t be. They don’t have proof because none exists, but will that really matter? If they throw enough shit against the wall, some will stick and judging by the mental state of the general public they’d lap it up. The waitress slammed down the foam container which presumable held his cheeseburger and shook her head. “Is what the news lady say true, boy” If it is, then you oughta be ashamed of yourself boy!” “I get the feeling,” McKail began, scooping his burger and sliding out of the booth, “I’m gonna be hearin” that a lot from here on out.” He left.
