Date: 3-16-09/Present I call it the Death Dream, named after the 1974 horror film of the same name*, only my version is two words instead of one and has the word ‘the’ slapped in front of it for titular purposes. I’m not sure when I conceived it -- at some point during the course of this life, no doubt, and probably at a point where one contemplates the meaning of life, what we’re here for, what our purpose is, etcetera. People who believe in Fate, they contemplate all these silly things endlessly. They over-think, over-analyze. People who believe in Free Will are so consumed by their ability to choose, they forget why they’re choosing anything in the first place. There’s even some crazy sect of narcissists who believe Fate and Free Will are intertwined, that each choice you make sets you on a new predetermined course. What they’re saying is life is one big choose-your-own-ending storybook. I believe in being. I just…live. I say. I act. I react. I don’t analyze or ponder or contemplate. There’s no time to do any of that. This is the Death Dream -- a barren, sterile, meaningless existence that ends when you die. In reality, everyone’s fate is death, but people who believe in Fate tend to overlook death as the ultimate course of life. After all, we’re only living so we can die. Put it this way: The more time you spend thinking about what is and why things exist the way they do, the more time you spend fucking up the universe, the more time you spend fucking up your universe. This is why I don’t believe in Fate or Free Will and why I’m not apart of that crazy sect of narcissists who believe in a hybrid of the two. This is why I believe in the Death Dream. After all, everything must come to an end. Everything must die, right Mr. Fox? Mr. Taylor? Mr. Anduril? -------- Previously… I take a swig of my beer. “Whatever you say, dude. How much you gamblin’ with?” Dice sort of chuckles. “Funny thing, man. I’m not even gambling with my money.” “What the fuck does that mean?” “Yeah, dude, see that guy over there.” He points to a guy at a blackjack table across the way. He’s short, stocky, brown hair, horseshoe balding pattern, no chin. “He walked up to me and gave me like 10 grand. In cash!” Dice pats me on the shoulder, like he can’t believe it. “Then,” he says, “he told me I didn’t have to pay him back. He told me it was an investment. And I was like, ‘I suck at roulette,’ but he didn’t seem to mind.” The look on my face is blank. “This isn’t ideal,” I say. ------- In the grand scheme of the universe, there is no reason for anything. Nothing we do really matters. Let’s say you believe in Fate as opposed to Free Will. If you believe in Fate, nothing you do will change the course of anything, because you believe that course is predetermined for you. Contrarily, if you believe in Free Will, nothing you do, by definition, has any greater meaning to life as you envision it. The choices you make are just choices you make, because you believe you’re not on some predetermined course, some Divine Path. I find it unnecessary to define life and try to solve all its mysteries and label things as being one way or another. Labels don’t define things or people or events or ideas. They simply make those things or people or events or ideas more accessible to whomever it is that’s forming their opinion of those things or people or events or ideas. If nothing was accessible to the average feeble mind, there’d be no reason for accessibility. Furthermore, it’s a general misconception anything is accessible. People make life out to be some complicated phenomenon that few, if any, have the secret to, like it’s some ancient hidden code written in a dead language that can only be deciphered by technology that doesn’t exist yet. Fact of the matter is nothing is ever that complicated. Things get complicated or convoluted because we make them that way. We over-think, we over-analyze and that leads to overreaction. Sometimes…I guess in times like these, there just aren’t answers. And there won’t be for quite some time. Sometimes things just are. If everyone listened to the Beatles, we wouldn’t have these complications or overreactions or misconceptions or arguments about Fate and Free Will. No one would be murdered in the name of God. No one would be cast aside because of their race, creed or sexual orientation. Sometimes you have to let it be, and if you let it be, it will be. You will be. Furthermore, it’s a general misconception the Beatles knew what they were talking about. ------ Jason Mezzonote told me once everything happens for a reason. He’s a man of Fate. I, of course, shrugged that statement off because, as you now know, I’m not a man of Fate. If anything would make me readjust my beliefs, however, it’d probably be Mezzonote’s presence at the Playboy Club in the late morning on Monday, March 16. It didn’t bother me Dice was gambling with money he’d be expected to pay back if he lost it, even though he was under the impression from Mezzonote himself he wouldn’t have to. It didn’t bother me Mezzonote was still sticking his nose in my business. What bothered me was I hadn’t done anything about this cat. I should have thrown his fat ass over the balcony in room 707 in Planet Hollywood when I had the chance. My mom always told me not to make idle threats. What’d she know though, the cunt. What happened next that late morning in Las Vegas was like learning Pythagoras’ theorem** the first time -- an exercise in patience, understanding and general principle. ----- Euclid was a Greek mathematician who lived in Hellenistic Alexandria during the reign of Ptolemy I***. Although many of his mathematical results had been stated by earlier mathematicians, Euclid was the first to show how geometrical theorems could be fit together into a comprehensive deductive and logical system, thus giving him his long-standing nickname: The father of geometry. In early Euclidean geometry -- a term not coined until the early 19th Century -- Euclid’s theorems were derived from five axioms, or postulates. The axioms were stated in terms of construction, the most popular one being the fifth and final postulate -- the parallel postulate, which states if two lines intersect a third in such a way the sum of the inner angles on one side is less than two right angles, then the two lines inevitably must intersect each other on that side if extended far enough. There were also five common notions that existed in early Euclidean geometry, three of which I’d like to point out now. Notion #1: Things that equal the same thing also equal one another. Notion #4: Things that coincide with one another equal one another. Notion #5: The whole is greater than the part. These notions are self-explanatory, and they only apply to this narrative because they have meaning within its structure and message -- meaning in regards to now, to the immediate future, to the extended future, to forever. Since Euclidean geometry is constructive in nature, as the five postulates show, we can logically deduce that not only do certain things already exist at any given level, but we have methods for creating and recreating them at any given level. Just another complicated phenomenon that few, if any, have the secret to. Perhaps the most important aspect of Euclid’s axiomatic system, however, is how he proved the system to be true in the first place -- through contradiction, or, as it was known in Hellenistic Alexandria, through conflict. ---- Mezzonote has goons, henchmen. And they had us, me and Dice, tied up back-to-back in the basement of The Palms. “We’re gonna play a game,” he said, lighting up a stogie. “Haven’t we been playin’ games this whole time?” I said. Mezzonote exhaled, said, “Absolutely. But you’ve been winnin’ and, well, we can’t keep playin’ that game.” “I like that game.” He smiled at me. “And I like this one.” Dice, I could hear his heart beating over my own. He stuttered a bit when he said, “I didn’t mean to lose that money, man. I was way up and then I ju--” “The money is of no significance, Tyson.” Mezzonote puffed, paused. “You’re simply just” -- exhaled -- “guilty by association.” I felt Dice’s heart rate increase. If you mapped out an equation of the tension level in the room it’d look something like this. I’ve known him a long time, and Dice can be a bad dude when he wants to be, but I know he’d never been in a situation like that -- tied up against his will, looking at an unwanted and unknowing debt in the face and, with the way things seemed to be headed, paying that debt with his life, and me with mine. At least that’s how it seemed to him. Me -- I knew better. Mezzonote doesn’t have the stomach or the icy blood to do such a thing. Nor is the loss of money the reason for all this. As he said, money is of no significance. Fact of the matter is this short, stocky, bald man is one of the most intelligent men I’ve ever come across, and if he had us tied up in the basement of The Palms with no intention of paying our debts with our lives, there had to be something else in play. --- Every time a thunderstorm occurs somewhere on Earth, similar happenings are occurring in Space, whether it be a meteor shower somewhere in a galaxy far, far away, rainfall on Mars or Jupiter or HD 189733 b**** or a hailstorm within a black hole. The most prevalent form of similar happenings is bursts of solar radiation. While these bursts aren’t a form of precipitation, they are created in the same way -- a change in atmospheric temperature. The most intense burst of solar radiation in five decades accompanied a large solar flare on January 20, 2005, dumbfounding NASA scientists and effectively disproving their space weather theories. The solar flare occurred at 2 a.m. Eastern Standard Time, tripping radiation monitors all over the planet and scrambling detectors on spacecraft within minutes. Scientists at NASA commented on how surprised they were to see how quickly the particles from the burst reached peak intensity and arrived at Earth. Normally it takes two or more hours for bursts of solar radiation to reach maximum intensity at Earth, but the particles from this particular flare over four years ago peaked about 15 minutes after the first sign. By definition, these accelerated bursts of solar radiation are reclassified, after rigorous studies and observations, as proton storms. -- “You had to abduct us and tie us up in this grungy basement to tell us that?” I craned my neck, working out a small kink, and rubbed my wrists, sore from rope burn. Mezzonote looked around the room, puffing on his cigar. “I wouldn’t call it grungy. It has a certain underground panache to it, no?” Dice and I glanced at each other but remained silent. Mezzonote shrugged, said, “You know a lot of people think Chekhov’s gun and foreshadowing are the same thing. If in the first act you have hung a pistol on the wall, then in the following one it should be fired. Otherwise don't put it there. That’s what he said. That’s where the phrase ‘Chekhov’s gun’ originated, see, and people -- they think because Chekhov said that he must be alluding to foreshadowing, but what he’s really saying is don’t include any unnecessary elements in a story.” I said, “Did he mention anything about unnecessary dialogue by supporting characters?” Mezzonote laughed one of those laughs where you look like you’re laughing but the sound isn’t audible. He said, “Everything happens for a reason, Brian.” “I don’t believe in Fate.” “That’s too bad.” A long puff, followed by a moment of silence, an exhale, then, “You know the best example of foreshadowing is in Romeo and Juliet. Remember at the beginning when both of them say they’d rather die than live apart? That’s foreshadowing. Fuck, that’s writing. Shakespeare -- the master, right?” “Didn’t he, like, not write his own shit?” Dice said. Mezzonote shook his head. “Such an uninformed, uneducated thing to say.” Defending Dice, I said, “I’ve heard a lot of informed, educated people say the same thing.” “Well,” Mezzonote said, shrugging and puffing, “the thing about people is they’re stupid. They don’t know what’s happening, what’s coming next -- they never pick up on the object that’s been planted since Act One and that’s gonna come back and play an integral role in Act Two.” “What’s that gotta do with Shakespeare?” I said. Mezzonote extended his arms out, not fully, palms turned up, head cocked to one side. This was the physical equivalent of him saying he didn’t know or, maybe more appropriately, it didn’t matter. Instead, he said, “Just find the fuckin’ briefcase.” - That event baffled NASA scientists then, and it still does. Some of those scientists hypothesized the event was a product of shock waves in the inner solar system; others a product of giant clouds of plasma in space. There were even some scientists who said the event was most likely the product of rotating sunspots. This became the most popular and most widely accepted theory, but its validity has been questioned because of the fact this particular flare came at the end of a string of five very large flares from the same area of the Sun. To this day, not one NASA scientist has explained why this one particular flare produced more sudden high energy particles than the first four. They said their lack of an explanation means we really don’t understand how the Sun works yet, and if we don’t understand how the Sun works, it’s impossible to fully grasp weather systems and patterns on Earth, in outer space, in the universe. Your universe. Studying how the weather works on other planets and in Space is helpful in understanding how it works on Earth. Weather on other planets follows many of the same physical principles as weather on Earth, but occurs on different scales and in atmospheres having different chemical compositions. And while the Sun’s effect on other planets and on life, the universe and everything***** varies, it’s still something that demands to be taken into account. Just another complicated phenomenon that few, if any, have the secret to. In your realm, in your universe, I’m afraid a similar burst of solar radiation, a similar proton storm in Space, a similar thunderstorm of rampageous proportion is brewing.
