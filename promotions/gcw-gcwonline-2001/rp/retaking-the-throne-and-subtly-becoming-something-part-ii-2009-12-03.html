Retaking the Throne and Subtly Becoming Something: Part II

3 December 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: December 3, 2009 The park wasn't typical by a long shot. Sure it had standard benches and normal gravel paths, but what set it apart was the atmosphere. The smell of Aunt Dee's bakery filled the air stuffing inhabitants' noses with the sweet smell of chocolate chips and cinnamon buns. It drafted through the wind even after hours holding her place while she was away. The trees seemed to prosper most from the aroma as they stood at attention towering higher than any others around. Their leaves lasted into the cold autumn days giving off vibrant oranges and reds that could be seen from the highway if one looked hard enough. Still, it wasn't the features that made the park special. There were those that believed walking the park was more than exercise. To them, it was an entire experience meant to bring a person in tune with not only nature but themselves. It channeled a certain energy that coalesced with the environment creating a euphoric experience greater than that of any man-made drug. It was as if breathing the air and walking the paths could force you to see things more clearly. It could enlighten you. If there was one believer in the movement it was Jeff Kozak. He couldn't remember a day that he hadn't walked the park giving testament to the unbelievable things it was capable of. He was sharper than his friends. He was quicker. He could reason everything and anything no matter how challenging the subject matter was. When asked how he kept up with everything he had no true answer. He only offered advice and each time it was the same: visit the park. Whether the story held truth or not, Jay was willing to test it. Kozak was his guide in the experience and off the bat Jay was denied his cigarettes. In hindsight he knew the reasons behind it but in the moment he had already scrapped the idea of feeling an out of body experience. Nicotine was his usual gateway and without it he wasn't ready to accept anything else. Still, he maintained pace with Jeff. "She doing alright? Mags I mean." Koz spoke in a calm way matching his persona. "She's up and down," thought Jay. He hadn't seen Maggie since the morning and she hadn't climbed any further out of her rut than before. The only good thing was that he had hid the last of his liquor from her before meeting Jeff. "She just can't seem to get back on track. Everything still reminds her of Steve. It's just hard to move on after that long. You know?" "Of course." He knew Steve better than most and understood what there was to miss. "She blaming herself then?" "Usually. It's a back and forth feeling." "I figured. She's always been one to take the fault even when it's not hers." Koz shook his head as he looked off into the trees. The leaves were dressed as casual as ever in their yellow and beige coats. "I just wish I hadn't been training that late. Took everything off the tracks I think. Especially for her." "You wrestle, yeah?" "Boxing. Took time off for Steve though. He was training with me. I told him that if he could keep up then I'd stand in his corner for his first time in the square. Shame it didn't get a chance to happen because he had a mean right hook." "I believe it." Steve McVay had swung his fair share when Jay had first met him and he could still recall an evening where he nearly knocked a guys' nose inwards. From then on he was the guy in the group that people remembered not to mess with. "You going to see Maggie soon? I think she could use the visit after being cooped up in my hotel room this long." "She's just sitting around?" "Well I mean - I take her out. Try to at least. She's just been buried under my comforter between tears and apathy." "I want to visit. Tell her that I'd like to come by, yeah?" "Sure." As they followed a curved part of the gravel path Jay took a second to breathe in the cinnamon bun air. It smelled like Christmas morning leading him to his first realization about the park. It had a way of bringing out a person's deepest most cherished memories. No matter how deep a person could bury them, especially in Jay's case, they were uprooted and manifested without a doubt. He could see his mom pulling the cookie sheet with the fresh batch of pastries steaming out of the oven. Her smile was the same one he wore in genuine situations and it tugged slightly on his heart strings. Koz interrupted the vision. "You said you wanted to talk about something?" As Jay came back into reality he was happy to have the scent still lingering. He could still taste the cream in the trenches of his mouth. "It's just something that Maggie mentioned. She said you had a way of putting things in perspective. You think you could help?" "I can give it a good shot. Go ahead." He had been creating the questions on his way over taking the work out of it all. "I'm in a situation now where I'm not in my comfort zone I guess you could say. I'm just so used to having control and doing things how I want I guess. Now I'm being told how I have to work and what I have to do. I'm not sure how to get over the fact that it's not my style." Koz had a patch of fuzz on his chin and he stroked it comfortably. "This has to do with Steven Caldera?" "You know?" "I'm a fan. Steve turned me on I guess." They exchanged a grin as Jeff focused on a nearby bench. He pointed. "See that bench? It may not have direct orders to stay but it's being obedient on its own. Sure, it can't move physically, but mentally it could go wherever it wants to go." Jay showed skeptical eyes, "You lost me. Am I the bench?" "Forget who you are for a second and think of it this way. You're beyond the bench because you actually can walk away and go where you want. You aren't restricted by the nails in the concrete or a lack of limbs. You're human and you can choose where you go. Though you may have mental constraints from those around you, you can override them. You can think and you can reason what's right and what's wrong to you." As he finished Jay immediately knew what Maggie meant when she described him as different. He was a thinker and put things into a perspective that no one else he knew could do. "So you think I should ignore what he says and do things my way?" "I didn't say that," he countered. "It's a delicate balance that you have to keep Jay. Sure you could ignore what Caldera says and beat the snot out of O'Neil tomorrow. You could stand over his bed so when he wakes you blast him with a shovel. What I'm trying to get through though is that you can piece together parts of everything until something works for you. Maybe you don't want to cut alcohol and cigarettes out completely. Don't do it so abruptly. You know?" "You knew about the alcohol too?" "Just a guess. A good one too I'm seeing." "I just don't want to be chained down I guess. I want to be the outlaw while still being established at the same time." "Then do just that. You're you man. Be you. No matter how you spin it, you're still getting the deed done." Suddenly he could feel the wind pick up. As it did, his mind seemed full as if it had been recently gassed. It pulsed in a maniacal but happy way that made him feel not only wiser but he understood things that he hadn't before. He truly could see why he was the man meant to eliminate O'Neil. He could see how similar he and Caldera truly were. Everything lost its slight haze and shined brighter than the North Star on a clear night. "You think it's right what I'm doing?" Koz seemed to be expecting the question and answered with a confident smile. "It depends on how it's done. There's a difference between finishing someone and crippling someone. I'm assuming Caldera's looking for career ending embarrassment so you'll have to walk that line and decide how it's going to be done. On one side you could give him a card to The Scooter Store and the other you could force him to pack up and leave on his own whim. I can't tell you which one is better but I think a balance between them is what will happen." Jay hadn't thought of the embarrassment option and he flirted with it mentally. "Crippling him wouldn't be right with you though. Would it?" "I think if it happens then it was meant to happen. How can I say if it's right or wrong if it was meant to happen?" "You know that you're the biggest philosopher I know now." "I've been told that before actually." "Figured." Jay and Koz were deep in the woods completely surrounded by blooming trees. As impossible as it seemed he could still smell the bakery forcing him to search for the elves. It was frivolous though and only revealed more leafy beings. As they pulled out of the shaded portion Jay once again could feel something new happening. It was more than just understanding; it was believing. The park may not have lifted his soul from his body but what it did do was something different. Something he could get used to. --- As Jay tried to dodge the hotel desk clerk he was deterred by his ringing voice. It carried through the wide lobby and centered on him seeing as he was the only one within the walls. "Mr. Talley, sir. If you would." Terror hadn't spoken to the man formally before and he didn't want to make a habit of it. Thanks to the urgency in his voice though Jay felt he needed to investigate the matter. He leaned on the marble desk and lifted his sunglasses onto his forehead. "What's up?" The clerk was tidy and spoke in short bursts. "Did you leave your television on by chance?" "My television?" He nodded, "Exactly. Did you?" Shaking it off, "I don't believe so. Why?" "Noise complaints. The neighboring rooms all mentioned that they could hear Tyler Durden and Fight Club in its entirety repeatedly all day. Is your female friend still with us?" The thought made him quiver slightly as he recalled the image of Jay carrying her to the elevator a week before. "She is. I'll check it. Thanks." He didn't bother waiting for a reply. Instead he found his way into the elevator and used it to climb to the fourth floor. As it reached its' destination he spilled out on his mission. The fact that Fight Club was quoted was reason enough to not care what was happening inside. As he thought of whom he had left though, his laughter immediately turned to shock. If Maggie had found the rest of his Jack Daniels there was a good chance he was bound to find a girl without a pulse lying on his mattress. Terror slid the card key down the line and opened the door. The noise was audible outside of the room for sure but inside was a completely different story. His things had been tossed against the walls which was made evident by multiple long black skids. The hotel fridge was open and so were the contents within it. Bottles of water, half-eaten candy bars, and discarded fruit created a clear path to Maggie McVay who sat in a chair directly in front of the television set. She hadn't noticed he'd entered but it made sense. If she had realized his presence she clearly would have been sober. As he approached her chair he came in at an angle to get a good look at her. The bottle of Jack Daniels still had a few gulps left in the bottom and he took the liberty of finishing it. Tossing the bottle onto the floor woke her from her quasi-coma creating a gut wrenching scream. "Mags. Are you alright?" She had a look in her eyes that sold how lost she was. "The fuck have ya' been Jaaaayyy?" He felt as if his mother had been looking but ignored the idea. "Let's get over to the bed. Come on." She wasn't ready for the bed and turned away from him pretending she hadn't heard. Jay grabbed the TV remote from the floor and turned the volume down. "Come on Maggie. I'll lay with you." The tactic backfired on him. "I am not going to have sex with you Jay Terror!" The moniker stung him again. The way she said it made him feel disgusted to wear it and he consciously allowed her to continue considering her condition. "No sex. Just to lie down. That's all." "No. Get out of my room!" Before he knew it his last nerve was snapped in half dangling tacitly. "God damn it Maggie! Get the fuck out of the chair and onto the bed! I'm sick of this dependence and I'm sick of you drinking everything I have left!" She was taken back by the loud noise but couldn't digest the words. "Stop yelling at me!" Tears began to break the floodgates as she dropped her face into the arm of the chair. Her sobbing was loud and vigorous filling the room with reverberating moans. Jay shook his head and threw the remote control at the far wall. He wanted to stop and help Maggie come to her senses but deep down he knew he wouldn't be able to. There was a breaking point in everyone and after babysitting her for a week straight he was there. In his dreams he figured Koz would come bursting through the door handing Steve back to her. Reality was far from what Jay wanted though and gave him no help. The pressure to snap had been building for an entire week and once the nerve finally broke, there was no going back. "Stop crying! Get on the bed and just go to sleep!" Maggie got louder making a point with each sound. His head was pounding and all he could think of was leaving for the park. If anything could cure his anger it would be cinnamon buns. Finding the next best option, he grabbed his pack of cigarettes and lighter. Deep down he felt guilty for leaving her inside but he knew that time was against her. She'd fade like every other time and he'd be left alone once again. He lit the cigarette and closed the glass sliding door leaving a small crack to hear for her. The nicotine was what he needed. It flowed into his system like a stream of vitamins nourishing him to the core. Her screams were drown out by the cars zipping across the street in front of the hotel creating a peaceful ambience. In this peace he remembered what Koz had said about choices and loyalty. It all finally made sense. It didn't matter how he was to do things; what mattered was that it got done. Like a chain reaction he was linked to the lunch he and Caldera shared. Between his comparison to O'Neil and Maggie's newfound addiction Jay knew that something had to give. He could be either one of them if he drank to get himself through the day like he had. How would that help beat O'Neil? AXEL? Anyone? If he was to walk in the footprints Caldera had made he was going to do it just as Koz said. Balance everything until it felt right. Maggie was silent and upon looking inside Jay knew she'd remain that way for some time. Kicking his legs onto the railing, he enjoyed the smooth ride he was given from the rush of Marlboro.
