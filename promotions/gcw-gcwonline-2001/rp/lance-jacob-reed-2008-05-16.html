Lance Jacob Reed

16 May 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/16/2008 Lance Jacob Reed. That’s what they named me. My parents had been at odds over my name during my entire gestation. My mother wanted Jacob, after her grandfather. My father favored Lance, a tribute to his nephew who had died in Vietnam. I became the living embodiment of their compromise. They say that you don’t have memories of your infancy? that you can’t have them. That’s not true. I have a memory of my birth. It is clear as crystal and lives in my heart, a constant loop repeating its brief drama again and again. “Jake!” she screams. “Jaaake!” It’s my mother, she’s screaming my name, the name she chose for me, as the doctors wrench me away from her arms. My newborn eyes lock with hers for just a moment? and then she’s gone. My mother died in child birth, moments after I was born. She held me for just that one brief moment and then passed from this earth. Every shrink I’ve ever seen says that the memory of my birth is a creation of my psyche but I know it’s real. They tell me that I heard the story and created my own account, but they’re grasping at straws, desperately struggling to explain the unexplainable. My father called me Lance, I guess it was his sick victory prize for being the one parent that survived my birth. I never liked it, and rarely answered to it. By 12 I had even him calling me Jake. At 15 when I made my in-ring debut for my father’s Blue Ridge Wrestling promotion he convinced me to take on the Lance moniker. He fed me some bull shit about a stage persona, separating the man from the wrestler, shit like that. Looking back on it I think he just wanted to win, wanted to best my dead mother 15 years after the fact. Jake became the passionate side of me, the man who lived outside of the world of wrestling. Lance became the man who would do whatever he needed to on the inside of the ring, risking his body daily to win matches. My father built Blue Ridge Wrestling into a respected training school and prominent regional wrestling promotion by using my blood, sweat, and tears. By 18 I could no longer shoulder the burden of being the main attraction of my father’s traveling circus so I packed my bags and headed to Japan. In Japan I studied mixed martial arts under Jinsei Kobayashi and I was Jake once more. I had a few pro fights over there, ended up going 3-2 with 2 victories by submission. When the MMA bookings dried up Jin hooked me up with a local wrestling promoter and I started working shows. In Japan I had a bit of name recognition from MMA so wrestling as Lance was out of the question. Wrestling in Japan was pure bliss because there I was Jake? just Jake. Not the son of Bill Reed, not the brother of Travis, and especially not Lance. I fought with passion inside the squared circle and the Japanese people loved me. I buried Lance, buried what he was and what he represented. As Jake I still flew high and took risks but not because I had to, not because I was forced to, but because I wanted to. I didn’t really talk to my family much while I was in Japan. I returned to the states to find Blue Ridge shut down and our training school turned into a fitness center in order to make ends meat. My older brother Travis blamed me for the closing and relations with my family were nearly nonexistent for the next year. Travis had always been the big man in Blue Ridge. He was the champion, the face of the promotion, but it was no secret that the fans of the late 90’s were less enthralled with Travis? old school approach than with my all out “attitude”. Travis was on the poster but the fans came to see me. When I left for Japan, taking my drawing power with me, the promotion died a slow death under Travis? lone watch. To this day he has never forgiven me for that. Time heals all wounds. I used to think that was bull shit but in my case it proved true. Eventually my father came back into my life, he managed my career for a time and lead me to one of my few singles titles. It took Travis a little longer to come around. Dad died in 2004 and we left a lot of things unreconciled but his death did serve as the kick in the ass Travis needed to finally start returning my calls. It took some time but Travis and I sorted things out and we even reformed our old tag team, Generation Gap. Our family name alone got us a spot in a top promotion, and we kicked around there for a few months, flirting with tag team gold. Then the darkness came. Someone got into Travis? ear, fed him lies and deceit, convinced him to turn away from me. Travis was my brother, my only living blood, I had lost him once already and couldn’t have that happen again. I bought into his promises, and the lies of those who had changed him, and I too descended into darkness. I don’t like to think about those days. The one thing I focus on is that our actions disgraced the 30 years of solid reputation we had inherited from our father. What’s more was that in my carelessness innocent people suffered, people like Jacob McKail. I regret that above all else. After Travis blew out his knee the house of cards we had built to insulate ourselves from the truth toppled down around us. I was left empty, dead inside, and resenting the only living relative I had left in this world. I took care of Travis for a time but the moment he could walk again I was gone. I couldn’t be there, couldn’t see him every day, he had come to represent my fall from grace and I had to break free. I had been so desperate to cling to what was left of my family that I had allowed him to warp the man I was, the man our father made me, into a twisted and unrecognizable abomination. I reasoned that if I wasn’t near him that he couldn’t do it again so for that reason I left my crippled brother alone and went out into the world. Serendipity had placed me in Sacramento the week of May 5th and being that I was there it was impossible to escape the flyers and radio ads for WorldWide. They all made sure to mention “Jacob McKail’s triumphant return to Global Championship Wrestling” as if they enjoyed taunting me with it. I bought a ticket, one of the last few nose bleeds, and entered the arena. I wasn’t really planning anything when I bought the seat but looking back I can see that it was predestined. Having worked so many televised shows I knew a little bit about how security might be organized and I used that knowledge to slip backstage at the most opportune moment. And that is how I find myself under contract to Global Championship Wrestling. I don’t really know if I want to be here, or more accurately I’m not sure I deserve to be here. The one thing I do know is that I have to start somewhere, I need a chance to become the man I once was and this seems as good a place as any. I have no idea who Dan Black is? don’t take that the wrong way, that’s no insult. I’m sure Dan has done plenty in his career to be proud of but I’ve no more heard of him than he’s heard of me. I hope to put up a good fight, but I’m already doubting myself. I know I’ll freeze, I know I’ll choke when THEY all look at me. I can almost feel their searing glares now. Ah yes, “they”. The one group I have thought of the least yet offended the most? the fans. I don’t know if I’ll even be able to step through those curtains on WorldWide because it will mean facing them. They won’t cheer, they might not even boo and that will be the worst part. I will have stirred nothing in them, I lost their love but they’ve so quickly forgotten me that I’m not even worthy of hate. They’ll look at me glassy eyed, waiting to see what I’ll do, how I’ll behave. I can’t take that. That’s why I need McKail’s forgiveness, he’ll forgive me and they’ll forget. They’ll cheer again, they’ll embrace me? they’ll love me. They have to?
