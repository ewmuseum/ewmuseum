The Sacculina is a parasitic barnacle that hijacks a crab's nervous system and forces it to live a life of eating and spraying more Sacculina into the ocean. Check it out sometime!

3 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 3rd, 2005 The night air's as good for Trey as it was an hour before. Alyse clings to his arm tight enough that he drags her along, her legs flailing with every swing. To the crackling electric hum of a street lamp, he tells her that he still has to piss and steps into an alleyway. Rats chirp and rush away from him and through a clicking chain link fence. He steps behind a dumpster, feet sloshing in an unquestioned liquid, and unzips. Piss splashes with something possibly worse than piss as he zones out, staring at a plate of meat laid out on a cardboard box. He looks at the fork, at the knife, then at the lump between them. It's on expensive looking porcelain with a blue inlay, so his thoughts drift to fine dining in trying to place the oozing meat--the texture's off, though, not like anything he's seen before. Eyes slowly adjusting to the night tell him that it's moving, then his brain tells him it's more likely that something on the meat is moving. The buzzing fades in, flies beginning to swarm on the maggot's feast. He starts to choke up when a worm falls from the stack and onto the plate with enough impact to hear over the pissing, and he jumps when he notices something rubbing against his thigh. His brain kicks in. Three shakes and he answers his phone. 'Yo, J. What's up?' 'Do you think Anarch'd like me setting up a Jordan White Foundation with their money?' 'I dunno, man. Usually ain't cool with that sorta shit.' 'Even if it was dedicated to establishing and preserving a positive image of Jordan White?' 'Shit. Doubt it.' 'Fuck.' Jordan's gone and Trey staggers out of the alley. Alyse laughs with her eyes shut. 'Talk to yourself much?' 'Nah, someone called.' 'Probably your girl, wondering where you were.' 'Ain't got a girl.' 'Bullshit.' 'Nah.' 'Really?' 'Shyeah.' Underneath the lamp, glowing auburn, she wraps her arms around his neck and assaults his smile--she forces her tongue into his mouth, twisting it with his as she sucks like a vampire at his lips. They linger until she finally drops to her feet and wipes at her chin. 'Your beard tickles.' 'I get that, sometimes.' 'Don't worry, I'll still fuck you.' 'That a promise?' She smiles like girls always do, half-drunk and almost coy enough to actually use the word. The stitches at her cheek stretch further apart, leaving a string to drift on the breeze. It's neon for the electric lights above. 'Depends. You wanna take a taxi?' 'How far we goin'?' 'Couple miles, but it looks like rain. Might want shelter, these storms get bad if you're not used to them.' 'Y'know, more you talk the more I doubt you're even from here.' 'No one wants to sound like they're from Boston.' 'True. So, cab?' 'Sounds good.' She steps to the edge of the sidewalk and throws her hand up to the passing cars as rain starts to fall in luminescent trails. Trey steps under an awning and rubs the pocket of his pants, almost expecting a pack of cigarettes. The city takes it toll on everyone. Before he can get comfortable on the brick face of the store, she calls his name and he notices the parked taxi. Something evil at his back takes immediate control of his senses; he turns around to see a man staring him down from inside the store. Trey shudders in disgust and speeds to the red back seat waiting on him at the curb. It crunches as he sits down and takes a second to get used to. 'You like wrestling?' The words come from another dimension, and come slightly nasal. He's drifted off again and snaps back to with a good, solid response. 'Huh?' 'Lightweight. I asked if you liked wrestling.' 'Ah, s'alright. Pays the bills, keeps me good.' 'Can't all be about money.' 'Nah. S'about fame, too.' He laughs. She laughs. The world spins. 'That's something. You get tons of easy fans, don't you?' 'Can't lie.' 'Don't.' 'Just won't answer.' 'Cute. Bet a guy like you doesn't have to worry about anything--like one that you just couldn't get.' 'Nah, not often.' She turns and throws a thigh over Trey's, stretching in closer to straddle him. 'Where you goin' with this?' 'Oh, nowhere in particular. Did you and Eva ever fuck?' 'Can't people just ask LeStatt that shit now? Damn. Yeah, we had our fling--wasn't nothin' but. S'over now, though.' 'This how you treat all your women after dumping them? Fighting them on live TV, all that.' 'Usually don't come up.' 'I bet.' She's close enough now to whisper and rupture his eardrums. She just breathes. Street lamps fly past and blank faces shuffle mindlessly in the night. Trey doesn't look to them and leans back in his seat. The string tickles his chest, hanging looser now. 'Almost there.' She grunts softly and falls forwards, the taxi pulling to a halt out front of an older, Colonial-style house; painted white with a second story balcony hanging over the porch. It hangs on top of a hill, staring down quietly with empty windows for eyes. He asks the driver, 'how much?' The man turns. Trey looks away from his face instantly, letting Alyse block his disgust. 'Couldn't expect Trey Tremendous to pay for a ride in my cab, hah. No sir, not on this old man's watch. Keep it up, kid.' 'Will do. Thanks, man.' 'Just breathe.' Out of the taxi, he follows her up the slick cobble stones laid out in almost-tiers on the steep incline. She fumbles in her purse along the way, pulling out keys and unlocking the red door as Trey makes it to the porch. He wipes the rain from his brow, leaving his hand slick and oily. He stares at it while Alyse calls his name and pulls on the sleeve of his jacket. Inside, the lights come on in a plodding succession and reveal a long hallway, open on the right and with a staircase on the left. The wallpaper, like vines around a pole, seems to crawl and twist. Trey looks to a framed photo hanging near the entrance; the girl with her family. Looked recent, a haircut ago. 'How long you been doin' the artist thing?' 'Couple of years. A teacher in high school really turned me onto it, must've realized my potential.' 'I'm lookin' forwards to it myself.' 'Are you now?' They walk towards the staircase, Trey glancing into the still dim living room. The lights in the hall drape tall shadows over a well-furnished modernistic area; furniture with smooth contours and a minimum of hard edges stand out in those brief glowing spots. 'Well, a lot of people don't really seem to get my work, you know? They don't even try to understand--but who cares about idiots?' 'Other idiots?' 'Exactly.' Up the steps. The third one from the bottom creaks under Trey's foot. 'And they don't matter anyway. We're gonna rule the world one day.' 'I've gotta friend that'd love you.' 'Aw, not you?' 'Just sayin', seem like his type.' 'Think you might not fully appreciate me?' She laughs, adding: 'it's alright, not many people do. I can tell you're different though.' The string from her cheek traces along the banister as she steps to the second floor. 'Can I take your jacket?' Trey pulls his wallet out and hands the coat over. Alyse disappears into a side room that pushes out a lifetime of freezing cold air. Trey's system eats it up, freezing him to his spot in mid-breath. 'Sorry,' she clicks on a light. 'Just used to navigating this place in the dark. Something wrong?' 'Just caught a chill. It's cool.' She's without her thick brown jacket now and wafey almost to the point of anorexia. Still cute, wearing a black spaghetti top that shows off a few tribal tattoos on her upper arms. 'That room's always had a draft. Like my ink?' She rubs the designs and tilts so Trey can see. 'Not bad.' 'Yeah, I got this one when a long-term boyfriend dumped me. It really captured how I was feeling like nothing else could--helped me get over him.' The doll brushes the string behind her shoulder and postures for a moment. 'And this one, well, I was drunk. Yours mean anything?' 'Sorta. I was loaded as shit though, so I dunno what--just somethin'.' 'Isn't that always how it goes?' She pushes open another door and the smell is flowers in bloom. The air is silk as he walks into the pink room, pauses for just a second to marvel at how girly it is. A poster with emaciated white guys with oily black hair confront him from every wall; photos of her and friends grinning like the deranged stick from the frame of the mirror on her white dresser. A television in the corner, a white board with people's signatures on the back wall under the window, and a stack of translucent boxes full of clothes surround her cranberry bed. 'Y'know, this don't exactly look like a college kid's room.' 'Hah. My parents left it like this; they're weird, just flaky old rich people.' 'So, this their house?' 'Yeah. They always spend the summer on vacation. I never really had the time to know them, I think it really comes through in my art.' 'Speakin' of?' 'Just wait. It's a surprise--sit-down.' She leads him to the bed by his hand and Trey collapses, stretching his legs out and staring down at his boots while Alyse disappears into her walk-in closet. Mud cakes on the toe of the left boot; he scrapes it off with the right's sole and leans back on his palms. He glances over his shoulder, skeptic and patient.
