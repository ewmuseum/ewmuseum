Washed Up

4 September 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/04/11 The House of Stone sits on an unassuming lot in the industrial district of Seattle, Wa. Across the street is a train yard that sits right on the water’s edge. Markus Stone never did enjoy trains. Most times that he has encountered one usually led to the inconvenience of waiting in traffic for the train to cross before being able to, finally, continue to whatever destination he had at that day. The irony is not lost on him. Markus stares past his reflection in the glass window to the trains parked in the yard. Most are in some state of disrepair as working class employees walk to and from them carrying all variety of toolboxes. When he was a kid, Markus dreamed of being a train conductor. Of course, at the time he didn’t know it was called a conductor. He can’t help but wonder how differently things would’ve been if he had been able to do that. Driving a train, meeting a wife and buying a house together; starting a family, and picking their first dog together. Coming home. Every night. The sound of a body colliding with a ring canvas snaps him out of it. Markus turns around and looks at the two rookies in the ring. One of the men, Sam, stands gloating over the other. On the mat is the newest rookie, Markus hasn’t even tried to learn his name yet. The rookie grimaces in pain, holding his back with one hand while trying to support himself on the other. “Sam! What the hell are you doing?” Markus yells at him. His voice carries quite well in the building. Sam’s smile is quickly wiped from his face as he turns sharply to face his instructor. “Shut up, don’t even try talking,” Markus holds up a hand to stop him, shaking his head. “Do you think you’re really that talented that you can take the time to enjoy all the simple things in life?” Sam tries to stutter an explanation. “Didn’t I tell you to shut up?” Markus pauses and waits a brief second to see if the message has gotten through yet. When Sam doesn’t answer, Markus continues. “So, are we all to believe that the great and mighty Sam has learned everything there is to know about professional wrestling? That you have suddenly, and quite mysteriously, gained all the knowledge needed to understand when a wrestler is hurt enough to gloat? That the entire purpose of showboating is to try and demoralize your opponent? To make them believe they are completely under your control?” Markus had been walking as he spoke, passing his handful of other trainees on his way toward the ring. They all stop what they are doing, without thinking about it, and watch. A few are even smart enough to listen to what Markus is saying to try and learn from it. Markus casually slides in the ring to stand face to face with Sam. “Is that a correct assumption?” Markus glares at Sam, hands on his hips. “No sir, I ju-“ Sam is cut off by a clothesline that drives him to the mat. The air rushes from his lungs as his head simultaneously bounces against the canvas. “Rhetorical question Farley. Next time I tell you to shut up, you do it. And then you listen. Got it?” Sam nods his head while trying to get his wind back. “Good. Now this is one of those times where I stand over you, and make you feel helpless. You do feel that way, don’t you? You probably feel like I’m in complete control right now, and you haven’t even realized that my ankle is right inside of your arm, ready for you to grab onto it and trip me down, completely reversing our positions. Hell, you probably forgot that it’s my right leg, which anybody who has followed my career would know has a long history of injury. Now, did everybody get the point of this exercise?” Markus raises his voice and turns to face the rest of his class. They all nod their heads or vocalize their understanding. Markus exits the ring, satisfied that he has taught them at least one valuable lesson for the day. “Alright then. Sam, newbie, get back to work. And new kid, if I see you pull one offensive move out of your ass, I swear to God that you won’t walk out of here today, and you’ll have to wheel your ass anywhere you want to go for the next six months.” Wrestling trainer. Markus Stone has become the one thing he had always looked down on. A wrestler who never really finished on any of his promises, who left the business too early, and he became a teacher instead of a star. The old saying, those who can’t do, teach, echoes in the back of his head. “Hey Jer, start everybody else on the rounds,” Markus directed as he walked towards his back office. Jeremy was the most senior student he had. Although the kid didn’t really show any promise, he kept coming back. He had a good mind for everything about the business besides the actual in-ring mentality. Markus was generally comforted with the knowledge that one day Jeremy would either make one hell of a manager, or one hell of a businessman. Markus settled into his office and tried to distract himself with his finances. Running a wrestling school generally isn’t a very profitable affair, and so far this month was looking to be a bit tighter than normal. Somebody had a habit of leaving the lights on and Markus’ electric bill came in much higher than usual. Lucky for him, Markus still has some savings left from selling his house. Now he sleeps in a small room in the back of the school. Of course, all of this runs through his head and simply reminds him of where he used to stand in the world. The former GCW Tag Team Champion looks at his book more intently and finds a quick solution for this months problem. “Looks like the ring isn’t getting tuned up this month. These green fucks won’t notice until after it’s taken care of though,” Markus mumbles to himself, less than pleased at having to make this sacrifice to keep from dipping into his savings yet again. Time passes in his office, while his students run through their morning rounds. Combinations of takedowns, sweeps, and reversals that look more like amateur wrestling and Brazilian jiu-jitsu than professional wrestling. Others are working through a simple chain of moves; body slam, head lock, switch to arm lock, followed by a snap mare, then switch positions. The moves are performed at slightly more than half speed, the purpose more to warm up and train muscle memory on four of the most basic moves in professional wrestling. At a set time the workers all switch roles and continue warming up. Every student is expected to do any physical conditioning outside of the school, and every hour of their time spent in the gym is learning everything that Markus Stone can possibly pass on to them. Jeremy takes a second to glance at the only clock in the building. Deciding that enough time has been spent warming up, Jeremy heads to Markus’ office. He glances in the ring at Sam and the new kid. Sam completes a sloppy looking suplex on the kid, popping back to his feet quickly. The new kid lays still for a long moment before he slowly rolls to his stomach and rises to his feet. Jeremy notices the angry glare the keep shoots in his direction as he passes. “Hey boss, we’re good to go,” Jeremy pokes his head into Markus’ office. Markus flips his book closed and briskly heads out the door. Jeremy can’t help but shake his head. Class never goes well when Markus is moving fast. “All right guys!” Markus bursts into the gym like a storm cloud. The students all stand still and tall, bracing themselves for what’s coming next. “I quit.” The words fill the school with tension and anticipation. Markus stops half way through his step and slowly lowers his foot to the ground. He turns his head halfway, and his gaze is met with a resentful stare from the new kid. “Really?” begins says in a tone that clearly conveys he could care less. He turns and casually walks toward the ring. “So then what the hell are you still doing in my ring?” “I want my money back,” the kid backs off a step as Markus rolls into the ring. “That’s too bad kid,” Markus stands face to face with the kid, not a hint of humor on his face. The kid’s eyes explode open in a look of shock. “Are you serious man? I paid for a month of training, not this crap! You honestly think I’m going to pay money to let you beat the crap out of me and not even let me defend myself?” Markus takes a moment to answer. “No kid, you paid me so that you could learn that you aren’t cut out for this business. Every single person in this class paid their dues to me, and you were expected to do the exact same thing. The same way I did.” “How the hell is this paying dues?” “You learn respect! Respect for the business, respect for the people you work with, respect for me, and by God respect for how much shit your body will put up with for a chance, a small sliver of hope, that you can one day make a living from this shit hole existence you want to put yourself through!” The rest of his students nod their heads in agreement, a few even voice their support. The kid looks to the class, and then back at Markus, back and forth. “Are you kidding? A washed up hack like yourself is supposed to teach me respect? Hell, you drank yourself to worthlessness and opened this gym up because you realized you couldn’t cut it! I’m supposed to come here day after day and let some never-was like you make me into some mediocre joke of a performer? For what purpose? So I can just crap my whole life away and sleep in the back of some shitty gym dreaming every night of what I could’ve done if I’d have just not fucked it all away? Hell, you’ve been running the same schtick for the past six years, always complaining about the same two people and always trying to wrestle them over and over again, only to disappear for months at a time when they show you how shitty you really are! You’re a fucking joke man.” “You done?” Markus doesn’t show any emotion and the kid offers no reply. “Get the fuck out of my building.” Markus turns and rolls out of the ring. He doesn’t bother telling the class he’s not going to train them today. They don’t need telling though, and most of them pair off into groups of two or three to work on their similar abilities together. Markus walks back to his office and calmly closes the door. Now that nobody can see him, he finally releases a bit of his self control and begins shaking, wondering if there was a word that kid said that wasn’t the truth. The following hours are a mash up of emotional reactions. Anger, hurt, resentment, blame, acceptance, denial. It’s a distorted grieving process shoved into the span of half a day. It doesn’t take long, not nearly as long as it should. Markus comes to a decision. He runs from his office and goes into a closet filled with boxes. Pulling them out two at a time, three at a time, as many as he can fit into his arms. Markus grabs them all and drops them in his office. Tearing through them in a frenzy befitting somebody looking for their inheritance, Markus rips dvds out of the boxes, throwing the majority of them into a pile in the corner. The rest of his evening is spent collecting a greatest hits of his GCW career. Morning comes quickly, and Markus doesn’t sleep out of childish excitement. Before the sun can finish ascending the horizon, he takes a package covered in stamps out to his mailbox. One detail of this entire situation seems ill fitting for Markus Stone. In large letters, dead center of the envelope, it reads: PRIME, NUMBER ONE BY DEFINITION
