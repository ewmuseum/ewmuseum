A bar, An Anniversery, a Bill, and a Truck

24 September 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/24/09 She was sitting spread eagle on her barstool knowing nothing of the vultures she was attracting. Envious eyes watched carefully as she swayed side to side talking a hole through her neighbors' ear. Her hair was a frizzy mess of brunette coated thinly with a layer of booze that wavered with each cigarette she lit. Placing her dainty hand on the man beside hers’ shoulder she forced out a garbled mess of words. "You just gotta' stop making...I need another drink." He was in his mid forties and not as interested as the others spying from their posts. He did however engage anyways. "What can I get you?" "One of these," she spat, nearly falling off the side of the stool, "I really like this." The man flagged the bartender and pulled him aside. "What're you feeding this one?" "She's been here since six. It was inevitable." "Six huh?" He nodded keenly, "Something like that. I'd keep my distance if I were you." As he finished, Maggie McVay slid close to the two grabbing hold of the bartenders collar. "You know, this is...well this is...AMAZING!" "I'm glad," he said quickly. Him and the middle aged man shared a glance before the bartender began to walk off. The man stopped him. "Get her another." "Yeah?" "YEAH!" She shouted. The bartender shook his head and added a slight scowl while turning his back to them and fiddling with the vodka. Maggie now grabbed the man getting close to his face. As close as possible. "What's your name?" "Kevin. Kevin Baker. Yours?" An excited smile crossed her face, "KEVIN BACON?! No way!" "Baker. Kevin Baker," he corrected. "Oh I get it, like James Bond. Bond. James Bond!" The bartender turned his face over his shoulder and gave an 'I told you so' look. Kevin Baker ignored it and put his hand on her shoulder now. The closeness made him forget about his wife and kids. "I guess I'm like James Bond. I mean, I may do some spy work." "Spy work," she retorted, "for who?" "The government of course. You can't tell though, people aren't supposed to know. It's a big secret." Completely ignoring him she stopped a passerby nearly hitting her face off his crotch. "See this guy. Spy. Complete spy." He nodded, looked at Kevin, and continued past. Kevin grabbed Maggie before she tumbled and propped her up on the bar. "You saved my life even. How dreamy!" "Vodka with strawberry and lime for you miss," came the bartender's voice. He slid it to her on a napkin and nodded to Kevin. Kevin opened his eyes wide and slightly flicked his brows upward before looking back to Maggie. She pulled the straw out and dumped the concoction down her throat. He grabbed her hand and slowed the pouring. "I was drinking that!" "I know I'm sorry. I just couldn't see your beautiful eyes with the glass tipped back like that." "Charming like James Bond too!" He smiled a toothy grin, "I try. Fact is, I find you fascinating. What brings you out here tonight? Just another night on the town?" "Something like that." For a second she paused and her eyes began to water. That second carried to a few seconds before she wiped her eyes dry with her shirt. As the shirt fell back into place it offered a better glimpse at her assets which were already pretty obvious. "Everything ok?" "Yeah," she wiped again, this time getting black eyeliner on the white fabric, "just thinking of something." "What's that?" "Calm down spy. I have a private life you know!" He seemed taken back by the comment and nodded apologetically. "I'm sorry, just trying to make conversation." He leaned his right elbow on the bar itself and looked into her green eyes. "What else do you have planned tonight?" "This. Just this." "Well if you'd like, I could show you some of my gadgets. The really cool spy ones, you know?" "Oh yeah?" "Of course," he smiled again, "I have a bunch of cool things they give me just like the movies. I'm sure you'd love it." "I would love it! So much." As Kevin began to open his mouth, he felt a hand on his shoulder. It wasn't Maggie's gentle drunk touch however, this time is was a man's. His hand had a firm grip. As he and Maggie looked up, separate expressions came to their faces. Kevin, of course, was taken back by the face. Maggie on the other hand allowed a tear to roll down her cheek and touch her white shirt. He was someone she never expected to see. And Now a View Into The Past *** It was sunny again on the ranch - she noticed pulling back the curtains to their bedroom. Rays of light filtered into the eclectic mess and over her husband's body reaching his eyes soon after. He turned over mumbling a jumble of words that choked to death in the cloth of his pillow. Maggie gave him a shake. "Come on Sweetie. Come on!" "Five minutes," he pleaded. Maggie grinned and decided to take full mount on his back kicking her legs and the covers over him. She straddled and grabbed at his ears. "Not today. We have things to do today. Don't you remember?" "Four Minutes, please." She pushed the covers down off her back and onto the floor. He was exposed to the stale room temperature air surrounding them. "Now! I'll make you breakfast." "Go start, I'll be down in a little," he tried. Maggie dug her hands under his chest trying to turn him under her. Although a pathetic attempt, she continued to believe that another minute would be all it would take. "I'm not starting without you. Come on up Steven." "Three minutes, that's it." "Not five minutes, four minutes or three minutes. Now!" Going for an almost sure fire way to get him moving, she dug her fingers into the fat of his stomach tickling him into a roll. She now truly did have a full mount as she sat across his stomach. "Two more minutes. You didn't say two minutes." "It was intended Steven. Come on - I'll get off," she bartered. "That's not the problem Mags," He grinned feverishly. She leaned down to kiss him across his dry lips. He mirrored once and fell back soon after burying his head deep into the pillow. "One more minute." "No, you've already had four." "They were fast minutes, I was counting." "I'm sure you were." He began to sit now looking at the clock on the bedside table. Twelve Thirty-Six. She looked down at him and he moved her chin, with his pointer finger, toward the clock. The minute digit changed to show a seven. He then used his finger again to push her vision out the open window in front of her. "What're you…?" She started. Suddenly a crop duster plane came into view outside swooping up and down as if manipulating clouds. Lines formed to letters and those letters combined to write words that spelled her name across the sky. In one final swoop the plane circled the name in a heart shape before flying out of sight. She looked down speechless. "Happy Anniversary Maggie McVay." She blushed and slumped on top of him. It was then that she knew that she was not only blessed but lucky. Another Day *** It was at the breakfast table that they sorted the bills and made plans for the gym. Instead of the traditional table and chairs typically found in a dining room set though, filing cabinets and crumpled papers populated the open spaces. Of course, there was a table but that wasn't the first thing noticed when walking in. In fact, the first thing most people saw was usually the tall piece of metal piping that hogged the corner. It stood by itself, a prize greater than any they could think up. Twirling her Cheerios was Maggie, the energetic brunette who went by 'my angel' to her husband. Her eyes chewed on the electric bill. "We turn the lights off at night, right?" "I mean, I try to," replied Steve McVay who poked his head out of the newspaper, "but sometimes I can forget. Especially the bathroom." "The gym Steve," she pushed, "and really, the bathroom lights? They're pretty easy." "Hey, don't judge. And yeah, of course. I always get the lights off. That'd be expensive." She handed him the bill and ate a spoonful of Cheerios. The milk dripped down her chin. "What's this?" "The electric bill. Unless they inflated costs, someone's leaving these lights on overnight." "I promise that..." "...that? He scratched his head before shaking it, "It may be Koz. I told him earlier this month that he could stay a little later to do a workout. I'll have to check." "Late workout?" She left her spoon in the bowl. "Yeah, he's training for that fight coming up. The one with the guy out of the gym. Tulley or something." "But still, is he turning lights off?" She took the bill back. "I'll check and make sure. Just think if he wins though. Really." "I know, and he will. Don't worry. Just checking that we watch out. We have to conserve all we can these days." "I know." He picked up the paper and rolled it into a funnel. Standing, he kissed her forehead before leaving the kitchen. "I'm gonna' get the truck started. Five minutes?" "I'll be there." He took off and she finished the Cheerios. A Little Later *** Just another day in the gym sucking in moldy sock and unchanged jock strap through their nostrils. There was nothing sanitary about the place, not even the outside looked very clean. The floors had dried blood, the mats had holes in them, the machines and equipment was out of date and there was a drip that fell from the ceiling to the floor always at the most inopportune time. What they did have though was a gem in the world of amateur wrestling. Their ring was not only pristine but it was the best on the East coast. Philadelphia's finest ring fans would say. Most agreed that without it, the gym wouldn't have made it past the first month. For this, the McVay's were quite proud. Steve took the lead heading to the back office. It was quiet for the hour and it would stay quiet for those fifty five more minutes before 8 A.M. Like most competitors, people belonging to McVay's stuck to their rigorous schedule. They were machines programmed to come when their bodies told them to and left the same way. Joe Huddan was always the first through the doors. Even at seventy-four, he still managed to return at eight o'clock just as he had twenty years before. Not only that but the man was in more shape than the entire retirement home down the street's residents put together. "Look, the lights are on Steve. You have to talk to Koz about this." "And I will, don't you worry. He'll be in at three and I'll make sure I pass the word." "You know we should really look into some new jump ropes," she began as her eyes passed over the rusty nails holding their current collection of ropes up. "Some of them have four knots in them even." "I thought about it actually." "...and?" "And I asked everyone. You know what they told me?" "What's that?" "That if I got new ropes they'd tear them in half and tie 'em back together. I figured we'd conserve a little and stick with what we got." "Makes sense. And the machines then?" "They hate them." "Really? We should look into it. Maybe it'd draw in some more people." He began to unlock the office door and shook his head. "I doubt it. People want to see beautiful people in the gym for motivation. Here, well, we aren't the prettiest people. I mean, we smile but the people fighting here are missing teeth. Reminds them too much of Halloween and when they find out there's no candy they sign up for Gold's Gym." "You've really given it thought then?" "Why the new people anyways. I think we're good with the people we have. It's a core group of them, you know. Like a brotherhood." She coughed and placed her gym bag on the floor. "It wouldn't hurt to add some new faces and to draw in some more cash. Not like we need it badly but I mean, you know." He took a seat in his spinning black office chair and patted his lap firmly with his palms. Maggie took the hint and crawled toward her master taking a leaping seat onto his lap. His arms embraced hers and he drew his face against hers. "Don't worry about it. Honestly, we aren't struggling so why break what's not broken. Let's just ride it out and see where things go. Besides, I'm supposed to be the one scared of the finances and all around here." "Uh huh, sure." Her eyes looked up and across the room meeting those of a man who dressed in a tight Armani suit. If she had a first guess, she'd say he was Donald Trump or the president himself. Unfortunately, he was neither. Acknowledging him, Maggie said, "Hello sir." He stepped inside and offered a stiff smile, one that needed little wrestling to submit into a frown. "Hi, I'm sorry if I'm bothering you. Just had a question for the owner." Steve nodded and raised his hand while using the other to cradle Maggie, "That's me. What can I do for you?" "I'll come out straight and say it. I'm going to have to take your gym." "That's pretty straight," he started. "How exactly do you plan on taking the gym?" "With these papers," he said while digging into his inside jacket pocket. "Unfortunately you're on my property now and I'm going to have to take down whatever this is and place my strip mall down. There isn't enough room to house both and pretty easily, my strip mall won the debate." Maggie intervened, "You're going to destroy McVay's?" "I'm not sure how yet, but yes, that is the plan." "I’m confused," tried Steve. "This is ours and the community's. How did you somehow take control of the land that we own?" “Technically, you didn’t own it.” Steve raised a brow, “Clarify.” “I don’t know why but you didn’t own the entire lot. Actually, you only own half of the spot where your place lies. So technically speaking, you only own half of a gym.” Steve and Maggie exchanged looks of bewilderment. Neither knew what he was talking about and neither was willing to believe him. “So you plan on taking down a community landmark and sending the people that go here elsewhere? How do you think that will go over?” "You see, I covered that problem. When I demolish McVay's I'm going to build in it's place a new and hip McVay's. Somewhere where people don't just go because they feel they have to. You know?" "No, I don't. You're telling me that we have no say in the matter? Nothing at all?" "I'd love to clarify for you now, really. But I have to run and take care of other business. Take these papers, read up on them, and I'll be back by tomorrow to sign things. Take care." He didn't wait for a reply from either McVay. The man simply turned his back and left through the open port in the room. Steve grabbed the paper and looked at it for a second. "You think this is real?" She asked. "I assume so. Why else would he come in here like that?" She shrugged her shoulders, "I don't know but I don't like this. We need an attorney or someone to read these papers. Really." Before long it was 8 A.M. and there he was, Joe Huddan. With that smile on his face, neither Steve nor Maggie could tell him about the gym. All they could do was hope that there was a way to keep the gym for guys like Joe.
