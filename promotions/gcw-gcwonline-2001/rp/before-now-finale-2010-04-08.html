Before Now... (Finale!)

8 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/08/10 There's a little known fact about our hero, Mr. Phil Atken. Something that only his closest friends (read: Dirk Dickwood) know about him. The truth is that although he has spent his working life in a wrestling ring, his true passion in life has and most likely always with be with the audio medium. If you asked 10 year old lil Phil what he wanted to be when he grew up, he'd have said radio. Heck, if you asked 20 year old Phil you'd most likely get the same answer. I'm pretty sure if you tied 30 year old Phil to a chair, you'd finally torture out the same answer. Phil's passion for radio and desire to be involved in the industry stems way back to early childhood. Even at eight and nine years old, he used the radio in his room to lull him into a secure sleep while the various talk personalities yammered on, talking to the resident psychic, questioning if aliens truly lived amongst, attempting to communicate with the dead. Even in his youngest years Phil knew that the topics were complete and utter nonsense, he was more fascinated by the personalities of the hosts. These boastful, egotistical men, who put themselves and quite often, their personal lives out there for the entertainment of the those who had no life of their own. Phil knew these sad, lonely, listeners lived their lives through these radio hosts and knew that in the future they'd live their life through him. One day young Phil hoped to be that loud mouth on the radio. He dreamed of a venue where he could espouse whatever came to his mind and be viewed as a genius. While other kids on the playground discussed what they'd seen on the television the night before, Phil would sit on the steps, listening to the cassette of the show he taped the night before, trying to catch up on what his missed. It's an interest he still carries with him to this day. If you were to look at Phil's iPod, you wouldn't see a collection of music, you'd see a mixture of radio comedy, discussion and debate programmes, with the occasional shock jock show to go along with it. As a child, his parents discouraged this passion and in many ways tried to suppress his personality. They felt that such a career was beneath Phil and certainly beneath the family. That didn't matter to him though... Phil Atken has, and always will, love radio. Which is why when his good friend Dirk Dickwood called him to inform him that due to his expert agent abilities he had been able to schedule Phil for the post-NC17 GCW Radio, Phil was as giddy as an incredibly giddy aunt. Phil had viewed the week as one of the best he'd experienced. First he'd returned to American and to the an industry that actually appreciated him and now thanks to his friend Dirk he'd get to partake, for just a few moments, in his true passion in life. -- The day of the GCW Radio appearance, for one of the few times in Phil's life he was actually, honest to god, nervous. To Phil this was a one-off chance to finally live a dream. He'd had years of exposure on television to display his... unique... personality but this was it, his first time to do it on the medium he loved. Of course, this was all before the radio show actually begun it's broadcast. Atken: THAT SON OF A BITCH! HOW DARE HE! HOW DARE HE! GET ME ON RIGHT NOW DIRK! I DEMAND MY RIGHT TO REPLY. Dickwood: Phil, you should know as well as anyone that this is just a broadcaster trying to show his edgey personality. You left without so much as a goodbye last time, you knew this wouldn't be smooth sailing. Atken: I don't care. This I can't let his lies go. I was caring for my ill mother and he and that little jewish boy are going to sit there and attack me? I want on Dirk. I want on NOW! Dirk sighed and headed out of the room as Phil began to drum his fingers anxiously on the table next to him. To him, his love had just turned against him and he wasn't about to sit and let that happen so easily. He was ready to put up a fight and he was ready for that fight right now. Dickwood: Fowler's producer said they're not ready for you yet. Atken: Not ready for me? They're happy to pass comment on me but they're not ready for me? That is total bullshit Dirk and you know it, now go agent your way into the call. Dickwood: I can only do so much Phil, they said they wanted to talk to some of the more important... Atken: More important? Eddie Fowler just spent five minutes bashing me unabated. I am ready to abate Dirk. Dickwood: Please don't say that on air. It'll just make things worse. Atken: Well who is he, a man who puts ice in his bloody wine, to talk about me, a talented, classy and beautiful man like this? It is nonsense and tomfoolery of the highest order and it won't stand. Do you have that idiot producers number? GIVE IT! With that Phil grabbed the business card that Dirk had been absent-mindedly twirling with his hand and stormed towards the hallways where the landline was situated. As he left the room, he heard another quick jab from Fowler which at this point was certainly akin to poking a bear with a rather sharp stick. Phil grabbed the handset and immediately got in contact with the producer of GCW Radio. Atken: I don't care what the hell you idiots are yammering on about, I demand my right to reply. I demand it! Go tell your idiot boss Phil stood in the hallway in silence as he twirled the handset wire, waiting to see if his request had been approved. Seconds later Phil heard a click on the phone line, his call had been patched in to the GCW Radio studio. Knowing the importance of the good first impression, Phil's opening line was... Atken: Fowler, you complete and utter CUNT! What followed was described by listeners as an upsetting mixture of racism, misogyny, sexism and a sprinkling dash of homophobia as Atken went out of his way to basically offend anyone who dared to speak while he was on his rant. All the while Dickwood sat listening in the living room, head fully in hands, fully aware that this would be his mess to clean up. Several times, Dirk tried to stop the madness but to little avail. Phil knew he was on a role. He knew that he was finally strutting his stuff on the radio and he was happier than he had ever been. He didn't care who he offended, only that his voice was heard and that his voice was heard over everyone elses. After all, he was by far the most important and interesting part of the show. Atken: ...and another thing, WHY THE HELL DOES THE GCW HAVE A GOD DAMN FURRY...hello? HELLO? It's went dead. Umm... DIRK! Dickwood: WHAT? Atken: THE PHONELINE WENT DEAD! Dickwood: I KNOW! THEY HUNG UP ON YOU! Atken: THEY HUNG UP ON ME? Dickwood: THEY HUNG UP ON YOU! Phil slammed down the handset and stormed back towards the living room. He noticed immediately that Dirk's expression was one that was normally reserved for visits from his ex-wife. Despite noticing the express, he didn't really care. He wanted to know why they had cut him off from the radio while he was still on a roll. Atken: Why did they hang up on me? That was purestrain radio genius! Dickwood: Did you listen to a word you just said? Atken: I rarely listen to what I say, I just know it's good stuff. Dickwood: They hung up on you Phil for a very simple reason. You had more or less managed to insult every minority group you possibly could in the span of ten minutes. Atken: Dirk, I know radio, if you mix controversy and charm, they lap it up, they love it. Dickwood: Maybe you should have, y'know, added charm. Atken: I'm plenty charming. Dickwood: I don't think you get it. You spent ten minutes of a live radio show insulting, basically any group you could think of and it sounded INCREDIBLY sincere. Atken: You're out of touch Dirk, it's radio. It's fun. Dickwood: You may have had fun but I'm going to... Dirk's cell went off. Dickwood: Oh great. I know this will be about you. Look, just stay here and talk to no-one, I'll fix this mess. Dirk flipped the cell open and answered the call. Dickwood: Dirk Dickwood! ... ... ... Dickwood: No what my client meant when he called that child a spacky mongoloid... With that Dirk left the room, giving Phil the death glare as he did so. Phil himself sat back in the couch, basking in the glow of a job well done. What did Dirk know about radio? This was Phil's zone and he just owned it. Atken: Showed that idiot who was boss. That show is mine. As the camera faded away, the last thing we heard was the sweet, sweet voice of Dirk Dickwood. Dickwood: No, when my client said he favoured all elderly be exterminated it was just a surreal comedy rant. He's British, it's a British thing. I'm sorry, I don't recall the comment about the cow anus, can you remind me? And it went on and on and on and on.
