Date: bonus reel Previously on Mark in the dark... I ain’t memberin the last time I been scared. Talkin the genuine, bona fide, wishin-to-be-anywhere-but-here kinda feelin. Delroy robbed me a that a long time ago. But this, well, this was somethin different. The sizzilin noise had stopt; was a sound that used to stick to the background just like the tickin of a second hand inna clock. But I knew from that moment, anytime I’d hear bacon stirrin in its own fat, it’d send me to shivers. And despite the subsidin a that noise, the smell still lingered. I never smelt anythin like it. Was the worstest thing ever crawlt up my nostrils. Maybe if I ain’t known what it was, I ain’t think it so horribly. Knew I shoun’t look, but I done it anyways. The sight of it all was even worse than I pictured: Six bodies all strewn across what used to be a beautiful diner. Three of em reekin foul, burnt up across theys faces. The other three riddled with holes, blood seepin out every which one. Poor Sergio. Thought bout how he might a had some justice, knowin the last thing he done heard was that god-awful racket he always playt. But then I membered, ain’t nothing louder than that there gunshot. Man stood there, all quiet, not even breathin heavy spite a what he just done. He lookt at it all with near closed eyes, and what I saw of em, I could tell theys empty. Ain’t nothin bout him feel sorry for them folks layin on the ground, prolly not even poor Sergio. But he din’t know him, he sure as hell ain’t know me, and spite a what that man said to him after he done killt all his friends, he ain’t seem to know him neither. But I coun’t keep from wonderin, how he ain’t gonna care bout what he done did? Then he done took a couple steps, but I was still frozen in my standin. Somethin in the back a my mind done told me to run, to get away, but the rest a it knew better. I was scared, and it shoulda been cause a him, but it wasn’t. Even though he might been the reason for poor Sergio’s sad state, reckon he’d also been the reason I ain’t join him up in heaven. I mean, if Mexicans go there and all. The phone number the burnt man done written on my orderin paper was in his hand, the other still holdin the fryin pan. Could see the gun tuckt in the front a his nice pants. He askt me for a phone, so I gave him my cell. I'da been stupid denyin him after what he done did. He spoke for a good minute-or-two. I listened to every word. I don’t member everythin spoke or what exactly was sayin, but I member enough: “Who the fuck is this?” “I asked first.” “From a dead man, now who the fuck is this?” “Fuck you, 'Caldera,' why’re you trying to kill me?” or somethin a the like. “Bullshit.” “Meeting? You expect me to fall for that?” at least thas the gist of it. “Fine, but if I find that anyone else on your payroll wants to take off my head, you’d better pray they're more successful.” That part I membert like crystal, cause it sount like somethin from a movie or somethin. There been some other stuff between all that, specificities and some minor junk. Only it might not a been all that minor, just seemt that way to me. Then he done gave me back my phone, dropt the skillet on the floor, apologized for the mess. “Sorry for the mess,” he said. And then he went to leave. I done told him to wait, finally breakin from the ice that kept me. I thought it was shock for the longest time, but if it were, I’d still be stuck there with all them bodies. He lookt at me all crooked, wonderin what the hell I was gettin at. He was somethin else, I could tell. Somethin strong and fierce. Somethin that took everythin I done had, but never wanted anyways. He was my ticket outta here, and I knew he’d keep me safe. I thought a Delroy, and how he’d be spittin fire when he’d learnt I’d done gone off. “I’m comin with you,” I told him. Despite everythin that’s happened since, I think we’re both the better for it. + + + Mark in the dark in... Protect Ya Neck + + + Waitress’ truck met its end more than fifteen miles back, yet it managed to coast another one and a half. She often said that she’d squeeze every last mile of use out of that relic. And though she knew its time had long grown short, she never thought it would be in the middle of a hundred mile stretch between one road-stop and the next. Waitress also never thought that she’d have to take the thing cross-country, but that's a thought that should have occurred before making such a generous offer. Her thumb stayed outward and upright, maintaining hope that someone kind enough would quickly pull off to the side and open their passenger door, offering a safe haven of Gatorade, Chex Mix, and air conditioned interior. Over ten miles later, God still hasn't remembered her. Footsteps had been shrinking in increments, increasingly sluggish. After walking for more than an hour, she’d settle for a lift even if it were heading in the opposite direction. Mark, however, marched steadily, a sizable distance between them. He was at least ten paces in front. His eyes remained fixed on the ground five feet ahead, caring not for the arduous goal before him, but that his path remained true. Not a single second, not even a fraction, was spent attempting to appeal to someone's charitable side, hitchhiking like some parasitic journeyman. Waitress' proverbial straw, in the form of a Lexus SUV, solely occupied by the driver, passed by quickly with absent regard for the roadside travelers. She threw her hands up and halted in her steps, crying for Mark's attention. "Thas it! I had enough!" With folded arms and jilted posture, her piqued frustrations flourished in the form of protest. Mark replied with a shrug and continued walking. "Alright," he said. Three steps away and she realized that he didn't particularly care, and her infatuation wouldn't let her stand idly. Though exhausted, she forced her steps and sluggishly caught up to Mark. "Ion't get it." He didn't respond. "I said, 'I don't get it.'" "Get what?" Finally. Waitress sighed; whether due to the heat, the rocks in her shoes or Mark's inability to read her thoughts was irrelevant. "This man spose to be tryna kill ya?" "So said a dead-man." "And you're fixin to sit with him and have a chat, that right?" "That's the plan." "So someone else done says he tryna kill you, and he says he ain't." She dared to ask the obvious, "Whuff he's lyin?" There was a feeling of comfort that accompanied the pressing of a hand cannon against his back, nestling snugly in the waistband. It was near cause enough to actually produce a smile. "That's pretty much what I'm counting on, Waitress." "What you mean?" "Well... It's like this, okay? I'm about to kill this guy, and he knows it's going down. So he gives me his boss in a desperate bid. But all he's got on him is a number. That's not unreasonable, this line of business being what it is. No names, just numbers: telephones and bank accounts. He's fearing for his life, he tells me what he knows." "And you killt him." "That's irrelevant. Important is the fact that he told me the information. Now, I got ten digits, so I give them a call and someone answers. They're just as confused as I am. I tell him it doesn't matter who the fuck he is, it'll all turn out the same - he's fucking dead. He tells me he has no idea who the fuck I am or what the fuck I'm talking about, and that he's going to do something impolite to my reproductive system for talking to him the way I am. Things don't add up." "What you mean?" "There was full disclosure! He told me everything: his name, what he did, where he was. Told me to go to his office." "Whassat mean?" "Do you always talk like that?" "...What you me--" "The number," he sighed. "Was it really the right number? Is this person on the other line just a patsy? Was that Agent guy even telling me the truth?" "The guy you killt?" The urge to violently strangle her arose. "You're really not seeing the big picture."
