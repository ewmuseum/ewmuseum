Forever

29 August 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/29/10 Imagine running head first into a brick wall forever… Phil Atken’s GCW career works the same way. I’m sure it’ll surprise a lot of people to read that Phil Atken was once a respected athlete. Not feared, not beloved but respected for his rather unique talents. He found his niche as the loudmouth everyone loathed, the man not afraid to speak his peace not matter who stood in front of him. Many classified him as reckless in his interactions, often putting his career at risk on a daily basis because of his basic lack of a filter and the most baseline of common sense. It was the recklessness that people could respect, they thought he was fearless, he wasn’t, he was just dense. That didn’t matter though, these people bought tickets to see what would come out of his mouth next. They wanted to see who he would offend and how they would react. They would tune in their television sets to see Phil stick his foot in it, cheered wildly if he was crushed for doing so and drowned him in a sea of boos if he managed to escape his well-deserved beatdown. Not many men had that rapport with a crowd and the ones that did generally lacked Phil’s talent in the ring. They were loudmouths - he was a loudmouth who could bring it. I suppose that’s what attracted him to me in the first place, I’d been a wrestling fan most of my life and always wanted to have a small finger in that pie. I didn’t realise how large that finger would grow but that’s a different story for a different day. I’d built my talent agency on substance over spin and my clients respected me for that. I was always the straight shooter which sadly did gain me the reputation of being viewed as the executioner with some of my colleagues. “Bad news? Send them to Dirk’s office, he’ll break it to them.” was an often uttered phrase in the building. Gradually, I started putting feelers out to Phil. My logic was sound - the honest wrestler deserved an honest agent and that’s exactly what he’d be getting from me. Little did I know how much that one decision would change the rest of my life. I didn’t expect recruiting one single client would cause me to lose the rest. I walked into my first meeting with Phil Atken as a powerful talent agent, I left it as a wrestling manager. Many called it my downfall but back then I would have called those many wrong. Now? Well that’s a different story. That’s the problem. Everyone changes. … Dickwood: You know why I’m here. Atken: You’re always here. You even have a bedroom in my house… Dickwood: Do I use it? Atken: If you don’t then I really need to speak to the police. 10am on a Saturday morning was a very rare sight for Messrs’. Dickwood and Atken, almost an entirely different world. Dirk Dickwood had shown up unexpectedly at the front door of the Atken residence, immediately pushed Phil aside and sat down at the coffee table. Dickwood: I’ve brought coffee! Atken: I don’t drink coffee. Dickwood: I know. The silence that followed hung in the air like a wet fart for quite for some time before someone mustered up the courage to speak again. Atken: So why have you woken me up on Saturday morning, something I assumed didn’t exist until 30 seconds ago? Dickwood: We need to talk. I’ve started work on my memoirs… Atken: Your WHAT? Dickwood: My memoirs. Atken: I can’t see “Adventures of a Fat Man in a Suit” flying off the shelves. Dickwood: No one goes to book stores anymore, it’s all electronic. Atken: Okay, I don’t see it flying off the e-shelves then. Satisfied? Dickwood: Not particularly. Dirk began to chug down the first of three cups of coffee he’d brought with him while Phil began work on creating some sort of tea. You know what those British types are like with their cups of tea. Creating them by hand and all. Atken: So you were writing your memoirs that no publisher will touch and you thought “Hey, Phil likes to be woken abruptly, I’ll go do that.” Dickwood: Not in those words, no. Atken: What words would you use then? Dickwood: That I was writing my memoirs and had a very sudden realisation that something needs to be done. I don’t mean we just sit here and talk about the possibility of doing something at some point in the undetermined future depending on the state of the Russian market and the position of the moon. I mean we need to have a proper discussion about both of our futures because right now, my life in written form is looking pretty fucking bleak at the end. Phil grabbed his mug of tea and began a staring contest with it. He grabbed a teaspoon and began stirring the mug. And stirring. And stirring. And stirring. Dickwood: Well? Atken: Well what? You want me to apologise because somehow I’ve ruined your life? Is that what you want out of me? I’m sorry I’ve inconvenienced you by having a career in free fall. Does that do anything for you? Dickwood: No! That doesn’t do a single fucking thing for me. I wanted to come here and have a serious conversation about our options but I forgot that I was coming to see a shitting five year old child. Atken: You wanted to see a shitting five year old? There’s a word for that. Dickwood: GROW UP PHIL! GROW THE FUCK UP! With every word, the frustration in Dirk’s voice grew. Phil didn’t react, he just continued to stare at his tea, stirring it over and over. He slowly and wordlessly withdrew from the kitchen and took his place opposite Dirk and the coffee table. If there was any atmosphere in the room, it had vacated in a fire drill after Dirk’s outburst. The room seemed to be frozen in time for minutes after, Phil focused with intensity on his cup of tea, Dirk staring a hole through him from the opposite side of the table. Dickwood: Look, clearly this isn’t the ring time to have this conversation. I’m sorry, I don’t know what I was thinking… Dirk began standing up to excuse himself when Phil raised his hand and gestured for him to sit down again, still never breaking his gaze with the mug. Atken: I know Dirk, I know. Dirk settled back down into his armchair, surprised by the reaction his was witnessing. He was used to Phil’s aloofness for almost a decade and even seeing him on a daily basis he had hardly ever saw the man behind the front. Atken: Bagwell was my chance at redemption. Bagwell was my final chance at career redemption. That convention made me face reality, the Bagwell match threw me head first into reality with little regard for the results. Dickwood: Then why… Atken: Because it’s not easy Dirk. It’s not easy to accept that you are that square peg trying to bugger the round hole. It’s easier to be dismissive and assure yourself that things can only better than to actually deal with the situation. It’s much harder to face up to the face that your failures aren’t some kind of d-ream. I wish I was lucky enough to be the observer Dirk but I’m not. This is actually happening to me. Dickwood: But I’m not the observer Phil… Atken: You’re not the one in the ring. You’re not the joke Dirk. I’m the one going out there every night to embarrassing defeat after embarrassing defeat. I can use the internet Dirk, I know what they’re saying about me. I used to mean something… Dickwood: Oh stop the self-pity. This affects me just as badly as you. Throughout the entire conversation, Phil never made eye contact with Dirk but within a millisecond of Dirk’s snipe, Phil’s eyes shot up, staring a hole right through Dickwood. Dickwood: I left my life as a successful talent agent to follow you around the country. I went to every arena and every company and found you opportunity after opportunity and you pissed my hard work away. You screwed up every title shot I spent hours, if not days negotiating for you and you now have the audacity to say I’m not tied to this? That I don’t have just as much on the line as you do? Who the hell is going to touch me with you on my record books? What kind of drooling idiot is going to think to themselves “Gee, that guys last client is a smouldering wreckage of a human being, he sounds like a winner!”? When this is over for you, it is over for me. Atken sat looking at Dirk ranting, emotionless and expressionless. When Dirk had finished, Phil did fire back with a witty retort or a flippant statement. He just continued to stare at Dirk, letting every single word of Dirk’s sink in. Dickwood: Well, anything to say? The air of silence returned to the room once again, like an unwelcome rabid, incontinent dog. Minutes passed, if not hours before the silence was broken. Atken: It’s over Dirk, isn’t it?
