Cry Havoc: Let Slip.

17 January 2012
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/17/12 The machine bleeped rhythmically, marking the little redhead's heartbeat. Her eyes were closed, but her rest was fitful, little twitches and flinches in her sleep. They were the first few minutes of sleep she'd gotten, and they hardly counted. She tossed her head, jerking to the side, and she stirred briefly, her tiny tongue eking out to wet her lips, and a shaky hand reached out for a paper Dixie cup. Amy Campbell had seen better days. She rolled onto her back, struggling to sit up to take a sip. She didn't even hear the nurse step up beside her, sneaker-clad feet silent on the tiled floor. "Here," the dark-haired woman said, slipping a hand behind Amy's back. "Be careful." Amy gave a beleaguered sigh, glancing over her shoulder as the nurse wiped off the countertop. Campbell shook her head at the flowery shirt. "Has... has anybody been by to--" She took another tiny sip. "No visitors?" The nurse shook her head silently, but didn't turn back. "Sorry, miss. If we can get you anything, don't hesitate to ring." "Did... I, uh. Did anybody call after me?" "I can check?" The nurse swept the trash into a bag. "I'm sure someone will come by." Amy sighed, eyes half-lidded against her own exhaustion. "Don't really have anybody now. I'm just gonna... don't worry about it, okay? Doubt anyone will show. Thanks, though." "Don't mention it. Just... just doing my job." The nurse turned back to the door. "Buck up. I'm sure there's someone out there thinking of you right now." "I wouldn't be so sure about that," the little redhead muttered. She forced a smile. "Thanks, though." "No problem." The nurse slipped out, her head down. Amy rolled onto her side, staring out the window longingly. The last few days -- hell, the last few hours -- had been a blur. The little redhead pulled the thin bed sheet up over her shoulder, hunkering down. She was cold, alone, tired, and looked smaller than she maybe ever had before. Just outside the door, her nurse leaned against the wall, staring up at the fluorescent lights, skimming her hand through her hair. She blinked, lifting her pinky to adjust the colored contact lens that turned her gray eyes blue. Alexandra Pierce pounded her fist against the wall, cursing softly, under her breath. She pivoted, laying her hand on the door handle, her forehead against the frame. But she did not open the door, and it wasn't long before one of the other nurses prodded her to get back to work. Some explanation is in order. * * * Five and a half hours earlier. Two days after WorldWide 156. Amy Campbell pushed her foot almost all the way to the floor, and the Cadillac engine growled in response, the car lunging forward. Her hands were clammy as she leaned over the wheel, trying to remind herself to breathe as she followed the red hatchback down the freeway. She tried to remember how Alex did it, tried to focus on the road, but all she could think about was that this was the first time she'd seen any of them since Alex had attacked Vivica in Las Vegas. When she came home, her lover was gone, enough of her clothes packed that Amy knew she wasn't planning to come back. Pierce didn't answer her phone, wouldn't return text messages or email. Quinn didn't know where her mother was, and Amy was... well, Amy was freaking out. So when she saw Roderick Ashe's dirty old Toyota hatchback lurking outside her favorite supermarket, she took the initiative and gave chase. Ashe might have been a better driver than Campbell, but a craftsman is only as good as his tools, and no way was that jalopy outrunning Amy. Roderick would have to be smart, and, well, it wasn't like the big man was known for brilliant strategies. He did have guts, though. The hatchback whipped across three lanes of traffic in the span of a half-mile, taking an exit at the last possible moment. Amy cursed, jamming her foot against the clutch. Gears grinded as the Cadillac slowed, drawing a flurry of angry horns as she jerked onto the ramp. We're she in a better frame of mind, Campbell might have considered the maneuver reckless at best -- the wear on her transmission alone would likely result in a pricy mechanic's bill -- but worry had made her desperate, and desperation had made her dangerous. The engine whined in protest as she floored it again, running a red light to put her back on the Toyota's bumper. She flicked her blinker, willing Ashe to pull over. "C'mon, c'mon..." she muttered. Ahead, the hatchback lurched forward, and briefly, it seemed the chase may begin anew, but her quarry abruptly slowed. The drifted into an empty gas station parking lot, and Amy was careful to box in the smaller car, fearful they may bolt for it. She left the Cadillac running, climbing out to face them. She was spoiling for a fight, and knew that if anyone in Alex's employ were going to give her one, it'd be Roderick. He took his sweet time getting out of the car, and twisting his bulk out of so small a space was always an amusing sight. "Whadda fuck's wrong with you?" he demanded, starting right in. "Ya can't--y'don' just charge after people like that! I coulda been hurt! You coulda been hurt." "You'd be surprised how little I care about that right now." Amy tried to put on a brave face, but she'd hardly slept in the last forty-eight hours, and exhaustion was starting to take its toll. "Where is she?" "Where's who?" He said it with such a lack of emotion that Campbell found herself sneering as she stalked across the pavement towards the big man and his little car. "You know who I'm talking about. Where is Alex?" "I love how y'figger any'a us'd have a better idea where Allie is than you do." "I really, really don't want to hear it. Don't fucking treat me like you would some dumb rube, okay? I know that you still work for Al, which means you either know ex-fucking-actly where she is or you can get hold of her, because she might've abandoned me, but you could still be useful. So spare me the bullshit and just fucking--" She closed her eyes, forced open her fists. "Please just tell me. Don't make me make you." On the face of it, it was ridiculous to imagine Amy could make Roderick do a thing; the mountainous man had some eighteen inches and two hundred pounds on her. But still he lifted a hand to stay her rage. "Aim, y'know I dig you, but I didn't live this long by spillin' shit I ain't s'pposed to say." "What's she gonna do, kill you?" she growled. "We both know she won't. That's not her. This isn't her. Just... just tell me where she is. Give me a direction. I haven't eaten, haven't slept... I need this, okay? Remember how often I've tried to help you." Ashe sighed, scrubbing a hand against his crewcut. "I know, I know, Aim. But y'gotta unnerstand. She said specifically not t'say. She don' want you ta--to see her when she fucked over that Viv girl. I figger she's scared you'll be sad an' shit." He lifted a shoulder. "I love you t'death, kid. You know I do." "And supposedly she loves me, so what the fuck's she got to hide from?" "You." The answer didn't come from the big man, but from his smaller, thinner partner, who Amy had never even seen. Elijah Hawke stood at the rear bumper of the Cadillac, leaning on a matte black cane far less fancy than the carved stone club he usually employed. Though the wind ruffled his hair and snatched at his coattails, he was unperturbed, only the tiniest downturn of his lips marking his mood. Amy wheeled on the thin man, acutely aware he'd gotten behind her. "Me?" Her brows shot up. "What does she--why would she have to hide from me?" "Alexandra did something terrible to someone you care about." Hawke leveled her with his unsettlingly intense gaze. "Do you know what you'll do when you find her?" "I mean... not really, but--" "Alexandra was seventeen when she came to the Order. I remember she was picking pockets on London street corners when Margaret found her. Her skill for self-preservation is as ingrained as any routine you have ever had." "What are you saying, that she's afraid of me?" "I really don't think she knows what she is. She loves you, Amy. She does, in ways that it would take my colleague's erstwhile poetry skills to explain. But it you have not learned that Alexandra is, for all intents and purposes, functionally illiterate when it comes to matters of the heart. She's hoping you'll understand, but she's not going to risk our work if you don't." "Your work? Your work?" Amy's voice rose shrilly. "I could give a shit about your work." "I believe she is aware of that." "Then why is she--I can't believe--" "Uhh..." Ashe cleared his throat, glancing from Amy to Elijah. "Mebbe we should--" Hawke's fingers twitched on the head of his cane. "Now is not a good time, Ms. Campbell." "Don't..." She advanced on the thin man, a jutting finger in his face. "Don't fucking 'Ms. Campbell' me, okay. I just..." She was pale, nearly white-faced, her lips drawn. "What the hell is so important that she has to cut me out? Like, I get the Viv thing -- okay, I don't really get it, but it's not just that, is it?" She scowled even deeper at the blank look that crossed his face. "Is it?" "I'm afraid I cannot say." "Then I'll find out for myself. After a little hunting, I'm sure someone who hates her will happily pick me up. Sure, they'll probably torture me, but..." She blinked suddenly, rapidly, like she was trying to regain some lost sense. "I'm sure she'd come out of her little hole to find me." "Aim--" Roderick began, but he was stopped by a his partner's lifted hand. "So you're saying you've gone completely daft." She lifted her chin proudly. "What other choice have you given me? If you're gonna throw 'I cannot say' at me, then I'm gonna do what I have to. Question is who. Not Drusilla -- it's not like she's got a clue about anything, and I'm not a masochist. Do either of you guys know Randall's number?" "Amy." Ashe grabbed her wrist, and he ignored Hawke's staying hand the second time. "Y'can't just toss yourself at the lions 'cause Allie's keepin' you safe. This shit hurts her like--" "Don't fucking touch me." She tried to rip her hand free and, failing that, kicked the big man in the knee. It was his bad knee, though, and Roderick grunted, letting go and staggering back. Her hand went to her mouth, eyes widening. "I'm sorry," she said, and she meant it. "You just can't tell me that I can't see her. I love her, okay, and that means that... that..." Campbell stepped back, leaning on the trunk of the car as Elijah hobbled to the big man's side, assisting him to his feet. "I'm sorry, okay? Just--you have to take me to her. Or at least tell me where she is. I can't--I just need to--" The men -- wrestling fans knew them as Black Ops -- heard the sound before they saw it. The hollow thud. The solid thump. Amy slumped against the rear fender of the car and down to the pavement. "Aim," Ashe sighed. "Y'don't gotta--" "She's not faking," Hawke said sharply. He picked up his cane from the ground. "Call for an ambulance. No names, and do try to control that ridiculous accent of yours." "What about... what about Allie?" The thin man slid a thinner phone out of the inside pocket of his jacket. "That unenviable task I will save for myself." He typed in a phone number -- eleven digits he had memorized, because the phone didn't have a single number stored in it. "Alexandra, it's Elijah..." * * * Several hours later. The man with the salt and pepper hair banged the heel of his hand against the counter. "Look," he said, gritting his teeth behind a polite but painted on smile. "I know my daughter is here. I know an ambulance brought her a couple of hours ago. So please, please don't tell me you can't tell me that she's here, because we're well past that." "As I said, sir..." The station nurse tried to match his calm, but ultimately failed. "Without you confirming the name she's registered under, I'm not certain how I can help you." "I told you..." On any other day, Malcolm Girard would have been the coolest customer in the room, and he might've been able to charm the information (and possibly even a phone number) out of the woman by now. But when it came to his daughter, he was discovering there were limits to his patience. "My daughter is something of a celebrity, and it is altogether possible she's been checked in under an alias. If you'll just page her doctor..." "I'm sorry, sir. I told you, there's not a Dr. Circe Leaper at this hospital." "I know that," he growled. "If you'll--perhaps I need to speak to your supervisor." "I'm not sure he'll be able to help you either, sir." The woman managed to keep the condescension out of her voice, if not her smile. "As I'm sure you're aware, all hospital personnel are bound by the very same guidelines..." Malcolm waved a hand dismissively. "Fine, fine," he said, stepping backwards. "Let me try her cell again." He did his level best to ignore the desk clerk's eye rolling response, retreating deeper into the lobby area. He didn't dial his daughter or her lover, however, as he'd tried both without success. The woman he did call answered on the first ring. "How come I've never tried painting before?" Kathryn Shaw asked, without preamble or context. Her little laughing purr brought a smile to Malcolm's lips despite himself. "As I recall, you much prefer to by the subject of the painting." "True, true. There's nothing quite like being drawn on canvas. "So how is our precious girl?" "They won't let me see her. It seems Roderick gave the paramedics a fake name, and for the life of me, I can't figure out what he chose." "Can't the great Malcolm Girard convince a lowly desk clerk to give up the goods?" He pinched the bridge of his nose. "I may have lost my temper. You're not out with your boyfriend tonight, are you?" "Mal, I told you, Phil's not my boyfriend." "Does he know that?" She snorted softly. "Did you need me to drop by to work my magic?" "Ordinarily, you know I wouldn't ask, but it's not like I can keep guessing without arousing suspicion. The clerk has a male supervisor, so I'm sure it wouldn't take you more than an hour. If you could get her reassigned to the mail room as well, that'd be lovely." "For you, the world. Let me shoot Quinny a text on the way. If she's not there already, she may know how to get hold of Lexi or may be even what name Roddy gave." He blew out a long, shuddery sigh. "Fine. I'll... I don't know what I'll do. Why does Alexandra make everything so bloody damn difficult?" "It's her way." He could hear her smile soften. "She'll be fine, Mal. If there's one thing I can say for sure about your girl is that it would take a freight train to stop her." Girard dropped heavily into one of the uncomfortable, vinyl-covered chairs. He tried to smile, tried not to glower at the officious woman at the desk. He only moderately succeeded at either. * * * "Dad?" The soft-voiced question came maybe five minutes later, but the lanky teen asking it was no more Malcolm's child than she was actually a redhead. Quinn Gregory was practically Amy's kid sister, though, so the emotion in the teen's voice was most likely real. Then again, this was Alexandra Pierce's daughter, and to say she was a chip off the old block would be an understatement. Girard stood, crossing the waiting room with a furrowed brow. He found he did not have to reach far to find "worried father". "Hey, hon. Is everything okay? Your sister--" "Pip's fine." Quinn might've jumped in a hair early in her eagerness to relay Amy's assumed name. Her hand dropped away from her necklace (the charm read "Jen" in a fancy cursive, and he had to admire how simple it was), wrapping her arms around him, her head on his chest. If he didn't know better, he'd think there were tears on his shirt. "I was so worried when they called me." "Is there a doctor or something who can tell me what happened?" She retreated a hair, looking over the frames of her glasses. "Yeah. There's a nurse who's been... she's been kind of helpful. I can take you to her." Malcolm dutifully followed as Quinn led him through the labyrinthine hospital corridors, finding a dark-haired woman nestled in a secluded area. Even with years of experience dealing with Alexandra Pierce's penchant for prosthetics and wigs, he still had to squint to find her underneath the persona. "What the devil is going--" "Keep your voice down." The venom in her hiss was hotter than he was used to, and her grip on his arm would have drawn blood if she had nails. "For fuck's sake, Mal, you're smarter than this." "By all accounts, your foolishness is the reason the poor girl was so stressed and exhausted she collapsed," His voice lowered significantly, but there was a solid growl to it, "Yet here you are, perhaps the last person to be scolding anybody for poor decisions." "Yes, and it's the last place I should be. Randall has ratcheted up the pressure, and she's a shiny red button you could practically see from space. I'm here because I was worried. I am worried." Quinn felt distinctly like the odd girl out, glancing over her shoulder. "Guys, should you really be fighting now?" Alex sighed. "She's right, Mal. Her doctors say that she just needs some rest. If you want to see her, Quinn can take you." "And what will you be doing?" He folded his arms. "You know this could all be solved if you just went to see her yourself." "The last thing she needs is that kind of stress today, believe me." "Why would she have been with Roderick and Elijah, then? She's looking for you, but you're too foolish, too busy trying to find reason and logic in cutting the core out of the only family you'll ever have," he sneered. "I've seen you selfish, cruel, cutthroat, but I never took you for a coward, Alexandra. Or an idiot, for that matter. Funny, how things change." "Your daughter--" Her voice got louder as she spoke, and she hushed herself as another nurse passed, pasting on a smile. "Amy means everything to me, Malcolm. I'm doing this to protect her." "From what?" "From me. What I'm--what we're doing. What we're about to do. I just... I need her to be okay for just a little while until this is all done." "You know that girl's head better than anybody," he said. "You know damn well she won't stop. She'll end up getting herself hurt or…" Malcolm sighed. "Or much worse. And it goes without saying that she'll foul the mission. There is no logic in this, but clearly you won't be convinced, so I should just be on my merry way." Alex glanced over her shoulder towards Campbell's room. "Just... talk to her. She'll listen to you. She's worried that I'm... that something is happening. That I've left her. She just needs to know that I'm still here." Girard laughed aloud at first, but quickly quieted himself. "Something is happening, and I'm certain she'd find it a little difficult to understand that you haven't left her, given that you have. I'll oblige, though." "If I'd left, I wouldn't be here right now." She sighed. "We're making them all safe -- Amy, Quinn, all of them. I just need more time." "As long as you're willing to accept that whatever this drives her to do is on your head." "Add it to the list. Just don't tell her where we are. Try to... you're a parent, Mal. Figure it out." Quinn trotted back, her brow furrowed. "Okay, the nurses station is starting to look over, so you guys should--" "Be parents." Malcolm snorted as he walked away. "While your mother is thankful it's only my daughter in that room." * * * A little less than a day later, a yellow cab pulled up to a Holiday Inn just outside the Oakland International Airport, and a redhead in a ski cap and shades handed the driver his fare plus a rather substantial tip. In turn, he gave her a "bitch, you crazy" look, but that didn't keep him from checking out her ass in those jeans. She grimaced, knowing just what was happening, but she knew she couldn't turn and certainly couldn't beat the man right there in broad daylight. The redhead stepped into the lobby, offering a thin smile to the desk clerk, and she took the elevator to the third floor, where she found room number 311 and rapped her knuckles against it three times. For what might have been only a minute or so but felt like ten, nothing came from the other side of that door, but then the security bolt was thrown and the door opened, a crack just big enough for one gray eye to appear. Alexandra Pierce stared at Amy Campbell unblinkingly. Amy stared right back, one hand braced on the doorframe, scowling. Alex's lips pressed together in a line. "Hey," she said finally. "Let me in." "I'm not sure that's a--" "Let me in." "Amy." "I swear to God, Alex, if you don't open this door... I know you're here for some kind of--" The door closed, longer than Amy would have liked before the security lock thumped against the doorframe and it opened again. Campbell took a single, stabbing stride into the room, and her right hand moved, lifting, striking Pierce in the cheek with an open palm. It was hard enough to turn her head, hard enough to draw blood to the corner of her lips. Pierce rocked back, staggering a stride or two. A hand on the kitchen island kept her upright. The taller woman looked back, meeting Amy's eyes again. "Better?" "Not yet," Amy snarled. "You've got no clue what kind of damage you've done, and I've got a lot more to work out of my system before I can even think of forgiving the shit you pulled." "Then you may want to hold off, because I'm not done yet." Campbell's lip curled as she surged forward, left hand leading, but Alex didn't flinch or dodge or try to parry as the little redhead buried her fist in Pierce's midsection. The blow doubled her over, and Amy grabbed her by the shoulders, standing her up, pushing her back against the island. And she rose up, pulling Alex into a hard kiss, an angry kiss. It was enough that it might have opened the wound on her lip further. Already short of air from the shot to the gut, Alex was breathless by the time Campbell finally released her, leveling a glare on the taller woman, arms folded. "Uhh... I'm confused." The voice came from deeper in the room, as Roderick Ashe could hold his tongue no longer. "Is she pissed or do we gotta go so they can christen the room?" Amy's eyes rolled -- of course they weren't alone -- but didn't move her eyes from Pierce as she ordered them all away. "Go," she said. "She and I need to talk..." "But--" "Without a fucking secret service on standby," Campbell growled, finally moving her glare to the occupied section. "Go." "Yeah, but if you're gonna keep hittin' her..." Ashe looked to Pierce for confirmation. "Mebbe we oughta stay?" Amy twisted towards them, spoiling for a fight, but it was Alex who answered, glancing away from the other woman only briefly. "It's fine," she said. "I can handle it. Just make sure we're not disturbed." Roderick's lips curved doubtfully, but before he could express his reservations, his soft-spoken associate tapped him on the shoulder with his cane. "Come on," Hawke said. "I think they have cookies in the lobby. Besides, Kathryn is due shortly, and I'd wager she's not on the list of acceptable visitors right now." Amy grunted in agreement, boosting Alex up so she sat atop the island. Pierce still dragged her gaze to Elijah. "Nem tanúk," she said. He nodded once, and the two men slipped out into the hall, the door slamming shut behind them. Campbell nudged apart the taller redhead's knees, stepping in close. "Wanna tell me what that was?" she asked. "You going to hit me again if I do?" "Depends whether or not you tell the truth." "That... was Hungarian. I'm practicing for the job." She gripped the counter, leaning down. "Look, I know you're probably upset about the Viv thing, but--" Thin, tattooed arms wrapped around the taller redhead's torso, and a pierced nose crushed into her collarbone. "Don't you ever do that again," was the sniffled order. Alex's lower lip jutted out in a small, sad frown. "I didn't want you to have to choose between Vivica and I." Her palm slid up, stroking Amy's hair. "I'm sorry. Didn't mean to worry you and I certainly didn't think you'd give yourself an episode. Didn't Quinn tell you I was okay?" "You left," Amy mumbled, though she jarred Alex back a little with a push. "You can't... Jesus," she finally looked up, scowling. "You're so fucking... gah!" Another little slap followed, and it was clear she was running on pure emotion. "You're emotionally retarded. Why would you leave? You just--" The next time Amy wound up, Alex caught her wrist in an iron grip. "I'm not going to keep letting you hit me." "Let go." "No." "Let go!" Campbell tried to jerk her hand free, and Alex slid off the island. "Stop it," Pierce hissed. Amy swung with her other hand, and Alex caught that one as well. They stood there for a moment, angry and hateful and worked up all at the same time. The moment passed like a century before the emotion transmuted into something else entirely. Pierce let go of both of Amy's hand, and it was like they flew together. The rage might have made the kiss a little harder, the probing fingers a little rougher, the torn clothes a little bit more violent. They ended up on the couch, but neither of them was sure who guided them there. It was a whirlwind of motion, teeth and hands and groans and moans and whines and pants and even screams, and it left them spent, with Alex halfway off the bed and Amy behind her in a move that was unusual for them, her arm draped over Alex's shoulder. "I'm sorry I hit you," the little redhead whispered, pressing a kiss to her shoulder, then another to a barely-bleeding mark on her neck. "And bit you, though that was totally an accident." "A happy one." Alex reached back, brushing her fingers on the other woman's hip. "I'm sorry I didn't... I don't know. I thought I was doing the right thing." "That's because you're an idiot." "I'm serious." It wasn't a very big couch, but Pierce still managed to roll onto her back. "I guess I didn't want... I didn't want to see you disappointed in me." "Well, I am," Amy sighed. "And I'm angry, but it doesn't mean I don't love you. You shouldn't ever run from me." "I panicked." She turned her head away. "I'm not going to apologize for doing it." "You left me alone. You moved out." "I like to think I took an extended vacation." "From the person you say you love," Amy's words were choked in a whimper, but she tried to regain her voice quickly, hoping Pierce wouldn't notice. "And I thought... I thought we were doing great for once." "We were." Alex lifted a hand, reaching for Amy's cheek, and the little redhead almost flinched away. "We are. I just... I needed to do this for me, and I was scared of how you'd react. I'm sorry I doubted, but you understand why I did, right?" "I don't want to talk about what you did. I can't... it makes me a bad person, but I have to say it's just business. She'll fight back, and I... I just can't be involved," Amy turned. "I've spent years dealing with people telling me I shouldn't be with you for a trillion reasons -- that, I'm used to. I just can't deal with... with you hurting my friends. You've got your reasons, whatever, but keep it reined in, because it can't be more than business." "Okay." She glanced back with a small, small smile. "I'm not going to lie, I kind of hoped you'd--" "No. No matter how much the world pisses me off, I can't go to that place again," Amy shivered. "God, I haven't been able to get warm since I went to the ho--actually, you don't need to know about that." "Please. Like I wasn't the first person the boys called." That answer only made Campbell squirm, reaching for a blanket from the bed to no avail. "You never came to see me," she said, still stretching to try to snatch a sheet corner. "Well, if I had been there, I could've kept you from choking on ice chips or something." "That almost happened, a nurse he--wait." It was hard to contain the little smile when she turned back. "You didn't…?" "I can neither confirm nor deny." Campbell's grin only stretched. "Why didn't you say something, jerkface?" "Your room was bugged." "And you're just telling me this now?" "Because you've shown that you can handle stress soooo well." "I don't exactly take the best care of myself, and when you're not in the picture, sometimes it feels like there's no reason to bother," Amy said. "Just… just don't do this again, okay? We were never stronger, and we can work through whatever this is that's going on in your head. I promise I won't just abandon you - give me the same." Pierce's smile was small, even a little bit sweet. "Well, I'm never really going anywhere. You may not see me, but I'll always be there." A hook curled through it. "I did not mean that to sound so creepy." "It's okay." Amy brushed her hand across Alex's cheek. "This may sound weird, but creepy is part of your charm." She stretched a hand over her head, stifling a yawn. "Can we just stay here all day?" "Cramped together on the couch?" "Well, I mean, this is a Holiday Inn, so I'm not thinking the bed is that much bigger. But what I meant was... this job where you're speaking Hungarian..." "That's not today." "Then tell everybody else to piss off, because I'm not letting go again." Alex did, and Amy didn't, which wasn't bad for them, at least for the next few hours. The people in the sedan across the street who'd tailed the little redhead from the hospital might have disagreed.
