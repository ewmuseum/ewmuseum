Flesh

9 January 2011

Date: 01/09/11 He had always hated hospitals. There was never any good reason to visit one, and every visit was worse than the one before. When he turned five, he fell off his new bike and broke his leg. A nurse fitted him with a cast and crutches, told him to keep pressure off as often as possible. When he was nine, he got into a fight with Derek Castano in the playground at school. The doctor sewed nine stitches into the cut under his eye. But as he sat on the third floor of St. Mary’s, bathed under the glow of fluorescent lights, it was not because of him. He was not there to be repaired from a pummeling or to be cured of the common cold. He was there for reasons he despised, reasons he wished to never set foot in a place such as this. That day, he would never forget. That day, Triumph lost his mother. ****** No one could have seen it coming. It was one of those freak things, the type that simply cannot be explained. She was having an early brunch with friends, as she did every Wednesday morning. They would drink mimosas and gossip about whom was dating whom and how horrible some socialite had looked in her garish white frock. On that morning, she was just suffering from migraines, nothing out of the ordinary. At the restaurant, she complained of feeling light headed. After the second drink, she fainted and would not wake up. They rushed her to the hospital and ran a battery of initial tests, yet they could not find out what was wrong. When they ran the CT scan, the picture cleared up; a ballooned blood vessel in the anterior section of her brain had burst. “An aneurysm,” the doctor would tell a confused Triumph. The same Triumph that had been pulled from high school that day by a friend of his mother’s, a woman he barely knew, couldn’t even remember her name. A woman that frantically swerved through traffic, sobbing and incoherently babbling about what had happened. Through the electronic sliding doors, Triumph rushed up to the counter, demanding to know where his mother was, but the receptionist didn’t need to bother to search. Doctor Chen approached him, put a hand on his shoulder, addressed him somberly as ‘son’, and asked him if he might want to sit down. ****** He was still precocious then, barely fifteen. He did well enough in school, got along with his classmates, and never started too much trouble. Model student, his teachers would say. Will go far. It never bothered him that it was public school, that his mom did not want him to go to a private academy. She wouldn’t allow her son to grow up like the rest of the snotty brats she had come to loathe over the years, ones that would make out like bandits on a Christmas morning and still manage to snipe “What, no sneakers?” Attending public school meant he was different for the wrong reasons. Sure, he was always popular. The other kids, they would practically fight over who got to hang out with him at his house and play. But even when he was all of eight years old, he was always cautious. Were they really his friends or did they just want to go to his big house along the sea to play with his exorbitant toys? How many other kids had full-sized arcade games inside their houses? None. He knew that much. And how he longed to just be like them. Sleepovers were always at his mansion, never anywhere else. When he would ask the other kids if they could stay at their houses, the reasons would vary, but the sentiment would stay the same. “But your house is so big!” Fight as he might to convince them otherwise, he would simply be rebuffed, bombarded by the same reasons again and again. One boy told him that his father was mean, that he would just yell at them to stay quiet, and Triumph could only think about how much he’d enjoy having a father figure scream at him for any reason. Eventually he would grow distant with his so-called friends, and he proceeded to search out new people, ones that wouldn’t know who he was or what kind of life he came from. When he was old enough, around twelve, he would hop on the BART and take the short trip out to Berkeley, hang out at Amoeba, find new folks, those that might accept him for him. It would always end up the same, though. When they eventually found out where he came from, what kind of life he lived. Even when he sought out the rich kids as a placeholder, hoping to maybe find kindred spirits, he’d quickly grow tired of them. All of their talk about clothes and phones and other material things he had little interest in, it annoyed him to no end. These were the children of the titans of industry, the future leaders of the free world. Yet every single one of them was an insufferable bore. So he went back to the other lesser kids, because at least they were tolerable. Yet he found himself being less courteous to them, less accommodating. He figured if they were going to use him, he might as well use them all the same. He’d offer up money for them to do humiliating or dangerous things, and as time passed along, the friends dwindled. He found himself caring less and less about things. He started displaying more violent tendencies than before. After much cajoling, his mother got him to see a therapist, but all that led to was a mellowing out, courtesy of a prescription to Valium. Triumph started disappearing for a day here and there. He’d leave for school at seven in the morning, and come strolling in two hours past midnight. One Sunday night, he spent hours wandering around Japantown, and when he arrived home, his mother was waiting. She went on the offensive, chastising his behavior, chiding him for drinking and doing drugs, two things he had abstained from. She yelled at him, he would yell back. They had never argued before, but argue they did. His mother called him irresponsible, and Triumph snapped back that she was a horrible mother, and stormed off to his room. Three days later, he found himself in St. Mary’s. And as he sat alone in that waiting area, enveloped by neon, waiting for the doctor, he began to wonder if this was his fault. ****** The nurse emerged from his mother’s room, stepping out into the hall. “The doctor will see you now.” The walk down that hallway couldn’t have been more than twenty yards, taken more than a couple dozen steps, all of a minute at most. Yet to him, it felt like an eternity. With each step, the walls seemed to close in on him. The hallway seemed to extend into an infinite abyss. It felt like he was walking forever to nowhere. With each room he passed, each one he gazed inside, and all he could see was death. He thought about running out of the hospital, getting the hell out of there and never looking back. He was scared, scared of what was awaiting him in that room. The thoughts that populated his head, of nightmarish visions of his mother, sickly and reserved. Would that be how he remembered her? Every step took longer than the one before, each one more excruciating than the last. His insides churned, his emotions boiling inside him. But he would not stop. He had to see her, even if it meant to say a final goodbye. His mother was almost unrecognizable to him. Wires protruded from under her gown and led to machines. Tubes entered into all of her orifices every which way. Insipid noises, the beeps and blips that claimed that she was still amongst the living, filled the air, floating about into his ears, tying his stomach in knots. He couldn’t bear to look at her at first. When he had, he refused to look at her again. Her family had arrived, or maybe had been there the entire time. He had no idea. There she lay on that hospital bed, surrounded by the family that she could not stand. Everyone accounted for, the entire terrible bunch. All except for Terry, but that might have been for the best. Triumph knew the ringleader, Alice Frost, she of the smushed-up face and shrill, condescending voice. His mother had spent days lamenting over Alice. Alice, whose only purpose in life seemed to be to criticize everyone else. A woman that Triumph had met only once, at his sixth birthday party, an event that ended with his mother telling Alice to fuck off and never come back. His mother had warned him that if something were to happen to her, to not let that woman and her hangers’ on weasel their way into his world. They are evil people, she would tell him, and when the blood would be in the water, the sharks would come for their meals. Now, here they were, hovering over their sister, their daughter, their own flesh and blood. And he couldn’t help but feel he was letting his mother down. Doctor Chen addressed Triumph and only Triumph. The doctor chose his words carefully. No terminology, no nonsense, just enough so that the boy might not end up a quivering mess. Triumph would listen, comprehend, even, but the words seemed to bleed together, like ambient noise without a filter. He still heard what he needed. Cannot operate. Nothing more can be done. Sorry. The doctor excused himself, letting the family to be alone with the woman that Triumph knew, had loved, and would now lose thanks to arbitrary happenstance. The Frost flock remained somber, saying nothing, all the while their bogus sympathy making Triumph want to scream. Alice made the first move towards Triumph, and as she approached, he kept hoping she would not ask about money. Don’t ask about the money. Don’t ask about the money. “Can we speak in private,” she implored, and led him out into the hallway. Don’t ask about the money. ****** “We need to think about our future,” was what sputtered out of her mouth, and Triumph quickly felt ill again. He needed a moment to process what she had said. “Our?” He let the word hang, like it was a turd he found left on his doorstep. Her face scrunched up. His mother claimed it made her look like a chipmunk, and he thought she was joking, yet here it was, plain as day. “Yes, ‘our’.” Triumph tried to walk away. “I really don’t wanna talk about this…” But she would not let him go, grabbing his arm, keeping him in place. He had thought about swinging at her, but he knew what she would do. She’d be on the ground crying even if he had missed. And she was not about to let him leave without saying her piece. “She was my daughter, you know,” Alice exclaimed. “I raised her myself.” Lies, was his first thought. Lies she continued to spit. “I’ve always been there for her. This is a tough time, I know. And now that she’s gone, as a family we need to stick together.” Alice grabbed Triumph’s arm. “You understand what I’m saying, right?” Every thing that she said, her mere inflection, churned his insides something awful. He was screaming inside his head, that his mother wasn’t dead yet, that she might survive, even if he knew that wasn’t true. And when he pulled his arm away from her clammy grasp, he would feel at ease for the first time in days. “I musta missed the party,” boomed that familiar voice. The voice that always made Triumph giddy. That affixed a grin to his face that he could never get rid of, no matter how hard he tried. Triumph spun ‘round, and there he was, in a loud Hawaiian shirt and puke green cargo shorts. Terry Frost, his uncle. He was his mother’s brother, as he so loved to say. The fun one in the family, the one that gave Triumph his first cigarette, that gave him his first sip of beer, that took him to Mexico once, an act that led to their falling out. Terry always maintained the kid needed to know that there was a whole world out there, one that extended past his lavish backyard. And while everyone thought Terry was a mooch and a general fuck-up, Triumph still enjoyed the company of his uncle. Maybe it was because Terry just didn’t care, or maybe it was because Terry was probably the only person that saw Triumph not as a rich kid or just a kid in general, but as an equal. Terry had come, and Alice had hated Terry. Luckily for him, the feeling between mother and son was mutual. “I see the vultures are circling already,” Terry joked, and Triumph tried to hide a laugh, but it came out anyway. Alice pinned her arms to her sides and addressed only Triumph. “What is he doing here?” Terry didn’t allow him to answer. “Gee, nice to see you, too, ma.” Alice snorted. “Figures you’d show up now.” “Of course I would,” Terry retorted. “She’s my sister, as you so often like to forget.” “Yeah,” Alice mumbled. “I’m in the middle of a private conversation here. So what do you want?” “Well, I was gonna ask for a ton of money,” Terry replied sheepishly. “But it looks like you’ve got that angle covered, huh, ma?” Alice’s face scrunched up even more, as if caving in upon itself. “No one wants you here,” Alice roared. “I think you need to leave.” Terry’s grin grew as he looked at Triumph, who by now had become mortified. “Man, can’t you just feel the love?” “I don’t want to hear it!” Alice leaned forward, her arms still attached to her sides, a bird of war upon the hunt. “You’re nothing, and you never amounted to anything! So just go run off like your father did with that whore!” Terry shook his head. “For chrissake, ma,” he groaned. “Do we gotta go through this now?” “For what I've put up with through all these years,” she would say, as if oblivious to her surroundings. “You're gonna listen to every damned word I say!” And continue she did, running down Terry with malice, with venom. Before long, she made it to Triumph's mother. “Your mother,” she scoffed, grimacing afterward like the phrase had caused her physical pain. “I sacrificed everything for her,” Alice yelled. “When she married that sonuva bitch, I told her not to marry one of his kind.” Triumph took every ounce of his strength to keep from lunging at her and pummeling her until his knuckles bled. But even though the violent thoughts flooded, he stood and listened to every single judgment she would pass, every slight against everything that he held near and dear. “…And she left us! Left us high and dry!” Alice’s eyes began to well up. Her voice cracked. “She was horrible to me! To her own mother!” Alice wiped the tears from her eyes, and the two just stood and watched, not a drop of sympathy between them. “I’m not going to end up in the streets. And if she wasn’t gonna do it…” Alice shoved a finger in Triumph’s direction. “We at least deserve to be taken care of by him!” A horrible awkwardness blanketed that hallway, a dark cloud come creeping from the ethereal. Terry covered his face, embarrassed by his mother’s audacity. Triumph, however, had been in pain for days. His mother was mere yards, encased in a slumber she would never wake from. He would set ablaze everything and anything he had ever or would ever own just to have her back. And this vile old shrew had come to stake her claim. “Get out,” Triumph nearly whispered. Alice tilted her head. “What did you say?” His lip quivered. “Get the fuck out of here now.” Alice scoffed. “I will do no such thing…” Triumph might have been a kid, but genetics and a growth spurt had been kind. A mere teen, maybe, but a teen that stood six-foot-one, and cast a shadow over the frail sexagenarian. He hung over her, and didn't bother to choose his words carefully. “Gather up those assholes of yours in my mother's room, and go away. If I ever see any of your faces again...” He took another step forward, and she stumbled a step backward. “Or you will regret it.” She trembled, maybe out of fear, maybe out of surprise. But she quickly turned indignant. “Is that a threat?!?” Triumph remained calm. He didn't pause for thought. “Yes.” He could see the gears turning inside her head. Maybe she might call security. Maybe the cops, try to get a restraining order against him. At this point, he no longer cared what she would do. So even he was loathe to admit that he was a bit surprised when she acquiesced. “Toddy,” she bellowed out, and Todd, her other lanky son, poked his head out the door. “C'mon, get everyone, it's time to go!” They exited from the room, a amorphous blob of the most awful people he had ever met, led by perhaps the worst of them all. And as they gave both him and Terry dirty looks and disappeared down the hall, he could hear the echoes of their protests. “But what about the money?” Just like that, they were gone. Terry and Triumph stood in the hallway. They were relieved, and part of them enjoyed their small victory in that moment, but both just exhausted from her assault all the same. “Well,” Terry chuckled. “That was fun, huh?” He nudged Triumph, trying to lighten things up, but Triumph was in no mood. So he said it louder this time. “Huh?” Terry nudged him harder, and this time, Triumph showed a smile. He knew he always liked Terry for a reason. ******* Terry rested on a couch next to that frightened and confused kid and pressed his flask into Triumph's chest. "Drink." Triumph refused, and pushed Terry's arm away. "I can't..." Terry pressed it again. Harder. "Drink." And so Triumph took the flask, and unswirled the cap. He took a sip, and the bite of the whiskey touched his tongue for the first time ever. He wanted to spit it up, but he decided to go with it, and swallowed it down. The liquor shot down his throat, stinging all the way down, wanting to shoot back up, before finding it's final resting place in his stomach. He gagged, and Terry laughed. "Good stuff, huh?" Triumph coughed as his head went light. He still couldn't look at his mother. Terry could look at nothing but. "We used to live up in Washington state. This town called Bonney Lake. Real boring place, you know?" Terry let out a smile. "Mom and dad were still mom and dad back then, and it was seven of us. The three sons and four daughters, in this tiny little house in what felt like the middle of nowhere." "We had this family friend, lived on the lake. Nice house, with a dock in the backyard that their boat was tied up to. Your mom, she had a crush on the kid. Nick, I think his name was." Terry took a swig from the flask. "Anyway, we'd spend weekends there, and they'd take us out on the boat. Real fun stuff, you know?" "Anyway, me and my buddy Mike, we were fucked up one night, and I had the bright idea of taking out their boat for a spin." Terry went distant for a moment. "We were your age. Drunk, high. Stupid, you know? We just wanted to have fun." Terry handed Triumph the flask back, and Triumph took another sip, this time without much fanfare. He handed it back to Terry, who brought it up to his lips and held it there for a moment. "So I knew where they kept the keys, and it's midnight. The moon's shinin' off of the lake, and I rev that bastard up as fast as I can." Terry took a sip, and wiped his mouth. "That fucker was so fast, and it's roaring. We're probably wakin' up the neighborhood, and I can't see straight, it's pitch black, and before I know it, we're heading towards land." "I couldn't turn that bastard worth a damn, and then POW!" Terry slapped his hands together, and the smack echoed throughout the room. "Right into some guy's tool shed in his backyard. We were lucky we weren't killed. So me and Mike give each other these 'oh shit' looks..." Terry began to chuckle. "And we just fuckin' hauled ass out of there. We didn't know where we were or where we were goin', and we musta ran all night." As Terry further reminisced, he started to laugh. "And when your mom found out, she ratted us out the first chance she got." Terry shook his head. "Your mom was always the good one." They sat for a spell, enjoying the moment, when Doctor Chen appeared in the doorway. He might have been there the whole time. It didn't really matter. "You two can go home if you like. You guys look like you could use the rest." Triumph feigned a smile, and Doctor Chen returned one in kind. "I'll contact you in the morning about the next steps," Chen would tell Triumph, and he solemnly nodded. Terry placed his hand on Triumph's shoulder, using him to rise up. "Don't worry doc," Terry said as he stumbled forward past the doc out the door into the hallway. "I'll get him home." Triumph got up, and walked over to his mother. He kissed her on the forehead and took one last look before following Terry out into the night. ****** Terry hadn't taken Triumph home right away. Instead, he fed him more booze and took him on a jaunt down to Chinatown. There, Terry figured the best thing for Triumph to do would be take his mind off of all the shit going on in his life. And Terry knew no better fix than getting the kid laid. So Terry took Triumph to a place he tended to frequent when he was in town, an underground brothel, and they stumbled through that seedy back alley, past the shady characters and Triad kids with tattoos and scars. Terry led Triumph down the steps, and through the plume of cigarette smoke, Terry gave the eyepatch-wearing man at the counter some money. He called out in Chinese, and almost like magic, a young Chinese vixen in a red see-through robe and nothing else underneath sauntered into the room. They conversed in Chinese some more, and Triumph could barely hold himself up when she took him by the hand and he followed. She led him through that darkened hallway, the one that smelled of sweat and lust. He could hear the moans and slams of the others up to god-knew-what, but his head swam and he no longer knew up from down, black from white. If he hadn't gone with it then, he figured he might have just as well gone mad. He had never known sadness like this. Maybe this could at least take his mind off of it for awhile. When she finally got him into the room with one red light shining above, she threw him onto the bed and crawled on top of him. She reached down to his pants and began to unbuckle his belt, whispering broken English into his ear. He was starting to feel uncomfortable; the primal urges duking it out with the rational urges, a steel cage match to the death with no one emerging the winner. When he finally pushed her off of him and stormed out of the room with her spewing forth Chinese, he decided to side with his own sanity. Triumph could not get out of there faster, and Terry gave chase. Terry grabbed hold of his arm, but Triumph's swung 'round and shoved Terry away. "Get off me!" They both stumbled in opposite directions, Triumph landing on the ground. Terry tried to help up Triumph, but Triumph took a swing in his direction, and Terry raised his hands and backed off. Watching Triumph crawl around, trying to get his bearings, was almost unbearable. "C'mon, kid," Terry said, his hands running over his head. "Get off the fuckin' ground." Triumph got up, and ambled forward, using a wall to catch him before he fell. With both palms pressed against brick, his face facing stained concrete, his stomach grumbled, his throat filled with bile, and Triumph poured the insides of his guts upon the ground in front of him. And Terry just watched and laughed. "You feel better now?" Triumph spat out the remainder of the vomit clutched between his teeth. "She's...gone." Terry's brow perched up. "Huh?" Triumph swung around to face his uncle. "She's gone. Never comin' back." Terry sighed. "Don't talk like that." Triumph leaned along the wall. "What do you care?" "She was my sister, you know," Terry responded. "I loved her, too." Triumph covered his face with his hands. "This is all so fucked up." Terry shrugged. "C'mon, it's not that bad," he said with a smile, hoping to get the same in return. "Not so bad?" Triumph groaned. "My mom's in a coma, one she's not ever coming back from, and here I am in some shithole with...you." "Yeah, well, that's life," Terry snapped back. "It never turns out how you want it to, and then it goes and kicks your ass. But you pick yourself up, you dust yourself off, and get ready for round two." Triumph shot forward. "What the fuck do you know about life?" Triumph shoved a finger in Terry's face. "You're just some scared loser! You don't know the first thing about anything!" "You're right, I don't," Terry snarled. "But you what I do know?" Terry walked forward towards Triumph. "I know your mother loved you very much. And none of this...none of this is your fault." And maybe it was what Terry had said, or maybe this had all been too overwhelming, but all the same Triumph fell to his knees. It was as if he could no longer stand on his own. As if his soul had been exhausted from his body. He had become an empty shell, and the shell could no longer hold back. Tears streamed down his face. "Why'd it have to be like this...?" Terry searched his head for something, anything, to tell the kid. But nothing would come. Only the only thing he knew for certain. "It's just the way it is." Triumph wiped away the tears, and they would reappear just as fast. He tried to speak, but the words would not come. They could not. "I..." He fought back the snot and through the sorrow, and dug deep down. And in that infinite abyss, he managed to find the will, maybe the last of its kind within, to say the thing he hoped to never have to say. "I never even got to say goodbye." Terry was left aghast. He wasn't equipped to deal with this anymore than Triumph was. He walked over and placed his hand on the sobbing Triumph's shoulder, but Triumph found no solace. He just told him he wanted to go home. Terry sighed out a "yeah" and helped his nephew to his feet. He found them a taxi, and informed the driver of where to head. When they arrived, Terry and Triumph hugged in the driveway, and Terry told him to take care of himself, and stay out of trouble, something that made Triumph smile one last time. Triumph embarked on the trek to his front door. Terry got back in the taxi, and the driver took him away. It was the last time Triumph ever saw Terry. ****** The sun peeked through the shades of his mother's bedroom. He sat at the foot of her bed since he got home, burning a hole through the wall, just thinking. Things would be different now. He always wanted to be on his own, doing things his own way. Never having to listen to anyone tell him what he should do or who he should become. Now, he had everything he wanted. But he never expected it to be like this. He wondered if he was ready. He was still a kid, even he knew that. He knew there'd be more like Alice, like the poor kids, preying on his vulnerabilities. But they would always be there, and there was nothing he could do about that now. Now, he had other problems to deal with. His father's empire rested solely in his hands, a juggernaut of a company that would now be his, and he hadn't the first idea where to start. He knew that would bring more nagging problems, ones that would linger for awhile. Ones he had no idea how he would even begin to solve. But he could get to all that later. He had something else more pressing than that. He had to face the one thing he wanted to avoid, the one he wished would just be revealed as some sick joke or an elaborate hoax. It was up to him now what happened next. It was his decision. And he knew there was only one he could make. That morning, while lying on his mother's bed, Triumph called Doctor Chen, and told him to take his mother off life support. She passed shortly after. ****** The next few days were something of a blur for him. He was present, sure, but everything was covered in a haze, a fog, even. It felt like some bad dream, one that felt all too real. One he hoped he would just wake up from. But, just as she had, he never would wake. And he did the only thing he knew he could do, and moved on. The funeral was solemn. Nothing lavish, nothing expensive. He wanted it to be small, just as his mother would have liked. He gave a nice speech, and he remembered spotting Alice during, but as quick as he could blink she was gone. He wondered if she was even really there in the first place. The reception that followed was just as contained, and people he knew and ones he didn't all approached and offered their condolences. They expressed their sadness, told stories and jokes, shed a tear or two, and Triumph nodded and smiled and pretended to take the comfort that would never arrive. They would pass like ships in the night, and when only his uncle on his father's side Kenji Iwamura was left, they both had a glass of scotch and he told Triumph they would meet later on that week to discuss the future. But he didn't care about any of that. He didn't care about anything, really. He just wanted to be left alone. ****** The meeting didn't happen for another three weeks, something that irked his uncle to no end. Kenji, the man that had tried to become his confidant, but came off more like a wannabe consigliere. Kenji's only real interest was Triumph's father's company. Another shark circling. Kenji called, e-mailed, faxed, and texted about the meeting, but not once did he ever ask if Triumph was alright. If he was feeling any better. If everything was okay. Kenji waited in a conference room that overlooked the city, flanked by suits, and poured a glass of water for himself. He looked at the clock on the wall. Thirty-five minutes late and counting. He finished his water and poured another. Forty-three minutes. He took out his phone and reached a voicemail. Forty-nine minutes. Triumph finally strolled in an hour and three minutes after the agreed-upon time in street clothes, his own lawyer in tow, and Kenji held nothing back. "Where the hell have you been, you..." And then, Kenji stopped, and eyed Triumph's lawyer. "Who is this man?" Triumph's lawyer adjusted his glasses. "My name is Joseph Mohr. I am Mr. Frost's counsel." Kenji grew uneasy. "What happened to Mr. Tanaka?" Mr. Mohr pulled out papers from his briefcase. "I will be representing Mr. Frost from here on out," he said, and straightened the papers upon his desk. Kenji paid no attention to the lawyer, only staring at his nephew, whose knit skullcap nearly covered his eyes. "Mr. Tanaka was a friend of mine." "Exactly why he has not been retained," Mr. Mohr interjected. "So let's get on with this meeting, shall we?" Kenji raised an eyebrow, offended by the way this man would dare address him. "Why am I hearing of his change only now? Tsuyoshi?" "Please address my client as Mr. Frost," Mr. Mohr interrupted, and Kenji became further incensed. "Now, I'd like to keep this short, and I'll start by informing you that the deal that you and your contingents have proposed has been rejected." One eye went wide, one narrow. Kenji could not believe what he had heard. "What did you say...?" Mr. Mohr pulled the contract from the pile of papers and slid it across the table. "Your deal was insufficient to my client's needs." Kenji laughed. "Three hundred million dollars is insufficient?" Mr. Mohr kept his composure. "Considering the other deal we accepted this morning..." Kenji stopped smiling. "Accepted?" Mr. Mohr shuffled through the papers and, when he found the one he was looking for, slid it over to the lawyer directly to Kenji's right. The lawyer scanned it over, and his face dropped. Kenji snatched it out of his hands. When he finished reading, it fluttered to the table from his grasp. His attention returned to his nephew. Kenji could barely speak. "Wh....why...?" Mr. Mohr, however, was fully capable of speech. "As of nine-thirty this morning, IwaCorp and all of its holdings were transferred over to the controlling interests of Takahashi Industrial for the tidy sum of..." Mr. Mohr paused. "Well, you read the paper." Kenji cupped his face in his hands, and rubbed his eyes with his index fingers. "You sold the company that...that had been in our family for generations..." He released his face, his hands becoming fists that slammed upon the table. "To our chief rival?!?" Mr. Mohr looked upon Triumph, his face still devoid of emotion, and then back to Kenji and his lawyers. "My client had no problem with the deal." Kenji looked upon his phalanx of lawyers, none of which seemed ready to be confronted. "He can't do this! Can he...?" They would give Kenji nothing in return. "Can he?!?" "He has, Mr. Iwamura," Mr. Mohr coldly responded. Nobody said anything for a few moments, a chill filling the air. Kenji watched his nephew show him nothing at all. Only an empty, hollow stare following the knife in the back. "So that's it?" Kenji shook his head and laughed. "For all I've done for you, you won't even bother to give me a reach-around?" Mr. Mohr remained as steadfast as ever. "Mr. Iwamura, I read through the paperwork you sent to my client. I met with your friend Mr. Tanaka for an hour and listened to every unbelievable thing that fell out of his mouth." Mr. Mohr pressed forward upon the table. "I also know about all the other things you've gotten up to." Kenji let out a labored sigh, and Mr. Mohr leaned back. "So, as far as I'm concerned..." A sly smirk crossed Mr. Mohr's face. "You don't even deserve a reach-around." Mr. Mohr rose up from his chair. "Now, if we're done here..." "Wait," Kenji bellowed as Triumph got up. "There's nothing more to talk about," Mr. Mohr fired back, and nodded the way of the lawyers. "Good day, gentlemen." Kenji would watch them exit, having bested him here today, but he would have the last word. "You have dishonored this family!" But Triumph never even bothered to look back, and followed Mr. Mohr out of the room. When the conference door closed behind them, Kenji grabbed his office chair and hurled it at the wall. ****** "Flight eighteen-oh-seven to Madrid is now boarding all passengers." He didn't know why he chose Madrid. London would have sufficed. So would Beijing or New Delhi. Any city in the world. But choose it he did. He could have easily flown first-class all the way there or chartered a private plane, but he chose to fly coach, just like almost everyone else in the world. Maybe it was because he didn't want to be treated special. Maybe it was because he didn't want to be alone. And after he shoved his bag in the overhead compartment and took his window seat, as the airplane pulled onto the tarmac and got a running start, as it picked up steam and took flight, not a single thought filled his head. He pushed the earbuds in place and pressed play on the mp3 player. He closed his eyes, and slept for the first time in days.
