When the Dust Settles

14 April 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/14/11 “Ugh.” The exasperations of an exhausted mother echoed throughout the entryway of the Windsor household. “What’d you do this time?” The voice of Tessa’s fifteen year old niece caught the former wrestler reduced down to a gimpy stay at home mom completely off guard. Tessa figured that with it being one of few frosty days left in the season her father, or at least the closest thing Christa had to it, would have taken her and the rest of the kids out to ski or sled. It was the logical choice most parents in the affluent suburbs of Danbury, Connecticut would have been all over if they had the time of day for their kids. But then again, Chris Storm was never one for much logic. Tessa shrugged it off nonetheless. She figured something had come up. Long before she even got out the house she figured something would come up. Something always seem to come up whenever she wanted to get out anywhere on her own. Call it the mother’s law of averages. “Hurt your knee again?” Christa chimed in yet again, as if Tessa had not heard her the first time. She figured if she beckoned loudly enough to where the whole house could hear the commotion she might get just a pinch of truth. Christa’s logic had failed her yet again. “It’s nothing new. Or at least nothing new for me.” Over the nine years spent raising her niece, it had become a question Tessa had become far too accustomed to. Anytime she walked in through the front door following any excursion involving a gym bag, her eldest assessed every cut, bruise, stitch, and bump that covered any visible part of her body. It had become part of their routine, Christa’s way of protecting the aunt that took her in as her own. “Sure, nothing new at all.” Christa’s eyes rolled full circle. That was easily the biggest pile of bullshit Tessa had fed her all week. Tessa was well aware of that too. Innocently shrugged it off yet again. “Trust me Christa. I’m fine.” By that point Tessa was halfway across the foyer, carelessly kicking her fleece lined Uggs off her feet. After having spent close to an hour trying to navigate her Honda CR-V through the elements of what felt like a never-ending winter, all for the sake of getting thirty minutes to herself at the track, the last thing she wanted to hear was nagging complaints of how she was not taking care of herself. “It’s still not better though, is it?” Tessa never bothered to look up at her niece to confirm or deny the answer to that. Christa’s head shook side to side. It was just another day in the Windsor household. “Course not.” Christa lamented. Sometimes she swore her aunt would never get better with the way she pushed herself to perform, even on leave. “What’d you do to it this time?” Yet again Tessa shrugged. “I told you. Nothing.” Christa bit down firmly on her lip, her head shaking side to side. Tessa must have thought she was a fool to buy into any of that. “Except?” “Nothing.” “Nothing?” “Yes. Nothing.” Nothing would have been a lot easier for Christa to buy into if Tessa had not become notorious for coming home just a little bit more banged up every week, even on her so called “off” weeks. Having four kids to take care of, one would have thought that Tessa Windsor would air on the side of caution a lot more than she actually did. “Alright then.” Christa held her arms up in surrender. Unlike her adoptive father, she long admitted she was caught up in a battle she would never win. “I suppose you’re ready for better news then.” This time around it was Tessa eyes that darted up and around. If there was one thing Christa had learned from her aunt, it was how to be sarcastic at the most inopportune moments. “Now’s not a good time to tell me you started dating, Christa. Trust me. I’m really not in the mood.” “Trust me.” Christa echoed Tessa’s sentiments. “You’d know if I was.” Tessa smiled lightly. “I can only hope.” “You would be the first to know. I promise.” Christa lied through her teeth. It chipped away another little piece of her heart. If there was one thing that ate Christa alive, it was not being able to play along with Tessa as they used to before the incident with Aislinn and a bag full of pain pills. Before then they were more like equals than they were niece and aunt, mother and daughter, adult and child. But now, ever since she had almost lost Aislinn that night, Tessa had seemingly lost herself altogether. Instead of her family and friends, all she cared about now was redemption. Like proving that she was not a bad mother, a washed up wrestler, or any combination of the two would suddenly save everyone she cared about. Being mature well past her age, Christa was well aware there was no point in fighting the demons that consumed her aunt. Between trying to salvage any mobility she had left, trying to find an alternative career path, and juggling the responsibility of four kids full time, Tessa certainly had her hands more than full. The last thing Christa would ever think to do is tell Tessa she was dating. It would be nothing more than an unnecessary distraction from the only role model Christa had left in her life. Christa kept her mouth shut as she followed behind Tessa up the stairs. With all the further commotion Tessa was about to step into she imagined it would only be wise. “Finally you’re home!” It was certainly not the way any mother wanted to be greeted by her step son, but being who his father was, it was to be expected. “Not today Chris, please?” “I need your help though.” And just as his father would have done, his sad little pout took over. “With?” “Getting Linni to shut up. She’s hurting my ears with all that crying. ” “Watch your mouth.” Tessa instinctively shot back. Lord only knew she did not need her children getting any sassier than their father could be when he wanted to make a point. “How long has she been crying like this?” “Too long.” Christa sighed, siding with her younger brother. “I tried to help, she just started screaming louder, so I left her alone.” “Oh god, is she okay? Where is she?” When hearing of their child’s suffering, a mother’s first instinct is always to panic, especially when said spawn had brushed up against the fingers of death in the past. The last thing Tessa wanted at a time where her life was finally starting to settle back down was another swarm of negative PR courtesy of the local news outlets and any savvy reporter looking to get their first big break. Christa stepped right back in. The last thing any of them needed was for Tessa to have another meltdown. “She’s fine mom, I promise.” After panic though, there was always inexplicable rage. “Why didn’t you call me?” “You needed a break.” “Did you at least try your father then?” Fortunately for Tessa Windsor, the onetime GCW World Champion had the luxury of being put right back into her place by an even snappier teenager. “He’s not my father. And seriously, do we need to go there?” That was of course unless Tessa was feeling just as snappy herself. “Yeah, we do.” And when it came to her children’s wellbeing, nothing would make a mother snap quicker than if she felt there was something that could have been done sooner to solve any of their problems, big or small. “And yes I did.” “And he said?” “He’s busy.” Christa put frankly.” What else did you expect him to say?” It was a response Tessa expected from both ends. It was nothing short of typical that her husband resisted the role of stay at home dad for sake of keeping his masculinity intact, and as per usual Christa loathed him for it. Some things would never change. Tessa’s head shook in disappointment of both. “You should have called me.” “Again, you were busy. Besides, don’t you deserve some time to yourself?” Unwritten parenting law number one states that arguing against a teenager is absolutely useless. No matter the outcome, you’re ALWAYS wrong. Tessa was well aware of that and instead chose to divert her attention back to the far more important topic on hand. “Forget it. Where’s Aislinn?” “Follow the whining.” Chris muttered under his breath. “Just listen closely, you’ll hear it.” Tessa heard every bit of it of what her stepson had said, but just as she would have done to his father’s whining she neglected every bit of it. Instead she bolted down the hall towards Aislinn’s room as if the house was doomed to explore in T minus 30. Some may have deemed her to be a bit melodramatic, but after having spent a handful of days in a critical care unit with her youngest six months ago under the scrutiny of the media, Tessa made sure to take every step she could to ensure she would never be placed in that kind of limelight again. As one would expect with any three year old, the moment Tessa peered into Aislinn’s room the little girl exploded into tears. “Mommy! Help me Mommy! It hurts!” “Told you so.” Chris’ lips curled into an arrogant grin that mirrored his fathers. Why Tessa bothered to raise the boy on her own when this is pretty much all she ever got in return was beyond even her own comprehension. “I’ve got it handled.” Tessa dismissed Chris with a wave. The last thing Tessa needed right now was an overly chauvinistic ten year old breathing down her neck that same way his father did anytime Tessa fell short. “Mommy my foot!” Tessa’s attention shifted back to her youngest and by far most spoiled. With Aislinn being the only child Tessa had actually delivered, she could not help but to feed into the tot’s tears. It had nothing to do with favoritism, and everything to do the chance to watch the one thing Tessa had gotten right in her life grow up right before her very eyes. “It hurts mommy!” Aislinn tightly clutched onto her pink painted toes as she dramatically sniffled. It was safe to say that being the youngest, Aisllinn knew better than anyone in the house how to play the sympathy card. Or maybe it was her bite sized shape that won the adoration of anyone who crossed her path, especially her own parents. “We’ll make it better, okay Linni?” Staring down at her red faced tot, Tessa’s heart dropped back into place. Aislinn was okay, or at least as okay as any three year old could be. “Just let mommy see.” “No, no, no!” Aislinn tearfully retorted, curling her pajama clad self up into a defensive ball. “I hurt it!” “Please, Aislinn?” “No mommy.” The tiny tot took a reprieve from her tears only to sass her mother with her own bit of advice. “It needs a Band-Aid. I want a Band-Aid.” “Fine.” Tessa exhaled with a defeated sigh. Regardless of how many times Jay Terror tore Tessa apart in the ring, somehow he always seem to leave her intact with more dignity than her own flesh and blood could at times. “Just let me see, please?” “Okay.” And just like that Aislinn calmly pulled her hands away from her bitty little foot. ”See mommy.” “Oh sh-“ Aislinn’s tiny palm sealed her mother’s lips just in time. “Daddy said we don’t say that!” Tessa was too caught up in the chunks of flesh missing from her daughter’s foot to care about any of the gibberish Chris fed to them to keep them in line anytime he actually wanted to be a parent. By then Tessa’s head had already planted straight back into her palms. Sure she had seen plenty of her own gashes, bumps, and bruises during her nine years within the wrestling business, but seeing such a gory mess on any of her children, and knowing how much it likely hurt, was a completely different ballpark. “What happened now, Aislinn?” Tessa groaned into her hands. She never intended for the tot to actually answer. “I dubbed it.” Aislinn innocently shrugged, tears still rolling down her cheeks. “Stubbed it? Where?” “My toe mommy!” Aislinn shrieked. By now this was all old news to her that she had to rehash. “I dubbed my toe!” “Outside, inside, where?” “Outside.” She looked up at her mother, completely confused as to what the big deal was. “Outside?” Tessa’s head had barely pulled itself back up before it planted right back down into her palms. “Great. Just great. On what?” “The carway.” “The driveway.” Tessa sighed. “How on earth did you manage that?” She paused to recompose herself. “Who opened the garage door?” “My bwother?” Aislinn shrugged. The tot was lost on how any of this pertained to her getting a Band-aid. “Where was your sister?” “Inside.” Aislinn casually answered, thinking nothing of it. “Ugh.” Tessa’s face planted back into her palms. Watching Jay Terror undeservingly strut around with the title like he had actually defeated Tessa at Dangerous Games was less frustrating than this. “One hour……I can’t leave for one hour without something happening, can I? ” Aislinn’s mind was elsewhere. “Can I have my band-aid now mommy?” Tessa groaned. “The doctor will get you one, Linni.” Why Tessa let that slip was beyond her. There was not a three year old in the world who could possibly stay composed with such a threat looming about. Needless to say, Aislinn fell to pieces in about, say, fifteen seconds. “No mommy, no!” “Yes Linni, yes.” Tessa frustratingly exhaled. This kind of ruckus was nothing new to the former world champ. While such trips were fairly common in any household with four or more kids, they never got easier for either party involved. “No mommy! I don’t like it.” “We’re going. I mean it.” Little Aislinn’s raven pigtails shook furiously from side to side. She figured if she wore her mother down enough, Tessa would ultimately cave in. After all, it worked quite swimmingly for her father in most instances. “Aislinn.” “Please mommy, no!” “Fine.” Just as expected Tessa quickly retreated, her hands turned up in surrender. “You win, Linni.” There was no way Tessa could ever win a fair fight against someone who took on her father’s strong stubborn will. At least, not without cheating. “But can you come hold my hand at the doctors? I need to go to get my leg fixed again, can you come help Mommy not cry? ” That’s exactly why Tessa swung way down low. Aislinn’s head perked up without second thought. “Okay mommy. I make you feel better.” “Thanks Linni.” Tessa grinned. It was nice to know that she had at least some form of control left. “Let’s go.” “Okay mommy!” The tot proudly proclaimed, as if she were Tessa’s own personal hero. It melted Tessa’s heart. Nothing killed a parent more than having to deceive such a precious little being, a being that Tessa herself had helped to create. But deceit was a part of life, a part of life Tessa knew all too well. And if there was anything she wanted her kids to learn, it was not to buy into just anything. It was one lesson she could have stood to learned months ago. ---- Dangerous Games 2010 “What the hell was that?” One would have sworn by the way Tessa Windsor flopped down back first onto the bench inside her locker room that her husband, Chris Storm, was on her heels again demanding some sort of explanation for what just transpired out there in the middle of the ring. Since then, the woman he had married had long since disappeared. Left in her place was everything he wanted to get away from in the business. “Well then what are we going to do about this?” “Just let her go.” Chris furiously chomped down on his lip. Just because it was his own solution did not mean he had to buy into its feasibility. “It’s the only way she’ll learn.” “So you’re going to let her embarrass herself? Break herself? And for what purpose?” “To get her back.” Chris fumbled. He was aware Christa hated him. She had absolutely no reason to believe him. But with her on his side, he actually had a chance. “It’s the only way we can.” “If that's how you feel, then I sure hope you’re right.”
