Navigating A Motivation-less World Calls for a Good Guide

9 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/09/10 "Sometimes I wish he'd just get out while he still had time." "You know he won't though. It's stuck in him. It's who he is." "But the title. Maybe the title will make him realize." "A title will only do him harm, Mags," explained Koz. He swirled his cup of ice water around, watching the lemon dip between the ice cubes. "If he wins that belt then he'll never leave." "So he has to lose?" "I don't know what will help." Maggie sighed and took a sip from her iced tea. Koz's presence and fatherly hands were enough to substitute her typical alcoholic beverage with a more refined one. The thought of a spiked tea was welcoming though considering the circumstances. She shook her head, "Me either. There's days that he's completely there, that he's good. Then there's weeks like this." "It's Caldera. Don't know how he did it, but Steven Caldera pulled a fast one on him." "Maybe Caldera can take him out of it." "Last I heard, he wasn't taking his phone calls." "He wasn't. But perhaps a few concerned friends could get through to him enough that he'd call Jay." Koz denied the idea with a head shake of his own. "Caldera's not ignoring him because he doesn't care. He's doing it because Jay needs to learn a lesson. It's tough love but Jay will just have to let it settle in on his own accord." "That's what I'm afraid of." Unable to abide by all of Koz's ideas, she found a cigarette in the bottom of her purse. A lighter came with it and she imitated Jay's flick and drag. Releasing the smoke above them, she braced herself for her long-time friend's words. Koz neglected to mention it though. For as long as they'd known each other, it was Maggie who'd kept herself together. She was composed, and always ready to prove it. Losing Steve though, certain principals fell through the cracks. Koz was as good of a friend to Steve as he was Maggie, and he knew that the cigarette was doing her more harm than good. "He'll figure it out. Jay's head might be loose but it's not off. If anything, he's more focused than ever." "To the point of insanity." "Insanity might be the kick he needs to turn over and flatten out." "You really think so?" "I hope so." She tugged on the tobacco, "It's just that I care about him. I don't want him to destroy himself if things don't go how he thinks they should." "We can't decide if that happens though." "We can watch him." Koz smiled and took a drink from his perspiring glass of ice water. "A man can watch a flower grow, but if the snow comes it's still going to die." She swallowed the proverb with a little help from her iced tea, chasing the drink with another dose of nicotine. Their food came and Jay faded into the wind. --- Anita Baker's office was a well dressed memory tucked between beige walls. She hadn't changed a thing over the years making the visit that much easier to digest. Still, staring at the Victorian design on the ceiling, Jay knew that the forests he once saw wouldn't hold his attention. Times had changed and his motives had done the same forcing his mind to think the worst. Nonetheless, he sat, listened, and watched the clock as it ticked through his session. "I was told that you needed some help finding your way." "I can find my own way," Jay answered. Her voice was soft; the kind that only a mother could manage. Both of her daughters had front row seats to the screening at the edge of her desk but Jay ignored the company. "I believe you can. You've climbed out of worse situations in the past." "Exactly." "Doesn't mean that you shouldn't be here though. They said that you were second guessing yourself and hung up. How so?" "Anita, I don't have a problem." "I never said you did, Jay," she reminded him. "They think so." She placed her notepad on the desk remembering that she wouldn't need it with Jay. Their time together had been short in the past, but each hour they spent was meaningful. She'd watched him grow into his shell and take on the name Terror at a time that he needed it most. Anita had given him guidance when no one thought it would help. In a sense, she thought of herself as his whispering conscience. All that aside, she didn't volunteer for the screening to help him find his way. In her field, she'd seen a number of cases dealing with the ego, but none like Jay's. He was a fine sample, the kind that people looked to in order to feel better about themselves. It was her desire to learn how he did it that forced her focused questions. "They think it because you've fallen off apparently. It's only natural." "It's also natural to lose a few. Happens all the time." "I know this. It doesn't mean that they can't predict why though. We live in a time where people want to put a reason to everything they see. Maybe it's not always right, but it won't stop." "You're telling me." "So you hate it when people doubt you?" "Doesn't everyone?" "Perhaps. But I asked you." "'Course I hate it. I've proven myself over and over again. Figure that deserves some kinda' nod my way." "It does, sure. I was just wondering if maybe you thought too highly on yourself." "Too highly?" "It's not uncommon." "You think I've gotten too cocky?" "Not cocky, no." She paused, thinking of how to navigate the thin bridge. "I think you've been privileged, Jay. A lot has been handed your way and that can only lead a person to expect more. I think, and I may be guessing completely, that the supply has been cut and you aren't getting what you used to." The hands on the clock had only moved a minute and a half and already he was ready to leave. He shut his eyes and took a breath. "You think I've been babied?" "Privileged, Jay. Don't get me wrong, it's not a bad thing. It's just an explanation I came up with that fit everything I was told." "It's a shit explanation if you ask me." "Then how would you describe it?" "Shit explanation, I said." "You. How would you describe you now?" Terror reached for his pocket but it didn't contain a box of cigarettes. The last had been smoked on the ride to the appointment. "What?" Anita sighed and moved her chair closer to his. She showed her focus by forcing herself into his view. "I know you don't want to be here. Times have changed; you're not the same kid looking for a few words of encouragement. Fact is, we still have twenty five minutes and that's not going to disappear by bullshitting." "You'd be amazed." "I am amazed. You're different, Jay. Not like most people." "I get that. Yeah." "You believe it?" "Why all this about what I believe?" "It's how I do my job. Just because someone says you're lazy doesn't mean you think it. Might mean that you don't feel like trying." "And maybe I don't." "Is it because of Caldera?" Jay smiled at the name drop. Someone had prepared a quality report for her free time and she'd done good to remember the facts. It was one of the things she was best at Jay believed. Some people could shoot shit 'til the sun set while others could regurgitate facts and spin them how they see fit. She had the latter and made sure she showed it off. Jay shrugged, "Sounds like the spin everyone's puttin' on it. Yeah. Mean, maybe it isn't. Not too sure anymore." "Must have some truth." "I don't know. Yeah, I'd like a little guidance, a little something, but I'm not begging for it. I can still win without him at my side." "This goes back to you being privileged." "You don't need to define me like that. I got a name." "The stats show a trend is all I'm getting at. Since he left GCW you've struck out in all of your matches. Could be coincidence or it could be something else." "Coincidence. I'm sure of it." "Maybe. Maybe not though. I know that there's a level of comfort between you and him and I'm sure you lost a piece of it when he took his leave from the show. Might be why you've started this trend." "Do people walk out of these things normally?" "No." "I just can't do this. I don't have a problem, Anita. I don't need to be broken down and processed. I breathe, I wrestle, I am. S'all." "Don't think about it. We're just talking, Jay. There's no right or wrong." "Sure seems like there is." "Because you're making it like that." Jay nodded, accepting the blame. "I don't like breaking down like this s'all. You know Terror more than me I think, and showing cracks in that armor isn't something I do openly." "You never have. Don't think of them as cracks though. You may break a piece of it off, but you'll grow back a stronger replacement." "I'm just lost. People don't get me anymore. I want the money, sure. I want the women, of course. I want that belt though. Call me crazy, but I'm not fighting right when I think about it. S'almost like I won't try hard enough until it really matters." "That's not a weakness though. Like I said, forget about what people think about you now. I know you're good at that. Focus on Jay Terror and where you see him going." "Isn't that the problem?" "I said it wasn't a problem though. Maybe you're gold hungry, but I think it's a new kind of hungry." "I don't." "Hear me out. I see you at a point where the belt is close enough to touch finally. Before, you only saw it. Now that you're within reach, you have a new mindset. Just like a bike without training wheels, you need to get used to it." "So I'll correct myself." "I figure so." "And if I don't?" "Then you hope that you get a shot at the belt. That might be worth it, huh?" "I'd say." In the remaining fifteen minutes, they got past the entire sport and focused more closely on relationships. Maggie was a powerful proponent in the conversation, but Jay reassured that he was merely helping her get back to her feet. A comparison was made to him following the same line that Caldera had for him, but he didn't add to it much. Instead, he let it sit and resonate. Deep down, he fully agreed and he liked to believe that he was heading in the right direction. --- Although Koz warned against it, Maggie found the phone number to Steven Caldera's Columbus headquarters through an online search. She pulled her phone to her ear as Koz threw a pair of heavy hands into a distant punching bag. After two rings, a female on the other end answered. "Caldera Enterprises, this is Stacy speaking. How can I help you today?" "Hi Stacy, this is Maggie McVay. I was wondering if Mr. Caldera was in today?" "I'm sorry Maggie; he's currently overseas at the moment attending meetings. What is the nature of the call?" Maggie huffed, "Just needed to get in touch and talk about something. When will he be back?" "Not until the end of the week. Would you like to leave a message?" "No, I'll try again. Thanks." She flipped the phone shut as Koz began her way. In a sense, she'd found an explanation for Caldera's lack of attention toward Jay, but it didn't satisfy the void she meant to fill. Nonetheless, it was a step in the right direction. "Did anyone call for me?" "No, no one," Maggie replied to Koz. --- Since adding Maggie to his baggage, Jay hadn't visited a city without her. With Koz joining the mixed martial arts wave however, she decided it was best to show her support where it was needed most. As he sat in the make-up chair, he wondered what it would be like to change scenery. There'd been times where he felt uplifted by kicking a man across the face, but others that made him wonder what he was thinking. In the latter, he thought he was dirty, an animal even, and he hated to think like that. In the before though, he embraced the power that came with it. It was the ultimate check to test how 'in charge' he truly was. As the ideas passed he swayed in the chair. A patch of pills was enough to get him through the powder and bright lights. It even made the director seem somewhat genuine when he greeted Terror. "Glad to have you on the set, Jay." "Glad you needed me," he lied. The director went by the name of Rodney and he wore a sweater vest over a blue button-up. He hid behind thick rimmed glasses, the kind that people thought were trendy. "So the idea we had was that you'd be seen in the corner of the ring standing across a big guy. We found a real big guy. You'd drink the energy drink, pull the bandana off your neck, and slam the big guy on the mat. Stand over him, pose some, and look mad." "These things make you mad," he asked, pointing to the Amp energy drink. "They keep you going is the idea." "Then why the mad face?" Rodney, the director, had dealt with clients like Jay on a regular basis, but none as stoned. "Trust me, it'll do the trick." "That's why they pay you the big ones." "Exactly." Rodney led Jay away from the make-up station and toward the ring. Everything was a rush in the commercial industry, or so it seemed to Jay, and he didn't know if he liked it. Once he took position, he was instructed to stand tall and straight. Jay did his best to do both. "Like this," he checked. "Perfect," Rodney complimented. After a few minutes of standing, the cameras were rolling. The scene didn't open like Rodney had explained and already, his back was arched and his stance less than straight. The camera kept rolling. A big guy, the big guy, came into view looking madder than Jay knew how to pretend. He stepped over the top rope, eyes focused on Terror, and Jay started to drink the can. "Cut!" Jay continued to drink while the large man loosened the anger from his lips and eyes. "Jay, you don't start drinking until you hear the bell." "What bell?" "The ring bell." "Didn't know there was a bell. Just thought I drank and lifted the big guy." "Sorry, yeah. He's coming in and then a bell will go off. You drink at the bell and do your thing." "Any other surprises?" "Just stand straight." "Oh. Right." The cameras swiveled back into place and the big guy went under the top rope to exit the ring. A countdown started and Jay brought the can to his lips. He pulled back, realizing it wasn't a bell, and watched the big man make his entrance again. His angry face seemed angrier than the last, perhaps from Jay's negligence. As he stepped over the ropes again, Jay grinned at his stereotypical handlebar mustache. He watched the camera begin to pan his way and turned the smile into something like a frown. DING! DING! The bell surprised Jay but he tipped the drink back as if he'd been trained. He gave it four hard swallows before tossing the energy drink to the mat and ripping his bandana off his neck. He could tell his anger was far-fetched the second he forced it, but continued to do so anyways. The big man and Jay met in the center of the ring where Terror grabbed him under the leg. Bringing him onto his shoulder, it was obvious that he was out of shape. The big man felt like the last rep of a max count and he was sure it showed on camera. After the struggle, Terror tossed him onto his back and stepped on his chest growling. He felt ridiculous. "Cut!" Terror removed his foot, "how'd it look?" "Better. We need the lift better though." "He's a big guy." "It adds to the scene, trust me." "Of course. Expert. Right. How'd the anger look?" "Could use a few turns on the intensity scale but it's close. Just need to fix that lift." "I'll try it." The big man got up from the mat and Jay returned to his corner. The after taste of the drink was dry and tough to handle and he wasn't surprised to see a new can handed to him. Of all things that he couldn't do right, of course it was the wrestling. It was his job title, the thing that actually defined him. Granted, the man was a monster, but so were LeStatt Knight and Andy Murray. On the third attempt at filming, the results went much the same. It wasn't until Jay had a break and smoked a cigarette that he finally came into his element. Twenty-seven takes later, they finished. It wasn't much to watch but it was his and that was enough to keep him going through the day. --- Jay was backstage getting his wrists taped when the phone call came through. It was Maggie and he waved off his trainer to answer her. "Mags." "Jay. Hey." "Hey." "How'd you do?" "About to go on actually. How'd Koz do?" "Second round knock-out. He caught him with a mean uppercut. Wish you would have seen it." "Maybe next time." "You ready for the fight?" "Ready enough. Just getting taped now. I'm feeling good at least." "The commercial didn't wear you out too bad?" "Nah, it went swimmingly." "Well don't let me hold you. Just wanted to wish you the best out there. We're pulling for you out here in Philly." "Thanks, Mags." He tossed the phone back into his bag and faced the trainer. The taping continued and Jay replaced his apathy with a smile. In a world without motivation, Jay couldn't think of a better way to raise his spirits. Although the belt wasn't on the line, he felt it was important enough to win for Maggie McVay.
