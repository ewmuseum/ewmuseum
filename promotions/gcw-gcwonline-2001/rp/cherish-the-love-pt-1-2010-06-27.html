Cherish The Love Pt. 1

27 June 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06/27/10 “You ever wonder what life would be like without any kids?” It was a rhetorical question. One that Tessa Windsor had no intention of letting slip out. Yet it trickled out from the depths of her imagination in the form of a dreamy proposition, a proposition most mothers would not dare to even ponder. “Already know what that’s like.” Nestled up tightly beside her, Chris Storm never so much as glanced away from the Asian sunset he had intended for both of them to enjoy. The only thing he intended to be on either of their minds was keeping the hammock they perched in upright. After the grueling two weeks Tessa had just gone through nursing her injured knee he figured she would love to seize the moment, shut off her mind, and just relax. It was quite the contrary. “You enjoyed it.” Chris shrugged the best he could. Like any deadbeat father, he preferred not to dwell on the days where he had left Tessa to fend for herself alone with four kids. In a time where he was far too successful for his own good, his kids were the last thing on his mind. Yet he still felt little regret for his absence. What kind of person would pride themselves on that? “I missed out.” He quietly suggested, clueless as to what he had actually missed out on. There were plenty of stories from Tessa, the kids, and everyone else involved in their life during precious moments, recreational events, bed time stories, and all the hullaballoo that came with being a parent, but it all seemed null and void to him if he was not there to actually witness it. With a whisper, Tessa assured, “You weren’t ready yet.” The tip of her fingers traced down his massive upper arm. She wished she could have convinced herself of the same. That he simply was not ready yet. That he had not chosen to walk away from her, the kids, and everything they had together for the sake of fame and notoriety. Yet anytime they were on the road together she always wondered if he would grow tired of them again and simply walk away. He had that luxury after all. His head perched against her shoulder, he wondered aloud, “You like making excuses for me, don’t you?” It seemed to be the question everyone asked her these days. Why she put up with him, why she refused to accept better, why she put both her and her children through all of this. He got the same answer everyone else did. “You’ll understand why one day.” Her eyes wandered towards the sunset. She had been praying for that one day for years now. The day where he would be a father first and wrestler second. The day they would finally settle down and be an actual family. Even now, eight years later, those days did not seem to be getting any closer. It made one wonder if Tessa was merely desperate, clingy, a lost cause, or even all the above. Chris Storm stated the obvious, “You’re a very patient woman then.” Twirling her soft brunette locks around his finger, he could not help but wonder how he got so lucky. There were plenty of women willing to date him, many more willing to marry him, and nearly all of them willing to have his kids. But most of those girls wanted money, cars, and everything else that came along with him. They could have cared less about anything else that encompassed the man that was Chris Storm. Tessa, on the other hand, knew everything. Not by pushing or prying, rather by listening to what he had to say. Even when he was mute, she knew exactly what was on his mind. She knew him for the proud yet insecure man he truly was. “You underestimate yourself.” Her head plopped back down onto his chest, eyes closed. She was plenty aware of how hypocritical her words were given her own plights involving the world title and her seeming lack of determination. Just a glance at the brace around his wife’s knee was enough to keep him from calling her out on her own bluff. Chris was well aware that the one thing keeping his wife around the business was her secret drive to win it all. She wanted to silence her critics, to prove she had stepped out of everyone’s shadow. She wanted to be world champ more than anyone could possibly comprehend, and he wanted her to walk away victorious at Rampage just as badly. Now with the pain Tessa was in it seemed almost improbable that she would survive and walk out with her head; Chris blamed the commissioner for that. But right now, that was the last thing he wanted to think about. Instead his thoughts drifted back to his wife’s original probe; one he never imagined she would dream of. “For the record, life without kids is not nearly as exciting as it sounds.” She laid there silently for a moment, clearly in disbelief of her husband’s words. “Are you saying that because you mean it, or are you saying that because you think that’s what I want to hear?” Why she ever imagined he would lie to her about such a thing was beyond his comprehension. The two had a history of being blatantly honest with each other to the point where it hurt. Why would he change that now? He bluntly looked her way, asking, “What do you think would change without kids?” There were plenty of things at the top of Tessa’s mind. Her bottom lip curled inward, her face slowly twisting away from Chris’ sight. It felt like murder to admit all the burdens that came with motherhood. Her voice muffled some, “Lots.” Needless to say it caught Chris off guard. “Lots?” Her eyes strained to stay open as she turned back his way only to plea. “Please Chris, don’t play stupid right now. I feel bad enough right now.” Tessa’s eyes twitched as she fought off the weight bearing down on them. She knew the moment they closed every bit of insecurity built up inside of her would trickle out leaving her completely vulnerable to thoughts she should have never had in the first place. She looked back at up her husband only to plea with a quivering lip, “Let’s just forget I said anything. Please?” “Can I at least ask why you’re wondering about such a thing?” He added on, “You’ve seem perfectly content while you’ve been stuck with the kids these past two weeks.” “That’s exactly my problem.” Chris blinked twice. He was completely lost at this point. “You don’t like being stuck with them?” “The fact that we’re using the context “stuck” with them. What kind of mother feels stuck being with their kids for more than two weeks at a time? Really Chris?” “Your knee got fucked up Tessa, of course you’re going to feel stuck. You can’t do the things you want to do.”His massive arm wrapped around her shoulder. He figured all she needed at this point was a little encouragement. She was, after all, practically raising their kids on their own still, even with him back in her life. “I finally get to spend more than a few days with them and yet I’m having to parent from a damn bed in a hotel overseas because I hurt myself on the job. You don’t see something wrong with this picture?” “You’re doing the best you can.” “What’s the point in me in having kids, Chris? Even better, what’s the point in us having kids? We hardly ever see them.” By then her eyes were puffy red circles draining themselves of tears. “They could have so much better.” “Shit. I don’t know what can be much better than getting to tour the world over your summer break. Did you ever get to do that?” “So we’ve got money? Big deal.” “So you would have rather we left them at home?” Chris already knew the answer, but he imagined Tessa saying it herself would lighten the mood some. “Of course not.” She paused. “But it doesn’t make up for all the other times.” He tried again to make his point. “You’re young yourself Tess. You can’t expect to give up your own life to try and make some kind of imaginary paradise for the kids, cause I’ve got news for you love, there’s no such thing.” “I’m not trying to create a paradise Chris, I’m trying to be a good parent! Is there something wrong with me for wanting my kids to have a better life? I…” Tessa found herself silenced by Chris’ index finger which pressed up against her lips. After everything she had been through in the past two weeks with her knee, the last thing he wanted as a husband was to have her tearing herself apart mentally at the same time. “Shhh.” He pressed his lips up against hers, “I promise you the kids are fine. They appreciate you, I can assure that.” Her hands lightly pushed into his chest, begging for her chance to speak before he won her over again. “I love them Chris, I really do.” She paused only to steal a kiss, she figured it’d be the only way she’d be able to recollect her mind and save what was left of the rare night they had out alone. “But I was so far from ready for any of them. I don’t know what I was thinking.” Chris pressed his lips up against hers again. He knew what she was saying was true. She truly never had a chance to live her life as a young adult. But he didn’t want her to worry about that. Instead he caressed her cheek as her mind wandered off to the kids again. Not a single one of the four kids they shared custody of were planned on Tessa’s behalf. There was Tessa’s niece Christa who Tessa had taken in as her own when her brother Brandon had perished. At the age of twenty one, it didn’t seem to be such a big deal. Back then Christa was eight, easy to please, and always smiling. It seemed easy enough. And sure, it was hell for Tessa to get custody, but it was better that than throw Christa with a whole new family, right? Then there was Evan, a boy who melted Tessa’s heart since the first time she met him in Central Park. He was shy, soft-spoken, and radiated fear. Naturally Tessa, whose heart bled for the boy, coaxed Chris into adopting him shortly after finding out the death of the boy’s mother via domestic violence. Little Chris was tossed into the family completely out of left field. A product of his father’s one night stand with another woman, Tessa admittedly had a tough time accepting him into the family, and even tougher time when Chris left her high and dry for nearly a year, leaving Tessa to raise his kid from another woman. Last was Aislinn, the only biological child of the two. The product of a celebratory evening in Hawaii, Aislinn was the least expected among the bunch, yet probably the most beloved. “Tell you what.” Chris interjected their moment of silence as he set the hammock off balance by jumping out of it. He caught Tessa by the arm right before the hammock had a chance to dump her on her ass, before he excitedly proclaimed, “why don’t we go back to the hotel and surprise them?” Her baby blues widened. It was the first time in seemingly months she had seen Chris burst with such sudden excitement. Since Markus Stone had left his side, Tessa had to become accustomed to her husband sullen overprotective. To have his spirit of adventure rekindled out of nowhere was certainly a relief. “You really think we should?”She inquired; just to be sure the moment was not fleeting for either of them. “You bet.” He grinned, scooping her up into his arms. “It’ll be worth it. In fact, I’m not giving you a choice.” Finally, he had managed to nab a smile from his wife, who folded her arms in a faux pout, “Picking on a cripple, shame on you.” Smiling down at her, he teased back with a grin she had come to miss, “Gotta find some way to weaken you before Rampage if I want that belt, right?” “You’re such a cheater.” “Shhh. Let’s just go.” She nodded. Moments like this reminded her just how lucky she truly was; even with a bummed knee.
