And the Angel's Wept

29 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/29/2005 [We fade up as the camera is looking from atop a bridge trellis to the street below. Easily visible are members of the Detroit Police Department and Fire Department. The camera pans up to show Sam Wolack looking down at the icy river below.] Wolack: And the angels wept. For Babylon the Great has fallen. Biblical nonsense that the minister at my church told me. But it wasn't Babylon that had fallen. No, it was man. When I was on my death bed, ready to succumb to the injuries that I had suffered in that car crash in Japan, I knew that if I survived, I would live my life differently. You see, I wasn't always this 'reckless' with my life. I wasn't always into extreme sports and extreme wrestling. At one time, you could almost say that I was meticulous with my aerial ability. I let that guide my career. Now, I look at my life as it was meant to be looked at. One day at a time. Coyle, you don't know what I've seen and felt in my life. You don't know what it's like to lose a wife and your unborn son. It's because of them that I live life a quarter of a mile at a time. It's why I still measure my life not by what I can do, but what I have done. I was told by my minister that God had spared me in Japan because he had a bigger plan for me. I still think that's a load of bulls***. If God had a bigger plan for me, then I wouldn't be here. I'd be back in LA taking care of my wife and my young son. You see, at Devil's Night, you aren't going to have to worry about what anyone thinks of you. Because after everything is said and done, I will add more scars to that body of yours. I will make you look like the last man that tried to end my career. When everything is all said and done, Babylon the Great won't be the reason that the angel's wept. They will weep for you, Coyle. Maybe then, my soul can come to terms with why I'm still here. [Wolack then leaps from the top of the bridge. The camera heads to where Wolack was standing to see a parachute carrying him to the banks of the Detroit River.]
