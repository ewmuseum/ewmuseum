The dizzying addition, but it all adds up.

17 July 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/17 My name is Johnathan Fitzwillis, and I am just a normal person. If I've learned anything in my entire life, it's that we are all just normal people; we are born, we have the present, we die, and then we either live on forever or we cease to exist. And there's nothing we can do about it. My whole life's dream has been to be a hero. Captain and quarterback of the football team, Mister Perfect and Popular Homecoming King, second father to my little sister, go-getter for my dad, and wrestling superstar. Now it's to be a father to my daughter and some sort of fucking savior or another to the scrapping loyalists of GCW. But it doesn't matter what exactly I'm doing, because it's the same, year in and year out, it's the same that it's been for twenty-six years, and it'll be the same when I'm twice, maybe three times my age. It's not depressing. It's not exciting. It's just...existence. I've learned to accept and embrace it. Two weeks before Warpath... The bar's not too noisy. Not too quiet. But it's not quite just right. I'd say it's a classy dive, or maybe a charming armpit in an otherwise completely bland city that I know as 'home.' Under the constantly dulling streetlights of Buffalo, New York, is a constant malaise. After all, this town isn't a winner's town. This town is a 'come close' town. With the infamous Buffalo Bills and their four superbowl runs, with Norwood's Wide Right and all that, and then the Buffalo Sabres with losing in the Cup Finals twice and not even making it there on their best year. We lost our Basketball team before I was born. The baseball team is triple A. On top of everything, there isn't a job to be kept around here save for one that travels. And yet, there are those of us that stay. And why? 'To perpetuation, Johnny-boy. May it never end.' The Devil raises his can of Genny and I tip my bottle of Jack Daniel's and the two of us throw back a drink as the banter of the world flies by on the television as fast as we can try to pay attention. Wars on the other side of the planet, wars on the streets around us. And if there's not a war inside my head, I don't know what I'm losing. 'To cycles, Don.' Don lights a menthol. I've explained how I'm friends with the Devil one too many times. I'm sick of explaining it, mainly because there is nothing to explain. The entirety of the explanation is that I'm friends with the Devil. Finito. Over with. If there was a way to put it other than that, I would do so. However, there isn't. My friend, Don, is the fallen angel Lucifer who reigns over Hell. And he's also a resident of Canada, in the woods near Ottawa, and he hates the Senators. He drinks cheap beer. He smokes cigarettes because he's addicted. And he retains the memories of countless lives throughout the ages of his being the Devil. Other than that, he's just another person going through the motions and living each day because, in the end, that's all he can do. I'm also not going to explain Johnny Borealis and Jack Daniel's, Jack and Jack, for the millionth time. I've gone over it enough and as proud as I am of the connection and the poetry I talk about with the 'beautiful intertwining of two friends, one consuming the other and clouding all that is,' it's really just a bunch of bullshit. I drink whiskey and it makes me feel good. Stop trying to kid yourself into thinking it's special, Johnny. 'To cycles. May they never end. May I never be reincarnated as something that goes through them.' He lights up another cigarette. First one's gone already. 'You've...you've never been a woman, Donny?' A sideways look is my reward for asking. His brown comes down and the insides go up a bit, looking confused that I would even ask such a question. I can almost swear for the first time I see horns on the top of his head. And with the cigarette still dangling from his lips, he asks a simple question that I should already know the answer to. 'Now why the fuck would I do that...when I don't have to?' 'Christ, I'm sorry. I didn't realize that you had a choice in who you were born as.' 'Of course I have a choice! And don't use that name around me.' Shrug. Who would have guessed that the female gender would have been one of his buttons. But, I guess with the number of probelms the man's had over the eons with women, it's not hard to understand why he'd not want to be one. I mean, they're complex, artful creatures. Even the brutish ones that perform at fairs and pump gas for big riggers are enigmas. Whereas all he has to worry about is producing, storing, and shooting off sperm. And blue balls. Blue balls suck. My phone rings. Check the number and I don't recognize it other than it's called me a couple times already today. Don lights up another cigarette. 'Who the fuck keeps calling you, Johnny-boy?' 'Santa.' 'Oh yeah? What's he want?' '...he wants all the Japanese kids to stop being good so his one work night a year is shorter. The fuck do I know?' And we return to silence. I don't think it takes a genius to see that I've been on edge lately. The last six months have been the vacation I really didn't need. Starting out with Sarah, Sarah, and Kate. That is, Sarah, my daughter. Then Sarah, the girl I've been sleeping with. Then Kate, my daughter's mother. You can see without looking the roughness of the road. And on top of that, my dad finally bites it. Didn't affect me that much, though I guess it was supposed to. Not like we've talked in years. But that's when Jane, my sister comes into play. So now it's father to one, father to two, supporter to one, and boyfriend to another. Isn't life glorious? And I wondered why Don didn't want to be reincarnated as a woman. The phone rings again. Same number. 'Answer it.' The simplest things are always hard. 'No.' The hardest things are always the most rewarding. 'You'll thank me. Answer it.' And the easy way goes nowhere. The opening of the phone is the opening of a vault. Six months of want washing away before I even get it all the way open. I know who it is. I watch the TV. '...hello?' 'Mister Fitzwillis?' 'This is Johnathan Fitzwillis.' 'This is the office of Mister Steven Caldera.' 'I know.' Poor girl on the other end sounds confused, befuddled. She's new, that's for sure. Nobody else would have asked for Mister Fitzwillis. Except Caldera himself, but he doesn't really make personal calls. Cuz he's a jerk. '...Mister Caldera would like to meet with you, Mister Fitzwillis.' I take another swig of Jack. It burns all the way down, washing piss-tasting resentment from rejection and regret down with it into the pit of my stomach where I can hold onto it and condense it and let it all harden and boil together and hold it there until my dying day when I can let it out with one last breath. If only I could hold the whiskey there too. 'I wanted to speak with Mister Caldera three months ago. He didn't return my calls.' Now I know she's confused. Papers rustle on the other end and her incompetence, mixed with the insult added to injury of the entire situation, is almost too much to bear. And even if the ring is somehow what's been 'missing' from my life, that doesn't mean that I am about to bend over and take it from someone who didn't return my calls three months ago. 'He wishes to discuss contract negotiations for-' 'Tell him I'm not interested in contracts. I'm not interested in contracts, I'm not interested in titles, I'm not interested in G. C. W. Thank you very much.' I can see Don looking at the table out of the corner of my eye as I hang up the phone. And I know that when Don looks at the table, he's not looking at the table. He's doing some sort of Devil voodoo where he's looking at my soul or some horse shit. And the Devil's smoke stained voice cuts into me like wind on a boat. 'You weren't exactly polite to her.' I shake my head and look straight ahead. 'No. I wasn't.' Donny-boy lights another one. 'Well, I can't say I blame you. After all, you've got a lot going on in your life right now. Don't want to mess that up, y'know? I mean, you've got it made. You are living off the interest of what you had in the bank, not to mention that you're splitting living expenses with a little rich girl that you're fucking. And on top of that, you're not paying allimony because your lawyer of an ex actually won't take your fucking money. But you're still on speaking terms. And you hit the gym for hours a day on end to accomplish not getting any bigger, not getting in any better shape, and in general wasting your time all over the place.' 'You're one to talk. You do absolutely nothing.' 'True. Remember, I have countless lifetimes. You've got one.' 'Right. Mine. Not anyone else's.' 'Don't you want the taste of stardome again? That pride clawing its way from your gut yet?' 'I've already had my taste stardome in GCW. It wears out, Don. Like chewing gum.' For the first time, he looks at me. Not at the table, but at me. '...that's why they put five sticks in a pack, Johnny-boy.' He laughs a bit and some smoke from the first two cigarettes finally comes out of his nose and mouth and probably ears. And for that split second I can see a person, a living person that you can pity, inside the skin of Satan. And perhaps his advice is just the advice of a friend. *** My name is Johnny Borealis, and I am better than you. I've been addicted, I've been dicked, and I've been a dick, and through it all nobody's ever had the last laugh. The closest anyone's ever come was Eva Van Lorne, and she just had to admit that she needs my help. I've seen groups come and go like stormclouds and champ after champ rise up only to fall. Dissipated, eradicated, subjugated, or just stagnated, nobody lasts forever and everybody fails. Those that accept this have a chance to rise above it and, paradoxically, even disproving it. Those that think they're immortal are wrong, just like those who don't realize that I'm better than them. Six days before Warpath (scheduled to air, of course) It's taking all I have to press the 'dial' button. It's taking a finger that floats over it and touches it slightly but backs off just before I commit to the plunge. After all, while I may be going after vanity, I am sacrificing pride. Isn't that counter-productive in the end? It's worth it, it's not worth it, reasons for each ping-ponging in my head. Back and forth, thinking about what I'd have to do versus possibly regretting what could have been. Like so many things in life this is one of those times where if I do it I'll kick myself and if I don't I know I'll be kicking myself later. And in a tornado of what ifs and wants and needs and desires and everything and nothing, somewhere in there I hit the button to dial and the dial tone in my ear is barely enough to snap me out of it in time. 'Yes, Johnathan?' God? 'Caldera. How'd you know it was me?' He must have known. He must have known that this is what I've been missing, been needing deep inside for so long now. Not just months, but my life. It's not just a drug you get addicted to, it's a way of living you can't do without. The rush, the crowd, the exhuberation. 'I have Caller ID, Johnny. And I'm glad you called. I was hoping you'd reconsider my offer.' 'Actually, your secretary never made it to an offer.' There's a tight laugh on the other end. He's putting on a great show, but it's not hard to see that Steven Caldera's a man who might just break pretty soon. And while those can be the most lucrative, they're also always the most dangerous. 'Of course. You've been hanging up or not answering at the least possible opportune time.' 'Yeah, well, that's one of the things that I'm good at.' 'Kind of like taking off without a trace just before you're about to recieve a title shot?' Ouch. His words sting like needles right to the heart and brain and...something else that's vital. Spleen? I didn't figure he'd be 'over' that whole thing just yet, but for him to throw his hand down on the table like that and basically call me out isn't really the move of the great tactician that is Steven Caldera that I was expecting. Maybe that's exactly why he did it. 'Yeah. Kind of like that.' 'Where did you go to anyway, John?' Something witty. Something witty! Come on, hit him with something witty! '...Iowa?' ...who the fuck goes to Iowa? He's not amused. Maybe if I had said something witty. 'Well regardless of where you disappeared to or why, I'm willing to extend the olive branch to you. I'd be willing to welcome you back into the fold if-' Other line rings. 'Just a second.' 'Excuse me John? I'm in the middle of proposing something.' 'It's important. Just hold that thought.' If I didn't cut him off and switch lines, I'd swear I could hear his jaw drop to the floor. 'Hollywood.' 'John.' 'Kate.' The battle lines have been drawn. 'John, I was wondering if maybe you'd know where our daughter learned a certain couple of words that she didn't have any knowledge of until she spent that weekend with you.' 'Shit, I don't know.' I said Kate and I were on good terms. We're not at all on great terms. As in, we speak, as you can see. 'John, you said you were going to watch it around her.' 'Well maybe I'd have more practice with that if I were around her more and you didn't insist on pretty much keeping her from seeing her father.' 'Well maybe if someone wasn't sleeping with someone that she's named after.' Yeah. That's another story I don't want to repeat. The gist of it is that Kate thought she was naming our daughter after my mother, whose name also happens to be Sarah. Turns out she named our daughter after my first love, who I'm now sleeping with and seeing and the sex is fantastic, by the way. Daytime TV in real life. 'Look, Kate, can we talk about this later? I have another call.' 'Johnny, don't you-' Beep. 'This is Johnny's phone.' 'What are you wearing?' Sarah's voice takes me by complete surprise. No, not my daughter's you sick fucks. The other Sarah. It gets confusing for me too. 'I'm currently wearing jeans, a T-shirt, and a lot of excess baggage.' 'Poor baby. I was wondering if you wanted to come over and...talk about it?' I can see her playing with her index finger, biting it just a little bit and smiling behind it. It doesn't take a genius to figure out what she wants. 'Give me...one second.' Switch lines. 'Caldera, I'll be at Warpath. We can discuss everything there.' 'Johnny, I'd rather-' 'Sorry, man. But if you were in my situation, you'd do the exact same thing.' No one ever said that because I've been unemployed that my life has been boring. Excitement always does rear its ugly head all at once, though. *** My name is Johnny Heroic, and I am a super hero. I had powers once, yet I had no drive. I could do anything, so why bother? With the cosmos at my fingertips, with the ability to control any degree of an electromagnetic field and thus control physics, time, and life itself, I realized the pointlessness of doing anything. After all, if you can do anything, nothing is an accomplishment. Guarding The City for years on end staled me, hardened any drive until my care atrophied. It wasn't until my powers were removed that I became a true hero. Turns out I never had 'control' of physics but that I was only just lucky. I asked the universe for things and it gave them to me, until one day when it just said 'no.' Restrictions meant want, no powers meant desire, just as my arch-nemesis Desire was the one who convinced existence to ignore my requests. Since then, I have become a true super hero because I am just a man, just a normal person who is better than you only for what he does, not what he can do. I enforce the Law and rise toward the Impossible when I shouldn't be able to. At Warpath Caldera's office is a constant. It rarely, if ever, changes even though his freak show travels from city to city. Kind of like his face...it never changes, and he just keeps bringing it with him. 'Are we agreed, Johnathan?' The contract looks oddly familiar. 'This is what I made before.' 'Exactly.' Sit back. 'Don't you usually pay more for people that you go out and actively...beg to come back?' Caldera doesn't look pleased. I wouldn't be pleased either. But I wouldn't have started this out with basically saying that he needs my help. 'John...I don't know exactly what Eva has told you...' I wish I could replay that moment over and over again in my head. Eva saying that she needs my help. That she could my abilities. That she, high and mighty that she is, actually asked me to help her. Not that I want Eva anymore. I mean, I think if I ever slept with her, my genitals would fall off due to frostbite. '...but GCW does need some loyalist backing. And you would be coming in to do that job.' 'Uh huh...' I read over that part of the contract. 'You think I'm some sort of GCW loyalist?' 'You'd be paid to be that.' 'Uh huh...' Re-read it. 'You don't actually expect me to say that I'm with The Ascention, do you?' He looks flustered. He wanted me to just sign it, admitting I need him as much as he needs me. But I don't play my cards quite that way, even if that is true. 'It...would strengthen resolve in a...lot of different ways.' 'No way.' 'Why not?' 'They're tools. I'm not a tool.' I pass the contract over to him. Unsigned yet. 'What's it going to take, Johnathan? I'm willing to make a deal. That's...why you're here.' The cycle starts again. Just like Don pointed out in the bar, just like it's been pointed out to me over and over again. Hiatuses, breaks, and all out firings, and yet again I'm getting back onto the proverbial horse. Isn't that what matters the most? Right here, right now, it's what matters most to Steven Caldera. That and for me to put my name on the dotted line. 'It's going to take understanding, Caldera. I need you, as well as everybody involved in The Ascention, to understand. Same thing I need to understand myself. I don't give a shit...about The Ascention. And I don't give a shit about the state of GCW. To me, it's a name.' 'Interesting way to jockey for position.' 'And I also don't care if Seymour Almasy has turned on you. Or if Eva needs my help. Or if the crowd chants for a hero to stand tall in the face of a group that's taking over this federation and you need someone to help bolster your defenses.' The truth is I'm accepting my return match against Seymour Almasy for the simple fact that I do not like Seymour Almasy. I don't like his style. I don't like this mantra. I don't like his name, I don't like his face. Yes, in his debut match he beat me. I don't blame that on chance or on a bad day...I dropped the ball. I dropped the ball then, and what matters isn't then. What matters here and now is the here and now, and that is that I'm starting over and I'm starting over with him. A man that I do not like. A man that probably doesn't give two shits about me because he doesn't give two shits about anyone. That is, except for spikey-haired retards who live inside computer games, away from the truths of what you can see and touch, and preach things that someone like him will never understand. I don't like Seymour Almasy because he's a prick. Is there any other way to put it? Can we not say that he defines prickness? That he exists and is a prick. That Seymour wakes up, yawns, looks around his room, and is a prick? Steven Caldera probably thinks that I'm accepting this match as my first on my contract to rise to a new standing. To make a return match and have a chance to climb the totem pole. But Steven Caldera can bite my ass. He could bite my ass when I was here before, he could bite my ass while I was gone, and he can bite my ass if he thinks that now. He's still looking at me from the other side of his desk. It's taken him a moment to see if I'm done speaking after I say the word 'defenses,' and I think a true wrestler still lurks somewhere inside of him. Because he simply puts the contract back in front of me and nods his head. And we're both ready to move foreward. 'Good to have you back, Johnny Borealis.' Somewhere inside of him, the heart of a wrestler does beat. Cycles start and end. And then they start again. And yet again, I'm a fighter for a paycheck. But at the same time, so much more. So much more. *** I am Johnathan Fitzwillis, a normal man existing every day. Dust already. I am Johnny Borealis, and I am never staying down. Simply incredible. I am Johnny Heroic, and I want no power but the power to want. Hero defined. I have seen a lifetime of sights, men and women tempered till they break, shaken hands with the Devil himself at his cottage in Ontario, coexisted with hate, slaughtered Fortune and Fate, looked in the mirror and seen what's there and what's missing, fathered a child, forgotten a father, confessed to a stranger, lied to my love, left the woman of my dreams and a life of happiness to see if the world would end, fallen down, gotten up, thrown back, and moved onward. And the world hasn't seen anything yet. And leaving off this way makes it all so much more dramatic. So much bolder in my statements. At least, I hope it does. Doesn't it? Eh, eat shit.
