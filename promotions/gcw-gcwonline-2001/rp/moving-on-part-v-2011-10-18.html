Moving On: Part V

18 October 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/18/11 It had been an emotional few weeks. Aaron’s GCW contract had expired and a new one was offered to him. At the same time he thought he had found a job in his dream field; meteorology. Technicalities and the last two months of his Master’s Degree stopped him from getting the job. He knew in the back of his mind there was only one thing to do now; agree to the contract. In an unorthodox manner he had signed and returned the legal document with a note asking that it not be tendered until he contacted the company. He was close to doing so, but he needed to see something from GCW to make sure his decision was right. Worldwide 154 – 3 P.M. As soon as Aaron received the phone call from James Bryan the night before asking him to come to the arena early for a meeting he was nervous. He had been working without a contract for nearly a month. Aaron knew it wasn’t the courteous thing to do; he was collecting bigger single event paydays than everyone on his level would get per their contracts. Aaron knew this would be when the ultimatum came. James Bryan and all of the other powers that be were going to tell Aaron he needed to sign a new contract or take a walk. Aaron walked into the conference room expecting to see James Bryan and Steven Caldera at most, but he was greeted by three or four other faces. He recognized them as the creative team for GCW. The meeting had started at one in the afternoon. Aaron glanced at the clock on the wall and saw they had been meeting for two hours. “I guess that’s that.” James Bryan said as he patted his hands on the table before he stood. Aaron followed suit and extended his hand across the table. James shook it and Aaron then turned to offer the same gesture to Steven Caldera. Steven already had his hand extended. “Have fun tonight,” Aaron’s boss said. “Don’t straggle around too long or else you’re going to get caught up in a whole different mess.” Steven chuckled after his final comment. Aaron walked out of the conference room alone and made the short journey to the locker room that had begun to fill with the guys. Aaron made his way to the corner he had chosen when he arrived at the arena and put himself on the floor. Aaron had gone into the meeting with nerves and came out with confusion. There was no ultimatum. There weren’t any threats of being forced out of the company. In fact it was quite the opposite. The GCW Creative Team and Steven Caldera were putting stock in a man that didn’t even have a contract. The meeting was called to inform Aaron of the new direction they wanted to take his character. By the end of the night he would become the Television Champion and would have a spot in the Rampage Final Four. The company must really see something in him to put all that on a man that could leave at any moment. Aaron stayed in his seated position on the floor for a while as more and more of the GCW roster arrived to the locker room. Eventually Johnny Borealis approached him and said a few simple words that made it all feel real. “Are you ready to become a star?” Friday – 2 P.M. Call him crazy, but Aaron was on his way back to Des Moines, Iowa to the WX Experts where he had his interview just a week prior. It felt awkward to him, but it was something that had to be done. But he wasn’t going back to inquire about other possibly job openings. He wasn’t even going back to try and create a working relationship with the staff at the company. Aaron was going back for one specific reason; and her name was Amanda. After returning from lunch with Scott Lofton the previous week, Aaron had noticed the beautiful young receptionist working in the lobby of the forecasting firm. After he had come so close to getting his dream job, Aaron knew he couldn’t leave Des Moines empty handed. It was time to forget the past he had with Chloe. He’d always care about her, but it had been made clear that she didn’t feel the same. Aaron knew what he had to do and asked Amanda out for a date. She seemed a little hesitant at first, but after some general conversation she gave in under the condition that they would go out in Des Moines. With all of the stress in his life it felt like a good thing to do. Friday – 6 P.M. Aaron walked through the front doors of the company he had almost landed a job with a different intention this week. He made great time on the drive over and hadn’t even gotten a speeding ticket like his last trip. He walked the short distance from the doors of the building to the reception area. “Well look who made it true to his word,” Amanda said with a smile. She was sitting behind the reception desk organizing her things. She glanced up to Aaron once or twice and smiled in Aaron’s direction. Aaron waited patiently alongside his date’s work area. He hadn’t planned much for their evening together, instead taking the chance of playing it by ear. He felt comforted by her comment. They hadn’t spoken since the last time he had been in Des Moines; not even a friendly text message. Aaron liked it that way. “I’m almost ready, Aaron. Why don’t you take a seat over there?” Amanda suggested. Aaron nodded and strolled over to the couches and chairs a few feet away. He grabbed one of the numerous magazines and took a seat to wait for her. After flipping a few pages through this month’s edition of a popular weather publication he heard the chatter of people reverberated off of the building’s walls. It was the end of the work week for most. He watched as the first few employees made their way through the lobby and out the door. “You ready?” Amanda shouted over the click-clack of shoes walking on the tile floor. Aaron smiled and stood, dropping the magazine back onto the coffee table. He made his way back across the room to the desk, dodging pedestrian traffic the entire way. He reached the desk and saw the contagious smile that Amanda beamed his way. He had that feeling that it was going to be a good night. “Where are you taking me?” Amanda asked. “I figured we could—“ Aaron was interrupted. His phone vibrated in his pocket and alerted Aaron of the incoming call. He pulled the phone out to ignore the call but when he looked at the caller ID he knew he couldn’t. James Bryan Aaron apologized to Amanda and answered the call. James was finally fulfilling Aaron’s earlier expectations. He started to explain that the creative team had some big plans for Aaron, but they needed to know that he was committed. The two of them broke into a discussion when Aaron heard a familiar voice call out. “Mr. Andrews. I didn’t think I’d see you back here so soon,” Scott Lofton said. Aaron turned to watch as the man that offered him a job he couldn’t accept walked up. Scott now saw Aaron on the phone and quietly apologized. Aaron didn’t acknowledge the pleasantry and concentrated on his call. It lasted only another minute, but James was insistent that Aaron make his decision soon. The call ended and Aaron turned to Scott Lofton with an extended hand. “Sorry about that. Business,” Aaron stated. “I understand. What brings you back to Des Moines?” Lofton asked. Aaron turned and simply pointed to Amanda. Scott chuckled and nodded his head in agreement. The three of them shared small talk about everyday things but oddly enough the weather was never mentioned. Aaron finally broke it to Amanda that they were headed out for a night of mini-golfing and go-karts then dinner. Scott said he liked the idea of a fun park for a first date and patted Aaron on the shoulder. “So what did you think of the message we sent you?” Scott asked. “What message?” Aaron inquired with confusion. Scott spotted the look on Aaron’s face and realized Aaron hadn’t gotten it. “Well, I’ve been thinking constantly since last week about how to get you on our team despite that pesky Master’s Degree,” Scott explained. “I think I’ve finally come up with something.” Aaron wasn’t nervous about his date with Amanda but his stomach turned in knots at Scott’s words. Amanda must have seen the small cry for help on Aaron’s face and flashed a sympathetic look his direction that helped to ease things a bit. “The only way I can guarantee you the position you applied for is by hiring you into a lower-level job that you could do from home,” He said. “Then when you complete your degree we can promote you.” It was music to Aaron’s ears. After being crushed by technicalities the week prior it looked as if things would work out after all. “Of course we’d have to sign a contract to make sure that happens,” Lofton added. Aaron’s stomach dropped. He thought he had found a way to live both of his passions but Scott’s words tore through Aaron like a serrated edge knife. The emotional wound was growing by the second and he wasn’t sure what to do. He looked at Amanda and got another smile. That’s when it happened. Aaron’s phone vibrated twice indicating the arrival of a picture message. He slid the phone open and ignored the rudeness he was displaying to the man who had been pining for a week to hire him. What he saw was another wound; a picture, photoshopped, of Aaron standing in the center of a GCW ring holding the 2011 Rampage trophy high into the air. “So what do you say Aaron?” Scott asked. Aaron wanted to stall to give himself more time to process what was happening. His mind had forgotten about the beautiful woman standing beside him, ready to be swept off of her feet. It was now focused on work. Aaron’s eyes jumped between the picture message he held in his hand and his potential new boss. “I think I need time to think it over,” Aaron copped out. “I’ve got another job offer on the table and need some time to think.” Scott nodded and told Aaron to call him on Monday before saying his goodbyes. Aaron closed his phone slowly and slid it back into his pocket. His date was going to be a disaster; no matter what he or Amanda did. The two of them made their way to the front doors of the building ready for the evening. Well, Amanda was ready for it. Aaron wasn’t so sure anymore.
