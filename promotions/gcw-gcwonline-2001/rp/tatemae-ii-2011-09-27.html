Tatemae: II

27 September 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/27/11 Quality Inn Phoenix, Arizona Had I known just how difficult it was to run a hostess club, I would have told the old man to go find someone else to do it. Never mind that I was thousands of miles away, in America, trying to figure out just what the Hell was going on through phone conversations and emails with my employees. Thankfully, the club more or less ran its own day to day operations. The employees knew what to do, so most of the actual running of the club could happen on site. It was a damned good thing, too, because I was absolutely exhausted. Just a few short hours ago, I had waged war with Ryan Gallway, and now I was looking over the club’s income and expenditures. By no means was I a financial expert, but things weren’t looking as good as I would have liked. The club was treading water – doing well enough to pay staff and expenses, but not much more than that. No wonder the old man wanted me to run it – he knew I was well off enough that I didn’t need to depend upon it for my living. My e-mail box was full to bursting. Some of the mails were comments from customers. Others were recommendations from staff, and by staff, I mean everyone from the hostesses on down to the guy who cleaned the floor. It would be a project for the morning, though. I was never the sort who thought well when exhausted. A few hours of rest would recharge me, and I could take on the task after a cup of coffee and a breakfast pastry. No sooner had my head hit the pillow did I feel a strong vibration in the pocket of my white tuxedo pants. As much as I wanted to ignore it, a few facts were unmistakable: one, it was 2:30 in the fucking morning, and two, I was getting a call at said ungodly hour. Considering I had few friends in the States who’d need something from me this late, that meant the caller was from Japan. A sigh escaped my weary body, and I did the only thing I could: I fished the phone out and looked at the number that was calling. Not Club Dekedan, but in some ways, far more important than my new gig. Heaving a heavy sigh, I clicked the button and raised the phone to my ear. “Kamigawa,” I said, waiting to hear the news on the other end. Judging from the number that had called me, I didn’t expect for it to be good. “Good evening, Kamigawa-san. Or should I say good morning?” The man on the other end of the phone chuckled a bit. I wanted to strangle him, but lacked the energy. “Congratulations on your victory this evening.” “Thanks, boss,” I replied. “I didn’t know you watched wrestling.” “On occasion,” the man on the other end of the line chuckled. “In Japan in the 80s and 90s, a surprising amount of business was consummated at professional wrestling matches. I find myself watching here and there today. It is also a rare occurrence that a member of my organization is competing.” “Yeah, I can imagine,” I said. Pleasantries were all well and good, but it was, you know, 2:30 in the friggin’ morning. “Your presence is required at headquarters on Tuesday,” he told me, and I groaned in response before I could help it. “I apologize in advance. I am well aware of your recent travel schedule. If this was business that could be handled over the phone or a tele-conference, I would not make this request of you. “ Just what I wanted: another flight across the Pacific Ocean. Still, he was my superior. “My dance card is a little full right now,” I said. “I’m trying to run a hostess club and carry out a professional wrestling career at the same time. I’d be more than happy to come back to headquarters, but if it’s not of immediate importance, could you give me another week?” “I appreciate your schedule,” the voice on the other end of the line said, “but unfortunately, I must request your presence on Tuesday nonetheless. Something important has come up, and we believe that you are the right man to fill a role that we need filling.” He paused for a moment. “It will not require much of your time, I promise. You will have ample time to prepare for your professional wrestling commitments.” It was a rare occasion that I couldn’t put off a trip to HQ by a few days if need be. Something was up, and judging by the line of work that we were in, it probably was important. Important enough that I was being called across an ocean for it. “I can make a few other stops while I’m in Japan, I suppose,” I managed, already not looking forward to the prospect of yet another fucking trans-Pacific flight. “I’ll be there on Tuesday. Going to catch the first flight I can find over there once dawn comes and stop by the Club before I see you. Want to see how it’s running.” “You are quite the man, Kamigawa-san,” he said. “Far more impressive than even we had hoped three years ago. It is no ordinary man that can juggle three careers at the same time as effortlessly as you. Even more impressive, considering that you are the Six of Spades.” Our group had dozens of hangers-on and yes-men that inflated our ranks, but the true made members of the Society of the Sightless Eye were designated by rank and suit of playing cards. For that reason, the highest fifty-two within the Society were given a secondary, sub-designation: Gozyuuni, or “fifty-two” in Japanese. “You make it sound like I do it easily,” I protested, weakly. “It’s a lot of work. Luckily, I’ve never minded hard work.” “We are well aware of that,” the boss answered. “As a Six, you have been one of the most productive for your rank that we have ever seen. I will be frank with you: you are in line for promotion within our organization. When you arrive, we will explain more to you. Suffice it to say that you will be working with someone of a greater rank than yourself. Unless my memory fails me, she will be the highest ranking member of our group that you will have yet associated with. She's a fellow wunderkind; like you, in many ways.” That bit of information sent my head spinning. I’d worked with three of our Sevens before, and one of our Eights. If what he was telling me was true, I’d be working with at least a Nine. Any higher than that, and I’d be working with one of the officers of the company at rank Ten or higher. “Well, doesn’t that just warm my heart,” I said, trying to remain casual if at all possible. “Tuesday, I’m all yours, I promise, but I’ve got a long way to go before we get there. Can we end this for tonight? I’d like to at least get a few hours of shuteye before I squeeze three-hundred pounds of shit into a tiny airplane seat.” I heard a soft chuckle on the other end of the line. “I will make the arrangements for you. First class, of course. I will e-mail you the details once they are confirmed. I appreciate the sacrifice that you are making on our behalf. The Society takes care of its own, as you are undoubtedly well aware.” I had to agree. They had taken care of me extremely well. Hell, they were the only reason I ever got out of jail to begin with. I owed them the sort of debt that a man would be hard-pressed to repay, and yet they were a large part of the reason that I never needed to work another day in my life if I didn’t want to. “I appreciate it,” my weary voice replied. It was all I could really say. “Good evening, Kamigawa-san,” I heard, just before turning off the phone. “I look forward to seeing you.” Just like that, the call was over. It had probably taken five or ten minutes, tops, and yet it had put my world on its head. Now, in addition to Terrence Kingsley and the future of Club Dekedan, I had something new to worry about. Not knowing when my flight would be, I un-optimistically set the alarm clock for 5:30 in the morning. I’d be fortunate if I slept an hour before the alarm went off. When my head was full, it was almost impossible to get any rest unless I was absolutely and utterly exhausted. At least in first class, I hoped, I’d be able to get some real sleep. Club Dekedan Osaka, Japan Stepping through the doors of Club Dekedan for the first time as owner rather than bouncer was an experience that I would never forget. I had hoped to make my way in quietly and discuss points of importance with a long list of people ranging from hostesses to the cleaning staff to bartenders. That game plan, though, went out the window in about five seconds, from the moment my good buddy Ryudo saw the white tuxedo that told him his best friend was back in Dekedan. “Hey, hey,” he yelled out, and the microphone in his hand told me quickly that everyone in the damned club was going to hear him, “we’ve got a VIP on our hands! I’m sure you all know that old man Kakihara, the visionary behind Club Dekedan, passed away a few short weeks ago. Before he died, though, he passed on the ownership of the Club here to the one man that he thought could best carry on his vision. Ladies, gentlemen, you know him and love him as one of our best bouncers, but now, he’s YOUR brand new owner of Club Dekedan, give it up for Kamigawa Ryuji!” All told, it sort of felt like I was being announced for a wrestling match. The applause and cheers of the patrons didn’t do a whole lot to lessen that feeling. Unlike in the arenas, though, I knew these people. I’d poured drinks for a lot of them over my time in the Club. I’d had conversations, both deep and meaningless, with an equal amount. Most of them were good, honest people who came to Dekedan to lose themselves in fantasy for a few hours. I owed it to them to make this place a continued success, as well. Dekedan had risen to its status as the #1 hostess club in Osaka thanks to their patronage. From across the club floor, I heard a “congratulations,” spoken in perfect, unaccented English. In the club, that could only have come from one person: my mentor, Seymour Almasy. Wading my way through the throng of people, I found him seated at a booth, alcoholic beverage in hand, sitting next to one of the newer hostesses on our roster, a petite young thing named Saki. Or at least, that was what she was called in the club. Sometimes it was never quite clear what a hostess’ name actually was. If I really wanted to know, I could probably just look at the paycheck that I’d be signing for her soon enough. That’d tell me, one way or another. “Not with Aya-chan,” I teased, clapping Seymour on the shoulder. He was damn near as obsessed with Ayako as he was with those damned video games he played. Saki, though, was a perfectly acceptable replacement in my eyes. For a girl as new to hostessing as she was, she had taken to it like a duck to water. “Aya-chan’s not on shift,” Seymour explained, “and it’s been a long time since I’ve spent any time with Saki here. I was afraid she thought I didn’t like her anymore.” “Seriously,” she chimed in, a pout creeping onto her lovely face. “It’s been at least two months!” “The dojo is always open to you, Kamigawa-san,” Almasy told me. “I assume this in relation to your upcoming Rampage bout?” Even as he said the words, there was a flicker of doubt in his eyes. He knew who my opponent was, and in turn, I knew that Seymour had met my opponent in battle once before. The results, as Seymour himself freely admitted, were not pretty. “It is, sensei,” I said. “Kingsley is one of the best they have. My fourth match in the big leagues is against arguably the tournament’s favorite. I need every edge I can get, and that means some hands-on training with you.” “I have some tapes on Kingsley,” Seymour replied, “including our match from GTT7. I’m low on trainees right now, so I think we can devote a few days to get you ready for him. I won’t mince words, though. He’s a tall mountain to climb, and I’ve been doing this since 2003. Beating him will require your absolute best effort; a perfect match. You are at the point in this tournament where every match will demand a perfect effort at your level of experience. “Sorry for interrupting,” I chuckled. “You two have fun, alright? But not too much fun.” By the time I’d begun to walk away, Saki’s arms were flung around Seymour’s neck. She was one of the most popular girls in the club for a reason; she was easily the most flirtatious of the Dekedan roster. Sometimes, that led to problems when customers couldn’t discern fantasy from reality, but for the rest, her ability to blur that line made her highly, highly sought after. “Bring a fried chicken basket over the table where Saki and Seymour are,” I said to Ryudo, rounding the huge bar in the center of the floor. “If you need me, give me a call.” I wasn’t more than five feet away from him before I heard the bouncer’s booming chuckle. I stopped in my tracks, and turned to find my friend standing there with a shit-eating grin on his face, beckoning me back over. “You’re gonna talk to Ayako, aren’t you,” he asked, eyes alight with amusement. When I nodded, he shook his head. “Geez, man, you should just ask her out already. It would save you a lot of time and trouble. It’s so obvious you like her.” “I’m the owner of the place,” I shot back. “That wouldn’t be sketchy at all or anything. Besides…” She was definitely pretty, definitely sweet, and definitely everything that I looked for in a woman. She was, however, a hostess, and no matter how much I had no problem with hostessing as a profession in general, I had a slight problem with the idea of a hypothetical girlfriend being one. “Well, whatever then man,” Ryudo sighed, shooing me back away. “I know it’s on your mind, though. You’re torturing yourself worse by not going through with it at all. Even if you just told her, it’d probably be a load off your mind.” “Yeah, maybe,” I snorted, trying to keep the thought out of my head. “Just make sure you bring that chicken basket over there.” With that, I began the long, slow walk over to the back. It was one thing to tell her when I was a bouncer, lower than her on the totem pole. It was another entirely when I signed her paychecks every two weeks. No matter how attractive she was, mixing business and pleasure never worked out in the end. That didn’t mean, though, that I couldn’t sulk about that fact. And sulk I did, all the way through the club floor until I disappeared behind an “Employees Only” door into the break room. There, what I saw set off loud, ringing alarm bells in my overstuffed brain. Ayako was sitting there on one of the comfy couches. Her shift, I knew, was in a couple of hours, but she was still in her street clothes. Usually, by now, she’d be fully made up and dressed, chomping at the bit to get out there. Hell, sometimes I had to practically hold her back from coming onto shift early. Today, though, was different. I looked at her, and she turned away from my gaze, unable to meet it. Something was very wrong. Swallowing hard, I decided to just come straight out with it. “What’s wrong, Aya-chan,” I asked, taking a seat next to her on the couch. She flinched, scooting over a cushion. It was the first time in two years of knowing her that she’d ever acted like this towards me. I couldn’t imagine what I looked like in that moment. Hurt, probably. Sad, definitely. I never was very good at hiding how I felt. In spite of being the Man in White Clothing for Dekedan for two years plus, I wasn’t exactly a stoic. Apparently, she saw whatever I looked like, and inched closer. There was a touch of fear on her lovely face, mixed with worry. Whatever she wanted to say, it wouldn’t be good. That much, I was certain of. What I didn’t know was just how bad it was going to be. “I can’t work here anymore, Kamigawa-san.” Devastated as I was at the prospect of not seeing her remotely as often as I once had, my mind went into business-mode for distraction purposes. What I found there wasn’t much better. She was the second-ranking hostess at Dekedan. Losing her was the sort of blow that could send a place on a downward spiral. I’d owned the place for a week and catastrophe had already struck. While I was processing that bit, though, she slipped the right cross to my chin. “My boyfriend…he wants to marry me. But he says he won’t marry me as long as I’m a hostess. I love working here, Kamigawa-san, but I can’t…I can’t keep going.” Never once had she mentioned having a boyfriend. Not to me, not to a customer (for obvious reasons), not to anyone. My head spun like a top, as I tried to figure out just what the Hell to say. Ryuji the businessman and Ryuji the human being fought for dominance in my fogged-up brain, while she just kept right on rolling. “I…know that I’m not being fair,” she continued, “to the club or to you. There’s so many things that I want to say right now, but I just…can’t. I want to talk to you. I really do. But I can’t face you or my patrons right now. I need…time. I want to do this the right way.” “Take the time you need,” I heard myself saying, in a voice that I could barely recognize as my own. “You know how to contact me. When you’re ready to talk, I’ll be here. There’s always a place for you here if you want it.” “I will,” she said, voice low. If I had an optimistic thought at that moment, I’d have thought it sounded like a promise, but forgive me if I wasn’t feeling particularly optimistic. “I’ll call up someone to cover your shift,” I managed, in response, only for her to shake her head. “I already took care of that, Kamigawa-san,” she said. “Emi’s on her way in, and should be here in a few minutes. I…have to go now. We’ll talk, I promise.” With that, she turned, gathered what few belongings she had, and began to walk towards the back door. I felt paralyzed then. Nothing I could do or say mattered, so I did the only thing that I could do: sit there like a helpless fool and watch her walk out of my club. More importantly, I watched her walk out of my life. I felt like I’d been kicked in the stomach. I couldn’t breathe or move. All I could do was feel, eyes squinching up as if I was going to cry, dry-heaving sobs that would never come. I couldn’t cry anymore. I hadn’t cried since my first night in prison. I liked to think that I was out of tears, but damned if this wasn’t making me wonder if I still had a few in me. Ryuji Kamigawa the human being didn’t come out much. Even now, socked in the gut and low as I felt, it didn’t take much for the Ryuji Kamigawa who did what needed to be done to come out. Once he took back over conscious thought process, a plan was easy to form. It took me approximately five seconds to dig my cell phone out of my pocket and give the boss of the Society a call. “Yeah,” I said, as the voice on the other end greeted me, “it looks like I’ve got an opening in my schedule if you want me over a day early. He’d barely even gotten out the word “yes” when I gave him a “Great, see you in twenty” and hung up the phone. There were times when I felt like talking to people, and there were times where I wanted to just get down to business. This, of course, was the latter. Back through the main floor I went, walking past hostesses and clients alike. The only thing I cared about was getting out. Well, scratch that. At the very least, I had to tell someone what happened. Ryudo was still stationed at the bar. He would do. “What’s up, boss,” he asked, before really getting a good look at me. He blanched once I was illuminated more by the dim club lighting. He’d worked with me enough to know when I was pissed, and judging by how he recoiled, I must have looked ready to breathe fire. “Ayako’s quitting the club,” I told him. “She’s getting married soon and her lover doesn’t want her in the business. I’m going to call around and see if any of our girls can cover for her tonight. We’re gonna have to hire soon. I’ll be in touch with you about that.” “Shit…” His voice trailed off, and our eyes met. He wanted to say a million things, judging by the look he gave me, but he was a good enough guy to know that now wasn’t the time. “I’m sorry, man. Give me a call when you’re up to it.” He reached out and clapped me on the shoulder. “Hang in there.” He paused for a moment. “And don’t worry; I brought Saki and Seymour the fucking chicken basket.” I couldn’t help but smile a tiny, tiny bit. “Thanks, Ryudo,” I managed. “You’re one of the good ones.” I marched out the front door of the club I owned. A club that I’d owned for a week and already had huge problems with. Replacing Ayako as a hostess would be next to impossible. She was too damned good. I hadn’t even told Seymour yet, and I knew he’d be almost as disappointed as I was. I’d had enough bad news for one day. Now, it was time to see if Headquarters could give me anything to smile about. The Yamashita Corporation Building Osaka, Japan No matter how many times I walked into the Yamashita Corporation building, I never quite got comfortable with the part that came next. While the vast majority of the businessmen making their way in to go to work went towards the left bank of elevators, I cut a sharp right just past security to head towards the less populated right bank. The security guard standing there nodded once at me, then stepped back to escort me into the center elevator. Inside was waiting a cheerful man in a well-pressed suit. “Good morning, Kamigawa-san,” he said, pushing a button hidden behind a panel in the elevator’s wall. It was a routine I’d gone through dozens of times, but it still always set butterflies aflutter in my stomach. To most people, the Yamashita Corporation building was forty floors, all of which were above ground. I, however, was one of the precious few people who knew that it was, in fact, forty-five. The five in question were, of course, underground, and the elevator that I was on was taking me down into the bowels of the earth to meet with only the third man in my life I’d ever called my boss. Old man Kakihara was the second. And the third man worked down here, underneath the streets of Osaka, Japan. His name was Araya Masahiko, and he ran the shadowy, clandestine operation called the Society of the Sightless Eye. More importantly, he ran the even more shadowy and clandestine inner circle known as Gozyuuni. The elevator dinged upon reaching the designated floor, and with a nod to my elevator doorman, I stepped out into what looked like a perfectly normal floor of an office building. I was greeted after a handful of strides by a fifty-something Japanese businessman. His clothing was custom-made finery. Not a salt-and-pepper hair on his thinning scalp was out of place. Looking at him, one would imagine that he’d stepped right out of a magazine. That was Araya-san. He’d rather have been shot in the head than not look his best at all times. “Welcome, Kamigawa-san,” he said, bowing deeply to me. I returned the bow and held it for a few seconds longer, an indication of deference. He waved his hand, and I stood once more, following him as he began to walk down the hallway. “I am pleased that you were able to arrive early. My apologies if your schedule has had to be re-arranged.” “It’s fine,” I replied. “There are a few issues at the club, but I can deal with those when I’m in a better frame of mind. Work’s good for me to focus on right now.” Here, in the bowels of the Earth, I had barely thought about Ayako or the Club. Araya had not sent for me in quite some time. For him to call me back, particularly knowing of my current schedule, meant that something important was about to happen. “I will be brief and to the point,” he said, as if I expected anything less from him. He made a sharp turn towards a door, and opened it up, gesturing for me to enter the small conference room. Stepping inside, I took a seat at one end of the well-polished walnut table. For the time being, Araya-san and I were alone in the room. I didn’t think that would last long, but trying to figure out what was on the boss’ mind was usually an impossible mission. He didn’t sit down. He never sat, instead preferring to stand up at the front of the room, before a podium emblazoned with the number “52.” He never spoke with papers or cue cards. His mind seemed to me to be a steel trap, clutching at any piece of information he needed. “Kamigawa Ryuji,” he began, in his usual formal manner. “You are the Six of Spades in our organization. You have served in that capacity for the past seven months and done so honorably. You are here today because there is an opening in our great brotherhood that you are being considered for. As you are likely unaware of this, I will give you the short form of what has happened.” My eyes widened a bit at this news. Openings within the family were rare, particularly in a position higher than mine. As a Six, I was a middle-ranking soldier. A higher position, Eight or Nine, would essentially raise me to the standard of a high-ranking soldier, below only the tens and higher, who served as the group’s officers. Openings for twos and threes were common; that was, after all, how I’d gotten into the group to begin with. Low ranking members came and went reasonably often. “Our previous Nine of Diamonds was the victim of an unfortunate traffic accident several weeks back,” Araya stated, voice even. “As I am sure you are aware, positions as high ranking as his are not filled hastily. Much discussion has occurred behind closed doors to find a replacement. You are our primary candidate to fill that role, Kamigawa-san.” There it was. My personal life might have been shit lately, but I was getting promotions left and right. Going from bouncer to owner of Club Dekedan was quite the jump up in stature. Now, I’d be jumping three levels up the food chain here. Not too shabby, provided I didn’t fuck it up like I was already fucking up my hostess club. “Being the Nine of Diamonds grants you great authority. Essentially, what it also means is that if any of our officers leaves the organization, for whatever reason, you are in a position to be further promoted. Precious, precious few individuals ever reach the heights of a Nine. Even fewer, obviously, become officers within our fold. Your trial promotion is an indication of just how highly we think of you.” “Thank you, Araya-san,” I said. “Your confidence in me is not misplaced, I assure you.” Sure, it sounded hokey, but what the Hell else did you say at a time like this? The higher up the ladder one ascended, the more money, power, and prestige he accumulated. The boss continued on, acknowledging my statement only with a polite nod. “Your trial period will be based around a single objective. You will be working with one of our officers– our Ten of Hearts. She is one of the best assassins within our organization, so much so that she continues to kill even at her lofty position within our ranks. “ I sucked in a short gasp. The Ten of Hearts was well known around headquarters for two things: being beautiful and being deadly. Most often, she was these two things at the same time. If dead men could talk, the rumors went, there were many who could testify to that. “To make a long story short,” Araya forged on, “she has begun to make errors. These are all very small errors, but they are errors nonetheless. We are worried that she has grown too confident in her own talents – perhaps understandable, given her spotless record, but we are worried nonetheless. Recently, we spoke to her about these minor failings. In our discussion, she told us that she thought having someone to consult with on her missions would be advantageous. We agreed, and furthermore, believe you an ideal candidate to aid her.” “Thankful as I am for this opportunity,” I began, not quite liking where this was going, “I will not—“ “We are well aware that you refuse to kill, Kamigawa-san. We will not, and will never ask you to violate that statement. It is, after all, in your contract with us. I will let her explain what she wants out of your business relationship,” my boss told me. “She will be here any moment, and I will leave you two to discuss things. It goes without saying, of course, but do not fail me, Nine. Success will allow you to keep this new position of yours on a full time basis.” Without a further word, he was gone, off to take care of any number of important things. With a moment to breathe, I acknowledged that my head had been spinning for at least forty-eight hours, and probably wasn’t going to stop any time. “Man,” I murmured, under my breath, “am I ever going to get a chance to rest? I’ve been going non-stop.” “Probably not,” a voice chimed in, from the doorway. “Mister Araya doesn’t seem to be the sort who thinks people need much rest. He never sleeps himself, so he expects no one else to, either.” I rose from my chair to find an impeccably dressed woman standing in the doorway. She was Caucasian, in her early to mid-thirties, and made me undeniably nervous as I looked at her. Perhaps it was because of the way she was dressed, with only a hint of cleavage showing that promised much more, or perhaps it was simply that I knew of her reputation. She was responsible, if the rumors I heard were true, for at least thirty killings on behalf of the organization. That was a stupefying number for a hardened yakuza, let alone a woman who seemed as if she’d be more comfortable on the runway than with a gun. “But yes, enough small talk,” she said, evenly, crossing the distance between us on two-inch heels that she moved in as if they were sneakers. “As I’m sure you’ve figured out, I am the Ten of Hearts. It is a pleasure to meet you, Ryuji Kamigawa.” “Likewise, Miss…” My voice trailed off. One of the key trappings of tradition within the organization was that you only learned a superior’s name (with the exception of Araya-san) once you reached the level directly below them. It led to a lot of interesting moments with people I’d been on jobs with. Sometimes, the name I mentally assigned them was shockingly close. Sometimes, it was totally off. Either way, it usually made for amusement. “Jennifer Summers,” she replied, extending her hand to shake mine. Most people’s hands were swallowed up in mine. Not hers. Her shake was firm, and her eyes never left mine. “Welcome to the ninth rank, Ryuji.” “Thank you, Miss Summers,” I replied, the vocal adjustment from my native tongue to English harsher than I would have liked. There was a reason that I had yet to speak on GCW TV, after all. The company’s language coaches were working with me, though, and I was improving by the week. Eventually, I’d be able to do this more easily, albeit still heavily accented. “The higher ups want you to be my consultant on my various and sundry missions,” she explained. “Together, you and I will look at each assignment, and discuss possible ways to execute each of them. Sometimes, it may be necessary for you to accompany me. While travelling alone makes things easier, having someone else with me allows for much more elaborate cover stories and possibilities. I pride myself on creativity, and I readily admit that I am slipping lately.
