Schr’dinger's Cat: Chapter 7

27 July 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 27, 2008 The pain had abated somewhat. It was almost gone, but he couldn’t help but feel like some other pain had taken a hold of him. The streets of San Juan were abandoned as he quietly walked with no direction in mind. He used to walk around in order to clear his head from all the bullshit that wasn’t letting him sleep. Ever since that gunshot had almost left him dead, he had not been able to get a night of sleep. There were feelings resurging that he never thought he’d be able to experience again in his lifetime. The silence of his surroundings was like the most beautiful music he had ever heard. He had hoped that all that had been happening was just some weird dream? that somewhere back in reality, he was still riding that train and he had fallen asleep. In some way he knew he wasn’t. It was hard to explain but he was sure that where he stood, it was reality. It wasn’t his reality but someone else’s. The mere fact that he knew he had been murdered and betrayed by his best friends was something he never in his life thought could happen. Burton had always been his rock. Burton was the only person who understood him and what he went through. He was there when Antonio had tried to kill himself and it was him who got him through the death of Justine. Justine? Seeing her again was too much for his sanity. It was like sensory overload. Many a day had been spent in the cemetery where her body had been buried. He remembered how the flowers of the trumpet tree fell and made a beautiful purple carpet. Somehow, he always felt like it had been the perfect place for her to go. Antonio sighed deeply and tried to inhale the air deep into his lungs. Even the air was different, harder to breathe. He sat down in a bench at the edge of a small park. The swings and monkey bars seemed like prehistoric landmarks in the grimness of what was left of that world. As he sat, he contemplated his purpose in that place. How come the fabric of space and time had opened up to him? He still couldn’t find a reason. Antonio had gone through his early life believing he was destined for great things. There was no way he would fail in life. He wasn’t sure that what he saw in the mirror was the person he wanted to be. Antonio for some reason had been sucked into these larger than life situations where gods waged war against each other and where time travel was possible and it just made him feel even more insignificant. Being around all those characters made him consider the simple fact that he had nothing to offer to the world. As he sat there, an unkindness of ravens stood perched in judgment on the skeleton of what was once a Flamboyant tree. They held their beaks high as if proud that they were one of the few remaining animal life that still survived in that barren wasteland. Antonio looked at them and in an instant he knew what they were waiting for. The battles for supplies all around the cities had given them enough to survive on and now they just waited. --- Little Milo sat in the living room of the house he had come to know as his own. The little action figures seemed alive as he put them in all positions he could think of. A great battle was occurring in that plastic world of his. Everything else outside of that, he was oblivious to. The blood still marked his forehead from the kill. He had tried to wash it off as quickly as he had been able to but Burton said that it would be an insult to the life he had taken. There were too many things going on at the same time that had his little mind confused, so he chose to live in a world only he understood. The door to the room was opened by an armed guard. Justine rushed inside and immediately kneeled besides her son and hugged him tightly. The sense of urgency she had felt throughout the day, abating for a little bit after she saw that he was still alive and in one piece. When she finally separated from him and gently put his hands on the sides of Milo’s face, she saw the blood on his forehead. A little gasp left her throat involuntarily as she searched for the nonexistent wound. The look on her face never changed, even after knowing that the blood wasn’t his. Behind her, Burton stood with a beer in his hand. She felt his presence hovering over her and stood up in anger. “What happened to him?” she demanded. Burton’s smirk almost made Justine lose her mind. As much as she wanted to hurt him at that very moment, she knew that it would be in vain. “He got his first kill today,” he said, a vile smile still etched on his face. “His first what?” It was hard for her to keep the smoldering anger from her voice. Ever since she saw Antonio again, her ruse had been falling apart bit by bit and it wouldn’t be long before Burton would know what was going on. “You should be proud. He is one brave little boy. Scratch that, he is already a man in my eyes and in the eyes of the guys,” Burton said with a sick sense of pride in the kid he knew wasn’t his. “So, how was your day” Anything of note happen?” She stammered a little. She knew that tone of voice and she wasn’t about to say something that would bring harm to Milo. “It was pretty uneventful,” she tried to evade the line of questioning. “Yeah” I heard from one of my men that apparently you’ve been taking care of a wounded man in some apartment. They found the bloody sheets after you left the place,” he said as he picked up Milo and sat him on his lap. “Where is he?” She could see Burton’s hand getting incredibly close to Milo’s neck. She knew Burton’s intentions by just looking at his face. The determination to end the life of Antonio overcame everything else in his mind. It was consuming him. She couldn’t let her son down though. “He was supposed to be in the room.” Burton punched the wooden hand rests of the sofa until it cracked loudly. “Don’t play with me. I’m tired of your games.” One of his hands held Milo’s neck tightly. “I have given you everything you’ve wanted all these years and this is how you repay me” You betray me for that?” The anger in him struggled to find a word to describe Antonio. ?” fiend.” Burton sat very still but his passionate eyes were wild. His grip kept tightening around Milo’s neck. Justine was about to jump up and attack Burton but she caught sight of the guard’s Kalashnikov out of the corner of her eye. She couldn’t do anything. She sighed deeply. “I don’t know where he is. When I left, he was still there. I’m sure if you check the areas around the apartment, you will find him there somewhere. Now please, I’m begging you, don’t hurt Milo. Don’t kill him.” The tears she had been fighting began to flow freely. “I don’t think she’s lying,” Sheila said as she appeared from the kitchen, beer I hand. “She wouldn’t lie and watch his kid die in front of her. Not even for him.” Burton released the grip and let Milo off of him. He ruffled the kid’s hair and moved over to where Sheila stood. “We won’t find him. I think we’ll have to flush him out.” “Watch them. If they try to get away, kill them,” he told the guard before leaving the room. --- The sun was setting and the sky seemed to change colors every few minutes. The mix of orange, red and blue was like something he had never seen. The ravens had left a while back, their perseverance not enough to wait as long as it took for Antonio to die. There was a sense of hope in the coming twilight. The sounds of a motor vehicle were clear in that desolate place. He could hear it nearing where he was sitting peacefully. Antonio knew it was Burton’s men. Part of him wanted them to find him and just end his life but another part wanted to keep going, to keep living for Justine and his son. Well? it wasn’t really his. Or was it? He didn’t know anything anymore. When he heard the car get closer to him, he hid behind one of the slides in the park as the car passed by. Before he knew it, behind the car came a platoon of Burton’s men scouring the streets. The sound of a megaphone was loud and clear. “Antonio, we know you are out there somewhere. If you give yourself up, no harm will come to Justine and Milo. This is your one and only chance. Do you really want to lose them again?” The words hung there in the air as Antonio thought about what to do. There weren’t many options. There was really only one option. There was no way he could just leave Justine and their son to die. Antonio sighed deeply and stood up. “I’m here!” As soon as the words left his mouth, there was someone in front of him aiming an assault rifle at his face. He grabbed a two way radio from his belt and called it in. “I’ve got him, sir. We are at the Juan Ponce de Leon Park.” They didn’t have to wait long. Soon enough, a pickup truck appeared out of the small streets of Old San Juan. Antonio saw Burton, Justine, Milo and Sheila in the back of the truck. Even from that distance he could see the horror in Justine’s eyes and the glee on the face of his pursuer. Having to be killed by the same man who had killed the Antonio of this dimension was bad enough, but losing Justine and Milo after he had finally found them again was more pain than what he had ever expected. The guard was behind him now, pushing Antonio with the butt of the gun. They were face to face once more. “I’ve missed you. You are one terribly slippery person. Not a lot like your predecessor. He went down quickly and effortlessly,” Burton said as he took out a cigar from his pocket. Someone was quickly there at his side with a light. Burton turned the cigar around and around as he put it on the flame and inhaled the peculiar aroma. “Can you smell it?” he asked as he blew the smoke in Antonio’s face. “Yes. Smells like crap,” Antonio answered defiantly. “It’s victory, Antonio. Not for you, but for me. I can finally rid this world and whatever universe it is that you come from of such a weak waste of space an air. Everyone back in your home will thank me for this.” Before Antonio could even react, Burton had a gun aimed at his face. “It’s time for you to die. No elaborate plans you can easily explain. Just me, my gun and some lead. Easy stuff. Any last words” A prayer maybe?” “You know I don’t believe in that stuff,” Antonio said. “Just get on with it.” Antonio’s eyes remained open. He could see and hear Justine crying and calling for Burton not to do it. He tried to block the cries. Suddenly, all he could hear was silence. Again, it was the most beautiful music. The best music he could wish for at the end of it all. The gunshot was almost completely silent. Everything was red. He couldn’t feel the pain of the wound. He guessed that he would be gone very soon. All that was on his mind on those last few seconds was the moment he saw Justine in that apartment, the sunlight making her look like an angel and the moment he met his son for the first time. He waited for the end. It didn’t come. For some reason he was still standing. There were still patches of red in his vision and he lifted his arm, surprised that it worked at all, and wiped his face. He could see the blood on his hand. “Wha-?” He looked up and saw Justine and Milo rushing over towards him. He looked down and saw Burton’s body on the ground, a puddle of blood forming on the floor. There was a look of wild bewilderment in Antonio’s eyes as he tried to find out what exactly had happened. Antonio’s eyes tried to understand the situation. He saw people aiming guns at Burton’s men. Where had all these people come from? Before he could answer his own question, he felt as Justine’s hands grabbed his head and pulled it towards herself. Soon enough, her lips were kissing him and the relief made him almost slump to the ground. Justine was there to hold him up. Antonio never thought he would be able to feel like that again? so alive. From the corner of his eye, he saw Sheila giving orders and holstering her gun. Had she killed Burton? It had to be? but why? She had betrayed him. All the questions that filled his head made him dizzy. He finally had another chance. He had one more chance to make something of his life, to make it mean something? to make it matter. He held them for hours. He was finally reunited with his family.
