The Best You Can Do

28 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/28/05 'Tell me who you think you're supposed to be.' I've wondered this myself, many a time. It's not an easy question to think about, let alone answer. Who am I supposed to be? Shit if I know. That's something that people go an entire lifetime without having the slightest bit of an answer by the end of it. It doesn't make it any easier that I'm upside down, doing handstand pushups right now. All the blood's rushing to my brain and I'm starting to see white spots everywhere. 'If you asked me that a couple months ago...I'd say I was supposed to be a hero. Someone for people to look up to.' Grand Master Chan Fo did his usual nod thing that I hate. It's the one that tells me that my answer was by no means 'wrong,' but that it wasn't what he wanted to hear either. Then again, I haven't said anything that I think he's wanted to hear, so I don't know what his reaction on that matter is. But he sits there, painting some ancient Japanese style painting involving dots on a piece of paper. God knows what random dots are supposed to be. And all I've been doing for what feels like hours now is handstand pushups. Nothing that I couldn't do at home. 'One person's hero is another's villain. Lesson number eight. You can't please everybody any of the time.' 'I thought it was 'everybody all of the time.'' 'You must walk before you can run. And if you are incapable of walking, you are incapable of running.' Interesting thought. I suppose for everything you ever do, there will always be someone out there who is opposed to it. Even if it's as simple as living your life...breathing...just getting by. There's always someone who sees you as the bad guy to his good guy, no matter what actions are taken. 'Well, my answer's changed. So that's not the case.' 'Forget a couple months ago, Kohai John. What is your answer now?' '...I don't know. That's my honest answer.' Again he nods. Really pisses me off. Like, at the core of me. Inside. Near the soul. ...a little to the left of the soul, actually. 'We can never be what we want to be. There always seems to be something far more than we can attain, and that is what we strive for. Whether that is perfection in the sense of our careers, bodies, intelligence, or even humility. Some how or another, we all fail. Look at me. All my life I wanted to be known as one of the greatest masters of the martial arts and a happy father and grandfather. That's all I've wanted. And I haven't attained it.' '...but you are a world renouned master. And you just keep talking and talking about how much you love being a father and grandfather.' He stops painting for a moment, as though realizing a epitomy. 'HA! You are right, Kohai John! My life is perfect.' With now a smile in his face and in his eyes, he goes back to painting. Japanese son of a bitch- 'Sensai...I'm wondering here...am I going to be doing anything besides an arm workout all night?' Master Fo has resumed his rigorous painting, talking to me in a way that I might as well have been cleaning up the restaurant that we are no longer in (though his dojo is only upstairs) rather than teaching me his great art. He only raises his eyebrow to his painting. He only talks while looking right at it. Each individual dot, nothing more than a round-ish black spot on the canvas, takes him minutes to make...minutes to decide where he's going to put it. It's honestly driving me nuts. 'You are unpleased with your training?' 'Not at all. I know that there has to be some sort of lesson in this... But I can't figure out what.' 'No lesson. Do the damn pushups.' I'm getting flashbacks of varsity. Coach had a bad night with his girlfriend and he's taking it out on us. No sex for coach means we all suffer. Run, run, run, pushups, pushups, pushups, run till you puke. Only thing was we never had the whole 'spiritual' side of it like Master Fo seems to have. Doesn't mean I'm not sick and tired of pushups by now. 'So when did you recieve your black belt?' 'About thirty years ago.' '...that doesn't make sense. You've been teaching for over fifty years. You taught some of my Pop's friends who were in Korea.' 'Yes. I have been teaching since I came to America in 1949. I taught a few people in Japan, even, back when I was very young. Very young. But I was not given a black belt until thirty years ago.' 'How?' 'A black belt doesn't mean you can go ahead and teach martial arts. It means that a large group of people that made some rules agreed that you have a license in the United States to show people how to fight. In Japan, I was taught since I was but a small child. I was told to fetch water. Told to make dinner. Shown how to strike and block. All of it was training. Just as it is training here, except that I was informed of the practical uses even back then. I became a black belt thirty years ago? No. I was given a black belt then. I had been a black belt myself for a long time before.' Interesting take. The old man's got some different takes on some things. 'So...what are you painting anyway?' 'Ancient Japanese art. The point is to make dots on paper.' 'Sounds hard.' 'It is. The dots cannot have a pattern. The human eye and mind wish to make patters of everything that they see. There must be a harmony for them. This painting is to have no patters. There can be no shapes, no symetry, and no way to see why I made what I made. And at the same time, I must be careful not to try to make it so, because then I will be falling into a pattern in itself. It is very hard.' 'How can you even do that? It's futile.' For the first time in a long time, he looks at me. I realize I stopped doing the pushups and I start up again, without him having to breathe a word. 'Kohai John, there are things in life that may seem futile. But you must try anyway. Take your hero business. Just because something is a failure doesn't mean you cannot do it. You can try. You can prove it wrong. You can always do what you can do and expect nothing less of it than your best. I do not sit down with the canvas and the paint brush and expect to walk away with the exercise perfected. Rather, I expect to walk away knowing that I have tried as I have tried and my work reflects that. It is the concentration on the task at hand that is important. Not the task itself.' 'What do you mean?' He smoothly turns back to his canvas and picks up the brush again. For the first time it's easy to see how furrowed his forehead is, how intense his grip on the brush is. He puts the utmost concentration to place each dot where they will not form a pattern with all the other dots. His hand comes down only with the most certainty of what he's doing. 'Do whatever you do with the intent to make it the best you have ever done it. Each dot on this piece of paper is the best possible dot that I can make on this piece of paper in the here. The now. Just like each individual pushup that you should be doing should be the best pushup that you can do. I have instructed you to do pushups, and that is your task. Your concentration should lie solely on doing the pushups, not on anything else. You must let go of the rest of your life. You must realize that the world will turn without you. Accomplish the task at hand and do it as well as you possibly can.' The tricky old man goes back to his painting. But before I return to the task at hand, I have to call him on something. 'I thought you said there was no deeper lesson in this. Nothing to learn.' A small smile comes across his face. 'There is an old proverb. It goes, 'Old Japanese man lies a lot. The end.'' He returns to his dots. I return to my pushups. It's tedious. But, in the end, everything in life is. Life itself is. Every day is more or less just doing the same thing, isn't it? Might as well concentrate on doing what you're doing as best as you can. The best pushups I can do. The best match at Fallout. The best reign afterwards. Until maybe I can sort through these flashes going through my head and start to make sense of it all.
