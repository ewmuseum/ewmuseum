Our Darkest Days, Part One: Me and My Old Lady

14 April 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 14/4/07 (written 20/1/08) April 13th, 2007, Glasgow, Scotland. It was the night before he was due to return to the United States after his operation, and Andy Murray couldn’t sleep. Two, three, nine. At this point in time, Andy hated those numbers more than anything else in the world. Carrot Top, Edinburgh, people who talk too loudly at the movies, and even international terrorism all paled in comparison to this dreadful, glaring flaw in the numerical system. At this point in time he would even prefer hanging out with Chris Bagwell to the numbers two, three and nine. Murray knew that it wouldn’t be long before the numbers three and nine were replaced with four and zero, much like they had replaced three and eight and the myriad of other numbers that had flicked past his eyes on their way through his alarm clock and into the empty void that should have been filled with a good night’s sleep. As the clock taunted Andy by presenting him with an escalating list of things to scorn, he was amazed at how many inane little details would pop into his head the instant he attempted to clear it. The squeaky hinge attached to the wardrobe door in his hotel room, the detestable sound of thousands of engines powering vehicles through the city’s streets in the dead of the night, the way the alarm always seemed to go off as soon as he nodded off to sleep, these and a constant barrage of other useless thoughts fluttered through his head, swarming as soon as his eyelids began to fall. Sleeping pills might help, he thought, but the Scottish King of Cool was in no mood to leave the comfort of his bed to head out into the wet and windy Glasgow night to purchase some. “For fuck’s sakes, why does this always happen on the night before I’ve got to be up early the next morning,” he groaned to himself, his frustrations manifesting themselves outside of his head and into the real world. He was right; these bouts of insomnia seemed to sporadically target him only on the evening before a big day. It came to him three or four times a year, most recently the night before his back operation just a few short weeks ago. On the night before he captured his first World Championship he only mustered two and a half hours of sleep; it was a miracle he even made it to the arena that night without dosing off, let alone competing in a wrestling match. Andy was at a loss to explain why this happened; he wasn’t usually one to agonize? must’ve been something subconscious. He’d been trying to doze off for nearly three hours and had finally had enough. Tossing the ridiculously thick duvet to the slide, Andy slipped out of the bed and slid his feet into the tattered, well-worn pair of blue slippers that he took everywhere. “Everything in this room is horrible tacky,” he mumbled to himself, “the pillows, the duvet, the carpet, the furniture” all of it?. Andy rose from his perch on the edge of the bed and began to shuffle around the moonlit hotel room, only a pair of white boxer shorts covering his modestly. He shambled towards the door and checked the lock. He knew perfectly well that he had already locked it, but he figured that checking it would be a good way to waste a couple of seconds at least. With barely a hint of tiredness in his eyes Murray walked slowly back across the room and slumped down in a seat at the foot of his bed. Resting his legs on the cheap, nasty Oak effect desk in front of him, he flicked the television on. He’d sit there and watch infomercials until the mind numbing similarity of them all sent him into a coma of apathy, only to be awoken as dawn’s rays invaded his house and tried to pierce his eyelids. His flight as at 8am, with check-in an hour and a half prior to that; at this rate, Andy wasn’t sure if he’d even be physically able to wake up when his alarm blared in his ear at 6am. An unremitting torrent of advertisements bombarded him, as the antics of salesmen peddling all kinds of garbage that’ll “change your life!” filled the hotel room. Miracle exercise machines which could turn an overweight slob into an Adonis in a matter of mere minutes a week filled the screen, eventually being pushed out by wonderful cleaning products which could remove any manner of stain from any imaginable fabric. As Andy pondered why such amazing products were only displayed in the dead of night, his eyelids began to fall and he could sense a few hours of rest in his future. The longer Andy sat watching the television, the slower time seemed to pass. The sights and sounds being broadcast out of the box engulfed him, as their mindless stupor lifted his consciousness from its earthly shackles and ferries it into the peaceful abyss of sleep. ---- “Just let me do it, please?” he asked. With a mischievous spark in her eyes she nodded with the sweetest smile that he had ever seen. “Would you let me rip it off of you?” She laughed, assuming that he was only joking. “What?” she asked. “No, seriously” Would you let me rip it off of you?” he could do it; he knew he could. “You’re crazy,” she giggled. “Only for you.” She nodded again with the same sweet grin that makes you think of warm lazy summer afternoons, of tight hugs and soft kisses, of a place you know you belong. She may have agreed so, but she certainly didn't expect him to do it. At least, she seemed surprised when he finally made the attempt. He grabbed the collar and strap of her cream-coloured tank-top and began to pull, gently; he didn't want to hurt her in any way, shape or form. The shirt didn't seem any different. It didn't budge. So he went at it again, this time a little more forcefully, the collar stretched a little bit. He knew he could do it. He kept at it pulling, and pulling, and pulling at this ludicrous opponent. The edges started to dig into his hands but he didn't care; it was too late to turn back now. He shook my hands, blew on them once or twice and re-gripped the fabric; meanwhile, she gave him the most unconvinced look he’d ever seen. “I can't believe you're actually doing this,” he could hear her eyes say. He would not give up; could not give up. He would not be defeated. Not now. He forced the cloth, had my way with the fabric, but it still didn't tear. He stepped back to see the damage done: her collar was stretched wide enough for her whole torso to fit through, but not a tear to be seen or heard. He was getting aggravated and her facial expressions spoke for how bizarre she thought this whole venture to be. He laughed at how funny it would seem to anyone that drove by them just then. It was night, and they would only be able to look straight-on into the blazing headlights and feel awkward. No one ever came down the street in front of his house, however, and his neighbours were far enough away for us to not care about them seeing an eighteen year old teenage boy attempting, and momentarily failing, to rip off the shirt of an eighteen year old girl. It might have made things a little uncomfortable if anyone had been there to see him struggle with this simple and stubborn garment. Frustrated, he let out a little laugh; he was being defeated by a tank top, but he refused to be beat by this stupid over priced rag. She was tired of him rubbing it against her neck as he pulled, however, so she raised up her arms. He finally got the gesture, and he took off her tank top to reveal a second tank top underneath. It had never been his intention to disrobe her completely, just to have the accomplishment of peeling off a single layer by my own strength, with my own two hands. He gave another tug at this teal rag once, twice, and on the third time it finally came: Rrrrip! He proceeded to tear it to shreds happily and savagely. Victorious at last. She laughed and shook her head at him with that grin like a summer afternoon. Her untamed eyes told him already what her lips followed with: “I can't believe you. You're insane.” They kissed, hugged and said good-night. Then she drove home into the night as he slid inside the house he shared with his elder brother. ---- The sound of quiet tapping drifted through the room, finally settling in Andy’s ear and rousing him from his long-awaited snooze. Rubbing the fog of sleep from his eyes, he read the small digital clock on the bedside table, which displayed three, zero, one, but the numbers did not register to his brain. The screen, rather than showing advertisements, displayed the phrase “Please stand by” on a solid yellow backdrop. I’ve never seen that before, Andy’s groggy mind thought, as he turned to gaze out the window. Blackness engulfed everything outside, blotting out the Moon and stars. Andy couldn’t even make out the faint glow of the lights of Glasgow International Airport just a few hundred yards away. “I can’t even see the streetlights?” Andy mused. In fact, looking out the window was like looking into an abyss, as if the hotel was alone in its own world of darkness. “Maybe the power grid went down,” was the first logical explanation his mind could muster. A little confused as to why he was voicing his thoughts aloud, Andy got up and reached for the pot of tea he had made a few hours ago, dazzled by the contrast between the silver and the blackness outside the window. He was so disoriented that he didn’t notice the sound of the room door being pushed open, only to be halted by the engaged lock. Then the room was quiet again, only the gentle purr of the thinning traffic outside was audible. After pouring a cold cup of tea Andy walked over and sat back down on the chair. With his tea in one hand and the remote in the other, Andy was again in the position he found himself in seconds ago. The same announcement was on every channel, leaving him sitting in front of an apparently malfunctioning television. With nothing to occupy his mind, he was more attentive when another endeavour at forcing the door open came, instantly snapping him awake. The Scottish King of Cool dropped his drink on the floor, the cold, brown fluid soaking into the carpet as he lowered into a crouch. He strained his ears to catch another attempt, but no sound came. With all his muscles tense, he began to imagine malevolence and wicked intent where there was none. The ticking of his Friederick Stein wristwatch seemed to run in slow motion, but when it made noise, it was impossibly loud. A gentle breeze whistled through a slight gap in the window, yet Andy only heard the sound of branches scraping against some of the windows on the lower floors of the building, which conjured up images of goblins and fiends clawing their way up it’s side, coming for Andy. In his own mind, Andy had been tense and ready, like a startled deer, for several hours. In reality, it had been less than two minutes since he was alerted by the door. Andy gradually relaxed, rising from his crouched position. He tip-toed to the television, taking great care to be as quiet as possible as he turned it off. He dropped onto all fours and crawled under the large window. Peering just above the windowsill, he saw a gang of deranged psychopaths waiting outside on the street below through his mind’s eye. Upon actual inspection, however, nothing but the creepy blackness looked back at him. He let out a deep sigh of relief and slowly walked back to the bed, avoiding the large stain he had created moments ago. This is terrible; he anguished, imagining his night’s insomnia to be an incurable disease, even though he knew he would sleep like a baby the following night. Why was this happening? he pondered mentally. Sure, he had a heavy day of travelling tomorrow ? an eight hour flight to Orlando, followed by a four/five hour drive up to Tallahassee ? but that wasn’t such a big deal, was it? Then it hit him. He missed Jane. He’d been away from his wife for a number of weeks now, and he couldn’t wait to get home just to see her. They’d hardly had the most harmonious of relationships, and a small doubt still hung in the back of Andy’s mind as to what his brother, Cayle, was talking about when he told him that he’d seen Jane meeting with a tuxedo-clad businessman a few weeks ago, but he didn’t care; he just needed to see her. They met shortly after Andy had moved to America, aged seventeen, in the first place, and just a few short years they were married and Jane had given birth to the couple’s first child, Marvin. However, things took a turn for the worse as Andy’s wrestling career blossomed, and by 2004 the couple were divorced with Jane gaining custody of Marvin, limiting Andy to weekend access alone. However, in the winter of 2006 the couple began seeing each other once again; putting their differences to the side and seemingly falling in love with each other all over again. They wed shortly before Andy’s release from his FUSE contract in early 2007, and by the end of February Jane and Marvin had moved back into Andy’s Tallahassee. The re-marriage had baffled Andy’s close friends and family given the messy nature in which the relationship had crashed around him in the first place, but Andy didn’t care; he was happy, that’s all that mattered. But there was no point in him pondering this for too long; the fact of the matter was he still couldn’t sleep. As Andy pulled the duvet back over his large physique and the night drew on the symptoms of his self-diagnosed ailment worsened. It started simply enough, with thoughts that were too loud and obtrusive to be sent out of his mind. They forced their way into the forefront of his consciousness, these inane and pointless musings on the weather and the outcome of distant political races. There they would sit; preventing him from obtaining the mental serenity he needed for sleep. As soon as he was able to push them out, others would cascade in, like an overfull closet that simply refuses to hide its contents. This would go on for an agonizingly long period of time. He would always lie in bed, motionless, waiting for the thoughts to make their way out of his brain, all the while trying to keep his gaze from the clock. If I actually look at it, it’ll turn out to be too late to actually get any sleep, Andy rationalized psychologically. After his losing battle with his trivial thoughts, usually sometime between one and two in the morning, he usually, when at home, would head downstairs and pour himself a fresh cup of tea. This is where I am now, he thought, this is where the paranoia kicks in. Once downstairs Andy would slowly finish his beer and sit and listen to the sounds of the house and neighbourhood. The wind, the creaks and groans of the old house, and the occasional bark of a dog would drift into his ears. He would look out the windows at a sky so polluted by light that the stars in the night sky were almost considered a myth. During his observations, he would begin to grow wary. The outside sounds would become less and less genial, and the noise from within the house became more and more sinister. Rain’s gentle pitter-patter would cover up the footsteps of imagined convicts, while snow on the ground would muffle the murderers approach. The running of either the central air or the heat would provide bothersome distractions, drawing Andy’s attention away from his paranoid sentry duty. If Jane went to the bathroom, the sounds of movement through the house would set his nerves on edge, until all was quiet once more. Clouds which only hid the moon brought evil with them, and wind chimes soon sounded the call to arms for all the nightmares which inhabited the recesses of Andy’s mind. Conscious of his position in his insomniac sleep cycle, he recalled a time during his AWC tenure in 2005 when Jane had snuck up on him while he was on the couch. He had just begun to nod off, and he didn’t hear her approach. She treaded gently, not wanting to wake him, and when she gently touched his shoulder, he leapt up and nearly struck her. His hand stopped mere inches from his face, just as the fog of sleep and paranoia had cleared from his eyes to reveal the terror in hers. However, as the intensity of his thoughts greatened, his body began to shut itself down. Andy drifted off. ---- The curtains were rustling softly in the cool night’s breeze as Andy Murray dozed. He lay there, facedown, his half-naked body covered from the waist up by a thin, white bed sheet, beside Jane who was seemingly wide awake. Clad in a silk nightdress she lay on her chest staring at the closed eyes of her husband; he had been in a deep sleep now for a number of hours, the time was now. She turned and gently pulled open the bedside table, making ever effort not to stir Andy. After a couple of seconds of fumbling around in the drawer, her hand made contact with the familiarly cold plastic handle. Jane turned again to face her husband, her movements causing him to stir under the sheets. Fuck, she thought to herself, no time to waste now. As her husband’s eyes crept open, she sat upright and pulled the sheet down just beyond the small of his back. Without a second thought she raised the blade, plunging it twice into the back of the sleeping wrestler. The pain hit him light a freight train, and as life seeped out of his body he was able only to utter the faintest exasperated sigh. She raised her finger to her lips, mentioning for Andy to “Shhhhhh” as the whites of his eyes widened. Andy Murray was dead. ---- BEEP BEEP. BEEP BEEP. BEEP BEEP. The sound of the buzzing alarm clock filled the air. BEEP BEEP. BEEP BEEP. BEEP BEEP. It woke up the Scottish King of Cool almost instantaneously. With a grunt he rolled over and bashed the snooze button in order to stop the clock’s incessant beeping. It was 6am, time to get up. “Fuck.”
