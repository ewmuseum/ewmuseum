The Next Step: Part I

10 January 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/10/11 It took him a week and a lot of long, hard thought but Aaron Fujita had finally decided it was time to go back to school. The spring 2011 semester was set to begin on the second Monday in January, meaning Aaron had no time to lose. He went back up to the campus of Northern Illinois University and asked his advisor to dig up his graduate school application. In a work of miracles, Aaron was processed and accepted for that semester. If someone called bullshit when Aaron said it wouldn’t be awkward serving as a teaching assistant to Chloe, they’d be right. He knew it would be. His heart had filled with joy when he had seen her the week prior. He filled up with a feeling he thought he would never feel again, only to have it drained from him when she introduced her boyfriend Tyler. That’s what made it awkward. January 8th – 2 P.M. Aaron walked through the door and exited the office of his faculty advisor. It was official; Aaron was in graduate school. He had received his schedule of classes and which classes he’d be the Teaching Assistant in. It was a relatively light schedule of work, only serving as the TA in one class; Synoptic Meteorology. Along with that assignment came the class roster. The fifteen students enrolled in the course were people Aaron had gotten along with during his undergraduate career but most importantly didn’t contain Chloe’s name. He found it odd, but didn’t question it. As he made his way through the simple hallways of Davis Hall he began making mental notes of what he would expect on the lab work he would be grading. He approached an elevator and pressed the down button. “Come on. When will they invent elevators that sense urgency?” Aaron mashed the button a few more times, ready to do a bit of apartment hunting. He knew it would be a tedious process finding somewhere to call home as he was trying to move up in the middle of a school year. He remained optimistic though and hit the button once more. Finally the elevator dinged and the door slowly opened. Aaron had begun to look through the paperwork in his hands and didn’t look up as he took a step forward to get inside. He stopped short as the figure of another human being crossed his gaze. “Sorry. Excuse me.” “Really, watch where you’re going jerk.” Aaron recognized the voice immediately and turned around to see Chloe’s beautiful face standing in front of him. He allowed the elevator doors to close and hoped nobody would call it to another floor. He dropped the stack of papers to his side and smiled at Chloe as she adjusted the bag hanging from her shoulder. “What are you doing here?” She asked. “I just met with Dr. Walker. I’m all signed up for the beginning of my graduate career.” Chloe exclaimed with excitement for him and came forward with outstretched arms. She gave him a light hug and then retreated back a few steps. The two stood in silence for only a millisecond, but it seemed like a minute to Aaron. He had to break it. “What are you up to?” He inquired. “One of my classes didn’t register online. I’ve got to see Dr. Walker to sign the admission slip.” Aaron’s stomach began to turn. He felt that the awkwardness he was sure had been averted was about to come into reality. His mind raced trying to come up with a way to continue their conversation without asking the obvious next question. Asking it would give him the answer he didn’t want to hear. Before he could say anything she spoke again. “I can’t miss out on Synoptic Meteorology when you’re the Teaching Assistant.” “So you will be in the class, huh?” She let out a small laugh and touched his shoulder lightly. Aaron’s stomach was turning even more now as one of the classic signs of romantic interest was put on display. He tried to brush the gesture out of his mind, but it remained set in stone. “Don’t think I’ll be taking it easy on you guys.” He joked. “Oh never.” Her tone was dripping with sarcasm. They shared a laugh and Aaron turned around to press the elevator button again. It had retreated to the first floor and would take its time getting back. He sighed and pressed the button a few times more. It still didn’t hurry the box up its cable. He looked back to Chloe and unbuttoned the top of his overcoat. It was getting warm in the hall. “So what are you doing now? You want to get something to eat and catch up?” She inquired. “Actually I’m going apartment hunting. I’m hoping I can find something in town.” He was relieved he had a legitimate excuse for avoiding the outing. He hated lying to anyone, especially Chloe. The time they spent together chasing storms allowed her truthfulness to rub off on him. She was a good person and he admired that. “How about you?” “I don’t have anything. Tyler flew back to school at UCLA yesterday and I’m already bored.” Aaron nodded with true interest. Long distance relationships had always amazed him. They took a lot of work and a lot of trust; something Aaron didn’t hand out too easily. He had tried the long distance stuff once and it succeeded for a mere two weeks. “Can I come hunting with you? It couldn’t hurt to have another person there.” “Really? You don’t think Tyler would mind?” Aaron let it slip from his mouth before it had been processed in his mind. He was afraid of what her response would be. His question was full of assumptions about what could happen. He looked to his feet and then back up to the red head in front of him. Her face was filled with a smile. “Sorry.” “Why would he mind?” Aaron smirked and looked toward her with an expression that seemed to confuse her. He quickly ran through how to ask her what he needed to and finally settled on something. He dismissed it and went with something else. Again she spoke before he could. “He doesn’t have reason to.” “He’s okay with the history between us? I mean I guess I’m assuming you’ll tell him where you’re at.” She inhaled sharply and looked away. Chloe’s expression turned to one of enlightenment. It seemed to suddenly become clear where Aaron’s questions had originated. Aaron knew what she was going to say so he jumped in before she could speak. “You haven’t told him?” “He asked about you last week, but I couldn’t get the nerve to tell him. We’ve only been dating three weeks.” Inside, Aaron smiled. He wasn’t sure why, but he thought it had something to do with the lack of foundation before the two got separated. On the outside his gaze remained quizzical, awaiting her answer. “I don’t want to give him reason to not trust me.” “I guess I can understand that.” Aaron nodded as she spoke. For the first time since the week prior there was a glimmer of hope inside of him. He liked having something to hope for. Life to Aaron was about setting goals and achieving them. Self accomplishment was one of the most satisfactory feeling s a human could experience. He made a split second decision and spoke. “I’d like it if you came with me.” “Awesome!” She turned on the balls of her feet and walked briskly to the office Aaron had just exited. Aaron turned around and stared at the elevator with disbelief. It had been a minute or two since he last pushed the button and it still had not returned. He mashed the button a little harder and then turned back around to spot Chloe coming back down the hall. Even though he had gotten past seeing women as objects of pleasure, he still admired Chloe’s physique. She was perfectly proportional all the way around and it attracted him to her all over again. She reached him just as the elevator doors began to slowly open. Aaron’s right hand was shoved in the pocket of his overcoat, leaving a gap between his upper arm and body. He felt her hands wrap around his bicep and clench tightly as they both stepped onto the elevator. Her grasp remained firm and he soon felt the light touch of her head on his shoulder for a split second. “I missed you Aaron.” He missed her too. He just felt it better not to admit.
