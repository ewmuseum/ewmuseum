Upon Further Review or The Best Advice I've Ever Gotten

31 May 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/31/08 I guess you could call it a ritual, this little habit I picked up from my father. Dad would watch every match I had, in person, at ringside. Then he would watch, and meticulously analyze, tapes of that very same match and force me to join him. I guess he somehow thought it would mold me into a better wrestler... I kept it up, even in Japan when he wasn't there to force me, and yeah, I guess I'll admit that it made me a little better. Being in the ring blinds you, the adrenaline keeps you from being able to really and truly understand the effort that you're giving. Watching those tapes all these years has allowed me to rise above the blinders of adrenaline and has blessed me with the ability to realistically critique my performance even as it is unfolding. I pop the freshly burned DVD from its case, just like I have so many times before, and load it into the portable DVD player that goes everywhere with me. The match begins: Andrews: The following contest is for one fall and has a fifteen minute time limit. Introducing first, from Boone, North Carolina. JAKE REEEEEEEEED! The arena is filled with a whispered metallic riff for a mere moment before the drums and screams kick in signify the beginning of "Will to Die" by Strife. All eyes turn to the entry and after moment we see Jake Reed emerge from the back, gray athletic Blue Ridge Wrestling shirt complimenting his wrestling gear. Jake meanders towards the ring, with little enthusiasm in his step. The fans are underwhelmed by this new superstar’s lackluster entrance? No one is around to hear me, but its somehow therapeutic to talk out loud? “Good lord, no wonder they didn't cheer for me. It looks like my dog just died. What was I thinking?” The scary thing is... I have no idea. The match is a blur, almost as if I wasn't the one wrestling it. I continue to watch in horror as possibly the worst performance of my 10 year career unfolds, and then... Black goes tumbling between the top and the middle rope to the outside. He tries grabbing hold of the ropes with one of his hands, but is entirely unsuccessful and ends up on the ground aside the apron. “Here we go?” Jake Reed throws up his arms, seemingly frustrated that Black had to fall to the outside of the ring. Black, still on the outside, climbs up to one knee holding his head with his right hand. “What?” Reed walks over towards the ropes to yell at Black to get back into the ring. “I did what” Yelled for him to get back in?! Fucking yelled for him for get back in?!” Even trying to understand it makes my brain ache. “I’m crazy, that’s it, a regular schizoid. I’ve developed some second personality that’s an ineffectual nothing and only shows itself when I’m in the ring. I could have fucking KILLED Black right there. Why didn’t I” Why in the hell would I space and go against everything? E-V-E-R-Y-T-H-I-N-G I know and stand for in a wrestling ring and ask my opponent to get back into the goddamn ring?!” And to think, I walked up to McKail and told him that I gave it my all in this match as if I thought that was a selling point for him to forgive me. Somehow, in my head, I actually believed this steaming pile was good? I have lost my fucking mind. Wait, but I know how crazy this is, I can see how bad this was in retrospect. Crazy people never realize how crazy they are. They flip out and lather themselves up in their own feces and when they wake up the next morning they aren’t surprised to have a second skin of dried shit clinging to them. So I can’t be crazy, I know I’m covered in shit, but if I’m not crazy then what? “It’s a punishment.” It seems the only reasonable explanation, a punishment for my past misgivings. This is the old testament God pulling this shit, not that pansy hippy-ass new testament forgiveness monger JC. Biblical justice, that’s what they called it. It’s my own personal equivalent of the fire from heaven that destroyed Sodom and Gomorrah. Except in this case he’s torching my whole career and the 40 year wrestling legacy of my family. Yep, that’s definitely that asshole God from the old testament, cause after that bitch had a kid he mellowed way out. Soccer mom God don’t have this kinda vengeance in him, I am most definitely getting ass-raped by the Lee Van Cleef “Angel Eyes” style God here. So it’s a biblical plague, that means that the only way to stop this is forgiveness? McKail’s forgiveness. Hell, even the cruel and ruthless God of old testament had had a little forgiveness in him, surely Jacob McKail does too. But this is going to be tough, I’ve tried asking, begging even, and it doesn’t faze him. I’ve got to find a way to appeal to him, to convince him to give me his forgiveness. I’m gonna need some advice. ======================================= “So, Marian, do you mind if I ask you about forgiveness?” Marian has never given me bad advice, she’s that rare find, smart and beautiful. Too bad it’s been more than a year since I’ve been to see her. “It’s your time Jake, we can do whatever you’d like.” Her voice has that warm and welcoming tone that just makes you want to tell her all your secrets. “You know that I returned to the ring right?” Even as I ask the question I already know the answer. “Of course I know. I saw you perform last week.” She says it in a matter-of-fact way, as if I didn’t even need to ask, which I guess is what I wanted. “Geez, sorry you had to suffer through that shit burger, that wasn’t one of my better performances so please don’t judge my talents based on that. To be honest that was probably my worst match to date.” I want to apologize for that match more, but I try to get back to the subject at hand, “Anyway, more to the point, let me ask you about forgiveness.” “Fire away.” She says with a wink. “What do you do when you’ve wronged someone, I mean really hurt them, to the point where they won’t even forgive you when you’re begging them on your hands and knees?” I hold my breath, she has to have an answer. She narrows her eyes, “You think that’s how you get forgiven” Asking? Begging?” “Yeah. You do something wrong, you ask for forgiveness, they give it, and then the world returns to normal.” As if I need to tell her that’s how things work, she’s smart, she should know this already. “That’s not how things work.” That matter-of-fact tone is back. “Then how do they work?” Even as I say it I already regret it because Marian is just the type of girl who will tell you, in no uncertain terms, exactly how it does work. “Well, first of all” She’s putting her lecturing face on, uh oh, “I’m gonna give you a pass seeing as how you grew up in that so called “wrestling school”, that sausage factory of sweaty men. I’m sure a bunch of your dad’s wrestling buddies squeezing and pounding on one another all day, and then talking about their corporeal conquests all night didn’t exactly impart a great amount of sensitivity onto your little psyche.” “Geez, take it easy on me.” I’m only offended because she’s spot-on with her assessment. “You want the truth,” she gazes at me over her cat-eye glasses, “then sometimes it’s gonna sting. That’s what you’re feeling right now” “I can take it.” I think. “If you really want someone to forgive you then you have to show them you’re worthy, show them that you’ve changed. Oh, and don’t forget that it never hurts to do something nice for them, it’s like paying a penance.” She pulls off her glasses and stares me down as if to say “you should know this already”, “Why the hell do you think guys buy women flowers when they fuck up?” “Do something nice” THAT’S IT!” Eureka! I stand up and throw back the curtains, flooding light into the cramped little hotel room. Why do I do that? Hell I don’t know, it’s just the sort of thing you do after a catharsis. “Oh, and it never hurts if you look her in the eyes and then, with all the sincerity you can muster say, “I’m so sorry.” That makes bitches wet, trust me.” I’ll take her word on it. I shake her hand. “I can’t thank you enough Marian, you’ve saved me again.” She pulls me closer, “So are gonna fuck or what?” I pull back and smile, “Do we ever?” “No” never, not once. Jake I don’t get you, you’ve been coming to me for years, paying me for an hour of bone’n and then just talking to me. I’m a whore, what would ever make you think my advice would be of any value to you?” I could tell her a million things, but I tell her the truth. “You have kind eyes, they remind me of my mother.” Her eyes widen, “Yikes, I’ve become the living embodiment of your deceased mother” well I guess that means we ain’t never gonna fuck.” I nod, “Yeah, don’t see it happening.” She lowers her head. “Whoa, no, not because I wouldn’t want to, it’s not like that.” I lift her head so that I can look into her eyes, “You’re beautiful, and smart, and after all these years you know me better than anyone else.” She smiles a little, and nods. “I can’t do this anymore, not like this.” That hits me from out of nowhere. “What do you mean”!” “Next time you need to talk to someone don’t come to a hooker.” She tries to look stern, but she breaks, “Come to your friend.” She smiles and I can’t help but return that smile. This makes me feel good, makes me feel like anything is possible? and its gonna save me a lot of money. As we part ways I reflect on her advice? Do something nice. I can do that. At High Stakes I’m going to do the nicest thing I can think of for McKail? make sure he wins that battle royal and the title shot that goes with it! Life is good and after High Stakes its only gonna get better! Forgiveness here I come!
