Little Monsters (and Tempest too)

29 October 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/29/10 “I win again! You guys are so bad at this game.” First to the couch by a mile, Chris reflected everything he had ever learned from his father with a screaming victory proclamation. Evan, who followed not long after his brother, crashed down beside him on the couch with quiet protest. “That’s only cause we let you win.” “Nuh-uh.” Chris bolstered back, his arms folded across his chest. “Uh-huh. I give you a head start and Christa doesn’t care and Aislinn’s too little to win.” Chris wanted none of that. The way he saw it he was simply the best. His father had taught him well. “Liar!” “Bad liar!” Aislinn jumped in, accusatory finger pointed at her older brother. She had not a clue what she was saying, but Chris seemed to be enjoy it. Evan ignored them both, turning his attention back to the giant green lizard on the screen. “Liar liar liar!” Aislinn continued to proclaim. She had not a clue what that meant, but it seemed like a fun new word that got chuckles out of her younger brother anytime she said it, so why not continue? Christa sighed, turning back in the other direction before she could get very far into the suites makeshift living room. She hated being stuck with her siblings on the road while all her friends back at home had the luxury of going to the mall, hanging out at the movies, or ogling over boys. She, on the other hand, got to spend her nights listening to her so-called siblings bicker as her aunt turned mother attempted to put her life back together at what Christa considered to be the cost of her own family. Life had never been very fair to either Christa or Tessa Windsor. “I’ll get the popcorn.” Christa sighed as she brushed past her aunt’s best friend. “You okay kiddo?” Tempest questioned as the teen continued on her path, never one glancing up from her cell phone. That and her laptop seemed to be Christa’s only sense of comfort on the road. “Yep.” While it was not sarcastic in tone, it was still pretty obvious Christa did not mean it. She was much more conservative than her adoptive mother when it came to sharing her feelings with strangers. “Don’t worry. We’ll put something cooler on once Linni falls asleep.” “It’s okay.” Christa quietly commented back. Never once did she look up from her phone as she slipped the popcorn into the microwave. As the eldest among her siblings, multitasking had become second nature to her. “At least Godzilla’s a good distraction.” Christa was far more mature than most fourteen year old. Rather than kick up a fuss about her adoptive parents continuous bickering, Christa had come to accept it as merely part of her life. It sucked, but as long as either Chris or Tessa were still alive, it would never end. They cared far too much for each other for their own good. “I’m sure Tessa didn’t know he would show up.” “I know.” Christa nodded along in agreement. After having lived with her aunt seven years now she knew exactly how the routine went. “He never tells her when he’ll be home. He just shows up. He’s just like that.” “Boys are yucky like that.” Tempest chimed in. “That’s why your mom doesn’t want you to date.” “She just needs someone better.” Christa lamented. She was far from Chris Storm’s biggest fan and rightfully so given everything he had put Tessa through over the years. Christa blamed all that was wrong in the family on him. Tempest certainly wasn’t Chris’ biggest advocate herself, but having fallen in and out of love plenty of times herself she knew there had to be something there for Tessa to stick around for. “Cut her some slack.” “I do.” The teens head actually peered away from her phone a moment to respond to that one. “He doesn’t deserve all the chances he’s been given though. He’s hurting her.” “Hmm.” Tempest thought on that one herself. Christa’s side of the story was pretty easy to buy into given there were plenty of things Tempest questioned herself. “He can be a party pooper, yeah, and he’s certainly not been as fun and crafty as he used to be.” But of course, Tempest also remembered all the times where her best friend strolled in gleaming, as if she had taken on the world all by herself and won. “But I trust your mother knows what she’s doing.” “I hope so.” Christa seemed nowhere near as confident in Tessa’s ability to take care of herself. She had witnessed far too many of her mother’s sleepless nights and tissue box therapy sessions for her lifetime. “Because if her knee totally blows out, there’s going to be no saving her without him acting like he at least cares about what she wants.” Tempest snickered not at Tessa’s struggle, but rather the thought of just how selfish men on a mission could be. She knew from her own experience of relationships within the professional wrestling industry just how difficult it could be to make things work out. It’s why she swore to never date within the business again. “Guys are just like that sometimes.” The sparkly one grinned from ear to ear as the thought lingered about in her mind, “And trust me, even if Tessa’s knee blew out, she wouldn’t take Chris’ antics laying down. She’d find a way to beat him worse than she has that silly smoker Jay Terror or even Markus.” “Yeah. I know that.” By then the buzzer on the microwave was going off, distracting Christa momentarily from the conversation. “I just wish she’d worry less about him and spend more time with us.” Christa thought on that one a minute. It seemed entirely wrong for someone her age to say and thus she jumped right back in with her own defense. “Well not me, but the others. They don’t need him, they really don’t. They need her.” “You guys deserve both of them. It’s why your mom spends so much time trying to help Chris. She wants the best for all of you.” Christa was taken aback. Tessa never cared to explain any of that. Instead she kept to herself about matters like Chris. She hated to drag the kids into that mess, no matter how visible her anguish was at times on the outside. “I just don’t understand it at all.” “You won’t until you have kids of your own.” Tempest suggested. “But don’t rush it.” She made sure to add in shortly after. She herself had little experience in the parenting field other than what she had been told by others. “You know from experience?” Christa sincerely questioned. While she could not quite see where Tempest was coming from, she respected her suggestion as the glittery goddess seemed plenty capable of taking care of herself. Sometimes she wished her mom was more like that. “No. I don’t.” Tempest peered back in the direction of her best friend, who started to march back intently in their direction. “ But that’s another story for another time. For now, just cut your mom some slack, please? I can’t promise to keep her out of trouble since she hangs around me, but I can certainly promise that I won’t let Chris do any irreversible damage without getting heck from me, okay?” Christa nodded and headed back for the living room. It was time for them all to take a much needed reprieve from all the chaos that had occurred, and that was about to occur at worldwide. Every last one of them deserved it.
