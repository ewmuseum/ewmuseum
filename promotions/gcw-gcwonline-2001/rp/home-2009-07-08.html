Date: July 8, 2009 "No matter how hard I try, you’re never satisfied. This is not a home. I think I’m better off alone. You always disappear, even when you’re here. This is not my home. I think I’m better off alone." “Home” by Three Days Grace = = = July 5, 1995. CRACK! It’s the sound that the wood of a baseball bat makes when connecting with the cork of a baseball. As it soars through the air at an average velocity of sixty-eight and a half miles per hour, the ball ‘screams’ its way towards the outfield, seemingly right towards the awaiting glove of Jimmy; one of the Brothers Action’s childhood friends. It’s a routine pop fly, which Jimmy immediately catches and throws to AXEL for the double play. It’s a completely normal, neighborhood game of backyard baseball that appears to be going off without a hitch. Having gained the final two outs of the inning with that double play, AXEL’s team switches to the infield the other moves to the outfield and gets into position. After a bit of discussion amongst himself and his teammates, it’s decided that thirteen year old AXEL will be the first to step to the plate. Taking a deep breath, he grips the taped end of his Louisville Slugger, choking up on it a little. His knuckles turn white while his fists clench the hilt of the bat. Assuming his batting stance, he spreads his feet shoulder width apart and readies himself to swing. Deciding it’ll be fun to pick on AXEL; Ace moves into the Catcher’s position as the pitcher winds up and throws the first pitch. “Hey, batter, batter, batter, SWING!” Swoosh! “STRIKE ONE!” AXEL kicks at the dirt in frustration before moving into his stance once again, readying himself for the next pitch. Looking back and forth between his brother and the pitcher, he taps his bat against home plate, signifying that he’s ready. Winding up once again, the pitcher throws out the second pitch. This time, just as AXEL swings, Ace reaches out and tugs at his shirt tail, throwing off his concentration. “STRIKE TWO!” Quite angry by this point, AXEL draws back like he’s going to smash Ace in the head with the bat when he notices the third and final pitch coming at him from the corner of his eye. Turning around and taking a wild swing… CRACK! Wood connects with cork, sending the ball flying past the pitcher’s mound and into the outfield. Soaring past the boundaries of the makeshift baseball diamond, the ball enters homerun territory. Taking off towards first base, AXEL is ecstatic. He has just hit the first homerun of his entire life, and there’s absolutely nothing that can ruin the moment for him… SMASH! Except that. The sound of shattering glass echoes through the mid-afternoon air. It’s that ONE thing that can and will put a damper on an otherwise glorious afternoon. That homerun ball had managed to fly through the glass of the Action family’s kitchen window, setting into motion a chain of events that will inevitably change the boy’s life forever. = = = July 5, 2009 The Florida State Hospital in Chattahoochee, Florida; the place where AXEL spent a rather large chunk of his teenage years. Its pristine, white walls and tile floors sparkle in the light as the sun beams in through the front windows. Men and women garbed in the purest of whites cart the patients around on specialized wheelchairs that they’re strapped into to prevent escape. All sorts of crazies and ne’er-do-wells inhabit the rooms that line those immaculately clean hallways, which are a stark contrast to people who live in the outside world. Further down those hallways, on the backend of the complex is the solitary confinement ward. The solitary confinement ward, or the “Quiet Rooms” as they’re called, are those rooms with the padded white walls that everyone always thinks about when a mental institution is mentioned. Those are the rooms that the most severe mental cases are placed in, so that they can’t cause harm to themselves or others. During his lengthy stay at the institute, AXEL Action spent quite a few hours in the Quiet Room, due to multiple suicide attempts. However, the attempts weren’t attempts to cope with guilt over the things he’d done. They were simply the effect of his trying to find a way out of the Hell he’d been thrown into. On this day, AXEL won’t be thrown into that Hell. In fact, he quite willingly walks right through the front door and to the receptionist’s desk, behind which a familiar face sits. “Excuse me. Could you tell me if Uncle John still works here or not?” Uncle John was the one person at the institution who actually took the time to listen to AXEL and try to help him. AXEL was at the end of his stay at the facility when he met John Davies (a.k.a. Uncle John), but he thought of the old man as the father that his own father never tried to be. “Well, sometimes. He’s semi-retired, you know.” “No, Lucy, I don’t know. Is he here today?” The woman’s eyes widen and she glances up, knowing there’s only ever been one person with the nerve to actually call her by her first name while she’s on duty. “Well, if it isn’t little AXEL Action! My, how you’ve grown, sweetie!” The woman known simply as Lucy moves out from behind her desk and throws her arms around the Unholy One, squeezing him tightly in a hug which he happily returns. She places a kiss on his cheek, leaving a red lipstick smudge which AXEL doesn’t bother wiping off. In the ten years or so since they last saw each other, Lucy had gotten a little older, but was still just as attractive as ever. “It’s good to see you, too, Lucy. Now, is Uncle John here today? I was on my way to an event and happened to pass through, so I thought I’d come see him for a bit.” “He’s here, alright. He’s probably in the recreation room, filling the children’s heads with his stories and tall tales.” That last statement strikes a nerve with AXEL, and his demeanor changes from one of happiness to one of the utmost annoyance. Narrowing his eyes at the beautiful, but abrasive woman, he tilts his head to the left a pinch. “Those ‘tall tales’, as you called them, got me through some very tough times when I was staying here. I’d appreciate it immensely if you’d speak about that man with respect.” He takes his leave and heads down the hallway, not giving her a chance to apologize or even respond at all. The farther down the hallway he gets, the louder the sounds of music from an acoustic guitar seem to be. “Ah, that’s definitely Uncle John…” = = = July 5, 1995 “Do you have any idea what it’s going to cost me to replace that window?!” “No, sir, I don’t. I’m sorry I broke the window, Dad. It was a mistake.” By this point, AXEL is in tears and hunkered down in the corner, due to the fact that when angry, his father had a tendency to be a very violent man. There’s been more than one occasion in which both AXEL and Ace went to school with one or both of their eyes swollen shut because of their father’s temper. “Mistake? A mistake?! The only mistake, here, is you, AXEL! From the day you were born, you’ve done nothing but cause me heartache and grief!” AXEL’s completely dumbfounded by the words his old man had just spoken. As he sits there in the corner of the room, quivering in fear, one question weighs on his mind. “Did he really mean that?” He just can’t seem to wrap his mind around everything that was happening around him and to him at the moment, his hands moving to cover his face as he begins to sob. “I’m sorry, Dad. I didn’t mean to break the window.” The poor boy’s mother steps in, placing a hand on Mr. Action’s left shoulder, trying to calm him. The man looks to her with a scowl, shoving her hand off his shoulder. “Honey, calm down, please? It was just an accident. This is our son you’re talking to, not one of your employees at the department store.” “Calm down?! You want me to CALM DOWN?! I AM calm! In fact, I haven’t lost my temper, yet, but if you keep undermining my authority, I’ll get angry in a hurry.” She puts her hand on his shoulder once more, trying to get his attention. He spins around quickly, and… SMACK! Mrs. Action falls to the floor, unconscious and AXEL gets to his feet, clenching his fists at his sides. He storms towards his father and swings, catching the man with a right cross to the jaw. “You can beat me all you want, and even string me up by the ankles and use me like a punching bag. But don’t you EVER hit my mother again!” Seemingly unphased by the right hand from his son, Mr. Action fires back with a right of his own, knocking young AXEL on his derriere. Scooting backwards along the floor, he tries to put some distance between himself and his father, sniffling as he tries to stop crying. Getting to his feet once again, he scurries towards the front door. “You’ll pay for this, old man. Mark my words…” AXEL was never the violent type, always preferring to use his words to solve issues, not his fists. That is, until tonight. This time, his father had gone too far, and it was high time that something gets done about it. Heading out to the garage, our young protagonist opens the driver side door of the family’s car before pulling the hood latch. This causes the hood to pop open just a little. Moving around to the front of the car, he feels around inside of the slight opening, finding and pressing the button that will allow the hood to open completely. “Oh, yes… You will pay.” = = = July 5, 2009 Finally finding the room that the music emanates from, AXEL knocks on the frame of the door after poking his head in. A bright smile curls his lips as he spots the familiar old man, sitting and singing for a mentally ill child while playing a tune on his guitar. Listening for a moment and waiting for the song to end, he chimes in happily. “Uncle John?” “I recognize that voice. Is that…?” “Yes, it’s me. It’s AXEL Action, sir.” The old man lets out a cheerful, bellowing laugh that seems to rumble within his plump belly and work its way up and out of his mouth. He quickly waves AXEL over, motioning for AXEL to take a seat anywhere in the room. “How long has it been since I last saw you, boy?” “It’s been about ten years. I’ve been busy touring the world and winning the championships. There hasn’t been a day that’s gone by that I haven’t wondered how you’ve been doing. That’s actually why I’m here today.” John sighs, nodding solemnly as he shifts his gaze to the floor. It’s obvious that there’s something quite wrong, so he heads out into the hallway, patting AXEL on the back as if inviting him to come along. “I know, I’ve been watching you on the television. I’m very proud of you, my boy. I take it that you’ve heard about my cancer, huh?” AXEL nods, brushing his fingers through his own hair as he lets out a sigh of his own, his gaze moving to meet the old man’s. “Yeah, I heard the news from Ace. He knows how close you and I are, and he called to tell me. I’ve got a show in New York in three days, and I was on my way up. I figured I’d stop in and see you to say goodbye, in case I don’t get to see you again.” Uncle John immediately begins to tear up, pulling AXEL into an embrace which he happily returns. The two hold each other tight for a few moments before letting go and stepping back from one another. “You’ve been like a father to me, Uncle John, and I don’t care who my biological father might have been; you are my REAL father. Thank you for everything.” “AXEL, I wish I could have told you myself, but I didn’t know how to reach you. I tried to get in touch with you so many times, but you were always on the road.” Nodding, AXEL hugged the old man once more, patting him gently on the back. This was one of those rare times that AXEL actually felt pain and heartache, and he was wishing his heart was still as hard and cold as a stone. “I have to go, now. I’ve got a schedule to keep, and I’ve got some autograph signings to do before the big show. I’m in the American Dream match this week on Worldwide. Win or lose, I’m going to give it my best effort, because I’m dedicating the match to you. I love you, Uncle- No, wait. I love you, Dad, and if I don’t get a chance to come back and see you before you’re gone, I’ll never forget you.” John nods and gives AXEL a slight wave of his hand as the two part ways. AXEL turns and heads for the front door, trying to hold himself together so he doesn’t burst into tears in front of everyone. He’d only felt this kind of pain one other time in his life, and it’s something he had hoped he’d never have to feel again. = = = July 5, 1995 AXEL looks around the garage for any kind of tool he can use to extract revenge on his father for the way he’d treated him, his mother and brother over the years. After much searching and nearly turning the garage upside down, so to speak, he finds a pair of wire cutters lying on the workbench. “Yes… These should do nicely.” Picking them up and heading over to the car’s exposed engine, he looks around for anything he can find that he can use to destroy the car. Searching around for the fuel line, he takes hold of a piece of rubber tubing that looks like it might be it. Pulling it tight, he makes a nice, clean snip in the middle of the line, watching as the fluids within leak out. Laughing to himself and smiling quite proudly, he tosses the wire cutters to the side and re-enters the house, heading up to his bedroom. “That’ll teach him. NOBODY hits my mother and gets away with it.” Sprawling out on his bed, he crosses his legs at the ankles and places his hands behind his head, resting said cranium on those hands. Deciding to take what he thinks is a well-deserved nap; AXEL closes his eyes and takes a deep breath, allowing himself to be taken by the clutches of sleep. He’s not asleep for long before he’s awakened by the sound of the car cranking downstairs in the garage. “That’s right, go to your precious bar and get drunk; just like you do every night. Only this time, you won’t be coming home.” Quickly making his way downstairs, he begins to look around the house for his mother. “Mom? Moooooom! Where are you?” The sound of the car backing out of the garage draws his attention to the front window of the house, where he notices both his mother and father in the car. Panicking immediately, AXEL rushes out the front door, waving his arms around over his head like a maniac. “No! Stop! Please, don’t go!” Despite his efforts to flag them down and save his mother from a nearly inevitable demise at his own hands, it’s too late. They’re too far gone, and even if they were able to see him, they would never make it back in time. Deciding his attempt has been futile, he heads back inside the house and curls up on the couch, hoping and praying to God above that nothing happens to his mother. A few minutes later… RIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIING! The boy jumps, startled as he moves to sit on the edge of the couch. RIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIING! He places his hand on the telephone receiver, picking it up and placing it to his ear. He clears his throat, speaking quietly. “H-Hello?” “Yes, this is Lieutenant James Porter from the Hollywood Police Department. Is this the Action residence?” “Yes, it is. Is… something wrong?” “Well, I’m not really sure how to say this.” “Just tell me the truth, Lieutenant. Are my parents okay?” He tries his best to sound concerned about both parents; even though in his heart, he’s hoping like mad that his father has died. “I’m sorry. Apparently, someone cut the brake line on your parents’ car, and there’s been a terrible accident. They’re both dead.” At that moment, the entire world seemed to come to a screeching halt for the thirteen year old, his heart filled with a sensation comparable to being pricked by a billion tiny pins at once. He drops the phone and falls to the floor, curling into the fetal position unsure of whether to laugh or cry. His psyche falls apart piece by piece, his grip on reality loosening more and more with each passing moment. “Mommy…”, is all the poor boy could say for the longest time. = = = July 5, 2009 Exiting through the front door of Florida State Hospital for what could be the final time, he stops to take one, last look at the place. Those red, brick walls and the pond out in the middle of the courtyard are things he’ll never forget. Though the one thing he’ll remember most about it isn’t really a thing at all. That one thing that he’ll remember forever is his true father, the man he so affectionately refers to as Uncle John. “Goodbye, Dad. I’ll miss you.” Taking one last look at the place that he spent most of his teen years, he finally realizes that it wasn’t really Hell, after all. Turning to head to his car, he finally realizes that it’s the only place in which he’s ever truly felt at home.
