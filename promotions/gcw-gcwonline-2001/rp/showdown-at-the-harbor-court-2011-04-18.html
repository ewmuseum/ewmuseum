Showdown at the Harbor Court.

18 April 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/18/11 Monday, April 18, 2011 2:29 AM Eastern Daylight Time The InterContinental Harbor Court Baltimore, Maryland Three women and a blond man stood outside an elevator. Drusilla Devonshire used the brass rail in the elevator to pull herself to her feet. "She was impressive downstairs, I'm not going to deny it," she said. "But this isn't some front desk clerk you're talking about now, and speaking German won't get you in that room." Quinn Gregory's lips twisted. "It's two o'clock in the morning -- what exactly do you expect to be up there?" "That's why I'm here. I'm pretty sure you're walking into a trap." "You can't just say shit like that," Quinn said. Her mother's response was more violent than just words. Alex Pierce stepped into the other woman, lifting her knee sharply into Devonshire's midsection. "Explain," Alex ordered. "Now." All the air left Dru's lungs in a rush, but she was grinning as she slid down to the carpet. "Nngh. Christ, you hit like a freight train." She pulled herself up with a choked-off groan. "All right. Grapevine says Randall and his band of merry men are upstairs waiting for you." "No way," Quinn insisted. "Lilith wasn't a cop. We did the legwork. We saw--" "We did." Alex nodded, but she was already deep in thought. "But if Randall thinks I'm up to something, he'd have the API office watched, and he'd piece together the same information we did." Donnie watched the exchange quietly -- at least up until that moment. "Lovely as this reunion's been, we've got an unconscious maid an' two floors 'tween us an' whatever the fuck's waitin'. Whatever we decide to do, we ought not do it in the fuckin' elevator, if you fuck well please." "You're cute when you get mad," Dru said with a cockeyed grin, edging closer. "With that accent, and the 'fuck' and--" "I wouldn't fuck you with her dick, lovely." Donnie dismissed her with a sniff, glancing back to Alex. "So what'll it be? D'we stay or do we go now?" Alex sighed, combing her fingers through her hair. "How do you know he's up there?" "I still have some resources left." Drusilla met Pierce's gaze unflinchingly. "If I'm wrong, big deal, you'll be a little over-prepared. But if I'm right and you don't listen to me, you're putting Quinn at risk, because we both know he'd put her in cuffs just to fuck with you." "What do you want?" "I just want to help. Is that so hard to believe?" "After everything we've been through?" Pierce's smile was tight, exasperated. "Yes. So what is it?" For the first time since she'd crashed headlong Quinn's into con downstairs, Devonshire looked uncomfortable. "If my information pans out, five minutes of your time. Five minutes without Amy fucking--without her there to make it all about her. Just five minutes." "Five minutes about what?" Quinn demanded. "Nevermind about that now." Dru waved a hand. "Five minutes if I'm right. Not until then." "I'm not promising to get involved with any of your shit," Pierce said. "I'm not that person anym--" "I know you're not. Just hear me out and make up your own mind." "Fine. If you're right." Donnie grunted. "An' jus' how d'we confirm that shit? Didja jus' wanna go up an'... an' ask 'em? If she's right, they'll be fuckin' waitin' on the great an' mighty Desade." "Then that's the last thing I give them." Alex turned to her daughter. "You're going to hate this, but--" "No," Quinn said. "I'm not waiting in the--" Pierce put a hand on the teen's shoulder. "You did great downstairs, sweetheart. And I'm not sending you out to the car. You're Plan B. If the three of us fail, I need you to come bail us out." "And if you fail because you get caught? I'm not going to leave you to be arrested." "You are going to do exactly that. Because Lilith and her son need what's in that room -- and that's what you agreed to. And what's Rule Number Six?" "The mission comes first." The teen said it sullenly, by rote. "I won't leave you." Alex summoned up a smile for her little girl. "So long as we get the package, I don't care what you do, but I hope you'll come and get me, yes. Find Amy. She'll come up with a way." "Clock's tickin'." Donnie lifted the coffee-skinned maid up with a grunt. "Hallmark moments later." He dumped her into the cart, none-too-gently. "Dastardly spy shite now." He plucked her keycard off her belt. Quinn smiled, tucking her chin in embarrassment. "Go. I'll hide somewhere." The other three were already moving, so she had to lift her voice slightly to call after. "Be careful!" Alex grinned, picking up her pace to catch up. Donnie turned over his shoulder, chuckling roughly. "Helluva girl you got there," he said. "Were you like that when you were her age?" "No, and I regret that she's had to be," Pierce admitted. "I was," Dru said, relieving Bell of the master key. "You shouldn't regret it at all." * * * Even after all this time, Amy Campbell had never gotten used to the sight of Roderick Ashe hunched behind the wheel of a hatchback. The big man's lantern jaw was set the entire ride, his teeth grinding right until he jammed his foot against the brake and the jalopy screeched to a halt. "Awight," he said. "I'll wait here." "You're not coming in?" Amy asked. She rode shotgun, twisted to the side so she could see both men. "If she's in trouble..." It was Mr. Hawke who answered. The thin man sat in the backseat, fishing in his metal attache case. "Then she'll need someone out here so we can get out of here in a hurry." Amy nodded, lifting a hand to smooth back the hair that seemed determined to escape. "So what do we do? What's the plan here?" Hawke came out with a Beretta 9mm pistol, as nondescript as the man himself. He checked the clip and racked the slide automatically, like he'd done it a hundred times before. "Now we improvise," he said, thumbing on the safety and tucking the gun into the holster he hadn't yet belted on. "I have something here for you, too." "Whoa!" Amy exclaimed, fumbling for the door handle. Rusted hinges groaned in protest as she forced it open. "I can't--I'm not taking... I might not do this often, but I'd never use a--" "Relax," Hawke hissed, grunting with the effort of climbing out of the car. The pistol disappeared somewhere during the motion, likely into his waistband or one of the pockets in his blazer, but Campbell didn't exactly have a hankering to go looking. "I am relaxed," she insisted, though the very vehemence she used to claim it put the lie to the words, and even she knew it. "Well, kinda relaxed. Trying to relax. I just don't like guns, okay?" She stood, pulling her ponytail tight to give her hands something to do. "And I was hardly about to give you one. Here..." His hand dipped into his pocket, and she had to fight down the urge to dive for cover. "Catch." He slid a black leather billfold across the roof of the car. "What's this?" Amy asked, thumbing the thing open. "We don't have time for--holy fuck." She glanced to the scarecrow of a man as he walked (slowly without the cane) around the car. "Is this real?" "Real enough they'd need to call to verify. Most won't. Just bull through them." "I can do that." It was an FBI badge, embossed with a seal and everything, declaring her to be Special Agent Cathleen Cantrell. "How do you people get this kind of--" Her eyes suddenly lit up with the spark of an idea - one that reminded Elijah to make sure he took the badge back by the end of the evening. "Can I arrest people with this?!" "At least temporarily. I'm Byrd, by the way." She nodded off-handedly, grin slipping across her lips. "So fucking awesome," she said. "Little bit scary, but awesome." Hawke stopped in front of her, straightening to peer down his nose like a disapproving teacher. He reminded her of Professor Snape. "This isn't going to be easy," he said softly. "As good as Alex is, smuggling her and Quinn out from under Randall's nose is going to be difficult." "I know." She stuffed the badge into her back pocket. "I mean it, Amy." His voice sharpened instead of getting louder, grasping her elbow with bony, talon-like fingers. "You're likely to have to make some hard decisions and I need to know you can do that." She wrenched her arm free. "--the fuck off me," she growled. "Let's just fucking do this." Campbell didn't wait for the man to answer, nor did she slow to give him a chance to keep up as she pushed through the door of the hotel, banging her hand against the bell. The man who stepped out of the back was tall and thin and far more awake than anyone should be at half past two in the morning. "Can I help you?" he asked. "Uhm, yeah." Amy looked over her shoulder to the door. "I need to see whoever's in charge." The man offered up a small, conciliatory smile. "That would be me -- I'm Jordan and I'm the manager on duty tonight. How can I help you?" "I'm..." Amy faltered. The door swung open, Hawke hobbling through with a thin-lipped frown. He glanced to the desk, and Campbell set her lips as she turned back, whipping the forgery from her pocket. "I'm going to need you to remain calm." She flashed the ID quickly. "Agents Cantrell and Byrd, FBI." Jordan's brows came up. "Is something--has something happened?" "We were about to ask you the same thing." Her smile was thin, terse -- unconsciously, she'd mimicked Alex's expression. "Has anything happened tonight?" "What kind of thing are you talking about? It's been pretty quiet tonight." Hawke sighed sharply as he reached the desk, leaning heavily. "Has he said anything yet?" "We just started," Amy said. Her smile stretched casually -- Campbell would always be more comfortable as the "good cop" in a situation like this. "I'm pretty sure he'll help, though." She shifted her gaze back to the man. "So you were saying it's been quiet tonight, yeah? Nothing strange or unusual or..." "I mean, other than the German girl and her mom a couple hours ago, yeah." "German girl?" She seized on the information -- Alex had mentioned that would be Quinn's role. "What German girl?" Jordan shrugged one shoulder. "Uh, just some girl. She wanted a nonsmoking room for her and some guy. Her mother came in and... like I said, it's nothing. I sent Imelda up with them to do something about the smell. Nothing we don't deal with on a daily basis." "Mm." Hawke's rheumy blue gaze slid to the redhead, brow crooked. "What?" Jordan demanded. "What does 'mm' mean?" "Nothing." Campbell flashed a grin she hoped would be considered disarming. "It's just... it sounds like you might have had dealings with the same people we're looking for." The clerk's brows rose. "Holy crap, really? The girl couldn't have been more than like seventeen or eighteen." Hawke grunted. "We've encountered operatives particularly adept at seeming much younger than they are," he said. "Is Imelda in danger? I can call her and let her--" Jordan reached for the house phone. "No!" Amy took the receiver out of his hand, slamming it back into its cradle. "Ever hear of people being charged for interfering with a federal investigation? Like I said, be calm. All we need are your answers." "Your friend will be fine," Hawke put in. "Do you by chance know where they went?" "The... third floor, I think. I don't know, it all happened so fast and the girl's mother was..." He glanced away from Amy to Hawke. "She was a little distracting, you know?" "I see," the thin man said, not bothering to pretend to smile. He glanced to the tiny redhead, but Campbell had already set her jaw and turned away. "I need you to stay here. Don't tell anyone we were here. They have eyes everywhere, you know what I mean?" Jordan had no idea what Hawke meant, but he'd seen enough movies to know to nod. "Sure thing," he said. "Mum's the word." With a last nod, the man turned away, hurrying to catch up with the fleet-footed redhead, though his limp kept him from doing so until they'd reached the elevator bank. "Are you okay?" he asked, not without a certain amount of concern. Amy jabbed her fingers against the up button repeatedly. "Yeah," she said breezily, stepping onto the car. "Fine. Just not in the mood to hear about how 'distracting' Alex has been tonight, especially from some fucking shit-job meathead." "Understood." He wasn't smiling or sarcastic. "You ready for this?" "I'm ready for you to quit asking me if I'm ready, that's what the fuck I'm ready for." This time his smile leaked through as the doors slid shut. * * * The housekeepers' station had an occupancy list, so it wasn't hard to find an empty room for them to set up shop. Donnie pulled the maid out of the cart and set to work on binding her while the two women moved into the bathroom. Devonshire occupied the mirror, stripping out the hairpins that held her wig in place while Pierce stood nearby, a black duffel atop the sink counter. They didn't say anything as they worked -- they hardly even looked at each other. The tension was clear in the glances Drusilla stole in the mirror and the huffed sigh from Alexandra as the other woman set a very similar bag beside hers. "Holy shit," said Bell, watching from the doorway. "It's like watchin' a bloody mirror image." "Shut the fuck up," Alex hissed, fastening a choker around her neck. Dru snorted her chuckle, pulling a pair of knee-high boots from her bag. "She's never going to admit you're right," she said. "Because he's not right." Pierce slid a plastic Ziploc bag out of her duffel, opening it to carefully remove a mahogany brown wig. Devonshire made a show of rolling her eyes, stepping barefoot to the toilet. "Sure she's not." Alex assiduously ignored them, using one hand as a wig stand as she styled the thing, drawing another shared smirk between Drusilla and Donnie. "Our lovely guest is buttoned up tight." Bell said, more to fill the silence than to relay the fact, which both women surely knew. "She should be dead," Drusilla said, dipping her toe into the tall boot. "No one dies." Alex didn't turn away from the wig. "No one." Her tone brooked no disagreement. "Fiiiiine." Devonshire tugged on the cuff of the boot, pointing her toe at Bell with a long smile. "I hope you at least found a hood. I can't have her identifying me to the police." Donnie drew his eyes away from her leg with some difficulty, rubbing at his neck. "This isn't the first time I've kidnapped a lady," he said, a brief, ashamed smile touching his lips. "Strange as that sentence might've been t'say a year or two ago." "She does that to people." She wrinkled her nose in her grin. "Can you give us a sec?" "Hmm? Oh." He looked to Pierce, brows lifted. "You need her permission to leave the bathroom so you don't see your mentor's ladybits while we're changing?" Dru laughed softly. "Wow..." He sighed, and Alex caught his eyes in the mirror, nodding slightly. "Right, then," Bell said. "I'll be out here. No, uh... no fistfights or nothin', okay?" "Uh-huh." Devonshire dismissed him with a look. "Shut the door behind you." He did so, sending the bathroom back into silence. Alex yanked a floral-print wrap shirt out of the bag with a little more force than was strictly necessary. "I'm not like you," she said finally, meeting the other woman's gaze in the mirror. "Not anymore." "Not anymore," Dru allowed as she rose. "It's not a far drive, though." She lifted her foot onto the sink, straightening her boot. "I mean, look at you, all ready to go take on the feds. Don't lie and tell me you haven't been waiting for months to put one over on that cowboy motherfucker. I was there, back when Amy wouldn't give you the time of day. She doesn't know how far you fell, but I do." "She saved me." "Oh, please." She plucked a black miniskirt out of her bag. "You follow that woman around like a little lost puppy dog, and you let people hurt her because you don't want to fucking 'scare' her." "I don't--" Alex sighed sharply, again using the mirror to focus her glare on her ex-lover. "Get in the shower." Dru unbuckled her belt, her grin stretching. "Care to rephrase?" "I don't want you to see my 'ladybits,' either." She snorted, snatching her bag off the counter. "You're such a child." "I don't need to justify myself to you." Alex waited until she heard the shower door close, and only then did she unbutton her fly. "And yet I bet you're about to." The ensuing laughter echoed weirdly in the shower. "She's good for me. She's good for me and more importantly, she's good for Quinn." Devonshire shook her head, even though Alex couldn't see it. "Yeah, because bottling that up is completely healthy. Kingsley is only going to get worse, and he's not a problem you can solve by beating him in a wrestling ring." "So I'm supposed to gun him down in whatever rotting swamp he lives in?" "Don't pretend you don't know exactly where he lives. I know you won't cross the streams, and you won't go after these arrogant pricks in GCW and PRIME the same way we're going after Randall and blah-blah-blah, but don't pretend you haven't at least thought about it. I know you, Alexandra. You can pour on the civilized veneer, you can pretend to be just the Best Wrestler in the World, but you can't stop being what you are. You're a killer, just like me. You turned Donnie from a guy playing Three Card Monte on street corners to someone who'll duct tape up a maid just because you told him to." Pierce fell silent as she exchanged her loose pants for skinny jeans. "I didn't make Donnie into anything," she insisted, but it sounded hollow even to her own ears. "No," Drusilla allowed. "That guy is... there's something wrong with him." "Because of the way he looks at you?" Alex snorted. "Donovan is a... a bit rough, but--" "But I know creeps, Xandra." Drusilla wound her braid up behind the back of her head, pinning it in place with two long chopstick hairpins. "I mean, takes one to know one, right?" "He's harmless." "Not anymore he's not. That's what you do. You make good people bad and bad people worse. I mean, you dressed Quinn like a whore just because you were going to go up there as a maid. And now you're not even using her." "I didn't dress Quinn. She chose her own clothes." Alex slipped on the carefully styled wig. "This is for a good cause. Lilith De Leon--" "--oh, like it's about her anymore." The shower thunked open and Drusilla stepped out, wardrobe in place. "The moment I told you Randall was waiting for you upstairs, it became about him. You want to get up there, get whatever this Lilith chick needs and get out without him knowing you were ever even here. You have to win. It's the same thing that draws you to two different fucking federations and leads you to fly around everywhere." Alex snapped a glare the other woman's way, sitting to strap on a pair of stack-heeled sandals. "I'm only wrestling for Caldera because he signed Amy and I want to spend time with her." "You could just fly there just to see her." Devonshire settled back in front of the mirror, opening a vinyl bag with her makeup in it. "Nothing is making you wrestle there except your ego." A rapping on the door ("Shave and a Haircut", to be precise) interrupted the debate, Donnie pushing the door open hesitantly. "Everybody decent?" "It's fine, Donovan." Alex sighed as she stood, joining Drusilla at the mirror to do her makeup. "We're just about ready." "Maid's awake," he said quietly. Drusilla lifted her compact, brushing blush across her cheek. "I'm telling you--" "Give her a shot to put her out." Pierce turned from the mirror, her glare silencing Devonshire before settling on Bell. "But only one. We'll be gone soon, and they won't clean this room again until someone uses it." He nodded, backing out of the room again. "I pulled some booze out of the mini bar. Prob'ly tastes like right shite, but it'll work." She nodded, popping open a white plastic container. She'd been doing this for years, but the last thing she added to a disguise was always the colored contact lenses to soften her hard gray eyes. She'd just never gotten used to the burn. "We'll just be a moment." "You sure about this?" Dru asked, folding her arms across her chest. "I mean, the maid's right there, and you can probably still bluff your way into the room." "I'm sure." Pierce blinked as she stood, her eyes an arresting shade of green. "This is a better play. Hide in plain sight, right?" "Right, sure." Dru offered her arm to the other woman, whose frigid stare was only slightly less frightening with normal colored eyes. "Okay, then." Though she wouldn't take her ex's arm, Alex did fall into step beside her. "Drusilla..." she said softly. "These five minutes... something serious is going on?" The other woman nodded slightly. "Yeah, but it can totally wait. Good cause, right?" When they emerged from the bathroom, Donnie greeted both with tiny bottles. "Cheers, ladies!" he said. "T'me bein' the luckiest bloke on Earth." He tossed back his own drink in one long pull. "Love this job..." * * * Quinn Gregory hated this part. Call it teenaged impatience or just a desire to do something, but Quinn found herself pacing as who-knew-what was going on just a couple flights up. She tried to look like she belonged, filling a pilfered ice bucket at the ice machine. She tried not to look to the stairwell, to just be another weary tourist desperate for something to drink at this ungodly hour. Still, her nerves were frayed, and when the elevator chimed behind her, she nearly dropped the entire bucket. Quinn put the metal thing atop the machine, pulling her blond wig into a messy tail. She took a deep breath and she faked stifling a yawn with her fist as she staggered down the hallway. Her heart was racing hard enough to hear it in her ears, and it felt like she had to swallow away the cottonmouth feeling every three or four seconds. "It's nothing," she tried to tell herself. "Just a tired guest. It's not the big bad feds looking for a straggler. They're totally waiting upstairs, not looking for--" "...Quinn?" The hissed whisper of her name jerked the teenager back to reality. She curled the bucket against her stomach, turning her head away from the elevator. She covered a second yawn with her whole hand as she turned away, hoping to keep whoever it was from recognizing her, but to no avail. Whoever they were approached from behind -- she heard a distinctive, three-legged walk on the plush carpet, and then another gait that came in quick, jogging strides. She braced herself -- no way was she going to outrun them, no way could she fight them. "So much for Plan B," she was thinking. She was not quite prepared to be grasped in a bearhug by a five-foot-nothing redhead. "You're okay!" her attacker sighed, relieved. Amy Campbell was strong for her size, but that still didn't mean she could lift the teen up, but she damn sure tried. "Amy?" Quinn's carefully styled eyebrows (also colored) rose, free hand instinctively going to Campbell's grip. "Is that...?" The little redhead let go, spinning the teen around to face her. "You're... blond." "I noticed that, yeah. Went with the whole German thing." A shy hand touched the strands of hair that trailed along her neck. "Do you like it? I think it's kind of..." "The word you're looking for is 'creepy'," Campbell said, shuddering. "Where's your mother?" "She's, uh, upstairs." Quinn thumbed over her shoulder. "There's a guy upstairs who has a lady's husband's will, and she thinks that his company is screwing him, and--" "And fucking Randall is up there to arrest her." The girl's eyebrows came up and she wiped her hands on her hips. "Uhm, yeah. We'd... we knew that. How did you know that?" Amy glanced over her shoulder to the thin man lingering nearby. "A little bird told me." "Uncle Hawke!" When she shared it, Gregory had a smile that could light up the room. "Oh, my God, I didn't even see you! Does this mean Roderick is somewhere?" The stoic man touched two fingers to his brow in greeting. "Downstairs, miss. I'm to tell you he has something for you after this is all over." For a moment, Quinn was a teenager, beaming. As was far too common in her life, it was all too brief, as Amy grabbed her by her arm. "She knows?" Campbell demanded. "Uh, yeah. Donnie and I were downstairs working on the... with the desk clerk and it was going great, but then, uh... then Annika -- my character's mom -- she crashed and--" "Yeah, I heard she was distracting the guy, which is... yeah, you don't have to repeat it." Gregory shook her head, her fingers trailing along her neck again. "It wasn't Mom who played her. It wasn't--she wouldn't do that. We have our own.. uh, our own little birdie, I guess you could say." It's nothing big, just--" "She's with..." Campbell snarled. "I'm gonna--where are they?" "Upstairs, sixth floor, room 614," Quinn said, and that was all it took for Amy to brush past. Gregory was still her mother's daughter, though, so she was able to keep pace, albeit mostly through a reluctance from Amy to leave Hawke in the dust. "Amy!" she hissed, grabbing at Campbell's elbow. "Don't do anything--" "As stupid as knowingly walking into a trap?" Amy's brow furrowed. "No worries, I leave that to my better half." The girl sighed irritably. "You weren't there, Aim. You didn't see that woman. These people took everything from her, and Mom can help them. You should be proud of her. And, uh, yeah, you should totally rescue her, too." So... uh, you really think I look creepy?" "I think you shouldn't feel the need to be anybody but Quinn." "I think I kind of like it." She shrugged slightly. "I feel cooler. Anyway. Yeah." The hug was brief but tight. "Good luck." "I'll be fine," Amy said. "What's the worst that can happen, right?" She kept her smile in place until Quinn had backed into a closet, and then turned slightly for Hawke. "Don't answer that," she said. He didn't. * * * Up on the sixth floor... Two men lingered in the hallway on the sixth floor. "So you're the new guy?" asked Nicholas Lee. He lifted a Styrofoam coffee cup to his lips with a bored sigh. "Brett something? "Brett Washington, sir," his partner answered. Unlike Lee, the small, dark-haired man was alert even at this ridiculous hour, and he hadn't needed any chemicals to keep him that way. "Just transferred in the other day." He put on a small, conciliatory smile. "Thought it'd be more exciting, you know?" Nick snorted. "Yeah, I bet you did. She's got some kind of overblown rep, broke into a bunch of places, killed some dude in the Sudan with, like, a grape or some shit. Smart, clever, sneaky as shit, blah-blah-blah." He lifted a hand, tugging on an ear. Something approaching fear colored his eyes. "But it's bullshit, right? That James Bond stuff doesn't--" The elevator bell chimed softly, interrupting his thought. "This could be them. We should--" Nick had already turned that way. "I know what we're supposed to do, dammit," he snapped, leaning casually against the frame. He stifled a yawn with one hand, attempting to look like he wasn't guarding the room. * * * Down the hallway, a man staggered out, waving his flask in front of him like a flag. "I feel badly, but you... this was really fucked up." "Yeah." Pierce lifted her hand to the earring's mate, but couldn't work the back. "You can just say it, though." She was quiet, almost muted. For maybe the first time, she seemed small. "I fucked up. So fucking used to winning. So fucking..." Alex trailed off, swallowing hard. "They all did their jobs just the way they always did. Even Dr--" Another sigh spilled from her lips, and again she gripped the sink. "And he took them. Because I fucked up. Randall was right... I started to believe my own hype. " She shook her head. "'Best in the world.' Who the fuck am I kidding?" "We'll get Hawke out. You know that," Campbell said. "And Drusilla can rot in jail. It's not death, but I'm willing to accept it as a decent alternative. You don't need to be the best at everything -- especially not when it comes to stealing, sneaking and killing." "I do when it means the lives of people I care about." She looked up to the mirror, catching Amy's multicolored eyes. "That could have been Donnie or you or Quinn. Because I couldn't put my ego aside. Because I had to fucking win. The first rule I taught Quinn -- the first fucking rule -- was to always think ahead. And I broke that rule and a man who I have known since I was twenty is going to pay for it." "Fuck your ego, Alex -- it could've been Quinn because you haven't cut her out of this yet. Don't let her go down this path. I've begged for you to step in. I begged you to stay away from an ex who's tried to kill me -- who tried to kill me again tonight -- but it's done no good, she was still groping you in the hallway with Donnie. I... I'll deal with what I need to do about the cheating when we're home, but I need you to listen to me about Quinn, if nothing else," she tried to mask that her own voice was cracking. "Because I know you're freaking out, but that could have been her -- and it wouldn't have been because of some misstep in a 'mission', but because you wouldn't make her sit at home like your daughter should." Pierce's head drooped. "I know." She sighed. "I know. I thought... I don't know what I thought. I thought it was going to be a milk run. It was a good plan. Not... not this." She gestured to her outfit. "But a real plan. It would have worked if Randall hadn't known we were there. I thought she'd be satisfied with some bullshit con on a front desk clerk and she'd go back to school and shut up about it for her senior year." Alex glanced down and back, looking at Amy from the side as she tried to get the earring free again. "I know, it was stupid. You don't have to tell me. Just... gah, this fucking thing." She sighed irritably turning to rest against the sink. "I don't know." "Here, let me..." The little redhead stepped forward, reaching gently for the earring Pierce was struggling to break free from. As she worked at the clasp, she mumbled. "'Stupid' is one word for it." "Dumb? Arrogant? Pick one." Pierce shut her eyes, tilting her head aside. "The worst part is that all I can think about is that Randall might have taken them, but... but I lost you. All the faith you had in me is gone -- and it should be. I burned it up by excluding you and then with... with Drusilla." She brushed the hair off Amy's shoulder, het voice tightening. "But I didn't cheat on you. I didn't. I just... I need you to know that." "She tried to kill me again tonight, Alex. Even speaking to her may as well be cheating." "You can hardly count it when you're trying to kill her, too." Pierce flashed a wan smile. "Look, she came to me terrified about something. She didn't ask me for anything, and she did help Quinn through the thing with the desk clerk, just like Elijah probably did for you. I'm not defending her--" The earring finally popped open, and Amy dropped the dangly thing into Pierce's palm. "That's funny, because that's exactly what it looks like you're doing." "I wanted to kill her. I almost did, too. Was ready to crush her throat like an empty pop can." "Why didn't you?" "Because I told you I wouldn't kill anyone, and I meant it." "All things considered, you know I'd be willing to make an exception for her. And she'll always make it so there's an excuse for you to give her the time of day - there'll always be something. You know that." Campbell sighed. "I gave her to those men, and I want her left there. No helping her, no... no anything to do with her. After I talked to Ashe and Hawke, I was gonna... and then I saw..." she shook her head, waving a hand. "That's not important. I just need that promise from you, and I need you not to break it no matter what shit she tries to sell you." "You have it. Of course you have it." Alex smiled, small and hesitant. "Just don't... don't say things like that. There are no exceptions. There can be no exceptions to that promise. As much as you think you'd be okay with it, you wouldn't. And if you were, you wouldn't be the woman I love, not anymore." "But she'd be gone." "No, she wouldn't." Alex reached out, taking Campbell's hands in her own. "She'd be there every time you shut your eyes. Trust me, I know. And as much as I want you to stay, as much as it'd kill me if you left, I would rather see you far away from me than to ever, ever have to go through that." Pierce's eyes shut, her hands falling away. "I have ruined every single good thing I have ever had in my life, Amy. Gods know what I'm doing to my little girl, I almost destroyed us once before and then again tonight." The woman who opened her eyes hardly seemed like wrestling's proud Spider. "I don't want to lose you," she whispered. "But I don't want you to lose yourself, you know?" Amy sighed and smiled sadly, shoulders slumping. "I think I know how to help ease the sting of tonight going wrong, but..." She breathed in deeply, almost inhaling the courage as she stuffed a hand in her pocket. "Tell you what -- I'm gonna do what I mentioned I was planning earlier, but you're going to swear to me that we don't deal with Drusilla Devonshire anymore first. At all." "When she comes to try to kill you again, that doesn't count, right?" "Alex." She blew another nervous smile, her smile flickering. "Don't nitpick." Pierce chuckled, leaning back against the sink. "You're actually nervous about this!" "Uh, a little bit and I was thinking that it would be, uhm... a little bit different than..." "I promise. You know I promise. Drusilla is part of who I was, not who I am. You're who I--" "Okay, can I just... let me get this out before I lose my nerve, okay?" "Okay." Alex's smile quieted, her head tilting back. "The floor is yours." Amy's hand slipped out of the pocket, something clutched in her hand. "So after our fight at the show, I was thinking, like... I don't know." She sighed, and it all came blurting out. "I love you. I do. I love how eloquent you are, and sometimes you say things... things about me that make me feel. Look, it's no secret that I'm not really the biggest fan of myself. I know I cover a lot with bad humor and big talk, but it... yeah. You know, even if I like to pretend you don't and you really think I'm this big, strong grown-up." That earned her a small, sad smile, but the little redhead just plowed on. "So it can sometimes be hard to buy that this powerful, attractive person who's so sure of herself and what she wants could really be into... well... me. And when you keep stuff from me, it's like I'm tossed aside and... and it makes me think of the other things I could be doing. Stuff I don't particularly want to do, but the things I'd be pulled to if I was left to my own devices. I'm so addicted to instant gratification, it's like..." She sighed again. "You probably think this is my way of telling you I wanna see other people, huh." "I may have, but you don't need to dig in your pocket for anything to dump me except maybe a Dear John letter -- would that be Dear Jane letter? -- and that's not a letter unless you've folded it a couple of dozen times." "You're not supposed to Sherlock Holmes it. Just stand there, look hot, and let me sweep you off your feet with my carefully prepared speech... that I'm delivering in the ladies' room of a Denny's at like three in the morning." Alex chuckled, raising both hands in surrender. "You're doing such a great job, too." Amy cleared her throat. "So, I got pretty down when you didn't... y'know, this wasn't exactly planned quite as extensively as I let on, I'm not gonna lie. I, uh..." She swallowed. "I got you... got you this thing, and it probably doesn't mean exactly what you want it to, but it... I'm traditionally so bad at this kind of thing, and... it just... wow, is it bad that I'm suddenly light-headed? Here." She took the taller woman's hand with her empty one, turning her palm over. "I don't know if I'm supposed to put this on you or what, so I'm..." She laid a small, golden band in Alex's hand, a single diamond set along the crown. "Yeah." "Oh, Amy..." Pierce covered her mouth with her hand, her eyes brimming almost instantly. "It's beautiful." "It, uh. It doesn't mean... y'know. Unless you really want it to, just... I've been down that road and it's never gone well, and there's so much anxiety and... and I guess what this means is, well, we'll see? Like, you've got me, I'm serious about it, I'm not just here because I got attached to Quinn and... and you need to keep your promises, and vice versa. It means I'm gonna try really fucking hard not to fuck up, okay? And maybe... maybe it can help you settle, get grounded a little." She shrugged. "Reassurance." "Reassurance," Pierce echoed, lifting the tiny circle up to get a better view. "Can I wear it? I mean..." Amy shrugged one shoulder, still somewhat uncomfortable. "Yeah. You can put it on your necklace or... or, yeah, you can wear it, but people will think that, uh..." "I know what they'll think," Alex said, her voice quiet but firm. "Are you okay with them thinking it? I mean, I can put it on the necklace if you'd rather not -- I know you're not as... as comfortable as I am, and Quinn will... well, you know what she'll do. There will be squealing. But if you're asking--" "Asking you to put up with me for as long as I last... and if you're good, we'll make it legal sometime." Campbell smirked. "Maybe. No pressure. Specifically no pressure on me. I get... we'll say 'cagey'. And I know there'll be squealing. I can deal. "As far as other people and what they'll think..." she continued. "You don't have to tell them we're not en--that we're not taking that awful dive, you can just do your Alex thing and be all mysterious if it makes you feel better. I don't care what they think, especially if it results in congratulatory gifts." "Gifts are good." Pierce smiled a little, slipping the band onto her left ring finger. "I think I'm going to kiss you now, if that's okay." "That's probably an appropriate response, yeah." Amy laughed. "Just remember they're waiting in the car, and I'm not exactly up for anything more than that in a Denny's..." Alex stepped forward, arms sliding around the smaller woman's waist. The kiss that followed was soft and sweet and probably as romantic as something under those circumstances could possibly have been. When they retreated, the taller woman's eyes were shut. They opened full of tears. "Hey..." Amy whispered. She reached up, thumbing away a tear. "What's wrong?" Pierce lifted her hand, wiping at her face with a small flicker of irritation. "Nothing," she said. "It's just... I never thought..." Campbell rocked up, a gentle smile on her lips. "Happy tears?" "Yeah..." A soft puff of a laugh escaped as she lifted their linked hands to her lips. "Definitely happy tears." "Sweet." The little redhead turned, bumping her shoulder into Alex's side. "So we can go? I'm starving and this is a Denny's, so... we have to find somewhere that serves actual food." Alex laughed again, reaching up to tousle Amy's hair. "We can do that. After all, somebody promised to put together a breakout." She tugged open the bathroom door. "Uh... yeah." Amy rubbed the back of her neck. "Was kinda hoping you'd help out with that." "Doable." The server raised a hand in farewell to the two women as they left. "So how long do you think it'll take Quinn to notice? Five minutes?" "Oh, less than that. I give it, like, two." They were both wrong. It took forty-two seconds.
