Fuck Zoe Williams (Slight Return)

26 June 2008

Date: 6/26/08 He got his rematch. He was unconscious when it was handed down. A few IQ points down, maybe; but those brain cells hadn't helped him out when he accepted the challenge, hadn't given him advice when he saw the mountain he had to climb. They hadn't done shit for months, mainly. Stragglers. Good to see them go. He listened to dead Bob Dylan and thought of dead Frank Black, of dead Jordan White. Each walking corpses, assumed names, past lives barely linking hanging shadow from them. A dominoing of some magnitude. A constant of betraying the old self for the present self; resentful of whatever imagined betrayals the future self will levy in its time. Everything as nothing, five years five months five days down the road. Melting, everything melting. He wondered if anyone had ever claimed a soda problem, if his light abuse had any parallel beyond his head, if he was just another listless stoner as uncertain as weather. It did taste true, sometimes, when all the hours had fallen away and left him on the last stretch of night and his head was lost somewhere in a milky golden daze in Charlotte or Tokyo or Phoenix or Vegas, like none were anything but backdrops to a continuous wander. He had gone further than he deserved, than he had any right to; he agreed on that until he began to doubt there was any greater way to prove merit than to just achieve whatever and be done with it. He had gotten there, then. What fault could he raise??he had earned everything in a way. There had been sacrifices; if not direct sacrifices towards an end goal, then casualties of the path he'd taken and corpses now regardless. Someone in the eqation was soulless, foul, inhuman. It was either him or it was the majority of everyone else; he was righteous or he was damned, and he just moved through without betting on either. He had direction like a steamroller; it granted him a certain apathy to whatever could step into the path ahead. Hell. Maybe sometimes, pride was the fall. It was instinct that dragged his phone out while the last cigarfull dried; memory that chastised him with a call a month removed: "Mike." "Yo, Franky. Fucks good wit ya? Aint heard from your ass in a muhfuckin hot minute." "None of that shit, man. Saw that interview you put up against me." "Wha?" "The only interview youve had in your fuckin life. Me and Leslie? You sellin us over?" "Wait, wait, you mean that bullshit with that little blonde bitch that came by?" "I dont know who the fuck you talked to’dont give a fuck, even." "Whatever. That what youre bitchin on?" "Yeah." "So, Im guessin they didnt make out yalls little love affair as a good thing, yeah?" "Dead on." "Well, damn. Sorry bout that, but. Shit’somebody comes to your crib with a couple photos askin if you can name the people in em for a few grand, hell you gonna do?" "Say I never saw em before and shut the fuckin door, man." "Uh huh. Aint hard to throw that out there when you workin a six figure job like you got. Im still tryna pay rent. You freal gonna hold that against me?" "Fuck yeah, I am." "You got some problems, Frank." "Yeah. Motherfuckers like you, mainly." essentially, and so on. He scrolled down, called Sage, eyed the Garcia. It rang through to some pacific female, stating calm Sage's unavailability and getting cut off middirections by a phone snapping shut. He lit a cigarette and stood, dragged a deep chair in front of an open window and stared to the city below, spoke nothing and saw less. He had never found Charlotte's soul, had never located any uniqe flavor to its humanity in his twentyodd years; just glass, brick buildings, streets that lead him to highways that lead him to better places. Only home for how many hours he'd slept or spent sleepless there by some choice that he couldn't recall ever having made. He'd consented, accepted by not rebelling in great walls of fire and wrath. Qiet, tremors in his spine like he had accepted mortgages and an eventual death with the thoughtless motion of ordering the same shitty lunch endless, of only running in a vertical circle. Thoughts cycled without emotions, clinical as they were wrong. He felt. His cigarette toppled spirals to the street below. Orange embers splashed on the roof of a decaying import lingering at a red light; the butt rolled harmless onto the asphalt. He introduced a different fire into his world, let resin coat his nervous system with an armor impenetrable. His stereo grew louder, lulled him away from monotony’or at least away from holding any particular grudge against it, and the immediate value was the same either way. He scratched through his hair at lingering ghosts of the stitches Adam had left him with and he thought about Leslie; thought fleetingly and forced it out for some subject imagined as greater to him. He thought about Leslie until he thought about smashing Vivica, until he thought about smashing her well enough that he gained thirtysome pounds of gold and leather. A smile cracked like rivers rerouted. In his years, he'd never been told he'd enjoy his eventual job, never been lied to so straightfaced’there was some pleasure in that, then, if nothing else. He appreciated the honesty several degrees less than he despised the violence he'd born host to; but he didn't move to excise. It incubated in his ribs, had latched itself onto his heart as a willing accomplice necessary for newly formed goals. Like rivers rerouted; he lied. Feigned ignorance like he'd had no choice in the decision, like someone else had been the one to point his direction out with a "go (whatever), young man" and he'd had no recourse but to follow. Culpability. His head had opened itself to hazard when he first entertained a divide between himself and his character; had grown a gulf so irreconcilable between a lashing rage and an introspective qiet that he'd been forced to terms with either side of himself, been forced to straddle the gulf without being able to fall into either like it was still the home he'd pretended them as in his time. Just another problem to bloat his head (commonly mistaken for an ego), just another reason to shut the world out and fake until everything made sense or went away, one. But he couldn't fake, or he couldn't fake enough to avoid leaks in his wall, and he couldn't put the faith in himself to assume that he wouldn't die, one day, a confused old wreck of a man with no great final decision. Not past image, no, he'd fallen somewhere below the line where it even mattered to him. He set goals just to have goals; had tried to keep them as close to what he wanted as possible and had ended up only a short distance away from any of them when the strings started snapping. So he thought about how hard he'd have to swing a steel chair to induce coma, and he aimed it at Vivica's face as one last reminder that, all things accounted for, his humanity had always been up for sale. He'd run the course before, enough times at least that it no longer bore surprise turns for him. Just another route, just like going to work. Then again. He thought about Leslie and he wondered if, maybe, he'd just fallen back into the hole he'd dug when it happened before. Deciding on that would've destroyed the magic; he let it fall into the wrinkled surface of his brain with eyebrows raised, with a light "huh." And he smoked, unaware that the human body could process oxygen unfiltered. He thought of wrestling; he dreamt of her’that was the division he allowed. He could put his head to work, could slam his flesh into the wars; but his soul was elsewhere with no temptation to rededicate itself. All eyes focused, at least. No more mixing the two, at least. He kept his palms to his temples, face to the world and refitted cigar hanging low; the totem of some indigenous peoples, the qiet statue of no external judgment, of no means for external judgment. Relaxed like igneous rock carved into something an eternity away from its world. The warm wind whipped at his hair. The city idled, a low rumbling beneath the revolutions in his skull’or the splinters that were left of his skull, the shards that rattled whenever the draft kicked. So he toked his peace like mortar and regretted every step of his life. It came to him in a red vision: if he'd beaten Lia, he could've still been free. There wouldn't be cheers that made him qestion his ethical code, wouldn't have been wouldbe heroes clamoring to prepare for tag matches backstage with him. He could've kept them all shut out and away, could've nurtured their hatred until there was no chance of them seeming to support what he'd become. He'd lost that unanimous reaction, left it behind with the Establishment and stepped out on his own; relatively good. It stung him, the apathy or the idiocy of the fans. That they'd roar for him without caring how much dirt was on his hands, that someone could proclaim their love for Jordan White without wondering how much hate they should hold for Frank Black. They'd made him fake, then. They'd formed this image of him in their minds, plastered it across screens, snuck the mask around his neck and let him step on stage as something he wasn't. Their interpretations, then. Their view had clouded him, separated him from himself. Ungrateful was too weak a word; alienation too obvious a progression. They weren't buying his love with the volume of money reqired to pull it off; they just gave him a buffer to fall into if all else failed. There was more than enough money available to ruin a man's life; he'd read and reread the number the bank had told him, had only stared blankfaced at the teller as if she were lying for her own amusement. The look on her face was a modest surprise, too. And sometimes he walked his city, carried his morning ugly through the evening like a medal, some rockstar merit badge for sleeping past sun set. Like endless night could be facsimilated by never being in the presence of day. Like he had anything against the day. His mind wandered further than he ever did; his feet didn't find routines, but they didn't stray far enough from the world to staggerstep unnoticed. He oozed fame, left trails of celebrity that invited cameras and fouler beasts even, wanting autographs or an ear to bark at or just a chance to share words with the undeservedly wealthy. No hours were too late for the hounds; but he walked his city with a mind to the anatomy of the world around, the world within. Parallels between. All that shit. He was a bad person, he decided at interims. It always came to that bottom line, the same moralistic fantasy on every rotation; he could only ever halfbelieve it, found himself unable to truly dedicate to all it entailed. It gave him a path, offered to streamline his life and he would just shrug, continue on running the tangled ribbons of being human instead of truly good or truly evil or even involved with their game of absolutes. That bottom line never supported him long, kept dropping him back to where he started. Hank Williams the dead, meek on another eternal repeat of "Lost Highway." He hated the spotlight. Under its glow every flaw hung evident, cast long low shadows along the contours of his mistakes, rode highlights on the raised edges of past scars that never healed qite right. He could live with himself, had come to terms with who he was; he hadn't learned to cope with the rest of the world having it spelled out to them. He was an underachiever with no room for new friends and shrinking space for the old ones. Drama as his anathema, his confusions cultivated by every complication. Just some guy with a job and without the will to cut every string. What was it Zoe's article had said? Came across kinder than the rest, maybe. He found enough justification to call her, at least. Ringing. 2 am had happened, that fact rested in digital red. Ringing. Smoking. Ringing. "Uh. Frank?" "Hey, Zoe." "Are you alright?" He considered. "Sure." "Okay. Um. Its, uh. "Two fifteen." "Yeahhh, that. Could you call me back like. After work tomorrow?" "No." "Oh. Whats up?" "Leslies bolted, my head feels like. Shit, really. An every time I breathe, I think somebodys stabbin me. Probably that sumo fucker that beat my ass." "Um. Okay." "Look, you wanna chill? Ill, Ill fuckin pay you whatever you lose on skippin work tomorrow, if it comes to it. I just. Fuck. I cant be by myself anymore." He tightened his fingers, balled a fist and dug dull nails at the ball of his thumb. He ground teeth. "Alright. You know where I stay. Just. Lemme get dressed first, okay?" "Better be qick about it." He snuffed the blunt and hung up, head still full of Charlotte with GCW taking up occupation only along the peripheries. Roached cigar in his pocket, he headed to the parking garage, to the BMW that came his way after High Stakes with a consolatory note attached to the steering wheel; better than the Caprice, at any rate. He cranked with one hand and returned fire with the other, taking in a world of smoke as he crept along a wavelength beyond the spectrum of the police. Modest. Discrete. Suits that never qite fit him. Songs that started where they'd been left. A world indescribably closed in and closed off. far too nervy observin this world, And the rest. Words. Music. Street signs and stop lights gone green for joy at some hidden electronic holiday. And nothing. And nothing. He pulled up, stopped his Beemer behind hers. He got out. He knocked. Yeah. Yeah, I do." She smirked, huffed with an eyebrow up as she backed into the living room. "Come on in. You already ruined my sleep. Now. A sumo beat your ass?" "Yeah." He idly played with the mood lighting, twisting its neck as Zoe disappeared around a wall. "You want some coffee? Its that flavored stuff, yeah, but. Coffee?" "Yeah, yeah." He hadn't found a good justification, really; the malice in him had died away and left an acceptance that some of his parts were on hiatus indefinitely, that he wouldn't bear anymore grudges until those lost bits returned to full functional order. He was there because he was tired of staring at the city, of introducing himself to new people under the light of his televised persona, of having to explain anything to anyone.
