Film director and producer Alfred Hitchcock, the pioneer in the suspense and psychological thriller genres, is giving a lecture to students. A quizzical young student filmmaker steps up to the podium during the Q&A session and asks Hitchcock about the top secret British military plans in The 39 Steps. The student wants to know why the plans are never revealed or discussed at length at any point after the opening scene of the film. With that sly grin that he himself made famous, Hitchcock chuckles at the student’s query and says, "We have a name in the studio, and we call it the 'MacGuffin.' It is the mechanical element that usually crops up in any story. In crook stories it is almost always the necklace and in spy stories it is most always the papers." When the student asks Hitchcock to elaborate further, Hitchcock says the MacGuffin is incidental. In a 1966 interview, Hitchcock explained the term MacGuffin further, using an example of his own. He said: "It might be a Scottish name, taken from a story about two men in a train. One man says, “What's that package up there in the baggage rack?” And the other answers, “Oh that's a MacGuffin.” The first one asks, “What's a MacGuffin?” “Well,” the other man says, “It's an apparatus for trapping lions in the Scottish Highlands.” The first man says, “But there are no lions in the Scottish Highlands,” and the other one answers “Well, then that's no MacGuffin!” So, you see, a MacGuffin is nothing at all." So, you see, a MacGuffin is a merely plot device that motivates the characters or advances the story, but the details of which are of little or no importance otherwise. The element that distinguishes a MacGuffin from other types of plot devices is that it isn’t important what the object specifically is. Anything that serves as a motivation will do. The MacGuffin might even be ambiguous. Its importance is accepted by the story's characters, but it doesn’t actually have any effect on the story. It can be generic or left open to interpretation. The MacGuffin, obviously, is common in films, especially thrillers. Sometimes, though not always, the MacGuffin is the central focus of the film in the first act, and later declines in importance as the struggles and motivations of the characters play out. And sometimes the MacGuffin is all but forgotten by the end of the film. ___________________________________________________________ "So, you see, Tyson, you could say the gyro is our MacGuffin."
