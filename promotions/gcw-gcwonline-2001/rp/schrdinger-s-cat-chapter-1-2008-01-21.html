Schr’dinger's Cat: Chapter 1

21 January 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: January 21st, 2008 The people seemed as if blurs of light. They got in and out of the subway going on with their lives, the rest of the world a mystery to them. They didn’t concern themselves with what they could see and they concerned themselves even less with what they couldn’t see. There existed things that most people couldn’t see and Antonio knew this. He hoped that he could keep himself ignorant of these events, but he had gone through much. This was his escape. He sat in that subway cart for hours, full of the company of people but always alone. For some reason, Antonio had begun to appreciate his solitude. His life was too hectic to remain sane for a long time. To be able to be alone and to actually think and meditate on things was something not everyone in his line of work had time for. People nowadays didn’t care much for thinking and instead bombarded themselves with work and activities to take their time. They no longer stopped to think about what they were doing. People were no longer going to work or coming from work. It was that time of the day when everyone is at their jobs, school, or whatever other place they could be at. Antonio sat all alone in the subway cart. The day had begun to get strangely dark, as if clouds were covering the sun. Antonio looked through the window but saw no clouds. There was something gripping his insides. He knew there was something going terribly wrong but he had no idea what it was. He stood up as the subway cart reached its stop. The doors opened and he was out. He was out in a world he recognized, but there was something in the air that told him that it was not the same place he had just been. Antonio looked around and was immediately struck by how the station seemed completely deserted. The painting in the walls was chipping away; the floors were stained with what he could only recognize as blood. The frustration in his eyes was evident. This wasn’t where he was supposed to be, that much was apparent to him, but where was he? “Hello” Anyone out there?” he screamed as loudly as he could. The echo was frightening. It didn’t feel normal. He continued towards where he knew the escalators to the outside would be. The darkness was receding with every step he took towards the outside. At that moment, he heard a noise. He turned around to try and find the source of the noise, but the darkness and the echo made it almost impossible. Antonio could feel someone watching him. “Who goes there” Show yourself!” Antonio got into fighting position, expecting the worst. He could clearly hear footsteps but thanks to the loud echo, he couldn’t tell if there was more than one assailant. “Don’t worry, I won’t hurt you. I don’t think I would even dare to step toe to toe with you. I prefer to use the courtroom as my ring and words as my fists. Well, I used to anyways,” the man said with a hint of sadness and longing in his voice. “Who are you?” Antonio asked as he turned towards the voice and saw the silhouette of the man that had spoken. The man smiled. “I’m just the messenger and guide. Nothing more. You should come with me, these parts are dangerous. You will be safe in my boss’s house,” he said as he turned towards the direction where the exit waited. Antonio saw no other course of action, so he followed. He walked besides the mysterious man. They walked up the stairs as the escalator wasn’t working and finally got to the outside. It was like someone had punched Antonio in the stomach. The air smelled of sulfur and half of the buildings that were still standing were going to crumble very soon. It was like a piece of hell had been unleashed on earth. The debris on the familiar streets made this place seem like something out of a post-apocalyptic movie. “Where am I” This looks like Puerto Rico. The streets have the same name and look the same, but this place is so? barren. Did something happen while I was in the subway?” he asked trying to piece together this giant puzzle he was walking on. “There’s not a lot I can explain. All I know is that about five years ago there was a huge drought. The people went crazy. It was like nothing seen before. People started to kill themselves and kill each other. There is speculation that there was some kind of virus that made people crazy in the water, but there is no proof,” he tried to explain. It was still a lot for Antonio to understand. “But where am I” I don’t understand. It was not like this when I got on that subway. Something must have happened.” “This is another dimension, another plane, whatever the hell people call it these days. Basically it is running parallel to the dimension you are from. That station is what is called a “soft spot” in the fabric of time and space,” the man tried to explain as they walked through the desolate streets of Puerto Rico. Antonio thought about it. He remembered a short story by Jorge Luis Borges. The Garden of the Forking Paths basically showed that for every decision you must make, there is another universe where the one you did not pick, happens. “My name is Antonio Vargas,” he said as he extended his hand towards the man. “Javier Nieves. I know who you are though. My boss knows who you are,” he said as he shook Antonio’s hand. They walked the rest of the way in silence. Not because there was nothing to say, but because there were many things in Antonio’s mind. As they walked, they saw the first two people since they had come out of the subway station. They held AK-47s close to their chests and kept vigilant eyes on the horizon. They say Javier walking towards them with Antonio as his side and they slowly lowered their guns a little bit. Javier opened the door to the structure that in Antonio’s dimension had been an office building. What he was getting into, he had no clue. --- Antonio had to walk up three floors to get to where he was supposed to be. The double doors were opened by another lackey with a gun and when he went inside the room, it was like the world outside was nothing like it seemed. The room was decorated like the President’s Suite in any five star hotel. There was a beautiful leather couch and in it sat a person. The man wore cowboy boots and a cowboy hat that covered his face. Antonio would have thought that he was napping if it wasn’t for the constant tapping of the boots on the floor. “Welcome to my lair, Antonio. I have been waiting for you,” the man said as he tipped his hat upwards and showed his face. It was almost hard to recognize, but under the coarse features that five years of guerrilla warfare had left marked, Antonio could recognize the face of someone he knew very well. He had spent years with this person. “Burton. You look” tired. What is all this?” Antonio says motioning all around himself. “I am tired. I’m tired of having to fight each day in order to survive. For five years I’ve led these men to survival. Now I am threatened by an old friend. This place” this place is my haven. You remember that house right in front of the beach and how you used to spend countless hours hearing the sound of the waves and looking at the horizon? This place is my refuge,” Burton said as he caressed the leather of the sofa. “Enough of reminiscing though, there is business we need to discuss.” “Indeed we do. You need to tell me how to get the hell out of this place and go back to my own universe. Why would you need me here anyways” Isn’t there a me in this world?” Burton smiled in a twisted way. Antonio had never seen his childhood friend smile in that manner. “There was. You died.” “What” How did I die?” Antonio asked, looking flustered. “I killed you,” Burton said without much emotion showing on his facial features. The look of astonishment in Antonio’s face seemed to give some sort of sick pleasure to Burton. “You know, this gives me an unprecedented opportunity,” Burton said as he got up and approached Antonio. “When I killed you, there was really no pleasure to it. I wasn’t like one of those villains in movies that put you in some weird contraption and then explained the whole thing to you,” he told Antonio as he sat in front of the man he was, in a way, torturing. “I didn’t get to tell you the reason why I shot you in the head.” Antonio knew that in a way, he wasn’t talking about him. Burton had killed him, but not really him. The confusion inside Antonio’s head was almost too much to handle. He stood in front of Burton though almost as if challenging Burton to kill him again. “Why did you kill me” How does a friendship that has lasted more than 15 years end up in murder?” Burton’s laughter was spiteful. “You call that a friendship” That was you and your feelings of grandeur and me, always behind you, helping you whenever you needed it. Then the damned drought arrived and as always you wanted to lead the way, be the hero you always dreamed of being,” Burton snickered. “Only you weren’t up to it. You were weak. We needed to survive and you wouldn’t do what was necessary for us to survive. You put our lives in jeopardy.” Antonio had no memory of what Burton was talking about. He wasn’t there when it happened. His words stung like daggers though. This was a man that two hours ago Antonio would have had no hesitation in putting his own life in Burton’s hands. Like he knew that the person Burton had killed wasn’t himself, he knew that this was not the Burton he knew. “Is that it then” You just pulled me over from my own universe in order to lecture me about how weak I was in your world? If there is nothing else you want to lecture me about, then I would love it if you were able to point the way home,” Antonio said as he gave his back to Burton. It was a gutsy move, any of the men in that room could have just shot him there were he stood, but there was a reason why Burton had brought him into his “haven”. Even though he had yet to hear Burton’s reason, he knew he wanted no part of it. Burton moved closer to Antonio and whispered in his ear. “Nothing would give me more pleasure than to shoot you again.” Antonio believed it. He could feel the hatred in Burton’s voice. “But you are right,” Burton continued as he backed off from Antonio. “I did bring you here for a reason. The truth is that every day our numbers dwindle. There are too many factions running around thinking they own it all and too few supplies,” Burton sighed as he thought of the men and women he had lost in the war. “We need your knowledge in something specific.” “What does that mean?” Antonio asked incredulously. Everything that had happened in the last few hours made him feel dizzy. He had gone through some weird things, but this was by far the most confusing. He didn’t know how to feel or think. “It means that you will be staying here for quite a while.”
