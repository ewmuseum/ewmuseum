Date: 10/18/36 And another one of those things that count for nothing! Woohooo! -- --------------- Thirty years from now; a wrinkled bear of a man, with very dark skin and very light hair leans in over an open casket. He mumbles something in Latin, clutches a rosary tight in one hand and whiteknuckles the other with a deathgrip on the stained wooden surface. His eyes, years away from white, twitch down the long, deep valleys and creases of the corpse's face as he pulls the beads to the open chest of his deep blue sports jacket. A black man, head shaved and gray beard extended, steps up in this allblack, threepiece suit and wraps his arm around the shoulders of the weepy brute--or gives it a shot, comes about 3/4 of the way to success. 'I can't do it.' The bear's voice barely has the energy to make it past all the gravel in his throat. 'Fuck me, I can't do this.' 'Come on, Torm. Old men like us die every day.' 'He wasn't old. Christ. We're not old.' 'Chill out, big man. What's got you?' 'I can't give this fuckin' eulogy.' 'Man. Jordan picked you.' 'Why can't you give it, Trey?' 'He picked you. Why can't you give it?' 'I never liked him.' 'He never liked you and he still picked you for it. Dude believed in somethin' about you.' 'And I believe in showing a dead man respect.' 'Which is something you just don't feel like paying him?' 'I can't pay it properly. I won't insult his memory.' 'Then I'd suggest start talkin? to that priestly looking chap over there.' 'Trey. You could give him what he deserves. You're the poet, I’m just a fucking neutered jake at a desk.' 'I've been drinking since I heard about this shit. I got nothin' left to say.' 'Something.' 'No. I'm gonna go take my seat. This one's on you, Sobe.' 'Jesus.' 'He might be available.' Trey doesn't wait for the laughter; Trey just makes his peace with a whispered glance and a cursory mumble and darts off to let the pews swallow him alive. Drenched in the rainbow tones of Jesus crucified and bleeding out on the hill, content to cross his arms and wait for the punchline to a joke that's lost the one guy who could finish it. Figures. A cellphone goes unruly and sings out the first three bars of a song Trey's a few generations away from naming, but right in the heart of hating. Someone shuts it up in the middle of a too long drumroll, not giving the chorus time to kick in. Whoever it was tries to keep their conversation unintelligible three rows off; Trey watches the line come and pass. A girl--with some vague air of being essentially devoid of nationality--stuns, all in white and glowing in the distorted reds and yellows of heaven hanging low across the way, steps up to the box and stares silent. Trey laughs, smiling as she turns to him. And he thinks: still beautiful. And her golden eyes shine. And she thinks, with mourning the most alien concept to her: you're doing alright yourself, Trey. And: Jordan said hello. He doesn't ask her the secrets of life or death or any of those. He doesn't know if she knows, he doesn't know if she'll say, he doesn't really see a point. Life's too short. She nods her diminished chin and exits down the long center row with the march of a na’ve royal. 'God damn,' he mutters. 'Jordan's fuckin? friends.' There's a sudden appreciation for how big the crowd is, how foreign it is to Trey. Had Jordan had this many friends through his whole life? No. Wellwishers and onlookers and innocent bystanders wanting a taste of the fame. Gotta be. Torm pushes past a roadbeaten white guy in the aisle, makes this storming entrance to his seat. 'How'd it go?' 'He said it'd cost extra.' 'Damn. Gotta afford a Cadillac somehow, I guess.' 'Yeah. I told him Jordan could cover it.' 'Hah. Right on. Thought you looked smug on your way back.' 'I just needed it off my shoulders. Not like Jordan asked me personally, either.' He pats both hands to his chest and pushes the whole matter away from him. Trey’s not looking to appreciate the gesture. 'Hey, Torm. That Danielle?' Trey points and Torm eyes. She's short enough but a little thinner than expected, moving in an ash gray shroud and veil like a loosely wrapped mummy. Torm catches her eye as she drifts to the unattended casket and huddles over, shoulders stabbing out in harsh spikes; she has her moments. 'Maria. It is.' 'Wasn't expectin' her to show up. But, hell, Jordan don't seem to mind.' Trey laughs a wheeze and slams his fist into his ribs to bang out a dusty cough. 'Five years ago and neither of us would've been here.' 'I would've, you old Catholic sonofabitch. What we did was wrong, but you always gotta pay respects.' Sobias grumbles and slumps down into the pew, eyes locked on frail Danielle. 'I don't think she's up for the Spanish fly.' 'Respect, Trey.' 'Respect's givin' Jordan the burial he'd have wanted. I know the boy didn't pick a church for himself.' 'Faith is a mystery.' 'Who knew Jordan longer? Outta us.' 'Me.' 'What I thought. You can't tell me you honestly see him comin' around like this.' 'Hhhhh.' 'I'm sayin', I expected at least an open bar.' 'Tonight. That's the reception.' 'Oh. I just thought we were invited to a wedding later on to fill out seats.' 'His wife.' 'What?' 'His wife might've picked a church.' 'He remarried after Alex?' 'I think he did.' 'Maybe I wouldn't've been here five years back.' Danielle pushes herself up to the edge and bends a hard right angle. People pay respect to the intimacy she forces on the scene, keeping their distance and avoiding every stare except for the briefest and lightest. Other than Trey and Sobias. And a tanned blonde with lipstick red like stained glass windows at midnight and pursed into a bottomheavy O. Her forehead wrinkles, eyebrows pitch down at her nose to give her ice eyes a nook to stab from. She heaves her chest out in some primate minded dominant thrust, sucks in the nothing gut she's got and preens. Tall and stunningravishingenthrallingradiantproud.. And even she looks away eventually. Shakes her head, grabs her purse, and steps out of a side door to smoke a cigarette. 'Well, Torm. That ain't Alex, and if she isn't the widow she's fuckin' crazy.' 'Jess, maybe.' 'Says her name's Dawn Jessica White in the pamphlet.' 'It's not a pamphlet.' Trey squints. 'And it's not a playbill either. You seen Alex around?' 'She didn't come.' 'How do you know?' 'She wasn't invited.' 'Was Danielle?' 'No.' 'You're a D.A , Torm. Supposed to be sharp.' 'It's not a good day.' She laughs just loud enough for the front row to hear. Twentyfive of thirty write it off as grief. Five aren't sure they heard it. It's definitely a laugh either way, and it bellows with her as an amplifier, and it drowns out a very soft click. Shaking her head, turning to doubletime steps to the side door. The crowd breathes; several remark on how refreshing it is. The preacher closes the lid and it all goes black for ol' Corpse Jones. 'Ahem,' it begins. 'I'm not listening to this pompous jackass talk about Jordan. You with me on stepping out for a smoke, Torm?' 'I quit.' 'I didn't say what.' 'Jordan White was many things, to many people.' 'Doesn't matter. I quit.' 'Can still cut out on this prick.' 'Trey. Respect.' 'Many things, to many people; but most importantly of all,' 'I'd have listened to your eulogy, so move your bigass knees and let me out.' 'and let this man's life serve not as a testament to his riches, but to the nobler cause he served, for even though he denied God in life, he served His higher purpose through his humanitarian' Trey shakes his head and slows for almost a frame. Front half's already through the door though. There's no point in turning back. Until: 'It seems the esteemed Mr. Miller has a few words for us.' And he has to turn. Their eyes meet, yeah, and there's something in it about passing the buck off. Speak or shoot me, they're saying as they dart from performer to audience and back again. Trey waves. The first prohibitory step; the crowd's wearing this heavy awe like there's actually something important happening. We're just old men. This is on me. He's cringing. Torm's got this bastard smile that's coming straight out of his gut with the kind of force that makes Trey look away and move with a little motivation. Jordan had money and did good things with it. No. It was so sudden and no one could've known and we all wish we'd had more time with that. That. That stupid fucker? No. Jordan wasn't a stupid fucker, really. No. Jordan was a good friend. No. Jordan was. Getting there. And I didn't have anything prepared for this and really didn’t even expect to be up here, but, well, there's only a room full of people more qualified to do this shit right now. Much better. They're always so small from the podium. 'Hey.' No reaction. He puts his hand down. Glory's watching from the front row, haunting like an inkblot. She never looked like her brother. Why this? Her nose is outlined in watery mascara, prominent and Spanish. Behind her’s a barrelchested whitehair that’s kept it long. Maybe Lexicon, cleaned up and not broken by the years. Been too long to tell. He looks like he’s anticipating something. And Trey thinks that maybe it is Lex, wellversed in Trey’s works and eager to hear him speak. And maybe it is and he doesn’t give a shit. He barely recognizes Jordan’s son, tucked deep in the outside corner on the fourth row. Names are gone from there, at best a passing face in an almost lost memory’two’or a chick from a bus’five’and at worst a total unknown’the rest of the crowd. All wanting something. Something good, something sharp. About twenty of the chicks make him wish he were fifteen years younger. And this one woman makes him wish he were five older. So this is how Torm would’ve felt. That fucker would deserve it. 'We used to wrestle.' He laughs and turns, a military march to the door. Father Whatever's quick to replace him, speaking in tongues or just not saying anything worth hearing. To the landing, now, Trey's looking down at the tired, the weak, the huddled masses of press lingering around the front gates and waiting for a sign of life. And he doesn't see Danielle, and he doesn't see Dawn. Down the steps to the parking lot, lighting his joint with a centuries old calm and looking for something that stands out as better than the rest of this rut. This empty space just yells at him. Then, something entirely different does. He turns around as the doors flail open at the mercy of a few tons of the crashing crazed. Something like 'fire,' and they're tumbling down the steps in an indeterminate wave. There weren't but so many people inside, after all, and not all of them could decide to plunge out the side exit. Trey steps out of the imminent front and lets the wave break. No way up. No need to panic, though. No way up. Who fights futility? Or, hell, who could? He kicks a pebble to the empty space and laughs. It’s not so much a funny punchline. But it’s definitely fitting. Just feels kinda bad being left out. That’s all. Right now, Jordan's splayed far away from the nearest shadow, smoking a small glass pipe with Danielle crosslegged at his head and tracing fingers down his scalp, stroking along the edges of his spider tattoo. He coughs into a laugh. 'I think I want a very public, very destructive, cremation.' 'When?' 'Whenever.' 'Huh. I'll see what I can do.' 'Oooh. Could you change the calendars so that first of January's always a Monday too?' 'That's just insane, little one.'
