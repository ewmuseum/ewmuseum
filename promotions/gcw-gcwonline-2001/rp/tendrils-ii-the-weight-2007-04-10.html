Tendrils II: The Weight

10 April 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10 April 2007 Hradpou’tn? Las Vegas, Nevada The computer screen created a sickly glow on the face of Lia Ambrosi. She felt like she had been glued in the chair for the past few days, and no matter how comfortable it was to sit in, she was beginning to go a little stir crazy. Weeks ago, she had started an encrypted file to keep track of everything she would need to take out Joseph Sinclair, Ian Childress's half brother, and for some reason, a person that he very much wanted to be rid of. He wasn't going to be an easy kill. The Hound of Longinus- he served Longinus much the way her brother served Cain. It was no easy feat to insinuate yourself with a paragon and earn his or her trust. It was no easy feat to continue to hold the job without becoming cannon fodder- then again, he almost had. Joseph Sinclair walked with a limp, and as best as she could figure, it was because of an encounter with his half brother that he did. She had not pressed Ian for details, as, for the most part, Ian had let her be for the past few weeks. She had seen another side of him through all of this- Ian wanted the job done, and he didn't really want to hear about it until it was. This was a rather different figure than the mentor that Lia had looked up to, the guide who had helped her achieve something that had been a long-time dream. But she supposed that things changed. A lot of relations in the Order were strained right now, especially among the KB:DI. It wasn't that people were angry with each other, it was just that... people were scarce. A feeling of aloneness had slowly begun to descend on her- with Dani, Gray and Josh all but disappearing from GCW, and the fact that Blaise and Ukyo (who she was rather closer to) had been practically unreachable for the past few weeks, the former Native American Nations half of the KB was a wall. A cold, impassable, unscalable wall. Add to that the fact that Vivica had left some time ago, and Dawn and Virgil had recently put in requests to go onto GCW's inactive roster, and it was like WMW all over again- nobody anywhere. Just her. Sometimes, the silence in the locker room was enough to make her want to scream. Sometimes, even the silence in her Las Vegas home was enough to make her want to scream. It wasn't that nobody was here- Virgil was here, and Rune, and the little twin girls that were under their care until Hope's situation could be stabilized. But even Hope and Keiko were gone, and it made her sad to think about the fact that an empire that had been so carefully built had crashed down on all of their heads without so much as a second's warning. Maybe that was why she hadn't bothered to make a move yet on taking care of Sinclair. It wasn't that she intended to welch on a debt, to speak in the vernacular, but she just didn't have the energy to do it right now, of all times, when everything was falling apart. Trying to push through the distraction, she zeroed in on the screen again. She had been staring at this for hours, but there was only so much enjoyment one could get out of watching a the thin, blurry pictures that a security camera took. Granted, these were some nice cameras, and had a good deal of special equipment built in that made it rather easy to tell not only who but what was entering their compound. That meant that when the time came for her to go into their lair, she was going to have to come up with a damn good reason why a vampire should enter the lair of the Society of Leopold, who hunted them. There were plenty of turncoat rebels, certainly, who would sell their allegiances just to make sure they had a little peace of mind and security. But there was much for her to conceal- clan for instance. No Tzimisce worth his or her salt would bother with the Society. And too, they would read in her aura that she was a diablerist, that at one point, she had slain and eaten the heart of another vampire (that was Lucien Forlani, and the thought made her smile) to gain his power. Unless there was a way for an aura to be camouflaged. She wouldn't be able to damper it entirely, though she was sure that the Order had technology enough to do that. She would need to change it, so that it read differently. But she didn't know what other sort of being she could pass as- if she came in as a vampire with the dirty streaks of diablerie in her aura, or if she came in with no aura at all, they would be on the alert, and she couldn't have that. But maybe she could pass as a ghoul. As a scared human who had been forcefully blood-bound to a vampire, and was looking for a way out. She jotted the idea on her pad. It had potential. More potential than anything else she'd thought up thus far, that is. She sighed gustily, and turned back to the security cameras. For several weeks now, she had been watching the comings and goings of Joseph Sinclair, trying to understand his rhythms. He was a fairly punctual person, arriving at the Society headquarters around seven each morning, taking a break from eleven to twelve, and often staying late hours, up until nine in the evening some nights, later when there were Society meetings or gatherings, which was often. He didn't seem to really be the type to live in the body- he wasn't physically strong, but very pallid and sickly looking, and obviously not aware at all of the fact that someone stronger than him could have easily snapped him in half like a little twig. Then again, why should he have been so cautious, living in the situation that he did? He was practically a pampered pet. For doing Longinus' dirty work he had received in return a very comfortable existence, in which he never had to worry about any of his needs being met, or even his security. His job was to sit behind a desk and do Longinus' thinking for him. Longinus would come up with a plan, but it would always be Joseph Sinclair who came up with its practical execution. Longinus was the idea man, Joseph the man on the streets, so to speak. Longinus, it seemed, was a rare presence. Either that, or the security cameras didn't pick him up, which seemed impossible for a being of his power, unless they were specially tuned to leave him out. She supposed that was possible- something to ask Aine about, at least. She scribbled another note. She had never really enjoyed doing the requisite research that went with a job like this. Collecting information was always the worst part of the whole thing, because there was so much anticipation involved. Lia had always had a want, after receiving an assignment, to want to go ahead and take care of it as soon as possible. But rash action would always lead to deeper consequences- and in this case, consequences that would see her killed. She wasn't particularly ready for that just yet, so she had to plan carefully- more carefully than for any other job she had accepted. Even killing Lucien Forlani wasn't this big of a deal; he'd been a Camarilla Justicar over London, but that was small change compared to being a paragon's hand. She'd had a conversation with Aine recently- Aine had become really the only person in the Order that Lia felt like she could talk to honestly about the situation. “It's not that I doubt my abilities, Aine. I'm well aware that Ian wouldn't have asked me to take this one on if he hadn't the confidence that I could accomplish the goal. It's just a little bit nerve-wracking, knowing that there is no option for failure, or even the tiniest mistake in this.” Aine didn't look up from her desk. “Tha's somet'in yeh've known since yeh accepted the job.” “Of course... but he's asking me to kill a paragon's hand. That's tremendous... immense. The biggest job I've ever had to do, without even thinking about the repercussions. I mean... usually, I get the job done, and I'm out of there, and any repercussions are not my concern. This time, I know that the repercussions are going to be very much my concern, and your concern... it will affect a lot of people that I care about.” “No sense in thinkin' about wha's gonna happen before it does. Ye know the consequence, so just put 'em out of yer mind until the time comes to worry about 'em. As far as killin' a paragon's hand goes...” Aine slipped her slim-framed reading glasses down on her nose and looked at Lia almost sternly, as though she couldn't really believe they were having this conversation. “When did ya forget tha' a paragon was after ye to be his consort” Na' even his servant or his hand, but his consort?” That gave her pause for a moment. Reconsidering Soma's offer, she was almost incited to shuddering- not to mention thanking her lucky stars once again that she hadn't accepted. A small, tense smile came over her features- it didn't show in her eyes. “I suppose you're right. It's just hard to keep in mind in the midst of such overwhelming information right now.” “Ye better keep it in mind... ye're goin' ta need all yer strengt' to get t'rew dis.” Lia didn't need much reminding about that. It was as though in thinking about Aine, Lia had dredged up her presence. The small cell phone that she had bought barely a month ago, a slim updated version of her old one, was buzzing across the desk in the special ringtone that Lia had reserved for calls from Aine's division of the Order. “Hello?” “Good afternoon, Captain Ambrosi.” “Aine... how are you?” “Not bad meself, but there's na a whole lot of time fer personal discussions today. And how are ya then?” “Doing alright, I suppose... I think if I stare at this security camera footage for any longer, I'm going to go insane.” “Save tha' for after ye've got tha job done, at least. T'ink about it this way... once ye know what yer lookin' fer wit' Sinclair, ye can go on an' get it taken care of. Then ye'll never have to see a security camera again. Hopefully, at least.” “No kidding... Got some news for me?” It wasn't like Aine to call just to chat. Much as Lia wished some days that she might have, this was more than likely about business, and suddenly, her heart felt rather heavy. She was a little mad at herself for feeling this way lately- after all, what did this job have to do with heart? It was cold efficiency that Ian Childress needed from her, not a weepy high-schooler who couldn't do anything without a friend's approval. “I do, at tha'. You remember those coded documents tha' you sent me a week er two ago?” “Yes...” She heard a couple of percussive clicks and taps in the background. “Well, they should be on their way to yis in a coupla seconds. The teams workin' on 'em finally managed to get the code cracked, an' it took awhile because we could'na let anybody see tha whole of tha message, or they might've figured out wha' they were about. I don' know about yis, bu' I'm not too interested in meetin' any of Ian Childress's special agents.” Lia's eyes widened. “No... definitely not.” “As is, he may order a mem'ry wipe on 'em, but tha's neither here ner there. Log ou' of your security camera access an' check yer Order account.” Lia logged out carefully, using the non-intrusive procedures that they had taught her early on so that nobody in the Society of Leopold would think anything of her signing in. They had already stolen a login for her, one from a low-down Society member that had been kidnapped by the Order some time ago for informational purposes. It hadn't been too hard to convince him to write an e-mail to his superiors and let them know that he had only had to run off home for a family emergency. A little chastising for the impulsiveness, and he was back into the program- and living in a new apartment, set up for him by none other than Ian Childress. And he was under orders that if anybody from the Society ever came to him, he was to keep his mouth shut, on pain of something much worse than death. That tended to set people pretty straight, she thought to herself as the logout confirmation popped up. Her IP address was matched to his new one so that nobody could tell the difference. Lia didn't know how they did that, but she didn't bother to ask, either. She logged into her Order e-mail, and saw that several messages from Aine’all with attachments’waited patiently in her inbox. “Looks like I've got them,” she said, clicking to open the first one and download it. “Tha' should keep you busy for awhile’tha rest o'today, at least,” she joked, with a smile in her voice, “Now have ye go' anythin' else fer me?” Lia paused for a moment, looking down at her notes. “A couple of questions, actually.” “Fire away, Captain.” “Well, the identity thing has been a problem for me... of course it's of the most importance that I don't get caught doing this, and the appearance changing won't be a problem, of course. There are a couple of things that concern me about it, though.” “Wha's that?” “Well, number one being the role that I'll need to play. I mean, I think I'll do fine playing it, but I had this idea... what if I was to play the role of a ghoul that is blood-bound and terrified, and feels like the only way out is to rat out her sire to the Society of Leopold” I can come up with a story for that, easily enough, and there's only one problem with it.” “Tha's an idea that could work... wha's the problem?” “How to change my aura. You know yourself that their equipment reads auras fairly closely to the natural state, and if they see mine- dark as it is, with diablerie running through it as well... I'll sell myself out, and be in a lot of trouble.” “Hmmm.... good point.” Aine appeared to be musing for a moment on what exactly could be done about this, but nothing came to mind on the fly. Lia went on. “I was wondering if the Order had any technology that could mask auras, or possibly change them into something else.” There was silence from Aine's end of the phone for a moment. “I suppose we could... possibly have some kind of color diffuser for it, but we'd 'ave to figure ou' 'ow ye could wear it wi'ou' gettin' caught.” “It's the only thing I could think of.” “No, no, I don' think it'll be a problem. I think ye're right, it'll probably be the only way to do i'. We can work on somethin' here in the lab, an' get you hooked up with Ukyo to run tests on it when it's finished.” Something in Lia's heart leaped with joy. Seeing Ukyo again would be worth testing the stupid device. “Great,” she practically shouted, then trailed off, trying not to sound too eager, “...let's see, there were some other things...” She looked at her note again, and paused. “Ah. One other thing... after watching the camera in Sinclair's office for some time, I notice that HE never appears,” she said with emphasis, obviously referring to Longinus, though it was always a bad idea to audibly call a paragon by his name, “I know he has to be there at some point, but you never see any anomalies on the camera, never any particularly strong or strange-looking auras... is it possible they have the cameras tuned to filter him out so that the lower-downs don't know when he's there?” “Good on, Captain Ambrosi, tha's exactly what they've done. Ian had me not tellin' ye, to test yer powers of deduction- I suppose he'll be pleased t' know that ye passed with flyin' colors.” Something about that aggravated her slightly. “What we do know about his movements is tha' he appears about roughly every one and a half weeks. We can usually tell by how often they've got their Elite Guard outside the place, which is pageantry tha' they usually only reserve for him.” “Good, good... so there's a good possibility I could catch Sinclair without him being there?” “Yeah, but don't count on it. Ian wants you to prepare as if he well be there the night you go, an' take it from there.” Sensible thinking on the part of the man known as Nephilim, as always. “Also, Aine... I've been reading up on my mythology sources, but I recalled something else recently...” “Wha's that?” “There are rumors in Cainite lore... I always thought it was very old, but it has resurfaced in modernity among some... that he was... well, one of only two or three vampires in history who was created without a clan, or any ties to one. Think that holds any weight?” “Hmmm...” Lia could hear Aine shifting papers around as she thought. “I suppose it's possible. Doesn't seem too likely, but it's a possibility. Somethin' tha' I'll look into an' report back to you next time we talk, yeah?” “Sounds good to me.” “Alright... I think I've kept ye long enough, and ye've certainly got yer work cut out for ye, digestin' all those borin' meetin' files. Enjoy.” “Thanks, Aine,” Lia replied somewhat sarcastically. “Anytime, Captain Ambrosi.” The line went dead. Aine Leanan was never really one for drawn-out goodbyes. Lia looked at her cell phone for a moment- the battery indicator was still on the fully charged level, which was somewhat disappointing for her, considering she would have loved an excuse to not talk to anyone else today, as sad as that was. She checked the time. Virgil, who tended to work like clockwork, wouldn't be up for another hour, at least. She had always risen earlier than him, but it had been a job lately to conceal just how much earlier she was rising- it would have aroused suspicion. When she knew he was going to get up soon, she always set a book nearby so that when she heard him, she could conceal everything she was doing, and pick up the book like she'd only gotten up early to read. That was one thing that she sincerely hated about all this, trying to pretend that nothing was going on to Virgil, who deserved to know that something, in fact, was going on, if only because that something could very easily result in her death if it wasn't carried out properly. It had been agony enough to be separated by it the first time’she couldn't imagine what would happen to Virgil or herself, for that matter, if it were final. She chewed on the inside of her lip, biting down so hard that she almost yelped in pain. Pushing the thought from her mind, she downloaded the rest of the files Aine had sent her. She was about to open up the first one and start reading, when her phone rang again’an elaborate fugue that said to her, in no uncertain terms, 'you'd better pick up this damn phone.' “Hello?” “Captain Ambrosi.” She already knew who it was, but her heart sank, hearing his voice. “Good morning, Commander Childress.” She tried to force some cheeriness into her voice, or at least some energy, some respect. She didn't think it was working very well. “Pardon me for interrupting you, when I know Aine has already sent you some things to look over for the day, I just wanted to call and see how you were doing with the assignment, since I haven't heard from you in awhile.” She wanted, very much, to go off on him, on a tirade about how it wasn't fair that she was practically the only one left in the entire KB:DI that was doing anything. The only one that was expected to undertake such a momentous task for him and at the same time maintain her commitments to GCW 'to keep up appearances.' Somebody would have known something was up if they noticed her shirking her duties as Champion when she had fought so hard to get to that point. But the truth was, she was exhausted. The other members of the KB:DI had dropped off the face of the earth in the past few weeks, and none of them were burdened with the kind of burden that she had. And it wasn't fair, she didn't have the energy to keep up the charade. G. Ian Childress would not have appreciated her carping. The words died on her lips. She sighed. “I'm bearing up well. Getting a little antsy because I haven't been able to move yet, but I always feel that way about an assignment.” It was only part of the truth- the real reason she was antsy was because she wanted her commitment out of the way. Even though that was so much of a lie to herself’after she offed Joseph Sinclair, the commitment to the KB and the Order would only become bigger. “You sound a little stressed.” “I am,” she said almost immediately. There was no point in lying to her commander, as he would have found that more aggravating than the truth. “I hope that's just a residual effect of not having a plan nailed down yet.” The implication was clear enough. Get your shit together. It annoyed her. “I'm a professional, with all due respect, Commander. I don't intend to make any mistakes that will reflect poorly on, or endanger the Order or any of its members.” “Good,” Childress answered coolly, “That's the sort of thing I need to hear out of you. Keep your head where it needs to be, Captain Ambrosi. You can blow off steam when this is all over.” “I know.” There was a brief silence, and Lia felt almost like they were enemy combatants, snarling at each other over an imagined dividing line. “I'll speak with you soon. Keep your schedule free.” 'Tall order,' she wanted to say, 'since I actually have to go in to my job at GCW, and you evidently have decided that you do not.' But she didn't say that at all. “I will, Commander.” “I'll talk to you later.” The line went dead without him waiting for her to say goodbye. For some reason, she almost wanted to cry when he hung up the phone. Because all of this stress was starting to build up to the point where she felt like she was going to go insane. It had always been hard for her to live up to the expectations of others. She tended to take those expectations seriously, but had a firm record of crashing and burning when the load got too heavy. You didn't have to look too far to find examples, and in this case, the thought was particularly alarming, because there was no room for error, not within her capacity in the Order, and not in GCW. She was torn between two conflicting responsibilities. They both demanded all of her attention- if she was to keep her own personal achievements, she needed to concentrate wholly on GCW, and defending her title against Bryan Mayhem, who would no doubt take most, if not all, of her resources to defeat. But she was also being asked to subsume herself in the desires and needs of the Order. Ian Childress had done her a favor, and she owed him a payback. Under the code of a boon, he could ask for that payback whenever he wanted, and she would not be able to refuse, or risk losing her reputation, which meant everything in this world. She felt like it was very unfair of Commander Childress to ask for his boon when he did. No sooner had he allowed her to experience personal success than he wanted her to put it all down and lay herself literally on the line for him. There was no middle ground, no time to enjoy what she had earned (or, what he had helped her earn, as he would have more likely seen it). One way or the other, she felt like she might break- she'd either slip up on planning this thing with Sinclair, which could and would be fatal. Or she'd slip up against Mayhem, which would be a blow to the ego, to the pride- a blow which she didn't know if she could get up from, being once again without any encouragement whatsoever. Without anybody watching who cared about her. She knew what Ian Childress would say if she screwed up against Mayhem. Oh well. I can help you win it, but I can't help you keep it. I gave you the tools, but you didn't use them. Now you've got more time to focus on Sinclair. Maybe he was right. Her hand balled up into a fist, and resentment welled inside her chest. There were times when she could feel herself almost outright hating Commander Childress for putting her in this position. She had no doubts as to whether or not he knew it. What she really wanted right now was just to forget about all of this. She wanted to enjoy some time with Virgil in their new house’which she hadn't had the time to really enjoy yet. She wanted to go wake him up and sit with him on the porch and watch the sunrise (something they'd really gotten an affinity for since it had ceased to be a danger). She wanted to help Virgil teach Rune the things that he needed to know, and she wanted to help with the little twins that Leandra had sired that were now under the care of the two of them. She wanted to enjoy what she had right now’which was the closest she could possibly get to a real family’and she wanted to enjoy it knowing that she might never have it again after her business with Sinclair was done. She wanted to be able to sit down and read the journals that Laszlo was finally able to get from the convent that her sister Anezka had written before she died. She wanted to be able to absorb herself in that family experience, and understanding what the world was like through Anzha's eyes. But instead, she was sitting down to read the meeting logs of the Society of Leopold’who would have very much liked to eradicate her and all of her kind, if it were possible to do such a thing. That was when the realization came crashing down on her, from what felt like a mountainous height. All these expectations, responsibilities, the fact that her own feelings on the matter weren't even considered, like she was just some sort of automaton who would do one thing or another, depending on who pulled her strings. This wasn't about her. She didn't matter. It cut deep. But there was no time for it to- she heard Virgil in the kitchen, and wondered to herself how long he had been up, and how she had missed him. Hurriedly, she stuffed her papers back in the drawer with the false bottom so that only the laptop remained on the table. She reached over to the shelf beside the desk and picked up the same book that she'd been pretending to make progress through for the past few weeks, and pasted on a neutral expression. With any luck, it completely masked the way she felt inside right now. If it didn't’well, there was no 'if it didn't.' It would have to. She steeled herself.
