Orchestra of Wolves, Part 4: Lonesome Traveller

19 July 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 19th July Beneath a sublime azure blue sky punctuated by wispy puffs of cotton wool cloud, Andy cupped his hand round his phone's plastic edge so that he may block out the sun's resplendent rays and see what was on the screen. Glided his thumb across the plastic surface and found the “Missed Calls” list on the third attempt. Groaned when he saw her name beaming accusations at him from halfway across the country. “Kayla”. She'd called four times and not once had he answered. Probably wasn't doing himself any favours; deciding to take a second full-time Just Wrestling tour on-top of his GCW commitments had caused him more than enough bother with his fiancée lately, and she wasn't exactly sitting on a silver-lined cloud at the moment. He'd pick-up eventually; initial conversation might be a little awkward, sure, but they'd get over it – they always did. It'd be fine. Maybe she'd be a little annoyed at first, but he'd talk her round. She knew what his career was like; she'd accept the yarn he'd spin about how busy he was with Just stuff and how his negligence had nothing to do with the fact that he just didn't want to deal with a grieving girlfriend at that moment. Kayla would accept it, yes; even if he wasn't so sure of himself. Andy heard the crunch of Timberlands on gravel to his left. Pushing his body away from the garage's wall, he saw his younger brother slowly pacing towards him, head hung low like a dog who'd failed his master. Andy's brow raised. “Well, what's the damage?” Huffing and puffing, shaking his shaven-head, Cayle stopped dead. “Ain't good. Radiator's gone; reckon it's gonna be tomorrow before they can get the thing roadworthy again. Sucks, man; was kinda hoping I'd get to hit the swell down at Steamer Lane before sundown.” “See, this is why I don't like letting you drive...” “Awww come on. I didn't do too bad a job--” “... no, but your car's a useless piece of shit.” Andy smiled. Clenched a fist and whacked it playfully into his brother's arm. “Cheer up, Bubbles. Shippers doesn't need me there 'til tomorrow anyway. We can lose a day.” “Hmph,” Cayle grunted. Looked up, scanned his surroundings. Nothing much doing; just a small stop-off settlement with a few grey buildings dotted around the roadside Sounds of mechanics at work and roaring engines from the freeway, the tang of old motor-oil stinging the inside of his nostrils. “Well shit. Little liquid refreshment?” he said, thumbing towards a bar across the road. “Ain't like there's anything else we can do today.” “Alright, but let's check out that motel first,” Andy said, nodding. “Last thing we wanna do's stumble out of a bar at midnight without a place to sleep.” “Heh. Good old Andy; always the sensible one,” said Cayle as he slid his sunshades from his t-shirt's neck and slipped them onto the bridge of his nose. Andy slid his phone into the dark recesses of his back pocket as they made for the overpass. * * * * * [i]Yo, it's me. How you feelin'? Yeah, yeah. I know. Should've called earlier – I'm sorry; just been busy with the whole travelling thing is all. Kinda hard to find phone signal in the middle of the desert, and Cayle's car decided to have a heart-attack on us, so we've been stuck in some dull half-town since four. Gonna leave tomorrow morning. Not exactly ideal, goin' up against a guy like Mags straight off a one-fifty mile journey; just happens sometimes, I guess. Least I got a half-decent reason if I screw up this time. Heh. What's cooking out there in the bluffs? Shit, yeah. Must be hard on the old fella; the kids should be putting their parents in the ground, not the other way around.[/i] * * * * * “You know what I don't get?” Cayle said before cocking his head back and washing his mouth with a refreshing mouthful of Coors Light. “You got everything, man. Championship, money, fame, family. You're about the biggest name in the game right now, and still you're doin' this rinky-dink toilet tour...” “If it weren't for this 'toilet you', you wouldn't be enjoying a nice little trip to Cali, would ya?” Andy said, smiling. “'Sides, you're only jealous 'cause they didn't take you on last tour...” “Huh. What's a guy gotta do to get a second since in this biz?” Cayle shook his head and dropped his gaze. The bar was small; it's wood-panelled walls plastered distastefully with more sports jerseys than either man had seen in their life. A small room of only seven or eight tables and chairs, and yet it was packed like a sardine can. Punters squeezed back and forth, jostling for the attention of the busy bartender, and the jukebox burst cheesy eighties hair metal over the crowd's collective clamour. “Still, man,” Cayle resumed, “seems kinda odd. Guy like you going up against a bunch 'a no-marks.” “'No-marks'?” Andy flashed his brother quizzically. “You seen this bloke I'm facing, Erik Magnum? Dude's a machine, and let's not forget Violence Jack, McKail...” “Alright, alright. I get the point,” responded Cayle before taking a final swig from his bottle before rubbing the condensation from the cool bottle into his old pair of Wranglers. “Just, up and leavin' everything at home behind, like you do. Takes some serious dedication, man. Shit, I'd be dead after three-days goin' at the speed you travel.” “It's just what I do, I suppose.” Andy finished his bottle and slipped a ten in the barkeeper's direction for a repeat order. “Just's a good place; it's a different kinda thrill, wrestling in front of a couple thousand. S'like getting all Thoeau – back to basics, no-bullshit. So my record ain't looking too hot at the moment and Magnum's a former JWC, but it seems like the dude's more concerned with out-foxing the Face-Eater than anything else.” Cayle hiccuped as the barman passed his sixth bottle of the night. They'd been sitting in this sweaty pit of a public house for a couple of hours and he was starting to feel a little more sure of himself than usual. “How's the woman, then?” he asked. “Uh, fine. I guess. Coping.” Cayle nodded. “Poor girl. Don't know how I'd cope if you or Sid bit the dust...” “Just gotta take it in your stride, I suppose,” Andy began, burying his gaze in the bar's rich mahogany surface. “Kinda shitty that I couldn't be there with her, but that's what you give to this career, I guess. It'll get better when we're married...” When he looked-up he saw his brother's attentions were already wavering. Scratching his thin beard, Cayle's lips tightened into a smile as he noticed a group of three young women sitting at a table across the room. “Hey, fuck-o!” Andy yelped. “Damn,” Cayle almost-whispered. He snapped back to reality. “Ohh, uh. What you sayin', bro?” “Fuck it. I gotta take a leak.” * * * * * [i]If it's any consolation, I know how it is. Lost my Pop last year, and my [b]own[/b] brother for a year. Well yeah, I prison ain't the same and you ain't ever gonna get Sean back; I'm just trying to relat -- *sigh* You sure you're alright? Well, you don't sound it. I'm here if you wanna talk about it, you know. No better way of dealing with a loss than a little venting. Come on, you know I wanna make it to the funeral. I just, can't, Kay. Your brother was a good man and I'd love to be there for his send-off, but I can't afford to take any more time-off with Shipley and Caldera breathing down my neck. And then there's the travelling, too; doesn't help that Just and GCW's touring the opposite sides of the country right now... No. No, I ain't just makin' excuses, Kay. I swear.[/i] * * * * * Cayle wasn't there when Andy returned, but he didn't have to look far to find his sibling. There, across the room, trying his best “hey, I'm Andy Murray's brother, you know!” routine with the four ladies in the corner. As he sat down among them, Cayle looked-up; waved his brother over, but Andy responded with a raised palm and a shake of his head. Instead, Andy took his place by the bar. Left Cayle to his own devices. Ordered a glass of Coke. It came to him quickly, and, isolated among groups of road-weary travellers, drunk truckers, and alcohollicaly-lubricated locals, The Scottish King of Cool took a drink. Turned. Looked at the bedraggled, toothless old man who sat next to him. Clad in torn denim jeans and a dusty t-shirt, with a mucky face that hadn't been washed in weeks and an unkempt mop of straggly grey hair, he looked like death itself as he hunched forward on the stool and leaned over the bar, leering at a barmaid. Andy looked away. Wondered what had happened to the man for him to end up the way he had. Looked over to his brother, saw him laughing and joking with strangers. Without him. Remembered his elder brother, stuck at home in Tallahassee, keeping tabs on Andy's twelve year old son. Realised how far he was from home and that Santa Cruz was still some distance down the road. Then he accidental brushed his hand across the bulge of the phone in his pocket, and remembered the wounded woman he'd left alone in the Midwest. Tried to remember a time when he'd felt more alone. Couldn't. * * * * * [i]Well it's pretty tough on me too, ya know? Rumbling down the road for days on end with nobody but a bunch 'a meatheaded wrestlers for company. I'd probably be going insane right now if I hadn't taken Cayle this time. No. I know it's nothing compared to that you're dealing with. Hey now, it's only been a week. We've been apart for longer without seeing each other; just 'cause Sean's dea – I'm sorry, Kay. Shouldn't have said that. Look, I know I ain't exactly been around much lately, and I wish I could've been a stronger should for you to cry on through all this, but things'll get better after this week, just you wait. How long you stayin' at Pop's for? You don't know? Oh. Funerals are tricky, I know. You just take as much time as you need up there. I got seven days left 'til I get a break, then I'ma head straight over to where you're at... Shit, whaddaya you mean by that? Course we're gonna be alright! You think I'd have asked you to marry me if I didn't think you and I were anything less than magic? I'm sorry, what more can I say? Schedule's been crazy, but don't ever think that there's a more important thing in this world to me than you. We alright then?[/i] * * * * * He left the bar and walked the short distance to his motel room. The night's air was clean and crisp – almost cool, nowhere near as damp and muggy as back home in Florida – and it kissed his sweat-damped skin soothingly as he trudged across cracked the tarmac. Climbed the stairs and walked along the balcony, narrowly avoiding the drunken sway of a young couple as he reached his door and slid the key in. Stepped inside, turned on the light. Modest interior; wallpaper with peeling edges and a cream-coloured surface marred by cigarette smoke stains. Dull brown carpet, overstarched bedding, furniture straight out of the seventies. In his closet-sized bedroom, the man who had everything pulled out his phone and dialled his girlfriend's number. * * * * * [i]I'll call you 'fore we head-off tomorrow morning, I promise Okay. Love you.[/i]
