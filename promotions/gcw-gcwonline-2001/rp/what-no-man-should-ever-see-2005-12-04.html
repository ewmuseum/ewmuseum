What No Man Should Ever See

4 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/04/05 December 14th, 2002. 3:37 p.m | Baltimore, Maryland ??????Skylar McGovern sat in his office, his mind only half on the papers in front of him. The Baltimore area criminals were clumsy and blatant. He'd never spent more than a day on a crime scene without finding the missing puzzle piece. From stray bullet casings to bloodstained gloves miles away from the scene, he had seen it all. ???The office's walls were decorated with the various awards Skylar had accumulated over the past three years. Letters from the mayor, plaques celebrating his efficiency; he had it all. None of these letters or awards, however, were his most prized possession. What he prized most bore not a signature, nor was it on paper at all. Detective McGovern had never left a case cold. ???Murder cases came to Skylar by the dozen, but they were solved as quickly as they came. The simplicity of these cases led Sky to one conclusion: murderers are completely and utterly stupid. They were always unorganized in their handlings, leaving clues in the most obvious places. They might as well paint arrows on the pavement. Skylar lived for thieves. There was something fascinating about the concept of theft. The care and precision required for a successful robbery was breathtaking. The calculations necessary to defy the security precautions were all but non-sensical. Skylar could not help but admire the handiwork of the mastermind criminals. ???Most robberies, however, were simple enough to be solved easily by Baltimore's police force. Only once since Sky had joined the agency had a heist been put on his palette. A collection of ancient Byzantine artwork had been stolen from The Walter's Art museum a year and a half back. The artwork was worth upwards of five million dollars. The case took the better part of four months for McGovern to crack, but a stray, unwiped fingerprint ended up giving the felon away. ???Antonio Bernini was arrested in his Victorian household that very day, and was discovered responsible for several prestigious heists along the east coast. ???Skylar received copious amounts of publicity in the months following the closing of his case, but no more heist cases came his way. ???He glanced at the plaque crediting him with the capture of Bernini, and he smiled. It would be one of the last smiles his face would hold for days to come. ???The ringing of the phone broke the silence like a gunshot, pulling Skylar away from his reminiscent state. He let it ring three times before answering it. ???'McGovern.' ???'Hello Detective McGovern,' the voice on the other end was gruff, with a hint of worry in it, 'this is Stephan Farrell, Captain of the New York Police Department, Manhattan District.' ???'I'm listening.' Sky didn't have the slightest idea why the NYPD would be calling him. He hadn't received a call from anywhere further than Richmond, much less New York City. ???'Well,' Farrell continued apprehensively, 'you have a seat booked on a seven o'clock flight to New York City tonight. First class. You're presence has been requested at the scene of the crime.' ???The law enforcement enterprise was massive in New York. Why in the world would they need a twenty-four year old detective from Baltimore in New York City? 'What's the crime?' ???'Double homicide. No clear motive.' ???Great. Another murder case, and this time he'd have to go two hundred miles out of his way. 'I don't mean to seem rude, Captain, but aren't there any other detectives that are more...how do you say...closer?' ???A stifled laugh, followed by a sigh. 'But of course, McGovern. Don't be so naive. There is reason for your presence wish I do not wish to discuss over the phone. Your flight leaves from Dulles International Airport and arrives at LaGuardia. I'll see you in five hours.' ???There was a click, and Skylar sat dumbfounded listening to the dial tone. Hanging up the phone, he reached into his desk and pulled out a calendar. He knew, though, that there was nothing happening for the next few days. The only reason he even came into the office today was a lack of anything better to do. ???The clock read three forty-six, meaning Sky had about an hour and fifteen minutes before he needed to be at the airport. As he was putting the calendar back in his desk, Sky chuckled. He had just realized something Farrell had said. 'Unbelievable,' he thought, 'a New York City police officer has the audacity to call me naive. Those half-wits couldn't find a murderer if he left a trail of blood to his own front door.' ???Sky dismissed the anserine comment, and pulled on a pair of black, leather gloves. He swung his coat on, and stepped outside into Baltimore. The air was frigid. Skylar McGovern couldn't help but wonder what the air was like in New York City. ???8:46 p.m ???Skylar walked out of the terminal into the main plaza at LaGuardia, muttering about Pauly Shore and poor in-flight movies. 'Everything can't be Sherlock Holmes, I suppose.' Skylar thought to himself. ???He was about to head over to a concession stand when somebody tapped him on the shoulder. Skylar wheeled around, coming face to a face with a striking middle-aged man. 'Detective. Stephan Farrell.' The man introduced himself, extending his hand. His raspy voice didn't match his spotless appearance. ???Sky shook Stephan's hand firmly. 'How did you know who I was?' ???Stephan's crystal eyes told of nothing but sadness. 'I've seen a picture.' His voice told nothing more than his eyes. Skylar opened his mouth to question, but Stephan waved him off. 'You'll know later.' ???Fifteen minutes and a sandwich later, Skylar was riding passenger seat in a police car. 'So, are you going to tell me why you flew me two hundred miles for a simple homicide case, when you could have easily contacted a hundred detective here in the city?' ???Stephan Farrell's answer was simple. 'No. I'm sorry, I cannot. You will know in the next hour.' Farrell stopped the car at a red light, turned and looked at Skylar. His crystal blue eyes met Skylar's equally crystalline eyes of green. 'Just brace yourself.' ???9:30 p.m | Manhattan, New York ???Skylar hadn't the slightest idea what to expect as he approached the yellow caution tape. Snow fell heavily, and breath was visible. Stephan had not said a word to Skylar since his ominous warning, and stood back as Skylar stepped into the crime scene. ???All the stores on the street were closed. Two bodies lay in the street, outside of a bar. A fluorescent green sign reading 'Thirsty's' illuminated one of the faces. ???Despite the snow, blood and broken glass mangling the face, Skylar knew immediately whom it belonged to. Upon recognition he turned around and heaved into the snow. ???Luke Glaze had been Skylar's best friend since high school. They went their separate ways when Sky made the decision to join the detective agency, and Luke came to New York to study at NYU. Never had they lost touch though. Luke had been in Baltimore only weeks before to visit. ???The place reeked of whiskey, but McGovern's detective instincts were too muddled to notice the smashed Jack Daniels bottle. He stepped back over the caution tape, tears in his eyes. Stephan Farrell approached Sky, and put a hand on his shoulder. ???'We found this in his wallet.' Farrell slipped something into Sky's hand. It was taken two weeks earlier, during Luke's visit. They were at a Redskins/Cowboys game, and asked a fellow fan to take a picture of the two. The two smiling friends were surrounded, in the picture, by middle fingers of Cowboy fans. ???Sky slipped the picture into his pocket. Who could possibly do such a thing to a man as kind as Luke Glaze? The answer lay only feet from him, but Skylar McGovern had already seen enough.
