Where He's Going

Date: Sept. 9, 2009 I. ___________________________________________ RECALL: PRE-RAMPAGE That night I ended up on a bar stool at a place they call Jack n' Bills down in the Seaside Heights area. It'd been a number of weeks; maybe even months that I'd sat at a bar alone and drank the night away. But I had a lot on my mind - justifiably so. To make things clear, Jack n' Bills wasn't some fancy upscale joint that seemed to be stigmatizing the area. Nah - Jack n' Bills is a shitty looking hole-in-the-wall right on the boardwalk. The floorboards are rotted, the bar top is stained, and you could damn near peel the sheetrock from the walls. But it was exactly what I wanted. Exactly what I'd needed. They got their name from it originally being a place where the owners envisioned you could go to "drink Jack Daniels and play some billiards." If those two pastimes weren't some sort of indication of the clientele you'd see there on a weekday night. Yeah, trash. One hundred percent pure white trash. But it did the job right. Granted, pool was never my cup of tea but I never minded shooting a few balls around. It was the Jack Daniels specials that lured me in after meeting up with Ryan. The mask I was wearing while we spoke was started to disintegrate and I needed an outlet. Preferably somewhere where nobody would see me. The aura J&B's gave off was a somber one. It was the perfect setting for a guy like me to really sit down and be able to reflect on things. The alcohol - well that was merely acting as my psychiatrist. "I'll take a double shot of Jack and a Cuervo Black and Cola," I mentioned to the bartender in his passing. "Rough day?" he asked. I responded with a shrug...and that said it all. "You could say that." As he ventured off to make my drink, I had to wonder - was it really that clear? Did I have depression written all over my face? The shitty part was that I hadn't the slightest clue. I've never been sad or upset. I've never seen nor felt depression. I'd always been so self absorbed with my accomplishments that the mere thought of dissatisfaction made my stomach turn. The bartender slapped two double shots of Jack in front of me and brought my Cuervo Black and Cola over next. "I just wanted one double of Jack, my man," I said hastily. "I know," he confirmed. "The other one's for me." He lifted his glass and toasted it towards me. "Cheers!" he exclaimed as we pounded back our drinks and slammed the glasses to the table. I shuffled in my pants pocket and pulled out my wallet. "Whadda-I owe ya?" "That rounds on me," he said as he walked away. It was at that moment I finally understood the hidden message that Andy Murray and LeStatt Knight were portraying at Worldwide when they shared the night together. I hadn't been in Jack n' Bills for years and most of my regular bartenders from back in the day were long since gone. But this guy made me feel like I hadn't missed a happy hour. My first round and it was on him. For no good reason except that he can relate to how I was feeling. I felt like I'd rediscovered the thought process of the human psyche. Maybe, sometimes, we don't like people because we can't immediately find a common ground with them. Maybe we're just too judgmental on first impressions that we miss out on the opportunity to meet someone who could truly be grand. While I gulped down my Cuervo Black and Cola, it got me thinking - have I been missing out all of these years? Could I have missed out on a better life if I'd just opened myself up a bit beyond stereotypes? I polished off my first drink, slammed the glass to the table and exhaled, "Nahhhhh!" You know when you're drinking; you hit that crossroad between being sober and starting to feel a nice buzz? It's the border between where it's you talking and then it's the alcohol. That's exactly where I was at this point. Surprisingly, it didn't take much for me to really get a good buzz going, nevertheless it was there. How in hell could I ever co-exist with someone like Andy Murray, someone like Jorge Samuelsson, or shit - even someone like...White? I couldn't. Ever. "Another Cuervo Black & Cola?" "And another double shot of Jack," I added. "And get one for yourself - this time on me." The bartender winked, appreciative of my gesture. It was the least I could do for his generosity on the first round. I mean it's not like the money for those drinks were actually coming out of his pocket, but I guess it’s the thought that counts. At least that's what they tell you. "You must've really had a long day, kicking one of those back that quickly," he noted as he brought the second round of drinks over. We raised our glasses, cheered, and drank. "Yeaahh, you could sure as hell say that again." After swiping away our empty shot glasses, he extended his hand, "Name's Jordan." If that wasn't irony at its best. "Chris," I replied with a hint of hesitation. "Pleasure to meet you, Chris." "Yeah, you bet." I was kind of at a loss for words. Here I was just bickering about Andy and LeStatt sharing a few Shirley Temples at Worldwide and I find myself having a drink with some asshole by the same name as the guy who could potentially put an end to my career. It's because of that prick I'm even here, doubting myself, in the first place. But I suppose I had to make some sort of conversation. "How long you been working here, Jor--Jordan." Oh, how it pained me to say that name in friendly company. "I guess it's been about five years now. Started as a way to pay my way through college. Money was good, college was bad - next thing I know I'm still serving drinks." "Money's still good though, I'd imagine?" "Eh," he mustered, "it has its nights. Summer is awesome. Holiday weekends are great. The off season can get a bit cruel, though. Why, you looking for a job?" He said it with a cackle, obviously kidding, but it still hit me awkwardly. The sonuvabitch already ironically shared White's name, but could he possibly be foreshadowing something, too? Too many damn literary devices for my tastes. "Ha," I forced out before taking a swig of my Cuervo. "Let's hope not!" Our conversation was steadily heading down the fast track for nowhere. Don't get me wrong, he was a nice kid - but something about sharing a drink with a guy named Jordan didn't work for me. "Ah boy," he sighed with disdain. "Here comes trouble." I looked over my shoulder and noticed a group of guys in their early twenties circling around one other kid who seemed to be there by himself. I wasn't really paying too much attention to what they were saying, but I did hear a few mentions about a girl. Typical. Always is about that. "These guys are assholes," Jordan confirmed. "They like to muscle around anyone who even looks at the one kid's girl." I smirked. Déjà vu? He must've seen the light go off above my head, "You've seen their kind?" I nodded. "Seen them? I've preyed on them." The whole scenario reminded me of a time long ago when I first encountered a guy who'd ultimately become near and dear to me. The night I met Zacharia Taylor, I came to his aide when a bunch of pricks - just like these guys - were fucking with him because he glanced in the wrong direction. It got me thinking. The bartender stepped away from me for a minute to go clear up any altercations that might occur with the immature, scumbag muscle heads. I took that as my cue to get the fuck out of there. I pounded back the last of my Cuervo Black and Cola and slipped my money underneath the glass. As I walked out, the bartender felt it necessary to bid his farewell, "Nice chatting with you, Chris. I'll be seeing you soon." I dipped my head and jerked my wrist, giving the poorest farewell I think possible. As much as I wanted to hate him, he really wasn't that bad of a kid. Despite having a piss-poor name, he was alright. Hell, he did cover me entirely on my first round. I guess he meant well. But the fact is, as nice of a guy as he might have been - I'm still Chris Bagwell. And I cheaped him out on a tip. II. "If everyone were clothed with integrity, if every heart were just frank, kindly, the other virtues would be well-nigh useless." - Moliere ___________________________________________ LATER THAT NIGHT My problem was that I could not leave. I stood outside that bar for a good five minutes, staring down the road and questioning my reputability. Here I was at a crossroads of good and evil - I'd just let some punks muscle around an innocent kid and I screwed over a guy who'd been kind enough to lend me a listening ear. I wasn't really sure what I wanted to do anymore. I was growing tired of the constant hatred and disdain that was spewed in my direction. It got old - very old. The fact is I could never escape it unless I'd just change my habits; but that's like trying to teach an eighty-five year old man how to utilize the internet. Sure, it can be done but the effort involved is immaculate - almost to the point where it probably isn't even worth it. I glanced at my car parked in the lot across the street. My meter was running low and the Seaside Heights Police force was stalking it out like hawks. The instantaneous second my meter expires, they'd be all over it to write me a ticket. They're vultures - blood thirsty vultures. Yet for some reason, it didn't affect me. I tilted my head to the side and caught the sound of mockery gallivanting through the air. Those punks inside seemed to be growing more and more edgy with each passing minute. It'd only be a matter of time before they'd copycat the same events I saw six years ago. That's when I walked back inside. When I stepped back through the door, I could sense Jordan looking at me. Maybe he was aggravated at the lack of a tip I'd left him - maybe he knew what I was about to get involved with. I'll never know for sure. Two of the muscle heads began shoving the loner kid around, smacking him upside the head. "What's the matter, Ryan? You seem to have a twitch in your neck," the apparent ringleader of the group said to him. The kid Ryan kept his mouth shut and lowered his head - he was outnumbered four-to-one and knew that the slightest move might've cost him a black eye or even a trip to the emergency room. I really had no business walking back in there. This wasn't my fight and I hadn't the slightest clue who this Ryan kid was. But there's one thing I did have and that was a passionate hatred for these orange-skinned Guido fucks that thought they ruled the world. I marched across the bar, grabbing a wooden stool along the way. I could feel Jordan's look growing more intense. With little warning, I shouted "YO COCKSUCKER!" The ring leader of the group turned around anxiously to see who he'd get to fuck up... ...and he ate wood. I wailed the stool up and over my head and shattered the thing atop of his. He dropped to the ground like a sack of potatoes. His friends were stunned at what I'd just done - it took them a second to process everything before they decided they had to get involved. And literally...all I had was one second. I instinctively jerked my arm back, still holding on to a single leg of the chair, and cracked the guy on my right in the chest. My reaction was strong enough to knock the wind out of the guy. I then snapped my arm forward and up, clipping the guy to my left in the shoulder blade. Although the blow was hardly damaging, it gave me another second or two to make a move. Seeing as my right hand was occupied, I threw a left elbow into the guy's nose. Typically, a weak left elbow would hardly be effective - but these were Guidos we're dealing with. The most pain they've ever felt was sticking a steroid needle in their ass. Unfortunately though, my heroism was hardly enough to keep me safe. The fourth guy, who all of a sudden seemed like the biggest of the group, blindsided me in the face. I can't particularly say much of his tactics, as I did the same to his buddies - but I still think he was a pussy. His fist caught me entirely off guard and knocked me back with a stagger. He came right after me and nearly crushed my stomach with a jab. While I was gasping for breath, I decided I was a moron for getting involved with this whole situation. I could've already been well on my way home and minutes away from resting peacefully in my bed. But instead I was here having my stomach punched up into my throat. PPPPPSSHHHHHHHHHTTTTTTTTTTTTT The last time I'd heard the sound of shattering glass was in a Stone Cold Steve Austin documentary, right around the time when he was being prosecuted for beating his wife. Strangely enough I'd never been happier to hear that sound again. Ryan, the loner kid, finally grew the balls to stand up for himself. When the fourth meathead had his chompers set on me, Ryan stood up from his chair and cracked his beer bottle over the guy's head. Needless to say, down went the Jolly Orange Giant. He extended his hand to me, helping to pull me back to my feet. "Thanks," he said graciously. "Really. Thanks a lot." I'd have loved to respond except for the fact that I had no air left in my lungs. I smirked and waived my hand - he knew what I meant. Jordan rushed over to us with three of the bouncers and another one of the bartenders. I was waiting for a war. But the bouncers didn't touch me. They, along with the other bartender, each peeled up a muscle head and escorted him out of the bar. "You alright?" Jordan asked with a sincere concern. I smirked, digging into my pocket. "Yeah," I said back. "I thought you were out of here," he replied. And he was right - I was long gone. I grabbed his hand and stuffed some money in it. "I was," I added. "But I forget to leave your tip." I turned away and walked myself to the door. My head still felt a little light, but nothing I hadn't experienced before. Jordan unwrinkled the money I stuffed in his hand. "Dude, there's $87 here." I stopped at the door, "Sometimes it pays to be warm hearted." With that said, I walked out of the bar. And just in case you were wondering, it wasn't Jordan who made out the best that night. Remember the girl that was with the Guidos? She ended up leaving with Ryan. III. "In the best institutions, promises are kept, no matter what the cost in agony and overtime." - David Ogilvy ___________________________________________ NORTHERN OHIO, PRESENT DAY I hadn't had the opportunity to sit down and relax for some time now. I knew when joining the company that I'd be on the road a lot and wouldn't necessarily have all the time in the world to myself but it was only recently that things really started to get hectic. Typically, I'd at least get a day or two on each tour just to cut back and relax - enjoy the cityscape and sip on a vodka sprite. But things had changed. Becoming the poster boy for the Establishment wasn't exactly all it cracked up to be. Sure, there were a ton of amenities that came along with the job but there was also sacrifice. You'd be surprised at how much Steven demands once he buys your soul. Regardless, the night was mine to spend wasting away. Fisher and DeBough were at an all you can eat TeeterTot buffet and Rebecca didn't bother to come along for the trip. She'd been excited to decorate our new home in South New Jersey, right along the beach. I never really understood girls and why they get so yippidy-skippidy over things as inane as decorating. To me, it'd be a week of hell and headaches but to her it was like week straight orgasm. If only I got that same feeling. The night is mine. KNOCK, KNOCK Was mine. "Ughaa." I couldn't help but grunt to myself. I so desperately didn't want to answer the door but I knew it was going to be someone from the hotel here to tell me Tyson fucked up somehow. He's like a child and needs adult supervision damn near constantly. I slugged across the room and flicked the lock hoping in some way I would hurt it. Immediately, I began cycling through excuses as to why I couldn't go downstairs and help Tyson - even before I opened the door. Pity, right? "Hi Chris..." Tyson proved me wrong - it wasn't someone coming up to bitch about him. Standing before me was a man I hadn't seen in almost a year. A man who, the last time I saw him, was lying on the floor because I just decked him across the face. "...Shane?" Shane was to credit for my entire career. He took me in from an independent promotion and gave me my big break in Ultimate Championship Wrestling as a singles wrestler. He showed me the ropes you couldn't ever learn by just wrestling around with high school kids who had high hopes to one day be a sensation. Shane befriended me and quite literally paved the way for my success. Frankly, there wouldn't be a Chris Bagwell without a Shane Skwarek. "Wh-what are you doing here?" I felt stupid. Real stupid. I was looking into the eyes of a man who'd done so much for me in my life whom I'd turned my back against...and I was standing there in a bath robe. "I was in the area," he answered. "I heard you were in town and just wanted to stop by. Maybe play catch up." "You just so happened to be in Northern Ohio?" I quizzed him. I knew there wasn't a chance he'd coincidentally be out this way. It just wasn't Shane. He'd grown up on the East Coast, as I did, and would absolutely nuts if he had to spend a week out here. People just generally moved a lot slower out here than they do over in our neck of the country. A lot slower. Hell, I was driving down the interstate the other day and could've sworn I saw a traffic sign that read 5 MPH. So unless they've got some other definition for the acronym MPH... "I just don't buy that." Shane smirked and shook his head. He knew I wasn't that gullible. "You goin' to invite me in or do you enjoy free-ballin' it out in the middle of the hallway?" I motioned my hand and shut the door quickly behind him. "Beverage?" I offered. "Sure. You have a bottle of water?" I moseyed over to the in-room bar the hotel so graciously provided (as per Steven's request, I might add) and prepped two pints full of ice. Then I confirmed, "And by water you mean...a light vodka sprite?" Shane found his way around my room and made himself comfortable on the sofa, pushing aside my night's entertainment. "Hustler?" I never said what kind of entertainment that was. "Seriously Chris, you're still reading these kiddie magazines?" I poured him a vodka sprite anyway - just out of spite. "It came with the room," I mumbled. A blatant lie. "I'll bet it did." "So," I went on as I brought our drinks to the coffee table in front of couch. "Honestly...what are you doing here?" I was genuinely curious. I wouldn't have come and seen a guy who knocked me in the face. But that was also the difference between Shane and I - I had far too much pride. "I'm here because we were once close friends," he assured. "And despite some things that may have happened in the recent past, I still consider our friendship to be strong." I stared at Shane with a blank look. A sip of my vodka sprite helped break the tension. "I understand why you did what you did, Chris. And although in my eyes, I was doing the right thing I realize now that it may have not been perceived that way." The reason this all began was because Rebecca had gone missing for a number of years and Shane had always been at my side for the search...from the beginning. He'd talked me through the hard times and continuously promised me hope. And I bought it - I bought all of it. I found out last year - around late Septemberish - that he'd also been behind her disappearance. Maybe not at first, but he certainly knew of it for quite some time. He'd helped her stay hidden away in Odessa, Texas for half a decade. In retrospect, he was doing the right thing. Rebecca's family was murdered in the late 90's in a mob-related incident. Her father had borrowed money to boost his business from the wrong person and the terms of the deal were a bit skewed. Shane and Rebecca's best friend, Abigail, helped her hide to avoid suffering the same fate. They never told me, however, and that's what pissed me off. I had spent the majority of nights during those seven years with tears in my eyes before I'd fall asleep. I was miserable and just generally a train wreck. I guess that's what hurt the most - the fact that Shane saw me in so much pain and continued to hide it from me. Until, of course, I found her. "I just wanted to say to you that I'm sorry," he said with a very remorseful tone. Shane wasn't a bad guy...not at all. And that's partly why I believed him. He was often shadowed by a looming reputation he acquired from the business but behind all the glamour, he was a kind, just man. So I raised my glass to him. "You know I'm not too up on all the sappy shit," I said. "So cheers." With a smirk on his face, he complied and took a nice swig of his "water." Spitting some of it back up, he said, "What the hell is this?" Apparently he still thought I actually poured him a glass of water. "Poland Spring,” I said with a hint of sarcasm. "I don't know what spring this came from but it sure as hell wasn't Poland's." We shared a laugh and another swig of our drinks before I decided to be a bit more intuitive, “Why'd you do it?" I'm sure he had a pretty good idea what I was getting at, but just to be sure he asked, "What?" "Hide her for so long. I just want to know...from you." Shane tilted his head back and poured the rest of the glass down his throat; his eyes winced and his body shuttered. I guess it was his way of getting ready. "It was just to protect her. We weren't sure if they were watching you too so we decided it best not to tell you. We figured if you knew she was still alive and okay, you'd be less likely to pull a convincing act." "I never was much of an actor," I added. "Truth is, it was our intension to reunite you two right around the time your father died. But I thought hitting you with such a wide mix of emotion could do far more damage than it would good." He was right. I don't really know how I'd react if I saw Rebecca right after my father died. I was extremely close with my father and never expected to lose him so early on in life. I was a wreck when he was taken from me - it was my first true loss. If I'd seen that Rebecca was alive right after, I quite literally may have had a heart attack. Although I'm sure some people would've preferred that. "I knew you needed time to come to your own," he continued. "So Abby and I decided to wait until the time seemed right. Ironically enough, it's that same thinking that brought me here today." I started to feel a little bit of guilt. Here Shane was, coming out of his way to make amends and I'm sitting here letting him take the blame for everything. Literally everything. "Well this time, maybe it was me that should've done the groveling," I said. He smiled and raised his empty glass, "You better hurry up. I'm getting a bit parched over here." I let my ego kick back in for a second long enough to pound back the remainder of my drink. Taking the empty glasses, I made way back to the bar to pour us each another cocktail. Those five or six steps felt like a mile. I got to thinking - Shane's timing could've have been any more perfect. He'd always been there for every big moment in my life and this wasn't going to be any different. Shane was standing at my side when I won my first championship, even though nobody really cared for me. And here he was again...at my side. It really made me weigh the possibilities - was this really just pure coincidence? Or did he purposely come here knowing he needed to be here--err, rather I needed him to be here before I fought Andy? And then he answered my question... "I guess the real reason I'm here is because of a promise I made to you a long time ago. And in the best institutions, promises are kept," he said. "They're kept no matter what the cost in agony." Not necessarily in the sense of a wrestling superstar but more so a celebrity superstar. I wished to be on television, perhaps as a star football player, and to draw attention no matter where I went. I'd always dreamed of having children look up to me as I looked up to some of the favorites from my childhood. My first big break in the wrestling business came in the year 1997. I'd just finished up a two and a half year stint in an independent circuit with a guy named Dukey Booty, when The Duke decided to shut down his promotion. At first, I was a bit disgruntled. The Duke was the reason I had gotten involved with the business in the first place and here he was just up and leaving me on the streets. As it turns out, Dukey had planned to merge resources with a friend of his and soon thereafter pitched me the idea to come along with him. The place was Ultimate Championship Wrestling. The UcW was established a year prior by a man who called himself Double §, and his friend Boozie. By this time, Double § had just bought Boozie out to become the sole owner of the company and negotiated a deal with Dukey to come on board his executive booking committee. Up until this point, I was what you'd call a face in the wrestling business. I was always the good guy who got all the big pops (if you could even call it that with an audience of 150-200 people). But in Ultimate Championship Wrestling, things were on the verge of changing. "I'm going to need you to attack Dementia this week. And try to make a statement." Double §, aside from being the owner of the company, was also a participating superstars. But behind the curtain, we all knew him by the name Shane Skwarek. When acquiring part of Dukey's roster from OBW, Shane had a number of important decisions to make as far as booking guys. One was making me a bad guy. I asked, "is Dementia one of your heels?" But the answer wasn't anything I wanted to hear. "No," Shane told me," Dementia is one of the members of the City of Angels, our most popular group to date. I want you to command a group opposite them." "I'm not really sure I'm the right fit for that," I pleaded. But Shane wanted to hear nothing of that sort. "You're going to have to be. You can't wrestle your entire career as a one dimensional character. This isn't a tri-state circuit anymore - we're going national." "Would it maybe be possible to work me in as a face first, though? It's something I'm familiar with," I went on. "It's easier to make people hate a new guy than it is to make them like one," he claimed - and I suppose he made a good point. The only thing that led me to becoming so popular with Dukey's organization is that I stood across the ring from guys that people hated more than me. It didn't necessary mean they liked me - it just meant they hated them. "Besides," added Shane. "Your microphone skills are sub-par. You'll have to work on those before you even get close to becoming the baby face of this company." My exchanges with Shane were anything but inviting. He seemed to only feed me criticism and commands and never really gave off much of a friendly vibe - he was the polar opposite of what I learned to live with, in Dukey Booty. Nevertheless, my decisions were made for me and I had no other choice but to listen to my new boss. As I developed in Ultimate Championship Wrestling, I began to get the hang of playing the bad guy. My altercations with Dementia brought in his City of Angels stablemates, Boozie (the former co-owner) and Vypr, to help out. Not before long, I was faced with a three-on-one. And so was born The Organization of Power. Wrestling had went down a road where stables reigned supreme. With the ever-growing popularity of the City of Angels, it was only logical that I benchmarked their opposition. Shane had signed a number of superstars and celebrities onto the UcW roster and thought it best to group a few of them with me. I was handed my new two best friends on a spoon and expected to like them - whether I liked it or not. I got my first lick of leadership when I was introduced to Tommy Gunn and Rolf Hannibal. And for a guy who spent his whole life on his own, I think I did pretty damn well. At that point in time, it was the worst thing that could've happening to me. But looking back, I realize that I've always been better surrounded by a group of people. The Organization of Power developed and I was hungry to acquire more members. In three months, I tripled our numbers and I was in the fast lane to my first World Championship. My ego developed. I started to live my character and people didn't like it. For a guy so egotistical, one could only wonder how I functioned so well in a team environment. Maybe secretly I'm insecure. Maybe I need people following me so I feel important - so I feel like I matter. Just at the cusp of my breaking out, I sat down with Shane once more. Boozie, one of his closest friends, and a number of guys in the locker room started to grow a fond distaste for me. Shane and I talked about it as I was worried about my future. I didn't want to piss anyone off. I didn't want to end up without a job. That's when Shane gave me a piece of advice I'd not soon forget. "You have enemies?" he asked. "Good. That means you've stood up for something, sometime in your life." The only problem was - Shane was the only one who knew what I was standing up for. At least...at that point. V. Shane's eyes had grown droopy and his walk became more like a stagger. What was supposed to be a friendly conversation over a single glass of water had evolved into one too many glasses of vodka. "Uno mas," I encouraged. But his lips were sealed. He waived his hand, motioning to me that he'd had enough, as he stumbled towards the bathroom. Needless to say, the door shut promptly behind him. It might not have been the way I had expected to spend the night, but it sure beat beating off to a porno magazine. Seeing Shane again really opened my eyes. Before I had Rebecca back, I was a miserable fuck. I hated everyone and shut out just about everyone who tried to get close to me. I always knew, in my head, that finding Rebecca would make that all better. And that was exactly the problem - your head is not your heart. Your head evaluates situations logically. It causes you to make decisions based on what you think is right or what seems to have the least amount of risk involved. If you follow your head, you're living a safe, predictable life. You'll never experience true happiness because you're too afraid to succomb to sadness. Life is about risk. You can't steal second with your foot on first. When you follow your heart, you're putting it all on the line with the glimmer of hope that you may one day find true bliss. You may hurt yourself along the way...but that's the beauty of the ride. Most of my heart lies in Rebecca's hands - but there's still a part of it that she doesn't have. It's a part that allows me still to live. When Rebecca and I were reunited in Odessa and fled, I left that small piece of my heart behind. I followed my head because I knew I'd be happier once she and I were together. But what I didn't know was that losing my closest friend would still leave me un-whole. You ever been in a relationship or know of one where either the boyfriend or the girlfriend don't let their significant other hang out with their friends? When the couple spends all their time together and can't even sacrifice a second alone? How many of those have you seen work? Rebecca wasn't forcing me away from my friends - it's just that I only really ever had one. That one friend happened to be the same guy I punched in the face and ran out on. When I opened my door earlier I was overcome with emotions, each of which have dissipated - each except for one. For once, I finally felt whole again. "Maannn, I keep-uh forgettin' I haven't drinked in forever. For-eva-eva!" Suffice to say, Shane's speech wasn't improving. He meandered back over to the couch and plopped down like a sack of potatoes, arms sprawled and head tilted back. "Always knew you were a lightweight," I joked. "Damn do I wish I had a video camera right now!" He responded with the middle finger - whatever that means. "Yeaah I'll betcha do. I'll kill you!" "So I guess that means no uno mas for you?" Once again, he waived his hand. That might have been the last drink for Shane, but it certainly wasn't the last for me. I made my way back over to the bar for number nine...or maybe ten? "You-uh ever wonder like...what it'd be like-uh if you won'd last Warpath," Shane asked with a steady slur. "I can't say it hasn't crossed my mind," I said with regret. "Come on-uh. Be real with me," he persisted. "I mean of course," I hesitated. It'd been a topic I never really brought up because I was always so ashamed of the results. "Andy and I had both grown exponentially and at the same rate up until that point. My Rampage loss was overlooked once I defeated him at High Stakes and so everything was riding on the line at Warpath." I took a nice, big sip of my fresh vodka sprite before continuing. "He built up so much momentum after that win - I attribute all of it to his success thereafter. His feud with LeStatt Knight, his dealings with The Terror-led Establishment, and eventually his World Title match. I think it all can be related back to that one win at Warpath." On the surface, none of my points really made any sense. But looking deeper - I had a really valid point. I came into GCW fists-flying and immediately called out the top dog, LeStatt Knight. Knight and I had a number of verbal exchanges and my intentions were always to use those to get myself a high profiled match. A match that never happened. A match that Andy Murray eventually got. I then picked a fight with The Establishment, President Caldera's golden child. I didn't necessarily do it directly, but all the signs were there. I mean for Christ's sake, I started a group that was called The Movement. How do you break the foundation of an Establishment? You move it. Everything I'd done in GCW was a carefully thought-out plot. Yet none of my actions ever converted - nothing ever came to play. As a matter of fact, everything I'd strived for...Andy got. I made radio show appearances...he became the radio show co-host. I was living under his shadow and had been since losing at Warpath 2008. Even when we're on opposite sides of the world, I'm always being compared to Andy Murray. Our accomplishments are always been matched and my career is - and probably always will be - taken under a microscope. I thought about this every day. Every second of every day. I was tired of being the Bert to Andy's Ernie; the Garfunkel to his Simon. I wanted to break out - I wanted to stand free. I wanted to be the Fergie without the Black Eyed Peas; the John Lennon without the Beatles. I was concerned about my future. What would another big loss to Andy Murray do to me? What would it do to my career? I was concerned and it finally started to show on my face. "In times like these," Shane added. "It always helps to recall that there have always been times like these." His words - unslurred words, I might add - were a bit philosophical for me. I wasn't really grasping where he was going with them, either. He knew that. He tossed the previous day's newspaper in my direction - the sports section to be exact. Perhaps it was irony or just mere coincidence, but the headline read... "Top seed Andy Murray loses in a devastating upset to 16th seed ___________ in the US Open in New York yesterday." ...or perhaps it was a sign of things to come. VI. "The world makes way for the man who knows where he is going." - Ralph Waldo Emerson ___________________________________________ REFLECTION, MINUTES BEFORE WARPATH '09 As I sit here, waiting, I've come to realize that life has taught me a lot of things. When you groww up, you're always told that winning is everything. You're shown how to win and how to be successful at everything you do. Hell, it's even gotten to the point where some school systems won't fail students because they're afraid of the effect it might have on the children's esteem. Growing up, I was always one of those winners. I got awesome grades in school and I rarely ever had to try for them. I was a natural athlete. I was one of those kids in gym class who actually used to try their hardest to win - one of those kids that used to not only break a sweat, but let the sweat pour down his forehead. I became accustomed to being on top. I learned to live a life where I looked at bad situations and said, "That could never happen to me". But what school never told you was how to lose. They never showed you what to do when those bad situations did happen to you. And when they finally came around, they left you standing breathless and without any hope at all. Life has taught me a lot of things. I had a prosperous start to a wrestling career - another sport I was naturally talented in. I rose to fame on a positive not and had a tremendous amount of success. I captured numerous championship titles, including World Championships, and even won the inaugural WrestleGod (RPGod) tournament. I was living amongst the clouds. I had it all. I showed no signs of coming down. And that's where life taught me the most valuable lesson of all...I could lose. I remember the day I found out my father had cancer. My family was heartbroken but I was not. Some would say I had strong faith or high hopes - but it was neither. I knew my father was going to be alright. I knew he'd get better. I "knew it" because that kind of stuff could never happen to me. I was the main character in a hit movie. I was the highlight. The rest of the world circled around me and my actions. I remember growing up, I used to wonder to myself if there was anyone in the world that could think the way I do. I always imagined I was put here for some greater purpose; that God intended on something spectacular for me. I figured the fact I was even having these thoughts was proof enough that my theory was correct. Afterall, why else would a person be able to think in that manner? And then that April, my father died. The last time I saw him alive, I talked to him like there would always be a tomorrow. I kept thinking he'd recover, even though he was put in a hospice to spend his final days. I imagined there was some miracle just waiting to occur that would save him. To this day, I'll have such vivid dreams that I'll wake up and actually believe he's still alive. I'll look around my house or wait for him to call. But the phone never rings. Life taught me that I wasn't invincible. It taught me that I can lose and that those same bad situations can happen to me. They can happen to anybody - as cliche as it may sound. When I was a bit younger and faced my first wrestling loss, I was so upset with myself. I thought I was a failure and thought I'd never be able to win another match. Then a guy I trained with gave me a postcard with a quote from Vince Lombardi. The quote read "If you can't accept losing...you can't win." I thought it was a bunch of crap - something a coach tells his team to get them fired up again so they don't feel too awful about losing again. Nevertheless, it made me feel better at the moment. It wasn't until my father died that I was truly able to understand the meaning behind Lombardi's words. Vince wasn't just talking about sports or grades - he was talking about life. He was talking about everything in life. He was telling us He was telling me that I'll never get ahead if I never learned to let go. And up until this point, I've been holding on. Over the last ten years, I've lost my girlfriend, I've lost my family, and I've lost my best friend. Today, as I sit here right now, I finally have two of those three voids filled again. I'll never have my father back - my family will never be whole again. But now I'm able to accept that. His memory will live within my heart until the day I die. And that's more than anyone can ask for. I'm finally able to feel focused - I finally feel like I had some direction. I've finally accepted my loss. If Emerson was right and the world truly does "make way for the man who knows where he is going," then move over world... Because I'm going to the top.
