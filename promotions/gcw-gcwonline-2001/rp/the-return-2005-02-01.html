The Return

1 February 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2-1-05 With a light flutter, a single strand of jet black hair waves aimlessly in the wind. The breeze is cold but refreshing on this quiet Ohio night. A large figure, clad in a long black trenchcoat, steps lightly through the field, snow crunching lightly beneath his carefully placed steps. The field stops a few feet away, giving way to the dormant limbs of a riverside forest. The figure moves just close enough to the treeline to peer down the hill towards the river below. The hillside is covered in snow and receding foliage, quiet and still in winter's midst. The river itself is slowly beginning to ice over at its edges, though its center remains flowing softly, trickling over fallen branches and peeking stones. With a slight smile, the figure exhales quickly, sending a plume of visible vapor into the night air. Behind him, a few lost fireflies twinkle in the twilight, desperately seeking a warm place to sleep. Satisfied with his view of the river, the black figure turns and retreats back to the large, snow-covered Victorian looming in the shadows. His boots leave deep footprints in the white terrain, as a soft flurry of snow begins trickling down to the ground from the barren branches of a lone tree above. 'Dave Santini...' The words roll off the man's tongue in a low, rumbling voice amid a flume of hot breath. They seem uttered with an odd combination of both familiarity and disdain, almost a fond recollection and a bitter taste all at once. 'I never thought I would see Dave Santini as World Heavyweight Champion. I'm sorry Dave, but after all these months, I still haven't been able to let go of those defeats you dealt me. Over all these months, I've been watching you. I surpassed you so long ago, but all this time I've been watching. Making sure that you would never, ever, become AWA champion. 'But you did.' The figure suddenly snaps backwards, his jet black hair flailing with the sudden movement. His face is hard and chiseled, blackened by the gloomy night, the snow failing to soften the impact of his stare even slightly. From beneath his black goatee he lets a thin grin slide through, his deep blue eyes piercing with their glower. 'You got lucky, Dave. But that luck ran out rather quickly. When I got wind of your victory, I called up the AWA powers that be and managed to get myself back in the loop. That's right, Dave, I'm back, and I'm back for one reason only... to make sure you never lay hands on that title again.' 'Think of all the great names attatched to that title. The Punisher, Weston Steel, Ray Owens, Mr. Jack Happy, Vapor Adams, The Immortal Low... Steven Caldera. Your name does not belong there, Dave... Your do not deserve to have your name associated with the legends that have graced this company. That's why I'm here. Because all along, I've been watching the AWA, doing whatever I could from the outside to ensure that it stays afloat... But in the end, Dave, you are the poison that's killing this company, and I refuse to sit back and watch you do it any longer. I'm here to end all that, Dave... I'm here to set things right again.' The hardened features of Steven Caldera turn away again, as he resumes his slow shuffle back towards the looming Victorian house. Another breeze knocks a flurry of snowflakes from the roof of the house, sending them raining down in specks on Caldera's coat. 'Trouble is, my old buddy Ray Owens has been the benefactor in all of this. And that's hardly the name to carry the AWA into a new era of prosperity. No disrespect, Ray, but your day in the sun is long passed. The sun set on your career a long time ago, right about the time I took this company over. You've done an admirable job of holding off Mr. Santini, Ray, but let's face it... you couldn't get it done without me. So enjoy that title reign while it lasts, because at Wrestlefest, it becomes mine for the third time.' 'Do you remember Wrestlefest last year, gentlemen? The three of us did battle last year at this time... Dave Santini, Ray Owens, Steven Caldera, and the Immortal Low in an epic battle for the AWA title. I pinned you there, Dave, just like I did the month before. And Ray, you fell to Low, just as you always did... And it was only a matter of time before Low surrendered that title to me. It was one year ago, gentlemen, one year this month that I won my first AWA title. And that reign lasted, nearly uninterrupted, for nearly five months. And it was then that I willingly surrendered the title to Vapor Adams, hoping to inspire a new wave of youthful exuberence into the AWA. I failed in that task... Vapor Adams was not the man to lead this company. And Dave Santini, you took over as soon as I was gone... Only to drive this company down into the deepest depths of apathy from which it may never escape. Dave, this company has too much history to go down without a fight. I'm here for the AWA... I'm here because the AWA needs a champion it can be proud of. A champion that can represent the company for what it truly is... The greatest in the world.'
