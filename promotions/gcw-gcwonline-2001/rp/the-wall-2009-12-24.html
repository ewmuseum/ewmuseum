The Wall

24 December 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12-24-09 Max Miller didn't consider himself to be a particularly smart man. He wasn't the best educated, and he hadn't read many books. But he was damn good at his job because of the one thing he had more than anyone else: vigilance. Tireless, effortless, genuine. He wasn't just watchful because it was his job to be. He'd taken the job because that's what he was good at. For most people it would seem to be a pretty cushy gig. Head of security at Caldera Enterprises, Incorporated in Columbus, Ohio, wasn't exactly a dangerous position. Columbus was pretty far from Darfur. Not much action going on. The worst he'd ever had to deal with was the occasional overzealous fan, and only once had he been forced to use his mace, but he thought that was rather cool. Sitting at the security desk on the ground floor provided ample opportunity to hone his skills. While the vast majority of people that came in and out each day were clad in identical suits and ties, and the women too modestly dressed to offer sufficient masturbatory material, Max did get the occasional character. Oftentimes he had to look very hard indeed to pick them out, but it didn't take long to catch the telltale signs. The odd tattoo peeking out from behind a well-starched shirt collar. A set of breasts too large not to be on television. A man fourteen inches taller than anyone else in the room. Ok, so the wrestlers weren't so hard to spot. But above all of them, there was one man that Max enjoyed watching more than any other. He would be one of the first to enter the building in the morning, and was always the last to leave, if he left at all. You could tell he was in shape, but he didn't look like a wrestler. He carried himself with too much grace, and a sort of presence that bordered on intimidating. Max wondered how much of that was true grit, and how much was meant to conceal the almost imperceptible limp that never seemed to go away. It was hard not to pay attention to Steven Caldera. Max had been there every time Caldera entered the building for the last five years. He was with the company from the start, doing low-level security at televised events. Eventually his keen eye and tireless efforts had gotten him promoted to the head spot at the home office, which he liked just fine. He missed the excitement of the road, but Max had made a home of Columbus, and he was happy to have his own pillow every night. He never greeted Caldera on the way in. Caldera, he suspected, preferred to enter the building unaccosted by underlings. But he watched him with an inordinate amount of interest, as if under some sort of spell. Max wasn't all that smart, perhaps, but he prided himself on his investigative mind, and one of Caldera's quirks had him stumped. Starting shortly after GCW relaunched and Max was reassigned to the desk, he noticed something odd about Caldera. Normally Caldera's eyes stayed straight ahead, looking past any potential eye contact as if he were the only one there. But every now and then, as if part of some grand conspiracy, Caldera's eyes would dart, just for a moment, to a certain wall in the lobby. There was nothing at all interesting about the wall. It was just a wall like any of the others in the building. Caldera Enterprises was notoriously spare with its decorating, and there was nothing to look at in this particular spot. Just a wall. It was the oddest thing. Caldera did a remarkable job of hiding his limp, though anyone who knew anything about their boss knew that he'd suffered major knee injuries in his career. They knew he was reclusive and unpredictable, and it was best not to look at him. But it was Max's job to watch, and he felt it was only proper that someone, somewhere, know what interest that wall held for Caldera. For a time it stopped. Years, in fact. It had been so long that Max had almost forgotten, until just a few weeks ago it started again. Only this time Caldera was less subtle about it. It happened every single day he arrived, maybe four times a week. Caldera would enter, and for a conspicuous moment Caldera's eyes would settle on the wall. He would never break stride, and his face remained stoic and unreadable. But there was something in those eyes that rose up from somewhere deep down inside when he saw that wall. He couldn't help it. So Max Miller, head of security at Caldera Enterprises, Incorporated in Columbus, Ohio, decided to do a little sleuthing. He dug through the architectural records for the building, under the premise that he was looking to optimize the locations of security cameras on the first floor. He understood little of what he found, but did manage to uncover the little-known fact that just before the building reopened, significant parts of the lobby had been rebuilt. At first this didn't seem interesting. The building had been vacant for two years, and it only made sense that some renovations would take place. So he sat and watched as Caldera continued to glance, almost nervously, at the wall each day. And then The Jhub came by. Max was quite familiar with The Jhub from the old days, though he doubted The Jhub would remember his name. It was The Jhub that inadvertently gave him the clue he needed. It was The Jhub that walked in to the building, stopped, and for a good two minutes stared right at the very same wall. Max racked his brain for anything from GCW history that might explain it. But nothing came to mind. He searched and searched the GCW archives for some footage, maybe some reference to the lobby at Caldera Enterprises. And then he stumbled upon a little-used section of the CEi Archives on floor six. There were some old videos and one DVD, the complete records of X-Wrestling. Max hadn't been with the company when it was known as X-Wrestling. Caldera's short-lived venture didn't make much noise, and Max had been working security for Metallica that summer. Bunch of assholes. He watched all eleven hours of footage. Then he came to it. X-Wrestling's first and only Pay-Per-View event. (r)Evolution. An hour and forty minutes in. ACWF Match. In the lobby of the then-abandoned GCW Headquarter building in downtown Columbus. The Jhub versus Jason O'Neil. Max couldn't believe the war he saw on his tiny security monitor. Here was Jason O'Neil, the man that killed GCW, back in the building with The Jhub attempting to defend its memory. What he got was a massacre. Max could barely stomach the match, but after a brief fast-forward he came to the moment of interest. The Jhub, standing on the edge of a forklift, was driven right through the wall of the GCW lobby. Jason O'Neil, in the driver's seat, blasted a massive hole in the very foundation of Global Championship Wrestling. Jason O'Neil. The man that killed GCW. For years Steven Caldera had been walking into that building looking absolutely unshakable. There was nothing, nobody, that could rattle him. That was his kingdom, and from his throne on the top floor, Steven Caldera ruled all. He had patched up that hole in the wall. But Max watched as Steven Caldera entered the building each morning. And for as long as Max had worked there, that wall was the only thing that could give Caldera pause.
