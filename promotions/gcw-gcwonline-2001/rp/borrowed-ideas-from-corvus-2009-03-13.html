I wish you would. ---- Mechanical Rhetoric Wasting away again in California. Got the shaker of salt, but I lost my dignity a few miles west of Portland. Somehow, some way, I got to liking it here. The more I try to wrap my head around that concept, the more I feel driven to my escape. Lately I've been finding that at the bottom of a never-ending bottle of booze. The past has no hold on me here, on the opposite coast from where I grew up. Million miles away from the places that made me a legend. No friends here, no family, nothing but miles of plastic bodies and cheap Hollywood veneers. I know no one but myself. Maybe that's how I ended up here in the first place. Driven to invisibility by a world that never really wanted me, and sure as shit never respected me. Four walls surround me, nothing but an echo of silence. She left me. I left Chicago. Maybe I left her. Can't fucking remember. It's all twisted up in my head. She left me. It started there, when she walked out the door without looking back. Georgie Nickles. How last fuckin' year. Every epic moment in my life can usually be tied back to some scene like that. Betrayal. Jealousy. Avarice. I seem to draw the crazies like a moth to a flame. I probably deserved it. I usually do. Things would never be the same. Not sure if that's good or bad right now. Probably good. Who cares? Normally, this wouldn't concern me too much, as I'm hardly a slave to routines. I'm used to sleeping in different beds, next to different women, nose candy in different volumes. After a while it all shakes out the same. Excess and addiction in varying degrees. I don't remember the past anymore. I don't have to. I just need to look in the mirror, look at these scars, and I know all I have to. I am human waste. Knock back the bottle, and drain the rest of the pisswater- one hell of a workout. Flex the arm, bend the elbow. Lather, rinse, repeat. Burns like gasoline down my throat before it ignites my ulcer like napalm. Feels pretty damn good. Nothing like a little agony to remind you that you're alive. The right thing at the right time. That's what my life has become. A series of fucked up little serendipity moments. Fuck Garbage Bag Johnny. Fuck the rest of GCW. I know now who I am- what I am here. I finally have a sense of belonging. I've got a headache, just won't quit. My skull feels like it's cracked in a million places, like it's going to come spilling out my mouth, with bits of brain goo clinging to it. Call me, and tell me you still need me. Tell me how you want me to fix this. You're right. I can fix this. Can, but won't. You just need? Shit, fuck it. I don't want to look at your face right now. I don't want to see that hurt look, those tears in your eyes as you drive the nails through my hands and feet. Nail me to the cross, and crucify me for those imagined sins. You keep forgetting it's over between us. It's not 2005. This is not Global. You look at me like that; I'm going to lash out. You say the wrong thing to me; I'm liable to put my fist down your throat. I'll educate that face of yours; let you know how I really feel. Stay gone. I don't want to lose anymore of myself in you. ---- Friday, March 13, 2009 Los Angeles, CA The door swung open without a sound, and he stepped across the threshold with a weary sigh, letting his gear bag drop to the floor. He stomped his feet, shaking off the numbness and jet lag before kicking off the boots. Silence reigned supreme, the darkness absolute as he crossed the living room, tossing the keys on the counter beside the answering machine with the blinking light. Fuck that, they could wait. He shook, trembling like a horse that was lathered as he faltered, staggering slightly. He sat down heavily on the leather couch, propping his feet up on the table. "Shitfuck," he growled, shaking his head before reaching up to rub his face. Clammy sweat there, and everywhere else. He needed a shower, but first, something to drink. Eight hours on a plane after two hours spent beating the shit out of guys that wouldn't have been good enough to jerk the curtains in most places he'd worked- he felt like some lower life form. "Goddamnfuckingcocksuckingpieceofsonuvabitchingassholeshitfucker." It was a breathless rush; nearly a string of gibberish, but the snarl on his lips, and the glower that narrowed his eyes to slits transformed the string of epithets into an alien language that clearly spelled anger. He shrugged out of his leather jacket, letting it drop to the floor beside the couch. The table held a half full bottle of Glenlivet, and a glass that was filled with an inch of scummy water. Jackson turned over the glass, dumping the water on the floor. Uncapping the bottle, he filled the glass with almost obsessively deliberate motions. The glass, smeared with old spittle and flecks of dried skin rose to Jackson's lips, and he took a long swallow. Breathing out slowly, he surveyed his kingdom. Dank and dark, it was nearly perfect. Utopia is rarely paradise. The entire kingdom was ready for collapse. GCW didn't know that he'd been whoring himself out to other places on the side. Gimmicked bullshit matches. Sick, suicidal shit. Thumbing his nose at The Establishment was not something that had crossed his mind. It was idleness. It was the need to be constantly in motion. He tossed the empty cup over his shoulder without another thought, not even batting an eyelash when it clattered against something, bringing with it the musical tinkle of breaking glass. Fuck glasses. He had the bottle. He leaned forward, pulling off the sweat-soaked t-shirt, and wadding it between his hands. Flexing his stiff, thick fingers, he felt the tension, radiating right down to his fingertips. Another week, another ten thousand plus minutes wasted on the road towards death. Pretty bleak, but that's about how he felt. He could still smell the lingering scent of perfume, clinging to the insides of his nostrils. Reminded him that he probably should be across the street, making sure that his girls were behaving. Sinn would probably skin him like the tomcat he was, if she knew how lax he'd grown with managing the place. Downward spiral into depression, he'd been Emo before misplaced angst was cool. He dropped the damp cotton, eschewing that tactile comfort for the smooth, clean lines of the bottle. Much better. The genius of the hole: no matter how long you spend climbing out, you can still fall back down in an instant. That's where he was right now, exhausted and dripping sweat as he stumbled to his feet. Three sheets gone had passed him by a few hours ago. He was at least eleven by now, and the deep-seated ache in his guts reminded him of that little fact. He leaned against the wall, bracing his palm against the ugly faux wood paneling that screamed late 70's, resting his forehead against his hand in a vain attempt to make the headache recede back into the darkness. Like usual, it didn't obey. "Dead. Tired," he muttered, as though placing a label on the exhaustion he felt would allow him a moment of peace. Just another lie dropping down on the pile. Bitter laughter filled the silence as he pushed off from the wall, fingers trailing against the framed photographs. His personal wall of fame. Photos of himself that were faded by time, that first World Heavyweight belt held over his head, screaming in triumph. Freeze-frame, snapshot memory. It served a purpose, since he couldn't really remember that moment clearly. A noise filled the silence. She was awake and stirring. Crying out in a soft, tentative voice. "Brad? Is that you?" That timid female voice drifted down from the darkened bedroom as he slid down the wall, sitting down on the cool hardwood. He said nothing, letting the exhaustion crash over him in waves. His arms were shaking, palms flat against the floor as he leaned forward. Outside the night pressed against the windows, inky black. "Hello? Is there someone there?" Desperation in her cries, and it was soothing even as it broke his reverie, counting his even breaths. Ninety-eight. Ninety-nine. His arms and back were etched with sweat, tattoos rendered surreal by the play of the shadows, and the backsplash glow of the security lights in the alley outside. He pressed his palms against the floor, shoulders and arms screaming as the muscles flexed. The pain felt good, the sweet torment that validated life- mind over matter. A soft sob, whimpering in the dark bedroom. How perfect. He released the pressure on his muscles, and rolled silently to his feet. From the bathroom doorknob, he retrieved his towel, mopping his face as he listened to the silence. Draping the plush terrycloth over those strong shoulders, he picked up the remote from the bookcase beside the door, pointing it at the stereo. Almost immediately the speakers came to life, the soothing sounds of that melancholy sax introduction to Coltrane's A Love Supreme. Heading into the small bathroom, he turned the cold knob in the shower, walking quickly beneath the spray to sluice off the sweat. "Brad?" Her voice was louder now, hands pressing against the frosted glass as he raised his head. Barely there silhouette. "Yeah." Cold water rolled over his face, filling his mouth. He spat it at his feet, seeing the blood there mixed with the saliva. "I was scared. Why didn't you answer me when I called you?" "Didn't hear." Another day he might have offered her a gesture of some sort. A word, a touch- something reaching out through his pain. Another night, perhaps, but not tonight. "You weren't here. You didn't return my calls. Where were you?" Twenty questions, was that an accusation? Things changed so much between them, like a rollercoaster. He couldn't keep up. "Told you. Portland, wrestling in that hardcore rumble. Nothing... really. S'ok, babe. I'm here now." He didn't mention the couple hours spent in the VIP lounge at the airport with his good friends Johnny and Walker. He didn't need to. She could smell it coming from his pores. Those hands were still against the glass, as though it was a lifeline. He didn't hear her sigh over the white noise of the water rushing against the back of his head. "Can I come in?" He considered it, and said nothing. A moment later the glass slid open, and she slipped inside, naked with bed-tossed hair and wild eyes. "Hold me?" She didn't say anything about the fact that he was still wearing his faded Levi's. His insanity had somehow become the norm for her. Only in Los Angeles. Warm hands touched his chest, slipping in the soap that hadn't rinsed away as she pushed against him, seeking his warmth under the onslaught of the cool water. "Brad?" "What?" She looked at his face. Bloodshot eyes, dilated pupils. Split lip with a scrape on his chin that hadn't been there before. She knew better than to ask after all these years. It hadn't been the drinks he was after. The drinking was more a side effect of the man he'd become. It had been something stronger. A bigger, more suicidal rush. There was a boot print on his ribs, already turning purple around the outlines of the treads. Blood trickled from the clotted scab around his arm. Razor wire, or something equally as stupid. She cuddled against him, face pressed against the wet hair on his chest. She touched him gently, tenderly stroking the new injuries without a word. She didn't offer him anything more than a loving embrace. A woman in his arms, naked and willing flesh pressed to his, and he couldn't return the gesture. He was frozen- wooden and immobile. The heat leeched from his skin under the onslaught of the water. Somehow he'd found a way to break himself after all these years. He was cold. The water was cold. He stared at her impassively, hoping to feel some sort of vestigial emotion. That fact moved him. Tears fell from his eyes, mingling with the water. One arm twitched, and wrapped around her waist. He bent his head, letting the pain wash over him in waves. It wasn't physical. He didn't care about the pink tinted water rolling off his battered body. It was emotional. Homecomings like this had a tendency to do that, even to a man as cold as Jackson was. "Take me," she whispered, trying to push the pain away. Her hands stroked his skin, kneading the muscles that screamed for release. "Love me." "Mia." Her name shuddered on his lips, falling out with a harsh exhalation. "I can't." He whispered, breaking the silence with a voice that was strained. "You can." Hope rose in her eyes, those full lips quivering from the cold as her hand slid down his slick abs in a seductive invitation. "Don't." He warned, cautioning her like he always did. This time she didn't back down. She knew what he needed. "Don't fight me, Brad." She tilted her chin, meeting his gaze. His eyes bored into hers, reflecting anguish and that shell-shocked pain. "I love you." She said simply, watching his eyes darken from blue to twilight black. She wrapped her arms around his neck, and forced his head down to hers, kissing him with every pent-up emotion she'd ever felt for him. She staggered as she pushed against him, and he caught her effortlessly as her feet slipped on the bottom of the tub. Flesh wrapped steel supported her as he pushed her against the wall. She needed him as much as he needed her. Body and soul. He groaned against her mouth, big hands splayed against her back as she fumbled with the wet pants. "I love you." The words burst from his lips as he exhaled sharply. Complete lack of sincerity in those simple words, but she ate them up, melting as the jazz drumming and saxophone sounds drowned out her moans. Her fingers closed over the length of him, stroking and squeezing. The phone rang loudly, echoing over the soft jazz, and he lifted his hand, raising his middle finger in the general direction of the infernal contraption. A moment later his machine picked up, and a smooth female voice issued from the speakers. "Jackson? Answer your goddamn phone. This is the third message I've left for you, both here and on your cell phone. There's a pair of plane tickets in a FedEx envelope waiting at The Killer Queen for you. You've got a match this week against-" The machine cut off with a mechanical whine, removing the rest of the intended message as the tape ran out. ---- Mechanical Rhetoric It was a long winter. Long December. I've got reason to believe that this year has the potential to be better than the last. Yeah, I realize I just ripped off a mediocre 90's band's lyrics. Here I am. Doing the same shit I always do. Psych myself up for the cause. Another fuckaree tag team match. I know it will end about as well as last week's bout against Vivica did. Fucking Johnny. Worthless-hippie-druggie-fucktard. I'm so sick of paying homage to this God of yours. Walking on coals, walking on broken glass, doing the land of a thousand dances on your whims. Expending too much energy on empty air. Nothing so vast it encompasses all. Fighting off the sick urge to end it all. Stupid. Don't you know it's already over? That's how it goes. Parasites don't win. Cockroaches are never kings. The golden sucker. The glorious mind fuck. Goddamn lies. I'm not going to lie. This won't be pretty.
