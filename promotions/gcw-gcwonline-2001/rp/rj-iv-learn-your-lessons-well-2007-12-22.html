CANTO X Flashback: Late 2005 Two pairs of eyes were looking at her like she had lost her mind. Pupils dilated, lids spread wide, and none of the two of them giving her a second's credit for knowing what she was doing. Moments like these, Lia Vlaszy wondered, already, if she was doing the right thing. But that was the misgivings of any new situation’the pains of the birth ritual, if you will. Those, too, would pass. There was just the matter of filling space until they did. She pressed the flat of her hands together, and composed her features into patient, unemotional lines. “If you'll just let me explain... It makes sense if you'll only think about it for a moment. She has just as much to gain by getting away from Global Wrestling as we do.” At that, Dawn McFarlane was finally forced to speak up, and she did so by shaking her head forcefully, her black-and-blonde hair, swishing back and forth against the back of the couch. “No... with all due respect, she is what we're trying to get away from. She has connections to everything about that place that's sleazy.” Lia's gaze drifted over to Virgil Ambrosi, her male paramour. She wasn't going to get any help from him, because the moment he had entered Global Wrestling, he'd kept away from anything marked 'Christian Zenith.' And to him, that meant Vivica J. Valentine too. Lia sighed softly. They weren't going to back down, not that she had expected them to. That was part of the reason why she wanted them along so much in GCW, within the safe, enfolded arms of her new venture’they were fully independent of her. They would give her their unvarnished opinion. She was going to have to give them an inkling, something, anything, to show that she understood their concerns. She folded her arms over her chest. “Look... all I'm asking is that you give it a chance. It's not permanent, it's not a sure thing. If it doesn't work for us, it doesn't. We'll cut her loose.” It wasn't necessarily the whole truth, because Lia couldn't conscience doing that to Vivica whom she knew much better than either Virgil or Dawn did. It was for that reason that she really wanted to get angry, to tell them that they were being petty and close-minded. But she kept her mouth shut, turning towards the front doors of the McFarlane Estate and smiling tensely as she heard the grinding of cab wheels outside on the drive. She turned back to Virgil and Dawn, who had just shared what could only properly be termed “a Look.” “One meeting. That's all I'm asking. Then we can discuss it again,” she said, finally giving far more ground than she wanted to. “That is agreeable,” Virgil said, finally speaking for the first time since Vivica's name had come up. Usually, when he did that, it meant that he was trying to restrain himself from exploding. He had such a snap temper she was surprised he'd been able’and she was guessing that it was only by virtue of who she was that he had. Dawn only grunted, but nodded her head. It wasn't optimal, but it would have to do for the moment. Swallowing her irritation, Lia glided into the main hallway to let Vivica in. Dawn turned to Virgil. Relations between them had never been the best, probably because at least in one sense, they had competing aims. More than likely, they would never be best friends, but both of them seemed able to accept that. She gestured towards the door with one graceful, long-fingered hand. “And you're okay with this?” Plainly, she had expected support in her continued dissent. The look that he returned to her could easily be read as one that said 'Are you, in fact, fucking crazy?' “She wants to have her cake and eat it too,” Virgil said, referring to Vivica, “I don't trust her, nobody in their right mind would trust her...” “Unless they're Lia,” Dawn said, for once actually finishing Virgil's thought. They shared another glance. It wasn't often that they agreed on, well... anything. “The first time she missteps, the first time she breaks this trust that has been graciously bestowed on her, by whatever reason...” Virgil could hardly control his anger. His fingers clenched, squeezing in a gesture that was clearly threatening. “You'll have to beat me to the first shot,” Dawn returned. She glanced over her shoulder in the direction of the noise that was rapidly proceeding down the hallway towards the library. There was something about the whole thing that had the air of a home invasion. Vivica appeared around the corner a moment later, a split-second before Lia trailed in behind her. Her eyes only registered Dawn and Virgil for a moment before they became plainly combative, her face a drawn mask in which her cerulean eyes were the only light. “I guess I'm on trial already,” she said, glancing between the two of them. It was perhaps natural that her paranoia was high at the moment’getting out from under Christian Zenith and finding out everyone suspected you of being a mini-him couldn't have been a pleasant experience. It was the wrong remark to make. Dawn blanched for a moment, then sat up a little straighter, tilting her head at her visitor and offering... a smile? “Welcome to the McFarlane Estate, Viv... I hope you enjoy it here, this place was practically made to receive visitors.” There was a moment's pause between them, as though unseen flintlock pistols were being aimed, trained dead-on to headshot. “I always appreciate hospitality,” Vivica said lightly, though the sarcasm was laced behind the words. She slipped over to a chair that would undoubtedly be close to the spot Lia stood when she began to deliver her plans to them. Virgil and Dawn didn't have to share a look, then. It was obvious how it was going to end up’us vs. them. An ultimate test of who was the most loyal. Lia stood in front of them now, gave Virgil and Dawn a look meant to calm them, and clasped her hands behind her back. “I don't think we need much of an introduction about why we're here... We might as well get into the nuts and bolts of things, while we've got a little time. Going in unprepared will absolutely kill us, so this is crucial.” From the way she said us, it was obvious. The winner of this test had already been declared. No doubt, she knew, at the very least, that she'd had the last word in this standoff, and that was as good as winning the round. .:: ::: ::. Chicago, Illinois 17 December Lia Ambrosi was fucking tired of snow. That was not to say that she disliked snow, because she was quite used to it, being from Central Europe. It was Chicago snow that she hated, always caking on the streets and turning into a disgusting, filthy sludge that people slushed through in their overshoes on the way to work. And she was tired of having it kicked up onto her clothes by passing buses. She grimaced as she made her way down the alleyway. It wasn't going to be hard to find a way into this place, she reflected, she just had to remember her way around it first. Lia was no stranger to Zenith Corp's imposing glass and steel building. The glass was all mirrored, and it was hard not to notice that as she slid around the side of the building, keeping a close eye on all the details. If you didn't keep an eye on the details, you could very well find yourself dead. In fact, as soon as Christian Zenith got word of her sniffing around Chicago, she fully expected an attempt on her life. That was why she had backup. That morning, she'd set the plan with Virgil, and worked out an agreement with Dawn. At this moment, Virgil was perched above the CEO's Executive Suite, staring down on a man he never thought he'd have to see again. Lia's mind plummeted from that assurance back down to the ground level, the grunt-work she was currently engaged in. There had to be an entrance to the testing facility somewhere down here’at least one that would eventually lead there. It was buried fairly deep underground, as was only intelligent. Catching sight of her own figure in the mirrored glass once again, she wondered if there was any surface in which Christian Zenith did not see himself. Hate surged in her chest. Down at the end of the alley, she could see an indention. A door. Memories surged back to her--the one time she had actually been down here, she'd not taken much note of the buildling itself, but she would have been stupid if she hadn't memorized the door through which they had entered. No guard. That seemed particularly stupid, she reasoned, but there was no accounting for it at the moment. Could have been changing shifts, could have had security spread thin... or may be Christian Zenith was just getting complacent. Holed up in this damned ebony tower like some hulking toad- he had to be proud of himself right now. Her footsteps crunched more loudly in the snow than she wanted them to, and she paused. Pay attention, Lia. She was too smart to make stupid mistakes- and it was stupid to focus on the end result before you accomplished the goal. More lightly, then. Pressing herself close to the edge of the building, she took one slow step forward, then another. With the practiced poise of a dancer, she started towards the door, eyes keeping it in sight at all times, except when she scanned briefly the area around her, sweeping back and forth to ensure that she was still alone. She inched closer and closer, a cold wind whipping down the alley and stealing her breath from between her lips. She braced herself against it, her long coat blowing forward against her frame. Amid all that noise, she easily missed a pair of footsteps, taking no such care as they crunched unevenly across the snow. What she didn't miss was the sound of a gun cocking, and the press of cold steel into her neck. "Private property, best be getting a move on." Something about the gritty voice was familiar. She couldn't place it right away- it certainly wasn't Zenith, as she would have known that condescending sneer from fifty paces. Holding her cards tight to her, she raised her hands from her sides, holding them up in a gesture of unarmed innocence. "Turn around real slow, lady, and then you can go as fast as your feet'll carry you and I'll forget this ever happened." She started to turn. "Real fuckin' slow, I said!" The guy was trigger-happy, whoever he was. Sounded like he'd been borrowed straight out of a bad action movie, what little experience Lia had with those. This was, at least, a chance for a surprise. She turned slowly, the smile on her face growing as she did so. Might as well present an intimidating front, even if her estimates of familiarity proved to be wrong. The face that she turned to look into was vaguely familiar. Aquiline, sharp features were accented by a shock of black hair, and eyes too dark at the moment to determine whether they were black or blue. She couldn't place a name with the face. Evidently, her would-be captor knew her quite well, however. Rather than uncocking the gun, he stood with it drawn, pointed straight at her chest, still locked and loaded. He looked to be jumpy enough to fire at the slightest move- so she didn't make one. Yet. "You're barking up the wrong tree, Vlaszy." Her head jerked to the side, her eyes slitting to look at him a little more closely. Instinct took over where memory had failed. "Jason LeMaster..." He seemed to flinch. "You're real fuckin' good at guessing games, honey. Spill it, what's the game?" Surely, he knew better than to think it could have possibly been that easy. She smirked. "And here I thought you were on the outs with Herr Zenith... my, how times change." That was the cutting blow, certainly. LeMaster's hands shook around the pistol. She probably could have kicked it out of his grip if she wanted to, but she was in no mood for theatrics today. In fact, a plan had started to grow in her mind, and her gleaming eyes betraye d it- had LeMaster known her well enough to read the signs. He didn't. Instead, he fired off a response typical of people who can't think for themselves, those who can't survive without subsisting off the means of others. Jason LeMaster was, in every degree, a menial. "Yeah well, Mr. Zenith can be a very forgiving man... if you offer him the right price for his forgiveness." "Oh?" Lia quipped, raising her eyebrows at him. This was becoming a rather amusing game of twenty questions... one in which pieces were falling into place. She could think of only one thing that would put Jason LeMaster back into the good graces of Christian Zenith- and that was offering up a way to crush Vivica J. Valentine, using whatever means he had to to do it. She wondered if Zenith had given him any style points, or if he had to be paddled to be initiated back into the fraternity. LeMaster was getting annoyed. "I didn't come out here to trade barbs with you, toots. This conversation is over. Let me tell you what I'm gonna do, and you're gonna listen to me. I'm gonna put this gun in my pocket, and you're gonna walk into Zenith Corp with me without so much as a peep. I don't care what you are, or why you're here... you don't come with me nice and pretty, I'll pump you fuckin' full of holes." It was a bluff, plainly. He'd watched too many Die Hard movies, and had probably practiced this shit in front of a mirror. "Lead the way," she said, giving him the most unsettling smile she could possibly muster. It came naturally to her, unlike the machismo tuxedo LeMaster was attempting to wear. It fit like a cheap suit. "Shut the mouth," LeMaster growled, shoving his gun in his pocket and gesturing for her to walk in front of him. He didn't have to worry- by this point, Lia had decided that the best way to find the answers that she wanted was to go straight to the source. .:: ::: ::. Now was no different, shouted words stabbing through the ceiling and rocketing towards the atmosphere with lightspeed. The whole thing would tear itself asunder before it ever got started. The library doors burst open with action-movie-like force. Standing between them, Lia Vlaszy, shoulders heaving with breath, made a rather imposing figure that caught both Vivica and Seymour off-guard’for a moment. “That's quite enough out of both of you,” she said, her voice full of icy still. “You can hear this rubbish all over the house, and I've had enough.” Seymour was the first to react, smoothing the lines of his face, even though the redness of his cheeks was clear enough of an indicator how involved he'd been in continuing the fight. Again. “I was wondering when you'd get here to call off your dogs,” he commented, shooting a barbed glance at Vivica, who still looked absolutely livid. “Seymour, shut up,” Lia said, turning almost immediately to him, and firing off the response with surprise accuracy. Go somewhere.” He looked incredulous. “You're kicking me out?” “No. I'm asking you to get somewhere out of Vivica's sight for the rest of the day, because I can't fucking take it anymore.” They had a brief standoff, hard green eyes to hard green eyes. She wasn't going to relent, and he finally had to give in. “If you want to wear the daddy pants, fine... I'll comply.” She was seconds away from snapping on him in like fashion, seconds away from agreeing with Vivica that she found him infuriating, a pompous jackass. But he turned the corner and left the library, leaving her alone with her thoughts, and Vivica, which were currently competing for attention. Unsurprisingly, a massive headache was developing. “Why him?” Vivica asked, stabbing a finger forcefully after him. Lia got the impression that if that finger was a knife, Vivica would have long ago hacked Seymour to bits. Lia didn't answer for a moment. Chicago, Illinois 17 December There were times where it was quite obvious just how much Lia Ambrosi was willing to endure in order to make her friends happy. This happened to be one of those times, she reflected, and noted with a bemused half-irritation that going to such lengths had never stopped anyone from questioning her loyalty if they felt like doing it. Then again, could she really blame anyone she knew for having the realism to acknowledge that no relationship was as perfect as it seemed? These thoughts ran by in a slipstream as she rode the elevator up to the top of the Zenith Corp tower. It was like a bullet to heaven, a needle shot skyward with the express purpose of piercing the sky. It could never be said that Christian Zenith did not go to great expense to outfit the various material ways in which he was represented. Zenith this, Zenith that, Zenith everything. Or so he must have seen it, at least. Lia couldn’t help but wonder how any of them could have managed to overlook something so simple. As much as they’d wanted to believe that Christian Zenith was out of their lives, like the brandname, he permeated. And when he needed to be, Christian Zenith was a very patient man. Perhaps his patience would pay off, seeing one of the ones that got away, so to speak, directly on the hook in his office. His eyes would fortunately not be the only ones on the scene--just in case things got sour. But Lia very seriously doubted that Zenith would have the moxie to kill her. He wasn’t stupid, just egotistical, and a murder was messy business. Christian Zenith found loose ends to be unprofessional. The elevator slid open with the efficient whoosh of a spaceship door. State of the art, as was befitting a man who manufactured military technology, if you wanted to call it that. “You’ll keep your mouth shut when we’re in that office.” It was as though Jason LeMaster could somehow read exactly what Lia Ambrosi was planning to do, which was give Zenith the third degree, pressure him into admitting something about where Markus was. Or force him to remember that there were other people on the planet, some with mental acumen at least equal, if not superior to, his own. Lia walked briskly, as she was forced to by the gun, now held closer to her back. They were in the office moments later, and Christian Zenith appeared to have missed the memo that he was to play villain today. His eyes pored over a row of figures on a spreadsheet. He was playing with the numbers on an account today, seeing how he could adjust it to be the most profitable. “Picked up something outside for you, Mr. Zenith,” Jason LeMaster said, his forceful grumble now sounding more like a puppy’s whimper. It amused Lia how even the most powerful men could be brought low to groveling if they felt like the reward would be worth it. Christian Zenith looked up, seeming annoyed at the intrusion. But once he saw what Jason LeMaster had capture, his eyes fixed on her, bored deep holes. There was no emotion within them. “I’ll handle it myself.” “Mr. Zenith, are you sure you should-? It was only then that Christian Zenith forcefully eyed his lackey. “I’ll handle it myself, LeMaster. Scram.” It only took a moment for Jason LeMaster to follow the order the second time it was given. He disappeared into the dim recesses of the office that led to the door, visions of his increased favor dancing in his head. Lia could practically feel eyes upon her- from in front and above. Virgil was lurking up there somewhere, watching, and a part of her was comforted by that fact. Another part of her was comforted by how Christian Zenith was trying to hide that he’d been caught off guard. He was trying to force her to speak first to do it. She wasn’t into playing that game, and stood demurely, hands clasped in front of her, eyes fixed on him. She was anything but demure, though. What brings you to my neck of the woods?” The words were forceful, territorial, with all the intention of implying the further question “And when are you leaving?” Lia only smiled fora moment. “Let’s get down to business, Christian. Where’s Markus?” The name evoked a reaction in him that only those he truly hated could evoke. His eyes narrowed, and turned colder, if they possibly could have. His upper lip twitched, the beginnings of a snarl that he was having a hard time concealing. “Why would I concern myself with a failure like Markus?” “Because he surpassed you at something.” The barb hit its mark, Zenith’s features beginning to glow with rage. If Markus Blair had been in Chicago, he’d be gone within the week, of that much she was sure. She’d either have to move fast, or play the waiting game. Someone with nothing but time on her hands could play it just as well, if not better, than someone who had his own pressing mortal concerns to deal with. Lia turned, walked towards the door without fear that Christian would do anything like shoot her in the back. He thought she was permanently rebuffed, that he’d set her off on a cold trail. He underestimated his opponent’s assiduity. She was sucked into the spacepod elevators before she could really slow the thoughts whirling through her head like errant snowflakes. As she plummeted towards the ground of the Zenith Corp building, aside from noting that nothing about the place ever changed, she could only focus on one thing: the game was about to get interesting. .:: ::: ::. CANTO XII Flashback: Early 2007 Lia emerged from the bedroom, shaking her head. She wasn't pleased, but then again, she didn't think anyone would expect her to be pleased with this sudden news. She knotted her hands together and pushed them out, popping her knuckles. Her fingers were tired from doing all the massage work she just had, running expertly across tendons and muscles that were sore, and in need of attention. It didn't help when said tendons and muscles were your husband's, and you were unceasingly worried about his condition. “What's the prognosis?” Lia turned suddenly, drawing in a quick breath. She was only alarmed for a moment, though, because she'd recognized the voice as soon as she heard it. Vivica smiled, her arms folded across her chest. It was a bit of a nervous smile, as though she was feeling all the tension that had been floating around lately about the general state of things among their group of friends and associates. That wouldn't have been hard to read, considering the situation. Lia grimaced. “It's not looking good, to tell you the truth.” “Ohhh no...” Vivica took a couple of hesitant steps down the hallway, her blue eyes zeroing in on Lia. She touched a hand to Lia's forearm, squeezing reassuringly. It barely registered. “How's he making out?” Lia turned to Vivica, almost with a sharp look. She pressed the frustration off of her features almost immediately. To her, it was a ridiculous question to ask, because of course he wasn't faring well’but she presumed it was asked in innocence. Why would she have had reason to think differently of Vivica? She and Virgil hadn't fought in months... “As well as can be expected, I guess,” Lia said, biting her lip and looking down at one of the large mosaic tiles of the floor. “He's not happy, and he's in pain to boot, but there's not much to be done for it. He may heal better than I think he will... but I'm not hopeful.” “Wow...” Vivica suddenly released her touch, and leaned against the banister, shaking her head. “Dropping like flies, huh?” Lia did give her something of a sharp look then, only because she didn't want to be reminded. It was bad enough to see, once again, everything falling apart underneath your very capable hands. It was even worse to be reminded that there was nothing that she could have done to prevent it, organize and fight though she had. “Dawn out...” Vivica was saying, “And now Virgil... and who knows what happened to the KB” I mean, they just dropped off the face of the map.” “They have other things to worry about,” Lia said, adding that in almost before Vivica could finish her sentence. The KB was not an organization that waited around for wrestling title belts and professional rivalries. There were things much bigger than that at work in the world. “Yeah, I guess so... still, it kinda sucks that we were starting to get somewhere, and they decided to just walk out without so much as a how do you do...” It was like rubbing sand in an open, pus-dripping wound. “I can't say that I blame them,” Lia said. She was in no mood to hear any line of conversation that would force her to argue either for or against the KB's actions. Not when she was still a part of its paramilitary arm. “Really?” Vivica said, raising her eyebrows. “And here you are World Champ... you'd walk away from that?” It wasn't that. It had never been that, and Vivica more than likely knew it. The question confused Lia, because she hadn't implied that she'd run out on her responsibilities. After all, she wasn't like... well, she might as well say it. She wasn't like Ian Childress. “It's not that...” Lia sighed, running her fingers through her hair. There was no easy way to explain this. She'd gotten more involved in GCW than she thought she would, and it had caused nothing but problems in almost every other area of her life. But why would she walk away at this moment, at the pinnacle of her success? What would she walk away to do? To become another piece of meat, ground up in the KB machine. A soldier, expendable to higher-ups. It wasn't the kind of life for her, and she'd always known that, even though she owed Ian Childress what was perhaps the biggest favor of her life. There were rare times when Lia Ambrosi felt completely in over her head, now being one of them. It wasn't conceivable to her that walking away was an answer to either problem’to the problem of GCW, or the problem of the KB. It took her a moment to realize that Vivica had seemingly moved on without her. “Dawn coming to WorldWide this week?” Lia shook her head. It was the default answer lately about Dawn being involved with GCW. She was on injured reserve, her rehab progressing nicely as far as Lia knew, but it didn't look like she'd be coming back. “No... she says she still doesn't feel up to being around able-bodied competitors while she recovers.” Vivica nodded. “Guess I can understand that. I'm not sure I'd want to be continually reminded of my injury either. Though it must be hard on you, having the two people who are supposed to support you the most not able to come to shows.” Lia looked up, suspicious that there was a barb contained in the statement, but Vivica's face was placid, and it only took a moment for Lia's face to resume its own placid lines. “Yeah, it really does.” She felt more comfortable letting her guard down around Vivica lately for some reason. It wasn't completely uncomfortable to admit to her how deeply it stung that nobody seemed to be happy for her success but her. There was no better way to make Lia feel unnecessarily selfish. “Hey...” She looked up at Vivica again. “I'm here for you, okay” I always have been.” It wasn't debatable. .:: ::: ::. Bow to me // Bow to me // At my feet you’ll bow to me Houston, Texas The mirror reflected the truth- inside it were a pair of ethereally burning green eyes. Those eyes were the eyes of a woman who needed no invitation to be mad as hell right now. If there was one thing Lia hated, it was people who were perfectly willing to spit on someone who did a favor for them. She was still irritated at Vivica for the slight at WorldWide 80, and she felt she had every right to be. To have it thrown in your face- to have someone honestly say “I don’t really know you, I don’t know what kind of person you are...” Well, for most people it probably would have been the honest truth. Lia Ambrosi didn’t give insight into her inner workings to any Joe or Sally on the street. Trust had to be earned. And she didn’t like it when people tried to use trust as currency. “You don’t trust me.” Those words rang loud and clear. “Pah!” The noise of disgust was unescapable. It came from deep within, and was something that Lia found she couldn’t close her lips to prevent. Vivica J. Valentine was no mind reader. Whether or not Lia believed her when she said she wasn’t joining the Establishment shouldn’t have mattered in the long run- when it came down to it, Lia was still beating tracks across Chicago trying to find Markus for her. There would have been no way for Vivica to concentrate at the same time on finding Markus and earning her World Title shot... so Lia had taken the burden of one for her. In allowing her to take on the other, it seemed that Lia had created a monster. A woman who acted like a young spoiled child. Someone who wanted everything in the world, and complained when it wasn’t delievered- so Lia saw it in her less charitable moments. A woman who had long claimed to be Lia’s best friend, the only one who had stuck it out with her in GCW... and now there was this. Now that it was World Title time, it was “you don’t trust me” and “I don’t even know who you are.” There was no slap in the face that could have resonated louder than Vivica J. Valentine’s behavior at WorldWide 80. No physical stab in the back could have so clearly communicated. The way Lia Ambrosi saw it, there were two options here: give, or take. She’d spent a lot of time giving. Probably more time than most of the people she’d given to deserved, if you were to look at it honestly. How many of them had given back? And how many had walked away when they were asked to do so? That’s really the main difference between us, isn’t it? You talk, I listen, I talk, you turn away. Oh yeah, I’m supposed to keep an open mind, even though you’ve already made yours up. I should have focused less, I guess, on finding your boyfriend, and more on your career, so that I’d have known the truth about the Establishment. Well, let’s put it where it is: Caldera’s a snake. We all know that by now- and the smartest thing he could do is drive a wedge between two people who were opposed to him, and tight enough with one another to actually mount an assault against his bullshit. So I apologized because I was taken in, and realized it. And you didn’t accept it, because you were taken in and didn’t. It is what it is, then. You want to be mad, let me tell you about mad. Let me tell you about betrayed. Markus? I never found him a threat, not like you found Virgil. There’s really no other way to read the way you treated him for months on end. Screaming fights, a complete lack of trust an respect. Jealousy. Oh, I’m sure it came from both sides, at least the lack of trust and respect. But it’s hard to respect somebody who threatens you. I don’t need to tell you that. And Christian Zenith? I never tried to put a bullet in his worthless skull, though by all means, I should have by now. Puts a tracking chip in me, takes medical samples to do god-knows-what with them, and then has the gall to lie to my face about Markus when I can practically scent the whelp all over him. Boy, you really ought to sit back and realize when somebody’s done something for you. Or maybe it was just the fact that I kept it quiet, that I didn’t tell you I had a lead on Markus so that you could focus on the task at hand in GCW. So this is the thanks I get for it? So long as we’re keeping it on the level, let’s just put everything out there. Let me just say, for the record, that you won. You did it. You got rid of everyone else who might prove a threat to you. The Virgils, the Dawns, the Ians, the Blaises. All gone. It’d only leave me one place to turn to. One shoulder to lay my head on. I learned a long time ago, though, that tears don’t get you anything. When you cry... nobody cares. A select few take note of your weakness, and then they move on to bigger and better things, things which don’t involve being taxed by the realities and problems of another person. It’s much easier to look the other way than to make the honest effort. You know that, though. You ought to, at least. I work alone, Viv. I know that in the end, the only person I can depend on is myself. The sooner you realize that, the better off you are. So you have something to prove to everybody under the sun who thought you couldn’t do it. Take a little advice from a cynical realist when I say... they don’t matter. You don’t have to prove shit to them, because you don’t owe them anything, when the bottom line comes into play. That’s why I’m not going to apologize to you more than once. I did the right thing. I admitted that I was wrong, and you chose instead to berate me without even understanding where I’m coming from. It seems we’re on the cusp of ending a friendship that’s been years in the buildling. It seems like this one match could make the whole house of cards fall down, as you evidently look at it as though it’s been a house of cards from the very beginning. I have my pride, Vivica. And I won’t grovel to you. If you don’t want to accept my apology, then don’t. But I only owe it to you once. I won’t hand over a World Title out of pity, either. The only way to take what you want is to earn it, fight for it, sweat for it, bleed for it. Match me if you can, beat me if you’re able- I’m not going to turn over what I’ve earned just because you’re still mad at me. Or maybe it’s all a ploy anyway. Maybe Caldera’s known from the beginning the best way to drive a wedge between two tag team partners, stable mates, best friends. I wouldn’t put it past him- and in that case, we really shouldn’t be so short-sighted, should we? But what’s done is done. Requiem in terra pax... She came away from the mirror not really knowing if she should be saying those words, thinking them. It could have all been kin to a voodoo charm, hexing her before she ever put one boot inside the squared circle at NC-17. The one thing Lia did have, in absence of the cockiness that many of her colleagues possessed, was a grit. A determination that, no matter what the situation, she would not give in unless the will to fight was physically drained from her. She was a woman with a cool head, tough as nails. That would have to serve her well as she faced her biggest challenge yet... looking into the face of her protege and discovering the ability to destroy what she had tried to help Vivica build, if that ended up being the will of the fickle gods. She turnedfrom the mirror. A weaker Lia would have crumpled in the face of this challenge, but a weaker Lia had no place in a wrestling ring. A weaker Lia couldn’t have faced down the professional demon that was facing Dawn McFarlane, nor could she have beaten LeStatt Knight. A weaker Lia couldn’t have been the PTC Global Champion, no matter the time span, and a weaker Lia couldn’t have beaten Bryan Mayhem with the grit and drive that she had. Maybe it didn’t add up to much in the end, but the truth was beginning to come clear- depth in Lia Ambrosi only ended when she decided that it did. When she needed it most, that reserve of strength offered up another wind. When the last stand had been made, Lia Ambrosi alone would have the courage to continue the fight. There were some things that no amount of maneuvering, conniving, or backstabbing could take away. There were some things that no guilt-trip could erase, no matter who wielded it. Stripped down, the alabaster marble Everest that was Lia radiated unseen force. To beat a woman in the wrestling ring, all you really needed was skill, and maybe a dash of luck thrown in here and there. But to move a mountain, what you needed was a miracle.
