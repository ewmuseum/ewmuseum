Date: Years Past and Today THE ROAD SO FAR I. The day was suppose to be filled with love and happiness. Humberto and his lovely wife, Maria, had dreamt of this day and talked fondly of it thousands of times before it actually happened. The day Maria got pregnant it felt like God had come down and given the world a miracle. Maria had been told that she couldn’t have children, that their best option would be adoption. Maria never believed it, not for one second, saying that God would make sure that she could have a child of her own. Even when it seemed like there was no hope, no light at the end of the tunnel, it was Maria who would find a way to lift Humberto’s spirits back to her level. Then it happened. The miracle that they had been waiting for. Humberto’s mind fills with the memories of that day, the day he walked in from work. Maria had decided to cook a special dinner that night, Humberto’s favorite, Enchiladas with frijoles, rice and home made tortillas. When he sat down he was surprised further to see that Maria had went out and picked up his favorite cerveza, SOL. Of course he knew something had happened, his first though was that she did something wrong and was trying to soften him up. So he asked her?. Tears immediately began streaming down Maria’s face and she leapt into her Husband’s arms screaming to him, in Spanish, “I’m pregnant! I’m pregnant!”. It took Humberto a few times of hearing it to actually make out what she was saying. When it finally sank in though? He was shocked. Tears formed in his eyes but never fell. He told Maria that the only day he would cry is the day their children were born. He held her tight for what seemed to be an eternity. Then they made love, more passionate than ever before for what seemed to be even longer than an eternity. Everything about that evening was perfect, dinner, drinks, happiness, sex. It was a memory that he could never forget, pure bliss. And now it has been torn to shreds. Painted red with blood. There were “complications”, at least that’s how the doctors explained it to Humberto. Humberto did not make a lot of money and he certainly couldn’t afford the best care. They didn’t know that Maria was pregnant with twins until it was too late. The first child, a healthy boy, came out crying like all babies should. He was a sight to behold, so very beautiful. But Maria was not doing so well, already weak and in a great deal of pain ? she wasn’t ready for the second child. No body was. But he was coming anyways. “Complications” ? that’s how they explained it. Another boy, things weren’t so smooth this time however. The baby came out not crying, barely breathing. Maria, even in her weakened state tried her best to find out what was going on. The doctors tried to assure her, make her feel more at ease. So she laid her head back onto the sweaty pillow beneath her ? her body relaxed. The doctors succeeded in this task. They even got the second baby breathing again ? Maria smiled as she heard him crying loudly. But the doctors couldn’t stop her from bleeding, no matter how hard they tried. That baby’s cry? Was the last thing Maria Abitua heard before dying on that hospital bed. Complications. Hours passed before Humberto could get the nerve to see his twin boys for the first time. Even then, as he walked up to the glass to peer inside, he was weak. His mind was racing and his body was tired. Grief stricken and angry, Humberto had yet to cry up to this point. He raised a trembling hand to the glass, using it as support and let his dark brown eyes look upon his babies. Everything flashed through his mind again. The entire process that led to this point. He missed Maria terribly, the thought of never being able to see her again, raising these two children, these angels, without her help seemed like an insurmountable task. Then another thought crossed his mind ? what if they weren’t angels? They are responsible for killing his wife, the one person in the world that he loved more than life itself. THEY took that from him. Now he begun to cry, years worth of tears flooding him, overwhelming him. His eyes began to burn, the salty taste of his tears invaded his mouth. He couldn’t control himself any longer. He knew what had to be done, there were no alternatives. He missed her so much. He slid a shaky hand into his coat pocket and grabbed the handle tight, sliding the pistol out and into plain sight. Humberto could barely hold it right, barely had the strength to lift it up to his head. His vision was so blurry that he couldn’t see anything in front of him anymore, his “angels” were distorted, more like monsters now. “Good bye.” Humberto pulled the trigger, the gun’s blast echoed through the hospital, his blood splattered on the window in front of him. II. They didn’t say much to each other, both of them were very nervous. Although they had been married for six years, they hadn’t even discussed having kids since Juan was getting his business together and Sandra just enjoyed the time they did manage to have together without a child getting in the way. Sometimes, your plans get altered by unforeseen circumstances. Juan got the phone call from Sandra’s Grandmother, Francis from the states. She was worried about the twin boys that were left without parents. She agreed to take one, help raise him but didn’t think she could manage to raise both of them without any help. Reluctantly she called Sandra, hoping that they’d be willing to help out a part of their family that was in great need. Juan’s first instinct was to say no, he wasn’t ready, even at the age of 29. The business was booming now, sure but things could go wrong. He still hadn’t traveled with Sandra like he always wanted to. Juan however had too big a heart, no he didn’t say yes right away but he decided to talk it over with his wife and together they’d make a decision. The decision wasn’t hard at all at the end of the day. “I’m sorry, we just can’t.” The answer wasn’t the one that Grandma Francis expected from her granddaughter. A tongue lashing from your Grandmother can really change your perspective on things. So Juan and Sandra spoke again, weighing the pros and cons. They decided that having a child in their lives could be a good thing. They also realized that they were being selfish, a child was in need. They couldn’t just let him fall into foster care. So they agreed to take the little boy and call him their own. He didn’t even have a name! Juan’s heart begun to beat faster and harder once he heard the doorbell ring. He was actually afraid that Sandra would be able to hear it from across the room. She couldn’t, of course, not when hers was beating just as loudly. “Ready?” She asked. Juan just nodded and pushed himself up from his chair. He didn’t know what to expect, what to think. He DID know that he still wasn’t ready for this, not by a long shot. Deep down though, Juan knew this was the right thing ? it had to be. If for no other reason than because it was happening right now. As he approached the door Juan couldn’t help but wonder what the baby looked like. He tried to imagine his face, his eyes, his mouth. In his mind however there were no detailed images, just very basic features that made the image in his mind seem more like a porcelain doll. Juan knew it was silly to be trying to get the picture in his head just moments before actually meeting the kid so he wipes it away like an etch-a-sketch. Sandra reached the door first and slowly opened the door but not before giving Juan a quick smirk. Sunlight flooded the entry way, making it hard for the couple to see the social worker standing at their door. Juan welcomes him in, both of them. The baby was covered from head to toe to keep the sunlight from irritating him. Jan found himself a tad disappointed that he couldn’t compare the child’s real features with the fake image he constructed in his mind. Sandra couldn’t keep her eyes off of the small wrapped up child, even as the social worker introduced himself and gave a few details on the adoption. In fact in the middle of one of his sentences, she chimed in? “Can I hold him?” The Social worker smiled and handed the baby over to Sandra. Juan quickly went to his wife’s side and looked down as Sandra slowly pulled the blanket off from over the baby’s face. His small eyes blink a few times as he adjusts to the change in lighting, his tiny hands come up to his face and they rub his eyes. He yawns and looks up at what would become his new parents. His bright blue eyes glistening. Then time seemed to freeze, Juan couldn’t hold in his tears. The baby was more beautiful than he could have ever imagined, definitely not the doll that he pictured. Sandra was crying too, mixed with the huge smile on her face ? Juan couldn’t, at that moment, think of a time that his wife was anymore beautiful. “What should we call him?” She asked her husband. He thought about it. The answer came quickly. “Roberto.” “Your father’s name.” Juan nods. Sandra smiles. “Roberto it is.” NOW III. Roberto’s mind races as he stands still, everything that’s happened in the last six years racing through his mind. Those bright blue eyes of his remain locked on the head stone in front of him. He slowly kneels down to place some flowers on the wet soil. It’s been raining for days, irritating to many but not to Roberto. He loved the rain, wished for it because he could easily hide amongst everyone else. Roberto had a lot of stress in his life, personally and professionally, and somehow the rain manages to soothe him. “It’s been a long time,” Roberto begins, stopping to clear his throat. “Too long I guess. After everything you did for me.” It was always hard for Roberto, leaving and coming back. Every time he saw his uncle Juanito or Sandra, even pictures his heart sank a bit. He knew, since the day he was sent away that a part of him never left this place, after the way things went down it’s not a surprise. Emotions were high, anger, sadness, grief, disappointment. Roberto hated feeling that he failed Juan, the one person he could call a father figure. The one man who actually loved him, trained him, made him a man. “I wish things could’ve been different. I wish you could see what I’ve become, how successful. Sometimes it’s hard, Mary is around and I love her with all my heart but ?” His voice trails off, getting drowned out by the pounding rain. “I wouldn’t be here if it weren’t for you. I just want you to be able to be ringside just once.” But he can’t be, Roberto knows that. “Good bye. I love you.” He rises to his feet, his gaze lingering on the head stone for a few more moments. Roberto turns around and walks away back towards his car where some people are waiting for him. At first they are just foggy figures behind a curtain of falling water but as he gets closer their features come into focus. “Okay?” Sandra asks. He smiles. “Of course. Just some unfinished business. It’s been too long.” “It has.” Roberto’s eyes shift, Mary is standing next to Sandra. He reaches out taking Mary’s hand and pulling her close. Mary smiles and wraps her arms around Roberto’s waist. He looks back at Sandra. “I promise you, I won’t let it happen again.” Sandra smiles at Roberto, the closest thing she’s ever had to a son. “And where to now?” The questions jars Roberto back into focus, he straightens his posture, tilts his head back slightly. “Hawaii.” Roberto smiles. “Business.” “Of course.” Roberto gives Sandra a hug, holds her tight. There isn’t a replacement for a mother’s love, Roberto realizes. He pulls away and opens the car door for both Mary and Sandra to slide in. He slams the door behind them. As he walks to the driver side door he stops and looks back towards the cemetery, fog has rolled in giving a very horror movie look to the whole setting. Roberto wipes some wet hair that is sticking to his face away. “Hope you’ll be watching.” And he gets into the car and drives away. Now that he came back home, other things have to take precedence. Hawaii. Shockwave. Andy Murray. The World Title. It’s now that he realizes that things haven’t really changed. He’s here now but it still feels awkward, still feels wrong. There’s a part of him lurking around in this place, hiding in the shadows that can never be found again. That shouldn’t be found again. He looks into the rearview mirror, watching Sandra and Mary talk to each other, enjoying each other’s company. Roberto couldn’t make out what they were saying exactly, his head was too cluttered. Too bad too, really is. Because they are never coming back here. This isn’t his life anymore.
