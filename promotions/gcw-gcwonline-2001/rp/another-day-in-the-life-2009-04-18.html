Another day in the life...

18 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/18 2701 E. Sahara Avenue Las Vegas, Nevada It was a warm day. Temperatures in the Nevada desert city were registering a mild 72 degrees. Here it was, yet another Monday and not exactly the best time to go to the Nevada Department of Motor Vehicles. Registering your vehicle wasn't in the plans today. The Department was full of people with different nationalities, religious and political beliefs. The crowd seems a bit agitated about waiting so long. As lines quickly empty, the clerks replace their “open” sign with “next window please”. Most of those in line groan and throw their arms up in the air as obvious displeasure is heard. Standing amongst the group in a line is Shaman, a look of agitation upon his weathered face. He finds himself between an elderly woman and a young mother with her child. To his left, seats were taken with people who had been there since the place opened up. Upon closer inspection, Shaman noticed a heavy set man who had been perspiring. His shirt showed his sweat pattern and he chuckled to himself over how those sitting next to him were able to handle the odor that was sure surround the man. Shaman ignores the crowd as best he can as he looks up at the number light. It reads 56, but he has number 79. The old woman smacks Shaman’s leg with her cane as Shaman looks back at her as she speaks. “Young man, you should let an old lady go before you.” Shaman stares at her for a moment and responds as politely as he can. “Ma’am you aren’t that old and get around a lot better than some people my age. I do not have time to give people line cuts and such. If you want cuts you go ask all the people behind yourself and see if they are fine with it.” The old woman smacks him in the shin with the cane this time. “How rude you are young man.” Shaman begins to clench is fist. His thoughts taking on a nasty tone; which he dare not speak. “You know I could take that cane and rap it off her head and save the country a few thousand dollars in Social Security. Hell might help the deficit issues and even the economy. Old people with canes should be outlawed. They are dangerous weapons. Maybe I can take that cane and shove it straight up her”.” A little kid pokes Shaman on his forearm and Shaman looks down. The kid couldn’t be much older than seven or eight years of age. Shaman’s weary eyes notice that he’s dressed in the latest GCW shirt. He can’t help but notice he’s wearing a Clyde Fox shirt. “Can I help you?” The little kid stares up at Shaman in awe. “Are you Shaman?” Shaman smiles a bit and looks down at him. “Yes I am, Can I help you with anything?” The kid looks about and grins. “My dad says you are washed up. You’re just an old guy who can’t even fight; much less climb into the ring.” Shaman grits his teeth and looks down at the kid. “Your dad is a couch potato, kid. He doesn’t understand the business and I am probably younger than he is. Go tell your dad you met a real wrestler and he thinks your dad is a joke.” The kid looks at Shaman and raises an eyebrow at him. “You are mean.” Shaman smiles. “Yup do you have anything else to say?” The kid looks up at Shaman and pulls his t shirt out and shows Shaman the shirt, looking up at Shaman with mean eyed look. “Clyde Fox is my favorite wrestler and he is going to beat you badly on the World Wide show.” Shaman looks down at the kid as he shakes his head. “Go back to your mom kid and don’t worry about Fox he will hurt enough.” The kid sticks his tongue out at Shaman. Shaking his head Shaman groans. He begins to think again on something. “I want to stuff this kid’s forehead into his mouth and watch a dentist and proctologists figure it out. Who the hell is this kid’s dad the Beverly hillbilly” Hell can’t he even tell the difference between who is capable and who isn’t. After all anyone has a good chance to beat anyone. Clyde Fox is a talented guy, but come on I can beat him as well. Clyde has proven himself and has showed he can do what he has to win. This time it is the Rampage Tournament. There is something I want out of this.” Interrupted during his thought he looks up and notices the number change. He steps forward as the old woman grumble something and Shaman shakes his head mumbling. “Menopausal old bitch.” Shaman notices a rather older woman looking at him. She gave him several glances. It appeared to him, almost if she was trying to summon up the courage to speak to him or do at least say something to him. The woman taps lightly on his shoulder. “Sorry my son is being rude. He tends to take too much after his father. His father is an unemployed jerk. He doesn’t pay child support and all he does is compares the wrestlers of today to the wrestlers of the past.” She rambles a bit, but stops abruptly as she realizes Shaman is actually listening. “Sometimes people can be jerks or assholes. Not everyone in the world deserves to be in the same category.” She looks up at Shaman and smiles again as she speaks. “So which one are you?” Chuckling a bit, Shaman looks around. “Well I like to think I am someone who doesn’t care really. In fact there are some things I care about. Then again most people are shocked when they listen.” She smiles at Shaman and runs a fingernail up and down his arm. Shaman looks down at her and then at her kid. “I don’t quite think this is the place for this. If you knew anything, you would realize that Kawanaha wouldn’t appreciate your playing around here and flirting. Unless, you’re interested on getting to know her on a more personal level. I, however, personally think this place is the tackiest place to try and pick up someone.” She looks at Shaman shocked. “What type of woman do you think I am?” Shaman rolls his eyes at the contact, “To tell you the truth, you came on to me like a two dollar hooker says it all! But if you want something more, I’m pretty sure Kawanaha could make you the next big porn star” She just stares at Shaman in horrid shock as the little boy speaks up. “Besides I don’t go for that cheap whore look.” “See Mommy, Daddy says the same thing to you all the time, but about other guys.” Beginning to laugh hard Shaman looks at the woman. “If the shoe fits lady.” Shaman reaches into his pocket and pulls out a business card and it hands it to her. “If you change your mind, give her a call. I’m almost a hundred percent sure she can put you to work.” The lady huffs and turns around snatching her son around with her. Looking at his hand and the ticket he turns and looks over at the clock. “Well at least I know I can still piss off everyone. Strangely enough I feel remotely content here now.” Turning his head the bell rings again and the woman and her child walk to the counter and begin talking with the lady. Turning the bell rings again and he steps forward and begins to pull out the license, information, and insurance stuff. The teller slaps a sign down that says gone to lunch be back in a half hour. Shaman stares for a moment and then looks at her as she walks away. “For the love of?” Turning Shaman looks around, his agitation at an all time high, growling low as the guard puts a hand on him to move him to the back of the line. Shaman spins, punches the guy in the face, leveling him. The man lays upon the ground unconscious as Shaman walks out the door angry. "If Fox thinks this will be a cake walk he has another thing coming. Come World Wide we will square off and I will do what I have too."
