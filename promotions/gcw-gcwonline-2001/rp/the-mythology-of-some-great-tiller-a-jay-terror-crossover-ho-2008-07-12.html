The Mythology of Some Great Tiller (A Jay Terror Crossover! Holy shit!)

12 July 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/12/08 July 7th, 2008 Whenever he got the opportunity, Frank would sleep forever. The only decision it ever involved, ever required, was getting too comfortable while too tired. The sleep came about as a natural extension, an evolutionary means of grounding him through the eons it took to tan his hide with the light of perspective. It made flashes of sense, followed strokes of logic; it faded in the daylight and left him no wiser, left just bare images of stone or steel creations’he imagined they had to be creations, imagined that nothing of their magnitude/importance/scale/power/violence could have formed by coincidence’crashing into the surface of the planet and sending twisting towers of dirt and rock and life and city into the stratosphere. Towers that fell with earthquake force and volcanic suffocation. Screaming. Dying, probably. Just images, noises. No thought beyond "my dreams are kinda fucked up" before what little of the visions he had kept faded and left him rolling groggy with palms clenched over his eyes. Then the day came: not his house. He didn't have a house. No house he recognized, then. Nobody he recognized’a lot of people to not recognize. He snapped his field of vision shut and clenched it darker, drowned out light and tried to remember the details of the situation with the same skill he used to grasp at those fleeting REM memories. Sweat pooled against the small of his back. Blood languid in veins. Chemical gel brain. Staggered, staggered, staggered. The weight on his ribs sighed and rolled slight degrees; he opened eyes and forget her name anew in the stabbing, lingering recollection of Kimbusa's beating. In the dawn light though, crept slow gold through pulled blinds, she was: something to see. He reached further back, slipped until he remembered the stretches that'd dragged him into Chicago without risking a foot to the whirlpool before it. A vague idea of location; some brick building in a dead corner of a neighborhood labyrinthine; if not brick, then cement grey, and likely suspended on a third floor somewhere. He wished sobriety had hit him better and set at summoning more than a presumed memory of the night before. Clothes granted deduction; deduction left him sexless and still reaching for something substantial. He found cigarettes tucked in the woman's back pocket tangible enough, lit one and breathed menthol. A bar. Woodgrain. Queen on the jukebox. A row of scythes reclaiming the Eurasian landmass. No, no. The girl asking: "why do guys always fixate on my ass?;" Frank with a "dunno" and a shrug, the girl sneered or she laughed. Then, nothing. From the bar to some cramped/overfilled apartment with no interlude, no build, no discernible, concrete motion. Speakers hissed empty static at him, their mouths wide and their throats torn raw from old smoke that still lingered on the air. He thought of their language as complex, prided himself on translating their level screech into: "leave;" into: "leave and never come back." It was a base translation though, left him afraid that he'd missed some subtlety of their tongue, that he'd maybe lost their intentions entirely; he wasn't prepared to set human/machine relations back several decades with one rude escape, so he listened again with an ear more precise. "Leave now and do not return here. These are people. You are some basic organism faking at stardom. You dont belong in this world. You dont even know how you got here." He didn't agree, but he saw some merit in their electrical reading of his morning face and, with one hand digging into the cushion and another trying to support the nameless, he sat at sliding himself far away without disturbing whatever balance existed in this communal swelter of peeling wallpaper, of spartan dressing and the eternal stink of drunk drunk drunk. Bracing himself on the ground, his foot caught a surviving can and tipped it over into his shoes; he stifled any response, playing at some tiny stoicism while he adjusted the unconscious. Almost free. She murmured, she sighed, she let an awkward snore as he slid away and left the sofa to her. Shoes under his arm, he crept over dreamers sprawled and tried to find the way out through some modern instinct, some new survival technique we'd learned in our domestication. If birds could find north, Frank could find exits. "Youre that wrestler dude, righ?" He stopped. He turned. He faced the kitchen with chin up and eyed He Who Questioned. "Yeah. Less theres another one hangin around here." "Haha. Man, you dont look much like no wrestler." "Might be coz I aint much of a wrestler." "Shiiit, dont matter much. Still famous’an you better be proud of that: Shanna dont just hang on any old white boy." "Hah. Aint like I fucked her though." "But you couldve and Im bettin wouldnt be the first groupie pussy you got up in. Am I right?" "First this month, I mean." "There you go. Askin me, you probably shouldve hit it. Sure you got your reasons." "I dunno. Not sure bout anything from last night." "Haha. Get tore down; I feel you. Just, uh. Lemme know this." "Yo." "Why you carryin your shoes?" "Was tryin to find my way out." "They go on your feet. You know that, righ?" "Yeah, yeah. They just got some brew in em. Was gonna, yknow, try an dry em out outside." "That case, you tryna hit the balcony an blaze?" Frank's eyes shifted sleep, shoulders slumped. "Nah, not really. Like I said, was sorta lookin for the way back to mine. Got like, obligations an shit." "Hah, better be important ones if they got you turnin a man down in his own crib." "Shit. Didnt even know this was yours." "Well, now you know. But you might have the right idea anyways’probably be better if you got your ass gone before Shanna wakes up. Girl likes the taste of money, sall Ima say." "I feel you, I feel you." And he left under an air of social awkwardness he couldn't shake, wouldn't even try. He had his wants like he had his needs, and they hadn't charted far enough down to measure how low he'd sink to get either; shrugging off tact in one ignorant display and disappearing into the thin light of beforenoon didn't even register as a difficulty. Slapping his lowtops along rails and posts as he made his way up the hill, up the street with socked feet starting to feel the brunt of rough sidewalk, broken uneven by sporadic trees or more frequent holes that may've once held trees. At the intersection he waited, slipped his shoes on and started to stagger along the busier of the streets available to him until, in a sudden wave of doubting the value of his lack of direction, he stopped to hail a cab and rode the rest of the way to his hotel, cleared a hefty percent of the distance left in an elevator that climbed too long or too slow, one; he rapped knuckles against the railing, pressed his shoulders flush to the wall and quit existing in any serious definition of the word. Until the ding set him free and, divorced of mechanical chariots that just couldn't make it through narrow corridors, he covered the rest of the trek by himself. The air tasted wrong. He fell out across his bed. No one ever answered their phones anymore. He fumbled dumb for a joint from his dresser. Zoe'd thrown him a line and he hadn't bitten; and then there was just him, just Frank, without the old safety nets and harnesses. So he smoked and he tried to find any reason for the things he did, any fuel that might've kept those dumb fires burning like pyres on the shore. They existed. It was enough for him. Dreary eyed. On the verge of sleep. Crashing. Crashing. Crashing. Buildings dismantled by a force so far below technical, so far below nuclear, so far below breathing flesh. Mountain ranges that put up all the resistance of sand castles. Ad infinitum. At least until his phone woke him. He twisted his head groggy to the faceplate, let the impact recoil through him just long enough that he could answer unsurprised: "Yo. Caroline?" "Hey, Jordan." "Is there anyway you could, like. Call me Frank?" "Okay. Hey, Frank." "There you go. Now whats good?" "Nothing. Absolutely nothing." "Shit. Startin to think its somethin in the water up here." "I havent really drank much." "Me either. To be specific, I think its Prozac or pyramids in the water. Only thing keepin the people here from killin each other, probably." "Hah. Maybe. How are you?" "Im," he breathed like it wasn't automatic. "Thirsty as fuck." "Whats wrong?" "Dont worry bout it. Talkin things out, shits never helped me much’but yeah, what's up with you? Jay go off again?" "No, nothing like that’at least not yet. Well, kind of. Look, are you hungry? Im supposed to meet him later on and, Im going to need something in me before I go through with that." "Huh. Time is it?" "Um, eleven twentyfive." "God damn, I didnt sleep long." "Oh. I could let you go." "Nah, I mean. I slept last night, just passed out again when I got back here. Yknow?" "Yeah. Sounds fun." "If it sounds fun, it aint exactly what Im talkin about. You talkin food like Burger King, or food like, I dunno, decent food?" "Burger King is decent, though. Its almost the definition of decent, really." "Then I meant to say badass food." "The kind that jumps through flaming hoops on the back of a little dirt bike?" "Haha. Yeah, that is exactly what I mean." "Definitely that." "See what I can do. Lemme call you back." "Okay." He called Sage. Sage didn't answer. He hung his head and let a moment (defined, at some point in his life, as a minute and a half or thereabouts.) pass. He called her back. "So Frank. What you got?" "Big ol nada." "Damn. Hows, Burger King sound?" "Platonic as all hell." "Good." And he found her, was greeted with a "nice car," and more words. And Burger King. Food. Eating. Talking. Talking. Talking. "Okay. First up, no way is Jay as bad as youre makin him sound." "Hes. Wait, how can you say that? Dont you remember the screwing you over, selling his soul thing?" "Kid is doin his, tryin to get his as quick an easy as he can. I mean, I did about the same shit, except Ive just been givin my soul away for awhile’cant really hold it against him without holdin it against myself, yknow?" "Do you?" "Hold it against myself? Shit. Probably." He hefted burger, delayed it midair for: "but I cant hold other people to the same standards I hold myself to. Aint fair." "So, what? Hes just a good guy that got a little off track?" "Never said he was, or, never said I thought he was good in any way." "You do know that he hates you, right?" "Cant blame him. Im a serious threat. Hah." Intensely selfamused, Frank devoured and fell farther into the vertical ribcage of his chair. "I still dont know how you can be that cavalier after everything hes done." He shrugged; she knit her fingers. "He cheated on me, last week." And he stopped chewing to swallow, to listen. "I found a pair of panties and. You know? Im not sure how much it really matters to me. I knew it was coming, eventually at least. Calderas done something to Jamison and. Fuck. This sucks." "What you gonna do?." "I. Don’t know’give him one more chance? I really liked him. I mean. Alright Frank. How do you handle something like that?" "I dont. You were with Jay, how long? Me an him, were both gettin paid to be these emotionally stunted little manchildren that act without thinkin. Its, its wrestlin on TV’the dumber we get, the higher the ratings. We dont really deal with shit; we just hit people with chairs until we forget whatever was buggin us’an maybe forgettin that is the same as forgettin who we are. Fucked if I know.." She tilted her head to some slight degree and he downed watery soda. Across a partition, a woman's voice rose against someone behind the counter. The room tried not to watch. "What I do, at least. Either channelin it or misdirectin it one’doesnt really do anything for the problem, but it wins a few matches and it feels good til the adrenalines gone." "That is totally not going to work for me, you know." "Yeah. Cant tell you whatd work for you, though; just tryin to give you some idea how he does it. I guess. Have to be tough as hell to not let a role you play that much start to take over. Act stupid long enough, nobody knows the difference." "Maybe." "Im just sayin. Hes not. He aint just, straightaway a bad person. Hitler was a bad person. Jays just, average I guess Id say. Had the chance for some serious career advancement and took it an. It sorta says where his priorities are at, yknow?" "Yeah. It still fucking sucks, of course." "Course. But dudes got a tendency towards suckin, so. Youve been sayin it for awhile’aint a shock to be found in any of the shit hes done." "He couldve surprised me with it, at least." "Haha. Yknow its worse that way’least you got to come to terms with it before he decided you were a little too good for his standards." "Its not over yet." "Kinda things linger, dont they?" "Fuck yes." Protest; the woman’s voice climbed, indignation writhing; Caroline looked to Frank, Frank to Caroline; he tilted his head back to the door. "You bout ready to get outta here? Atmosphere just aint as classy as it was when we showed." "Mm. Might as well. Ive probably made him wait long enough already." "Thinkin like that," he stood, "givin him the benefit of the doubt probably isnt your best idea anymore." Grabbed the tray with trash and half a burger regarded as trash. "Ima grab some more drink. You just wanna wait by the car?" "Thats cool." She did, or she disappeared and he assumed. Filled his drink. Played voyeur to the woman's argument. Left to his car and to Jay Terror's girlfriend. Maybe. He didn't know and he didn't think about it beyond a primal twitch instinct to conquer in the name of conquering; he let her in and he sat down, cranked up and let it idle out its coarse German rumble. "Sorry he fucked around on you." "Thanks, but you didnt do it. Youre not the one that needs to apologize." "Im just sorry you got fucked over. Sall." She smiled. "Well. Thanks. Again." "Anytime." Reverse. "Did you hear what she was yelling about?" Turn. "Hah. Yeah’orders had gotten all mixed up or somethin, and she was sayin the guy in the window just ate her shit and gave her somethin random." "Wow." "I know. Its a hell of a city. Wheres old boy at?" Directions. Automatic. Like "left here" and a subsequent left there. Like "stay in the right lane" and the unwavering dedication to it that followed. He thought that he maybe should've just let her drive; thought after that she already was driving and that he was just some facile machine placed behind the wheel to follow commands, to get his shoulder soaked, to provide support. "What should I do?" It snapped him from his subsubconscious procession. "About Jay?" "Mmhmm." "Huh. Why not." He reached for a phantom pack, paused for the realization. "Why not just be straight up about it? Be quicker that way." He tasted bitterness and shot his eyes to her side through narrowed lids; he added again to her bill as he glanced her over, wondered how she might take the realization that his time wasn't free as he overrevved on the way to second coming around another required turn. Squawking before the rear end lost traction and slid lateral towards a honking van that was never in any true danger’Frank corrected and Frank was gone. Caroline's voice quickened, slightly. Nothing else. "Here he is. On the right." "Just want me to let you out?" "Yeah, thatd be fine. You should. Hm. You should probably park down the street and just wait for me." "Haha. I got it covered’been in somethin like this before." "Leslie?" He cringed, remembered to remember to keep his mouth shut at a counterfrequency to the one it normally ran on. To meter himself like he'd metered Jordan White; to keep it up, to not fail or quit either way. Meanwhile, the passenger's side of his face rose into a guilty smirk. "Somethin like that. Take your time." She slipped her seatbelt away and waited diligent by the handle until Frank's creep became a stop; she stepped out into the light and covered her face with a hand for a visor. "I really doubt he has anything to say. Ill be quick." Shut. He rose slow on the odometer, inspected the shift of her ass (labeled as such somewhere in his tangled gray matter. nothing proper. nothing friendly.) and drove with an eye for a place to turn along a downtown crowded with parked cars and walking machines. He turned the stereo louder and didn't listen to the music; he only let it further isolate him from the world surrounding. He didn't know what he was going to do anymore than he knew what he wanted to do or what was the right thing to do. Jumbled, he found a lot that let him slip around and wait for a break in the steady trickle of traffic that boxed him in. Freed to park again, to wait again; he parked again and he waited again with seat falling backwards and speakers slowly lowering until he felt fully divorced from those beyond his glass and steel. He opened the glove compartment and dumped a blunt out of a plastic tube. He smoked with fans running and engine off, with paranoid eyes that twitched from mirror to mirror until the calming came. Too famous to worry about a minor possession charge. Too famous to worry. The money comforted him. The luxury too, and the convenience, the ease, the vague respect he'd find in sporadic bits (more and more sporadic as he radiated away from Charlotte, reached the edge of some invisible radius of concern.) and above all, the power. He called it freedom because it felt like he'd imagined, because his motion through the new world was frictionless, because he couldn't feel a social drag. A perfectly engineered existence. He ignored a call and checked his voicemail when it came through. "Frank? Hey, Zoe here. Just wanted to apologize again for making things weird the other week. If, um. If youre around sometime, you can hit me up and" He got the idea and deleted it without the tenacity of burning bridges but carrying all the same implied repercussions. Cracked a window, smoke rolled. He shook his head. He laughed. Terror stepped outside and he laughed again as he turned the ignition; the beast roared. Destruction's as valid a goal as greed. A flash of geysers of soil obscured apathetic claws that rose past infinity. He nodded. Misplaced rage becoming violence as more than just a series of habitual actions; as an ethos, a mantra, an only way to be. He pulled forwards and wondered, idle, threatless, what Terror’s head would look inside out; Caroline came outside, joined Frank in the hazy car. Her boyfriend, her exboyfriend, her whateverthefuck, made eyes at Frank. Is how he processed it. Not rage, no; he defined Terror on his own terms: confusion; maybe a hint of jealousy. "How'd it go?" "Couldve been worse." And it all repeated. They ate. He tried to help for some hidden reason or for no reason at all. Then: the driving, the waiting away from whatever happened. The watching without listening, without wanting to listen. Frank Black held the belief that people should be happy. Frank Black felt no obligations towards making it true. He changed discs in the CD player, turned Bun B loud and let the beat ride. And candlelit Terror grabbed her shoulder on some orange dusk. And she smacked him. Frank leaned his head back, started to roll forwards in what he dreamed of as a steel menace, inescapable, implacable. And Terror grabbed her again. And she smacked him again. With a "fuck off, Jay" faint over the music, Frank let the window lower. It was enough. She broke from Terror's grip and headed for the passenger's side as the title contender loaded and aimed himself at the driver. But Frank had reverse. And Frank had freedom. Okay. He was free or he was cheap. "Jesus. Frank, do me a favor." "Whats that?" "Piss him off. Ruin his fucking week." He shrugged. "See what I can do."
