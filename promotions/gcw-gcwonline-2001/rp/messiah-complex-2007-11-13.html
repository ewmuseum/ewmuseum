Messiah Complex

13 November 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 13 November 2007 Messiah Complex “Forgive them, Al, for they know not what they do?” I’ve learned from experience that people don’t like hearing the truth. Then again, admittedly, how you tell them has a bearing. Through the low morning sun’s glare off the SUV’s windscreen, I watch him thunderously slam down on the horn, trying to blast me from his path with the onslaught of piercing toots. Through furious eyes he watches as I simply hold up my hands, passive, lowering my palms towards the asphalt, requesting that he calms himself. The bass that shakes the ground underfoot subsides, and his yelling is heard over the muted stereo. “Yo! Fruit Loop!” is muffled by the windshield, but the aggression with which it’s spat out gets the message through. “You want me to flatten you” Huh?” So as not to be impolite, I calmly answer his question. “No, sir, I would not like to be run over.” I get the feeling that perfect manners will do little but assure myself that I acted respectfully. “You got 5 seconds, boy,” he warns, his nose almost collapsing into the middle of his face such is his snarl. “Now get the fuck outta my way.” His beast growls into gear, and the bass returns, pulsing through the earth where I stand with such ferocity that I expect it to open up beneath my feet and swallow me. The crisp, almost brittle air takes in the exhaust fumes with no futile resistance, the sickly-sweet petroleum tickling the taste of vomit up my throat. I swallow back the sickness, blackening my stomach. Drawn towards the disturbance, the beginnings of a crown linger around me. One perma-tanned woman, her face baked terracotta with wrinkle-cracks shoots a me a glancing look of sour curiosity, turning up the corner of her painted-on mouth at me. She thinks I’m a weirdo. She carries her dog in a bag, though, so her opinion doesn’t matter. Through the bestial rumbling and gut-busting bass of the carbon-belching monstrosity before me, I hear a further barrage of abuse, mainly in obscenity form, with toots of the SUV’s horn acting as the censor. Then, the monster lunges at me. Like St. George facing the Dragon, I stand my ground, beliefs holding me firm. In our game of chicken, he blinks first, slamming on the brakes and coming to a halt with his body-mangling bull bars prodding my chest. “May I have a word with you now, sir?” I ask. I often find that people find politeness odd. Even a little disturbing. I blame the telesales industry for forging the now unbreakable link between pleasantry and the urge do things that Al would surely disapprove of. God too, but especially Al. The engine gasps and splutters as it’s turned off, and the SUV’s door swings open. A pair of Timberland boots crunch down on the tarmac, overly-baggy jeans with their ground and frayed ends softening the impact. “I don’t usually do second chances,” strained, gruff words warn. Compared to the SUV, the man is to scale. Just like the bull bars adorn his grill, metal rings and studs decorate his bulky body. A nightmarish collage of tattoos paint a gruesome picture up each arm to where a tight white vest, stained with sweat and grease, does little to mask the muscle definition beneath. He doesn’t so much as stifle a shiver in the frosty breeze. His eyes are squinted with hostility, shadowed by a prominent, anger-wrinkled brow. “Neither does the planet, sir,” I turn back against him, craftily, giving me my “in”. “Would you care for a pamphlet about hybrid cars?” He’s that snarling rottweiler, wound back, ready to lash out at any passer-by unlucky enough to antagonise it. Only the belief that I am somehow not sound of mind acts as a leash. “Because, well, let’s be honest here, sir,” I open up, nodding over his shoulder to the gas-guzzling planet-killer. “What you’re driving at the moment, in these times, isn’t really that socially responsible of you, is it?” I’m doubting that he’s seen An Inconvenient Truth. It’s a pity I handed out my last free copy to the woman buying the non-recycled toilet paper at the grocery store. “What the fuck you say?” he barks, each word emphasised by a snarling, aggressive nod of the head. “You escape from the fuckin” hospital or somethin?, boy?” Sometimes the message has several thick layers to penetrate before it can sink in. “Oh, I haven’t come from the hospital, sir,” I jovially state. “But if a clear conscience is an illness, then I’m in a critical condition.” He doesn’t quite get it. “Now, here’s that pamphlet,” I press, reaching out and tucking the truth into the pocket of his jeans. And the leash snaps. Before I can recoil my hand, his head lunges forward, propelled by a bulging neck even thicker than his jaw. The impact misses my nose, but catches my cheekbone full on, detonating a dull pain down the left hand side of my face. I stagger a couple of steps back, pamphlet still in hand. “You” you really should take this?” I urge, left eye squinting as a nerve misfires. His right fist doesn’t, and my jaw compacts, breaking the circuit of my central nervous system for a split second. The blink of an eye lasts about 5 seconds, and next thing I know I’m propped up by awkwardly crossed legs, a warm, metallic taste to my tongue. “It’s some?” I pause to shake my senses loose, propelling a mouthful of blood onto the pamphlet. The words in my head un-jumble, finding the right order as they drop off my bloody tongue. “Some interesting night-time reading, sir” just make sure there’s an energy-saving light bulb in your lamp.” I slump forward, slapping the blood-doused literature onto his boot, leaving a red streak down to the toe as my groggy hand slips to the asphalt. “Oh, that’s it now,” he expels, flinging the blood-streaked boot into my ribs. The impact lifts me up off the asphalt, propulsion helped by the air being jettisoned from my lungs. As I violently cough through a gag of phlegm, I manage to force out just a few breathless, wheezing words. “Forgi-? forgive him, Al” for he kn-? for he knows now what he doe-UGHUH!” Another kick forces me to almost wrench my lungs out, triggering a violent heaving fit as the vacuum in my lungs feels like it’s sucking my entire torso into a black hole. “You don’t get to preach now, boy,” he sneers, this time stamping the sole of his boot into my kidney. “You ain’t talking.” A volley of body-warping stamps pepper my back, before a few scuffed steps backward spots him a mark to begin his run-up. I pull my arm in to protect my side, and I sharp warmth fills through the tissue where the limb takes the brunt of the blow. I take this because he knows no better. Another kick resonates through the bones in my arm, exploding at the shoulder to send tremors through my collar bone. I can’t make out his words anymore, only able to detect changes in pitch and volume. My head is limply swatted to the side as he leaves a footprint on my already indented cheek. And with that, he signs off? save for another half-hearted kick to the arm. I hear the thud of his stomping steps heading back to his SUV rumble up through the tarmac as I flop onto my back, plumes of heavy breath erupting into the crisp air. No good Samaritan comes to my aid. This is my duty in the grand scheme. I talk even when they won’t listen, to remain passive even when they turn aggressive, the politeness to their rudeness, the wisdom to their ignorance? “the right to their wrong. Every kick, every stomp, every punch. None can disprove the truth, none can stop the message, and none can stop the messenger. In a parking lot, on the street, in a police cell, on the lawn of the White House, in the lobby of Ford Motor Company, between the ropes of a wrestling ring. So long as there’s a there’s the message, so long as I have Al watching over me, I’ll keep getting up. Rolling onto my front, I plant both hands into the asphalt, gritting my teeth as I push my aching body up. Just as my arm is about to collapse from under me, bone feeling as if it’s splintering like a Popsicle stick, I manage to get a foot under myself. With the arm that still has feeling in its fingers, I reach into my jacket pocket. “Maam,” I splutter to the first woman to cross my stumbling path. “May I interest you in a pamphlet?”
