Wounded pride

1 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Sept 1st, 2005 Jim Brandt turned a grim smile to the bulky pack he’d just thrown into the back of his “86 Caprice Classic station wagon. Mentally he ran down the list of equipment inside. Spanghelm, breastplate, pauldrons and arms, gauntlets, gorget, greaves. All the required pieces. Next to the pack lay his SCA regulation war axe with closed-cell foam “head”, which was on top of his shield (which is roughly a standard door with a carry handle). In his mind he knew he was all set, yet something was eating at him. Mostly it was the lack of a second pack in the back of the “war-wagon” that had him thinking. Jaw set determinedly, he turned back toward the 3 bedroom house the he and his best friend were renting, intent on locating it’s other occupant. As the front door swung open a guttural roar rolled through the house. “DIN! WHEREYERAT?” Heavy footsteps resounded through the house as Jim traversed the seldom occupied kitchen and thundered down the stairs into the darkened basement that was affectionately known as “The Dungeon”. It was here that they kept the enormous repository of literature and paraphernalia that made them quite possibility the most well read and informed geeks west of the Mississippi. Through the perpetually dim lighting of the Dungeon, Jim could make out the sight of his friend trying to quickly hide the heat pad that had been on his neck most of the past week. The soreness had slowed Dave Montgomery to the point that even with a good 5 second notice, he hadn’t been able to properly hide the pad and return to his seat before Jim had entered the room. A sour look appeared on Jim’s face, as it often had during the last week. “There’s a heavy fighter practice today over in Albany. Did you forget or something?” Dave (better known as The Rogue Paladin) rose gingerly from his seat after acknowledging the chatroom he was involved in with an “AFK” . “I think I’m going to have to pass this week. Can’t risk getting hurt before the match can I?” Jim leveled a serious look toward TRP. “Don’t you mean can’t risk getting hurt any worse” I know you’re pretending you’re ok.” The Rogue Paladin simply shook his head, although he did so slowly to avoid any sudden movements. “It’ll be alright, really.” Jim looked down at the floor, guilt flushing his face with lightish pink hue. “It’s not fair you know.” “What” The match. There was no way we should have won. We’re rookies and they’re all seasoned veterans. It’s like wandering into a PvP zone immediately with a new character. No chance. We need more experience, gotta level up first.” Jim kicked at a shadow on the floor to keep from fidgeting, which he is prone to do when agitated or emotional. “It’s not that. You win some and lose some. It’s just not fair that they kept picking on you.” Now it was Paladin’s turn to smile grimly. “It’s always going to be like that Jim. Always has been.” “What’d you mean always?” TRP turned a thoughtful eye to his friend. “Of course, you’ve just never seen it before. It was like that in junior high, high school, all of that. No one would mess with me when you were around, but whenever you were out of sight it was a different game. I’ve always been the skinny guy. People pick on the skinny guy. I'm used to it, it's my lot I guess. Sometimes I could outsmart them, or out-quick them, or out-tough them; and sometimes I couldn’t.” “Why didn’t you tell me! Who was it” The Russell Brothers? I bet it was, those guys were assholes all through school.” “It doesn’t matter. It wasn’t your fight.” “But it is now, we’re a team. You’re not in this fight alone. I won’t let them pick on you.” Meat’s face was bright red now; not from guilt, but adrenaline. His eyes flashed in the dim light, somehow in-human; almost feral. His urge to smash something was palpable. “Meat, I don’t need you to protect me. We need to work to our collective strengths. Let me outsmart or out-quick them. Let me try. If that doesn’t work, then it’s time to be the Beserker, be the Trainwrecker, The Siege Engine. Odds are, somewhere in the balance is the path to gold and glory.” Meat’s semi-commital nod indicated that he understood, but the fire in his eyes proved he still wanted to smash something. “Alright Din. I’m with ya. Thunder and lightning, the Arrow and the Axe. Now let’s get our gear packed for Portland.” “Why, we in a hurry all of the sudden?” Meat smiled a toothy, near evil grin. “Yeah, gotta level up.” ~fin~
