Black Clouds

23 July 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07/23/10 He didn't wake on the first alarm or the second for that matter. He shut the thing off entirely when it came down to it. Didn't matter when he got up so long as he made the show later that night. That was six hours away so he smacked his scabbed lips together trying to keep dreaming. He was swimming in a field, or perhaps a pool, and it was gold and it was endless. There were walls but they weren't horizons. Everything extended on and on like a 'Godspeed! You Black Emperor' track and Jay kept swimming through it. He'd gone miles upon miles past obstacles, creatures, and people, until he stopped entirely and let himself sink. He touched the bottom, or he was about to rather, when he found out there was no bottom. It evaporated like the rest of the world and filled itself with the gold stuff. He kept falling, letting himself fall actually, until the sun from the curtains became too bright. Anaheim's afternoon rays replaced the golden world with a drab and dreary hotel room. He blinked a bunch and tried to make something of the white walls he was imprisoned within, but nothing formulated. He rolled and dipped his face further into the dried blood stain his upper lip had created and tried to shut out the sun. The gold world wasn't coming back though. He pretended it was gold but even the pretending was a mere silver and gray. Finding his back again, he grabbed his knotty hair and pulled it over his eyes to shield the bleakness of it all. Unfortunately, he was forced to answer a ringing phone. The number was a bunch of numbers on the screen and he pulled it close to his ear. "Jay?" "Yes?" "How're you?" "This have a point?" "Do you know who this is?" "Did we have sex?" "You being serious?" "Serious. This have a point?" "It's Caroline. Remember me?" "Name rings a bell. One from Kentucky or the one from Philly?" "You met one in Kentucky?" "I met one in Georgia too. Common name." "I'm in Anaheim. I wanted to see if you wanted to meet. Maybe we can eat. Maybe?" "I'm in Anaheim too but I got work to do and people to see. Not today." "Jay." "Still me." "Meet me at Yves Bistro in an hour." "That with an E?" "Will you be there?" "What's this all about? I thought we said goodbye and all that." "I wanted to see hello." "Guess you do that after goodbye. How 'bout a half hour?" "Yeah. Works for me." Terror hung up the phone and placed it back on the nightstand. He shut his eyes for another five minutes hoping the gold would come back, but his lip was bleeding again. Jay huffed and used the linens to wipe it clean before finding his jeans from the night before. There was a plain white shirt on the floor as well and he wore it. He lit a cigarette on his way out of the room and rode the elevator down ten floors. There were people waiting for him when the doors opened and he left them a lingering cloud to ride with. A man at the desk tried to reason with him and tell Jay that he wasn't supposed to smoke inside, but Jay was already out the revolving doors and inside his ride. As the limousine moved, Jay's cell phone rang again. This number had a name and it was James Bryan. "Hello," Jay offered. "Jay, hey. How are you? Everything OK after last night?" "As good as it can be, sure. Going to lunch now." "Good. And the lip? How is it?" Jay checked with his shirt collar, "bleeding again. Wouldn't you know." "We'll have to try and keep it closed tonight then. You'll be wrestling Shipley as it stands." "Yeah? Isn't that something." "You OK with that?" "Was hoping for Windsor." "She's unavailable." "Who's she have?" "She's not wrestling. She had a busy night last night as you should know." "Ha. Fighting champions. Gotta' love 'em." "You sure you're OK?" "Couldn't be better. I had a great dream last night. When will I get my rematch?" "Rematch?" "The world title match. Remember? I have a shot still. Or. Well. I mean, at least I was told that. Right?" "Give it some time, Jay. Things will come together. Anyways, just wanted to check on you and see how it was going. Hoping that you weren't down or anything." "Down? Why would I be down?" "Great outlook. I'll see you later. Try and patch the lip." "Yes sir." They each hung up and Jay found an ashtray for his finished friend. He lit another to remember it by and leaned hard into the cushions. They were soft on his back and he felt he could breathe easier inside them. He almost forgot that he, or Tessa rather, had hurt his ribs. Shutting his eyes, he pictured his boot slamming her face straight into the steel cage. It happened in his mind and he could almost hear the reaction they gave him. What hate they had but what fear they also had. There was nothing like stirring the pot to make a point. She'd roll over he imagined, and she'd be faceless almost. The skin would be hanging and Daddy would look over in rage. It's how it should have gone. But they were at the bistro by the time his second cigarette had been smoked so he lit a third on his way out. There was outside seating and he opted to find a table in the corner near the wall. Their policy was against smoking as well and Terror, with one final drag, launched his burning cigarette over the stone wall. He'd skimmed the menu by the time Caroline showed up and was able to fully stop and marvel her while she approached. She expected a welcoming, a hug, or something, but instead he asked, "alcohol or sweet tea?" She flicked her brows and moved her dyed blond hair out of her eyes. "Sweet tea." The waiter caught the order in passing and left the two of them alone. "One of those days," Jay asked. "It's nice to see you too." "Can't say I was expecting this. What're you doing in Anaheim?" "I came to see you. I came to say hi. I don't know. Just figured we could talk and have lunch." "Sure. Yeah. I mean, people do that. Friends do that." "Are you OK?" "How come everyone's asking me that today? Something wrong with me?" "I don't know. You seem - well you seem a bit on edge. I saw the match. Is that it? Is the wrestling what's got you down?" "Down? It's like I'm a black cloud or something." "So you're OK?" "With the match? Yeah. It's just a match. It's just a title I'll win. I mean, let her have some light. I don't need all of it." Caroline accepted it and took a glance at the menu. It was diverse but in a typical way. They had burgers, French fries, wraps, and soups like any other Bistro they'd been too, but wore different tags and clever descriptions. "You see the lunch special? The ten dollar one?" "You can spend more than ten dollars, Caroline. I can cover it." "What are you getting then?" "The ten dollar thing." She smirked and remembered what lunch used to be like between them. They were lunch people in their finer days as some people prefer to have dinner and wine. Most of the time they agreed that they could afford to skip breakfast with a heavy lunch. Sleep was precious when it came to keeping the wheels on the track and by extension, lunch was pivotal. "I wonder what the soup du jour is." "Soup of the day, dear," he reminded her casually. "I meant the flavor, hon." "Crab bisque. Said so on a sign out in the front." "You'd think they had enough bisques already." "S'a good way to let the lobsters rest maybe." "Fair enough. Any women in your life besides Caroline from Georgia and Kentucky?" "I raped a girl in Hawaii. That was fun, I'll tell ya'." "Are you being serious?" "You didn't see? I took her virginity and made her believe in Jesus Christ. Don't you miss the things I do?" She didn't believe him. "Have you talked to Frank recently?" "Black?" "Yeah, that's the one." "Last time I talked to him he took my title and probably my lunch money while he was at it. Vicious guy, terrible friend. S'all good though. I'm trying to forget grudges these days and focus on the present." "On Tessa Windsor?" "You're all wrestling and no fun." "It's what you do. You used to love talking about all of this." "I do. Just. Not today." "Tessa Windsor?" "You know, I haven't been this annoyed of the name in some time. Think you could stop?" "Look, I just figured you could use a shoulder to lean on, someone to lift you up." "That's why you're here?" "And to say hi." "Hi. Bye." Jay started to stand and she did as well. Their eyes locked for a second before Terror closed his. There was pain deeper than his ribs and she could see it. "You've got blood on your collar and you hardly want to talk. There's something wrong and you need someone to be with you. I'm not going to laugh." "Laugh all you want. I'm the next champion, Caroline. Losing sucks because of all the phone calls you get and the people that think there's something wrong. Beyond that though, it's perfectly alright." "But there is something wrong." "Says?" "Says me." The waiter came and Jay shooed him away casually as if they were in the middle of something. "I'm good. You can tell James Bryan. Tell Tim Shipley. Tell Maggie. Tell everyone. I'm not letting a skipped beat mess up my stride." "It's not right to bottle things up like this." "I'm not bottling, I'm moving on. Grudges, remember? Last night was the past and now I'm onto Tim Shipley and the present." "Stop resisting, Jay." "You know, I think I lost my appetite." "Don't leave." "Let's forget me and do you then. Talk about you. You know." "What about me?" "Guys? Life? Say something I don't know." "I'm engaged." He shrugged a moment and then kind of laughed. "Great shoulder you'd make." The rest of their conversation was one-sided and focused on Caroline and the life she'd made post-Jay. He listened to some of it, had a few beers, and left after his sandwich and Bisque was finished. He knew she had good intentions but what she forgot was that Jay had moved past shrinks and psychics. He didn't want the compassion or the kind words. He wanted to let it simmer and let it stay until it truly affected him. It's how he'd always been. There had been a limousine ride, an elevator ride, and three cigarettes in the time between leaving the bistro and returning to his room. Looking at his clock, there were only three hours before the show. His first instinct was to land on the bed and he took the proper steps in doing so. Before falling though, a knock came at the door. Jay sighed. He opened it without asking and to his surprise, Maggie McVay stood in the frame with a box of cigarettes in her hand. She attatched a bow to the front and handed them to her friend. "Here, let's celebrate." "If god had tits and a smoking problem, I'd be ready to die today." "You know they don't let people smoke in the lobby?" "You tried too?" "I think I learned it from you." Jay smiled his first genuine smile of the day and accepted a hug from the widow. She found the television remote first and flicked it on. It was some movie about zombies. "How'd you get to Anaheim?" "I flew. Hope you didn't mind me dipping into your account." "That's why it's there. That and the cigarettes." "Made sure to use it for them too." "You know who else was in Anaheim?" "Oprah?" "Caroline. Remember her? We dated. Didn't work out but it was cool. Called me though and we had lunch." "Any good?" "Lunch was decent, conversation not so much. She's engaged." "Funny how that works." "Exactly." They lit their cigarettes together and watched a minute of the zombie flick to get a feel for it. Neither one felt it. "When do you have to be at the arena, Jay?" He glanced at the clock, "Another two hours and forty-five minutes. Heard I'm facing Shipley tonight." "What happened to gift basket consolation prizes?" "I think it's an Andy Murray thing." "Eh, smoke while you got 'em." "And we got 'em," he said, grabbing his own half-smoked pack. "Wondering. You think they have some guys with resumes just for Zombie flicks? It's all they do maybe?" "If you're good at it keep doing it I guess." "I don't think it'd be all that hard." "To be a zombie?" "That and to be dead. So easy it seems." Maggie broke concentration for a minute to express concern. "You want to die?" "Don't start, Mags." It was too literal and she knew she'd crossed the line. Standing between making Jay happy and bringing him down further, she tip-toed back to safety. "I figure if I go out it's going to be standing down a troop of shotguns. It'd be just the right scene." Jay smirked, "I thought a hail of gunfire was a good idea. Yeah. Maybe a grenade. A real good one though." "You'd be one broken-up zombie for sure." "Naturally." A bunch of zombies on the screen had a group of teenagers cornered. Jay and Maggie shrugged at the irony of it all and smoked another cigarette. "You been getting a lot of calls," she asked. "If you would have called then that'd be the end of it." "I figured." She wanted to dig deeper, find out how he really felt, but she couldn't bounce the train off its tracks. Not with what had happened. Not with Tim Shipley hanging in the balance. She kept calm and remained on neutral ground. "Oprah really is here you know. She's having a book signing." "Hers or someone from book club?" "Hers maybe. Didn't look that hard." "You think she's really annoying in private? I feel like she's talkative, and pushy, and would brag too much if I hung out with her." "I could see it. Funny thought though." "Us?" "Sure. You and Oprah? That's a pair right there." "Guess so. S'like Frank Black and Barry Goldstein or something." "Who?" Terror smiled mid-pull on his cigarette and reeled her in close. She was a perfectly plump cherry sitting on his melted mess of vanilla ice cream. They talked and later watched the movie until time was against them. Jay had to be at the arena and Maggie had to catch a plane home before the morning. There was a goodbye, two cigarettes waving in the hotel lobby, and goodbye again as Jay entered his limo and Maggie rode away in her cab. The Honda center was nearby and only took five minutes to find. Even with his fingers crossed, the arena was nothing new. The air smelled like stale water while the lighting was mediocre and fair. Still, he made his way through the halls, gear bag and all, until he stopped upon a familiar face. She was sparkly in a way he hadn't realized before. In her hand she held a drink. Some kind of coffee. "Tempest, hey." She was surprised some. "Jay. Hello." "Heh. Been a while. You-uh, you doing good?" "Been better. Still sparkly of course. You?" "Same boat, different river I guess. Just settling in though." She chuckled and fanned the cigarette smoked from her face. "That's one way of looking at it I suppose. I recommend raspberry mochachino instead of those things though, if you need an incessant addictive pick me up. Way healthier." "Too bad that's only one and I get twenty of these in a box. Besides, I don't do patches on the neck." They each took a moment to digest it allowing an awkward silence to build. "Don't mean to prod but, that Shipley match. Damn shame. Any pointers though?" "Yeah, watch your back." She smirked. "Although... tonight you may not have to." "Fair enough. Some advice from me too; ditch the belt on Atken and come play up top. Not trying to be nice. Just - I've been there." And a shrug from her followed by, "I don't like Atken. if I bequeath it, it will be to someone I do like. But I take your point. Behave yourself... if that's possible." "You know me. Keep rowing, Sparkles." They each took opposite directions but Jay turned as he heard her voice again. She said something along the lines of, "exactly my point! You do the same," but it was lost under her tiny sparkling luggage wheels. His locker room wore a bronze plate with his name on it and he admired its beauty before unloading inside. As it would be, he was cordially greeted by James Bryan himself. The play by play commentator stood in the center of his room with a pad of gauze and a tub of Vaseline. "Your lip doesn't look too bad. That shirt though." He'd been using it as a napkin and shrugged. "Gives me some character I think." "Trust me, you got plenty of that." Terror took a seat by request and James Bryan grabbed him around the chin. "You ready for the match?" "There's been a lotta' people asking me that." "It's a big one." "You ever watch Just Wrestling?" "I caught a few shows. How come?" "This one night I was booked to fight this guy in a basement, right? Big guy. Hadn't been beaten before." "Missed that one." "Well anyways, it was his hometown. The people were on his side. The locker room was on his side. I think the referee was on his side actually. Pretty sure Shipley cashed out huge that night thanks to him." James Bryan used a q-tip to apply a thin layer of Vaseline to his lip. "He tank you?" "I don't think I ever heard a place so quiet." "So you beat him?" "Pinned him in front of his best friends." "Good story." "Yeah. Anyways, I figure Shipley owes me something after the revenue I drew afterwards. Maybe he'll roll over or something." Bryan wiped his lip of excess Vaseline and tapped Jay on the shoulders. "You're good to go." "Thanks, James." "A word of advice if I may. Shipley's not your average wrestler. He's stronger than you, smarter than you, and pretty much better than you. Stick to what you're good at though. It's enough." "Sure. Yeah." James Bryan left and finally, for the first time all day, Jay Terror was by himself. Looking down, he caught a slight glimmer, a stray sparkle that had landed on his hand. It was gold and glowed under the lights above. On this night there were wars to settle and people to see, but for at least another twenty minutes, Jay Terror was free to be the next World Heavyweight Champion -- no questions asked. --- - The mention of Frank Black is in reference to former GCW superstar Jordan White. - The appearance from Tempest was coordinated with Leah and written by the both of us. - Eat your heart out, Hyde.
