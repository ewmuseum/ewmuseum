Reaping What You Sew

15 July 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 15/07/2008 I Three Days Ago? “Aw man!” Archie exclaimed, emerging from the toilets. He extracted an oil-stained cloth from the back pocket of his dirtied jeans and wiped away the sweat from his brow. “I just took a shit the size of a birthday cake in there!” His friends at the bar screwed up their worn and tired faces in disgust, but this only encouraged him. “Good god, I had to beat the damn thing to death with a shovel just in case it tried to escape into the community!” The bartender cleared his throat and subtly nodded at the woman sat at the bar. But Archie didn’t take the hint. “That was a four flush minimum right there!” “Oh Jesus,” the bartender muttered. He shook his head in dismay and shot the woman an apologetic look. “I’m awful sorry about Archie, ma'am. Damned fool likes to keep us updated is all.” Archie mounted his barstool and develops an innocent expression. “What’d I do now?” The woman chuckled and shook her head. “Don’t worry about it. Boys will be boys after all.” The bar door swung open and granted admittance to the glow of the afternoon sunshine, which proceeded to wash over the unsuspecting bar patrons who reeled back in shock and horror as though they’d never before seen the light of day. A scruffy looking man stumbled into the bar and the sunlight quickly evaporated as the door closed behind him. The man wore a creased black t-shirt, torn blue jeans and black tinted shades, which seemed a little out of place to the casual eye when combined with his unkempt jet-black beard and moustache as well as his out of control hair. He slumped down onto the barstool a couple of spaces over from the woman and gently massaged his forehead. Jacob-fucking-McKail! The woman silently exclaimed, regarding him carefully and trying to maintain her composure. “Bout damn time he showed up. She’d been waiting for two hours for him to show and she was just about sick of trying to ignore the locals ogling her and politely avoiding the bartender’s clumsy advances. Play it cool, Faye. Stick to the plan. The bartender scurried over to serve him right away. “What’ll it be, McKail?” “Bourbon,” he grunted. “Straight.” The woman shook her head. Same bitter drunken bastard as I remember. Good. That’ll make this easier. Or so she told herself -- she didn’t really believe it. “Comin” right up,” the bartender told him. “Hey McKail,” Archie shouted from across the bar, “I was just telling these guys about the dump I just took back there, I--? “Not interest, Archie,” McKail told him. “Jesus Christ,” the bartender muttered. “Will you shut up about that damn turd already” Nobody is interested, okay?” “Hell, I’m just making conversation,” Archie grumbled. McKail took a sip of his bourbon and then went right back to massaging his temple. Out of the corner of his eye he saw the woman sitting a few barstools to his left glaring at him. “Can I help you?” “I just thought you oughta know,” Faye began, “the only people who wear sunglasses inside are assholes and the blind...” “Well I ain’t blind,” McKail replied, not bothering to look at her. Faye grinned. “Guess that makes you an asshole then, huh?” “I’ve been telling him that for weeks!” Archie announced with a chuckle. She ignored him, removed herself from her barstool and took one right next to McKail. Easy girl. Calm down. He’s a drunken hobo, he isn’t going to recognise you. He can’t even recognise himself in a mirror! She held her hand out politely. “The name’s Faye.” McKail glared at her hand, then her face. “Don’t I recognise you?” Fuck! “Probably not,” she lied, “I’m new in town, just passing through.” McKail nodded, silently. He could’ve seen her on the road someplace, but he didn’t bring the subject up. Truth be told, he didn’t much care. He was here for one reason and that reason wasn’t to make friends with beautiful girls. He knows! Fuck...no. Remember. He’s like this. Unapproachable, ignorant, cold. He doesn’t know anything. “So what’s your name?” She finally asked, persevering through the uncomfortable silence. McKail took a mouthful of his bourbon and shook his head, remaining silent all the while. “Don’t take it personal, ma’am,” the bartender told her, seemingly as uncomfortable with the silence as her, “he just ain’t the talkative type is all.” “Good,” she announced, “because I can talk for hours...” At the other end of the bar Archie chuckled to himself. “Oh, he’ll just love that.” Last time he must’ve been drunk, she told herself. Real drunk. It wasn’t really a flattering notion, she realised, but her pride had taken enough of a beating over the last few months to deal with it. In any case, her plan was all that mattered now and if that meant McKail had to be drunk to do it then all the better. “Set my friend here up for another,” she told the bartender. “He looks kinda thirsty to me.” It was the same damned thing she’d set on the night they’d first met, but if McKail remembered he didn’t show it. II Three Months Ago? “Who the fuck are you?” Faye’s eyes flickered open, as consciousness shook her free of her sleep and found herself wrapped around an unfamiliar chest. It was hairier and a whole lost more muscular than her boyfriend Brian’s and for a moment it scared her. But then memories of the night before came flooding back to her, as well as the monstrous hangover that came with it. “Oh god,” she muttered, desperately trying to prevent herself from throwing up right then and there. “Nice.” Faye looked up at Jacob McKail’s face and saw disgust, pity, even contempt. But he wouldn’t look her in the eye. Suddenly, she felt dirty. She removed her head from his chest and drew the s bed sheets closely around herself. He grunted in approval, swung his legs over the side of the bed and reached for his cigarettes. “Hey,” she said, hoping to put herself at ease. “Hey yourself,” came the reply. He put a cigarette his mouth and lit it, before coughing up a lung. Faye didn’t feel very reassured and wondered whether she’d made a mistake. However, before she could really analyse her situation, the after effects of the previous nights drinking caught up with her and she bolted naked out of the bed and into the motel bathroom. “What the--? McKail managed, before the bathroom door slammed shut behind her. “Blllllaaaaaaarrrrrghhhhhhhh...” The sound wasn’t flattering, but at that moment in time she didn’t much care. Her body was purging itself of the toxins and poisons currently swimming around her gut and there wasn’t much she could do about it. If Jacob truly cared about her, he’d understand. If he was the man she thought he was, he’d be sweet about it. He’d-- “I’m gonna get breakfast,” he shouted through the door. “There’s money on the counter for a ride home.” --understand? “Jacob, wait!” she replied, wiping the remaining sick from her mouth. “I’ll come--? she opened the bathroom door and wandered out. He was already gone and there was a hundred dollars too much for a cab fare on the side. She held herself and cried. What have I done? III Two Days Ago? “You’re in danger of becoming a regular,” the bartender told Faye, as she swaggered into the bar. Her summer dress blustered in the afternoon wind and not a man in the place failed to take at least a fleeting glance at her too-fine legs. All except McKail of course. He sat on the same damn barstool as the previous day, staring at the wall and nursing a bourbon. It wasn’t the first of the day for him and it damn sure wouldn’t be the last. She noted that he wore the same clothes as the day before, minus the shades this time. Mustn’t have been that bad of a hangover this morning, she idly noted. “Doesn’t sound much of a danger to me,” Faye replied, pointedly taking the barstool next to McKail. “Besides, where else would I get to hear all about Archie’s bowel movements?” Archie raised a glass in appreciation. “Damn right! That reminds me, I forget to tell you guys about this morning. Goddamn it was like shittin” lava! I--? “She was being sarcastic dumbass,” the bartender informed him. “Not even the doctor wants to know about what kinda god-awful spawn of Satan came out of your butt this mornin”.” “Hey, I was just sayin”...” Faye diverted her attention toward her target. Enough horsing around with locals, there’s work to be done. The previous night she’d accomplished what she’d set out to do; get his attention. Plying the idiot with drinks did that well enough, even if her body didn’t. He was a little talkative, but not much. She wanted to hear his story all again to make sure it wasn’t just another one of his lies. She wanted to know about his wife and his wrestling career. The last time he’d told her some of the story, but she could tell there was more to it than that and she had to know it all. She had to know what made him tick, in order for her to destroy him. Just like he destroyed me, she reminded herself. “Long time, no see stranger,” she greeted with a playful smile. “Thought you were just passin” through?” McKail responded, his eyes still fixed to the wall. He took a mouthful of bourbon and winced at the after effects. “I am,” she confirmed. “It’s just I’ve not found what I came looking for yet.” McKail allowed a half-smile. “And what might that be?” She returned a sultry, yet playful smile. “Wouldn’t you like to know?” “Not really,” he replied, finishing his drink. Play it cool, girl. Play it cool. “Good,” she told him, “because I wasn’t gonna tell ya anyhow.” This time McKail allowed a chuckle. Gottcha you bastard! “So what’s your story, McKail” How come you’re littering up this place all day and all night?” She watched his chuckle evaporate into the air liked a burst bubble and smiled inside; she was setting him up for the killer blow. “Don’t you have a wife to get home to?” McKail closed his eyes and looked downward. Faye thought he was going just start blubbering like a baby right then and there and when he didn’t, she felt a little disappointed. She wanted to witness his tears first hand, she wanted to see his pain up close and personal and she wanted to see him ruined. Not yet, she told herself, but soon. Very soon. “Oh dang!” she cursed innocently. “Did I just say something wrong?” “It’s okay,” he told her. “You weren’t to know.” Ha ha, that’s what you think! “Weren’t to know what?” “His wife died a couple of years back,” the bartender informed. “Cancer.” Stay out of this, dispshit! “Oh my god that’s terrible. What was her name?” McKail glanced up from his emptied bourbon glass and looked the woman in the eye. His head told him to jump back into defensive mode. To shut down and blow it off like he always did. Talking about it would just open up a whole heap of shit that you do not want to wade through. But his heart told him something entirely different. It told him to just go right ahead and do it. Spill your guts. Trust her, confide in her and get at least a fraction of the burden off your chest. Faye lay a reassuring hand on his forearm. “It’s okay, Jacob.” That’s right you bastard, you can trust me! “You don’t have to tell me anything you don’t want to.” But nevertheless he did. He told her about Mary and how she died. He told Faye about promising Mary on her deathbed that he would achieve his dream, he’d come out of retirement and achieve success that’d he’d only dreamed of before. He told her about being an alcoholic, about being a damned loser. He told her he was waiting for death to take him just so he could be with her again. VI Two Months Ago? It was the biggest mistake of Faye’s life, she knew that now. How could she not? Her boyfriend left her, the bar she worked at fired her, her family had pretty much disowned her and she felt alienated from her friends. And for what? A stupid night of lust with a man she thought was somebody else, a man she thought was different from all the rest. What an idiot she had been. She just wanted to forget it all, put Jacob McKail in the past and move on. But how the hell could she? The bastard was all over the television, competing in wrestling organisation she liked to watch; PTC: Infinite Gauntlet and GCW Worldwide to name but a few. Besides, every time she thought of her family and friends, hell, even that goddamn stinking job, his damned face was always there, fixed in her head. And then there were the nightmares, reliving her god-awful experience with the bastard over and over again every night. It was too much. It got to the point where she was scared of going to sleep at night. She was an emotional wreck, she couldn’t get a new job or a new boyfriend and she was a stones throw away from living out on the streets. No, Faye told herself sternly. You’re better than that. You need to move on, you need to put all of this behind you. But how could she? It was there at her lowest point in this whole sorry affair she hatched her plan. It was devious and cruel and everything Jacob-fucking-McKail deserved. She grabbed her bag and packed it. She was going to see this through to the end and had no intention of coming back to this place. V Yesterday? Faye stood outside the bar and watched McKail sitting at the same damn barstool throwing his life away. Her bags were packed and her cab had been called. She’d be out of this god-awful place within the hour and she couldn’t wait. She’d arranged to meet him here today, to have another drink with fool and allow him to coax her back to his motel room. Then she would’ve ended it, or so she told herself. Perhaps she’d never know whether she’d go through with it or not and that suited her just fine. Last night he’d opened up to her and had told her his life was a living hell full of misery and bitterness. Sure, she knew about his dead wife but she sure as hell didn’t know just how goddamned awful his life had become because of it. My plan would’ve been far less cruel on the sorry son of a bitch, she’d realised once she returned back to her hotel room. He wanted death, he wanted to be with his dead wife again and she wasn’t about to fulfil the bastard’s greatest desire. Fuck that, she told herself. Fuck him. In the bar, she watched the bartender give McKail her note and grinned as his face instantly grew dark and grim. She remembered what it read and wondered which part had hurt him the most: Dear Jacob, A couple of months back in a night of drunken stupidity, we fucked. You obviously can’t remember it and I’m glad of it. But that doesn’t matter now. You ruined my life, Jacob. You took away everything I loved and cared about and then just walked away like nothing had ever happened. I came here to find you, I came her looking for justice, but I see justice is already being done. Your life is a disaster. You’re bitter and twisted and Mary would be ashamed of you. You’ve cut yourself off from the world and you’re dead inside. What more could I have possibly done to you to worsen things? You’ll never know how close you came to getting exactly what you wanted. Faye. She removed the handgun from her handbag and dropped the thing into the nearest trashcan. She wouldn’t need that now. Faye could get on with her life. She could start again, she could live her life. It was more than she could say for McKail. END
