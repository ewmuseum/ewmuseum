A Family Affair

22 May 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5-22-07 Where do we go from here? Sixteen years in a profession that can be so physically draining. Do you retire and take the money you have saved, or do you continue on? You’re thirty-one and you have only one dependent in your life, a daughter that you adopted six years ago. Do you continue to work just to support her through college and her endeavors, or do you throw it all down the drain? The scene opened inside of a small apartment, not the most luxurious of places. It looked to have been decorated by a woman, and the books on the table looked to be those of a college student. Michelle-Marie Ramey, Jesse Ramey’s adopted daughter came into the camera’s view with a confused look on her face. “Dad,” Michelle began, “what are you doing?” Jesse quickly sat up from couch, swung his hair back, and wiped the crust out of his, “Oh, nothing. I guess I just dozed off.” “Dozed off?” Michelle chuckled, “Looked like you were dead asleep to me. Shouldn’t you be getting ready for your match this week?” “Yeah,” Jesse said leaning back on the couch, “I probably should, but don’t you think your dad should have a little down time with his family every now and again?” “In all honesty, I think you need to spend a little less time with your family and at the gym, and more time looking for someone who is going to make you happy in life, dad.” Michele sighed, “You need to find someone to spend the rest of your life with.” Jesse smiled, “Now why in the world would I want to do that when I’ve got someone to spend the rest of my life with. You’re the only woman I need, honey.” Michelle laughed and then sat down on the couch next to Jesse, “You know what I mean dad. You’ve always done right by me and I think it’s about time you start doing something for yourself. You’ve worked your fingers to the bone just to support the two us and honestly I don’t see how you’ve done it.” “That’s exactly why I’ve done it,” Jesse paused for a moment and wrapped his arm around his daughter, “I’ve had to support us. I wasn’t going to let there be a day go by when there wasn’t going to be food on that table. I didn’t want to see you go through the same kind of life that I had to deal with growing up.” “Well, dad, it’s not like I haven’t been there before.” Michelle paused while reflecting on her life, “If it hadn’t been for you I don’t know where I would be right now. I definitely know I wouldn’t be going to college right now. Heck, before you came along I figured I would probably end up living on the streets as soon as that orphanage kicked me out.” “Well, you don’t have to worry about that now, because you’ve got me and I’ve got you.” Jesse smiled looking Michelle in the eyes, “Now don’t you go out looking for someone and find a guy who is going to break your heart. I’d hate to have to break someone’s nose for hurting you.” Jesse winked at Michelle, who in turn chuckled. “Don’t worry dad, I’m not like most of my friends. I don’t go out drinking and try to find guys at the clubs. I’m usually the one who has to keep the guys from taking my friends home.” “Good girl,” Jesse smiled, “I guess you picked up at least one trait from me over the years. Always respect yourself and try to help others respect themselves. This world is so crappy right now and morals are at such a low that most people don’t care what happens to them at any given time.” “Dad, can I ask you something?” Michelle questioned Jesse. “Sure, honey, go ahead.” Jesse looked a little perplexed at what his daughter’s question could be. “Why did you pick up a second job anyway?” “Well,” Jesse started, “you know me. I’ve always been a competitor and with all of the down time we’ve been having at the Legacy of Champions I needed something more consistent and I figure with that being a set place to wrestle each week I’ve got enough time to go on the road as well.” “I just hope you know what you’re getting yourself into, dad.” Michelle sighed, “I’d hate to see you go on wrestling and injure yourself. Lord knows the last time that happened you were a pain in the ass to have around all the time. You’re so freaking annoying when you won’t let anyone help you do anything. If it wasn’t for your promotions ordering you to go to therapy I doubt you would even do that.” Jesse chuckled, “You’re right about that. I’ve always been very self reliant and I don’t think that will ever change. When I’m on my death bed I’ll probably be swinging a bed pan at the nurse who is trying to change my diaper swearing up and down that I can do it myself.” Michelle cringed, “Not a mental image that I really wanted to take in, but I could honestly see you doing that.” Michelle chuckled, “I just feel like you’re digging yourself into an early grave though and I don’t want to see you do that.” “Honey, come on. You watch wrestling with me; you don’t honestly think that’s true do you” I mean, come on, Ric Flair and Hulk Hogan are still doing this thing. Both of those guys are in their fifties and I’m only in my thirties. I figure I’ve still got a good twenty years under my belt, so you’ve got nothing to worry about.” “Yeah, very true.” Michelle paused for a moment, “Just do me a favor and take it easy on yourself. You’re not like those two guys in anyway.” “How is that, sweetie?” Jesse questioned. “Well,” Michelle chuckled, “you can wrestle and neither of those two guys can.” Jesse laughed, “Oh, that was such a shot below the belt, but in all honesty I couldn’t agree with you anymore on that matter.” “Well, just rest up some dad. I’m going to head out to the store and pick up some stuff for dinner tonight. So, if you decide to leave just be back in time to eat with me. I’d hate to eat alone, again.” Jesse smiled, “I can’t help it if I get stuck at the gym sometimes doing a little more than I should, but I promise I’ll be home tonight for dinner. I need to spend more time with you before I have to go back on the road. However, I am going back to gym. So, we may as well just leave together. I can walk to you the store and then I’ll go spend a little time at the gym, babe.” Jesse got up off the couch and then helped Michelle get up off the couch. Jesse took a moment to stretch and then the two left the apartment together.
