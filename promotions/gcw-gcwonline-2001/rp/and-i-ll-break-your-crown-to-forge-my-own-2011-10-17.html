And I'll break your crown to forge my own.

17 October 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/17/11 Terrence Kingsley sits in his seat, the cabin of the short bus vibrating from pockmarked strips of highway. He's pale, nothing more than tight sinew thatched to bone. His ragged plaid shirt hangs from the stoop of his shoulders. His cheeks are round with childhood fat and thick tufts of dirty brown beard. He grips the vinyl upholstery and it whines under the heels of his hands. The swells over and around and underneath his eyes make it hard to focus on the distance. All he sees is his own face in window's reflection. He's leaking blood down the bridge of his nose but he doesn't feel it. And neither do I. And I appreciate the words of Billy, and of Katie, and of Justin who handed me a win over Katie and Sean, and of Dan because he knows I speak the truths and I hope he wins this because he deserves this for how hard he's worked to get better. Because he doesn't care right now whether he wins or loses, just that he does the best piece he can do. I'm sick of playing to the level, of focusing on the minute week to week pieces rather than the branching story. Of worrying that I show my readers that I care as much as my opponent does because I will throw words upon words upon words just to keep the page length long. Of judging based upon a word count, or a scene total, rather than enjoying the organic process of growing and becoming better and learning each and every time. And while Billy handed me this golden opportunity to take hold of the brass ring of being a World Champion for the first time in PTC, and while I flushed it down the toilet, I'm not going to fake another round of this. I'm not squeezing blood from a dried stone. I place blame on myself solely. The fire didn't burn hot enough for me to care. But if you want to be a standard, you have to stand as one. No more comparing to the rest of the field. No more playing this to win. No more petulant behavior rotting the guts of the last place this kind of writing can take place. Tournaments are tournaments, and they come and go and I've won and lost them. One on one against people, that's easy. Too easy. Whether it is seeding, or bracketing, the energy always disappears. I've only been in one Dangerous Games match, and I finished second in it. Just like the Halo, though that happened twice over in two different generations. I'll gladly fold my hand now, not because the contest isn't worthy, but because I don't want a single question to be left lingering. I don't want a single person who can sit there and second guess. You can't hide when you go against everyone. I'll detox for now, enjoy the quiet spaces. Gather my energy and keep my head out of the political muck that ruined much of this hobby like a cancer. See you at Dangerous Games.
