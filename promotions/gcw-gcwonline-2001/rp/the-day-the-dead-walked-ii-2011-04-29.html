The Day the Dead Walked II

29 April 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/29/11 Inks sat in the passenger seat absorbing all that he can from the visit to Potter’s Field. Something is irking him more than anything in the world, something about this entire case is starting to bother him. Something almost supernatural, something almost unbelievable. As he sits in the seat and tries to piece together the puzzle a call comes over the radio. “We have a 187 at a North Lawrence home, victims DOA.” Wilson grabs the radio, “419 on our way.” Wilson gets the address into the GPS, turns on the lone light, blares the siren, and heads towards the crime scene like a bat out of hell. “Hey man, you can slow down, you heard the call, DOA.” Wilson looks over at Inks and shakes his head, “What’s been your problem lately man?” Inks keeps the rest of the ride in confidence. Things have been bad lately for Inks, but he needs to realize to separate work from home. However, his failure to do that would never have put him in the predicament he’s already in. They arrived at a palatial, well landscaped suburban house in a neighborhood that fit’s the same mold. Wilson looks at the house, chuckles, “I wonder if they will make us take our shoes off.” He gives Inks a small punch to the shoulder than heads up the steps. Inks is amazed at his surroundings and how people can afford to live in places like this especially with the state of today’s economy. The grass looks almost like turf, the hedges are precisely trimmed, not one branch a fraction of a centimeter different from each other. The rose bushes are in full bloom, spewing pink, red, and yellow out of his branches. The front step resembles the Lincoln Memorial, huge pillars stretching what seems like miles into the air. Wilson and Inks crawl under the crimes scene tape and walk in the front door. The stench was overwhelming, but the visual was even worse. For the first time ever, well as long Inks has known him, Wilson was speechless. They walked into what looked like a scene from a slasher film. Blood was splattered all over the living room, some of the blood so thick it was nearly black. Inks turned his back and stepped outside for a split second. The room started to shrink and get warm. The smell gave him a nauseous feeling, but the site was too much for anyone to bare. Wilson poked his head outside the door. “You OK man?” Inks nodded, “Yeah sorry, just a little intense.” Wilson nodded. Inks walked back inside with Wilson as the local police were taking pictures and dusting around for prints. Wilson looks around, and asks the room which is now filling up with all sorts of officials, EMT’s, and even the coroner van showed up, “Who was the first on the scene?” “I was sir.” A young cop, most likely a year or two from his rookie graduation, walked towards Wilson. He was a good looking kid, mid 20’s maybe, short dark hair, green eyes, and clean shaven. He extended his hand and it was received by Wilson. “How long?” “I got here maybe 20 mins ago.” “What was the initial call?” “Well I was in the area heard a domestic call come through the radio. Neighbor complained of screaming, so she called us. We’ve been out here before. He’s a rich lawyer that likes to smack her around when he’s drunk. She’s an editor of some upscale magazine.” Wilson was scribbling down everything he heard as Inks moved about the room looking for anything that seemed off. “Go on.” “I knocked a few times, rang the doorbell and the dog was going nuts. I noticed the window was open, and I took a peek inside. That’s when I saw the blood on that recliner over there.” He pointed towards the leather recliner. “I kicked the door in and saw this mess, that’s when I called for backup. Here’s the wife’s body.” The young deputy walks through a small hallway that leads towards the kitchen. Lying in the middle of the floor, missing her head and hands was Alison Windsor(as she was later ID’d) face down, her clothes missing and flesh wounds all over her body. Wilson circles the body as the coroner drops the gurney to wheel her out. He crouches down and looks at the wounds all over her body. “Looks like a small knife, most likely something household. Where’s her head?” “In here!” Wilson stood up and looked down the hall at his partner looking inside the refrigerator. “The entire family.” All the officers rushed into the kitchen and stared into the fridge. Husband, wife and two children, both under the age of 12. Their heads stared at them, The young deputy, wretches and turns around and vomits in the sink. His body heaves a few times, than stops. He wipes his mouth and runs the water to put all his vomit down the drain. “Sorry. I searched the house, but none of the other bodies are around. That’s new to me, I never would have guessed to look in there.” Inks calls the coroners over and moves out of their way as they remove the severed heads in evidence bags. “In most killings like these where body parts are removed they are removed as a sign for the authorities. In this case the unsub knew we would not check in here until way later which gave us an opportunity to ponder where the severed parts are. In all actuality if someone is killed and has body parts removed in their very home chances are the remains are either in the fridge, like what Dahmer did, buried like what the Lundgren cult killers or Gacy did, or taken with the murderer as a keepsake for their own personal amusement like what Fish and Gein did. The unsub is going to toy with us.” Another officer looked over at Inks, “So you are saying there’s going to be more bodies?” Inks nods, “Yeah, a lot more.” “Jesus Christ!” Wilson walked away from the pack and started to look around the room. He looks outside and see’s nothing but acres and miles of woods. “He’s watching us right now.” The group of officers almost in unison said, “What?” “Yeah, something in my gut is telling me he’s watching us right now. From out there.” The Lieutenant of the local police, LT. Mizzino got on his radio and asked for an immediate search of the perimeter including police dogs. The young deputy raises his voice over the rumble of the crowd, “Why isn’t there any boot prints or footprints with all this blood? It’s quite obvious he walked around, there’s no way in hell he could tiptoed and missed something.” Inks looks over at the deputy, “Well that’s why we are here, but you’re right kid, we are missing something, something important…but what? Most importantly where are the other three bodies? We need cadaver dogs and some construction equipment, they have to be around here somewhere, hauling three dead bodies out of this house in such a short window of time is next to impossible, unless there‘s an accomplice?” Back at the office Inks and Wilson are reviewing both cases. Most homicide detectives don’t work on more than one case at a time, but these past few years have been different. So many cuts have come across governors and mayors desks, and for some reason they always cut the police and rescue forces for whatever reason. So now Inks and Wilson have to work on two murder cases, both in the same day. Inks is rubbing his temples, it’s been a 14 hour day and the exhaustion from a weeks worth of zero to little sleep is starting to catch up to him. He chugs more than one energy drink at a time. He takes numerous energy shots a day followed by a ton of coffee, yet he’s still zombie like as of late. Wilson stands up, stretches, than sits back down. “Ok look, let’s divide these. You take the morgue murder and exhumation and I’ll take the beheadings. You really think there’s an accomplice?” Inks wipes the sleep from his eyes. “I don’t know, just seems unlikely that he takes those bodies out with him. Maybe the unsub kept the hands for his own demonic pleasures, but three bodies? So in one day we have a murderer that leaves things at the crime scene like at the morgue, and another that takes things. Two totally different worlds within 20 minutes of each other. What’s our pension like again?” Wilson chuckles and begins to review his notes. “Unless…….” Wilson lifts his head and peers over at his partner, waiting for what initiated the unless…. “Unless they are attached. What if it’s the same person for both?” “How do you mean? You even said it’s two totally different styles. The M.O’s are different in each murder” “We may not be dealing with your average run of the mill killer..serial killer whatever the media will label him. He’s sloppy yet clean, he’s intelligent, but a step behind as well. He’s a sociopath that’s still trying to find his calling.” “Ok you got my ear, go on.” “Ok listen here. Bryan O’Neil was an enormous man. Nearly 7 feet tall, well over 300 pounds. His body ends up missing and is replaced with a bludgeoned victim. The victims at the house were beheaded, but it wasn’t clean. The unsub sawed away at their necks before the with what is most likely the same knife or knives that were used to stab the victim at the house.” “Ok now you’ve got me lost man.” “The first family that O’Neil killed he beat them to death. Than he started to hack away at them. We found all sorts of body parts in this little shed out in the woods. This week is the year anniversary of when we killed him at the strip joint.” “Oh yes, I remember now. So you are saying we have a copycat?” “No I am saying we have copycats. No one person can dig up his body, remove it, than behead 4 people and remove 3 of the four bodies without a little help. You remember the cult following O’Neil had online called The Agents of Oblivion? ” “Yeah weren’t they setting up some kind of funding for his burial? They are a bunch of devil worshipping teenagers I do recall.” Inks nods, and opens the file on Bryan O’Neil. “ What if they are doing this in homage of their hero? People killed for Manson, why not O’Neil? Get someone from IT and to get a check on to see if the Agents of Oblivion are still lurking. Call the locals and tell them to get a search of the areas. Footprints in the grass, tire marks from a van perhaps, blood droplets outside, than find out if they found anything in the woods behind the house.” Wilson smacks his hands together and let’s out a loud “WOOOO!!” Inks turns towards him and squints his eyes in confusion. “What was that all about?” Wilson smiles, gets up, puts his coat on, “That’s the partner I’m use to! Get home man, I’ll take this, you look like shit.”’ Wilson jabs Inks shoulder again and heads out the door. Inks let’s out a smile, followed by a yawn. Wilson was right, he needs to sleep, and if his thoughts are right about this, he’s going to need some sleep. Inks almost fell asleep twice at the wheel. Luckily it’s almost 3AM and there’s barely anyone on the roads tonight. The only thing he can think of is his bed. Not the case, not the fact his wife took the kids and left, nothing. He’s becoming delirious and needs some sleep. 4 hours will do just fine and he can spend the rest of the day tomorrow working the case. Inks puts the keys into the door of his new apartment, opens the door and tosses the keys onto the island in the kitchen. Boxes are still littered all over the building because he has not had anytime to do any unpacking. He never bothers to even turn on the lights, he just stretches and heads blindly towards the bathroom to take a piss, brush his teeth than hit the couch. He rubs his face, than turns on the light and is met with something he has never seen in his life. Dangling from the ceiling is the a beheaded man, the body stripped, the hands missing and a note stapled to his chest. Written sloppily on the paper in what looks like magic marker or Sharpie is, “You thought it was over, it’s not over, I came back, I brought my axe.” Inks backs up, bumping into the wall of the hallway. He never see’s the blunt object or the person swinging it. He feels the contact, see’s a flash, than black as his body folds to the ground like an accordion.
