The Journey's End?

December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: December 2005 Some time in December 2005 Virgil Ambrosi’s residence in Las Vegas, Nevada The living room was ornately decorated in a style that danced a border somewhere between 'designer chic' and 'overly gaudy.' A fire roared in the fireplace despite the fact that the owner of this house had a very distinct loathing for all things involving fire, considering his... peculiar situation. The two women who were in the living room were all too aware of his peculiar situation, for they had dealt with him before, and besides, had met many people with situations more peculiar than his own. Being a vampire was small change in the world of Sanda and Thuya- singularly named, exotic-looking women imported to the States from Myanmar. By trade, they were assassins, but in truth, they worked a job somewhere in between assassin and what the U.S. Army might have called 'Special Ops.' He'd called them in because he was at his wits' end. Sanda was seated in one of the armchairs, bolt upright. Her eyes were wide and almost innocent looking, a mirror of her twin sister's. Her back was not touching the fabric back of the chair- a reminder. She was there for business, not to get comfortable with his hospitality. Thuya hadn't even bothered to take a seat. Instead, she paced on the rug in front of the fire, her stiletto-heeled boots occasionally thudding on the hardwood as she paced a little wide of the rug's borders. Her slim form was silhouetted in a black catsuit, her long hair tied back away from her face, and her eyes slitted in a half-distasteful expression. Virgil would have assumed that they didn't like him, but he didn't think it was really safe to make any kind of assumption about how the two of them thought. Even to him, they were mysteries. “What did you call us here for?” Thuya said, her voice clipped. Plainly, she wanted to get this over with, to get down to business before Virgil broke out the vintage wines and bored her with elegant entertaining. Virgil turned his somewhat concerned expression to Thuya, not surprised that she was once again all business. After all, that had been one of the things about her that had always enchanted him, in a business sense. When someone needed to die, disappear, or other suspicious activity, there was no need for civility. “I want you to find someone,” he said, narrowing his eyes slightly, “someone who is likely doing her most to keep from being found.” The last few weeks had been driving him to the brink of insanity, searching endlessly for a woman that knew how to keep herself hidden. Thuya folded her arms over her chest. “We are not a missing persons' service, Senor Ambrosi.” Virgil smiled, dragging the tip of a sharpened fingernail over the leather arm of his favorite chair. “I’m not asking you to be. The two of you are skilled far more than myself at finding targets, and killing them... I’m only saving you the trouble of the last step.” Thuya half-bared her teeth at him. There were no fangs there, but it was a gesture just as intimidating if only because of how clearly it indicated her displeasure with this arrangement. “Flattery does nothing to serve us.” Sanda, who had been quiet all this time, leaned forward a little, casting a quizzical look at the man who had hired them for various jobs before, and was now hiring them for something that seemed a little too... tame? “Senor Ambrosi... Let me put it to you plainly. Why should we do this for you” What's in it for us?” Virgil held out his hands, gesturing to them knowingly. He knew both of their situations, and perhaps the most powerful weapon he had in this negotiation was knowing what they were in need of at the moment. “There are, of course, some things you need here... Myanmar is a place in which it’s easier to hide oneself, but it’s hardly a convenient location for two in your trade. I can offer you one of my properties in a secure part of the country, and I will gladly outfit you with what supplies you need as well... I’m not asking you for much.” Sanda nodded, sitting back slightly, but not losing any of the rigidity of her posture. There were some things that could not be bred out of one, especially when they were bred into one by years of rigorous training. Thuya stood directly in front of the fireplace, legs apart, arms folded over her chest, and her big, dark eyes focused directly on his face. They held an expression that said she knew what a hard spot they were in, how stupid they would be to turn him down. They also said that she knew that he knew it too, in their half-stony glare. “We need details, then. Then, we will discuss whether or not it is as simple as you say.” He nodded, secretly relieved that his offer had been as successful as he’d hoped, though showing no outward sign of it. There was no weakness in this room from the assassins, and there would be none from him. “Perhaps it will be... I do not have the espionage skills that the two of you are renowned for, and so I haven’t had the opportunity to investigate the target location any further than the top level.” He pressed his hands together, sighing. “There is a bar on one of the routes heading out of Las Vegas called Hell’s Mouth. Inside, you will find a ghoul who calls himself Dominik. What happens to him doesn’t concern me... What I want to know is the identity of his master, and what lies under the hollow floor of the bar. I have my suspicions, due to the fact that Dominik very obviously played the fool, that the woman I want you to find is underneath the bar. I want you to find out.” “How do you know she is underneath it?” Sanda asked, perking up from her passive position once more, brows furrowed as she took in all the details with her practically encyclopedic memory. What she forgot, Thuya would remember. Virgil turned his eyes toward her, keeping the same expression. He hoped they were as thorough in their work as they were in their negotiations. “I do not know, per se, but in such a place as I’m sending you to, the likely places for someone to hide are very few and far between. There are practically no other places that she could possibly be.” They both nodded briefly, and shared a glance. “What is her importance to you?” Thuya asked. It wasn't as though she particularly cared about Virgil's personal relationship with the woman that they were to find... she was simply asking to find out whether or not they should rough her up once she was found, or if this was a matter of a more... delicate nature. “Personal reasons... She is not an informant, and thus she is not to be harmed. It’s not very likely that she’ll attack you... If she does, you should take care to mention me.” Thuya raised a brow. “And what sort of force should we be expecting if she does attack?” Sanda questioned. “One of your own, perhaps?” Thuya followed up, the glimmer of white teeth once again showing her disdain. Virgil nodded, slightly proud... After all, it was slightly flattering to be powerful enough to cause concern, but that was for another time. “Moreso than myself... But if you come across her, it’s unlikely that she’ll even be conscious of her surroundings. One such as her could not be restrained otherwise. As for the master of this place, do not confront him. The moment you find anything that could lead me to him, leave and contact me at once.” They both nodded again, slipping into a cold contemplation of the task. Once they were free of Virgil's presence, they would discuss strategy, and from there, split their efforts into two to complete the plan more quickly. From Virgil's demeanor, it was plain that this required speed. Sanda stood, the cue that the deal was done. She stared Virgil down for a moment. “We will do our utmost, Senor. We have never failed you yet, and do not intend to do so this time. You have our word of honor that we will return this information to you as soon as we have it.” As that was really all that she needed to stay, Sanda headed for the door of the study, ready to leave the house and get down to business. Thuya, however, lingered for a moment. “You make large promises, Senor,” she said, her eyes slightly narrowed, “I hope you can keep them as well as we keep ours.” Turning coldly, she stalked out, an angrier exact mirror of her sister. Virgil watched them leave, impressed by the way that they handled their business... There was nothing to say at this point. Nothing needed to be said. They knew their mission, and he knew what he owed them upon its inevitable completion. They had never disappointed him before, as Sanda had said, and he had no expectation that this time would be any different. Now, all he could do was wait. ---------- A few days ago... In a desert plain to the east of Las Vegas Once again, he was alone, in the deserts near Las Vegas, what had become practically his home, despite everything he could have wished. To him, there was no choice in the matter, no possible other course of action but to wander like a delirious madman, not knowing whether he was progressing toward his goal or only hindering himself. Every day had become the same. Wander the dark sands, searching for some sign that she’d been there, some tiny indication of her continued well-being, or even life, and retire with the rising of the sun, to stew and finally give in to fitful sleep sometime in the late morning. He had begun to lose hope, despite the fact that theoretically, he could search for eternity. One thing stood strongly in his mind as a source of confusion. Some time ago, he’d come across a bar called Hell’s Mouth. Inside, there had been a dodgy ghoul, and a hollow floor, coupled with the unmistakable scent of blood. In the middle of a desert, it seemed completely ridiculous for an establishment to cater to his kind, much less not be full if it was doing so. Thus, he concluded, there was no other explanation that something must lie under it. Tonight had been slightly more productive, as now, he finally had some kind of clue where to look... Suspicious places had been nonexistant here. Aside from the occasional piece of tumbleweed that carried the faint scent of blood from some random brush with carrion in the desert, sensory input had been at a loss. His eyes scanned the spindly scrub grass of the desert, a slightly cool breeze drifting lazily through the dry night air. A flicker of moonlight danced off of something in the grass... A piece of metal? He immediately started toward it, his pace gradually picking up as he got closer, recognizing it as much more than a piece of errant scrap that had been blasted by strong winds from Las Vegas. As he approached further, he could feel the blood racing through his veins, as his mind glowed with fanciful imaginations of what he had just stumbled upon. He slashed the grass away with his long claws, eyes wide at what lay underneath. A motorcycle... And a very particular one. He recognized the smooth lines of the shell of a sportbike that he had repaired once, what seemed like eons ago. It was a sportbike that belonged to one Dawn McFarlane, which had last been seen blasting out of the McFarlane estate driveway, with Lia Vlaszy in the driver’s seat. He smiled, and turned toward the tiny black shape in the hazy distance, where the neon sign that read “Hell’s Mouth” glowed in the night. ---------- A week later... The phone hummed, practically leaping out of Virgil's pocket as he walked down the street. He scowled, not amused at all at how it had surprised him, purposefully, maliciously. He snatched it from his coat, and flipped it open. “Hola.” The taut nervousness in his voice was quite evident. He felt like he was on pins and needles, waiting, waiting, waiting. But the wait, luckily for him, was over. From the other end of the phone the clipped, quiet, steady voice came to him. “Our objective is complete, Senor Ambrosi.” Whether it was Sanda or Thuya was hard to tell. But it didn't really matter. Slowly, the voice continued, maddeningly slowly. “We cannot confirm the status of your correspondent. You were correct in your assumptions about the young man and his master.” Virgil’s expression was unchanging, although at this very moment, it was difficult to keep his stoic fa’ade going. He struggled for the words to reply. “Your speed never fails to amaze me. I will meet you in the place I indicated in less than an hour.” No confirmations were needed. Sanda or Thuya only needed to be instructed once, and Virgil knew they would be waiting for him when he arrived at the spot where he had found Dawn’s motorcycle. And, reminded of her, he flipped open his cell phone, and dialed a number, the thought that he didn’t particularly care for Dawn not concerning him. The rings were slowly paced, as the connection shot across the Pacific Ocean. “Hello?” “Dawn.” “Um... Yes?” “We’ve found her.”
