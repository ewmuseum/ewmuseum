The Lost Years Pt 4 - Past Imperfect

2 May 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 05/02/11 The nights were getting harder for me. I hadn’t had a full night’s sleep in months. Some nights, it’s dreams of Keiko and Dan if they were alive today. Other nights, it’s the looks on Mike and Samantha’s faces as Serena’s casket was lowered into the ground. Every time, I wake up, look over at the empty space next to me, and fight the urge to call Toronto for the fifth time that day. I’ve found that in my life, when things are going too good, that everything will eventually come crashing down. I pulled myself out of bed, threw on some cold weather running gear, and continued a routine that I started the same night that the dreams did. I figured if I couldn’t rest like I would like to, I would drain myself of so much energy that it would be impossible for me not to sleep. It didn’t usually work, but I figured I would try anyway. As I was about to head out, my phone started ringing. I looked at the screen and saw that it was from Toronto, but I didn’t recognize the number. I answered, but the conversation didn’t go on too long. I found myself running…only it was to get to my car. I didn’t even pack, something was wrong, and I didn’t even think about waiting to get there. “She Needs Rest” I drove through the night through Ontario, the only thoughts that were going through my mind was what the doctor had told me on the phone. Araceli had been rushed to the hospital and that there may be something wrong with the baby. She didn’t tell me anything more. I looked down at my speedometer; I was easily doing more than the legal limit in Ontario. If I was driving a DeLorean, I probably would have ended up in 1955. All I know is that if a cop caught up with me, I’d be in deep shit. Luckily, it was early enough in the morning that I didn’t have to worry about traffic. I only worried about getting to Toronto. I finally arrived at the hospital shortly after 7 in the morning. The young lady at the reception desk pointed me out to where Araceli’s room was. I got there, and saw that she was asleep. Seeing the IVs in her arm made me worry. “You must be the father?” a somewhat sweet sounding voice came from behind me. I recognized it from the phone call. “We were able to stabilize both her and the baby.” I honestly don’t remember what I said after that. I just felt a lot of gratitude that they were both ok. “She needs rest. Please, follow me.” I was obedient to the doctor’s request as she led me back to the waiting area. “Ms. Black was severely dehydrated when she came in, and was running a fever. There was an infection, but I believe that we are battling it the best we can.” I couldn’t think straight. All I kept thinking was that I should start thinking about finding a place in Canada so that I can be there for her more. “She’s going to be here for a few days, we want to make sure that everything goes fine with the treatment. Due to the pregnancy, we’re going to go carefully, so that no further stress is put on your unborn daughter.” I looked up at the doctor. “I’m going to have a daughter?” At that point, I didn’t know. I knew that Araceli didn’t want to know the sex of the baby until she was born. I was always curious, but I followed her wishes. “Mr. Ryan, from here on out, Constance is going to need bed rest. Otherwise, she may find herself back, and we don’t know if your daughter could survive it.” I knew that I had a decision to make. I parted with the doctor and found my way back to her room. I sat next to her and held her hand while she slept. Hours went by, but I didn’t have the inclination to leave. It was around ten when she opened her eyes. “Hey there.” she said with a soft voice. “So, Where Do We Go From Here” I spent the next few days in Toronto. Every day going to the hospital, seeing how Araceli was doing. I kept my mouth shut about finding out that we were going to have a daughter. She was set to leave the hospital though, and I knew we had to talk about the next few months. “So, where do we go from here?” she asked as she finished off the breakfast that the hospital had prepared for her. I didn’t have an answer for her. “I look at two options. I move here, or you move back with your parents. You need someone around to take care of you.” I knew there was a third option, the one that I wanted her to go with, but I wasn’t going to press her into something like that. I knew that she wanted our child to be born here in Canada. After all, this was home for her. “Dorian, with what’s going on with my parents, I know that I can’t move in with them. And I know you can’t afford to move here either.” I could see the tears coming to her eyes, as it looked like she would have to betray her feelings on having her daughter born in her home. “Maybe, do you think that…” “I can call them.” I knew I didn’t need to, Jason had brought up to me months prior that if something happened and Araceli wasn’t able to stay in Canada, that she could stay with us. I walked over to her and sat on the bed. My hand, lying on top of hers, you could see that she was having a hard time with this. “Why are you here?” The tone in her voice wasn’t one of someone that was trying to push me away, but of someone that didn’t think that this was going to be what life would hold for her. “What do you mean?” “Anyone else in my life would have been miles away by now.” I saw it now. She was thinking about what happened with her and Jake, and many of the other men that had been a part of her life before him. “Because, I care about…” “Our daughter?” The doctor must have told her. “I told you when this…” “No, not just our daughter. I care about you.” “No you don’t. You’re just saying that, just like they all said that.” Instead of telling her how I felt about her, I pulled her in and rested her head on my shoulder. “I want this to work out.” I whispered in her ear. “I want you to know that I’m here for you.” “How’s This Going To Happen” The next few months were difficult. I enjoyed them, having Araceli here, but difficult none the less. Finally, it got to the point where she moved into the main house so I could start working on what would become the nursery. “So, you never told me what’s going to happen when she gives birth.” I turned and saw my brother standing there. He looked around and found the contents of my office are now in the dining area. “I don’t know yet. I don’t know if she’s going to get a place of her own here, or find a new place in Canada. I’m just getting things ready for when our daughter comes.” “Kid, I know you well enough to know that you aren’t putting this much work in unless there’s something else going on.” “Jay, I don’t know what you’re talking about.” “Ok, maybe I don’t. You should tell me, how’s this going to happen? She’s going to have your daughter and you’re going to let her walk away? Forgive me if I don’t believe you.” “Ja…..” “Get your head out of your ass. You spend as much time as you can with her. When she went into the hospital, you hauled ass to see her.” For some reason, he was pointing at me, like that would get the point through to me with some Jedi Mind Trick. “There’s nothing going on with…” I didn’t get the chance finish my statement before my brother slapped me in the back of the head. “What the hell was that for?” “Dorian, you fuck this up, you’re going to regret it. Her and Kelly talk all the time. She cares about you more than you realize. What you’ve been doing, I think it’s more than just caring about her. I think you really have feelings for her.” “Ja…..” “Dorian, I want you to realize that you just might have.......”
