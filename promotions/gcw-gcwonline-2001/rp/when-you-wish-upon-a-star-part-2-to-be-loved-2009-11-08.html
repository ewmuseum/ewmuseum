When You Wish Upon A Star (Part 2: To Be Loved)

8 November 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 8, 2009 My name is Vivica Julian Valentine. I am a professional wrestler. Growing up in an upscale suburb outside Chicago, I was the little tomboy who could. Forced to act prim and proper during my childhood years, I found the love of my life on a television set at age six in the form of bodybuilders throwing each other around in tights. Since the first time I watched professional wrestling, I knew it was what I wanted to do for the rest of my life. Against the wishes of my parents (who to this day refuse to watch their little girl participate in a "barbaric bloodsport"), I ran away to the city to pursue my dreams of fame and fortune. My dreams came true in Global Championship Wrestling, when Steven Caldera gave the little girl from Highland Park a chance to show what she's made of. A decision that's padded his wallet nicely, of course, but one that he may still regret to this day. I'd be lying if I told you I did it alone, though. My success is largely thanks in part to my best friend and mentor, Lia Ambrosi, whose guidance has been an immeasurable influence on what I've become today. Living a straight-edge lifestyle since I was a teenager, I like to think I'm a good role model to little girls and boys alike. My story is one of ups and downs, highs and lows, victories and defeats. I've graced the covers of wrestling magazines and tabloids from around the world. A week doesn't go by where I'm not asked for a radio interview or a television appearance. I try to use my influence to make a positive difference in the world; to send a message to everyone that I believe you can do anything you put your mind to, if you're just willing to put in the hard work and dedication. I'll be the first to admit I've made my share of mistakes along the way, but those mistakes are what has made me who I am today. If I could do it all again, I'd take the exact same path in life because at the end of the day I'm happy with who I am and what I've become. People have called me an unrealistic dreamer. They say that a little girl who beats people up for a living can't possibly be considered a positive role model. I think they're all wrong. Apparently, so does The Make-A-Wish Foundation, who today has decided to award me for granting my 100th Wish. The children I've met from all around the world know me as as The Fearless Phenom, The Aerial Artisan, The Woman Without Fear, and The Image Of The Invisible. Being Worshipped like a goddess among men is something every little girl grows up wanting to do. When I see the looks in the eyes of the children I've worked with, I feel like I could leap tall buildings in a single bound. I'm more than happy to be their superhero if I bring a smile to their face, even if just for one day. But at the end of the day, after all the fame, fortune, and accolades... I'm just me. I'm just Vivica J. Valentine. This is my story. --------------------- "We are gathered here today to award a noble accomplishment earned by an exceptional young woman." The well-groomed voice of GCW President Steven Caldera echoed over an archaic announce system, hissing and popping on its own as he paused. Hundreds of eyes staring holes into him was certainly nothing new, but he wasn't used to a crowd focusing on him being so silent. In all honesty, he missed the jeers from a capacity crowd. Sort of. "We are people from four different countries. We are from two continents. Although culturally different all of our lives have been touched by the one they call The Fearless Phenom." Reading off a folded white sheet of paper, the man known on X:TV to be Vivica's hated enemy continued on, "Her mischievous smile has graced Global Championship Wrestling for over three years and her list of accolades goes far beyond what I'd be able to cover in the time I was granted today. None of them, however, makes myself as the owner of GCW more proud than the one I'm presenting today." Taking a pause, he surveys his surroundings. Balloons, steamers, and signs lined the removable walls of the Hyatt conference room. The intimate atmosphere was a far cry from the big time production of WorldWide. Yet something about being in front of a crowd seemed familiar. Perhaps his time on television had put everyone involved at ease. "Two months ago, Vivica J. Valentine granted her 100th Wish for the Make-A-Wish Foundation." Steven Caldera flashed a smile as the hundreds in attendance began to clap, hoot, and holler for her accomplishment. "Even though her first attempt with the Make-A-Wish Foundation ended in tragedy, she showed the tenacity and perseverence associated with her in-ring persona to continue down the path of making dreams comes true." In the front row the mother of Mary Sue Venton, the little girl who died of cancer just weeks after having her wish granted by the award-winner, shed a tear as she looked up at Steven Caldera. She missed her little girl more and more everyday. Yet she knew after meeting the woman she idolized, that Mary died happy and far from alone. "Today, although she'd never ask for it, Vivica is being awarded for her efforts by receiving a medal of honor from the Make-A-Wish Foundation." Children and adults alike cheer for the woman of the night, "Being the first member of my company to achieve such an award, I felt it necessary to come here tonight and present the award myself. I could not be more proud of Vivica and hope the superstars of tomorrow take her lead in giving back to the community in a way that only they can. So without further adieu, let me introduce to you the former GCW World Heavyweight Champion... Vivica Julian Valentine." With those words the crowd in attendance rose to their feet. Hitting the play button on a boom box near the podium, master of ceremonies Jamie Hargrove of the Make-A-Wish Foundation watched in delight as the crowd reacted to the sound of "Image Of The Invisible" by Thrice. Wearing a dress for quite possibly the first time in her life, Vivica J. Valentine also wore a smile from ear to ear as she entered the room. Her mother would be so proud of her right now, if they were actually on speaking terms. Dressed up like a lady and wearing makeup. It was something that was completely against Vivica's personality, but at the same time seemed eerily natural. Perhaps it was just the fact that she was a naturally beautiful girl. It also may have been the fact that she had spent so much time on television that she'd become somewhat of a movie star. Either way, Vivica had butterflies in her stomach the entire walk from the door to the podium. It was like high school graduation... at least, it's what she always thought it'd be like if she actually ever graduated from high school. "Don't fall. Please God, don't fall. Don't fall. Don't fall." She repeated to herself over and over again underneath that big media smile. Approaching the microphone she smiled and gave a hug to Jamie Hargrove, whom she'd met about a dozen times in the past through working with the charity. After the hug, she approached the podium and unfolded a white sheet of paper she'd had in her hand with a sigh of relief to herself. Thank God she didn't fall down. Looking around the room, she saw faces both familiar and unfamiliar. People she'd never met before and others whom she'd shared intimate moments with over the past few months; moments of candor beyond the lens of the camera which always seemed to be focused directly at her. She tried to wipe the big dorky smile off of her face, but just couldn't as she glanced next to her and saw the very man whom she'd tried to kill on more than one occasion holding her medal in his hands. The word irony just doesn't do this scene justice. "Thank you guys so much! Please sit down." Vivica spoke into the microphone, not even noticing the horrendous feedback that came screeching out of the decrepit speaker at her feet. Her ankles were shaking, but it wasn't so much nerves as it was trying to balance in high heels for the second time in her life. It took nearly all her concentration just to think about standing. As everyone took a seat, she surveyed her surroundings before closing her eyes for just a second and taking a deep breath. The silence came quickly after the rustling of plastic chairs came to a halt. Even though she'd made a few notes on the piece of paper in front of her, she'd promised herself that her speech would be heartfelt and spur of the moment. When the words just didn't seem to come she realized that the paper was a good idea after all. "They say it takes a special person to do charity work." Vivica read the reminder off the note in front of her, written in the wee hours of the morning the day before, "I don't think that's true. I think it just takes a little time and a little hard work because we all grew up with a dream. Whether it was to work with animals, or be a firefighter, or be rich and famous on television... dreaming is something we all have in common." Steven Caldera eyed his former World Heavyweight Champion with curiosity as she continued. "I dreamt to one day grow up and be a professional wrestler." She stated confidently, "I would share it with anyone who listened. That's what I was going to be. While I was often met with smirks and chuckles, it never bothered me what other people thought. It was my dream and mine alone, so I wasn't willing to give it up for anybody. The same can be said for the ninety-three children who are in the audience today." Vivica looked up from her paper to the first few rows of seats, occupied by the kids who could make it to her ceremony today and their parents. A little red-headed boy waved hello to her in excitement as she began to speak. "They have dreams too. Some want to be wrestlers like I'm proud to say I am today. Others would just like things that most people take for granted. Things like the ability to walk again or to live long enough to have their own children, so they can watch them grow up and achieve their dreams." Vivica made eye contact with the red-headed boy's mother who was beginning to cry, "While many of them consider me to be their hero, the reality of the situation is that while I may be known as The Fearless Phenom... no one is more fearless than the children who sit in front of you all today." Vivica pauses and takes a moment to recognize the kids in the crowd by motioning towards them. Quickly, her action is recognized with a big round of applause, bringing smiles to the faces of the children staring up at her lovingly. She'd changed their lives, and in turn they'd also changed hers. "I'm not the hero standing in the room today." Vivica's voice made the clapping fade back into silence, "The real heroes are those kids. Today I accept my medal on their behalf, because they have changed my life and how I look at what I have much more than I could ever put into words. Thank you so much. I'll never forget the times we've shared together for the rest of my life, and I hope to have many more with you all down the road. Thank you." With a loud cheer everyone in the room rises to their feet as Vivica backs away from her podium, clapping for the kids while everyone else claps for her. Looking over at Steven Caldera, Vivica then turns to face him ready to accept her medal. Leaning over she tilts her head down so her boss can slowly wrap the medal around her neck. As he does so, cameras from all around the room begin flashing in unison for their big photo opportunity. They're not disappointed as she raises her head and then shakes hands with her employer wearing that smile that just won't go away. "Now if we could have just a minute..." Jamie Hargrove interrupted, leaning over into the microphone, "I'd like to ask all the kids here that spent time with Vivica to come up on stage. We're going to take a group picture to send home to all of your families." A number of children cheered as Vivica looked over at Jamie, surprised by the latest development but certainly not disappointed. Steven took this opportunity to make his exit off stage, out of the conference room and into the hallway while the kids amassed up on the erected stage. Standing up in the last row with all her granted wishes surrounding her, Vivica spoke out to them all. "Hey kids..." Her voice became noticeably higher pitched in the presence of children, "Don't forget to say cheese!" "CHEEEEEEEEEEEEESE!" SNAP. --------------------- After the day is over though, even the best facades come crashing down. --------------------- Tick. Tock. Tick. Tock. Tick. Tock. They say a watched pot never boils, but from the looks of things Steven Caldera had obviously never met "they" before. Or if he did, he certainly didn't think much of their thoughts or opinions. Staring relentless down at his watch, it almost looked like he was admiring his own reflection in the glass. It wasn't completely against his character to do such a thing but in this particular instance, such was not the case. He was a busy man. He had things to do, people to meet, and places to be. While a senator on the campaign trail took moments of his time to work along side the "little people" and kiss a few babies, he often considered himself above such pleasantries. As he stood in the hallway outside the Hyatt conference room, Caldera reasserted to himself that he didn't care about "the little people" in the slightest. Loud music, dancing, and celebration just weren't on the itinerary. Neither was sticking around this place so late. "I don't have time for this, Vivica..." He spoke to himself. On cue, the door a few paces down the hallway opened, breaking his concentration on his watch for the first time in what might have possibly been an hour. Or at least it felt like it. Again, with the "watched pot" nonsense. "It's about time you got out of there." Deciding to speak before seeing who was leaving might've been a bad decision, but he doesn't make mistakes... just ask him. You could even ask GCW play-by-play announcer James Bryan, who was the man who actually walked out of room. Looking up at his boss, the man known as JB was a bit befuddled. "What do you mean it's about time I got out of there?" James asked cautiously, wondering if he'd done something wrong. "I need someone to get Vivica." Caldera's words were short and confident. "I have a plane to catch in an hour and a half." "You couldn't just go in there?" "You're kidding, right?" Steven wasn't joking, "No. These things drive me nuts. I don't even know why I'm here." "It is for charity." JB tried to soften the blow to no avail. "I don't care." Those were three words that The Fearless Phenom was more accustomed to hearing from the lips of Steven Caldera than the praise and recognition he fumbled his way through inside the conference room. She walked out almost as if she was beckoned. "That's the Stevie-boy I know and love." Vivica smirked as the door closed behind her, silencing the hallways again from the party going on inside. With or without their woman of honor. "Are we done now?" he said, curtly. Shaking her head, Vivica couldn't believe the way he was acting. Well... actually... "No." Vivica answered before laughing a bit, "Fuck no. What makes you think I'm leaving?" "Well, then I am." He was riled up tonight. For whatever reason, all the children and people having fun really took a toll on the owner of Global Championship Wrestling. It made him appear very Grinch-like given the circumstances. Again though, he didn't care. "What the hell?" Vivica can't hide her annoyance with him any longer, "You go in there all chirpy with a big fuckin' smile on your face talking about how proud you are of me and how I'm the crown jewel of your company. How you want everyone to be just like me... which might've been the most difficult statements you've ever had to make in your adult life. Then, when the hard part is over and it's time to loosen up and have fun you're all uptight and acting like a hard-ass." "Like you're a saint." He answered immediately. "Excuse me?" Vivica blinked in shock, "What in the hell have I done tonight?" "'I did it all for the children.'" Caldera answered in a mocking voice, "You're going to make me vomit. You step onto a WorldWide set and you're all 'Fuck this! Fuck that! Fuckin' fuck fuck!' then when you show up to these things you're suddenly a role model for children?" "I think I do a good job." "I think you'll think anything if it gets you a little bit of attention." "Oh... fuck you Steven." Vivica adjusted her dress, obviously uncomfortable as Caldera formulated the most irritating and harsh speech he could possible give her in his brain. Oh, it's a doozy. "Now that we've exchanged formal pleasantries... if you're done with the spotlight now, I've got much better things to do than sit around and watch this nauseating display of insecurity." The head of Caldera Enterprises checks to make sure he has everything he needs on him, just in case some cancerous child came and picked his wallet, "Just remember this about the spotlight, Vivica: it may comfort you now, but take it from me, it doesn't last forever. When your spotlight runs out you're going to be utterly lost, and I sincerely hope I'm there to see it." He'd done an absolutely marvelous job of ruining her night as he finished with a smile, turned his back, and walked away from her. Leaning against the wall she could feel the bass pounding through it against her back. Biting her lip, it took every ounce of restraint she had to keep from running his elitist ass down and breaking his legs. Again. So much anger in such a petite and foul mouthed package. You could only find this in Vivica J. Valentine. "This may be a bad time but about that..." James Bryan spoke up from behind her. She didn't even remember that he was standing there. His words made her jump in place just a little bit, but not enough that he noticed. Turning around she tried not to take it out on poor JB. Lord knows he took enough shit from David Yale on a regular basis. "About what?" Vivica responded, politely. Well... about as politely as a bull in a china shop. He knew her well enough though and at least concluded immediately afterwards that her intentions were well. "This charity work." He went on, trying not to laugh at the mental image come to life of Vivica J. Valentine wearing a dress, "It really seems to make you happy. Is something like this something you could see yourself doing for the rest of your life? Working with children?" In a way that sounded funny to Vivica. Working with children in her mind is probably the last thing she should be doing. Steven was right to an extent. She's brash, temperamental, foul-mouthed, and grew up an only child. Playing well with others and setting an example wasn't quite something she was raised having to do. Surprisingly even to herself though, she did enjoy it. Working with children wasn't a natural skill for her, but it was something she was beginning to acquire. Maybe she wasn't as terrible as she thought. "I don't really know if I should be trusted with kids on a permanent basis." Vivica laughed a bit at her own expense, "Can't you see me running around going 'come back here with my hair brush you little shit!'?" "Well you haven't done it yet." JB didn't laugh, he was very serious, "It's something you should consider." "I'm not too worried about it." Vivica answered, brushing her hair out of her face as unlady-like as possible. "I think I have a gig now I'm pretty good at, wouldn't you say? It's not like I'm pushing fifty and I've lost a step or six." "You know you're not going to do this for the rest of your life, right?" JB breaks the news to Vivica as gently as possible, hoping not to get punched in the face. "No, I know." She seemed understanding but at the same time, she was slightly confused about why he was bringing this up now. "I fully plan on retiring one day. Getting some house in Florida and yelling at kids about being on my fucking lawn." "That's good and all, but I think you're being awfully short-sighted." He tries to ease her into the conversation. "Why?" Again, she's very much a bull in a china shop. It's very well impossible to ease Vivica J. Valentine into anything. "I've seen you when you arrive to the arena in the morning." JB says, "Don't kid yourself, you're pretty beat up Viv. You have strong convictions and you get a lot of respect from me for the straight-edge lifestyle thing, but it isn't doing any favors for your career at all. Sometimes after matches you can hardly walk anymore and it'll last for days." "I'm fine though. I mean I've been do..." "Listen Viv," JB interrupts her, possibly against his best interests, "No one wants to see a thirty-five year old woman with a limp in the ring. Steven has a point. One of these days you're going to stop putting asses in seats as they say in this business. Don't take it personally, it happens to everyone. I just want you to be prepared for it." "But that isn't going to happen anytime soon, JB. We both know that." Vivica retaliates immediately. "You don't know that." He answers, "No one knows when their time is up." "No," She comments sternly. Her voice is intimidating as the look of confusion from her face turns into one of conviction, "Do you not hear the crowd when I come out there? I have time left. I know you've been around the business for a long time and you've seen bad things happen and you're trying to help. I get that. But please... that's not happening anytime soon." "I'm just sayin..." "I bust my ass too hard and have been through too much to just give it all away now." It almost sounds like she's cutting a promo, but there aren't any cameras to be seen. Just her and James Bryan in the hallway of a Hyatt. "I'm the face of this company. I fought my way here. I'm not leaving because I have trouble walking after matches sometimes..." "I'm not trying to make you leav..." "If this business wants me gone they're going to have to carry me out in a coffin." That was exactly the words James Bryan didn't want to hear. Mainly because he was afraid that one day, that statement may be true. --------------------- But the hardest part is waking up in the morning and looking at yourself in the mirror... --------------------- It seemed like every day, six in the morning came earlier and earlier. It might've been the rush of adrenaline that kept her from falling asleep at a reasonable hour every night; it also might've been a problem with anxiety that she was diagnosed with as a little girl. Those are the reasons she'd give you. The truth, on the other hand, is that it was probably her inability to get comfortable at the end of the day through all the aches and pains. Every morning, it took every ounce of strength she had to roll over and hit the snooze button on the alarm clock that sat next to her on every nightstand in every hotel room. This one was no different. Squinting through the pain, she reached over and let her arm drop on top of the alarm clock. Luckily, this time it stopped. There had been mornings lately where the alarm had gone on for minutes. She wanted it to stop, but her body wouldn't will her to do so. Since joining the ranks of professional wrestling's elite, simple tasks had become a struggle. The time in the ring looked like so much fun, but so rarely do the fans and media get to see what it's like later that night. The toll it takes on the greatest athletes in the world are usually a fatal one. Crippling back injuries. Multiple knee replacement surgeries. Brain damage from multiple concussions. After the lights go out and Vivica returns to her real life, you almost wouldn't recognize her anymore. The Aerial Artisan isn't running at the speed of light after the show. Sometimes she needs help out to her car. The Woman Without Fear isn't staring at the face of danger with a smile. Sometimes she's scared that she won't get out of bed in the morning. The Fearless Phenom isn't leading the crowd in a battle cry. Sometimes she needs hotel staff to come help her get her stuff out to her car. She'd never admit it, but James Bryan was right. He had seen her in the morning. Last year after GCW's Dangerous Games match, he had to explain to the doctors what "straight-edge" meant to keep them from giving her vicodin. They had told him that straight-edge wasn't admirable; it was clinically insane in her line of business. Some of the men she stepped into the ring with could rip her in half, and believe her they try. Weekly. Those were the things that crossed her mind as she forced her way to a seated position on the end of her hotel bed. It wasn't often she got to stay at a Hyatt, so she was enjoying every minute of it. The bed made her feel like a queen and was a much needed break from the mediocre hotels GCW sprang for all around the world. It'd be nearly a month since she'd slept at home. Between GTT and GCW, she just didn't have any time. Whenever she did, someone always managed to find something for her to do. After the third try she rocked herself up to her feet, listening as every bone in her body cracked and snapped into place. She'd spent thirty minutes in a bathtub full of ice the night before, but it wasn't from wrestling. It was those damn high heels. "Never again." She said to herself out loud as she curled her toes. Even though she felt significantly better than she did after her match with Dave Hurst in the first round of GTT, she still didn't feel right. She hadn't felt right in nearly three years. She had lingering back problems since her feud with Shaman back at the beginning of 2008. A right shoulder problem from her feud with Bryan Mayhem at the end of 2007. A pinched nerve here and a tight muscle there were the norm. Honestly, she'd forgotten what it felt like to be healthy. That never stopped her though. She was living the dream after all: traveling the world doing the only thing she'd ever wanted to do in her life. After all, if it wasn't for going after her dreams, she wouldn't be standing in the middle of a $300 a night room in a Hyatt Regency wearing a red cami and a matching pair of boy shorts. Taking baby steps she slowly began to make her way to a mirror on the other side of the room. The things she'd do for a massage this morning. Finally, after what seemed like the longest five foot walk of her entire life, she looked back at her own reflection in the mirror. In the eyes of the entire male population of the planet Earth she looked like a dream girl, but in her own eyes she saw a train wreck. The red dye in her hair was beginning to fade out. Without makeup the scars left behind from the deep lacerations on her forehead were very visible. In her mind they almost blinked like Rudolph's glowing red nose from the claymation movie. The left side of her face was slightly swollen from her last match at a house show and she thought it made her look like the Stay Puft Marshmallow Man. Not to mention the crow's feet she thought she had discovered developing on the outsides of her eyes, on her birthday none the less. Don't even get her started on the rest of her body. How her boobs were too small, but she'd never get surgery for that sort've thing because it was petty. The ribs that stuck out on the left side of her chest from the time she'd jumped off the swing at school in the second grade. She was double-jointed only in her left wrist and she thought her fingers looked like old woman fingers. These might've been the same insecurities that every girl had but to her, they were magnified. In her line of work, thirty five apparently was the magic number. An elephant graveyard so to speak. That's when you become old and ugly. That's when your body stops working for you and you start fucking up everything. That's when the men in the audience just don't find you attractive anymore and you've lost your demographic for women. All those little things she noticed when she looked at herself were signs of the apocalypse of her professional wrestling career. She wasn't ready to let go. At twenty seven she felt like thirty five was tomorrow. The thought of losing everything she'd busted her ass for made her angry. As she thought those same thoughts that went through her head last night with JB, tears began to slowly stream down her face. How dare they, after everything she's done, even consider just pushing her aside for the next girl who comes along with dyed hair and maybe bigger boobs, just because she's younger and has a pretty face. It pissed her off to no end. So what if Steven Caldera was right? What if she was insecure and would do anything to stay in the limelight? After breaking nearly every bone in your body and leaving ounces of blood in nearly every arena across the United States wouldn't you fight for what you've accomplished? Four years into living a life that you'd dreamt of ever since you were a child, would you be willing to start thinking about life after your dream? Maybe the straight-edge thing would kill her someday, but if it came down to it what was more important... keeping the foreign substances out of her body or keeping her career? Maybe a few pills to stop the pain wouldn't hurt. Hell it may even add years onto her career... "No." She said out loud to herself, sniffing hard to keep snot from running down her nose. That was all she needed to look less attractive. Snot running down her nose. What a fucking train wreck. Wiping the tears away from her eyes helped her see, but certainly didn't help her look any better. It wasn't like red, swollen eyes was the new hip look. I'm in the business of misery let's take it from the top She's got a body like an hourglass that's ticking like a clock... Her cell phone shattered her fixation on herself and brought her back into reality. Stumbling across the room to the tune of "Misery Business" by Paramore, she fought through the stiffness and pain in a frantic search for her cell phone. Finally she found it, buried under a pile of sheets and comforters near the headboard of her bed. She must've fallen asleep with it in her hand last night, but couldn't remember why for the life of her... "Hello?" She answered the unfamiliar number against her better judgment. "Hey, is Vivica Valentine there?" The voice was much more familiar than the number. "Jamie?" She replied, "Jamie Hargrove from the Make-A-Wish Foundation right? We spoke last night for a bit." "Yes, I'm impressed you remember." Jamie answered, clearly shocked. "Did you need something? I'm still at the Hyatt if you forgot anything." "No, no it's not that." Jamie replied, "I was just wondering if you were interested in 101." "101?" Vivica smiled, "What's their name?" "Jake Henders." Jamie was reading off a paper on the other side of the phone, "10 years old, just got out of chemo for the second time. They said if it comes back this time he might not make it." "That's terrible." "Yeah, it is. It says on here that his dream is to get a chance to see the GTT tournament." Jamie answered, "We've had this one for a while, but you know for a few years the tournament was cancelled and we weren't sure if we were going to be able to grant that one. Starting speaking to his parents about maybe something else, but we heard you were wrestling in it this year. Since you've been such a great sport for us, we were wondering if maybe you could give him a special look at the tournament he'll never forget." "You bet." Vivica answered, putting on her media smile and voice even if no one could see her, "I have a match against Karina Wolfenden in Round 2 here coming up soon." "Great! Well it's early so I'll let you go." Jamie went on, "I'll keep in touch with you about getting more information." "Sounds good, I'll talk to you then Jamie. Bye." With that, Vivica flipped her cell phone shut and fell backwards onto her bed, staring up at the ceiling. Sometimes she just didn't know how to say no. She was busy enough; the last thing she needed was having to babysit through the GTT tournament. Maybe it'll add a little extra motivation, certainly the hype wouldn't be bad. Extra media appearances and all that. But she certainly couldn't go out like this. Sitting up on her bed once again, she clung onto the hotel comforter. It helped ease the pain if just a little bit. Her cerulean blue eyes again gravitated towards that full-length body mirror in the corner. Although she couldn't see her reflection, it was very clear in her mind. "Fuck." She spoke out loud to herself, "You think Karina goes through this bullshit?" --------------------- ...then realizing the horrifying truth is that you're the greatest facade of all. Does that make me a bad person? Or am I just... human?
