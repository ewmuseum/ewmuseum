How it all began

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: January 4, 1993 The wind was blowing hard that night, not leaving one leaf right side up on its stem. Branches were taunting the inhabitance of the top right bedroom in the front of the house. The screeching and gnawing at the windows would feed any subconscious into a frenzy of nightmarish dreams. Not this soul. He had no reason to fall under the darkness of the night and into the dreamscape of the Sandman’s torture chamber. He was a happy child with no worry in the world. He had his health, he had is family, and he had his smarts. He felt invincible when he was awake and running around the house or schoolyard, and felt impervious to any wickedness in his dreams. There was nothing to fuel his anger, rage, or sorrow. When the big hand just locked onto the three, making a perfect ninety degree angle with the little hand on the twelve, the faint smell of pain lingered into his room. Still untouched by any of the smoke or heat that was devastating the rest of the house, he was woken up by the screams of his mother less than two hundred feet away. As he jumped up he saw a satanic glow of reds and oranges emitting from the bottom of his bedroom door. Standing there, awestruck, he inched off his bed and wondered towards the hypnotizing ever-changing glow. As he reached for the crystallized glass doorknob, the door burst open. The heat hit him like a wall would if projected through a door, knocking him off his feet and onto a tiny nylon bean-bag chair he just received for Christmas. The cause of the exploding door was a fire-engulfed father running to get his son, but the boiling pain was tearing through his skin and drove the man to madness. The man ran for the bed, only to see that his son was not there. As he stood there whimpering into the raging flames, he ran back towards the door, slamming it closed right before hearing a tumble of limp body fall down the staircase that was right in front of the boy’s bedroom. Stunned by the screams and events seen, he ran to his bed and grabbed a small blanket and wrapped it around his face. He looked down and saw where his father placed his hands in search of his son, there was burned flesh forming a handprint. The boy had no more time to hesitate, for the smoke shrouded the already dark room. He lifted the window and reached for the swinging branches. As he stood reaching for what seemed like his last hope of survival, he slipped and tumbled down in what seemed like a slowed spectacle of time in his eyes. As he was falling, he saw flames engulf the room that he once called his own. After an eternal second, he it the ground and everything went to black. He woke in the arms of a firefighter, which had a breathing mask up against his face and the blanket wrapped around his body, no longer on his head. Only a matter of minutes must have passed, for the boy’s home was still resisting the parasite inside. He moved out of the ambulance and onto the front yard. To his surprise, the fire did not only strike the inhabitance of his house, but three other houses to the left of his own. The wicked wind did not start the fire, but enriched the devilish flames with the new ability to travel over areas that had no fuel to burn. What seemed like legions of men in black and yellow armor battling a resilient opponent were fighting on against an enemy that spread to a whole block of houses. As screams from neighboring houses terrorized the streets, the boy could only focus on the unrelenting wind and fire that caused his family’s home to fall like his father did after trying to save his son. There was no hope for his family now, for the house started to fall to the right with the grand assistance of the wind. The once tall standing white building was now no more than a lean to against the neighboring building, providing shelter for the once open driveway. Seconds, minutes, and hours went by as the fires waged war and the firefighters fought back. All he could do was stare entranced as home after home began to crumble. He was finally ushered away to a home across the street that was now the only refuge left for the healthy that got out of their homes in time to survive this midnight disaster. No eight year old should ever witness their father burn and suffer, or see everything they ever could call home or his own turn to nothing more than ash. Once the fire succumbed to the relentless will of water and foam and the firefighters moved on to fight down the street, Zacharia Taylor left his place of refuge and walked to his burnt out home. He walked as far as the front steps and collapsed to his knees, weeping into his hands, which were now a shade much darker than his own skin. When he felt compelled to open up his hands and open up his eyes enough to see, he was able to make out a glint of light from the ever so dark ash. Crawling a few feet further into the rubble, he saw a golden object that was tarnished with by the flames and ash. He reached out for it but then stopped. He looked around, taking in anything else that was in the rubble, but nothing else caught his ever-searching eye. He reached out once more and grabbed the object and pulled it up. Mesmerized by the heaviness of the golden cross he now possessed in his hands, he did not realize it was burning his right hand. The smell of burning skin and the smoke that emitted from behind the cross was nothing compared to the feeling that he felt when he grabbed this object. Nothing else survived the house but himself. When he lifted the cross into the air and looked towards the heavens, he knew that it was no miracle that he was still alive, but the Almighty’s will. A new sense of purpose came over him. He was not just a doctor’s son anymore that lived on the nice side of town and attended a nice school. No, he was now compelled to do something more. He looked over at the rubble one last time, then walked down the street, away from the raging fires.
