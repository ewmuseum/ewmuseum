Noir

13 June 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06/13/10 It was a warm August night in that year of 1936. It was a Friday night, a slow night, just like every other night. I clasped my hands behind my head and put my feet up on my desk, and closed my eyes. It was one of those days I hoped to forget. Rodrigo decided not to clean the glass pane on the door frame. The office itself was cluttered with papers and files of cases past, but I didn’t mind. It lent a bit of charm to the place. But that glass pane was filthy. The one that read "T. Frost, Private Detective." The one that the glass-maker decided he was not going to fix, when I told him to add my first name. But that’s what you get when you pay a man up front. At least Rodrigo had a kid to take care of as an excuse. So I closed my eyes for a moment, and remembered what today was. Just another day. I eyed the clock as the hour hand passed seven, and realized it had been at least a week since I had a paying case. I just couldn’t take it anymore, and did what I usually did: reach into my desk drawer and pull out my bottle of Glenlivet scotch. My trusty bottle of scotch. The one I befriended my first year on the force. The one that guided me through case after case of killers, thugs, mobsters, and rapists when I made detective. The one that helped me off the LAPD after I shot that bastard child murderer Jeremy Walton to death. The one that stood by me as my marriage fell apart. The one that was always there. I stared at him for a moment, and pray someone, something, wedged between us. Just this once. Then came that auspicious rap on the dust-covered glass pane. For a moment, I thanked God, as I stashed the bottle away. "Come in," I called out to my guest. The door swung open, and my mouth went agape. I couldn’t help it. It was the most beautiful woman I’d laid eyes on in quite some time. Her shimmering crimson dress flowed past her feet, the straps barely holding everything in place. Her blonde hair flowed like spun gold, her eyes blue, sparking like the most exquisite gems, her lips as red as the fire of Hell itself. I knew from first glance that this vixen was going to be trouble. She sashayed towards my desk with arrogance. "So you’re the private dick, huh?" She eyed me up and down for a moment, laughed, and flipped her hair back. Her right hand rested on her hip. "Don’t look like much." "It’s been an off-day," I snidely remarked. I straightened myself up. "So what can I do for you, Mrs..." "It’s Miss," she replied sternly. "Miss Alexis Thorpe." "Well, Miss Alexis Thorpe," I returned, laying as thick a charm as I could muster, but she wouldn’t even give me a quiver. "What can I do for you?" "I need you to find my husband for me," she said. So much for my chances. Suddenly, I had the urge to test her mettle. "Then maybe I should call you Mrs.?" Her stare burned down to my very soul. "We’re...estranged." I could hear her teeth grinding, as she slapped her palms down on my desk. "And let’s leave it at that." She turned her back, and sauntered around my office. "I need you to find him. He took something valuable of mine, and I want you to get it back." I pulled out a pack of Chesterfields, and put one in my mouth. "And who…" I offered the pack in her direction, as she pulled out a tin holder from her pocketbook, fishing out one of her own. "...Might your husband be?" I lit my cigarette, and extended the lighter her way. She leaned forward over the desk and inhaled from the flame. My nostrils caught a brief glimpse of her perfume. The aroma of spice. Cinnamon. Suddenly, I was feeling a bit hungry. She pulled back, and exhaled a long thick stream of smoke. "Ricardo Alvarez. But he usually goes by Ricky." The name wasn’t familiar, but I had been out of the game for awhile. I took a long drag, as I stealthily admired her beauty, and flicked the building ashes into the silver ashtray on my desk. "What is it you need me to find?" Her brow furrowed. Her eyes narrowed. An indifferent sigh escaped from her lips. "Is that really important?" I sniffed. "I like to get full disclosure for my clients," I said. "Makes my job easier. Don’t like it?" I shrugged my shoulders, and put my feet on my desk. "There’s the door." The room turned chill, and I awaited her move. She stalked up around my desk right in front of me. My feet jumped off the desk and I straightened up. She leaned close into my ear, and I was again overwhelmed by her scent. "When you find him," her silky voice whispered. "You’ll know." Her words hung in the air, as she lingered over me. She pulled closer, and I could feel her breath on my neck, as her free hand put out her cigarette in the ashtray. She slowly pulled away, and made her way the door. The sweat dripped from my forehead. She stopped short of the frame, and turned her face slightly. "I take it you’re in?" I nodded my head twice. She smiled, and it was like she was never here to begin with. I could do nothing but watch her go, and think of where I might start on this mess I’d gotten myself into. *** I lay in bed, staring at the ceiling fan spinning ‘round and ‘round. The thoughts flowed like a soothing summer rain, and I knew I wasn’t getting sleep anytime soon. It was late, ‘round midnight, and I figured I could use a drink, so I found a bottle and poured away. I lit a cigarette and sat along the windowsill, watching the city down below. Things hadn’t changed much since I walked the beat. Crime still runs free. I spotted a lady, walking alone, when a mugger jumped in front of her. He screamed at her to give up her purse, and she clutched it close to her breast on instinct. The punk pulled a stiletto, and she shrieked for help. The struggle was short, and it took one swing of the blade cutting along her arm to free her of her belongings, and the punk scattered off into the shadows with his prize. I took sips of the scotch as I watched, thinking back to a past time when I might have been bothered to help her. Played the shining knight, the hero that swoops in to save the damsel in distress. But that time had long passed. I simply wasn’t that man anymore. I finished off the scotch and poured myself another drink, and thought of the pickle I had gotten myself into again. Speaking of which, I decided it was right about time to get a bite to eat. *** I hit up the Brown Derby on Hollywood, because Musso and Frank was closed. I ordered a club sandwich from Pamela, the lovely waitress I’d befriended nursing many a hangovers over the ages. Before I left my place, I called up an old buddy on the force and invited him for a late night snack. I was there about ten minutes and had started in on my fries when Detective Jimmy Callahan decided to slide into the booth chair opposite me. Jimmy was good police. In a world of the crooked, he was one of the straight-arrows left. But one look told all you needed to know about what the man had seen. He was looking gruffer than I’d remembered as he took off his hat and placed it next to him. "So what brings me down to your neck of the woods?" "Not even a hello," I chuckled. "The more things change..." Pamela made her way to our table, and Jimmy held up his right index finger. "Coffee. Black." She shuffled away, as I took a chunk from my sandwich. "What’ve you gotten yourself into this time around?" I swallowed down the food, and wiped my mouth with the cloth napkin. Jimmy began to rub his temple. "You know a Ricardo Alvarez," I queried. "Goes by Ricky?" The rubbing stopped dead. He raised an eyebrow. "I do..." "You happen to know where I might find Mr. Alvarez?" I asked. Jimmy lowered his eyebrow and laughed, rubbing his face with his hands. "Good lord." His fingertips crossed down his face. "You’ve really gotten into it, now, Frost." He shook his head. "Do you know who Ricky Alvarez runs with?" "It’s been awhile, Jim," I retorted. "I’m surprised you even took my call." Jimmy sighed. "Tell you what," he said, reaching into his pocket. "When you’re done chowing down, head over to the Meridian Club." He tossed a few bucks on the table, grabbed his hat, and slid out of the booth. "Then you’ll see what I mean." He put his hat on. "Don’t call me again if you can help it." With that, he walked off. "See you later," I said to no one in particular, as Pamela approached the table with the coffee. I looked at her, flashed her half a smile, and devoted my attention back to my meal. *** I rolled down La Cienega, with no idea what Jimmy had meant. But I had to start somewhere, and right now, this was my only play. I pulled up a half a block from the club doors, and opened my glovebox. I grabbed my revolver, and checked to make sure I had remembered to keep it loaded. I clicked the chamber back into place and holstered it under my armpit, and made my way to the door. The club sign sparkled, blinking blue stars surrounding a purple crescent moon. It was enough to make my head light. The two mooks at the door were wearing suits too nice for the likes of them. One held the door open, and I made my way to the coat room. I could hear the music pumping through the doors, as a third thug patted me down. He saw the gun, and I flashed my old detective badge. I could sense his distaste, as he begrudgingly waved me in. I hated to use the thing, but it was a nifty skeleton key in a pinch. I pushed through the doors, and the place hit me like a wave. The joint was hopping. It was the classiest spot I’d frequented in quite some time, straight out of a Roman history book. Velvet curtains draped from the ceilings, with gold stripes crisscrossing the ivory columns. On the stage, the big band jumped and jived with melodies that woulda made my old man cringe. The drinks flowed, the dames howled, the guys whistled. I spotted a pin-up here and there, but I was in no mood for stargazing. I surveyed the spot. It only took me a quick glance before I understood what Jimmy was talking about. I pressed my way through to the VIP section, where a mustachioed man in an impeccable three-piece suit devoured a steak, but my progress was impeded by a few more bullies. The man and mine eyes met. He lowered his fork and waved me past. I stood in front of his table as he took another bite. He wiped his mouth with the napkin bib, and took a sip from his wine glass. With that, he smiled. "Ahh, Detective Frost," he boomed, his accent as thick as ever. "How good to see you." I held my tongue. I knew this bastard all too well. "Wait." He paused in thought. "You’re not a detective anymore, are you?" His laugh was nails on my soul. The light dawned when there was only darkness before, and I regretted taking the case. Jimmy’s words made all the sense. I was up against Victor "The Iceman" Moreno. *** Me and Victor, we go way back. I remember it like yesterday. The raid on the warehouse, pistol drawn, flanked by my own personal army, me kicking down the front door. There was Moreno, a few of his men, and boxes filled with an arsenal to conquer a small country. Thompson. Remington. Colt. The names that fed death for years. Victor "The Iceman" Moreno. He also went by "Vic The Pick." Both names stemmed from his weapon of choice, though even the simplest of folks coulda figured that one out. Some folks called him something else pertaining to his heritage that rhymed with Vic, but the ones that said that, well...Let’s just say they didn’t get to live to regret it. I was fluent in the man’s history. Abandoned by his parents close to birth. Came to Los Angeles at 13 from Tijuana. A foot-soldier for the Mattozzi crime family at 17. By 22, he was feared up and down the Western seaboard. No one knew what he did for the next fifteen years of his reign of terror. Some said guns. Some claimed drugs. Others believed he was Boss Francesco Mattozzi’s personal hitter. Whatever the case, that night I had him dead to rights. Months of cat-and-mouse games and back-and-forths, all paid in full. He was done. And the bastard just smiled his smiled, and said, "I give up." The next thing Moreno did took a lot of folks by surprise; he turned on his gang. Gave up everything. Safehouses. Warehouses filled with loot. Everyone and anything with even a remote connection to the family. The whole dirty mess. I argued with my superiors until I was blue in the face, but even I was sharp enough to know the score: in exchange for his testimony, Moreno walks, and the house of Mattozzi burns to the ground. When the G-Men stepped in, well, that fate was all but sealed. The trial was an almighty one, and then some. Press came out from the woodwork. The buzz spread from here to Bangor. Hollywood even cobbled a movie together; a fictional account, with Spencer Tracy in the lead. The top four Mattozzi lieutenants were present and accounted for. Anthony "The Shark" Giovanni, "Razor" John Stagnatto, Peter "Stag" Gualdi, and Jimmy "The Prodigy" Mattozzi. The media dubbed them Francesco Mattozzi’s stable. His "Four Horseman," all four, staring down both barrels of the guns of justice. All of them stood against judge and jury, except for the capo himself. Francesco Mattozzi was on his way to boarding a plane to Cuba when two cars full of Feds filled him and four of his bodyguards full of holes. The politicos saw it as a great victory for America, and the media ran with it. On the cover of every news rag stood J. Edgar Hoover, with cigar smoke and thumbs up, next to Francesco’s bullet-riddled corpse, with the headline, "FBI Deals Blow to Mobsters!" Funny how easy it always is to call out victory when you’re behind a desk, thousands of miles away from the front lines. During the trial, Moreno was so well guarded that if he brushed his teeth, three agents skipped dental appointments. He survived a hit that the public knew of, and two more they didn’t. By the end of it, Pandora’s Box had been emptied, the Feds felt vindicated, Moreno went and built his empire untouched, and we were left holding the bill. I often wondered, as I crawled inside of a bottle, why Moreno didn’t just take out the Mattozzi family himself. He had the cache, and certainly had the guts. But I figured he ultimately got his way. Of the four, only Stagnatto is left breathing, and just barely. I guess Moreno knew his people. You looked at that crew and you saw entitlement strutting around in fancy suits. Moreno was a man of his people. He had a 100,000 watt smile, a silver tongue, and a mean streak as long as the ocean is blue. I suppose if I were in his situation…I’d have played it the same. *** So here I was, in his Meridian Club, with his own crew eyeing me, with Moreno flashing that same damn smile, sitting pretty, just as free as I was. A waiter walked up and grabbed his empty plate and scuffled away. "Maybe you have to change that first name of yours, eh?" Moreno joked as he went through the process of pulling out a cigar and cutting the tip. "Something less...victorious." He laughed, and his boys got a chuckle at my expense, and I took it in stride. The penance for being prideful. He waved for his boys to zip it. "I kid, I kid." Moreno put the cigar in his mouth, and some well-groomed goon leaned forward with a match and lit it, Moreno taking as many puffs as it took to sustain the flame. "How’s your Maria doing?" I shot him a look, and he got the message right quick. He held the cigar straight up. "Love," he said, as he stared deeply into the cherry of the cigar. "Muy mysterioso, no?" "No," I stated staunchly, and he eyed me curiously, just for a moment. He cradled the cigar between his right index and middle finger, and extended his arm across the table. "Where are my manners? Please, Mr. Frost," he beckoned. "Have a seat." "I’ll stand," I replied, hoping to keep any leverage I could muster. Moreno flashed that smile again, and took another drag. "Well," he pressed on. "What brings you here on this lovely evening? I hope it’s for pleasure, and you were simply hoping to catch up on our good times." "I need to find Ricky Alvarez," I said, and I saw I unexpectedly touched a nerve. Moreno took a slow drag, and I fought back the hint of a smile. "I’m assuming you’ve heard of him?" Moreno chortled, as a boy of his I did recognize walked to the forefront. Benito Perez was a storied prizefighter from Inglewood. The guy was legend. The neighborhood kids called him "Flyswatter," ‘cause all it took was one swing from Benny to turn your insides into mush. Of course, guys like Benny always end up finding their ways to men like Moreno. I guess all the money and women you’ll ever need goes a helluva lot further than getting your head pulped every night. Moreno snapped his fingers, and Benny stopped in his tracks. "Nice to see you keep your dog on a leash," I snapped, and Benny clenched his fists. Moreno said something to him in Spanish, and Benny relented, sweat beads rolling off his shaved head. But I got what I needed out of him. Moreno turned full attention back to me, taking another drag from the Cuban. "What interest does an ex-flatfoot like yourself have in my nephew?" News to me, but I kept playing it like I was in the mix. "Me and him have some unfinished business. So maybe you know where I might find him, and I’ll be on my way." "Funny," Moreno said, as a stream of smoke followed. "Seems like we both have something in common, then." Moreno rested the cigar along the gold ashtray on the table. "You see, my wonderful good-for-nothing nephew took something I hold near and dear to my very corazon." He tapped his heart with his index and forefinger, eyes fixed right on me. "That seems to be goin’ around," I returned. "Yes," he huffed, taking a moment to regain himself. "Well, I don’t like it when folks take what’s mine. Family be damned." My interest had piqued. So I was curious. "Why are you telling me all this?" "You were a good detective. Back in the day." I couldn’t tell if he was joking or not, but I wanted to hear what else he had. He raised his fingers to his temple, tapping the side of his head repeatedly. "You’re a smart boy. Maybe you do this thing for me. Find Ricky. Bring him to me. And I make it worth your while." He fingers slid from his head to my eye-line. He rubbed his fingers together, the universal sign for payola. The last place I wanted to be was under Moreno’s thumb. "Can you let me think about it?" "Of course." He pulled a blue-and-gold fountain pen from his jacket pocket. "But, while you’re contemplating my offer," he tells me, as he scribbles on a napkin. "Why not take a trip. Talk with someone that might be of interest." He slid the napkin across the table. I picked it up and took a gander. It was the aforementioned woman’s name and an address. Sasha Malladay. I pocketed it, and decided it was time to go. He flashed that damn smile once more, picking up the cigar. "Happy hunting, Mr. Frost," he ended, as he took another drag. I could hear him conferring with his goons as I made my way out, knowing I might not have become his pawn, but I was still one of his playing pieces. I was liking this case less and less by the moment. *** I pulled up to Sasha Malladay’s apartment building over on Normandie. It was a rundown spot. As I walked up the steps, I could hear the sounds of babies crying, couples arguing, and the thumps of jazz. The soundtrack of the ghettos. I made my way to her door, and knocked twice. The door swung open, and there was Sasha, a short little number, in a pink bathrobe. Her wet black hair flowed down her back as she dried it with a towel. She wasn’t even looking at me at first as she spoke with love. "Man, Ricky," she sighed. "I thought you were gonna be..." She finally spotted me, and her voice trailed. "...Forever." That loving look turned to spite. I immediately thought of my wife. She rolled her eyes, and I could tell she took me for a copper. Pixies like Sasha weren’t too fond of the law. But then again, the law wasn’t fond of the oldest profession in the world, either. She turned away and walked back into her tiny studio. I took the liberty of shutting the door behind me. She sat down on her sofa and grabbed a cigarette off of the table. "Whaddya want," she sneered. "I’m looking for Ricky," I told her. "I just wanna talk with him, is all." I could tell right away she wasn’t buying it. "I don’t know anybody named Ricky," she lied. "So get lost." I scoffed. "Is Ricky what the kids call complete strangers these days?" Her nostrils flared, and she crossed her arms. "I don’t gotta say nothin’ to you, pig." I turned on the charm once again. "Who said I was a cop?" "Please," she giggled. "I ain’t been born yesterday. I know one when I see one." So much for that charm. I decided to go with Plan B. Her left leg started bobbing up and down, as I took a gander on the coffee table, and spotted my in. I reached down at the open cigar box on the table and snatched it away before she could react. "Hey!" Her voice screeched, and I flicked through the contents. At least an eighth of Mary Jane. She jumped up and made her steal attempt, when I pulled out the trusty badge, and she stopped in her tracks. "Sit down," I barked as I held it right up to her face. "Now!" She grimaced as she complied, sitting down on the couch and crossing her arms and now her legs, as well. I held up the baggie, and dangled it in front of her. "You’ve been a bad girl," I smiled. She wasn’t pleased, and closed her eyes and turned her head. "That ain’t mine." "Maybe it’s Ricky’s?" I got her attention back right away. I tossed the bag on the table. "Maybe I don’t say anything. Maybe you fill in the blanks. We see where things go from there." She sighed and gritted her teeth. I had her buffaloed. "Ricky said he had it all under control. He had this way of explaining things that they couldn’t make anything but sense. Said he got enough to set us wherever we wanted to go." "How much did he take?" I inquired. "Two-hundred and fifty," she solemnly replied. I knew you took Lincoln’s mug from Moreno and that was all the incentive he’d need to bring the wrath upon a poor soul’s weary head. A quarter of a million? That made Ricky public enemy number one. I disguised my disdain as best I could. "I need to get to Ricky before anyone else does," I told her. I can tell her trust in me is still wavering, and there’s not a damn thing I can say to change that. But I still try. "I might be his best chance. His only chance." Her mood shifts back to indifference. "Your guess is as good as mine. I ain’t seen him in days." She shook her head back and forth, pouting. "Bastard’s probably long gone with the dough." She pointed right at me. "This wouldn’ta been a thing if that broad didn’t stick her nose where it didn’t belong." A new piece in the puzzle dropped in place. "What broad?" She snickered incredulously. "For a copper," she explained. "You ain’t that quick on the uptake, huh?" I smiled. "I never said I was a cop." Her eyes grew wide, then switched to slivers. "I think it’s time you left." I heeded her advice, and as I exited, she slammed the door hard behind me. As I walked down the steps of the apartment building, I realized one simple truth: Sasha wasn’t the only woman I knew that wasn’t telling me the truth. *** I weaseled my way past the reputable doorman standing guard outside the lavish Beverly Hills apartment complex that Miss Alexis Thorpe resided in. An uneasy conversation with Jimmy got me the info, and he finished the conversation by telling me we were quits. I figured the way things were going, by the end of this ordeal he’d be right in more ways than one. But I pressed on. What can I say? I’m not the type to quit. I knocked on the door to apartment 5F, and the woman that dragged me into the trash heap swung the door open. She was in a red silk number that woulda had me drooling like a cartoon wolf were I not a man of repute. A glass of Chardonnay rested in her hand, as she waved me in with a grin. "I see we’re both creatures of the night." I resisted every urge to give into her ways, as she swung the door shut behind her. Her bare feet glided across the carpet, and I surveyed the room. Funny, I thought. This place was just as I imagined it. A lavish place for a lavish girl. The paintings on display were worth more than I’d make in ten lifetimes. My second thought was that there was no way in Ty Cobb’s personal hell that she paid for this place on her own, and it was all I needed. I figured makin’ the pre-emptive strike was my best shot. Plus, it mighta made me feel better. Too bad she made the first move with a glass of scotch pressed into my chest. "What can I say," she remarked, doe-eyed as ever. "I’m an observant girl." I wearily grabbed it and took a sip. A bit of liquid courage in this situation might not be a bad thing, seeing as how I’m dealing with the second coming of Medusa. Her finger traversed my chest, on her face the look of a Spanish conquistador found Montezuma’s treasures. "You are an impressive specimen, Frost." She is convincing, I’ll give her that. I grabbed her wrist before it went too far, and brought her concentration back to my own. "So much for disclosure," I remarked, tossing her hand away. She played dumb, as she stepped away. "I...I don’t know what you’re talking about." I downed the glass, and tossed it on her well-maintained upholstery. I grabbed both sides of her shoulders and pushed her back into the wall, as her glass of red spilled onto the floor. I invaded her personal space, breathing the fumes of whiskey in her face. She grimaced. "Don’t play the fool." The smell was probably overwhelming, but I didn’t care at this point. "It makes us both look stupid." She knew the game was up, and tilted her head downward, closing her eyes. I let go as she stalked away. Her back was to me, as she stared down at the stained carpet. She smiled. "You’re not as stupid as you look." "So I’ve been told," I replied. She passed by the table on her way to her balcony, grabbing her cigarette holder. She pulled one out and lit it up. She took a puff, swinging back in my direction. "So you know about Ricky and his score?" I nodded. She rolled her eyes and shook her head. A piffle. "That man," she reflected with a scowl. "Never was much for anything." She walked out to the balcony that overlooked the Hills, and I followed. I pulled my pack of Chesterfields and fished out the penultimate cigarette. Lighting it on my way out, she hovered over the railing that looked out towards the sparkling city limits. I took a glance at her face, and it seemed to have an air of peace about it. I didn’t say anything, figuring she was entrenched in thought, when she just let it all go. "I was fresh off the bus from Minneapolis, just looking for something. Anything. And I found Vic." She exhaled. "Or better, he found me." She leaned forward, positioning her elbows along the guardrails. "And a few months of serving drinks and food and getting my ass slapped by every bit of scum that Los Angeles had to offer, I was his personal assistant." Happiness rolled across her face. "He was different, you know? He was nice. He was attentive." She turns to look at me. "He cared, you know?" I wondered who she was trying to convince, me or her. And the happiness went away with a grunt. "But I wasn’t his only ‘baby-doll’ or ‘sweetheart’. You’d think you’d learn, but…" Her voice trails off, as she tosses her finished cigarette over the railing. "That’s where Ricky fits in," I interjected. A half-smile. "I thought he was different." She brushed her perfect hair behind her ear. "Just the same Moreno stock." She shook her head in disbelief. "I’m just a sucker for their blood, I guess." I took a gander at the exquisite view, and got lost in the lights. But as I came back to the situation, Alexis threw me for a loop and turned on the water works, clutching my chest and sobbing into my shirt. I took pause, and tap her on the back with my open hand, but she won’t stop. I’m out of my element here. She pulled away back into the apartment, tears running down her cheeks. "I don’t know what to do..." I couldn’t figure if this was a game or not as I followed her back in. So I stabbed in the dark, and hoped for the best. I rebuff her with coldness. "You wanted the money all along, didn’t you?" Shock ran over her, as she wiped the tears away. "How can you...say that?" I took another stab. "You knew about Ricky and Sasha Malladay, didn’t you?" She swung away from me. I couldn’t gauge her reaction. When she turned back, I got my answer. "So what if I did?" "And you found out about Ricky’s double-crossing of Moreno," I stated. "Figured you deserved it just as much as they did." As I spoke, she grabbed a napkin off of the coffee table, dipped it in a nearby glass of water, and dabbed her crusty eyeliner with a napkin. When she got a free moment, she paused and smirked. "Told ya you weren’t dumb." She dropped the napkin and stalked right up to me, rubbing her finger back down my chest seductively. "So where do we go from here, detective?" I pull away again, against every fiber of my body. What can I say? I’m only a man. But after I pull away, I pressed back into her with a finger to her chest. "I’m going to find Ricky," I stated. "And then I’m gonna finish this case." She pressed forward, my chest poking her harder than I’d probably ever imagine to, getting right into my personal bubble. "And how do you plan on finding Ricky," she near-whispered. I hesitated, if only for a moment, as I pushed my mouth to her ear. "I’ll figure it out," I whispered back. I slid by her, and walked out of her place. I had no idea what the hell I was doing. But I had a backup plan. Something I was hoping to holy hell not to use unless circumstances called for it. And the circumstances, unfortunately, had become just right. *** After going on a random trip through the back roads of the City of Angels, I found a street phone, in case prying eyes had decided to keep tabs on me. I pulled a card with a number on it that had been moldering in my wallet for quite some time, and punched in the numbers. Before I hit the last digit, I swallowed hard. What I was about to do was something I did not do lightly. I hit the last number, and waited as the phone rang. After the third ring, the other line picked up. I knew the deal in this case. When you called this number, if it answered, you made the first move. I had done enough research to know that this was the only way. I faltered for a split-second, before speaking. "Okay," I agreed. "I’ll do it." A snort of approval came through the other end. "I’m glad you see things my way," came the voice of Victor Moreno. "Perhaps, in another life, it could have always been this easy." I imagined him with that damn smile as he spoke, and it made me want to vomit. "If I find him," I continued. "Where do you want me to bring him?" "I’ll be at my club," Moreno said. "I prefer to keep things simple." A man after my own heart. But I had run out of leads, and was up the creek with no paddle. I figured since he was calling the shots, he might not mind calling one or two more. "I’ve a hit a wall on my search, though." "Are you familiar with Murphy’s Law?" he asked. "I am," I returned. I could tell he had all the cards. "Then maybe you should consider the source of our business." I pondered for a moment, and a thought became clear: Sasha Malladay. "Huh," I grunted. A bit disappointed I didn’t think of that first. "Frost, it’s nearly morning," he said. "Do you not maybe want to sleep on all of this?" I looked at my watch. It read five ‘till five in the morning. I was tuckered, but that never stopped me before. "Just make sure you’re there. I’ll do the rest." I could hear Moreno laugh pleasantly. As much as I hated to admit it, it was a nice change of pace to hear someone respect my ethic for once. "I look forward to seeing you soon, amigo," he said, and I hung up the phone. *** It didn’t take long to get to Sasha’s gangster kingdom, and I wearily pressed on. As I made my way to the ‘palace’ staircase, the doors burst open and a shadowy figure came barreling by, blasting right into me and knocking me on my butt. I sat there, looking at the figure as it bolted off down the street, pissed that I didn’t even get a good look at the perp’s face. But there were other things on the docket, and I lifted myself off the ground, brushed myself off, and made my way to Sasha’s home. As I stood in front of her door, I laid on a thick knock, and the door swung slightly agape. I pulled my piece and went in, hoping to find Ricky at the very least. Instead all that I got was Sasha, laying face down on her sofa, two bullets in the back of her head. I walked over to her body and noticed something familiar in the air mixed with the gunpowder, but the thought got pushed out right quick when I peered down at the side table next to the couch. There was a letter with a curious return address. I eyed it in my hand, and stealthily pocketed it when I realized I wasn’t alone. I swung around, gun pointed for the killshot. Only my admirer turned out to be Benny Perez. It was obvious that Moreno wanted insurance that things would go his way, and I couldn’t blame him on that account. My instinct was to leave the gun trained on the Flyswatter, but since we were now bedfellows, I holstered it out of professional courtesy. I looked into Benny’s eyes, and I saw why he got the reputation he had. A guy like him, he was as cold as steel. He passed by me, making sure not to brush by, as he stood over Sasha’s prone body. I figured it wouldn’t hurt to ask. "Was this your doing, Benny?" "Not my style," he responded, as he inspected the body. "I don’t like guns." I stupidly pressed further. "Then maybe you were here to finish the job?" His inspection of his body ends and assessment of the apartment begins, as he passes me by again, without acknowledgement of my presence. He kneeled down near the door, checking something out. "Looks like someone beat me to the punch." He stood up, his massive frame blocking my only plausible exit. Suddenly, his gaze found mine. "You ain’t gonna turn me in now," he asked, devoid of all emotion. "Are you?" My veins turned ice cold, and I couldn’t move for an instant. I walked towards him, hoping to avert my gaze, but I simply couldn’t. He was a ten-car pileup, and I was an expressway rubbernecker. I moved to his side and out the door. But before I could make my escape, he grabbed my left shoulder. There was an uncertainty in the air of how this would unfold, until he spoke. "I only say this ‘cause I like you. You should let this one go, Frost," he boomed. "For your sake." I turned slightly and caught his face out of the corner of my eye. He was sincere, but when you make the bed, you better be prepared to lie in it. He of all people knew that creed by heart. I didn’t do anything else but continue away. *** I found myself on the wrong side of Sunset Boulevard, and pulled up to the address on the envelope. I admit, I was a bit nervous, and checked my revolver to make sure the chambers were still full of worthy lead. They were, and I clicked the metal back into place. This was the end of the road, and Ricky didn’t seem the type to just lay down and die like a mutt. I walked up the stairs of the rickety apartment building, thinking of Ricky’s arrogance. The letter I found in Sasha’s place had a return address on it. The name along the top read "R.A. Malladay," a clever attempt by Ricky that a child of four could see through. As I made my way inside, gunshots rang out, and I knew I was behind the times. I bolted up the staircase to Ricky’s fourth-floor apartment. I pulled my pistol along the way, and passed by curious gawkers, and sidled along the wall. I hung by the door, and, for a moment, was stopped in my tracks. A familiar stench passed by, and suddenly I wasn’t sure I would eat for a week. I couldn’t linger on it, though, and I kicked in Ricky’s apartment door, and knew I was too late. There was Ricky, back pressed against the wall, bleeding out. Ricky’s finished. Not that I needed the tale told, but a quick once-over filled in some blanks. Three shots fired. Ricky fired once, and missed. The other fired twice, and didn’t. I walked towards Ricky, and spotted the gun next to him. I kicked it out of the way, out of habit, and checked on him. "Damned...fool," Ricky mustered through the blood pouring out his mouth. I put my pistol away, kneeled down and checked his wound. Our eyes met. Neither of us said anything. We didn’t have to. Both of us knew the score. The same terrible truth. There’s nothing I can do to save him. "Who did this, Ricky," I asked with futility. He smiled through the sanguine, grabbed my shirt and pulled me in close. An eerie look of peace crossed his face. "Shoulda never...have trusted..." With that, his last breath faded. I stood up, as his limp hand’s grasp faded off of my shirt and fell to his side. I wasn’t sure what to do, so I did the only thing that popped into my head: I shut Ricky’s eyelids. No man should die with his eyes open. I heard furious footsteps out the window, as I grabbed my gun and reacquired it. I looked out the open window, and spotted a shadowy figure. I didn’t have time to wonder who it was as they fired shots at the window, and I leaped out of harm’s way. I peered from cover and saw the figure run down the alleyway. There was no way I was letting this end here and now, so I leaped out the window to the fire escape and rushed down to the alley below. I gave chase, barely keeping up with the mystery person as they jumped over fences through the back passages that lined Los Angeles. The person busted through a door to the boiler room of a building, and I stalled as I hit the door. *** I kicked it in, and made my way into the darkness. Steam pumped from rickety boilers, as I held my gun ahead of me. There was no light, and I was stumbling around like a blind man. But I wasn’t just going to let whoever this was just skate scot-free. I was determined to end this game. I didn’t know what the hell I was doing or why I pursued this, as the heat from the boilers caused the sweat to permeate about me. I was jittery, ready to fire on whatever may come. I thought I saw a shadow pass by, and I screamed out, "Freeze!" Then I felt pain in the back of my neck, as I crashed to the ground. I didn’t know what or who hit me, but I turned over, and saw the figure peering above. I could barely make out the figure, but I spotted the shine of the pistol, and at least knew that mine was out of arm’s reach. I was had. I figured I had it coming. Too many mistakes. I could have found another line of work. Been a doctor like my mother wanted. Joined the army like my father wanted. But I didn’t. Triumph… Jeremy Walton was a child rapist and murderer, but I could have let the court’s had their way with him. I could have just walked away. But I didn’t. Triumph… I could have listened to Maria that I had a problem with the sauce. I could have found help. At least try to salvage the relationship. But I didn’t. Triumph… So here I was, with god-knows-who staring down their sights at me. I tried to muster it up. Feel some regret. Some kinda remorse. Instead I hear the hammer rear back. A flash. I hope for a moment to see the person that’s gonna take me out. I hope… *** In a suite at the Peninsula hotel in Hong Kong, Triumph Frost, in a yellow Lacoste polo, faded jeans, and white Nike kicks, has his shoulder shaken repeatedly by Alexis Frost in an elegant green dress and matching heels. "Hey!" She screams out. "Triumph!" Triumph’s eyes open. He blinks repeatedly as he tries to figure out where he is. He sits up, rubbing the back of his sweaty neck. His eyes dart about, flying past Alexis, who has both hands pressed against her hips, and the television, which rolls the credits of "Touch of Evil," as he rubs the bridge of his nose furiously. "Wh..." He barely musters anything intelligible. "Where are we?" "Hong Kong," Alexis barks. "And I’m tired of sitting in this goddamned hotel room. I’m hungry! And you promised me sight-seeing! You pulled the same shit in Australia, and I’m not about to let you do the same thing again, goddamnit! I want to..." As she banters about, he rises, pressing his hand forward in her face. "Just...shut up." She slaps it away. "Are you fucking serious?!? Don’t you dare try that again!" He rubs his hands across his face, and looks down at her, as if not all there still. He tries to shake the cobwebs loose. "It was this…" He pauses, as if searching for the right words. "I had a really weird…" He stops cold again, and just shrugs. "Nevermind." Another slight pause. "I’m sorry." She’s taken aback, her mouth slightly open. "Umm...you’re forgiven?" He stretches out his arms and yawns, bringing back an extended arm to his mouth with a closed fist. He rubs his eyes and shakes his head. As he clears his head, he gazes into her eyes, but for a moment. She looks at him odd as he presses forward, and kisses her passionately. He pulls back. "I love you." She gets weak in the knees, a kid locked in Toys’R’Us overnight. "I love you, too," she gushes. He cradles his right arm. "Shall we?" She nods and hooks her arm into his, intertwining with each other. They walk toward the hotel room door. He opens it, letting her through. Before she egresses, he holds her back by the shoulder. She peers into his eyes. "You don’t know a Victor Moreno." Another pause. "Do you?" Confusion washes over her. "Who in the blue hell is Victor Moreno?" He smiles. She chuckles, and shakes her head in disbelief. "You really gotta lay off the booze." She saunters through the door, as he follows, shutting the lights off as they leave. The door closes, and the lights of Hong Kong dances along the balcony’s glass doors.
