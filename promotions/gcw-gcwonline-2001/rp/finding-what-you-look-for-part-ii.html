Finding What You Look For: Part II

Date: Annihilation: T-Minus 20 hours I was really contemplating getting a bit drunk before doing this. Part of me wanted to not be responsible for what happens tonight. Because that way, if I regret it later I can just blame everything on having too much to drink. And then her and I can just return to our normal lives and keep on trying our hardest to forget about each other. It's a kind of happiness, and one that I'm prepared to deal with. But then there was this part of me, and I can't really describe any reason why this is, that wanted to get a front row seat for what's going to go down. Whether it's trying to watch the breathtaking beauty of an ice sculpture being carved, so delicately, or if it's bloodlust felt by watching a train wreck, I can't be sure. All I know is the conflict of interest amongst myself. But in the end, I came here sober. Which may not be a bad thing. I have trouble matching wits with Kate when I'm on top of my game. If I came here drunk, she'd simply prove to herself and me that she is, in fact, better than me and the downward spiral would just continue. I can't give her that gratification. I can't endure that much of a failure. And so instead I'm going with the gamble that I can be on an even playing field with her while sober. A tough job, but I'm up to it. I seem to be up to a lot of tough tasks lately. But standing here, outside her door, everything else seems to melt. Like ice put in a fire. It doesn't even stand a chance of cooling the situation off; it is only consumed by it completely. 'Kate...I'm sorry to inform you that I can no longer live in this silent world. But through everything, everywhere, I've never lost trust in you, despite what my actions might say. So, if you're willing to just...let me try again...' Nah. Terrible. She'll see right through that and know what you're really trying to say. Try something less poetic. Nobody really ever speaks in poems, and Kate's smart enough to know this. If you come up and start running off free verse, this is pointless. 'Hi, Kate. Listen, I was thinking, and-' Utter crap. You've gotta cut to the chase, but not in such a way that you skit over all of the obligatory small talk. Small talk is what's needed; it's what makes the world go round. She's always had a conviction that small talk is one of the guilty necessities of life, and she'll just be angry that you try to ignore it. Sigh... 'I should've stayed in Canada.' The door opens to catch me unawares. It might as well be a gun pointing in my face when I see Kate standing there, holding a garbage bag, with just as much surprise on her face. Of course, she doesn't show her surprise. But I see it in her expressionless glance, as she tries so hard to hide it she actually shows it in her attempt. I think it's safe to say she wasn't expecting me any time soon. Her jaw pretty much drops when she opens it for words to come out. And her eyes are wide and surprised. And she looks absolutely, completely, and totally sexy. 'Johnny?' There's no question about it, and she knows that. Of course it's me. Of course I'm here. And I can't tell if she wants either of those to be true, but there are. 'Kate.' Round one of small talk is over. Participants have been identified. And now we get one of those uncomfortable seconds of silence while we set up the chess board that we're going to be playing on for the next couple hours. Or less. 'Wh-...what are you doing here, Johnny?' And so round two begins. She's calling me out. Part of her wondering if this is a dream. Part of me wondering that too. 'I...' I look at her and I forget tact. I completely forget what it means to put on a fascade or hide your cards. She completely draws the truth out of me with some sort of truth-staw or something...I don't know. But it works. I know that her eyes are going to see through me anyway, so there's no sense lying. 'I've been thinking about you, Kate.' I wonder if it's one of those obligatory games to use each other's names in every sentence. She has to think for a couple of seconds now. I think the honesty was something she didn't see coming. With a milky smooth hand on my shoulder, she walks briskly past me without a word and goes to the garbage can on the street. Opens the lid, puts the garbage in, and turns back to me. I can already see the tears welling up in her eyes and she's a mix of furious, overjoyed, and incredibly sad. She still loves me. 'God dammit, John. God DAMNit John! I almost forgot about you. I almost went an entire day without thinking about how much I miss you! Every time I get to the point where I think I can go on without you, something reminds me of you! For six whole years, I keep thinking over and over about what you did to me and why I should hate you for it! And then you do this. You show up with your...your stupid smile. And your eyes and your voice and the way you word your sentences...' I find myself fascinated by her stoop. Staring at the concrete below my feet, part of me hoping that it'll just split open and I'll fall into an abyss to get away from here. At the same time, it's too painful to endure and nicer than I deserve. 'I'm sorry Kate. Maybe this was a mistake-' 'And you haven't changed a God damn bit, you know that!?' Eyes shoot back up to her. Tears have made their way onto her cheeks. Her mouth is a crumbled line on her face and she can't hold back looking small. Looking terrified. Looking like someone who can't hide their emotions and want to more than anything. 'John, I feel like I've aged a lifetime in these six years. I don't even know who I am anymore. Because I'm not strong, I'm not confident, and I'm not myself! But you...you come here...and I swear to god it's 1999 and we're holding hands at a restaurant and we're in love. And it's everything great that happened before you-' 'Fucked up. I know.' 'Before your view of the world got ahead of mine.' Que? She walks up to me and she has regained some of her composure. Some of her godliness is returned as she looks me in the eyes, her deep brown pools taking in my hazel beams. And with almost a shake of her head to wake her up, she walks past me and opens the door. 'Come inside, John. We need to talk.' *** 'Coffee, John?' 'Sure.' 'Columbian or French?' 'You kidding? Columbian.' 'How strong?' 'Just strong enough that you'd get mad at me if I made it and you drank it.' 'Heh...you never did know how to make coffee. I swear to God you just liquified the beans.' 'Wakes you up, though.' 'Maybe. But there are a lot of points in life where staying asleep isn't so bad. Even when you're awake, there are these times here and there where it'd just be better if you were asleep and not...aware.' 'Well, I feel like you need to be awake to make that decision and choose whether or not you'd rather be asleep.' 'I always hated your logic.' 'I hate it too. But sometimes it's right.' 'Sometimes. You make it seem like it's always right, Johnny. Like the time we went camping? Do you remember?' 'Hey, I'm just saying that it would, in the end, be easier if you made nature work for you. A lightning rod starts a fire ten times more efficiently than rubbing sticks together.' She smiles. I smile. What can I say? Memories are crazy things, and they do crazy shit to you. At the exact same time, you're happy because you're remembering good times that you wish you had back, and you're incredibly sad because you know that they'll never come back. You'll never relive them, you'll never see them again. All you can do is hold on for dear life to the pictures and sounds that you remember of the best times of your life and hope that you can go through them again in your mind. But it'll never be perfect. Her and I smile and know that the past is gone. And the only sound I can hear for a couple moments is the coffee perculating slowly. And the thing is, I don't mind. The two of us don't exactly stare at each other. We don't check each other out either. Believe me, as soon as I saw her I knew that I still had every inch of her body mapped out. Her figure hasn't changed in the slightest, and I could easily walk up to her, close my eyes, and just feel her skin with mine. And I know exactly where to put my hand to get which facial reaction, and I can see it in my mind without ever opening my eyes. It's true. I should have done this a long time ago. 'Aren't you glad all that bullshit small talk is out of the way, Johnny?' '...yeah...' And with that said, there is another minute or so of silence. The coffee finishes brewing. The world continues to turn. Out there, somewhere, there are people who aren't happy. There are hearts breaking, people dying, and couples fighting. But that doesn't matter. Because right now, in this cartesian coordinate in the universe, in the smallest upon smallest of dots, Kate and I are slowly building the wall of what once was. Smile by smile, brick by brick. 'Tell me, Johnny. Why did you do it?' 'Why did I do what?' She formulates her words slowly. Carefully. Thoughtfully. 'Well...you were in bed. You were with a girl that you've said you loved. She was sleeping next to you. And she would do anything in the world for you. And you, Johnathan Fitzwillis...you got up and you just...' Walked right out. She doesn't need to say it. She knows I know and making her say it would only just make this problem worse instead of healing anything. 'To be honest?' 'Completely honest with me, Johnny. Tell me why.' She's going to hate me. But it's the truth. 'I...wanted to see what would happen.' Her anger doesn't show. But I can tell she doesn't completely understand, and thus she does not forgive. 'What would happen? What did you think would happen?' 'I wanted to see what would happen to me if my world suddenly crashed around me. I wanted to know if the sun really would rise. I wanted to know if I could go on having drastically lost something so important to me for no reason other than my own volition. I needed to know...just how important you were to me.' Surprisingly, she doesn't look as angry as I'd figured. She nods her head, her eyes and mouth showing thought. 'And did you find that out?' I pour myself a cup of hot black escape, so that I don't have to look directly at her for a moment. Though I do want to. She's so gorgeous... 'I found out that I can't go on without you, Kate. I found that out a long time ago. But I tried, in denial, to prove it wrong. I went on without you for years, and the longer I waited the harder and harder it was to comprehend coming back and trying to save what we once had. Until one day I just...gave up. Stopped trying to convince myself to come back. I figured that what we had was completely dead, and it was all my fault. I figured that blaming myself was the only plausable thing to do. If I blamed myself, I could corner the culprit daily and interrogate him, find out why he did it. Why he threw away the best thing in his life so recklessly.' I finally look back up at her. Take a sip of the coffee that she's all but forgotten about. 'So why do you come here tonight?' There's a big part of me that believes it'd be wise to avoid the whole thing where Don is the Devil. Of course, Kate probably knows that since she did go out with him a few times and Kate is a motherfucking genius. 'I don't know exactly. I just...felt that I had hidden too long.' Kate nods her head thoughtfully. Everything is perfect. And then...something completely different. 'Mommy?' I just about drop my coffee as Kate turns around and we both see a little girl. Standing there, wearing pajamas and brown hair, she stands in the doorway looking at Kate, then me, then Kate again. Confused, somewhat scared, she must be lost in a house that isn't hers, because there's no way that her Mommy is here. 'Sarah. What are you doing out of bed?' 'I heard people. I needed to know if it was strangers.' Kate looks at me almost apologetically. Not because of the child, but because of herself. She turns back to the girl, Sarah is her name, and speaks soothingly. I love it when she speaks soothingly. 'This isn't a stranger, Sarah. This is my friend, Johnathan. Can you say hi to Johnathan?' 'Hi Johnathan.' Her little hand waves and a tired look washes over the little girl's face. 'Hello...Sarah.' Kate turns back to her and looks for a moment like she's shooing her away. But it doesn't take much to realize that she's shooing away something that she wasn't ready for. Wasn't ready for me to know about. 'Sarah, why don't you go back to bed? Mommy will be up in just a minute to tuck you in.' 'Will you tell me a story?' 'Sure. But only if you hurry up there. You need your sleep, little girl.' With a child's innocence about her, Sarah runs out of the room and I can hear her little feet pounding up the stairs. It's a moment before I choose to open up a whole new can of worms. But Kate looks like she's relieved, as though a huge weight has just been lifted off of her shoulders. 'Mommy?' Kate smiles and pours herself a cup of coffee. She puts creamer and sugar into hers. '...yes. She's my daughter, Sarah.' 'Kate...' 'She's five years and ten months old.' She turns to me holding her coffee with a bit of a smile on her face. She's proud of her daughter, not ashamed. And she'll be damned if anyone, even me, dares to do anything to disrespect that. 'She likes me to brush her hair. She loves Cheerios. She is going to kindergarden this year, which is good because I won't need to pay for day care anymore. Two hundred dollars a week is a lot of money, even for a lawyer. I was starting to get afraid I'd have to sell this house and move into a loft apartment because money was getting so tight. Whenever I have a case I have to hire a babysitter but I never trust them to know quite what they're doing and Johnny I'm sorry that I didn't tell you.' She's almost to tears again. She sips her coffee, the black liquid of healing. '...Sarah was my mother's name, Kate.' 'I know. I'm well aware.' 'You said five years and...ten months?' 'As of Tuesday.' I cannot believe this. 'Kate...why didn't you tell me? Are you sure she's-' 'I haven't slept with anyone else but you. I haven't slept with anyone but Johnathan Fitzwillis since we met. I always told her that her Daddy was away at war. That he was out serving the nation or healing the sick or playing a hero somewhere. In the last couple months, she just stopped asking. That's part of what made me start to forget, become numb, to memories of you.' She's openly crying now. Kate is as vulnerable as her child that went upstairs. Crying like a little girl in her kitchen. Instinctively, as if I haven't missed one beat of her life, I find my arms around her and our coffee cups on the table, all security from each other lost. Now we're simply trying to secure the idea of Kate and John. Once again. 'She's beautiful. And you are too.' Kate cannot even make words. She cries into my arms, the arms that she loved so much. All small talk bullshit out of the way. All excuses are gone. We're just two people trying to survive. And this time, I am not going to screw up. I guess sometimes, you just find what you're looking for in life...
