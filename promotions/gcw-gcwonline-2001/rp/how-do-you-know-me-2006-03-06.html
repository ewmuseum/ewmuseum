How do you know me?

6 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/06/2006 “I can’t take this anymore.” Dawn’s exclamation received no response, isolated as she was in her guest rooms. The Steel Rose was sitting cross legged on the floor, her legs and arms arrayed in what was traditionally called the Lotus Position. For the last several hours she had been sitting in the dim light, alone, attempting to achieve the singularity of mind that was supposed to allow her to transcend her own chaotic mind. “I’d like to transcend my aching behind.” She grumbled, untangling her legs and easing herself up into a standing position. The pale skin on her arms and legs felt chilled exposed to the winter air by the thin fabric of her shorts and tank top. Even with the rooms heated, the winter cold had penetrated the walls of the academy and made warmer clothing a must’but Dawn had always felt constrained in long sleeves or pants, and so had chosen to meditate in more comfortable attire. The result had been somewhat less then successful. The tatami mat she had been sitting on was rumpled and disturbed, a testament to her constantly changing position in a vain attempt to relieve the strain of aching muscles and sore posterior. Her longish black and blonde hair hung in messy lock down the back of her neck, having been shifted from tail to long to bun to pig tail to long again over the course of her attempted meditation. Growling in frustration she shoved a few errant strands out of her face and rubbed her upper arms for warmth. “I need to get out of here before I go out of my mind!” She exclaimed suddenly, kicking the tatami mat under the bed and stomping to her chest of drawers. Shirking the top and shorts she dug through for warmer clothes, deciding on a dark green turtleneck sweater and a worn in pair of dark cargos over underwear chosen for function and not style. Grimacing she drug a hair through her unruly hair, staring with regret in the mirror at how long her normally controlled mane had become. “I really need to get this cut?” She grumbled, running her fingers ahead of her comb to try and loosen up some of the rougher areas. ??still’Lia seems to like it.” The thought brought a heated blush to her cheeks and memories of cool fingers threading expertly through her hair and over her scalp. The redness of her cheeks gave her pause, staring at her suddenly rosy complexion in the mirror. “You’d think,” she remarked to herself, “That by this point in our relationship I’d be long past blushing.” And yet, as her comb went back into her hair, the red and pink tinge to her cheeks did not fade. Setting the comb down she gripped either side of the dresser and stared into her reflection, looking as deeply into her own brilliant green eyes as possible. “What. The Hell. Do I do now?” The thought had been brewing in her mind since the Rampage tournament had been announced, bouncing in and around the rest of her thoughts like a twisted game of pinball inside her psyche. And she was still no closer to the answer. Turning away from the mirror she snagged a polar fleece vest from its hanger and slipped it on, following it with the comforting weight of her familiar duster. A white toque would protect her head and ears from the cold and keep her hair subdued, while leather gloves and a red scarf would serve to cover the rest of her. Flicking off the light she stepped into the corridor and strode towards the main gates. “Where are you going?” The rich baritone of Michi’s voice halted her, booming out from one of the adjoining passageways she had passed. He stepped out into view in nothing but a pair of workout pants, a white half towel wrapped around his neck to dry the sweat on his dark skin. “Out.” Dawn replied with a shrug, gesturing around with one hand. “I need to get out of this place, stretch my wings and breathe a little.” “You want some company?” He peered at her suspiciously, drying a stubborn sweat spot near the back of his short hair. “Not really. Thanks anyway though.” With that she turned on her heel and strode into the front room, grabbing a pair of high cut boots from their place by the door and plopping into a seat to lace them up. “Nothing personal Michi,” She thought as she worked, “I just don’t feel like having a chaperone today.”It wasn’t a fair assessment, but since her run in with Marcus during GTT5, everyone had been on edge in regards to her safety. It was wonderful to have people who cared, but overall the attention was starting to become more then a little stifling. Boots in place she stepped out into the snow dusted courtyard, a few loose strands of her hair immediately finding cause to dance to the tune of a stiff breeze. Grumbling against the chill wind she tugged the collar of her duster up and shoved her hands into her pockets, gloves and all, before trudging across the courtyard and out the front gate. It was like an entire other world, separated only by a few centimeters of wooden gate, and Dawn paused for a moment to drink it all in. The sidewalks were packed, throngs of people moving along like rivers, driven by the current of commerce, entertainment and work. The flow of people generated its own heat, but was easily dwarfed by the sheer sound every large city in Japan seemed to produce. Cars and other automobiles roared by, cell phones rang and people spoke loudly to each other in passing or in deeper conversation. Street vendors hawked their wares and company mascots shouted catchy slogans while dancing to a silent tune. Underneath it all was the chiming of hundreds of bells or wind chimes, small religious symbols left on benches and corners, hung from lamps and telephone poles’so quiet that she had to strain to pick up the musical sound underneath all the chaos. Looking back over her shoulder at the peaceful quiet of the walled Academy, Dawn heaved a heavy sigh. “I don’t think I’m going to be out here for very long after all.” Closing the gate she stepped into the throng of flesh, effectively creating her own little island amidst the eddies and flows, businessmen and students alike parting around the large foreigner in their midst. At exactly six feet tall Dawn was large for a westerner, and almost gigantic by the standards of the normally smaller Asian women. The strands of blonde hair blowing around her face were less out of place then she had expected, almost plain amidst the shockingly bright and flamboyant hair dyes she was seeing among the younger population. She fancied that she cut quite an image, the Amazonian foreigner cutting through the wave of locals. It was no easier for her then for anyone else however to push through the steady traffic, and would have been effectively impossible had she chosen to turn around. The space she was afforded was nice, but only extended far enough that she was brushing elbows with people every other step instead of constantly. There was an order to it, she reflected with amusement, to all of this seemingly random chaos. The throngs of people always moved and flowed in the same way, moving unerringly towards their respective destination with as little deviation as possible. There was always space provided on the sides for people who need to branch off or move in, and overall there was a sense of unity’at least unity of purpose. Dawn closed her eyes, letting the jostle of bodies push her this way and that, her own sense of balance from years in the ring keeping her upright. “It’s not like I had any place in mind?” She mumbled to herself, ??this works out just fine.” It was odd, feeling a growing sense of peace amidst all the chaos when she’d been unable to find any alone in her room. The sounds melted together, producing a comfortable buffer instead of the distracting chaos of individual sounds. The shapes and bodies around her seemed to blur into a solid mass, broken occasionally by small gaps or spaces in the flow of bodies’like the large one approaching from just behind her. “I thought I told you I didn’t want any company.” She growled without looking back. From his own island in the river of people, Michi gaped at her in surprise. “How the heck did you know I was even here” I can’t hear a damn thing over all this racket.” “It’s hard to miss a guy like you in a crowd like this.” She was quite right. At Six feet, five inches tall and just over two hundred and fifty pounds, Michi Noketsuna cut an imposing figure over the rest of the Japanese natives. Pushing through the people between them he stepped into Dawn’s “island” as people closed off the space he’d left behind him. “I guess’you don’t need to be mad though y’know.” She looked over one shoulder, her teeth practically grinding in frustration. “I didn’t want any company I said, and I meant it. I needed to get away from a while, and having you glued to my ass isn’t helping anything.” “You know a lot of girls would be happy to have me glued to their ass.” He shot back, his face splitting in a bright grin. “Not funny.” She glared, turning her back on him again. “Awww c’mon Dawn, don’t be like this. After what happened the last time you went out alone we can’t just leave you undefended, you of ALL people should know that.” She huffed, but couldn’t find a fault in his logic. “I’m a big girl you know Michi?” ??and I’m a big guy, but even I need help sometimes. Besides, we all know Lady Lia would have our heads on poles if we let something happen to you again.” He replied with a grimace. Dawn grinned at the thought, a slight warm feeling inside her growing at the thought of Lia’s protectiveness. It was hard to stay so mad with a feeling like that. “Fine’but as punishment you get to buy me lunch.” “What” Why didn’t you stay at the Academy for lunch?” “For Miso Soup” Blech. I wanted some canned coffee and some schezuan noodles. And since I wanted to enjoy them by myself, you’re going to buy them for me.” Her tone had taken on the petulant pout of a small child, and Michi knew better then to argue with her when she got like that. He shrugged at the nearest man, getting a sympathetic smile in response. “What can I say to that” Where are we going anyway?” “The mall.” She replied, slowing a little so he could fall into step beside her. “The vending machines there have the brand of coffee I like, and the noodle stand out front does schezuan better then anyone else.” “Fair enough I guess,” He offered his right arm. “Shall we?” She paused for a moment before accepting, weighing over the pros and cons of physical contact before looping her arm through his and settling into step beside him. It was soothing in its own way, the rhythmic tapping of their feet as they walked and the feeling of almost symbiosis that grew as their footfalls fell into perfect step. She tugged him a little closer, closing her eyes and letting the feel of his body against hers guide her as she sought the same state she’d felt just before he arrived. For his part, Michi seemed to understand and appreciate what she was attempting, so he kept his mouth shut and his thoughts to himself, occasionally steering her around obstacles with gentle touches on her elbow. Although they were certainly not alone, Dawn felt almost isolated from the people around her, like the only people who mattered for the moment were Michi and herself. It was like a dream, walking arm and arm with an old friend through a blurred and pastel landscape that didn’t so much stand out as just’was. In perfect synchronous they turned corners and stepped over small obstacles, Dawn occasionally taking a few quick steps to catch her feet up to Michi’s and stay in time. She feel him breathing through the fabric of both their coats, the gentle rise and fall of his expansive chest as plain to her as if she were looking for it. As moments passed she became aware of an increasingly loud noise, like the building of a tidal wave before its fatal crescendo. Her reverie broken, she turned to see what it was? “and barely moved aside in time to avoid being trampled under foot by a stampede of teenagers in half removed school uniforms. Her arm broke from Michi’s and they pulled apart to let one student through’only to find that once the space had been opened there was no closing it. Swept along by the crowd, Dawn didn’t feel any panic, just a sense of’oddness about it all. “Dawn!” Michi called, waving to get her attention. “Meet me at the Mall! Food Court!” She waved to indicate she’d heard him, and then turned her attention to staying upright in the suddenly surging traffic. As if water from a broken dam, the students pushed and prodded, moving the flow of human traffic towards where they wanted to go, breaking off for individual destinations as they arrived. Dawn kept her attention on the turn she wanted, finally managing to break from the horde to stumble into the outside atrium of the mall. Pausing to catch her breath, Dawn rested her hands on her knees for a moment before she straightened, looking around to see if Michi were anywhere to be seen. Nothing. Figures. “He probably got scooped by a herd of little girls.” She mumbled, shaking her head as she turned back towards the mall’and froze. The little noodle cart she had come to enjoy was certainly out today, but it was not the brightly coloured umbrella that caught her attention, nor the flamboyant sandwich board sign that proclaimed the best noodles in the world, no the universe. Rather it was the man standing before the cart. He had short dark hair that had grown long in the bangs, and his face bespoke a heritage that was an equal mix of east and west. She could not she his eyes, but it didn’t matter as it was the coat he wore that caught her attention. High collared and long like a trench coat, the heavy white fabric hung down to almost his ankles, concealing the dark blue suit she knew he wore underneath. The last time she had seen him; he had been brandishing a sword and had driven her murderous ex boyfriend away from her. Stumbling a little at first, then picking up speed as she went, Dawn hurried over to the cart, her hands fumbling for the wallet in her coat pocket. The elderly man behind the cart saw her approaching and smiled his wide, friendly smile before offering a polite bow. She smiled as well, returning a hurried half bob while keeping her eyes on the man in the coat. “Good afternoon.” She spoke better English then most Americans, having been taught it while still young. “A large order of Schezuan noodles please’and I’ll pay for his too.” At that he turned to look at her, sapphire eyes staring out from bangs the colour of an unpolluted night sky. Her breath caught in her throat, one hand unconsciously stealing to the base of her neck. For a stinging moment, she thought he might have been the most beautiful man she had ever seen. “That won’t be necessary, thank you.” He replied, his Japanese flawless. As he turned back towards the cart she found her voice again, stuttering around the warmth rising from her belly. “P-please, allow me. It’s a small repayment’for your help a few weeks ago.” Looking over one shoulder he raised an eyebrow, eyes expressionless. “I’m afraid you must have me mistaken for another, I’ve never seen you before.” Dawn felt like she’d been slapped’hard. Her mind reeled as he accepted the Styrofoam carton containing his lunch and handed over payment. Why was he acting like he didn’t know her? Why was he pretending nothing had happened? Was she wrong? No it couldn’t be, this was him, she was sure of it. I can’t let him leave. The thought hit her like a lightning bolt, and she looked around quickly, catching the telltale swirl of his coat as he disappeared around a corner. Bowing repeatedly and babbling a halting apology to the confused vendor, she sprinted after him, praying he was not already gone. She rounded the corner to the walkway, relieved to see that he was only halfway down the path ahead of her. “Wait!” She called, still running. “Please wait!” He stopped again, turning to face her as she closed the distance between them. She’d hoped for something, recognition, anger, irritation’but his beautiful face remained expressionless. “Yes” Can I help you?” “Please’don’t, don’t blow me off. I know it was you, you saved me!” The words tumbled from her mouth, falling like rocks dropped out a window. “You were there when Marcus attacked me, you, you saved me’please’wait.” He shook his head slowly. “I’m sorry, as I said you have the wrong person, perhaps you?” “No!” It came out with more force then she meant, but it was too late now. “I KNOW it was you, PLEASE! You have to help me, I don’t know’I don’t know what’s going on, and no one will tell me ANYTHING.” He paused, and for a moment she thought she saw a softening in his eyes, something that thawed them from the impenetrable ice she’d seen. She could feel her eyes growing warm, and scrubbed frantically at them with the back of her gloves. “Please.” She said again, this time barely a whisper. He looked at her for a long moment, his eyes flicking up and down her whole body in a manner most unlike what she was used to. Then he reached into a breast pocket, removing a small square of white cloth and handing it to her. “What do you want to know?” Her heart leapt, a surge of elation rushing through her veins. She could feel her face split into a wide smile. “Thank you! Uhm’who are you” Why did you save me?” “My name is Reito, Kanzaki Reito. I’protect places and people from people like your ex-boyfriend.” She coughed a little, trying to catch her breath in the cold air. “You said, said I was beyond him now’what does that mean” What’s happening with me? Who am I really?!” “Who are you?” He raised an eyebrow, “Last I checked you were Dawn McFarlane.” “That’s not what I mean! Lillith said I was changing, that I’ve never’been entirely human. She said?” “That you were hers?” His voice had sharpened, chilling the already cold air. She nodded, feeling the all too familiar tears of helplessness coming. “She said, that I couldn’t help myself’that I was bad on the ins?” She didn’t finish, cut off by deep laugh that boiled out of his chest. A slight smile tugged at the corner of his mouth, giving him a slightly lopsided look. “And you believed her” Tell me child, what is bad? A sword? Or the way it’s used?” “The way it’s used I guess?” “Exactly.” He nodded firmly. “Lillith no more controls your actions then I do. You are free to choose your own life, within certain limits obviously. The only control others exert over you is the control you allow them too.” Scrubbing at her face again, she turned away from him, tracing a design on the front of the vending machine next to her. “She also said’that my nature is what makes people love me. They can’t help it.” The smile slipped from his face, deepening into an irritated frown. “An’exaggeration. Lillith speaks from her own experience, not yours. There is not enough of her in you to warrant that kind of’behavior.” “Not enough of her in me?” She looked back at him, searching his face. “So you know then” What she means when she says my “nature?”” He nodded again, although his face began to close as if he was anticipating her next question. “What does she mean then” What have I become?” “Nothing.” The reply was fast, too fast, and he took a moment to compose himself before answering. “You have become nothing more then what you have always been’and what you are is more complex then she knows. Trust me when I say that Lillith does not know you, nor does she have any grasp of what makes you up.” “But you do?” “I do.” He nodded, holding up a hand to forestall any further questions. “But you already know what I’m going to tell you.” She paused, an all too familiar feeling of disappointment rising like gorge in her throat. “That I have to find out for myself?” “Not quite’simply that I cannot tell you.” She sighed, letting her eyes slide shut in an attempt to hide the disappointment she felt. He was supposed to have been the answer, supposed to have answered all her questions’instead she just felt like she had more questions. The vending machine next to her coughed and rattled and she opened her eyes to see a steaming can held before her. “Coffee.” He said simply. “I remember you like it.” Gingerly she accepted the drink, popping the top and drinking in the strong smell. She could feel his eyes on hers, and when she opened her own again she was almost lost in the azure depths. “Do not despair. Things will come in time; that much I can safely promise.” He turned and walked away, his long coat billowing out behind him. Dawn watched him as he left, her eyes on his back until he turned the corner and disappeared. Absently she sipped from the can, the simple gesture calming her whirling mind. She looked down at the can in her hands, really looking for the first time since he handed it to her. Her favorite brand, ever since she was little. “How’how do you know me?”
