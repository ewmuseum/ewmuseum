A Story!

10 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 10, 2005 The word rolls over and over in Trey's head and eventually forces his tongue to twitch it out long enough that he starts to mouth it out. Breathlessly, then breathing with the word. The air rushing out gives it an almost definite sound. He stops, afraid to hear it. Afraid of the nuclear bomb that it activates. Afraid of the ego death he lets himself into. Afraid of the him it means. Closing his eyes and thinking of something else as the table by his head rattles as someone calls, or to tell him that he's missed a call, or that he's missed another call. His stomach's wrong, spiraling bile, and he knows he can't answer for anyone until things get better. 'You're doing this to yourself, babe. Just calm down.' Alyse sits up beside of him, casting a glowing cancer smile down on him. 'It's gonna be alright.' She pulls the nightstand open and lifts a mahogany-stained jewelry box, ornate with Celtic designs, to place between her and Trey. A little mirror comes out and she nimbly shaves a few lines from a waxy white rock onto it. Her skin must be fresh. Trey laughs under his breath. 'You first.' He leans down and snorts a line up through a cut-off straw. Half gets caught in his nostrils, sticking to the mucus membrane and slowly absorbing as the rest slams into the back of his throat. When he looks up, he remembers that he's alone in his dank New York hotel room, Alyse left behind in Boston. He keeps the laugh quiet to not disturb the powder, then takes two more lines and leans back on the bed. The comforter's spent a night scratching and digging around in his back with its deceptive barbs--he puts the box and its box accessories back into the nightstand and rips the brown barbed-wire burlap from the bed. It hangs in the air, caught in the laws of physics, then crashes down as Trey flings it over his head. A chair gets stuck under it and crashes down, its leg in turn sending a glass table twisting into wall. It shatters, loud enough to crack the skies. Nothing. He puts his ear to the wall and hears his own heartbeat. He turns and leans against the beige wall-paper, flicking on a light switch. Shards hang in a corner he never uses. Someone else is always there to clean it up. In his pocket there's a box of Marlboro Reds, short and filtered. He flips one out and lights it with a torch left on the dresser. Sinks to the floor. The wall behind his head twitches, breathes, clicks. They don't give up--whoever's calling--so he finally gets up and turns the phone off. His imprint's still fresh in the wall and floor, comfortable and contoured to his own bad dreams, and he sits in it; he slowly digs his head back into the wall, wedging into it a new hole to rest in as he stares up at the ceiling, dumbfounded at the drip--Jesus Christ, the drip--it rolls down his throat, bitter sludge, and creeps into his every vein, his every fiber, his every muscle with a thousand screaming decibels, for subtlety's sakes, instantly taking his soul away from him and handing it back sharper and cleaner, more useful than he'd ever seen it. He tries to shatter the word. Nothing. He'll never be convinced of synergy. Careless, he flicks the butt and lights another cigarette as his feet play on the rough comforter--still more comfortable than the carpet. He stands and finds shoes with speed, quickly throws on a shirt and a jacket that he slips his cell phone into. It vibrates against his chest. Muscle memory flips it open and puts it to his ear even after he understands the blank screen. It breaks easier than the word, harder than the table, and he's out into the hall. The elevator behind him, the classical kind with a wrought-iron gate beyond the car instead of cold steel doors, casts the same empty glare it's had on display for days. It never runs, never opens. Just sits and watches. Trey shakes his head and snorts hard to force out another drip. It rides in on the coattails of the last and fuels him down the short turn, the half-flight of steps, and through a wooden door that clicks shut after him. The sun's hot and the rain's a light haze that stakes hours to flush clean the gutters and stench of the city. He watches its work as he stalks the alleys and streets between the Cyclopean towers that disappear into the clouds, passing unrecognized by the few that felt to wander out into the gray-and-grayer ruins that say the Big Apple's not as cleaned up as they claim. He avoids Times Square. Hookers, caught on the undertow, slip through the grates and into the sewers. Alligator food. The alleys come clean of hustlers, pimps, dealers and addicts and the towers spew out their accountants, attorneys, managers, executives, Presidents and CEOs--all swept away by the tide to leave the world to the average. Trey takes advantage and uses the newly cleansed shadows to duck into whenever he needs to reup on his high. His chest vibrates with the beat of his heart. A redhead with a few facial piercings smile at him as she passes by, quick to her car. He watches her watch him in the rear-view with his painted-on smile, smeared and soaked. She drives off and Trey's left, too determined on his path to step inside for something to drink. His stomach's still burning, impatient; it knots itself into oblivion, then unties just to go back through the motions. His head's still not his own. He keeps walking. Words come to him, shitty jokes that lose their punch line, but the word stays far, far away. It's always waiting in the hollows of his mind, blasting through the unexplored and nearly empty halls in the deepest recesses at king-hell speed, until it gets to play the game again. It's so tired of waiting. But hey, it doesn't matter right now, so don't even worry about that shit. Has no meaning at all. The other words, though, they pile up in his neck until they start to spill from his mouth in muffled grunts. No one notices. Sentences form and leap from his throat. He chokes keeping them down and slips into a phone booth to get some air--or another bump, or both. The door catches behind him and he jerks it shut, clanging loud enough to weld that sky he broke earlier back together. Peace. His hand's paid and dialing when he's still weighing the idea of calling someone. It clicks to find a signal, rings, gives up and goes to a voice mail. 'Jordan White.' It beeps. 'J, man, this is Trey. Been trying to get in touch with you, broke my phone earlier--what's up? You left me in that rotting ass hotel, now I can't get in touch with you when I'm tryin' to get the fuck outta NYC, y'know, an' it's a little hard not knowin' why you sent my ass here in the first. I know you know where I'm stayin', so gimme the word on what you're plannin'. Ain't long until Worldwide, man, get to go on, haha, Marauder? That shit's ridiculous. Whatever, though, s'business like always. Fuckin' business--you talk to Eva, J, let her know that ill-gotten gains gotta be paid for like any other. Revenge first, gold second. You tell her, man, she don't seem to like me as much as she used to, y'know, probably wouldn't return my calls anyway. So yeah, fuck, just get back at me. I'll be back up at the hotel in 'bout an hour, or so, yeah. Peace, man.' There's another beep that cuts off a sentence Trey almost jumped into, and a voice lets him know that the message's been saved. It's a small comfort that he quickly moves on from to keep from growing doubt. Outside the phone booth, the rain's tapered off and he hears the mewling of the swine as they fight the sewer's freakish evolutionary cast-offs. A few survivors push up through the grates, their fineries and jewelry soaked in refuse that won't be brushed off. Trey looks them over, letting his black boots control the vibration of the world with their heavy falls. Life deforms to reform itself in Trey's image, the subtle patterns of black and red rising to the surface on every wall. A stencil, ten foot tall and sprayed on the face of an abandoned shop, stares down at him with hollow SWAT mask eyes. It tells him to have a nice day. Beside it, someone's sprayed a thin V in a circle. Advertisements are everywhere, now. He averts his gaze to a mail box, rusting from years of the light rain. An older man, sweatshirt as stained by sweat as the weather, walks from it and looks to Trey--muttering--looks away as he disappears into the building. Words Trey's heard before, and words he quit caring about years ago. The stiff upper lip he's pops had given him became more useful everyday he was loose, a few reigns that could at least get the mangy creature inside to think before it snapped its shackles. A small comfort. Back in his room, he upends the table again and rejoices in its destruction for the tenth time in three days. His roar is mighty, threatening to uproot the building and send it into the heavens. Tempted, he picks up a metal leg from the chair and slams it into the wall once--breaks through the plaster to a narrow crawl-space without insulation--and twice--breaks through to the room next door--and three times--overkill. He tosses his tool to the floor and starts clawing at the hole with his hands, letting the rubble fall to his feet. It piles up, each drop sending forth a new dust tornado to whirl out a foot away. The passage is soon big enough to actually get called a passage, and Trey uses it to step through to the other room, a mirror image of his. A small fruit basket sits on the glass table, the attached note made of nothing but scratches in pen. He sits, carefully, and pulls a locket from the breast pocket of his jacket. It flips open easily, dumping blow on the table. Another line or two's enough to think and too much to sleep. The recliner falls backwards easily. The ceiling, again. He snaps to and goes through the wall to his room, calls room service. A girl with a small, frightened voice answers with his name. 'I miss any calls?' 'You did, sir. Jordan White left you a message to meet the neighbors.' 'The fuck?' 'I don't know, either, that's all he said.' 'Nothin' else? Tell me to call him back?' 'No, no sir. Just that. Is there anything else you'd like?' He pauses for a second, hand to his chin. 'Gimme a bottle of your best liquor. Gonna take his advice.' 'Best liquor?' 'I wanna be drunk, and I want it to cost someone a lotta money.' 'Yes, sir. Is that all?' 'Shyeah.' 'It'll be down in just a minute.' He collapses onto his bed, comforter to claw at his neck and head, and conjures up nothing new to man as his head dances around a thousand tired fantasies. They're soulless ghosts, emptier than what he's looking for. A quick knock-knock-knock at the green door rushes them away and calls Trey to the hallway, where he jerks a bottle from an unattended cart before slinking back into the newly remodeled two-room suite. Taking a swig from the bottle, warm vintage rum, he looks through the peep hole and finds nothing of interest. The drink becomes holier and holier on his journey, his only companion save for the intricate wooden box, and leads him through the portal to the next door. 'Hey.' He sits again in the recliner by the glass table, thudding the box and the bottle heavily on the table. 'Let's go.' Shot glasses, conveniently, are compliments of the hotel. He lines six up and downs one, chased with a lift from the bottle. 'Didn't really expect you to show up, y'know? S'been years.' Another line eases the burning in his throat and quiets his stomach for a minute. 'Coulda been under prettier conditions, but you do what you gotta do.' He leans back in the recliner, nodding and motioning to the couch opposite it. 'Hah, I know, I know. Don't worry, s'just a fling. Y'know I can't keep this shit up forever. Week or two's all I need.' Drowns himself in another swallow of rum, and keeps it down with a second shot and a laugh. 'S'nice to hear. But yeah, gotta come of terms sometime. Everybody measures up one day, y'said it yourself, an' today was lookin' like that kinda day. Thing's changed a lot lately--fame, alla that. Never prepared me for that, y'know, but you didn't hurt--left some big shoes to fill, I'll say that. Dunno how well I'm doin' at fillin' 'em, but people like me. Isn't a battle anymore.' He leans forwards and steadies the straw again, killing the last of what he'd cut up earlier. 'It's always going to be a battle, whether you're fighting it or not. Even if you take a break, there's going to be people out there dying for what's right.' 'Big lesson for a kid to learn.' 'Most important one. You inspire a man to look to the truth, you enlighten a neighborhood, you bring the word to the world, and no one's got a right to condemn you for a few bad habits.' 'S'what I'm saying.' 'But if you just roll in the spoils and smile for the cameras, what good are you really doing?' Trey's silence is immediate. 'Damn, I can't lecture you. I was into worse shit by your age, God knows, and wasn't half as successful in doing it. A man's perspective changes with age. You'll learn that someday.' 'Tried growin' up for awhile. Never seemed to stick.' 'Hell, you tried. That's more than I can say for most of my old friends. How's your mom?' 'Been awhile since I talked to her.' He nods and looks back to the bottle. It flushes down his throat like a river. 'Little chidlish, shyeah. But hey, I cared' The final shot and another line. 'I guess. Take it easy.' He falls back in his chair and the spirits of golden days fade away with his concentration. The silence fails in killing Trey and proves the old adage right. He gets to his feet and makes it back into the room that he'd been sent to in the first place. The comforter on the bed's taunting and cruel--he throws it to the pile of glass and lights a cigarette. That crazy one-second idea lodges in his mind and he holds the lighter under the corner of the woven-hell; it spreads and plumes smoke up to the ceiling. Trey inhales on his Red and watches the fire spread. The siren wails. Water cascades from the ceiling and Trey lets out a holy 'fuck' as he goes to leave the room. His broken cell phone crunches under his heel, doesn't slow him. In the hallway, he screams out a challenge. It runs to the roof of the building and echoes down in his own tense voice. 'C'mon, motherfucker.' His muscles tense as he stares at the elevator, convinced of his once quiet delusions. The sound of his footfalls, the only sound in the building, roars like lions at his back and pushes him on, as much curious as he is determined to keep the rhythm. He puts his hand to the grate, cold metal soft to the touch. The shaft beyond it's vacant as ever. And it creaks. He jerks back shocked, then finds himself curious and staring as far up as he can. A harsh rasp of a voice comes down, unintelligibly quiet but carrying on Trey's rhythm. A car follows it, slowly breaking the darkness as the voice grows. '...I've had a few. But then again, too few to mention. I did what I had to do and saw it through without exemption.' The face of the car's a tight series of coils, working with the outer grating to obscure everything of the rider but his cold blue eyes. 'I planned each charted course, each careful step along the byway. And more, much more than this,' time crashes to a halt as they stare, marked only by the passing of a single drum. At the very least, they have a few words to throw at one another. 'I did it my way.' Then another word. 'Croatoa.'
