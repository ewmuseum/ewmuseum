Furnace

29 August 2010

Date: 08/29/10 Six-thirty on his watch, heart thumping in his chest, and the gripless soles of old trainers flip-flapping on cold concrete. Murray practised this catharsis every morning. The fresh air cleared the head, the exercise loosened the body, and the hour ensured his privacy, but things had been difficult lately. When you live your life in the public eye you learn to cherish the moments away from the spotlight. You look forward to those little breaks from the limelight, no matter how short or mundane. You lose the rights to your private life the minute they put your name in lights, and every second spent driving your kid to school or washing the dishes becomes a blessing. Such opportunities don't often appear when you're one of the most marketable sports stars in the northern hemisphere, so you grab them with both arms when they do. But the blessing becomes a curse when your head's full of junk, and Andy's was just that. A smouldering furnace of doubt and apprehension, two months burning and not yet dead. He held thoughts that stewing and contemplation would only worsen, yet he still set out day after day, sunrise after sunrise, trying to find his solace. A futile quest for lucidity that wouldn't end until his demons were slain. He ploughed through Houston's cool morning in a sweat-soaked vest and loose black shorts. The streets were quiet before the morning rush, but still his size and celebrity drew onlookers. Commuters, postmen, other joggers; they all cocked their head for a quick glance that he wouldn't return, for they all wore the same face. Those beady blue eyes and that fucking smirk. That tidy clump of dirty blond hair and that perfect peach complexion. That million-dollar photoshop face. That unblemished boyband face. His face. Shipley's face. That same face he'd battered and bruised at WorldWide 136, now fully healed and attached to everyone in the city but him. Andy kept his head low: if one of the Shipleys made eye contact, if one of them spoke to him, he'd do a lot worse than bust lips and bruise foreheads. Shipley, that fucker, that worthless piece of shit. Rampage should've been the end of their little thing. He and Bagwell would stand side-by-side and their business with Shipley and Cheap Labor would be laid to rest. Andy would forget the broken rib and the verbal spats: pinning English shoulders to the mat would've been more than enough for him, but no. Tim wasn't so keen on settling their score. Infuriated by the incompetence of his partner and realising that his tournament participation might've meant a further two matches that night, Tim walked-out. He turned around, he walked away, he left Cheap Labor to die. Murray, deflated, took the victory. He'd barely laid a paw on Shipley and the result wasn't as satisfying as it should've been, but the man had made a fool out of himself. Tim Shipley showed the world everything he was made of. His cowardice was exposed and his yellow streak had grown a thousand miles wide. In one fell swoop he lost the little respect his name still carried in the locker-room. In that moment, Tim Shipley ceased to exist as a credible force in Global Championship Wrestling. And then he won Rampage. And everything changed. Suddenly his walk-out meant nothing. Suddenly David Yale and the online hordes were showering Shipley with praise, hailing his "genius" decision to conserve energy and focus on the matches that were more important to him. In the space of a couple of hours he'd gone from yellow-bellied choke merchant to brilliant schemer. His nickname, "The Cavalier," had never seemed more appropriate. A strange feeling overcame Murray. Not anger, not frustration, but the same feeling he felt when Jay Terror destroyed his 6-0 debut streak. The same feeling he felt when LeStatt Knight ended his record-breaking World Title reign. The same feeling he'd feel the following week against Jared Sykes, then the week after against Valentine. Defeat. Tim Shipley had defeated Andy Murray without even fighting him. He'd won a tournament Andy had conquered two years prior, and he'd done it, in-part, by bailing on their match. Tim Shipley had unzipped his jeans and urinated all over Andy Murray's legacy, perhaps deliberately, and the Scot seethed. It had been a while since someone had gotten under Andy's skin like Tim had, but Andy picked himself up. He dusted himself off, put the night behind him, and started looking forward. He was thirty-two – younger than Danté was when he journeyed through hell, but only by a couple of years – and he'd learned to accept that closure is closure, regardless of the outcome's favourability. Maybe this was how their story was supposed to end. Maybe. Then came 136. Then came Shipley knocking at his door. Then came Shipley trying to set him up again. Then came Murray's fist into Shipley's face. Bang, bang, bang and down goes Tim. The little bastard just couldn't stay away. He tasted defeat by Rasa's hand and knew he had to do something to make him the talk of the show again, so he stirred Andy Murray's pot again and High Stakes was signed. He had his chance at revenge but he'd have to be careful. Shipley was a fine wrestler without a moral code; a man who'd surely stoop to any length to steal victory. Recent disappointments against Valentine and Sykes had forced him to sharpen-up and regain focus, but he'd need more than that. He'd need every drop of piss and vinegar, every burst of fire and passion. He'd need to be on top of his game, he'd need to be as good as he's ever been. As good as he was when he beat LeStatt for the first time or when he wrestled Jason O'Neil. But most importantly, Andy Murray was going to need something he'd been missing for a long time: a clear head. He made it back to his hotel and leaned over. Short, shallow breaths slowed gradually as his normal heart rate return and sweat dried on his brow. Eventually he looked-up as two men walked down the staircase, both wearing Shipley's face. * * * * * Andy Murray wasn’t alone in the mire. She sat sobbing in her quiet house – Alison Hood, wife of John – her night’s reverie not yet broken by sleep. Another night lost to war between her head and heart, and all she had to show for it was the mountain of tissues at her feet. Alison was a ship without a rudder, a sheep without a herd. It should've been a simple decision, to bring her husband home, but it was one that had torn her apart. Those minutes spent in the visiting room had been among her life's most humiliating, but that was nothing compared to the knowledge her husband wasn't an innocent man. She asked him the question and he just sat there. Couldn't say a word, couldn't do anything to alleviate her quandary. He'd done it; he'd taken advantage of a dying man to line his own pockets. John, the man she called "darling", the man who'd got on one knee for her in Baha almost a decade ago, was a monster. But he was her husband. He'd given her two kids and a lifestyle she could only have dreamed of as a child, and nothing he'd done could change that. There was more than enough money in their account to fund his bail; the power to free her husband and reunite him with his family was at her fingertips. But could she do it? John had let her down in ways he could never repay. He'd lied to her, he'd sullied their family name, he'd done something so ruthless that Alison wondered if they could still share a future together. And she knew exactly why he'd done it. He'd been looking for a way to get back at Andy Murray for years. An eight year old misunderstanding had turned John Hood to Colonel Kurtz. It had lingered over their marriage like a dark shadow for years, and finally it had overcome him. He was too proud a man to forgive and forget, and now he suffered for it. Alison gazed out the window and gently dabbed her nose with the last tissue in the box. The sun was beginning to creep over the garden fence; her self-imposed deadline was about to pass, and she was shaking. Her eyes stung and her lips quivered, but there would be no more tears. Alison Hood reached over the kitchen table and picked-up the telephone receiver. She dialled. * * * * * If there was one good thing about being stuck in Houston it was having such easy access to the gym at GCW Headquarters. Two floors of work-out machines, saunas, and swimming pools, never occupied by more than a dozen people at any one time, and none of the juiceheads you'd find preening and posing around most American gyms. Andy always made good use of its facilities whenever he was in town, and that morning was no different. The ice baths were stuck in a far corner of the building's very top floor, and travelling all that distance alone was enough justification for their use. He dragged his sore limbs across the gym and plunged. Coldness stabbed his senses. Ice swirled around his cooling skin. Breaths became quicker. Muscles stiffened. Andy gripped the edges tightly, fighting against his body's every urge to escape his cold hell. The first ten seconds are always the worst, he told himself. Breathe. That's it, nice and slow. And slowly he started to feel comfortable. The bodyshock subsided and he closed his eyes. Some took the plunge immediately after exercise because they said it was good for the muscles. Andy did it for the shock; the slap in the face, the reminder that he was alive. His eyes crept open and he gazed through the door's tiny porthole window. A man he'd never seen before strolled by. It was the first man he'd seen that day with a face of his own, and not Tim Shipley's. Andy slid downwards, cocking his head back. The ice removed the weight from his shoulders, the chill calmed the storm in his head. However detestable he may be, Shipley knows how to take care of business, he admitted. He's a doer, a go-getter. He does what he does by being proactive, by applying his varied skillset, by slithering through the grass and striking when the time's right. Doesn't sit around and wait for opportunities to be handed to him, just goes out and gets the job done. He's Machiavelli. He's The Prince. But Tim Shipley wasn't the only thing on his mind. The John Hood situation was a mess, and would require Andy's attention sooner rather than later. He'd never be able to wrestle with a clear head with Hood in his life. This was a problem too great to ignore. The downturn in Andy's career since Hood had become his agent was too great to be a coincidence. Something needed to be done. Then there was his health. He'd blacked-out two weeks ago and staunchly refused an MRI scan, but a dizzy spell during his 136 clash with Dan Black may yet force him to reconsider. Then there was the slump. He'd beaten Black, sure, but beating Dan Black is not the same as beating Jay Terror, Tessa Windsor, or even Tim Shipley. A career that had shone brighter than all others for almost three years was dangerously close to burning out. A few more high-profile losses and he'd become little more than a gatekeeper, and the thought of that didn't even bear thinking about. Then there was the fact that he'd barely been home for four weeks, and that, a few phonecalls aside, he'd almost completely ostracised himself from his loved ones again. A strong family foundation had always been the bedrock of his success, and without it he was nothing. He needed to get back to Tallahassee, he had to see his son, but there were more pressing matters in Houston to deal with first. If he continued to sit back and watch his problems mount then it'd be the end of him. Maybe it was time to be a bit more proactive. Maybe it was time to be a bit more Cavalier. Five minutes in the bath brought him what he was looking for. Soon Andy was back in the changing rooms, drying himself off and pulling clothes over his body. He zipped-up his hoody and stuffed his hands into the pockets. One set of knuckles hit a thin slither of cardboard. He pulled it out, examined the name, examined the phone number. He had his starting point. * * * * * The urge to leap onto the top bunk and smash Ronnie Sobchek's face to a bloody pulp had become almost overwhelming. He knew she wouldn't let him down. * * * * * "How long do you need? One hour? Two?" The man's grip loosened. "Good," he said. "What do you want?" John repeated, clearer this time. Andy Murray reached inside his jacket and dropped Floyd's documents on John's chest. "I've come to take my life back". He stood up and watched John unfold the documents. He was wearing Shipley's face. * * * * * Andy was on his feet, throwing a pen to the downed Hood. "Sign them, John. End this now." He squinted and gawked at the documents, John, but his alcohol-sodden brain could make no sense of their words. Murray had sobered him, but not that much. "Whatt'a hell are these?" he said between laboured breaths. "They're deeds." "Deeds?" John raised his head. "For what?" "The Dallas Group." John's facial expression turned like milk. "That's my comp'ny!" "No, it's not. It's Rueben's company; you stole it." "Rueben's fuckin' dead!" It took every fibre of goodness in Andy's body not to picked John Hood up and beat him several shades of purple there and then, but he resisted. The wounds opened by Rueben's cancerous end still hadn't healed. "Just sign the fucking papers, John. I didn't pay all that money to bail you out just so we could sit here and have a nice chat." No bile, no venom: just John Hood raising a weak arm and pointing. Andy looked over his shoulder. A small piece of paper stood-out on the living room floor. "What's that?" "Jus' read it." Andy turned his back on John, who wasn't a threat. He took the handwritten letter and unfolded it curiously. His eyes scanned across the words and its message soon became apparent. "So she finally had the sense to walk away, huh?". His tone was cold, callous. He screwed the note into a ball and tossed it over his shoulder. "Boo-fucking-hoo." Hood was incandescent, but too full of alcohol and in Andy's control to rise and strike. Instead he pushed back to the wall and propped himself up. "You ain't gettin' my agency. Agency's all I got left!" "Yes, it is, and you have nobody to blame but yourself. You did this; John Hood did this. Nobod--" "No, YOU did this!" The ferocity of John's bark took Andy by surprise. "How mu--" "You fucked my wife." "That old pearl. That's what all this is for, John?! That's what all this mess is about?" Andy strode menacingly towards John. "She slept in my room. I did her a favour; she lost her room key, so I put her up for the night. And it was almost ten fucking years ago, John! Jesus. Fuck. How many times do I have to tell you? Nothing. Fucking. Happened." Nothing verbal from John, just a ball of spit and phlegm landing at Andy's feet. Hood glares, teeth showing. Andy gloured. "You'll never run another business in this town, John. Your name is mud. Everyone knows what you did; you're finished." "I'm still your agent. I can still run your career into the ground." "You're a fucking disgrace. Look at that state of you; I bet you can't even stand up straight." "State of me? What 'bout you?!" John pointed an accusing finger. "Last I heard I wasn't the only one having trouble standing, pal." Andy simmered through controlled breaths. Though inebriated, John Hood knew exactly what he was doing. His claws were out; he was digging. But Murray wouldn't get dragged into it. "Says he who's drank himself paralytic." "Least when I pass out, I have the decency to do it in my own home." He saw Andy's face sour. Saw that he was struggling to stay calm. "Yeah, s'right. I know your secret." Snap. Andy knelt. Grabbed Hood. Lifted him, pinned him to the wall. "You piece of fu--" "Go on! Hit me!" saliva hit Andy's face as Hood screamed. "Hit me! Beat the fuckin' shit outta me! Ain't gonna make you remember how to win a match, ain't gonna bring your wife back..." Andy pushed his forehead into Hood's. Kept it there. Balled a fist, ready to slug, but no. He's better than that. He could not lower himself to a man like Hood's level. He let go, took a step backwards. Shook his head as Hood stood, smirking. "Maybe you and me can be friends, Andy," John said mockingly. "My wife's been gone a couple hours, yours has been gone a couple years. Maybe you can give me tips." "One more word." "And you'll what? Push me against the wall and grunt again? Ohhh, scary." "One more word and I'll--..." And then it hit him again. Thump. Thump. Thump. Just like his match with Dan Black. Just like the first time he blacked-out. "I'll..." His vision grew fuzzy and vice-like pressure gripped his head. He could feel his heartbeat pounding his skull. "Awww, whats'a matter princess?" Hood said, stepping haphazardly towards Murray. "You look like you need to lie dow--" Four knuckles across Hood's jaw. Four knuckles sending him staggering against the wall. Murray hit with force and grabbed him by the shirt. With both hands he flung John into the lounge and kicked him onto his back. Red mist overcame him. Andy smashed his lunchbox-sized fist into John's face. Again. And again. And again. A cut opened across his knuckles as he struck Hood's mouth, but the two teeth the flung out made it worthwhile. Andy retracted; breathing, sweating. He used his trousers to wipe the blood from his hand, then hopped up. There wasn't much time left. He retrieved the deeds and came back through. John had rolled onto his stomach and was spitting out blood. "Sign it." Not a word. John put a finger in his mouth and poked at a bloody stub where there once was a tooth. "Fucking sign it!" Hood cocked his head aside. His eyes met Andy's. "You can't make me--" "Yes I fucking can!" He put the pen in John's hand. Gripped it tightly. Guided the pen towards the paper and etched an uneven scrawl across the last signature box. Just as John had done to Rueben. Murray sprawled to his feet and held a palm to his forehead. The pain was coming back again. No time to stay, no time to gloat. He barrelled out of John Hood's house as fast as his legs could carry him. Hood, defeated, rested his head by the pool of vodka and closed his eyes. * * * * * Andy got the to car, put the key in the ignition. Drove. A minute in and lights turned to shapeless blurs. Sight was failing him and all sounds has been reduced to a gargled woosh. Dizziness. Headache. Limbs turning to jelly. Gotta stop. Gotta stop or you're gonna crash. He stopped just in time. The car halted on a grassy verge and he spilled from the door. He crawled on all fours and closed his eyes, preparing for the inevitable. But it didn't happen. Instead an unstoppable torrent of bile and vomit shot-up his wind-pipe and exploded from his mouth. A noxious pool formed around his hands and he tried to stand but it was too late. He threw-up again. He was shaking when he stopped, and he leaned back, resting his head against the car's front wheel. He opened his eyes and everything was gone. No headache, no blurry vision, no sinus pressure. Gone, all of it. His body was flushed, cleansed, free of all that had plagued it. And soon his mind would be to. He sat roadside in an unfamiliar city, unable to move. Cool night air kissed his clammy skin as he spat the vile taste from his mouth and looked to the stars. "Thank you, Tim," he whispered. "Thank you." * * * * * Polished black brogues and a neat pinstripe suit stepped through the front door and into the lobby. A man of spindly frame and pale skin tucked his fedora under his arm and puffed cigarette smoke into the air. Not a sound broke the silence. The serenity struck him, but he said nothing; just casts his cold, dead eyes around the house and smokes. Then he heard it. "Ughhh." The faintest of groans from the other room, long and drawn-out. His heavy footsteps echoed loudly on the hardwood floor as he negotiated his way around strewn debris. He found what he was looking for crumpled in the corner; a bloody mess showing no signs of life but the heaving of his chest. He knelt beside his target, ignoring the damage around him. "Oh, John," he sighed. "What a disappointed you turned out to be." The man rose languidly, dangling the cigarette between his thumb and index finger. He noted the puddle of clear fluid by Hood's body and stepped backwards. "If only you hadn't gotten yourself arrested. Guess we'll just have to find somebody else for the job." The cigarette fell from his fingers as the man turned away, igniting the pool of vodka on contact. Flames spread quickly across the floor and up the walls, but the man was far from earshot by the time John started screaming.
