Autumn.

1 April 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/01/11 December, 2010 Anne Lowell was fat. If she had much in the way of personality, a kinder term like 'sturdy' or 'larger' might have applied. As it was, she was fat - not excessively so, only to the point that calling her chubby or chunky seemed too polite - and had clearly stopped dying her hair a few months ago, apparently having given up on hiding the grey hairs that were mixing with the dull, dirty blonde. The copper bracelet she wore was an oddity since the appointment where she'd dismissed the idea of using metals and magnets to assist joint pain as 'bullcrap', but Amy Campbell actually had a far more favorable opinion of her painfully flat persona than most of her other patients. For that (and the excellent insurance policy she normally had), Doctor Lowell actually endeavored to help the little redhead a little more than she did for the usual aches, pains and cash-cows with headcolds that came through the office. ''So we'll start on the Levodopa like I mentioned, then,'' the huge oak desktop where she did most of her paperwork had started showing its age long before Campbell registered as a patient. ''It's the cheapest way to check by far. In about a week, you should notice improvement if I'm right. We'll continue it for a little longer after that to rule out a positive reaction as a side-effect. There are other tests, like I said, but nothing is really that accurate, and they're honestly not worth putting yourself in debt over while you don't have insurance.'' ''I might have to take another job just to get insurance back. Not much of a choice there.'' ''You will. Maybe try something where you're not constantly being abused this time?'' Anne offered a small smile. No teeth, just a grin - but it was a big gesture for someone who was usually monotone and mask-faced. Amy was clearly annoyed by the comment - nose curling in a snarl - but she calmed quickly. The woman writing the prescriptions had a right to poke fun, after all. ''I do mean that sincerely, though. If this is any number of the things we talked about, you're looking at a long road that doesn't physically get any easier.'' ''And if it's not...'' ''It's like I told you,'' Dr. Lowell interrupted, plunking a meaty forearm down over her prescription pad. ''No matter what we see from the medication, you can still bet on a lot of tests, specialists, wait times and bills. And on top of that, you're intent on seeing a plastic surgeon now. That gets expensive.'' ''I can get the money for that,'' it wasn't an option. ''I'm sure I can. It's a one-time thing, not a long--'' ''It's something you should really see someone about in any event.'' After appointments, Amy always tried to figure out what a doctor's office smelled like. Dentists had a smell. That office, though... nothing. The only thing she ever really remembered about it was the annoying crinkling noise the sterile paper made over the padded exam table. That, and she always wondered who in the hell had knit little covers for the stirrups. Probably the elderly nurse; She had the strange cat lady vibe that made anyone believe she'd take time out of her home life to make someone's feet more comfortable during their cervical exam. ''Amy?'' ''Hmm?'' ''This is serious. I said you should see someone about--'' ''Well, I made this appointment.'' ''Not just me. I can refer you to someone who can really sit down with you, who's qualified to help you work through the anxieties and trau--'' ''The only trauma is physical, trust me,'' Amy snapped. ''And it'll be fixed soon.'' ''Right. I'll make that referral when we're done, but you should consider letting me refer you to a psychologist as well. They're the lesser evil we talked about - they're not like psychiatrists, they can't throw prescriptions at you.'' ''I'm honestly fine as it is, I swear.'' ''I won't push. Just keep it in mind if you're ever finding any of this a little overwhelming. I can get you in to see someone at no cost in this case, so just let me know.'' ''I will, but I'm fine. It's not gonna get like that.'' ''Well...'' Anne leaned back in her chair and heaved a sigh. ''We can hope not.'' When Amy left that day, the elderly cat lady waved like she always did. She smiled back. One day, Campbell thought, she'd be that woman, but bound to a wheelchair if she even made it that far. ********** The engine was running rough again. It wasn't the rain - the noise was a constant - so she discounted any electrical issue or open wire that might have been a cheap fix. Like everything else in her life, the thing was probably just showing its age, slowly degenerating. If oil changes weren't such a pain to keep track of for someone who might fly to three different states in the run of a single week, she’d still had some kind of warranty to lean on. But, like her own insurance, that was long gone. She'd have it looked at. Maybe get it into the garage off Fifth and Main as early as next week, if The Kent Fusiliers Company actually got her a residual cheque for merchandise. That wasn’t likely, though; their site was running out of stock, and their track record was to not pay her, even when they were in the black. She helped build them, but they couldn't be bothered to compensate her for the damage done to her - something she'd have to fix out of her own goddamn pocket now that their flagship had shut down - that led to their demise as a company, they'd probably laugh at a call to ask for timely payment so the clunking noise in her Cadillac's motor could be checked out. They had an attitude, like she owed them for being the victim of the act that got them pushed from television. Like she was the reason they'd gone downhill. If anything, she was the reason they hadn't drowned years before. Not that she was keeping score, of course. She ran the Cadillac as hard as she always had. It wasn't having an immediate death – truth be told, she worried about it breaking down on the turn down the next block - but the choke was more than some underlying problem. It was crippling the rest of the system. Now, the gauges and CD player hissed and blinked and popped static as momentary seizes on the expressway made her grip the steering wheel. The problems were stacking up. She wished she'd paid any attention to the thing when it was running well - maybe she'd have more of an idea what was really wrong now that it wasn't. They were both running on empty. She was a fantastic fighter when her hands agreed with her, but that wasn’t the case anymore. Her body, like her car, was just a machine with troubled handling from pushing the pedal to the metal. Thankfully, the Cadillac got her back to what was now passing for home. She'd once scraped the side of somebody's parked car in the back lot of Claire's building, and even though it made her hesitant, she still parked back there. Just on the other side is all. The side whose street lights were burned out from before she’d moved in. The walk inside could get sketchy at night. It was a nice part of Oakland, but that didn’t say much. They were still downtown, which was full of dipshits who'd try coaxing a wrestler into a fight just at the chance to puff out their chest. The thought of it was amusing, actually, and she'd love to have taken the opportunity once or twice just to see the look on the drunkards' faces when they realized she didn't have anything but a car to sue her for. By the time she locked the grumpy Caddy and began the slow, sore walk up the stairway, that pathetic scenario had carved the mother of all self-deprecating grins onto her face. She dug into her jeans, her heart skipping a beat when she came up empty. She’d lost the key, didn’t she? One day, she'd resign herself to the fact that she really was living there, but her pride wouldn’t allow it. She never put it on a chain like Claire had told her to. She was her guest. A guest who had to knock on the door because of wounded, foolish pride. The knob turned and the door hung open. The lady in the doorway was taller than her by a good few inches, as most people were. She wore dark hair in a sort of boy's cut, short and swooped to the left. Amy thought she was pretty, even after the disaster that was their very brief bout of dating years before. They dressed a lot alike, keeping it simple with fitted t-shirts and skinny jeans. She lacked visible tattoos. ''You left the key by your computer again,'' Claire chuckled when she turned from the doorway, her eyes and hands shuffling through pieces of paper by the massive iMac monitor on her own desk. ''Don't worry, if you weren't back before I left I was just gonna leave it unlocked. How was your appointment?'' ''I'll be fine.'' Amy’s voice was sheepish and muted. ''She said that? 'Cause you've been getting worse.'' Claire didn't look up from the printouts, instead comparing two color photos. One of them was actually of Amy, laying half-asleep on the couch next to her cat. Her best friend. ''I know you're not down to talk about it, but I saw the other day when you could hardly get that jar of salsa open.'' Amy shrugged as she stepped inside, shutting the door behind her. ''That was just a nervous moment.'' The apartment itself was gorgeous - an art deco-inspired loft with an open-concept kitchen/livingroom and a single bedroom. A lot of the unit space was taken up by the Whirlpool tub in the bathroom, which was larger than the space Amy had to herself. As lovely as the place was, it was cramped for a couple, much less two independent individuals. No matter how solid their friendship was, Claire was doing her a rent-free favor in part to repay her for convincing her ex-husband to let her stay with them years before, when she'd first moved from where they'd grown up in Nova Scotia to be closer to LA's art community. ''If you say so,'' They'd known one another long enough to know the boundaries, and Claire had already been picking at one. The fight wasn't worth it - the other woman knew her concern already. ''You gonna be cool without me for a few hours? I've got that meeting with the university about the gallery show next month.'' ''Seriously, I'm - hey, sexy!'' Amy's smile returned when she saw her closest companion - a blob of wet diet food and feline fur she, and her ex-husband, had affectionately named Reaper. She cringed when she bent down to pick him up, and elected to run a hand from head to his tail as she answered her friend instead. ''I'll be fine. I'm not six, y'know.'' ''I know, just... I dunno.'' Claire shrugged, still whipped into a frenzy by her impending appointment. She plucked her thick-framed glasses from the desk, stuffing the copies she'd been inspecting into a folio she'd slung onto the sofa that centered the room. ''I worry about your crazy ass, especially after that last going-off with Kingsl--'' ''Can we not do this?'' ''I know, I know. You handle so much more shit than anyone I've ever met - I can't help but worry. Plus when you're not here, I'm the one who answers your mom's calls.'' Amy's laugh was a genuine thing. ''And I'll never be able to fully repay you for dealing with that.'' ''Oh! Before I go...'' The lanky whirlwind of a woman paused for a moment to face her, gesturing lightly around her own chest. ''Did the doc get you an appointment for the...?'' ''She's doing a referral, yeah.'' ''And you know that if you need any help with the cost... I know they stiffed you...'' Amy waved her off, shaking her head. ''I might be a mess, but I can handle this.'' With folders and portfolios gathered into long, thin arms, Claire looked overwhelmed. ''Don't I know it.'' She paused, making sure she had everything gathered together as she made for door. ''Okay, so I'm like twenty minutes out, but I don't think they're looking for me for another--'' Even though Amy was trying to hide how much that little bit of care meant to her, she planted a kiss along her bob of hair before her friend made it out. ''You can go, y'know. I'm not gonna spontaneously combust or anything,'' she chuckled, hopping onto the couch. She was fishing in her pocket again, producing a small wad of bills. ''But... wanna do me a favor on your way back tonight?'' ''Whatcha need?'' ''My car's almost on empty, and I'd rather avoid refilling until next week if I can,'' she held the bills between her fingers, wrapping them in a small piece of paper and dangling her arm over the back of the sofa. ''Can you stop at the Walgreen's down the way and get this filled? Saves me a trip, and I'll call ahead so--ah, shit!'' She tensed to grip. A wave of trembles slithered through her hands. ''I've got--'' Even though Claire moved to put her things down and pick it up herself, Amy had rounded the couch by the time she could act, snatching the money and prescription slip from the floor. Her hand was violently shaking. She tossed them onto the end table next to the door - closest to her friend - before shoving both hands in her pants pockets. ''Uh. I'll call ahead. Just take that and--'' ''You don't need to give me cash for something like that, Ames, I can--'' ''No, no. You take the fucking money,'' she snapped, pink coloring her cheeks. ''Don't treat me like this, okay? I just... I'm not gonna be that fuck-up who can't take care of themselves, alright?'' The weight of those words punched Claire to the heart. ''I... okay. I'll pick it up before the art director takes me to drinks. She always does, makes her feel special or some shit to throw her...'' She was going to say 'To throw her money around'. ''To... y'know. To butter the artists up.'' A nervous smile spread across her face. ''I should be back around... like, one-ish?'' ''Whenever,'' Amy sat back down, trying not to face her. Reaper hopped up onto the couch, nestling against her thigh. Her wobbling hand rubbed his belly. ''Don't rush. I'll probably chill here with McChubbycat and Netflix for the night.'' ''Cool. I'll just set you on fire if you're asleep.'' Claire joked from the doorway, trying to diffuse the tension. ''Hey, you wanna take my bed tonight? It's better than the floor, and I'll probably be out here working all night after I'm home anyway...'' ''Nah. I'm good here.'' ''You sure?'' ''I'll tell you later if I'm not. Promise.'' ''Whatever you say,'' she repeated. ''I'll see you in, like... twelve hours? Don't get bored and start chewing the furniture.'' Claire always had a way of bringing a smile to her face. She raised a hand to wave her off, and once the door closed, she let out a sigh she'd been holding in since she'd gotten there. ''What the hell are we gonna do, buddy?'' Most people look at talking to a cat as pathetic. In her situation, it's par for the course. Pathetic was the fact that she had to fake a smile for his sake. ********** ''Sinemet...'' The towel twitched in her hands. ''Madopar.'' Rather, one of them twitched the towel. ''Stal--what was it--Stalevo?'' It seemed like some days, something as simple as drying herself off was a chore. ''Prolova, No. Prolopa.'' In a frenzied motion, she mussed the damp towel through her hair. ''The fuck was the other...'' she grumbled. ''Ah! Atamet!'' She leaned down, staving off aches and pains in her back and bad knee to retrieve the towel she'd thrown underfoot for traction, wrapping it around herself. Wiping a hand across the steam on the mirror, she met her own mismatched eyes. ''I like Atamet. Starts with the right letter. Good, solid name for a drug.'' Reaper looked bored. If it wasn't for the fact that the bathroom was the only room in the apartment aside from the kitchen that could fit a litter box, she wouldn't have locked him in there while she showered. She couldn’t have him ruining the upholstery of Claire’s furniture, at least not with medical bills and car trouble and no job and nothing left in the bank. She cracked the door open, and he poured out of the small room along with the cloud of steam. As cool air flooded in from the living room, the fog staining the mirror dissipated. It wasn't long before she was staring at more than her eyes. The towel clutched to her breasts, but it left her tattooed heart laid bare. Around it, her wings were cracked with jagged lines of scabbing. She wanted so badly to look away, but there it was – the undeniable reminder of what she’d endured the last time she stepped inside a ring. It wasn’t just a bad memory - she’d spent the days and weeks since reliving it as fresh and vivid as the night it happened. Her hand fell to her chest unconsciously, a nervous tremor making her fingers cut uncoordinated lines in the air, others tracing the contours of the carefully-inked heart and the wings and the flowers she'd had tattooed around it. She shut her eyes when she touched to the first of the damp scabbing. The scab was superficial by now, but that didn’t change the fact that it was still there. Some of it had flaked off when she'd been drying off - she hadn't looked at her chest, but she'd seen a few little flecks fall to the floor. The thin tissue underneath was still sore when she pressed into it. She’d let her nails grow out since her last match - there wasn't really a need to cut them anymore. It made her feel like a normal woman, having a little more to put a coat of gloss on - and that's what led to the first motion. She'd scratched - her skin was itchy, especially with the tattoo ink being so disturbed - and caught something under her nail. She should’ve have stopped, but the urge to see what was underneath was too strong. Maybe it was her OCD, the same strange sensation that had made her pick at her arms obsessively as a child, but there was the overwhelming question... had it truly healed? Was she normal underneath all of that excess skin and dried puss? So she pulled. She squeezed her eyes shut again and she peeled the skin back. The first bit of scabbing was over normal flesh, so it fell into the sink as brownish, clear sections of skin. As she advanced, the pieces got colorful - green and black from the wings, yellow and red from the lifting of the long-healed ink on her heart. She only paused to press a shaking hand against her chest, steadying her breathing. She would not have another panic attack over him. Not then. Not when she might finally be free. It seemed like an eternity before she finished. Blindly running water in the sink, she let the pieces of herself circle the drain, coaching herself to keep her eyes open as she rose them up, finally meeting her visage in the mirror again. She blinked tears from her eyes. The 'A' was there, carving a dark pink line. Same with the N. In between, looking closely, she could read the lines that distorted the ink. The flesh had mostly healed - her tattoos weren't ruined, but the word was there. The fog was gone. Autumn wasn't. Peeling back the death hadn't done a fucking thing. If she could have paid to replace it, the mirror would have been broken. Her fist would be bloody, but the shards of glass and Boxer's Fracture might have distracted her just enough. Just a little. That scar would remain. Terrence Kingsley’s will was done. SCCW was gone and so was her pride, her career... her body was the only thing that might have still given her some dignity. She could have had something, some kind of a life after... Instead, the name of Kingsley's love remained on her heart. No man or woman would stare at Amy for what she was - they'd be too busy trying to read the unsightly gashes. They'd ask 'What's that? Why Autumn?'. Her chest rose and fell, and she steepled hands that were growing out of her control with their shakes over her eyes and nose, brows knitting together. It all rose up, the anger, the bile, the unbearable sense that she was living through that one defining moment where the loser finally hits rock bottom. It wasn't going to go down like that. She shook her head and let her arms fall. The towel fell, too, but she was past caring. She nearly tore the door off its hinges going into the livingroom, reaching into the Rubbermaid tub that held the few things she hadn't put in storage. This home wasn't hers, she told herself, she couldn't destroy it. She had to contain the feeling. She had to get dressed. She had to leave without going to the freezer and getting the gin Claire kept. He could take a pound of her flesh and a pint of her blood right alongside her dignity, but he would not claim her sobriety. The pants were confining enough. She didn't bother with a belt. She ignored bras, too - zipping a thick hoodie up to her throat over her tanktop. Nobody would look at her chest tonight night. She didn't stop to pet the cat, and she'd later realize she forgot her key again, but it didn’t matter right now. Her laptop was left on, displaying the Wikipedia page for L-DOPA - Levodopa, among the other clinical names she'd recited to the mirror. She just grabbed her phone and an elastic to tie her hair and just ran. ********** She could have thrown up, but it wouldn't have helped. She should have showered before she even thought about interacting with people, but there was no way in hell she wanted to go near that mirror again. Instead, as the endorphins and natural morphine coursed through her, Amy turned into the door of her favorite bar. She shut her eyes when she saw the drinks advertised on the chalkboard outside. He wouldn't take her sobriety. The bartender lived in Claire's building. He thought she and Amy were room mates, and neither ever corrected that she was, essentially, just a squatter. After one look at her that day, it seemed like he expected the worst. He smiled wide when she asked for water. Just water. Because she could barely feel her legs - because she was so exhausted - she didn't shake. There were no tremors. There was no Terrence Kingsley. She was still working from the rush just after midnight, when she pushed away from the bar with a defeated sigh. It was still early, but the last three hours had done nothing to help her. It had been a line of uninteresting people having unintelligent conversations. That wasn't to say she was looking for much more - just a distraction, or someone to punch, if she could afford to fight an assault charge - but the crowd that night was below par, having been drawn in by the cheap drink specials she'd told them would bring out the morons that weekend. It didn't get any better as she stepped away to leave, either - she bumped into one of the chimps, some mountain that had been lurking a little too close for comfort. He smiled sweetly - dimly, she thought - at her, one huge hand offering some cocktail. ''Drink?'' ''I'm not drinking.'' ''I know. Bartender told me you're here a lot but you don't drink anymore. I just couldn't come up with any other way to start the conversation, so I ordered something I wouldn't mind drinking for you.'' He laughed and lowered his head to sip from the tiny red straw, still meeting her eyes and grinning as he went. Amy had to admit that he didn't seem to care much for looking manly, and that did a good job of setting him apart from the other apes who'd tried striking up a conversation. The facts remained, though; He was tall, muscular, attractive and very likely just looking to get laid. ''You get points for the enlightened pickup, but I'm really not looking to--'' ''You're not gonna believe it, but neither am I.'' ''I didn't even tell you what I was avoiding.'' ''But I can guess it's the fantasy of some jerkoff who you think just wants to get head in the bathroom.'' She chuckled. ''You've had problems with douchebags trying to pick you up too, huh?'' ''With an ass like mine?'' He licked his lips. ''C'mon girl, can you blame 'em?'' The little redhead smiled again at the joke, this time with more sincerity and far less defense. It was infectious, and he couldn't help but do the same when he continued. ''Seriously, I didn't come over here thinking what you think I was. And yeah, I know that's probably what we all say.'' ''Well aren't you adorably self-aware.'' ''Do you work tomorrow?'' ''Do you know who I am?'' ''Nope, but my name's Ryan.'' He extended a hand that she stared at instead of shaking. ''And you're a cute, tattooed lady in a bar. Maybe a model?'' ''Is that flattery?'' ''Most girls with that much ink make good use of it.'' Amy was struck by his eyes. They were sky blue, offsetting dark hair and stubble perfectly. ''Not a model.'' ''Blogger, small business owner?'' he waved a hand. ''Nevermind. You can tell me at lunch.'' Her brows furrowed. ''Lunch?'' ''On your break from whatever you do, come to lunch with me.'' he said. ''No,'' Campbell put her elbows on the bar. ''Look, guy, you've got no idea the kind of day I've had. Hell, you don't know the kind of year I've had, so maybe you should just--'' ''Please come to lunch with me?'' Her answer had been so matter-of-fact, but clearly a part of a front. ''It's the middle of the day, so you can't think I'm trying to get you to spend the night.'' ''Begging, really?'' ''Asking nicely. Figure you won't be so damn on guard if I'm not just some asshole talking to you in a bar.'' She tilted her head. ''I'm going home in a few minutes. You're gonna bug me until I do, aren't you?'' ''If I had much of a plan, guess you could say that'd be it, yeah.'' ''I'm not giving you my number,'' Amy snapped. He took another sip, then unwrapped a brown bar napkin from around the glass. ''Figured that, so I wrote my number here. E-mail's there too, in case you're scared I'll track down your phone on the van ride back from the elementary school where I pick up my other dates.'' ''I thought it wasn't a date?'' ''Jesus,'' he threw two meaty hands in the air. ''A guy can't make one child molestor joke without getting shit from you!'' They both smiled. ''Lunch,'' she finally said. ''I don't wake up before noon, so I'll meet you at one thirty. You know the bistro like three doors down from here?'' ''I do now.'' ''Don't dress up or do anything too douchey,'' Amy shook her head. ''I owe you a meal for working against the arsenal I put up.'' After that, they settled. He kept her for another fifteen minutes, exchanging playful barbs, and Amy marveled at the idea that he was either a pro at mimicking people, or actually shared her abusive sense of humor. His persistence was admirable, but she had no idea exactly what kind of man he was. He didn't take 'No' for an answer. That said, the back-and-forth was a fantastic escape. He didn't know who she was - didn't know her damages - and, briefly, she got to be somebody else. Someone normal. For a little while, she was a woman who had nothing to run from. ********** She didn't even think about her key until she hit the stairs. All over again, her heart skipped a beat - though maybe it had just been the run - because she didn't exactly relish the idea of waking her host up just to let her in. When she turned the knob, though, Claire smiled. She’d left it unlocked. Upon entry, Amy discovered that she’d held true to her word - she was up, sitting at her computer, and spun around in the chair to greet the little redhead. ''Ooooh, someone was out late!'' The inflection spoke of a joke, but Amy could see in her eyes that the woman questioned her sobriety. ''You, uh... everything's okay, right? I saw towels on the floor in the bathroom and some of your stuff was on the floor out here, I didn't know if there was an emergency or--'' ''Nah,'' Amy didn't take her hoodie off, and sweat was beading on her forehead again. Her hair was stiff and sticky from what had already dried after her run. ''I just needed to get out, get some air. Feel human again, y'know?'' ''You were getting air until two in the morning?'' ''I stopped by the bar, and--'' Claire stood up from her chair, grabbing a paper bag that had been set on the desk beside her. ''Tell me you didn't...'' ''No!'' Amy quickly cut her off. ''Jesus, do you really think that little of me?'' ''I just worry. '' Claire bit her lip, lolling her head before straightening her neck. ''I know it's rough.'' ''Oh, trust me, I wanted to.'' She was quick to point out, shutting the door to make sure she didn’t wake the neighbors. ''That fuckwit... it doesn't look like I'll ever heal from where he carved me up. I'm gonna have to get the plastic surgery, I think, and I'll still look fucked. I wanted to overdose, drink, do anything to get out of my body for a minute.'' ''Gonna be honest... you look like shit.'' ''I ran for a couple of hours straight,'' She barely caught the bag when she tossed over. There was a prescription printout stapled to the front. ''Thanks, by the way. But yeah, I ran and then I... well, I kind of met someone.'' Claire couldn’t help but roll her eyes. ''Oh, God. Not this again.'' ''Not like that.'' ''You say that every time.'' ''Well," Amy started. ''Going to lunch on someone else's dime is a hell of a lot better than sitting here doing nothing all day while you get rich and famous.'' Claire laughed. ''Flattery will get you nowhere. Just promise me you're not gonna--'' ''Seriously, I barely like the kid, I'm not gonna fuck him.'' She interjected, tearing open the bag. She carefully examined the pill bottle inside, including the warning sticker about nausea on the side, holding it up near the light in her ever-so-slightly trembling hand. ''Do you mind if I go into your room and just check this out really quick?'' ''Go ahead. You need water?'' As an answer, Amy shook the water bottle she'd set down on the table. ''Oh - the pharmacist told me you've gotta be careful about taking it with food. Protein does something shitty to the way it works or whatever.'' ''Then it's good thing I've been too fucked up to eat all day.'' The day ended with her first dose of the medication that might prove her diagnosis. Even though she had to fight the urge to vomit once it set in, it had been a victory. Though nothing could be proven for a week or more, she'd already started to feel better. Not physically - the tremors were still there - but mentally. That day, she'd hit the bottom of the barrel, but retained the only sense of identity she had left. She had a friend who cared, she had her sobriety, and she had something promising by way of an off-duty police officer in a bar. She'd fallen, she'd failed. But, one day at a time, she'd try. She'd try until it killed her. And at times, she wanted it to.
