Mind Games

19 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 19/04/2006 02:00am Tuesday 18th April 2006. Some motel room. Pick one. They all look the same. Somewhere. Anywhere. Nowhere. McKail lay on the lumpy, uncomfortable bed, glaring up at the ceiling, the same surreal feeling of alertness, unhealthily mixed with heavy fatigued. It had just turned two in the morning and he’d yet to get any sleep. Insomnia, most called it, but the sad truth of the matter was, without Mary lying next to him, he’d always found it difficult to sleep - even before she...went away. Another day, another motel room. The same cycle, with virtually the same interior decoration he’d seen in hundreds of generic motel rooms before this. Although, he did have to admit this was one of the better ones he’d had the misfortune to frequent. Some were unspeakably grotesque, but often he took what he could get in terms of accommodation. Such was life on the road. None of the rooms were anything like the apartment he and Mary had once occupied. Okay it wasn’t much, but they’d at least made it liveable. Mary had always had an eye for decorating. McKail often joked about it with her, but one night she’d told him that her dream was to decorate professionally. Such a simple dream and now, Mary would never see her dreams come into fruition. Deep down, she knew that it was all his fault. He was her husband. He should’ve noticed something was wrong and although there was no feasible rationale behind this paradigm, it was how he perceived it to be and nobody thus far had been able to talk him out of thinking - feeling - this way. Not that he’d listen to them, if they tried. McKail did his best to shake these thoughts from his head and sat upright in bed in order to help further shepherd them away. Making himself feel guilty would not aid his cause to get at least few hours shut-eye before his scheduled appointment with the court-appointed counsellor in the morning - although he half suspected they’d be plenty of opportunity to get a few hours sleep during the session for all the good the appointment would do him. McKail sleepily ran his hand through his hair and carried it on to gently massage the back of his neck, before slumping back down what the motel laughably constituted as a bed. McKail allowed the thoughts of guilt and doubt wash over him like a great tidal wave, until his mind was utterly exhausted and just couldn’t subject him to any more of the self-imposed torture. McKail embraced the pain heartily; it would keep him more focused than any amount of sleep ever could. ---- During my debut match, in the newcomer battle royal, I faired a lot better than even I expected. My tactics were flawless and my in-ring ability was superior. Pure and simple. It was a positive step toward my ultimate goals - a positive step toward my redemption and promises made. I was actually surprised things went so well, considering how the night started out. One of the GCW’s blonde bimbo reporters, Kelly Ford I think her name was, tried to get an interview with me. Some footage of me answering her stupid questions, begging the fans and the audience around the world for their ever so precious attention. The very idea disgusts me, but I have to admit, one point in my career it didn’t! I couldn’t tell you precisely when my mindset in this regard changed. Perhaps during the three months I spent by my wife’s bedside as she slowly slipped away from me? Perhaps the sudden shock of being all alone in the world for the first time in many years? Perhaps my utter determination to fulfil my promise to Mary and succeed at any cost? However way you look at it, it did happen and I’m not a simple jester any longer. I’m here to dominate the inside of the ring in order achieve the kind of glory that I truly desire, the kind of glory I need. The sooner people like Kelly Ford and the rest of the vultures backstage understand that, the less insignificants problems will arise in the future. I manage to put the newcomer battle royal out of my head and focus on my future. The victory was a great one for me, but I cannot dwell in the past for too long, especially with a new challenge waiting for me just around the corner at this week’s GCW Worldwide. It’s only my second match in the organisation, but just looking at the booking you would never consider it. A three way dance, between me, Virgil Ambrosi and Jayce Bradley, two well established GCW veterans, for - and get this - the GCW Television title. My second match in the GCW and I wind up with an opportunity to win gold. Can you imagine that? The GCW Television Title strapped, effortlessly, around my waist, as I progress up the GCW hierarchy toward my destined goals? Well I can. I will maintain my focus and persevere at any cost. At the rate I’m going, reaching my ultimate goals won’t be that long in coming. ---- 17:20pm Tuesday 18th April 2006. Counsellor Flannigan’s office. New York. McKail sat back in the extremely comfortable leather chair and simply glared at Miss Flannigan, the court-appointed counsellor, questioningly. The meeting had started almost twenty minutes ago and all that the counsellor had done thus far was to glare at him. The government was paying for all of this, believing it to be the proper punishment he deserved after causing a scene and creating havoc at the hospital just after he’d the news and from what McKail could tell, the government were being ripped off. No matter how hard he tried, he just couldn’t conceal a wide, toothy grin, thinking about this. The silence was fine by McKail, he didn’t particularly want to talk to her about himself anyhow. He hated talking about himself at the best of times, but with his recent...loss, he really was opposed to it. However, McKail was not fooled by her games. She was trying to play him, get into his head and rummage around inside, looking desperately for secrets and weaknesses. He’d seen, or had it explained to him by some of the other more “challenged” guys from the roster of his previous wrestling organisation, a hundred times before. “Do you blame yourself for the death of your wife?” She finally blurted out, trying to catch him off guard. She hoped the extremely emotive question would him angry, angry enough to talk. McKail didn’t get angry. He knew something like this was coming and had managed to prepare himself for it. “No.” If she was surprised by his answer, she didn’t show it. She just raised her left eyebrow slightly and scribbled some notes down upon her pad. As she adjusted herself in her seat, McKail noticed her wince in pain, cradle her ribs slightly and then checked herself, becoming as placid as she was before. This time it was McKail who raised his eyebrow and made a note, albeit a mental one. “I don’t believe you,” she told him. McKail rolled his eyes. “That’s your prerogative, I guess.” “How can you not blame yourself?” The counsellor questioned. “I mean, she was your wife, you must have realised there was something wrong with her.” McKail felt a flash of anger rise up from the pit of his stomach and tried desperately to keep it in check. He stood, turned his back on Flannigan and stood by the window, glaring out at the beautiful, sun-drenched surroundings. He thought she’d do this, but he didn’t think she’d go this far. She really knew how to pull his strings. “Look, Mr McKail,” Flannigan said. She removed her glasses and gently messaged the centre of her forehead with her index finger. “We don’t have to play it like this. I wouldn’t need to be so crude, if you weren’t being so difficult.” McKail remained silent, but glanced over his shoulder at her, as she painfully pushed herself up from her own chair, still cradling those ribs. “I suggest we adjourn this session for now,” Flannigan told him. “We’ll pick up from here next week.” McKail nodded in response and made his way toward her office door, stopping to whisper in her ear. “I’d get those ribs looked at if were you.” Despite her completely placid face, McKail could tell from her eyes that she was both ashamed and angered by his observation. Obviously she had something to hide, everybody does, but it didn’t much matter to McKail what her dirty little secret was. He’d extracted his own form of petty revenge and that’s all that seemed to matter to him at the moment. The false sensation of gratification was enough for him at the moment; enough to forget the pain he’d probably be trying to escape for the rest of his life. As he exited the office, Flannigan slammed the door shut behind him, allowing her anger to finally manifest physically and McKail smiled. End
