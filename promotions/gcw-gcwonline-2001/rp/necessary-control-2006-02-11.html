Necessary Control

11 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02/11/2006 Jacob McKail sat in the GCW locker room, staring at his feet and a wet towel draped over the back of his head. The reasons for which were twofold. For one, it helped with his headache. His rage in the ring with that damned slow counting referee was just about the worst that’d ever struck him and brought on a colossal migraine that hurt more than all the headaches he’d experience in his life all crammed together to make his life a living hell. Secondly, it covered his face and masked his features from anyone who wasn’t specifically looking for him. He wasn’t feeling too proud of himself right about now. So the anger thing was a bit of an issue, he considered. But what did you expect? For the past week or so, he’d put himself through some gruelling things in order to get something of an edge within wrestling industry; an edge he so desperately needed. Things hadn’t been going so well for him as of late and he needed to jolt himself onto the next level. he needed to evolve or quit and he had no intention of quitting. He hadn’t finished yet. Control yourself, damn it! He silently chastised himself. You have to handle this! He’d allowed, even welcomed, long forgotten -- and incredibly painful -- memories from his past to resurface and he once again became the man he’d thought was lost forever. It’s said that your past will always come back to haunt you, but in McKail’s case, it’d come back to help him. Or at least that’s how the theory held up. In practice, as in the little performance earlier that night, it played out a lot differently from what he had envisioned. “Oh, Mary,” he muttered under his breath. He fought back the tears. Not here, he told himself, this isn’t the place. “Can you ever forgive me?” His late wife, Mary, was the main reason why all of those memories were long since buried and why he’d become a changed man over the years. She’d never say it, but McKail knew she didn’t much like the man he once was. She didn’t like what those memories he held on to so tightly for all those years made him. So he changed. With her help he changed. And I lost my edge, McKail added. He didn’t like to admit even to himself, but it was the truth. Thing of it was, now he was in a position to remedy that. With Mary gone, he decided that being that same good person she loved all of those years was not a necessity. Fulfilling the promise he made to her was. He was going to dominate the wrestling industry, no matter what. But like this? He questioned. Losing my temper every time a decision doesn’t go my way? It couldn’t continue, this couldn’t continue. If what happened that night happened every time he stepped into the ring, then he had a problem. This edge he was desperate to unleash would be ultimately counterproductive. I have to control this, he told himself. I have to keep the edge, but not in it’s current state. It’s too rough. It’s too raw. It raced through his mind over and over again and he couldn’t come up with any real conclusion, especially under the subject head of “control”. But if I can’t control it, how do I lose it again? It was a good question. This time he didn’t have Mary’s strength to help him through it all and he certainly didn’t have enough strength on his own to forget all of those painful memories all over again. There was only one option left open to him, only one direction to head toward, no matter what and regardless of the consequences. He needed control.
