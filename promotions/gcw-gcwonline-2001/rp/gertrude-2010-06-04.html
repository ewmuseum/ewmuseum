Gertrude

4 June 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06/04/10 The music was way too loud. The drinks, too expensive. But the women were cheap, and that made Stubby’s Maynard McGarrity’s favourite place in New York City. He sat at the bar with his right hand wrapped tightly around a dusty glass of Jack, leering around the pub from behind his mirrored aviator sunglasses for something he liked. After a time, he’d raise the glass to his lips and drain it in one gulp, and call for another silently by tapping the cup on the bar. The bartender asked no questions. Stubby’s wasn’t the kind of place to ask a stranger his business. Not unless you wanted to become his business. The swirling beat of the jungle song thumped through the PA system as he watched the women dance on the tacky, light-up dance floor; something out of a John Travolta movie from the 70’s. And that’s when he saw her: a tall, slender blond with legs that went on for days wearing a blue tank top and a white skirt. She might have even been beautiful if her face wasn’t suffocated under a pound of make-up that poorly hid the black bags under her eyes. He smiled cooly to himself, taking off his sunglasses and hanging them on the front collar of his t-shirt before draining his glass once more. The bartender motioned to refresh his drink, but Maynard waved him off and rose from the stool. He walked confidently towards the woman as she danced alone on the dance floor. One gets the impression this was by design on her part. Maybe she wasn’t the type who liked to be disturbed. But such things aren’t of much concern to a man such as Maynard. He liked a challenge anyways. With a slight swagger, he walks past her, brushing her shoulder delicately with his as he passes. He motions towards her apologetically and cuts through the sea of dancers to a spot about ten feet from the almost-beautiful blond. He takes the hand of a short, plump woman with curly brown hair and spins her towards him and begins to dance, if you could even call what he was doing dancing. He simply swayed haphazardly as the brunette slithered around him. Every now and again Maynard would turn his head slightly as to get a glimpse of the blond. Their eyes would meet for a split second and the corners of her mouth would upturn ever so slightly into a sort of smile. But she would never stop dancing. Was she waiting for Maynard to make a move perhaps? The song ended and the bouncing sea ceased to move for a moment before the next began. The brunette smiled sweetly at him, but he seemed not to notice as he wheeled around to see the other woman. She’d gone. Maynard scanned the room but quickly found that the bouncing sea had come to life again and he could see nothing. He pushed his way out of the crowd and emerged in front of the bar, where the blond was sitting, attempting the get the bartenders attention, who was tending to a pair of burly bikers with tattoos covering virtually every visible inch of their skin. Maynard took his original stool, two seats away from the woman and tapped his glass on the bar. He got no response. With a smirk, he turned towards the woman and said, “getting a drink in here is murder, huh?” She smiled politely. “I don’t usually have a problem” “I wouldn’t think so,” he said, and returned his attention back to the bartender who was still in full conversation with the bikers. “What’s your name?” “Maynard,” he said without looking at her. “That’s a nice name,” she said. “Pretty original. Don’t hear of many with people with it, you know?” Maynard smiled her way quickly before turning away and tapping his glass again. Still no response. “Gertrude,” she blurts out, more desperately than she probably anticipated. He turned towards her again. “I’m sorry?” “Gertrude,” she reiterated with a saddened look. “That’s my name.” “Oh,” he said with a small smile. “Thank you.” She frowned angrily at him. “A girl tells you her name and all you got to say is ‘thank you’?” “Sorry,” said Maynard, earnestly. “What else was I s’posed to say?” “I dunno,” spat Gertrude. “Anything but that, though.” “Well then, can I maybe try that again?” “Try what?” she asked irritatedly. “The whole name thing,” he said, “I want to try it again. Can I have your name?” “Gertrude”, she said slowly, not sure what to make of this. Without sounded sarcastic, Maynard said, “that’s a lovely name. I would love to buy you a drink Gertrude, you know ... if we ever get the chance to order at all.” Gertrude smiled at him as her eyes darted shyly to her fingernails. Maynard returned the smile and tapped his glass once more, this time the bartender heard him and ended his conversation abruptly and walked towards them. “What can I getchoo?” ge asked them quickly. “I’ll have my glass refilled, and the lady with have ..” Maynard said, motioning for Gertrude to fill in the blank. “Whatever he’s having”, she said. The bartender poured their drinks and returned to the bikers. “You sure you can handle Jack?” Maynard asked, wrapping his hand around his glass. “I can handle any man,” said Gertrude mischievously with a smirk, sipping at her glass. “Can you now?” said Maynard. “Haven’t met one yet I couldn’t,” she said as she threw the drink at the back of her throat without so much as the smallest grimace. Maynard smiled wickedly, draining his own glass with haste. “Why, you interested or something?” “I bought you a drink, didn’t I?” “Oh!” Gertrude cried with feigned offense. “And here I thought you were just a mere gentleman buying a lady a drink!” Maynard laughed. “Let me tell you, there a’int nothing gentle about me.” Gertrude looked half interested, half offended by his brashness. He quickly backpedalled. “I mean, like ... personality-wise. I’m a hard person, you know? Don’t have many friends.” Gertrude softened. “Why not?” “Long story, really,” said Maynard. “But mostly it comes down to my job. I’m on the road a lot. Don’t have much time for friends.” “You a trucker something?” asked Gertrude. “Nah,” he said with a smirk, “not a trucker.” “What do you do then?” “I wrestle”, said Maynard simply. “Professionally.” “Like the stuff on TV?” She asked, impressed albeit slightly. “Yeah,” Maynard chuckled, “the stuff on TV.” “So you’re like,” started Gertrude, the gears whirring in her head, “a star or something?” “Someday”, said Maynard with a wide smile. “Hopefully.” “I don’t really watch the stuff”, Gertrude confessed. “You any good?” “I like to think so, yeah” laughed Maynard. “Then how come you’re not a star?” she asked playfully. Maynard shrugged. “Someday.” ---- ---- Maynard was jolted awake by a screaming siren outside the window of his hotel room. His head was pounding so hard that he found it difficult to keep his eyes open long enough to see the lamp’s switch on the table next to his bed. Finally, after fumbling for many frustrating seconds, the light was turned off and he reached for his watch. 6:30am. He’d have to be on his way. He reached beside him and shook the woman under the covers softly. Gertrude stirred only slightly before settling back into her slumber. Maynard shakes her again, just a touch harder than the first time. She grumbles and rolls to face him. He was right about her. Without all the make-up, it was plain to see that she could be beautiful if she could only be rid of the bags beneath her brilliantly blue eyes ... ”Sara had blue eyes ...” he thought for only one infuriating moment before pushing it from his mind. “Can I get you a cab?” he asked sweetly, pushing her hair behind her ear. “What for?” replied Gertrude groggily, glancing across at the alarm clock. “It’s like ... seven in the morning.” “I’ve got an eleven o’clock flight to Australia, remember?” Gertrude thought for a moment. “Vaguely ..” “What’s the matter?” Maynard asked, kissing her forehead. “Nothing,” she said, pulling the comforter off the bed and wrapping it around her before beginning to climb out of bed. Maynard reached for her arm gently, pulling her back down. “No, no. I’m not stupid. What’s the matter?” “I just ...”, she started. “I, you know ... never do this” “Do what?” “Oh come on ... this. Go home with a stranger.” “Oh, neither do I”, Maynard lied. Though even he could tell there was something different about it this time. “Really?” asked Gertrude. “Really”, said Maynard with a reassuring sort of smile. That seemed to raise her spirits. She kissed his cheek and the pulled herself out of bed and made her way to the bathroom, closing the door behind her. Maynard followed suit and got up to dress himself. He pulled on his jeans and his favourite Pink Floyd t-shirt and made his way to the mirror to tie his hair into a scruffy top knot. A moment later, Gertrude emerged from the bathroom wearing the same clothes as the night before. It was in this moment Maynard realized that she was indeed a very beautiful woman. “You look”, Maynard began, “amazing.” She blushed. “Please, I’m not even wearing make-up ...” “I know,” he said earnestly. “You’re beautiful.” She kissed him on the cheek. “You’re sweet.” Been while since anyone’s said that to him. “Well, I guess I’ll let you get going”, she said, throwing her arms around him in a tight hug. He could feel his whole body tingle as she squeezed him. She pulled away and pushed a piece of paper into his hand. “What’s this?” Maynard asked, before realizing it was her phone number. “Just, you know ... call me, will you?” Gertrude said shyly, her face flooding. “Or text. Whichever works for you.” Maynard nodded, accepted another kiss on the cheek and then she was gone. Maynard McGarrity has always prided himself on his ability to be unflappable; the man who could walk to the ring while every single person in the building was against him and still remaining focused on the task at hand. He's always considered himself to be one of the best in the business at keeping a cool head under even the mostly extreme of pressure. But right now, AXEL Action, the Rampage Tournament and professional wrestling as a whole were among the furthest things from his mind. In fact, he had a hard time thinking about anything that wasn't the tall, slender woman who had, just moments ago turned his world upside down.
