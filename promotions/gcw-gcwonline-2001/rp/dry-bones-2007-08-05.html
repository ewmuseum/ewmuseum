Dry Bones

5 August 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/05/07 They sit in a spot of daylight dim under the steel overhang of a busstop, the older nursing a cigarette and a bottle of caffeine. The younger man shifts against the stiff metal of the seat and hunches forwards to spit on a patch of grass breaking up through the sidewalk. Any useful drugs have worn off and left them down to the bare chemicals and their involuntary firings; the smear of a grey cloud overhang giving at least the world a grand afterglow. "Sobias said you probably shouldn't have the necklace with you. You know, in the match and everything." The younger man brings the small steel cylinder to his face, tugging against the black cord around his neck. "Does kinda sound like a bad idea. What about just a little bit to get things started?" "I. I don't know. Hate to see someone have to deal with a comedown in the middle of something like that." "True, true." "Yeah." "When's the bus run?" "Whenever." "Shit, schedule on your side?" The older man pulls taped ads down from a clogged board; shakes his head and holds a wince. "Should probably get a cab." "That bad? Damn. Hey. Why ain't you drivin'?" "Hate renting cars, don't have a license here and don't really understand how that works; take your pick, really." "Oh. Shit. How long we got 'til the press op then?" "A few hours. Why?" "Was wantin' to find somewhere to chill. Trainin' was a bitch this mornin'." "Gonna advise against getting shitfaced. I want these people's money, you've gotta understand that." "Some alright clothes. Think they speak for themselves." "Do you feel odd wearing your face on a shirt?" "Nah." He shrugs and pulls the shirt out from his chest to look down at the negative abstraction of himself in white across the dusty orange of his long T. "Ain't many people look this good. Shit, anythin' that points out I'm famous." "Fuck it. If it sells." "Dog, no offense, but if this is your kinda business meetin?, then you ain't very good at some business meetin' shit." "Yeah. Not good at business either. Somethin' to ramble about, though." "S'what I thought. Looked like you cared more 'bout the dollar signs than the designs." "Fashion isn't my thing; throwin' money around, maybe. They've done alright so far, you know." "Already told me about the sales an' shit. Remember? I was all, 'oh shit, smooth.' Somethin' like that." "Probably. I dunno, man; why not just grub around here?" "Word by me." "Good.." The older man pushes off the bench, tossing the smoldering butt at the same hint of grass before he heads down the sidewalk. The younger follows in a slow walk motion at a quickstep pace, pushing his way through crowds that could have just been avoided and nodding to brief mentions of his name and smiling to acknowledgments of his celebrity. "Sage. Hol' up, man." Digging in his pockets for another smoke and lighting it with the deep kind of pull that brings his fist to his chest and leaves him hacking; Sage listens. The kid tosses his hand up backpedaling, jogging back to the bench and dropping to a knee against the sidewalk. Jerking the string on a black gym sack, dragging it out from under the bench and past an abandoned soda bottle, he tosses the bag over his shoulder like an actual burden as he plays catchup through the same optional crowds; laughing it off again. "You're a fucking dick, kid." "Hah. Yeah, yeah." They pace the sidewalk under the light shadow of a silver overcast, faces to a cool blue breeze as they pass open air bistros that sit under the soft rustle of faked orchards all surrounded by crimson bricks and cement walkways divided by black wrought iron. Past concrete that glints and large plate glass edifices with intricately painted logos and hanging baskets somewhere between "too late" and "too early" in their cycle; through storefront congregations huddled around steaming styrofoam cups; weaving once through an awkward arrangement of patio furniture with a faked patina and an umbrella gargantuan. The older leads into a franchise joint, loud and bright under sanitary white light. Falling in line between an elderly man with a brittle wheeze to his voice and a gawking teenage tourist on her third lunch of the day, they run the waiting routine like they run the ordering routine like they run the condimenting, drinkfilling (with a flawed drink-to-ice ratio on one count), and sitting routines. Autopilot, the grey life. Letting his burgers seep grease through the thin paper wrapping, the young man drops his bag to the table and rifles through it to pull out a wrinkled pack of papers folded in half as one unit. Sage glances over with an already passed interest and doesn't make the mistake of letting bad food turn cold. The other slurps idle at a straw for minutes, dragging through the printed word to the point of obsession. "Gonna be pretty much the same as the other press stuff we've done’same script, even. Didn't really have time to come up with a new one." He nods, still scanning the crumpled sheets of paper until Sage jerks it away. "Yo, what up?" "Not that good of a script. You've run through five of these things so far; think I can trust you with this shit by now." "Oh shit, throwin' pressure at me?" "No, no. Don't confuse it’I don't trust you around pressure." "Then why you lettin' me keep the match?" "Really?" The older man leans in over the table, setting his drink to the side and straightening out his knit cap. "Doubt I can talk everyone into forgetting it was ever mentioned." "Y'gotta have some faith in me, dog." "I don't, really, I don't have to. If you were the only hope’okay. If you were of only a few hopes’I could even see that. But as is, y'know? There's a lot of people being courted under the brand at the moment and, if you fuck up, I can always just retreat to the other fronts." "Nice bein' rich as shit, innit?" "You should try it sometime." "Doin' what I can." "There's way more luck to it than that." "Luck like, beatin' people I really shouldn't?" "I don't think we're talking about the same levels of rich here." "So I should steal your company an' shit?" "Worked for me; fuck. Give it a shot sometime, kid." "Seems like a lotta hassle to get into a lotta hassle. I'ma pass on that for now." "Hey, I could be resting comfortably. I just. Want more money, that's all." "Greed's a bitch." Sage laughs to himself. "Wouldn't know." "Oh, so it ain't about turnin' more money than the next dude in line?" "It is; we've just all got our own reasons." "And yours." He lets it trail off, nodding his head after a pause to indicate that he's still waiting. "Mine? Crazy, big. Don't think I understand “em, really; just know that step one is to get a lotta gold. Sittin' on it just seems wasteful, though." "How 'bout a raise?" "Hahmmph." It's close enough to an answer. "Ain't really expect you'd've said yes without this Rollins thing goin' on." "Worry about that later." "Man, you ain't seem too worried about that at all." "All an emotional investment can do for a job is fuck it up. Apathy’s the name of success in some places." "Burn. Nice jab though." "So maybe I should've tried to be a little less blunt about how fucking stupid you are for actually getting mad at some guy in a bandana." "Hah, nah, nah, dog. Least you're honest 'bout the shit, y'know? Tellin' me I just got this shit like it ain't even a hassle." "Yeah. I'm not gonna lie." "You gonna show some concern?" "Probably not. Know I can't win this thing for you. Trust me, I've thought about it." "And you're on some Mr. Miyagi 'the answer lie within' shit." "First off, I don't even understand what Pat Morita has to do with this. Next, I mean, I can't win this match for you as in they're not gonna let me run in with a baseball bat and beat the man to death." "You asked?" "Forgot that that's the sorta thing you have to ask about." He goes back to eating, dedicating himself to make up for time wasted. The younger man watches until he's learned to mimic the action. Time passes. The kid gets bored, maybe scared of the immediate silence around him. "F'real, though. How you gonna show up to some media shit in a bus?" "A taxi." "Same thing. S'not, y'know, pimp." Sage drops his brow and finishes the burger. "I'm using a little fame to promote something that a lot of money's gone into. No one's going to be hounding every piece of public transportation hopin' to get a shot of my ass. Fuck, there's a thousand junkies in this city that look just like me right now." "Hah, true. So you fall in with the stealth quickness, then dip back up into the shadows. Smooth, smooth." "All right. Besides, not America. I feel less, well, compelled to spend money in units I don't understand." "You roll, you jus' pick your shots. S'how to play it." "I get sidetracked, but not that easily. You can’t suck up to me enough to get a raise." "What if I win?" "You don't get a pay cut?" The older man slurps loud into a dead cup, sighing with a crooked grin at his earlier icecapade. "So, ready to shill?"
