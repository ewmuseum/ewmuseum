Seeing Red

14 March 2012
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/14/12 March 12th, 2012 – 10:52 AM Millennium Hotel St. Louis, Missouri The clock reads 10:52 AM. Waking up this late in the morning isn't out of routine for myself. My head is pounding, my mouth dry and stale. Still, no change in my routine. My half of the bed reeks of the alcohol, sweat out from the night prior. The other half has faint smells of perfume on the bed sheets and pillow case. Again, typical routine for a new city. But something isn't right. This feeling I have in the pit of my stomach, the nervous, anxious churning isn't normal. Today could very well be the biggest day of my career heading into Dangerous Games. The possibility of leaving with my gold intact, and the number one contender to the prize everyone dreams about. The opportunity I have tonight is of massive proportions, and I have mentally prepared for this day since the moment I left the Sprint Center some three and a half weeks ago. But that isn't it. This feeling is mixed with guilt. That I have done something wrong that I should be sorry for. I have boozed many nights in many cities, and woken up mornings where the women have pulled a Houdini act. Their names and faces to never be remembered. All they leave behind is the sweet smell of various perfumes that surely was part of the attraction the night prior, and the obvious signs that a shower is needed. I have lived this life for a long time, so what could be so different this morning? Slowly I pull myself out of bed, scanning the darkened room. Staggering over to the window, I spread the curtains open, reacting much like a vampire would. Eyes squinted, arms trying to block the UV rays, slight hiss at the horrible sun that intensifies the pounding in my skull. As my eyes adjust to the new found natural light, a quick visual inspection doesn't reveal anything out of the ordinary. The room, in comparison to many hotel rooms prior, is actually very well maintained at the moment. My gear for tonight's event neatly on the arm chair in the corner. A subway sandwich wrapper in the trash as opposed to the floor. My personal effects: toothbrush, mustache comb, cologne, all on the desk at the foot of the bed. AH HA! My beloved bottle of Crown Royal Dark sits on the night stand, hidden slightly behind the lamp and alarm clock. The unusual? It is half full. What the fuck is that shit. I crush that beautiful liquor like it's water. Maybe that's where my guilt lies. Neglecting the very friend that has always been there for me in my darkest of hours and brightest of days. We will reconcile in a few, but I got to go take a piss. Walking towards the bathroom, I am still quite perturbed that the room is as neat as it is. I think it's fair to say, it's probably the cleanest any of my hotel rooms have stayed. Maybe I am turning over a new leaf, becoming slightly more responsible. Last Night Was Amazing. Our Little Secret. The words written in bright red lipstick on my bathroom mirror. Messages left behind aren't a new thing, but usually aren't of a nice nature. 'Our Little Secret' certainly doesn't bode well either. I hate secrets, and suck at keeping them. And why would she want me to keep this a secret? That god awful churning is getting worse, and certainly isn't being helped by my liquor filled bladder. The sweet relief on my bladder brings that slight eye rolling moment of comfort, but after the thirty some odd second fire hose struggle, my anxious self begins to pace throughout the hotel room. Pulling the bed sheets back, lifting chair cushions, rifling through desk drawers. I need a clue, a reminder of some sort. Nothing. Squat. Zilch. No other hints as to why this is a secret that must be kept, or better yet, who I must share this secret with. Sitting on the floor, my head hurts, not so much from the alcohol induced headache anymore, but the thought induced panic. Obviously this can't be good. My subconscious has me worried, so odds are I fucked up huge. What is that sound? An annoying muffled ring overpowers the sound of my thoughts. It's location of origin, unknown. Again through the bed sheets, under cushions, in the desk drawers. Nothing. The sound is it's strongest near the bed. Under the mattress, in the pillow cases, behind the headboard. Nothing. One hand swipe later and the feeling of idiocy, I drop to my knees, and look under the bed to reveal an iPhone. I myself am a Blackberry man, so this discovery is huge. Missed Call: Alexander Redding Well this certainly can't be good. Ding! Text Message: Alexander Redding Swiping my finger across the screen, the phone unlocks revealing the details of the text. Where are you? I thought we were doing breakfast/lunch? Hurry up, I'm not waiting all day. Maybe for some reason I have Grady's phone? He always leaves it behind wherever we end up, and I usually end up dealing with the non stop texts and calls from his 'clients'. Yeah, it's probably just Grady's phone, the lipstick message and phone probably have no relation to each other. March 12th, 2012 – 1:16 AM Black Thorn Pub (Outside) St. Louis, Missouri “You think your some fucking big shot? HUH!” A swift kick to the ribs follows the shout out. On my hands and knees outside, several of St. Louis' finest lay the boots to my downed body, throwing the occasional punch to mix it up. Not the first time I've been at the bottom of one of these piles, certainly won't be the last. “Think you can stick your tongue in MY girlfriends mouth and walk out unscathed?” More kicks. More punches. Definitely worth it too. This guys girlfriend was hot. Having been in this position many times before, I've got the turtle position down to an exact art. Elbows on either side of my head, hands locked tightly at the base of my skulls. Ears protected, as is neck. Knees under my stomach, arching the back to expose only the ribs. A few bruises on the back of my head and possibly a couple broken ribs means this technique was executed perfectly. “Get up and fight like a man!” “NO!” It's not being a bitch declining his invite. It's simply being wise. I am way too inebriated to throw a coordinated strike, and this group would easily handle me, even if I wasn't drunk. “Stop being a bitch! You kissed my girl!” “And I put my hand up her skirt too!” The flurry of strikes continue. I mean seriously, is there not a single cop patrolling this city? Back in Canada a fight slash beat down lasts all of thirty seconds before cruisers are busting those involved, maintaining peace in the lovely land of the north. “Stop it! Leave him alone!” A females voice cries out to the testosterone enraged pack. Her voice is slightly familiar, but definitely not that of the girl I tongued minutes prior, also known as: one of these guys gal. Her pleas fall on deaf ears, until that phrase no he-man wants to here is shouted. “He is mentally challenged!” Immediately the kicks and punches stop. Lowered voices talk amongst themselves in a semi worried tone, and not too long after, those who were beating me are helping me to my feet. Dusting my shoulders, straightening my torn shirt the best they can. “How could you guys do that to my little brother? He is harmless!” “I swear miss, we didn't know. I mean, he kissed my girlfriend!” Little brother? Who in their right mind would go out of their way to help me out. The ultimate womanizer being saved by that, a woman. My sight is blurred from the nights worth of adventures, so making out the face to her voice is a challenge, let alone trying to see those who dragged my ass outside. “What do you mean you didn't know? Look at him! How could you not tell something is wrong with him?” Hey now, wait a minute. “She's right Dave. His eyes say it all.” Their faces are blurry, but I can tell they are all inspecting my face. Collectively, a long exasperated 'oh' is heard amongst the group of them. “Dave, your chick basically cheated on you with a guy who has down syndrome.” Down syndrome? I don't look like I'm challenged in any way. Do I? Struggling with this new found knowledge, the groups once worried 'oh' has changed it's tune to that of laughter, at the expense of their leader who I've now learned goes by the name of Dave. “Need I remind you immature dicks that you just assaulted my brother. How would you like your face plastered amongst the front cover of the paper for attacking the mentally handicapped?” “Miss, again, we didn't know. Lets not blow this out of proportion. I'm sure we can come to some sort of agreement?” “Five hundred dollars and I never want to see you guys around my brother again.” “Five hundred dollars? Are you kidding me?” “The cops are three digits away...your call...” The group discusses the pros of buying their way out of this predicament as opposed to being labeled retard beaters. The woman reaching for her cell quickly prompts them to stumble through their wallets and count amongst them what they have combined. “All we have is three hundred and ninety six dollars.” “That will do.” She says, quickly snatching the cash from Dave's hand. “Now get out of here.” As the group make their departure from the alleyway beside the bar, I sit on the cold wet concrete, back pressed against the wall. The rain that has gently began to fall is somewhat relaxing, almost therapeutic as the adrenaline of the beat down begins to vanish and the blood flows to ever single soon to be bruise. My sight is still somewhat distorted, but at this point, I'm not even trying discover the woman's identity. She got was she wanted: a handful of cash. At least, that is what I thought she only had interest in until she sits down beside me, staring at me as my head hangs toward the ground. “Aren't you going to thank me?” “I thought stealing their money would have been thanks enough big sis.” “Brilliant, wasn't it? You should have seen their faces when I told them you you were challenged. Priceless!” “Brilliant indeed. Now everyone think I am retarted.” “Don't be an idiot. You are on television every week. I'm surprised they didn't recognize the guy they were beating the shit out of.” “Ah, so you know who I am then.” “Of course I do, I know all about you. I hear about you weekly from Alex, and all the shit you get yourself into.” Weekly updates? Alex? Does she mean Red? Who in the hell calls that guy Alex? He hates being called Alex. Says it is a chicks name. Looking up and doing my best to focus on the female in front of me, I squint, widen, and squint my eyes again. Her laughter is sweet, and she places her soft hand on my cheek. “They really did a number on the back of your head didn't they. You don't remember me at all?” “Honestly, I can't see worth shit right now. But I would assume that by Alex you are referencing Red, which mean's you are close with the guy in some way.” “More than close. It's me, Grace. You know, his little sister. In town to see you guys perform.” “Grace?” March 12th, 2012 – 11:30 AM Millennium Hotel St. Louis, Missouri I am the shittiest person alive. Sitting on the chair where my gear once resided until it was tossed to the floor, I am slouched as low as I can go, feeling as low as I possibly can. I have very few boundaries and limitations when it comes to women, but rule number one was always no friends sisters or moms. Yes, moms are on the list, especially after the Johnny Graham fiasco, but that's another story for another day. Redding has been my best friend, and the closest thing I have had to a true friend, for a long while now. His views and mine differ very much on life as a whole, but ultimately it's those differences that make us a very entertaining duo to be around and watch on a weekly basis. He was there for me when I was suspended and down, when my own blood brother left my life forever. He helped me get my life back on track (if you consider this lifestyle back on track). I met Grace after last years NC-17 event, albeit briefly, and although there was an immediate attraction to her, I knew she was off limits. That is the rule, my rule. Lawrence needed to remind me at the airport on our way out of New York, but deep down I knew it was my code, and she quickly become a suppressed after thought. But last night, she sought me out. She found me in the scummiest of locations in the St. Louis area, and saved me from a beating that wasn't letting up. Did she experience the first sight feelings too back in New York? She must have to seek me out a year later. But still, she is Redding's sister, and he must never find out. And this can't happen again. And oddly, the one time in my life I want it to happen again, it can't. For the sake of a friendship which is all I've had in my life that's been steady, it just can't. I've ruined so many things in my life, and I have to do all I can to avoid ruining this too. The distinct ring the Blackberry possesses goes off, breaking me from my thought process. A short distance from the chair, I pick up the phone off of the desk, revealing the name which is the absolute last I want to see at this moment. Calling: Red Shit. Does he know already? “Um, Hello?” “Just waking up after a night filled with fucking?” “Umm, you know, just us, a regular, you know, night I guess?” “You're still smashed aren't you? Well get up anyways, join me and my sister Grace for lunch.” “Umm, I got some shit to do to get ready and...” “Listen, this ain't Canada. They start serving alcohol in the wee early hours of the morning. I'll text you directions to where were at. I have to talk to you about Dangerous Games tonight. And I have a few things to discuss beyond the pay per view too.” Before I can reply, the other end is dead. I drop the phone on my bed, hanging my head slightly. Lunch with the girl I slept with and desire, but can't have. Lunch with her brother, who can never know the truths of what happened last night. Lunch on the biggest day in my short career to date. Dangerous Games indeed...
