A Prelude to the Unholy- Part II

10 August 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/10/2008 After suffering months of physical and verbal abuse from Karl Zarn, the big day had finally come. I was just moments away from my first match as a professional wrestler, and I was wrestling on the undercard of the Omega Wrestling Alliance's weekly show. Right about now, any normal man would be nervous. However-- for me, nervous was an understatement. You know the feeling you get when you've cheated on your lover and you just KNOW that they know it? Yeah, that's it. That's the feeling I had. My heart was pounding in my chest, banging against my ribcage as if it were trying to escape its bony cell. My stomach wrenched in all directions, tying in knots and cramping as I awaited my big moment. All I could do as I sat there was pray to God above that everything went well. "God, if you can hear me, please just let me make it out of this match without an injury. With your help, I've managed to achieve my dream of becoming a wrestler, and with your help, I hope to make my parents proud. These things I ask in your name, Amen.", were the words that I spoke in my head as I silently prayed. I knew that prayer alone wasn't going to help me. In fact, it wasn't going to help at all. The only thing that could possibly save me were the skills that I learned in training, and I wasn't so sure about those. I was having a lot of doubts about myself that day, and I wasn't sure that I should go through with the match. I was very insecure about myself and my abilities at that moment, and it didn't help matters any that my debut match was against none other than my trainer and mentor: Mr. Karl Zarn, the TyeDye Guy himself. This wasn't going to be fun, and I knew it. If his training was any indication of what he was going to do to me in the match, I had plenty to be worried about. I could still hardly move from all of the abuse I took from him, but all the other wrestlers tell me the same thing-- "Ah, he was just toughening you up!" Fuck that and fuck them! All of the veterans were the same. They were all washed-up and at the end of their careers, and the only reason they were even still on top was because of the fact that they knew how to politic-- but I wouldn't be the one to tell them that, oh no. I had too much respect for them at that time. If I knew then what I know now, maybe I would have kissed ass, too. Then again, maybe I wouldn't have. After all, I'm not one for kissing ass. If I don't like the way something's going, I'm the type of person to say, "Hey. I don't like this." I digress, however. So I'm sitting there, and next thing I know, the booker walks up to me and says, "You're on in five." Five minutes?! How in the Hell am I supposed to prepare myself for "Killer" Karl Zarn in five minutes? The fear was beginning to mount rapidly as the seconds passed. I knew that I had to go out there and give it everything I had if I wanted to make a lasting impression on the fans. As I was scrambling around for the courage to go out and compete, a familiar voice called out to me. "Hey, Axel!" I turned to see where the voice was coming from, and it was my brother, Ace. "What are you doing here?", I asked him, wondering how he had made it backstage. "I simply talked my way in." I should have figured as much. Ace was always a very persuasive person. His skills of persuasion would later get him a contract with Omega Wrestling Alliance, and we would go on to win their World Tag Team titles. That is a story for another day, though. The conversation went on for a couple of minutes before he said something to me that put my mind at ease. He said, "Axel, I bet you're nervous right about now, huh? This has been your dream ever since we were kids, and you've finally achieved it. I'm sure if Mom and Dad were here right now, they'd be just as proud of you as I am. I want you to go out there and do your best and just have fun. I believe in you." Those words meant more to me than he would ever know. After all, he's my big brother, and it felt good to know that he believed in me. He's the only family I had known since the passing of our parents, and he'd been there for me through it all, and now was no different. We embraced, and then he headed to take his seat in the crowd to await the start of my match. The moment of truth was drawing near, and I would soon have to go and leave it all out there for the crowd. "Thirty seconds, kid." Those words echoed in my ears, and I felt my body tense up. It was almost time, and I wasn't anymore prepared than I was when I arrived at the arena. I was panicking, searching for a way out. Anything would have been appreciated at that point. It was no use wishing, though, as my music had hit. It was just simple, stock jobber music, but it did it's job well. It got the crowd pumped, and it got my feet moving towards the curtain. I stopped long enough to whisper one last prayer, then headed through the curtain to a small, yet rousing ovation. I now knew how all those veterans felt each and every time they entered the ringside area. It was an amazing feeling. As I made my way to the ring, I turned my gaze towards the sky. Mom, Dad... This is for you.
