Breaking Out: Shells (The Wind-Up)

6 September 2011

Date: 09/06/11 Roderick Ashe awoke with a start, so quickly that he nearly tumbled right off the couch. The big man stifled a yawn, fumbling for the cellphone he knew was somewhere nearby, but hell if he could remember where. He found the thing upside-down under an empty McDonald's fry container. Unlike the rest of his fellows, Ashe never got into the smart phone thing; he'd used prepaid burner phones for so long they no longer annoyed him, and it wasn't like people with mitts the size of his were expected to text much anyway. The face of the clamshell said it was half-past eleven, and even with thick curtains obscuring the windows, there was enough light that he knew it was almost noon and not almost midnight. That meant he'd slept almost six hours, which was more than he usually got in the weeks following his partner's arrest. The first few nights after Baltimore, Ashe dreamt Elijah had gotten free himself and come home. The old codger definitely had the smarts for it, and the fact that he'd finished the op the day his leg was fucked up proved he had the grit to tough out whatever Randall's people threw at him. Of course, Roderick had also imagined Hawke getting out and coming back because he'd broken. He could credit Elijah with all the guts of a Wild West gunfighter, but the truth was, loyalty only went so far in their business, and the smart play would be to flip on them, to spare himself the pain. It was easy for Ashe to claim he was tough when the hardest thing he'd had to do these last few months was climb out of the bottom of a bottle. For Elijah to have made it even this far was a testament to his courage. Any longer and the odds said he'd either turn or die in custody, alone and unremembered. It was no surprise it was the second kind if dream that kept Ashe up at night, and he was immediately ready to credit another one with waking him up this afternoon. But that hadn't been it, had it? He closed his eyes, racking his brain. If he were a dog, his ears would have pricked forward, concentrating on the ambient sounds. He listened hard, trying to pick out footsteps or voices or something that would explain why he was so on edge. It occurred to him that he should have moved, that he shouldn't be somewhere that Hawke could've found him, that he really shouldn't have had that last pint last night. There. There it was, a featherlight step on the gravel -- probably a sneaker sole, definitely trying to be quiet as they came around to the side of the building. Roderick's whole body tensed, expecting... he didn't know what. A SWAT team? Delta Force? Lots of shattered glass, that's for sure. Fleetingly, he wondered whether they'd put him in a cell with Hawke, but that seemed kind of unlikely. Ashe might have been expecting the worst (guys in mech armor with laser swords!), but what he wasn't expecting was a five-foot-nothing redhead to have her face pressed to the window. Amy Campbell used her hands to shield her eyes from the sun, and her worried look melted into something much happier once she saw movement inside. She stooped to pick up something (he thought he caught a flash of white paper, maybe a bag of some kind), and her steps back around to the door were louder this time, sending rocks flying. Roderick groaned again, scrubbing at his eyes with the heels of his hands as he pushed himself to his feet. His left knee ached again, probably the same injury that had ended his wrestling career that he never had scoped. He snagged the big bottle of ibuprofen from the kitchen counter as he passed, downing a handful of pills dry before Campbell banged on the door, and he yanked it open before she could knock a second time. "What?" he demanded. "Hey, I, uh..." Amy tried not to look overly concerned. She held up the white In-N-Out bag. "I come bearing gifts." Ashe heard his stomach growl, and he realized he couldn't remember when he'd last had an actual meal. "Thanks," he said, reaching for the bag. Campbell snatched it away at the last second. "You may be short a couple fries," she said. "They smelled really good and I didn't realize how far out in the boonies you actually lived." "No biggie." He reached again, and this time she let go. "Been awhile since I had anythin' you can't buy at the gas station onna corner." She shrugged one shoulder. "Don't mention it. Can I... do you mind if I come in for a minute?" "Did Allie send you?" Flat, beady hazel eyes met hers. "She ax ya to check up on me?" "No, I'm pretty sure she doesn't know I'm here." Amy's smile leaked through. "Well, as sure as you can be with her." Her brows lifted. "Sooo...?" Ashe didn't respond, but he left the door open when he turned, and she took it as an invitation. It was a small, two-bedroom apartment, and, to be frank, the place was something of a pigsty, the big black trash bag by the kitchen door overflowing and teetering precariously, but, perhaps surprisingly, it didn't smell too bad, likely owing to two separate air fresheners plugged into the wall. The big man circled into the kitchen, pulling a paper plate off the stack atop the fridge. He tore open the paper bag, putting its contents onto the plate. Belatedly, he looked up, bushy brows lifting. "Can I getcha anything? I got, uhm, beer--maybe a Guinness if you're lucky--an' pop an' I figure there's a pitcher of Kool-Aid..." Campbell's brows came up again, this time expectantly. "Kool-Aid?" she asked. Roderick jammed three fries into his mouth at once, not waiting to finish chewing before he answered. "Hawkers likes it. Sugar-free or whatev. I made a fresh pitcher yesterday, I think." He shrugged, looking down. "Habits, y'know?" "Yeah, I get that." Amy smiled sadly, brain reaching for any change of subject and coming up short. She'd have to face down reality with the big man, little as she wanted to. "But that's not a bad thing, y'know?" She shrugged, sitting down against the arm of the worn-out couch near the kitchen area. "He'll be back soon." "Yeh." He didn't look up as he carried the paper plate into the living room, but he didn't have the same control over his voice that Alex did, and it was impossible to miss the note of doubt in his voice, even when he repeated it, firmer. "Yeh, I know. 'sJust... didn't realize how quiet this place got, y'know? Allie's busy or whatever, so I dunno what t'do wit' myself. Awful small place to feel so empty is all." He hunched over the plate, not looking back to the redhead behind him. "Ain't this where yer s'pposed t'say you was inna neighborhood?" ''Yeeeeah,'' Campbell drew out the word, smirking. ''But I wasn't.'' "Well, Aimzsy," he drawled with a jovial grin that made him seem like his old self, if only for the briefest of moments. "Much as I like you, I'm afraid I couldn't do that to poor li'l Allie. Not to mention I'm sure I'd split you like a pig like a luau..." Amy cringed, but she chuckled. "Pleasant as that image is, I totally forgot to pick up condoms on the way. Looks like all my wildest dreams won't be coming true today after all." She grinned. "Guess we could substitute hanging out, though. I could take you to a shady strip club, maybe the zoo -- either way, we'll see cougars." Roderick's own grin reemerged briefly, before he buried it in the burger. "Yer jus' here to make sure I ain't sad, that it? 'Cuz as great as that is... I'm... I'm okay, I figure. Eli's tough, an' if that sumbitch had him dead, he'da sent word to Allie jus' to get a damn reaction. I been workin' onna plan to raid that snake pit an' geddim, but I figure Allie'd call it 'reckless' or whatever. Better'n sittin' on my ass an' hopin', y'know?" "She'll figure something out. It's kind of what she does, you know?" "Yeh?" Ashe looked over his shoulder, his brow lift carving furrows into his forehead. "No offense, but it really looks like Allie flies all aroun' the goddamn world wrasslin' dudes an' fills the rest'a her day tryin' t'figger a way to keep you happy." "Hey, wait a minute..." "I said no offense! Ain't like I'm mad 'bout it. I just kinda wish that Eli was a priority for you guys is all. Away from that fuckhead Kingsley or Shakur or whatever the fuck the deal with your exes is. Guy's a stand-up dude, y'know? He don't deserve what that rat-tail sumbitch is prob'ly doin'." Campbell's lips curled down in a frown. "She hasn't forgotten, Roddy. I haven't forgotten. As sad as it is, you two were pretty much the only people in that group except for Al that I actually liked. Why else do you think I drove all the way out here, for my health?" "I figure y'did it 'cuz you feel bad, an' if you see that ol' Roderick's okay, you can go on an' worry about other shit. An' I appreciate that you give a shit -- none'a the others've been here to say hey -- but I don' need no charity." ''It's not charity,'' Amy sighed. ''We're friends. I'm worried about him, too - this is what I should be doing. I'm not just gonna leave you out here to mope and be lonely.'' "I ain't lonely. I mean, I got my buds onna computer an' stuff. It's..." He met her doubtful look. "Okay, maybe I'm a li'l bored. Still. Ain't like it's your problem. Hawke did what he did 'cuz it was the right thing to do. He wouldn'ta changed shit if he'd known, an' you don' gotta feel bad just 'cuz you were there an' hadda leave 'im." ''No, I'm pretty sure I do have to feel badly about that. It's the last thing I wanted to do, and he was brave as hell for what he did. I just...'' Campbell shook her head. ''I don't think we should be wasting time getting him back either, you know? I don't wanna do anything else. I can barely sleep. It's fucking shitty to just sit around when we could be... I dunno, doing something.'' "Hope you ain't expectin' me t'disagree." Ashe shrugged, coming up to his feet. "Trouble is, you an' I ain't gettin' in there ourselves -- not without dropping a whole lotta bodies." He stepped over the nearby computer desk, swiping a bunch of wadded up papers off a small corkboard. "I did some research, but I don't got the kinda resources other folks might, an' I ain't as good at askin' questions. I tend t'lose my patience." "I know what that's like," Amy said, nodding. "Is it really that bad? I mean, I want him back, but... aren't most of Randall's guys just, like, normal people doing their jobs? Not the kinds of people who might..." She sighed, leaning back. "I don't wanna say who might deserve it, but... you know what I mean. They're just guys." "They're not just guys, though. They're the best guys. CIA, FBI, NSA, even foreigners like Interpol nowadays." Amy's brows came up. "I thought he was smalltime. When Alex and--" She almost said "Drusilla". "When Al and Malcolm and them worked with him, I got the impression that Randall and his guys were basically rejects chasing urban legends that nobody really believed in." Ashe grinned. "Yeh, but we know those legends are true, right?" ''Well, what're we gonna do about it? We're both sick of sitting around, waiting on some genius plan, and we can get resources. I mean, Kathryn's decently under my thumb after the whole 'got drugged and fucked' fiasco, and as far as I've seen, she's decent at getting information out of people.'' "Good, 'cuz it ain't so easy to storm the castle till we know where it is." "I thought they had that creepy underground complex in... New Mexico, wasn't it?" Roderick flipped up his board, and there was a map of the United States pinned to it. While there was a green pushpin in the Santa Fe location, there were also black-headed tacks in as many as five other cities from Delaware to Montana. "Not anymore. Most'a these places are just rumors or new wings in other government buildings, but... sumbitch ain't wasting time." "I guess not. Jesus." The big man rested his hip against the desk, which creaked in protest at his weight but held firm. "I dunno what to do," he mumbled, folding his arms. "This kinda shit... it ain't what I'm good at." "What do you need to make it work? I told you, I'll do whatever it takes. It's my ass he saved." "I need a damn army." He sighed. "Or I need Alex Pierce." "It just so happens I have some influence there, too." "I know you do. I know. An' normally, I'd be all over you askin'. It's just... I mean, if I knew where to point her, that'd be different, but..." He sighed, thick and wet, like he was holding back tears. "But I think he might be gone, Ames, an' I don' want anybody else t'get nabbed 'cuz I'm overeager an' miss my little buddy." Amy didn't know what she should say -- or even if she should say anything at all. She sighed, sliding to her feet, and found there were tears in her eyes, too. "She'd drop everything for him. You know she would. We both would." "I know." Ashe sniffled, wiping the side of his index finger beneath his porcine nose. "But he'd kill me if Al or you or anybody else got caught or hurt or whatever because I let you go in half-cocked. I want him back, believe me, I do, but right now, we just plumb don't know enough to do anythin', not even if we had Alex's brainpower here to help." ''We're not gonna find anything sitting on our asses, talking about how little we know, either,'' Amy said. ''So let's take the next day or two, figure out anything we can individually, and by the end of the week, we go find that gimpy motherfucker. And don't even think of turning me down right now, no matter how guilty you think you'll feel if I get hurt.'' "Yer pretty sick'a that shit with Allie, I figure." "Like you wouldn't believe." The little redhead had to crane her neck to meet Roderick's gaze, but she refused to shy away. "I know you guys think I'm just a civilian or whatever, but that's not really true, not anymore. Let me help however I can. I owe him that much." A smile snuck onto Ashe's lips. "Yer a stubborn thing, ain'tcha?" "I'm sure Al could tell you stories." She arched her brow. "So you'll let me help?" "Doesn't sound like I've got much choice, does it?" He heaved a sigh. "Fine, fine. Awright, talk to Kathi, maybe call your dad. See if either'a them's got any friggin' idea where we start. Once we got somethin' worth sharin', then we'll go to Allie wiffit. But only then, okay? Don't drag her inna this at jump. She's got enough plates in the air." "Yeah, fine..." "I mean it, Ames. Not till we've decided." "I said fine. Not until it's been decided." Ashe's lips twisted. "Well, awright, then." He stuck out a hand. "Let's do this." Campbell didn't shake, but she did clasp his thick forearm, feeling vaguely like a child clutching a baseball bat. "So you're okay now?" "I will be." "Then my work here is done. And I didn't even have to look at half-naked women to do it." "Well, yer goin' t'see Kat Shaw, ain'tcha?" Amy gave him a level look. "Touché," she said, but she wasn't able to maintain her composure, laughing along with him. "No more disappearing, okay? It's a hell of a drive out here." "Yeh, well... let's get Hawkers back before I make any promises." "We will," Campbell said, reaching up to embrace the big man. "We will." When she dropped back to her heels, she was smiling ruefully. "Don't suppose you need any therapeutic video game playing?" "You just don't wanna go see Kathi, do ya?" "Maybe not," she sighed. "Can you blame me?" "Not really." He scrubbed at the back of his neck. "Battlefield?" "Like you have to ask me twice?" He trundled to the entertainment center. "No taking it easy on me," he said, handing over a controller. "Wouldn't dream of it," she lied. "Bring it, bitch." The combination finished with the silhouette ducking an unseen blow from her imaginary assailant, kicking blindly behind her twice more, two pinpoint kicks that would have met the side of the knee and the back of the ankle, respectively. With her mock opponent presumably staggered, she leapt into the air, rotating her hips into a spinning kick to the back of his "head". She ignored the heavy landing on the mats stacked specifically for that purpose, rolling onto her stomach for a set of push-ups with her legs further apart than most people would have dreamed trying. Wrestling for two separate federations at the same time (not to mention her other, less legal side ventures), one might wonder just how Alexandra Pierce found the time to put herself through the brutal regimen that kept her in fighting trim. The truth was that she didn't know the answer to that question, and so she stole whatever time she could -- ten minutes here, forty minutes there, and simply increased the pace of her workouts to compensate. Unable to commit to a set schedule (and too paranoid to commit her name to the registry of any single gym for fear of giving her enemies another place to find her), Alex had purchased a rundown dance studio and converted it to meet her needs. The only things she'd left were the long wall of mirrors and the ballet barre, and the place was like an oasis of solitude for her to work through whichever demons were plaguing her this week (and there were many to choose from), away from the horde of scoundrels and reprobates that lived in her orbit. Only two people -- her lover and her daughter -- knew where Pierce's makeshift exercise room was, and, for the most part, they left her to it. The front door was a huge metal thing with hinges Alex never bothered to oil, so it groaned in protest when her visitor wrenched it open, making the follow-up call of her name sort of unnecessary. "Al?" The voice still brought a small smile to Pierce's thin lips as she adjusted the tape wrapping her hand. She didn't answer; even if the sound of her exertions weren't enough to give her away, nothing on earth could stand in the way of Amy Campbell's curiosity. "Al?" Amy repeated, cursing as she fumbled along the wall for a light switch. "Jesus fuck, who works out in the dark?" Once she found the switch, the overhead, fluorescent lights bathed the place in a pale, almost sickly radiance, revealing just how much of the gym's pea green paint was flaking off. "Man, you could shoot a horror movie in this place." Amy shuddered, stepping into the main room. Alex had her back to the door, which would have been unusual had there not been the mirrors there for Pierce to use, and the taller redhead moved with a kind if feline grace as she threw another flurry of blows at the beaten red bag. It was like she was dancing to a song only she heard, the rhythm of lefts and rights and kicks almost entirely unpredictable. "You are here," Amy observed unnecessarily. "I am." She looked over her shoulder, but Amy knew that was more for her benefit than Pierce's -- Alex was more than capable of tracking the little redhead by her reflection, but didn't want Campbell to think she was being ignored. "One of those girls is Brandon's new girlfriend, okay? I wanted to meet her without the 'oh, hey, I'm his ex-girlfriend' bullshit hanging over us. That's it, that's all." "And you happened to have a fucking wig with you?" "Yes, actually." The teen folded her arms, a little more snap than usual to her words. "My friends and I are doing Not-So-Happily-Ever-After for Halloween and I'll be Zombie Sleeping Beauty, and it was cheap, since you guys won't let me dye my hair. Is that okay with you?" ''You put a wig on to talk to that jerkoff's new girlfriend? Seriously?'' Amy folded her arms, turning to Shaw. ''And I suppose you thought that was a good idea.'' One of the brunette's brows raised. "I know how she's been while you and Lexi were gallivanting around the globe. She needed closure. She needed to know that it wasn't because of something she did." "I think she knows better than that. If that kid doesn't get what he lost, she shouldn't be torturing herself to--" "She is standing right here, goddammit." Quinn's eyebrows were high and her scowl deep. "I am so sick of you treating me like I'm some wet behind the ears child, Amy. Whether you like it or not and whether you approve or not, this helped me today. Okay? Be happy for me for a change." ''What if they'd realized it was you? Did you think of that? They'd label you a--'' Campbell threw her hands up, heaving a frustrated sigh. ''Y'know, I've got no clue how to deal with this. I'm going home, and I guess I'm sorry for failing so hard as whatever the fuck I am. Obviously sociopaths are more helpful, and... whatever,'' she growled. ''Shaw, I'm holding you to what you said. As far as the rest of this... Al and I fuck her up enough on our own - I'd appreciate not having you there to complicate it. Have fun putting on your wigs and playing preppy teens.'' Gregory sighed. "Amy, wait, okay?" She sighed, lifting her hand to slip the wig off. "You're right, okay. I know you are." Her voice was softer as she tugged a wig cap free to go with it, dark hair fall over her shoulders. "I know this was crazy or whatever, just... don't be mad." "You have no idea how dangerous this stuff is, okay?" Amy might have been projecting a little bit. Seeing her lover sink into a role was at best disconcerting. "You're right, I don't. But they didn't recognize me. They couldn't. We don't go to the same school, and it's not like he's going to hang out with his ex enough for them to know me. If Brandon had shown up, yeah, maybe it would've been a problem, but the point was that he didn't and this gave me the chance to know..." She sucked her lower lip in between her teeth, sea-green eyes shimmering in the awful fluorescent lights of the bathroom. "To know it wasn't something I did. You can say it, Mom can say it, Aunt Kathi can say it, but... she's a great girl, and if Brandon's happy, maybe it's okay that I'm happy, too, you know?" ''I wish you realized sometimes it's okay to just be mad without having to push something further.'' Amy sighed. ''But okay, as long as you're happy. I'm still going home, though, I've got some... stuff...'' "Stuff? Is everything okay?" "Yeah, it's just..." She waved the question away. "It's nothing. Hey, is there a shoe store in this mall? I..." She shot a side-glance to the suspiciously quiet Shaw. "I have a thing that sneakers may not be suitable for." Quinn's eyebrows came up, and she turned fully to her godmother. "Could you... just give us a minute?" "Yeah, sure, fine... I watch your back, but the moment something personal comes up, I'm the bad guy again." The teen nudged Amy, who sighed, long and loud. "Sorry I jumped down your throat. Just being a little... you know." The smile touching Shaw's lips was the closest Campbell had ever seen to an honest one. "It's no problem, really. I'll leave you two alone." Kathryn kicked the block out from under the door. "I'll try to keep anyone from interrupting you." "Was that so hard?" Quinn asked as the door shut. "She really means well, you know. Sometimes, at least." "That doesn't make her methods or results any better," Amy said. "Meaning well has gotten me into countless problems." The girl shrugged infinitesimally. "She had my back today." "Sure she did. Today. Tomorrow might be different." "I'll face tomorrow tomorrow." She stepped over to the sink, lifting her shopping bag to retrieve her clothes. "Today, you're going to tell me why you need fancy shoes. Are you going somewhere fancy for GCW or something?" The girl's voice echoed strangely in the bathroom as Quinn closed herself into a stall. "Spill." "Nothing business-y. Just... I dunno, your mom deserves to see me be normal every so often." "You mean like a date?" Amy's ears burned, and she turned away from the stall, tossing Quinn's wig into her bag. "Why does everybody say that like it's some big shock?" "Uh, because it is." When the teen emerged from the stall, she was back to the way people were used to seeing her, back to the blue jeans and faux sports jersey, and she fit on her eyeglasses. "Face it, Aim, you two? Kind of homebodies. Your idea of a hot date is watching something streaming on Netflix or playing Xbox." "Is that a bad thing?" Mismatched blue and brown eyes lifted to meet the girl's in a mirror. "I'll have you know that I've made it through two marriages and a hell of a lot of relationships doing things not too different from--whoa, okay, bad comparison." "Wasn't gonna say it." Quinn lifted the bag off the sink, grinning. "I'm not objecting. Mom would love to be wooed, I guess. There are a couple stores. Calvin Klein, there's a Nine West... I'm sure you can find something. Maybe a pump or a good set of boots." Campbell closed her eyes, her grin kind of rueful. "This is going to end up with me in heels, falling on my face, isn't it?" "You'll be fine." "Come with me. Come on, ditch Kathi, help me pick out something." The teen laughed. "I can't. She came through for me today, and this whole shopping thing is on her dime. Besides, I'm a little uncomfortable helping you pick out clothes so you can have a hot date with my mother. No offense." Amy smoothed the hair out of her face. "Okay, okay..." She nodded, touching the teen's arm. Her expression was suddenly serious. "No matter what Shaw says, remember, you're smarter than she is, and you can always tell her no. Should, if she's asking you to do something dumb or illegal or dangerous." Quinn reached across, her hand closing over Campbell's. "It's fine, Aim. I'm a Pierce, you know? I've heard we're pretty smart. Besides..." She stepped to the door, grinning over her shoulder. "We don't want your hot date to end up with some tragic phone call." "You know that's all I'm going to be thinking about now, right?" "You'll do fine. You have a big advantage -- she loves you to pieces, and she'll find any kind of screw-up endearing." The door swung shut after her, and Amy briefly turned to look at herself in the mirror. "There can't be any screw-up," she told her reflection, then she shook her head. "There won't be any screw-up." Campbell pulled the door open, stepping out onto the concourse just in time to watch Quinn take Kathryn's proffered arm. The teen's head tipped back in laughter at something her godmother said, and Amy couldn't help the uneasy scowl on her lips. She'd blame nerves for the three separate pairs of shoes she bought. * * * Quinn took Kathryn's arm, and her godmother leaned in tight. "I cannot believe you just got Amy to apologize to me," she murmured. "I really thought she'd slit her wrists rather than ever admit she didn't need to jump on me." The girl laughed quietly, shaking her head. "It might happen more if you didn't go out of your way to antagonize her." "But it's so much fun!" Shaw bumped the door open slipping on her sunglasses. "So you want to tell me what really happened down there?" "Nothing," Quinn said breezily. "It was fine." "Uh-huh." She slid her eyes to the side, an eyebrow emerging from behind the designer shades. "Sure it was." The teen sighed, shielding her eyes with one hand. "Juliette is great. She's sweet and nice and smart and adorable." "And?" "And I hate her. Like blinding, raging, pain-behind-my-eyes hatred." She pulled her godmother to the side, in the shade and away from any passersby. "I... I know how this sounds, believe me, and maybe it makes it worse that I know, but I can't just let this go, and nobody else will understand but you." "What are you trying to say?" "I need your help," Quinn said, her voice quiet but firm. "I want to -- no, I have--" Her voice cracked, and she took a long, shuddering breath before continuing. "I'm... help me hurt her, Aunt Kathryn. Help me hurt them Please. I know you've done this before." Her small fist balled up. "He left me. The son of a bitch left me sitting in my room bawling, and he never told me why. I can't -- I won't be happy until I've..." She looked down, angry and ashamed, her hands knotted together, index finger picking at her thumb. "I want her to cry like I cried. I want him to hurt like I hurt. I know this isn't rational or logical or... or mature or any of that, but..." Shaw reached out, squeezing the teen's forearm. "I know. But it's normal." Quinn looked up, lifting her glasses to wipe at her eyes. "Can you... will you?" "I don't know, sweetheart. Amy, your mother..." "They won't understand. They don't understand. Promise me." She grabbed both of Kathryn's hands, eyebrows arching. "Promise me we'll at least talk about it." "If that's what you really want." Shaw smiled hesitantly. "We'll... we can look into it. But no guarantees." She slid in to the girl's side, an arm around her shoulders. "Let's get you home, huh? Let's just get you home." The teen managed a mute little nod, allowing her godmother to lead her away. They say that sometimes, the longer the fuse, the bigger the boom. If that was true, then before all was said and done, Quinn Gregory just might be the biggest explosion of them all.
