Before Now... (I)

10 March 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/10/10 It's been a long time since we last saw these two gentlemen, they are sitting in a large lounge, decorated like for many years a crazy, insane cat lady lived there. The room contained multiple cat pictures, more doilies than you could ever need . The two gentlemen in question? Phil Atken, who at this present moment is sitting in a couch covered in cat hairs while professional agent and Phil's only friend in the whole wide world, Dirk Dickwood, is sitting in a recliner that had the distinct stench of cat piss. The television in the room was set to one of those wonderful rolling news stations, volume down low but still barely audiable. Atken: I can't believe she's gone... never in a million years did I think this day would come. My brain can't comprehend that she's actually gone. Dickwood: I know this must be a trying time for you. Me and Helga wish to offer our sincerest condolences. Atken: What? Dickwood: ...I'm just saying that I know how hard it is to lose a family member. We now go live... Atken: The hell are you talking about? All I'm saying is I never thought the spiteful cow would die. I thought she was immortal. I was certain she was a direct descendant from Nosferatu, I even had a researcher look in to it. Dickwood: Hating her isn't going to make this any easier, you just have to accept she's gone. We are gathered here today in memory of Moira Atken, who sadly passed after a battle... Atken: Oh I accept it, oh god do I accept it, I wanted to throw a god damn parade with balloons and banners and a fucking marching band to celebrate this day. Father said that may be tacky though, may have been due to me trying to arrange it for the day of the funeral. Trying to have it happen on the church grounds may have been in poor taste too. A great woman who's generosity meant a lot to many... Dickwood: So things were rocky toward the end? Atken: Things were rocky during my entire life. Dickwood: I thought you were spoiled and rich. Atken: As true as that is, she controlled EVERYTHING. She didn't want me to be a disgrace to the family name. The drugs? She was fine. The alcohol? No problem. Those special massages? Boys will be boys... Dickwood: How is this controlling? Her charity work... Atken: When she got back from Africa last year... Dickwood: She was in Africa? Atken: Overseeing the building of some schools or something. I don't know, I stopped paying attention to her yammering on about the good she was doing years ago. When her... Her son Philip could not be here today... Atken: CAN YOU TURN THAT OFF? Jesus Christ, I'm trying to talk to you. Dickwood: I would have thought... Atken: If I wanted to see it, I'd be there. I'm not there, I'm here. Here where I've been stuck for the last year taking care of her god damn army of cats. Here where I was told if I wasn't going to amount to anything, I might as well be her carer. Here where all my clothing now stinks of god damn cat piss and it's all because of her. TURN THAT BLOODY THING OFF. She cared deeply for him... Before Dirk could reach the television to turn it off, a boot when flying towards the television, knocking it off it's stand, unplugging it in the process. Atken: Yes, cared so much she yanked me away from the Yanks and back into this bloated corpse of a house. Dirk didn't reply immediately, partly because he was still trying to catch his breath back from his evasion of the falling television and partly because he was seeing an entirely new side of Phil. Phil had never spoken about his family much in the past and Dirk had always assumed that Phil's return to Scotland was entirely his own choice. To Dirk, the revelation that Phil's family was less than perfect was an incredible revelation. Atken: I still can't believe after the drugs, the booze, the girls, the gambling and that one incident with the ram, the thing that angered her the most was the thing I was actually proud of. I was making money from it, I was well respected... Dirk gives a derisive snort. Atken: ...I was making money. Good money too. People were giving me money to go out in front of the cameras night in and night out and be a raging asshole. I was getting paid to just go out in front of the cameras and do whatever the hell I wanted. Sure, it generally lead to me getting my ass kicked but it was still great and I loved it. Then she returns to Scotland and the minute she met one of her gossipy fucking church friends... Dickwood: Cat piss? Atken: Cat piss. Dickwood: I honestly can't believe I'm hearing this. I really thought this decision was entirely independent. I thought you actually wanted to be with her in her last days. Atken: What was I going to tell you? That my mummy was going to cut off my allowance if I didn't come home? Dickwood: But what about your wrestling money? Atken: Didn't I just mention... Dickwood: The drink, drugs and girls. Right, I got you. Atken: I was going to say gambling. To be honest the girls are cheap if you know where to look. Dirk jumps out of his recliner and begins to rummage around the wooden desk behind him. Atken: What the hell are you doing? Dickwood: Where's the booze and cigars? Atken: None of "Satan's Vices" in this house. Yes, he did use air quotes. Thank you for asking. Dickwood: No wonder you're not at the funeral right now. Atken: Well there was my complete hatred and also the request from the church I don't attend, or if I do, stay completely silent. Dickwood: So instead you invited me to your mother's house? Atken: Isn't that what you do when you get an empty? Dickwood: This empty is more permanent is it not? Atken: You raise a good point. On the realisation this house may now belong to him, he looks at the television sparking in the corner with sudden regret. He really should not have done that. Dickwood: So, do you and Matthew know the details of the will? Atken: Father? As far as I know, he doesn't want anything to do with the Atken Estate. I don't blame him, he's screwing the hell out of models, why would you come back to this place? Dickwood: So, do you know... Atken: My mother just died you bloody leech. Dickwood: You hated her. Atken: There's such a thing as decorum. Dickwood: Really? Really? You're going to use the decorum card with me? The man who has in the past, informed my wife, quite often in fact, that the reason we can't have kids is due to her poisoned womb. Atken: She does have a poisoned womb. Dickwood: If I didn't know any better, I would legit kill you right now. Atken: I thought the norsk were made of a stronger constitution than that. Dickwood: Norse. Atken: That's the bit you choose to take issue with? You really are a terrible husband. A second boot goes flying. This time from Dirk's direction towards Phil. However, thanks to Phil's cat like reflexes... Atken: OH GOD THE PAIN! Dickwood: That qualify as defending my wife in your book? Atken: It qualifies as abuse charges. Phil flops about the couch, lifting his head as far back as possible in the hopes to stop the bleeding. Atken: I can taste iron. I CAN TASTE IRON! Dickwood: Oh just shove a tissue up it. Atken: I'll shove you up here in a minute. Phil leaves the room in the hunt for something to stop the bleeding. In the meantime, Dirk Dickwood, in his agent snoopery manner, notices a bunch of documents that have been sitting in front of him on a desk during the whole conversation. Dickwood: I can't believe they were sitting in front of me the whole time. Atken: WHAT? Dickwood: I WASN'T TALKING TO YOU! Dirk grabs the documents of mystery and starts to browse through them while Phil continues to silently weep and look for some tissues. Dickwood: He gets everything? I don't believe this. Atken: WHAT DID YOU SAY? Dickwood: I SAID I'VE GOT SOME TISSUES HERE. Atken: WHAT? Dickwood: COME BACK TO THE LOUNGE! Atken: I CAN'T HEAR YOU, I'M COMING BACK TO THE LOUNGE! Phil returns to the room, leaving a trail of blood behind him. Clearly cleanliness was never one of his top priorities. Dirk jumps out of the recliner, wiping the cat hairs off the couch and offering Phil the hankerchief from his pocket, guiding him down to the couch gently. With Phil seat, Dirk joins him on the couch in uncomfortably close proximity. Dickwood: I think I can help you forget about this mess. Phil looks down and notices Dirk's hand on his lap. Atken: I'm not interested. Dirk now notices his hand placement and dives to the opposite end of the couch. Dickwood: NOT THAT YOU IDIOT! I know how to bring you back to America, and all I'll charge is my normal consultancy fee. Atken: Go on...
