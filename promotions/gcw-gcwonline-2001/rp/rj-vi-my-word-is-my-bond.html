R’j VI- My Word Is My Bond

Date: recent ***** Jason LeMaster had been turned to their purposes easily enough; it could be said that nobody served Christian Zenith out of their own pleasure. It was his handiwork that made this job possible. He was the reason that Lia and Virgil had the uniforms, not to mention the floor plan of the facility. They had rented a chalet far up in the mountains. It wasn't terrifically convenient a location, but about as close as they could get without making some suspicious kind of camp in the woods. Jason was coming to pick them up in half an hour, or so he said’if he didn't, Lia had a feeling that she, Virgil and Rune would be on the run. If Zenith found out where they were meddling, there'd be no chance whatsoever of him letting them get out of the situation alive. For all that, the three of them were relatively calm. Then again, they couldn't afford to be nervous and blow it. Virgil was already dressed. The uniform pants were a bit short for his liking, but this was no time for him to be picky’plainly, these pants hadn't been made for a lanky vampire, that was all. Lia slid her arms into the thick jacket. As she zipped it up, she noted from a glance in the mirror that it made her slim form look near-unhealthy, with the way that it obscured any curves. There was something incredibly impersonal about the uniforms. Downright sterile, dehumanizing. Of course, considering Zenith had chosen them, she reflected, that was probably purposeful. He'd never been the most concerned with his employee welfare. “Rune!” Virgil's voice had not yet reached a bellow, but Lia could tell that he was getting impatient. “What?” Rune's voice came from the bathroom, just as half-irritated. He'd insisted on changing in the bathroom. Lia had been perplexed by it, perhaps because she and Virgil had long ago lost any hint of modesty whatsoever. It still bothered Rune that he would often find his 'parents' (as he sarcastically called them) lounging about au naturel. “Are you done putting on your lipstick?” Virgil was saying, “LeMaster's going to be here soon, and if you're not ready, I'll make you stay here. With a book.” It was the most threatening thing he could come up with. Not that it would have bothered him to do so’but Rune was another story. “I'm fucking coming, alright?” Rune had taken to English cursing rather easily. “What's taking you so long?” Rustling reached Lia's ears. Though she hadn't looked up from the notes she'd jotted down the last time she talked to Jason, a smile came over her face. “This thing doesn't exactly fit right.” Lia's eyes flicked up from the paper just as Rune stepped out the bathroom door. Immediately, she bit down on her lip. The jacket fit so slimly that Rune couldn't put his muscular arms down at his sides. It was a little short at the bottom, exposing a half inch of Rune's pale stomach. Virgil smiled slyly. “Rune, you ah... look a little bit uncomfortable.” “If that's not a goddamn understatement... Of course I do!” A grin crept over the smile. “Is there anything I can do to help you?” Lia felt the couch move as Rune slumped onto the end of it. He folded his arms over his chest as best as he could, which wasn't very good at all. His face contorted in something resembling a wince. “Not unless you know a way to make my armpits feel less like raw meat,” was the grumbled response. Lia could tell he was approaching resignation. The whole thing had a family feel about it already’oddly not tense. It was a marked contrast to the past few weeks, when the mood had been drawn, speech terse. The search had been stressful on everyone, Lia knew, because no matter what any of them wanted to say, they would have rather been at home, relaxing... even enjoying each other's company. Without the Order to worry about, things had started to approach normality. Of course, they never stayed that way, not when you'd made enemies out of a man like Christian Zenith, and pissed off a good portion of the world's paragons by doing what you wanted instead of what they had written down in their little apocalyptic planbooks. Now was relaxed, though. The plan was in place, everyone knew their roles’it was going to go off without a hitch. Markus Blaire was coming home tonight. ***** The trip across country roads had jounced Lia's teeth together so many times that she felt like they all should have been broken. LeMaster had warned them that the roads would be bad, but she hadn't know they'd be so much so. The black SUV bounced along. Given the time estimate Jason had given her, she didn't figure it would be long before they got to the complex. This was quite a different route than the one she'd used when she was scouting the place, but Jason was an insider. He knew his stuff. Nerves hadn't come yet. That was either an incredibly good or incredibly bad sign. Lia didn't care to venture which, not being much of a betting woman. In the backseat, Rune seemed to be the only one with any sense that things weren't going to go well. He'd always had a habit of bouncing his right thumb when something made him nervous, tapping it repeatedly on the back of his left palm. He was doing so now, because the hugeness of the mission was weighing on him. This was the first time he'd really been called on to help Lia and Virgil with anything important. Virgil's eyes simply scanned the landscape. He was cool, but then, he'd done jobs far more complex than this when he'd been dealing with Tibet. Not to mention, this wasn't personal for him. It wasn't really personal for any of them, come to think of it. Markus, despite living on Lia's property, had never wanted to be buddy-buddy with the little familial trio that Virgil, Lia and Rune had become. He'd kept to himself’only came out to be around the group when Vivica had visited. That seemed to be the sum total of Markus Blaire's life. Vivica J. Valentine’Virgil couldn't understand it, but then, he and Vivica hadn't ever been the best of friends. Lia could; she could see a lot of herself and Virgil in Vivica and Markus, in the way neither of them felt quite complete when the other wasn't around. That's why this was important. Lia's empathy had driven her to make the promise, because her own feelings of separation from Virgil weren't so very far in the past that she could forget about the hole that it dug in her insides. It was unconscionable that a friend should feel the same. Virgil thought she was crazy. “Alright, we're gonna come up on the entry on the left side in a couple of minutes,” Jason said, interrupting the silence of the car with a slight exhalation. “I've told you guys protocol already, and I'm sure you remember, but windows down, get out your ID cards. If they ask about your weapons, show them. Don't make any sudden moves, or you're gonna get us thrown out’or worse. Just stay cool, and everybody's gonna come out in one piece.” Nobody answered him. They could see the gates through the windshield a moment later, orange and looming in the snow. A brown and white guard tower rested to either side, and as they pulled close, a brawny man in a Zenith Corp security uniform stepped out, holding up his hand for them to stop. Lia dutifully pressed the switch on the automated window. “ID's.” It was a flat request, though there was a bit of hoarseness in the voice, as though its owner had spent a bit too much time out in the Canada cold. Lia was willing to bet he'd done something to get stuck out here, asking for people's ID's, instead of working inside the facility. The cards were handed over in silence. Rune still seemed a little nervous, but the guard didn't seem to notice. His eyes flicked up to check each ID with the face before him. Then, his brow furrowed. He looked up, dead on into the eyes of the car's driver. “Jason LeMaster...” There was a break in speech, as though the young man was trying to connect dots. Then, his face lit with recognition. “Jason fucking LeMaster, are you serious” I thought you got the boot!” Jason's eyes had gone briefly wider, but he controlled himself with a long, slow breath, in and out. He forced a smile. “Nah... Zee and I had our problems’two strong minds, you know, don't always see eye to eye. But it was bound to work out in the end.” It was a pat explanation. Anybody who really knew Christian Zenith personally wouldn't have bought it’every single person in this car was living witness to the fact that Zenith didn't quit with his grudges until he'd damn well had his revenge. “Shit... never thought I'd see the likes of you up here. You bringin' up some trainees?” It was like a giant sigh of relief went through the car all at once. “Yep,” Jason said, jerking a thumb into the backseat to indicate the three of them. “Bringing some greenhorns up to get 'em trained up so the boss can rotate out some of the guys who've done their time up here.” The young man scoffed. “Good luck with that. I'm gonna be up here for the rest of my goddamn natural born life. Go on through.” He waved the car through, and Jason drove, evenly, without much increase in speed. It was the way a man who had long ago steeled himself to pressure drove. Within moments, they were inside the compound. “Does he always hire rubes?” Virgil said, once the windows were all up. “Ah, they slip through the cracks sometimes,” was LeMaster's answer. His tone suddenly turned bitter. “And sometimes, they just crack. Sometimes, this pressure's too much, and they can't take it. That kid was brilliant in combat school.” The car fell silent. Nobody needed to tell stories about Christian Zenith and his bullheaded need for absolute inhuman perfection. They'd moved past legend and into truth. ***** It did not surprise Lia in the least that the walls inside the compound were sterile white, not just in the jail portion, but all over. It was a living hell. Lia wondered if there would be anything left of Markus's mind once they got him back into safe hands. They were on a mock tour of the facility, since the tower had radioed ahead about who was coming in. LeMaster was a little bit edgy, considering that hadn't been part of the plan. They'd already been here too long, gotten caught on a handful of security cameras that would for certain bring wrath down on their heads once all this was over. But he tried to tell himself that it'd be alright’not like Zenith wouldn't know who busted Markus out anyway. Save the fact that he could have saved his own skin and let the freaks take the fall. It wasn't a very charitable thought, but Jason LeMaster had trained himself to think of his own survival these days. They were on their way to the detainment and testing wing. The vague, coppery taste of blood was in the air. Combined with the antiseptic smell of ammonia, it was making Lia a little nauseous. The sooner she got out of here, the better she'd feel. She was resisting the urge to creep down the hall with her hand on the pommel of her gun. Turning a corner, they came to a small room labeled 'Sample Lab.' LeMaster gestured with great pretense as he turned to them again, speaking loud enough for anyone passing to clearly overhear what he was saying. “Obviously, this is one of our sample labs. We've got six such rooms in this facility, all equipped with state of the art microscopes, centrifuges, etcetera. We do so much medical work that just about anything you might need in a hospital lab or forensics lab... we've got it. I presume that'd be of interest to the three of you?” Rune nodded with genuine curiosity, Lia and Virgil a bit mechanically. As soon as they were inside the door, LeMaster turned to them furtively. “This is practically the only room in the fuckin' place that doesn't have bugs all over it.” “Why?” Virgil asked, raising a brow. LeMaster shook his head. “Bottom line. The radio equipment was interfering with the readings in here, and above all else, Christian Zenith is about the bottom line. He didn't want to lose money because of his own paranoia... so this is one of the only rooms that's not bugged. Not that they know that here or anything, most of these people probably presume the whole place is crawling with spies and mics. Probably better for em that way.” He waited a moment, as curious eyes roamed around the room. Then, LeMaster sighed, and all eyes were on him once again. “We're gonna be in a little bit of a bind,” he said softly, ruffling the hair at the base of his neck. “We've been here too long, people have had time to get features in their minds... gonna be on the security cameras too.” For a long moment, no one said anything. “What, then?” It was Virgil's gruff response that broke the silence. “We change the plan,” LeMaster said, raising his head to look at Virgil with a somewhat cold, impatient glance. “We do what we gotta do. I tell them another story instead of the one I was gonna tell em, and the three of you follow my lead.” There was nothing in his tone but an order. ***** “Hold up one minute... you can't go in H-Block without special access privileges.” The guy was going to be a pain in the ass. It was just that easy to tell. He looked like one of those people who did their jobs obsessively, robotically well. Always followed the manual. “Look,” Jason said, leaning down to talk quietly. “I'm gonna show you my ID. It's not gonna be on your list, but if you're smart, you'll recognize the name.” He flashed his ID card. The guard's eyes went wide, because everyone knew the names they should be looking out for. Christian. Jason. Dmitri. Being one of the few people Christian Zenith trusted gave you access to a lot of things. “I'm sorry, Mr. LeMaster... rules are rules.” “Look, Bill,” Jason said, looking down to read the guard's nametag before he said it. “I want to go in because these are new recruits, and I want to show them what we do here. Bio recruits. Zenith wants them to know the import of what they're doing here, so I'm gonna take them in, and let Blaire demonstrate his powers for em.” “But he's... he's on restrictions!” LeMaster nodded. He'd gone from bluff to full-on bullying. “You think I don't know that shit” I'm moving on express orders here, straight from the man himself. I'm gonna go in, I'm gonna take this key, and I'm gonna do what he asked me to do. You got a problem, you can call him and confirm, okay?” “Y... yes sir.” Zenith wouldn't liked one of his guards being nearly timid in the face of Jason LeMaster, but then, he wasn't here to see it. He would have liked even less the fact that they'd gotten in in the first place. H-Block's door slid slowly open, then shut behind the four of them. Lia recognized the hall she'd been able to stare down from the tree the last time she'd been here, but it was disorienting to actually stand inside the winding maze of hallways. LeMaster knew where he was going, though, and had his key in hand already. “What the hell did you tell him that for?” Virgil was asking, immediately inside the door. LeMaster didn't lose an ounce of momentum. It was all Lia could do to keep up with the three men, all of them with much longer legs than her. She was moving practically at a trot as Jason turned to answer her husband. “Bluffed him,” he said, obviously, “I had to tell him something or he was gonna call right in front of us before he let us in. They're trained to be his little lapdogs, you should know that. He'll still call, but now he'll call while we're already in here unlocking a cell.” “Shit...” Lia murmured. Their footsteps pounded down the concrete-floored hallway. A dozen baleful sets of eyes reached through the bars and plucked at Lia. As much as she had sympathy for anyone, she felt for them’being caged up like this, having god knows what done to you... It made her despite Christian Zenith all the more. As soon as they reached Markus' cell, Jason was unlocking it. “Blaire,” Jason hissed. Markus stirred weakly from sleep. “Get your ass up!” He was jerked, wobbly, to his feet by the forearm as Jason took one stride into the small cell. It wasn't that he was unused to rough treatment, he'd just had a hell of a time sleeping lately’and it hadn't helped that he hadn't exactly been fed what he needed to stay healthy either. He felt like he was sick again, like he had been before he'd died. Lia paused a moment, shocked at Markus' appearance. Deep black circles were set under his eyes as though carved in stone. He had thinned considerably since the last time she'd seen him. “Looks like you're the lucky fucker today,” LeMaster bluffed. “Getting to come out in the yard for your walkies.” Markus' eyes were wide even as he was still dragging himself into complete consciousness. He recognized the faces, of course he did, but some instinct within him told him to keep his mouth shut. LeMaster's gun was suddenly in Markus' back. He quick-marched down the hallway, and averted his eyes as he'd been taught to do while the guard punched in the code. LeMaster's nimble fingers went through the numbers as though simple muscle memory had fixed them there. The door to the yard slowly slid open. It wasn't much of a yard to speak of; Markus had always hated it, hated that people referred to it that way. The door slid shut behind them, and the group was greeted with the bleak sight of snowdrifts piled up against a twenty-foot fence. Two steps into the yard and Jason LeMaster was running. Markus, bewildered, stumbled along behind him because he had to, because Jason hadn't yet let go of his forearm. Adrenaline was kicking into high gear as they reached the fence, whose metal was slick with ice. “Hope everybody brought their climbing boots,” Jason said, a grimness in his tone. It wasn't going to be fun trying to scale the fence when even the combat-grade boot soles would slip on the ice. But it was the only way out now. Lia looked up the expanse of metal, realizing it'd be difficult even for people who were in top physical shape. She shook her head. “Jason... he's been caged up for months now... he's not going to have the strength.” Jason hadn't thought of that when he was reformulating the plan. It would have been simpler if they could have gone out the warehouse exit, which wouldn't have involved so much effort. It had been the perfect plan, but now it looked like the end of the line. But Jason LeMaster didn't much fancy the idea of dying in the middle of nowhere in fucking cold Canada. “Virgil... can you carry him on your back?” It wasn't Virgil's idea of fun, but it was certainly within his ability. He nodded. “As long as he can hold on, there won't be a problem.” Rune adjusted his uniform uncomfortably. Even living around ice and snow all his life, this wasn't going to be fun’the snow on the other side of the fence was at least knee deep, and running through it was going to be exhausted for him. He was well-fed, and bigger than Markus to boot. Four pairs of eyes scanned the terrain ahead of them, five precious seconds gone before anyone spoke. “I'll climb.” They all turned. It was Markus that spoke, a quietness in his tone, one almost completely without steel of any sort, but resolute in its own way. It was said with the resignation of a decision that had been long ago fated. Nobody argued. There were times when a man deserved his dignity. “Let's go,” Jason said, command returning to his voice. “We're probably gonna have to leave the car and take something else.” It was going to be a long night. ***** Bumping down the road in a borrowed transport van, the group had lapsed into silence. For part of the way, someone from the facility had tailed them, but LeMaster was a good enough driver’plus, there was every change that nobody'd been alerted yet. They'd have probably expected them to try to get the more maneuverable SUV back instead of ditching it at the facility. Markus was lying in the back of the van, prone, buried in between a stack of blankets. He hadn't wanted to, but Lia had insisted, and her medical training had won out, for once. Either that, or the threat of Virgil tossing him out in the snow for refusing to comply, which had been used at least once. Jason's eyes dug into the road. Virgil, beside him, glanced back every now and then to set eyes on his wife. He liked watching her work, for what reasons he couldn't necessarily pinpoint. She tended to Markus now, checking him over for obvious wounds and mixing something for him to drink. Drugged with a sleeping aid, more than likely, since he wouldn't have to do any more running today. At least she had insisted not, which meant that if they had to bail, Virgil would be carrying him. “Is he gonna be okay?” Rune's soft Norwegian lilt broke the silence in the van. His eyes hadn't left Markus, obviously eyeing his shallow breathing. “He'll require some tending,” Lia murmured softly, swirling the mixture in her hand. “Mostly, it's exhaustion. Nutrition issues. They've probably been feeding him laboratory synthetics. Enough to keep him alive’barely.” Nobody had called Vivica yet. They were still close enough to Zenith's facility that it felt premature to do so, like maybe if they called her, they'd jinx the whole thing and get the whole group recaptured because of their own silly hubris. It was getting to the point where Lia couldn't stand it anymore. She lifted Markus' head and held the bottle to his lips. He drank slowly, and without opening his eyes. It looked as though his throat muscles were straining to swallow. She laid his head back down and folded her legs indian style underneath her. The bottle clinked against the floor of the van as she set it down. Again, no one spoke, but the tension was still high. Exhilaration would come later, once the whole thing was over. “Lia...” It was, surprisingly, Jason who spoke. Lia looked up and caught his eyes in the rearview mirror. “Maybe you should give Vivica a call. Let her know we're going to be in town some time within the next few hours.” They were halfway to Bangor, Maine. It seemed like as safe a place as any to debrief and chill out’nobody had any ties there, and it was remote enough that nobody'd think to look for them there. Once they got back to the US, it'd be much easier, save the fact that Markus Blaire would have to stay well south of Chicago at all times. Lia slipped her cell phone out of her jacket pocket. It was hot in the van, and she was thinking about squirming out of the uniform jacket as well. But for the moment, she stared resolutely at the signal bar, willing it high enough that she could actually get the call out. Vivica was number three on the speed dial. The phone was ringing, and Lia was breathlessly waiting for a voice on the other end. “Hello?” “We'll be in town in about three hours. Don't go outside the hotel room from here on out... things didn't go exactly to plan, so we're going to have to be a little more careful.” It was all she had time to say before they hit another dead patch, but Vivica, she hoped, would understand. At the very least, she'd be happy again when all this was over. Lia didn't know how she could have looked Vivica in the eye and told her she'd failed. Lucky thing, then, that a promise meant a lot to her, even as jaded as she was by this point in her life. Some things never changed.
