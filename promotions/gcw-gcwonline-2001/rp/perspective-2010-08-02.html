Date: 08/02/10 I awake from my slumber at 3:18 AM in my apartment in Tokyo nearly a week after that thing I did for that place that I can't care to remember right now. I sit up, and wipe the crust from my eyes, and let out a nice, big yawn, one so big it makes my entire body shudder. I'm not sure why we're still in Japan, except that Alexis tells me she's been having visa issues. Something about some paperwork not being signed or some other such nonsense. I told her it's just a call to immigration, I know a few people in the Justice department, and she stopped me right away before I could even finish. I know she's hiding something. But she always seems to be hiding something. Still, I look at her. Sleeping, peacefully. She's an angel from this light, illuminated by artificial lights, a neon city glow, and sheer beauty. Man, I hate her sometimes. I get out of bed and walk into the living room. I'm not really familiar with this place. I rarely frequent it. I forget I have the place half the time, but it's here whenever I'm in the area. It's nice, I guess. The view is of the shimmering red steel of Tokyo Tower. But still, I don't like being here when I can help it. This place isn't home. It's never been home. I grab a bottle of Maker's Mark off of the bar counter and jump onto the couch and turn on the television. I flip through the stations, through NHK and Nippon and TBS and Asahi, and I don't care about any of it. It's all just the same junk I can get back home, just in a different language. So I decide to flip to the OnDemand features, and find a movie worth watching. It's slim pickings, but most movies suck these days. But that's when I remember a producer I know lent me his DVD copy of 'Inception', so I browse through one of my luggage bags and grab it and pop it in. At least it'll take my mind off things. As the action picks up, I swig from the bottle and I let my mind wander. I glance down at the table, and I spot it. I spot it once again. I spot it once a-goddamned-gain. God. I look at this title. I gaze long, hard, forever...and fuck, it's stupid. The hardcore title? Really? And it says "hardcore" across it? Is someone gonna forget I have this title? If they don't see "hardcore" written across it in all caps, like some teenage masturbater did it? I feel like I've wasted months. Months of beating up stank assholes with garish tattoos, pasty skin, and stenches that would put county dumps to shame. Wearing black lipstick and dark sunglasses inside, jabbering inane nonsense so banal it makes my fucking ears bleed. Big tits, whiny whores, pointless banter. Christ. This fucking thing. It's so ugly. What a waste. None of this makes sense. I'm the Hardcore champion, and I couldn't be more ashamed. I take a long, long sip from the bottle and choke it down. I almost gag it up, but I don't spew it everywhere. I know better. Not much, but enough. ****** She slept in until around noon, when she saunters in the room in a black lace bra and matching panties. I'm just topping off a bottle of Glenlivet, watching Downtown. Those guys crack me up. I love Matsumoto, but why's Hamada gotta be such a dick all the time? She storms over, picking up the empty bottles and yelling at me, but I can barely hear what she's saying. Part of it's the inebriation, part of it 'cause I just don't care. But she yells and postures and I drown her out and just nod repeatedly, blinking my eyes and telling her that I understand over and over again. She barks for me to get my shit together before leaving. Why the fuck did I get married so young? I could be on a 100-foot yacht off the coast of Brazil, fucking big-assed models and hanging out with Drake, or snorting coke off of the tits of big-breasted eager blonde vixens in a New York city high-rise, fueled on Moet and weed after a high-stakes poker game with John Mayer, Jay-Z, and Al Gore. Nope. I'm in a city I hate with a belt I loathe after an event I despised organized by a company I couldn't give two shits about. Fuck. ****** Alexis doesn't tell me until the last possible minute that she didn't have visa issues, but wanted to stay and go to another soiree thrown by my shithead uncle Kenji for the benefit of the company I abandoned like a meth baby in a rusty refrigerator in a junkyard, that he instead swooped in to be the saving grace of the place. I want to argue but I realize it's for naught, and she really wants to go, so I just concede because I don't feel like arguing right now. She tells me to get dressed and I tell her I am. She looks at my white Nike kicks, my jeans I paid $5,000 to have some jerkoff pre-rip holes in them because that's cool or something, and my black t-shirt that reads 'I Like All The Same Shit You Like, Let's Fuck', and she grimaces. I tell her that if I go, it's on my terms. She screams that I look like a hipster douchebag, and tells me to put on a suit. I tell her I'd rather be hipster trash than a guy with a stick up his ass. She doesn't take kindly to the comment and throws a three-piece made by someone whose name I forget on the bed, tells me to put it on. I don't want to, but I don't feel like fighting. I give in with one concession: no tie. I have standards, you know. ****** The elevator we ride in that climbs to the 49th story of the Midtown Tower moves slower than a geriatric bowel movement. I light up a Lucky Strike, and Lexi immediately points at a "no smoking" sign. I glare at her, with a bottle of Yamazaki in my grasp, and she returns the gaze, but her's is different. Of anger, of hatred. I stub out the cigarette on the window of the elevator, and she remains unpleasant. It's going to be one of those nights. I know it is. The doors open and we walk through and it's a bevy of stuffy white-collared assholes passing judgment from afar. I can hear their whispers as clear as day, as if they were funneled straight into my ears. They may think I can't hear them, but I can. I know of these ilk. They're the ones I did everything I could to escape. They're hangers-on. They clutch to the side of the lifeboats of the Titanic as the ship sinks into the cold, blue, unforgiving ocean. They block emergency exits in airplanes to get priority in exodus. They claw and scream and cajole their way from harm's way while they sign checks for a hundred dollars to the Boys and Girls Club of their nearby metropolis, and smile ear-to-ear as they reiterate they're making a difference. I loathe them all with passion. I'd kill them all if I had the chance. These people are goblins. They're the ones that try to ruin Nick Drake by posthumously selling him out. They'd try and destroy Black Sheep with dancing hamsters hawking box cars. I hate them all. Babbling morons, siphoning the life from the world. Leeches on our backs, on our faces, on our chests. Faceless cretins, each one with hands cupped, extended and at the ready for windfalls of wealth. They all reek of dirty money. Alexis has been chattering in my ear the entire time about making a good impression, not doing anything stupid, and keeping up appearances. I've blocked out everything she's said, and instead make a bee-line for the bar. Free booze amongst people I hate remedies a recipe of disaster I salivate over. I tell the unassuming bartender that I own him and he should do what I say lest he wants to never work again, and he does as I please, which includes me slamming the empty bottle of Yamazaki on the counter top and him handing me the bottle of chilled Grey Goose. Smart guy. I make my way through the sycophants to the DJ, so I can air out my disdain for his choice of Beethoven's chosen assfuck, and that something with enough bass to shatter glass and set off the security system's earthquake sensors would be fitting of the night he's about to bear witness to. He puts on some Ironman-era Ghostface Killah, and everyone grows colder than dead fish. I can see Alexis talking with Kenji from afar, mostly the two of them beyond angry, as I'm on the dance floor, screaming the lyrics to "Daytona 500" at the top of my lungs and shaking about like Michael J. Fox on a roller-coaster. I never wanted to be here, and this party sucked anyway. Just needed a boost. Kenji bolts right up to me and grabs me by the shoulder and pulls me aside and for a brief moment the thought pops into my head of defending that title against that big ol' clown fucker or that faceless freak or one of those slits and it turns my stomach something fierce. The thought passes when we're out of the party's earshot and he's hollering at me like a madman, admonishing me every chance he gets. He says a lot of things. I don't listen to most of them. I take a sip from the Grey Goose, and realized I've done the right thing. ** **** The two of us are in a tricked-out Nissan Skyline GT-R, blazing through a private highway at speeds up over 120 miles per hour. I got the car by trading my Girard-Perregaux Magistral Tourbillon watch to some high-on-paint-thinner Bosozoku-obsessed kid with blue hair and a fashion sense for shit. The watch’s worth two-hundred seventy-five grand, but the joke's on him, 'cause they gave me five more of the fuckers for free. So I take his majesty of engineering and automotive ingenuity and run it ragged through the cityscape. The KPH gauge hits well past 210, as I look over at Alexis, who holds on for dear life. She never liked the reckless life, and yet she still married me. A question for the ages. Not my question, though. She said yes. That's on her, not me. She begs for me to slow down, but I'm too inebriated to pay attention. Instead, I turn the radio up full-blast. Mobb Deep pumps from the speakers and it’s loud and abrasive and obnoxious and it makes her uncomfortable and I love every minute of it and just enjoy the ride. There’s a tight turn coming off the straightaway and I don’t slow down, I speed up. I can hear her whimper, her nails digging into the side of the door, as it comes closer and closer. At the last possible minute I downshift and decelerate, and we slide through it. It feels like slow motion. It’s beautiful. We barely make it out alive, and the highway is nearly at an end when she blows up and goes apeshit. I’ve never seen her in such a state. She’s animated and gesturing wildly and the tears ruin her mascara as they stream down her face and her voice goes hoarse by the end of it. We’re just a few blocks from the apartment when she screams at me to stop the car. So I do. She's had enough. She wants to get out. I unlock the door and offer her the egress she so desperately desires. Our eyes meet, and I know that look. It's familiar. It's called disappointment. It's an Iwamura family tradition. But she took the opportunity, so I don't give her the chance to change her mind or even fully shut the passenger-side door as I speed off and head for the causeway. At least the uptick in sudden speed slams it closed for me. Guess the movies got something right. *** * ** I'm in a hostess bar in the Shinjuku drinking a nondescript bottle of Saki when an all-too-young vixen in a tight pink miniskirt and too much makeup saunters up and finds her way to my booth. Her heels click-clack as out of sync with the shitty music they play as is at all possible, as she sidles up next to me, smelling of spray-on tan and knock-off perfume. She's asking what I'm drinking even after clearly seeing me take a swig from the sake, and I don't bother paying attention to her. She insists on making conversation anyway, and blabs about how cute she thinks I am and how wonderful of a night it is and I couldn't give less of a fuck at this moment right now. I just want to drink. I can't even do that in peace anymore. She continues prattling on, and I check my phone. A thought crosses my mind to call up that Brit cat, the one person in that entire stupid fucking company that doesn't immediately give me a rash. Lexi told me of the shit he was dealing with, maybe figured I'd send my condolences or whatever. Even if it ain't my style, I still felt bad. No one needs to go through that shit. I swig from the bottle and search my contacts for the letter 'S', but I barely get to 'P' before I think better of it and put the phone away. Probably not the best time. At least not right now. The bitch is still going on and on, and I'm getting annoyed. I hate this fucking place. I pull out my pack of smokes and fish one out, and put it in my mouth. I don't get a chance before this bitch lights it for me. She's all smiles, like she's done me a favor. Who's this fuckin' stupid bitch think she is? I just tilt my head back and stare at the ceiling, as I release the smoke from my lungs. I grab the cigarette, and clutch the bottle. ** * * * * I light up another smoke and add to the burn of tobacco in the gambling den. I’ve been looking at my cards, and I’ve got jack shit so far. We’re playing Texas Hold’em, poker’s answer to the common whore. The guys all around me are tattooed and grizzly. I spot one with a missing pinky, another sans the ring finger at the knuckle, too. They’re laughing and posturing and drinking and hollering and causing bedlam. I feel right at ease. The fifth card goes down, it has a technical name but I don’t know it because I don’t follow poker and it nets me a straight. The last round of betting comes and we throw in our chips and show our hands. I’m last, I hold mine up and slap them on the table, as they collectively bitch and moan and I take the pot. Pussy gaijin wins again, says the guy with the scar that runs under his left eye. They all laugh and I do, too. They all see me as lesser. Tainted, even. I doubt they even respect me. But tonight they like me, and that’s all that matters. The youngest kid at the table, probably ‘round my age, recognizes me from the event and says he saw me won the belt, but I have no interest in talking about that. I tell him as much, and his elders tell him to shut the fuck up about it, in so many words. He clams up. We play a few more hands and we shoot the shit about the World Cup and oil spills and cosplayin' nerds and shitty J-Pop and ganguro girls and the Jersey Shore and American arrogance. I drink rice wine and smoke cigarettes and win money and then lose money until I get bored and decide to cash out. They want me to stay, that they’re just killing time until the underground fight in the back starts soon and they’re gonna bet on that and I should stay, it’s gonna be fun, they've got a cage and everything. I figure it might be worth the watch, so I tell them okay. But I’m hungry. I could really go for a Carl's Jr. Western burger. * * * * * 8 Fuck. Where the fuck am I?
