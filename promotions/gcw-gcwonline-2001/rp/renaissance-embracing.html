Renaissance: Embracing

Toshiro Mifune’s School Sapporo, Japan The first fingers of dawn crept through the miniscule slats of the wooden blinds that were fastened to the dormitory window for mornings such as these. Now and then, a mote of dust wafted by, but by far, most of the shafts of slowly-widening light were pure on the wooden floor. The sun lit like a contrasting tropical bird in the trail of ebony hair streaming across the pillows of the bed, unfettered; within it, so much more than just black was illuminated- rich crimson, mahogany, a touch of raven’s wing blue. And too, as time slithered through the hourglass, the light began to steal across the moon-bleached skin of two beings who, despite the time of day, were animate at this hour. Jade kissed cerulean, flashed away playfully, and then back to its original resting place, as waves upon a beach. Today, though, Virgil did not seem in a playful mood. A tempest brewed within the frighteningly intense depths of his eyes. Soon, it reached out and engulfed the spark in Lia’s, drawing them down to a quiet verdant hue. She probed further with a look, asked him what was wrong without asking, but he looked away from her. Despite his arms being wound tightly around her lean form, she hadn’t felt so far away from him since they were physically miles and miles apart. Something tense and wicked hung in the air. It was unexplainable, because she knew it wasn’t just them. Certainly, things had been tense enough between their insular little group lately, but this reached beyond that. Something hung over every step each of them took, a low-hanging doom threatening to descend like a bell jar whenever the moment arrived. She sighed, a sound with tendrils of annoyance beginning to work their way through. “Virgil” what’s wrong?” It was the type of question that usually resulted in “I don’t love you anymore” type of answers, at least if you trusted dime-store romance novels. A disgusted look wrought its way across Virgil’s features, the cruelty of which was usually reserved for times when awareness had fully descended. This wound broke the veil. “Vivica,” he spat. The way he pronounced it made it sound as though it were the name of a disease, not the name of a tag team partner, not a teammate, “Vivica is what’s wrong.” The way he said it carried obvious gravity? until she laughed. It wasn’t a mocking laugh, nor one that resulted from hilarity. It was confused, disbelieving. Without disturbing the tangle of limbs that bound them to one another, she leaned her head away from his to look at his whole expression. No joking. “Are you joking?” she asked, her eyes widening, “You’re still stewing over that?” A visceral, animalistic expression came over his features’the bared teeth that were often considered a threat in the world of vampiric relations. But he knew better than to outright threaten her. “No, this is not a joke. And I don’t see why you think it’s so amusing for me to still be irritated at the little whelp, after what she’s pulled in the last few weeks.” “Oh, now she’s a whelp. That’s adorable, Virgil, are you always going to extend the prejudices of our kind onto the people we’ve chosen as teammates?” “When warranted,” he snapped, eyes glimmering. And suddenly, though quite unbidden, Lia could feel her own ire beginning to rise. “Warranted” You haven’t yet explained what it was that Vivica did to earn your ire in the first place, Virgil, much less what she could have done last week that would provoke this kind of response. Or are you just looking for a reason to hate her again so that you don’t have to try to let her in like you promised me you would?” His sharpened fingernails dug into her shoulderblades. In that moment, and for the first time ever, she wondered if maybe he was holding her so tightly to try and keep from an attempt to tear her apart for not agreeing with him every time he invented a crime for Vivica or Dawn. “A promise you extracted from me with guilt.” That stung like he actually had attempted to rend off an arm. But of course, one blow was never enough, not when the anger was already seething among their kind. Rarely did reality or what humans might call “common sense” override vampiric pride, and the pressing need for autonomy. “She has given plenty of reasons,” he went on, “The biggest and latest offense being that she cannot seem to keep herself out of the business of others when the time comes. If you must beg specifics, though I cannot imagine you are as ignorant of her wrongdoing as you pretend to be, my match against Kannon, and her behavior within it?” The rapid-fire of Spanish-accented bullets dug underneath her skin with the audacity of an intruding claw. “Please, Virgil!” For the first time during this whole ordeal, she raised her voice. “Dawn and I were down there during your match too, and I don’t see you bitching endlessly about that.” “Because what you did couldn’t have gotten me disqualified, and it wasn’t selfish! We’ve all agreed’everyone fights their own battles, but when needed, we support one another. Why don’t you ask yourself if Vivica really cared about supporting me, or if she was more concerned with pressing her own foolish little revenge against Kannon for making her look like an ass the week before.” “Why not kill two birds with one stone?” The question, which she felt was a reasonable one, was met only with a snarl from Virgil. She took that as her invitation to bulldoze ahead. “Virgil, does it really matter what her motives were” Whatever reason she came down there, the end result of it was to help you win. Yes, she could have gotten you disqualified, but she didn’t.” “She screwed me out of a title,” came the lightning-strike counter. “She helped you keep your spot in the tournament. What harm did it do?” He growled under his breath. “When did you become so damned Machiavellian” We all had an agreement. She stepped over the line, and under said agreement, that means she’s out.” Finally, whatever restraint she might have been harboring snapped in half like a thread being severed. “Maybe you stepped over the line, Virgil! God be damned if you haven’t been the only person who’s been impossible to get together with the rest of the group since we’ve gotten here, and I’ve got a sneaking suspicion that it’s not even because you have serious concerns about what’s going on” you’d just rather disagree, so long as you and Vivica can stay on the opposite side of something.” “You’d take her side over mine?” Glowering, she shoved herself away from his grip and practically leapt out of bed. Her footfalls made the floor creak in protest as she snatched up a red silk kimono that was draped over the back of a chair. She slid into it with a violence of gesture that suggested if she could have, she would have stabbed the kimono into ribbons. “Now who’s trying to play guilt to their advantage?” The morning’s peace was shattered as she stalked out of the room. The hem of the kimono flaring out behind her as she tied its belt was the last thing that Virgil saw before the door slammed shut, and he was left on his own. Left on his own, the sheets raked half off his body and a definite chill in the air, he sighed, combing his fingers through his long hair. After all he’d fought to get her back, there were times when he wondered if things were ever going to be normal between them. .:: ::: ::. Across the school from the dormitory some minutes later, Lia didn’t honestly feel much better. She was hoping that a little time away from staring Virgil in the face all day would do her temper some good; surprisingly enough, she was still seething, her mind still reeling from all the venom that she and Virgil had spat back and forth at one another. “Excuse me, Miss?” The voice that intruded upon her thoughts was unwelcome, and she tried to resist the urge to snarl as she heard it. But as she turned around and saw the diminutive form of the woman behind her, she forced herself to hide any hint of animosity. There was no sense terrorizing Master Mifune’s students, because he could just as easily ask them to leave as let them stay here, taking up space in his dojo. “Yes?” The woman bowed, and somewhat awkwardly, Shadow bowed back. Despite the time she’d spent here with wrestling, she was still quite unused to the customs. “I don’t mean to interrupt you if you’re doing something important. But Master Mifune saw you walk by his office a few moments ago, and he asked me to make sure that there was nothing you needed.” For a few moments, the irritation still rumbled in her stomach, threatening to come forth in a snapped response, a command to leave her the hell alone. “Actually, could you direct me to somewhere with plenty of light?” She patted the bag under her arm. “I believe I’d like to try to capture a bit of the landscape while I’m here.” “Certainly,” said the young woman, bowing again, “The gallery is right this way, if you’ll just follow me.” Absently, Lia forgot to bow back to her escort. The conversation being over was a welcome respite back into the realm of her head, in which that argument was still sloshing back and forth, as fresh a scab to pick as it could be. .:: ::: ::. Despite what she had told the young woman, Akiko, as it turned out, she hadn’t come in here to paint what she saw out the window. What was beginning to take shape on the sketchpad in front of her bore absolutely no resemblance to the snow-clouds swooping overhead, and the majestic curve of mountains in the distance. Rapidly, almost as if from muscle-memory, the outline of the shape had sketched itself out. It was as though her brain was guiding her hand, but without thought. Her mind wanted her to remember something, and telling it no at this point would have been difficult. Her right hand rubbed the black oil pastel harshly up the side of the house’s outline. This house, as she remembered it, had been all harsh angles, such was the style of building at the time. It had been a tall house, with a spire on the back end of it. When she was a child, she had imagined it to be where princesses lived, with their prince at their side (that part had come when she was a little older)?they must have surveyed the land they ruled with a smile, knowing every morning that the sun that rose and the wheat that was harvested and the flowers that bloomed on this land was all a possession. One could tangibly hold these things and remember what one had, the paradise that one ruled. Of course, now, after the jade of many hundred years had slipped past, she knew it was only a watchtower. And the man who lived in it was a lowly servant, kept in the family with a payment of powerful Vlaszy blood that promised enhanced abilities and protection from the fearsome Tzimisce lords that roamed the land. He’d been a servant, nothing more and nothing less, keeping watch out for invaders from the nearby village, just in case anybody got wind of what the roaming Magyar really brought into their midst. Ahhh? memories. The Kine seem to turn them over with happiness, reliving each moment with joy, although its original truth can never be recaptured. For my kind, it is not so. Everything becomes crystalline clear, no matter how long it has been since something happened. Reality hurts, the reality that comes with knowing that a mother, a father, a husband, are gone forever. Gone is the assurance that you will “meet them in heaven” or whatever asinine pipe-dream it is that mortals cling to when they wish to feel better about losing those dear to them. There are times when I wonder what is myth, and what is truth, but why try to decipher them anymore? Why does my kind even try? Why do we not bury ourselves underground in torpor, or cast ourselves into the sunlight, or leap headfirst into a bonfire the moment we realize these horrible truths? Lia’s brow furrowed in concentration, and gently, she set the black pastel back into its place in the box. Now gray, a dark bluish-slate color to sketch the mud that served as a sort of grout to keep the chill from leaking in through the stones in the winter. Mind that strangely shaped stone by the door that all the children used to hide their toys behind? .:: ::: ::. Zdice, Czech Republic The Vlaszy Manor House “Laszlo, I saw you do it! So did Anezka!” Lia’s hands were on her hips, her childish chin thrust stubbornly forward as she accused her brother of high treason’for an eight year old, at least. Laszlo grinned at her. It was an expression that both twins possessed, but Lia, the more serious of the two of them, rarely seemed to use it. “I didn’t do anything.” Lia stamped her foot. “Liar, I saw you! You took Alisz from behind the stone and you put her in the well bucket!” He still grinned at her. One thing that could be said about Laszlo Vlaszy was that he was a very unrepentant little imp. “So what if I did” I can just put you in the bucket and send you after her.” Lia’s mouth hung open for a moment, the perfect picture of childish indignance. But before she could mount a response, Laszlo darted away, out into the yard. “Good luck finding her now! Bet she drowned!” he called. “GRANDMAMA!” For a little girl, Lia had a very powerful set of lungs. .:: ::: ::. Things hadn’t always been like that, though. Now, as Lia sat across the thick wooden table from her twin brother, she could hardly suppress her sorrow. “Laszlo, I’m not sure that I want to leave?” For once, Laszlo was serious. There were no smart-aleck comments to be made at this moment in time. “You have to.” “I know, I just?” He held up a hand. “Lia, don’t make this any harder than it already is. After what happened with Jaroslav, you can’t stay here. The only way for you to get out of this situation without any trouble is for you to go marry that man, whomever he may be.” “Off in Iberia!” she wailed. “Yes, Iberia. But you’ll be alive, and you know quite well what will happen when somebody catches wise to the fact that Jaroslav didn’t have an accidental run-in with a pack of wolves.” A pained grimace came over Lia’s face. How, oh how on earth, had she managed to get herself into this much trouble? A dead noble’s son, and instead of asking questions about what the local women thought of that worthless Jaroslav, they were ready to draw and quarter someone over his death. Her. Eventually, they’d trace it all back to its source. Nobody knew the whole story but Laszlo, and she was lucky he’d decided to stand by her through all this. Lucky that he and Master Ionescu could conspire to find a solution. If only she could keep her heart from breaking at the thought of having to leave Zdice for a very foreign land for the first time in her life, at the thought of leaving her twin brother behind. .:: ::: ::. Lia’s eyes darted around the gathered crowd. For all that she’d wanted to stay in Zdice ten years ago, at fifteen years old, now she wished she was anywhere BUT her home. A Church-ordered execution could do that to a person. So here she stood, prepared to be burned as a witch, because these idiots actually believed that she’d conjured some pack of wolves to kill Jaroslav. Of course, if she’d screamed at the top of her lungs that she’d killed him herself, she’d still be in the same position. She hadn’t seen anyone from the family. The priest told her that her family had rejected her, which seemed rather flimsy an excuse. More than likely, they wouldn’t have been able to bear this sight. And then? A flash of long, black hair, tied back. Bright green eyes that stuck out all the way from across the crowd. Fine crimson silk clothing, a pair of leather bracers hand-tooled with the curled dragon prominent on the Vlaszy family shield. Laszlo. Her eyes widened as she looked at him, tempted to cry out, but he placed a finger over his lips, indicating that she should be quiet. She bit down hard on her lip to prevent herself from announcing his presence. Whatever the priest said after that didn’t matter, because this was turning into an exhibition anyway. His arrival only meant one thing. She wasn’t going to die. He wouldn’t let her die. .:: ::: :::. Toshiro Mifune’s School Sapporo, Japan The moment the first memory had struck her, she had frozen, clutching the oil pastel in her hand almost hard enough to snap it in half. But as they continued on, an un-ebbing flow of things perhaps best left forgotten, she had moved on with her drawing. And now it stood there, in all its glory. A rather apt portrait of the manor house that she had grown up on, only not quite right somehow. Growing up behind the building itself was a hulking black stormcloud, ominously penetrating the mood of the picture. Below it, a blood-red sky that would have sent anyone who lived back in those times running for cover and screeching about the devil returned to earth to claim his own. “This is some place you know?” The soft male voice came from behind her, and as she turned, Lia recognized the slightly stooped but proud form of Toshiro Mifune, headmaster of the school. Reflexively, she bowed in greeting before answering. “Ah” yes? the manor house I grew up on as a child.” “Hm.” Mifune’s eyes were not on her, but rather scanning every detail of the picture as though he could read her thoughts by doing so. And what thoughts exactly had gone into sketching this? abomination? For the first moments since drawing it, Lia looked at it with someone else’s eyes, and realized how truly hideous of a picture it was. Something black seeped out of its very core, oozed onto the floor, and enveloped the room like a shroud. Slowly, all the emotion began to seep through the numbness. Of course, Lia had been saved that day at the town square’saved by a horrific monster, who later revealed himself to be Vaclav Ionescu, vampire patron of the Vlaszy clan in Prague and outlying areas. Laszlo had engineered the rescue somehow, but it was a question she’d never been able to ask him, because she’d never seen him again after that night. Bitterness’why had they gotten her to come back after her first child had died? Why had they brought her back from her husband’s side? What sort of selfish motivation had caused her family to drag her back to Czechia when they knew very well what would happen? And had it all been planned, regardless? The gloom of negativity pervaded ever deeper. She couldn’t even remember the name of that husband that she’d been absolutely head-over-heels for anymore. Time had stolen that from her, and left her with only the painful memories to consider. And on top of that, it was now her task to obsessively pursue any knowledge of Laszlo Vlaszy and what happened to him until she had the answer. She was chasing chimeras. Without realizing that he’d switched his focus, Lia looked up to find Master Mifune’s eyes boring a hole into her, instead of her art. He shook his head. “I know why you call yourself Shadow now?” “Mm?” He pressed a hand to the center of his chest. “There is darkness inside you.” .:: ::: ::. Mifune’s private porch overlooked a lush garden. Lia could see why he would want to spend a lot of time out here’quiet, isolated? a haven from the rush of city life, a way to isolate oneself from the hectic conditions often surrounding life at a wrestling school. Clasped in between her fingers was a long black calligraphy brush. “I will teach you my art now,” Mifune said, gesturing to the thick white sheets of paper sitting on the low table in front of them. Lia crossed her legs and watched as his hand made intricate, precise movements, and slowly, the black ink from his brush began to make a beautiful etching on the stark white of the paper. “Now you try.” Slowly, with laborious concentration on her every motion, Lia mimicked what she had seen him do. When she finally finished, she had the same character? sort of. For the first time in several hours, she smiled’barely. “It’s ah” not very good. I don’t think I’ve adapted to Japanese writing any better than I have to the culture.” “You are too hard on yourself,” Mifune rejoined, “No one expects you to know all the answers in a day, just like you cannot master the art of writing in five minutes. Try it again.” Licking her lips to moisten them, Lia launched into the figure again. Her hand moved laboriously, her eyes slitted in concentration. “Better,” he said, when she was finished, “Only I can tell from looking at your form that you try too hard to force things.” ??What?” Lia switched her eyes from the still-imperfect figure on the page to Master Mifune, confused. “You are like Dawn, in some ways. You possess many attributes that, when they stand alone, would be enough for you to get through whatever adversity you would face” and if you knew how to trust yourself, perhaps you wouldn’t struggle so.” “I?” Master Mifune chuckled quietly. “Yes, Dawn has a similar response when I tell her things she doesn’t want to hear. The both of you are too dependent on forcing yourself to perform at peak, no matter the situation. The truth is, you could perform much better if you would simply close your eyes and let the strength flow from within. Don’t try to react, just react.” Lia paused, quietly turning this over in her mind. As befuddling as it sounded when spoken, somewhere inside her, it rang true. “You know what you have to do, don’t you?” Mifune’s voice stabbed a fang into her consciousness. This anxiety that had been gripping her companions? premonitions of the Elders of whosoever’s particular race coming to sweep them into oblivion? scenting Lilith, listening to Lilith’s lies? She practically upset the table as she leapt to her feet. Yes, she knew what she had to do. There was no sense sitting here in a dojo when she knew damned well that someone, something was waiting out in those woods for whichever one of them it was going to try to take this week. “You mean Lilith?” “No” I don’t know much of your mythology, but this is no Lilith, this is not the pall that was cast over this place when she came to see Dawn.” She swallowed, then confirmed verbally what she could practically scent on the earth around the school. “Something older” stronger.” He nodded. “I have seen much in my few years on this earth” I’ve learned to believe in that which I might not have before considered real. Thought whatever it is is beyond my comprehension, I have no doubt that it is not beyond yours.” “What are you suggesting?” Every muscle in her body was beginning to tense at the thought of a fight. “Face your fears.” “The last time I left to face something on my own, I didn’t come back for several months.” “So take your dogs with you. They are trained well, certainly they will come back to the school alone if something ill befalls you.” She nodded slowly, but the strength began to rise up in her, unhesitating. “Thank you, Mifune Sensei,” she said, with a slight bow. And then a flash of steel came into her eyes, “I’ll be back.” Perhaps many years ago, the eyes of samurai warriors had looked similar, before running into battle with swords drawn’certainly, though, the whole of them had not had half her courage in facing the unknown, for she was already in a realm quite beyond fearing the death of her physical body. One hand gripped the iron balcony railing as she leapt over it and into the garden. The winds swept along at her side. Without hesitation, and as though flying on demon wings, she bolted into the forest, too fast for Master Mifune to follow with his aging eyes. .:: ::: ::.
