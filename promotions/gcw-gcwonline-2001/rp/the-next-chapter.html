Date: Directly after Devil's Night The glasses were lined up in front of him. He’d staunched protested their removal. The ashtray was full to overflowing, he wouldn’t let them empty that out either. The excuse he’d given was that he wanted to feel like he’d accomplished something, the ashes and empty glasses these were the reminders that he’d done something. Slowly degraded his liver, furthered the blackening of his lungs, still something had been accomplished. He watched the lines between the glasses slowly blur before him. The liquor was finally starting to take effect. Well, effect that he could actually notice anyway. It’d taken an entire bottle of whiskey just to get him out of bed this morning. Putting on the bandages had been another six pack. Actually going out in front of a crowd and taking all that abuse, he couldn’t even remember how much it had taken. After the show he’d ended up here, away from the congratulations, away from the cheers of the fans and the music. He couldn’t take it, all those people chanting his name like he was some kind of hero. Yeah, right? Hero’s don’t kill, hero’s don’t need to be three sheets to the wind just to face the world. Of course it’s not like anyone in the crowd knew those things. Still ignorance was no excuse. They shouldn’t be cheering for him, they should be throwing things. Cursing his name, burning him in effigy. That would have at least made everything a little easier. Of course, he couldn’t expect things to be easy for him, he didn’t deserve that anyway. Tilting back the last shot on the table Stevens began motioning for the waitress to return. Between squinted eyes he carefully placed the shot back on the table, and swayed forward. It was everything he could do to merely keep his balance as the room had decided to turn suddenly on it’s side. He was ready for just such an event however, placing his head down on the table, just for a minute until the room righted itself. After a second he felt a sharp pain in his shoulder, struggling he lifted his head from the table and tried to focus on the person in front of him. It was the waitress, at least he thought it was. The blurry form was mostly feminine vaguely human, there wasn’t many other people it could have been. Letting his eyelids droop he caught her with what he imagined was a very “come hither” glance. He wasn’t sure if she was attractive or not, but it didn’t matter. Right now, nothing mattered. It was a question of drinking everything to feel nothing, and being with anything to feel something. Wracking his brain he attempted to say something witty, perhaps a clever little quip laced with innuendo. Instead, he belched, directly in her face, and was barely able to spit out the words. “I’ll have another.” “I think you’ve already had enough, it’s time to go.” Her response really didn’t take him by surprise exactly. He’d been waiting for her to say it. The fact was he’d had more than enough by any reasonable standards, before he’d even stepped foot into the bar. Of course he’d be damned if he was going to let her know that. He could still see, still think, it wasn’t enough. It’d never be enough. The more he worked it over in his brain, her response made him angry. Who was this bitch to tell him when he’d had enough? She didn’t know, couldn’t possibly understand what he’d been through, what he’d done. If his reflexes were sharper he would’ve caught her arm by the wrist. As it was he miss her by about a mile, it was almost as if he were snatching at someone just to her left. Since he saw two of her anyway, that wasn’t completely outside the realm of possibility. The motion slid him off the barstool and onto the floor. “I’ll tell you when I’ve had enough, now bring me another damnit.” He could almost see the motion of her shaking her head at him. Squinting hard he noticed a sad look in her eyes, it reminded him of Sheena. She’d give him that look sometimes when he was doing something too self-destructive even for her. The thought nearly completely sobered him up. Nearly, but not quite, as it was he just started feeling that sick sensation in the pit of his stomach. It wasn’t the liquor, no, he’d felt this everyday, the booze was the only thing that stopped it. He’d lost his appetite for the drink. He just wanted to go home and sleep. That would be nice, a few hours of numbing blackness. If he was lucky he wouldn’t dream, but he wasn’t holding out too much hope for that. Dragging himself back up to his feet he snatched the leather jacket off the barstool that had set next to him. With only moderate effort he was able to slide the jacket on. He used the motions to do what little he could to collect what tattered shards of dignity he had left. Peering out from behind nearly closed eyes he started stumbling backwards towards the door. Even as full of the drink as he was, his motor skills would function remarkably well. The product of years of wrestling drunk he supposed. Taking in the waitress and the bartender behind her, he adjusted the jacket around him, glancing around to the entire bar. “PISS OFF!” Somehow that made him feel a little better. It was a small gesture, and completely meaningless, but still it helped. At least he went out with the appearance of indignation. He wanted the bar to believe he’d been wronged, that he was completely in the right. He wanted them to believe he should have been allowed to stay for the rest of the night, drink himself into oblivion. Turning towards the door he nearly fell out into the street. He’d taken more steps towards it than he’d realized before turning. Stumbling out into the darkness of the night, he imaged how they must be laughing at him inside. The clumsy clown. The thought didn’t lend him much comfort. Usually he wouldn’t have cared what the people thought, but this was just more people laughing, more people seeing him for what he really was. A washed up hack, a nobody, just another weepy sloshed drunk. As he trudged forth down the sidewalk, lost in his own little world of misery, he never noticed the figure slink out of the shadows behind him. It followed at a short distance, just like it had many nights before, like a guardian angel keeping him from harm. ~To Be Continued~
