House of Genesis: Part One

1 October 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 1st, 2007 Pushing every muscle in his body as hard as he can, Scott managed to pull himself off the canvas and looked up into the eyes of Jerrod Darson. “Pathetic.” Scott Drake’s head dropped back down as he relaxed and allowed himself to collapse on the mat. His entire body was exhausted and wanted to just give up on him. Darson walked next to him, looking down, and grunted. “And you want to be something in this business?” Mustering up what strength he had left, Scott replied back “but it’s been eight hours. Haven’t we trained enough?” For a second time, Darson grunted, looking as disgusted as he could. “And I thought you were serious about this.” “I am” Scott protested, “but you are killing me here.” Using the ropes, Scott pulled his body up to finally get into a standing position. Before he even got a bit of breath in him, Darson slammed him in the chest with a big fist. The force dropped Drake like he was a dead weight. Jerrod Darson stood over him, looking down. “Don’t tempt me” he warned before stepping over the top rope and hopping down to the ground. “You can take a break, but I expect to see you in three hours.” Scott Drake rolled over onto his chest, clenching it with both hands. His eyes closed, he wanted nothing more than to melt into the mat and disappear. He wanted to be a professional wrestler; he just never knew it would be this hard.” Rolling out of bed, Scott Drake rubbed his head. One hand moved to his chest, checking to make sure it was still there. “Man,” he said in a low voice, “what a time for that dream.” Scott stretched out, letting out a huge yawn. A crack of thunder stole his attention. Looking outside the window, a dark array of clouds greeted him. Drake snorted, “Going to be another gloomy day.” Scott’s attention shifted away from the window, to the door, as it creaked open. Tip toeing inside, came his fianc’e, Tessa, whose path ended at the end of the bed. She gently plopped down onto the mattress, and looked over at Drake as if she’d never see him again. Scott was well aware that her concern was centered around, and hoped to quickly pull it away from that, by voicing his own concern, “Why are you up so early” “Would you just please reconsider doing this?” Though it wasn’t like Tessa to be so blunt, she didn’t have time to pander around the issue. Grabbing his gym bag, Scott slung it over his shoulder, a clear sign that he wasn’t about to back out now. “I’ll be home in a bit, I promise” he assured her, but that wasn’t the answer she wanted. As expected, he felt his wrist being tugged in the opposite direction of the door, “But why” Scott you’ve got everything you need here. Why blow that for a man who’s done nothing but torture you? You know he’s going to try and pull you away.” Her drooping eyes added even more meaning to her words. Scott just laughed, “Nothing could pull me away from you.” He said as he leaned in and gave her a peak on the forehead. It still didn’t win her over. “It still doesn’t answer why.” That thought was put on the backburner though, due to the high pitched cry in the background, which simply could not be ignored. In an instant, Tessa went from nosy fianc’e to concerned new mother. “I guess I better go handle that though, since you’re on your way out.” She stabbed him with every tinge of guilt she could. Scott seemed to miss it though, smiling as she left and waving to her, “love you”. Tossing his gym bag over his shoulder, getting ready to head out, a growl from Tessa stops him dead in his tracks. Drake looks over his shoulder to her, wondering what the holdup was. "You're not going to help me with her?" She hissed. Dropping his bag, Scott knew there wouldn’t be any chance of avoiding that, not if he wanted to avoid sleeping on the couch tonight (he really needed to get a new, more comfortable couch). Kicking his bag so that it was next to the door, Scott didn’t want any more distractions to hold him up after this. He would help with the baby, grab the bag, and bolt. After all, though he didn’t want to do this anymore than Tessa wanted him to, he still believed his was a man of his word. “What do you need me to do?” Of course she had to state the impossible, "Stay home." Scott looked at her, rolled his eyes, and turned to leave. Picking up the bag, he threw the door open and walked out before she could make another protest. At the cafe where Scott is supposed to meet Darson, it was easy to pick out Jerrod in the crowd. He stuck out like a sore thumb, his massive six foot-nine frame doing little to hide him among the others. He looked like an aged giant out of place in a fairy tale; a grim tale. “You look older” Scott commented. The man once called Genesis just chuckled, “time has passed me, don’t forget that.” The very idea seemed odd to Scott. For whatever reason, he just never thought of him as an old man. In the back of his mind he was still that monstrous man of dark. But now that raven black hair faded to a softer grey (one no doubt he cursed), his skin sagged where it didn’t wrinkle, and he looked so very tired. Even that fire that once resided in his eyes looked tired and ready to extinguish. Taking a chair opposite him, Scott studied the man that used to be his mentor. This look, he decided, did not fit him. Age had not been as kind as it should, something that he probably deserved. Darson extended his large arm across the table, waiting for Scott’s reply. Grasping his hand, Scott gave it a firm handshake. Maybe he did intent to bury the hatchet between the two. “So?” rolled off Scott’s lips; looking for some way to start their conversation off. Jerrod Darson took a long sip from his glass of water, placing it back in front of him. “I found a guy” he told Scott. “Found a guy?” His head turning in each direction, Scott tried to make heads or tails of the comment, “like as in a body?” Laughing again, Darson looked to him, “goodness no.” Scott let out a sigh of relief. “I found a guy I want you to train.” The words floated around in the air for a bit before they finally stuck to Drake. It wasn’t what he had expected to talk about, nor had the thought even crossed his mind. When they finally begun to sink in, really mingle in his brain, Scott started to shake his head. “No” he said, “I’m not training anymore. I tried that once before and it didn’t go over so well.” Jerrod shuffled in his seat a bit, taking a slow drink from his water. “Think about it” came his crisp words, “the kid needs some training. He’s got talent, but it’s too raw. He needs someone who knows the business to get in there and mold it. As you can see, I am too old now. But he reminds me a lot of you. I think you could really help him.” “A lot of like?” Scott scoffed, “I already don’t like him.” Rising up from his chair, Scott looked down at the man who brought him into the business, “look, it was good seeing you again but I’m just not interested in taking in any students.” Making no attempt to stop him, Darson allowed Scott to leave him in silence. His eyes just watched him walk away, a small glisten of that old fire still fanning the flame in the back. Back at his place, Tessa is surprised to see him return so soon. “Well” she asked, waiting a moment for him to fill in the blank for her. When Scott didn’t, Tessa had to finish the question for him, “what did he want?” “Nothing important” came the quick response, “he just wanted me to train someone he found.” The entire thing came off as sounded so routine and normal for them, despite the rarity a person has to say that in their life. For people like Tessa Windsor and Scott Drake, rarities happened far too often. “Well obviously you told him no” Tessa fired back, hoping that if she said it with enough confidence, it would of course have to be true. Scott assured her it was as such, “yes, I told him that I wasn’t interested.” Tessa smiled, pleased with his response. “Though?” the smile faded away, “training someone may not be that bad.” “Oh no,” Tessa said, shaking her finger at him, “you have too much already. You need to focus on Dangerous Games; it is going to be very important for both you and Markus.” Letting his head hang low, “I know” came tumbling out of his mouth in an utmost defeated manner. “But it would be nice to pass along what I know to someone else. I would like to think that even if I left wrestling, I would still be helping the business out in some way.” Tessa allowed a laugh to escape her, “another Chris Storm in the business wouldn’t be helping anyone out.” Grabbing the dish towel in front of him, Scott throws it at her, something Tessa is easily able to bat away as she let out a giggle. “I think I might do it” Scott said out loud, or at least loud enough for Tessa to hear. Her giggle quickly died away and a sour expression was all that was left. “Oh no, don’t tell me you are talking yourself into it.” Scott stood up, pushing the chair back. “Come on now, Tessa.” She let out a large sigh, “you are.” “Just give me a break!” he begged, “I mean think about it for a second. I think maybe I should pass on what I’ve learned to someone else.” No longer looking at her, Scott made his way over to the window where he could look over the New York landscape. “I mean shit; Youngblood and Almasy are both training people. I know I’m twice as talented as both of them combined. Goodness knows if there is going to be another generation Almasy running around, there needs to be another generation of Storm to embarrass him.” Tessa just stared at his back, “so this is what it about” Your pride?” Spinning around, Scott met her eyes. “No, I don’t know. I mean, look, I can’t do this forever. I am going to have to retire one day. And ideally I would like to retire while I can still walk with my children. But if I am going to retire, I want to make sure I have at least trained one person who can carry on for me.” “Man” Tessa spitted out, as if the mere word in her mouth tasted vile and foul. “That is all you think about, your legacies. How people will remember you.” Scott wanted to tell her it was more than just that. Inside him he could feel himself being pulled from all different angles. His ego told him he needed to train someone to prove just how great he really is. His pride told him he needed to help the business by leaving his knowledge and skill behind after he leaves. But deep inside, buried far away, his heart told him he should stay with her. It was a shame he never listened to his heart. “I’m going to retire in the next few years. If I train someone to take my place, I think I can be more comfort in my retirement; it would give me less reason to return to the ring.” His statement sounded more like a plea for permission that a direct statement of his intent. It was odd, because regardless of how it sounded, it might as well have been fact. Approaching her and giving her a kiss on her forehead, Scott told her he would “give her some time to think about it” and left her alone in the kitchen. Dropping down in a chair, Tessa sighed to herself and started to stare out the window. “I hope you do retire” she said to herself, “but just know that I don’t want to get my hopes up. Not again.” Closing her eyes, she allowed the sound of the distant thunder to wrap around her like a blanket. For a moment she wished she was like the thunder, somewhere far away.
