Is This The Beginning of the End Or Is This The End of a New Beginning? (Part 2)

27 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/27/10 Day After Rampage June 2009 8:12pm “Why do I get myself involved in these things?” Flopping down onto the king sized mattress back at her shoreline home overlooking the beautiful beaches of Chatham, Massachusetts, Tessa Windsor let out a heavy sigh. Staring blankly up at the ceiling offered little assistance to her mind this time. Usually when the petite princess locked herself up in the confines of her bedroom, inspiration showed down upon her after realizing where she’d gone wrong. This time that well had long since dried up. At this point even the likes of B.F. Skinner and Ivan Pavlov would find it damn near impossible to dig up an explanation as to why Tessa Windsor was a glutton for punishment. Her resiliency never merited any form of reward and nine times out of ten the end result was always the same, Tessa getting left high and dry. At times like this, Tessa found refuge in the radio she had perched beside her alarm on the cherry oak nightstand beside her bed. Without second thought she rolled over towards the nightstand only to be caught mid roll by both her pajama clad sons who came charging through the bedroom door without a knock. “Mom mom! Guess what?” “What’s up guys?” She feigned a mom-like smile, regardless of what news they could have dropped on her next. In all likelihood she wouldn’t be ready for it regardless. But with four kids, expecting anything more than an hour’s worth of alone time during the day was the equivalent of expecting peace of earth; regardless of the tremendous effort put forth it was simply not meant to be. Simultaneously jumping onto the end of the bed, both boys excitedly proclaimed, “Uncle Markus is here!” Before Tessa could restore or even establish any kind of order, two pairs of sock clad feet used her mattress as a springboard for the escapades they expected to get into as soon as their surrogate uncle settled in. It was difficult for Tessa to get a word in edgewise with either one of her sons considering anytime one landed the other hopped upwards, both completely convinced that their beloved uncle Markus had come to visit solely to hang out with them. Of course it was Tessa who had to break the new otherwise. The highly proclaimed and dearly loved duo of Banned & Exiled~! spent a good portion of their time outside the ring taunting Tessa about being a party pooper. It got to the point where both Chris and Evan, were whole heartedly convinced anytime their denied their wishes it solely cause Tessa had no clue how to have fun. This time was no different than any other. “Not tonight kiddo’s. It’s eight thirty, time for both of you to start heading off to bed.” Immediately both boys ceased their bouncing in exchange for wonderful trick their father had unintentionally bestowed upon them, the puppy dog eyes. “But mommm!” That time it failed. Refusing to look their way Tessa headed straight for the hallway with little regard to the heavily exaggerated sighs both boys let out. As much as she loved them, caving in then and there would have done zero good for anyone, especially given the reason Markus was there. “It’s still a no boys.” “Please mom, pleaaaasssse?” Even with the boys keeping on her heels all the way to the kitchen table, Tessa refused to go back on her word. Times like this had her fully convinced that the hardest part of being a mom was not the sleepless nights, the temper tantrums, or having to juggle everything at once. The hardest part of being a mom was sticking by a two lettered word that every kid hated to hear. “I think you guys have had enough excitement for the week.” Exciting was certainly an understatement for the lifestyle the Windsor children led. The more appropriate terms could have been adventurous or even privileged according some. They were certainly well traveled given that anytime Tessa was able to take them on the road with them, she would. And if they were not able due to school or other their own obligations, there was always something lined up for them to make up for it as soon as Tessa got back. Spoiled much? Maybe they were. But given everything they had been through in their lives, Tessa chalked it up to them deserving it. If they could not have a steady family life with any kind of a father like figure, they at least deserved anything else Tessa could provide for them. “But we didn’t get to do anything!” The elder of the two, Evan, huffed while dragging along behind Tessa. He had always been the most vocal of all the kids when it came to expressing what he felt was right and fair. “Yeah mom, we didn’t!” Echoing his brother’s sentiments, seven year old Chris tagged along beside his elder brother as if Evan was his best friend in the world, “Remember you promised that we could go to Central Park this morning? You forgot to bring us.” While neither boy biologically belonged to Tessa, she had somehow managed to conquer genetics to form a tight knit bond with both boys and assume the role of mother in their lives. And while their plenty of precious moments that came along with that, there were also moments like these where it would have been helpful to have someone else around. How she ever managed moments like this when she first started out with all of them, she never knew. By now however she had learned to keep all her answers locked and loaded at the tip of her tongue. “Take it up with your father.” This time it was his fault after all. But what kind of mother would tell her kids tell her kids that she went to Central Park alone to help their rarely seen father search his drunken best friend, also known as their dear Uncle Markus. Lying to them really sucked, but the truth would hurt all the worse. Their naivety to it all was the one thing Tessa hoped they would never lose. “But.” “Yeah mommy, but!” A far deeper voice heckled from the kitchen which only riled the kids up more. Automatically both boys disregarded their mother’s presence and went to plead their case to the man they knew would at least listen before making a call. While hearing Markus was music to their ears, it made Tessa completely uneasy after everything that had happened that morning. Finding the man you consider a brother passed out in Central Park after he had gone missing without a trace will kind of do that to you. Regardless of all that Tessa put on her brave face and pretended like none of that had ever happened. It seemed convincing enough to the kids. So much so that Evan carried his protest into the kitchen and demanded, “Uncle Markus, make her change her mind! Please?” For once in his life Markus actually chose the reasonable side, if only because he too was not quite up for an evening of utter chaos. “No can do little dudes. You heard what she said.” Well versed in guilt trips from the little time spent with his adoptive father, Evan sulked back, “You’re no fun when dad’s not around.” “Evan!” Unintentionally shrieking, Tessa hoped to at least get an apology out of her eldest... ... … …it never came. Instead Evan proved to Tessa once again that even the slightest bit of influence their father, Chris Storm, had on them was completely overwhelming. Yet here she was trying her hardest to be a good parent and anything and everything she ever tried to drill into their heads bounced right off it like it was rubber hitting concrete. It drove Tessa absolutely insane. Her fingertips instinctively buried themselves between thick strands of brunette hair and her scalp. As experienced as she should have been right now, she still had absolutely no clue how to teach them any sort of manners; at least while they still had their father occasionally popping in. Firmly planting his hand on her shoulder, Markus reassured with a light shake, “It’s fine. They’re kids Tess. Chris’ kids at that.” “Exactly the problem,” Tessa groaned. Pushing the boys carelessly discarded shoes aside from the middle of the kitchen floor with her bare feet, she led the way to the living room, “they’ll never learn until it’s too late.” Unexpectedly to Tessa, Markus chimed in over her shoulder. “They take after you then too.” Tessa blinked twice, completely unaware where Markus was going with that. Chances were he was about to rib her. She readied herself for battle a wits before answering back, “I don’t get what you’re saying.” “They’re stubborn.” Sarcastically Tessa heckled back, “You’re just realizing this now?” Then quickly dove in to save her own face, “And I’m far from stubborn. If anything I cave in too easy. You of all people should know that.” “You’re stubbornly kind.” “Don’t remind me.” Tessa sighed while sinking herself into the loveseat closest to the wall. Instead of mocking her as he normally would, Markus cut straight to business. Planting himself down in the adjacent recliner he looked to Tessa as straight faced as a man of his demeanor could possibly muster. “I’d love not to, but I imagine that’s why you wanted me here.” Tessa gripped down on her bottom lip, recalling her words earlier that morning. Looking back on it she realized how desperate she likely sounded. “ We’ll talk later Markus. Please call me?” Apathetically Markus had responded back, “If that’s what you want.” “Yeah…guess that’s what I want.” Trying to convince herself of the same now seemed almost useless. After stumbling upon Stone’s half conscious self this morning, Tessa felt far more like she was in the room with a stranger than with one of her closest friends of the last seven years. “Yeah…” She anxiously started up, “I suppose that‘s why.” Markus instantly picked up on her uneasiness. It made things all the more awkward given he had it figured that it had everything to do with this morning. He tried his best to brush it off and switch the focus back to her, “Well? What’s wrong?” Being stern was far from Tessa’s strong suit. Given her gentle nature and petite frame, most damn near impossible for to take her seriously. Adding Markus, a known jokester, to that equation made it all the more less likely for her inquiry to be taken seriously even within her own mind. Yet she swallowed her pride and pushed forth; praying for the best, expecting the worst. “How bout you tell me first?” Tessa’s bright blue eyes dropped towards the ground, her fists anxiously clenched. Any outsider would have sworn she was the one being put on trial. “About last night?” Playing stupid in the ring weekly with his best friend Chris Storm had managed to land Markus Stone a dream job with a six digit salary and multitude of adoring fans. The only thing he earned playing stupid in the middle of Tessa Windsor’s living room at eight thirty on a Monday evening was a dirty look. “You know what I’m talking about.” Knowing did not make Markus Stone any more likely to utter a single peep about any of his tribulations. Instead the two sat in silence for what felt like an eternity before Tessa backed up ten steps and attempted to break down the brick wall between them again. “I don’t need to tell Chris, you know.” Though hesitant her offer was genuine enough to elicit a small nod from Markus. After everything he had done for her in the past between the business and even her own family, Tessa felt as if she at least owed him at least an open ear. “I can keep it between us.” Markus waved it off. “I partied just a little too hard. That’s all.” Tessa’s brow perked up upon hearing this. It would have been a highly viable excuse if had Tessa not needed to resuscitate him and Chris countless mornings following any reason they could find to have a party. After seven years on the road with him and Chris, Tessa was widely aware of the difference between Markus merely drinking to have fun and Markus drinking himself into seclusion. Yet Markus Stone who drank himself into seclusion was eternally in denial about the whole thing, regardless of his many short lived rehab stints. He seemed almost insulted when Tessa’s brow perked up in question. “You went out by yourself?” “What do you suppose I do when Chris is not around? Mope?” “I didn’t mean that!” Carelessly Stone shrugged her protest off and propped his feet up on the leather ottoman in front of him. Tessa had failed to rally any sort of emotional rally within him. He had heard it all before from her when it came to his behavior. If anything he and Chris chalked any and all of her concerns to her motherly nature. Anytime Tessa’s concern kicked in a stern thirty minute lecture was sure to follow. First she would go on her mini tirade about how immature he and Chris were. Then after rolling her eyes when she failed to win the argument she would huff and puff about how they never take her seriously. And of course there would be the mandatory emotional breakdown when she came to the conclusion that she could not change a thing. It was simply how things went with Tessa. …unless Markus Stone cut her off at the path. “Regardless, it’s not worth you worrying about Tess.” Unknowing, Markus lead himself straight into a loophole. “And you actually showed up here because?” As always Markus Stone was twenty steps ahead of Tessa. Ever so eloquently he suggested with a slight tilt of his head, “To assure it’s not worth worrying about. After all, you’ve got bigger fish to fry, like say, the world champ himself, Andy Murray.” “You’re changing the subject.” “And so are you.” Dead silence hovered between the two. Tessa’s eyes fell to the floor. She hated this conversation more than anything. Quite possibly more than Chris and Markus hated her rambling on about responsibility. As much as she pushed them, she refused to push herself just as hard. Call it fear of failure, or more or less the fear of disappointing everyone that ever believed in her. Regardless of her million of meaningless excuses the tandem of Banned & Exiled failed to let up on her. Both knew she was capable; it was getting her to accept it that would be the real prize. “Well…” Markus directed his glance towards the digital clock perched on the end table across from him. 9:05 It was too early for him to leave without making things anymore awkward than they were, too late for him to simply walk away without another word. Instead he readjusted his feet on the ottoman and tried again for any kind of response. “Not so easy is it?” It had at least gotten her to lift her head up some. “Huh?” “Dealing with the pressure of being someone.” She had never looked at it that way before. Then again she had never seen herself as somebody special; rather somebody’s fling or closest friend. Chris Storm, Seymour Almasy, they were somebody’s. Somebody’s that shattered records, somebody’s that conquered almost every mountain they climbed. Somebody’s that Tessa Windsor managed to get romantically involved with somehow. Then there was also Vivica Valentine, Dawn McFarlane, and Karina Wolfenden. They too were somebody’s. Somebody’s that had managed to revolutionized the business. Somebody’s that most considered great role models for women around the world. All somebody’s that Tessa Windsor had shared close friendships with. And of course there was Lestatt Knight, undoubtedly the biggest name in GCW history. Sure most loathed him, but it would be hard pressed to find anyone who did not respect him. Somebody was an understatement for him. He was godlike in status; godlike enough to make or break someone in the ring. Tessa had no clue what to consider him. Friend, mentor, colleague, love interest, or maybe even all of the above. Lestatt Knight was always impossible for Tessa to read. Perhaps that what made him into such a big somebody. Tessa was quite the contrary of that. Despite twisting every last muscles in her lips as upward as she could during those odd moments of silence between her and Markus her frown still won out. Disappointment was always impossible to conceal. Tessa blinked twice. It always seemed to work well when it came to shifting the attention back to the opposite party. “Are you asking or telling me?” Markus Stone was like no other though. He only shrugged. “Shit if I know.” “Well what would your answer be to that?” “Fuck.” “I’ve got kids you know…” “Not anything they haven’t heard before. Trust me.” “So what’s your real answer then? How do you deal with being somebody?” Times like this were where Markus found a bottle of Jack handy. Instead, empty handed Markus paused, leaving Tessa to jump to the forgone conclusion, “You drink it away?” “Do I dare say yes and chance a fucking lecture?” Surprisingly, she waved him off this time, “I’m not going to waste my breath Markus.” “Yet you wanted to talk. See your dilemma there?” While Markus Stone may not have been the most intellectual person Tessa had ever met, he certainly had her thought process figured down to a tee. Sometimes she swore he knew what she was thinking before she ever thought it. This time she had no choice but to admit, “I got ahead of myself.” “At least you admit it.” She attempted to dodge another one of his hidden bullets, “You still haven’t given me an answer to that somebody bit.” “You answered it for me.” “Cheater.” “How about you answer it then Tessie dearest.” Plucking one of the kid’s notebooks off the end table beside him, Markus mockingly jumped into the role of psychologist.”Tell me how you deal with being a somebody Ms. Windsor.” Standing from her seat simply to pluck the notebook out of his hand, Tessa failed to respond back with the same amount of playfulness, “I don’t.” “So you cease to exist?” “Exactly.” “Don’t you say it’s time to you changed that?” “I think it’s time for you to head home. You already know why we needed to talk anyways. Go home on work on it.” “Sure thing.” Rising to his full height, Markus stopped halfway through his trek to the door, “How about you start working on being a somebody? I’d hate to associate with a nobody.” That one touched a nerve. Instinctively Tessa latched back, “Tell you what, next time you do anything significant on your own you can teach me all about how to be a somebody.” Markus knew better than to let it get to him. “Already working on that. Don’t let me beat you there though.” Tessa bit down on her tongue. There was plenty of bitterness and resentment waiting make its way out. Stone’s kinship and respect were far more valuable that anything she had to say. “I won’t.” “Good, and one more thing.” “What’s that?” “Keep yourself guarded; it’s the number one rule of being a somebody.” “Got it. Thankfully I’ve got you to watch my back.” Markus grinned, pulling the front door along with him as he headed for his car. “Of course Tessa, of course. Careful though, alright?” “Alright.” “I’m testing you on this later.” She half halfheartedly smile, waving him out the door with a girlish giggle, “I’ll ace it. Mark my words.” Mark it he did. Plain and simple.
