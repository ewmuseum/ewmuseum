Date: when the prodigal son came home “We missed you, CJ.” The kid reached out amiably to the superstar before him, brow glistening with the sweat produced by the bright shining of the arena lighting. The return had been successful. Step one: Take names. Step two: Kick ass. Check and check. CJ ran the length of his forearm across his brow before taking the offered autograph book, implanting his John Hancock on the opened page. “I know, kid. I missed you, too.”--- ç --- “I am in blood Stepp'd in so far that, should I wade no more, Returning were as tedious as go o'er” Billy Shakespeare --- ç --- It’d been such a long time since I’d stepped inside the ring. A few months. Maybe more. Being sidelined meshed all the days together and it was so hard to see the forest for the trees. They cut my shoulder open like a peach and fiddled with the intricasies of the joints. Something hadn’t felt right in sometime and I need a little tune up. Everybody needs a service every now and again. Hope the again is farther away than expected. I’ve got a career to resurrect. Stepping out in front of the GCW fans was like a drug that I didn’t realise how badly I’d missed. It left a feeling of cotton mouth. A dryness. Emptiness. A hunger that seemed to never fill. This great big empty whole. So much had gone wrong it was amazing to feel something go right. Wrestling gave me such a buzz. Or was that the drugs?--- ç --- My head felt heavy in my hands. Like a giant lead-weight sitting in my palms. My eyes felt like they were swimming, I could feel the dryness and the moisture extracting itself into the atmosphere. It was surreal in the way the weight of my head fought against my muscles. I knew I were stronger than my mind was alluding to but unable to fight the sensation of dropping my own head into my lap. My muscles were calming and loosening all over and consciously, I tightened my grip on the bong. Spilling water into the couch again was out of the question. I couldn’t be fucked cleaning the fluid again. If it happened again I swore I’d buy another couch. Lean forward and place the bong on the coffee table, amidst the Cheetoh wrappers and grape Gatorade bottles. Empty bottles. I hated the cottonmouth that came with weed. I hated the empty hunger that echoed in my belly that came along with smoking weed. But I liked the cloudy feeling. It helped me hide from the troubles that fed on my fears and inhibitions. So much had gone wrong with my life I was clinging to anything that made me feel good. The weed made me feel good. Rose from the couch and waded through the cloud of unbalanced steps caused by the inertia of the last hit and faded depth perception. Wading through the fast food wrappers toward the kitchen, I tip-toed where I felt there might have been food remaining in them. Open the fridge and twist the cap off another grape Gatorade. Nothing quenched like that shit did. Nothing. On top of the fridge was the bottle of Vicodin they’d prescribed me so long ago. Not the same bottle. Same kind just a different means of appropriation. Unscrew the lid and spill a couple in the palm of my hand, tossing them down my throat and wash them away with the grape goodness. Confidence arose in me as I shut the fridge door, still swigging greedily from the Gatorade bottle. My cell began to rang and I unflipped it, looking at the caller ID before answering. It was Raoul. “Raoul… sup companiero? You the bringer of good news I hope.” Raoul tells me the details of where he can get the next batch of Vicodin. I liked to stay on top of things. If there was anything I needed, Raoul could get it. The prescriptions were the easiest thing. That was my safety net should I get piss-tested. If I were made to piss in a cup without that documentation I could kiss my career good-bye. At least, for 30-days anyways as the GCW Wellness program stated. I’d only just got clearance from their medical team to compete again. I wasn’t letting something as trivial as piss side-line me. “Aight. That’s what I’m talkin’ ‘bout, Raoul. That’s why you the man and I’m not. Or somethin’ like that. Drop off the papers. Drop the pills. We can toke a little jay or somethin’ when you get here. I just need some of them Cheetohs, too. Maybe a Babe Ruth. Bring it and the rewards shall be bodacious in the greenbacks and the greenery my friend. BO-DAY-SHUS!” Flip shut and pocket the phone. Swig some more of the graped goodness. Run my hand through my hair and stare around the room, looking for answers to the place I’d gotten to in my life. I could feel the clouds I were standing on slowly giving way to my weight. Could feel the fluffiness dissipating into the atmosphere and everytime I felt it giving way I took something out to thicken the comfort it brought. Booze. Weed. Vicodin. It all helped the comfort. It all filled my hole. Ambling into my bedroom I fumble at the light, stopping before my dresser and pressing my hands flat to its top. Letting my head drop and my eyes close for just a moment to gather my thoughts. Raise my eyes to look myself in the eye. I see a stranger. Someone else staring back at me that has weathered due to the experience of life. In the corner of my eye the picture of my children stare back at me. Snatching up the frame in my right hand, I run the index of my left over both their faces gently. “Where are you?” I whisper to them. “Why aren’t you here with me?” Raise the frame to meet me and gently kiss both of their faces before resting the frame back on the dresser. My knees wobble, the muscles give way and I fall to the ground. My hands race to my face in an effort to thwart the tears. The effort is futile. I cannot help but feel the sorrow coursing through my body, chewing at my insides. My heart feels like it is falling into a deep, dark hole. This happens everyday. I try to fill my hole. My void. But only my children can heal me.--- ç --- (love)love is a verb Love is a doing word Feathers on my breath Gentle impulsion Shakes me makes me lighter Feathers on my breath Teardrop on the fire Feathers on my breath” Teardrop Massive Attack --- ç --- Woods took the autograph book off the child’s friend.
