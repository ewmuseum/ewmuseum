The Best Day of Your Life

14 April 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/14/11 Pure happiness is a fleeting sensation which is preserved in death like a mosquito in amber or is eroded day by day like cliffs licked into the ocean by the crashing waves of life. One / Now As Jack draped your jacket over your shoulders, you toppled from the torturous heels you’d foolishly perched atop. Your heart raced as if defibrillated by his touch and a bloodstream polluted by Shiraz blushed your skin. Devoid of human contact for so long, you latched on the most innocent of gestures and wrapped your gamine frame around him. His lips evaded yours in a poorly choreographed chase scene. “You’re drunk,” he lectured, words drenched in disappointment. It had been ten years since you’d seen Jack Carpenter. Ten years of snowballing regret. So you’d hidden behind the wine bottles, only your emotions were slower to uncork. “I love you,” you slurred. “No you don’t.” Two / Then The raw heat from the swollen midday sun fried the sunscreen which Jack had plastered over your lightly freckled skin. Your shuttered eyelids glowed luminous peach as beads of concentrated salty sweat boiled up through your pores and trickled down into your eye sockets. Just as the fiery heat began to prickle your skin, the glow behind your eyelids was extinguished by a shadow cast across your glistening face. Dry lips kissed your beaded forehead, salt crystals almost cementing them to your baked skin. “I love you,” he whispered, softly enough that the wind would tear his words apart before they reached any ears but yours. The bead of sweat which hung from the tip of his nose ran down the bridge of yours as they touched. He took your hand, a dusting of sun-bleached sand grinding between your palms. “No you don’t,” you told him as overhead a chorus of heckling seabirds voiced their disapproval to your coyness. “I always have,” he said, ignoring your protestations. Trembling lips gave yours a tender, tentative kiss. “I love… that little smile you try to hide behind your hair as you turn your head away.” His fingers softy ran through your straw-blonde hair, drawing the curtain back to leave your plain face naked to him. You tried to turn your head away but a magnetism overpowered your bashfulness. “I love how you close your eyes before you turn to look at me.” He shaded your eyes with his hand, tempting them open with the promise of what awaited. “Then open them… like it’s just for me.” “You don’t love me,” you urged, words weakening as your world opened up on his dark treacle eyes. “You’re not as dumb as you are cute.” The softest of touches across your lips sealed them. “I love that when I go get you some more sunscreen you’ll watch me all the way,” he continued, his honest advances refusing to be parried away, “then pretend not to when I look back.” He held his lips just an inch from yours and drew his finger away. “You don’t love me,” you echoed as your popping lips where attracted to his. As the surf frothed just a few feet away, you closed your eyes. You felt the molecules of air fizz in the most microscopic of gaps between your lips and his. “I’ll be right back,” he delivered with the most mischievous of grins which you could picture, vividly, even behind the shutters of your eyelids. “I love you.” As your lips gave fruitless chase, he hopped up, letting the full force of the sun scorch you into the sand. Just as he said they would, lids crept open and your eyes peered out through your fluorescent lashes at his back. “I love you,” betrayed your blistering lips as the sun kissed them in his place. Three / Then Just as Jack was about to disappear from sight, he turned to look back at you, but was a second too slow. Through the sand your finger dragged, etching a message to him. this has been the best day of my life Your finger hovered for a few seconds, then added two more words before you stood. thank you Four / Then The sun kissed, glistening horizon hypnotised you and you stepped into the ocean. As the surf lapped at your dry, sand-baked feet you could feel the frothy water revitalise your skin. The seductive whispers of the Atlantic breeze whistling off of the choppy waters were a Siren’s song which beckoned you out to them. They told you to run away with them; away from hurt you‘d only end up causing him. You felt your fair skin fizzle as you dove through the waves. The currents reacted to your threat, strengthening against the girl who dared to defy them. Your youthful stubbornness rose to their challenge, crashing and clawing through the waves. Your arms began to tire, lactic acid searing your muscles. Each heaving breath doused burning lungs with salty sea-spray. The vast ocean pushed back, piling on top of you. You’d ran as far as you could. Your legs were crippled by cemented shoes of cramped muscle. “Fuck it,” was forced out through grinding teeth. Instinct roared at you to fight, to tear knotted muscle apart, fibre by fibre to crawl back to land. When that failed, instinct dared you. Instinct goaded and insulted you. It pleaded with you. No, you answered in defiance. The world slowed around you, calm, refreshing breaths sedating your racing heart. In the ocean’s depths were a peaceful end to the best day of your life, a day shared with the one you’d always love. The depths suffocated your selfishness. Back on land were a thousand ways you’d end up hurting him. “Bye, Jack,” you gasped as your deadweight began to sink. “I love you.” Five / Then As you drifted beneath the surface, the undertow took your hand and guided you down; but there was a rival for its affections. Jack’s hand clamped around your wrist, dragging you from the depths. One man waged war against the colossal currents of the Atlantic; one man with more to fight for than the tides. Six / Now “No, Kari.” Jack’s words shed their soft coating and hit your ears harshly. “No, alright?” He peeled you off of him, conscious that curious eyes were beginning their natural gravitation towards drama. Perilously perched on heels, with a dress hanging off of you which left you shivering in the sea breeze and make-up which could best be described as amateurish, you’d played dress-up as the woman you thought he’d want to share dinner with. He’d spent the evening trying to look through you, not at you. “Why?” you barked back, squirming within his stern grasp on your shoulders. “You said you loved me.” “Don‘t,” he replied. There was a wince which could barely been disguised, as if you’d simultaneously plunged an icy dagger into his heart and sent a fiery, furious knee into his groin. “Don’t.” “You told me you loved me, Jack,” you pressed, words dribbling unchecked off of your alcohol-lubricated tongue. “Ten years ago; the last time I was here. You told me you loved me.” “And you told me that I didn’t.” His words were encased in cold, stony armour. “Then why did you save me?” you asked, the words raw. It was an plea as must as a query. “What?” rebounded. “You’re seriously asking wh-” “You dragged me out of the ocean, Jack.” Slurred words bubbled up to the surface. “You saved my life and now… and now I’m here. I’m alive because of you… so you have to take care of me. That’s how it works.” “That’s not how it works, Kari,” he repelled. “We’re grown-ups.” “You think I‘d never swam out that far before?” Ten years ago he had been at the vanguard of a cavalry charge into the Atlantic. As his fellow would-be-rescuers were repelled by the waves, he had torn through them and parted the waters to drag you to shore. The next day you were gone. His paltry reward for saving your life was to lose you from his. He’d never questioned his actions. Never regretted them. “Did you even wonder why I was out there?” As tears trickled over your cheekbones, your sketchily-applied mascara traced dirty raindrops down your face. Your words had been unsheathed, their cerated edges purposefully hacking into him. “When I came to on the beach I thought I was in heaven, Jack. I’d accepted that. That was the ticket I bought when I swam out there. I left you that message in the sand as a goodbye.” You were only telling him what he knew deep down; but it stung none the less. “You’re drunk,” he tried to dull the sharpness of your words by retorting. “I’m alive,” you bit back. “Yeah, I’m drunk, too, and I’m broke, I’m pretty severely fucked up in many, many ways. I have no friends left, I’ve got no family who want to admit I exist. I don’t know how to be happy anymore. But I’m standing in front of the one person left in this world who I ever really loved… and… and right now, I’d just take you not hating me.” “There’s a pretty big distance between loving and not hating.” “In your world, maybe.” His grip loosened on your shoulders as you suddenly felt delicate in his hands. “Look, I know I’m drunk,” spilled from your gloss-smeared lips, “and I know that tonight has been another massive fuck-up, but… Jack… I need you to save me again.”
