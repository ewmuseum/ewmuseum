Arizona Skies Pt. 2 (Back Alley Deals)

29 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Tuesday, April 29th 2009 Sitting prompted up against a side wall of the local drugstore, where sun was not welcomed, a small thud echoed through the alley from one end to the other. A splash and the thud continued on as if it had never stopped. Looking up to the sun, it was still too early for the pharmacist to be dumping the trash. Thud Thud Thud It was quite annoying, to be waiting and suddenly disrupted by someone. Reaching to the left, grasping a tin can, I extended my arm out and tilted my head. “Spare some change?” That was the first time I had ever met Scott Drake. He was in mid step when he crossed my path. His head stared straight ahead down the alley but the wrinkle in his brow acknowledged my presence. Most people did one of two things when confronted by a reality they didn’t like; they either hurried past or they toss what change they had and hurried past. Either way, they hurried past as if they actually had somewhere important to be. Not Scott though, that wasn’t his style. I don’t think it was ever in his nature to hurry past anything. He took his time, he looked at things, and he made impulsive decisions like they were well-thought out. Feeling that my change cup reached out too far, he kicked it with his foot and sent all the contents flying through the alley and shattered about in disarray. He hadn’t even bothered to stop to see my reaction. He just kept walking through the alley as casual as if he had stepped on a lowly bug. I leapt to my feet, instantly wishing I hadn’t. Pain shot through my knee and it reminded me that I hadn’t yet had the privilege to raid the pharmacist? dumpster of its trash. Sucking it up as best I could, I stood up straight like a mighty oak in the forest, and barreled toward him. Scott kept forward even as my shadow overtook him and cut off what little light had dared to enter the alley with us. Only when he felt the heat from my body does he turn around to confront me. The look he had on his face grated against my skin so violently, I wanted to punch him then and there. He knew what he had done and almost seemed glad he had did it. “What?” he asked, as if he had just casually strolled on by minding his own business. Failing for words, all I could do was point in the direction my tin cup had flown to and expect him to translate the thoughts rushing recklessly around in my head. He turned his head, looked for a second, and then came back to me with a smile. “Oh” he said, “that?” He took a second glance at the now empty cup to make sure he had not missed anything. “You are better without that, made you look pathetic.” There was only so many insults a man, even one of my stature, could take before he was dragged to aggressive action. Taking a swing at him, my punch went wild when he easily ducked under it. Cutting behind me, he passed on his chance for an open shot, and instead decided to punch me with his tongue rather than his fist. “Shit man!” he exclaimed as he grabbed his nose, “you smell like fuckin” piss. I would think that if you had the entire outdoors as a bathroom, you could at least avoid pissing on yourself.” I turned to face him, heaving my body to its full size. Like a bear trying to intimidate others, I lifted myself up as straight as my knee would allow, showing off as much of my three hundred pound frame as I could. Scott stepped back to get a better look at me, not having a look of fear, but rather of contemplation. Just when he looked stoic and passive, he burst forward and shoved me hard. I almost stumbled back and fell onto my ass but I braced myself with my good leg and kept my chest puffed out. Feeling threatened, I took another swing at him, again missing by close to a mile. Stepping back, Scott looks me over as if he is milling over something in his head. “That the best you can do” Punch air?” He taunted, driving me to the point of frenzy. My body had left my control, switching to automatic, and tried as best as it could to crush him into the grim covering the concrete. The entire situation seemed surreal to me as I thought about it. Never in my life had I been driven to such action as this with such little prompting. It was as if each word was some ancient and mystical spell designed to evoke any action it pleased on the victim. Even as my arms swung wildly and I realized the futileness of my efforts, I still could not draw myself away from the course. Soon the pain shooting through my knee became nothing more than a blur in the afterthought. All consciousness was devoted to the sole goal of slamming my gorilla-like fist into this thick skull. Then it ended. With one well placed punch, he caught me off guard in my fury of misled swings and knocked me up against the brick wall. My chest clinched up and my arms fell to the side defeated. When the surge of the wild spirit escaped from my limps the sharp realization of my knee came quickly to follow. I couldn’t dare bring myself to show my weakness in front of a threat like this but each pound upon my knee felt like a wet sack of sand piling up further and further until the joint buckled and collapsed. My hand shot out at catch the wall but he posted it up from the elbow and watched as I crashed down on my face. Wet and cold slug splashed against my skin, sending shivers down every fiber in my body, yet the cool, damp feeling felt comforting in contrast to the red-hot burn that singed my knee. Eyes staring blankly ahead, I saw the side of his shoe step beside my head. He extended his hand down toward my face, telling me to “get up.” Grabbing hold of his hand I could feel reach muscle tighten as he hoisted me up from the group and back to my feet. He stood back and followed me to lean against the wall to keep any unneeded weight from my knee, an act I was desperately grateful for. If I had to exert any further punishment to my knee, I was certain it would have turned to dust. There was that awkward moment of silence, the one not unlike that silence after a torrent affair when the woman awakes to realized she had just been used for nothing more than gratification. I wanted to say something, anything, to break the silence but I had neither the words nor the thoughts to speak. “So what’s your name?” “Arizona.” His brow wrinkled again as he processed my response. The look he gave was mixed in annoyance and hesitation just as much was it was with anger. “I didn’t ask where you were born, I wanted your name. You stupid or something?” “Name’s Arizona” I replied back, trying my best in a meaningless gesture to again stick out my chest as if it would somehow convince him of my answer. He thought about it for a second and nodded, “don’t want to give me your real name?” “Don’t feel like you need it.” He laughed and snorted, “I didn’t care for it. Just asking to get it out of the way.” Slowly I could feel his eyes traveling down toward my injured knee. His eyes lingered for a moment but returned back up to face me. “Knee doesn’t look to be so good” he said matter-of-factly. I slapped it, causing a sharp and bitter pain to shoot through it, and laughed. “Its fine enough” I bluffed. He didn’t seem to buy. “You did all that off a busted knee?” He let the question linger in the air as if he actually expected a response. “You willing to work” I won’t pay shit, but it’ll be pay all the same.” Looking to the left and right casually, he turned his attention away from the gloom and murk surroundings, “certainly better than what you got now.” Then entirety of it had me at a lost. My mind drew a blank, matching the one etched out across my face. “What kind of work?” was all I could manage to spit out. “Simple work” he told me, “real simple.” “Legal?” “Hell yeah it’s legal. What kind of guy do you take me for?” “What kind should I take you for?” “A prick and an asshole, but one willing to pay you decent enough.” He grinned, looking satisfied with himself as he slid his hands into his pockets. “It’s real simple. I got some people who don’t have such a high opinion of me, hell they would prefer if I woke up in the morning and rolled over dead. Normally I don’t give two shits about that, and I don’t, but the problem is that there are more of them than there is me.” I looked him over from his slicked back blond hair to his expensive shoes that were a stark difference from the crap he stood in. He certainly seemed like he had that natural charm that repulsed people. If oozed from his skin and whiffed into the air like a soiled current blowing in the wind. “What do you want me to do?” “Nothing bad, I just need a body that can absorb some blows from them in case they try anything stupid. You take some hits and I get a quick escape. Sound simple enough for you?” I mulled the idea around in my head, “so basically I’m a punching bag for them so you can run away?” “I hate the term run away” he hissed, “I like to think I’m just staying one step ahead of them. Now you in, or should I just keep walking?” I didn’t need much thought for my next question, “how much money we talking?” “Three hundred a show. So you’re looking at over a thousand a month just to take a few bumps.” He allowed those figures to settle, watching to see what my reaction would be. “Simple right?” A thousand dollars was a lot of money for a man who didn’t make piss in a tin cup. Then the thought crept into my head. Even three hundred dollars could buy a lot of painkillers. I might be able to get enough to actually go a day without the pain. How fucking great would that be? It was good enough to make me agree to anything. “I’m in.” He smiled and pulled a cell phone out of his pocket. “Excellent, I’ll make get everything set.” He tossed the phone at me, which I caught as it bounced off my chest. “Hold onto that and once everything is cool I’ll call you with the when and where.” I nodded my head. Like that my first encounter with Scott Drake ended. He walked into my life, though my dark alley, and stamped himself into my existence in such a way that it could never be removed. After as many years as it has been the entire thing almost seemed made up now thinking about it. He had this unnaturally cool and collected aura of him that followed the ebb and flow of his emotions. He could turn from angry to happy to cool in less time than a driver turning a corner. But it had happened, and it all took place under the crimson Arizona skies.
