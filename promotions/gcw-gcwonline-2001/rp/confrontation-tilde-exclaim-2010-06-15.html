Confrontation Tilde Exclaim

15 June 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06/15/10 For many a day, Phil Atken had not been seen. Mr. Dirk Dickwood had searched high and low for the man, voyaging down to the darkest depths and climbing up to the highest heights. There was no sign of the mysterious and ever elusive Philip Martin Atken. Sure by darkest depth, it really meant random strip bars and by highest heights, it also meant random strip bars but Mr. Dickwood had been trying his gosh darn hardest to hunt down this apex predator, whatever that may mean. Just as he had reached the end of his rope, in a figurative sense not a spelunking sense, the figure of the man that had haunted his mindbrain for the last many a day stood before him. He looked broken down, dishevelled, unshaved and unwashed. This may have been due to the lack of showering facilities where he had disappeared to but it may have been something deeply sinister. Personally, I’d go with options one but you know… AIR OF MYSTERY! Stunned that the man that had consumed his mind for the better part of three weeks now stood before him, Dirk immediately let his feelings of this whole gosh darn situation known. Dickwood: Where the hell have you been? I’ve been trying to call you for two, maybe three weeks. I lost track a couple of days ago. Atken: It was a medical emergency. Dickwood: That’s not what my sources told me. Atken: I don’t know if you can call a drunk you pay in sandwiches a valued source of information. That’s just bad business. Dickwood: He’s more reliable than you. I’m almost certain he’d make a better wrestler. Atken: Yeah, if only he didn’t vomit every five yards and had my dashing good lucks he might even be able to wrestle in a dark match or two for Fucksticktown Championship Champions. Dickwood: Is that a thing? Atken: No, no it is not a thing. Dickwood: Look, Phil, I want you to take both this situation and me seriously. Atken: Why would I do that? Dickwood: Do what? And it was at that point that the conversation that had be simmering for the longest time in the grand relationship of Dickwood/Atken was going to finally come to a boil and from the boil the steam of frustration, anger and mistrust would float forth into the office of tension at the desk of mild discomfort in the chairs of passive aggression. Both men knew that one day this issue would arise but they hoped it never would, locking it away in the chest of indifference but perhaps due to his second first round loss in the Rampage tournament, Mr. Atken was finally going to open that chest with the key of…difference. Okay that one still needs some work. Atken: Take you seriously. I mean, why now? You’ve been a walking punchline for years and suddenly you want to start actually acting like my manager? Hey, I have a great idea, why don’t we time travel back four years and you can start acting like this before my fucking downward spiral. Doesn’t that sound grand? I can actually be a threat in this industry and you can actual be a serious manager? Hang on, I’ll go and see if there are some TARDIS blueprints up on the google. Dickwood: Oh, now you’re just being obtuse. We were both drunk on our individual successes back then, we didn’t have to worry about planning for the future, we WERE the future. We could goof off and arse around as much as we wanted because we were valued talents and then… Atken: Yeah and then you, as my manager, as my agent should have made sure I didn’t end up as the fucking wreck I did. I can get drunk on my success, I’m the talent in this relationship, I’m allowed to do that. You, as my agent should have been taking my career as seriously then as you pretend that you do now. Dickwood: Pretend? Atken: Rampage. Dickwood: If you disagree that Rampage was the best thing for your career right now, I don’t know what to tell you. Atken: Remind me again, how did that experiment of yours end? Dickwood: It could easily have been a different result if you actually gave a fuck instead of, y’know, promising me that had something bigger in mind without ever explaining or even hinting at what the hell this big plan was supposed to be. Atken: Yes because making me look like a god damn idiot time and time again was a far better idea than allowing me to try and get a long time plan arranged for the better of my career. It’s far better to shove me in a tournament that I was unprepared for and unwilling to compete in. That’s the sign of excellent management, actively screwing over your client. Dickwood: Please, please explain to me what your big plan was. What grand scheme were you concocting thinking that it would establish you as something beyond a joke in GCW? Another terrible podcast? How about one of those “I am relaunching myself” promos that you always seem so fond of, was it one of those? Were you planning to attacking some chick from behind to establish male dominance in the wrestling industry? That one always goes well for you. Face it Phil, the tournament was the best decision you could have made in this stage of your career but you went and actively fucked yourself over like you always do. You are the most self-destructive competitor I have ever met. It’s no wonder half the industry doesn’t trust you when half the time you sign a contract you just float away into the ether whenever you bloody well feel like it. For example, y’know, the past two weeks. Atken: Oh ho ho, attacking my repertoire, that’s gonna get me siding with you on this one. And no, the plan wasn’t and still isn’t one of those fine options. It was something I’ve never done before… Dickwood: The plan was competent wrestling? Atken: Fuck off. Dickwood: There’s that old Atken wit. Atken: You know what, I don’t need this and I certainly don’t need to deal with this “new” you. Phil was about to split like a banana split, without the ice-cream, or whipped cream, or the banana, he was going to leave the room is what I’m getting at. However, Dirk knew something. I’m not sure what but I’m certain he knew a thing and it was that thing that help heal all wounds and end climate change once and for all. If only I could remember what it was the world would be a more perfect a harmonious place. As it stands, we’re pretty buggered. On the other hand, Dirk also knew something wrasslin’ related. Dickwood: Phil, you and I both know this is just you in a strop. You pull this all the time and I’m sick of it. Atken: Old Dirk wouldn’t be. Dickwood: Old Dirk didn’t have a house extension to pay off. Atken: This change in attitude is about a house extension? Dickwood: It’s a really nice extension; it has a pool table and everything. I’d hate to lose it because my client is a bloody drunken wreck who in recent years has developed a fear of success. Atken: A pool table? Dickwood: Full scale. Atken: And you didn’t invite me over? Dickwood: Well… you see… Atken: I don’t think I’ve ever been invited over and you’ve been to my house lots of times. Dickwood: I came over as a courtesy, do you really think I wanted to spend my weekends in a decrepid Scottish mansion stinking of cat piss? Atken: Well you’ve been to my new flat too… Dickwood: Which somehow still stinks of cat piss, despite a lack of cat. Atken: That IS odd isn’t it? Dickwood: You’re letting me get distracted from my actual point? Atken: Am I or do you have the attention span of an infant? Dickwood: Pretty sure it’s you. Atken: Did you ever see that film “Up”? It’s pretty good. Dickwood: Case in point. Atken: Look, I’m going to be honest. This conversation is a little hard to swallow. I mean, you’re trying to tell me you know what’s best for when you haven’t shown the faintest interest in doing that since I’ve known you. As much as Phil may have thought this, the truth was something deeper. I’m not telling you though. Then where would all the fun and mystery come from? Nowhere, that’s where. And that’s not even a place, it’s nowhere. Dickwood: I think what we’re looking at right now is a tricky situation, I’ve matured in my profession while you... smell like a two week bender. Now, if you think about that for just one little second, who do you want to make the decisions in this relationship. A man who now has a purpose, or the aimless drunken hobo? Atken: Hobo? Dickwood: Have you seen the “beard” you’ve developed? I think I can see a whole fried chicken in that thing. How the hell did you even manage that in such a short time? Atken: Genetics. Dickwood: Remind me to never trace your ancestry. God damn it, we’re doing it again… Atken: I don’t think jazzercise works as well as advertised. Dickwood: I’m have to say… NO WE ARE NOT DOING THIS. We are seriously talking about your future. Atken: I’m bored of that. If you call my actions in wrestling cliché, you trying to talk seriously about me future is the most clicheiest cliché that ever did cliché. Dickwood: So where do we go from here? Dirk still had a wrasslin’ thing in his brain that he needed to say. No really, I’m serious this time. If you can’t trust me, a disembodied figure, who can you trust? Atken: After Rampage, I really have to question your decision making abilities. Dickwood: After Rampage, I really have to question your passion as a wrestler. Atken: It appears we have reached an impasse. Dickwood: Maybe, maybe not. Atken: How very coy Dirkwood. Dickwood: Why are we talking so stiltedly? Atken: I’m not sure. Dickwood: Can we stop? Atken: I’m not sure. The conversation carried on in this manner for a good half hour, with Dirk continuing to be coy about information he held and Phil… well I don’t really know. Several Hours Later Dickwood: So, that’s how I managed to secure you a United States title shot. It wasn’t easy, or pleasant, or sexually appealing in the least but I managed to do it and I done it all for you. Also I done it for me because as I said, extension with pool table. But mostly it was for you. Atken: But what about the puppies? Dickwood: I never mentioned any puppies. Did you hear that thing about the US Title shot? Atken: And who holds that again? Dickwood: Valentine. Atken: And you consider me going against Vivica Q. R. M. J. Valentine a GOOD thing for my career? Dickwood: If you win, it’s a great thing. Atken: And what are the odds on that? Dickwood: Well I could answer that, or I could let you know that the puppies returned home safely. Atken: Awww, I love a happy ending. NOT IN THAT WAY. Also, in that way. And with that… You know what I don’t even fucking know. Seriously, I’m clueless here, I don’t think there’s a morale here at all.
