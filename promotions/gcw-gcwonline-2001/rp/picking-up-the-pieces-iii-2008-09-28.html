Picking Up The Pieces, III

28 September 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Written: September 28, 2008 We Left Off... Life can throw you curveballs and, often times, you're not expecting them. That's the beauty of the curve. Everything appears as though it is predictable and.. BAM! It all changes in an instant. But more often than not, you're able to adjust right away. There are, of course, those few instances where you do not adjust quick enough. And in those times, you get hit with the ball. That's what happened to Chris Bagwell. Chris has risen to infamy on the principles of greed, self-centeredness, and apathy towards others. His tendancies have made people refer to him as an asshole and deuchebag -- and not feel guilty about it. His cruel actions even so much as created a wall around his talent, making his ability often overlooked. Some, however, may consider this a plus. With his talent masked by his ego, many do not expect his explosiveness inside the ring. Many may expect the contrary and are soon thereafter left in shock.. and awe. But others view it as hurtful, especially in the case of Chris Bagwell. Chris thrives on attention and pains for the spotlight. When he's not the center of attention, he pushes the envelope. So much so that he jeaopardizes his very well-being. The mask that has been painted around his talent is like a blanket thrown atop a ticking time bomb. It all comes down to a single moment .. when the bomb finaly explodes. Since finding out that Rebecca is still alive, Chris Bagwell has been losing sleep over searching for her. More specifically, searching for answers. He's traveled from show to show, city to city, and state to state looking for any bit of information that may, in fact, lead him to her whereabouts. Just recently, Chris was finally tipped off in the location of Rebecca. In an angry fit, he prepared himself to meet with the person who'd been toying with his emotions over the last several months and dropping him clues. Their meeting time was set and was broken. And with an unstable man, this isn't exactly the way you want to conduct business. But this mystery fellow kept his word and showed up. He may have been a bit late, but he sure as hell deserved some leniency. ---------- Present Day "So when exactly are you going to fill me in on all this?" Chris had just finished packing up his things in the hotel room after Worldwide 97. His victory over Phil Atken had him feeling mighty well of himself and the only thing that could cap off an otherwise eventful week was to get the answers he had wanted about his girlfriend. As he stuffed his clothes messily into his suitcase, he continued to plead with his long-time friend, and mentor, Shane "Double ?" Skwarek. Shane had once been an intricate part of Chris' wrestling career. He gave Chris his first real paycheck and first real wrestling gig. Now granted, this wasn't Chris' first time in the ring or first time being paid for it... Shane just gave him a primetime opportunity. A chance to shine on television and make some serious cash. You see, Shane had been the owner and founder of Ultimate Championship Wrestling many years ago. At that time, the UcW was leading the pack in their circuit and so there was really no greater dream than to be a part of such a prestigious organization. Chris milked his opportunity as best his could. He gained famed and superstardom. However, his real taste of the spotlight wouldn't come until a year after Ultimate Championship Wrestling closed its doors and Platinum Wrestling opened theirs. Platinum Wrestling was a spin-off organization that Shane had created in order to preserve the legacy of UcW. It featured the merger of five independent organizations into one beast of a federation. A beast that Bagwell quickly grabbed by the horns and was eventually able to carry as World Champion. "It's a bit more complicated than that, Chris." Of course, it was the absolute last thing Chris wanted to hear. It was the only thing he'd been hearing for months.. or even years. Naturally, his attitude got the best of him, "You've got to be kidding me, Shane. You're going to practically show up at my doorstep and drag me along online to tell me it's complicated? What the hell is that about?" Shane steps into Chris' room with a glass of chilled Merlot in his hand. He glances at Chris, sipping on his drink, showing no intention of giving him an answer. "You know I don't like when people do this shit, Shane," argues Chris. Shane nods his head in acknowledgement, taking another sip from the glass. "You know, this hotel Merlot is surprisingly refreshing." Chris drops the clothes in his hand into a heap. He tilts his head around, locking eye contact with Shane. Shane smirks. "You disagree," he states. Chris laughs, but its a fake laugh. He gives a laugh of irritation. That kind of laugh you give when you really can't believe whats going on. "Why'd you come here, Shane? Are you looking for a free ride to relive your glory days or do you really have something for me?" Shane raises his eyebrows, taking yet another sip from his glass. "Relive my glory days? You know I don't need to do that." Chris was hoping to have strucken a nerve with that comment, but to no avail. He was running low on remarks to get under Shane's skin and fast. Shane's character was hard to break. He'd always been so relaxed and stress-free. Very little actually bothered him and that wasn't really helping Chris out any. Realizing his was fighting a lost battle, Chris turned back towards his suitcase and tried to shove a couple last minute items inside. He was admitting defeat in the battle of words. Admitting defeat is something Chris rarely did, period. And Shane knew is. "I found her in Texas," he said. Chris stopped and stared down at the bedside. He was without words, for once. Instead, he was trying to cycle through his mind all the reasons she could've or would've even been there. But came up with nothing. "I was doing some promotional work in the area for a new training school that just opened. They needed to feed these kids some motivation so I figured it'd be fun to check out the guys who could very well be the future of the business," Shane continued. Chris continued to stare. He was listening but he wasn't responding. Shane had entertained the idea that Chris wasn't paying attention at all, but decided he'd continue talking anyway. Maybe he'd hit a nerve? "Afterwards, I'd been on my way to a restaurant outside of town that I've heard good things about. On my way, I pulled into a gas station to fill up and there she was. She was at her own pump, just finishing." Chris finally responds and turns towards Shane. He doesn't speak, but you could tell in his posture that he'd been listening. He was all ears. So Shane went on. "I didn't notice her at first. I sort of glanced in her direction on my way into the small convenience store. It was actually on my way out. I literally dropped my bottle of water when we locked eyes." "What did she say," Chris asks in a decibal above a whisper. "She didn't say anything," Shane responds. It certainly wasn't the answer Chris was looking for. But at least it was something. "She had just put her pump back when we locked eyes, so she just jumped in her car and took off." Chris lets it all sink in. He tries to imagine the scenerio in his head. A rural town and a western gas station. He can even see the dustballs rolling through the streets. Sure it seemed far-fetched, but it made sense. She had to have been somewhere that nobody would notice her. Right outside a major city would be a perfect cover. But Chris had his doubts, "Are you sure it wasn't just someone who looked like her?" Shane finishes off his glass of Merlot with one last gulp. He dangles the cup at his side while holding a gaze with Chris. After only a few seconds of silence, he shakes his head slightly back and forth. "I thought it may have been until she looked back once she started driving away. She looked back twice and on the second time smashed the gas peddle." Chris plops backwards, just barely making it onto the bed. His eyes drop to a spot on the carpet and his chin falls onto his palm, fingers cupping his mouth. "It was her, Chris. I saw her. And she saw me." An uncomfortable silence sits in the room. Shane decides its best he leave Chris to himself to think. He begins to turn to leave the room when Chris' voice stops him in his tracks. "...then it was her," Chris says again in a near whisper. Shane turns back into the room, taking baby steps towards Chris. "So then the rumors were true?" Chris moves his eyes upwards until they connect with Shane's, "Rumors?" But before Shane is able to explain, Chris answers for him. "That Rebecca came to my hotel room?" Shane nods his head. "Yes," Chris replied. "She did. But I would've sworn it was some kind of look-a-like or something. My head was in a million places." Shane digs deep into his pocket as he draws nearer to Chris. He pulls out an envelope thats folded in half. "Here," he says, tossing the envelope onto Chris' lap. Chris takes it in his hand and begins to open the envelope. He pulls from it two airline tickets. Shane adds, "We've got a flight to Texas." RINNGGGGG, RIINNGGGGGG, RIIINGGGG The obnoxious standard tone on every new Verizon Wireless phone begins to blare from inside Chris' pocket, overtaking Shane's voice. Chris slips the phone from his pocket and raises it to his ear. "Hello?" "C-Baggs! Whats up?" A familiar voice with a heavy Staten Island accent. "Timmy?" "Yeah man! Listen, I've got this charity type event set up that I need you to attend. Its for Special Ed kids with disabilities. Mostly down syndrome. Think you could snag a flight to Texas in the next day or so?" Chris looks up at Shane, eyes wide open. He covers his hand over the speaker on the phone and whispers, "Timmy has an event set up for me in Texas..." Shane stares at him blankly. From the phone, "Chris? You there? Can you do that?" Shane shrugs his shoulders. "I'll be there Timmy," Chris finally answers. ---------- Two Days Later Battling Down Syndrome Charity Event Odessa, Texas Narrator: Chris Bagwell I couldn't believe I was actually here. I had taken the flight that Shane scheduled, which happened to leave the afternoon he had presented me with the tickets. Obviously, that was his intention all along. He knew he was going to fill me in on everything the entire time. You've gotta appreciate, though, how the sonuvabitch knows how to play with my mind. One of these days, I swear I'm going to get him back. That's for damn sure. I'm not going to lie though, I'm a bit nervous. I'm in the approximate area that Shane said he saw Rebecca driving around in. Now the question is whether or not she's moved since then. I'm sure if it was really her and she saw him, she had to know that he'd eventually come and tell me. But perhaps thats why she came to the hotel room that night? I still can't figure out, for the life of me, how she knew where I'd be. I guess thats the shit you deal with nowadays with the internet and the damned paparazzi. Nonetheless, Rebecca had to be put on the backburner for a few more days. Its the first time in a long time where I've actually felt comfortable saying that, too. I think the fact that I know a bit more information on her whereabouts is holding me over. What I've got on tap now, though, is a bunch of bullshit that Timmy's agent had booked for me. In some sort of effort to travel across America and raise my stake, Timmy's guy has been trying to book me at different venues to do charitable work. He says that my merchandise sales will drastically increase if I pretend to care about other people's problems To be honest, I couldn't give a shit if someone wore my t-shirt. But they say its a great way to generate income. I never cared to have myself made into an action figure or to have some stupid catch-phrase on the back of a t-shirt. Hell, I don't even know what the point of a catchphrase really even is. So some kids run around reciting your words? Whats the big deal? I was never a fan of making fans. All they do is serve as a distraction. But I guess if it makes me money, I'll give it a whirl. "HHiiii derr Mistur Bagwheel!" It was my first encounter with a severly special kid. I thought maybe I could be cute with the kids for a little while. Just long enough for the retards to buy some t-shirts or something. But after this kid came up, face looking like his head left under a heat lamp, I wanted to get the fuck out of there as soon as possible. However, I did try to entertain it a little bit. "What's going on, chief? You can call me Bagwell!" I guess it was kind of cool how the kid's face lit up when I acknowledged him. He looked like he'd just seen a ghost. I suppose I am a pretty big celebrity. And I'm sure these kids have a hard time getting high school geeks to talk to them, let alone a big name like myself. "Bagwheel yew crayzee! I give yew Hex Bweakar yew mess wif me!" It was at this point where I really wanted to find Timmy and break his little neck. I can't even understand the words this sultan of the short bus was mumbling out. Hex Beaker? What the hell is that? It sounded like some sort of fucked up science project. Hex Beaker...? He couldn't have meant Hex Breaker, could he have? I guess I should've been a bit more observant and taken at a look at his shirt before I entertained his company at all... "The hell'd you say, droopy-face?" I jerked my upper body towards him, making him think I was going to lay him on his ass. But I didn't need to touch him. He got so frightened that he hopped backwards and tripped over his own feet. He tumbled to the ground in probably one of the funnier episodes I've seen all month. I couldn't help but laugh. I mean the droopy-faced bastard couldn't even take a step backwards without losing his balance and toppling over. How was any sort of fundraiser going to help fuckups like this kid? Were they going to teach these kids balance? Or maybe they were going to fix their saggy faces and pull them back to where they really belong. It was a waste of my time, regardless. After my comment to Gary the Retard, he quickly retreated in what looked like a set of tears. I guess I kind of felt bad at first, for making a retard cry. But after I gave it some thought, I was well over it. I mean, the kid who who I was. Could he really be so stupid as to think mentioning a guy like Andy Murray wouldn't get him hurt? Moron. About forty five minutes after I pulled into the lot, I finally found Timmy. Mind you, I spent a good 30 sitting in the car debating on if I should even get out. Unlike myself, Timmy looked thrilled. You'd think he was one of these idiots and was just given a lollipop with the look he had on his face. "So whats up, my man? You ready to make some money?" He was estatic. He was convinced that this was all going to work and fall into place. Its not like that. I don't think these kids even have any access to cash. So basically I'd be wasting my time here all day so that others would think I was some kind of great guy. Fuck that. I had other plans. "I don't think so, Tim. I just had one of these retards try and tell me he was going to give me a hex-breaker," I said to him. "SHHH!!!!!" He shouted. "You can't be throwing that word around like that here. You're dealing with sensative kids." I really wasn't in the mood to be dealing with him raising his voice. "Come on Chris," he pleaded. "Just give it one more chance.' But I guess he probably did do a lot of planning to get it to this point. It couldn't hurt me to try and entertain it again. As for the retards... well I couldn't make any promises. "This better get better," I confirmed. He smiled and patted my back and we were off. "I've got someone I need you to meet." We toured through the event looking for God only knows who. It seemed that as we went along, each 'specially educated' kid looked uglier than the other. In a sense, it was a damn shame. I mean, I couldn't actually believe these parents would put their kids through this. If it were up to me, I'd have had the kids killed at birth. "Chris, I'd like you to meet Mr. Thomas Bartlett. He's the man in charge of this charity event." And so I was brought to the brains behind the whole affair. I'll be honest, I expected him to be a bit better looking. He had dark red hair. Not a deep red but almost like brown hair that you can tell used to be red. He probably dyed it or something. His face was scattered with freckles in no real pattern. He was probably about 20 pounds overweight, but hid it well with the way he dressed. "Hello Chris, its my pleasure to meet you. I just want to thank you for coming out here and helping us out with your support. It means a great deal for the kids." "It's Bagwell. Only my close friends call me Chris." I had to set the fellow in his place. Realistically, I couldn't give a shit who called me Chris but I wanted to bust the guy's chops and let him know who's boss. "I apologize. But I cannot stress enough how much you're helping out here, Mr. Bagwell." A mister? That was something I didn't expect from him. I guess he wasn't a half decent guy and if I could just stand on the sidelines for a few more hours, I'd be out of here scott-free. And hell -- it'd be the first non-selfish thing I've done in a while! But Timmy had to toss his two cents in. "Its such a shame what happened to these kids. I'm sure its tough being their parents." "Oh yes," Thomas added. "It's definitely got to be hard on them. But the love they give to these kids is unmatched." "Tough?" I had to interject. "Tough being the parents? Are you kidding me?" It was at this point I knew I was going to explode and that this event wasn't going to turn out as peachy as Timmy probably invisioned it. "These pathetic excuses for parents are the reason these kids are retarded in the first place. If they didn't smoke or drink or take drugs when they were pregnant, their twenty and thirty year old kids wouldn't still act like they were six years old." I could see Thomas' face beaming. "I have absolutely no pity for them. I do, however, have pity for people like yourself. People who think this is sad and devote their life to trying to help out these parents. They brought this upon themselves. They crave the attention and you're feeding it to them, along with a big fat paycheck." Thomas began to tremble. And I was eating it up. "Why should these people get any additional cash because their kids are fucking stupid? Why aren't there donations for the people who WERE responsible during their pregnancies. Or the people who bust their asses at their jobs for bare minimum pay? Where's the financial aide for them?" "THESE PEOPLE WERE CURSED AT BIRTH!" Thomas shouted. He must not really know who I am if he's going to try and argue with me. "Cursed?" I questioned. "So then they should've been put out of their misery then and exterminated. There's still time though. Give them all a glass of arsenic and tell them its Kool-Aid. You can catch up on all the lost time." It might have been wrong of me to say but I couldn't give a shit less. Its how I felt and I wasn't going to be like every other person in the industry and put on some lame front, pretending I actually gave a shit. "I think you need to leave," Thomas said to me. And his words were like music to my ears. "Thank fucking God," I responded. "If I knew getting out of here was going to be THIS easy, I would've opened with a nice FUCK YOU!" With that, I turned and walked away. Timmy looked dumbfounded and chased after me. Part of me wanted to battle it out with this Thomas fellow and have a mix of words, but I knew I'd end up knocking him out. I didn't want to do that at a 'Tart Carnival. Just as I was approaching the parking lot, I stumbled across an old buddy who seems to have no short term memory. "HEX BWEAKA TUH BAGWHEEL!" He just didn't learn. So I phrased it in a way he'd understand, "You know why you're retarded, pal? Because your mother was blowing lines of coke while she was pregnant with you, getting gang banged by a couple of big, dirty niggers." Needless to say, the kid's face held an expression uglier than his face already was. And I sure as hell didn't think that was possible. ---------- That Afternoon Odessa, Texas "Rebecca!" Abigail sprinted into Rebecca's bedroom in a panic. "What's the matter, Abby?" "Chris is in Odessa!" Rebecca's face lit up. You could tell she was excited. But Abigail was about to ruin that. "We can't let them find us here," Abigail said. "It's not time. Not yet..." Fade.
