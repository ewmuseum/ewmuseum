Being A Man (Aftermath part 2)

Date: Sometime after How long he’d been here exactly, was a mystery, even to him. After the benefit show, he’d feigned illness in an effort to slip away. There were too many questions, too many people wanting to get a small little piece of him. This is the price of fame, even worse the price of being cheered. For years he’d been the bad guy, the worst he’d have to deal with were those ignorant slobs who’d take any opportunity to heckle or jeer him. Not that he’d minded, it was actually pretty funny when he thought about it. For the most part people had just left him alone, sure there was the occasional fan that “loved heels” but it wasn’t something that came around too often. Those were his best times, when he wouldn’t have minded an autograph seeker. Then everything changed, now he was a favorite, the crowd loved him, and by result everyone wanted to be around him. Autograph seekers came out of the woodwork, it was hard going somewhere without finding at least one person who recognized him, and wanted to say hi, or get a picture taken with him. Of course because fate’s a cruel woman, this was exactly the point in his life where everything went wrong, and the only thing he wanted was be to left alone. He’d found a bar, some quiet little dingy place where the regulars bellowed in annoyance whenever the door was opened and outside light found it’s way in. He still wasn’t able to drink, but he’d been able to sit and smoke in piece. A beer sat in front of him, untouched. almost certainly warm and stale from the tap, it was now even more so. Stevens had made sure to get some small bills before entering, he’d learned his lesson well in London. Staring into space he tried to put together all the events that had led him to this state. All in all he decided there was only one reason he’d been left like this. Lestatt. Yeah, it was all that bastards fault, if he wouldn’t have kidnapped Sheena in the first place, maybe she wouldn’t have felt the need to escape, maybe he wouldn’t have gone off on Lance. It’s no use thinking in maybes the past is solid, unalterable, he was powerless against the eternal rushing river of time. After he’d gotten the phone call from Alex, informing him of the situation with Lance, Stevens had gone into a voluntary seclusion. Shortly after the call he’d booked a flight for Alex back to California, so he could be with Sheena as she decided what to do, he figured they could both use each other for support. Sheena had called him several times since then, but he’d never felt the strength to answer. He couldn’t believe he’d be such a spineless asshole that he couldn’t even comfort his love in her time of crisis. Yet, that was the case. The guilt was just too much, the lies the deception. He couldn’t keep them up any longer, he was going to have to tell her what he’d done, that much he couldn’t deny any longer. If it meant going to prison then so be it, he deserved no better. The problem was he couldn’t figure out exactly how to tell her what he’d done, how on earth are you supposed to tell the woman you love, that you killed her brother? Moreover, after you’ve told her, how can you explain why you lied about it? A tangled web indeed. What made everything more difficult was the lack of alcohol. It seemed silly to even think, but he knew just a couple drinks would make everything so much easier. The warmth would seep into his blood, ignite his brain, clear away the fog. That’s what it felt like since that night, as if he were living in a perpetual fog, that wouldn’t lift. Everything was seen through hazy eyes. A drink would clear all that away, he should have a drink, he was a grown man, he knew what he was doing. Sure maybe it’s a crutch, but if you break your leg, don’t you need a crutch? Sometimes you just can’t stand without one. Steadily his hand moved forward, about to lift the glass to his lips. Yet he stopped just short, his finger tips barely touching the sides. He noticed his hand was shaking, never a good sign. He’d been shaking a lot lately, like he had some terrible palsy that’d strike at any unexpected moment. Too much stress, that must’ve been it, overwrought nerves demanding to be eased. Fat chance. As bad as he felt he knew it must pale in comparison to what Sheena was going through. Sitting at Lance’s bedside, wondering what she should do. His brain function had steadily declined, gone away to almost nothing. Legally he was brain dead, thus it had become a question as to whether or not to pull the plug on his life support, for all he knew Sheena had already decided. The choice couldn’t be an easy one, watch someone you love suffer, clinging to some shallow semblance of life, hoping desperately that something could change, it’d take a miracle but it was possible, stranger things had happened; or give up hope, and allow your brother to die, essentially at your hands. Thinking about that made his pain seem somewhat less important, he after all deserved his pain. He was guilty, an evil man, his punishment was a just one, but Sheena certainly didn’t deserve any of this. The woman had helped him, brought him out of more than one depression, she’d been good, and kind, and innocent. He’d done this too her, just more guilt for him to deal with, he was responsible for all her suffering. And the worst thing was knowing that she still believed it was some random act of drug violence. All she could possibly be feeling was an inarticulate rage at the streets, maybe at Lance for getting himself into the situation, maybe at herself for getting too drugged up to be with him in his last conscious moments. If she knew the truth, at least she could put the blame where it belonged on him, yet he was too scared to tell her, she meant far too much to him. The truth would be the nail in the coffin, she’d never speak to him again, he wasn’t sure he could handle that on top of everything else. But again he came back to the thought of how shallow his motives had been. He’d lied to cover it up so he wouldn’t go to jail, he’d not told her because he didn’t want to be made, he’d beaten poor Lance, because he saw something of himself in the young man. Wow, that thought came out of nowhere. It wasn’t until that moment that he finally realized why he’d been so vicious, why he couldn’t stop. He killed Lance, because he wanted to kill the worst parts of himself. The revelation wasn’t an easy one to deal with, after all it’s hard to admit that you hate yourself to the point that you’re capable of killing someone who reminds you of you. The poor kid had never had a chance, everything would have been so much better if he’d never called Sheena to bail him out. Poor stupid fucking kid, why the hell did he have to call her? It wasn’t like they’d talked a lot before that, why couldn’t he have just called someone else? Blaming Lance was stupid, yet he couldn’t stop himself from doing it. Just another turn of a guilty mind that refuses to deal with it’s own crimes, blame someone else, anyone else. Especially blame the victim, if they wouldn’t have been there after all, they couldn’t have been hurt. Yeah, that’s right it was Lance’s fault Stevens beat him into a bloody pulp, yeah, right. “Fuck” The word came out under his breath, but he was sure a few in the bar heard. He saw a couple look up, glance warily in his direction. He couldn’t stay here much longer, had to get out, get some fresh air. He was sick of wallowing in his own misery, sick of trying to shift the blame away from where it really belonged. He was going to do something right for a chance, he’d call Sheena, and he’d tell her what had happened. It was time to step up, and be a man. ~To Be Continued~
