Deliverance

11 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11-11-05 You’d think someone living on disability allowance yet working tax free on the side for the best part of ten years would have enough money to buy a damn up to date lawn mower. This one was one of those old manual rotary blade deals and would have really pissed Marianne off every time she was sent to do the yard if it wasn’t for the blessing of being outside the house, away from her Uncle for the best part of a few hours. She’d drag the chore out as long as she could. Outside she was safe, outside he couldn’t do anything just in case a snooping neighbour noticed. Or at least that was the case most of the time. There was that one occasion when she was mowing the back yard and he asked her to get something from the shed for him. Garden shears or some such. How fitting it would have been to go fetch him his precious garden shears and deliver them right to him, through his chest. That thought alone bought a fleeting smile across Marianne’s face as she gazed up at the willow tree in the front yard. The smile didn’t last long though. Her smiles never really did anymore unless she was alone, up late talking to Mary about the day and life and how the real world was gonna look once they got out of this god forsaken place. No the smile didn’t last long because she remembered what happened when she went to fetch those garden shears. He’d met her at the doorway and offered to help her look. He’d said it nice and loud, loud enough for anyone overhearing to believe a helpful uncle was aiding his young niece in her designated yard work or vice versa. It didn’t really matter, both possibilities were lies. The truth of what really happened is a legacy written in blood and semen stained into the concrete floor of the shed. It took two hours to tease the splinters out from under Marianne’s finger nails that night, but that’s what you get when you claw the walls of a wooden room trying to get away from your demon. “Yea that’s what I get.” The words were from a song she’d heard but they meant little to her, not right now, not beyond the literal. That was simply what she got. Since that day though she always did the backyard while her Aunty Shona was hedge cutting and made sure she finished before she did so she wasn’t alone out back, or she started a conversation with Mr Nelson next door, yea that helped too. It was growing to late evening now, she’d taken her time doing the yard. It was somewhat of a therapy to her and Mary liked the outdoors, she loved to swing from the willow tree when it was in full season. It was her birthday today. There had been a cake and a few presents. She had friends at school but even though her Aunt and Uncle said she could invite them over she decided against it. If she bought outsiders into her home everything would fall apart. Everything inside her as the children gazed at the ugly world she lived in through permanent rose tinted glasses. Lucky for them. Marianne’s glasses got stamped on and crushed into sand when she was five. She never did get another pair. Funny that. Not funny haha. Just funny. Aunty Shona walked down the path and got into the family car. “I’ll be back in a couple hours Marianne. Just going to get some containers from Tracy.” A couple hours? It didn’t take that long to go 20mins across town and back, but it was Tracy. They would snack, yack, and snack some more, then Aunty Shona would come home and throw it all up when she thought no one was looking. She was pretty was Aunty Shona and every night she would wake up and empty her dinner into the toilet bowl to make sure she stayed that way. Marianne swore she looked at her funny sometimes. Again not funny haha but funny like if she didn’t know better she would say she was’jealous of her. Ridiculous surely. Marianne’s ears pricked up and her breath caught as she heard the porch wood creak with the weight of? “Marianne hun, why don’t you come on inside, it’s getting late. Put the mower out back and I’ll make us some cocoa.” Just like that, like butter wouldn’t melt he was. Like no wrongs had ever been committed like he wasn’t a sick twisted fuck who’Her train of thought was interrupted as her heart sank and she yanked the mower out back “Yes sir.” She wheeled the ancient looking thing round the back of the modest house passing the basement window. That part of the house was down the side and away from prying eyes. Something caught her eye. She kneeled down and smiled at the face she saw in the glass, the face smiled back and winked at her. “Happy Birthday missy.” “Thank’s Mary.” Marianne replied sounded a lil deflated to say the least. “I can’t stay, Uncle wants me to come inside.” She looked hopeless and sad. In the year that she and Mary had been speaking, Marianne had almost given up on the idea of ever finding a way out. She’d near forgotten why Mary was around in the first place. Mary smiled at this before whispering. “Turn that frown upside down Girlie.” Her face seemed to lean closer to the glass as Marianne leaned closer also. Her voice was almost a whisper now. “It’s time.” Marianne’s eyes went wide. She shook her unruly curls from her eyes and furrowed her brow, “Time for what?” “Well what ya think?” Mary smiled childlike and innocent. There was excitement in her eyes and beyond that, something else, something that you would feel aught to be chained. Something that for Marianne’s sake was far from it. No it was unchained and free and it was ready. “Member that song that Momma used to play” That song about blackbirds?” Marianne thought back. Accessing memories about her parents was still painful and often fruitless. “black birds”.Black birds singing in the dead of the night?” Mary nodded and grinned. She whispered again. “What did they tell the black bird to do Marianne?” Marianne was on her knees and elbows now whispering too. The mower was perched up against the side of the house. She forced her mind to remember the song. She had a photographic memory she didn’t even know about but whether it worked the same way with sounds was anyone’s guess. “Err’um?” She sang the song in her head and her eyes lit up as the rest of it fell into place. She announced the next line, “Take these broken wings and learn to fly.” She smiled triumphant but someone confused. Mary nodded again her hand raising to the glass, Marianne’s hand followed and again they touched fingertips. Mary’s face was filled with what looked like’something Marianne had given up on. Hope, as she said “It’s time to fly blackbird.” “But’how, I haven’t got?” She slumped against the side of the house hearing her Uncles voice calling her inside again. It chilled her, made her shudder and her eyes welled up. “I cant do this no more Mary. I just wanna be away from here, I just want it to stop hurting, I just wanna be happy.” Heat burned her eyes and slow tears spilled from her lashes. Mary’s heart sank. “No, no more pain, no more tears. Mary fixes this tonight. Done. Member last year you promised to let Mary drive if she askies for the wheel?” Marianne nodded wiping her eyes with the hem of her oversized bruise hiding t shirt. Mary looked up at her deathly serious. “Give Mary the wheel, and she’ll make this all better. Tomorrow, we be far away from here, safe. Just let her drive, trust her and she’ll make it all better.” Marianne’s sobs eased. She glanced down at the reflection in the glass and nodded. “Save us please?” and her eyes closed. The sobbing stopped slowly. The tears stopped flowing and Marianne moved to get up out of view of her reflection for a moment. She backed up and put her hand on the law mower, “Coming Uncle!” She yelled as she pulled the machine into the garden shed. She did so slowly and deliberately, her fingers caressing the wood of the door as she closed it then locked it with the bolt. She walked over the yard to the back door, and suddenly looked like she was limping slightly, dragging her left leg oddly. But she wasn’t limping, ever so slowly the walk turn limp became’a skip. She was skipping. She skipped across the yard and up the stairs getting to the back door that was more glass than wood. She looked up at her reflection in the glass of the door and her eyes were wide and bright. The red from the tears was gone and her full pouty lips widened in a dangerous smile. Her head tilted to the side as she looked up towards the second floor of the house. Her shoulders rose than sank in a glee filled sigh. “Oohh, exciting times Marianne, Exciting times.” She yanked the door open and skipped inside. “Blackbird singing in the dead of night. Take these broken wings and learn to fly. All your life. You were only waiting for this moment to arise. Black bird singing in the dead of night. Take these sunken eyes and learn to see. All your life. You were only waiting for this moment to be free.”
