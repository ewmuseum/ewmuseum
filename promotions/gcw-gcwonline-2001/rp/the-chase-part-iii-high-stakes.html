The Chase: Part III (High Stakes)

Date: 08/20/10 - From 06/29/10 An arguable fact of life is that everyone spends their span searching for their soul’s counterpart in another. Many times during that period people are convinced they’ve found just that, but just as often people are left wondering if their search is over. A successful partnership includes sharing of feelings and past experiences. One can gauge their connection to another person by how quickly they begin to share those feelings and past experiences. “I can’t believe we’ve been out here for three weeks and haven’t seen a tornado yet.” Chloe’s voice filled the cab of Aaron’s battered SUV. The day was over and the two of them were heading for their next hotel. It was hard to believe, but the central and southern plains, often referred to as tornado alley hadn’t been kind to them. There were plenty of tornadoes, just not on the storms close to Aaron’s watchful eye. “We’ll get one. We’re in tornado alley during tornado season. It’s inevitable.” Aaron’s reply came quick and confidently. He glanced over at the redhead as she peered out her window to the flat terrain of Kansas. Aaron’s attention returned to the road ahead as he searched mentally for something to keep her talking. “How much further is it? I kind of have to use the little girl’s room.” Chloe chuckled as she spoke. Aaron joined her laugh as they continued blasting down the highway at seventy-five miles per hour. Aaron again glanced over at Chloe, who caught him this time and sent a smile his way. “What?” “Well, there is always one event that makes somebody want to study weather. I was just curious what it was for you.” Aaron’s question spilled out and filled Chloe’s ears. She smiled and sighed a bit before turning in her seat to almost face him. As she did this Aaron reached forward and turned the radio to complete silence from its low volume. “When I was eleven or twelve it was storming during our Social Studies class and I started to hear the tornado sirens going off in the distance.” Aaron kept looking from the road to her. This time not at the cleavage escaping the top of her tank top, but into her eyes. “Right after I heard them they told us all to go out into the hall and assume that stupid hands-over-head-butt-in-the-air position. After five minutes they said it was all clear so we all got up and went back into the classroom. Like five minutes later they told us to get back into the halls and when I looked out the window I saw the tornado. It was less than a half mile from the school.” “That’s nuts.” Aaron spoke while still looking from the road to Chloe and back to the road. “What did you do?” He knew she was going to continue the story without his prompting but he poked the question in anyway. “I stood there watching it for a minute. My teacher actually had to pull me from the window into the hall.” Chloe smiled as she remembered the day. “We were in the hall for about an hour and the entire time I was trying to figure out a way to get a look outside. That was the day I knew that weather was what I wanted to study.” “That’s pretty neat.” Aaron spoke the simple words and looked to Chloe as she still smiled. As he turned his attention back to the road he spotted a marker sign along the road that read Lodging 3 Miles. “So how about you?” The question that Aaron feared was coming had arrived. It wasn’t a subject Aaron liked to talk about but a risk he was willing to take to learn more about the redhead. Aaron sighed and looked back to the road before he started to speak. “You know how they say you aren’t supposed to remember anything from really early on?” Aaron looked to Chloe and watched her nod in agreement. “I swear I remember things from literally days and weeks after I was born. My mom went into labor on the morning of August 28th, 1990. I was born about five minutes before the F-5 tornado hit Plainfield, IL and killed 29 people.” “You were born during the only F-5 tornado recorded in the month of August?” Chloe seemed to be in a state of disbelief as she waited for him to continue talking. He sighed again and looked back to her attractive presence once again. “I don’t remember much about the tornado hitting, but I do remember coming home, or as close to home was.” He cleared his throat and took a deep breath. “The twister had destroyed our neighborhood. I remember being carried around by my mother as she scrounged the perimeter of our pile of rubble for anything salvageable. They said ‘tornado’ so much those first few weeks it was my first word. So I guess you can say that I was born to be a meteorologist.” “Wow. That’s crazy.” She reached over and put her hand to a soft touch of his right shoulder. “I’m really sorry you had to go through that.” She kept her hand on his shoulder for a few seconds before retracting it and sighing. “Thanks, Chloe.” Aaron looked ahead and saw their destination and maneuvered the Blazer into the parking lot of the Motel 6. He stopped as close to the entrance of the lobby as he could and hopped out, ready to stretch his legs after a four hour drive. “I’ll go in and get us a couple of rooms.” Chloe didn’t speak, she just nodded as she stretched her arms over her head, revealing a pierced belly button; one of Aaron’s strange attractions. As soon as Aaron had entered the lobby, Chloe pulled her cell phone from the pocket of her jean shorts and dialed a number. “I don’t know about this, Megan. I don’t know if he’s into me. We talked about what got us into weather. Well he told me about his house being destroyed when he was a baby. I just don’t know. Well I’ll call you tomorrow or something. Bye.” “Bad news.” Aaron came out of the door and began walking towards her. “They’ve only got one room left and it’s a single.” This posed a problem. One bed. Three weeks ago Aaron might have tried pressuring her into sharing the room and then allowing the chips to fall where they may. Not now. “So I went ahead and rented it for the night. Here.” “Here what?” Aaron approached Chloe and handed her a key to the room. She looked semi-confused as he walked to the back of the Blazer and popped the rear hatch open to begin moving stuff around. She moved from her stance at the front of the vehicle to the back as Aaron grabbed her bag from the back end and handed it to her. “You take the room. I’ll sleep out here in the truck. Not like I haven’t done it before.” She took the bag from his hands and smiled as he turned back to the back end and continued reorganizing things. He climbed in and grabbed a sleeping bag as Chloe continued to stare at him. “Are you sure?” Aaron unrolled the sleeping bag and nodded to her. “Thanks Aaron.” “Hey don’t mention it.” He moved a few more things around as she turned to find her room for the night. She got about ten feet away before he called out to her again. “One condition. Make sure you wake me up in time to get some of that continental breakfast.” “You got it.” Chloe matched the smirk on Aaron’s face with another of her own before she turned and continued to her room. The back end of the SUV slammed shut as Aaron reached for his bag and a change of clothes that would provide a more comfortable sleeping experience. After he was changed and lying on the sleeping bag before drifting off to sleep, Aaron did something that a lot of people do at night. He began to reflect on the events of the day. His mind jumped from the storms they had seen that day, but it always settled on his actions just ten minutes prior. Maybe this girl was different. Three weeks ago he cared about getting laid and the opportunities Chloe’s success would bring him. Now he had given up a single bed room to her to do something he thought was over once he joined GCW. She had to be different. She just had to be special.
