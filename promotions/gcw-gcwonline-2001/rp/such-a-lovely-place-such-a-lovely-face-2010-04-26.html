Such A Lovely Place, Such A Lovely Face

26 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/26/10 Wearealljustprisonershereofourowndevice Your body was buckled tight inside a leather pressure cooker. Every pore erupted sweat from your molten core; your screaming body desperate to cool itself. Bound in leather, the stale air between skin and suit swelled with steam. Frantic to escape, it seeped into the fabric housing of your zips, saturating the fibers and sealing the microscopic exit routes with salt crystals. You clawed at your mask, heaving breaths barely sucking any air through its sodden fabric as your chest tightened. Fingertips, dulled by leather gloves, couldn’t pry it from your face. The tissues of your extremities began to numb, save for the prickles of unlashed carbon dioxide bubbling acid in your blood vessels, stinging fingers and toes. As you staggered into your locker-room, the cascade of sweat settled in your boots, every scalding step squelching. You began to wilt, muscles warping you as they were worn down by the relentless grind of their leather straightjacket. The door remained ever-so-slightly ajar. What was a proud, victorious stride through gritted teeth had descended to a mangled, hunchbacked shuffle. The red raw slap of heatstroke stung your skin from the inside as you collapsed into the shower tray. Paralyzed, clawed fingers hammered at the buttons as your tortured body began to convulse it its own queasy rain dance. A silent scream mirrored your anguished mask. You felt the downpour pepper the leather of your suit. Every droplet a pin-prick. Every second it took for the water’s icy relief to penetrate your leather casing was stretched out in agony. As the leather cooled, the insulating padding that bulked out your suit held the line. Desperately fighting against oxygen-starved, roasting muscles, you again pulled at your mask. Gnashing teeth tried to gnaw and hack away at the sweat-drenched fabric and rubber. Fingers jammed into the mask’s aghast mouth and stabbed at the sodden fabric beneath, prying its jaw apart. You felt a fine droplet of icy water splash on your tongue, fuelling one last effort as you tore your ghoulish face in half. The gasping, flushed and sweaty face beneath emerged like a grotesque birthing. Gulps of icy water steamed and hissed deep in your core’s furnace, venting a slurry of vomit into the shower tray. As the paralyzing heat was flushed from your system, muscles gave soothing sighs of relief as they relaxed. Your stiff, twitching body yawned, going limp as consciousness trickled down the drain with your diluted vomit. Let nobody say you don’t suffer for your art. Youcancheckoutanytimeyoulikebutyoucanneverleave Like a butterfly emerging from its death-black chrysalis, your moist, shivering body slid out of its unbuckled suit. An arid tongue dangled from the corner of your mouth, lapping against the tiles. The astringent taste of disinfectant cleansed your palette of vomit’s syrupy tang. “The fuck long was I out?” you asked yourself; the question ricocheting around inside your throbbing skull. Clammy hands massaged numb face like you were molding clay. “Shit. Shit, shit, shit. Shit.” You were laying, naked, deep within the bowels of the arena with your mask torn in half. You were weak. You were still too weak. Within the suit you were the warrior who had beaten, choked, spiked and pinned Triumph Frost. Out of the suit, you were slipping about on the floor, legs trembling like a newborn foal. “Pull yourself together,” you urged. “Think. Just fucking think, already.” The hands that had been massaging your face now slapped it. “Stupid, stupid, stupid,” was accompanied by slap, slap, slap. “Man up.” Burying your face in the rough towel, you mopped up the sweat and vomit, each rub agony on your steam-tenderized skin. The door was shut. Raising your head from the soiled towel, you were left looking at yourself in the mirror. You couldn’t help but wonder if this was now your mask. “You look like shit,” was your truthful assessment. “Wet, runny shit.” Mummified in dirty towels, and with your cold, damp disguise wrapped up inside a torn down event poster, you slithered out into the hallway. Eyes closed, breath held, motionless and silent, you listened. Every door creak, hushed voice and distant footstep. They were closing in on you. Into the locker-room opposite your own you slipped, surgeon’s fingers delicately locking it behind you with the faintest of clinking and grinding. You had no time to be meticulous. Bags were torn open, contents sent spewing across the bench and floor. Black sweatpants, grey hooded sweatshirt, blue GCW beanie and (clearly) cheap imitation ray bans. You didn’t question the stains, just as you didn’t notice the lower half of your mask tumble out of a tear in the sodden event poster as you bundled it into the emptied hold-all. You took $80 from their over-stuffed wallet, tucking it under a shoe as way of hollow compensation, and then you left. Somedancetoremembersomedancetoforget It seemed so, so long ago. The days grew depressingly long; the sun hanging low in the sky for what seemed like hours, goading you to reach up and slam-dunk it over the horizon. There was a masochistic seed germinating within you which whispered incessantly in your waking hours, yearning for the time when the zips and buckles would imprison you within the torturous, leathery skin of Rasa. For Rasa was purpose, it was direction… and next to it, everything in between was insignificant. An echo, depleting in impetus each time it was questioned. A by-product; a waste. At dawn you yearned for dusk. At dusk you yearned for dawn. You’d run your feet raw, torching your energy reserves and consuming them in feverish wildfire. You’d unleashed a snarling, feral onslaught upon your punch bag, wild fists swinging until your oxygen-starved muscles almost tore themselves to sinewy shreds. Every exertion frayed the ropes binding you to consciousness. Every blood vessel in your brain amplified the pounding bassline of your pulse. The wave of quenching ice-water crashed against your tongue, flushing the quintet of sleeping pills down your welcoming throat. Limply, you flopped down onto the bed, where a spiteful, low sun backlit your eyelids burnt amber. Deadly still, your body lowered all its defenses to the sedatives invading its bloodstream, conceding to their will. Your body slowed down, time sped up. As the last fiber imprisoning you in the waking world snapped, the sun set behind your eyelids, and you tumbled into the darkness left in its wake. Hello, old friend.
