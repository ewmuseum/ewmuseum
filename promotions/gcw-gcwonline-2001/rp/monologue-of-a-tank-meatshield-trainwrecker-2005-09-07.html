Monologue of a Tank (Meatshield Trainwrecker)

7 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Sept 7th,2005 CLANG! A ringing chime echoes across the casually cluttered shop in a rural Missouri garage. Over and over again it rings in a clash of metal on metal, as a hammer rains down on steel. Over time the repeated blows slowly shape the metal into an arching sheet to be trimmed, fitted, and formed. Eventually it will form the breastplate of suit of armor that will be auctioned for Hurricane Katrina relief; but currently it serves as a canvas. One upon which Jim Brandt is etching his plan of action. Blow after blow, hammer on anvil. Each swing is without mercy, without pity, without fear. Jim Brandt took up smithing a long time ago out of necessity. When Jim and David (Paladin) decided to try their hand at chivalrous combat via the Society for Creative Anachronism; they were met with certain armoring requirement that had to be met before they could prove themselves in combat. For David, this seemed no problem. Any number of retailers catering to the renaissance crowd could have easily supplied the required items for a man of his size. However, the same could not be said for Jim Brandt. His massive girth left many an armorer daunted, and Jim was rebuffed and rebuked for having let his weight “get away from him”. In all the time of searching, Dave Montgomery refused to do business with any merchant that would not first do business with Jim. Many weeks passed and no end to their quest had appeared. They were at their wit’s (and the internet’s) end. Refusing defeat, Dave concocted a plan. “We’ll build our own.” Many hours of research followed. Slowly, through trial and error, piece by piece they built their armor in a handcrafted manner. The quality and design of the pieces far exceeded the all sizes fit none approach of the merchants they had previously queried. Jim’s designs while built in the most traditional of methods were an extension of his subconscious; straight forward and aggressive, yet utterly durable and efficient. They allowed him a range of articulation and protection that was unmatched, particularly for a suit of such massive size. His gift for smithing did not go unnoticed for long. As he became more and more of a force in the lists and on the field, Jim’s armor became something of a sensation in SCA and LARP circles. Indeed many of the armorers that had previously spurned his requests, offered him praise and employment. It was without sorrow that he in turn told them to “go forth, be fruitful and multiply asexually” (but with far less tact). He continues to make armor for select clientele. It became such a profitable enterprise that it allowed him to quit his regular job working for the city (a job which he despised) and allot his time to more entertaining endeavors (like GCW). An all of this is because one man refused to let him be excluded. Refused to let him be cast aside because he didn’t fit an image of what people are supposed to be like. It was a simple thing that Dave would never and had never though twice about, but it had resounded in the mind of Jim Brandt. This is why this particular piece of armor is taking shape. The savage process of shaping it is only a prelude to more savagery that will be directed at The Dimensioner. In his mind can still hear the insults. Not those directed at him, but at his friend. The Dimensioner would exclude his friend from GCW, cast him away because he doesn’t fit the image of what a wrestler should be. Oh, there are a great many things that Jim Brandt would like to say to The Dimensioner. Things about loyalty, friendship, honor, and courage. Many things? But he won’t. Because tanks don’t talk. They act. ~fin~
