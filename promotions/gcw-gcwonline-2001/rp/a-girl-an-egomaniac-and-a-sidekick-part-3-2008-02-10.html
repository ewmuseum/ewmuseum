A Girl, An Egomaniac, and a Sidekick: Part 3

10 February 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/10/08 (Takes place 8/2002) August 2002 Standing against the railing in silence as he watched the spectacle taking place inside the squared circle, Chris scoffed at the security guard who tried to heckle him back into his assigned seat. He wouldn’t be there for very long after all. He had all of one match he’d wanted to see there tonight, which gauging by his interest, was the one currently taking place this very second. Angelo Deville, a man Chris had limited run-ins with, was battling against Tessa Windsor, another person he had limited run-ins with. Things were not fairing well for Tessa, who still tried her very best and refused to give up. But with the lethal submission move Angelo used against the petite, who always was wearing pink, victory came and Tessa was eliminated from the tournament she worked so hard in. Her near Cinderella story tournament crashed before midnight. Some might assume that Chris would be glad to see Tessa lose after the argument the two had engaged in last week. But as he watched her roll out of the ring, he actually felt horrible for her. She had tried her best in what was deemed a man’s world and still managed to walk out with her head held high. Cutting back up the aisle, Chris headed toward the backstage area. Though he wasn’t wrestling, he still had enough clearance to at least talk to her after her match. He just hoped Markus wouldn’t be there to harass him about it later. It was doubtful Markus would show anyways. Without Chris, Markus didn’t have a pass to enter the backstage area. Not unless another wrestler vouched for him, which once again was doubtful. Unlike Chris, Markus didn’t have a household name. Lifting his pass for the guard to see, Chris cut past him and headed where the locker rooms were. He figured he if hurried he could most likely catch Tessa before she could get back to the locker room. Worst case being that she had already gotten to the locker room, in which case all he would have to do is wait until she exited. Actually, thinking it over again in his head, the worst case scenario was her just going straight home disappointed. He shook his head, refusing to think about it. He would wait outside the women’s locker room for a few minutes, an hour at best, and see if she ever showed up. As he waited, he thought of what he should even say. He wanted to tell her he was sorry, he just didn’t know how to say it. Chris wasn’t the kind of man that apologized much. But even he knew he spoke out in anger, not at her, but rather at losing to a man like Draven in a fashion that he did. An hour had passed, and still no sign that Tessa was in the building. Chris anxiously looked down at his Rolex, and watched the second hand complete its rotation. Then watched it do a full 360, again, and again, and again. “What a waste of time,” He muttered to himself, angrily throwing his pass to the ground, “why’d I even come back?” “Good question.” A female voice heckled from the background. He need not look up, as it was obvious who it was. Now the question was how to explain himself. “You were bored?” Even after losing, Tessa’s spirit had yet to be crushed. Chris supposed she was still on a high about getting as far as she did. Chris stared down at the ground, suddenly losing what he had intended to say. “Look,” he started, hoping the words would come to him. “I just wanted to say?” He never got to say what he wanted though, getting cut off by a heavy sigh from Tessa, “You don’t need to say anything. Look, I’m sorry for the other night. I guess I got a little full of myself.” Another delayed reaction coming off her match, this time however, she was on the losing end. And now her spirits were slowly being drowned by the thought of having to go back to XCWF, and wrestle the same women day in and day out. Her fifteen minutes of fame were over. “No, I’m sorry, I was being rude” Chris answered back, kicking himself for not apologizing first. “I said some things I shouldn’t have, took my anger out on you when you didn’t deserve it.” For a moment, Tessa looked relieved that Chris wasn’t there to gloat over her loss like he had predicted their last meeting. But she still couldn’t shake the feeling that something was wrong, something that he wasn’t saying. She couldn’t tell why, it was just a feeling based off his body language. Her eyes stared over towards Chris, waiting for him to say something else. He could feel her eyes on him, but he kept his own eyes down at the ground where his foot rubbed imaginary dirt into the concrete. “So uh, I was wondering if you wouldn’t mind meeting me.” He paused for a moment. “Later.” “What for?” He finally lifted his ocean blue eyes up to her, “Just to apologize more properly.” His head turned, looking both ways, “Plus there is something important I wanted to talk to you about.” Again he paused, as if he was waiting for something to happen out of the blue. “Alone.” “Alone?” She questioned, as if he were speaking a language totally foreign to her. For one, he hardly seemed that interested in her based off their past encounters. And if there needed to be another reason to feel so caught off guard, you could add in the fact that HE WAS kinda in the peak of his career. He was rich, famous, handsome, and here he was asking her for time alone. “You don’t have to?” He intervened, after her pause seemed to insinuate that she wasn’t all that interested, but too nice to say no. “Oh no!” She quickly pulled herself out of her daze. It certainly was not what it appeared in his eyes, “I’d love to. But where would you want to go?” Chris shifted his eyes so to look both ways down the hall, to make sure that Markus had not managed to sneak his way in. “I know a place” he said to her, “not far from the arena.” Those crystal blue eyes turned to her, examining her up and down. “Great,” As gleeful as she sounded, she was twice as nervous, “what time?” “We’ll make it for eight” he said, taking a moment to look at his watch as if he were confirming the time with himself. “Eight o’clock will be fine.” “Alright, eight.” The pink gym bag that sat on her side, was now slung around her shoulders as her ponytail bobbed up and down with her head, “See you then.”
