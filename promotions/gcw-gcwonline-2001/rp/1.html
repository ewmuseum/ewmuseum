Date: for Christmas Chaos Tommy 1999 “What're they doin' to Pop, Honey?” Benny's breaking voice grates with a whine, like fingers down a chalk-board, to the ears of Honey Donahue, a.k.a Honey Bee. Together, they stood in the hall. Honey holding the ten year-old's hand while the other presses against her chest, stilling her own beating heart. He'd been in there for over thirty minutes now. Her knees had begun to grow stiff. She couldn't believe how well behaved this kid was, standing still holding her hand for the last thirty minutes. Barely making a peep until now. He was a good, honest kid and Honey had a soft spot for him. Even if he wasn't of her womb. She loved him like he was. “I dunno, darlin', but they's got him in there for some time now, huh?” Texan drawl, inbred with the Jersey dialect, left Honey with a sexy and dangerous tone. Her tongue darted over her lips as her peridot coloured eyes watched the door, as if she could stare through it and into the conversation beyond. “Quite some time.” “They angry at him or somethin'?” Peering wondrously at the buxom blond clutching his paw, Benny peered at her more like an oracle of wisdom than like any other males she knew. Benny had friends, even at ten years of age, calling her a MILF (thanks to the recent release of American Pie). Benny hadn't quite figured out the abbreviation, or what it actually means, but he knew they found his step-mother attractive. Some of the older boys made comments that made the ten year-old cover his ears and made his belly squirm. He didn't know what up the ass meant but he was certain it was a known cause of cootees. “Did Pop do somethin' wrong, Honey?” “Yer Pop ain't done a thang wrong, darlin'. Don't you worry that pretty little head of yours 'bout nothin'. Yer Pop's gon' come outta this smellin' like roses. I can feel it in muh waters.” Subconsciously, her right hand patted heart, as if trying to calm it. Honey worried it might burst out of her chest. She squeezed Benny's hand just a little more. To soothe herself more-so than the kid. She'd read the newspaper headlines. She'd seen the guy from the Times snooping around their bins. Following from a few blocks back as they drove down the street. That nosey bastard. What's his name? Eddie Poe. What an asshole. When she got her hands on him... that sonofabitch would have to scratch his throat if his balls were itchy. “I mean, it's gotta be like five hours now.” “Ain't been five hours, Benny. Half an hour. Tops, Tommy.” “Feels like it.” “I know, darlin'. You jus' be patient now. We just gotta be here fer ya pop when he gets outta there. He's gunna needed us.” Her hand patted her heart again. “We gotta be strong, Tommy. You can be strong.” “How much longer's this gonna be?” “I dunno.” “Me either. This sucks. Wish they'd just let Pop outta there.” “Me, too, sweetie. Me, too.” Honey glared up the hall, exhaling heavily as if she might be able to let all her troubles dispel with her breath. But it just seemed to make more room for the weight to gather in her chest. Gave her troubles room to grow. Her stomach rumbled, catching the ten year-old off guard and he giggled at how loud the grumble was. “Gosh, Honey. You sure must be hungry. You sound just like a big ol' Grizzly Bear.” As if on cue, Benny's belly grumbled a small grumble that pushed Honey Bee to chuckle, covering her mouth with her free hand. “Looks like I'm not the only one. You sound just like a baby bear.” A grin spread across Benny's face like a fire through a field of dry grass. Grinning stupidly up at his oracle with a solitary thought consuming his mind. Baby Bear? Just like Pop. That grin of his plastered thicker across his face. Just like my Pop. Nick 1989 The fans roar echoed in the Guerilla position, resonating off the steel frames holding the stage up. The mask on his face seemed to be growing tighter and Nick Esposito, a.k.a. Baby Bear, poked a finger in the underside of his mask and loosened it from his throat. He could've sworn the walls were coming closer and it felt like the floor were threatening to rise up from below and kiss his face with the tenderness of a cinder-block hurtling one hundred mile an hour. Holding the steel frame, Baby Bear steadied himself, feeling his mask fill with sweat. His heart beat at the top of his throat, making it harder for him to suck air. I'm havin' a panic attack. How's Pop gunna like this?, he thought, pressing his back against the cold steel. At the end of the hall, a door opened out stepped an ecliptic figure looming in the flourescence of the hall lighting. He bounced his head from shoulder-to-shoulder, as if they were rubber before taking his first luggish steps, ambling toward Baby Bear. Baby Bear had never seen his father backstage like this. He'd known the presence the man had in the industry but seeing him lumbering down the hall toward him seemed almost surreal as his presence preceded him and made the butterflies gently flittering in Baby Bear's gut feel like a swarm of killer bees threatening to burst out of his abdomen. “You ready?” His voice rumbled as he adjusted his own mask. The Grizzly Bear mask. He loomed over his son, hands on hips, waiting for the boy to respond. It was al management's idea to put them together. For Dad to show his boy the ropes of the biz. Grizzly Bear had never tag teamed in his life but he was satisfied enough to bend his rules all for the sakes of his son learning the ropes. He wasn't happy with the name they'd come up with for the tag team. The Teddy Bear Picnic. He couldn't see the funny side. What with him being the Grizzly Bear and all. He told them to come up with something else. The Bear Necessities. After Baloo's song in that Disney film. He figured it was better than the pansy name they'd first thrown on the table and Grizzly Bear set the monniker before they could sicken him with something worse. “Was born ready, Pop.” “Was born ready. Listen to him,” he said, speaking to nobody. “Dat's my boy. Born ready. You need a piss?” Baby Bear shook his head before second-guessing himself. Do I need a piss? Maybe. I dunno. I can't tell nothin' from my body right now. He shook his head again. “Nah. I'm good.” “You sure? Ya don't wanna go pissin' ya pants out in front'a dem fans, kiddo. You need ta piss... ya better go get on wit' it.” Half stepping away, half staying in place, Baby Bear couldn't decide. His body was sending him so many mixed messages it was almost impossible for him to understand what it was trying to tell him. Poor Baby Bear didn't know if he was coming or going. Eventually he conceded. “I better piss.” “Hurry up wit' it. And make sure ya shit is tucked in again. I remember poor ol' Joey Kamakazito went out dere with his tackle hangin' out. Smallest thing I ever seen. Broads was callin' out... askin' who'd bit his Oscar Meyer's in half.” A chuckle rumbled from Grizzly Bear's belly. “Poor fuck.” “I'm goin' to piss.” “Then go. Fuck me.
