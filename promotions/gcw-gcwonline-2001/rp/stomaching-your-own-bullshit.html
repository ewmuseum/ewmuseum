Date: Once upon a time... Once upon a time, a long time ago, in a world that seems so far away from where I am right now, they called me a star. They called me a champion. They thought I was the best thing to ever step-foot inside of a wrestling ring, ever grab a microphone and run. They called me things like "the Next Big Thing" and "Pro-Wrestling's Fastest Rising Star". And they didn't lie. Hell, I even believed the hype myself. Who could blame me? I could have put rat shit in my hand and the fans would eat it up like sheep at a petting zoo. I could give them any bologne I damn well wanted and they'd listen. And cheer. And cry their little eyes out if I took the time out of my life to sign an autograph here and get in a photo with a crippled kid there. I gave the fans what they wanted and they gave me what I wanted. A career. A long-standing career within the fickle walls of professional wrestling. The fickle, FICKLE walls of professional wrestling. And now I stand here the shell of a man I once was. Angry at the buisness? Nah... how could I be? I took that risk myself to put myself in THAT position that's put me this way and I can't finger the blame at wrestling. No. Can I blame the fans? Hell no. They made me into what I was and I wanted to give them everything I had. No holds barred. Everything it took to get them standing from the seats they paid their "hard-earned" for and cheer voraciously for me. I can't blame the fans. Can I blame HardCase? GOD no! The man put me in the spotlight. Probably the best program I ever worked in a promotion. Hell, I had the best time of my life in Action! Wrestling and the worst time of my life. Yeah, definitely the worst. These days when I look down at the shrivelled wrinkly stub that was once a wrist for a hand I once knew I just simply sigh. I look at ol' lefty, the one who remained, and think to myself "ya miss him, don't-cha lefty?" April continued to tilt her head from one side to the other, peering into my eyes as though she were trying to tell me something telepathically which was really not working. And my powers of deliberation were telling me she'd had a stroke but still had the ability cook whereas she lost the functions to communicate. She let out one of those content "Hrmmmm" sighs as she took a deep breath to get to her point, which obviously lay deep and dormant within the confines of her body. "I'm..." then she did the point at me, point at herself, point at me, point at herself routine "...WE'RE pregnant!" And the she did one of those girly squeals. I hate those squeals. Seriously. I hate 'em. She shrugged her shoulders and wrapped her arms around me, pulling me further from my breakfast and planting a big, wet, soppy kiss on the side of my cheek which I thought of wiping off but decided better of it. She wrapped her hands over the sides of my face and planted another wet one, right on my lips. She got up and skipped out of the room leaving me with my pig flesh, eggs and new case of indigestion due to the new ulcer which seemingly appeared out of nowhere. Pregnant? I gave him a "pffft" and swung my good hand in uproar at his suggestions. My mind had been in this character for such a long time it was hard to tell where the character ends and I began these days. April pulled me up on it around the house constantly, telling me off for trying to belittle her for a cheap pop. 'Cepting the fact there ain't no fool 'round to hear my witty remarks. The fly turned his beady head around to see if anyone else was seeing the display I was putting on. I was a professional to the end and probably forever would be. I'll be one of those guys with his zimmerframe in the mall still calling on his catchphrases, hoping someone asks if I was somebody. "Too bad. Grab a number. Get in line. No spoilers. Not for you. Not today. Bye-bye now," and with that I shoved him in the shoulder and walked by him. The transparency of my little spat against the "MAN" (wrestling tabloids) was disgusting. That guy would go back to his little computer desk and remark about how Brock Shepherd has been cast aside and forgotten about. Those three EWA Intercontinental titles? Shine 'em up on your mantle piece, Brock, coz nobody gives a shit anymore. Yeah. I've read all the stories. All the lies and all the truths. The lies don't bother me. Its like water on a ducks back, rolling down his spine and dripping off his tail feathers. It's the truth that stings. The reality I'm having to face of finding something else in life. Hell, I couldn't even get a job stocking a supermarket shelf with a hand like this. Speaking tours? Please. That's what all the has-beens do and there's nothing I'd rather do less, and that's become a has-been. 9 years in this business and I've found myself at a cross-roads that I don't think I can overcome. I walk down the street of Seattle, my hometown, a place that once roared my name into the rafters night after night, and I feel like a ghost. I couldn't walk down the street once upon a time. If I went into a supermarket it'd take me three hours to grab the bottle of milk and leave. Now? The pimply runt at the counter reminds me about how my last cheque bounced and the owner will only accept cash from me these days. Spinebuster Wrestling stung the cash flow. Damn motherfuckers going into liquidation and leaving me and a bunch of other guys high and dry. I pocketed my prosthetic hand in my right pocket and I sigh longingly thinking of those golden times where being me was good. I needed these walks. To reflect and tell myself that everything would be alright. I could tell myself that 'til the cows come home, I still never believed myself. I couldn't even stomach my own brand of bullshit. I stopped at a men's clothing store, staring at the Ralph Lauren Polo shirts in the window, shading my eyes with my good hand and pressing against the window. There was a tug at my leg and for a moment I thought I was being pick-pocketed. I spun around to find myself staring at a little kid. Maybe eight years old. I dropped to one knee and placed my good hand on his shoulder. "Hi," he confidently said to me. "Are you Brock Shepherd?" I gazed around to see if anyone had put this kid up to this. Why anyone would bother was beyond me but when you start being fingered as a shoulda-woulda-coulda-been you get a little edgey and paranoid about everyone else with a career. "Well, yeah. And what's your name, kid-O?" "Its Andrew, sir. My daddy is a big fan of yours," he mentioned to me. I smiled. A true smile. The first one that seemed to address my weary face in the longest time. So long in fact I don't remember the last time I did smile. Then again, I'm not one to mark smiles on my calendar so the next time I smile I'll probably say the same damn thing. "Well, Andrew, your daddy sounds like a clever man," I replied, ruffling his hair doing the goodwill to kids thing. Kids were the best thing about wrestling. All them years those little brats cheered me on, their faces lighting up when I tossed them a pair of neon green shades. Squealing with delight at a simple signature. That's what it was all about. I always made time for the kids, even when I was playing the bad guy. As long as nobody else saw me. "I like you, too. You're funny. When are you wrestling again?" the kid asked, with a complete truth that seemingly left an empty feeling inside of me. I could feel myself breaking away on the inside and a strange feeling developing in my throat. A lump had grown and it wasn't cancerous, though it was for my career if someone saw me. I choked on it a little as I tried to battle with it to murmur a response. My hand rested on the kid's shoulder a moment as I tried to gather myself together. "Ya see, Andrew. I dunno, pal. I just... I just dunno," was all I could muster. Another ruffle of the hair and I could bear it no longer, moving on down the street and wiping some dew from my eye. A tear. A misty, solitary tear that managed to battle the defence my eyelids had been upholding for quite some time. This kid just downright broke my heart. When was I wrestling again? When could I get inside a wrestling ring again? When? Oh fuck, God. When?
