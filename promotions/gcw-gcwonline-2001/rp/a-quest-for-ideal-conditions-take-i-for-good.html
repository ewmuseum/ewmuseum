A Quest for Ideal Conditions Take I: For Good

Date: Past/Present It's 280 miles from Los Angeles to Las Vegas, but it feels more like an entire day's journey. Once you navigate out of the greater Los Angeles area, Interstate 15 takes you the rest of the way from the California desert through the Nevada desert and up into the heart of anything and everything sin. I've made this drive too many times before, but never with DeBough. Dice is driving my Escalade and I'm passed out in the passenger seat. How am I narrating this, you ask? I'm omniscient, so relax. It's 4 a.m. and this trip is spur-of-the-moment ? back the fuck off my narrative choices. Anyways, none of that even matters, because in a matter of moments, I'll be wide awake. --- Three years ago in Carson City, Nevada, I was arrested during a wrestling show while working for Carson City Wrestling (C2W), an affiliate promotion within the umbrella organization known as the World Wrestling Alliance. This wasn’t one of those fake, for-the-show arrests either. Nah. See, a long-time nemesis of mine, "The Wolverine" Michael Lennox, had signed his death warrant, allowing me to book our big blow-off match at a C2W pay-per-view. The result was something involving various weapons and a pit full of snakes. I'm not sure exactly what was involved besides the snakes actually. I don't remember much from that night. What I do remember is Lennox whipping me with a boa constrictor until I finally got my wits about me, knocked him off his feet and cut the snake in half with a chainsaw. This is why I went to jail. Animal cruelty, if you can believe it. Color me a little confused, because Lennox ? the guy striking me repeatedly with the massive reptile ? got off scot-free. I wound up bludgeoning dude nearly half to death moments later with something ? not sure what, maybe a sickle ? and he didn't get up for quite a while. That match was the beginning of the end for C2W. That show would turn out to be one of the promotion’s final shows. The snake thing didn't go over well with a lot of people, and they showed their displeasure. I don't remember how the riot began, but I know I wound up in handcuffs and C2W wound up in the red shortly after. I was able to make bail ? 25 grand ? and avoid any legal repercussion, save a few hours of community service. The press ? both positive and negative ? that came out of that match established me as a star not only regionally, but alliance-wide. Two weeks later, I was WWA World Heavyweight Champion. It was on that night, however, that I promised to give up wrestling forever. It just wasn't worth it. Sure, I stuck around physically for a little while longer. But mentally ? I was already gone. What I remember from that night ? the snake, the handcuffs, the promise ? is what I'm dreaming of at the moment DeBough starts screaming like he's being chased by a machete-wielding Jason Voorhees. It’s what I dream about whenever I’m about to sin again. --- "GAAAAAAAHHHHHHH!" DeBough's scream would have shattered glass if this were a cheesy roleplay. Instead, it just violently wakes me from my slumber. "The fuck, man? Must you be all screamy when a dude's sawin' some zees?" DeBough is breathing heavily. "Dude. There. Was like. A fuckin'. Moose. Or something." I yawn, shake my head. "Wait, what?" DeBough pulls the car off to the side of the road. I sit up straight. He puts the car in park, leans back, puts his hands on top of his head, says, "A moose, dude. A fuckin' moose. In the road. Scared the shit outta me." I furrow my brow, look around the inside of the car. "Where's it at?" "The moose? It's gone." DeBough makes a vanishing motion with his hands. "Vanished into thin air." "Not the moose, you fag. The acid you're on." DeBough double-takes me. "Wait, what? What acid?" "Do you even know where the fuck we are? We're not in Alaska, homey. Do you see snow and ice and hot chicks named Bristol? There aren’t any moose in the desert." "Isn't it mooses?" "Why the fuck would it be mooses?" I say. "What the fuck is your deal? You're seein' animals that don't exist in this part of the country and you're makin' up plural forms of words." DeBough rubs his hands over his face. "I'm sorry, man, I'm sorry. I've been out of it lately." "Well, snap out of it, ese." Looking around I say, "Where the fuck are we anyways?" "Not even to Nevada yet." "Fuck," I say whiningly. "I'm goin' back to sleep. Wake me up when you see a Del Taco." --- Did I mention that match, between me and Lennox, was for 500 grand? True story. Complicated one too. Before I even entered into this spat with Lennox, I met a man by the name Jason Mezzonote. He owns and operates a few casinos in the state of Nevada. Rich man. Connected man. Generous man too. But if you ever meet him, don’t cross him. As it turns out, Mezzonote had sort of invested in me through a mutual friend ? one Eric Dane. Mezzonote sent Dane on a mission to recruit me, for lack of a better word. Dane showed up at my apartment one night, briefcase in one hand, cocaine in the other. He said, "We need to talk." He made it apparent to me Mezzonote was prepared to offer me a million dollars to punk out this cat named Lennox. After a few lines, Dane propped open the briefcase. "Half a mill," he said, a small dab of white hanging out of his nose. He said, "Dude thinks you’re the only one hard enough to beat this guy at his own game." If you know Dane, you know he wouldn’t say anything like that unless he believed it himself. Could have easily lied, that one. No reason he couldn’t have done it himself either, to be honest. "He wants him out ," he continued to say, "for good. Half now, half later." I looked at him for a moment, I remember, before snorting another line. I sat back, sniffed, rubbed, thought about just what the hell was going on here. I remember saying, "For good?" Dane nodded, got up, let himself out. Open briefcase left sitting on my coffee table. --- "GAAAAAAAHHHHHHH!" DeBough's scream would have shattered whatever glass was left after the previous one if this were a cheesy roleplay. Instead, it just violently wakes me from my slumber again. "The fuck, man? Must you be all screamy when a dude's sawin' some zees?" "Didn’t you say that last time?" I yawn, shake my head. "What the fuck is goin? on now? You didn’t see a panda did you?" "Like 40 minutes ago, but I decided not to wake you. Del Taco, though, dude. Next exit." I rub the sleep out of my eyes. "I said wake me up when we get to a Del Taco, not when we’re an exit away. Shit." "Actually you said when I see a Del Taco. But either way, what’s the difference?" "Well, I have to sit here and talk to you now or somethin?." DeBough stink-eyes me with his right eye. I reach out and turn the satellite up. "Witchy Woman" by the Eagles. DeBough sighs. I say, "You hate the Eagles, don’t you?" --- "You’re gettin? what?" DeBough’s visibly confused, furrowing his brown at the drive-thru menu that’s still a few cars away. "Number three," I say. "Tacos del carbon." "I prefer nitrogen." DeBough laughs one of those laughs where no noise comes out ? his head just moves like he’s laughing. "You’re a funny guy, dude," I say sarcastically. "Seriously. Hell, if I didn’t know any better I’d think I was sittin? here with Milton fuckin? Berle on Texaco Star Theater." DeBough furrows his brow. "Huh?" "Exactly. Shut the fuck up and learn somethin?. Get the tacos del carbon ? best thing on the menu." DeBough waives his hand in the air like he just don’t care. "Whatever." --- "Man, these nitrogen tacos are wickedly delicious." DeBough bites into a steak taco del carbon, drips chili sauce down the front of his shirt. "Aw fuck," he says. I glance over, pausing from my chicken taco del carbon, say, "See, Dice, the key to eatin? the nitrogen taco is to not spill the nitrogen all over the place, especially in my car." With one hand still on the wheel, Dice furiously wipes away at his shirt with a napkin. "Well if you’d fuckin? drive your own car I wouldn’t spill shit, dude." I’m getting irritated, so I place my taco back into its wrapper on my lap. "Lemme ask you somethin?, dude. In this relationship we have here, who’s the one bringin? home the bacon?" Furrowing his brow, DeBough says, "What bacon?" "Thee bacon," I say. "In other words, I gots some gold now." I pat my waist. "That’s why you’re drivin? me to Vegas, bitch. This is a celebration! And I’m the guest of honor!" DeBough’s expression is blank. "I’m kidding," I say. "No celebration ? just gambling, alcohol and prostitutes." --- Turns out Dane was the one who recommended me to Mezzonote for the job. I found this out when I went to Henderson to meet him a few days later. I told Dane to tell him I needed clarification on some things, but really I just wanted to meet the guy. Mezzonote is short and stocky, like George Costanza sans glasses. Smokes cigars like they’re going out of style. Actually went to high school with the editor-in-chief of Cigar Aficionado and can get you any kind of cigar in the world. When I show up, he had a box full of fresh Cubans with my name on them. We sat down for a drink and Mezzonote told me he’s connected to people I don’t want to know. He said, "I’ve got people all over the place ? every state, every country, on fuckin? Jupiter." He said, "I got people you know, people you’ve traveled the highways with." Already knowing the answer, I said, "Dane?" and he nodded. He added, "Among others." He slid the box of cigars across the table to me, said, "If the 500 kay wasn’t a sign of good faith, take some of my own personal stash." I sipped on my whiskey as he stood up and buttoned up his suit jacket. "I don’t mean to run off," he said, "but I’m a busy man." I nodded and he began to walk away. "Jason," I said. He turned around, pulled the cigar out of his mouth, exhaled. I cleared my throat. "For good?" He nodded, turned, walked away. Last time I ever saw him. Well, at least until DeBough and I got to Vegas.
