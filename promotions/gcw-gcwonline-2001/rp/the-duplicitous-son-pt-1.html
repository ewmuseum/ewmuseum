The Duplicitous Son, pt.1

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Present Day Present Day The middle of the evening. A dingy bar in Portland, Oregon. Thomas James LeStrange sat in the driver's seat of his Aston Martin DB-9. He couldn't have chosen surroundings more incongruous to his choice in automobile if he'd tried. In complete contrast to the sleek, elegant lines of the car, its stunning British Racing Green finish and luxurious cream leather interior, the neon light advertising the 'Aces High' bare flickered, a fitting testament to this shoddy area of town. The property on which the bar was located was situated between two busy streets. The noise was unbearable as vehicles sped past, the constant crescendos and diminuendos of the engines creating a strange, ever-present throbbing in one's ears. Still, thought Thom, one couldn't blame motorists for wanting to charge through at the swiftest speed possible. Lining the streets on either side of the bar were dingy, dismal looking stores, all barely covering their costs and fighting constantly for their survival in this hellish part of town. Above the shops were cramped apartments, in which the dirty-faced impoverished valiantly limped through each and every day, knowing full well that their situation was practically untranscendable, just murdering time until they could shuffle off this mortal coil. The streets were lined with the potent combination of litter and dog-foul; whenever the wind blew the former blustered, whipped up into the air before floating back down to a new position, and the stench of the latter wafted towards one's nostrils. Welcome to Skid Row, LeStrange murmured to himself. It wasn't exactly the locale one would expect to find our intrepid Englishman, but perhaps therein lay its very appeal. He felt a sense of freedom here, of absolute liberty to do what he wished without fear of consequence. He was barely recognised here. People here were far too involved in desperately trying to keep the seams of their existence from tearing to really pay attention to him anyway. The police didn't dare poke their nose in this area; they just allowed its citizens to do as they pleased, and hoped to keep the chaos contained within this one district. Controlled anarchy, if you will. A gang of foolhardy youths had attempted to part Thom from his expensive car. They had been nothing but a gang of youths, probably not even eighteen years of age. Had they been older, Thom may have considered stepping back, but they were young, and hadn't the look of hardened criminals. They were brash, and filled with the unfounded bravado and swagger of the young, assuming a few threatening words and some idle, empty threats to be enough to intimidate the majority of the population. Thom had quickly set them to rights, rapidly grappling the eldest and cockiest into a hammerlock, wrenching the boy's arm until he squealed in pain, and forcing his nose down into the tires of the car, telling him to sniff the rubber, as it was as close as he would ever get to that particular automobile. And so it was that Thom found himself alone and at peace, aside from the constant roar of passing cars. He sat in the driver's seat of his car, but the engine was off. He had kept the stereo on, and was currently listening to a soothing Ewald brass quintet concerto. The greeny-yellow light of the stereo's LCD screen provided the sole illumination, casting deep shadows in the recesses of Thom's face. He began to drift off into his thoughts. The loss at Rampage still stung. The frustration could easily have been appeased for a loss to anyone else, to a respectable opponent who had fairly outwitted and outwrestled him. But every time he cast his mind back to the motley crew of oddballs, misfits and simpletons that had managed to best him in Japan, he shuddered and hung his head in shame. He constantly chided himself for allowing himself to let his guard down, for allowing Clyde Fox to incapacitate him. How had it happened?! The former Television Champion seemed to be on about the same intellectual level as a cricket ball, and equally thick-skulled. From what LeStrange had ascertained, his primary interest and raison d'etre was a strange Oriental phenomenon called 'Pokemon'. And, from what LeStrange could tell, a fair proportion of children regarded the craze with a certain degree of cynicism and contempt. And this overgrown, hyperactive little juvenile had somehow toppled he, Thomas James LeStrange. It was a horrifying and disconcerting thought. And what of the eventual victor, Zacharia Taylor? He had been handed the victory on a silver plate, thanks to the interruption of Tyson DeBough, who had finished off Fox single-handedly, allowing Taylor to make the easy pin. What a way to win a title - completely ignominously. The fact that Taylor had been allowed to win in such a shameful, disreputable fashion only served to reinforce the bitterness and bile that coursed through Thom LeStrange's body every time he cast his mind back to the sordid events. He didn't deserve to win, he mused. The fellow could barely hold himself together - a religious nut who followed Chris Bagwell, perhaps the least saintly man known to man, around like a pathetic lapdog. How could someone so busy scurrying around preoccupied with the hypocritical gibberings of such a broken and sanctimonious institution manage to pin he, Thomas James LeStrange. It beggared belief. Beaten by a nitwit and a pious nut, Thom thought. Whatever next? As Thom came to from his reverie, he returned to the task previously at hand. In his lap, on one of his thighs, sat a cigarette paper. Opening the glove compartment of the car, he extracted a packet of tobacco, and placed a handful in the open paper. Returning the tobacco to its home, he then withdrew from the glove compartment a booklet of card filter papers, and tore off a strip. He carefully folded the end down, back and forth, until he had created an 'M' shape, with the last downward slope of the M descending down into the remainder of the card. He rolled the excess around the 'M', creating a perfectly cylindrical filter, the 'M' shape sitting in the middle and filling out the shape. It was now time for the final ingredient. Thom reached into the front pocket of an elegant leather satchel will lay on the passenger seat, and groped around until his fingers clasped around a plastic object. He removed his hand, and the cannabis grinder he clutched in his hand caught a ray of light from the neon sign of the bar, and duly reflected it into his eyes. Muttering and expectorating to himself, he twisted the top of the grinder whilst clutching the bottom, the sharp plastic teeth grinding the buds of cannabis it contained into a finer, more powdery form. Satisfied that he had ground the cannabis for an ample length of time, he carefully removed the top half of the grinder, and with his thumb and forefinger, took a generous pinch of the drug, which he duly sprinkled evenly over the tobacco. He repeatred this process several more times before putting away the grinder and picking up the now-loaded paper with both hands. He rolled the paper back and forth between his thumb and forefinger, compacting the mixture until it attained a cylindrical form, before nimbly rolling it, tight and even. As the music rose to a crescendo, Thom raised the joint to his lips, flicking his lighter and instantly illuminating the interior of the car. As he breathed in, the end caught the flame and glowed amber as Thom inhaled. The joint now lit and his first inhalation now in his lungs, Thom relaxed back into his chair and sighed, slowly exhaling smoke and savouring the sensation. For the remainder of the Ewald movement, Thom slowly smoked his joint, a satisfied but serene and tranquil smile crowning his handsome features. After a short while, the joint was smoked all the way down to the filter paper. Thom wound down the window, and tossed the butt outside, where it skidded a few yards across the asphalt before coming to a final resting place. Thom wound up the window again, collected his personal items, and got out of the car. With a slam of the door and the 'beep beep' of the central locking, Thom strode over to the entrance of the bar, now relaxed and at ease, and entered the hazy, dark lair beyond. The interior of the Aces High bar was dark. The only light came from the bar, and the jukebox, filling the room with intimidating shadows from which clients rose and into which they shrank again, like beasts from a marsh. Smoke from cigarettes made the scarce light hazy, as if Thom was walking towards some secluded town through mist. He strode to the bar and sat down on a stool. A gruff-looking barman appeared; broad, with no neck, a bald head, and a beard. He took Thom's order - Jack Daniel's on the rocks - and retreated into the gloom to fetch the Englishman's order. His drink arrived and paid for, Thom took a sip, savouring the sharp, sour taste which he enjoyed so much. Placing the drink back on the bar, he gazed around to take stock of his surroundings. Beside him sat a young woman, her vim, vigour, youth and looks all crudely juxtaposed by the seamy surroundings. She was not stunning (although what the majority called "stunning" these days - dyed blonde hair, fake tan, short skirt and large breasts - was not exactly to Thom's taste), but attractive in an unconventional fashion. She was extremely petite; slender but not skinny, with curves that fit her body naturally. Her hair was shoulder length, brown, and wavy. Her jaw and cheekbones were soft, but she had large, deep brown eyes and a warming smile. They caught eyes, and Thom smiled and raised his glass, politely, to which she silently responded. They each took a sip before the young woman spoke. "Do I recognise you?" she asked. "I shouldn't imagine you do, I fear I'm far too unexciting to be recognised." "Ah, I know now - I recognise your accent. You're that British wrestler, aren't you?" "I am indeed, although I must be incredibly rude and correct you - I am English. Britain incorpates Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland too, and I shouldn't like to be sullied by association with those nations!" "My mistake." "Twas no mistake - merely a lack of meaningless information - do not trouble yourself. So how, if it's not too intrusive to ask, do you know that I wrestle?" "My fianc? is a GCW fan. I get forced to sit and watch it." "Ah, I see. So might I ask your name?" "Of course - Karen MacKay. I'm a journalist here in Portland" "An exquisite name, as I expected. I'm Thom LeStrange." "LeStrange - I knew it was something like that!" They both chuckled, but the conversation was swiftly punctured by the nasal screech of Thom's mobile telephone. Excusing himself politely, Thom turned on his stool to face away from the young lady. He accepted the call and raised the instrument to his ear. "LeStrange." "Thom, it's Derek." "Derek?! How are you!" "Well, but I'm afraid I bear bad news. Are you alone?" Thom glances back at Karen, apparently nervous now at the prospect of bad news. "To all intents and purposes. If it's bad news, I want to know now." "Well... This is hard to tell you, Thom... Your parents are dead. There was a car accident. I don't know anything more - I'll let you know anything else I hear as soon as it comes to me." "I... I see... Th-thank you Derek..." Thom flipped his phone shut and returned it to his pocket. He turned back around on his chair, back to face Karen, silent now, his face ashen. He reached for his glass, and swallowed the entirety of the remainder, grimacing at the bittersweet burning at the back of his throat. Karen had sensed the news was grave, and sat silently and awkwardly, torn between conflicting thoughts - should she leave this relative stranger, or should she ask and risk being rude, or worse, being lumbered with the responsibility of support and succour? "I'd hate to be the source of any discomfort on your part, Karen. You can go, I shan't burden you. It was a pleasure to meet you." Karen left, silently and swiftly, leaving Thom alone, gazing into space. After a short while, he moved his blank, pale, shocked gaze up to the barman, and ordered two more Jack Daniel's on the rocks, now feeling as if he were on automatic pilot, like the universe was unfolding around him beyond his control. They were dead. Fuck.
