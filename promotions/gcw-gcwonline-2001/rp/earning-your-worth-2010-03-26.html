Earning Your Worth

26 March 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/26/10 Lumpy's sports bar wasn't the finest establishment Jay had discovered in America, but it did Salt Lake City proud. As they said, they had class - even if it was coach. Pounding back another Jack Daniels, Terror turned to his Worldwide partner, Clyde Fox. The two of them made Lumpy's their home, riding the mediocre catchphrase by the coat tails. For Fox, he washed his memories of LeStatt Knight while Jay swallowed the idea that he'd been ousted by the most normal man in Just Wrestling. Between the two, there was nothing worth striking up conversation-wise. The only thing keeping the bartender serving them, in fact, were the two Salt Lake locals they'd found before arriving. For the price, they were two of the better looking women Utah had to offer. Terror turned to Annie, his hired hand, and grinned. "Remember when I beat tha' Brawler an' fronta' his people. That was some'in. People loved it. An' they said he was tooooo much." Annie, whose legs were spread and eyes were stuck on a couple in a corner, nodded. "I remember, hon. You're really something." "I'm the some'in! You're my some'in!" Terror turned his attention to Clyde who tipped his white glass of nutritious milk back, swallowed the end of it, and slammed his cup onto the bar. Fox wore a creamy mustache. "Need another, Clyde?" "I'm in tewworible need of one! Get it! You're Jay Tewwor and I said Tewworible!" Barbie, Clyde's female, shook her head. Terror smiled at Clyde and raised his hand to get another round of drinks. The tender supplied Terror with his Jack and refilled Clyde with another dose of two percent goodness. Jay took a fresh sip before pulling out his box of Marlboros. Lighting a cigarette, he leaned back on the barstool, nearly falling over, and turned again to Annie. "Remember when I beat Jason O'Neil? That was some'in too. That guy had a punch to 'im!" Annie's attention wasn't with them. Her eyes were fixed on the couple, namely the man. Her open skirt sold as she offered a wink. It wasn't until Terror nearly fell on her that she replied, "I remember. You're the best, hon." "Tha' best ever. I know." "We're the bestest team. Maybe they'll call us Jayde Ferror!" "That sounds like'a girl's name, Clyde." Clyde giggled and took a swig of milk. "Tewworible Clyde?" "What makes you so Teww - er - terrorible?" "When I don't shower like mom tells me, I 'mell tewworible. An' I give good wedgies!" "Do it to Knight," laughs Terror. Neither man is stable with the large sums of Lumpy's beverages clouding their minds. As for the women, Annie only stays for another five minutes. Her sex sells for the relationship-bound man and the free show is enough to pull him away from his table and toward the bathroom for twenty minutes. Barbie manages to slip away just as easily, although it's alone, leaving Clyde and Jay with the dairy and Jack. Terror lights a new cigarette, offers the box to Clyde, and retracts it after the Prince of the Playroom turns it down. "Not a smoker?" "Tobacco is whacko! I saw a fwog 'moking a cigarette on a pwoster once and I heard they have warts. I don't wike warts." "Warts're for life." "Exwactly!" Terror finds his phone vibrating on the bar and slides it across the wooden face. Maggie's name and picture flashes across the screen and he ignores it. He knew what she'd say; there'd be nothing good to come from the call. Adding to it, her name wasn't Steven Caldera. For the first time since joining him, none of Jay's calls had been answered, nor had he received a text message. All forms of contact had been severed leaving the prodigal son alone on the decision making road. He was his own man, yes, but the idea of failing was overwhelming. He'd had arms to lift him in his moments of need, but without Caldera, he didn't know how to move up on his own. In the end, all he wanted was someone to hold the ladder. "So can we pwetend that I'm Ken, Bawbie?" Clyde turned with a childish grin plastered across his chapped lips only to be deflated. Barbie's disappearance was a pin in the balloon Clyde had found, and he turned to the milk to mend his broken heart. He took the three quarters that were left and swallowed it until the glass was dry. "I think we got dwuped, Tewworible. Women!" "You said it, Clyde. I'll take ya' home, kid." Terror swirled the end of his Jack Daniels around before following Clyde's shining example. He ashed his cigarette and carried it out the door with him. Stepping outside, they breathed in the salty air, stumbled down the sidewalk, and shared stories of late night sexcapades. --- He woke up to another unfamiliar ceiling, jeans still clinging to his legs. Jack Daniels laid by his side where a thousand dollars was meant to be. It didn't matter. He and Jack had torn down bedposts and soiled carpets in the past -- he knew better than caring about the financial harm his drinking had. Still, he had never found a way to beat the head trauma. Brass knuckles could have cracked his skull wide open for all he knew -- all he had to go on was the idea that it hurt. Trying to sit up, Terror found out that it might be best to stay down. Maggie McVay looked on with heated eyes, hands placed on hips, making sure Jay understood. She was his mentor as he was hers and Jay knew that she'd have something to say about Mary and Jack. "Have fun last night?" Terror's teeth grinded, accentuating the tick in his head. "It was a night, that's for sure." She held her ground, "I'd say. What was all that about being done with it?" "You can never be done with a few drinks, Mags. Besides, I was with Clyde." "That didn't work when I used that line," she said. "And that might be illegal." She walked to the bed and took a seat beside him. Her voice was louder in the closer range, turning his blood to concrete. "Jay, I just don't get it. You won. You beat Jason just like you wanted to and now you're slated to move in on the world title soon." Jay tried to sit again but knew that it wasn't happening. He could already feel the rush of fluids climbing his esophagus and figured that they'd stay down so long as he remained ceiling-side up. "Caldera won't pick up my calls. It's like I failed, Mags." "This is about Caldera?" Her tone was laced with shock. "He cut you loose, Jay. You're on your own now." "I just don't get it." "He wasn't permanent. He was the guy who held the ladder. S'all." "Won't even pick up a call though. S'like he doesn't care what I did. S'like he doesn't care about anything with me involved. I'm lost, Mags, and that bottle of Jack Daniels helped me through the night." Maggie shook it off, "I think we learned from Murray that sulking doesn't win matches. He learned his lesson. How do you think you'll look against him and Knight this week if you don't buckle up?" "What's it matter? Not like I could even beat Johnson." "You're being serious?" "'Course." The Jack Daniels bottle rested on the sheets above his shoulder like a dirty conscience. She grabbed hold of it and held it in front of his face. "You've been listening to this for too long, Jay. Fuck what you're thinking - it ain't real. For Christ's sake, you beat The Back Alley Brawler. People thought it was impossible and you did it." "He was off his game." "You were better than him and you know it." She shook the bottle with emphasis, creating unnecessary waves in his mind. Jay could feel the vomit climbing. "You realize that you just defeated the biggest poison the business has ever known? O'Neil was loaded with venom and you sterilized him." "That was with Caldera though. I don’t have that guidance, that helping hand, or that confidence." "Listen to me then." "It's not the same." She tossed the bottle against the wall adding to Jay's overloaded mental confusion. The crack of the glass against the wall resembled a high speed smack during the Daytona 500. He grabbed his ears but the movement was enough to open the pipes inside of him. The army of unhappy bile marched at a full sprint up his throat and pried open his mouth. The collection of discharge spilled onto the carpet turning the slate gray to an off-orange. Maggie watched from a new vantage point, finally seeing what Jay had dealt with in the prior weeks. "Think that'll shake the floor for you some?" Jay wiped his mouth with the sheets and rolled his head back on the mattress. The odor began its graceful rise from the fresh pile and Jay covered up. "Just means I'm ready for another night." "You realize that when O'Neil woke up the morning before the match he probably finished a bottle of liquor, right?" "And so? Took him longer to feel much." "Doesn't mean he walked away on top," she said hoping her point was being absorbed. "You're killing your stamina with every sip and your heart with every feeling you get after the fact. There's a reason why you beat O'Neil and it's beyond your skill set. Walking into Worldwide blitzed will make you crumble just as easily as O'Neil did. It might take time but you'll have less of a memory and more bandages to brag about." "You don't wrestle. What do you know?" He knew it was a mistake before it left his lips, but there was little he could do to conceal it. She was a wrestling main-stay through her late husband and denying her knowledge was denying all her husband, and his good friend, stood for. Maggie tightened her eyes and leaned forward. "You know what, good luck with it all. Maybe losing to them will get Caldera back in bed with you." She stood, fuming, and found her way out. The room was silent without Maggie, leaving room for her parting words to fester. He knew she had a point, but the fact remained that he was lost. He'd been placed in mazes in the past but there were arrows to follow. Without the path noted, it was almost easier to sit and wait for someone to find him. Almost. --- Terror shoved his fist into the bag with little flare. His typical one-two combo finished at one while Koz, his newest training partner, took note. They'd known each other only through Maggie, but there was no denying the impact the spiritual leader had. Koz was one of those guys that understood life. He could dissect things, chew them in manageable chunks, and iterate everything he'd learned about the process. He was a realist but, what made him perfect for Jay, he was also a believer. Releasing his grip on the heavy bag, he leaned against it eyeing up Jay. Terror smelled of cheap liquor and stale cigarettes. "Long night?" "Why's everyone keep asking me that?" "Maggie too?" "She just doesn't get it, man. She doesn't understand what it's like being in this position." Koz smiled, "A wrestler? I think she's been around wrestl - " "Yeah, I know." Feeling the animosity, Koz extended his arm grabbing Jay's shoulder. He could see the man inside fighting his way out. It was typical Jay-demeanor to bottle his feelings and Koz had been around long enough to realize the signs. "I don't know what's got you down, Jay, but I do know what's got her down. She lost Steve, man. That was her husband, her best friend, and all she ever wanted. I hope I'm not assuming here, but I think she views you as all she's got. You knew him best and what you've done for her has kept her afloat. I can't tell if you've been hitting the bottle again, but if so, you gotta' try and lay off. She needs a role model right about now." Terror shakes his head and allows a glass of scorn to brew in his eyes. "I was a role model. I did everything right for her. Hell, I took care of her for a month straight when she was on those benders. What about me, Koz? What about how I feel. Yeah, I miss Steve too, but I'm also in a situation now. I'm just as on my own as she is and who's there to hold my hand? No one." "You have each other." "Won't help me win matches and won't help me get over the fact that I'm a passing phase. Steven Caldera ran off and left me on my own. What am I supposed to do? Where am I supposed to go?" With a look as perplexing as Maggie had, Koz only stared. "This is about Caldera?" "Of course it is." "Jay, you have to understand that he wasn't going to be there forever. He helped you get to where you are and now it's up to you to move along on your own." "Move to where? I got shafted when it came to the title again." "Did Caldera help you beat O'Neil?" "He set it up." "And you overcame," he exclaimed. "Johnny's setting you up the same way. He's giving you your matches so all you have to do is overcome. They can't keep you out forever." "They can do whatever the hell they please." Terror turns to his gym bag and finds his cigarettes. A flame kisses the face of one and he inhales the relief. "Caldera was the only one who gave a shit where I went and now that he's gone, I'm left on the outside." "They have plans, Jay. They always have plans. Ride the wave and see where it takes you. Worst comes to worst, everyone in the division looks like an ass while you show how steady you are. You're a rock, Jay. A sturdy rock sitting in a powerful tide. Just fight the tide and they'll notice you when the waters go out." "You don't get it either." "Get what?" Koz makes a move for the cigarette, but Jay fights him off. "You shouldn't be smoking either." "Like that ever stopped me." "Get what?" Jay shut his eyes, took a seat on the floor, and ashed on the wrestling mats. "You don't get where I am. You don't understand the politics behind this. The way I'm being screwed." "I wrestle too, Jay." "Not in GCW though." Koz laughed for a second shoving the comment off with a shrug. He was better than Maggie when it came to resistance and held his feet steady. "Say what you want, Caldera's gone. You can lose all you want, stand in the same place yelling until he hears you, and nothing will happen. You're off the milk, Jay, and it's time to knuckle up and take what you want." "I can't just - " "Then wait for LeStatt Knight to pass away because that's the only way he's going to give up that title. You think you deserve something, but you've been helped everywhere. Caldera owns the company and led you to where you are. He gave you the final entry in the Dangerous Games match. He made everything align for you and always has. You think Andy Murray had that kind of support? LeStatt Knight? Vivica Valentine?" Koz paused, sat down beside Jay, and shook his head. "They deserve all they've gotten. You - you still have to prove your worth." "I've proven myself over and over." "Think it all you want. Won't change anyone else's opinion. There's a reason why people boo you when you come out, Jay. You might think they're jealous but, in reality, they hate the road you've taken. You cut corners, made deals, and cheated the system. No one likes that." "Fuck 'em." "That'll get you places." "I don't need this, man. Just stay out of it." Terror stood from the mat, grabbed his gear bag, and puffed on his cigarette as he exited through the gym doors. There was truth all around him, pearls of wisdom whizzing by his ears, but he couldn't bite. Accepting everyone else's synopsis wasn't his way. It wasn't how he'd done things in the past. Only when Caldera said it was it right. --- Maggie McVay sat inside the limousine with a glass of champagne in hand. Her nails had been properly cut and painted going along with the new hair cut she'd received thanks to the Salt Lake City local business group. As new as she felt though, old feelings still brewed. The glass of champagne had been her fifth. It was the third time she'd asked the driver to travel around the block and, deep down, she knew she'd ask for another lap. The Moet was good, a breath of fresh air if anything, and she leaned back feeling the bubbles settling heavy. Her intake had been cut, nearly axed completely, until Jay's recent outbreak. She didn't think about it though. Numbers never ran her life in the past and she vowed that they wouldn't after Steve's passing. Coming around the corner the third time, she could hear the driver's English voice. "Would you like to get out, Miss Maggie?" "Just once more," she replied, swallowing the end of the glass. She poured another helping to finish the bottle. There was a lot she wanted to do in life still. There were places she wanted to go, people she wanted to meet, and things she wanted to do before she died. Knowing Steve couldn't live out his bucket list was enough to fuel her desire to finish hers. Unfortunately, she'd acquired an anchor known only as Jay Terror. The thought alone warranted a cigarette. She lit her Marlboro and shook her head. As much as she appreciated everything he did for her, she knew that she wasn't living. If she wasn't drinking, she was taking care of him. If she was drinking, she had no idea where she was. The endless loop played on like a broken record. When it came down to it though, she knew that their time together wouldn't last. Jay was a drifter by profession, an enigma who'd never truly settle until he, himself, was dead. It was only a matter of time, she thought to herself, before they split paths. Taking another sip of champagne, she looked out the window. Salt Lake City had a welcoming air to it. Spotting a kid on the sidewalk, she waved casually. For her troubles however, she watched a small rock leave his balled fist and hit her window. The thud startled the driver and Maggie yelled, "Stop it quick." The driver listened to the order, upset that his limousine had been damaged, and listened as Maggie opened her own door. She carried the glass of champagne with her as she sized up the teenage boy. "What do you think you're doing throwing rocks like that?" "You got the money, woman." "That's what you think." "I'm sure Jay's got enough to help you out." The comment took her off guard for a moment. "Jay Terror?" "That's what I said." "What do you know about Jay Terror?" "He's a never-was playing a game he isn't fit for." "Oh yeah?" "You think he fits in?" "I don't know." "Well if you think he's done anything ethically in that ring then you've been watching the wrong guy. I hate him, lady. He's the reason why I quit watching altogether. I just can't stand it." "You're being serious?" "I can't believe you'd even travel with him." "He's not in there." "You know what I mean." She admitted in her head that he was a teenager and that his opinion meant very little. Nonetheless, she listened. As much as she hated to admit it, the boy knew what he was talking about. "You think he'll ever turn around?" "I don't care. He's a piece of dirt and I don't want anything to do with him." As if by fate, Jay Terror stood on the other side of the road holding his gym bag. The street was devoid of cars and the words had traveled far enough for him to catch each decibel. For a third time in the same day, he listened to the same tune. Neither the boy nor Maggie realized that he'd heard, and as their conversation carried on, neither seemed to care if he did. It's said that everyone has a time of clarity in their life and Jay, dropping his cigarette to the concrete, felt that it was his moment. With a deep breath, he carried on down the sidewalk with one thing in mind. It was time that he earned something in his career.
