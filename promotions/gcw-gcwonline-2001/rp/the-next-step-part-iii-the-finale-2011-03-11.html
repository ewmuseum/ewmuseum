The Next Step: Part III - The Finale

11 March 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/11/11 What a Thursday night it had been. Aaron’s house warming party on Thirsty Thursday turned out to be a success. He had initiated a few new members into a life-long drinking game and had a few successful games of beer pong. More importantly to Aaron, though, was the way he had been able to open up to Chloe. For the first time since their storm chasing trip the year before they had allowed feelings for each other to fill the air and discussed them. They even went as far as confessing their love for each other. Now it was the afternoon after and Aaron dreaded the next time he’d have to face the redhead. What would they say? Friday 1:30 P.M. Aaron sat in his office staring at a computer screen. In front of him on the desk was a large piece of paper marked out in a spreadsheet type form. His eyes drooped shut every now and again as the night before caught up to him. He tried to shake away the exhaustion and wrote a number down on his spreadsheet before clicking through a few more frames on the weather map he looked at. As he found another number to write down, the door to the office swung open and closed as one of Aaron’s colleagues walked in and approached a nearby desk. “Hey Nick.” Aaron said. “Are you doing alright man? You look tired as hell.” Nick asked. Aaron took a deep breath and dropped the pen to the desk. He brought his hands up and rubbed his eyes before swiveling in his chair and turning to Nick. “It was a long night. I don’t even remember what time I went to bed.” Aaron explained. Nick laughed as he gathered a few things from his desk and packed them into a bag. He turned to leave and stopped next to Aaron. “A few of us are going to grab some pizza at Huskie’s. You want to go?” Nick offered. Aaron took only a second to think about it but shook his head. “I’m going to hang around here until my office hours are finished.” Aaron replied. “It’s the second week of classes, Aaron. Nobody is going to come in for help on that lab.” Nick said through a laugh. Aaron turned back to the computer and began clicking through a few more frames of the weather map that filled it. “I know. I just want to get this forecast done before I leave. Thanks for the offer though.” He said. Nick nodded and grabbed the door handle to leave. He didn’t say anything more. After the door had shut again Aaron looked up at the clock and wondered how long the next half hour would take to go by. He needed a nap. Friday 1:30 P.M. Chloe pushed her car door shut and clutched her keys. The horn on her vehicle chirped as she pressed a button on the key chain, locking her doors. She paused a moment to compose herself before walking towards the parking garage’s exit. Numerous thoughts and feelings ran through her mind as she carried herself down the stairs. The night before lingered in her head and she was still working out the details. She had hoped to escape seeing Aaron until class the next week but she ran into problems with the assigned lab and needed to see him for help. She waited as a car passed, made her move and crossed the street. She walked across the little drive and up the sidewalk leading to Davis Hall. Her stomach began to turn as she opened the stairwell door and began the climb up to the second floor. She tried to calm herself and say that everything would be okay, but it did nothing to put her at ease. “Seven. Six. Five.” As she continued up the stairs like she had done so many times before she counted how many steps were left as she neared the landing. Before she knew it she said “zero” in her head and reached forward to clutch the door handle that would take her to the halls. She didn’t hesitate, walked through and turned to the left. She saw Aaron’s office door shut but could hear two voices inside. She slowly approached the door, but backed away enveloped in nerves. “It sounds like he’s busy. Maybe I should come back later.” She thought to herself. She took a few deep breaths and adjusted the backpack that hung from her shoulder. “Just get this over with.” She whispered under her breath. Chloe took a few steps forward and reached for the door handle. Before she could grasp it, the door swung open just enough for another Teaching Assistant to walk through. He turned and shut the door quietly before he saw her. “Hey Chloe. What are you up to?” Nick asked. “I was just looking for some help on the lab for Synoptic.” She replied. Chloe knew that Aaron was in his office, now alone after Nick’s departure. She had to go in now that Nick had seen her. She knew if she didn’t Nick would mention her visit to Aaron later. “Well he’s in there doing his forecast. I invited him out for pizza, but he said he wanted to stay until the end of his office hours.” Nick said. Nick tipped an imaginary hat to the lovely redhead and walked past as she nodded back. She waited a moment longer for Nick to get into the stairwell. She took one final deep breath and planted her knuckles into the door with a few solid knocks. Friday 1:40 P.M. Aaron looked up to the door as he heard three thumps against its exterior. He invited the person in and looked back to the computer screen. He wrote down another number from the computer model as the door swung open and then closed. He finally looked up and immediately felt his stomach tie in knots. Chloe flashed a small smile in his direction as she set her bag down alongside Aaron’s desk. The moment quickly filled with an awkward silence that begged to be broken. “Hey Chloe. What’s up?” He asked. “Not much. I just had a few questions about the lab.” She said. Chloe reached down and accessed her book bag. When she straightened up she placed a packet of paper down on the desk in front of Aaron. Chloe was a bright girl, but the front page of her lab was blank. Aaron flipped through the two remaining pages and found them blank as well. He looked at her and leaned back in his chair. Aaron’s arms went into the air as if to say “I’m ready for anything you can throw at me.” “I know we covered basic hand analysis in another class, but I’m blanking completely.” She said. “Well that’s no problem. Contouring a map by hand is an acquired skill. It takes practice.” Aaron replied. He pulled his desk drawer open and pushed a few things aside before coming up with a couple of writing utensils. He clicked some lead out from his pencil and pointed to the weather map on the paper. “You should be able to look at this map and tell me exactly where the center of low pressure is.” He said. Chloe accepted the challenge and immediately pointed to a location in central Kansas. Aaron nodded “All the station plots surrounding this area have winds coming from different directions. This is where all the surrounding air is flowing into.” She explained. Aaron nodded and placed his pencil on the paper. He quickly drew a rough circle around the area and labeled it with the number 976. “I always find the low and work my way out from there. Do all of your isobars in increments of four and always, always use a pencil first.” Aaron said. “What do you use after that?” Chloe asked. “I picked up these fine tip felt pens from the bookstore. They work great. You can trace over your pencil marks and then erase the pencil without smudging the ink.” Aaron said. Chloe nodded and smiled widely. It was clear she legitimately needed help on the lab and hadn’t come just to see Aaron. The look of enlightenment remained on her face as she picked up the lab and looked at the other two maps. She nodded once more and reached down to put it back in her bag. “Is that all?” Aaron asked. “Yeah. Thanks a lot Aaron.” She said. When she stood up, Chloe brought her bag with her and placed it over her shoulder. She thanked him once more and turned towards the door. As her hand came into contact with the handle, Aaron knew he had to speak now. “Did you have a good time last night?” Aaron inquired. “I guess so.” Chloe replied as she turned back around. “I don’t really remember much of anything besides the fact you were completely wasted by the time I got there.” “The last thing I remember was greeting you at the door. It sure was a crazy night. I think.” Aaron said. Neither had read the other yet, but they both played the stupid card. Neither wanted to admit that they remembered the encounter and would never let it go. “I woke up at home though. I’m glad I walked over.” Chloe said. “I heard the cops were all over the place last night so I guess that is a good thing.” Aaron said. The situation seemed awkward to both of them and neither knew how to make it better. Aaron was the first to break the short silence by jumping the gun. “My office hours are over in twenty minutes. I don’t think anyone else is going to come by. Would you like to grab some lunch?” He asked. “Well,” Chloe said as she hesitated. She thought about her boyfriend, Tyler, and knew she couldn’t threaten their trust again. Frantically she searched for a way out. “I really want to just get this lab out of the way. I’m working all weekend and won’t have any more time. Besides, it’s probably for the best. Tyler was pretty angry I went to the party.” Aaron nodded and told her he understood. Chloe had tipped her hand. Aaron knew Chloe remembered. They exchanged abbreviated goodbyes and Chloe left the office. Aaron watched the door close behind her and picked up his pen to go back to work on his forecast. He looked at the computer monitor and tried to focus, but he couldn’t make himself stop looking at the door. He wondered if she was still there. She was. She was staring right back at the door and wondered if he was doing the same. Friday 7:30 P.M. The loud and heavy music of Dragonforce filled Aaron’s apartment. He sat on his couch staring at the television. He played Call of Duty: Black Ops but found his mind wandered constantly. Aaron considered himself an above average player but never seemed to find his groove. His eyes kept moving to his phone that laid on the coffee table in front of him. He wanted to hear Chloe’s voice. ”I wonder what she’s doing right now.” Aaron wondered to himself. He shook the thoughts away and turned his attention back to his game. He decided to start fresh and exited the current game to find a new one. He was quickly placed in a new lobby and readied himself for the start of the game. As hard as he tried to, he couldn’t push Chloe from his mind. He soon found himself getting careless in his game play. Aaron tossed the control onto the couch and leaned back. He brought his hands to his face and took a deep breath before stretching his arms out. When he relaxed again his glare dropped back to the phone. He reached for it and punched in Chloe’s number. ”I have to talk to her.” He thought to himself. Friday 7:40 P.M. The smooth chords of “Vanilla Twilight” by Owl City filled Chloe’s apartment. The stereo speakers strained to drown out the sounds of her sobs. She sat on the floor, leaned up against the couch. She clutched a pillow whose case was drenched with her tears. Her sobs subsided sporadically but only long enough that she caught her breath. After a few more moments they stopped all together, but the tears still streamed down her face. ”I miss him so much.” She said to herself. Chloe held onto the pillow and laid her head down on it. She didn’t care that her cheek made contact with the soaked surface. She took a few deep breathes to calm herself and sat up. It was then that she heard her phone vibrating on the hardwood floor next to her. She wiped the tears from her cheek and picked it up. She sighed loudly as she saw Aaron’s name on the caller ID. She didn’t take any time to think and quickly pressed the button to ignore the call. She sobbed lightly once more and took a few more breathes to regain her composure. When she felt she was ready she punched a few numbers into her phone and pressed send. Friday 7:50 P.M. Aaron dropped his phone back to the table as he heard Chloe’s voicemail message. He knew there was no chance she would leave his mind now. She consumed him. He had to talk to her. He had to see her. He stood up and snatched his coat from a nearby chair and made his way towards the door. He put the jacket on and opened the door before he hesitated. He turned back and tossed the keys he had retrieved from his pocket onto the counter. ”This is crazy. If I go looking for her, that’s borderline stalker.” He said aloud. Aaron slowly took the coat off and placed it back on the chair. He sat back down on the couch and grabbed the game controller. It was in his hands for only a moment. He dropped it once more and grabbed his coat again. He was out the door in a few short seconds and headed towards his car. “But if she feels the same way, I’d never forgive myself for not seeing her.” He said aloud again. Friday 8:30 P.M. Chloe walked slowly down a path that was part of the large network of sidewalks across the Northern Illinois Campus. Some of her friends had called the late night walk ill-advised but she insisted she needed to take a walk alone to clear her mind. The tears had stopped and were nothing but a memory. In reality she hadn’t strayed too far from her apartment. The night was clear and that allowed the stars to show brightly in the sky. She found herself looking to them more than where she was headed and made the decision to sit down on a bench. Her gaze remained to the sky, focused on the bright glow of the moon. She smiled as one of her constant thoughts passed through her mind again. ”I wonder if anybody in the world is looking up at the moon at this exact moment like I am.” Friday 8:30 P.M. Aaron had left his car parked in a fifteen minute parking spot ten minutes prior. Chloe didn’t answer her door when he knocked on it and he suddenly knew where she’d be. He jogged down a sidewalk on the edge of campus in search of her. He just had to see her. He needed to tell her he knew what they had said the night before. As he rounded a corner the toe of his boot caught on a crack. He almost fell forward, but caught himself. He took a deep breath and looked up to the sky. He stared at the moon and found himself unable to break away. He continued to move down the path towards a campus landmark. He knew Chloe would be near the “Love Tree.” It was a location known to all the Rico Sauvé’s that populated the campus. The tree was a beautiful blossoming creature during the warm months. During the winter it was still beautiful. Its branches grew almost perfectly and were a good example of what nature was capable of. Aaron rounded a corner and brought his eyes back down from the sky. He spotted Chloe staring up at the moon and quietly approached her. “It’s beautiful, isn’t it?” He asked. “It is. It looks peaceful.” Chloe replied. Aaron sat down on the bench next to her and watched she brought her head back down and turned to look at him. They held a gaze in each other’s eyes for a moment before either said a word. “What are you doing here Aaron?” She asked. “I had to see you. I need to tell you something.” He said. Chloe looked to the ground and felt the butterflies in her stomach. Aaron could spot the nervousness on her face lit up by the bright moonlight. He chuckled lightly and grabbed her hand. “I know what we said to each other last night. I was playing dumb in my office today.” He explained. “What I told you was true. I fell in love with you last summer and I haven’t gotten out of it since.” “I know you did. I can see it in the way you look at me.” She said. “Even during your lectures; it’s like you’re talking directly to me all the time.” “I can’t stop thinking about you.” Aaron said as he nodded. “I have to have you in my life.” Chloe looked back up at Aaron and he saw a few tears slowly fall down her cheek. She sniffled and took her hand from his grasp. Aaron allowed her to in an attempt to prove he cared for her needs. “I fell in love with you too.” She said. Aaron fought the urge to fill his face with glee and was able to restrain himself. He waited in anticipation of her next words and found the joy escaping his body like radiant heat from the ashes of a dead fire. “But I’ve gotten out.” She said. “I’m in love with Tyler.” Her words tore through him violently. They ripped him apart like a chainsaw through the trunk of a tree. He turned his head and fought away the tears that wanted to rain down his face. He needed to be strong. “What do you mean?” He asked without purpose. “Are you saying that what sparked between us; what we created last summer doesn’t resonate inside of you in the slightest?” Chloe had looked back at the ground but raised her head back to look at him once more. She spotted a single tear that ran down his cheek and brought her hand up to wipe it away. He reached up to grasp her hand again, but she pulled it back. “What was left disappeared when I met Tyler.” She answered. “We were apart for so long and it’s only natural to find yourself distanced from a previous love.” Aaron frantically searched his mind for words to respond. His body trembled with nerves and emotion, but the right words wouldn’t come to the surface. Before he could find them Chloe’s voice filled the air again. “I’m transferring to UCLA.” She said. “To be with Tyler.” Aaron felt the levee break. He rarely showed emotion, but felt no limits when he was around the redhead next to him. Tears ran down his face as he spoke, his voice unbroken. “Chloe you can’t go.” He almost pleaded. “Why can’t we give it another shot?” She remained silent as she stood and leaned down. She wiped the tears from his face again and gave him a soft kiss on the cheek. As she pulled away she spoke softly. “Goodbye Aaron. Don’t cry for me. I’m not worth it.” She turned and walked away. Aaron watched her figure disappear into the night and then quietly spoke to himself. “You’re worth everything in the world.” He said. Aaron remained seated on the bench for a while longer and openly wept. He finally sighed and wiped the cold tears from his face before he stood and looked across the walk. He and Chloe had not met in front of the “Love Tree.” He stared at the ugly and lifeless form of the less popular “Break-Up Tree.” The remaining portion of its trunk hunched over and threatened to fall at any moment. The moss and weeds that grew around it nearly consumed it. It made sense to him. Maybe it wasn’t meant to be. Maybe it wasn’t fate.
