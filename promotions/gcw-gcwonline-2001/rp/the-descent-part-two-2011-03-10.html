The Descent (Part Two)

10 March 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/10/11 The Descent (Prelude) The Descent (Part One) You felt your grubby, brittle fingernails fracture as they clung onto sanity; a stamp crumbling them to mustardy dust. As you fell into Rasa’s black, bubbling tar pit, there were no hands reaching to pull you out, only to buckle the straps of its prickly straightjacket. This is how you fell. The Observer Harmony’s bare feet nestled into the cloud, its wispy fingers creeping over glittered silver toenails. A barbwire galaxy of glistening stars orbited an endless mane of cascading, flaxen hair. Intricate lattices of virgin white silk wove around her, its delicate fibres perpetually fraying and reweaving on the delicate kiss of the breeze. “Sometimes I think we’re too cruel,” Harmony accused. The light which radiated from her diminished as regret overcame her voice, and the glowing gold bangles which snaked up her arms fell into shadow and succumbed to rust. Sorrow made of her a star who had burned too bright too soon. “We test, we judge, but we’re our most cruel when we just… watch.” The rarefied air hummed as a glacial tear trickled down Harmony’s cheek, causing the cloud at her feet to fall into a bruised, mournful whirlpool. Some must fall into darkness before they shine can their brightest, was spoken from the ether, words of warm, fragrant honey dripping into Harmony’s ear. The most glorious of dawns follow the longest nights. Harmony turned and stared into nothing, a subtle-yet-sincere smile breaking across her lips which reignited her effervescent soul. She conceded a knowing nod of a lesson learned from wise father by his young, thirsting daughter. “I should never of doubted you,” she said, words soft and apologetic. “I’ll keep watching, to the end of her story.” The Goddaughter Backpack slouching her shoulders, Erin Wolfenden stood at the school gates. As the charcoal clouds above swelled, the frenzy of activity around her began to subside; dense streams of classmates slowing to a trickle of strays. Light, almost apologetic spring rain began to speckle the sidewalk and Erin nestled her cold hands deep into her pockets. Nobody was coming. Nervously she shuffled to the curb, shivering within the long velvet coat which engulfed her short, wiry frame. The whoosh of the traffic tickled her lightly freckled cheeks as a toe dipped down onto the asphalt as if she were tentatively testing the temperature of her bathwater. Glances were batted back and forth, the lanes of traffic threading together to strand Erin on the curb. Her cautious programming was overridden by a naïve, premature sense of adventure. As Erin stepped out to try and thread the traffic’s needle a hand clamped down upon her shoulder, tethering child to sidewalk. The Sister-in-Law Ignoring Jacob Barker’s polite, pitter-patter knocks at their hotel room’s bathroom door, Alice Wolfenden plunged her face, cracked by mascara-tear-tracks, into a basin of cold water. Ice-cold clarity was locked in as her pores winced closed. Tearing her head from the basin’s sensory-depriving hold, the mirror painted a truer portrait; a wife and mother who realised she’d been selfish, stupid, and just wanted to go home to her family. “Jake, go home,” Alice urged, her words soft so as not to further bruise his pride. Scraping her darkest-of-raven-black hair back into a pony tail, Alice wiped the last remnants of cosmetic mistruths from her face. “You okay?” Jacob called through the door. “You went kinda… well, um, you okay?” “I’m okay,” she answered. The smile which dawned across her bare face reflected that she truly believed that. She silently echoed the statement, twice. “I’m also married, I’m also a mother, and you‘re also my boss. Go home, Jake. Please.” Retrieving her wedding ring from beside the sink, Alice slid it back onto her finger, holding it there for a few lingering seconds. For the first time in years, she actually looked at it; at the stone which had been heated and squeezed and birthed by the earth. A bleating cellphone interrupted Alice’s geological ponderings. Snatching it up from her purse, Alice answered without checking the caller ID. “Jake, just fuck off, okay?” The Disappointment As you staggered out of the bar, even the scant light penetrating the ominous clouds above hit you like a flash grenade. Gulping down lungfuls of fresh air, you tried to purge the stale-alcohol-lethargy from your system. The Goddaughter‘s Teacher “Um, Mrs Wolfenden?” Grace Peterson awkwardly enquired. “This is Miss Peterson, from Erin’s school.” A torrent of embarrassed apologies flooded the phone line which only ceased when the words stumbled over themselves and clotted. “I‘ve left messages for you and your husband but hadn‘t heard anything back,” Grace pounced on the silence with, her tone taking a more lecturing turn as she looked at the young girl sitting alone in the corridor. “I’m just calling because nobody has come to pick up Erin yet. I don’t know if you’re running late, or if anyone else is coming to collect her, maybe? Your husband? Another family member?” The Disappointment As you stamped down on the brake pedal, the car let loose a pained wail of friction-burned brakes and blowtorched tyres. The world froze, stranding you all by your lonesome in the world of motion. For the briefest of seconds, your senses caught up; every stupid decision you’d made laid bare on the dash in front of you. The speedometer needle quivered between reckless and idiotic. Understanding In A Car Crash was always your favourite Thursday song. Your head cracked against the steering wheel, and a splitting pain cracked your skull as if your swollen brain were erupting up through it. The Brother & The Sister-in-Law “How could you?” Alice Wolfenden didn’t need to rig her words with explosive obscenities; each one of them was fizzing with napalm as it left her lashing tongue. “She can’t even take care of herself and you trust her with our daughter?” Max Wolfenden sat, elbows on knees and head in hands, thumbs massaging his eyes. “She’s my sister, Ally,” he spoke with raw honesty, words hollowed out by their infestation of guilt. “I really, really thought that she… with Erin… that she…” “That she what?” Alice pounced on his weak return with a seething volley. “That she’d crawl straight out of some rat-infested bar into her car and drive to pick our daughter up from school with enough alcohol in her system t-” “She wasn‘t over by that much,” Max spoke to the floor. “And she’ll have to face those charges.” “Really? That’s great news,” bristled with irritable sarcasm. “Every time I’ve tried to keep her away from us you’ve tried to make me feel guilty, that I was being too harsh on her. Every time, Max. If she was a pet you could at least send her on some sort of obedience training, but what do you do with people like her? Do they teach people how to be responsible adults anywhere? Or do we just have to wait until they end up in prison to stop them being a danger to anyone else?” “I don’t know,” Max spoke; in general as opposed to in answer. “I really don’t know.” “Here’s what I know, Max,” Alice said, her words unrelenting. “If you still want her to be in your life, then it’s a life you’ll have to live without me or Erin. I don’t want to even hear her name. She’s hurt us for the last time.” The Disappointment & The Sister-in-Law A shotgun blast of consciousness exploded in your face, its muzzle flash a power surge which reanimated your hibernating brain. Blotches of light swirled around your eye like it were mottled marble. Panic’s prickly tendrils stung you as your body refused to obey your scatterbrained commands; every limp, unresponsive muscle was a deadweight which held you beneath your fleshy surface, drowning. The white ceiling above gave you no focal point to rally your senses, you eyes sliding off of it. Every sound was a nagging, faint whisper or a paralysing crash. An invasive, sterile stench seared your nostrils and welled alkali tears. Where? you thought. How? When? Why? Not who?, though. You always knew who you were; it was a burden branded deep. Your memories overlapped and interwove into a jumbled-up timeline. Frothy surf tenderly tickling your bare toes set to the sound of a dog’s defensive growl. Blurry traffic criss-crossing your view over a mesmerising light show of red, amber and green while Christmas carols played and the chirps of children’s laughter filled your ears. Frigid, alpine wind closing the pores of your skin and your nostrils filling with the heady aroma of gasoline. Your trembling body being held tightly, sweat welling up in the small of your back as the deafening screech of strained brakes cleaved your brain in half. “You have brought nothing but misery to my family,” Alice Wolfenden’s voice took your memory’s unravelling strands and roughly, painfully knotted them. Peering out through the glassy portholes of you disobedient body, you looked up into ice-blue eyes which boiled with contempt, like a volcano erupting through the bed of a pure, tropical ocean. Alice was etched into your brain, and your haphazard thoughts scribbled over her, trying to see what torn away memories were indented beneath. Max; a phone call. He’d asked… he’d asked a favour. Erin. He asked you to pick up Erin. It hit you and it hit you hard; just as the steering wheel had. Blurry traffic criss-crossing your view over a mesmerising light show of red, amber and green. Children’s laughter filled your ears. The deafening screech of strained brakes. The heady aroma of gasoline. Your trembling body. “How many times have I told you to stay away? To not go near my daughter?” Alice’s venom-tipped words were determined to negate every millilitre of pain relief your drip provided. “But every single time you worm your way back in to our lives, knowing that Max doesn’t have it in him to disown you.” Numb muscles wired your jaw shut. “But not this time,” Alice hissed. Despite the swollen, intrusive sun peering in through the window, Alice‘s words sucked every degree of warmth from the room. You could barely wiggle your toes but the absolute disdain her voice carried snapped at them with agonising frostbite. “No, this time he can’t even bring himself in here to look at you. If I dragged him in here right now, you know what he’d do? He’d spit on the floor and walk straight back out again.” With utter dread you asked yourself, what did I do? Alice leant over the bed, her nose tickling yours. Raven hair fell around you like a curtain. The blood vessels in your brain threatened to erupt like frozen pipes as you strained to part your dry-plastered lips just a fraction of an inch. “But he’s not going to be coming in here,” she whispered, every word‘s iced cerated edge slashing at you. “Do you know why, you stupid, selfish bitch? It’s because he’s in the next room with Erin - our daughter - waiting to see if she’ll wake up. If I had my way, they’d harvest you for spare parts to help make her better, because that will be the only thing of any worth that you’ll ever achieve in your miserable failure of a life.” Your heavy heart sunk, splintering your spine before bursting out through your back and crashing through the hospital bed. A darkness far deeper than the shadow of Alice’s hair descended upon you. You stopped trying to breathe; it no longer felt worth the effort. “If you had any conscience you’d beg me to fluff one of those pillows up and suffocate you with it right now.” Alice’s dry-blood-claret fingernails tantalisingly strummed at the corner of your pillow. “But, as I thought… you’re too much of a coward. Now, Karina, believe ever word of this; if I ever have the misfortune of seeing you again, I will do everything in my power to make sure that you don’t hurt my family any more. I don’t care if you’re a wrestler or a fighter or whatever you think you are, sad truth is that you’ve got nothing to fight for. I have. So believe that I’ll put up more of a fight than anyone you’ve ever gone up against.” As the clickity-clack of her departing heels plunged hot pokers into your ears, your eyes welled with tears and your stomach didn’t so much turn as contorted itself into a thousand torturous configurations. You destroyed everything you touched, and your teeth ground enamel to dust straining to lay one of those cursed fingertips upon yourself. The Disappointment Your rubbery body thumped heavily off the floor, cheekbone, shoulder and hip absorbing the impact. As bone ground into tissue, you snaked along the bleached-clean tiles, extremities waking up in agony, inch by inch. When you could no longer wriggle, your fingernails dug in between the tiles, dragging you closer to the door. When your split, bloody nails could no longer grip, you chipped your teeth against the tiles, scraping for traction as the sterile tang puckered your mouth. Don’t you fucking quit, growled within your head. Move. Into the corridor you snaked on a tortured cushion of bruising flesh, the bright fluorescent lights above offering little in the way of salvation. On scar-cross’d knuckles you dragged yourself, pungent disinfectant stinging raw flesh and lungs alike. “Miss.” A nurse’s concerned cheep was barely audible to you. “Miss, you really shouldn’t b-” “Where?” you prised your jaws apart to breathlessly ask. Exhausted, you spread out across the floor, a limp arm flopping down to point into the vacant neighbouring room. The strain threatened to pop uprooted teeth from their gums as you formed your words. “The girl… in this room. Erin. Where… where is she?” The nurse hovered a few feet away from you, poorly disguising her gestures for security to come and escort you back to your bed. “Um, I think you’re still a little confused, Miss.” Beneath the polite polish to her words there was a deeper lurking disdain; a judgement that had been bottled up by the cork of professionalism. “My… Goddaughter…” you gasped, lungs flaccid. “Was in the… crash… w-with… me…” Your lungs verged on collapsing as you forced out every syllable. Where before there were shadows there was now impending darkness. A thunderous heartbeat slowed to a timid flutter. Your life was in its twilight but all you cared about was seeing Erin; to offer up your apology no matter how grotesque the shell which encased it. If your last embers of life could be consumed in doing so, that was something you didn’t need a lot of time to come to terms with. “Miss.” Again with the faux-politeness. This time the tiniest drop of pity was lost in the swirling pool of disgust. “I can assure you that nobody else was brought in from your accident. Just you.” The Brother & The Sister-in-Law “I should talk to her,” Max said, is shoulders finally rolling back. “If she‘s awake.” “No,” Alice snapped, her words so fierce that barbed echoes clung to her husband’s ears. “This needs to come from me.” The Disappointment The afternoon traffic was hell-bent on sabotaging your school-run time trial. Staring at the dash’s clock, you tried to look through the blurry digital magic-eye-puzzle hidden within the 88:88. As you looked back up, your eyes refused to recalibrate, and 6 sets of blinking traffic lights confronted you. The Family Knock, knock. The heavy collision of wrought iron echoed through the still night. Behind you the full moon loomed large in the clear night sky, its pale light bleaching your bruised skin an ill, ghostly shade. “I’ll get it,” you heard Alice shout through the door. “Erin, keep stirring; but be careful on that stool, sweetie.” Your fists clenched as you heard the lock turn. Steam felt like it was erupting through every pore, vengeance stoking the furnace. When anger overcame you, it exerted a dominance which crippled all other emotions. You were practically clawing at the door as you heard the chain slide off. You’d hobbled through feet of snow with broken bones stabbing your petrified flesh. You’d ripped and twisted ligaments from bone, torn muscles fibre by fibre and ground through the cartilage between your joints. But the woman behind the door had hurt you in a way which you had never felt, on a level which mercilessly slashed through any defences you had erected. She’d spat on your heart and ground it in with her sandpaper sole. When the door swung open and Alice was made-up face to fucked-up face with you, you went feral. Your splintered nails embedded in her scalp, unleashing a banshee scream which could have reduced the brick mortar to dust. “You fucking bitch,” you hissed. Alice’s head bounced off the varnished floorboards, each impact shaking her eyes back in their sockets. Her desperate, clawing fingers gouged at your eyes and nostrils until you bit her ring finger, your incisor chipping against its diamond. “Mommy!” You froze, not even flinching when Alice‘s thumbnail punctured your cheek. From nowhere, Max’s fist drilled into your jaw, knocking you out of alignment with the world. Erin’s words echoed around your skull and Alice’s scream-contorted face burned itself into your retinas. “What the fuck is wrong with you?” Max roared, his tightly-wound restraint unravelling with a violent, protective outburst. Another sloppy punch finally dislodged you from his wife. “Erin, go to your room. Now, Erin!” Grabbing you by your dry, split-ended hair, Max dragged you along the floor, the worn soles of your sneakers struggling for traction. “I can’t do this anymore, Kari,” he wilted, dropping you on his doorstep. “Just… just go. I don’t even know what you are anymore; you’re not my sister, you’re not the girl I grew up with. I tried, Kari. I tried. I just don’t have it in me to find out where you went wrong. Just… get out of my sight. Get out of our lives. Forever.” The door slammed shut, sending you tumbling down the steps, lungs expelling all but their emergency reserves as you thumped down onto the sidewalk. As the full moon above covered its disgusted, crater-eyes with the thick, churning clouds above, darkness descended upon you. This was how you fell.
