Date: ---- 21st September 2006. Rupp Arena. Lexington, Kentucky. I knew it was only a matter of time before I got what was coming to me. I’m a prick, I know it, and I piss people off because that’s how I get my kicks these days. Strange though, I always thought I’d see it coming. It’s a house show, it’s not supposed to be anything special, just a few extra bucks and guaranteed sparring partner to warm me up. Doesn’t quite turn out like that. Seems the “sparring partner” I’m provided tonight, is the very same damned rookie I pissed off a couple of weeks ago backstage of the infinite gauntlet. Small world. Anyhow, seems this fella doesn’t much like having a lit cigarette thrown in his kit bag. Hell, he told me just before walking down the aisle, he didn’t even realise it was in there until he got to the parking lot. By that time it’d burnt a hole clear through his bag. Funniest damned thing I’d heard, or least I thought so at the time. He bears a grudge toward me and I can’t hardly blame him for that. When I do the things I do, I realise I’m being a prick and I understand there are always consequences to my actions. That’s what makes it so damned funny. It’s not so damned funny any more. Seems I pissed him off enough to make him seriously want to hurt me and even facing off against a rookies, it’s still dangerous territory. We’re ten minutes into the match when it happens. I’m doing well, barely letting him get a sniff in edgeways and he’s taken much more than I would’ve ever give him credit for. I get cocky, let my guard down and he catches me with a luck shot to the gut with his left knee, just I’m dragging him back to his feet. The rookie doesn’t get much behind it, but it’s enough to take the wind out of my sails for a moment. He grabs the back of my head and the top of my tights and hurls like a fucking javelin at the ring post. In usual instances, I at least parry the blow some by going in shoulder first, but this rookie is strong and he hurls me with such speed, I don’t have time to make the appropriate adjustments and so I hit the metal ring post head on. I don’t remember much from that point on. There’s pain for a while, but it all goes black soon enough. Afterwards, when I’m being stitched up, I’m told that I roll out of the ring and ref counts me out. The rookie tries to pick me up and roll me back inside, so he can get the clear win, but I’m dead weight and even though he’s strong, he isn’t that strong. Much to my everlasting embarrassment, he wins by count out, but that’s just the beginning of my worries. 10th September 2005. St. Barnabas Hospital. The Bronx, New York. Mary McKail sat in the doctors office, nervously twirling the leather straps on her handbag around her fingers and completely tired of waiting. She’d been sent to the cancer department of the hospital shortly after undergoing a simple check up, last week. Apparently, her physician didn’t much like what he saw on the routine X rays he took and immediately booked her an appointment at St. Barnabas, though it was more than week before they could fit her in. Lung cancer. That’s what her physician thought it could be and just a couple of hours ago, she’d undergone a pretty invasive test, the doctor called a “bronchoscopy”, which had basically came down to them shoving a damned tube down her throat. It was a pretty gruesome thought and she really didn’t want to think about. Where the hell is he? She understood that these things took time, but she’d been waiting for more than forty five minutes in here alone, scared about what they were going to find, or more accurately, what they have found and just haven’t told her yet. How the hell am I going to tell Jacob about this? She’d been avoiding most lines of thought regarding her actually having lung cancer, but sat there, alone, in the doctors office, there wasn’t much else to occupy her mind and sure as else didn’t want to think about the other thing. Her death. Jacob had been so down lately. He’d had trouble making any money on the independent circuit and they’d had to rely on her income at the local diner and that wasn’t much to write home about. He’d never tell her, but she knew that he felt humiliated by not being able to provide for her and because of that humiliation he was prepared to do anything to right it. He’d joined a new wrestling company just last week, where he was promptly forced to dress up and perform as a clown for the audience. True, he was earning more, but Mary knew this produced an entirely different kind of humiliation for him and he was already getting more and more miserable with each passing week. She didn’t even want to consider what effect her condition would have on him now. It could finish him. But she was getting ahead of herself. She hadn’t had her result back yet, she still might not even have anything wrong with her. Her physician could’ve made a mistake with X ray, or whatever he did see, could be something else, something less severe. The officer door opened and her doctor, a kindly old man, with a worn face and white hair that matched his white coat, entered, carrying under his arm a brown folder. She could see the white label sticking up at the end, with her name neatly scrawled in black marker across it and, as he sat in his comfortable-looking leather chair, she glanced up at his face. It told her everything she needed to know. She felt sick to her stomach. The doctor laid her file out on the table and opened it, taking the longest time to pluck up enough courage to look her in the eyes. “There’s no easy way of saying this...” Mary couldn’t stop the tears anymore. What am I going to do now? How will I tell Jacob? Get a hold of yourself woman... “...you have large cell carcinoma.” Most know what they’re in for when walking into the office and with the internet they usually have a fair idea of what his medical jargon means, but he can tell his words are lost on Mary and quickly adds, “Lung cancer.” The words hung in the air for the longest time, but she managed to pull herself together enough to fight back the fear that had begun to crawl up her throat. “Bu-but I haven’t been coughing up blood or anything like that....” The doctor nods his head. “It’s not uncommon for lung cancer’s to not show any symptoms before they advance,” he explained. “We may have caught this one in time, before it has had chance to spread to any other part of your body.” Mary wasn’t really interested in the why’s and how’s at the moment, she was just going through the motions. She felt somehow obliged to ask at least one question under that category, now came the important issue; what next. She’d heard of new treatments and the like, and how cancer research has developed over the years. This may not be the end for her and she needed to believe it. If she was to be there for Jacob in his current rough patch, then she had to believe it. “So, where do we go from here?” She asked. “What next?” The doctor sighed, the weight of carrying the bad news now lifted from his shoulders. “Well, first of all we have to do a CT scan to determine whether or not the cancer spread to any other part of your body.” Mary nodded, absorbing the new information, despite her mind still being numb from the shock of it all. “What about treatments?” “Well, lung cancer is one of the more dangerous cancers,” he explained, pulling no punches. Mary liked that. “There are treatments available, but mostly they only work to prolong your life.” He paused, thinking very carefully about what to say next. “Complete cures are very rare.” Mary choked back her tears. That’s not what she wanted to hear at all. At the moment, the concept of her death hadn’t begun to set in yet and she had no intention of letting. She was more focused on the future and ensuring that Jacob would survive it all. She had to make him want to live after she was gone and in there lies the conundrum that would plague her for the months to come. In the end, she did find a solution, but it would be a little rough for Jacob along the way. Mary had faith he was strong enough to handle it, that he would go on a forge a new life for himself, regardless and become all the stronger for it. She was wrong. 23nd September 2006. Some Crappy Motel Room. Louisville, Kentucky. The bourbon dulls the pain some, but not by much. In light of this, I’ve perched myself on the uncomfortable motel room chair, only moving to bring the rim of the bourbon bottle to my lips and back again. At the other end of the room, I stare at the moonlight entering via the window, as it gracefully dances across the bed, only interrupted by the forceful shadows of passers by as they go about their business. Occasionally, I hear the same womanly forced giggle, as some folk pass by, which draws me to the conclusion that a very successful hooker operates out of one of my neighbouring rooms. I didn’t let anybody in here... “What?” The man continues, stepping out from the shadows, allowing the moonlight to illuminate the right side of his face. He looked like Mickey from those damned Rocky movies, hell, spoke similar to him as well. I’m often too intense to pass for easy- going, but at the same time I don’t care enough about anything to be angry all the time. That’s not to say I don’t get angry. I open my eyes and see the hallucination slumped on the edge of my bed directly in front of me, with a knowing grin plastered across it’s face. It’s pushed all the buttons necessary to piss me off, and what’s more, it knows it too. Bastard. “Fuck you!” I scream at the thing, immediately chastising myself for not keeping my cool. That fucking rookie... “I guess I hit a sore spot there, kid.” I close my eyes again, this time wishing the thing’ll just fuck the hell off. If it is part of my mind, shouldn’t I at least have some control over it? “Good, ya needed it, ya bum.” My taste for bourbon has dimmed some over the last few minutes, but I take another mouthful of the stuff anyway and I pretend to enjoy it. Hell, I only did it to piss the thing sitting across from me off, childish, I realise, but I don’t have many options here; the thing’s a fucking hallucination! “Ya remember the promise?” It asks. “Ya remember promising yer wife, on her deathbed no less, that yer’d make an effort to win, succeed and prosper?” It pauses. Don’t bother me much, I don’t intend to be using that bed anyway. She is -- was -- my wife! Fuck... “Seems real pathetic to me, kid.” “Yeah, well I never missed a promise I made to that woman in ten damn years of marriage,” I tell it. “I ain’t planning to start now.” “So yer turn to drinkin” to try an forget it?” That better be a rhetorical question. “That’s even more pathetic!” “Yeah, well, I’m a very pathetic man.” The thing raises it’s eyebrows. “Yeah, kid, we’ve established that.” It sighs. “So what amde ya turn to tha bottle this time?” “A whole heap of things,” I tell it. This thing is a part of me, shouldn’t it already know all of this. Of course, it does. The annoying fuck just wants me to say it out loud. I oblige it, hoping it will make the fucking thing go away -- for good. “I started well. I won matches. I won the GCW television title in my second match, hell, I even won the Infinite Gauntlet championship.” “Nice,” The hallucination comments. Win, no.” “Kid, that wasn’t your fault,” it says. “That damned masked moron cost you that match.” “Still, there’s no excuse,” I tell him. “And it’s been downhill from there. It’s the best memory I have of our time together and pretty poignant after the evening I’ve just had in there with my imaginary “friend”. That fucker... Anyway, come the morning, I awake at an ungodly hour and find both the stalker and the hallucination are gone. I breathe a heavy sigh of relief. Did that happen last night? I don’t know how, but I know regardless. A lot was said last night and I wish to god that it hadn’t, but at the end of the day I needed it. Drinking my sorrows away isn’t going to do me any good, if I am to succeed and to keep my promise to Mary. The twenty-fourth round of the Infinite Gauntlet is coming up and I intend to be in it. The rest of what was said and the damned stalker, I will ignore. I’m not ready for either and I doubt I ever will be. Fuck you, Burgess Meredith. Fuck you.
