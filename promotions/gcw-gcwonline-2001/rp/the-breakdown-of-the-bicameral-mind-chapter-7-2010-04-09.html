The Breakdown of the Bicameral Mind: Chapter 7

9 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/09/10 The waves crushed the side of the fort with incredible force. White foam rose incredibly high as the clash between water with stone was once again won by the wall that had seen countless battles before. The sea was ever persistent though. It would not stop until that fort was destroyed, even if it took centuries, even millennia. It wasn’t sad… the look that you gave the lonely bastion of the old city’s defense, but it was resignation. You couldn’t help but root for the chaos that was the ocean. There was like a feeling of solidarity between you and the ocean. The smell of salt water invaded your nostrils as you sat on the green grass in front of El Morro. Normally, the place would be full of families spending the day with picnics and flying kites, but this wasn’t such a day. It was rainy and somber. It was a day to stay home and watch television. Not for you though. It was one of those nights for the children of the night to prowl in. It was also silence. Someone once said that silence was the most beautiful music ever made and you agreed with that. In fact, it was so silent that there was no way to hide any sounds. Including those of someone hiding somewhere near where you sat. There was no reaction from you though. You kept calm and collected as you waited for the person to reveal themselves. There was no other sound coming from the hiding spot of whomever it was that hid in that place. “Come out, come out, wherever you are,” you playfully sang out loud. It was not a direct threat but anyone could hear the subtle menace in your voice. After waiting a few moments for an answer, you kept calling out for the person to reveal himself. “Once I get up from this spot, there will be no mercy from me,” you called out. There was still no answer. “Suite yourself,” came the threatening growl. You started to stand up but before you could do it completely, the person finally revealed herself. “Take it easy, big boy. It’s just me,” she said rather too casually for you taste. “It’s me. Just little old me.” “Who are you, exactly?” the menace never left your voice. Although there was interest now in the way you asked the question. If this woman had been following you all along, she must be someone to pay attention to. She puts her hand on her chest and feigns offense. “I can’t believe you don’t remember me. I feel insulted after all the time we spent together,” she begins to get closer and circle around you close to how a lioness would encircle her prey. “Does the name Matheson ring any bells?” As soon as you heard that name, your fists tightened. The knuckles of your hands turned white with the pressure of your clenched fists. The memories came flooding back. Days that you spent drugged up and with no hope to escape as your very being was ripped out from you and replaced with a shell. Not just any shell though, but the one that would change the world. The one that would create the super soldier to end all wars. “Matheson is dead. The death I gave him was my gift to him before he left us forever. I can’t help but wonder why do you still do his bidding? He’s no longer there to handle our strings. We are two of a kind,” you told her, trying to reason with Eve. “You are right. We are two of a kind. There is no one as valuable as the both of us right now,” she agreed, she still paced slowly and methodically. “You are wrong about one important thing, though. Matheson is not dead.” The fury emanated from your eyes as they bore holes in the flesh of the woman that tormented you. You began to pace as well, trying to counter her positioning in case something went down. “What do you mean he is not dead? I killed him with my own hands. I destroyed that pompous bastard. He thought he was as good as we were. He was wrong.” She leaned her head back and laughed hard. The sound of her laughter irritated you. It must have been that and the sounds of the wave battling the stones that egged you on. You felt it, deep inside you, a sudden rage that surged and enveloped you in a way that there was no way to control. “You feel it, don’t you? The anger… the fury? As if you will never be complete unless you are one with it? Embrace it!” Your muscles tensed. The feeling all around your body was like it was contracting into itself and it would at one point explode to the outside. And you did. You rushed towards her and tried to tackle her to the floor. She had been practicing this for longer than you had so she was able to quickly dodge your attack and grab you by the neck as you went by her. She dropped you and the floor and put all her weight on your back while she twisted your arm, much like cops do when they catch a criminal. “It’s there! The power is definitely there, I can feel it! You need to harness it, to control it. It is too frenzied. Control it,” she whispered in your ear. ------ Years ago… The sun shone brightly on the horizon. It seemed to be brighter than ever as it attacked the eyes of the people who were getting out of the plane that had brought them to this place. It was a barren place. The sand burned as much as the sun. You could see that weird reflection in the distance when the floor is as hot as the air. He got off that plane last and stood back for a moment as he tried to get used to the heat. They had told him the place was sizzling, but they never told him how much. His uniform didn’t help either. He shifted his body against his uniform and felt the sweat dripping down his back. Being someone from cold climates, it was hard for him to enjoy that place too much. “Ugh… I’m just going to have to get used to this damned heat,” he said to himself out loud. The man in front of him spun around and punched him in the arm playfully but hard. “Stop whining, dude. We are finally here!” The man in front of him couldn’t help but exude excitement. For years he had spoken about one thing he needed to do in order for his life to be complete. He already had his beautiful wife and his beautiful daughter. He had his best friend since elementary school and now he was honoring his father by protecting the freedoms that his father had long ago protected as well. Kenneth Kinch was in Iraq on the orders of his president George H.W. Bush. Politics had never really been his thing and if his father had found out he had voted for Dukakis, he would have probably been disowned. He trusted Bush, though. His voice echoed inside his head. ”I've told the American people before that this will not be another Vietnam…” He believed him. Kenneth’s best friend and brother-in-law was never much of a war enthusiast. He pretty much disliked most violence and thought it was a disease that should probably be eradicated from the human condition. It was not the violence that attracted him to Iraq; it was the chance to prove that he could do what needed to be done. They slowly walked towards their welcome party and they prepared to change the world. ----- “I know. I know. Don’t worry about me. It’s all been fine here…” Ken looked back and made a face to his fellow mates. He was used to the over protective nature of his wife and was used to hearing all her orders. “Honey, please, you sound worse than my drill sergeant.” Ken’s squad mates laughed out loud. “I’m sorry, honey. I know you are back home taking care of Becky alone. Don’t worry. My time here will fly through and soon enough we will be together once more,” Ken said trying to soothe his wife. His friends ‘awww’ed. “Fine, I will put your brother on.” He forgot something very important. The scream from the other end of the phone probably gave the Royal Guard longitude and latitude to the base. “Sorry, honey… I love you.” All of Kenneth’s squad mates screamed ‘We love you too!’ Ken looked at his brother-in-law, shook his head and handed him the receiver. “Hey Beks, how are you doing?” he asked in an upbeat voice, trying to get her not to worry as much. It never worked. “I know. Don’t worry about him.” He sighed loudly. “I promise I will get him back home alive.” One of his mates shouted for him to hurry and he did. “Love you, sis. We’ll be back before you know it.” He hung up. It was hard for him to be away from his sister. Besides Ken, she was his only other friend. It was hard for him to make new friends. It wasn’t that he was socially impaired, but it just didn’t come naturally for him. He had high expectations for the people he called friends. “Your sister is a handful, man. I don’t think she gets why I’m here,” Ken said to his friend as they both sat on their own cots. “Me being here is incredibly important to me.” His best friend, who people had started to call Iceman in honor of the movie Top Gun and the fact that his personality was just that, nodded in agreement. “You have to understand, though. She is worried about us. Put yourself in her position. Would you like her to be here in some futile war that could get her killed? I wouldn’t,” he tried to reason with Kenneth. “I understand that. I do,” he sighed sadly. “I just feel like this is my calling.” ------ You thrashed about until Eve got of your back and you were on your feet once again. The anger kept rising and rising inside you. There was no way to stop it. The clouds started to darken in the sky, as if the gods fought their own battle in the heavens they ruled. Wasn’t everything but a battle for survival and power? Your head felt like it was about to split in two. The pain was too much for you to handle. She bellowed out laughter into the coming darkness. The clouds had won their skirmish against the sun and El Morro was thrust into a sudden and complete darkness. It was an evil laughter that now seemed to be everywhere around you as your vision blurred from all the rage. “What’s wrong, Antonio? Are your eyes troubling you? What about your hearing?” As soon as she said it you began to hear a high pitched buzzing sound that attacked you senses. Soon enough, you crumbled to the ground in a heap and covered your ears with your hands. She was at your side as soon as you hit the ground. The touch of her hands helped to soothe you somewhat. You were losing consciousness fast. She lowered her head and whispered softly in your ear. “Try to control the rage. Concentrate on pushing it deep down inside you.” You tried. The pain wouldn’t subside. It felt like your head was being smashed by a sledgehammer. “Concentrate,” he voice became smoother and it seemed to alleviate some of the pain you were going through. After a while, your body began to tremble and the pain stopped. Your eyes closed and you went unconscious. “You still have a lot to learn, my Adam,” Eve said out loud as she passed her hand through your matted hair. “And I am here to teach you everything.” ------- “AAAAAAAAAHHHHHHHHHHH!!!...” he coughed as he wrestled against the cables that hooked him up to the machines. The nurses rushed towards his side as he thrashed about. The harsh sound of metal hitting metal woke up a couple of the patients trying to sleep. The nurses retrained his extremities until the doctor appeared and gave him a shot. As soon as the liquid entered his blood stream, his body relaxed and slumped back down on the bed. None of the staff could believe that the man had actually gotten up. It had been a year since he had been interned in that Veteran’s Hospital and the man had never shown any signs that he would get up at all. They let him rest. The tranquilizer would help him to relax a little before he got up. The hospital staff had patched him up after hours upon hours of hard work. Apparently, an RPG had been fired in the direction of his squad and decimated most of them, as they tried to defend themselves from a surprise attack. The explosion had sent a piece of cement flying at a high speed and it struck him on his head. The staff worked diligently to save the man’s life. They had to put a metal plate to protect his brain, but they did it. They were all very proud of their work. When they heard his story, they were destroyed. Nobody wanted to be the one to tell him. They were relieved that for so long no one had to. Now that he was awake, someone was dreading the job of telling him. He woke up the next day. Disorientation was usual in coma patients, but he was immediately asking for the only family he had. It was not fun telling him that he had no family left. The man looked at the wall all the while the story was being told. He looked at the wall but stared off into some distant place nobody but he could see. The RPG had struck right besides his brother in law. There had been nothing they could have done about him, or the few pieces they found of him. When they told the news to the man’s sister, she didn’t handle it too well. She had lost her husband and father to her daughter and her brother was as good as dead. She snapped one day. Neither she nor her daughter ever saw the light of day again. The story was told to him with all the details. They thought he deserved to know the whole truth and to move forward from there. Besides, he would eventually find out anyway. His brother in law and his sister where the only family he had had. Tears formed on his eyes. There was no emotion on his face, but it was obvious that he had heard and understood everything they had told him. The staff left him there with his own thoughts. He never moved that whole night. The next day, he passed his hand softly through his head and felt the scar. ------ The scar was the one thing he could never make disappear, that and the memories that would haunt them forever. He traced the scar on his head with his fingers. What happened still angered him. He couldn’t believe how he had been so stupid and how his best friend could have been so useless and how his sister could have been so weak. Someone knocked on the door. “Dr. Matheson, your three o’clock is here.” The memories vanished. There was still a lot of work to be done. “Send her in.” The final stages were beginning to take shape. “Welcome, Miss Rodriguez. We have his location and have gotten the plane ticket for you to go and meet him.”
