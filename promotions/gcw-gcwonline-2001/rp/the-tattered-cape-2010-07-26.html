The Tattered Cape

26 July 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/26/10 Clyde Fox. Age 7. Alternate universe. 1967. The bunny ears on the television set are crooked at best. Maybe that’s why there are lines of static filtering through the picture. If anything, it’s some sort of homage to the latest economic trends. Up, then down and up again. The market has seen it’s fair share of shakeups, none of which should be any concern to a seven-year-old Clyde Fox who finds himself plunked right in front of the television set. The gold trim around the picture is a signature for televisions from this time. The rest of the room’s decor most certainly follows suit. "Annnnnnnnnd now, boys and girls, the moment you’ve all been waiting for... get ready because here he comes... you’re "Hero of Hearts," COMMANDER SKIP VIGNETTE!" A showy voice rings from the speakers. The sets black and white display continually struggles to maintain a solid signal, as all that can be seen is a curtain being spread open. The squeaky sound of the brass rings sliding along the curtain rod creates an awkward moment. Then... slowly... a drum roll... starts. Clyde doesn’t notice anything, but his child-like body slowly starts to lean forward in excitement. The anticipation rises in the air... Clyde Fox. Age 22. Current universe. 2010. "The myth of Commander Skip Vignette," Fox’s voice reads. That title remains in bold block letters atop the comic book in our hero’s hot little hands. It’s a brand new issue for the month of July 2010. The printing is nothing less than exquisite. The yellow background really catches the eye as a fully figured, muscle bound freak stands heroically over a vast canyon. The figure is none other than comic book legend, Commander Skip Vignette. Known for his heroics in every form of the word, Skip has entertained generation after generation. He’s one of the few superheroes that have managed to reinvent himself as the times have changed. "I so want this!" Fox mutters to himself. Skip is sporting his typical outfit for this generation. A full Under Armor bodysuit that has it’s own unique red and white design. Clyde has never seen anything like it before. Heck, it’s even got a rather large Under Armor logo on the breastplate! That’s what we call 21st Century commercial advertising at its finest. Fox rubs his oily thumbs up and down the edge of the comic book. He likes how smooth the cover feels. He also likes to imagine himself as the illustrious Commander Skip Vignette. Oh em gee! The newest Commander Skip Vignette comic book is out! I’m so Jell-O! I remember when my Mommy used to buy me subscriptions to Commander Skip’s excellent adventures! I had almost every comic book of his from like 1994 to 1999. Then my Mommy was cleaning out the basement one day and threw them in the garbage! She said I was too old for them and she stopped buying me the subscriptions. I wost every single issue I had; even the special birthday edition with those neat-o 3D glasses! Boy, was there anything that the great Commander Skip couldn’t do? He always saved the day... except he didn’t save my comic book collection. Clyde continues to fondle the book as he thinks back to the glory days. As he proceeds to look out of place standing there in the middle of the action adventure aisle, a plump man waddles his way over to Clyde. His nametag shines under the florescent lighting, clearly indicating that he’s part of the staff. "Sssssshhhhhsssshhhh, Excusssse me sir," The store clerk slurs towards Fox, "But ummm, are going to buy that comic book or just get your greasssshy paws all over it? Becausssse if that’s the cassssse, shhhhhhhssshhhhh, I’m going to have to assshk you to leave." The clerk’s lisp is quite clear as he gurgles his words in a mess of saliva. Clyde notices the man’s heavy breathing and can’t help but overreact in thinking he’s having some sort of heart malfunction at the moment. Clyde lowers the comic book in front of his waist and stares lovingly into the man’s eyes. "Heto there, little man," Fox greets, "Pwease forgive my busy little digits. I just got too excited when I saw that the new Commander Skip comic book was out and I didn’t know what to do." The comic book guy promptly backs off. His bad boy attitude has that effect on people. Still, nonetheless, Clyde remains standing there with the comic book in hand. The chubby gentleman can see the thumbprints glisten in the light. Obsessive thoughts about cleaning the comic book run wildly through his manic mind. He takes such pride and joy in scrubbing down each new item the store receives and it just so happens that Commander Skip is by far his favorite. "That’ssssss okay, I guessh. Jussssht don’t hold onto the comic book for that long pleassse. I have many people that want to come into the store and buy a copy today and no one wants a mucked up cover," The comic book rep states. Fox nods as the man carries on with his business. Clyde keeps his eyes on the man’s girth for as long as it stays in sight. Once he is completely out of view, Clyde promptly folds the book over and stuffs it down his pants. Tee hee. Now I don’t have to pay for this comic book! Consider this payback for my Mommy not renewing my subscription! And to think, all I had to do for a new issue was to walk in here and muck up the cover! Score! Now I just have to walk by the front cash and I’m home free! But that’s not the usual Clyde, that’s for sure. It seems like his past grudge against his mother declining his Commander Skip subscription has really stayed with him through all these years. How would Commander Skip view this devious action? How could Clyde truly go through with it considering everything good and just that Commander Skip Vignette stood for? Well there’s certainly more than meets the eye with anyone’s hero... Clyde Fox. Age 7. Alternate universe. 1967. TA DA! "Oh my God! It’s him! It’s really him!" "It’s Commander Skip! Hey, look! Over here!" "Skip! Skip! It’s really you!" Everyone bows down to Commander Skip. That hunka-hunk of steaming hunk makes women want to have his babies and young men to live the just life. He makes criminals cower in fear and he so well known that he’s even able to upstage himself. Why yes, Commander Skip can do can do. He’s a man’s man with a brash sense of style, life and culture. He’s a child’s hero and woman’s fantasy and an all around American icon. Why he’s Skip Vignette. Who wouldn’t want to be him? The crowd shouts out at will as Commander Skip Vignette steps out from behind the curtain. Flash bulbs crackle as he flashes his trademark side smile. His teeth are whiter and stronger than yours. He nods to the presume crowd on hand. He’s got a bigger fan club than you. His rootin, tootin cowboy costume is spotless. He’s got more style than you. His face is perfectly powdered. He’s the single greatest looking man you’ve ever seen. He’s not an actor. He’s an ideal. He’s a late 60’s ideal that swoops up everything in its path. Clyde bites his lower lip, as the excitement is just too much. His hero finally appears on screen. There’s no better way to describe his physique than to just gaze upon his fully featured face. For Clyde, this is the moment he’s been waiting for. It’s finally show time! "Why hello there friends," Skip welcomes in his fake western accent, "Nice seein y’all again. Welcome to Commander Skip’s Wild Western Shootout!" The fans go crazy. Inside, Clyde Fox goes crazy. It’s not everyday that he gets to stay up late and watch his hero on the television set. These are rare times indeed. Times that not even the likes of Clyde Fox could forget. "I want all y’all in the audience, and all y’all in television land to get your buckaroo cowboy hats on because we’re going on an adventure!" Skip promotes. Unfortunately for Clyde, his family isn’t wealthy enough to own many other things outside of a television. It took them much longer than expected to save up for just that anyways. But that never let Clyde get down. He knows that his imagination can be greater than any one toy or prop. So he puts on his make believe hat. "Alright now skippers, y’all ready to go kill us some Indians!?" Skip asks. "YEAH, SKIP!" The crowd shouts back. "Alrighty then, let’s go kill us some Indians!" Skip celebrates. Clyde Fox. Age 22. Current universe. 2010. Clyde waltzes up to the front counter. He freezes. There’s just no way he can actually go through with this can he? I mean, committing theft goes against everything Skip Vignette stands for and Clyde Fox especially stands for anything that his comic book hero does. "Oh, itssssh you," A familiar voice sounds. Indeed it’s the cliche, overweight comic book worker slowly making his way to the employee side of the desk. Clyde stands there, praying he doesn’t notice the awkward book shaped bulge in his pants. "Ssssso. Are you getting anything sssshir?" He asks. Clyde just stands there and mashes his lips together. He turns his head and notices a young lad standing diagonally behind him. Fox kind of side shuffles out of the way and nods to the lad to go ahead of him. The child promptly skips up to the counter and places a pack of gum and a chocolate bar on the desk. The worker sluggishly grabs them, scans them with his eyes; because he wants them, and then rings them into the cash register. "Kay, that will be three bucks," The man says. The kid reaches into his pocket and gently tosses some change onto the counter before collecting his goodies and running off. Clyde watches the child fondly as he could relate back to his childhood, whether it is his actual one or another one in some offbeat, distant universe. Regardless of era, regardless of stance, regardless of persona, Skip Vignette would always remain the same. His impact on Clyde as a child was forever tainted by outside interference. None of which should be held against a hero though, right? Just then, the boy trips over a stand of Suzy Action Girl comics! The iron rack comes crashing down towards the boy just as Clyde swoops in and saves the day. With his agility, Clyde is ability to catch the rack just before it slams down on the kid. "Are you otay?" Clyde asks instinctively. The boy nods nervously. "Hey, kid! You gotta watch where you’re going!" The comic book man yells, "You get up and... hey! HEY! WAIT!" But before the man could carry himself over to the scene of the accident, the boy gets up and speeds out of the store. Clyde places the rack back on it’s feet and begins to pick up the scattered comics. "Oh, don’t worry about thossssse," The comic man says, "Thossssse are girl comics. No one really likes thosssse anyways." You know what, Mister? You’re a big meanie. Now I’m not one to judge but I do know this; as a worker it is your job to avoid all biases. And if my bwain is serving me correctly, then the great "Hero of Hearts" Skip Vignette would say that each comic book, or for that matter, each interest should be treated equally. Even though this rack is on sale and even though this rack holds some of the worst comic books in history, guess what; they’re still comic books. They still have a place in this store and they still have a place in people’s hearts. Clyde rises to his feet, a much braver man. He hasn’t quite realized what he’s done but it’s more than just defending a set of horrible comic books. It’s helping a fellow person when in trouble. It’s being reactive in the face of pressure. It’s... it’s being what Skip Vignette is. A symbol of truth. A symbol of hope. A symbol of heroism. Suddenly, Clyde looks down and notices that the Skip Vignette comic must’ve fallen out of his pants during the whole ordeal. "Oh, hey! Look at that!" The comic book man states as he notices the crinkled treasure, "How did that get there? That kid mussssht’ve tried to sssshteal it or shomething. Hmph, oh well. Hey ummm, you wana buy thissssh? I’ll give it to you for half off seeing that it’sssh damaged." Fox simply shakes his head. "Nah, that’s otay. You know what, I’ll pay full price for it," Clyde states as he pulls out some cash and hands it to the man, "You have a nice day now." The comic book man graciously accepts the money and returns the sentiments. He then hands Fox his comic book. Confidently, Clyde marches out of the store with his renewed collection of Skip Vignette comic books in hand. All one of them. You see, to Clyde, a hero doesn’t have to be an ideal. A hero doesn’t even really have to exist. Just as long as you’re willing to do what it takes to become like one, can make you one. There he stands. Clyde Fox. No cape. No fans. Just another person doing just another unnoticed deed. Little does he know, but that would all change... soon...
