A Legacy

14 April 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/14/11 Legacy. Everyone wants to have one in this industry. Everyone wants to be remembered for something, to leave an imprint on the business they spent their life with. Sadly, not everyone does. Most are forgotten because although they may have been hard workers in this industry of ours, the never managed that special something. You know, I envy those people. My legacy is linked to the death of promotions. For years I was the guy brought in as a last ditch effort, one that never paid off. Online I was a source of hatred, in the industry I was a punchline. Hell, I'm fortunate enough to have graced the cover of a book: The Death of Action! Wrestling. Right there, on the front cover, getting handed the World Title. Not earning it. Getting handed it. As if my career wasn't already joke enough, I get remembered as a backstage "politician" before I get remembered as a wrestler. That was and is my legacy. So why reflect on my career now? Well disembodied internet voice, I'm reflecting now because I realised mere weeks ago that I'm very much in the twilight of my career. I mean when was the last time I do anything impactful in this industry? Why was the last time everyone went around talking about Phil Atken? At least as a figure of hate I was spoken about, now I'm just the former star, struggling to stay relevant and one bad fall away from retirement. Yet, like a barnacle I cling on. Surely though, surely my next big moment is just around the corner? Well... No. As I reflected I realised that I have no big moment left in me, so last week I went in front of everyone and explained my new mindset. I'm here to get paid, maybe build myself a nice little retirement fund while I'm at it. You won't see Phil Atken pulling the strings anymore, you won't see the loud mouthed "I may have lost but I'm still the best" ridiculous interview logic. All you will see is a man who has the end very much in sight. I don't need to be "that guy" anymore. It's not worth being that guy. Which I suppose brings everything nicely to Rampage. You see the Rampage Tournament is sold as an event that makes stars but if you look at it's history, all Rampage does is make one star, the winner. Look at any opening rounds, hell even quarter finals of any past Rampage and tell me where the losers are now. Most of them disappear without mention, they may have put up a hell of a fight but they lose and are forgotten about. It makes me wonder if I should have let myself be forgotten about when I was a stepping stone of the launch of Andy Murray's career. Maybe it would've been better to be remembered as the jobber who played a part in the making of a superstar. Maybe, maybe, maybe. I didn't though, I stayed with GCW, I toiled away, I lost match after match but was not to be deterred, my day was just around the corner. I was finally going to reclaim my name, I was finally going to correct the Atken legacy. No longer a politician without talent, I was going to climb the ranks from the very bottom of GCW and once again make a name for myself. That didn't happen. Instead I disappeared on one of my famed no notice “vacations”, and if I recall correctly missed the following years Rampage. Instead... I don't know what I was doing. Probably losing wrestling matches. That isn't important. So last year, Rampage rolled around again, surely I could better my last effort? This, this would finally be the time for me to climb the ranks once more... Welp. Oh well, clearly I wasn't made for Rampage tournaments but 2010, that'd be the year of Atken. Oh, what's that? No one cared about me and I got zero votes for “Most Improved Wrestler” much to the delight of Eddie Fowler? As if I wasn't enough of a joke, I got an international broadcast to rub it in. That can do funny things to a man's moral. When the awards were announced, the realisation that the end was nigh was slowly sinking it, it wasn't quite there yet but it was getting there. It sunk in when I saw that one, I was assigned once more to my nemesis, the Rampage Tournament and two, I was placed in the group with some of the hottest properties GCW has. I may have drew with Aaron Fujita last week, but let's be realistic, that had nothing to do with me. Fujita made a rookie mistake thanks to my associate, I was too busy trying to get the number a pretty lil lady (might as well do it while I still can). This week I face the formerly undefeated El Moreno. Man, winning streaks, I wonder what they feel like. As I realist, I know odds on, El Moreno will win, I mean he'd almost have to. I don't have that spark anymore and he's a forest fire unto himself. So while others may have been the most charismatic star of their generation, the best wrestler, the lovable underdog or the brutal monster, my legacy in this industry is a little different: I'm a politician, a joke and a jobber. Making peace with that fact has at least given me some comfort. At least I've got a job in this economy.
