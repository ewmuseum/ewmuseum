Nothing to Say

15 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 15, 2005 'When we were at that bar--' 'Cantina' 'Whatever, you said you were gettin' into the federation.' 'Did I?' 'Shyeah. I called you insane.' 'I meant I'm getting Marauder in.' 'I know, just wanted to make sure. Y'never corrected yourself.' 'Is that all you've been thinkin' about?' 'It's been buggin' me, yeah.' 'Christ, Trey. Come on now. That pay-per-view thing's a lot more important than my verbal iniquity.' 'To business, yeah. Ain't got much of a head for business though.' 'Me either.' 'I know. You never been professional enough for this whole suit act to work.' 'I'm trying.' 'How's it goin'?' 'Still trying to live up to the talent we've got.' 'To the me you got.' Trey cocked his head back and laughed, breaking up the smoke that was stagnating in the air. It was a second-hand high cackle and full of boisterous glee. I laughed at it as he took a swig from a brown paper bag. 'You're not the only star.' 'But I'm the only one you can trust, yeah?' 'I trust Erik, Eva. We go back.' 'That ain't trust in your voice, man. You know that little chick's fuckin' around behind alla our backs.' 'How was it?' 'Huh?' 'The fucking.' 'S'alright. What, you haven't hit it?' 'Too tall for me.' 'Shit. Why you askin'?' 'Well, I don't think 'alright' sex is enough to bring LeStatt over to our side. Assuming.' He nodded at me, King Jordan the Disappointed. 'Just pretend I've got a handle on her.' 'Can't pretend you trust her. What about her bro?' 'What about him?' 'I mean, dude ain't doin' much. Still hasn't shown himself as much other than bein' German, an' I'm not sure I buy that.' 'Yeah, they're German.' 'Well, you do go back or whatever, so I'ma take that as true. Just sayin' I won't hesitate to deal with either of 'em, it comes up.' 'Trey.' 'Yeah?' 'She's a goddamned cunt and I'm gonna end up with a knife in my back if I keep this company, yeah. If it comes up, though, it'll come up in a board room, from the far end of a comically long table. Not sure you'll wanna bust in and punish the traitor then.' 'There a window?' 'Yeah.' 'I'm cool.' 'Blood in the water today?' 'Nah, just gettin' the table ready. Where we headed now?' I looked at the clock, truthful bastard, and knew I'd fucked up. 'Nowhere now.' 'Miss the plane?' 'Nah, they closed the gates.' 'Gates?' 'Yeah, on the road to glory. Remember where the hotel was?' 'You sure we can't bumrush the guards?' 'Won't work. Anyone there would know we didn't fit in; we'd be killed before we got anywhere. Hotel?' 'Nah, man. This glory idea sounds cool, I'm tired of shitty hotel rooms.' 'We get suites, Trey. Plural.' 'Yeah, but they ain't executive. Shit man, I ain't even buy my own car. That was on Anarch money.' 'It matters?' 'Shyeah, it matters. How much fun you think it is bein' this close to all the money in the world and not havin' a wallet full?' 'Must've been expensive blood.' 'Hah, guess so. Habit forming shit.' 'It's like crack.' 'Nah, more destructive. Got a kid like you wearin' fur coats? Fame's a crazy bitch.' 'Always fall for the crazy ones. Wasn't it that way?' 'What?' 'The hotel. I think we have to hit the highway.' 'Fuck the hotel, J.' 'Tulz wanted liquor. Think about the poor force of nature waiting on us.' 'She can chill for a little bit. This road to glory shit got my interest.' 'I was bullshitting.' 'Bastard.' He picked the bag up again, then dropped it back to his lap and messed with levers until his seat dropped back. 'Shit, headache.' 'Pot?' 'Nah, still clean.' 'You think Anarch's gonna push a piss test.' 'Haha, nah man.' 'GCW? Christ, every one of those swine's on painkillers.' 'Nah, you know it ain't even like that.' 'Yeah, going straight to save your life, sure. How's that working out for you, drunky?' 'I'm celebratin'.' 'That you haven't lost anything major yet?' 'That I'm famous as shit and only gettin' bigger. And shit, who loses?' 'I don't know--losers?' 'I was gonna say 'the other guy,' but that works. 'Ey, isn't that the hotel?' His hand jerked up to point at something that was already more well suited to the rear-view mirror. It looked right, so I slowed down and pulled into the next parking lot up. 'Is it?' 'It was the Boston Harbor, right?' 'Yeah.' I lit a cigarette and looked over, trying to catch something familiar. 'I think.' 'I mean, it looks like the place.' 'Yeah, yeah. I remember pissing off the balcony into the harbor last night. Good call.' We went through the procedures--whipped back out into the street, pulled into the proper parking place, that shit--and stumbled blindly into the lobby, babbling like retards would if influenced by the same sources that we were finding inspiration from. Nothing was familiar, my mind remembering a dozen little details from all the hotels we'd slipped through in the last few weeks, reservations running out and falling to the next in line, always moving and finding a new place to rest during the long wait. It didn't have to be explained to us or to the fans; it was a needed break for all involved. Never assume otherwise. 'Do you need help, Mr. White?' They knew my face. I was paranoid until it became clear that anyone would remember a face as far from lucid as mine had grown by then. It never got the time to settle. 'Room,' I blurted in sheer terror. 'Rest.' The duffel bag in my hand clinked as he tried to steal it from me. I sneered as best I could. 'Right this way, Mr. White.' I thought he winked at someone in the corner right before he turned to march up the steps. Our procession was a horrible joke, my head still more keen on conversation than motor control and Trey's head in another world entirely. 'If you'd keep the bottle away, Mr. Tremendous.' 'Goddamn, Trey. Your name's ridiculous.' The attendant, a clean-cut blonde kid with an inch on me and nothing on Trey, snapped his head back and bared his teeth to us. Trey took it in stride and took another gulp from the bottle. 'Shyeah. Tremendous. Who'd buy that shit?' The elevator dinged and we quickly whisked away before the spectacle could ensue--a shame, by any rights--and got dragged to the upper levels of the hotel with the clear intent of hiding us from the public. The swine had us of course, and left us with no alternatives but humming to the muzak and quietly shuffling down the hall when the time came. 'Gimme a few, J. Tired of smellin' like fish.' 'Everything here smells like fish.' 'Exactly. I take a little pride in bein' the stand-out, y'know?' We split up and found our rooms, if a little clumsily. I was seconds from giving myself a full strip search when Tulz pulled the door open, disturbed by my frantic knocking. She smiled and took the duffel bag from my hands and tossed it to the bed with the scary thud of lots of glass holding lots of liquor. 'You doing alright?' 'Thirsty.' Her body loomed over the bag, casting it with what seemed like the only shadow in the room. I walked over to her, glancing over my shoulder to try to explain why I felt like I was outside. I was displaced, off-guard, felt tiny in the massive landscape I'd bought my way into. Hopelessly lost, I tried to make it past the bed as Tulz shoved a bottle in my face with playful zeal. I took it and went to the balcony to look out over Boston's financial district. 'Heh.' Tulz voice came from behind me, softer than I was used to it. 'Nice one.' 'I'll never get used to the mind-reading thing.' 'Oh, I realized it last night. Just didn't see why I should stop you.' 'So my life wouldn't be such a comedy of errors?' 'Don't worry.' 'I'm not worried.' Her arms wrapped tight around my waist, driving sharp elbows against my pelvis. 'I'm not.' 'Jordan, look--' 'Drink.' 'I'm leaving.' 'Hey, it was your idea.' 'Not now; soon.' 'What, I grab the wrong booze?' 'I'm serious. I'm more of a burden at this point, and you don't need the added stress.' 'Everything's cool.' 'You're losing your mind. Give up the god hunt for now. Run your company. I'll find you again, when the time's right.' 'One more week.' 'What?' 'Just stick around long enough for one more week of random, gratuitous psychoses.' 'I appreciate the offer.' 'That's no answer.' 'I'll let you know.' 'Take your time, by all means.' 'Jordan.' 'What?' 'Don't try to read my thoughts. You know it won't work.' 'Always worth a shot. Hey, what do you think about Trey's chances?' 'I told you to not worry, Jordan.' 'Well, I saw it coming--the you leaving.' I cussed under my breath, a hearty 'fuck,' for imagining a need to explain myself. 'I know. It's why I told you.' 'A stitch in time saves nine.' 'Cute.' 'But a wrinkle in time just fucks everyone's shit up.' Loose in the city, my thoughts turned to survival. The lights of storefronts and billboards and monolithic logos that surveyed all of Babylon began to die bit by bit and served as a better guide to my hard timeline than any clock would've for my trek through the urban maze. I feared Trey a casualty, a thousand times more bent than I and lost somewhere near the harbor. There was no doubt that he'd decided to swim back to Detroit and I became accustomed to the idea even without evidence--precautions must be taken in event of the worst. I could always find someone with a passing similarity and let him go out to and break his neck while wrestling for the missing Trey, half-full of PCP and deranged enough to not question the wrestling ring or the tens of thousands of screaming geeks. Christ, that's a good idea. Rain broke lightly on the back of my neck, an escape from the city that held me down. Thoughts passed in quick rotation like joints and I found myself huddled underneath a street light. Tulz kept her pace and checked around the corner, lighting one of those joints I referenced with a sense of security like only she could know. The smell drew the bar crowd, faces as fresh as the sweat that covered them, and I smoked when she passed it. 'They'd hang Dread Scott downtown right now if they got the chance. Nothing's changed in a hundred and fifty years but the faces--has anyone seen Trey?' A few negative voices rose and waned. 'Christ, he's lost for good this time--really lost. Not just like everyone else, suckling at the pallid teat of the American dream, but actually fucking lost. Nowhere for him to go, nowhere to turn. This what you kids wanted? Push the asshole until he went insane for the self-doubt, then laugh under your veils at the wake--fucking right.' The smoke was a Californian August in my throat and came out as hellfire in the cool night air. It carried my words up and into the clouds, a hollow protest against all injustice--and in being against all injustice, losing its worth and impact. People lingered in between sentences and paragraphs. 'How many of you bastards even knew that the game was over?' Voices, again. 'What was the score? Who won? Fucking right no one won. We were too busy picking up the shattered fragments of our parents' coke mirrors to mend our own lives, or realize what the hell we were holding on to.' Tulz laughed while others sighed or moaned or cheered or jeered or merely passed with a moment's interest. There was no smoke left, so none rose from my lungs and my words lost a little more of their power. Didn't matter. Doesn't matter: 'What of the fucking bulldogs that run this country?' Doesn't matter, either: 'What're we going to do when we're supposed to take up their mantle?' They'd had enough. I screamed at the top of my lungs too long for them to care anymore, and screamed until they hated me, and screamed until they all walked away except from the small core that were psychotic enough to believe all the shit. Like a ghost. 'Trey.' 'What's up, J?' 'Knew I'd find you somewhere.' 'Heh, more like I found you.' 'Sure thing. Let's get the fuck outta here. I don't like this crowd.' 'Not until you let me in on that road to glory shit.' 'I told you already.' 'Nah, I mean, you had somethin' you wanted to do and fucked up. Missed it.' 'Doesn't matter.' 'C'mon, man.' 'Really.' 'You can trust me.' 'But I can't trust Tulz. She'd hear it if I told you now!' Trey stared at me blankly.
