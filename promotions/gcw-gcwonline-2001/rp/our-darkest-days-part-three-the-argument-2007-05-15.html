Our Darkest Days, Part Three: The Argument

15 May 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 15th May '07 11:30am Unbeknown to most ? who assume them to be pacifistic, docile creatures completely adverse to conflict ? the badger is a fiercely predatory animal that can easily fend off much larger beasts. They will protect their young and themselves at all costs, and have been known to ward off coyotes, wolves and even bears with their sharp, razor-like talons. Generally they will take cover in underground burrows ? or setts ? and rarely venture up into the outside world, and usually only engage on conflict when there is no other option, but make no mistake; the badger ain’t no pussycat. When they’re not in their setts they can often be found lurking in sandcastle-like structures that they have fabricated using their own dexterous paws. Usually built from mud, leaves and sticks, these unnamed hideouts offer refuge for the badger when he or she requires armament or fortification from invaders. The animals? relationship with humans hasn’t exactly been a pleasant one either. In Europe many badgers were gassed during the 1960s and 1970s in order to control the spread of rabies throughout the continent (practice that was also repeated in 1980s in Great Britain to prevent the spread of bovine tuberculosis). Hunting badgers ? despite being illegal in most countries ? is still popular across the globe, for sport and for pest control, and their hair is often sought to use as bristles for high-quality shaving brushes. So given that these creatures are relatively dangerous, live underground in order to avoid predators and are viewed by most as a pest, it would be safe to assume that nobody in their right mind would want to keep one as a pet. Nobody except Andy Murray, that is. The Scottish King of Cool kept the mammals in a darkened shed around the back of his Tallahassee home. He must’ve had about ten to twelve of them, all stacked up in individual cages. One badger sat proud at the end of the shed in a cage significantly larger than the others. Attached crudely to the front of this cage was a small plank of wood, the word "Snowball" scrawled in black paint across its surface. Snowball was the queen of Andy Murray’s rather impressive collection of badgers; she was the first one he’d owned, and of you were to have a conversation with Mr. Murray on the subject he’d do his very best to convince you that she was able to breathe fire. Whether or not Snowball had mastered the fine art of fire-breathing was debatable, but something that was undeniable was the command that Murray seemed to have over the animals. An air of calmness seemed to descend upon the creatures as Andy strolled through the surprisingly tidy (but horribly smelling) hut, poking bits of what looked like tortilla chips through the gaps in the cages for the badgers to feed on. Why exactly The Scottish King of Cool fed his pets Doritos was another question you could ask, but let’s face it, with Andy Murray it’s probably best not to question such matters and just accept the issues for what they are. It was only 11:30am and already he was having a pretty rotten day. He’d woken up at the same time he did every day, 8am precisely, to find that his wife, Jane, had already left for work. This thing puzzled Andy as she’d normally depart at 8:30am regimentally, day in day out, regardless of what day of the week or time of year it was. Normally something like this wouldn’t really weigh on Andy’s mind for too long, but coupled with her recent bizarre behaviour meant the thought lingering in his head for longer than he would have liked. From what he understood she had no real work-related reason to leave early ? she had to be at the office for 9am every morning, and the office was only a fifteen minute car journey away ? maybe she was going to the bank or the grocery store before a day’s graft? He’d repeated such thoughts throughout his head over the past couple of weeks that his belief in them was beginning to wane. Andy hadn’t yet confronted Jane about why she kept leaving the house without explanation ? or why Cayle had seen her conversing with a suit-clad man on a number of occasions why Andy was out of the country ? but he knew he’d have to soon. He needed to put his mind at ease. So, over breakfast, he relayed his thoughts to his younger brother? a problem shared is a problem halved, right? Problem was, Cayle was a heck of a lot more suspicious of Jane and her motives than Andy was. After all, he had seen her sneaking around while Andy was in Scotland recovering form his back injury. As Cayle shared his suspicious thoughts with Andy he immediately dismissed them; he knew that Jane and Cayle had never seen eye to eye and realised that it must be especially awkward for both of them now that they were both living under the same roof. Andy had no problem whatsoever with Cayle being suspicious, that’s just how he was. What Andy didn’t like, however, was Cayle’s suggestion that he should follow her around one day to see what she was up to. "Ya know, follow her on her way out, hire a PI, somethin? like that?" he’d said, deadly serious. He even went as far as to fish out the telephone directory so that he may find the contact details of local Private Investigators. "Are you honestly suggesting that I spy on my own fucking wife?" Andy spat, furious at the suggestion of his sibling. "Are you for real?!" Cayle nodded sagely. "I’m serious man! She could be seeing that dude, you know?" "Shut up!" Andy barked, he didn’t even want to think about what Cayle was proposing. "You’re out of your god damn mind, you know that!” We only just got remarried a couple of weeks ago and already you’re trying to stir the bloody pot! Get a fucking grip." And that was that. Without response Cayle stormed out of the house. The Scottish King of Cool hadn’t seen him since then, but he wasn’t that worried; his head was still full of discontent, and besides, Cayle could take care of himself. As he slipped the last of the tortilla chips through the cage bars, Andy reached down into the pocket of his black Nike sweatpants and pulled out his cell phone. Perhaps he should call Cayle, make sure things are okay? "Screw it," he mumbled. ---- 5:07pm "I ain’t feelin' too fine, fellas," Cayle slurred as the May drizzle rained down upon him. He glanced up, letting the rain pitter-patter across his face, and felt himself becoming slightly queasy. "Oh this is ain’t gon? end well," he muttered to himself as he took one final giant swig from the bottle of supermarket-brand whiskey, before carelessly letting the bottle slip from his hands and smash on the pavement curb. It was just gone five o’clock in the afternoon and already Cayle Murray was intoxicated beyond belief. After walking out on his brother he’d headed round to the apartment shared by his long-time buddies ? Forrest and Ike ? to wind the day away bitching and moaning about his sibling. However what Cayle had originally intended to be little more than a lengthy "letting off steam" session had turned into an all-out binge. Suddenly he felt a vibration in his pocket and pulled out his buzzing cellphone. The screen was flashing green with the words "Andy Calling" displayed in black. Without thinking he immediately tossed the phone across the street, sending it smashing into a thousand small shards of plastic as it connected with the concrete. "Screw Andy, man!" he yelled into the muggy early evening air. "I don’t need that bitssch or his stoopid-ass wife! Who needs “em when I got you guys?" "Damn right man, you tell “im" babbled Ike incoherently, staggering into the side of the inebriated Murray brother. "Hey watch it man, this some expensive material," Cayle proclaimed as he brushed off the sleeve of his black denim jacket. Ike paid no attention however, seemingly far more concerned with trying to stay on his own two feet more than anything else. "We all outta booze, lets hit that store," Forrest proposed as he turned towards Cayle, knocking the Murray brother right off his feet. Cayle, trying to get back up to his feet, grabbed onto Forrest’s leg as across the parking lot a group of youths noticed the state that the group were in and collectively laughed. Forrest pulled Cayle up up, "Hey man, you're will on your way, I know whatcha need..." he said as he pulled a small bag of white powder from his pocket. "Noooo, I dont doooooooooo that anymore..." "Ah shut up you prude, got you drunk, so let’s go, you’ll love it." Cayle had had experiences with drugs in the past, and none of them were pleasurable. Even in his current state he was smart enough to turn down the offer. "Sorry fellas, that’s a road I ain’t goin? down tonight?" The laughter from the group of youths across the parking lot grew louder, attracting the attention of the former FUSE wrestler. Cayle’s head immediately shot around in their direction. "Hey bitches, you gots a problem or somethin??" ---- 5:10pm Andy snapped his cellphone shut and slid it back in his pocket. "Damn," he muttered. Why on earth wasn’t Cayle picking up? It had been a good few hours since their argument, he couldn’t surely be angry? But then again, Andy knew just how petty his brother could be at times. He shrugged it off, no biggie. It’s not like Cayle was completely dependant on him or anything. It was fast approaching the evening and Murray found himself in a crude gym he had built up in the basement of his house. He had meant to do a little workout session earlier but had been procrastinating all day. Even though he knew that he needed to get his fitness level up right away ? having been out of in-ring competition for four months ? he didn’t want to push his injured back too far. The rowing machine was out of the question, as was going for a swim. So he found himself standing before an ancient-looking punching bag which hung from the rafters. The bag looked like it had originally been white but years and years of grime had etched into its surface; there were spots of dirt, sweat, dust and even some blood here and there. This was one piece of apparatus that had certainly seen better days. Andy began to slug his fists against the heavy bag and the initial upheaval of dust from it caused him to sneeze momentarily, he soon recovered though. The bag was a lot heavier than he had remember and already he was feeling the muscles in his upper arms beginning to hurt, but you know what they say; no pain, no gain. As he proceeded in his first workout session since his departure from FUSE, thoughts of Cayle and Jane begin to enter his head once again. The dust had settled on the argument and his head was no longer full of hostility, and for the first time of the day he actually found himself able to see the argument from Cayle’s side. What if Cayle’s claims were more than just the jealous rivalry between Cayle and Jane rearing its ugly head again? Her meeting up with some guy in a suit was incredibly odd, but surely she wasn’t cheating on him? That was one thing that Andy wasn’t willing to consider? he thought he made her happy, and they hadn’t been remarried for any longer than a few months. But if she wasn’t cheating on him then who the hell was that guy? And why on earth was she on "business" at unusual hours of the day? She was clearly doing something that she didn’t want Murray to find out about, but could he go as far as to spy on her? Did he really have it in him to follow his wife around like some crazed, sick stalker? Of course he didn’t, that wasn’t The Scottish King of Cool’s nature, but he was coming round to the idea that something should be done. "Shit!" Andy yelped as he stepped back from the bag, holding his right hand in pain. With his last punch to the bag two of his knuckles had split open sending a small trail of blood spurting across it. Andy held his fist close to his mouth as his knuckles began to sting; he’d forgotten to take his hands up, a rookie mistake. "Gotta get this under some water?" ---- 5:26pm Detective Jerrold Vires slid back into the driver’s side of his patrol car where his partner Tillie Ramirez. "Here we are then," he said in a soft Southern drawl as he pulled out a chilled bottle of Coca Cola from the carrier bag of goods he’d just purchased and handed it to Tillie. "Thanks again partner," the female police officer said as she screwed open the bottle and took a long, refreshing gulp of the soft drink. It had been a long, hard day of fighting crime and their shift was over in under an hour. She couldn’t wait to wrap the day up and get home to her family; the last thing she wanted was for another crime to rear its ugly head that would mean her getting caught up with paperwork for the next couple of hours. "Not a problem," sighed Vires as he dipped into the bag. "I figure we could just lay low for the next thirty minutes or so? We don’t want get into an-" suddenly something caught his attention. "Awwww crap?" Ramirez saw it a split-second after Vires; two groups of young men scuffling at the other side of the parking lot. Immediately the police officers leapt out of their car and pulled their guns from their holsters as a man of about 6’1" with shoulder-length brown hair through his fist straight into the jaw of a younger-looking gent while four other guys stood around and watched. "ALRIGHT, BREAK IT UP!" screamed Vires before Cayle Murray could land a second punch on his adversary. Forrest and Ike panicked immediately in the presence of the police officers knowing that if they were caught with drugs on their person then they’d both be in very deep shit. "NOBODY MOVE, KEEP STILL! HANDS ABOVE YOUR HEADS!" barked Ramirez as she pulled a set of handcuffs from her belt. However, nobody felt worse than Cayle as the officers approached; he’d been caught read-handed punching the guy who’d laughed at him, and offence he’d already done time for in the past. His heart sunk, he knew he was done for. And as the cold steel of the handcuffs embraced his wrists with a painful death-grip he sobered up pretty quickly. ---- 5:30pm The knuckles on his hand still bleeding, Andy walked briskly through the empty house towards the kitchen and made a b-line for the sink. After turning on the cold water faucet he stuck his hand under the cool jet of liquid, washing the mixture of blood from the wound and dirt from the age-old punching back from his knuckles. Initially wincing in shock as the surprisingly cold water connected with his tender knuckles, Andy soon found the process to be incredibly soothing and therapeutic. He stared out through the window at the grey early evening skies that hung over his land like an unwanted guest and hoped that things would brighten up tomorrow so that he may attempt to go out for a jog. After a couple of minutes cleansing his wounds, The Scottish King of Cool turned the table off and grabbed a soft, white towel from the towel rail, gently dabbling his knuckles dry so as not to irritate the wound further. As he did this he turned towards the table, noticing that the telephone directory still lay open on the kitchen table. The text at the top of the page stuck out at him like a sore thumb:- p287: PRIVATE INVESTIGATORS He let out a sigh and pondered the argument with his brother further; the idea of the private investigator was becoming less and less ridiculous with every second that he thought about it. Fuck it, he thought, it’s not like I cant afford it. And besides, I can send some guy in once, get this whole thing cleared up and put my mind at rest. Quick, easy and harmless? she won’t have to find out about it, nobody’ll get hurt? His ran his finger down the page of the book, picking out one of the advertisements at random; he didn’t care how they advertised themselves, they were all the same to Andy. He reached down into his pocket to reach for his cell phone, pulled it out and dialled the number. The phone began to ring. "Hello, I’m back!" a female voice suddenly rang out through the corridors of the house accompanied by a rather loud crash as the front door closed under it’s own weight. "Anyone home?" "Shit," muttered Andy, Jane was home. He hung up. Guess that’ll have to wait.
