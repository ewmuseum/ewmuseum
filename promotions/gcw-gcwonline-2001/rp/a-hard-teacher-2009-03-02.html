A Hard Teacher

2 March 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/2/09 “Where the hell have you been?” Devon Drake paced frantically in front of me, as per usual. Her long black and red hair bouncing along the way with each annoyed and angry step, she mumbled expletives to herself in between her random bouts of screaming. It didn’t seem worth arguing at the moment, seeing how my career had really been the last thing on my mind as of late, and perhaps it had caused it to suffer a bit. It was a bit of a blow to my psyche to come and lose the first time out. Sure, I’d had a lot of time off and been experiencing traumatic events during that time, but what bothered me was that I’d never had to make that excuse before. Defeat wasn’t new to me, but it sure had been a long time since it had graced my presence. I’d managed to come back and even it up the next week, but I still wasn’t satisfied with that. However, Devon was even more infuriated at the fact that my attention couldn’t be placed on the task at hand. Clearly it was having an effect on my performance. “Why, Lantlas” Why did you bring me back if you didn’t even really care about it in the first place?” That I wasn’t expecting. An accusation and a hint of her not even wanting to be around caught my attention immediately, and my eyes returned to hers. A deep story of sorrow lay embedded within them, unleashing a story perhaps as conflicted as mine. Her anger quickly clouded them as she yanked me up by the shirt lapel. “I know what’s been going on in your mind. After all, I’ve done it to you before, so that’s not why I’m so angry with you.” “Then what is it?” I asked innocently, knowing full well what the answer would be. “Personal tribulation always brought out the best in you when it came to wrestling! What I did to you, Neko, and even the hard deaths you had to face, they always seem to light a fire in those blue eyes and make you unstoppable!” she explained, a slight smile returning to her face remembering the past. It quickly faded. “Now, it almost seems like you don’t even care, like you’ve just given up on life and don’t care what happens.” “I have to admit, I do feel that way at times,” I admitted. Her expression became enraged the second that sentence escaped my lips. The cold strike of her hand clapping across my face surprised me a bit, and I slipped back onto the couch on which I was originally sitting. My stomach cried out in pain as her knee jabbed into it as she pounced, smacking the ever-loving hell out of my face. After a few frantic seconds, I finally managed to shove her away. Out of breath, face beet red and sweating, she kicked the door next to which she was standing, and a mirror crashed down to the floor and shattered on impact. For a moment, she slightly turned her head, gazing at the broken glass scattered about the floor. “What the hell was that all about?” I finally stammered. Devon knelt near the site of impact, and gazed into what was left of the mirror. Her hand traced a strand of her bangs that had fallen out of place during her explosion, and she seemed lost in the image. After a few minutes, I once again heard her voice escape her lips, this time in almost a whisper. “Do you remember what I used to look like?” Choosing my words carefully around this short fuse, I wasn’t sure where she was going with this, but knew better than to not answer at a moment like this one. “Of course I do, Devon. How could I forget?” “It’s been six years, hasn’t it?” Her expression didn’t change, her stare not moving from the shards remaining on the border. The question obviously in reference to when our relationship had begun, I nodded. “It has,” I concurred. “It still crosses my mind from time to time.” That wasn’t at all a lie either. For some reason Devon was the only thing on my mind. It shouldn’t have been either; for I knew our relationship had ended a long time ago, and she’d made it abundantly clear that in the past is where that was going to stay. This recent outburst had shown me that her internal conflict was still a huge factor in her life. I knew she’d been struggling with it long before she saw Dr. Virgill about it. Kieran was all but suppressed when we were together, but the sour ending to our relationship sure had brought out that dark side pretty damn fast. At the time, I had no idea that her dark side was actually the Kieran I’d known almost my entire life. This was a time before I’d figured out a lot of the long lines of coincidences and crossovers from the days of Eldarin, perhaps a more na’ve time in the annals of my existence. At that time, I merely thought I’d met Devon Drake by coincidence, a chance encounter through a mutual acquaintance. My memory drifted, thinking about our relationship. Our paths had finally knowingly crossed for the first time when I was employed with Hardcore Hell Wrestling, the first major promotion through which I was employed. My HHW career had just started to take off, although I was nowhere near the main event, nor would I be for another two years. A fellow wrestler by the name of Logan Vicious was having a get-together, and for some reason had invited me. Being a newcomer at the time, not to mention desperately wanting some time away from my horny brother, I accepted the invitation. Vicious had a nice place; a small condo in a community well distanced from the city. Surrounded by trees and rather secluded from the main road, this July day was not only perfect in the temperature sense, but the nature setting provided a nice relaxation from the hectic pace of the wrestling business. The party, which I guess was for his birthday, took place in the pavilion in the center of the development. A swimming pool and a wrestling ring were setup nearby, and I could already tell the theme of this party. Some of these guys in the HHW lived their wrestling, and brought it with them everywhere they went. Through all the years of my career, I’d never reached the point where my career was my life, and I could only imagine where it would be if it had. Despite my recent struggles, I’d achieved a perennial amount of success in the last decade, and had my mind been completely focused on it, I had no idea what I could’ve become. These were decent guys for the most part, and I could tell it would be a good day. Not exactly being the social butterfly type, I remained near the corner and observed, as I habitually practiced anytime I was in a group of humans I either didn’t know or had barely spoken to. Occasionally a conversation in passing would arise with a few of the people I knew, but given my limited time in the area, that didn’t happen too often. Logan and his partner Vince Coldblood were having a pretend match with each other to show off some of their new moves. The lack of seriousness in their over-exaggerated slow-motion wrestling made me chuckle a little bit, and it was good to see that they had a fun side. They were the Vow of Destruction; arguably the most dangerous tag team in HHW history, so I let them have their fun and didn’t really intrude too much. Who knew if the self-mockery was intentional or not? My social skills weren’t exactly at the point in that time where I could play along with something like that. A battle simulation was not something with which I was acquainted. Later that evening, a girl caught my eye. This didn’t happen too often, as my experiences with the other sex were quite limited through most of my life. My upbringing, not to mention being six-seven with long blue hair, didn’t exactly give me the experience every kid had growing up and learning these things. I couldn’t bring myself to talk to her, so learning her name was out of the question. Because of the red streaks in her long, black hair, I dubbed her “Red.” Something about the spring in her step and the smile on her face drew me closer to her. She was spending a majority of her time with Joey Cranston, the President of HHW, and as I would later find out, a childhood friend. Assuming the two of them were dating, the last thing I wanted to do was get myself fired. Cranston had a habit of firing people for reasons such as that. In fact, he seemed to genuinely get a kick out of it. At one point, the pavilion all but cleared out, and Devon walked in while I was still sitting along the cinderblock wall, reveling in the silence that bestowed the building and the slight brush of the leaves on the trees from a slight breeze to the north. A few seconds passed, and she looked me dead in the eye. “What’s your name?” “Lantlas.” She looked towards the entrance for a few seconds, almost as if to make sure no one was going to walk in and see her talking to me, then turned back to me and pulled out her cell phone. “What’s your phone number?” This took me by complete surprise, and I think it was the first time I’d ever been asked that in a non-professional setting. I quickly gave her the number, and she disappeared before anyone else came into the room. Not sure what to make of the situation, I wondered if she’d just been a fan who recognized me from HHWtv in the last couple weeks. Growing tired, I decided it was about time to leave. I looked in my bag, and there was a small piece of paper protruding from the zipper. I pulled it out, and I found an email address, and a phone number. “Purely Jaded,” I read aloud. “Nice to meet you.” HHW left Pennsylvania a couple days later for a show in Fort Myers, Florida. The entire plane trip down was usually reserved for planning match tactics and keeping Neovan from getting us thrown out mid-flight by hitting on the flight attendants. This time, Neovan got himself in trouble the entire time. My thoughts were elsewhere, as thoughts of Red kept playing throughout my mind. A new feeling had taken control of my entire body, and as scary as it was, I’d never felt better in my entire life. Neovan also managed to set a personal record, getting slapped in the face twenty-seven times within the span of the three hour aerial journey. I couldn’t help but feel bad for him, as I always hypothesized that Mother disappearing at such a young age had always been traumatic for him, and he embellished in his physical prowess in a hopeless attempt to ease the pain. On that week’s UNCUT!, we lost a match for the tag team championships against B Styles and Geno when Neovan got himself hit with the Paradox while I was outside the ring. Not that those two weren’t deserving champions, as they’d both been trained by the legendary Danny Zorich, trainer of Al Laiman as well? but my head wasn’t completely in it that night. I was looking forward to the flight home, because thinking about Red had me in a different state of mind, one of a rare comforting feeling ? and I wanted more of it. June 30th, 2003 was the date when we first went out together. We ended up on City Island in Harrisburg. Halfway between the West Shore and the city itself in the Susquehanna River, the small island housed a baseball stadium for a minor league team and other various attractions. That particular night, though, it housed an amazing view of the lit-up city and the Walnut Street Bridge as the sun disappeared over the horizon. A time so intimate that despite our past difficulties, I didn’t believe she’d forgotten it either. Not a lot of time passed before we were engaged and living together in a small apartment. Although my career suffered for it, I was having the best time of my life with the girl with whom I’d fallen in love. I never believed it would come to an end, but it eventually did a few months down the line. “I was so beautiful back then,” Devon’s voice disrupted my reflection. “You still are, Devon,” I honestly replied. Despite our roads of difficulty, there was no denying that her presence was indeed a quite attractive one, despite intimidation usually taking place upon initial inquisition. Devon turned to me, a small tear escaping her eye, which caught me completely off-guard. “I’m sorry, just hearing you say that brought back a little of the sentiment of the reason I ended our relationship in the first place.” “How so do you mean?” I inquired cautiously. “Don’t get me wrong, I love you as my best friend and I always will, but that was what drove me crazy about you; your inability to take life as it was and make the best of it. Your mind was always on the past, what you could’ve done better, how you could’ve saved a relationship or not lost a friend” It drove me insane, Lantlas!” Blunt as ever, I accepted what she had to say and did my best to process it, but she wasn’t done. “I realize this one hit you harder than ever, going through with the actual marriage and everything, but that world, the world to which you wanted to escape, it wasn’t real, Lantlas. Time is different, people are different” There’s a Christine in this world somewhere, one with whom you’d probably fall hopelessly in love, and then have her cheat on you without the slightest bit of guilt or remorse. It’s just the way it happens. While you’re the only one in the world who has to deal with this problem, it doesn’t change the overall sentiment, Lantlas. Your experiences in love have not all been great, but you hardly ever reflect on the positive, instead choosing to focus on the negative connotations and emotions that follow. It always made me feel slighted, and when John came along and charmed me the way he did, I admit it, I fell for it.” I cringed at the memories of reading her diary; the image conjured by those words penned in the deceit and betrayal of her actions. “But remember what happened when you emerged from the shadows, when revenge was the only thing on your mind?” she asked. “At first, I’d expected a rush of rage, the uncontrollable passion that willed you to the championship victories he’d already achieved at that point in your career. I was awaiting the glowing eyes, the clenching of the fists, and the wave of destruction that was certain to follow?” Only it wasn’t. I remembered being paralyzed, unable to physically comprehend with what had happened. After the inevitable beat down at the hands of her new alliance, I had to be helped to the back, and I disappeared in the bowels of the building for a good two weeks, no one knowing of my location. During that time, with the help of an old office desk and a tablet of paper, the writing began. Scribbling furiously without knowing what was coming from the end of my avid pencil, day and night were spent leaving accounts of names I didn’t know and emotions I didn’t understand behind the trail of blazing graphite. Only the silence comforted me, as I expected another onslaught from the source of the deepest betrayal. I was furious with myself for ignoring the signs. All the red flags were there, waving furiously in the wind, but I chose to disregard them as mere coincidence, relying instead on her loyalty and our love to carry us through. Two weeks after disappearing into that confinement, I quietly left his new office of sorts and went on a search. After the long hours of writing on that paper, it was time to find some other pages. Pages that I knew contained all the answers I’d been seeking since everything had taken place. What I didn’t know was how I would react to reading the words that had led to such disaster. I knew so little of my heritage, and how different I really was in handling emotions than humans. This was definitely not the most optimal way to find out. Each lust-filled word scribbled on the middle pages of a cheap school notebook sent me deeper into a state of madness. My eyes began to glow a little brighter green with each passing image that was conjured in my overactive imagination, learning just how far this deceit had gone. It was planned to the most intimate detail, and had even taken place for the first time in my own domicile? my own bed? where I had laid to sleep with her, imagining our future together, and wondering where the world would take us Two months passed, and Devon Drake was among her alliance cronies, finishing off another failed challenger to Cranston’s HHW championship. Seemingly no defiance or resistance in sight, they had no problem in taking advantage of a numbers game so stacked in their favor that they couldn’t help but get off on it. They thought no other would be foolish enough to attempt to stop them. Seven minutes later, they all looked at each other as if they’d seen a ghost. It had been me, or at least a version of me, that had handled all of them with so much ease that they almost forgot they outnumbered me seven-to-one. The only one who wasn’t the slightest bit surprised was Devon Drake, as she had been the only one to know me when my eyes would glow green and the darkness would come about. I hadn’t myself yet understood what was happening to me, and wouldn’t for another two years or so, but Kieran had explained to her exactly what it meant, and why it had to happen? why she had to betray the one she loved more than anything. Kieran had told Devon that her destiny lied elsewhere. At the time still not being able to understand why she’d let this Kieran Evenstar talk her into tracking down this blue-haired wonder, only to completely fall in love with him, and then be forced to betray him on the deepest level. She knew only that the wheels set in motion were beyond her control, and the consequences would be dire if she didn’t follow through. Never one to take any attitude, Kieran was a bit different when it came to disagreements. With a fuse even shorter than Devon’s, no less than her life would’ve been at stake. “Do you remember what happened, Lantlas?” “I do,” I responded. “Every second of it.” “Why can’t it be like that again?” she asked. “Where’s the fire, where’s the determination” I know all that is going on inside that head of yours, I can hear you screaming at night, but you used to be able to take all of that emotion and convert it into energy, something you’d use on the poor sap scheduled to face you inside of that wrestling ring! Don’t you see, wrestling isn’t secondary to you!” “What do you mean?” I questioned. “You didn’t need to escape from wrestling, nor is wrestling your escape” You are a warrior, Lantlas! All the way back to your roots when Eldarin was created, you are a warrior! That warrior’s instinct, the experiences from the battles in which Eldarin waged, all are a gift to you as a wrestler now, don’t you see? When you use your past, your anger, your frustration, and your sadness as a weapon, there isn’t a human soul in this business or any other that can stop you! Instead, you’ve let it steamroll you and distract you, to the point where you embarrassed yourself by making a first impression as a deluded, distracted competitor who got beaten his first time out coming after an unprecedented undefeated streak!” “Thanks for the newsflash,” I muttered. She yanked on my hair and brought my eyes to hers. “So now, nobody is expecting anything from you, use that. Take all that you’ve been feeling, from Christine back through Jade, Neko, me, and even the loss of Emerald, and let it loose. Unleash it upon anyone who stands across that ring from you, whether they are deserving of it or not.” I was slightly concerned about this approach. “Where is the honor in battling someone for reasons that aren’t their doing?” “Where is the honor in allowing others to defeat you because you’re too distracted and emotional?” She smacked me on the side of the head, as if to tell me to wake up. “Everyone is fighting with a muse, it’s not cheating and it’s not wrong. While humans do not share the emotional makeup that you do, they still have a past with which they much deal, and difficulty comes from it. If you think you’re out there against them and you’re the only thing on their mind when you compete, that would be frivolous beyond all explanation. Dishonor would lie in not using the abilities with which you’ve been blessed to bring out the best of you as a competitor and a warrior! There is so much potential buried beneath that emotional raincloud constantly covering your soul that even I am scared to find out what would happen if it completely removed itself.” “Scared, why is that a good thing?” Devon laughed a little bit. “Because if you can scare me, you can scare anyone goddammit!” I smirked in return. “That is true.” “So this week, while you’re preparing for Dario Nazaguli or however the hell you say that name, keep that in mind. Find your inner strength, and apply it to those emotions of which you can’t get rid” And use it, just let it all out when you step inside of that ring. I’ll be there at ringside to help if necessary.” “What are you going to do,” I asked, “shout the names of the people who’ve hurt me?” “If that’s what it takes, Elf-boy,” Devon smiled. Finally relaxed, Devon returned to her pacing and plotting, while I thought about the upcoming encounter. There were lessons to be learned and scars with which to deal, and if I was going to establish myself as anything but a weird guy with blue hair, the time was now to take advantage of it. ? 1563 “My son,” Galathan announced to his son, the young spitfire named Eldarin. “Yes, Father?” “Beings, those of a human descent of which I’ve never seen before, are coming from the sea. Unlike those we’ve encountered previously, these have fair skin, almost as we do,” Galathan explained. Eldarin, a mere fourteen years old at the time, peered through the thick hedge that concealed their whereabouts from the arriving colonists. These men were different than the natives, as his father had told him. Instead of animal skin, they clothed themselves in metal for protection. “Father, are these beings aggressive?” “Why do you ask, son?” “Their clothes are iron, their weapons kept on at all times. Their boats have weapons, their animals have weapons. Have these creatures come to battle us?” Eldarin inquired, rather concerned. Galathan, with his hundreds of years of wisdom and existence on the earth, found himself for the first time in a situation where an explanation was beyond his grasp. Searching for the words while looking into the curious eyes of his only son, he needed to say something to save face in front of his young one. Eldarin would soon overtake his position as the leader of his band of warriors with the progress he was making, and if these humans coming from the waters were to become aggressive, he would at least try to stall the overtaking long enough so that Eldarin was fully developed and ready for battle. Once that time would come, Galathan would not fear the loss of his people, for a warrior as mighty as he would keep the village protected and the race safe from harm. “Perhaps they come from a place where their foes were frequently attacking them,” Galathan guessed. “Like the natives were when we settled here, but of course you don’t remember that.” “What was it like, Father” Please tell me again.” “Son, when the Earth saw fit to bring us here, those who were living around us were not pleased with our presence. Though their battle skills were limited at best, they dealt with their curiosity by attacking the settlement. Once they learned that we brought them no harm and lived with nature as we did, despite speaking a different tongue and looking much different than we do, we agreed to co-exist. Perhaps these fair-skinned humans come from a place where their neighbors are not so friendly, and they’ve come from their land on the water in search of land where that is possible,” Galathan pretended to foretell. Eldarin was not so convinced, as these weapons he was seeing were not for defense. Long swords with double-edged blades, arrows that could be lit on fire, loud giant explosive catapults on the sides of their ships? Eldarin grasped his sword tightly. Concentrating deep within his soul, his energy within the life of the sword, a vision came to his mind. Blood, surrounded by fire and the ire of an Elven Warrior seeing those he loved perished around him. Humans in their battle armor celebrating, victorious, and proud of their conquest of another species. One man, almost as fair as the Elves themselves, with red hair? Sitting in front of a giant dwelling, sharpening his sword and planning his attack on the village that lay just beyond the river, that man would lead the attack and slay his own in order to establish his dominance. Eldarin opened his eyes, and his father was shaking him, trying desperately for a response. “Eldarin, son! What is going on” What do you see?” Eldarin, himself in a state of shock over what had transgressed in his mind, blinked his eyes a few times and shook his head feverishly. All that was coming to his mind was a name; a name that sounded very unfamiliar from either his kin or the natives that lived around him. “Eldarin, what did you see,” Galathan repeated. Eldarin responded with an English name, not even sure what it meant. His voice was faint, so his father couldn't make out what he'd said. “What did you say?” Eldarin repeated the same name. Shortly after the words escaped his lips for the second time, he lost consciousness for several days. When he awoke, a young girl was stroking his hair and gently pouring water over his forehead. She smiled, and Eldarin could instantly sense a cold existence. Her eyes told many stories of trial and tribulation, but Eldarin couldn’t help but feel attracted to her, despite his knowledge of a pre-planned kinship with the girl he knew as Narenwyn. This was the girl of whom the other villagers spoke poorly, but all that left Eldarin’s mind when he felt her warm hand caressing his long locks gently. Eldarin had never felt such a sense of care, of love and compassion. Yet, deep within his soul, he could sense that darkness lay deep within her, as Eldarin knew about himself as well. Perhaps the commonality of it all is what drew them together. Kieran Evenstar stared back at her source of lust. She’d been desperate for a chance to meet the young stud before he was paired off and forbidden forever, and with the only medicine woman in the village being her mother, Eldarin’s sudden episode was her chance. She of course knew everything he had seen, and had a dwelling deep within the caves beyond the river for when the time of the human overtaking would come. She’d also foreseen a young girl by the name of Galliere Drake, who would accompany Eldarin in his escape from the desolation, and it would be there where Kieran would be able to morph with a human, live eternally with the one for whom she was falling, and life would be as it should, as it was for the other Elves. Kieran, unlike the rest of her species, was not blessed with immortality. Alone and isolated, she would age and die like a human. The only way for her to retain her Elven gift of eternal life would be to exist inside the human body, for sharing space with a human soul would repair the damage in her genetic makeup. Galliere would be her only chance, she could see. After furthering the young girl’s life source longer than the average human’s, her blessings would be restored, so the risk was worth the chance. “What was I saying?” Eldarin asked the young beauty. Kieran, not quite ready to establish her role as the one who would accompany him in solitude for hundreds of years, instead would try to steer the young Eldarin’s mind away from such things. “Nothing, dear,” she retorted, brushing his hair away from his face and giving gentle pressure, letting him know that he needed to remain laying down. “Who are you?” he questioned. “Kieran, dear,” she answered promptly, “I’m the medicine woman’s daughter.” Eldarin already knew this, but he was so awestruck at this point that anything that came to his mind he had to say, for fear that silence would create an awkward moment. Never having this feeling before, he was unsure of what to make of it, but he knew he didn’t have this feeling with Narenwyn. Narenwyn, while she cared for Eldarin, did not have the connection that Eldarin seemed to have with this Kieran. She was irresistible, and she knew he felt that way. One thing Kieran would not foretell was Eldarin’s rise in the ranks, his pairing with Narenwyn going through, and the child they would conceive several years later. At that point though, they were just a couple of young Elves in love, and there would be no need to explain who B. Wyrmwood was at that point in time. After all, when you’re immortal, time is all you have. ? Preparing for this opponent was different than anything I’d ever had to in wrestling before. Having a few laughs at the opponent being a woman was certainly not crossing my mind. Between having Devon as my manager and seeing what she could do in the past, and all the women of Elven lore, I knew spirits would transcend just to kick my ass for that kind of ignorance. The problem was that I knew nothing about this competitor, or even how to pronounce her name. Around the size of Devon, she must certainly be crafty and quick, likely a striker as well. If she was good enough to make it this far in the business, she was certainly good enough to beat me, at least if this state of mind that Devon wanted didn’t kick in. What I needed to do was find that focus and approach her as I would any other opponent, and achieve victory in the match regardless of circumstance and outside distraction. Like approaching a swordfight while in battle against an unknown enemy? Their faces hidden beneath their shields to keep them from being identifiable, you must glance into their eyes to know their intentions. One impression of a human’s eyes could tell you if they intended to kill you mercilessly, or if they were afraid of being killed; an important distinction on the field of battle. Those forced to be there and fearing for their own lives were being led to slaughter, but sympathy is not something you can allow on the battlefield, for it will likely mean your own death as well. The first order of business would be to see Daria’s eyes, to know if she was a true warrior and would fight to the death, or in this case the victory. If she was more like Devon and less like my brother Neovan, it would increase the difficulty in putting the match away, but the plausibility wouldn’t be affected much. The other thing the human eyes can tell is their intentions when it comes to their next move. Physical bluffs and fake-outs can certainly be distracting, but only in the human eyes can you truly predict their next move. Some humans were admirable warriors in and of themselves, but even the best of the best could not fake stare their way to victory against an elf. The second task would be finding out where she’s going to go and what she intends to do with the physical gifts her body has given her. The range of opponents I’d seen throughout the years had be prepared for any type, so it would be a matter of finding it out before danger arose that would be my objective. Grasping the sword in my hands and closing my eyes, I went through the motions of a few key practice moves, and could already feel the emotion welling deep within me. While I knew I couldn’t let it get out of control and expose my vulnerability, I knew this was the last task? letting her know about me as little as possible. When an enemy is given vital information, it changes everything. If the common perception was that I was a man pretending to be an elf, so be it, I could use that to my advantage. For the non-believers who aren’t aware of the Elven emotional vulnerability, nor completely comprehending their tremendous skills in battle, it was their problem, not mine. Instead of being angered by the lackadaisical approach to my heritage that most took, I would instead use it to my advantage as I had several years ago when I first came to PCW. If I were to have any success in GCW, the element of surprise would be most vital. As I returned to the fighting stance, I caught notice of the glimmer on the sword’s blade. Turning my attention for a minute towards the hovering moon above the night sky, I pointed the sword toward the orbiting satellite. “Your light has given me a message, but I shall make my own destiny,” I proclaimed out loud, and I turned with a quick slashing strike, the air even being sliced by the velocity of this swing. As I held my sword near the ground, seeing the reflection disappear from the blade and feeling the cold air brush my forehead, a smile crept across my face. The warrior within me had truly returned.
