An Introduction - Diary Entry #117

3 April 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03 April 2007 FROM THE DIARY OF DAMON ALDAIR, ENTRY #117, JUNE 04, 2004 Dear Diary, I realise that for over a year I have been writing to you about my life, and about the world in which I live. I also realise that I have never told you who I am, or what I stand for. You may think that you know me, or that you can draw conclusions about me, but you would be wrong in believing this. I've let you into my life, but I have not yet told you what my life is about, what my reson d'etre is - where I've been - and where I'm going. Tonight, I intend to rectify this. If a newly-born baby is taken from its mother and locked in a room for 25 years, with no contact from the outside world, what mental capacity would it be in were it one day released? It would not have the gift of language, or the gift of logical reasoning. It would not understand mathematics or science, and would have no regard or understanding of social etiquette or accepted behaviour. The world we live in shapes who we are, we do not shape the world. No one is born with a set personality, nor is there such a thing as unlearned, natural behaviour. Nature vs nuture is not a legitimate debate, merely a foolish, desperate attempt by those too afraid to admit society's short comings in creating responsible, respectable humans. These cowards are too afraid to accept that perhaps the world we live in doesn't truly match up to their narrow, morally objective view of it. So they blame nature. "Some people are born evil", they say. "Some people are too frightened to accept the truth", I say, in return. I am a product of this world. My world was one of riches and material wealth. My mother divorced my father when I was four years old, and I saw her only fleetingly from that point forth. By the time I was fourteen, my mother was dead to me (mostly because my father had hired a hitman to kill her). Since my mother's death I have known neither love nor affection. I assure you, this doesn't bother me, for I see what these things do to men. They reduce the strongest men to weak-willed servants of their passion, they turn the most composed, calm man into a crying, quivering, pathetic mess. I have no time for such weakness. I dine and socialise with Manhattan's most powerful elite, but I would not for a second consider any of those who occupy my company as "friends". To the untrained eye what we have is very similar to that of a friendship. We laugh together, share common interests, and I shall even confess to occasionally enjoying their company. Where our relationship differs, however, is that whilst they reveal themselves to me, show me the very darkest corners of their soul, and the most vile fragments of their personality in good faith, I have not, and will not ever, let them see the real me. There is no friendship, only a knowledge of all their failings and their continued insistance of revealing weaknesses andways to hurt them when the time comes for me to show my true colours. The face I show the world is a facade, a mask to hide my true existence. It sickens me that I must so often conform to the norms of our culture, but it is but a means to an end. "Once the King siteth upon his throne, no man, neither prince nor jester, may be displeased by his qualities". I patiently climb the ladder to the top of the Manhattan elite, knowing that when I eventually dominate the landscape that I can reveal myself once and for all. I am a man, but not as you know them. I will use you, I will hurt you for my own pervasive pleasure, I will indulge you before dismissing you, I will judge you and I will wish for harm upon you, all for my own personal benefit. If I told anyone this I would, naturally, be called an immoral, bad man. I disagree. I question the notion of morality, I doubt it's existence. Morality is nothing but expresing an opinion. If there is genuine moral laws, then why are cultures and values so different? Why do so many want homosexuals to be able to marry, whilst some want them castrated and put in jail? Why is a woman's testimony worth only half that of a man in a Saudi Arabian court, yet in this very country we cherish the value of all people being equal, regardless of race, colour or creed? It's time for the world to wake up. There is no such thing as morality. If there is no morality, then, it follows that what I am doing and what I stand for is not wrong, and that there is no one who has the right to judge me, or to even for a second try and pigeonhole me with some sort of vague label, That's what so many of them want to do, I see it in their eyes. The jealousy burns in their pupils like a fire dancing in the moonlight. They want to look down on me, to sweep me under the rug and forget that I exist. I will not allow this to happen, because I am better than them in every conceivable way. This isn't a declaration of arrogance, merely of fact. It is as much a fact as gravity, or science. Don't allow yourself to be misled, though. I'm not all that different from the others. I may be better, and more aware of the world we live in, but I still enjoy the same things. Fine Armani suits, Gucci shoes, illegally imported Cuban cigars, a fine bottle of 1963 Marseille Blanc chardonay, the touch of a beautiful woman, beating and bloodying a hapless victim around my training ring, the sensation of a hot shower on a cold winters' morning, the sounds of a bustling Manhattan metropolis at rush hour. Yes, we do share many similarities. But for all our similarities, I possess one striking, important difference, I am a product of this world, but I know how to change it. Beat the system by being part of it, and then when you've earned the system's trust stick a knife in it's back, and watch with pleasure as the blood trickles out slowly, as red as the sun on a mid-summer night's dusk. As the system falls to its knees, clutching and begging for life, show it no mercy, but rather laugh at it's inadequaces, exposed in its final moments for all to see. Because, really, that's all people are, isn't it? Slaves to a system.
