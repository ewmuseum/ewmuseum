Just a Smile

9 May 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 9th, 2007 I had picked my Psychology dissertation topic over the summer, when I was traveling by way of my old “87 Pontiac Fiero. It was streaming yellow, like a yellow jack that had just earned its black strips. It was the one with the Ferrari style front, so you could forget that you never could actually afford a real Ferrari. But the car treated me right, and I traveled across this great country of ours trying to figure out what to write my dissertation on. I found it in a place I would have never expected. Traveling out in the Florida area, looking to possibly score a tan if nothing else, I recalled my old college roommate had moved out this way in Bonita Springs. At the time I had stopped in Gainesville, checking out the university library. Each time I passed by a major college; I would stop at their library and read the third chapter of the fourth book in the Psychology section. I figured, if nothing else, it might inspire me. It never did though, but that never stopped me from driving an hour or more out of my way to stop at the next big school. From Gainesville, it was a four hour drive to check in on my buddy, so I figured I would give it a shot. After all, what were another few hours when you had already been driving for sixty-plus? I had started out in Mason City, Iowa and traveled all the way out to Phoenix, Arizona. I had hoped the desert would inspire me. From there I went out to Waco, Texas to see the center and where eighty-two people had been put to rest. After that I stopped over at Augusta, Georgia for no other reason than to taste the peaches. Having had my fill, I traveled south to the swamps of Gainesville, and that was how I ended up where I was. The phone rang three times before Justin finally picked up. I was surprised to see that he still had the same cell phone number. “Rob?” he muttered, as if he had just woken from some great slumber. We exchanged greetings, caught up a bit on the past, and I told him about how I was on the search for my dissertation paper that would put the exclamation point on ten years of college. Justin was more than glad to catch up and share a few beers after our short conversation. That had always been his way, he was more than glad to toss a few back, even if it was with a complete stranger. He never cared as he told everyone he was part Irish so it was in his blood to drink. But from Talos? brown hair, blue eyes, and heavy skin tone, not much about him screamed Irish except his love for the brew, not even his last name (which was Greek). It was finally decided that since it was a special occasion, we would have to relive our old college days once more by drinking the night away in Miami. I had the feeling Justin was just using this as an excuse to go into Miami, but saved any comments to myself. I was just glad to be out drinking and forgetting about my damned paper for a moment. For me, in Gainesville, Miami was another five hours, but for Justin it was just two. We decided to meet at this new club that had opened up under the shadows of some new arena. The two places shared a name, though Justin couldn’t tell me for the life of him if they were owned by the same person. At seven that night I arrived in front of the building at the address Justin had given me. It looked hidden, despite nothing being around it. Though being assured it was new, the club still had a worn and aged look about it, as if it lied about its age. By no means did it look to be in poor shape, but rather grey and tired. Even the sign, which read “Club Underground”, fit the motif of being underground. Surprisingly the parking lot was jammed pack with cars of all makes and models. I had trouble finding a spot to park, having to resort to parking down the street, nearly a block away. I met up with Justin, finding him already at the bar and pounding away the drinks. By the looks of it, he already had the lead by three shots of Jack Daniel’s finest. I ordered up one for myself, and took a seat on the stool next to him. “Been a long time.” His eyes squared, turning to look at me from his shot glass. “Yeah” was all he could mutter, “been too long.” He let out a heavy sigh, spun around on his stool, and leaned back against the bar to look out at the crowd of people already dancing. “I sure do miss it.” My own eyes looked out to the crowd, trying to pinpoint just what he was in reference to, “dancing?” He swung around, once again facing the bar. His head shook with a sense of sadness, “no, partying.” His brown eyes looked directly at me, sending a shiver down my spine. “This is the first time I’ve been out partying since I left college. Sad ain’t it?” Those heavy brown eyes dropped down to his glass, his thumb and middle finger tipping it toward the bartender, “another, please.” Not sure of what else to do, I slapped my arm over his broad shoulder and gave him what was supposed to have been a cheerful smile. With my other hand I already had my wallet out and was trying to rummage around in it for a few bills without having to remove my arm from Justin. Finally feeling the paper of money against my skin, I snatched them with my index and middle finger and pulled them from my wallet. “Come on,” I said, “let’s lose this place. Sounds like there is some noise next door. Let’s check it out.” I threw the bills onto the counter to pay for the drinks and used my arm around Justin to maneuver his near six-foot two frame from his stool. Justin seemed in the mood for some drinking, but I wasn’t in the mood for watching him get slap drunk. So guiding him past the patrons, I used my other arm to push open the large wooden door to the outside. Cool, crisp air quickly grabbed us by the collar and shook our senses. It was the kind of air you could taste, like sipping water from a fountain. It hit you fast, filling your lungs full and exploding your chest into twice its original size. It rejuvenated Justin, who know was preoccupied with the noise coming from the arena just across the street. His sober and dreary look lifted into childlike excitement, feeling once again that the night could end anywhere. Grabbing my by the bicep, he rushed me across the street and between slow moving cars that violently blared their horns at us, despite they themselves only going at most twenty miles per hour. We reached our destination in one piece, a feat Justin seemed proud enough of. He looked at the posters that decorated the side of the arena, studying each one for some hidden meaning of purpose. His lips partly slightly, giving but enough room for breathes of air and a murmur of “Legacy”. He turned towards me, “sounds nice, huh” You ever heard of it?” Looking up at the arena, I only shook my head. ??Fraid not. I’m not much of a wrestling fan.” Which was true. I had never watched, attended, or cared much for some fake sport where the men all wore spandex underwear and pretended to beat each other up. But Justin didn’t seem to care about any pretend fighting; he just was looking for his own escape back into the life of a reckless college student, his own personal pretend world. Like a child at the fair, he bolted from my sights as if I was his mother and headed straight to find the main entrance. Or at least so I had thought. I followed him as we rounded the circular building until we approached the parking garage and a set of stone steps that led to a fire entrance. Two young guys smoking cigarettes were paying nothing any attention as they got their nicotine fix, never bothering to see either of us coming. Justin led the charge, going straight for his back pocket. The way he was moving, I would have thought he was reaching for a knife or something. But that wasn’t Justin’s way. He wasn’t stupid, not like that. Sure enough the two finally noticed him just as he got out his wallet and held it up for the two to see. I came up behind him, looking over his shoulder to try and see what was going on. The two gents with the cigarettes hanging from their mouths had plain yellow shirts on and a name badge hung around their necks. Josh and Derek. Simple enough names, I guess. “So how much is it going to cost for the two of us to get in through that door?” Justin said, nodding his head in the direction of the fire escape. The two looked at each other, then the door, then back at Justin’s wallet. Their jobs must not have paid as nicely as I thought, as both took a rather meek sixty-five dollars for what we were asking. Derek hopped up to the top step and opened the door for us, which had been left slightly a jarred so they could easily get back in, and waved us in with his other hand. Justin nodded to the man, handing over the money to Josh for the two to split on presumably more cigarettes. Once inside, Justin seemed full of glee, so much so that I expected it to start pouring from his ears like a faucet. “Can you believe this?” he asked me, “do you think we’ll meet anyone famous?” I wouldn’t have been able to tell him. Hulk Hogan himself could have bumped into us and I wouldn’t have recognized nor cared about him. They were fake athletes in a fake sport. Music blared from some unknown source and instantly caught Justin’s attention. Buzzed like a bee, he moved through the halls, doing his best to track down where the music came from. Much to my wonder, as I followed him, no one else backstage seemed to even notice our presences. Never once were we questioned or stopped. Rather everyone was far too busy running about in a panic to even stop and wonder what two untagged people were doing wandering around the halls. Justin failed to notice this, keeping all of his senses instead focused on the music. It captivated him, strangely justifying this entire adventure to him. If he could only find where the music came from, then he could say the night was a success and that he had once again been wild and crazy. Not wanted to get lost in this endless labyrinth of halls and corridors, I followed behind Justin as closely as I could. In that simple quest that I hadn’t given any deep thoughts about, I met him. It was strange really, through all my travels I had been searching desperately to find my inspiration, for my muse. Yet in that dusky and noisy corridor, my muse had found me. Consequently, my muse forcefully shoved me up against the wall, muttered “move it kid” as if I was nothing more than a stupid and annoying fly to him. Coming up just a few steps behind him was another fellow of even larger build in rigidity clothing. The second man just grunted at me and continued to loyally follow my muse away and through a satin curtain with little worry, even if they were to march across a thousand deserts. Justin stood back in awe, trying to peer through the curtain. His head then turned to me, failing to notice that I was still pressed up against the wall, and asked “did you see that?” “Yeah” I muttered, my eyes stayed on that black curtain intensely, “I sure did.” I pushed myself from the wall and brushed the dust from my Thundercats t-shirt and jaded Levi’s jeans. The music Justin hypnotically followed appeared to be coming from past the curtain, being blared over the public announce system of the arena. The song’s firm grip on Justin had been struck, however, with the appearance of the muscle-clad warrior and now Justin paid no attention to anything other than his new fascination. I, too, was struck by this character who had such an air about him. From just that brief encounter I could already tell that he was rude, loud, thoughtless, and brass. But within that I saw a deep expression of pride, respect, dignity, and honor. How could such words come together in just one person? And more importantly, how could they so easily be expressed in such a short act? In that one moment I felt like I knew more about him than I did my friend standing beside me. It mesmerized me in a way that I hadn’t felt since my first summer love, but this was different. This wasn’t love. Rather I felt anger, hatred, and distain. Poking my head through a small slit in the curtain, I witnessed a grand stage. Hundred, nay possibly thousands of people sat in the arena and rabidly devoured every second before them with unrelenting enthusiasm. Never before in my days had I witnessed such a scene of cult-like behavior. This was on a different plane from any other experience I had had in my twenty-seven years. For the next several minutes I kept my head poking through the curtain to watch as my aggressor moved poetically around the ring in violent beauty. But things took a turn for the worst for him, resulting in a new set of music being played after nearly seven minutes of brutal fighting. Less than a minute later a man wearing an eye patch come through the curtain and walked right by us without even so much as a glance. He just traveled past us, like a specter, he passed through us and on to the next life. He wasn’t the man we waited for though. That man came through the curtain later, clutching his ribs with one hand and using the other to keep his balance. Stopping just past the curtain to dry heave, he caught us staring out of the corner of his eyes. “What?” he growled, “you and your boyfriend enjoying the show?” “Don’t go picking on the helpers, they haven’t done anything.” All eyes turned to look at the source of the voice. Before us stood a beautiful woman, undoubtedly in her mid-twenties, and completely pregnant, that captivated our attention. The mass of muscles before us suddenly forgot all about his fatigue and stood straight up, moving toward the woman. Rolling his eyes, he wrapped an arm around her waist and pulled her close, “ah nah, I’m not picking on them. This is me we’re talking about, I’m a fucking angel.” The woman did get bust to stifle a laughter, poking him in the ribs, “angel, huh?” Stepping away from him, she gave him a once over to examine for any injures. “Well Angel, how are you feeling?” The two completely forgot about our presences, becoming wrapped in each other’s world. It was as if they lived in a different plane from us, a separate entity that was just happy to be with the other. “You know me, I’m just fine.” The man flexed his massive arms, showing off a Greek like physique. “I’m like Superman” he started, but stopped as a second thought came to him, “actually scratch that. If I was to fight Superman, I would tear that bitch apart.” Smirking at him, she hands found a resting place on her hips, “Well it’s too bad you didn’t face Superman out there tonight, you might have won.” He laughed, mockingly, then added “yeah, but Ulysis is a damn dirty cheater.” “Really?” she cooed, “Because to me it looked like you beat you fair and square.” Coming up from behind them, the one that had followed the other by two steps, the bum, came out from the curtain to join the others just in time to hear the muscle bound one say “he only won because I saw Arizona take that chair shot and was worried about him. It threw me off my game.” Stopping for a moment, he ran a hand through his spiky blonde haired, which in origin looked to have been black by birth. He flashed the woman a deviant smile, “Otherwise he would have never bet me.” “Ah, thanks man,” the bum started up, smelling rather putrid. “That really means?” “Yeah, whatever” came the hastily reply that cut him off. But that too stopped suddenly when the man’s nostrils flared up. Tilting his head, he looked over his accomplice, asking “did you get hit so hard, you shit yourself?” Checking himself, the man flashed a crooked grin that revealed many gaps between the teeth, “a little, I think.” All four of us drew back, but not surprisingly it was the muscle bound that was the one to speak up. “Ah man, that shit’s nasty. ? His arm rose and pointed to a distant location. “Go take a shower; it would probably be your first for the week.” Hanging his head, the bum spoke no others words, for they had all left him to be alone with his stench. All he could do was follow the path the arm pointed for him, letting the world see his shame. As he passed by the porcelain doll, she clung herself to her man like ivy to a wall. With the foul wretch gone, the two turned their attention back to their private world that had been interrupted. When they started to leave, I knew I had to speak up. “Excuse me!” was all that came to my head. His piercing blue eyes turned to stare into me; not at me, into me, and he lipped a “what?” back. All words failed me at that moment. Years of English and study flew from my body, escaping my grasp. Mouth hanging open, all I could mutter was “uh?” like a brainless hick. “Tom want a meeting with me?” he asked, trying to figure out what I was getting at. The name didn’t register for me, causing an involuntary “Who?” That was all he needed, citing “if Black Tom doesn’t need me, then I’m gone.” His arm closed in tighter around the pregnant woman, “because my woman is in town, and I’m going to show her Miami.” Then it happened. The smile. Everyone smiled; it was a part of everyday life. Many times a smile was a meaningless act, something done out of habit. However, his smile didn’t fit the puzzle. It did not belong on his face. He was brass, arrogant, rude, and self-centered. That smile was warm, loving, affectation. It simply did not make since. How come someone like him produces a smile like that? Leaving, I was too untangled by the mystery of the smile to be aware of their flight, otherwise I would have tried to stop them. I needed him, for my dissertation. I knew now that he had to be my subject. I needed to know, how could he create that smile? My delayed reaction resulted in my failure to assert myself. So rather than standing up to him, introducing myself, and telling him my theory; I watched him walk down the corridor with the girl around his arm. At that moment I felt cold. Justin flopped his arm over my shoulder, leaned into me, and nodded his head off to a direction. “Come on, let’s blow this place. I’m bored.” I just stared at him for a moment, not really looking at him but at the same time trying to see him. “Yeah,” I muttered low, “let’s go.” I suddenly felt like drinking, which I originally had no plans to do. I just wanted to hang out with an old college roommate and watch him get slap drunk, mixing in memories of the old days to forget the new times. Heading out of the building, Justin showed no care over the one-hundred and thirty dollars he had spent just to get in. Rather he was fresh-faced and ready for the next adventure. He wanted to be young again, to act without regard. He just had a meeting with a mentor in such a lifestyle, and desperately wanted more of it. I could see by the wild flickers in his eyes that his brain was processing information at a much faster rate than it normally would. Justin was trying to plan where to next, going over every bit of detail to see which would be the most fun, or trouble. Either way he knew his night could not end yet. The eyes stopped, closing for a second, and then reopening with a bright shine. He had it. “This way!” came the shout, as he bolted off into a direction in the darkness. I did my best to follow, avoiding cars and juked by trees. At most I could only gain a step or two on him, though I always remained a handful behind him. Still, he managed to never leave my sights, not even when he would cut between buildings. I could get where he was and spot him before he got too far away. Then, he stopped. It came abrupt, enough so that I was able to catch up with him and nearly plow right into his back. We had ran for five blocks, a feat that would have taken half the time if we had only used my car that was parked across the street from the arena. When I finally caught my breath, I noticed we stood before a decorative building dressed in fine marble and silk. Out front stood a boy in a red vest, ready to park any cars that may pull up to the club. Justin nodded to me, keeping his tongue bit. Moving at a much slower pace now, he headed on around to the side of the building. I feared he might try the same stunt that he attempted before, but arriving at the side of the building showed no slacking workers to bribe. Luckily Justin had different plans. Tossing his over shirt aside, he stretched his white muscle shirt underneath. “Come on” came the order, meaning that I should do likewise. From the back of the building was loads of hustle and bustle. Bust of steam would rise, disperse into the air, and then repeat in a seemingly everlasting loop. Justin stayed on the move, becoming in sync with the noise and blended in. I did my best to follow, to emulate his actions, hoping that I could figure out what he was attempting to do. I knew that I had better things to do then to play petty games with an old college friend, but mark of me wanted to let go just as much as he did. I wanted to forget about my dissertation, my Ph.D, and just focus on having a night of unyielding fun. Behind the building, away from the marble and silk, a wooden door slapped open against the wall and came back shut. With each opening came another cloud of steam that hurried to be free, only to find itself engulfed in the wind and separated. Upon the door opening, Justin cut into the building and disappeared into the white layer of steam. I stood there listening to the shouting and the clanking, wondering if I should take the leap. Do I follow him into the abyss, the unknown, and leave behind what I am sure of? I can see the walls, touch the walls, and feel the walls. I do not know what is behind that veil of steam. Closing my eyes, I leapt forward as the door swung open. The first sense that rushed to my body was smell. Hints of olive oil and garlic danced under my nose and enticed me to open my eyes. What I saw was Justin holding out a white coat for me. It didn’t take much to figure out he wanted to play waiter. I decided to play along, as it wouldn’t hurt much I thought. Worst thing that happens was I got bad tips. Putting on the coat, a chief instantly handed me a plate and fate brushed its gentle hand against my cheek. I looked over at Justin for support, but he winked and gave me a thumbs up. I nodded back, dropped my head low, and headed through the double doors that led to the main dining area. Stopping to gather my bearings, I examined the crowd to see which tables didn’t have food yet. My eyes stopped, having felt that they may have betrayed me. What I saw couldn’t have been real but rather a figment made up by my mind to trick me. With my free hand I wiped at my eyes, trying to remove the figment, but it remained stationary. It was no trick; fate had dealt me a second chance. I knew I couldn’t afford to lose this one. Maneuvering around the various tables, I finally found my place at the destination. “Bout damn time you showed up.” Then our eyes met. At first I didn’t think he would recognize me, there was a bit of a glimmer in the corner of his eye. I could tell the gears in his head were starting to turn. That glimmer turned into a spark and he pushed himself from the table, nearly knocking over his glass of water. “What the fuck man,” he asked, “you some kind of homo stalker” Cause I don’t take it in the butt.” I half expected his reaction; after all, the entire situation seemed too odd to be real. Even I had my own doubts on a sub concisions level. The woman at the table with him, the same pregnant one as before, looked over to me to try and gain some clue as to what was going on. She hadn’t seemed to have paid enough attention to me before to have noticed I was backstage at the show as the mistaken worker. “I’m sorry” I stumbled out, trying to gather my thoughts in a cohesive manner. “But you must understand! You are my dissertation!” The words came out of my mouth, and I could tell they sounded as bad as I thought. The look I received proved it. “Your what?” The muscle bound man rose from his chair, looking ready to knock me out. I had to take a step back and raise my arms in defense. I pleaded with him, “Please, just hear me out.” My begging did little to sooth his savage intent of bodily harm. Thankfully the woman intervened, possibly saving my life. “Oh come on hunny,” she cooed in his ear, “just hear him out.” There was that smile again. “THAT!” I shouted at the top of my lungs. “That smile! That is my dissertation! I must know where it comes from.” Now his fist did drop to his sides, a reaction of utter confusion. The smile faded, replaced by a curled lip as he tried to figure out just what I was going on about. The woman stared at her man’s face, trying to get any clue as to the smile I was in reference to. As odd as it may seem, it appeared my outburst had sparked their sense of curiosity and they allowed me to join them at the table. Rather I should say Tessa, the female as I came to learn, allowed me at the table. Scott, the muscle bound male, would have just as rather tossed me through a window. Seemed my assumptions were true in that he was not much of a social being. After being allowed to fully explain my theory, Tessa seemed more than interested in allowing me to continue. Scott showed little emotion, only passing glances over to Tessa to see what she thought. In the back of my mind I had completely forgot about Justin, and whatever escapades he was trying to get himself into. Two hours passed and Tessa continued to listen to each word with gauged interest. She would “hmm” and “ah” and “oh” when the situation seemed right, letting me know that she took in each word and gave thought to it. Scott remained silent. Then, I stopped talking. I had said my peace. Tessa sat there, looking at me as if I was going to continue, then rose from her chair. “Well, I think it is obvious that we should let you do it.” I couldn’t see it, but I’m assuming my face must have lit up like a Christmas tree at that moment. “You can follow around Scott, take notes, but should be careful to mind his private time and not to become too much of a pest.” I nodded, and then looked to see what Scott would say. He, to his credit, remained stoned faced. Noticing Tessa had risen from her chair, he did likewise. “Well?” he started off slowly, eyeing me over, “it looks like my mind is made up.” His hand extended out for mine. I welcomingly accepted it only to find that had been a rather bad mistaken. His hand gripped tightly around mine, giving me just a glimpse of the strength he possessed in his body as he hissed, “But be known that it’s against my protest.” His hand released mine and I was left to try to shake off the pain. Seeing that my business was done, I lifted myself from my chair and headed toward the exit. I didn’t bother to remove the white coat, or did I remove the tray I had been carrying from their table. Truth be told, I had forgotten about both articles. Rather all I could think about was the overwhelming sense of freedom I had. Finally, I was free from my journey. Looking around, Miami didn’t seem like such a bad place. In the background, though faint, I chuckled as I heard Scott shout, “I didn’t order this crap.” I guess I should have removed that tray after all. Oh well, there is always tomorrow. Yeah, tomorrow.
