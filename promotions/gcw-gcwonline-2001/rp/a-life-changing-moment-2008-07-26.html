A life changing moment

26 July 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07/26/08 I looked nervously at the door in front of me, knowing that behind it lay an unpleasant confrontation. It was something that I had to do if I didn't want to ruin the rest of my life, but a part of me wished that could simply skip town for a couple of months until the problem went away. Not to mention, I had a title shot coming up at Warpath, and it was really in my best interest too concentrate on nothing but that. But, whatever other negative traits that I possessed, I was not enough of a coward to break up with my girlfriend by simply disappearing. Timidly and partially hoping that she was not home, I knocked on the white door. As it turned out Erika was indeed home, and the second she caught sight of me, I was enveloped in a hug that threatened to crack my ribs. “I am so sorry,” she sobbed to my complete surprise, “I yelled at you, I gave you all that shit, and it turns out you were telling me the truth the whole time! It really was just a trick to get Daria to trust you, so you could get a clear shot at her.” Actually I remembered telling her significantly less than that but I doubted that it would make much difference in the end. I had very little experience in breaking up with women; in fact they usually just break up with me because I run out of energy to do everything that a boyfriend is “supposed ? to do, but I could guess that it was a bad idea to break up with her right after she gave me that emotional apology. So for a minute I just looked at her with confusion churning the midst of my stomach. And damn it if I didn’t wonder whether I was making the biggest mistake of my life. On the face of it, she was almost everything that I could ask for without becoming too good to be true. She was beautiful even in the late stages of the pregnancy; she could understand five syllable words, and too be quite frank she did things in bed that I doubt most girls could even imagine. But when I looked at her, there just did not seem to be anything there. Call me a romantic but I am not content with just the traits I listed above. Alone they are fine for a fling, even a lengthy relationship that is obviously not going anywhere real, but I knew Erika believed that our relationship had gone past that point. I did not want to hurt her, but no matter how hard I tried I could not imagine living the rest my life with her. I ran the standard scenarios through my mind, a wedding, a honeymoon, taking the kids to the beach, all the standard stuff that a couple could expect to go through. But when I imagined it, it was not her face that I saw on the head of the woman I was with. In fact, I saw nothing but a blank where the face was supposed to be. That was enough of an answer for me. “Erika,” I said reluctantly and something about my voice made her step back and looked at me. “Look, you are a great girl, but I can’t do this anymore. I thought we might have a shot, but I know you are starting to believe that our relationship is going way past casual. But I just haven’t found myself loving you, and that is something I need to do before I can take that step with anyone.” I forced myself not to cringe as she worked her mouth furiously without any sound coming out. Years of watching my father anger my mother allowed me to figure out the best course of action, which was to stay silent while she yelled at me until she told me to get out, and then to get out as quickly as I could. I knew that leaving immediately was actually a bad idea, because there was a very real possibility that Erika would follow me and yell at me in the middle of the street. I waited impatiently for the tirade to begin, but when Erika was able to compose herself enough to vocalize, she made a sound of intense pain. To my horror I saw that her hands were on her stomach and she was clenching her face, “my water broke,” she forced out before sinking into a chair. Oh God. This was the scene of a fucking movie, not something that was supposed to happen to me. I had fully planned on getting yelled at, cursed at, and I was even partially expecting Erika to throw a knife or two at me. I was certainly not expecting that she would decide to have the baby a full week before it was due. Seriously if I did not know better I would have guessed that she was trying to guilt me into staying with her, fortunately (depending on how you look at it) I was a bit too busy panicking to worry about that. “Um,” I stuttered like the intelligent and composed man that I am, “I guess I should take you to the hospital?” “What the fuck do you think you piece of shit?” she literally screeched, “I am having a goddamn baby here, get me to a fucking doctor!?I could feel my heart pounding so hard that it threatened to jump out of my chest. I could feel my legs trembling as I helped Erika up and out of the door. I seriously considered calling an ambulance, but in New York city you could probably walk to the hospital before an ambulance could get to you and back. So I called the only service I knew that employed drivers with enough skill, and lack of sanity, to get Erika to the hospital as fast as possible. I called for a New York Taxi. Erika's whimpers of pain ripped through me as I waited anxiously for a taxi to arrive. We had taken classes that taught us what to do in this situation, or at least gave Erika some calming exercises. But she seemed to have forgotten them all, and I did not think that I was in a position to remind her. In fact, I am relatively sure that if she was not out of options she would have told me to get out of her life five minutes ago. But I had no more time to muse on that for the taxi had arrived and I was busy helping Erika in. “Get us to the near-? I stopped short as I remembered about the suspect reputation of the nearest hospital, and instead told the driver to go to the hospital near my apartment. “I'll give you an extra hundred if you can get us there in ten minutes,” I told him knowing that it was an impossible feat. Or rather a near impossible feat. The driver did not even bother to acknowledge me, he simply slammed his foot down on the pedal and preceded to break every traffic law ever created. Looking back I had no idea how we managed not to get pulled over, or crash, or kill someone. But at the moment I was too busy trying to calm Erika down to realize that we were going at speeds a taxi is not meant to go. “Dan,” Erika whispered to me in a voice that made it clear she was holding back tears, “I can't do this by myself. I can't have this baby by myself, and I can't raise him by myself either. Please don't leave me.” I closed my eyes desperately wishing that I was as heartless as Bagwell. He wouldn't have any trouble saying no, hell knowing that guy he would probably have broken up with her over the phone. But whatever my other faults, I simply could not match Bagwell in being an asshole. “Its alright,” I heard myself say as I put a comforting hand on her shoulder, “you'll have me the entire way.” At that point she did burst into tears and I just held her until we arrived at the hospital minutes later. We rushed in, luckily during the ride I had the presence of mind to call ahead and inform the hospital that Erika was on the way. The doctors took her right away and they told me I could follow assuming was the dad. Keeping in mind that I told her I would be with her I followed, and that is when the proverbial shit hit the fan. Most of the pregnancy is a blur to me. Just vague images of Erika's face twisted into a visage of pain, the doctor's mask unable to keep the concern of his eyes, and the sound of machines beeping and Erika screaming. I only remember one thing very clearly. I remember some machine soundly making a loud screeching noise, the doctor looking up and his eyes widening. “She's crashing,” he yelled, and the nurses around erupted in a flurry of activity. I silently stepped to the back of the room, dazed and confused. The hellish scenario in front of me, with Erika's eyes closed and the cries for a c-section filling the air, was simply not supposed to happen. Erika died shortly after the baby was delivered. “There was a complication during the pregnancy,” the doctor told me later. No duh Doctor House. “Sir, your son has come out with a problem,” the doctor continued unaware of my inner turmoil. “We are not quite sure what happened yet, but essentially he is deformed. We believe that with a few surgeries the deformity can be fixed however.” I closed my eyes as the implications, and the choice before me was made clear. The hospital obviously thought that the kid was mine, and they had no way of knowing the truth unless I told them. If I told them that the kid was not mine then I could wash my hands clean of everything. I would have no legal responsibility toward the kid, and he'd be alone. I seriously did not want to think about the future a kid with a deformity would have with social services. The sad truth is that a couple looks to adopt the perfect baby, with all ten fingers and toes and nothing blemishing his cute face. They'd see the kid, they'd pity him and say what a tragedy it was that he did not have anyone, and then take someone else home. He would grow up alone, and be kicked out onto the street when he turned 18. Any decent person would have spared him that. The right thing for me to do was to take him home. Instead I stuttered, “I'm...” every moral fiber in my being screamed for me to stop, “I'm am not the father. I was just a friend of hers.” I saw a hint of surprise (and condemnation?) in his eyes. “Ah, well then I shall go call social services. You should go home.” That was the last time I saw the kid.
