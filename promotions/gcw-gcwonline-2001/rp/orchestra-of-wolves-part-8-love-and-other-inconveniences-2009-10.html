Orchestra of Wolves, part 8: Love and Other Inconveniences

October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 2009 The piston behind his ribcage was about to burst through. Thump. Thump. Thump. Its pounding was incessant, unrelenting; as if someone had removed his body's most vital organ and replaced it with a jackhammer. He pushed a palm into his chest to try and stem the thumping but it was hopeless. An intense anxiety, the likes of which he couldn't recall, had decided to take residence beneath his flesh and bones, and he was helpless to succumb to it. Thump. Thump. Thump. It was getting to his head. As he stood outside her hotel room, Andy felt a cold sweat begin to leak from his brow and a splash of dizziness sway his balance. C'mon Andy, pull yourself together, he thought as he balanced himself against the doorframe and rubbed the perspiration away with the back of his hand. He tried to take a moment to control his breathing and ease the stress on his heart, and slowly the beat's force began to dim. No longer could he feel it in his throat, but it would not disappear from his chest until the night had gone. Coming up to her floor from his own was so simple. He wasn't quite sure of himself, but after what had happened earlier he knew where he stood. He navigated four floors' worth of stairwell without a hitch, and it hit him from the first moment he stepped into the fifteenth floor's corridor. Washing over him like a tsunami, obliterating his trademark cool and leaving him with all the confidence of a nervy little boy bumbling home from school early to tell his abusive father he'd been suspended. Now he'd reached her door and things were so much worse. Andy clenched a fist and tried to knock but could not find the strength to do so. Instead he let his raised forearm fall down by his side as he pushed his forehead into the doorframe, gritting his teeth. Thump. Thump. Thump. It was coming back to him. Hard and strong. A nuisance, a blight. “Oh God,” he whispered through trembling lips. Why was this so hard? It shouldn't have been. She'd put her cards on the table, and while he wasn't sure of where he stood, Andy knew how he was going to play it. But he'd been through a lot over the past twelve months. LeStatt Knight had torn his psyche to shreds during his quest for the GCW World Heavyweight Championship. CJ Woods, a wrestler he'd taken under his wing and nurtured during his formative days as a GCW superstar, had been put on the injury reserve for three months by Andy's own hand. Jordan White, GCW's unsympathetic protagonist for so long, and one of Andy's few allies in the business, had been forced out after Andy himself brushed him aside in his first title defence. And Kayla. Oh, Kayla. 2009 had started so well for them and both thought they'd be married by now, but one missed brother's funeral and one terminated foetus later and they were done forever. He was strong, though. Unflinching in the face of adversity, he'd stood firm and done his best to take everything on the chin. He'd looked in the mirror and told himself so many times that he'd cope and he'd get over it, but now even his own words weren't convincing him any more. Andy was struggling; he was in danger of being overcome, and that was why he needed to sort this shit out, there and then. The trouble was, in situations like Andy's, turning your thoughts to actions can be incredibly difficult; especially when one of your closest friends has just told you she's in love with you. * * * * * Andy Murray in... Love and Other Inconveniences * * * * * All Sid wanted to do was lounge in bed all day, but the beams of light that were pouring through the gap in the curtains wasn't giving him much choice in the matter. Shattered, he pulled the thin, cream-coloured sheets over his eyes and tried to blot out the rays but they burned right through. He groaned. It was no use rolling onto his back, the pain wouldn't let him rest in that position for any longer than a couple of seconds, no matter how many pills he popped to remedy the situation. It was the same thing every time he spent any longer than a couple of weeks on the road; he'd come back to his brother's home and his body would fall to pieces. Each time a new ache or pain on top of the patchwork of sores he'd accumulated over the years, and then there was his back. For years it had been the bane of his existence; bearable at times, insufferable at others, Sid knew that he there was a real risk he might end up doing some permanent damage to his posture if he didn't take time out to have the operation, but he needed to earn. Andy had more than enough money to keep him afloat and help with the medical bills, but Sid was too proud a man to get on his knees and ask his brother for money. He already felt bad enough for taking up a room in Andy's Tallahassee mansion when he'd been evicted from his own bungalow on the other side of town, and he wasn't about to become even more dependent on his high-earning younger sibling. Abandoning his quest for more sleep, Sidney pushed his hurting body up against the headboard and gently wiped the sleep from his eyes. He could hear birds chirping outside, and from where he lay it seemed as though there was barely a cloud in the sky. Another perfect day in northern Florida; another day he'd no doubt waste on the physiotherapist's table as a specialist tried to carefully to massage the aches and pains from his thirty-four year old frame. It took him a few minutes to muster the strength to venture from his bed but eventually he dragged himself towards the ensuite bathroom and took a long, cathartic shower. Having washed the sleepy grog from his body, Sid ran a comb through his straggly sand-coloured hair and tied it into a ponytail, before grabbing a pair of jeans and a white Mudhoney t-shirt from the floor and dressing himself. He pulled the curtains fully open and made for the door; the bedroom was significantly smaller than any of the house's other sleeping quarters, but there was still enough room to house a queensize bed and a cupboard big enough to contain two Narnias. It was more than enough for a man who only wanted a place to lay his head after a hard day on the grindstone. Sid left the room and walked along the corridor. Soon enough he was slowly making his way down the stairs, carefully planning his every step so as to avoid as much pain as possible. His wobbling waddle was that of a flashbang victim as he focus all his senses into navigating the staircase as painlessly as possible, and by the time he reached the bottom Sid felt a warm satisfaction that not one of his tired, stretched muscles had twanged on the way down. Pausing, his senses came back to him. It was then that he heard the familiar bassy boom of a voice pouring from the speakers of his brother's expensive, over-the-top home cinema system. Immediately assuming that someone had just neglected to turn the television off before they'd left, he made his way across the great room to turn it off. It was with great surprise, then, that he noticed Marvin sitting slouched on the leather couch, nursing an empty glass in his hand and with a closed rucksack nestled under his armpit. Pointing the remote at the plasma screen, Andy's son flicked through the channels, aware of his uncle's approach but paying him little attention. “Hey, kid,” Sidney said as he approached with all the grace and poise of a ninety-year old. He'd not seen the scrawny twelve year old since he'd returned the night before, but it had caught him by surprise to see him sitting there in his school uniform at eleven o' clock in the morning. Marvin said nothing as Sid hobbled towards the lounge area and sat himself down on a black recliner, wincing as he straightened his busted back out. Sidney watched the television's images reflect against the lenses of Marvin's glasses before it finally clicked. “Ain't you s'posed to be in school right about now?” * * * * * The suite was exquisite and much fancier than what he was accustomed to. All wooden panelling, thick carpets, and silver fixtures, with sizeable sleeping areas, two bathrooms, and a communal area that was almost as big as back home, Cayle would quite happily have stayed there forever. As he stepped from the shower rubbing a towel into his shorn, black hair, the youngest Murray brother padded slowly towards the window and looked out to the world below. Crowds of people danced on golden sands little more than a hundred yards away as waves of perfect, crystal blue water lapped gently against the shore and a warm, autumn sun beat down from above. Smiling, Cayle Murray thanked the lord for his elder brother's wealth. After dressing himself, Cayle – bright and buzzing; full of life – walked through to the communal room, where he found his brother hunched over a cafétierre by the kitchen's sole worktop. “Morning, old-timer,” he said. “Morning,” Andy responded, half-assed and half-interested; a tone that Cayle failed to pick up on. Stretching out his torso with a pleased “ahhhhhh,” as he walked, Cayle moved towards the french doors leading onto the balcony and stepped out to a glorious Floridian afternoon. Warm and bright, the temperature wasn't quite as high as it was in the height of summer, but that's how he liked it. Coming from Scotland, none of the Murrays had ever quite acclimatised to the nearly intolerable heat and humidity of Floridia in July and August. Fall really took the edge off the heat; it was still hotter than anything they'd experienced back on the British Isles, but at least they could stand it. He squinted in the sunlight as he took in two big lungfuls of the Miami sea breeze, thinking of what they day ahead had in-store for him. Dangerous Games was only a couple of days away and Andy would surely spend the bulk of his time in the gym or doing promotional work, leaving him with nought to do on this wonderful day than play; and that suited Cayle Murray to a tee. Having come off the independent tour a few days earlier than Sid, Cayle had made his way straight down to the state's east coast to join Andy on a another high-rolling GCW jaunt. This is the life, he thought as he leaned forward on the balcony and gazed upon the beach-goers below. For him, it really was. It was only recently that he'd started joining his brother on these PPV trips, but they were quickly becoming the highlight of his month. His life had been one of reckless decisions and missed opportunities; two stints behind bars had reduced his wrestling career to little more than trolling the US indy toilet scene, where once he'd been a FUSE Fatal Faction champion. Nobody wanted to touch him with a bargepole for years, and so his brother's unending hospitality was a godsend. Pulling a pair of shades from the pocket of his jeans, Cayle felt a smile crawl across his imperfect, battle-scarred face. The view was worth a million dollars; it was a good job, then, that the bill was on Steven Caldera and Global Championship wrestling. Then something grabbed his attention. A crash from inside, like plastic cracking against stone and shattering into a thousand tiny pieces. Seeing movement within, Cayle turned and walked back into the suite. Andy, breathing heavily, stood above the plastic cafétierre that lay cracked in two on the linoleum floor below, splaying ground coffee beans all over the place. “Andy?” Cayle quizzed as he walked closer to get a better view, removing his shades as quickly as he'd put them on. “Shit, we ain't gonna have to pay for that, are we?” he asked, assuming Andy had dropped it. Andy said nothing as he pulled his eyes from the broken coffee maker and took a seat at the breakfast bar, biting his teeth together and exhaling through them. “Couldn't give a shit,” he eventually growled. “Fuckin' thing was stuck anyway.” Kneeling, Cayle picked up the two pieces of cafétierre and placed them on the worktop. He looked up at Andy; something was burning behind his pearly blues, something he couldn't put a finger on. His brother's face looked slightly redder than usual, and his short, black hair was ruffled and uncombed. “You feelin' alright today, bro?” he quizzed, knowing nothing of what had happened after the press conference the day before. “You're looking a bit, uhhh... LK-ish.” “Fuckin' coffee makers,” Andy spat the words as if they were a chunk of tobacco that had lost it's flavour. “What's wrong with granules?” “Okayyyyy,” Cayle trailed as his brother leaned back and ran a palm down his clean features. “'Sup? You stressed about facing O'Neil or something?” “O'Neil?” Andy looked up. “O'Neils a backstabbing piece of shit. I know how to handle him.” That was all he said. Cayle was surprised; he usually had trouble trying to get Andy to shut the hell up when it came to talking about his opponents, but he seemed reluctant to even mention his Dangerous Games opponent's name. It wasn't the match, no; something else was bugging GCW's Scottish King of Cool. “What then?” Cayle said. Finally Andy's eyes met his. A silence hung over them, lingering for a good few seconds longer than Cayle found comfortable. Eventually Andy looked down again and hopped off of his perch. “I gotta go,” he said, quickly pacing towards the door. “Go wher--?” It was too late. The door had already slammed before the younger Murray could get his words out. With a shrug, Cayle stood up and went back out to the veranda. * * * * * “I'm not going to school,” Marvin Murray as his channel-hopping ceased on a news channel that he had absolutely no interest in. “I'm sick.” “Sick, eh?” enquired Sidney, unconvinced by a young, Scottish-tinged voice that sounded perfectly healthy. “Yeap. Sick.” “Huh.” Sidney eyed his nephew curiously; not once had Marvin turned to make eye contact, and it was distracting the bearded patriarch of the Murray clan. Slowly he stood up, biting his lower lip as barbs of white-hot pain coursed through his veins. He leaned over Marvin and placed the back of his hand on the kid's forehead, looking for a high temperature. “Seem alright to me.” “Get off,” Marvin said, gently swiping his uncle's hand from his head but still didn't look at him. Confused, Sid sat back down and glared at Andy's son. It was always quite hard to tell if the kid was sick; his skin was so milky and pale that he never looked particularly sickly, and he sure as he didn't have an unnaturally high temperature. His voice wasn't the usual sympathetic croak that tended to accompany minor illness, and his eyes looked as bright and sprightly as ever, even if his demeanour wasn't so cheery. He ain't ill, Sid thought. “Want me to call the doc?” he asked. “No.” Yeap. Definitely not ill. Leaning forward in his chair, Sid sighed. Now it was up to him to find out why the kid was sitting at home, watching the television, when he should've been at school. He was his uncle, but Sid was about as natural a father figure as Adolf Hitler was a humanist. The fact that the school would surely be on the phone soon to find out where Marvin was was lingering in the background, too. “Alright kid,” he said, “what's up? Why ain't ya going to school?” Even at twelve years old, Marvin could tell that his uncle wasn't convinced by his feigned illness, but he hadn't exactly put much effort into the façade in the first place. He sighed too. “Dad.” Sid raised his brow. “Dad? What about your Dad?” Marvin just shrugged. Sidney rolled his eyes. This was going to be a little more difficult than he'd envisioned. “You cheesed off 'cause he didn't take ya down to Miami?” “A little.” “Heh. Well heck; if I was bein' left out, I'd be feelin' the exact same way. But let me tell ya somethin'; with Cayle runnin' 'round down there without any responsibilities, that ain't exactly gonna be the best environment for a kid to be hangin' 'round.” Sid chortled. “B'sides, wouldn't have been right for him t'drag ya outta school for a week... although I can see that ain't exactly high on yer priority list at the moment.” No verbal response came from Sid's brother's son, who resumed flicking through channels before the pixelated Fox news anchor had the chance to spit more filth. What puzzled Sidney was that Marvin had clearly planned on going to school that morning; he was fully dressed in his uniform and his back seemed to be packed, yet he'd changed his mind. “Aint Miami, is it?” “No,” Marvin said. “I miss Winston.” Winston? Sid pondered. The dog. Of course. Kayla Jarosz had brought the chocolate-coloured Dachshund pup into their life little over a year ago and he'd become Marvin's best friend. The kid loved that little bundle of fluff, and Winston loved him back. Whenever he came home from school the dog would be dancing around his feet, leaping up, pawing his legs, trying desperately to get his attention, because he knew the boy would scoop him into his arms and pet him for hours on end. Marvin had almost made the dog his own. He adored Winston, so when his Dad had sent him to the dog pound a couple of days prior it had been a bitter pill that he still hadn't managed to swallow. Sid almost understood why Andy had to do it; he didn't want anything to do with Kayla any more, and he had to purge the house of everything that was hers or would remind him of her. The dog was the last thing on the list, and while Andy knew his son was particularly fond of the pup, it had to go in Andy's eyes. Every time he caught sight of Winston he saw the face of the lady who'd bought it reflecting in his inner cortex, and how was he supposed to move on with his life with that constant reminder flashing at him every day? But still, it was pretty fuckin' harsh on the kid. Andy had told Sid how Marvin had cried when he'd first heard the news, and how they'd barely talked since it'd happened. That certainly explained a thing or two about Marvin Murray's behaviour that morning. “Awww, look son,” Sid began, trying to sound as sympathetic as his gruff tones would allow. “I know it ain't easy t'deal with; losin' a pet's almost look losin' a member of the family sometimes, but you'll get over it, trust me. Sod it; I'll buy ya a new pup outta my own pocket if it makes ya feel better.” “Don't want another dog,” Marvin whimpered. “I want Winston.” Sid forced the slightest of smiles across his worn-down, rugged features. He leaned over and placed a hand on Marvin's shoulder. “Well, how 'bout I give Andy a call and see what I can do 'bout that?” Marvin still refused to turn. His “thank you” was distant and uninterested. * * * * * Andy stepped out of the chain coffee store with his fingers wrapped round a warm, cardboard up; he didn't like using Starbucks at the best of times, but it was the closest place to the hotel that he could grab a cup of caffeine without having to deal with faulty cafétierres himself. He took a seat in the shade at the only free table on the shop's busy terrace. Often he wondered why on Earth people in Florida would want to drink piping hot coffee when the weather outside was so toasty. And shit, it was Miami; not exactly the most stressful place in the world to live, and if it was a status thing, surely they'd just go down to the beach and sip cocktails on the sand with the tourists and holidaymakers. Andy stopped thinking about it was he took his first sip and took-in his surroundings. The street couldn't have really been any further removed from his Tallahassee home, despite being in the same state. Whereas the state's capital's buildings were split up by the lush greenery of trees, gardens, and parks, everything in Miami seemed to be squashed as close together as possible. Rows and rows of buildings painted in light colours lined what was a reasonably quiet street, the sun's bright rays bouncing off their matted surfaces as if they were made of glass. The city was plastic. The men were jumped-up middle-class Daddy's boys desperately striving for that dishevelled beach bum look, and the women were so pumped full of botox and collagen that Andy had to remind himself on a couple of occasions that he wasn't in Los Angeles. Definitely not the kind of place he'd want to make a home in. Beginning to munch on the complimentary orange-peel cookie that his server – a massive GCW fan, apparently – had given him, Andy watched something noisy unfold across the street. A man wearing a dirty, creased white shirt and some scruffy jeans balled a fist and banged it against a heavy, blue door, having abandoned his quest to get a response through the intercom. Even though he was at the other side of the street, Andy could see that the man's hair was laced with sweat and grease, and deducted that he probably hadn't washed in a couple of days. Too clean to be homeless, too grubby to be housed. Soon enough Andy found his mind wandering from the scene across the road as the events that had transpired in the gym last night returned to him. It was a shock; a haymaker to the senses that had knocked him for six and blown him completely out of sync. They'd been close for a while, yes; during a time when it seemed like they'd both ran out of friends, they'd banded together out of necessity and their friendship had bloomed. But nothing like this... It was the last thing that Andy needed in the wake of another particularly messy split. Barely two months had passed since he'd finished with Kayla, and now one of the most desirable women in the United States of America had apparently fallen for him. At a time when all Andy Murray needed was some peace and quiet, the man upstairs had thrown him another curveball. Washing a mouthful of plain, black coffee down his throat, Andy heard an anguished cackle from the man across the street. Against he bashed his fist into the door, wailing something that was as loud as it was unintelligible. For a moment he seemed to back off and look skywards so that his words would be better projected to the floors above, but it was to no avail. The man pushed his hands down into his skull as his calls went unanswered. And then there was Jason O'Neil, a man more than worthy of Andy's consideration. Over the past couple of weeks the GCW World Champion and the Hall-of-Famer had built up a bizarre relationship, culminating in O'Neil inevitable betrayal on the previous week's WorldWide broadcast. Following their tag team victory over Brian Fisher and Tyson DeBough, the Scarecrow clocked Andy over the head with the belt and made his motivations perfectly clear. He was coming for the one piece of gold that had eluded him throughout his career, and he wasn't going to let a common hatred for Steven Caldera get in the way of that. Andy felt like a fool. Of course Jason O'Neil was going to betray him; he knew that, soon enough, the Scarecrow would forget all about Caldera with his shot at glory looming, and yet he'd been suckered in to being blindsided by his challenger when he should've seen it coming a mile off. This is a man, of course, who's one of the most reviled hate figures in GCW history. Andy had been foolish enough to trust a man who's career had been so full of twists and turns that it made a Chris Bagwell feud seem as interesting as a thirty-minute Dan Black interview, and he wasn't happy about it either. Back across the street, the scabby man seemed to be getting somewhere. A window opened two floors up and a tanned woman with short, blonde hair poked her head out. Even from distance Andy could see a puff redness surrounding her eyes, suggesting tears. Her presence seemed to stoke the man's fire as he launched into another impassioned volley of nouns and adjectives. Was Jason O'Neil a threat? Of course he was. Perhaps not as menacing as LeStatt, but twice as unpredictable, the Scarecow was like nothing Murray had ever faced. He was a GCW Hall of Fame member for a reason, and while Andy had taken down men of that stature before, he'd never gone into a match feeling so God-damn uncertain of himself. How was he supposed to prepare for O'Neil? The man had barely wrestled in years, and Andy had only known him for a month. He wasn't even close to getting inside of Jason's head, and he had absolutely no idea how the challenger was going to approach the match. Andy was lost at sea, and it wasn't a feeling he was comfortable with, but he was the GCW World Heavyweight Champion for a reason. He was among the best wrestlers in the world, and while Jason O'Neil had his mystique, he was out of shape and rusty. Andy was at the peak of his powers, and with his long-term injury problems seemingly behind him, he knew he could out-wrestle O'Neil, it was just a matter of keeping up with the Scarecrow's dark imagination. This was a man that had once tried to single-handedly force Global Championship Wrestling out of business; would he think twice of destroying Andy Murray's career for the sake of winning the GCW World Championship? Would he hell. It was a unique test. He'd faced bigger and better wrestlers than Jason O'Neil, sure; but had he ever crossed paths with a man so diabolical? A man who he knew would stop at nothing to get his hands on the GCW World Heavyweight Championship after a career-long wait. A man who's brother Bryan, lest we forget, had his career ended at Andy's hands during a buried alive matched a few months prior. Something terrible was coming Andy's way, and he would have to be at his very best to overcome it. The decibel was rising in the warm afternoon. Both man and woman were bawling at the top of their voice now; shouting and screaming without giving the other the slightest of chances to formulate a coherent response. Suddenly she disappeared. For a few seconds she vanished back through the window, leaving the man shrouded with confusion for a brief few moments. She returned soon, though; clasping onto handfuls of clothes and personal effects that were quickly bunged out of the window like confetti at a wedding. Immediately the man tried to gather them. Scrabbling around on the pavement like a hen picking for scraps, he tried to bundle his clothes under his arm but she was too fast for him to keep up with. Realising the futility of trying to gather them, the man stopped and looked up, continuing to bark at his estranged lover, but she paid him no heed. Andy was surprised that nobody else at the coffee shop had noticed them. Well, perhaps they had noticed them, but chose not to pay attention. He couldn't blame them; each of the people on the terrace had their own issues and problems to worry about, and he was no different. There was Jason O'Neil, and there was her, and even if those qualms were resolved then there was little doubt that Steven Caldera would have something dreadful in-store for him after Dangerous Games. Murray's patience with the GCW President was growing thin. On-air he would put up his walls and hide beneath his thick skin as the boss threw barrage after barrage of poison at him, but he was getting a little bit tired of a man who was supposed to smoothly regulate the company sticking his nose in his business. Caldera's seemingly insatiable desire for the GCW World Championship was so strong that, if he successfully defended at Dangerous Games, Andy would even consider offering the president a shot at the belt himself, just to get it over and done with. The girl in the window was crying now. She'd stopped tossing his items out the window, and was hunched over the ledge, tears trickling down her cheeks and splatting down on the dusty pavement below. Andy finished his coffee. Things weren't so bad. He was the most recognisable figure in professional wrestling and one of the country's highest earners. He was surrounded by a strong, loving family who'd never question his dedication for the sport like Kayla and Jane had, and with a couple more successful defences he'd surely be able to mount a serious charge for the title of “great GCW Champion of all-time.” Sure, Andy Murray had dilemmas, but unlike those of some others, they were good dilemmas to have. He got up and walked away from the terrace as the woman slammed the window shut, leaving a former lover to pick up the pieces and trudge back to his pit of loathing without a clue how to carry on. Andy saw him gather the last of his close and slowly stumble away, defeated. He wouldn't let himself become the man across the street. * * * * * It had been almost an hour since Sidney had left to go to the doctor's, and even though Marvin had promised his uncle he'd go to school for the afternoon period he was no closer to heading out the door. Instead he sat in his father's lounge, curled up in front of the television but no longer paying it any attention. He'd been there for hours now, but for weeks he'd pondered the step he was about to take with conflicted trepidation swirling around his mind. Things had been difficult ever since Kayla had left. Dad was always gone, and his uncles never seemed to be there either. So he'd come home from school to an empty house, make his own dinner, and sit in front of the television all night with nobody to keep him company but his dog, and now even Winston was gone. It was no life for a twelve year old. No life at all. Clutching one of the house's wireless phones close to him in a weak, trembling grip, Marvin raised it up and skimmed his way through the digital phone book. He found the number he was looking for. Dialled it. Ring, ring. Ring, ring. Second thoughts began to seep into his head. He should hang up, he shouldn't-- Ring, ring. Ri- Click. Too late. “Hello?” A female voice responded, one that he'd heard a thousand times, but not once in eight months. Where he thought the sound of her smooth, English tones might bring him comfort, they only increased his unease. “Hello? Anybody there?” His hand was so wobbly that he almost dropped the receiver. Marvin tried to part his lips but his jaw was so tight, locked almost. “Ummm, I think you have the wrong number.” She was going to hang up. He found his voice. Last gap. “Mom?” Click. Too late. She was gone. * * * * * Cayle grabbed his keys and pulled a shirt over his shoulders. He was just about ready to head out of the day when the suite's door suddenly flew open, and all 6'7” of his elder brother strode confidently into the room. Andy immediately made his way to the fridge and pulled out a cartoon of Tropicana. Grabbing a glass from the drying rack, Andy poured himself some juice and downed it in one. When he slammed the glass back down on the worktop, Andy saw Cayle staring at him. “What'chu looking at?” he said, smiling. Cayle snapped out of it. “Uhhhh, nothing. You've... cheered up.” Andy nodded and looked down to where a shattered cafétierre and scattered coffee beans once did lay. They were gone. “And you've tidied up.” “What's going on, Andy?” Cayle said curiously, sliding his keys into his pocket. “You've been almost unapproachable for the last couple of days, now it's like the sun's shining outta your mouth. What's the deal?” “Huh. Worried were you??” “Nah, just thought you where 'in the zone' or whatever the fuck you wanna call it.” Andy chuckled and set about pouring himself another glass of juice. “Nah, just needed to go out and clear my head,” he said, but would tell him nothing of the couple in the street or the realisation they had brought about. It was time to grab his problems by he horns and deal with them head-on. No more worries, no more qualms. Just taking care of business in the only way he knew how. In a couple of days he would discover if he had it in him to overcome Jason O'Neil and stake his claim as one of the all-time greats, and he'd discover if those three precious words she'd uttered to him were more than just the confused feelings of a mixed-up mind. “So, what was it?” Cayle asked, stepping forward. Andy gulped down a mouthful of juice. “You'll find out at Dangerous Games.” So would Jason O'Neil. So would Vivica J. Valentine.
