SCANDAL IV: CRIME AND PUNISHMENT

19 October 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 19/10/2008 PROLOGUE If you ask most folk, they’ll tell you that Jacob McKail is one of the biggest pricks they ever did see. They’ll tell you he’s a drunk. They’ll tell you he’s a bad man. They’ll tell you he’s a monster. And they’re right. He’d even tell you the same damn thing himself, if you asked him and he didn’t just flat out ignore you. Seems understandable then that the man called Howson and his associates perceived McKail to be the type of man that could be bought. Besides, as Howson told it, McKail’s biological father could be bought for a pack of cigarettes and a pitcher of beer back when he was working the wrestling circuit. And Howson would know. He was his tag team partner for more than ten years, his friend for longer than that. The way they figured it, the apple didn’t fall all that far from the tree and McKail would jump at the chance for a bigger payday than he’d ever seen in his life. They figured it wrong and McKail refused, hanging onto the last shred of dignity he had left regardless of what it might cost him. But it didn’t matter anymore. Howson didn’t much care about what he wanted and cared even less about his dignity. McKail was their target, he would be “their man” and there was nothing he could do about it. Just like all the rest who dared defy them, he’d fold? CHAPTER 1 NOT THEIR MAN McKail took a sip of his coffee and pretended not to notice Howson climbing out his car in the parking lot. The sight of the miserable old bastard made him want to rush out of his booth and the diner and beat him within an inch of his life. After what they’d done to him, it was more than understandable, it was almost expected. But it wasn’t smart and he needed smart a whole lot more than rage right about now. The diner was at the height of the lunchtime rush; a crowded place with plenty of people, plenty of witnesses. The way he figured it, it was protection for both of them. For Howson, it’d prevent him trying something stupid as a way of revenge and for McKail, it’d ensure he’d play this thing smart and not attempt to break the table with the old bastard’s face. That was the theory, at any rate. Right about now, he was wondering whether he’d just dug himself a hole. A couple of minutes later, McKail feels something lingering over his left shoulder. Silent, but definitely real. He didn’t need two guesses. “Howson,” he greeted, through grit teeth, “take a seat.” “Yer wanted to talk?” Howson asked him. “Shouldn’t we go someplace a lil” more private?” McKail shook his head. “Fuck no.” Howson grinned. “Don’t trust yerself alone with me, huh?” He allowed the question to hang in the air for a while, taking McKail’s silence as the a clear enough answer. “I can understand that.” Howson slipped into the booth opposite McKail and the waitress put a cup in front of him and poured him a coffee, in too much of hurry to bother asking him whether he even wanted it or not. McKail shoved his cup towards her and she hit him with a refill and a smile. The latter he ignored. McKail glared out of the window, making sure not to look the old prick in the face fearing that his anger would take over if the bastard so much as looked at him funny. “I don’t care.” McKail imagined that Howson was now glaring at him blankly, but didn’t bother to find out for sure. “Did I miss tha start o’this conversation or somethin?”” Howson asked him. “What don’t yer care about?” “Most things anymore,” McKail responded. “Except Mary and my wrestling career. You fucked with both and now you expect me to work for you” It ain’t gonna happen.” “We’ve been over this before,” Howson replied, with a bored sigh. “Yer brought this on yerself, boy. Yer shoulda just worked with us from the start and none of this would’ve happened.” McKail rolled his eyes and extracted a smoke from the packet of cigarettes lying on the table. “Like I’m gonna lose sleep wondering whether your right about that...” Out of the corner of his eye, McKail saw Howson shake his head. “What tha hell is this about, boy?” McKail lit his cigarette and looked the old bastard in the eye. “It ain’t gonna happen.” “What ain’t gonna happen?” Howson questioned. “You know what,” McKail told him. He took a drag of his cigarette and exhaled, watching the smoke gently drift away, somewhat illuminated by the sunlight bursting through the window. McKail closed his eyes the beautiful golden glow washed over his face and realised since making his decision that it was the right one to make. “I won’t be your man, I won’t be bought and I won’t be held responsible for what I might do if you try to fuck with me again. I’m tellin” ya face to face just so we’re clear and there is no misunderstanin?.” Howson rose from his seat in silence and shook his head. “I’m disappointed in yer, boy.” He throws down a few bucks on the table for the coffee and eyes McKail warily. “Yer say yer only care about two things: Mary an” wrestling. Well, Mary’s dead and soon, so will be yer wrestlin? career. Yer made a bad decision here, boy and now there’s no turnin? back. Be seein? ya.” McKail ignored him and took another drag of his cigarette, idling wondering what manner of bullshit they’d throw at him next. CHAPTER TWO TALK OF THE TOWN KNOCK! KNOCK! “Jacob McKail, are you in there?” KNOCK! KNOCK! “Mr. McKail?” “He’s in there alright, Joe. The guy behind the desk says he ain’t left his room all night.” KNOCK! KNOCK! “Answer the door, Jacob! We know you’re in there!” “Ugh...” McKail muttered, snapping back to life. “What the hell?” “Did you hear that, Joe?” “Yeah. Movement.” KNOCK! KNOCK! McKail sat up in bed and grabbed for his cigarettes resting on his bedside table, all the while eyeing the door and the shadowed figures under the door and outside the window. What the fuck is this now? He silently questioned, waiting for his brain to join the rest of him and wake the hell up. He balanced a smoke on his lips and lit it. Once up and running, his mind raced back to the previous morning and the meeting with Howson in the diner. Possible repercussions? Has he sent some muscle around to try and knock some sense into me? Doubtful. Making too much noise. He glanced at the gap under the door and amidst the shadows saw the ever-loving glow of the sun. And in open daylight? Even Howson’s not that stupid. “Can you see anything through the window, Bob?” “Nah. Blinds are drawn. I can’t see shit.” “He’s definitely in there though. Call it journalist’s intuition.” Journalists? Fuck! How the hell did they know I was here? I don’t even tell the GCW road agents where the hell I’m staying... His mind raced, still trying to figure out just what in the hell is going on. And what the hell do they want with me? I’ve done nothing. that would warrant this attention? This isn’t good... KNOCK! KNOCK! With difficulty he pulled on his jeans and scooped his t-shirt resting on a chair, thoughtlessly forcing it over his head and cigarette in one smooth movement, all the while tiredly stumbling toward the door. Miraculously, he burnt neither himself nor his t-shirt and once at his destination, he flicked the deadbolt off and opened the door. The men standing outside his door were caught unawares and much to McKail’s shock he realised that there were more than two perched at his doorstep waiting for him, there were dozens. Oh fuck! He silently cursed, casting his eyes over the mixture of oddities before him. It took them all a few seconds to realise the door had actually opened and McKail figured that at least half of the crowd had already begun to return to their respective vehicles, perhaps assuming they’d been sold some bad information. It didn’t take long for the camera’s to start flashing and the crowd to lurch forward, asking questions and driving their microphones in his general direction looking for some kind of comment. What the fuck’s happening here? He questioned. What the hell did I do to warrant this kind of media attention? But their voices were too intermingled and from what McKail could work out they were just asking for comments on what he’d done, assuming that he’d know just what in the hell they were talking about. After a couple of seconds, he realised he didn’t need to know that badly and slammed the door shut before the vultures got too close for comfort. McKail turned and leant back against the door, ignoring the questions and the knocking the best he could and took a drag of his cigarette. His stomach lurched and his bowels threatened release. This wasn’t going to be good, that much was obvious, so the question was just how bad is it? Idly, McKail flicked on the television and directed the channel towards local news. Good chance if whatever the hell was going on was as major as he’d already been lead to believe, it’d be on there. He didn’t have to wait too long to hear his own name. He turned up the volume and sunk to the floor. “Breaking news this morning,” the news anchor began. That pretty brunette. Gail something...?A report from an anonymous source has alleged that a professional wrestler by the name of Jacob McKail has been involved in possible match-fixing activities at a local organisation called AFWA. Our reporter on the scene--? McKail flicked the TV off and gently massaged his forehead. “Motherfucker...” Howson had done it again. Fucked him and then manipulated him as though he were nothing but a fucking child. And why? Because he wanted to do the right thing. Because he wanted to be a good man? Well fuck “em, he decided. Fuck “em all. He stood and grabbed his coat. He had a match to get to, taking place later that day and he was fucked if he was going to let Howson fuck everything up for him. Again. This wouldn’t be the end of McKail’s career, it’d only be the beginning and no amount of lies could change that. He only wished he believed it. CHAPTER THREE SHUNNED Walking into the AFWA arena that night was the worst damn thing McKail had ever had to do. He figured it hit him harder because he didn’t expect it, though he very definitely should have. The glances thrown at him by the technical staff and other workers whilst walking backstage told him that everybody in the damned place had heard the news and what’s more, they’d all believed it. If looks could kill, he’d be a dead man walking right about now. He should feel disappointed in them for mindlessly believing what they read about him, but he didn’t. After all, this was the business they loved, this was their life, and for all they knew McKail had shit on them all from a height and brought shame down upon them all. He was looking for his locker room when it happened. Butch Massacre, another AFWA worker, caught him off guard, got him by the collar and shunted him up against the wall. Worse thing was, damned near dropped the cigarette from his mouth. “Can I help you?” McKail asked him, desperate not to dislodge the smoke from his lips. “Don’t need that smart mouth of yours,” Massacre told him, ? not now. Not after what you did.” McKail figured he was right, but couldn’t help himself. After all, he’d done nothing wrong but then if he tried to explain that to them, would they listen? In Massacre’s case he doubted it. That fucker was so dumb President Bush would feel intellectually superior in his presence. By now, a small crowd had gathered. mostly backstage tech guys, but there were a few workers too. Usually, McKail would just fuck with Massacre and let everyone believe whatever the hell they wanted to believe about him. But not this time. His wrestling career was on the line, regardless of what he did or didn’t do. It was too important to piss away just because he liked to piss people off. He had to set the record straight, regardless of how useless it may or may not turn out to be. “I didn’t do shit, ya dumb bastard,” McKail told him, looking the big fucker in the eye. “That’s not what the papers say,” Massacre replied. “That’s not what the news lady says.” McKail rolled his eyes. “Don’t tell me ya believe everything you see on TV, Butch. If you do explain why the hell ain’t driving around in flying cars like the Jetsons.” Massacre leaned in closer and rammed his forearm against McKail’s throat. “I could choke the life out of you right here and everybody who saw would swear it was an accident. That’s how much you’re hated right now. So don’t get smart, okay?” McKail reacted right away, spiting his cigarette right in Massacre’s face, forcing the big man to reel backwards, brushing the fire and ash from his face wildly. McKail took a few seconds to compose himself before stepping forward and planting a boot right into his crotch, dropping the big man instantly. McKail eyed massacre who was now rolled up on the floor in a foetal positioned cradling is crotch in one hand and his face in the other. McKail stooped down just by his head. “I’m gonna tell ya once and once only ya dumb shit. I didn’t throw no match. Believe or don’t, I don’t really give a fuck.” With that, McKail shot the crowd a cautionary glance. Most still wouldn’t believe him, but they’d probably at least not try to fuck with him again. Here’s hoping... Without another word, McKail continued along to his dressing room and slumped down on the wooden bench, leaning back on the flimsy plasterboard wall. Damned thing felt like it was going to give away any minute, he felt exactly the same way. He reached inside his jacket and pulled out a pack of smokes, looking to replace the cigarette he’d just wasted on Butch’s face, when he noticed somebody crowding the doorway out of the corner of his eye. He just wished it was Howson so he could beat the old bastard to death and piss on the corpse, but it wasn’t. It was Matt Munroe, AFWA’s head booker and McKail reckoned the man’s face looked about as grim as his own. McKail lit his cigarette and produced a weak smile. “Figure I already know what this is about, right?” Munroe nodded. “I reckon so.” “Listen, the man’s a dick, ? McKail told him. “He backed me into a corner and I reacted. If you need me to fill in for Massacre tonight just give the nod.” Munroe shook his head. “I ain’t here about, Massacre.” McKail sighed. “You’re here about the other thing?” “I’m here to tell you we don’t need you tonight, Jacob,” Munroe told him. “Someone became available at the last minute and well, we’re going with him.” McKail stood from the bench and glared at him; Munroe couldn’t look him in the eye. “What” That’s bullshit, Matt!” Munroe extracted a plain white envelope from his pocket and handed to McKail. “Don’t worry, Jacob. Your appearance fee will be paid in full regardless.” McKail looked at the envelope and then back at him. “I don’t want your damned money,” McKail told him, throwing the envelope back at him. “I didn’t throw any matches and I never will!” Munroe nodded. “I know, Jacob. I believe you, I really do, but we can’t put you on the card. Not after all this. Maybe in a few months time, when this all blows over--? McKail didn’t need to hear anymore, he’d heard enough bullshit already today. He brushed past Munroe and headed right back towards the parking lot. God help anyone who gets in my way... EPILOGUE McKail glared at the sunrise from the building top and just couldn’t find the beauty in it. There was a time when he could, there was a time he could see beauty in most things, but all of that died right along with his wife. Since then, he’d been wandering through life just existing, never living, but at least accomplishing something with his wrestling career. Now, through no fault of his own, he didn’t even have that. Finishing off the last mouthful of Jack Daniels, he hurled the bottle against the ground and watched the shards of glass burst apart from it with bizarre pleasure. He needed destruction right now. Ideally, he wanted it to be Howson face smashed against the concrete, but the glass bottle worked just as well. All of this, he thought, because I was trying to be a good man. Well, fuck that! That’s not me, not anymore. He lurched forward, closer to the edge and looked down at the twenty storey drop to the street below. When he came up here, a couple of hours ago, he’d done so with the drunken intention of ending it all. Ending the misery and just doing it. At least then, he’d be back with Mary. She’d forgive him, she’d understand. But he couldn’t go through with it. Not because he was scared, lost his nerve or even had second thoughts. It was because if he did that, then that bastard Howson would win. And why? Because he’s a heartless bastard with no hint of remorse nor mercy. It came down to McKail liking the idea of Howson winning less. That was all. Since making the decision not to jump, he’d been thinking about what he should do and only one option came to mind: be a bigger bastard than Howson. McKail knew he had it in him, he knew it was as plausible as any other option that might be open to him, but he still had reservations. What if I do this and I can’t go back? What if I’m opening a door that cannot be closed? McKail grabbed a fresh bottle of Jack Daniels he’d already rested on a wall beside him and took a couple of mouthful’s to fight away the bitter October breeze. Face it, McKail, he told himself. The door has already been opened. To Be Continued?
