Seen & Not Seen: Not as tangential as it seems, I swear

3 December 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/03/08 "You saw them, though? Right? You saw them?" Sage Black's hands curled into claws against his desk. "I was trippin balls, I mean." "Theres another way?" He laughed and Frank shifted in his seat. "Not everyone has those eyes, kid. Ive got em--Ive seen the snakes before, I mean. Beautiful trails, weaving, confused tracks both ways through time. Every human a culmination. Yeah. Holy shit." "You, uh. You sober?" "Nah." Sage was smirking and he jerked a phone from its base. He held a finger up to Frank and he turned sideways against the windowwall. Frank wasn't listening in; he was forcing himself into an unassuming state of mind and he surveyed the office, cataloged the changes, the shift from then to now. New baubles. Less sheen. Fewer paintings. More chunks. None of it weighed deeply and he sank further into a crimson chair, lit a cigarette idle and slid an ashtray his way; he arranged his lighter parallel of it and his eyes rolled up to Sage. He swallowed sneers and knotted his fists, dug fingernails into the cushion of his thumb, tapped rhythms arcane at the air with the foot of his crossed leg and he darted his eyes anywhere else, to ambient nonfixtures, to absences held with the same regard as presences; silence as important as the notes between it, indentations that spoke volumes. He tried not to eavesdrop, to make himself privy to information whose timing implicated some involvement by himself; he had convinced himself that he fully expected it to work yet only found some surprise as he began to take in, first, Sage's tone--excited, voice rolling rapid--and, almost immediately thereafter, the words themselves. There was no debating; he had already heard and he understood that there weren't precisely degrees of going too far in, that it was an analog decision of within or beyond, yes or no. "the big man. -- Yes. -- Him, him. Yeah. -- I dont give a, I dont care. Get him awake. -- Two days. I want him awake by then. -- No. No. -- No, because I fucking pay you and youre going to wake him up. -- Its always fucking difficult. -- Youre sure? Fucking, fucking positive? -- Yeah? Good. -- Its Frank. Frank Black. -- Yeah." And Sage hung up with a swift cackle, smiled monumental and swiveled his great chair to Frank. "Some people leave, leave ghosts. Little faint slips. Some people leave these, these great walls. Visible from space--not like that puny fucking Great Wall, no. Some men, they form themselves inseparable from the world." Frank's head was cocked to the side and the angle of inclination increased as his confusion mounted, joined the fray against an apathy and a bitterness. "I aint come here to, uh. Share trip stories." "Yeah? Well, you shouldnt have brought it," he leaned his head down, shifted his eyes; he snapped back up. "You shouldnt have let me bring it up. Now youre obligated into something." "Uh. Okay. Thats cool. Can I, can I actually get to my point? I dont wanna be outta the country any longer than I got to." "Yeah. Sure." He smacked a button on his desk. "Yes?," came electronic. "Record this. Write this down. Something." "Yes, sir." Frank sits. Frank watches. Frank's eyebrows roll. And Sage motions him on. "Aight. You, yknow Leslie, righ?" "Of her--yeah. Yeah, I know your situation. But what do you need?" He stressed need. He put his fucking heart into it. "Well. Shits fallen through, her marriage is over, and her husbands got the kid. And, shit. The goal is for him to not have the kid anymore. Basically." "And its on me, Great Sage, to get those wheels turning for you, yes?" "Well. Yeah. I guess." "Huh. Yeah. Yeah, Ive got no soul at risk. I can do that--but youve got to do this for me, okay? Dont miss this thing I have given you. Can you, will you promise me that much?" "I dont even know what it is." "You probably wont afterwards, either. Doesnt matter. Will you do it?" "What is it?" Sage shrugged. "Also. Doesnt matter. Yes, or no. I scratch your back, you just do this one thing for me." "I won the World Title, yknow. At Dangerous Games." "I know. You lived up to your hype. Youve got to defend it, yeah? Do this first. This will come first. Youll appreciate it--its not just. Its not an easy thing to meet this man. Fuck, no." "You really didnt give the first fuck how that turned out, did you?" "What? The belt? Man, kid. Of course I give a fuck. Its just. You arent just a wrestler. Youre a representative, youre a model, youre a spokesman--shit, kid. Youre a fucking person. I dont want to, to get you distracted. If you started like, holy shit. If you started caring about wrestling like, like fucking Johnny Lexicon, LeStatt Knight? That would be bad. Money or no, bad bad bad. This will help. Say yes." "Cant you just, yknow, demand that I do it?" "I could, but I would--itd be better if you agreed instead of forcing it." "Alright, then. You cover me, I cover you." "No. You cover you, I cover you. This isnt for me. This is your thing." "See about that." "Yeah. You will. Ill. Ill set you up a meeting with Sobias. Later on. Down the road, a week, two. Nothing major." "You said Sobias aint your best lawyer." "He isnt. But what youre asking may involve lying in the way of destroying a mans life--and in that regard? There exists only this one Sobias Torm, and the rest are just. Puppies." And that was how Frank Black agreed to be where he is, after a flight back to his apartment, a shower, a primitive exchange of hello goodbyes with Leslie, a shower, and a second flight across the country. He is drunk when they touch down, and he staggers through security without impetus. A waiting taxi. Travel. Idle chatter without a subject. Desert sky. Gravel roads. Destination. He steps out into the same night he left, having stalked its creep through the country. Sand crunches beneath his feet and he hacks, fist to his chest, until he's summoned something worth spitting up. It smacks against the ground; he abandons it next and drags trails across an emptied parking lot, washed over by a fine layer of grit that blows in the faint wind. He lights a cigarette on the way, pausing to guess at the distance traveled, the distance yet to go in this absolute night; he measures it as halfway or some slight variation thereof and he continues along in a nicotine glow on inhalation, following the rapidly faded smoke trails on exhalation. Stomping at grains when not kicking a path. He understands that he is apprehensive and files it away. "Jesus." He's shaking his head and he's hesitating, an out of place brick building barely emerging from beyond his field of existence. "Wrestler Frank Black, best known as GCWs Jordan White missing, presumed dead. Last known whereabouts, shithole in Death Valley. Qestionin his boss Sage. Keep you updated." He waits until he's sated and he grinds his cigarette into filter and shreds, mixes it with the drifting earth and, with a great shrug that radiates through the thick layers of cloth he hides behind, finds the doorway and lets himself in. Following orders--the light isn't blinding, it's momentarily disorienting and he takes the time needed to adjust by looking down, by shading himself with a forearm held blocking. "Frank?" "Yeah, yeah." She's diminutive and he cannot place her accent. Her jet hair is wound tight into a bun and Frank does not glimmer her ever looking his way, ever looking up from the pine desk that separates them. She points to the far end of the space, to a simple door, past a distance of narrow hallway and allconsuming, everpresent illumination. "Hes waiting for you." "What is this place?" "JXW Enterprises, Death Valley Branch." "Uh. Aint much to do out here, is there?" "Mr Black has an interest in Death Valley." "I know he used to have that monument shit somewhere out this way, but. Whats it mean, hes got an 'interest' in it?" "Death Valley is a strange place. Beyond that, your qestions are best directed to Mr Black personally." "Well. Whos behind door number only?" She smiles; she doesn't seem to entertain glancing up. "Your appointment." He removes his outermost jacket and drops it on a single hook outstretched from the wall; he straightens the layers beneath it and marches. He regards the entry to the Death Valley Branch or the hallway or the anteroom or whatever arcane position it serves within the architecture, foul thing in the darkness, of light spilling uniform, as though every surface were illuminated out of some sacrosanct pact of qestionable honor; he learns to despise it after twenty feet, begins to demand any hint of clutter, any focus to hold beyond the doorknob gleaming silver and defiant from the void and, where there is none, there exists within him an indescribable urge to spit, to express his disgust, to leave some lingering remnant in the emptiness. By fifty feet he entertains the notion of accepting it, considers it as just a bland room. Seventy-five and he has relented that dream, grown to despise Sage, the Sage that, psychotic meticulous, is surely to be found as the root cause of this distress, whose bizarre twists had determined this as a spot of dread, where physical distance is nothing, is minor in comparison to the mental duress. It has nothing for him to dream with and he can only think of himself or what he is wandering into and, and, and. He despises and that qiet fact is not forgotten as his palm grips around cold steel, as he turns the knob and pushes into an artificial dark, if shadow can be achieved by falsified means. "Close it." The voice is a soft rumble through the structure, sophisticated as it is unyielding as it is the bass of tectonic shifting. He obeys. "Thank you." A flame sparks and a candle is lit, light dancing the outer rim of a giant man with his back to Frank, shrouded in ashen cloth. He treads careful towards this breathless being, its hair hanging long in strings that go transparent in the glow. "Sage wanted me to, uh, to meet you." "Sit down, sit down." "Yes; sir." He passes by a low table covered in wax, the living flicker of fire dancing crazed shadows across its pocked surface. He sits on a couch, opposite, and whatever meat in his brain exists to serve recognition begins to function. He bows his head and avoids asking his most obvious qestion. Death Valley is a strange place. He remembers it; he capitalizes Strange Place. "How long you known. How long Sage known you?" "I couldnt say. It seems that time is a joke with poor delivery, a forgotten punchline." He stops, his eyes shot down to capture in their blacks a single vibrant speck, a star in a distant sky. "Years, at least. A few. We met after the cremation. Im in Tahiti too, you know. Its beautiful there, uh, right now. Have you been?" And he looks up to Frank, fingers resting along his broad jaw. "No, no. I should." "The people are free, there. There arent, there arent, uh, classes. Theres a harmony. I love it." "Sounds. Amazin." "It is, it is. So, tell me. Why do you think youre here?" "Well, I dont. I dont really know. Sir." "You dont have to call me that: sir. Anything else--just not that. I had dreamt of, had wanted I should say, anonymity in death. At least that anyone I might meet wouldnt have such, overblown expectations of me. Im no sir. Id rather Id never been." "Alright. Then, well. I mean. I can guess why Sage did it. I just. I cant be sure." "Then guess at it, Frank." "I. I mean. Give me a second." He hangs his head, feels the mammoth's eyes upon him as he searches for any pieces of himself to collect. He runs his palms backwards through his hair and cranes his neck upwards. "I guess, an thats all this is, cuz I cant even pretend I really understand him, but. If I had to try to understand this? Id say its because he thinks Ive made it, that Ive, like, established myself and that meetin with you, that thatd gimme some perspective. I guess." "Mm." Frank looks into a languid form, a waiting mass. He continues. "An maybe thats just, maybe thats what Im gettin from this. I wouldnt be surprised if that was it, at least. Hes, it wouldnt be the first time he tried to push somethin on me like that, like. Some character buildin exercise." "Do you believe you need it?" "Yeah. Yeah, probably." The man's head shifts to the side and he is staring at Frank or into Frank and Frank is unsure if he even needs to speak; but he opens up regardless, he opens up in a deluge. "Im a fuck up. God, man. Sorry for that, but its right. I guess, I mean. Im. I try to be a good person, but its like. Makin myself happy, tryin to get mine outta this life, I always manage to. Screw somebody over on the way. I never want anythin thats free. It all seems to be someone elses an, man. When I do the right thing, like, the kind of things that benefit the world or those around me, its like I only manage it because I think its gonna help me out an even then, somebodys gettin stomped on for it. I try. Im tryin now, but. Im a wrestler--you knew that, didnt you?" He nods. "Yeah. Righ now, Im hopin to give somebody a shot at this championship belt I got, somebody that might not get that break otherwise, an I cant separate myself from the idea that no matter who I decide to help, Im gonna end up fuckin over more people than that. Fuck. I gotta stop saying that." "Do you think youre offending me?" He laughs. "Hah. Guess youre right." Frank shakes his torso and finds the calm that leans back into couches, that makes itself comfortable. "Okay. An that, thats as best Im doin. Right now, Im livin with this girl Ive known for, for somethin like most of my life an Ive loved her almost every one of those seconds, but I had to ruin her marriage to get her back. Her husband, man, I know almost nothin about him, I know so little, but he wasnt bad, wasnt bad by any stretch of the imagination. He was just a guy in love and, because of me? He doesnt have that anymore. Christ. Hes got custody of their daughter now, an. And." "Youre going to take her too?" Frank agrees silently, shows it with a curt nod. "And their marriage. Its over, you believe?, that theres no hope for reconciliation?" "Yeah." "Would it be kinder to ruin his life, or hers?" "He didnt cheat on her. That I know of, I mean. Its not like its his fault." "But its her daughter, yes? He didnt contribute a tremendous amount. He didnt create life--he participated, yes, but she bore the pain. Tell me, Frank. Does she make you happy?" "Yes. Fuck yes." "Then you return the favor to her and you do your best to improve the world. My accomplishments and yours combined, against even something as small as the combined human experience compared to space beyond, dont measure to a single mote. If you have to help yourself, you help yourself to the point that you can turn those attentions to the people around you, uh. Its, its repaying a debt you may never accrue, its just common kindness. God knows, I only did what I could--I was never a great man, no, but I, uh, I did do everything within my power to give back to the the world that had given me, perhaps wrongly, far beyond my share. I can think of no other way Id have spent my life. I understand no greater value, no greater purpose than a global eqality. For every hundred, thousand men living in sqalor exists one of us fortunate, white American men, and the system smiles to us more graciously than it does others because of something as insignificant and useless as the color of our skin, as random as our gender. Love is amazing, Frank, it is. But even love is beneath trying to right a society built on wrongs." "You an Sage get along great, huh?" "We get along. Hes always been fascinated with talking about my films and, for what hes given me, Im prone to entertaining him. I respect his goals and what he does, and thats why I humor him, but I wish hed have the heart to more often discuss things of import . He treats it. He treats it as though we were already come of terms together, that our beliefs mesh so closely that we are allowed frivolities." He smiles, almost pleading with Frank. "I guess I am, in my condition. Do you feel that way?, that talking about your craft is the most absolute, tremendous bore you can end up at?" "Talkin about works worse than workin." "Exactly. I spent so many hours pretending to be things I was not, was decidedly not. The best years of my life were after the peak of my career, when I could take my time to be who I was, to do what I wanted. I never wanted someone I considered a friend, at least someone I respected, to drag me back into the studio, kicking and screaming. Everyone acts; why should I have been singled out for a presumed talent?" "I feel you on that, freal. Hah." "Whats so funny?" "I caught myself; almost said that lifes too short." "Hah. It is; what comes after it may be too long. I havent decided either way yet. At least theres no ruling class here." "So. Uh. This is gonna sound all sorts of rude, probably, but. What are you? Like. Ghost? Zombie? Vampire?" He hesitates. "Bad trip?" "Id like to know, myself. Ask Sage sometime and let me know what he says. Let me know what he means, if you can understand that much." "I can manage that for you." "Good, good. I can see him now, hah. Hah!" He rolls his neck and he slouches, shrinking into his chair. He crosses his leg and his face draws downwards as his eyes twist manic. "'Kind of fucking qestion is that, man?'" And he has affected Sage's accent, somewhere between California and the world. "'Hes whatever you want him to be. Same as everything else.'" "Hah. Hah. Yeah, yeah. I imagine itll go somethin like that." "It might, or. He may just tell you. He has an odd way to him. Like. Hm." His eyes close tight, hand rolling over the seconds. "A man so tired of not knowing the rules of the world that he made up his own and abides them completely. He operates different from most. Hes a uniqe thing, your employer--arent we all, though? Heh." A smirk. "Id hate to believe in a lowest common denominator, or that my fellow man would fall to it." "What about, what about something worse than that?" "Hm?" "I mean. Okay. This is gonna take a second." "Ive got all the time you need." "Hah." Frank's head swivels as he laughs, freezes as he understands. Dips as he considers. "Aight. I, I dont think theres many people, any people maybe, that really boil down to like, shallow clones, yknow? Everybodys got somethin that makes em stand out. What scares me, man, what I dont wanna believe in, its that maybe we aint, like, innately good creatures. So yeah, what if we're all uniqe an everythin, but weve all got this capacity in us, to bring misery down on other people; to, just destroy shit like animals. Like we maybe arent any better than that. You know?" "If we are all destructive by nature, arent we indebted to fight against it? If we were all kind and gentle souls, if we were all eqal, thered be no reason to improve the world. That you can see those flaws, understand them, it entitles you to correct them. If you are right, if people are just their shadow and not their light, then recognizing this enlists you against it. Knowing that we are less, uh, less than we could be is a reason to push harder, to make progress, to fight with everything you have. You may never balance out for the, atrocities men have committed in even your own life, but at the very least, youll have protected yourself from engaging in them, youll have made it just that much harder for the monsters to gain the ground they need to commit these terrible, terrible crimes." He pauses. His eyes cast to the side, to somewhere else entirely, and Frank understands reflection. "Nature is destructive. Entropy is the natural order--giving in to that is nothing important, nothing permanent. If you can manage to bring just one light to that darkness, youve delayed its victory, youve postponed the apocalypse. Even for those, those scant seconds; youve battled courageously and youve done something." Frank watches him, carefully, as he shifts his ponderous frame, repositions his grand shoulders. "Its funny, isnt it? The things the world teaches us about brutality seem almost, trivial compared to what we can come to from just a moment of qietly looking into ourselves. I sometimes wonder if Ghandi felt the same, if all peaceful men dont come to it as a way to suppress some inner beast." "Hey. Ghandi was tryin to talk Britain into lettin Indians join in on killin Africans in the Zulu wars, so dude did have some actual experience on the subject of fucked up shit." "Hah. Ghandi? Really? Thats incredible. Thats. God. Thats everything summed up, isnt it? Here there be dragons, ahah! Beautiful. I always feared the mapmakers more than the monsters they warned of. That a man could look at the mysterious and take from it only abject terror, the lengths a person that crazed could go to. But no, no. Its the wavering voice of our terrors, that animal inside, you were talking about. You have, youve qite an intimate understanding of violence by now, dont you? You thrive when youre covered in their blood and you wane when its your own--has it taught you anything?" His mass pushes forwards, his elbows rest against his thighs; he's leaning in, his eyes wide and his mouth hanging a slight gape. "Huh. Maybe. Nothin you wouldnt already know, though. Hell, probably nothin you hadnt already decided was all sorts of dumb." "I want to know, regardless. Tell me, Frank, tell me about what you do." "You mind if I smoke?" "I can only smell the ocean, Frank. Dont dream that you could bother me." "Aight." He searches his pockets, Frank does, and, in a second of realization, curses the distance between himself and his cigarettes, regrets that he left his coat anywhere that is not immediate to him. "Shit. Theyre, uh. Theyre elsewhere." "You can get them, if youd like." "Yeah. Yeah, lemme do that. Feel somethin of a jones comin on." "We can all relate to that." The man falls again to rest, reclining into wicker. Frank stands, struggles against a slight weariness in his knees and finds his balance halfway to the door; the handle is warm on this side, almost soothing. He pulls the door behind him and is returned to the light, surgical and absolute. It does not make him wince, nor cringe, nor daydream antipathic to progress. They are only obscured lighting fixtures, after all; are nothing of the potential to destroy a man's day, to wrack his soul. He takes his coat and smiles to the receptionist and she smiles to him as he returns down the hall with it thrown over his shoulder, box of cigarettes fished from some pocket in the midst of the folds. He lingers outside of it, long enough to ignite and to breathe in, long enough to catch himself. What he knows of violence, he thinks and he laughs. He doesn't believe he's learned anything beyond the futility of it all, beyond the meaninglessness found only in conflicts borne of fighting over trinkets of assumed value, of no real benefit to humanity. A dirty part of him has learned to enjoy it and he has named it pride. He turns the handle and it is locked and his hand slips around it, ninety degrees, before he is coming of terms with it. He raps and there is no response; he leaves it, jackboots back to the desk and leans in, enveloping the woman in his shadow. Her head is down, again. She doesn't regard, again. "Hey, uh. The doors locked." "Yes. It is." "Could you maybe. Unlock it?" "No." "I told him Id be back. He had a qestion for me an, I felt like I needed to answer it." "If that door," she points blindly, accurately, "were unlocked, youd have no one to answer it to." "So hes gone?" "Yes." "Uh. How exactlys somethin like that just leave?" "Have you ever tried to stop your mind from wandering?" "Yeah." "Its difficult, isnt it?" He nods. "Yeah." Her soft face turns to his. "He will be back, eventually. I can have Mr Black schedule you another meeting, if youd like." "Yeah. Do that. Id appreciate it, at least." "Alright. Thank you for your time, Frank. We look forwards to seeing you again." "Youre runnin me off, righ?" She nods. He doesn't argue; he turns on his heels and exits into the desert parking lot, into a cool gust of morning as the sun crests some distant strip of mountains. He stretches backwards, yawns before surveying, before recognizing his car. He does not remember driving there; he remembers something entirely contrary to that idea, in fact, and cannot qite comprehend the possibility of incorrect memories or, what he feels more likely, is incapable of accepting background changes that obsolete or overwrite his recollections. He shrugs it off; he has his keys and is discarding the thought that he would hold understanding existence in high regard, in any regard. He drives into the desert, following a trail of gravel and sand packed tight and he has the weeks ahead in his skull already, knotting their congealed strands into something of a plan.
