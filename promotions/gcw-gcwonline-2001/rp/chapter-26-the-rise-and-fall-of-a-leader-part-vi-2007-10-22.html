Chapter 26: The Rise and Fall of a Leader- (Part VI)

22 October 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 22, 2007 __Start Number four. It was an alright number for the most part if you thought about it in terms of all the numbers in the world. Hell, it was better than alright under those circumstances. Four, however, out of thirty wasn't the number for Jay Terror. He'd earned praise when he passed through the curtains on his long walk backstage but none of it mattered. None of it soothed him. The shear fact that on his way to his locker room he was able to watch the final seconds of the Dangerous Games match made him genuinely upset. He was supposed to be the one left over. Not LeStatt Knight, not Vivica J. Valentine, it was supposed to be the Outlaw. Looking at Vivica hanging from her burned arm and finally defeating the man that eliminated him was unsettling to say the least. So where does a man go? Does he consider himself a failure and end his short-lived career with one failed mark? Perhaps he tries again? What would yield the best outcome? Chapter 26: The Rise and Fall of a Leader Part VI The Office 'Did you ever think that it was a nervous habit?' Anita Baker asked from the warm confines of her tall leather chair. She could smell the smoke sifting into her office as it exited the tip of his cigarette. She wasn't a fan of smoking in general but hardly cared if her patients partook in the activity. In fact, she asked them if they wanted to upon arriving. It told a lot about a person. Jay stared at the flowery pattern embedded in the green wallpaper and thought over her question. It'd be beneficial to note how much Jay actually wanted to be here. He didn't. 'You think I'm nervous now?' 'Not exactly. It's been proven in the past though.' Jay tipped the paper and dropped a shower of ashes into the tray she had provided. 'I'm not a nervous guy. I just like to smoke the things.' She nodded, 'That makes sense too.' Her hand scribbled words onto the pad of paper in her lap. 'When did you start?' 'Long time ago.' 'How long?' He figured the answer wouldn't shut her up, 'Back when I was in middle school I'd say.' 'I see,' she noted it, 'how did you get your hands on them at that age?' Trying to shift the subject, 'I thought you were here to tell me if I was an angry guy.' She grinned and laughed to herself, 'I think you're starting to let me know whether you are already Jamison.' 'It's Jay. No more questions 'till we get that ironed out.' He added. She looked away from the paper for the first time today and seemed slightly stunned. 'Jay it is then.' In her line of work, she was the boss. There was no pushing from her patients or any swaying of her decisions, she called the shots. When a guy like Jay came around though, things were to be tested. 'Did your father smoke?' 'He never would have touched the things.' 'Then you got them from friends?' Jay pressed his head against the leather. It was cold on his neck. 'It was something to do at the time. Plus it made us look pretty damn tough.' 'So you smoke because it makes you feel powerful?' She'd seen it many times before with past patients. When a smoker came into her office her first job was to find out why they did it. Once she figured that, things would tend to sway her way and conversation about the actual problem became easy. 'Or did it make you happy that others looked at you differently?' 'I smoke because I like the idea of having some sort of gas crawling down my esophagus. It relaxes my throat muscles and it makes me feel like life has stopped. I'm alive and free all at once.' And even when the words came out he was shocked. In essence, he'd never thought of smoking in those terms. It was always "because it was good", not for technical reasons. Anita smiled, 'Sounds like some sort of dream then.' 'Every time I light one I feel like I've hit the best orgasm of my life.' He took an extra long drag on his cigarette this time inhaling the yummy goodness to its full extent. She was getting somewhere on him and she couldn't help but love every second of it. 'So at this burger place you and your friends were at...did you have a cigarette there?' His memory was fuzzy but not that fuzzy, 'Nah. I tried though.' 'Do you think that's the reason why you lashed out on Phillip?' 'Doubt it.' But did he really doubt it? He wasn't quite sure anymore. Then again, did he care? Anita now leaned back in her chair and felt the burden she had recently lifted return. It was heavy. 'So something else set you off and had you lashing out on him?' 'Naturally.' The flowery pattern seemed to move as he spoke. Perhaps it'd been a little too long in the room. His eyes darted to the far wall where the sole clock rested. Ten minutes left. Anita glanced at her watch as well and sighed. He wasn't someone who was willing to crack in one session. 'Perhaps he said something that offended you then?' And that's when it hit him. For a while he'd slid that idea to the back of his mind but now that she asked it seemed to wander its way back to the front. The scene at the burger joint played in full motion before his eyes and he watched his friend. He spoke with such harsh words and showed how little he cared about Jay. Everything he said had a sting to it that pierced his skin and sunk in creating deep holes out of his pores. He was worthless to Phillip at that moment and that's all it took. Friendly disapproval set him off. Jay had forgotten to answer the question at hand and Anita, being the professional she was, picked up on it. 'Something wrong Jay?' 'Not at all. What was the question again?' That burden fluttered back up to the heavens and she breathed a little easier. The ball was back on her end of the court. 'I asked if maybe Phillip said something that offended you. You know, like something rude or angry that came out wrong.' Bang, he was trapped. The bitch caught him in his moment of weakness and now she'd pester the fuck out of him till he spilled it. Maybe he could play it off for the...he looked at the clock....seven minutes he had left. 'Not that I remember. It's all a blur.' 'Like it wasn't even you then?' 'I guess so, yeah.' She nodded and a slight smirk returned to her lips, 'This is common Jay. Typically, when a person goes through an event like this, they tend to forget what happened. This is due to an overload in the human body. Anger has a way of erasing a person's rational thoughts and replacing them with feelings deep in your core. Take for example someone who is being bullied. They usually tell themselves how much they want to kill that person so they'll stop teasing them. Rationally, they wouldn't kill them. If the situation comes to a climax though, they will find that feeling deep down and it will become their sole mission.' Five minutes left...It was a good thing she was a talker. 'Right.' He replied. She looked at her watch and saw it was the home stretch. 'So my guess is that your outburst was one that was set off by anger. Think hard about anything he may have said that would have made you lash out and break his nose.' Anita hoped he wasn't lost completely. Too bad for her that he was. 'It's a big mess in my head today. I don't know, ends just aren't matching.' He spun on the leather and looked at Anita for the first time since walking into her office. He'd forgotten that she wasn't exactly the worst looking woman in the world. Hell, for thirty one she was a great catch. 'Now now, we still have two minutes.' 'When do I get to do ink spots?' Damn he was cunning. 'They wouldn't help you.' 'Are you sure? I mean, you never know...' He was gone and there was no coming back. It was time for Anita to throw in the towel and give him a date for their next meeting. 'Look Jay, I would like to see you again next week. Same time, same day if that's ok with you.' 'I don't know, I travel all over the place and it might be a little too far...' 'You're required to do at least three appointments Jay. It's up to you when we can get them over with.' 'Next Monday it is.' He smiled and looked at the clock. Home free. 'It was a pleasure doc.' He jumped down from the leather chair and walked straight past her to the door. Once gone, Anita shook her head and pulled out her voice recorder. A click rang aloud. 'Patient Jamison Talley, three forty five. Dig into his and Phillip's conversation prior to the attack. He knows what happened but is not willing to share the secret.' __________ If he decides that wrestling isn't something you just quit and instead it's something you keep at, where would it lead him? In theory, quitters never win. But what about people that never quit? What happens to the person that is so persistent that god himself asks them to give up? It hasn't been proven and if it has there are no records telling of the outcome. What's the worst that could happen though? Maybe he could come back and show the world that he was better than number four. I mean, it isn't exactly a label set in stone. He had a chance to amend the awful title and jump up the ladder of success to a more suitable spot. Although it would take work, it was what must be done. He had no other choice. __________ There hadn't been any official paperwork sent in to keep Jay and Phillip separate after the incident leaving it quite ok for them to see one another. I mean, Jay never did hate him, he just wasn't fond of the guy and how he acted. He knocked on the wood paneling of the door before him and waited for either one of his friends to answer. After a few moments, it was Art that welcomed him. 'Jay, dude, wow.' It wasn't exactly the response he was looking for but since when was Jay Terror picky. 'Wasn't expecting you.' 'Shit happens.' He had a cigarette hanging out of his lips. 'Phil here?' 'That depends.' 'Come on, we're cool.' 'To you maybe,' he paused, 'he's still a bit shaken up by how things went down.' Jay nodded and took a drag from the 27. He pulled it away from his lips and puffed a ring out. 'I gotcha'.' 'I mean, what the hell happened to you?' He was like the rest of the world trying to figure out what had happened in those few moments between Jay and Phillip. Jay wasn't sure what the big deal over it was. 'I don't know, he just pissed me off.' 'What'd the police say?' Jay shook his head and grunted, 'They sent me to a shrink. The bitch is trying to tell me I have anger problems and that I need her help.' 'Aye.' 'See if Phillip is ok to see me.' He asked. Art started to walk away but stopped. 'You going to apologize or something?' Jay took a last drag from his cigarette and tossed it at Art's shirt. He brushed at it frantically and opened his eyes wide. 'Look, I just want to sort things out with him. Plus I hear I clocked him real nice in the nose.' 'Just wait there.' Art disappeared and Jay was still standing outside. He felt like a dog being told to stay. Last week they were busy clowning around running the streets like they owned the world. Now he was stuck outside of his childhood friend’s apartment like some damn obedient dog. Life's messed up. He found another cigarette from the box in his pocket and lit the end with his Zippo. The first drag made him want to die and move on to his afterlife. He found a seat on the railing behind him and made his backside a nice comfy spot. It took another three minutes for Art to come back. Phillip was nowhere to be seen still. 'Didn't want to come in?' 'I didn't know I was allowed.' Art sighed, 'Look, I don't hate you Jay. I'm just confused. You scared us last week. What am I supposed to do?' Jay sucked in the toxins from his cigarette and ashed it on the sidewalk by his feet. 'You're supposed to act like a brother and say that things are cool. I didn't kill anyone or sell anyone down the river. Phil pissed me off and I showed him that I wasn't down for his act.' 'It just went by so quick.' Jay was sick of hearing it, 'Does he want to see me or not?' 'He's not feeling it man. I told you, he's shaken still and doesn't know what your intentions are as of yet.' Art tried to explain the situation on behalf of their friend but it didn't compare. Jay didn't need subtitles and captions, he needed straight answers from the person he wanted to talk to. Not his translator. 'My intentions? I want to talk to my buddy Art. I don't want to clip him or break his nose off. I just want to talk to him and get things straight.' 'Maybe next week we can try...' Jay shook his head and swallowed the smoke as it came toward his face with the wind. 'Maybe next week you guys can find a new leader. Nurse the dude as much as you want, my conscience is cleared. I mean, I tried right?' 'Jay,' He started. 'Come on man.' 'Maybe I'll see you around. Tell the runt I said hey.' And with that he walked back down the concrete steps and onto the sidewalk. A trail of smoke followed him and Art took in the last bit he could through his nose. Bandits for life? What a sham. __________ With it being his only option, he will step into battle again as the underdog. Like his first day of combat, he's fresh and his future is full of possibilities. The only real question is what do you make of all the chances you have at hand? Can you fight your hardest and show everyone that you're better than a rookie jumping into a new field? Sure you can. But it's work. You have to stomp on people to climb the ladder and you need to make examples of them just to show you're serious. Is the top too high for an example though? Depends on who you ask. __End
