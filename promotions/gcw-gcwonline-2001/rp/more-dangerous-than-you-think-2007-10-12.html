More Dangerous Than You Think

12 October 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10-12-07 "Can I help you with something?" Ray Holland stared over at the man across from him, standing there with a broom in one hand and the other on his hip. "I was told to come here and meet a . . . Benny Minor?" "He's not here right now," the man replied, as he gave Ray a quick once over. "Did you have an appointment or something?" "Nah," Ray answered his question quickly, then added a quick wave of his hand to further dismiss the question. "I should've been here earlier, but I got lost. Damn internet maps aren't worth the paper they're printed on." "Tell me about it," the man laughed. "Anything I can help you with?" "Well, someone gave me the address here and told me to look up Minor about training me a bit." The old man nods as he rested the broom against the wall. "Yeah, he used to be considered one of the best tag team wrestlers to step foot in the ring. Well, before the accident; he was never the same after that." "I'm not getting into a tag team, old man," Ray corrected the man, not wanting him to get the wrong impression. "I'm supposed to be a singles competitor." "Supposed to be," the old man asked with a quizzical smirk adding to his wrinkled exterior. "What do you mean, supposed to be?" "Well," Ray began, "I've been given this contract to wrestle for a company called GCW, but I don't have any experience when it comes to technical wrestling. I mean, I can brawl with the best of 'em, but just last week this guy put my arm behind my back and gave it a bit of pressure . . . Man, I could've sworn he was going to break it." The old man slapped his knee and laughed. "Ain't that funny, old man." He could tell Ray was getting a bit upset, so he quickly defused the situation. "I'm not laughing at you, son. I just find it funny that a promotion like GCW would sign someone without much experience is all." "I wondered the same thing," Ray responded with a nod. "But hey, the inks dry and the money's good." "Well, Benny won't be back tonight." Ray let out a sigh. "You have somewhere you can bunk, or you need to find a place?" "I'll find a place," Ray answered with a shrug of his shoulders. "What time will he be in tomorrow?" "Try next week." "Next week?" Ray was a bit surprised at the old man's answer. "Benny went on vacation, his daughter's getting married." "Son of a bitch," Ray mumbled under his breath. "I have a big match coming up, how am I-" "I tell you what," the old man walked over to stand beside Ray, and draped an arm across his shoulders. "You come back here tomorrow and I'll get you started on some basic moves. How does that sound?" "Like I'm going to have to bring you a jar of BenGay." "Oh, don't you worry about me none," he stepped back and rolled his shoulders. "I still got a little gas left in the tank." Ray just smiled politely, it's not like he had any other choice. Anything else would have been a waste of time. ~{ o o }~ Ray's never been the kind of guy to draw attention to himself, choosing instead to blend in to the background. He always felt that showiness seemed like a last ditch effort for someone who knew they couldn't measure up to the task ahead. Ray didn't need a barrel of monkeys or dancing bears to get his point across, and there was no doubt he wouldn't be changing his mindset anytime soon. He would never be considered the typical modern day wrestler, considering the 6-pack gut that hangs over his jeans and the lack of definition on his body. So why even bother? What possesses the man to hop into the ring and leave a trial of battered bodies in his wake? Is it childhood trauma? Perhaps this is the revenge of the outcast teenager who sat around in his basement drinking beer, smoking weed, and listening to Megadeth. The rage, hate, and pain that remained bottled up for so long, festering like a gangrenous sore, finally exposed to the light with an explosion of bloodshed. Maybe he just likes to hurt people. Slumped against a brick wall, a battered black hoodie and tattered jeans his apparel of choice for the evening, Ray takes a few minutes to give his opponent's a taste of what is to come. "I did some research and found out that the goal in a battle royal is to be the last one standing. The sole survivor gets the prize, the cash, whatever. Now, I did a little more research into this Dangerous Games match coming up, and I start reading all about who people think are going to have a good chance at pulling this thing out. Now, I could care less what anyone has to say about me, because I don't think I could wrestle my way out of a wet paper sack if it already had a side torn off. But to see the people that the fan boys are picking to win this shindig, you'd think that guys like myself, Bryan Mayhem, and Shaman weren't even signed up for it. I mean, Storm, Valentine, and Terror are names that keep popping up like Pop Tarts." There is a pause, probably a thoughtful one if we could see more of Holland’s face than his chin and the outlines of his lips. Lips that are curled slightly, a bit of a sneer on his face. Time for the truth. "I don’t even want the damn thing. When most wrestlers say they don’t give a shit about titles and championships, it’s a load of bullshit they spout to make themselves feel better about losing, but I’ve been in this sport for a couple of weeks and I already hate all these so called champions that prance around like they're the Lord's freaking gift to the ring. It’s nice to be paid for doing something you’d probably be doing anyway. Most of you don’t know me. The common opinion of me seems to be that I’m some redneck with a lucky punch. You'd pretty much be right, except for me being a redneck. I have no wrestling skill to speak of, so I rely on brawling, using weapons, and general cheap bullshit to get myself over and pick up wins. I have no business being in such a reserved environment. Blah blah blah, yadda yadda, you get the fuckin? point. This is professional wrestling, damn it. Not a freak show. I, being one of the also-rans, am going to go toe-to-toe with some of GCW’s best and brightest also-rans in a battle royal to see who's going to be the next person to challenge the champ. That’s awfully severe, I think. After all, for a lot of us, this is our first chance at achieving something semi-notable out of our GCW careers. Perhaps this is the beginning of a long and arduous comeback to the sport for others, but fuck them. I’m speaking for me now." Ray pushed the hood back slowly, and he rested his nearly shaved head back against the cold brick wall. "If I win, I win. I won’t say it’ll surprise me to win, because it won’t. Battle royals have as much to do with luck as they do with skill; you pay attention to one man too long and someone’s already dumped you out of the ring while you weren’t looking. The individual accomplishments, or lack thereof, of the other men in the ring don’t really mean anything at this point." Holland breathed out a slow sigh, a scarred hand rose to rub at his forehead. Most people weren’t carrying around that many scars, especially not ones as deep as the ones etched into the flesh of Ray Holland. Those thick hands were pitted with tiny scars from years of fiddling around underneath the hood of old cars, and no doubt the rest of him showed as much surface damage as his exposed flesh. "A shot like this can make or break a man’s career. If things play out right, you’ve got a new superstar on your hands. It’s a story ready-made for the TV movie world; man overcomes adversity and his own ignorance to capture prestige and acclaim, or man achieves too much too soon and crashes hard beneath the weight of his own over-inflated expectations. I have one thing to say to that . . . who gives a fuck? I don’t want to make a fucking movie, I want to grab a hold of the so called future of GCW and knock them the hell out! The fanboys can line up and kiss my ass when I’m dead and gone; the last thing I want now is for people to say I’ve hit my peak, not when I’ve just stepped into the damn door. I have too much time to think this is the best it is going to get, and I'll be damned if I become just another name that Ambrosi, White, and Caldera don't think they have to remember." Ray smiled slowly, his lips curling into a thick, smirking grin. If it wasn’t for the look in his eyes, that grin could almost be called amusement, even friendly jesting. But those eyes are dead serious, and just as cold as steel. "No one thinks I have a chance at wining this, and considering the odds, I can't say I blame them. They don't know me, they don't know what I'm capable of. That blind arrogance may not be the thing that catapults me to victory, but it sure as hell is going to be what makes them stop and take a double take to find out who the hell just knocked them on their ass. Everyone in that ring that steps across from me is going to know my name, even if for a brief moment, and that's all I expect from this match. All I need is for people to know that when their name is scribbled across mine on that marquee, they are going to be in for one hell of a fight. That is my success. That, is my victory."
