Talked into One's Own Burdens...

undated

Date: WW116 The early morning sunlight kisses the top of the lake, turning it into an orange glow. Not even the slightest breeze stirs the air. Every object lay dormant under the spell of the early hours or their slow waking is too subtle to disturb the perfect atmosphere. The lake looks as if it were made of glass, so smooth that if you were to slide an ice cube across it, it would travel on forever. Nothing can ever remain perfect.... An object of steel soars through the sky, the early morning light glistening off of the tip before it cascades into the still lake, shattering the stillness with infinite ripples of disturbance. The rope settles at a relaxed angle. The world attempts to return to its euphoric state, but the rope stiffens and begins to move around. The steady pace of the movement sends nearly perfect ripples through the lake in a continuous fashion, until the steel anchor surfaces, slightly gliding half in, half out of the water. The anchor raises out of the water and its hook catches underneath the wooden dock. The rope slackens slightly and then tightens once again as the anchor is lifted onto the top of the dock where Zacharia Taylor holds the dripping rope in his hands. He lifts the anchor in his left hand and reaches backwards before heaving the heavy object back into the lake. He quickly drops to the ground, legs out in front of him and pulls it back in, hand over hand. The sun begins to rise higher, expressing much more of a yellow light instead of an orange glow. Taylor stands up again, pulls the anchor up to his greedy hand and heaves it out once more. Lather, Rinse, Dry, Repeat. By the time the sun has fully risen into a hot mid morning light, Taylor is stripped down to just shorts. His shoulders, arms, and back bulge with every drag on the rope and sweat beads fall off at a steady pace. Once the anchor is in, he slams the dulling tip against the piling, holding it into place. He reaches behind him on another piling for his now sweating gallon of water. He take a couple quick swigs of it before tossing it into the row boat tied up next to him. Taylor takes a very deep breath before launching himself into the boat with an agenda. He grabs a hold of the two oars and begins to row backwards across the lake... His kitchen lacks none of the necessities. He reaches for a skillet and throws several eggs onto it and one strip of bacon. Attempting to multitask, he grabs two slices of wheat bread and throws them into a toaster oven. After turning a few dials, he turns around and grabs a carton of Egg Whites off of the counter and begins to pour them onto the skillet, mixing them in with the whole eggs already cooking. The eggs smell mediocre, but the smell from the bacon is intoxicating. Taylor reaches into a big cabinet above the sink for a plate as something outside catches his eye. He squints his 20/20 eyes at the neighboring house towards the front door and notices the banister is broken. Frozen in shock, he nearly burns his left hand which is resting on the toaster oven. The growing throb breaks his focus, causing him to nearly drop the plate in his right hand into the sink, but he catches it. He peers out the window once more to verify and then looks down at the skillet. He tosses the eggs around before scooping them onto the plate. The bacon is thrown on the side of the plate and he sits down at his table. Taylor cracks his neck and begins to massage his shoulder before bowing his head in prayer. The room becomes quiet and smokey...smokey? SHIT THE TOAST! He jumps up at the sight of the smokey toaster oven with the now black remnants of toast on the main tray. Taylor pulls out the plug and opens the door, sending billows of smoke into the room. He jumps for the towel, almost routinely and begins to fan the smoke alarm. His quick reaction doesn't save him from the loud whine of the old smoke alarm above him. Taylor slowly lowers his toweled hand in defeat and hangs his head, but not in prayer, in embarrassment. After finishing his nearly ruined breakfast, he looks out the window to see the mail truck pulling off down the road. Taylor has been only getting his mail every other day or so because of the very few articles he's actually received, but it is Monday, and GCW should be beckoning... Still walking around in his training shorts, Taylor makes his way for the mailbox next to the street. A flyer sticking out of the closed mailbox catches his attention. Attention all Wrestlers! As many of us know, the Salem Wrestling Team has started its Summer Training for all incoming Freshmen, Sophomores, Juniors, and Seniors! Sign up at the main office in the High School for the last couple weeks of the training camp! The next open session is August 16th, don't miss it! Taylor takes the flyer from the mailbox and opens the rest of the mail. The first letter is from Mr. Calhoun. The first couple lines are about moving in, the next about how it was an honor for Zacharia Taylor to take him up on the offer of the three houses...The next letter was from Verizon. Taylor glances up and down the letter but only remembers a few phrases in big red letters: "America's Largest and Most Reliable Network" and "Can You Hear Me Now?". The last letter, and always the biggest, though the Verizon one was nearly the same size, was from GCW. The reason why Monday was so important, especially for Taylor, was that ring assignments were given out on Monday. Taylor never stays around after the World Wides or Pay-Per-Views, where the assignments are usually given, so Taylor gets his mailed out to him on Monday. Had he had a phone, it would be called in, but he doesn't have one of them either. The deals for Verizon phones weren't bad... Taylor opens the letter with a leisurely haste and takes out the first letter, congratulating him on the win over Mark in the Dark, yet he knows that the fake signature on the bottom of the letter from Mr. Caldera shows all what the letter is about; niceties in the most false way. The second letter shows the location of the next show in Baltimore, Maryland and assignments for dressing rooms in the arena. The third piece of paper is the most important; who he is facing. Taylor's hands begin to tremble and his mouth becomes instantly dry. New sweat takes the place of the sweat from the morning and Taylor nervously runs his free hand through his hair. He quickly fumbles with the three letters from GCW and rereads them to make sure they were addressed to the right person and the assignments were right. After reading them over several times, Taylor freezes at the middle of the page. The big, bold letters, centered on the page. The two names secluded in the center... Zacharia Taylor vs. Chris Bagwell A hurried, yet delayed intense feeling of intense hate and fear rushes into his head. First, it begins to pound in the back of his head, but then it presses up on the sinus cavity, making his eyes hurt. Then, the very name of Bagwell runs through his breath and vocal cords. The pain of the past and present boil his blood to uncontrollable pressure levels. His heart in turn begins to struggle and beat fast and hard. Taylor nearly collapses to the ground as he grabs his chest. Both the words FINALLY and NO struggle in his chest under the intense pressure of his heart. Finally, eyes beginning to tear, he turns towards the house and makes his way into the kitchen... Taylor comes out of the shed next to the house and comes out with a heavy bag tossed over his shoulder. He walks over to the edge of the house next to a perfectly straight tree without any low branches. He tosses the bag to the ground and begins to hop on the tips of his toes. He warms up by throwing his arms out and about and interchanging between quick bounces and high ones. Right as he goes into the air for another high bounce, he lets his feet go out to the side and falls onto the heavy bag in an elbow drop. He gets back to his feet and does the same thing again. After several in a row, his eyes go blank, but his body still goes through the routine... Why would Caldera throw me up against Bagwell? Surely it's not going to be a fair fight! After the events at the last couple shows, not to mention Fisher behind my every move. The Establishment is going to step in for sure. But, why Bagwell? Why now? I mean, sure, we have a history and it's going to make a hell of a show, but why now? Why not at a Pay-Per-View? Why would Chris agree to it? I'm sure he had to! Chris doesn't just fight anymore, he is very selective over his matches... Is this just a play to get me out of the ring? Is this going to be a last stand? Am I the next victim like Terror or B and E? If this is it, I need to be on top of Everything! Everything...I need to be on top of everything.... Taylor shakes his head and snaps out of his trance. He is on top of the bag, elbow still fiercely on into it. As he stands back up, he lifts the bag up with him and places it on his shoulder. Once at the top, he pauses for a moment and then slams it to the ground in front of him. After doing it two more times he throws the bag off to the side and makes for the shed again. He grabs a bag hanging on the wall which is much bigger than the previous one. This bag has a blue band around the top and bottom and is made out of stretched canvas. A bag weighing in at around 200 pounds. Nearly the weight of Chris Bagwell.... Taylor tosses it down onto the ground where the other one was. A small dust cloud flies out from under the bag. Taylor takes a deep breath and continues with the exercise of suplexes. Hanging his hands above his head, sweat bullets cascading off the tips of his hair, Taylor paces around the small workout area trying to regain his energy. He makes his way over to a small cut tree stump and begins to drink a red substance in a gallon jug of water. Some of it leaks out of his mouth and streams down the stressed muscles in his chest and stomach. He takes several deep breaths before his breathing steadies enough to continue his workout. Taylor repositions the bag perpendicular with the straight tree near the house and steadies himself. Taking in a soothing breath he runs at the tree, places one boot about knee high up on the tree, then another boot right above it. Taylor kicks off with a third step and curves backwards in a moonsault and lands perfectly on the bag without much misstep. Unlike in the ring, there is no opponent, and now awe of commentary or reaction from the crowd. All there is is the cool air off the lake and the bag in place to do it again. So he does... As the sun begins to fall West and starts to go down, Taylor finds himself propped up against a tree with a towel around his head catching the sweat and shielding against the sun and an empty gallon jug next to him. Out of sheer exhaustion, Taylor refuses to move or care to move. His chest inflates and deflates at a safe, regular pace. The atmosphere is as still and perfect as it was nearly twelve hours ago, except for the hurried footsteps passing near the house. Footsteps that Taylor misses... It isn't until a door slams off in the distance does Taylor look up from under his towel and look around. He looks for the birth of the sound but finds nothing. Right before he goes to close his eyes once more, something catches his attention near the front of the neighboring house. With sweat in his eyes, it is hard to make out what it is, but it looks like a person running from the porch. Taylor jumps up, but his lazy muscles in his legs prevent him to do much of anything. He makes it to the house, but nobody is there. He uses the towel to wipe away his eyes, but nothing appears. Whatever it was, whoever it was, they are gone, but the feeling of being watched continues... Zacharia's boat cuts through the placid water as a duck would on a pond. Each stroke of the row is nothing short of mechanic, propelling the boat forward flawlessly, much unlike the first two weeks Zacharia attempted. He takes the same route he always does, through the lily pads, past the thick trees and towards the swamp, but there is something different about his route. Due to all of the rain in the last week, the water level has risen and the swamp looks nothing more than a shallow wooded pond. Taylor checks the sky before angling his boat through the trees. After ten minutes of maneuvering and delicate strokes through the water, Zacharia comes upon something very much so unexpected; a church. Intrigued, he looks around for a place to dock and makes his way up the small soft hill to the base of the church. From the distance, the church looked dirty and small, but it isn't until Taylor gets closer does he realize that the church is badly damaged and decaying. By the look of the barren trees, it seems as if the area was hit with a wildfire, charring everything in its path, including the church. Behind the charred wood lies the remains to a ministry that seems to be long forgotten. A dirt road seems to be untouched and the sad excuse for a gravel road looks to not even have one stone unturned. As Taylor makes his way into the church he feels much more at home than he could ever have imagined in this small Salem town. Extreme shadows are cast along the remaining walls, yet the ground is oddly lit. Patchy holes in the ceiling from the fire provide a dusty light where an endless steam of fine particles fall to the church's ground. Small rays of light reach the alter, or what is left of it. A mass table lays cracked in half and badly burnt and the cross that once stood tall now hangs lopsided and upturned looking more like a sideways "t" than a religious cross half on the ground. Both of the alter candles remain part of the church, completely melted in long streams of thick grayish hair encasing the stand. The alter, destroyed as it may be, seems to still hold a sort of religious prestige in Taylor's eyes. Walking over to one of the four remaining pews, Taylor's foot goes through the floor up to his ankle before he hits the cement base for the building. Taylor is finally broken from the spell the church provides and looks down at his foot in the cracked floor. He very slowly pulls it out without splintered injury and continues to the pew. Once he sits down, nothing is the same... Someone clears their throat behind him and he quickly turns around, nearly petrified to see who has followed him all this way. Much to his surprise, nobody is behind him. He looks back at the alter and sees a man in all black laying on the broken table. His head rests on the side of the one half and his feet hang over the side as if sitting the wrong way on a large family room chair. Chills creep down his neck like small termites looking for a place to hide. For the first time in as long as Taylor can remember, he is terrified. Instead of approaching the alter and moving the black cloth away from the person's face, which was his first reaction to do, Taylor puts his head between his cupped hands and begins to pray, or plead, for everything to go right. The louder he pleads and the more he rocks back and forth in temptation, the darker the church grows and the louder the silence sounds. Finally, the silence is so deafening that Taylor's ears begin to hurt and a cold sweat begins to slide through his skin. It becomes too much and he breaks out of his protective arms and begins to yell in agony. He looks up at the alter, rocking back and forth with adrenaline spiking, and sees everything he never expected to see... Nothing... The church is exactly what it was, charred and alone, just like Taylor. The alter lays destroyed and burnt as it did before and the light begins to grow more broad as the sun rises to the peak of its arc. Taylor looks around in a very disturbed way, tears still streaming out of the corners of his eyes. He quickly looks all around him and then focuses on the empty alter. Slowly, he sits down onto the dirty floor and holds himself stern with a hand on the burn pew. A ripple of water at the bank of the lake breaks the erie silence and makes him quickly react and tense up in the direction of the lake. He lets out a slow, stuttering breath and rubs away the tears under his eyes with his right hand, smearing soot across his bare cheek bones. Trying to steady his breathing, he sits up as straight as he can, stretching his diaphragm but every time he looks up at the alter, he sees the shrouded body. The church seems to calm hit, yet also plague him with memories and images of the past, yet like a car accident, he can't move away from it... Navigating the lake under the twilight is much more difficult than daylight, but once out of the swamp it is a straight shot to his house. Completely relaxed and spiritually enlightened, Taylor steadily makes his way for his dock in the distance. Rowing in the reversed fashion, Taylor looks straight ahead as he gets closer to the dock. Thoughts of putting a light on his dock or at least on the outside of his house cross his mind, but it is only playful banter to occupy his mind before getting home. Instead of tying his boat up to the dock, he pushes the rows for a final thrust to make it onto the bank of his property. The readjusting of rocks and displacement of mud fill the air for a matter of seconds as Taylor gets out of the boat and drags it up onto the grass. He tosses his water aside at the very moment that a car door slams and tires chirp from in front of the house. Taylor sprints as fast as he can into the front yard of his house but the car is long gone by the time he reaches the front. With all haste he gets to the front door to see that it is still open. He gets in and looks around quickly to see the front window broken and one of the kitchen chair upturned. Why can't I just be left alone... "What you speak of sounds much more like an evil spirit more than a confused one..." The town priest, Father Jackson goes on, "sometimes, the Tempter gets a hold on us and makes us see evil things that drive the victims into insanity. With everything you have experienced and seen, it just seems that you need to cleanse your body of this evil.." Taylor looks down at his scarred hands and flips them over to see the burn scar of the cross. His head begins to spin with all the memories of the cleansing he has done. It doesn't seem to make sense that he himself would be a vessel of evil. Then again, maybe Father Jackson would have a different perspective on the whole matter if Taylor told him EVERYTHING that has happened to him. "Then, Father, what do you think I should do?" "What you see is evil manifested in flesh. These images are man, no doubt about it, but they are not real. You make them real. You make feel and look real, but they are not. They are just evil manifested in flesh. Your flesh...Your flesh holds this evil...." Taylor looks down at his scarred skin and feels it crawl and hurt. He begins to scratch at it, making it feel like his skin is resisting him. He suddenly feels dirty and unworthy. He feels...different. The words of Father Jackson fall into the background noise with the passing of cars on main street and the chatter of people in the back of the church. His words fall onto now deaf ears and his words are replaced with the ever clear sermons of Father Emanuel. Taylor flips over his hands continuously, staring into the pink or ivory white scar tissue thinking of all the many sermons about pain and evil. My son, pain is weakness leaving the body, yes, indeed. It is something that the Marines held onto even with separation of church and state. Pain is weakness, therefore you must be strong. But, we must not forget blood. Blood is held below the membrane of flesh. Flesh holds in the weakness and evilness that we are trying to expunge. The flesh can be the evil we are searching to destroy. Therefore, if you were to disfigure or destroy the body, there is a chance the evilness will leave with it. However, there is always an exception, and that is for the truly pure and innocent which hold the capacity for evil but are not evil. This makes the damage of their flesh a sin for it inflicts evil. Do you understand what I am asking of you, Taylor? The last words of Father Emanuel resonate though Taylor's mind. Do you understand what I am asking of you, Taylor? It isn't before Father Jackson moves his shoulder does Taylor respond to Father Emanuel's question. "Yes, Father, I understand." Father Jackson smiles with accomplishment and dominion. "Now let us pray, my son..." Father Emanuel kneels onto the knee pad in front of the pew and begins to pray out loud, but the words mean nothing to Taylor, for it is not his prayers. Taylor follows in kneeling, but keeps his eyes open and stares onto the cross, asking for his own forgiveness and blessings in his own way. "Amen" ****** Thunder rattles the joints of the house, sending creeks and brittle noises of snapping throughout the house. The rain sounds more like an angry hive of bees after a bear storm's their home for honey. The bee-like rain pummels the boards of the house and shingles of the roof, trying to gain access to the warmth within. The rain has a hushed sound against the family room with the plywood board covering the shattered front window. In front of the window lays Taylor reading "Paradise Lost" by Milton under lamplight. On the adjacent couch lays Christian, also reading, but he sheds a different light on the devil, reading "Good Omens" by Neil Gaiman with a sinister smile put on his face, casually looking over at Taylor at every page turn. The next thunder bolt crashes loud, shaking the foundation of the house and putting a small amount of spackle dust onto Taylor's book. He casually wipes it off and finishes the paragraph before getting to his feet and making his way to the refrigerator. The journey isn't a long one, but the walls feel elongated and the fridge feels like it is forever away. Another crash of thunder or two later and Taylor opens up the fridge door and reaches in for a bottle of Vitamin Water and closes the door, revealing Christian sitting on the counter in front of the kitchen bay windows overlooking the dark lake. Lightning strikes the lake off in the distance casting long, bright shadows around Christian... "You know, I've been thinking about this living arrangement and I think that we can consolidate..." His wry smile plagues his face while Taylor looks on in disgruntlement. "Yeah, I mean, we don't need so much junk around here, like, for instance, that table next to the door.." "It's for mail and the phone book..." "For the little mail you get and the phone you don't have?" Beginning to enjoy himself, he opens up the draw directly under him, the draw slightly going through his leg. Noticing this, he moves subtly. "What about all this shit?" He holds up a whisk, "is this really necessary?" Taylor lunges forward and pulls the whisk out of his hands and tosses it into the draw, sending a few items inside into the air and out of the drawer. "Now look what you did? Can't even keep the damn utensils in the draw! Come on numb nuts...oh, what do we have here?" As Taylor is quickly gathering everything from the ground, thunder cracks again and he slowly raises up to meet what Christian is holding in his hand; a potato peeler. Taylor eyes it up and down and then quickly looks up into Christian's eyes. "You know, Taylor, Father Emanuel would want you to do it. He always wanted you to help yourself instead of going for help. I mean, only he would send you out into the middle of the night to work out your issues on an innocent stranger..." Taylor looks at him without uttering a word, Christian is doing all the talking for him... "Come on, you know you should. Do as good Father Emanuel would want you to do..." There is a slight silence that is quickly broken by more thunder and more creaking of joints in the house. Taylor eyes the potato peeler and Christian slowly puts it into his hand, smiling uncontrollably like a child that deceived his friend into thinking something outrageous. Taylor looks at the rogue knife in his hand in a look of near disbelief. He remains mindless at every enunciation Christian makes. So...Christian keeps talking... "To think, all you need to do is cut through some flesh and you will get what you truly desire, but then again, won't things kinda be lonely without me? I mean, really? Do you want a party for two or a single for lunch? Huh? Huh?" Christian laughs, but Taylor puts the blade of the potato peeler to his arm. He presses it flat down, cutting into Taylor's outer arm. Christian silences himself but can hardly contain himself until he can no longer take the anticipation... "Go ahead..." Christian's voice grows dark and commanding, "Carve me out of your skin..." Taylor pulls back on the blade slightly as fresh blood pours from the knife. Tears already begin to flow from his eyes and he stops, but keeps a firm grip on the kitchen utensil. "Oh, come on. You need to do it faster and harder. I mean, I think a couple hairs are gone from my 5 o'clock shadow, but, yup, still fully in tact. I mean, why not just cut your arm off, I think that'll work." Christian's laugh penetrates his ears like daggers flying through the air. Taylor begins to take quick, deep breaths. Every breath quiets Christian more and more before finally... "UUUUHHHHHHHHHH..." Saliva pushed between tightly clenched teeth with great force and yelps of pain mix in the evening air. Christian's laugh disappears with the next crack of thunder, leaving a bleeding, wounded Zacharia Taylor on his knees, peeler on the ground next to him. He crawls to the counter and grabs for his kitchen towel and presses it against the eight inch gash on his arm. Taylor falls backwards onto his back, taking in deep breaths and clenching his arm trying to suppress the pain. The towel quickly soaks through as he moves about on the ground. His grip never loosens to stunt the flow, and his eyes become dilated over the course of a couple minutes. He takes a couple deep breaths and steadies his heart. He resists the temptation of closing his eyes and blinks away the tiredness. Blinking to keep his eyes open. Blinking to keep his eyes from closing... His eyes blink several times to adjust to the light outside. He reaches forward and grabs a hold of his toes and bends his head to his knee. After a couple seconds of stretching he stands up and takes off his sweatshirt and tosses it against his mailbox. He looks at his arm, heavily gauzed up and taped with surgical tape found in a cheap first aid kit he took from the last event at World Wide. The wound stopped bleeding many hours ago, but his arm remains extremely tender. The only way to get into town to buy more stuff is to run, so that is what he is doing...running. Taylor sprints forward to the sidewalk at the edge of the street and then steadies his pace to a jog, not to get his heart beating too vigorously. Every five minutes or so he reaches over to the gauze and presses it gently to see if any new blood saturates the top layer of gauze, but after a half an hour, nothing. Taylor used to pack his wounds with salt all the time when he was in Clifton to avoid going to the hospital, and usually it worked. It seems that it has worked for him once again, but he isn't chancing it any longer and turns onto main street and heads for the CVS at the intersection. The trip into the store is quick. "Where do you keep medical supplies?" Then a simple point to isle seven. A quick grab of a couple things off the shelf, the slip of a twenty, and change of $3.21. Taylor takes the white plastic bag and heads out the door for the most popular eatery in town, Fred's Diner. By the time he gets to sit down it is nearly noon. The diner has the regulars and some friends and family for lunch, but Taylor barely recognizes anyone. He is seated in a booth next to the window, but in direct view of the front door. A waitress named Peggy comes over to the table and fills his empty cup of coffee with some really dark and mediocre temperature coffee that smells more like chocolate and hands him a menu. Taylor barely looks at it, with more concern on his arm. He suddenly realizes that he is sitting in the diner without a shirt and goes to excuse himself but Peggy is already back to take his order. "Hey, I didn't want to say nothin' but, uh, ya kinda need a shirt to..." "Yes, I just realized, I'm sorry I'll just be off then..." "Ah, I understand. I can have Larry give you one of his Chef coats if ya like? I mean, ya gotta have some breakfast or lunch, right?" "Yeah," Taylor nervously laughs as he realizes everyone in the diner is looking at him. "I'd greatly appreciate that..." Peggy comes over and gives him a Chef coat that barely fits him once buttoned, so he takes Peggy's suggestion in leaving it open and trying to sit straight up instead of slouched. You would have thought she was an expert at dressing men in wrong sized clothing, but then again, you never know. I'm sure she has no idea she is waiting on a Wrestling superstar that has killed more men than years she has been alive for. That is the plus side to living somewhere where nobody knows you... "Food?" "Yeah, can I have the pork roll and eggs, whites please, and wheat toast without butter..." "Taylor ham n' whites with dry wheat? Ya got it..." Peggy walks away as Taylor attention is caught at the front door. Four people walk in, around the age of college students, but the one guy with them looks oddly familiar. Taylor stares right at the guy as he walks down the isle, not paying any attention to Taylor, but sits only three booths away, so Taylor gets a good look at him before he sits. The guy is only about five foot six but stocky. He is in really good shape actually. His arm makes the short sleeves fit pretty tight and his back shows definition though the shirt. Maybe from Bagwell's gym? The guy takes off his Gaspari Nutrition hat and puts it down on the booth behind him, messing up his dark brown hair. Taylor takes a sip from his room temperature coffee and looks over at the guy again, who is now looking at Taylor, but not in a "I think I know that guy look" but in a much more "He doesn't fit in here" look. This guy was definitely not from around here by the way he dresses, but from where? The guy in the booth hits the girl next to him in the arm and points over in Taylor's way when Peggy comes sliding in from nowhere and slides a white plate with blue and red checkers in front of him. The egg whites are mounding on the plate with three thinly sliced pieces of pork role still sizzling from the grill. Taylor nods in appreciation and looks back over at the booth, but the table no longer pays any attention to him. Taylor closes his eyes with hands folded and says a short prayer to himself and then begins to eat. Peggy comes back with a check in her hand once Taylor is finished eating, only leaving a few burnt pieces of pork roll left on the plate with a dollop of ketchup. Taylor pulls out two fives and goes to give the bill to Peggy but she's already gone. Taylor looks up and sees someone go up to the register with a bill like his own and pays, so Taylor throws a five on the table and takes the other five and the bill and gets up. As Taylor approaches the table where the guy is sitting, a knife slides off and the table laughs as the guy bends over to pick it up and Taylor hits him. "I'm sorry, I didn't mean to..." "Nah, it's ok. It's cool..." They both stare at one another for a moment and the guy reaches up and pats Taylor on the back and sits back on the booth. Taylor turns around after taking a couple steps and sees the guy looking at him in the mirror behind his friend's head in the booth. Taylor quickly gets to the register, tosses over the money, check, and chef coat and walks out with CVS bag in hand. Taylor jogs down the street when he comes across a park bench to sit down on and redress his arm. Taking off the surgical tape was the least painful part, but taking the gauze off as it sticks to open parts of the wound, now that was painful. Taylor uses the gauze to wipe away any excess salt still in his wound and then rubs in an antiseptic and liquid band-aid. Once clean and sealed, he puts a new thing of gauze over it and new surgical tape before anyone can pass by and notice what he's done. Taylor takes the rest of the gauze, antiseptic and liquid band-aid and puts it into his pockets before jogging off again, tossing the used materials into the trash can beside the bench. Running through town has a relaxed feeling for most, especially in a quiet town like Salem, but not for Taylor. Every couple minutes the feeling of being watched plagues Taylor. If it isn't a car that slows down as it passes him or someone looking out the window at him its the memory of broken windows, banisters, and chirping tires. He has every right to be concerned, but not everyone is out to get him, just a select few. But the question is why. As Taylor turns the bend he looks to his left and sees a gun shop with a man outside of it in what looks like priest garbs. Taylor notices a Gatorade machine outside of the shop so he decides to stop. "Ya know, sometimes it takes a lot more than just your fists to serve justice unto those that deserve it." The man that looked to be in priest garbs looks up at Taylor and tips his hat to him as he presses the "Frost" button on the machine.
