Hero and the Anti-Hero

October 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October (DG) Prologue How do you decide if someone is good or not? Classifications of man have been divided up all through history, but where do you draw the lines? Ovid came up with four stages of men, but Hesiod came up with five, with different names. Ovid describes men in terms of metal. The first age of men, or the Golden Age was a time of peace and love where people lived off of the land without planting or plough in the forever season of Spring, and no confrontation of any kind. The Silver age ushered in the four seasons so people took shelter in homes and had to harvest food, but it was still peaceful. The Bronze age ushered in great warfare, but the men were still good. It is not until the final age, the Iron age, let the floodgates open and all forces of evil took control of the good men. Morality became less apparent and brute force was the way of the land, setting up kingdoms and boundaries. Hesiod puts the Heroic age before the Iron age, where the men fought at Troy and were Gods of war and masters of the art of war. So who s to say we are in the Iron age of cruelty? A hero is not concept that we deal with so much anymore. A police officer who saves a hostage is a hero. A firefighter that saves someone from a burning building is a hero. A soldier on the front lines fighting for the freedoms of his people is a hero. But what about the men of God? What about the boy who stands up to a bully? What about the kid that says no to drugs? Do they not satisfy the requirements of being heroic? All take bravery and have loss involved. Why should we classify is declassify these people as heroes? The man that fights evil would be a hero and of good blood and mind back in the days of Ovid and Hesiod, but today, he is just as much evil in he actions he takes. Anger is anger, deception is deception, murder is murder. Stepping over the lines and falling into the traps set by the evil cause the mind to go out of the norm. The torture that the individual goes through as a balancing act creates more confusion and more pain. Heroes are not the same as they used to be because society has changed the ideals of a hero. The one that stands up against evil and puts his life on the line in the name of his faith is no longer a hero, but an anti-hero. You feel for the executioner, but you do not touch his hand. He is no longer human, but an animal. _________________________________________________________________________________ Chapter 1 The sun is just going down over the highest peak of the church’s steeple.
