Second Heartbeat

Date: A long time ago... Second Heartbeat Even a sugar high can’t numb away all of a vibrant young woman’s problems. Even with a strong heart and will to survive, pressure and pain find ways through everyone’s soul eventually. Most wounds heal; others linger and create smaller but far more persistent annoyances. Smiling through it can only work for so long. Young Mindy was still trying to learn that, but the way it came about didn’t leave her much time to process what was happening around her that caused it all in the first place. Mindy almost didn’t want to wake up that morning; having to visit the doctor routinely wasn’t something easy to get used to for someone who- though physically underwhelming- was rarely sick or in poor health. “It’s not like bein” a spitfire like yourself ain’t gonna come with consequences,” Michael always told her. Of course she always knew in the back of her mind that injuries were part of her line of work, but never once did she believe that they would ever set her back the way a herniated disk and a torn ACL would. The thought of being in so much pain that a simple energy drink couldn’t fix it was like blasphemy to her. Ten months of rehab didn’t change those feelings. Color her clueless, however, when the doctor said to her, “Well, Mindy, I can say with certainty that you’re ready to return to training.” That was that. No jubilant cheers of triumph, no ticker tape parade had been scheduled. Her cousin Michael, who to his credit had been helping her through the problems of what had been an all-around terrible year, had waited outside to her the news on how she was recovering. Aside from him, though, no one really cared about her well being- let alone any other family members. From the way Mindy sulked out of the doctor’s office, Michael almost expected her to tell him that there had been another setback with her recovery process. “Oh geez, what’s got you so gloomy this time?” he asked; something he couldn’t believe he’d ever have to question Mindy, of all people with. Mindy’s response left him even more confused than he was before she lifted her head to answer. “Mm” Oh, the doc said I’m all better. I can continue training if I want.” “And yet you look like someone drove a dune buggy into your crotch” why?” Mindy just gave a faint shrug, not even phased by her cousin’s vulgarity; then again after hanging around him long enough, she’d grown numb of it to begin with. “I dunno, Mikey,” she said. “I’m ready to go home now, though.” Michael stood up from that uncomfortable “cushioned” chair he was sitting in and stretched his arms over his head. “Nngh, sure thing, cuz. I’ll take ya home.” He had already started walking toward the glass double doors to head back to the car when the last sentence he expected to hear had stopped him. “I mean back to Verona.” Fortunately Michael wasn’t drinking anything at that moment as he stumbled over his words. “Wait, what?” He spun around, staring at his cousin in disbelief. “What the hell did you just say” You can’t be serious. Are you serious?” He would wait for a response that never came, the young woman having cast her eyes down at the tiled floor and rendered unable to look up at her cousin to acknowledge his question. “Yanno what,” he said finally, “I don’t even need to hear you talk about it in here. Just get in the car.” Michael held the door open for Mindy, who took a deep breath and wandered out in front of him. He watched as Mindy shielded her eyes from the sun as though she were a vampire, afraid that someone would recognize her and see her so sad. Michael said nothing on the way to the car, opening the passenger door for her and letting her in before he joined her behind the wheel. Having no idea what she was thinking that would make her want to go back to Italy, of all places, he didn’t feel as though anything he had to say would be of much assistance. ~ “Mikey?” the saddened woman called. Michael turned his head briefly from the road to look over at Mindy who hadn’t even glanced back at him, eyes focused on the passing scenery outside the window. “Why do I still hurt?” Michael snorted. “What am I all of a sudden, a fuckin” psychologist?” He chuckled a bit to himself, but got a bit more serious before he risked upsetting Mindy further. “Really, I don’t think I got any answer you’d wanna hear. Shit, I always hurt. We always hurt. You’ve been wrestling and competing in MMA for four years. Consider yourself lucky, Mindy.” “It’s not just the injuries,” Mindy answered. “What is it, then?” Michael questioned, to only get more silence. “For fuck’s sake, what?” When Mindy finally responded, it was with a question of her own. “What do you think my mom and dad have been thinking for the last ten months?” she asked, finally turning towards Michael, who didn’t seem all too pleased. “The fuck do you care about what they think now, eh?” “I don’t know.” “Fuckin” A right, you don’t,” Michael said with a sigh. His hatred for his aunt and uncle and their treatment of not only Mindy but the entire family in general was far from a secret. “Look, you’ve been through the shitter, “ight” I get that. Markus leavin? and gettin? hurt right after? that ain’t fun, I know.” “Don’t bring Markus up, please?” Mindy requested. It wasn’t an angry, forceful command; even if she was angry, she didn’t seem like she had the energy to expend on it. One day you’re gonna get hurt and I won’t be able to do a thing about it, and I don’t ever wanna be put in that position. I’m sorry. Mindy, you just don’t seem to care about anything involving us anymore. Of course, still being able to hear his voice in her head didn’t help matters any. That, and worrying about your safety, it puts as much stress on me as it does for you, but it’s all on your word when the worrying stops. The sad thing is that I don’t think you’re ever going to care enough to let competing go, ever. Maybe someday I’ll be closer to you if I believe. I’m sorry, but I just can’t wait that long for the time to come. “I don’t want to talk about that.” “Right” sorry. What do you wanna talk about then?” Mindy seemed pretty unsure of that herself for a moment before she answered. “Don’t you have a fight next week, Mikey?” “Yeah, so?” “You should be concentratin” on that anyways, shouldn’t you?” “Me, actually concentrate on fightin?”” Michael snickered to himself. “Shit” course I should, you and I know that I don’t really care about what I should and shouldn’t do.” They both locked eyes for a moment. Mindy cracked a smile. “You’re right, you don’t.” “You know what we really should do though?” Mindy tilted her head. “Mm” What’s that?” “Get you a gun.” Mindy blinked, her pretty green eyes conveying a look of confusion. “A gun?” “If you’re serious about goin” back to Italy, yeah, I’d say you should arm yourself. As good a way as any to defend yourself from overprotective parents, eh?” Mindy wasn’t sure what to make of that statement. “You’re kidding, aren’t you?” “Kidding?” Michael almost looked hurt by such an accusation. “Read my lips” look at this face.” At that point Michael put on the most serious demeanor he could pull out of his features, but even so he still looked awfully comical doing it. “I’m as serious as a heart attack.” “That’s pretty serious,” Mindy gulped. “But uh” how d’ya plan on goin? about gettin? me a gun?” “I’m glad ya asked.” ~ “Twelve-gauge awto lohduh.” The somewhat stout man behind the counter gave Mindy an odd look before he slowly went over to the shotgun that the little lady pointed out. “You sure you wanna hold this thing” Looks bigger than you.” Upon not getting an answer, the store owner gave in and handed the weapon over anyhow. Mindy tried her best to hold it up but eventually settled for holding it at her hip instead. “You can go pump or auto with that,” the gun owner continued, “but I gotta say, I’m not sure a gal like you would be able to handle the recoil on it.” It didn’t take long before Mindy had enough and hoisted the gun up over the counter to hand it back. Still, she didn’t waste time before pointing to another gun. “Fohtee-five longsight, wit” lazuh sighting.” The man retrieved the gun from the glass case, holding it out to Mindy with a bit of a giddy grin. “That one’s brand new, it just came in. That’s a good gun. You set the laser on your target wherever you want the bullet to go.” Mindy seemed in too much awe simply by just how shiny it was to care about any lasers. That, plus the fact that she was actually able to hold the gun up somewhat properly made her more than happy. “Anythin” else, sweetheart?” asked the man, returning the pistol to its case just in time to hear the next request? “Phased plasma rifle in a fohtee watt range?” Quickly, the man raised his hands and waved them defensively. “Hey, just what ya see, gal!” Mindy almost looked heartbroken, but never the less continued naming weapons. “Oozee nine meelameetuh?” The gun store owner grabbed the weapon and hesitantly allowed Mindy to hold it. “For a little girl like you, you sure know your weaponry. Any one of those choices is ideal for home defense. But anyway” which will it be?” “All,” Michael would cut in, having been watching inconspicuously from a corner of the store while reading a magazine. He retained the same absurdly Austrian accent that Mindy had done a horrible job of mimicking herself. Still, it was enough to make Mindy turn around and look over at her cousin. “Uh,” she stammered, “why’re we doin” this again?” Michael just gave her a blank look in return. “Because it’s funny?” ~ “That was a blast, wasn’t it?” the elder of the two asked, offering a grin Mindy’s way. She didn’t seem all too impressed. “At the time, sure.” Michael grunted. “What would fix it then?” he questioned, leaning forward in his seat on the couch. Across from him was his cousin who stood in the kitchen, staring into the refrigerator. He’d ask again. “What?” Mindy took her time to reply, finally huffing in defeat. “There’s no more pop in here.” “Well shit, I better run and get some, don’t want you bumming the fuck out and all,” Michael responded, rolling his eyes. Mindy settled for some chocolate milk and wandered out into the living room, sitting on the arm of the couch with a sigh. “I just dunno what to do wif myself.” “What do you wanna do?” Michael asked. Mindy paused again. She had most of the morning to think about such a question, but the answer continued to hide from recognition in her brain. Michael stared up at her with as much patience as he could muster, which at that point was not much. "Did you used to have people worry about you gettin' hurt, Mikey?" Michael sighed, but he decided it best to answer the question without argument. "Why do you think Jaden left?" He leaned back on the couch and kicked his feet up on the coffee table in front of him. "It's natural to worry about the safety of someone you care about, what ain't natural is tryin' to stop them from doin' it so long as they make a good livin' out of it." Mindy thought about that for a second. "So... if you became a manwhore, ya wouldn't want me to st-" "Whoa," her cousin immediately cut in, "whoa whoa whoa. Okay, that, Mindy, is grossly fucking inappropriate. And secondly, if that was what I wanted... well look at me. I'm a grown fuckin' man. I'd like to think I can do as I please, y'know? But that's just it. I have the common sense to want to be paid for beating the shit out of other people, not for giving someone a rusty trombone in a back alley for eleventy-nine cents." "...eleventy-nine?" "...you have more chocolate milk?" Michael promptly stood up and brushed past Mindy to go into the kitchen, fetching the same drink from the fridge that his cousin grabbed earlier. "As I was sayin', though," Michael continued, "there's a diff'rence between worryin'... and interferin' with someone's lifestyle." He paused long enough to concentrate on the glass he was pouring himself, before putting the carton back where he got it from. "Trust me, for every one fighter there's two random schmoes out there worryin' for their safety. And if yer not either of those types of people, yer most likely the overprotective kind who thinks the shit we do is barbaric and that this ain't the right way to make a livin'." Mindy nodded silently as she drank and listened to Michael talk, watching him take his drink back to the living room as he took a sip from it, leaving a bit of chocolate milk above his upper lip and soaking into his stubbly moustache. "I know what I am, and if I wasn't a fighter I'd be some meaningless jackass at the local corner store. I do what I do 'cause I'm fuckin' good at it and there ain't nobody who can tell me otherwise. Point bein'... and this is some old fashioned corny shit I'm about to say but I don't give a fuck... those who mind don't matter, those who matter..." "...don't mind," Mindy finished. Michael nodded in approval and set his drink down on the coffee table when Mindy raised a finger to gesture out to him. "You have a moustache." Michael turned completely towards her. "No shit." Mindy sighed and grabbed a tissue from the box also on the table, reaching forward to wipe the childish man's face. "No shit," she grumbled in the best way that she could. Her mousy voice wasn't built for grumbling. Michael noticed the chocolate milk there and coughed. "Ahem... heh." "You silly goose." "Guilty as charged!" Michael rolled his eyes and slumped back down into his seat. "You really don't plan on goin' back to Italy, do you?" Mindy had glanced over her shoulder, having wandered back into the kitchen to throw the tissue out and put her now empty glass of milk in the sink. "Dun really see why it'd be a bad thing," she said, rinsing the glass out. "Really don't think you're ready to deal with that shitstorm quite yet," Michael said. "If you plan on dealin' with your parents you'd prolly get injured again... like... break a twat bone or somethin'." "Mikey!" his cousin pouted, to which he threw his arms up in the air before moving to grab his own glass of chocolate milk to drink more. "I'm serious! God, you don't need me to tell you how much of a... total dickhead your dad is, you came all the way out here because of it to begin with, remember?" That part rendered Mindy silent again. "I'm just sayin'," Mike continued, "if you need to go back there, do it when you're sure you're healthy again. Not when yer all emo and shit." There was a brief silence with the two of them exchanging glances, before Michael finally shrugged a shoulder. "You've been askin' for my advice, I might as well give ya all I have right now." "Of course," Mindy answered quickly, "after all, yer advice is the best I'll ever get." "Fuckin' A right," Michael stated with mock pride. "Seriously, though... I don't think you should go- not right now anyway." "What do you think I should do, then?" Michael shrugged. "You're an adult, you should figure that out for yourself. Course I think you should get back to trainin' now that you're all healthy physically, but 'ey... I ain't your father, so..." He trailed off again for a second, dismissively waving a hand, "do whatever you feel like doin'." Mindy nodded in understanding. Perhaps training was the right course of action, after all; it would act as stress relief for having spent the last ten months with limited movement and action, and it would take her mind off of the bothersome things clouding her brain. At the same time, however, she just couldn't go right back to thinking about mixed martial arts... not after suffering three embarrassing (to her, anyway) losses- the last of which causing the ACL damage that had put her out of action. "I think I need somethin' else," she said, "somethin' a little... easier, I think. At least, easier fer me." Michael tilted his head. "I don't follow." "Well, you know... what if I uh... wanted to wrassle again?" she asked sheepishly, causing Michael to guffaw. "You want something easier so you want to train for pro wrestlin' again?" "Hey, I said easier for me. Just because there's more risk involved... I dunno, it always came easier when I could jump off a cage instead of not bein' allowed to grab onto it." In what was a rare occurrence for her at the time, Mindy let out a childish little giggle; that kind of giggle most people wouldn't dare think came from someone who was ever successful at professional wrestling. "Well hey, ain't ever been anythin' wrong with that," Michael said as he stroked his stubbled chin in thought. "Where would ya be lookin' to go, though? Didn't think ya wanted to go anywhere other than Global." Mindy nodded. "Yeah, but back then Global was still around. So... meh. I dunno." "I hear you." Michael went silent a bit, joining his cousin in thought of the situation, before he finally reached out to the table; this time, he grabbed the remote control and turned on the flat screen television in front of them. "Well, can always see where the competition is on TV at least." On that note, he began flipping through channels. "Read a book! Read a book! Read a muh'(beep) book!" Click. "Darnell... you are NOT the father!" Click. "You can be my wing man anytime!" "Bullshit... you can be mine!" "Okay..." Michael grunted, "maybe now's not a good time to check." Click. "I need to hit the gym, anyways." Standing up, Michael turned and flicked the remote control over to Mindy to caught it out of the air. "Tell me how the job hunt goes, eh?" "Uh huhs." Mindy smiled a bit easier and waved to her cousin as he headed for the front door. Briefly, he stopped and turned his head. "Yer gonna be alright, yeah?" Mindy nodded. "I'll do my best." Michael seemed content enough with that response and had reached out to open the front door. He couldn't even get one foot outside before Mindy closed the distance between them and gave him a big hug. "Thanks for all yer help," she managed to get out without getting teary-eyed. Michael quirked a grin and gave Mindy a pat on her pigtail adorned head. "Don't mention it." Just like that, he was gone. ~ The days after that had gone by fairly slow. She had started running again, getting her cardio back up to where it was before being sidelined. It was an arduous time for her, but Mindy was at least grateful to feel as though as she could do something about it. In the end, the only hard part was thinking of just where she was going to go. Several days had gone by of keeping an eye on the television and news online to try and make a decision on what company would be best to get in touch with. Almost a week had passed without any sort of idea to lead her one way or another. It was a random Global Championship Wrestling late one night that got her attention. Not even the show in general, but a certain face on the show that she had remembered somewhere before. "Vivica..." Vivica J. Valentine- an old rival of hers who was now apparently hitting it big. That really wasn't the part that piqued Mindy's interest... but the fact that she could hear the crowd's universally positive reaction to her was what did it. The same Vivica from Dies Irae? Really? Her run-ins with that former stable were few and far in between, but each one was memorable to her. While never having faced Vivica in an actual match, either, there hadn't exactly been any love loss between the two of them. Oh, how times changed. "GCW then? Sure about that?" Mindy couldn't resist talking to Michael right away and getting his opinion on the situation as they spoke over the phone. "At least there's one familiar face there, eh?" she reasoned. "Well yeah, but is that a good thing or a bad thing?" "Not sure yet. Right now it's jus' a thing." "Right... well. One reason to go there is better than no reason to join anywhere else. Only real question left now I guess is whether or not you're feelin' healthy enough." "Uh huh uh huh. Now or never an' all that jazz, eh?" "Fair enough." Michael paused for a second. "But uh... that mean a trip to see the 'rents is outta the question for now?" Mindy nodded as she spoke into the phone. "Jus' fer now," she answered, "'til I get mah head on completely straight." "But yer good enough to wrestle, eh?" "Like I said, Mikey, wrestlin' is easy fer me. Bein' face to face wif mah mom and dad... not so much." Mindy paused a moment. "I still wanna see'em, but now jus' ain't the time." "I understand." "Besides," Mindy said, "with wrestlin' I already have one advantage over the opposition." "What's that?" "Well... ya remember when Danny Glover gets brave in the end and runs onto the Predator ship?" "Yeah...?" "I'm Danny Glover right now. With boobs."
