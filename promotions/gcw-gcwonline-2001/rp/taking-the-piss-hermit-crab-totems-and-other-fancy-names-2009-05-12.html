Taking the Piss: Hermit Crab Totems and Other Fancy Names

12 May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 12, 2009 He's drunk, he's not as drunk as he's ever been and maybe he just believes that out of hope; but he is drunk and only halfway to anything and Leslie is hours gone, taxi catered and apartment delivered, and he is taking his time to draw a jagged line from the first there to the next, where next is walking to a store or walking to his building and it's each step an argument over the proper direction to either that drags his feet, that gives them the time to decide as he shifts himself to fortyfive degrees, bracing continually against cement façades and bricked walls and chains linked, and he is swaying between this and that, rebounding from lampposts to trash cans and propelling himself as a function of momentum, as the absence of will that lights a cigarette under gold night and the drifting smoke that leads him along, twisted straggler, the unrepentant and the irredeemable, sweating himself away, humming medleys and avoiding eye contact with the cars that run in processions intermittent, that cordon him off in his own head, where he was born a doubting man and in which his years spent still haven't shown him the value of rocks and it's another thing to fling himself into, to lower his crown towards and to charge. He recreates his city in miniature, he makes it with grids and grass and wire wound tight weaving through shadows that never let up--his city is always night because he doesn't remember what it looks like under day, and he exists only between the lines that define its form. He makes himself a cage and he paints himself a victim and it's all his brain is giving him as the war for eqilibrium continues in battles lost and battles won against the ghosts of casualties left behind to become problems for another. Head hung low, he has an infinity of words that he keeps on the inside for fear of vomiting his soul in chunks against the cracked sidewalk, that he keeps silent for fear of listeners. He brushes it off, he brushes it off as another "whatever" and he remakes himself paper steel, reforms with a rigid spine and the idea of balance, if not the mastery, in his stomp. He knows who he is, he knows goddamned well who he is, and he is in love with himself and he is in apathy with the majority of what's left in his world. He trims. He sweats away toxins and he's starting to remember sacrificing fondly as he listens to a barely loud female pack cigarettes in a passing Jeep; he imagines other lives, he imagines towers of outcomes that descend in glamor as he approaches the idea of a bottom line of complacency, tries to divide his existence down to the simplest set that leaves him happy, nothing but happy, and eons from enlightenment. Most of all, most of all it's that none of them leave him as a B-list, at best, celebrity spiraling down the drains of an ugly sport, too coddled by the life he fell into to ever be inclined towards responsibility. So, he's mostly unaware of the corporeal and the edificial and he's lost even his own rhythm under amber lamps and in the rushing lights of traffic that always seems to sneak up on him from behind, to leave him watching a cast shadow that he can never fill; the night swallows each whole, leaves him with shades that trail or drift, dwarfed beneath his feet. Everpresent. Regarded rarely. He has his men in suits and they no longer have to follow him to be at his back, to join the other spirits on their perches. More of Now as a result of Then, without meddling, without human interference; it's that he won't accept causality as purpose. It's that or it's that he's trashed out the frame, that there's not enough blood to dilute the alcohol or enough stress to ruin the high. He clutches a post and spins around it and it's the first time in his life he's channeled Astaire; even it ends with him collapsed on a bench, rubbing at his forehead with one hand and playing chopsticks with the other. He closes his eyes and he listens to rain beating cascade against the roof of the shelter; easing back, he tries to make himself comfortable and he tries to hide from weather and he never qite succeeds, Frank Black. He never qite succeeds. His gut is in his throat and he barely notes it; he just stifles coughs to keep it from rising higher and he sucks menthol to keep the coughs infreqent with no reasoning spent on the eventual damage. This isn't his biggest mistake, if there's any single one that takes that superlative, and the comfort he finds in that grows with every passing day--but flowers grow the same as weeds and neither is at home in the city. All he has is to sit, calm, and let each minute drag him a little deeper down in the hopes that each hour will see him rising; it's junkie dreams, it's one of those endless variations on the flavor of addiction. It's finding self in a heap and hoping it's autonomous enough to stand up and let all the dirt away without needing to consciously intervene. It's a stomach rotating and a head just shy of bolted down. It's his undeclared fiancée drinking chilled wine in his apartment, watching an old movie on satellite television and it's the invisible line that leads him to her again, again, again, with no new convictions, no reasons beyond those he'd formed a halfdecade, a decade before. It's everything. Everything, everything, everything crammed into his skull. It's cracked from hemisphere to hemisphere and his brain is and has been leaking out and he has lost the means to contain that which rests idle in it; where the means consists solely of caring, where what rests is the faster road to public condemnation. He has two words for them but he doesn't expect that they ever listen and he, at least, holds those in his heart, because he has not lost his awareness of it: he keeps it in his ribs and he does not let it wander unsupervised. He decides that everything is a matter of scale. He decides that kings would be ringleaders if they lost the mass approval, he chooses against the innate and he laughs at fame fleeting away. He throws a burning filter into the street, sends it twisting through the crashing sheets and he's still laughing as it rolls into the gutter and washes away. His finger sqeaks; he's dragging it across the plexiglas over some symbol or phrase scarred illegible in the surface and he's drawing in grease and oil and sweat the stolen spirals of his spirit animal, his hermit crab totem. Stragglers in the alley behind him, falling into congregation, throw whispers that spiderweb among them as they huddle tight in the dry angle of buildings. Frank isn't aware enough of their presence to let them slip to nevermind; Frank isn't particularly aware of anything beyond humid creep when he sees the first of them rounding the corner. She moves by herself, falling against the bench and turning to him with these green eyes that pierce, just pierce. "Youre new." She slides in next to him, her head lost under hood, her face drifting the boundaries of shadow. He just shrugs. "You got a name, stranger?" "Jordan." "I like that. Im Krystal. With a K." "Jordan with a G." "Oooh. I havent met a Jordan with a G before." "Aint very common." "Is that like. Gordan?" "Nah. Like Jordan." She's no longer turned to him. He looks over his shoulders as five or seven or ten shuffling surround the bus stop, going to orange in streaks as the few that room allows file underneath the roof and it's standing room only that bunches him tight into the corner. The girl has her back pressed against him and he loses her voice, after some measure of effort, in a mass of hushed conversation and electric air. He tries to hide and he becomes, with more freqency, the subject of their hushed sentences and he thinks that he should stand and ask them how much they would pay to watch a leper dance. It passes and he fights to drag his crumpled box of cigarettes to the surface, to free another and to bury his company under smoke. Hard stares and mock coughs; he sucks the filter and the cherry is born uneven and grows further out of control with every drag. Impatience, spite. He thinks about conspiracy, some mob kidnapping, until he remembers the sheer unimportance of who he is and, leaving fear, he lets slack his spine, he slouches and he rests. Worried, yeah; he's worried but it's not the fatal kind. It's the curious, the confused. He's not the only one that needs to clear his throat.
