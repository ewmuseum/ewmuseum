A Window to the Present

27 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/27/10 It was 1997. Giles was younger, then, and he drank less because he barely had enough money to eat. At 22, he was living from one paycheck to the next, with little more than the clothes on his back. A very generous coworker of his, Frank, had offered Giles a place to stay on the couch of Frank's one-bedroom apartment. He didn't know it, yet, but Giles and Frank would go on to become best friends until Frank's death thirteen years later. In April of '97, they had only been friends for a few months because Giles had just moved to New York from a rural city in Manitoba called Brandon. Giles wouldn't admit it, but, he was having a hard time adjusting to the change; his decision to move was risky at best, foolish at worst. Only once had Frankie asked him why he moved to New York. Part of the truth is that Giles felt suffocated in a rural city; another related part of it is that Giles had no future there. His Dad, who owned a chain that sold agricultural implements, was one of the city's richest and most powerful men. It followed logically that Giles would grow up and be integrated into the family business; in that time and place, that was just how things were. Being introduced as a “Giles” in Brandon was both a burden and a boon; it meant that he always already had a foot in the door but it also meant that everyone, even those who should have known better, assumed that he was going to be the future owner of Giles Equipment. Many twenty year olds would have traded places with Giles any day of the week; he had his career mapped out for him before he could even walk. Giles didn't blame them for thinking that not having to make such a huge decision on your own was desirable because there weren't a whole lot of opportunities in the city back then. But, for Travis Giles, having an arranged career was orthopaedic. It allowed him to stand, but, on the other hand, it was crippling. He remembered the looks he would get when he told people that he wasn't going to take Economics or Business Admin at the local University. Instead, he would end up in New York with a cancelled credit card, a bag of clothes, a bus pass, and a sleeping bag. When he left Brandon he had a romantic, On-The-Road-inspired idea that he would remove himself from material possessions and lead an ascetic life. It had been working, so far, but he also had to rely on other people a lot more, too: for a place to sleep and rides, mostly. When he woke up on a Saturday morning after a night out, it was drizzling outside. It was early – about 5 AM – and he was in a bed that he hadn't slept in until the night before. It was quiet, here, compared to his usual living arrangements; a radio was on in the bathroom and the rain patted gently against the window. Curled up against Giles's shoulder was a woman, a year younger than him, who looked remarkably cute while she slept; her dark brown hair was spread across her cheek and Giles's bare chest. Her name was Natalie, a girl that Giles had seen, every day since he arrived in the city, at the coffee shop below Frank's apartment. After making up any excuse to go down and get coffee, he eventually did catch her eye. She looked; she smiled. They talked. They laughed about how Giles could barely afford the bus to work in the morning but he spent over ten dollars a day on coffee. He said that it didn't seem like that much, to him, when he was being served by such a stunning waitress. She blushed. Thinking about last night, Giles optimistically grinned, something he hadn't done in months. When you spend all day hoping that your roommate makes extra food so that you can eat his leftovers, there's very little to be optimistic about. Without much education or any trade skills, there were very few opportunities for employment. Still, he liked the gym that he worked at; the pay was minimal, but the work was rewarding. But Travis still felt as though there was something missing; something important. Maybe he just felt disposable and wanted to feel needed by someone. It's not an uncommon impulse, at 22, to think that there must be more to life than This. Whatever This is is arbitrary, of course; for every person, This is different. This as in Giles's personal narrative, the thing that he had been contemplating in the days before he picked up his belongings, left a cushy life in Brandon and moved to an unfamiliar city without much to his name. But, for all the glam and excitement that New York City had afforded, there was still that fundamental lack. Maybe he wanted Natalie to fill that gap. Thirteen years later, he would look back and realize that yes, he definitely wanted Natalie to fill that gap and he wanted her to do it without getting anything in return. Thirteen years later, though, he would be a little more mature and responsible. In '97, as he looked around 21-year-old-Natalie's apartment, he wouldn't be above reaching for his package of cigarettes and lighting one up, right there in bed, without asking if it was alright. It felt natural, though; it felt right. And that's how Giles, at 22, did things: on impulse. Give him an inch and he'd have taken a mile. Natalie awoke and brushed the hair from her face. She rubbed her eyes and noticed what her bedmate was doing. As she looked at him, coyly, she casually said, “I never knew you smoked.” She looked even more fond of him at that moment, as if he had done something to confirm Natalie's choice to bring him back to her apartment. “Only the morning after,” Giles returned, coolly. Even though he was acting like one, his ladies' man act was just that: playful and ironic. In Giles's twenties there was probably a little too much play and a little too much irony for him to be taken seriously by someone as cultured and educated as Natalie. She believed in things and Giles didn't. Things like social institutions and values – the kind of stuff that makes society work. Having just departed from “order,” Giles was interested in disorder and risks – like trying to seduce a waitress he had only met a week ago. It worked, but, it wouldn't last forever, at least not in the way that either of them hoped. Partly because Natalie, though younger, was mature enough and ready for something more involved than what Giles was ready for. Giles wanted it, too, but his own instability made him feel insecure and inadequate; this affected him, and his future relationships, in a profound way. Natalie yawned, then closed her eyes, mumbling something about going back to sleep. A few minutes later Giles had finished his cigarette and Natalie was lightly snoozing. Quietly, Giles got out of the bed and started to wander around the apartment. The night before, he didn't have time to take in Natalie's living arrangement; though he instantly dreaded it afterwards, it didn't take long for him to wish that he had. There were beautiful paintings hanging from the walls and a huge shelf of books with titles that Giles wasn't familiar with. The top shelf seemed to be dedicated to particular hardcovers. Beloved. Wuthering Heights. Pride and Prejudice. Tender is the Night. Giles was in awe, but it also made him nervous. The paintings, the books. He could hardly remember the last time he had read one. It was as if this wonderful girl represented a level of sophistication that him and his bag of clothes and futon bed could never reach. * Natalie did have an appreciation for art, but she was not arrogant about it in the way that Giles feared. Being modest, though, sometimes made it difficult to make small talk at the coffee shop she worked at. At 21, she was working toward a degree in English Literature and working part-time to pay for living and tuition. When customers asked about her present education and future career, she always had a difficult time responding because she feared that they would think that she thought herself better than them. She did not, of course, think that, but she knew that that didn't stop other people from thinking that. Before she became pregnant with Giles's future daughter, Jessica, she aspired to go to graduate school and teach at a University. That was the plan, anyway, until her dream was no longer feasible with a child on the way and an on-and-off boyfriend that could barely keep a steady job. She was at work and it was the day after her first night with Giles. It wasn't particularly busy. It wasn't busy enough, anyway, to prevent her from thinking about him and how things had turned out that morning. She had awoke some hours later and he was gone. He had had the nerve, though, to leave a note on her kitchen counter: it was a long and involved letter about how he wasn't good enough for her and she would be better off to just forget that he existed. Natalie knew that this ... this boy was well-meaning but it still left a bitter taste in her mouth; the kind that just doesn't go away. The coffee shop where Natalie worked also hosted a collection of used books that were for sale. When she wasn't busy she loved to go back there and comb through the shelves; moving stuff around, flipping through different editions, and keeping her eye open for some gems that people had foolishly released from their possession. The book section was part of the reason why she worked there; her love of literature made it all the more worth it. She didn't mind that her recommendations were shot down for Danielle Steel and Nicholas Sparks, because every once in a while somebody with an open-mind would come in and buy something that Natalie loved. Her deepest desire was that the novels that had affected her would leave the same mark on the people that walked out of the store with a work of fiction that Natalie had chosen for them. To her, it was the best job in the world. Her co-worker and friend, Jamie, was helping Natalie clean tables when she said, “So, I saw you and that boy that comes in here all the time chatting last night ...” Jamie looked at her and smiled, “What's his name again? Tra --” Natalie blushed. “It's Travis, and yeah, we had a coffee together.” Jamie looked suspiciously at Natalie and waited for her to continue. Natalie smiled. “What? It was ... it was nice. He's a nice guy.” She stopped. “Very friendly.” Jamie continued cleaning the table. “Did you ...” “No!” Natalie shot back. “Well, not ... not really.” Jamie raised an eyebrow. “Okay,” Natalie conceded. “Yes, we did. Don't judge me, okay! I never go home with guys like that. You of all people know that.” Her friend laughed. “I'm not one to judge anyway ... but it doesn't matter. Good for you, girl. A little fun never hurt anybody, did it?” * It was nine months later and Natalie was in the hospital, about to give birth to Jessica Giles, 6 pounds. Travis was there, a little flustered and somewhat out of place because he did a lot of pacing and asked every ten seconds if he could do anything to help, but he was more in the way than anything and Natalie had never been more impatient with him at this moment. She had reason to be, of course, as she was trying to push a human out of her body, and Giles couldn't possibly understand the months of physical and emotional pain, discomfort, and inconvenience that he had caused for her. After it was all over and she was rested enough to sufficiently tolerate Giles, he entered her room. He was on his eighteenth cup of bad hospital coffee, but he had just gone to the washroom so he was ready for any extended version of “the talk” that they needed to have. “The talk” was about to enter its eighteenth remake. Out of character, Giles had also brought her a cup of water. She had one already, but the gesture was what counted, he figured, so he set it down on her bedside table before taking a seat on the chair that was next to her bed. “I know that we're not exactly on the best terms, Natalie,” Giles began. Natalie was looking the other direction. “And I know I haven't earned the right to say very much, either.” He took a sip of his awful coffee just to afford him some time to choose his words carefully. “But, I want you to know that I'm going to do everything I can to raise our daughter – and I want to do it together.” Natalie's eyes shifted toward him but her face was still expressionless. “Together?” She looked bewildered. “Haven't we been through enough? Haven't we given ourselves enough chances and fucked it up just as many times?” She was getting worked up, now. “It's exhausting, Giles. I'm through with it.” Giles didn't have a response and he didn't want to argue, but, he said, “Okay, I understand that. But, I really think we should just set all that aside and focus on being parents. We can still do that without the romantic element, right?” “What, you want me to move in or something?” Natalie looked at him in disbelief. “Haven't you had nine months to ask me that?” Giles cleared his throat, awkwardly. “Well, y'know, we just met nine mon--” “Yeah, and you also got me pregnant nine months ago!” Natalie interrupted. “I hate to burst your little bachelor bubble, but we don't exactly follow the normal couples' trajectory.” The sarcastic understatement was a punch line that Giles couldn't dodge. He got up from the chair and reached in to his pocket. He set a key on Natalie's bedside table and left the room without saying another word. * Present day It was a Saturday and it was the end of a very poor week for one Travis Giles. He had lost to “Normal” John Johnson and voluntarily missed the Road to Rampage. He wasn't answering his phone because he knew that GCW head office was trying to get a hold of him; he had a contractual obligation to appear on certain shows and he had failed to fulfil that contract. The few people that believed he could succeed in his new profession were beginning to doubt that he had what it would take to make his name known. He brushed aside criticism by saying that his loss was a result of bad timing; Frankie had just died and he was in the middle of another falling out with Natalie. This time, it wasn't a falling out from a romantic involvement; he wasn't sure what they were, but it seemed that now things weren't the same and it felt a lot like a falling out. As much as he hated the feeling, he also knew that his history with Natalie was too long and complex to be able to patch over and start fresh. Giles was only on his second drink at his bar of choice. It was a slow Saturday, and the bartender, Chris, was going to close early. Chris sat down across from Giles. “So, how are things?” Giles took a sip of his beer and shrugged. “Could be worse. Natalie and Jessica aren't happy with me, again, but I don't think I fucked up enough to not be able to make it up to them.” Chris nodded. “You have a good head on your shoulders, Travis, and you're a good Dad. Don't ever forget that.” Giles smiled modestly and raised his glass. “I'll drink to that.” And he did. “Thanks, Chris.” “No problem. You gonna be alright to drive home?” Giles thought about it. “Yeah, I think I can make it. Been a lot worse.” Giles's thoughts at the bar would continue into his drive home. He thought about what it would be like if he had never met Natalie, but he quickly pushed the thought out of his mind. By this point, she was so much a part of his life that he couldn't imagine it without her. And he didn't want to, either.
