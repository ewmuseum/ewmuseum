Decisions

21 October 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10-21-2007 A man in a dark suit enters the room. He’s carrying with him a briefcase. He stops next to a table and places the briefcase on the table as if it was made of the most fragile and expensive piece of ceramic ever made. The man is quick to put in the combination onto the locks of the briefcase and opens it. “Just like I promised.” the man said as he pulled out two packages that looked to have contained a white powder. “Do you mind?” another man said as he walked into view and pulls out a switchblade. The man cuts a small slit in the package and takes a little bit of the white powder out. He holds it up to his nose and inhales the powder. “100% pure Colombian. Not that garbage that other people bring you.” “Yeah, it’s good.” A phone can be heard ringing in the background as the scene just freezes. “Hello?” Apparently, this wasn’t live. We swing away from the TV as Dorian Ryan is seen walking into the bedroom. “Yeah, I got it ordered. It wasn’t easy to find that pipe prebent like that.” Talking about work. After all, even though he’s a pro wrestler, he still has to do something else with his time. “Well, next time he wants to get something like that, tell him that it’s going to add more time to the job. The only reason I didn’t have time to bend that exhaust is because our pipe bender is down. Even then, there are six other bikes that need custom bent exhausts that we were working on before he even ordered it.” Oh, he was pissed off. Getting reamed out over the phone because of someone else’s fuck up, Dorian didn’t like that. He just wanted to sit back and enjoy the DVD he rented. “You tell Mr. Henderson that he needs to get those parts in. I already have a guy that will be there to fix it, but we need to order the parts. Anthony, get the crap out of your ears and listen. You promised someone a custom rebuild and put it on me. I have three other bikes that I’m committed to. I have to be there this weekend to get one of them to Powder and Paint.” Dorian didn’t like talking about the shop when he was at home. “You call the customer and you tell him either he’s going to have to pay a little more than what you quoted him, or he’s going to have to wait a little longer.” Dorian walked out of the bedroom and put the cordless phone on the table next to his couch. He lay back down and grabbed his remote, playing the movie again and eating some popcorn. He doesn’t get too far into the movie when the phone rings again. “Hello.” A little less inquisitive this time, he knew it was the hospital as he pauses the movie for a second time. “That’s good. You think she might be able to handle a weekend away from there?” The look of disappointment comes to his face as he gets his answer. “Listen, all I want to do is bring her home, let her know that they world hasn’t ended, that everything is alright.” Dorian turns off the DVD Player and the TV. He sits up. “Yeah, I understand. Don’t mean that I like it. Thanks for calling and letting me know.” Another in a year’s worth of disappointments. He looks down at his coffee table, next to the popcorn and the remote, on top of a couple of magazines and under a glass of Mountain Dew, an envelope with the GCW Logo on it and his name typed neatly on it. “Maybe I should take a look at this.” Dorian lifted the glass and pulled the envelope out. A little over a week ago, he agreed to a two match deal with GCW. Dangerous Games and one more match to be determined. They paid him well for the two appearances. In the envelope was something he never thought that he would look at again. A contract with his name on it, that if he were to sign would bring him back into the GCW roster full time. It had been 10 months since he had officially been released by the company, taking time off to take care of his personal affairs. He thought about completely retiring. But now, he was looking at a way back into the world of GCW. They knew he was wrestling again. Dorian made appearances under his old guise of “Prime” over the last few months in the Minnesota independent scene. The money was right. Just enough to continue paying for Andrea’s treatment and live comfortably without having to worry about working at the shop anymore. Light schedule, something that Dorian had wanted to enjoy. Even in his previous tenures with GCW, his schedule also called for appearances on TV or at promotional events. Now he could pick and choose most of where he went. Obviously he impressed someone in CEI during his limited time in the Dangerous Games battle royal. Not only did they give him a title match for the very next show, but they did that without knowing if Dorian would still be there afterwards. Maybe they were confident that he would sign the contract. Or they were confident that Tyson DeBough would defeat Dorian. New wellness policy. This wasn’t in his old contract. He would read this later. Dorian looked through the paperwork, making sure that everything in there was satisfactory to him. He had a lawyer look at it yesterday, said that it was a very fair agreement, and was nearly in line with the last contract. He stood up and walked into the kitchen, still reading the papers. He pulled another glass out of the cupboard and then took a bottle of rum out of another cupboard. He filled the class halfway and sat at his kitchen table. He just couldn’t figure out why he would even contemplate returning to GCW. Most of the people that he associated there were either gone or wouldn’t be all that pleased to see him back. He knew that he wasn’t going to be well liked in a locker room that could very well say he abandoned. He didn’t know if it would be even possible for him to set foot in that locker room as a contracted employee of Global Championship Wrestling again. He was having his doubts. He looked hard at the paperwork. He didn’t know if what he was going to do was the right decision. The more he thought, the more he wanted to fill the glass all the way. At least if he made the decision completely wasted, he would have an excuse. He thought some more. Ciel, La Sombra, Eva, they were all gone. Tessa was still there, but there were probably hard feelings for how he left. Dorian looked at the bottom of the glass. He then thought about what the consequences would be if he won. If, after a year away from GCW, he managed to take the Television Title from Tyson DeBough. If he didn’t have a contract, Steven Caldera could just say that the title switch wasn’t valid, or worse, just strip the title from Dorian. What would Andrea think? She would tell Dorian to do what he thought was right in his heart. Not to let the past affect his judgment for his future. But all he cared about was her getting better. Her coming back and being the guiding light that brought him from his despair years ago when he lost Keiko and Dan. He needed that, more than she knew. He set the contract down and stood back up. He grasped the back of his neck trying to decide what the right thing for him to do. Does he return to GCW, or does he fade back into the shadows. Then he thought why he would be doing it. He walked over to a drawer and pulled out a chewed up Bic pen. He sat back at the table and flipped to the last page of the contract. He hesitated for a moment. Finally, he made his decision.
