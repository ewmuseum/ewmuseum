Andy & Andrew

11 July 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07/11/10 This is a story about two men. Andy Murray, 32, is a former GCW World Heavyweight Champion and one of the most popular athletes in the northern hemisphere. Andrew Murray, 22, is an overworked university student trying to balance an active lifestyle with a demanding writing hobby. * It was a Thursday when the card appeared on the website and Andrew was excited. Though he'd known his match's make-up for months, it was good to see “Cheap Labor and Tim Shipley vs. Chris Bagwell and Andy Murray”finally appear in-print. It was official, now. Rubber-stamped. Time to draw the line under ten weeks of writing with three of his favourite handlers in the promotion, but not before what would hopefully be a fiercely competitive battle on the roleplay board. Joe was the only question mark. A spate of unfortunate events had hit his personal life one after the other, and nobody had heard from him since WorldWide 131. Whether or not he'd have the time to load-up a word processor and compose a roleplay remained to be seen, but there would be no ill feelings if he couldn't. He'd made it quite clear the start of the feud that his spare time was at a premium, and though the prospect of a no-show loss may not have thrilled Joe's partner, there was always the Rampage Tournament to keep Hyde and Shipley occupied. But Andrew prayed for a two-on-two battle. Since losing the title he'd become more competitive than he'd ever admit in any IM window or message board post. Something about that loss to Rob had re-lit his ambition's wick. Losing in GCW, up until that point, had been completely alien to the young Scot. He'd joined in December 2007 and, to his amazement, nobody had been able to defeat him in a competitive match until over two years in his tenure. Plenty of fine writers had come incredibly close, and there had been more than a couple of occasions prior to the title change when he'd read an opponent's roleplay and questioned his ability to win, but he knew it was over as soon as he'd read Rob's piece. His record-breaking title reign would come to an end against an old friend: he knew it even before his own piece had hit the Interviews page, and he'd given himself plenty of time to make his peace. When the inevitable happened, a tired, weary former champion felt the weight of expectation disappear from his shoulders. His had been a gruelling reign. Nobody had held onto the belt for as long as Andy had, and though it had been an honour to represent GCW as it's World Champion, it was time for a well-earned break. The timing was perfect. Hyde had just joined and he and Shane had talked for months about writing the Murray/Bagwell relationships from a whole new angle. Soon Joe hopped on-board and the action was go. A fun, fast-paced feud with three creative, hard-working handlers without any of the shackles and responsibilities of main event handling. Just what the doctor ordered. The feud had flown by. Months passed like weeks and they were wrapping-up when it seemed like things were just getting started. It had been good for Andrew on so many levels:- 1. It had taken his character away from the spotlight for a few months. Though recapturing the belt was definitely on his 2010 “to-do” list, Andrew knew the consequences of another few months at the top of the pile could've destroyed him. Burn-out will do that to a man. 2. It was a chance to write with Shane again. Their feuds had never been the most thought-provoking of GCW storylines, but the guy was an absolute breeze to write with and one of the most co-operative handlers in the game. 3. Andrew and Hyde had never really written together. They'd known of each other's existence through AWC and Just for almost five years, yet they'd never put pen to paper and had their characters appear in the same place at the same time. The opportunity to collaborate with a handler he respected as much as Pierre was too good to turn down. 4. The appeal of competition returned. Andrew had been more competitive during the Rampage cycle than he had in a long time. Now he was writing roleplays on a bi-weekly basis again: the feeling reminded him of working through the Rampage field back in 2008. It was evident in the quality of his pieces towards his reign's end that Andrew was finding writing a turgid and arduous process, but the thrill had returned. Now it was PPV time and the battle would be stern. Hyde and Joe were both fine writers, but Andrew remained confident. Armed with the best story he'd composed in a while and steadily improving prose, Andrew was looking forward to testing his skills against two of the most under-rated handlers in the hobby. The hour was drawing late when he saw the card, but the cogs in his head were already grinding. Rampage was his event. In 2008 he defeated Chris Bagwell to win the Rampage Tournament. In 2009 he beat LeStatt Knight for the GCW World Heavyweight Championship. In 2010 he'd be responsible for opening the show and setting the tone for the rest of the evening. Despite a seven o' clock start, Andrew stayed awake until 4am that morning. He was excited again. * But it was Saturday before he had another chance to put any thought into what he was going to write. Friday was too busy a work day to even think about opening Notepad at his desk and he had plans for the evening. So, forced to break his own “no ewrestling at the weekend” rule, Andrew trudged into work at 8:30am, set-up his computer system, and opened a blank document. It was slow progress, though. Andrew worked the service desk alone on Saturdays and customers were buzzing around him with awkward enquiries and obscure ticket requests. Two hours in and all he'd accomplished was the briefest of outlines for an opening scene and a couple of half-baked conclusions. It wasn't an environment that lended itself to creativity and Andrew was struggling. Things improved as the day progressed. Soon his stock of bus tickets were selling out and the crowds thinned. Finally, just after lunch time, Andrew had a chance to power through half of his plan. Five scenes were plotted and five more would follow, but an elderly lady with a trivial complaint not worth filing (a bus arriving two minutes late or a driver accidentally tearing her ticket. Just some bullshit.) derailed his train of thought. A flurry customers follow and he found himself daydreaming after they'd dispersed. He took off his glasses and closed his eyes, tired after spending most of the day single-handedly performing a task made for two, and it was then that Andy decided to pay him a visit. “What the heck are you doing?” Andrew's creation asked a voice far softer and more articulate than his own. Andy's breaming blue eyes and perfect teeth reflected in Andrew's inner cortex and he woke-up. “Stop wasting your time. You've only got an hour left and there's no way you're writing tonight or tomorrow. Get to it!” Andrew grumbled and maximized Notepad. Andy was right: Saturday evening was reserved for drinking and socialising and Sunday was for friends and football. If he didn't finish his plan at work then it would be Monday before he'd have a chance to complete it, and Andrew hated leaving things so close to the deadline, especially for a pay-per-view. So he dragged himself through another section's worth of planning before the clock hit five and it was time to go home. He loaded an unfinished plan onto his USB stick and Andy cursed him for failing to finish. “How dare those selfish customers come to your desk and demand the services that Stagecoach pay you too much money to perform,” The Scottish King of Cool said in a voice laced with sarcasm. “Anyone would think you--” Thump. Andrew slammed the office door on his way out and left Andy behind. * It was the start of deadline week and Andy Murray was pissed. He waited for his bearded creator to wake before getting in his ear. “Look, man: this week's schedule is intense, but you can do this,” he said as Andrew sat-up and rubbed sleep from his eyes. A calmer, more patient Andy stood over Andrew as he got out of bed and grabbed a towel from over his chair. “You're probably not going to have a lot of time to yourself but we've been in this situation before.” “Go away,” Andy grumbled. It was 5:30am and his body was still half asleep. “Too early for this shit.” “You can't procrastinate like you did on Saturday. Do that and you'll no-show. Need I remind you about Hyde's stance on deadline extensions.” “Fuck off.” “'Fuck off.' Yes, that's helpful.” After showering Andrew washed down cornflakes and fruit with a warm cup of tea. He headed to the bus stop and Andy followed him all the way to work; even as the new Big Boi album pumped from Andrew's headphones he could feel Andy's accusing glare bore holes through him. Heavy traffic meant he was five minutes late on his first day of covering for the company's Customer Services Manager. Andy sat down opposite Andrew's desk. “You're not going to leave me alone, are you?” Andrew asked as he slid his rucksack under the light ash desk and booted-up the computer. “Not until you start putting turning those Notepad ideas into Word sentences.” Andy's tone was firm but Andrew knew he was right. He had to get started on his piece as early as possible but it wasn't going to be easy. Monday was the first day in over a year that he'd worked this particular job and he spent most of the day finding his feet. Tasks that should have taken seconds were taking minutes as he reacquainted himself with an unfamiliar position of power, and barely a word had been added to his plan by the end of the day. Monday evenings were spent playing football with the Aberdeen-Music.com lot and it would be bedtime by the time he got home. Tuesday was looking like a much better day for writing. By then he'd surely be feeling more comfortable with the job, and though he had plans for the evening they only involved eating cheap pizza and watching World Cup football with his friends. Nothing like the physical exertion of the previous night at all. He took the bus back to his parents' house out of town and let the ideas flow. Grand ideas of powering through his first two scenes filled his head, but they proved too ambitious for Andrew. He sat at his desk until 1am without contributing a single word to his piece's first draft, and again he went to bed frustrated. Planning was often a difficult process for Andrew. It seemed like the more he thought about what he was going to write, the more he struggled to make up his mind. Suddenly the ideas he'd come up with on Saturday didn't seem so hot. Should Rueben Dallas die this week, next week, or should he make a miraculous recovery? When was the right time to show Cayle's reaction to being thrown out of his brother's house? Had he forced the Kathryn Toomes character on readers too soon, without giving her enough build-up and characterisation? How was he going to develop the Floyd Holland/John Hood relationship without giving too much away? Sometimes Andrew felt like he was too much of a perfectionist for his own good. He spent so long stressing over small details that it was often to the detriment of his pieces, and though the situation was definitely getting better, it was still a problem. By the time he went to bed on Tuesday night he'd finished his plan, but would he still be happy with it when he woke up? * Everything changed on Wednesday. Andrew didn't even need another one of Andy's pep-talks to kick him into action. Work was quiet and with half of the office stuck in a management meeting, Andrew got to work. Letters poured from his fingers and sentences formed with a fluency he hadn't seen in months. A smile crept across Andrew's sunburnt face as he perfected ideas on the fly and surged his way through two scenes in little over an hour. “How am I doing, Andy?” he asked, and his creation just responded with a grin and a nod. This is good, Andrew thought. Very good. His characters were writing themselves and his description felt crisp and clean. Improvement in his writing had been slow and steady, but his prose was becoming less and less bloated every time he sat down to write. Andrew found it ironic that a big reason for that was his opponent, Hyde, who had given him a fantastic piece of feedback for his NC-17 roleplay months prior. It was 1pm before he knew it and time for lunch. Andrew made the short journey from the bus station to the nearest park as he did Monday to Friday of every working week. The sun was shining and Union Terrace Gardens were buzzing. Suit-clad professionals gathered with packed lunches will groups of holidaying school kids sat on the gardens' many hills and grassy patches. Seagulls and pigeons glided through the only green space in a city renowned for its dull greyness. This small area was an oasis of tranquillity in a bustling city centre, and a place Andrew and his girlfriend had used as a lunchtime meeting place since they started dating in 2009. Andrew met Kirsty on a bench by the rosebushes and all thoughts of Global Championship Wrestling immediately left him. Every word that came from her mouth seemed like the most important thing in the world, especially when compared to fantasy wrestling, and Andrew was entranced even if much of the conversation was typical “how has your day been?” material. The peculiarity of their situation still amused him. They'd known each other loosely since starting high school over a decade ago but had never been anything more than the faintest of acquaintances until being grouped together for a university project at the start of 2009. Now two people who'd spent the majority of their lives in vastly different social circles were planning a future together. Andrew would've scolded you for even suggesting something so ridiculous if you'd suggested it a few years ago. Andrew knew as soon as he sat back down at his desk that he wasn't going to get any more work done that day. His football team, the “Henpeckit Loons,” had a tough league clash with long-time rivals Graeme Terry FC that evening and meeting with his girlfriend always removed his mind from the trivial world of ewrestling. Andy's eyes started bullets through Andrew as he saved his two-and-a-half scenes and opted to read the Tim Shipley roleplay that had appeared instead. It was good. Really bloody good. Andrew had always seen Hyde as an under-rated writer who'd never really produced body of work consistent enough to meet his undeniable talent, but he was doing a good job of doing just that in GCW. Hyde hadn't produced as much written output as most of his peers had over the years, but he was intelligent enough and read enough quality literature to make his writing a cut above the standard efedder's output. This was going to be tough. Andrew got home, went out to play football, and drove home again peacefully, but Andy wouldn't let him sleep. Not with so much work still to be done. * Thursday was a write-off and Friday wasn't going to be much better. Rushed off his feet at work during preparation's for the end of Stagecoach's financial period, Andrew barely had a chance to sit down, let alone work on his roleplay. He and Kirsty had made plans to dine and drink after work and it wasn't until he was sitting in Aberdeen's upscale Bar 99 with a belly full of Italian food that Andy decided to pay him another visit. “What are you doing, man?” Andy said, sitting down in the seat Kirsty had vacated when she'd left for the toilet. “It's Thursday and you have to have this finished by tomorrow.” “Deadline's Saturday,” a bitter Andrew responded between mouthfuls of overpriced apple cider. The gathered throng of drainpipe-clad hipsters and art school students with £200 haircuts seemed oblivious to his deliberation. “And when, exactly, are you going to find the time to write on Saturday?” Andrew groaned resignation. A few old university friends were coming up to Aberdeen for the weekend and he was heading straight out after work again. There was no way he was getting anything done on Saturday. He rolled up his black jumper's sleeves and frowned at his creation. “Well no offence, but I'm hardly going to choose you over her, am I?” “Of course not. Don't be stupid.” Andy Murray shook his head. “You don't have to play football tomorrow though, do you?” “I've already paid for it.” “But it's not competitive. What will it matter if you write instead?” “I'll write after I've played.” “You'll be exhausted.” “I don't care,” Andrew said with indignation. “There's plenty of coffee in the house; I'll do an all-nighter if I have to.” Andy could only sigh. There was no reasoning with his creator sometimes. Andrew glared into the remains of his pint and Andy was gone when he looked up again, replaced by his girlfriend's almost-black hair and deep hazel eyes. “Another drink?” he asked. * Three hours of football in the sweltering heat of Aberdeen Sports Village's stuffy indoor hall had drained Andrew Murray, but he had to write. He parked himself at his desk after a quick shower and loaded Word. A heavy groan escaped his lips with the realisation of how much work he still had to do: seven and a half scenes plus editing. He put his fingers on the keyboard but nothing had come out an hour later. It was Friday night and he was shattered: there was no-way he was getting any writing done. He was left with two options: no-show and dash his hopes of winning 2010's Dangerous Games competition, or get on his knees and grovel for a deadline extension. It was with a sense of shame that he fired-up AOL Instant Messenger and tracked Josh down, but the exchange was quick and painless. Andrew had bought himself an extra day, and the relief was so intense it was almost tangible. The circumstances weren't ideal and his last minute extension would surely draw scowls from a few, and this wasn't a situation Andrew enjoyed being in. He'd always prided himself on being the guy who'd always get his stuff in on-time, but some things are bigger than Global Championship Wrestling. Andrew curled up in bed and drifted away. He was tired. So goddamn tired. * Andrew woke up in his girlfriend's bed with a hangover in his head. It was Sunday morning and he had a busy day of writing ahead of him; one which he started by swallowing some paracetamol and sharing a hastily-prepared lunch betrothed. Andy was waiting for him when Andrew got back to his parents' house. Sat on the couch with a school teacher's glare on his face, Andy didn't need to say a word before Andrew had gone upstairs to retrieve his laptop. He turned the television on, blasted through a couple of games of Bad Company 2, cracked his knuckles, and loaded his piece. And then it came back to him. The indecisiveness that had plagued his planning process for years hit him lip a left hook and he was floored, utterly floored. Friday's “good ideas” turned to “bleh” and all of a sudden he was lost. Panic. Worry. Sweat. What did he do? Did he continue working on a piece that he was becoming increasingly unhappy with and risk mediocrity, or did he clean the slate and start again? For all the frustration that the piece had caused, Andrew felt it would be a shame to just start again, but he was drawing blanks. He need a flash. He needed inspiration. He needed something. And it came to him in an IM window. It was an idea he'd thought about for quite some time, and one that he knew he could churn out in a fraction of the time it would take to continue the Rueben Dallas story. An idle suggestion from an AIM friend was his impetus, and Andrew was ready to write. So Andrew discarded the past week's writing and started constructing a new piece of work. It was a risk, yes, but what worth is a writer if he never leaves his comfort zone?
