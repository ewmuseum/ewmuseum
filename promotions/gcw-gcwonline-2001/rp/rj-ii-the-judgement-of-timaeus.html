Date: The Present .:: ::: ::. CANTO IV each soul restor'd to its particular star, // believing it to have been taken thence, // when nature gave it to inform her mold... She was nestled in the downy feathers like a child in its bed. Her fingers laced through, clinging tightly, but without fear, and her eyes, reflecting the gold light all around her, took in the sights. Despite the wonderment with which she gazed, however, there were still too many questions that needed to be asked. She was fearful to ask them, however, not because she was fearful of her situation, but because this was already confusing enough. For a being so sure of right and wrong when in her own, waking element, this situation was completely different. To tell the truth, it didn't make sense why people should be made to embody the guilt of breaking a vow for all eternity. What sorts of vows counted? What in the case of vows that were made seriously only by one involved party? Or vows broken by no fault of the vow-maker? Though evidently, the uncontrollable nature of fate was of no consequence when it came down to it’it was simply the breaking of the vow. Lia's brow furrowed, her fingers shifting position in the tufts of soft feather beneath her. They were still flying, gliding through the updrafts in a great moment of calm. She had no desire to see where they were going, because the moment worked well enough to live in, each one on its own like a golden temple in the sky. They were well beyond the curvature of the earth, but it was not what she would have expected to see once reaching there. “You realize, of course, that your own thinking, while rather ambitious, verges on the heretical.” Those words made her sneer. What did those words even mean to someone like her? “It's your relativism that makes your judgement so... incomplete, if you will. You've your own notions of what justice is, everyone does, of course, but to presume that you can pronounce it upon the world by deciding what's justice and what's not... It is slightly egotistical, to tell a truth. But then, that is why you are here... to learn.” The situation was beginning to feel entirely out of her control. She was insulted, but knew she had no right to be. Frustrated, but knew she had no right to be. She wanted to leave, but that wasn't possible, not so high, high above the earth and everything she knew. “Many philosophers of old made the argument that everything happened as it was meant to happen. Each star has its own particular place to hang in the skies. So it is with all things. Only One can understand how they are mapped. Do not concern yourself with the details.” It was no easier to swallow after these words. But swallow she must, no matter how bitter the pill. .:: ::: ::. Last Week Hradcany, Library Las Vegas, Nevada “Dammit!” A fist, clenched half-heartedly, banged down on the padded palm rest of the keyboard. The keyboard itself jumped a millimeter upwards and then back down from the force of the impact, but the gesture was made almost as though its maker knew how futile it was to get angry in a situation like this. The screen still glared at her. Plain white, with a small Order of the Hammer logo on the bottom right-hand side. The login boxes were unchanged, still showing one to type in her username, and another to type in her password. The only difference was the message, typed in red above it: Your security clearance does not allow access to this database. Your third attempt has been logged. You will be redirected to the main page of the Order of the Hammer in 5 seconds.... 4 seconds... 3 seconds... Only those words changed, blipping and flashing as they counted down, and she was shortly redirected to the flashy front page of the Order of the Hammer. She only watched the no-doubt expensive animation for a moment before she was gone, storming out of the room on clacking heels that sounded like the successive footfalls of the hell-spawned mount belonging to the Horsemen of the Apocalypse. “VIRGIL!” It was an unattractive bellow coming from lungs which were unused to producing anything aside from a mellifluous, throaty purr. He was sitting in the study, which adjoined the library via a doorless entry on its eastern side. He hadn't really been reading, because it was hard to read when Lia was a tempest of motion and noise next door. He kept seeing her out of the corner of his eye. Despite the urgency in her voice, though, he took his time standing up, closing his book, and sauntering into the room. He arched his eyebrows at her, and leaned in the stone arch of the doorway, looking neither perplexed nor annoyed, but a neutral expression that only Virgil Ambrosi seemed to truly own. “Yes, amor?” It was difficult to tell sometimes whether or not Virgil was being sarcastic. She turned to him, furious. “My Order account isn't working.” He wasn't sure exactly what she meant. Neither of them were computer geniuses, but they knew enough to use the various technological devices that made their jobs easier, and the resources of the Order were among such things. He stepped over to the computer, a small desktop perched on a desk that was, at the moment, full of very un-Lia-like clutter. “You'll need to login with your account,” she said, peering over his shoulder as he clicked on the login screen himself. “I planned to,” he returned, glancing over his shoulder at her. She sensed that he was becoming a little aggravated by her frenetic annoyance, and she tried to rein it in. Virgil clicked through the many verification screens until he came to the main database page. “Can you check my status?” It was a question laced with a dark undertone. Both of them knew what they'd see when he checked, but it was a matter of confirmation. Ambrosi, Lia R. V., Field Officer, Captain, Knightmare Brigade. Beneath that, her official Order picture. And beneath that, what she was looking for. Status: SUSPENDED. “God damn you, Ian,” she growled, her hands clenching into fists at her side. She looked away from the laptop screen. “That explains that,” Virgil said, in something of a deadpan. But it wasn't all disinterest for him. There was one thing, after all... “I wonder why on earth I still have access.” “You didn't do anything,” Lia said, allowing an annoyed sigh to slip through her lips. “Protocol. They're not going to punish you for a choice that I made.” He knew. But it grated on him, because it put him in a position to choose between his wife and the Order. He knew what choice they would expect, because implicitly, they expected him to reason her back into submission to their rules, and to the more-than-likely messy inquest that was going to take place regarding her incident with Ian and Aine. “Ridiculous,” he growled, finally, and moved to sign off. “Wait,” Lia said, laying her hand over his on the mousepad. She maneuvered the mouse deftly, and clicked over to the database access. Another couple of verification screens, and she found herself exactly where she wanted to be. A search screen. She hadn't saved the chip’it would have been too dangerous to preserve that chip’but she knew exactly what brand it was, because she'd caught the tiny laser lettering with her sharp eyes. She typed ZTEC into the box. It was not surprising to see the name pop up in the search results. The company was big in armed forces technology, if she had to lay bets. She scanned through the first few hits with quick eyes, Virgil watching with arms folded over his chest. Then, something caught her attention. Fifth entry down. It was not a deposition report, it was not a field report. It was a medical report, a kind of inventory just like the Order took when prospective agents filed for admission. And it was hers. Her fingers trembled with eager speed as she clicked on the report, and scanned for the moment where she'd find that high-lighted word. Two-thirds of the way down the report: “...patient's bones in good shape, granted... blah blah blah... small anomaly amid the vertebrae of the neck. Preliminary scan reveals a small microchip embedded in the neck, serial number Z836N...” That was all she needed to see to feel like something had come out of the ether and socked her straight in the gut, with no warning. “They knew...” Suddenly, her hand withdrew from the mousepad, and she drew herself up to her full height, anger tearing a path through her insides. “Knew...” Virgil prodded, waiting for her to explain herself. Instead, she exploded. “They knew about the chip! It's in the fucking medical report that they took when I first started! They knew!” “They couldn't have...” “Read the fucking thing.” That was all she said before she stormed out of the room. In what direction he couldn't really tell, because his eyes were already scanning. For once, he wished she was overreacting to something, but it was here, plain as the nose on one's face, as the saying went. The Order had known that, for two years now, Lia had been carrying a tracking chip inside her neck, because they had found it on their initial physical examination of her. He read on. “...a brief investigation reveals that the chip is a ZTEC model, very similar to what is used for military-grade spy work. ZTEC's origins appear to be in Chicago, connected nebulously to the Ulrich Information Agency, a technological management firm. No further information is available at this time.” Perhaps things really were black and white sometimes. It occurred to him that Lia was most likely plotting a very messy confrontation at the moment, banging around upstairs as though she was packing a bag. He didn't know where heads would be rolling’at the Order's headquarters, or at the Chicago headquarters of Zenith Corporation. .:: ::: ::. CANTO V open thy mind to what I now unfold, // and give it inward keeping. Knowledge comes // of learning well retain'd, unfruitful else... The wind picked up on the phoenix's great back as they turned, wheeling freely around in the ether. Perhaps they were circling something’it was impossible for Lia to make any sort of guess about that. The heights were dizzying, and she closed her eyes against them, unable to summon her usual stoicism about such things. “A lesson you must learn...” The great creature invaded her thoughts once again, as effortlessly as it had the first time. “I have told you, and you have known, that Justice is in the hands of the One who wove this tapestry, and you know this to be true. Think of the lesson of Philostrata.” Lia thought, but she did not much remember any lesson that she might have taken away from that discussion, aside from the fact that the Creator's judgement must have rendered love vows entirely too high on the scale of justice. “Even she understands why she is there, and not in another place. Do not begrudge her the words that she spoke to you.” The words floated, flittered back to her, spinning lazy circles down into her psyche on the cool air. “...we would do well to remember the power of our words before we say them.” There was a pause, as the phoenix flapped its great wings, as though it knew exactly how long it would take for her to remember, and repent her hasty questions. “There is the matter of the lesson, you see. Words are only words, of course, unless the spirit of the vow be behind them. She made her vow without the intent to follow through, for even without knowing of the future, she knew it would come to such a fate. That one day, one of them would leave the other, and break the vow. Take her lesson to your heart.” For a moment, she wanted to go back through her psyche, ticking off every kept promise she could remember making, but there was no time for that. The glow of gold intensified as the pair pierced upwards still, into the next sphere. Here, it was even brighter than before, and the phoenix slowed to a near halt. Out of the light around emerged a being somehow still brighter against the darkness. “Hail!” If she had been charged to remember the first lesson, then here was another’so much to concentrate on that Lia's senses were overwhelmed, and she very nearly lost consciousness. .:: ::: ::. The Present Hracany, Master Suite Las Vegas, Nevada Lia paced back and forth, phone in hand. She was not looking forward to making the phone call she was about to make, if only because she knew exactly how it was going to go. She might not have been able to transcribe it word for word if you asked her to, but the simple fact was that she had to have an alibi. Vivica J. Valentine could not know where she planned to be for the week leading up to Devil's Night. This was going to be a difficult prospect, seeing as Lia and Virgil were about to travel to Vivica's hometown of Chicago. And if they ended up anywhere randomly crossing her path, they'd have stuck out like sore thumbs. They couldn't risk being caught. The best way to do that, naturally, was to draw on one's resources. It had been some weeks since Lia had talked to Dawn. She, of course, felt guilty about her rather poor communication skills, but with as much as she'd been through leading up to Dangerous Games and immediately afterwards, there was at least reason for it. No use putting off the inevitable. With fingers so practiced she didn't even have to think about placing them in the right grooves, she dialed Dawn McFarlane's number. “Hello?” “Dawn?” “...Lia!” Her voice sounded surprised, and not a small amount put-out. Lia figured she at least deserved that. “...how are you?” “Oh, I'm fine, I suppose... taking the day off to enjoy the weather a bit. To tell you the honest truth, I was on my way out the door when you called. Heading down to the park for a jog.” “Oh...” Lia paused, swallowed, and wound the phone cord around her fingertips. “You're free to call me back afterwards if you like.” In the silence, it was almost painfully obvious that Dawn would have rather done just that. But pleasantries exchanged, there would be no sense having to repeat the process later on, whenever it was convenient for Dawn to call her back. “Well, I don't want this to have to wait, because from the way you sound, love... there's something important on your mind.” It was easy to forget sometimes how much Lia had let Dawn in. “I've a favor to ask of you.” “You know I'll do whatever I can for you... provided you plan on making it up next time I see you.” The teasing note made Lia smile in spite of herself. “I always come through on my promises...” “Shoot.” Lia took a deep breath, because despite the jovial mood, once the subject matter turned real, this wasn't going to be pretty. “I need you to cover for me this week.” Brief pause. “Sure, but... what are the circumstances, if I may be so bold as to ask?” “Well... I told you about Markus. I've been working on finding him, and I think I've got a lead, but I've got to follow through on it myself. Virgil's going to come with me just in case I need the help, but...” “You don't want Vivica to know.” That was the only logical conclusion to the thought, the only reason Dawn would have to cover for Lia’it wasn't what she'd expected. She'd expected having to lie to Virgil about Lia's wherabouts, which was equally distasteful. Or perhaps more so. Lia's fierce independence in such situations often ended up getting her into lots of trouble, and she really didn't want to put herself in that kind of situation. “Exactly,” Lia said, her voice suddenly sounding rather tired. “I need to know why.” Dawn was certainly in a position to demand that of Lia at this juncture, mostly because Lia had no right to demand something of her without telling her all the implications. A deep sigh filtered through the phone line towards Toronto. “I have to go to Chicago. To deal with... Zenith. He's involved in all this somehow--? “Bastard.” It was almost a gut reaction’and who wouldn't have reacted the same way? Lia chewed her lip, trying to figure out the best way to put it. “I'm afraid he's more dangerous than even we realized. More conniving. I wouldn't be surprised if he'd managed somehow to get surveillance equipment into the Estate, in other words.” Pause. “Dawn, basically... Vivica cannot know that Markus' disappearance has anything to do with Christian Zenith. In fact, I'm not sure that it does so far, but it's a lead that I have to check out because if he does have his hand in the proverbial pie... this just got personal. And you know he won't go down without a fight.” “I do know.” She responded almost eagerly, with a strength of voice that Lia hadn't expected. In fact, Lia had expected that they would get into an argument, right here, right now. It didn't sound like that was going to happen. “But because I know, I want to invoke what you owe me for this right now.” “...alright?” “Listen to me for once’don't go. Whatever's going on with Zenith, this is not worth a personal confrontation because he doesn't have what you have. He doesn't have honor. He'll stoop as far as he needs to go to get you out of his hair if that's what he ends up wanting.” Lia nodded along with Dawn's words, because they were absolutely true. “I made a promise to Viv, Dawn. If Chicago's potentially where Markus is, then I have to go to Chicago.” “You won't be dissuaded?” ?Can't be.” “Let me come with you.” Lia was immediately shaking her head, running her fingers through the long black hair that bumped against her back in its ponytail. “Dawn, I... you just can't, okay” This is something that I want as few people involved in as possible.” “I can, and you need me to. I'll cover for you’tell Vivica to call my cell phone. And I'll also give you a third person. I'll do whatever you tell me to do, even if that's just playing backup to whatever you and Virgil end up doing.” She was making suggestions, but there was absolutely no mistaking the hardness in her voice. Dawn McFarlane was not going to let this go until she got her way, even if she had to turn this into a confrontation to get it. “...alright. We'll meet up in Chicago, and coordinate efforts from there. I've got a few things to fill you in on too, but I'd rather do it in person.” She felt like she was going to take that precaution forever from now on. Even that was not guaranteed to be any safer than talking over the phone. “Okay. I can get a flight out of here on short notice, and get a hotel set up. You'll be out tomorrow afternoon?” Lia had to smile, even if it was only slightly. Dawn knew her habits quite well. “Yes.” “See you then.” The conversation was over rather as abruptly as Lia had made her request of Dawn. This was not exactly the happy occasion that she had hoped for in terms of meeting Dawn again. There would be no celebration. There never was, when Christian Zenith was involved. .:: ::: ::. CANTO VI to his hands, // the living Justice, in whose breath I move, // committed glory, e'en into his hands, // to execute the vengeance of its wrath... “Well met, friends. I see you have come from the below. And will you be joining us?” “I...” She had never felt so ill-prepared to answer a question in her life. She stumbled over her words, but there was no need to answer that question, for the phoenix spoke, and it was confirmed that his power spoke not only to her, but to others as well. “She is an Outlander, if we must use so crude a term. Summoned here for the lessons she must learn, and the test she must take.” The being nodded, and amid its great light began to take the shape of a human face. It was nebulous, though, ever-changing as the seconds passed. “The lesson...” it said, and for a moment, it only shimmered back and forth, not speaking. But there was certainly more to say. “Your kind finds it hard to understand the concept of justice, do they not” Consider your own life.” The brief pause was intended to give her time to do just that. But the span was too long to sift through in just those minute moments, which, too, might have been eternities. A soft sigh came through the gossamer air. “T'is historical with your kind, and that is a sad truth. From the moment Cain struck a blow against his brother, your kind was doomed to understand justice in ever-increasing shades of gray. What was once an easy decision is now one that requires great debate. All truth is subjective, and all crimes can be rationalized. You see the danger, of course.” There was really no way to deny that accusation. She had always been taught that part of becoming what she was was learning to reject those values from a previous life that held you back from what you wanted in the new one. “It is a sort of curse of your kind. You are blessed with many gifts, but many burdens as well. The one is easy’the consumption of human vitae. The other... the incapacity to perceive your own shifts in moral valence... is not so simple to pin down, to understand, to, in the end, eradicate. That will be your task, Outlander. To understand your tendency towards rationalization, and to return to objective truth.” Something about the directive made her stomach seethe uneasily. But there was no time to think about it. The figure appeared to bow. “Welcome. Here stand, sit, kneel... we who concentrated on the moral virtue of honor. Though there was not much else in life that we made our business, there was this one thing... and it is difficult to argue with honor.” Lia wasn't so sure. But she'd be sure by the time she left, she knew. It was like a hammer hanging in the air, right above her head. The lesson would come soon, something else for her to ponder, to grapple with’some other fault of her own stripped bare in the midst of all this flax-shimmer sunlight. .:: ::: ::. The Present Hradcany, Office Las Vegas, Nevada It was rather late. Lia had made the requisite calls to Vivica to try to plant the seed of her visit to Dawn, but to no avail. Vivica hadn't even picked up the phone. Lia didn't want to conjecture about why that was, but it made her uneasy. That, in fact, was why she was still up. She was sitting at the desk, in the office that adjoined the master bedroom suite, bent over the desk and scribbling in a leather-bound book. Lia Ambrosi had always been something of a fanatic diarist, and the years had only intensified her habit. She had years upon years of journals saved up and stored. Sometimes, she liked to trot them out again, just to relive those experiences in as vivid a detail as the words would convey. Now, though, she was not reliving; she was currently in the process of coming to a conclusion. ...there has to be a solution. No one knows who had anything to do with Markus' abduction, but there are strong clues pointing to Christian. All things told, that's probably the most likely alternative, since his name keeps popping up every time I try to research anything that's gone on lately. I can't say I'm surprised, either, because he's just that much of a jealous control freak. The idea of Vivica being happy with someone else must turn his stomach. His anger turns my stomach just a bit too, to know that he's capable of making life quite miserable for beings of my kind. Not that I wasn't already aware of how vicious Christian Zenith can be, but it's a bit different when that viciousness is visited on you and yours, be they blood related or not. Vivica can't find out what's going on. I say that because there's already enough going on in her life right now. She takes the whole career thing very seriously, and I can't blame her for having the passion to pursue what she loves. It's a rare person on this planet who finds not only that passion, but the courage to follow wherever it leads. I can see Vivica as a role model for many, in the way that I never could be. Our charismas are of rather different sorts. We draw in different types of people’and I suppose where hers are great in number, mine are great in the personal qualities that I believe are necessary to whatever project I happen to be working on at the time. Back to the point, however... it would crush her hopes of doing well leading up to NC-17 if she were to know that HE is involved again. I know exactly what she's going into at Devil's Night, and... well, she's my partner. I have to work to protect her interests, because there aren't many people you can depend on to do that for you. She needs the utmost of concentration walking into what she's walking into. I'm sure there are a lot of people who would say that it's somewhat noble of me to look out for Vivica like that after what happened last week. I don't know why she won't talk to me, but I know that a lot of people are starting to think that this World Title issue will be the end of this tag team. Perhaps they're right. Perhaps, in the face of a mass of metal and leather, bonds begin to creak and strain, and eventually, they break. I can't really say where Vivica and I are concerned, though I'm certainly aware of what happened to Dawn and I when we clashed heads over the same thing. And maybe that's the scariest part of all. The part of me that considers that I stand here, putting forth all my effort to help Vivica realize her dreams, to protect her... at least that's what I think I'm doing. But how long is it going to take before competition takes over? And after something that bitter, what's left between two friends? Does Vivica even DESERVE my protection and concern? But those are all things that doubters think about, and when it comes to what I know is right, I don't doubt. I made a promise to Vivica that I would find out what happened to Markus, and try to rescue him, if there's anyone still left to be rescued. I didn't make any promises about the method, and I didn't make any promises about making sure that every single piece of the investigation is open to her. My way of doing things will have to do’and my way believes in a good secret, here and there. Maybe this absence from each other will do us some good. To tell you the truth, the prospect of losing another family member over all this is something that makes me sick. But maybe we can stave off what seem to be the inevitable effects of competition. Perhaps Vivica and I can convince ourselves (delude ourselves, if you must) with the idea that we're going to have a friendly little competition to decide who the true better is. Maybe if I keep that thought in my head this week, it'll help me remember exactly why I've done something for her, lack of response and all. That has to be good. This has to be the way, doesn't it? Even if it's not, it will have to do. Fate will take care of the rest, and deliver the just rewards that we've been stewing up for months.
