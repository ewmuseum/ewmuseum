The Ascent

19 February 2010

Date: 02/19/10 It was a hot night in Purgatory, even for Dallas. This particular winter and its unaccustomed chill had still failed to bring down the temperature inside the hottest dance club in town. It was an extraordinary multi-level complex in which one could easily spend the night getting lost, between the raucous dance floors of the Hell level, and the more toned-down ones above in Purgatory and the aptly named Heaven where, seeming to float amongst the airy seating and decorative elements, even the waitresses had wings. Tempest’s ascent, like most others in her life would start in Hell. It didn’t quite live up to the name in spite of the flame kissed color scheme of swirling light that bounced off the reflecting surfaces of bars, tables, and chairs, not to mention the stone encrusted floor. Brimstone never looked so good. Swirling around in this abyss, the only demons present were heard and not seen; those of rhythm and the frenetic tune that whipped the crowd into a frenzy. Here Tempest was living up to her name, renewing her vows with the dance floor in a shimmering short gold dress with dazzling holographic spangles and a daringly low neckline that allowed for a view of the soft line bisecting her well sculpted abdominal muscles. Whether the stir she caused among the other revelers was because of this or because of a flicker of recognition wasn’t immediately obvious. But a few fingers pointed between her and Tessa Windsor, resplendent in a short denim miniskirt and black top adorned with dark spangled roses, seemed to show the latter to be true for a few fortunate “lost” souls. But if Tempest noticed, it wasn’t obvious. She swayed and shimmied to the music, lost in its smoldering embrace. Tessa, who had just been introduced to this pre-big match ritual of Tempest’s, was overwhelmed, but hardly resistant to the chance to step away from her own troubles for a little while. She looked around every now and then until Tempest finally caught her and hollered through the loud electronic haze of music, “Stop looking around for Markus Stone. He’s not coming. Not tonight. Not here.” She shook her head and winked at Windsor before grabbing her hands and spinning her around. The two danced and for the most part found it easy to ignore the pointing fingers because this was not the sort of club in which they were likely to be assailed for autographs. Tempest did not do clubs that were anything less than polite. Hell had never been so easy on the guests and Purgatory waited above with a drink and soft couch. ~ “How can you be so sure?” Tessa asked, sipping lightly from a chilled martini glass that contained something pink and doubtless powerful. They both kicked back on a pair of chaises covered in soft blue and green fabric, the pattern swirling like flames, but starved of the intensity of their color. Tempest chuckled and said, “I told you. I don’t go to dive bars. I called two days ago and booked us a table upstairs and emailed them Markus’ picture. All the doormen know not to let him in.” She made a swift, triumphant nod. Tessa seemed taken aback and blinked at Tempest as she said, “How’d you pull that off? You have friends here or something?” Tempest giggled and sipped her own bluish concoction through a glass straw and said, “On the club circuit? Actually, I do have friends. I made such a habit of frequenting good clubs in my earlier career that I started being a bit of an endorsement.” She shrugged and looked around at the glittering tables and subdued colored lighting and sighed contentedly. “I fit right in. And now that I’m back, club owners are happy to see me. Besides, look around. This isn’t a place that wants Markus Stone. Everyone in here is dressed better than he would ever be, and you think they let guys in here who stink like a weeklong bender when they hit the door?” Tempest snorted and shook her head while Tessa’s nod and upturned palm seemed to indicate some agreement. “No, this place isn’t like that. They don’t want fights, or even scenes. They want women to feel comfortable here and men to be gentlemen, to get their attention without being idiots. And preferably for everyone to spend money.” Tempest smiled with satisfaction, taking another sip of her drink as Tessa laughed and pointed with a small tip of her glass. “Gotta admit I didn’t have you pegged as the high society type,” she said. “But it definitely has its advantages if you can buy yourself an evening free of being badgered.” Tempest shrugged and said, “Well, I don’t exactly live five-star all the time. But I’ve been through enough Hell in my life that I deserve a little slice of Heaven now and then. No pun intended.” “Mmm hmm,” Tessa agreed with a mouth full of her drink, which the color in her cheeks was beginning to match. “Speaking of going through Hell, it’s too bad Andy wouldn’t come with us.” “Yeah,” Tempest said, nodding slowly. “I’d heard Murray was fun and outgoing. That’s why I thought we should invite him. You know, lift his spirits and all. But I guess everything going on now has put a damper on that.” Tessa nodded, “Viv has just… I dunno what’s wrong with her. But yeah she’s definitely taken the dance out of his step for now. He’ll be all right. And you’ll get to see the real Andy Murray again. But now…” She shook her head. “I think it’s too soon.” Tempest nodded and smiled hopefully. “Good! Cuz it’s just sad that he didn’t want to come out with two great dates, both of whom are taken and would not have any expectations. But I guess you’re right. I dunno, I think it’s too hard to hold down a relationship with someone in this business. That’s why I’m not gonna do it again.” Tessa sighed and said, “It’s tough all right. But hey, have you told Cheap Labor that? He could use the wake up call.” Now it was Tempest’s turn to be taken aback. She blinked a moment and said, “Cheap Labor? Well, why would he…” She was brought up short by Tessa’s rolling of the eyes. “Oh come on, Tempest,” she said. “You’ve got to be kidding me. Anyone can see he’s totally gaga over you. After last week, even you have to see that. He wouldn’t even fight you.” Tempest stirred her drink uncomfortably and frowned as she said, “Well… I think he felt it was an unfair fight. Which it was, you know.” “I know, but still I don’t think that’s why he wouldn’t do it,” Tessa said in a sing song voice that seemed to indicate the drink might be lightening her mood as intended. “Maybe,” Tempest said, looking far off for a moment. “You don’t think… it’s going to be a problem at NC-17 do you? I mean it’s the big stage and for his title. He’s got to fight me then, right?” Tessa shrugged. “Who knows? He’s a man and they don’t make any sense to me. But I think you need to nip this thing in the bud.” “I’m not doing anything to his butt!” Tempest said with a little huff. “I think that would counter-productive.” “Ok that is not what I said,” Tessa said and shook her head as they both laughed. While they spoke, one of the waitresses came up and asked, “Anything I can get you ladies? I don’t think your train has dispersed outside yet.” She nodded toward the large panes of glass in the far wall of the lounge, through which a small crowd, mostly of men, stood milling about, a little more expectantly than the average club goers. Tempest sighed and said, “I knew this whole ladies lounge thing was going to come in handy. Most of our fans aren’t women, and the few that are are nice. And that one way glass is working out well too. At least we can size up the crowd.” Tessa peered over her shoulder and said, “Yeah, that’s not a bad crowd. I don’t think we’ll have to run out the back.” The waitress looked back and forth between the two women and asked gently, “Um, which fans would you be referring to? I’m sorry, but I don’t know who you are. Should I be arranging a private VIP suite? I mean if you’re worried about people bothering you…” “Oh no no, it’s fine,” Tempest said, waving her off. “We’re both wrestlers with GCW. If you don’t follow it you wouldn’t know us. So no, it’s not like you’ve got Beyonce and Shakira here.” Tessa laughed, as did the waitress, who said, “Ok, I admit, I don’t follow wrestling. But wow, that’s pretty awesome. So I guess you don’t really need those bouncers outside the lounge to keep the guys at bay, huh?” “Oh yes we do,” Tempest said and raised her glass. “It’s nice not having to take out your own trash all the time.” ~~ Email dated 2/8/10 From: [detail withheld] To: [detail withheld] Dear Rachel, Hey baby, so sorry I haven’t been able to write sooner. Yup. Last week,” she said and pulled her fuzzy black hoodie tighter around her against the breeze on the rooftop patio outside the hotel’s restaurant. “It’s touch and go, but with all he’s doing I can understand that. He’s got more important thing’s to do than write me love letters.” “Awww,” Frankie Valleta mewled comically in a voice that didn’t fit his size and shape in the slightest. “Saving lives is serious business and all, but I would think he could scratch off a few sweet nothings on the eve of his daring heroine’s big match.” Between nibbles of toast Tempest glowered at him and picked up her fork, wielding it in Frank’s direction like a tiny sword. “Don’t make me hurt you. You know he doesn’t think this is heroic,” she said and then tossed the fork down and sighed. “I just hope he watches and is ok. He’s got to get used to it sometime.” She rubbed her temples lightly and Darren Stearns chimed in saying, “Well as long as he doesn’t read the dirt sheets and catch wind of you hitting the club with Andy Murray Iast night I’m sure he’ll be fine.” He watched Tempest sip her Coca-Cola delicately and added, “You need a little something for that hangover?” Tempest rolled her eyes blearily and said, “I am not hung-over. I’m just sleepy. And Troy knows I go clubbing with friends all the time. I… wait, what? Murray didn’t go with us. It was just me and Tessa. He didn’t feel like…” Tempest was brought up short by the piece of paper waved in Darren’s left hand as he smiled, embarrassed, behind it. “’Fraid someone decided to get inventive with your adventures last night. You must have been overheard talking about him.” He tossed the sheet across the table as Tempest groaned and picked it up. “Who knows? These dirt bags will say anything to get people talking and get a hit on their website.” Tempest read the printout and flopped her head forward onto the table, then whimpered as the impact apparently made matters worse. “Hey! Hey, don’t do that!” Frankie said and patted her head gently. “I’m sure you rattled enough stuff loose in their last night already. You sure you don’t want an Exedrin or six?” “Already had four,” Tempest said weakly and muffled from her still face-down position. “Uh huh… ‘Not hungover’ huh?” said Darren, folding his arms across his chest and looking as authoritative and managerial as he could while being this amused. “Good thing you’ve still got a couple days and Fanfest before your big title match.” Tempest raised her head and frowned petulantly as she said, “Ok, a teensy one, now stop fretting already. I’ll be in the gym in a couple hours and glowing like a meteor for Fanfest.” “Oh man, I forgot about Fanfest,” said Frank, mopping his mouth after a downing chunk of pancakes the size of Tempest’s fist. “You want me to run interference for you there? Seems like you got enough trouble going on you might need it.” At the word “trouble” a soft “Woof!” came from beneath the table and Tempest’s chubby, elderly Border collie trundled out from under it to look around expectantly at the two men with her. Darren snickered and said, “Right on cue.” “Ok that’s starting to creep me out,” Frank said as the dog walked slowly around his way and sat down, staring up at him with big brown eyes. “And no I’m not going to feed you. Softie is over there.” He pointed to Tempest. “I mean, what possessed you to name a dog Trouble in the first place?” “I didn’t,” said Tempest, poking at her scrambled eggs. Darren eyed her knowingly in the silence that hung over the table for a moment and Frank’s eyebrows rose as he looked between the two, apparently sensing that he may have stumbled on a sensitive subject. After a moment’s thoughtful chewing, Tempest said quietly, “Trouble was given to me as a puppy by a former lover who said that anything that would follow me everywhere I went and love me its whole life, no matter what, should be called ‘Trouble’.” She looked at Frank and her familiar mischievous smile crept back onto her lips. “Pretty much sums it up, huh?” Frank shrugged, suddenly uncomfortable and said, “Kinda,” with an apologetic smile. Darren cleared his throat and said, “Not to blunder onto another sensitive subject, but speaking of man troubles, you still haven’t dealt with Cheap Labor and his case of… mistaken infatuation, have you?” Tempest groaned and said, “Ugh, I haven’t had the chance. It’s not like I’ve been encouraging him.” “What?!” Darren spluttered over his coffee cup. “You hugged the guy and he looked like he was on Cloud 9 last week!” Tempest chewed and pointed with her toast before responding, “I was upset! Yes, I hugged him. I’ve hugged both of you. What? Can’t I just be friends with a guy?” Frank threw up his hands and let them fall to the table with an alarming clatter. “Yes! But you can be friends with Darren who’s married and me, who’s crystal clear on our relationship and not confused about the fact that Troy would kill my ass if I stepped out of line. That’s if you didn’t break all my fingers first or something. You gonna eat that?” He eyed the untouched sausage links on Tempest’s plate as Trouble whined simultaneously. Tempest rolled her eyes and gave one to Frank and one to the dog before saying, “Troy doesn’t like violence of any kind, that’s why he hates what I do. You know that. He wouldn’t kill you.” “Whatever. He owns a lot of power tools and pours foundations. I wouldn’t mess with his girl, no matter how much of a pacifist he says he is,” Frank said, downing the sausage in two bites. “He’s right, you know,” Darren said. “You need to set Cheap Labor straight that you are not on the market.” “Yeah, just… slip it into some conversation,” said Frank. “It’s not like you have to make a big deal out of it. Just drop something about your boyfriend and that’ll be all it takes. Believe me, even guys as thick as me pay attention to that stuff when we hear it.” He grinned and added, “Should we put this to a vote? Who thinks Tempest has got to do this?” He raised his hand and was quickly joined by Darren and then Trouble perked up and barked at her. Tempest gasped and frowned at the dog as she said, “Traitor, no biscuit for you.” Trouble whimpered and she relented and scratched him behind the ears with a sigh. “Oh, you guys are right. Why do I always get this stuff wrong?” Darren smiled and said, “It’s your worst fault – Sometimes you’re just too nice for your own good.” ~~~~ How do I get myself into these things?
