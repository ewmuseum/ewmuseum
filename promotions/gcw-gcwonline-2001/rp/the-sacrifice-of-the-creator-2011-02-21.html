The Sacrifice of the Creator

21 February 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02/21/11 Steven Caldera wore a frown as he sped through the hall. He cut a path of cowering crew as he made his way through the production area and came to a halt behind the curtain. A golden glimmer preceded the GCW World Champion as he entered the room. A sweaty, grinning Badger Benandanti ignored him and walked past. The crowd was still roaring from the other side of the stage. "What the hell are you thinking?" Caldera forced with way into Badger's sightline. He stopped. "Get out of my face." Caldera moved closer. "This wasn't part of our agreement. You do what I tell you to do." A group was forming around them, staff and wrestlers doing their best to look inconspicuous. Badger shook his head. "This ain't workin' for me. If you don't back the fuck up, we're gon' have a real serious problem." "We've got a real serious problem." Caldera didn't back up. He cast his glance from side to side, welcoming the attention of the gathering crowd. "Everyone look at this. This is the sort of thing that can bring down an entire company. A champion who isn't interested in doing what's best for everyone." "Oh, and you're the team player?" Badger spat a few inches away from Caldera's shoe. "Look, I've got this belt, so there's not a lot you can do to me. But if you want me gone, just say the word. Life's too short to deal with your shit." "We all have to make sacrifices, Badger. I've made mine. I expect some out of you." "That's a joke. We done?" The room went silent save for the rumbling of the departing crowd. All of GCW stood still. Steven Caldera turned his back and left. Badger gripped his GCW World Championship belt and pushed past the onlookers. They went back to work. _______ Janice sits in her spare and unassuming office, idly shuffling paperwork and eyeing the clock. She takes a nervous glance at the phone, hoping it won't ring. It rings. "Janice, step inside for a moment." The modest white doors give way to a vertiginous view. Towering floor-to-ceiling windows back the desk of Steven Caldera, who sits on an elevated section of the room, looming over all who enter. Shrouded by the setting sunlight filtering through a canopy of Manhattan skyscrapers, Caldera seems deep in thought. Janice approaches. "Yes, sir?" No response. Finally, "I'm sorry, I know it's time for you to leave. But could you place a call for me?" "Of course, sir." "I'd like to speak to Corey Fisher." Janice hesitates. Caldera makes eye contact to reiterate. "Right away." She turns on the point of her heel and leaves. A strange request. Or not that strange. Caldera had become sentimental in the past few weeks as he planned GCW's tenth anniversary events. To date, he had not phoned Fisher. Janice dials the number from memory. The phone rings. Twice. Three times. "Hello?" "This is Steven Caldera's office calling for Corey Fisher." Silence. "Sir?" "Fine." Janice pushes two buttons on her phone. "Mr. Fisher for you, Mr. Caldera." She transfers the call and waits. Thirty minutes pass. The phone rings. "That's all, Janice. Thank you." She collects her things and walks to the elevator. She looks back at the clock. Eight in the evening and she's finally leaving. Does Caldera leave at all? He has never left before her, and never arrived after. _______ "You're fired." Corey Fisher sighed. "Heard that one before." "We're through this time." "Well hell, Steve, what reason did you come up with this time?" "Unsatisfactory performance." "Whatever. I'll see you in a couple of weeks." "Not this time, Corey." "So what? That's it? After a year and a half of climbing up together, you're just going to kick me to the curb?" "Something like that, yes." "What is it with you, Steve? You get a little bit of success, a little bit of power, and suddenly you forget how to share. We were a great team, you and I. You were always a prick, I just thought you had some loyalty." "I'm loyal, Corey. But I'm loyal to something bigger." "What? This GCW of yours?" "Yes." "It's going to fail, Steve. We've both seen the cracks. You can shake things up all you want, but it's not going to fix it. Things will fall apart, and when they do you'll have alienated the few people willing to call you a friend, and you'll be alone." Steven Caldera stood up. He shook his head. "That's where you're wrong, Corey. Your time is over. This company will live on long after you're gone. This company will live on long after I'm gone. I'm loyal to the bigger picture, the thing that will carry our legacy on longer than either of us could hope to do on our own." "So what, you're killing me off, then?" "No, Corey, I'm making a sacrifice." "What have you sacrificed? I'm the one going home while you keep pocketing your cash and taking all the credit for the things we've built together. I'm the one getting sacrificed." "My friend. I've sacrificed more than you'll ever know." "Fuck you." "You can finish out the month. I expect Mr. Danger at Shockwave." "Oh. I'll be there." _______ "I think he's sexy," says Emily. The girls giggle and sip their martinis. Janice rolls her eyes. She's had this conversation before. "And he's rich," says Abbie. You've known him long enough. If he can't open up to you, who else can he open up to? You've been with him since the start. Maybe he wants someone to talk to." "He did ask me to call someone tonight. Someone from his past, someone he hasn't spoken to in years. Maybe he's hurting." "I love it. Powerful man with a painful past. You should make a move." Janice drains her martini and asks for another. _______ Octavian wiped his face and inhaled deeply. His entire body burned. He opened a bottle of water and drank greedily. "Hello, Gaius." Steven Caldera was in the corner waiting. "What are you doing here? Hitting the gym? Is pushing a pencil not enough of a workout for you?" Caldera grinned. "I've got my own gym." "Of course you do. What do you want?" Octavian kept right on walking, inviting Caldera to follow. The two pushed their way out of the gym and into the cool Columbus air. "I want to talk to you." "So talk." "I think a change is needed." "Heard that one before." "I know you have. But this time it can work to your advantage." "Not interested." "Yes you are." Octavian stopped. He turned and looked Caldera in the eye. This man had helped groom him, helped build his career. Then he passed the torch to someone else, and Octavian was left on the outside looking in on the favor of the creator. "I already know what you're thinking." "And that's why it's got to be you." "We're going through this again? You turned your back on me before. Why should I trust you?" "Because you understand the reason why." "I really don't. Explain it to me." Caldera puffed up his chest, and a strange light came about him. In moments like these, Steven Caldera became something else. Not just the world's best salesman, but a prophet, foretelling greats acts that would come to transcend time and become immortal. "Because you see the bigger picture. The idea that when I've taught you all I can teach you, I cast you out and force you to be your own man. The idea that the brightest stars are born in the fiercest fires. That the harder I push you, the greater you become, and this ensures that only the most worthy will stand at the top of this thing we've created together." "So all this, this thing you do. It's for us? Is that it?" "It's because I believe in this place, Gaius. I believe more than you'll ever know." "Funny way of showing your love." "I'm willing to make the sacrifice, Gaius. Are you?" Octavian grew quiet. He knew the sacrifice all too well. He'd already made it once. He wasn't sure if he had it in him to make it again. _______ "Quarterly earnings are still trending up. This marks our sixth consecutive quarter in the black, and two straight quarters of significant growth. While our music and film interests are holding steady, the vast majority of income is being generated by the wrestling company's improved ratings in the last year. I would say that leadership is a strength and that the sky is the limit for Caldera Enterprises as long as the current support structure remains in place." Roger Pryce gives a subtle nod at the table to emphasize his pride. The rest of the board members stand and trade smiles and handshakes. A cork explodes in the corner where Gerry Rosenthal is raiding the wet bar. The conference room door opens and the jubilant atmosphere is hushed. Their lips curl slightly from genuine to polite. The stern face of Steven Caldera commands the attention of the room as he stands with an inscrutable look of expectation. "Congratulations on a great year, Mr. Caldera," says Pryce. "Yes, we just finished and things could not have gone better in 2010," says Wallace Unhrue. Caldera nods soberly and salutes. "Keep up the good work, everyone. You all have a lot to be proud of." The group goes back to its boisterous celebration as Caldera takes his leave. The meeting room adjacent to his office grows brighter as Caldera returns to his brooding ground, sitting high above New York and staring ponderously out the window from his lofty vantage point. Janice is at his desk shuffling papers. Timidly, "Sorry sir, I'll be out of your way in a moment." Caldera exhales and loosens his tie. "You know, Janice." He stops. She stops. "Yes, sir?" "It's been ten years. The day is almost over. Maybe it's time you started calling me Steven." She can't hide her smile. "Really?" "Really. Drink?" "I really should finish-" "Forget about it. It's the end of the week. This is the time to sit quietly and reflect. Have a drink." A sudden wave of suspicion hits Janice and she hesitates. Caldera seems to sense her confusion and relaxes the situation by kicking his heels up on his desk. She walks to the private bar by the window and pours a vodka tonic. "Something for you, sir?" "Scotch. Rocks." She pours the drink and brings it to him. She considers taking a seat, but doesn't relish the thought of moving to the weak spot below the desk. She leans against the window and looks out. Caldera sips his scotch and is silent. "I've never really looked at the view from here." Janice presses her head against the glass. "This is a remarkable thing you've built." "I really do agree." Janice clears her throat and sips. In ten years, she has never seen the top button of Caldera's collar undone. Caldera gives her a long, penetrating look. "Tell me Janice." Janice gulps, a fluttering feeling in her lungs. "Yes?" Another pause. Caldera doesn't take his eyes off her. "Have you ever done something you deeply regret?" The question takes her by surprise. "Well, yes." A nod, followed by a drink. Caldera gives her a wistful smile. "I have, too." "Are you okay, sir?" "Steven." "Sorry." "What's to be unhappy about? You're right, look at what I've built. Things have never been bigger. And we're still growing." "It just seems like. Pardon me for saying, it just seems like you've been a little. I don't know. Sad lately." Caldera's icy blue eyes latch onto her again. A roar of laughter goes up from the conference room. Janice nervously sips her vodka tonic. "Have I?" Perhaps the vodka emboldens her. "Yes, sir. Steven." "If you could go back and change the thing you regret, Janice, would you?" She gave it an honest thought. "Yes. Of course. Would you?" He smiled at her. He had smiled at her before, but this is the first time it seemed true. The phone rang. Caldera sat forward and looked. "I have to take this. Will you excuse me?" Janice smiled and nodded. She kept her drink. He crossed the long distance to the exit, and Caldera didn't answer the phone until the door was shut. _______ "I think we missed the exit." "Fuck! From now on, I drive." Janice exits the elevator and walks to her desk, looking for her forgotten cell phone. Her drawers yield nothing. Sighing a frustrated sigh, Janice heads to the door and pushes her way into Caldera's office. The lights are dimmed but the room is brightly lit by the reflection of the rising sun on the windows opposite the Caldera Enterprises building. She looks on the sofa and around the desk, finally locating the phone on a credenza. A noise from behind her startles her. "Hey, Janice, I didn't think you were coming in today." Caldera appears out of nowhere, suddenly arriving at his desk without warning. "Mr. Caldera! Sir, I'm sorry, I was just looking for-" "It's the weekend, Janice." "Sorry. Steven, I. I forgot my phone yesterday. I didn't realize you were here, I would have knocked." "It's fine." Caldera, wearing a casual ensemble of a black t-shirt and jeans, sits at his desk and reclines. Janice tries to subtly scan the back of the room, looking for Caldera's means of entrance. "Well, I should be." She stops. "You know, you never answered my question the other day." "What question?" "If you could go back and change the things you've done. If you could stop yourself from doing the thing you regret. Would you do it?" The question seems to catch Caldera off guard. His eyes lower this time, another thoughtful smile crossing his lips. With a faint gesture of his head he beckons Janice closer. "It's something I think about every day of my life. Every day I wake up and go to sleep thinking about all the people I had to step on to build this place. All the lives I've made harder, all the careers I've wounded, all the people I've hurt and betrayed. You don't get to this level without doing some horrible things, you know." Janice nods appreciatively, her eyes growing wide with sympathy. "You've done great things, Steven. You've given everything to create something that makes millions of people happy." "I know. GCW is great, the greatest promotion on earth, which is exactly what I set out to achieve ten years ago. I've done it. But it doesn't come without sacrifice." Janice looks around. The immense office, a shrine to the Caldera persona, suddenly seems to shrink around the exposed core of a lonely man. "All this. This isn't you. Don't you just want somebody to..." Their eyes meet. "That sacrifice, Janice. The sacrifice I made, that I've forced others to make with me. There's a harsh truth to it. I've hurt people." "I know. I know." "That truth, Janice, is that, to succeed at what I do, to make those people happy, to ensure the success of this company that ensures the success of its talent. It takes something nobody else has quite been willing to do. It takes the absolute ironclad certainty that the best way to stay on top is to be cruel and unmerciful. So the question was asked, would I change anything? I think about all the people I've destroyed, and the answer is yes. I'd do it all again, and I will hurt them again and again until the breath leaves my lungs." Janice hesitates. Her stomach turns. "You mean..." "That's the sacrifice I've made, Janice. To be the hated one, the sinister one, the conniving one, the evil one. The one that all the world rallies around to topple but can never quite destroy. Because I'm the best at what I do, and nobody can take me on. And as I sit here on the top, I prepare myself each day to go to hell, because that's the price I pay to give the people what they want." Janice takes a half step back. She covers her mouth and shakes her head. "But you don't really mean it. Deep down, I know you do the things you do out of love." "I wish it could be that way. But it's not. It's not enough to just play the role. That's why I've succeeded where everyone else has failed. They're just characters playing a part. You see, the most effective leaders are villains. And when it comes to villains, it only works if you're the real thing. Close the door on your way out." She can hardly move. She grips her phone and backs away from the desk. Caldera watches her go, his eyes, unblinking, never leaving her. The force of his stare sends a chill down her spine as she scuttles out of the door, closing the door behind her. In her spare and unassuming office Janice packs the last of her things and stands to leave. She pauses, looks back at the door. She remains, longing to enter but afraid of what she may find. She turns and leaves. _______ Sunday night. Steven Caldera exits from the hidden door in his office, leaving the small bedroom and moving to his desk. He rocks back in his chair and loosens his tie.
