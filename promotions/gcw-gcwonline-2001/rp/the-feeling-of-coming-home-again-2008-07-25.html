The Feeling of Coming Home Again

25 July 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/25/08 Do you ever really think about buildings? What they were, what they are, what they will become. What they mean to you... I never thought too much about buildings until I rounded the corner and saw this one for the first time in over 6 months. It's nothing special, just a cube, brick walls topped off with a roof that probably started leaking the day it was put on. Dad said it was an old warehouse before he bought it, probably held textiles. I have vague memories of my brother helping Dad gut the place, I even remember walking loads of demoed wood to a big dumpster out back. I was five. He found some girl, a "hippie" he called her, to paint the mural on the front. It was simple, just an outline of North Carolina and the words "Blue Ridge Wrestling" in those 'college' style letters. You can barely see it now, raped by time and the elements it has become just a shadow. This warehouse was home to hundreds over the years. Wrestlers passed through to be trained by my father and they'd get a mattress on the second floor and 6 months of the finest wrestling instruction money could buy. The school gave way to the federation and the building evolved again, a training school in one half and the Blue Ridge Wrestling 'arena' in the other. As Blue Ridge Wrestling died the building underwent another metamorphosis as work out equipment slowly began to edge the wrestlers farther and farther into the back corner. Today the building stands there, looking almost ashamed of what it's become. Yes, a ring still stands within the walls, but it's unused, more likely to host a boxing sparring session than a wrestling match. "Reed Family Gym" is the title that adorns her now, stretched across the front on the most inelegant of vinyl banners. Yet no matter what state the building is in, or what activity is going on inside, I can't look at this simple structure without thinking of one thing... home. We never had a house, just an apartment, if you can call it that, which my father had built into the building. When I couldn't sleep at night I simply walked out of my room and into the gym where I'd run the ropes until I was exhausted. I'd collapse in the ring and sleep right there on the canvas, only waking when my father roused me with an elbow drop the next morning. So many memories here. ---- "Jake!" a voice calls out, catching Reed off guard as he walks through the door into the Reed Family Gym. It's Dirk Smith, long time family friend and the trusted proprietor of the gym while Jake is on the road. Dirk, a balding silvered haired man of 55, snatches Jake up into a bear hug. "Ughmph" Jake groans as Dirk's well toned arms squeeze the air from his lungs. "Alright, alright, I'm happy to see you too." With that Dirk relents, putting Jake back on his own two feet and releasing him. Reed dusts himself off and gives Dirk a pat on the back, "Looks like you've done a great job keeping up the place Dirk." Dirk nods, "Renewals are up, new memberships too. Now that you've been back on TV people have remembered this place exists again." "At least something good is coming of it." Jake snarks. Dirk just leaves the comment unacknowledged and continues, "Several of the newer members will want to meet you. Just a hand shake and autograph, standard stuff to keep them happy." "If they want an autograph why not get one from a real man?" A voice booms from behind Jake and Dirk. Jake clinches his teeth, that voice is as familiar to him as the air he breathes, it's his older brother Travis Reed. Jake bought Travis out of his half of the family gym after their falling out, since then Travis has been asked not to return. Dirk immediately turns and rushes at Travis, "You know you're banned from these premises Travis! If you don't leave I'm going to toss you ou--" His words are cut short as Travis pie faces him and pushes him to the floor. Big Trav grasps a handful of what hair Dirk has left and draws back a fist, "This must be embarrassing for you Dirk." "No more embarrassing than this will be for you..." Jake exclaims as he grabs Travis by the arm, jerks him around and lays his big brother out with one crisp right cross. ---- At first there's only darkness. Then sound. Metal clangs and voices blend together into a wall of noise until one, much closer than the rest, rises above. "Finally coming around eh?" Light floods in, too bright at first but pupils contract eyes adjusts, bringing the room into focus. The single voice is that of Jake Reed, standing over his brother, our subject, Travis Reed. Jake tosses an ice 'pack onto Travis; chest and sits down in a chair a few feet from the backroom cot Travis is splayed out on. "Where the hell have you been Travis?" Jake doesn't exactly look at his brother, he looks more in his general direction. Travis takes the ice pack and holds it to his jaw. "Hell of a cross you've got, I guess you learned something from Dad after all." Jake's head whips around, his eyes now clearly trained on his brother. "Don't you dare bring him up, you have no right to claim to be an authority on his ways." Travis lets a little chuckle escape his lips. "Is that right?" "You bet your ass it's right." Jake is on the edge of his chair, nearly out of it. "That man taught us to be honorable, to fight fair, he taught us that the fans made us and that we should never turn our backs on them. You shit on all that..." Travis rolls into a seated position. "Don't you lecture me on what that man taught. I got news for you boy, there's fifteen years between us so that's fifteen more years I had with that man than you. You wanna talk about what he taught? How about survival? How about doing whatever you needed to in order to get your next wrestling gig. Oh, that's right, you didn't experience any of that because by the time you came along Dad had settled down into this school... had a steady paycheck and all that." Jake's body tightens, he had never really considered how much different Travis' idea of their father could be given the age difference. Travis sees he struck a nerve. "I see the gears turning, you ain't never thought about that before now have ya? Jake we starved, we slept in cars, hell I didn't get past 6th grade in school cause we bounced around territories so damn much. You think you had it bad because you lived in this school? I would've given anything to have my own bed... and to know it'd be my own bed a month from now." "Look Travis I..." Jake stammers and Travis cuts him off. "Whatever you're gonna say just save it, I ain't here for some tearful Dr. Phil moment." Travis removes the ice pack and massages his jaw. "Then why are you here?" Jake regrets the question the moment it's asked. "To talk some fucking sense into your ass." Travis states in a matter of fact tone. "What the hell are you doing boy? I see you on TV begging McKail for forgiveness, for what?" Jake doesn't hesitate to answer that. "For what we did." Travis looks puzzled. "What the fuck did we do? We sided with Sage because it was good for our careers, you think McKail wouldn't have done the same?" Travis seems to think for a moment and then continues. "And hell, I don't recall you ever actually laying a finger on McKail? What the fuck is he supposed to forgive you for?" Jake looks for the right answer, but it eludes him. "It's complicated." "Fuck it is." Travis always had a way with words. "I don't know if you were drunk or high or both when you approached him that first time but the fact is McKail was right, there's nothing to forgive you for. Your ass got lucky, you staggered into that arena out of your fucking mind and walked away with a job in one of the hottest wrestling companies in the country." Jake firms up. "I was in a bad place ok? I was confused. Forget about all that, that shit's over. McKail could forgive me on world wide TV and it wouldn't stop anything. He tried to kill me, I don't suppose you saw that?" "Yeah I saw it." Travis waits a well calculated beat. "I can't say I blame him." Jake scowls, unamused. Travis quickly tries to calm his little brother. "Goddamn son it was a joke, drop the hard face. Look, I think you two both pushed some buttons but just go out there at Warpath and get it settled in the ring. Now see, there's something dad taught both of us, no matter how bad it gets you finish shit in that ring and you leave it there. After Warpath you walk away and all is forgotten, no more forgiveness, no more arguing over who tried to kill who, it just drops." "If I'm standing at the end of the contest I'll offer the man my hand, but you've seen him, he won't take it." Jake's voice is tinged with disappointment. "This all started so strangely... I look back at that man who was crawling on his knees and begging Jacob for forgiveness and I don't even know who that was." Travis nods. "That's what this sport does to you. Do you really think I was able to rationalize my actions after what we did? Now granted, I dealt with my feelings well enough that I wasn't begging any of the other players for forgiveness on bended knee, but it was still hard." Jake chuckles a little but then seems to get serious. "Where do we go from here Travis?" "Hell if I know kid." Travis lowers his head, still favoring that jaw. "I don't know that things can ever be the same between you and I, but we're still brothers so that much will remain." "True." Jake seems to want to say more but he pulls himself away. "Well... hang out here as long as you want but just don't let Dirk see you when you leave. He thinks I pitched you out into the back alley." Jake rises and heads for the door, just before he opens it Travis' voice beckons one last time. "Where you heading?" Travis seems genuinely interested. "I've got hands to shake and autographs to sign... enrollment is up but I've gotta keep those people happy. After all, there should still be at least one Reed in the Reed family gym." Jake turns and exists the room. Travis lowers his head, he knows that the path he led his brother down some months ago has forever altered their relationship. These two brothers, once as close as any two siblings could be, will now have little more than than a half dozen awkward interactions a year. Things will never be the same. Somehow that doesn't bother Travis. He knows that he should have never pulled Jake down that path, never misguided his little brother. Now Jake is a changed man, true he's not been to hell and back but the experience with Sage and McKail revealed to Jake a part of himself he never knew existed. Thanks to Travis' actions Jake had to learn to cope with that side of himself all at once... and all alone. No matter the path Jake has taken to get here he did somehow arrive safely, and looking more and more like their father every day. Travis knows that Jake will roll into Warpath and that he'll give even a wrestling legend like Jacob McKail one hell of a match. Win or lose Jake will do it on his own terms and Travis will be proud of him... even from afar.
