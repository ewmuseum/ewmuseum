Make War, Not Love

4 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 4, 2005 I am death. Deathly tired. Brutally inebriated without being intoxicated. I rarely drink, which is unfortunate considering my place and condition. I soak up all the pains and embrace them in matrimony. Unholy as it may be. My headache and I are enjoying our honeymoon. The bar itself is clean, although worn and burdened with the archetypal curtain of fresh and acrid smoke, creating a smog that’s waiting patiently to poison the alveoli. It’s an unpleasant smell, but psychologically satisfying. A reminder. The world is full of down on life Joes who want nothing more than to drown their thoughts in boisterous, drunken laughter and potent whiskey. Seeking solace and mitigation in self-pity. I’m here because I need an escape from mine. I am Rei. Not another Joe. The sounds of classic rock music foray into my ears from an old jukebox, rousing a terribly off key karaoke from a few tipsy locals in the corner. One of them stands up, dancing off balance before falling to the old but delicate floor, jumping back up in a flash proclaiming his skill of not spilling his beer in slurred speech. Constant use of the all purpose F-bomb. It’s many things. Noun. Verb. Adjective. Adverb. Interjection. Interadnouerbjective. Oh, f***, I made a new word! I bet you never learned that in elementary school. A television over the bar plays the local news shrouded in grainy color. The pretty anchor women looking as though she suffers from acute Seborrheic Keratoses. It cuts away to an advertisement about revitalizing skin cream by chuckling coincidence on her cue of a botox smile. My attention diverts. A long bar table spans more than half the length of the inside, stopping at the corner of the arch doorway of the restrooms. Years of use are unequivocal, gatherings of splintered wood and stains on the surface. Etchings and markings enunciating different names and dates. Sharing secrets and in turn, keeping more. Green stools align the front in fragmented consonance, some over-turned and worn, exposed cushion a mustard yellow. It gives away to a dull orange at the corners of some. The lighting is dim to the point of almost darkness, with the only direct beams coming from dangling fluorescents over three pool tables in the far corner. The felt and surface of which are a screaming enraged red. Crimson blood. Behind the bar, shelves stacked with assorted glasses reach from the floor nearly to the bleached ceiling. Different bottles and brands of whiskey, liquor and beer, all ready for consumption and to soak your troubles in a steadily flowing stream of alcohol. Billboards posted to the end of the bar provide announcements. Lost dogs. Band fliers. Local wanted reports. Plugs for candidature campaigns. Free beer until midnight. Little league coach wanted. The atmosphere is friendly and hostile. Active and lackadaisical. In a sense, it is me and I am it. Call me Rei. Speaking with mysticism makes me mysterious. Anyway, this is an alien world. The life on this planet is strange. I study it under my microscopic zoom of perception, gathering the little details that make up the central core. It’s not my kind of place. I don’t frequent bars anymore than [insert cheap M. Jackson joke here. When it comes to that subject, everyone’s a comedian.] You get the idea. I stay away from night clubs. Dance clubs. The only appeal they hold for me is the thought of leaving. Social clubs, they say. Screw that, says Rei. I’m as social as I want to be, which is almost anti-social. When paranoia kicks in, I’m quite unpleasant. Come travel with me. We’ll witness the sea. Drinking herbal tea. Where else would you want to be? Definitely not with me. That last rhyme was unintentional. The bathroom. Station changed in an instant. Of course, the pungent odor jolts me from the fantasy paradise in my head. A cocktail of urine, beer and flatulence singing their fetidness in harmony. Overhead fans churn the air, but the odor seems everlasting. Not reflecting the almost immaculate state of the bar, the bathroom is the exact opposite. The meaner cousin who doesn’t brush his teeth. Broken urinals, bowl light yellow in color, with water constantly streaming to cigarette clogged drains. Urinal mints now for no other purpose than a target. The floor tile is smashed and broken, dried urine and Comet cleaner accenting the clay red. Sinks are even worse. Fake porcelain surface peeling and rusting, faucets and handles covered with countless stains that range from soap scum to the unidentifiable. Large mirrors above the sink serve my reflection, but not on a clean platter. Fingerprint smudges, dried water streaks, and permanent marker written notes fragment me into a sideshow freak. This would be my home. Call me Rei. GCW’s resident Picasso masterpiece. The bathroom stalls are covered in writings. Phone numbers. Dirty and tasteless limericks. Pointless and meaningless rambling that started a bar poetry phenomenon. Now, to the important stuff. The golden question that signifies a state of intellectually curious bewilderment. Why? Why am I here? Many possible reasons scamper into the shining bulb of ideas, as I stare at my tired face in one of the dingy mirrors. Thoughts of Rebecca Hendley. Thoughts of wrestling. Thoughts of my downward spiral of a life I seem to be herding. Thoughts of GCW. The cruiserweight belt that I failed to attain. I shake my head, an echoing sigh shattering the near silent hum of the overhead fans. Aiden Jackson was a beast, breaking my spirit as well as my spine. It was a brutal match that cost both of us a piece of our own humanity. His is dripping entrails off of the Cruiserweight Championship. Mine is slowly crawling back to its organic meat bag. I turn the faucet on with my fingers, splashing spurting cold water across my face. There should be no excuse for failure, and there’s not. I slipped up at a crucial moment and I paid. Aiden Jackson collected. A modified DDT stopped my hopes and replaced them with a three second obscurity. After I sat up, the cheer of an entertained crowd both calmed me and incited an epiphany. I wanted to entertain. I did. And I lost. Somewhere along the hellacious train ride of a match, my focus seemingly switched tracks. I wanted to win and I wanted to entertain. My brain on the side of winning. My heart only on the crowd, where it should and shouldn’t be. With that last move, I couldn’t hear the crowd, anymore. Their voice, which had become my voice, lost in a sea of nothingness. The feeding tube severed and leaving me to starve. After the match, I was shaking from the effects. The anticipation that had built coupled with nervous excitement refused to die even when I did. But I was ready for another bout with Aiden, surprising as it may sound. Round one was sloppy, too many missteps. Following a lecture from the trainer in my mind, I was ready for anything. But it was too little too late. Those three moments when reality had become nothing more than a vague perception had taken away the very thing I came for. There was another void. In a life filled to the brim with them, it’s only one more characteristic token placed in my bank of individuality. I was used to disappointment. It followed me like a loyal dog. My life as a kid was disappointing. My life upon graduating high school was disappointing. And now, disappointment sits at my side, tongue swinging in the air. I reach down to pat him and he licks me. I glance away from the mirror and my tired reflection. A clich? dangles above my head, light swaying side to side as if it’s answering the only question I can muster. Am I ready to be a champion? “No,” says the old dim dangling bulb. Can’t argue. If I were ready, I’d be celebrating with a different set of problems. A Cruiserweight Title and heightened GCW fame would be sitting at my side. Magazine articles. Interviews. Press conferences. Physically and emotionally draining. Right now, Aiden Jackson is carrying the torch. Lighting the way for his success. And his own downfall. His success won’t be as loyal as my disappointment dog. If he’s not dethroned by the time I’m back in contention, he can expect tougher competition. I refuse to lose to the same man twice in a row. Anyway, that’s for the past and the future. Aiden Jackson goes on to face X, and I go on to face Angel. Another chance to get my spine contorted to the canvas. Squeeaak! Rei, interrupted. The door squeals in agony as it’s forced open by bulky, dirty hands. Oil and grease splattered about, tangling hand hairs together, covering the thick and discolored fingernails in a sticky coat of grime. He sneezes loudly, wiping his face with the dirtier backside of his hand. His work overalls are ripped and torn, showing deeply tanned bare skin in patches. His leathery face forms a drunken smile when he sees me standing by the sink. “Nice night, ain’t it?” Nice wasn’t the way I intended to describe it. I wouldn’t be in a place like this if I were having a pleasant evening. “It’s fine, considering.” A statement that’s not much of a statement. It’s a lingering clause. Not many catch it, and thankfully, neither does this man. “Hard day at work, definitely.” The sounds of him unzipping his fly are incredibly loud. I can hear the zip bouncing around my forethoughts. “It’s always a hard day at work for me.” And it’s not going to get any easier. “I can understand that. Being a plumber, I have some really shitty days.” He winks and laughs, rising a chuckle from me. I move over and let him wash his hands, which he does without using soap. “There’s some women out there. Fellow like you could probably pick one or two of them up with ease. Maybe make that tough day a little more exciting.” I imagine my hands running along the enticing curves of a beautiful woman. Arousing her senses, enthralling mine. Engorging myself in her beauty, being greedy in the pleasures I take. Her moans of ecstasy increasing in volume as I tease her sensitive areas - The massive slap from Rebecca that drops me to my knees. The realization that I’m a fool beyond description. Rei Two telling Rei Tiu how much of a moron he is. My Jimmeny Cricket is a relentless asshole. Not your Pinocchio Jimmeny who sings songs about being good. My Jimmeny can kick your Jimmeny’s ass. Call me Rei. A whore to my thoughts. “Make war, not love.” I say that to him and myself. My love is back in Oregon and I’m still in Canada. With the conclusion of FallOut, there’s nothing here for me, now. So why am I still here? “Sounds to me like you’ve got some deep discussion going on up here.” He taps his head in a motion that reminds me of my monkey brains shirt. He’s almost as hairy. “Take it from me. Love is a battleground all in itself. It’s a war, either way.” He smiles again, before slinging water from his hand to the mirror. I watch as they slide down to the base of the mirror, settling in small puddles just above the sink. Squeeaak! The simplicity of wisdom. A wise man just left the bathroom with words dipped in virtue. I take in a deep breath of the rancid air, stifling a cough. It’s time I head to Vegas. I’ve got many wars ahead of me.
