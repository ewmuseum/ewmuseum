It Deals a Blow.

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Two weeks ago Time's funny. When you're a child it passes gradually, and next thing you know you're fifty and your childhood fits into a little box. Right now it feels like a lot of time has passed, but it hasn’t. The long silences make it seem like hours ago since Katie came bursting through the doors. For the past five minutes she hasn’t said much at all. I can’t help but look at my watch every chance I get, not only do I need to get away but I want to. “Are you going to be alright?” I ask, shifting in my seat. “What?” she asks, looking up. “You’re not going to die” I say, reaching out and putting my hand over hers. Katie sighs loudly, before pulling her hand out from under mine. She pushes a strand of hair behind her ears, and runs a hand across her face. Her cigarette still burning slowly in her other hand. She smiles. “I'm going to die. That's a hell of a thing to tell a stranger, isn't it?” I don’t know what to say, exactly. I feel as though we’re two complete different creatures, heading in different directions. And this is our passing place. Tears stroll down her cheeks freely now and she sniffs, trying to keep them back. It’s no use, within seconds she’s crying and attracting attention again. I smile a belligerent smile as I cast a glance around the room. I pull my chair closer towards the table and reach an arm out, wanting her to calm down and stop crying for my own selfish reasons. I think about how her crying and noise is reflecting on me, what these snooping idiots have running through their minds as they gleam over. I think about the hurry I’m in, the fact that I’ve got to say bye to Cal and then catch my plane. I think about getting to Washington, and making an appearance at the next show, even though I haven’t been signed up for a match. I think about skipping the show entirely, which is the most likely action I’ll take and stopping at Texas instead. Still there is something pinning me down in my seat. Keeping me with Katie. Something I don’t know stops me from getting up and leaving. “I killed him” It almost sounds like someone else is speaking. I look up. My mind goes blank. Katie shakes her head, tears still streaming down her face. Silent tears. “I killed him... I did, I killed him” Her voice becomes a panicked whisper. “Who?” I ask, even though I’m not sure I want to know the answer. I want to tell her this is no time for sniveling, but then how would I know? “David” she hides her mouth behind her hand. “Your boyfriend?” She nods. I thought he was chasing her, wasn’t he the reason she came in so scared? I become aware of our surroundings again. There is no one close enough to hear. The couple that were sat behind me got up and left not long after Katie started to smoke. They made a complaint loud enough for us to hear and then left in annoyance after the girl behind the counter had shrugged her shoulders, claiming that it wasn’t her problem. “I told myself he was going to kill me, I pretended I thought he was going to kill me, even though I knew he wasn’t.. and I killed him” Katie confesses, breathing into her hand. She screws the cigarette out against the table, even though it burned itself out minutes ago. “I asked God to let me kill him.. and.. he did.. let me” When she looks up at me, I can tell she’s waiting for me to say something. But no words form in my mouth. This is a good example of why I try and avoid people. Why I don’t like involving myself with strangers. As soon as you give them a shoulder, they drag you right into their problems. And you never see it coming. And if you’re not careful, they drag you right down with them. “And now I’m so scared, and I’m going to die.. And this- this is what he felt” Katie sobs, her face disappearing behind both her hands. Still no words form in my mouth. We sit quietly for a moment, Katie sobs into her hands and I sit blankly, trying to decide what the best thing is for me to do. Maybe this is the time to get up and leave. I don’t want to lose my opportunity to get out. I don’t want my chance to catch the wind like a spider’s thread, and be lost to me. “You killed him?” I hope she can hear me. Katie nods, still sobbing. I try to imagine what she’d be like without the drugs, without the drink and the smoking. If her face and hair were clean, if those long marks down her arm weren’t on show, if she had a natural smile and life in her eyes. It’s hard and I give up before I have a picture in my mind. She looks older than she is. I decide that if she were in a different life and had made different choices, she’d be beautiful. “He can’t hurt you then” I say, more to myself than Katie. This puts my theory out the window. If he’s dead, he can’t be the one she’s worrying about. I imagine a pissed of ghost, but that’s still not enough to kill her. I used to live down there, says the ghost, pointing down at the Earth. If I didn’t walk through walls, I’d kill her he says. I smile, looking at Katie. “He can’t hurt you”. “You don’t understand” Katie says, dropping her arms to the table. “There is nothing you can do.. it’s going to happen”. “No, I don’t understand” I shake my head. “Because you won’t tell me what you’re talking about..” “I like you, you’re a good strong person” Katie says, wiping her wet face. “Look, I don’t think you need to worry. I don’t agree with what you’ve done, but.. you just need to get out of here. Get away from it all, start something new” I offer a smile. Truth is, no ones going to miss a homeless kid, drugged up to their eyeballs. Some would say he had it coming, even as the knife is pulled from his body. Katie shakes her head “I’ve no where to go.. it’s pointless anyway” “No, I can give you money” I say. She blinks at me. “I’m not a charity case, but I can give you the money to get out... then you’re on your own..” “Am already on my own” she says matter-of-factly. “I’ll be fine, even on my own. I can take care of myself. It’s all fine” she says looking straight at me. The clock on the far wall behind her catches my attention. “So you’re going to be alright?” I reason. It’s hard to imagine where the past two hours has gone. She nods. “Fine”. “You sure?” “I’m sure. Thank you for listening, for being here... thank you” she smiles up at me as I stand up, collecting my coat from the back of my chair. “This is just what I wanted” she nods in appreciation. “Look, you should seriously think about getting out, start a new life. You don’t have to die to get a new life” I smile “Just get out.. don’t be so paranoid”. Katie nods and says another thanks. In my hurry to meet Cal, I forget about the madness. As quickly as Katie entered my life, she exits it. It doesn’t take me long to get there. I expect Cal to be waiting for me, ready and waiting to give me a lecture about being on time, his finger wagging in my face for emphasis. Not that he’d be expecting me on time at all. Something always comes up, or I’m deliberately late just to wind him up. Although I don’t suspect he’d believe my excuse for my delay today at all. “So, you’re a teller of stories, some of them tall” he’d said to me back when I first got to know him. And this is the icing to the cake. I’m ten minutes from his home and the road is blocked by a crowd in the street. About ten people all huddled together, some people are a little further away, their back turned to the rest of the crowd. I stop the car. I don’t even need to get out to know that there has been an accident. Someone is sprawled out on the road, and these are the vultures. I wait for a moment. Nobody moves. I drum my fingers on the steering wheel. Patience is a virtue. As I get out of the car and close the door, a few of the crowd turn to look at me. Their eyes quickly avert back to the body lay out before them. I hate people like this. Why human misery is so entertaining I never know. “Has somebody called an ambulance?” I ask the obvious as I get closer. Through the crowd I can see a bloody leg, a glistening roller-skate, my worst nightmare. I step through the crowd, and one by one they step aside to let me past. Until there is no one else in front of me, and all I can do is look down. My stomach drops. My heart beat seems to stop before immediately speeding up, banging hard against my chest. Oh God. No.
