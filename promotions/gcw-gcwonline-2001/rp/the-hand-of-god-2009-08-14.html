The Hand of God

14 August 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/14/09 Six months ago ... A thin layer of snow covered the empty streets and sidewalks, like a white blanket over everything. The wind was blistery cold, howling like a wild banshee, its bite on unprotected skin felt like that of a serpent's fangs sinking into flesh, then releasing its ice-cold venom into its victim's bloodstream. Old Man Winter had staked his claim on the land, leaving no doubt who would reign until the first thaw in March. Footsteps in the freshly packed snow, making a crunching sound with each step, broke through the moaning of the wind, as three individuals made their way along the deserted sidewalk. The footprints they'd left in their wake were already being swallowed up by the continuously falling snow, erasing evidence of their passage. Two of them walked side by side, while the third -- the shorter, but stockier of the three -- trudged along behind them, grumbling to himself. "Bah," the shorter man, Thibbledorf 'Rumple' Stitskin, complained. "Only a darned fool would be out in this weather!" One of the taller men, the slender one with a skeletal face, named Darius Drannen, glanced over his shoulder at his bellyaching companion and shot him a taunting wink. "And only an even bigger one would follow the fool willingly, no?" "Ah, eat me soiled underthings, ye dolt," Rumple retorted. He stopped and planted his fists on his hips. "Me legs ain't built fer walkin' through all this snow. And what's worse, me balls're frozen solid!" "That's one mental image I could've done without," Drannen leaned close to the third member of their party and said sarcastically. Overhearing the flippant remark, Rumple waved a pudgy fist threateningly at Drannen and said, "Yer all talk, boy. If it ain't for Cade bein' at yer side, I'da knocked yer stinkin' teeth down yer throat already. Matter of fact, maybe I oughta do it now. Might help keep me warm." "If you don't shut up," said the third member of the group, who had thus far suffered the other two's constant bickering in stoic silence. Without turning back, Cade Carson said, "I'll shut you up myself." Rumple opened his mouth as if to argue, but quickly thought better of it. Clamping his mouth shut with obvious difficulty, he ignored the victorious smirk on Drannen's face, and trotted at a fast pace to catch up to the other two men. "Will ye at least tell me where we're goin'?" Rumple asked, fighting for breath, his tiny, tree trunk-thick legs pumping as fast as they could to keep up. Cade stopped suddenly, turning to face his vertically challenged companion. With his jacket's hood up and a scarf covering his mouth and nose, all that could be seen of him was his dark brown eyes, which in a certain light almost appeared red. "A meeting with an old acquaintance." As Rumple appeared to be on the verge of asking for clarification, Cade said, "And that's all you need to know." Cade turned, then, and resumed his march through the almost knee-high snow, toward their destination. Rumple glared at Drannen, whose smirk had never left his expression, then grumbled, "Bah!" and threw his hands up in the air in a gesture of defeat, before continuing on with great reluctance. ~ ~ ~ As they walked, Cade lost himself in his thoughts. He could faintly hear his friends bickering behind him, but it was as if he was listening to them underwater. The deeper into his memories he sank, the more muted their argument became, until he no longer could make out a word of it. Even the bitter cold seemed to dissipate, until the wind, the chill, and the snow was nothing more than a vaguely cool sensation against his skin. Instead, his mind went back in time, to when he was barely in his teens. His parents had abandoned him, and he'd moved from home to home each time his foster families grew tired of him, never giving him a chance to get settled. He was a vagabond, a boy who drifted from place to place, without ever finding his place in this world. And then I met him. Father Michea, a kindly man in his mid-fifties, who was the pastor of the neighborhood church where Cade's first foster parents had attended services each Sunday morning. He had a thick salt-and-pepper beard that covered most of his rosy cheeks, his thinning brown hair was also streaked through with shades of gray. And he always wore the same outfit; a pair of black pants and a matching black button-up long sleeved dress shirt, regardless of whether he was conducting a sermon or at the supermarket buying groceries. For reasons Cade only came to understand later, Father Michea showed an immediate interest in him, eyeing him in the crowd each Sunday when Cade and his foster family had attended service. One Sunday, which coincidentally happened to be the last weekend Cade would be staying with that foster family, Father Michea had introduced himself to Cade's foster mother and father and, by extension, to Cade as well. Cade could still recall in vivid detail the first words Father Michea had said to him those many years ago. After his foster dad had introduced Cade to the kindly priest, he had shaken Cade's hand and bent to whisper in Cade's ear, "Don't worry, son. God is watching over you. Always," and then shared with him a conspiratorial wink. At the time, Cade could only nod his head and frown, unsure what the priest's cryptic words had meant. A week later, when Cade had already been moved into his second foster parents' house, he'd heard the news -- he hesitated to call it 'sad' news, since he'd never gotten along with either of them -- that his former foster parents had been killed in a home invasion. Despite the loss of life, and despite a momentary pang of guilt for such thoughts, Cade couldn't help the grin that threatened to blossom on his face. He recalled the words the priest, the kindly man named Father Michea, had said to him, and thought to himself, I guess God really is watching over me. Shaking his head, returning from his trip down memory lane to the present, Cade couldn't help the sardonic laugh that escaped between his lips. He had no doubt that the events that had followed were guided by an invisible hand. But he knew with crystal clear certainty that that hand did not belong to God. ~ ~ ~ "We walked through a darned snow storm for this?" Once again, the complaints flowed from the mouth of the short but powerfully built man known as Rumple. Bushy reddish-brown beard, a thick head of messy hair, a big red nose, and the kind of wrinkled, weathered skin you'd expect from fishermen who spent most of their days on the open seas, he looked like one of those dwarfs right out of a fantasy movie. "A fackin' church? And it ain't even Sunday, for cryin' out loud!" Darius Drannen sidled up to him and whispered in a conspiratorial tone. "Don't you know it's a sin to swear in church?" Rumple shot him a scornful glare. "First off, ya dimwit, we ain't inside the church. If we were, we'd not be freezin' our cherries off. And second," he tapped two fingers to his temple, "Any sane person'd know God ain't real. He's a fictional character the church'd thinked up to get all these poor saps to support 'em. If he's real, he'd strike me down for me blasphemy." Just then, the rumble of thunder shook the fillings in whatever teeth were left in Thibbledorf Stitskin's mouth, and his smug grin was replaced by a sudden look of wide-eyed worry. "Oh, Jebus. I'm sorry for ever doubtin' ya ..." Rumple began, his gaze darting skyward. But he was cut short by the raucous laughter of Drannen, who was pointing in the direction of a massive snowplow which had just turned onto the street they were on, and was making a trail through the foot and a bit high snow. Its blade was lowered, and as it scraped along the snow-covered roads, the sound of blade on asphalt made a thunder-like noise which echoed through the otherwise quiet streets. Rumple felt his cheeks go red, then muttered, "Ah, shut it, boy. I was just messin' with ya." "Quiet, the both of you." They turned at the sound of their companion's voice, to see Cade standing at the top of the steps that lead up to the front door of the church. He had his back to them, and his hand seemed to be hovering over the brass handle of the door, as if he was afraid to touch it. Then, as if finally mustering up the courage, he reached for the door handle and pulled the door open. He went in first, with Drannen following behind him, and Rumple entering last. They stood in what appeared to be a lobby, where the priest would stand and greet his parishioners as they entered or exited, away from the actual church proper, but within the protective confines of the roof and walls of the building. Two tables ran the length of each wall, and on the tables were various pamphlets stacked in neat rows. On each wall were replica paintings of various scenes depicted in the Bible. Cade's eyes lingered on one mosaic, which depicted an elderly priest with one hand resting on the shoulder of a small child. He winced, but neither of his companions seemed to notice. After a few seconds of awkward silence, Rumple glanced between his two companions and said, "So, uh, guys. We gonna just stand here in the lobby lookin' stupid, or what?" "Does it really matter where you stand?" Drannen asked, one eyebrow arched. "You'd look stupid wherever we stood." "Oh, ha-ha! Yer a funny man, Drannen. I bet ya'd look even funnier after I plant me fist in yer grill," Rumple responded, taking a step closer. Cade silenced them both with a menacing glance. He reached up to pull the hood from his head, then unwrapped the scarf from around his nose and mouth. Unzipping his jacket, he steeled himself for a moment, then said over his shoulder, "Wait here. I won't be long", before opening the glass door to the inside of the church proper and walking at a brisk pace down the aisle toward the alter, between the two rows of empty seats. ~ ~ ~ "I'm not sure I'm ready for this," Cade said, his head lowered slightly. He was leaning against a polished wooden desk, his one leg straight, the other bent. His arms were crossed over his chest, and his jaw muscles twitched as he clenched his teeth together, contemplating the uncertainty in his mind that was the reason for him being here. "Of course you are," Father Michea said in his thick Irish accent. "You've been ready for weeks." Cade glanced up and shot the old priest a skeptical look. He saw that the priest had an expression of absolute certainty on his face, the one that Cade had learned through years of experience that brokered no room for debate. When Father Michea gave you that look, you had as much chance convincing him that Jesus Christ had never stepped foot on this Earth as you did convincing him his opinion on whatever subject you were currently discussing with him was wrong. "I ...," Cade struggled for the words. "It's just ..." Father Michea waited patiently for Cade to continue. Cade shook his head in frustration, then looked imploringly into Father Michea's eyes. "What if you're ..." he began to say 'what if you're wrong', but then thought better of it. Instead, he said, "What if I fail to live up to your expectations?" Father Michea offered him a paternal smile. He stood from where he was sitting behind his large polished oak desk, which was covered with various religious trinkets, stacks of papers, and a portrait of himself as a younger man standing on the front steps of the church. He circled his way around the desk to stand with Cade. He placed a comforting hand on Cade's shoulder. Cade did his best to keep from flinching away from the touch. "Remember, failure is only one confessional away from forgiveness," Father Michea reassured him with a loving smile. He lowered his hand from Cade's shoulder, and the younger man seemed to let out the breath he'd been holding. "And as you're already well aware, my office door is always open to you, my son." Cade nodded in a gesture of gratitude. Inside, however, the Catholic priest's last words sent a cold shiver up his spine, bringing up memories of his past. He quickly shut out those thoughts, and moved the conversation along. "I've received a few decent offers, but I'm leaning toward one company in particular." Cade looked at Father Michea, who had made his way back around his desk, and was now sitting with his hands resting on the desktop in front of him. "But, I wanted to hear what you thought before I signed anything." A gentle smile found its way onto Father Michea's face. "I'm honored that my opinion means so much to you." Cade forced himself to smile in response. The priest gestured with a wave of his hand, indicating for Cade to have a seat. As Cade did so, Father Michea said, "So then, tell me about these offers ..." ~ ~ ~ Half an hour later, Cade emerged from his meeting with Father Michea, seeming a lot more at ease than he'd been before he'd gone in. It was as if a giant weight had been lifted from his shoulders. He walked with a quiet confidence in his stride, and even the expression on his face looked different to Drannen. Gone were the haunted eyes, the nervous twitching of his jaw, and the sense of anxiety that kept Cade in constant motion. It was like watching a child emerging from one of those haunted house exhibits at an amusement park, an expression of utter relief on a face that had previously been stricken with fear. Drannen hopped down off the table he'd been sitting on and kicked Rumple in the boot, stirring the little man from a nap he'd been taking on the lobby floor. "Huh?" Rumple yelped in sleep-induced confusion. "What in ...?" "Cade's meeting's done," Drannen said, pointing to their friend, who was making his way toward them down the aisle between the row of wooden benches. "'Bout time," Rumple complained. "Me back's killin' me." Drannen snorted. "Yeah, well, there's a reason most people prefer beds, to sleeping on a dirty floor." "Ah, shut up," was all Rumple had time to say, before Cade had joined them at front door. "You good to go?" Drannen asked Cade. Cade nodded. "Yeah, let's get outta here. This place gives me the creeps." Drannen and Rumple shared a look, before Drannen replied, "You and me both, buddy."
