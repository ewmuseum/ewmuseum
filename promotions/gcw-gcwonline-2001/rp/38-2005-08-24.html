Date: 8/24/05 I'm going to hold your hand. I'm going to hold your hand because I believe you're too stupid to understand the implied. I'm going to hold your hand because I don't credit you as analytical enough to make substance of the subtle. I'm going to hold your hand because you're small, weak, and useless. I'm going to hold your hand because everyone else does. Jordan's laugh is the first thing Trey hears. It rattles like empty beer bottles in his admittedly loaded head, finally coming to a stop after circling inward in tighter and tighter loops. 'You're fucked up, kid. The hell you gotten yourself into?' 'Not much, shit. Woke me up, man.' 'Yeah. And?' Trey thinks for a second and, finding nothing wrong, lets Jordan continue. 'Christ, get your head right.' 'What you mean?' 'I mean, get your head right. How simply you want me to say it? You're fucking crazy--should look into getting that fixed.' 'The hell are you talking about?' 'God damnit. Alright, bluntly, this is your conscience--your Jiminy Cricket--manifest in the form of your asshole best friend and kicking in to tell you that you're starting to look like, I don't know, a goddamned fuck-up.' 'My conscience?' 'Shit. Maybe. Might actually be your asshole best friend. Really though, matter much to you either way?' Trey shakes. Means 'no.' 'Yeah, exactly. Someone, some entity, some whatever's calling you out and definitely refusing to fade away, no matter how hard you squint your eyebrows and try to pass back out. You've gotta deal with it. So, deal.' 'Man, I just got to sleep. Lemme fuckin', y'know, crash.' 'No.' The maybe conscience, maybe manager slinks over to the bed, endlessly pulling on a Newport. He pulls tinted sunglasses down his nose far/long enough to stare Trey for just a second. His fur coat, darker than an acid trip through Alcatraz, trails slowly behind him--clearly caught in a time warp. 'There are things to do, you son of a bitch, other than geeking the fuck out over this brainwashing kick. We get it--you're a lot more fucking angsty than we thought--so wake up and get your ass out of bed. Don't you understand when the word 'imperative' can actually be used?' 'Never really thought about it.' Trey jerks the cover over his head and rolls to his side. 'Well, this is one of those times. There's evil afoot, mad fucking evil hell-bent on crushing the world.' 'Shit. We're fucked.' 'God damnit. If I have to shoot you and save the world myself, I will.' 'Fuckin',' mumbles our hero before sitting up in the bed. 'Look, J, s'been three days since I slept, so, y'know, shut the fuck up. Too tired for jokes.' 'No one's joking here. This is serious. Why else would I completely ruin this elusive image I've been working at for a few weeks? Assuming that I'm not just your conscience, I mean.' 'Shyeah. My conscience.' 'Don't give me that emotionally distant shtick, asshole. You've got a conscience.' 'No shit; just never pictured it as you.' 'Should've--you'd have been ahead of the game right now. No time to worry about catching up though, there are things to be done.' 'Last time man--no means no.' 'I don't think you understand, Trey.' A cloud of smoke rolls from the lungs of what I'm calling Jordan from now on to ease confusion. 'You're coming with me. I tried to trust you by yourself long enough so I could clear up some things of my own, and I take a break from that, all that shit that actually matters to my life, to find you coming down in a shitty hotel bed? We're not playing games anymore, kid. You earned your fucking leash back.' 'Fuck you, man.' 'That a sniffle I heard in there? I know you're not getting sick, so what is it? Fallen into the candy?' Trey rolls again, burying his brain in a soft white pillow. The moment lasts long enough for Jordan to tear the sanctuary away, still roaring like the damned. '-damnit, you listless motherfucker. I'm lighting you on fire in five, four, three...' And the only person guaranteed to be real, at least for the sake of the narrative, snaps up. He snorts deep into the back of his throat and wipes his nose, easing out a hiding drip. 'What's up, J?' 'Oh, not much. Standing in awe of a cokehead letting me keep him from sleep.' 'Ain't a cokehead, man.' 'Fuckin' Christ, sure, you're not, whatever, doesn't matter. Just throw some clothes on--something nice, we're blowing lots of cash--and meet me outside. And don't try barricading the door, that's not how I got in.' In the time lapse of a blink, Trey's alone. He fishes the pack of Marlboro Reds from his nightstand and clumsily lights one for his shambling. The clothes are whatever clothes, the overpriced slacks and the required thigh-length leather jacket, down to the Cheap Trick t-shirt stretched on underneath in a hasty fit of rebellion--one engineered to plunge him into the dank world of life with too much shit on. Cold shivers hold his spine erect for now, the big come down from that sainted dream of an everlasting rush. The drip fails him, a half-hearted amount in a battery acid sludge, and he doesn't notice the hole burnt through the chest of the shirt until after the hand's been dealt. Responding, he kills the Red quicker than he'd expected and goes to light another from the filter when Jordan's remembered. The shamble becomes a dash and he jets into the hallway--flinging the useless butt to the floor and dragging the survivor in peace. Alone. In his head for a second, at least. 'Got money?' That hazy sanctuary breaks and Trey turns to see Jordan. More silhouette than man for the setting sun streaming in the massive plate glass windows behind him, he's laughing and smoking a Newport. Conflict. Trey nods. 'Good, you'll need it.' 'What's the plan, man?' 'There's this strip club downtown I've been meaning to check out.' 'You're fuckin' with me.' 'Why would I fuck with you? We went through this before: got nowhere then, going nowhere now. This place, Dame's, I've heard it's fucking magnificent. Building it's in was a theatre back in the 30's, one of those classic types with the tiered balconies and covered with those art deco pillars and swirls; ended up closing down for about sixty years--still one of the only ones lucky enough to survive all those fuckin' earthquakes. Yeah, got rebought, owners fixed it up to the original conditions and ended up with this sprawling house of sin; private rooms in the balconies, constant influx of music, best bartenders in the goddamned state, and women like angels. It's a Mecca, the ultimate place for a guy to completely unfuck whatever bad shit's gotten into his head.' Trey orders another drink and turns back to Jordan. 'This ain't exactly savin' the world, y'know.' 'We'll get there.' The woman in front of Jordan, taller than him for the heels, spreads her legs and leans forwards until her brunette hair flows to the stage and her eyes lock in. He throws another twenty out. 'And besides, this is a lot more important than you think.' 'What, gettin' a lapdance?' 'Clearly. Your nose.' 'Huh?' 'You've got something.' 'Where?' 'On your nose.' Trey wipes without looking, the drunken twist of lights useless in lending any clarity to the situation. 'Thanks.' 'It's nothing, sicko.' 'What?' 'You know what I mean. Don't try and hide it from your conscience--no way you're that stupid. Something happened, something bad, and it's still eating at you. Right?' A shrug. 'Be noncommittal, doesn't bother me. I'm not the one falling under to ghosts here.' 'Seriously, Jordan. S'cool.' 'Yeah. Always is.' Brunette spins lazily around the pole and splays backwards to her hands with a smile. She springs back up and unhooks her top, tossing it at Jordan. A light strobes white, reveals it as lilac--he takes it in with a pointless chuckle. ''When men study monsters, they become monsters too. Gaze not into the abyss, for-'' ''-it gazes also into you.' Blake?' 'No, Nietszche. Looks like you're remembering.' 'Always remembered.' 'Figured. How much?' 'Bits an' pieces. Few words here, few words there, y'know. Enough to get the picture.' 'Guess you liked it.' A shrug. 'You went along with it anyways. Needed a break from being old lackluster Trey, but you just couldn't shake the idea that you really did suck. Let it boost your confidence some. Right?' A nod. 'Right. Now, you've come to realize a few things about the real you that don't sit too well by how you want to view yourself--happens to all of us sometimes. Some of us deal with it as teenagers. Some of us just let it keep piling up until it's easier to ignore than to fix, and it works pretty well as long as no one brings it up.' Trey stares. 'And I forced you to deal with it. You could finally vent, pure rage and steam and terror, until you fucked it up. Told me to stop. It was too late. You'd figured out that there was a way to release--no way you're ditching it. Am I caught up yet?' 'Gettin' there.' 'Fuck. That was my best guess. One more shot though; there's this monster lurking beneath the waves that scares the shit out of you more than anything else--that thing inside that just let you zone out all killing machine without caring.' 'Sounds cool.' 'Good. Perfect for the job.' 'What job?' 'Saving the world.' 'Thought we just did.' 'You egotistical bastard. Survival of the human race doesn't fall solely on the beleaguered breasts and gyrating asses of these lovely ladies. Fuck, they are helping though. Gotta get you in the right mood first.' 'Don't really feel like the right mood for a savior to have, y'know.' 'No one's beyond the allure of pussy, and no medicine is so easily applied and universally useful. Just chill and keep drinking, it'll be a minute until shit really starts to get heavy.' 'Just a minute? Damn.' He calls the bartender for shots. 'It's a phrase--more like twenty minutes.' 'Still dunno if I got the time to get lit enough.' 'You've spent three weeks lit enough. Bought the ticket, might as well take the ride.' 'Small World?' 'Little more menacing. So, what do you say?' 'Didn't think I had a choice.' 'You always have a choice. Don't you listen to that voice in your head? I know you regret at least half of the shit you've pulled over the last year, and there's no way a person like you is able to sleep at night for all the second guessing. Can't pretend you don't have choices.' 'I say no, then.' 'Smart call. Let's see how much it matters.' 'You're always gonna be a sick bastard.' 'Yeah, but it's not me this time. Just fate.' 'Neither of us buy that fate shit. C'mon.' 'Like it or not, shit happens. We don't cause everything we fix, and we sure as hell don't fix everything we cause.' 'An' this is just fallin' in my lap, right?' Trey lights another cigarette and kills a shot with zeal. 'It's hard to take your ass seriously, y'know?' 'I noticed. Persistent as cancer, though.' 'Some sorta double meanin' there?' 'Didn't think one out, nah. You can force one, if you want--maybe some parallel between cancer ravaging your system and me crushing your soul, but that's pushing it.' 'Syeah, you're alright. Even for all that shit you pull.' 'Save the hero worship, things call me.' Trey nods his head back and looks to Brunette. Chilled blue eyes reflect in his; patient ones that seem almost ignorant of their owner thrusting her barely covered clam in the faces of strangers and strange people alike. They're alone together and still miles apart for the holy shield that protects the stage from the clientele, self-absorbed enough to not spend a word on the other. He puts a fifty out and the girl flashes purple-then-green teeth while slipping the bill into her garter. It's one-sided camaraderie that lasts as long as someone's fronting money; the exclusive club of one and their sacred image of a friend. She sprawls to her elbows and knees on the polished stage and slides to Trey, lips pursed and expression straight. 'You look down. Need any help?' He laughs. 'Not lookin' for what you're sellin'.' 'Don't be so quick to judge. You're still young.' 'Not that young.' 'Time's measured in what you do,' she moans as her pelvis slips over the floor to Trey, 'not how long it takes to get there. We're not all ditzy sluts, kid. I was listening to you earlier, think I might know how to help you out.' 'Listenin' doesn't mean you caught on. Hell, I'm just bullshittin' along half the time.' 'Well, I'm here.' I'm going to hold your hand because things don't always make sense. I'm going to hold your hand because not everything matters. I'm going to hold your hand because it makes you feel awkward. But, still, I'm going to hold your hand.
