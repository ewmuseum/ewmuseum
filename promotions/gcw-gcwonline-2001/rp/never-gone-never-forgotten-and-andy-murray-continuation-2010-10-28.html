Never Gone, Never Forgotten: And Andy Murray Continuation

28 October 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/28/10 His hands hung comfortably in his pockets as he looked over the St. Louis skyline. The breeze was gentle against his Scottish frame and he breathed to take in the general taste of the city. While he knew what it was like to live the good life, he had forgotten what it was like to live without worries. With John Hood’s signature, everything that had sprouted since Reuben’s untimely death had finally fallen back into place giving the former world champion time to cope. Moreover, his victory over Tim Shipley brought a long awaited peace to his career. Although it took two weeks to settle, Andy Murray finally recognized himself as the king once again. Shutting his eyes, he thought of the people he had pushed away and the conversations he had feigned. James Bryan was trying to help, Sid wanted him to slow down, and everyone else backstage wanted him to make the right decision. Although he couldn’t charm the idea that he’d seen a doctor and received the scan, he could honestly say that he felt better. There was closure in the bile and disgust that he spilled on the Houston roadside. It was an honest cleansing of his entire system that seemed to have ultimately stemmed from the stress of an unruly time period. With his back turned to the turmoil, he knew that things were certainly going to continue to pick up as time went by. Dangerous Games was around the corner and he’d be lying if he said he didn’t want to walk out the winner. Of all the things he had done in his career and the names he had beaten, Dangerous Games would be the capstone to it all. There was an heir of brilliance that came with outsmarting twenty-nine others, and a sense of true accomplishment that he could carry in his pocket for the rest of his life. While he may not agree with the elite list of past winners at times, there was no doubting the boost their careers took after taking home the ultimate glory. If not for the boost, Murray knew that he could officially claim that he’d done all he could in GCW and no one, not even Jay Terror, could refute it. Running his hands through his thick Scottish mane, he turned his back to the city and made his way back inside. The hotel was well-furnished, equipped with some of the more comfortable mattresses and chairs he’d seen in some time. Still, comfort from amenities wasn’t what he was looking for. While they were a nice reminder of his lifestyle, they didn’t bring his son on the road or his wife back. As lavish as they made his life seem, it was a lonely life and one that would have to do. Laying down on the comforter, he shut his eyes as the fan blades spun overhead. As he started to drift off though, a piercing knock startled him. He sat upright and looked to the door where another knock rang through. Murray seemed confused. He stood and made his way over, taking a look through the peep hole. An unrecognizable man in a mustache stood on the other side with a hat in his hands. Murray grabbed the handle and opened the door for him. “Hello,” he inquired. The man smiled and spread his legs in a comfortable stance. He had a long beige coat on and a casual shirt and pants underneath. “Andy Murray, I presume?” Murray flicked a brow and nodded warily. It wouldn’t be new if the man was a fan looking for an autograph or picture. “Yep, that’s me. What can I do you for, sir?” His voice was low and measly with a smoker’s accent. It reminded Murray of the moments him and Jay Terror spoke. “I’m Frank Gianetto,” he said with an extended hand. Murray accepted it and shook with a strong grip. “I’m a detective for the Houston police department and heard that you were in St. Louis for the week. Mind if I ask you a few questions as it relates to the last time you were in Houston?” Andy was taken back by his profession and on top of that he was curious of what he’d need to know about Houston. It was two weeks past by now, obviously too long to see justice about knocking one of John Hood’s teeth out, and that was the extent of his business. Nevertheless, he shrugged and stepped aside. “Sure. Please come in.” Frank Gianetto waved his hat and courteously stepped into the hotel room. Unlike the King, he wasn’t used to plush amenities and spacious rooms on the road. Taking a seat on a nearby chair, he let out a huff and placed his hat on the table beside him. Murray opted for a spot on the edge of the bed and leaned in, looking as intent as possible. “I’m going to be honest, Houston is beyond me at this point. There were a lot of things on my mind that I recently suppressed and if I don’t recollect it all, that’s the reason,” Murray admitted, honestly. Frank nodded, “Understandable. If you don’t mind though, I’d like to start by talking about your relationship with John Hood. You two were friends?” The name was still a sour note in his mind and he did his best not to scowl. Their history was inescapable and he knew that no matter how far apart they were, his name would always find its way of haunting him. “Hood was my manager before he was arrested. I’m a wrestler and he represented me under the Dallas Group.” “And he was placed under arrest because of an event that happened between him and Reuben Dallas, correct?” Andy nodded. Reuben Dallas was another name he’d likely never forget, thankfully for other reasons though. With everything that Reuben had done for Andy, Murray believed that he owed him more than he could give. While watching him die was the most painful experience he could have imagined, he knew that he was in a better place. His struggle with cancer was a hard fought battle that Murray wished upon no one. It was his compassion for the man that led him to a deeper hatred for John Hood and the heinous act he did. “That’s correct,” Andy replied. “Trust me, Hood deserves every second he can get behind bars.” “Which makes me wonder why you were the one to post his bail,” the detective trailed off. For the first time in the conversation, Andy felt like he was being interrogated for a crime. “I’m not sure what all this is about, but I have a feeling something happened that I don’t know about.” Frank reached into his pocket and pulled loose a photograph. He handed it to Murray to look over and digest before speaking. “That’s the scene we found after arriving at John Hood’s residence two weeks ago. A fire tore his home to the ground and in the end took the life of Hood. He was burned alive and we have reason to believe that this wasn’t an accident.” Murray recognized the trees and mailbox. He’d been to the home enough times to see the similarities and debunk a bluff. As Andy searched for words, his eyes continued to scan the picture and wonder what had happened. Two weeks ago, Murray had forced his hand to sign the deeds to the Dallas Group, but he didn’t remember a fire. He remembered the smell of vodka, Hood’s blood that he had wiped on his shorts, and the vile words that Hood spoke about Andy’s home life. Nowhere in his memory was there a fire let alone a murder. Andy looked back at the officer and then the photo. Gianetto crossed his arms and shoved his weight into the back of the chair. He swayed with it carefully. “We were told that you were seen leaving his house earlier that night and that’s why we need to know about the relationship you had with Hood. We need to know what happened that night and why you posted his bail, Andy. You understand?” Murray understood, but he was still too flabbergasted to form a sentence. As much as John Hood hated Andy Murray for the incident with his wife, Murray wasn’t one to reciprocate it with murder. He was hesitant to even punch the drunk fool. Finally finding something, he looked at the detective. “I didn’t kill him. I don’t think I could kill a man let alone John Hood.” “That’s a good start,” he said. “Tell me about your guys relationship. It sounds like you two had some kind of dispute by the way you talk about what happened with Dallas.” “Reuben Dallas was cheated out of his company on his death bed and you expect me to see eye to eye with that?” “I think you misunderstood me, Mr. Murray.” Andy realized the tone he had taken and shook it off. “Look, this is all a bit of a shock. John Hood and I haven’t always seen eye to eye and him doing that to Reuben was the straw that broke the camels back, figuratively. On top of that, I had personal issues that I was tending with having to do with work. To put it simply, I wasn’t in much of a mindset to deal with people like John or anyone else. In fact, I was ready to move past everything and try and figure things out on my own before I spoke with John’s lawyer.” “You approached him?” “He approached me. Called me.” Andy pictured the large man in the park petitioning to lock Hood away for good. While he was an accessory to the crime, he was a good man with a true heart to admire. “With everything that Hood did and the fact that he was in jail, he wanted to know if I’d help in putting him away for good.” “Yet you set bail for him,” intruded the detective. Murray shrugged. “I didn’t want a trial.” “I don’t think I’m following you, sir.” As Andy tried to recollect his thoughts and remember the events before John’s death, he could feel the pounding returning. It had been two weeks since his last spell and like a bad dream, the migraine symptoms came knocking on his skull. He could hardly hear the detective talking and only watched his lips move through fuzzy eyes. Grabbing his head, he could feel a hand on his shoulder. Murray looked up at the detective and tried to talk. “I thi tha a an,” he trailed off. He didn’t know he wasn’t completing the words and tried again. “Yo ha to hel.” Before he knew it, Murray was on his back staring at the spinning ceiling fan. The blades were spinning faster and faster making him more and more dizzy. Shutting his eyes, he remembered seeing Frank Gianetto on his cell phone. --- There was still something special about toppling Tim Shipley. Whether it was bragging rights or the fact that he could look a man in the eyes without seeing that vile face, he didn’t care. Making it even better, Murray was able to watch him pack his bags and hit the road before anyone knew he was gone. Even through their turbulent years together between AWC, JUST, and GCW, Murray knew that he did what was right. For too long had Shipley gone on trying to ruin Andy’s good name and reputation. When it came down to it, every man had to pay a price and Tim Shipley just so happened to meet his maker early. With that past, Murray could finally see a sun on the horizon. It crested slightly over the hill showing what the future could look like. Getting there though was no easy road. There was blood, sweat, and tears to shed in order to place his name on top of the record books and into eternal glory. While he’d tasted success in all of its sugary forms, there was still one parade he hadn’t attended and that was Dangerous Games. In his time with the company, he had watched the match and seen winners such as Vivica, Roberto, and Jamison each come out with their hands raised. On his chest though, he hadn’t pinned in his Dangerous Games patch. Thankfully, that ship was finally boarding. With Tim Shipley in the dust, Andy Murray had no distractions. He was a decorated man stepping into the main event with relative nobodies. Besides Vivica, he was one of the only people to hold the world title, let alone for such a long streak. What felt like a cake walk would possibly be. Like any great man though, Andy Murray never treated it as such. He trained harder than most and put his body out there. Of course, it paid off in the long run, but anyone who claimed that he didn’t spend the time to become someone would be dead wrong. As easy as people would claim he had it, there was more to it behind the scenes. When it came down to it, there was no measure of how successful a person could be in the battle royal. Anyone could see their feet hit first or last. What Andy knew was that he was more ready for it than anything else in his life. Behind every great star is an equally great game plan and Andy Murray knew that he would walk in prepared. He was ready to win it for Reuben, for Sid, for Marvin, for Cayle, and for everyone that believed in him. He was ready to win it for himself. --- When Andy Murray awoke, he was in the back of Detective Frank Gianetto’s car. The guess was fueled by Frank’s frivolous driving and the siren wailing overhead. Andy grabbed his skull to try and suffocate the constant whining of the siren but it was to no avail. Sitting up, he watched Frank turn his eyes away from the road and into the back seat. “Mr. Murray, please lay down. We’re going to the hospital to get you looked at.” Murray took a second to digest the idea before spitting back, “no.” It was defiant and came out clear unlike his previous sentence. “I’m perfectly fine, Frank.” Frank’s eyes were dashing between the rearview and the road. He watched Murray take his hand off his head and then coax a slight smile. “We’re still going to get you checked out.” “It’s a migraine and that siren isn’t helping, detective. Honestly, nothing’s wrong. I haven’t eaten today and that’s what I get.” Murray’s plea seemed reasonable, even to Andy himself, but the siren continued to scream and the car continued to speed forward. Frank was passing the ideas back and forth trying to make sense of the situation, but his mind told him to keep going. He’d been trained to find the hospital in times like those, and he was one to stick to training. “Look, it’s protocol to have you checked out. It might be a migraine but it doesn’t defeat the fact that you fainted. I’d feel better taking you in to get it checked out.” “Not to be rude,” Murray started. “But we’re in St. Louis, not Houston. What’s protocol there shouldn’t apply here. Besides, I’m awake. I’m OK. Please, just turn off the siren and pull over. I’d rather get to the questions again and we can both be on my way.” Again, Frank seemed to think about the compromise. He was a smart man, but he also knew the importance of talking with Murray. Buying into Murray’s ruse, he flicked the siren switch off giving Andy a chance to breathe regularly again. His head was pounding loud enough that he was afraid Gianetto would hear. Luckily, he didn’t say a word and instead negotiated a left turn into a parking lot. There was an abandoned gas station in the center of it and Frank pulled the car beside an old pump. He turned to Murray and shrugged. “If you say so. When all of this is said and done though, I want you to get checked out. If you have something and know about it then that’s one thing, but if this is new, I don’t want to be responsible for not getting you to see someone.” “I’ll get right on it. I promise,” he said in a convincing manner. It had become a standard response when asked about his recent fragile spells. “Honestly, it’s most likely the food and the migraine.” “I’ll buy it.” An awkward silence went over the car, neither man knowing where to go from there. While Frank wanted to ask questions, he was afraid of coming off indecent. Murray on the other hand didn’t care either way. To him, the sirens had finally turned off and he was beginning to feel comfortable in the back seat. Feeling like he should make a move, Murray moved his lips first. “So what did you want to know about John?” Relieved, Frank flipped open a pad of paper. He’d been taking notes. “You mentioned that you didn’t want a trial. A trial to put John Hood in prison for life?” Murray nodded. “In my mind, going to trial meant that there’d be a dark cloud following me for the next six months. While it might have been worth it to see him squirm, it wasn’t worth my well being or my family’s. I take my career seriously because it provides for them. Losing sight of that would bring me just as low as John would likely feel.” “So you posted bail for him to roam free.” “I posted bail to meet him that night. That’s why I was at his home.” Gianetto rubbed his hairless chin and scribbled it onto his pad. “What happened during that meeting?” Murray paused, wondering how he should handle it. While he wanted to admit to punching him in the face and forcing his hand to sign the deed, he knew that it might cause more harm than good. There was a fine line between retribution and decency and crossing it might be enough to sway the jury’s votes against him in a murder trial. Andy looked Frank in the eyes. “I showed up and he’d been drinking. In fact, he was really drunk. After the note he showed, me, I understood it too.” “I’m sorry,” Frank interupted. “What note?” “Right,” Andy remembered. “His wife had left him a note telling him how she couldn’t live with a man who’d do such a thing to a dying man. It said that she was taking the kids and that he wouldn’t be a part of their lives any longer. It was sad, but it was deserved in a sense.” While Andy hated to talk down on a man, John Hood had taken a special seat in his heart leaving little room for cordiality. “Anyways,” he started again. “I brought the set of deeds his lawyer had drafted to his home. All I wanted was a signature saying that he’d sign over the Dallas Group seeing that his name was already tarnished.” Again, Frank interrupted stating, “deeds?” Murray, always with composure, nodded slightly. “Between myself and his lawyer, there was no way that either of saw him living a life of luxury. While he’d be free from trial, he wasn’t going to keep the one thing that he stole in the first place. These deeds were carefully drafted and would turn the Dallas Group over to someone better equipped. Naturally, Hood refused to let go of the last thing that he had left.” “Naturally,” he said while jotting down the notes. “Instead, I continued to push him and remind him of what he’d done. In his state of mind, I figured that maybe his soul would shine through and he’d show a ray of hope. Unfortunately, he continued to refuse.” Andy paused as he thought back to the night. He believed that it wouldn’t be the last time that he recalled the time they spent together. “John Hood was a broken man and he wasn’t going to go down without swinging. Thankfully, we didn’t have to swing.” Frank looked up from his notepad, interested in what would come next. While he believed every step of the story, he also believed that it was strangely leading to the fire. Everything that Murray had said seemed to point to a nasty ending, and Frank was unsure where else it could go. Murray continued. “When it came down to it, I saw a better man in John Hood. he couldn’t keep his game face on long enough to push me aside. In the end, I got his signature and the rights to the Dallas Group.” Andy sold his lie while scoffing at the fact that he admitted Hood was a good man. While he didn’t like making Hood seem human, it was best that he kept the story in a good place. Frank, somewhat letdown by the ending, cocked his brow questioningly. “He just gave in? It doesn’t seem right.” “It still doesn’t seem right to me. I don’t know if it was a step to gain his wife and children back, or simply him finally admitting that he had wronged a good man. Either way, I saw John Hood in a new light before I left and I will never forget it.” “And you left immediately after?” “Immediately,” he echoed. Frank thumbed his notepad. “And what happened with the deeds? Did you file them?” Murray shook his head. “I haven’t yet.” Between travelling from show to show, the extra workouts for Dangerous Games, and his overall general lack of time, he couldn’t find a minute to finish what he’d worked so hard to get. “Then you still have them,” he questioned. “They’re in my hotel room. Would you like to see them?” Frank Gianetto nodded enthusiastically. “You’re damn right.” Although he couldn’t say he believed Murray’s ending to the story, he knew that the documents themselves would be a useful tool when working a case. For all he knew, it could make or break Andy’s future. Turning the keys, the engine came on and Frank pressed the gas pedal. They merged back onto the main road and drove, this time without the siren. --- With Dangerous Games so close, there was still a road bump that Andy Murray had to cross. Devil’s Night. It was customary to showcase the talent that would be competing at the largest pay-per-view of the year, and Andy was no exception. As it stood, Garbage Bag Johnny had shown his prowess at booking events, and the fans were happy to see that Andy Murray would square off with evil himself. Jay Terror. The name had riddled his career since the day he walked through the doors of GCW. It was Terror who ruined his undefeated streak before it could reach seven, and it was Terror barking up his tree whenever he felt like getting rowdy. While Murray held the better record between them in competition, not the mention an impressive NC-17 victory, Terror had become something more than before. He was a growing threat with the title around his waist and a man not to be taken lightly. In the past, Murray had learned to understand Terror and how he worked. With his new mean streak though, there was no telling what he was capable of. Terror had shown no more than a shrug when griding Tessa Windsor’s face across a steel cage, and only a week earlier he nearly tore Tempest in two. It was certain that Terror would come out swinging. Still, there was something that Jay didn’t understand about Andy Murray. He didn’t know the effort he’d been putting in, the extra days he’d been working. He didn’t know how easy it was for him to switch gears with the crowd roaring behind him. As Terror worked with fear, Murray worked with courage and congratulations. He was riled by the fans and pushed to new levels by packed arenas. On top of that, he was the longest reigning world champion in GCW history, a record that Jay had only scratched. While the Dangerous Games match was what he wanted, winning the title from Jay Terror was a chance that didn’t come often. He knew that whatever happened, he’d show that ruthlessness wasn’t the answer. There was a division between talent and pain, and Murray would show that there was no stopping a man with abilities as honed as his. --- Upon returning to the hotel room, they each walked toward the kicked in door with caution. Frank being the detective issued orders for Andy to stay while he drew his pistol from his side. Creeping along the wall, Andy Murray wondered what would have happened if John Hood had surrendered himself and signed the deed. Perhaps he’d still be alive and he’d have a chance to make amends with his wife before the kids had to understand the gravity of it all. As much as Andy wanted to see him suffer, murder wasn’t what he had in mind. Frank finally turned into the room, pistol hanging out front and legs spread, as he looked around. Moving in, Andy could hear him opening the closet doors and bathroom. After a minute, Andy saw him return and tuck the gun away. “Looks like whoever was here didn’t stick around long. He was likely waiting for us to leave.” Andy nodded. “I guess so. What did they want though?” “I was hoping you could check your things,” he prodded. Murray knew it was a good idea and walked into the room. The lamp had been smashed in the middle of the floor, couches turned over, and sheets torn in half. Feeling as if he had an idea, Murray moved to the television stand. Where the used to be a drawer, an empty slide sat. Andy looked around the room until he found the drawer stuffed into the corner. Unfortunately, as he dug through the papers, there was no sign of the one he was looking for. He turned to Frank and sighed. “It was the deed. Whoever it was wanted the deed to the Dallas Group.” Frank let out a heavy breath and shook his head. “I was afraid of that.” “Do you know anyone who may have had interest in them?” “My guess is the same person that killed John Hood to be honest. I don’t know what they’d want them for though.” “Your guess is as good as mine.” He looked around the room again and took another deep breath. “When I woke up the morning, I figured that I’d have things easy. I thought that having you at the scene of Hood’s death was a shoe in. Now,” he paused, now looking at Andy. “Now I don’t know what to make of this. I’m going to be in contact, Mr. Murray. Here’s my card.” Andy took the card and shoved it in his pocket as he had with Hood’s lawyer, Floyd. “I’m free to go?” “I cant hold you on anything and I have something new to figure out. For now, you do whatever you have to. If something like this happens though, my phone better be ringing.” “Of course.” The men shook hands and Frank was gone. While he didn’t know what had happened to the deeds or who had killed John Hood, he was relieved to know that he was free to wrestle Jay Terror and free to prepare for Dangerous Games. Taking a seat on his ruffled bed, he shook his head. No matter how far he turned, he knew that there would always be Reuben Dallas and there would always be John Hood. Dead or not, it was a part of him and he had to either live with it or disappear. Naturally, the King decided that living was always the safest bet. --- The man in his fedora sat comfortably in the back seat of a limousine. Sending a cloud of cigar smoking into the air, he paged through the deeds to the Dallas Group with a cunning grin on his face. As they say, the best job is done yourself, and he couldn’t deny it. Placing the papers on the seat beside him, he leaned back and enjoyed the ride.
