A recollection of the years

30 September 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/30/08 If there is one thing which has always held true for my life it's that I should always go about expecting the unexpected. Growing up I'd always thought that I would be a professional wrestler. Whenever I'd plan anything for the future it was always around that dream ? not of romance, wealth, or fame. I knew where my place would be and when I had finally found a true home for my dream everything took off from there. Damn me. Every time I sit by myself and am alone with my thoughts for long enough I always tend to get retrospective. It's a small yet unsubstantial handicap of mine. As I look over the small pile of applications of these kids who are pining to spend time with me in my hometown of Southern Tracks, Arizona and train to be wrestlers I find that I can't seem to keep complete focus on the task at hand. This bi annual cycle is coming around once again ? when the old leaves and the fresh new faces come to me looking for training and guidance for their passion and hopeful careers in the sport of professional wrestling. I stop staring blankly into the application I am holding with the picture of the rather gawky looking 19 year old from Kentucky to sit back and indulge myself in my retrospection. I truly wasn't getting any work done in this haphazard ?office? of mine anyways. It may as well be a card table with a bare light bulb hanging overhead with a chair and a file cabinet in the corner. There may even be a phone somewhere but it's hardly ever in use as I prefer to use the internet to communicate in this day and age. While the simplistic nature of my observation my be slightly unfair to how things really are, there really isn't more to it than that. How did I get where I am now? There are few people who truly remember much of who I am and what I've done and there is little else than time to blame for such. I've been wrestling ever since I was 19 years old but never truly hit anything home until I made my way into Global Championship Wrestling, my aforementioned home for the dream I held so close to my heart. Me tenure there may have not been one epic in length but I certainly made an impact for my time. I'd been through many things ? some good and some very bad which brought me to face parts of myself I never knew existed. During 2005 I hit a huge bump with my dream; an injury which was constantly re aggravated due to my schedule and pacing as the GCW World Champion, and many personal problems stemming from the unprofessional and cruel nature of a man whom I'd called my friend up until the point to where I saw him come literally inches away from ending my life. Faced with continual injury and a broken spirit I forced myself to take some time off and try and heal physically and mentally. I'd come a long way and fought too hard for much too long. I think everything was finally catching up to me at this point in my life. I took time off and decided to not go through surgery for my neck. While the pain was unbearable when I was on a 3 or 4 day week of fighting for myself and my spot at the top I never wanted to undergo any type of major surgery unless it was absolutely necessary. I spent the next three months of my life essentially sitting back and trying to catch up with myself. I'd been through so much and fought so hard but at the end of the day things just didn't feel the same. I was unhappy and I did not know why. It was at this point that I more carefully developed and mapped out the ideas I'd had for a very small academy for those hungry young individuals, just as I was in my teens, to learn and love more the sport which I devoted my life to. As I rested for those months I started to have an easier time dealing with the pain ? or perhaps it was because the pain was actually diminishing? Either way daily life became easier and easier to manage and I was feeling as good as ever within a relatively short amount of time. I grew increasingly bored with my situation and decided that I needed to get back with my plan. My dream. When my phone rang I immediately jumped for it, as I already knew what was on the other end. I was returning to Global Championship Wrestling and making one hell of a resurgence. One of the unfinished chapters of my career to that point, an old nemesis whom I'd fought many times before but never to the point of victory or defeat, was at the other end of a return and it was an opportunity I couldn't pass up for the world. That night came and went flawlessly. I was victorious in the right after one hell of a fight if I do say so myself ? but there was just one problem at the very end of it all. The victory, the night, and everything about it all felt hollow. There was nothing wrong, everything went spectacular! At the end of everything I felt like my place wasn't there, and I knew I could never stay if my heart wasn't in the right place. Steven Caldera and I have not always seen eye to eye on everything since our fallout but I have to give him the credit that he's a very savvy businessman. I explained my situation to him at the time as best as there were words for and I think for the first time in a long time there was a one hundred percent understanding between the two of us. While wanting me to stay with the company he was more than happy to allow me a release for my current standings. He's a very tough man at times but that was one of the few times when I think I've also ever seen the very fair side of him. To this day I can't thank him enough for the understanding. My Hall of Fame inductance a year later was perhaps one of the best moments of my life and made me understand how much my time was really worth while in GCW. I still can't think Steven enough for that. I disappeared from GCW as quickly as I reappeared and led myself down a path which down in my heart I think I knew would make me happier all along. I got the ball rolling on my training facility. I first invited some local kids who had a good head on their shoulders and were interested in the trade. I set up six month sessions with a hand chosen group with me being the only person there to teach. It was a very intimate way of doing things with the kids and at the same time it was very cathartic for me. For the first time in years I was able to settle down, be happy, and be at peace. Don't get me wrong, there were times when I would go a little stir crazy and I'd take some of my personal time to accept a brief period of independent bookings or short-term Japanese bookings. All were willing to welcome me with open arms and were very open and friendly. I think I learned a lot more from these experiences than I had in my last whole year with Global Championship Wrestling. Wrestling people with drastically different styles who didn't quite fit into the standard ?GCW mold? was refreshing and taught me many things I could pass on to my students. To be completely honest I also believe it were these bookings and appearances that brought a flame alive back inside of me that I thought long dead. Five training cycles later I look at the new blood that is interested in coming fresh into my school or older kids who are interested in returning for more. I normally look at the list and applications I have with the utmost care but for some reason I'm feeling antsy. Almost unsettled. This has been the first night in a long time where distractions and uncertainty are getting the best of me. I think I need some time to sit and clear my head. This is something not familiar, yet not far from my heart. It's so difficult to put into words I'm not even sure why I'm going to try. It's time to leave the office, I think some food will do me good. I pass by the rather plain clock I have on the wall next to the door frame I have. Wow. I didn't even realize it had gotten to late in the evening. I've always been able to have such a long amount of time pass without my awareness when I'm really lost in thought. It's been such a long time since I remember that happening. I stand up from my chair to which my stomach greets me with one hell of a growl. Food it is, then. I walk around my rather paltry desk and through the naked door frame. I've never needed to put a door on it simply because I like to make a point to anyone that my door is always open if someone needs it. I suppose you can say I've become much less reckless in action and emotion in the years it's been. I like to think I've matured very well in my time away from being an active wrestler of a major federation. I take the step out of my office and into the training facility. It's not much to look at but the essentials are there. The land to build this more or less small warehouse on was cheap to come by and the ring, although top of the notch wasn't hard or expensive to procure thanks to my namesake. Give a few months of training to a in-state manufacturer's son and he considers it an even trade. I couldn't have been any happier when that spoiled brat left. Dammit, the lights. I always forget to turn those off when I'm leaving. Turning around mid-stride past the ring assembled in the middle of my facility I make the small trek back to my office and the series of switches inside the door. Reaching around the open frame I feel the wall, looking for the switches. That was when the vaguely familiar sound pierced the darkness. Was it...the phone? I could count the number of times someone has actually called this place directly one two hands. Two or three of those times were wrong numbers and the others were fairly inconsequential. I have the number listed on the website for my facility, but it's buried under the other two primary numbers. Besides, my ?Office Hours? are listed as stopping 6 hours ago. I'm content with ignoring it. I reach for the cellphone I normally keep with me. It's been of more use than any landline has ever been to me; but that's when it hit me - I left it back at my house this morning. That's when the ringing stopped. Confused but not very alarmed I'm content with leaving and trying to figure out who that might have been later on. I turn off the lights and take one quick step out the door. The phone is ringing again. I don't know how to explain it, but it just sounded...I don't know, different. Like I knew it was important and like subconsciously I understood this would mean a lot in the long run. I hurry back and turn the lights on and pick up the phone: ?Hello?? The voice on the other end. It's very familiar with his greeting. With a half second of thought I immediately realize who it is and my heart skips a beat. That feeling that is running through me, that indescribable sensation. I think I already know what this is and my heart already knows the answer I want to give. ?Sure Steven, of course I have the time to talk...? The one thing I've always learned time and time again from my life: Expect the Unexpected.
