A Quest for Ideal Conditions Take IV: Hide and Seek

16 March 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/16/09 (for WW 112) Previously… “You ever think, like, what if this elevator just doesn't stop on the way up?” DeBough looks a little cautious. He continues with, “Like, maybe it just goes shootin' up through the roof, into the sky and we just...float...” I scratch my cheek. “Honestly, dude, that'd be the best thing that'd ever happen to us.” He nods. “You're pretty fuckin' abstract.” I shrug. “I don't know what I am.” “I'd say you're abstract,” he says, still nodding. The elevator dings. Seventh floor. We step out and to the right. Walk past a few rooms. Obviously old room service sitting in front of one. DeBough stops and lifts the top off one of the platters. It's empty and he's disappointed. “Shit,” he says. Still walking, I say, “Yeah, god damn those people for eating all their food.” I step in front of 707 and I knock once, twice. “Security,” I say, and as I enunciate that last syllable the door creeps open a bit from the force of my knocks. DeBough steps beside me and I look at him hesitantly. He says, “Pretty...well...” -- clearing his throat -- “fishy.” I eyeball him. From inside: “Come on in, guys.” I recognize the voice. I look at Dice again with some hesitancy. “This was a bad idea,” I say. “Why?” I shake my head. “Go back down. I'll meet ya in a few.” DeBough's confused. “Please,” I say. He nods, turns back toward the elevator, takes a few steps, stops, turns, says, “Where?” I look at him, to the door, back to him. “Craps.” Another look at the door, back to DeBough. “Twenty minutes.” DeBough nods and pushes the down button for the elevator. I look at him, give him a slight nod. I look back to the door, at the numbers, and push through it. * The sight my eyes settle on when I enter room 707 is one for sore eyes -- a monstrosity of a man named Jason Mezzonote. Short and stocky, Mezzonote looks like George Costanza without the glasses. Brown eyes, brown hair with the classic horseshoe balding pattern, no chin. He’s a cigar aficionado and smokes them like the cigar manufacturing process is about to become extinct. He also dabbles in the cocaine trade, and what appears to be an eightball’s worth is scattered about a coffee table in front of the couch he sits at in this suite on the seventh floor of Planet Hollywood. I haven’t done cocaine in almost three years, and the sight of it and him together is making me nauseous. The last time I saw this guy, he paid me half a million up front to make someone disappear and I didn’t. I came close. But I didn’t. I failed and now he wants to collect what I owe him. That thought makes me gag on my Del Taco a bit more. I bite my bottom lip to calm myself down and maintain my composure, and he notices. “Brian, relax, man. Grab a line,” he says, offering up what’s on the table with an extended arm and turned up palm. I shake my head. “I don’t do that shit no more.” He shrugs, says, “Too bad. Grade A Columbian,” and leans down and snorts a line through half a straw. I nod toward him. “You own casinos in this state and you can’t afford anything better to snort outta?” He leans back up, white residue around his nostril, sniffs and it disappears inside. He looks at me, his eyes red and heavy and his face drooping a bit. “You know somethin’…when you have everything you want…and people wanna be you...and want what you have…you kinda envy them while they’re envyin’ you. You forget what it’s like to be simple and have goals and desires and motivations.” He picks up the half-straw, shakes it at me. “This reminds me of what bein’ simple was like. Before I had money…before I could afford to pay some below average professional wrestler to rid me of a problem I needed to be rid of.” I sigh, run my tongue around the inside of my cheek. “I assume that’s why you’re here.” He nods. “You owe me half a million dollars, Brian.” He shrugs. “That’s a lotta money, even to me.” “How’d you know where to find me?” He sits back, smiles. “Eyes in the sky.” He sniffs. “And you’re not really the best at hide and seek.” I shrug. “Everyone always found me first.” He nods. “How’s the porn business?” “It’s coming.” Mezzonote chuckles at my pun. He says, “I’m not much for pornography these days, but I have seen some of your work. You’ve got a good eye.” “Sweet talkin’ me ain’t gonna get you your money.” He shrugs. “I’ve got a feeling nothing’s gonna get me my money.” “You’d be right. You made an investment. You lost money. Happens all the time. I know you play the stock market. It’s a gamble. You of all people should know that. You strapped?” He furrows his brow. “Am I strapped?” I nod. “Yeah. You armed?” He shakes his head. “Good,” I say. “Then get the fuck outta my room before I throw your fat ass over the balcony.” He shakes his head. “I’ve got this room till tomorrow. Way I see it -- you should get the fuck outta my room.” I crack a smile. “You’ve got 10 minutes. When I come back, I better not see you in here.” * After the elevator incident and after Mezzonote packed his things and hit the bricks, I had to find DeBough. Dude had been gone for over an hour now. I know we’re in Vegas, but it’s not like him to not return my texts at least somewhat swiftly. I don’t know what to do or where to go, and Vegas is Vegas -- Dice could be anywhere from Old Town to the airport and I don’t feel like going on an FBI-type manhunt for my tag team partner, stablemate and porno cash cow. Thankfully, I won’t have to, as my text alert goes off and I open up a message. It’s from Dice and says: “Ideal conditions found. Come to the Palms. 52nd floor.” I chuckle because he’s one of those guys that punctuates properly in texts and instant messages. Lame. I text back: “on my way” And I’m down the elevator, across the floor, out the door. I hail a cab and he asks me where to and I tell him the Palms. He nods and we’re off into late morning Vegas traffic. After a couple of minutes we arrive at the Palms. I pay the cabbie and enter the casino, heading straight for the elevators. Once I find them, I push the up button and one to my right immediately dings. I step in and look for the button that says 52. I find it easily -- it’s very noticeable -- because there’s a Playboy bunny emblem on the button too. I shake my head. “Dice, you sonuvabitch.” * The scene on the 52nd floor of the Palms isn’t one you can really put into words. There’s blackjack, roulette, bars and hordes of girls walking around in bunny outfits. This place is crowded for 10:30 in the morning on a Monday and I don’t have time to wait on Dice to return a text, so I call him up. He answers immediately. “Dude, where the fuck are you?” he says. “Where the fuck am I? You’re the one who disappeared for an hour.” “Whatever, whatever. Are you here?” “Yeah, I’m here. Where are you?” “Roulette. Back by the bar on the left if you’re looking at the place from the door.” I glance around and find the bar. “Got it,” I say, and I hang up. I stroll back towards the bar and the roulette, nodding at everyone, acting like I’ve been there before when I obviously haven’t. I see Dice placing some chips on the roulette table, two blondes in bunny outfits on each side of him. I don’t even notice them at first. I get up to the table and say, “They got roulette at fuckin’ Planet Hollywood, dude. What the fuck? I’ve been waitin’ there for like an hour.” I feel my face getting red, and the looks I get from the blondes aren’t endearing. Dice glances up but finishes putting his chips on whichever numbers. After he’s done, he says, “Fish, relax man.” He nods towards the four blondes and introduces them to me from left to right, pointing at each one when he says their name. “Fish, this is Lindsey, Nikki, Elicia and Ashley.” I finally realize there’s four blonde bunnies draped on either side of Dice and the redness in my face disappears and is replaced by a wide, toothy smile. “Ladies,” I say. “Are we fuckin’ or what?” Dice’s eyes get big, like I said something wrong. “What he means, girls, is he’s looking forward to our day together.” He steps away from the table -- “Take over the operation,” he says to one of the blondes, pointing to his chip stack -- and motions me to follow him to the bar. At the bar, he orders a beer for both of us. He turns to me after ordering, says, “These girls get off in like an hour. Then we’re gonna grab some lunch down at Nove--” “What’s Nove?” “Just a floor below. Best Italian in the city.” “Pasta,” I say. “I dig. Good for a full day of fuckin’.” He smiles. “Exactly. But, you know, don’t mention fucking.” “So they’re not sluts?” “Not totally.” I shake my head. “I must say that’s disappointing. Although I’m up for a challenge after that elevator business.” “Huh?” “Nothing.” “Right. Well they do work at the Playboy Club, so…” Barkeep hands us our beers, and we start back towards roulette. I hold my hands out. “So…what?” “What do you mean ‘so what?’” “Usually when people say ‘so’ there’s something that comes after it.” “I kind of use it more as a transition in the conversation.” I take a swig of my beer. “Whatever you say, dude. How much you gamblin’ with?” Dice sort of chuckles. “Funny thing, man. I’m not even gambling with my money.” “What the fuck does that mean?” “Yeah, dude, see that guy over there.” He points to a guy at a blackjack table across the way. He’s short, stocky, brown hair, horseshoe balding pattern, no chin. “He walked up to me and gave me like 10 grand. In cash!” Dice pats me on the shoulder, like he can’t believe it. “Then,” he says, “he told me I didn’t have to pay him back. He told me it was an investment. And I was like, ‘I suck at roulette,’ but he didn’t seem to mind.” The look on my face is blank. “This isn’t ideal,” I say.
