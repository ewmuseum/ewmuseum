Caroline Part I: Stitches And Exaggerations

16 June 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 16/06/2008 “Sir, will you quit struggling!” The heavy-set nurse yelled, that husky yet whining voice that’d been driving Jacob McKail crazy for the past two days or so. “Fuck that,” McKail responded, with a grunt. He helplessly struggled from the burly nurse’s vice-like grip, but it turned out to be mere folly. She cast him back into the wheelchair with minimal ease and placed a heavy hand upon his shoulder to ensure that he stayed there. “I’m afraid it’s hospital policy sir,” the nurse informed him, not that McKail was listening to her anymore. “You gotta leave in a wheelchair.” “Yeah right,” McKail muttered in response, willing the exit to get closer real fast. “Whatever.” Just beyond the door the cold night air hit him like a sledge hammer to the face, but he welcomed it. Anything was better than spending another five minutes in that damned place. It was right out of McKail’s worst nightmare. He hated visiting relatives in hospitals, never mind having to spend three nights in the damned place. It was the smell, the other annoying people in the same ward as he, the restrictive nurses and the smug doctors; the food wasn’t fit for a dog and the service was worse. It could’ve been worse, he could’ve been seriously injured, bed-bound even, then he would’ve had the pleasure of shitting in a damned bedpan in front of other people. Luckily, that wasn’t the case. “Well then,” the nurse began, with a strained smile, “this is your stop!” McKail grunted a thanks and stood, clutching his bandaged ribs carefully and wincing in agony under the pressure of his own weight. The frosted glass in that damned casino mostly cut his chest and abdomen and the stitches were still a little. “Fuckin” Reed,” McKail muttered. “I’ll show him who’s sorry?” He held his hand up, ready to hail a cab when he heard her voice “Jacob?” McKail froze on the spot immediately. “Caroline?” He never thought he was going to hear her voice again, much less her call his name with what he perceived to be affection. And why would he? Last time they met face to face, he’d moronically told her that he didn’t want to be part of her life anymore. It was a lie and they both knew it; he didn’t feel he deserved to be happy. At least, not until he’d gotten over his late wife, Mary. Get over Mary? He asked himself. It was two years ago! You need to move on with your life! But it wasn’t that simple; with McKail, nothing usually is. He’d made a promise to his wife on her deathbed to make it in the wrestling business; to be a big star, to win championships and have his name up in lights. He’d managed some success here and there, but it was never enough and if he was truthful with himself probably never would be. Nevertheless, his focus was firmly fixed on his career and he didn’t have time for anyone or anything else. Caroline deserved better than that. She deserved somebody to committed to her and her little boy. Besides, regardless of how hard he tried to ignored it, he felt a little weird about the whole thing. She was Mary’s sister after all, his sister-in-law in extension and it felt a little wrong to him. Maybe it was just fine in the South, but it wasn’t where he grew up. All of these, he felt, were valid reason not to begin a relationship with her; the negatives only just outnumbered the positives, but the deciding factor was something else entirely. It was selfish and self-centred of him, but the truth was he just didn’t want to feel that bad again. When Mary died, so did a part of him and it crippled him. The thought of that alone, put his alleged “nightmare” of overnight hospital visits into perspective. Caroline approached him pensively. “Are you okay?” McKail couldn’t look her in the eye. He was ashamed of himself and he had every right to be. “Yeah, sure. It’s nothing.” “Doesn’t look like nothing to me,” she replied, cautiously drawing closer. “You’ve got cuts all over your face.” “I got cuts everywhere,” McKail told her. “Hundred or so stitches in total.” Caroline raised her eyebrows. “You trying to impress me there, champ?” Despite himself, McKail couldn’t help but grin. They were flirting and he didn’t even realise he’d initiated it. Still, he couldn’t bring himself to bring it to an end. “I am if it’s working.” She giggled a little; that same damn giggle she knew he loved. “Very good, Jacob. How have you been?” “Good,” he lied. “That’s not what I hear,” she replied, allowing her words to hang in the air for a time. “I hear you were out of work for a month or so and some guy pulled a gun on you in a parking lot somewhere” Then, my son is watching GCW High Stakes on pay per view and all of a sudden your acting like an animal, charging at some other guy and heading straight through frosted glass in some casino!” She paused to breath. “Are your doing fine?” McKail sighed. The playful confrontation had changed into a serious discussion about his life and how he should be living it better and not closing himself off from the rest of world. It was the beginning of the same old argument the two of them had been having for the better part of six months and no matter how much they wanted to be with each other, the same topic would inevitably be dredged up. At the moment, that was the last thing he needed. “So, how come you’re here?” McKail asked her. “Your boy sick?” Caroline shook her head. “No. I’m here waiting for you.” McKail developed a confused expression. “For me” Why?” “The hospital called me,” she told him. “I’m on your next of kin list. The nurse was worried that you’d had a bad concussion and thought you could use a lift home.” McKail nodded, understanding, but still didn’t like it. “Thanks, but I’m fine.” She nodded and grinned. “Yeah. I thought you’d say that, but I’m taking you home anyhow.” McKail sighed. An argument would be futile, she’d won every argument they’d ever had. That’s one of the reasons why he hadn’t seen her in a while. “Where are you parked?”
