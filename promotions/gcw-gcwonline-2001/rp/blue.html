Date: various .:: ::: ::. The Maryland Inn Annapolis, Maryland The door slipped open with a quiet slowness; she didn’t want to disturb the meditative solitude in which her world slept. From the bed, there was no movement’he must have fallen asleep waiting for her. She knew he wouldn’t have gone willingly into sleep, but would have fought it like a tiger so long as she wasn’t in his arms. She let go a soft sigh as she shut the door, expecting to be plunged into darkness, but there was only blue. The moonlight flooded through the curtains, seeping around every crack where they had been pulled tight, and the room looked like it was underwater, which was coincidentally how she felt she was moving at the moment. Exhaustion plagued her every limb, swooping in to steal away her coordination as she fumbled with the laces of her corset, desperate to finally get into bed. The past few days had been agonizingly long’a church in West Virginia, then a trip to Kentucky to talk with Blaise, Aine Leanan and Korwyn Malik, and then out to D.C. to meet Kokabel, a virtue, an angel. To say that the week had been trying was a broad understatement. In the cerulean, ice cool twilight of the hotel room, she slid out of her velvet hip-huggers, a shadow writhing sinuously in azure outlines. She felt like half of herself at the moment, as though some naiad had sucked out her energy as soon as she stepped into the room. But as she sat down on the edge of the bed, breathing in the air conditioner’s Freon scent, a glacier-like calm descended upon her, and she realized it was only him’only the way she felt finally coming back to her nest for the week. And this room was reminiscent of the way she felt when she looked into his eyes’drowning under his spell. Sighing softly, she slid underneath the covers, and a wave of peace came over her. Virgil’s arms slid around her reflexively, protectively, though she could tell that he was not awake. A soft smile turned upwards the corners of her lips and she nestled her head on his pillow next to him, settling into the navy gloom as though it was the only place she had ever known. It might have been wishful, half-awake thinking, but the way she felt right now, she could have slept forever like this, under the restorative powers of soul that he seemed to have endowed the room with while she was gone, like an oasis of calm and safety. She closed her eyes, and drifted off in his current almost instantly. .:: ::: ::. Once Upon a Time Zdice, Czech Republic Vasek Vlaszy’s big oak desk was always covered with his business papers, but today, there was only one among many of those papers that held any weight for fifteen-year-old Lia and her twin brother Laszlo. He glanced furtively at the door. “Are you sure Father isn’t going to be angry with you for reading his letters?” She gave him a withering, pointed look. “It’s only one letter, and he told me that I could read it.” He tried to ignore her superior tone, because he knew it was purposeful. “Well read it then, let’s not stand here all day. I’ve got other things to do.” She smirked slightly as she leaned over the desk to pluck the letter’exactly where her father had told her it would be. He had an organized system, though it might have been a system only discernible by Vasek himself. And as for Laszlo’s posturing, she knew that he was as interested in the contents of the letter as she was. She cleared her throat. ?Signor Vlaszy? what a strange word that is! Why do you think he called Papa that?” “Oh for” just read it, alright!” Laszlo grumbled, “You’d make The Agamemnon out of a two-sentence letter.” She glowered at him. Normally, this would have meant a protracted argument, but both of them were too interested in the letter to make much of it. ?It is with great regret that I write to you to inform you that my father, Lord Sterescu, is dead. Perhaps the great distance between our clans has precipitated this disconnect. Perhaps, had our allies been closer, Father would not have died. But I suppose it is no use talking of these things. It is a violent land that the Sterescu family has chosen as its own, one which has claimed both my father, and my eldest brother Dante. I am left his sole heir, as my brother died some time before he did. This is how I come to write to you regarding your proposed alliance between our families. It is perhaps against the common propriety to do so, but there is no one else for the task. I am happy to inform you that I would be eager to accept your daughter’s hand. In my station, a man can become very lonely for companionship, as I am sure you can imagine. At nineteen years of age, my chief desire is to find a wife with whom to share both my joys and burdens. Please, if it is still in compliance with your wishes, send your Lia to join me in Iberia as soon as she is able to come. Your gracious? etcetera etcetera.” She tossed the letter back on the desk, and for a moment, both of them stared at the walls in a moody silence. A few steps conveyed her closer to her twin, a male mirror of her discontent. “Laszlo, what if he’s cruel!” Laszlo’s stormy, sea-foam eyes thrashed wildly. “Then you need only write a letter, and I will bring you home, Father’s wishes be damned.” “Or” or? what if he’s hideous?” That seemed to put a cold stop to the drama of the situation. Her brother fixed his eyes on her for a moment? and then started laughing, causing an indignant pall to wrap itself around her expression. “Lia, you are the vainest creature that ever walked the earth,” he said as best he could, wiping his eyes, “What if he’s hideous, my God” only in your mind would that come directly after wondering whether or not he’ll beat you.” “It’s important!” she insisted, and her indignance only made him laugh harder. .:: ::: ::. Once Upon a Time Iberia Lia peered around the side of the carriage, trying to see more closely where it was taking her. The manor house swooped majestically up above her; secretly, she was thrilled by it, no less thrilled by the thought of being its mistress, but she wanted to reserve her judgment until she saw its master. The carriage clacked through the open gate, the horses snorting foam, tired after such a long journey. They had arrived a few days early, so Lia could only imagine that this was going to be a surprise for young Lord Sterescu. Her heart beat up in her throat with the hopes that it would be a pleasant one. A dark-haired page appeared from around the side of the house, his brown eyes wide. “May I help you?” Obviously, he presumed that they’d gotten the wrong address. “Ah?” She could hear her coachman, Valek, struggling with the unfamiliar tongue, “I have brought Miss Vlaszy, from Zdice” your master’s intended?” The young man’s eyes lit with recognition of the name, or perhaps just the particulars. “Oh! You’re early! Lord Sterescu is in the stables tending to his horses” I’ll run get him!” Before Valek could make any response, the young page ran off across the yard towards the stables, which she could now see off in the direction he had gone. “Lord Sterescu! My Lord! She’s come early!” Lia shifted uncomfortably, and Valek dismounted, paying some small attentions to the team before he stepped over to the crude door, opening it to hand her down. He leaned closer to her to talk in her ear with unaccustomed familiarity. “It is your moment, Miss Lia. You make your father proud.” At the mention of him, her eyes twisted slightly downwards with homesick anxiety, but he grasped her hand and helped her to stand. “Here comes your young master?” She stepped carefully out of the carriage at the same time that he ducked into view, smiling at his page, who was cavorting several steps ahead of him. Her eyes had a brief moment to scan’wearing what she would have expected any gentleman of the time to wear, a loose set of pants, laced-up boots and a loose swordsman’s shirt. From his bearing, she could practically imagine what he’d look like with his armor on, cotehardie with his coat of arms draped atop that. At the moment their eyes locked, she knew she’d not have to worry about him being hideous. She found herself lost in a calm sea of azure, her heart seized by a nervous somersault. Long black hair streamed down his back, unfettered by any tie, and sweat gleamed softly in beads on his forehead. He had about him an earthy vitality, like a wild creature emerging untamed from the woods. He paused in his step, seemingly seized by that same feeling as his affianced stepped to the ground. She looked to be some inches shorter than him, possessed of the tantalizing girlish curves that would later slope into a woman’s grace. Her skin was creamy pale, her face angularly elfish, and within her face, eyes that were animated from within. All of this was framed by a long halo of black hair that hung all the way down her back to her waist, tied up by a loose wreath of flowers’flowers that she must have picked from the hedgerow several furlongs back. White roses. An enchantment hung silvery in the air around both of them, at which even aging Valek had to smile. He’d rarely seen the austere Miss Vlaszy so discomposed. “Ah” My Jesu, I’m so rude for not introducing myself,” said the young lord, stepping forward to take her hand, “I” am Lord Sterescu? and I apologize for my appearance, I was not expecting you for another few days.” “It isn’t any trouble,” she said, suddenly shy, and at Valek’s nudge and reminder, she colored, “I am Baronessa Vlaszy.” The title felt strange, as did the formality with a man who was going to be her husband. “Baronessa,” he said softly, the word rolling over his tongue with an accent that she was entirely unused to, “I am sure you will like it here?” At that, she colored deeper, felt her hand tremble in his as she tried to smile, but ended up only nodding, keeping that seriousness of expression that was so native to her. He grinned roguishly. “Shall I show her to her room?” interrupted the page from earlier, practically bouncing from toe to toe. That wrenched the two of them out of their own little world, and he dropped her hand, both of them turning to look at the young man. “No, Armando, I shall show her myself,” said young Lord Sterescu, offering his future bride his arm, which she took. It was all automatic as he led her into the manor house and the young page set about unloading her trunks. “Would you like to see the house?” he asked, his blue eyes gleaming with excitement. “I?” and of course she wanted to see, but exhaustion crept through every bone, “Could I see tomorrow” I? it’s been a long journey.” His face fell slightly. “Of course. Forgive me for not thinking of that,” he said, quickly putting his facial expression to rights, “It is upstairs” I hope it will be satisfactory.” She was certain that it would’in a house like this, with these elaborate furnishings practically dripping down the walls, she didn’t think anything could be under-done. She followed his steps up the stairs, having some small amount of trouble keeping up with his longer legs, and it seemed like no sooner had he started to pull her a little closer to his side that they had reached her room, where he was dropping her hand to open the door. He pushed the door open and stepped aside to allow her in. As soon as she stepped in the room, she fell silent, having to work conscientiously to keep her jaw from dropping. The inside of the room was a gossamer dream, deep navy brocades pinned to the walls to keep the cold out during the winter and the heat in the summer, a sky-colored rug with an intricate pattern the likes of nothing she had ever seen before, and a bed furnished in a similar color’and the bed curtains were a blue filmy fabric, so thin it seemed to hardly be of their world, shimmering faintly with the sunlight. And now that she looked closer, she could see those same silver threads shimmering in the wall hangings as well. “It is” good?” he asked, and when she looked back at him, he looked incredibly bashful, looking away from her gaze, “I picked it all out myself?” “It’s beautiful,” she said, trying to form the words in his language, which was still foreign and thick on her tongue, no matter how hard her father had tried to school her in it. Slowly, he smiled again, taking a bold step closer to her. “I will leave you to get acquainted with it then,” he said, but before he could make himself go, he plucked her hand from her side and dropped a gentle kiss on its back side before looking up at her with those eyes, burning blue bale-fire, “I am so glad you’ve come, Baronessa.” A deep blush started at her cheeks, coinciding with the pounding, woozy feeling coming directly from her heart. She thought she smiled, but she could have been mistaken? everything seemed a dream. .:: ::: ::. Once Upon a Time Iberia Three weeks in his household had flown by. They were on the cusp of their marriage now, and Lia felt that she had what so many other of the woman in her village, even in her family had not’a friend in the man she was marrying. He pressed her hand close to his side as he walked with her around the gardens. It was a pastime that she never tired of, being escorted around, and educated on the various plants in her new home? today, the roses were blooming in full, a majestic sight that he had been raving to her about for weeks, promising her. He’d woken her fairly early with the promise of a walk after the morning meal, and that had been enough to pull her from her dreams and into her morning routine, which had slowly gotten more and more comfortable to her. A glance at her showed a soft playfulness in his eyes that made her heart skip a beat. “Baronessa, tell me about your family. I confess, as close a relationship as our fathers seemed to have, I do not know much about your clan.” She smiled slightly, and cleared her throat, wondering just how much there was that she needed to tell. “Nor I of yours, but I am trying to learn,” she said, smiling softly, “As for my family” the town that we live in is a small one, but I suppose still bigger than here’there is not so much sprawling free land in Zdice, everyone knows one another’s business.” She made a face at that, and he laughed softly. “My family is a respected family, Vlaszy a good name there, but” we are not the wealthiest family in the town? I was supposed to marry into that family until?” He skin turned whitish, and she looked away. “Master Ionescu did not wish me to do so,” she said, quoting the story she’d been told to tell. It wouldn’t do for her future husband to know what happened, why she was really here. Not before they were married, at least, and there was a chance of him changing his mind and sending her back to her assured death at the hand of Zdice’s priests. His brow furrowed. “Master Ionescu?” “My” ah? he is my father’s Lord. He is very concerned with my family, and our service to him. My brother and I particularly interested him, as we are twins? a good omen where I come from? Jaroslav was? a brute. Master Ionescu saw this, and wished me better circumstances, so father wrote to his old friend and? that is how I ended up here.” He nodded in acknowledgement, though he still seemed somewhat confused. But he forced a smile regardless. “I shall have to meet him one day and thank him for his good sense.” At that, she blushed again. The storm that had crossed her brow was quickly chased away by the huge trellis of roses twenty yards ahead, practically creaking under the weight of hundreds of white blossoms. “My Lord! They are beautiful!” He beamed, squeezing her hand. “The pride of the gardens” It’s why I love spring so much, they make everything smell so fresh, chase away the cold? next year, I’ll train a trellis over our bedroom window so that we can open the glass and smell them in the mornings?” He had trailed off into wishful thinking again, which he often seemed to do with her. It was a sort of easy relationship they had developed over the past few weeks, one in which he felt free to tell her of his plans, and she free to be awed by them, and made happy by her inclusion in them. They approached the trellis, her curious fingers reaching forward to touch the closest blossom to her. He did not look at the blossoms, but at her face, enchanted by the curious innocence with which she reached out to each new experience. Her fingers grazed the petals, refusing to touch hard enough to bruise, and a joy flittered across her countenance, truthful and open, unfettered. It was then that she felt his eyes upon her, but it was only a moment that that lasted before it was his lips instead. A sharp shock jolted through her’scared to kiss him back, scared to caress him, but wanting to with every fiber of the cloth that wove itself together as her form. It was a dizzy feeling, she thought as she pressed her lips tentatively back to his, like being borne up on the backs of the clouds, tangled through a thunderstorm that bucked like a wild stallion and spat out on the other side, alive and panting to be thrust through again. He pulled away, that bale-fire burning in his eyes again, his thumb stroking her cheek affectionately. And in that way that was so uncharacteristic of what she had seen lords do to their wives, he smiled at her, candidly, looking a little shaky himself. “I am so glad we’re getting married this next fortnight?” Her fingers slid up his forearm to encircle his wrist. “As am I.” And finally, propriety be damned, she felt comfortable in this, awash and drowning in an unfamiliar ocean, but more than willing to take her last breaths if going under meant staying with him forever. .:: ::: ::. Once Upon a Time Iberia The fortnight had passed agonizingly slowly, but it was finally the hour for truth. The family had come, and the land-holders skirted the fringes of the small chapel, as well as the pages, and the Sterescu army. All people she didn’t know, but stationed across from him, she felt no shyness at that, no frustration or fright. She was in awe of his presence this day, the way he looked in his plate with his hair still flowing down his back like a stallion’s mane. There was something fierce in his honor, in the way he gazed at her across the altar as though he would have died to protect this sacred vow once it was spoken. She felt almost inadequate in her soft satin, a rich-toned beryl, a wreath of those roses that she favored reigning in her thick locks, which had been braided away from her face. She felt spell-held, unable to look away from him. The priest was simply an accessory to all this, she knew. There was no force in heaven nor earth that could have stopped this from happening, it was so necessary, so true. “You may pronounce your vows.” He heard the priest's voice, but it sounded tinny, far away, as though he had suddenly moved to the other side of the chapel. His life hadn't been long, but he knew with the utmost certainty that this was the sort of thing he'd been told about as a child; how one day, he would find a beautiful noblewoman to marry, and how the chapel would be filled with admirers from miles around... but somehow, he'd never expected it to be quite so close to what his mother had always told him. 'Lia... time has finally relented upon me, and brought you to me from your distant homeland, and I can say little that could express my truest admiration and love for you. Never have my eyes fallen upon a creature so beautiful and I know for certain that they never shall again. I have awaited this day for what feels to be centuries, and I swear to you an oath of honor, that I shall protect you from all harm, shelter you from all evils, and remain true to you as my only love in existence. This ring, made from the fairest stones the earth has to offer, will signify my will to provide you with all that you shall ever need or want, physically and spiritually, until I no longer live.' Steeling his resolve, for the purpose of steadying his hand, he slid the ring over her finger, until it rested snugly. A smile crept over his face, that he had little hope of suppressing. She swallowed, practically reeling under the happy weight of his words. Her fingers trembled, and the priest smiled, by way of reassurance, and encouraging her to go on. “My Lord Virgiliu” I have scarcely been under your roof more than a month, and yet these halls are already so much more than stone to me. They are a home to me, and you have endeavored to make them so with ceaseless effort’hospitality that I dare not expect from the closest family friend, and yet am all the more thankful to receive when it comes from the man who is to be my husband. I am honored by your oath, and to your swearing it, swear one of my own that I will uphold the dignity of your family name by taking it as my own, that I will make your house into a home for you, a sight always welcome to your eyes after an absence, that I will be as true to you as you to me, yearning for your presence no matter how far your duties may take you. My gift to you, this ring, is from the house of my father, and signifies not only our two families unifying as one, but of my gift to you of myself. You are all that I am now, and all I shall be.” Her fingers unashamedly shaking, she took his hand, and slid the ring onto his finger, the sanctity of her vow to him, the earnestness with which she spoke it nearly moving her to tears. His smile reassured her. “Your vows have passed upward to the Father in the Heavens, and He has smiled upon your sincerity,” the priest intoned, “May God sanctify and bless this union with earthly comfort and heavenly virtue. Amen.” This time, she didn’t have to be persuaded to kiss him. .:: ::: ::. Once Upon a Time Iberia He set her down gently only seconds ago after carrying her across the threshold as custom dictated. The wedding party had seemed to drag on and on, and she had learned a thousand new names that she would never remember, but she was happy’happier than she had ever been, especially now that she was alone with him. She was somewhat in awe of his chamber. It was a place that bespoke of his essence, scented in masculine sandalwood, and decorated with deep, dramatic sweeps of color that she found quite dazzling. Not so dazzling as the view from the window’a scorching Iberian sunset, like fire sprouting over the horizon in a show just for the two of them, a visual display of the passion and excitement this day had brought. She stood at the window a moment longer, before noticing him behind her. And this time, she did not have to shy away from her wants, and his wants, because he was her husband, and this time was their own, away from prying, nosey eyes. “Do you think you will be happy living here” As happy as you were at home?” There seemed still to be some doubt in his eyes, but she pressed a hand to his chest, fervently looking up into his eyes. “I have never been so certain of anything in my life.” He smiled, and it faded away just as quickly into seriousness, his hands sliding around her waist, lips pressing softly to the base of her neck. She gasped, a shot of energy rocketing through her madly with just that touch. His hair fell slightly over his eyes, shading his face from the day’s dying sun and brushing against her shoulders with a maddening tickle. His fingertips pulled her towards the bed, ever gently leading her, guiding her, and pushing aside the bed curtains. She sat down gently on the bed, her chevalier kneeling in front of her to slowly unlace the ivory ribbons holding her blue cotehardie in place. It fell from her shoulders and left her only in the simple blue shift underneath, made of raw silk’an exotic fabric that made her feel like an exotic creature indeed. Slowly, her fingers twisted through his hair, reaching over his shoulders to untie the cord in the back of his shirt, loosening it so that he could pull it off in one sleek motion. It was the first time she had ever seen so much of his tanned, smooth skin at once, and it practically took her breath away. A hunger took over then, a need, and it almost felt like fingers were moving in concert with a divine destiny. Her shift slipped to the floor, followed by the simple leggings he had worn under his leg-plate that day, and then there was only the commerce forbidden to all but those lawfully married in God’s eyes? Her breath picked up. She realized, quite suddenly, that she had no idea of what to expect. The bed curtains swung shut behind them, leaving husband and wife obscured, on a plane all of their own making, one from which she hoped she would never escape. His fingers traced a slow, burning path across her skin, across her collarbones, one by one, and trickling downwards like hot ashes strewn from a flint-box. Her body twisted to meet his hands, a response that at the same time frightened and enlivened her, set her alight with a fever running through every limb, thick and pervasive as any sickness she had ever heard of. He shifted on top of her, his kisses trailing lower, lower to her collarbones, lower still, and she gasped, loudly enough to make him stop and look up at her. “I” I’ve never done this before,” she said, her voice a half-frightened whisper with still the hint of a low tone behind it’fear mingled with an unexplainable need that grew in the pit of her stomach and stretched outward through time and space, radiating through her limbs and lighting her universe with its fire. He closed his eyes for a moment, lost in the heavy, foreign scent of mingled sweat and the sweetness from the roses still hanging in her hair. “Neither have I,” he said softly, “But upon my oath, I will never hurt you.” .:: ::: ::. Unknown Time Unknown Location It felt almost like she was sitting on top of Vysehrad Castle in the middle of Prague again? only something wasn’t right. The blackness around them did not twinkle with stars, and the spiked tips of the stone scrabbling their claws toward the sky looked somehow hollow, as though made of cheap cardboard, and badly painted. With her knees drawn to her chest, she couldn’t help but stare. And when she looked up, there he sat, as regal and frighteningly graceful as ever. “Master Ionescu,” she said softly, the ending of the word nearly lost in the wind that whipped wildly around the corner of the castle at his presence. He nodded only once, his affirmation to her. But aside from that, he did not speak, as though waiting for something from her to continue. She stared at him for long moments that seemed like eons passing within seconds before she found her voice again. “What about Virgil?” Vaclav’s black-brown eyes took her in seriously, refusing to let her go. “Listen to me carefully, Lia. The Maryland Inn Annapolis, Maryland Lia shot out of sleep as though from a catapult, flung against the wall of consciousness at a velocity so high that she was practically talking as soon as her eyes flew open. “Virgil?” Frantically, she turned over, scrabbling through the sheets and covers to find him? but he wasn’t there. And that was when she realized that sunlight was slowly spilling through the draperies, making uniform squares of light on the carpet, save for the silhouettes of the drapes dancing back and forth in the air conditioner’s artificial wind. She sat up, holding the cover’s to her chest’though why she bothered with the modesty, she didn’t know. Upon looking a few feet to her left, into the living room area, she could see Virgil pacing, holding his cell phone to his ear. Though bursting with information, she could say nothing without disturbing her sense of propriety and politeness. Finally, he snapped it shut and paced back into the bedroom, an exceedingly perturbed look on his face. She tried to force her voice, but there was nothing at first, and she struggled within herself to say it’but he spoke first. “Lia” I didn’t expect to find you up so early after such a hard night.” But there was something flat in his tone, something worrisome in his manner. “Virgil, I” Can you sit down? I need to tell you something. It’s important.” He looked torn for a moment, as though he wanted to hear her out’and normally, he would never have said no to her. But this was not a normal circumstance. “You know how much I value things that you must tell me Lia, but this time, I don’t have the time to spend?” She looked wounded before he could explain, but he barreled on, his eyes seething with a choppy sea sort of energy. “Rune has gotten himself into an exceedingly great deal of trouble. I’m afraid that if I don’t make it to him soon, we will lose another friend to us, and?” “His safety is more important,” she said, her eyes completely torn about the issue, telling the truth of the matter where words only served to downplay her anxiety, “Hurry.” Virgil nodded curtly, his accustomed habit when he wanted to stay, and speaking might have encouraged him doing so. But he didn’t stay, not this time’and she was left still holding on to something that felt like a lead weight right in the middle of her chest. It was a pitiful excuse for a craft in her gale-force storm of surging emotion. As the door shut behind him, she found her mantra, a way to keep from driving herself insane while he was gone. Tell him, tell him, tell him, tell him, tell him, tell him, tell him, tell him, tell him? What else could she think of, left alone in the ultramarine and glacial grip of memory’if it faded, it was ashes and dust once again, but telling Virgil might somehow make those dreams true. The pressure weighed down. As she worried her lower lip with her teeth, she laid back on the bed, lying to herself that she would try and get some more sleep. All she could see when she closed her eyes was blue. .:: ::: ::.
