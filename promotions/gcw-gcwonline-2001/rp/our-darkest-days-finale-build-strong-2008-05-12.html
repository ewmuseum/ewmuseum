Our Darkest Days, Finale: Build Strong

12 May 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12th May, 2008 THE HOME OF ANDY MURRAY, TALLAHASSEE, FLORIDA, MONDAY 12th MAY, 2008, 12:15am Andy leapt up from his knelt position on the bedroom floor as he caught a glimpse of his own reflection in the mirror. He felt a smile slowly working its way across his lips as he glared against the reflective surface; he was surprised at how good he looked. The past twelve months in Andy Murray’s life had been nothing if eventful. He’d taken on more adversity than a lot of people would encounter throughout their entire lives. From personal injury to costly legal proceedings, just about every emotional curveball imaginable had been thrown at The Scottish King of Cool. Such was the intensity and frequency of Andy’s misfortunes that a lesser man would surely have descended into a depraved pit of despair had he experienced them in such a condensed period of time. But not Andy Murray. He had built his career ? nee, his life - by overcoming adversity, even in situations where it appeared that all hope had been abandoned. The year 2007 was no different. And despite all that life had thrown at him over the past year or so, Andy couldn’t remember a single instance where he had ever felt as anxious as he did on the twelfth of May 2008. This was supposed to be a good day, a happy day. It was supposed to be the final nail in the coffin for all he had gone through since leaving FUSE in February 2007; since a recurrence of a potentially devastating back injury looked to have put the name "Andy Murray" out of commission in the wrestling world for good. Despite all of this he couldn’t stop the wave of trepidation that rippled its way up his spine as he looked in the mirror. This should be a walk in the park compared to what he’d been through. Since the start of 2007 he had suffered from the aforementioned injury, assaulted a man in his own home, divorced his cheating wife Jane for the second time and witnessed the painful deterioration of his relationship with his beloved son Marvin. So why on Earth was the seemingly menial task of collecting his brother from prison causing Andy such discomfort? As he straightened out a crease in his white Stone Temple Pilots t-shirt he couldn’t figure it out himself. "Looks like the King of Cool is all set," a female voice gently cooed behind Andy, startling the Scotsman. Kayla Jarosz wrapped her arms playfully around Andy’s stomach, glancing around his shoulder and into the mirror. She wasn’t a short woman by any means, but even after almost a year of courting her Andy still found the ten inch height different amusing. "Something like that," responded Andy with an awkward sigh. Kayla tightened her grip. "Awwww, don’t be sad, poppa bear!" she softened the tone of her voice, clearly sensing the apprehension in Andy’s demeanour. "I’m sure everything will be fine; he’ll be delighted to see you?" Murray slowly stepped away, breaking the embrace, as a smile slowly returned to his face. He wasn’t as optimistic as Kayla that his reunion with his brother would go without a hitch, but there was something about her happy-go-lucky outlook and idealism that he found completely intoxicating. "And you’re sure you don’t have any problem with him staying here for a little while?" he enquired for what must have been the tenth time in the past couple of days. "Like I said, it’s only “til he gets himself ba-" She had pressed a finger against Andy’s lips before he could finish what he was saying. "I already told you, I don’t mind. I’m kind of looking forward to meeting the mini-Murray, actually!" The Scottish King of Cool did open his mouth to speak again, but before he could do so a high-pitched yelp filled the air. He glanced over the shoulder of his girlfriend as a small, black and brown daschund puppy sped into the room as fast as his little legs could carry him. Wagging his tail frantically, he made a b-line for Andy and made a vain attempt to climb up his leg; an attempt that was interrupted by Kayla who scooped the small dog up, cradling him in her arms. "Awww, look who’s come to say hello!" she said as she gently tickled Winston’s ears. Winston; rather peculiar name for a dog, Andy thought. Winston had only been in their possession for around about four months, but already he was a fully-fledged member of the household. Kayla had bought the dog without Andy’s consent to keep her company whilst the Scotsman was on the road touring with Global Championship Wrestling. Understandably, when he first heard the news he wasn’t best pleased? that attitude changed the minute he first glimpsed into Winston’s loving, adorable face. "Look! Even Winston’s looking forward to meeting Mr. Cayle!" Kayla smiled, motioning towards the dog’s tail which was still wagging at an impressive pace. Andy leaned in to pet Winston, but as he did so he caught glimpse of the time on his watch. "Dang, I’d better go," he announced. Tallahassee Regional Prison was only four miles away from his home, but the road layout was set out in such an incomprehensible way that it may as well have been twenty. "Bye bye Daddy!" Kayla squeaked, pretending to be the voice of the dog. Andy leaned in and kissed her on the cheek, before gently patting Winston on the head and making his way out of the room. As he crossed over the threshold all thoughts turned to his brother. ---- Ahhhh yes, Andy and Cayle? where to start? Ever since Cayle had hopped across the Atlantic Ocean many years ago to join his brother, the two had been practically attached at the hip. While the two have only been officially involved in a wrestling promotion together twice, Andy had taken his brother under his wing and worked non-stop to try and help him replicate Andy’s own success in the business. Unfortunately, things didn’t go quite to plan. Within a year of moving to the United States of America Cayle found himself locked-up behind bars for the crime of assault. When he was released he found the wrestling world to be a lot less hospitable towards him than it had been before; after all, what wrestling promoter wants to have a known felon in their ranks? Andy fought tooth and nail to try and clear the name of his brother, sending out references and good reports to just about every promotion in the country. When Andy’s employers at the time ? the Atlantic Wrestling Club ? turned down the chance to hire Cayle he felt that all hope had been lost. But Andy Murray wasn’t the type to submit, and this case was no different. He wouldn’t give up on aiding his brother’s entrance into the career he had longed for, and soon enough, following the collapse of AWC, he convinced FUSE to employ both him and his brother as a tag team; The Amazing Technicolor Dream Team. The Murray brothers would go on to enjoy a little taste of success; capturing the FUSE equivalent of the tag team championships early in their career, until a recurrence of the aforementioned injury threatened to put Andy on the shelf for good. And so, the contracts of The Amazing Technicolor Dream Team were cancelled. Andy’s body was a wreck; promoters around the country were convinced that he would never be able to work a full-time schedule again without something going wrong, so FUSE pulled his contract. With Andy gone they also so no reason to kept Cayle around; they knew that the younger sibling was running on name value alone, and they had received so many complaints about Cayle from his fellow workers that having him around was actually doing more harm than good. The general consensus was that Cayle Murray, in stark contrast to his brother, was in love with the glamour and glitz of the wrestling world, and wasn’t up to putting in the hours of hard graft it would take to carve a niche in this hostile environment. The worst was yet to come for Cayle Murray, however. As his brother fought against other personal demons, Cayle was jailed for a second in June 2007 for the same crime as before; assaulted. Andy’s brother might as well have driven a knife through Andy’s shoulder blades; in fact, that would have probably felt a heck of a lot better for The Scottish King of Cool. Cayle had let him down for what seemed like the hundredth time in his life. Andy had put his own good name on the line by constantly vouching for his brother in the industry; heck, he had even taken him under his own roof in effort to curb his hedonistic lifestyle, all for Cayle to throw it back in his face. Not once had he visited his younger brother in prison; he couldn’t bring himself to do it. At a time when he was trying to cope with the collapse of his marriage and relationship with his son, Andy didn’t need the distressed words of an incarcerated brother hanging over his head. In fact, it was only after a long, drawn-out conversation with his elder brother Sidney that Andy had decided to let Cayle back into his life following his release. Cayle had no idea that Andy would be coming for him, and Andy had no idea how he was going to react. He could understand why Cayle might feel a great deal of anger towards him; after all, he hadn’t seen him in almost a year, and he fully expected his younger brother to completely fly off the handle at the first glance of him. However, regardless of his reaction, Andy knew that Cayle deserved another chance. Not once in his life had he given up on his brother, and he wasn’t about to start now. ---- TALLAHASSEE REGIONAL PRISON, TALLAHASSEE, FLORIDA, MONDAY 12th MAY, 2008, 1:05pm The cool summer’s breeze gently caressed Cayle’s arms, providing a refreshing respite from the relentless heat of the sun as the prison yard gates slammed shut behind him for the final time. For the first time in eleven months he was a free man, and boy did it feel good. Muggy Tallahassee air filled his lungs as he felt beads of perspiration begin to form on his brow; to a Scotsman, the Floridian summer was very uncomfortable, even with the breeze. His rucksack slung over one shoulder, Cayle didn’t quite know what to do as he stood on the sidewalk, surveying his surroundings. The general locale of the prison was devoid of almost all human life, but it was by no means unpleasant. A seemingly endless forest lined either side of the road, giving the impression that the prison was a lot further away from the city than it actually is. The black Mercedes parked across the street didn’t catch his attention until a muscular, 6’7" figure gently pushed open the door and slid out into the heat of the outside world. Behind mirrored Aviator sunshades the figure cast his gaze on the former inmate; and for the first time in almost a year Andy and Cayle Murray laid eyes upon each other. A cascading tide of mixed emotions crashed over Cayle at the sight of his estranged sibling, and yet nothing could halt his march into Andy’s waiting arms. The embrace they shared was both strong and warm, but more importantly it completely annihilated any fears that had been lingering in the mind of either brother prior to the meeting. "It’s good to see you, man," Andy quietly said almost in a whisper. Cayle nodded. "You have no idea, bro." Andy extended his hand towards his brother, who removed the rucksack from his back without words. As Andy trudged around to the rear of the car, slinging the bag in the boot, Cayle paced around to the other side and entered the comfortable, air-conditioned world of his brother’s car. Andy soon followed, eager to get out of the beating sunshine, sliding back into the driver’s seat. "Jeesh, nice wheels man!" was the first thing that came to Cayle’s mind as he admired the leather interior of the very same care that Andy had used to aid his invasion of Benjamin Grunwald’s home many months ago. "Tell me about it; I wanted the one with hover pads and lasers? you know, all kinds of ill-shit. Apparently they don’t do that anymore; this ain’t exactly a bad substitute though, huh?" The brothers shared a spout of laughter; shooting the shit about completely inane topics with his brother was something that Andy had missed dearly. Almost inevitably, though, the topic of conversation soon changed. "We’ve got an assload of catching up to do, Andy," Cayle understated. "Yeah, I think I owe you an apology," Andy said, abandoning the task of turning the ignition key for just a moment. "I should’ve come to visit, I’m sorry?" Cayle nodded. "Brother, you have nothing to apologise for. I shouldn’t have gotten into this whole mess in the first place; it’s all my own doing. I should be the one doing the grovelling?" If there had been any doubt lingering in Andy’s mind it was now completely eradicated. His brother had just said exactly what he wanted to hear, and Andy couldn’t have felt any better. "Water off a duck’s back, man," he said with a half-smile, looking Cayle dead in the eye. "So I heard about you and Jane," was Cayle’s almost instant retort. "Sid told me. Tough break, man?" Andy found it hard to tell whether or not the sincerity in Cayle’s voice was genuine. Cayle was the one who had first discovered Jane’s infidelities in the first place; giving him even more of a reason to hate the woman than before. Despite living under the same roof for a good portion of their respective lives, Cayle and Jane had never really seen eye-to-eye. Andy let out a mournful sigh. "Yeah well, you know, life goes on". He wasn’t letting up on his attempts to sound carefree. "Forget that battleaxe; that’s been done, no point in dwelling on it." The Scottish King of Cool turned the key, sparking the car’s engine into life. ---- The divorce proceedings weren’t pretty; but then again, they never are. In terms of assets, Andy, being the one who filed for divorce in the first place, came off better than Jane. The financial implications were minimal, and the few material possessions he had to forgo were expendable to Andy anyway. The emotional consequences, however, were far from agreeable. Jane was given custody of Marvin; Andy had seen that one coming a mile off? given the nature of his chosen career, no judge was ever going to give him custody. An un-married professional wrestler was hardly the ideal guardian for a growing child, so he was prepared for losing day-to-day contact with his son. What he wasn’t prepared for was having his contact time reduced to every second weekend. However the hell the judge had made this ridiculous ruling was beyond Andy, and he found it incredibly hard to accept. Not only that but the relationship between Andy and Marvin had soured to a point where it could hardly be classed as a "relationship" anymore. Obviously Jane had planted thoughts in his head; he didn’t know exactly what she had told him, but it had worked. His son was 100% convinced that the collapse of the marriage was Andy’s fault and that his father’s constant absences were because he didn’t care for his son, nothing to do with his career. Now, every fortnight, when Andy sees his son, he looks into his eyes and feels nothing but sorrow. He offered his beloved child so much in conversation, but rarely did Marvin answer back; at times Andy felt like he might has well be talking to a brick wall. In some twisted way Andy sometimes wished that he had never found out about his wife’s infidelities. If he hadn’t, then his relationship with his son would be just as warm and loving as it had been before, he was sure of that. But like Cayle, Andy would not give up on Marvin; one day he would repair the damage done by the divorce. One day? ---- THE HOME OF ANDY MURRAY, TALLAHASSEE, FLORIDA, MONDAY 12th MAY, 2008, 1:30pm The Mercedes crept along the gravel driveway before Andy eventually killed the gentle purr of the engine with a flick of the ignition. Immediately Cayle pushed the car’s door open and stepped outside, all mystified and wide-eyed. This was, of course, the first time he had laid eyes on Andy’s new home, and he liked what he was seeing. Despite retaining the rights to his previous home, Andy made the choice to move away from it just before Christmas 2007. At that point he was starting a new life with a new partner and new employers in GCW; quite simply there were far too many reminders of days gone by for him to consider sticking around. After the divorce it seemed like every item or piece of furniture in the old house was constantly dragging him back to another unpleasant thought of what his wife was doing behind his back. Besides, the place was faaaaar too big in size for him to justify owning, especially considering it was home to no more than three people at any one time. "Nice lookin? digs, man!" Cayle excitedly proclaimed as his brother stepped out the opposite side of the car. "I mean, it’s a little smaller than before, but it looks a heck of a lot more inviting!" That it did. The four-bedroom, two-storey building could have easily set Andy back over a million dollars, and the quickest of glances showed why. The pristine, white exterior seemed to glow radiantly on as fine a day as this and the small, green shrugs that lined the ground surrounding the house were so neatly trimmed that they might as well have been maintained by a professional. "Glad you approve," Andy smiled. He loved days like this; they brought out the best features in a home that he had grown to adore. After retrieving his brother’s bag from the door he took some time himself to admire the building, before gently tapping Cayle on the shoulder and leading him towards the door. "Come on, Kayla and Winston are just dying to meet you?" "Winston?" Cayle raised an eyebrow. "Jeesh, Andy, you didn’t have another kid, did you?!" "Not exactly?"
