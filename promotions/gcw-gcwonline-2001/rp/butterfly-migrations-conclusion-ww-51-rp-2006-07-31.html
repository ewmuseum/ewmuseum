Butterfly Migrations, Conclusion (WW 51 RP)

31 July 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/31/2006 (OOC: Of the three-part Butterfly Migrations series, this one is for judging toward Katie's debut match in WW 51.) “Well, I can’t say that you seem to have lost any of your speed, or polish,” Stuart LaPortia said as he watched Mary working out in the gym. It had been almost a year since they’d seen each other, even though they’d kept in touch regularly since the closure of the old wrestling league, SAWF. “Sure and not bad for a colleen pushing 32, eh, Stuart?” Mary grinned mischievously, working over a heavy bag with a barrage of accurate savate kicks so fast that only Mary herself could determine the exact left foot ? right foot sequence; and nobody else in the gym could determine the exact color pattern on her hi-top athletic shoes as her feet flew through the air with lightning quickness. “And you’re not even breathing hard yet,” Stuart marveled. “I’d best not, lest I want to find meself consigned to permanent midcarder status or worse.” Stuart’s face took on a look of concern for his friend and client. “Are you sure you want to do this at all, though, Mary?” he asked. “Are you sure there isn’t any other field of employment you could pursue?” Mary delivered one last forward roundhouse ? back roundhouse kick combination to the heavy bag and took a step away from it so she could look Stuart in the eyes. Stuart’s use of her real name wasn’t lost on her?- he wasn’t quite ready to start calling her Katie again until it was completely “official” that she was taking up wrestling again. “I wish I could,” she said simply, “and sure and I haven’t entirely given up on it yet. I’ve sent out r’sum’s with Percival’s help, and answered a few ads... but so far the results have not been encouraging. I have been looking, but what matters is results, and that is what I haven’t been able to get yet.” Stuart nodded quietly and gave Mary a hug. “Well, you just need a medical exam and check-up, and I can submit a portfolio to some wrestling leagues. I’m a free-agent now, instead of working exclusively for one league, so I can be a contact for a number of different leagues. I’ll try some of the Prime-Time Central leagues first; they’re all affiliated with one another, meaning if you’re accepted into the network, they’re guaranteed to assign you to one of the member leagues.” Mary smiled a little weakly, but warmly, at her once-again agent. “I appreciate it, Stuart. Sure and I shall keep ye apprised of me job search.” Stuart being in her corner was very reassuring. He was a savvy agent with a lot of contacts, and as the saying went, “it’s not what you know; it’s whom you know.” Not overly impressive in physical size or build (much like Mary herself, which was probably one reason they bonded in the way they did), he was very shrewd and smart. He had the know-how and he had the experience. Mary couldn’t have asked for a better agent or friend. The doctor Stuart arranged to give Mary her check-up and exam, was professional and warm. She treated Mary with all the respect and compassion the diminutive Irishwoman could have asked for. She pronounced Mary in exceptional health and fitness for a woman her size and age, and signed all the forms Stuart had provided for her to sign. Once all the paperwork was filed, there was little to do other than wait. Patience may have been a virtue, but it could also be painful... like sitting on pins and needles. Mary passed the time by finding a good school to enroll Denise in. The young girl wanted to travel to the different wrestling venues, but her adoptive parents were against it. Even John would remain at the apartment with his father and sister, unless there was a long trip far from Vermont. And even if there was a lot of traveling, Mary was determined to come back to the apartment every single chance she got so the family could be together. Fortunately, the Prime-Time Central network, according to Stuart, had a looser, easier show schedule with a little more off-time than any of the leagues Mary had wrestled in previously. This meant more opportunities to return home... or in the case of the Rottonbottoms, home-away-from-home. Denise was enrolled in an open-corridor private school with opportunities for her to catch up on classes in case of any pay-per-view shows that might be held in Canada or another country outside the United States, in which case Percival and the kids would go along. This was assuming, of course, that none of the other job opportunities panned out. They didn’t. The best offer Mary received was a short-order breakfast cook at a decent diner. Mary’s time as an innkeeper had made her suitable for cooking breakfast meals for the diner’s guests, but the pay and benefits, or lack thereof, were unsatisfactory. The Rottonbottoms had bills to pay, the least of which was the rent on their new three-bedroom apartment... and while Percival’s new job was comfortable, Mary was not about to make the family rely on it for their expenses. Denise’s new school wasn’t exactly cheap, and the ever-rising price of gasoline was harsher in America than in the United Kingdom. The only other opportunity that even gave Mary consideration was a dinner chef position at one of Burlington’s finer restaurants; one which required reservations and formal attire. Mary was one of the final two candidates for the job; her rival being no better or worse at cooking than she was. But Mary had two years professional experience as an innkeeper; her rival had five years experience in a restaurant. He got the job. “Sorry to hear it, kid,” Stuart said sympathetically when he called a couple of days later. “At least I have some better news.” Mary grabbed a notepad and a pencil. “Sure and let us hear it, then,” she answered. “You’ve been accepted into Prime-Time Central... they’re going to give you a tryout match with the GCW league on August 7th in a show in Tacoma, Washington. You’ll be facing another new hire by the name of Anguish. Aerialist; technician... a little like yourself.” Mary grinned sardonically. “Sure and I shall be a raw rookie again,” she quipped, jotting down the information Stuart had given her. “Think you can handle it... Katie?” That was all the Monarch Butterfly needed to hear. “Sure and I can handle it,” she answered. “When do we sign the contract?” “Two days, in the GCW headquarters in Columbus, Ohio. They’ll provide your tickets to Tacoma once you’re signed. Here’s the address...” Katie O`Clare jotted it down and nodded. “Ye were always a good contract negotiator, Stuart,” she smiled. “I have little doubt that even with raw rookie pay and status again, I shan’t be left wanting once ye are done negotiating. Ye draft up the forms... I shall do the moonsaults and the Indian deathlocks.” “Then don your clip-on butterfly wings and your antenna headpiece, and get ready for the Monarch Butterfly to fly again. And Katie... welcome back.”
