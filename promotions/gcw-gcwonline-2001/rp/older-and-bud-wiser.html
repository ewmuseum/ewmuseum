Older and (Bud)wiser

undated

Date: Tonight In my younger days I would go bar hopping, or on a pub crawl. Whatever you wanted to call it where you hit as many bars as you could in one night drinking as much as you could before you swore the ground was moving underneath you trying to throw you off your feet to land face first in the gutter. Once I started wrestling I had to stop that. I had to keep my body in prime condition to kick ass and take names. I couldn't win matches, and more importantly titles with a hangover. Don't get the wrong idea, I didn't swear off drinking. I swore off getting drunk. Hence the reason I decided to get out of the confines of my hotel room and find a nice bar to sit in and toss back a few. The thing was finding just the right place. I didn't want a hole in the wall bar, but I didn't want some overly commercial, smoke-n-mirrors place that had more neon per square inch than the Vegas strip. Like the first place I passed. Maddens. No doubt named for former Raiders coach (and video game icon) John Madden. The place was floor to ceiling windows that showed off the 20-something spoiled rotten shits inside, with their designer clothes, cell phones, expensive cars in the parking lot and 'trendy' fashions. All probably paid for by mommy and daddy. I could also hear the latest crap pumping out of the club as I sat at the stoplight. I had no idea what flavor of the month was singing, nor did I care. I passed a few more places like Madden's that had neon, big screen TV's, sports memoribilla, and other high-end trendy shit in them. I hated places like that. Maybe it's because I wasn't 18 anymore. Either that or I was jaded at 29. I passed the place and circled back. The name had put me off at first, but a second look told me that this was just the out of the way place I was looking for. The Oakland Raider. It didn't blare it's name out into the street for everyone to see. Just the name in Raider's colors over the door with the Raider's logo next to it. It was small, kinda dark, and not packed to capacity, with vapid Britney Spears wannabes. I walked in and there was no smoke and mirrors. The only smoke came from the cigarettes hanging from the mouths of the patrons at the bar. The lights were subdued to say the least. A handful over the bar, a few spread out throughout the expanse of the place (which truth be told wasn't too expansive to begin with), a couple of old, battered, cigarette burned video games in the corner that looked like they hadn't been played since the Playstation came out, a few tables and chairs scattered about, and an old beaten looking jukebox currently playing AC/DC's 'Back In Black', followed by 'Rock and Roll All Nite' by Kiss. The only lavish thing in the whole place was the plasma screen TV behind the bar playing ESPN. 'My kinda place.' I mumbled under my breath. A few heads turned in my direction as I walked in and took a seat at the bar. I rarely got second looks, a few people looked like they were studying the tribal tats on my arms which started at my wrists and disappeared under the sleeves of my black Harley Davidson t-shirt. 'What'll it be?' the bartender asked after I situated myself. I studied the beer listings on the wall and ordered an Icehouse. Most of the people were watching the Toughman competition on the TV. The jukebox played out the Kiss song and fell silent. After killing my first beer I ordered a second one. As I did all eyes went towards the door as it opened. The bartender rolled his eyes and muttered 'Oh shit...' and headed back to the cash register to get my change. The guy that walked in looked like he was fairly big, but not in an imposing way. In a way that said he never met a beer he didn't like. He had a beer gut on him, hair that looked like he just ran his hands through it, and five...no make that six, maybe six thirty shadow. He came over to where I was sitting and looked at me as if wondering if I was real or not. For my part I ignored him, and contined watching the steroid cases on TV pull a train. He tapped me on my shoulder. I glanced over without turning my head and said 'What?' 'Yer sittin' in my seat pal. Wanna move?' 'No not really.' I said not looking over, turning my eyes back to the TV and taking a couple swallows of my beer. 'Maybe you didn't hear me.' he said. 'Now Jake...' the bartender said. 'Shut up, Steve.' the neanderthal named Jake said. Then he turned his attention back to me. 'I said yer sittin' in my favorite seat. Get out of it.' The people that were sitting around me got up and backed away, taking their drinks with them, apparently Jake had a reputation of being a professional asshole. 'I didn't see your name on it when I sat down.' I said, still not looking at him. He slapped a meaty hand on my shoulder, 'Get out of my seat or I'll make you get out of it.' Now I looked over at him, turning my head enough to my left that I could see his ugly face, and raised an eyebrow. 'You have three seconds to get your hand off my shoulder or I'll break it.' I said and took another swallow of my beer. He laughed, 'Really? A pipsqueak like you?' Pipsqueak? Did this cockslap just call me a pipsqueak? Now granted I wasn't the largest guy in the world, but I was six foot six and two fifty five. I was hardly what I would consider a pipsqueak. Oh yeah, and I was a professional wrestler. 'And your three seconds are up.' he said. 'You're right. They are.' I flung my left hand back, knocking his hand off my shoulder and spun around launching a right hand into his ribcage, hearing an audible crack as I connected. He dropped to his knees, 'Fuck..I think...ahhh...shit...I think you broke a rib...' 'I did.' I said matter-of-factly. 'You might wanna get to a hosptial and get that checked out.' I turned back to the bar and killed the last of my beer as he staggered to his feet and made his way to the door, grunting in pain. 'Thanks.' the barteneder said, 'It's about time someone put him in his place.' 'One thing I've learned over the years is no matter how big and bad you are, or think you are. there's always someone who's bigger and badder.' I said, 'Can I have another beer?' 'Sure.' he said fishing another bottle out of the cooler, I reached for my wallet, but he stopped me. 'It's on the house.' In the old days I woulda taken Jake to task and mopped up the floor with him, but I was older, wiser, and smarter. I didn't break his rib though, I just bruised it at the very least. The old Mike Savige would have sent him to the hospital in critical condition. Looks like married life tamed me more than I thought. Anyway I had to save all my engery for work. I'm a professional wrestler, not a common street thug. At least not anymore.
