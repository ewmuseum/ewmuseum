Hero?

5 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5 Nov 05 ** This RP has nothing to do with LeStatt Knight. I just have some things in my head that I need to get out and so I began this story. I will periodically write this story and maybe even turn it into an e-wrestler. For now though, I'd like to share it with you. Ben you don't have to count this as a RP or anything, or any of the ones that will follow. ** Four years. Four fucking years. I stand tall, a light breeze blowing in my face as I stand in front of all of them. They watch me but they don’t move, hell I can barely tell that they are breathing. I don’t move either. I stare at them just as they stare at me, their eyes fixed into my movements. It’s a mass of green and black, two colors that have come to mean more to me then I would ever imagine when I was younger. Two colors that became more than colors, but symbolic of a lot of emotions that have mixed up inside of my body. Then again for the last four years it hasn’t really been my body at all. All I had to remember was how to breath and eat, anything else was delegated to me. Not that it was all bad, its not like I was exactly some kind of slave. I did as I was told, in the uniform I needed to be in, at the time I needed to do it. I took orders ? but there was a certain pride in that. It is not like taking orders from a McDonald’s manager late at night who wants to sleep and make you do all the worth, it means more than that. “COMPANY! ATTEN-TION!” The sea of green and black move just once, in unison. Its like a well oiled machine except this machine breaths, sees, and lives. It’s a machine that I have proudly been apart of, although I haven’t always shown my pride in it. Every time that green and black touched my body a sense of pride and accomplishment rushed through me, as if a surge of energy ran through my veins. It makes you want to smile, to hoot and holler with excitement but you know you can’t because you cannot ruin the machine, you cannot be the one out of place part. I remember the day this all began, four fucking years ago. 18 years old and just finding my way out of High School, which was a damn challenge in itself. What was I going to do with my life afterwards? I was far from rich and hadn’t the grades for a college scholarship. I didn’t want to be working a dead end job, such as working the cash register at Taco Bell. I knew there had to be something more, something that would help my future. I found what I was looking for. Sacrifices had to be made though, the kind of sacrifices that I had never seen myself making as I was getting into my teenage years. My family was everything to me. Anyone who knew me for even a second knew that the ones I could trust the most weren’t friends I had met in First grade or High School ? but my siblings and cousins that had been there since day one. I had always envisions myself in my city, living and loving, hanging out and “chillin”. The thing I never thought about is the day I had to grow up and become the man that was expected of me. How was I to support the woman of my dreams that I had found? The woman that finally showed me that she cared for me the same way I did her? The Green and Black. I was approached on a late May afternoon. I originally would have brushed him off but something about it made me listen to what he had to say. I don’t know if I believe in fate, but sometimes I wonder now if this is what I was meant to be, meant to do. I got my opportunity to provide myself with a life and I figured I’d take it. “POST THE ORDERS!” A strong voice comes from the side of the sea of Green and Black and carries through the late afternoon air. The voice speaks to me deeper than it could ever know. It speaks of what I have done in my time wearing the green and black, what I did in my time as part of this well oiled machine, what I accomplished as a protector of all that lives. It speaks of words such as Honor, Loyalty, Personal Courage, Selfless service, Integrity, Duty, and Respect. It tells me that I showed each and every one of these values in my everyday work, that I was a trusted and valued member of the team. It tells me I should be proud of all I have become. Then again it will tell every piece of this machine the same words one day. Regardless of the time you do, the job you have, and the work you accomplish the words are always the same ? the meaning never different. Its this that eats at me and it always has. DO I really deserve the same kind of showering of love and admiration as someone who has battled through far greater adversity? Do I really deserve to be the one to get a medal pinned on my chest for merely going through the motions? To me a hero is more than that. I signed my name on a piece of paper and instantly became some sort of hero to many people around the country ? but after being in for these four years I know better than to take that kind of credit. I took a chance, a calculated chance but I took one by signing my name. I ended up in a frozen tundra, but a frozen tundra that was hardly hell to live through. The voice said its peace and stopped. Applause. I look out at the sea of green and black, the machine had taken a break and now I saw faces. They smile up at me, standing in front of them. I look down and then at the large man next to me who is leading the applause. I smile slightly before looking back at the people who had been at my side the entire time. In their eyes I saw my life for the last few years. In those eyes I felt their annoyance, their longing, their happiness, their laughter, and their sorrow. Since I arrived we had to be together ? but STILL something was missing. The events of my time flash through my head. I think of everything from the day I signed my name, to when I had to say goodbye to my girlfriend ? the woman I would one day spend the rest of my life with, to the day I first put those colors on my back, and even to the day I first stepped off the plane and landed here on my tundra. Was any of this heroic? I don’t think so. I love My girlfriend, I love my family and I miss them all with every ounce of my being. The sacrifices I made were put at ease the day I arrived here. I thought it was what I wanted, keep it calm and cool. Did I really wish to be risking my life for a cause that I didn’t understand? Bombs, blood, sweat, and tears falling down around me. Minds shattering as if they were window glass, fear building up inside like a bottle under water. Is that the price I wanted to pay to be a hero? No ? not at first. I wanted to talk to my loves, play video games, eat junk food, watch pornography, hell maybe even jack off while doing it. I wanted to go see a new movie when it came out and the only fucking sand I wanted to see was on the Hawaiian beaches of Honolulu. Something funny happened in my first year in my safe Haven though. Something unexplainable to me even as I stand before my friends and family of the past four years ready to say goodbye for the final time. For some reason I didn’t feel the contentment that I thought I had. I wanted more. I spent my four year career sitting behind a desk, pushing buttons, looking at e-mail, answering phone calls, and dealing with Civilian and military customers alike. I was in the Army ? but was it really the Army? I wanted that sense of meaning to what I did, and what I stood for. I wanted people to look at me not just for the colors I wore, but for the man underneath. But it was a conflict. Some part of me wanted to know for sure that I could be there for my soon to be wife. The safe life was the only way to know that for sure. She didn’t want my life risked and she thought that It was what I wanted as well. I said the right things to make her think that I disliked the military life as much as she did, but deep down it was nothing but a fa’ade. I cherished my days in the Service. I craved that pride and sense of respect you were given when you put that Green on your back and that beret on your head. I loved how people would stare at my boots, seeing their reflection in them and KNOW I was a great soldier. Deep down I wanted to be over in hazard’s way, helping my comrades who deserved the great honor of being called a hero. My time never came. So as the stage was handed to me and I had to find the words to tell my comrades here in our Safe Haven how I felt about it all, I hesitated. My eyes looked upwards for a brief moment, again recalling my feelings, and asking all of our fallen friends to give me guidance. I let my eyes drop and look back at the sea of green and black once more. I smile now and clear my throat. “It’s been a long road that’s for damn sure?” It wasn’t completely true. To me the four years flashed by so fast that if you had blinked you would have missed it. I wonder if I will ever tell people that I served in the military? I wonder if I will ever allow anyone to give me that respect, the respect that I am not so sure that I earned? I wonder? Did I even do my part? Four Fucking Years ? And there is still a whole in my life that I just can’t seem to fill. Four Years ? I loved every one of them.
