Between The End and Where We Lie Pt 1; Myself TO Blame

26 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 26 2006 An unusually warm November evening sun rested just over the horizon as my feet trudged through a path laid out in an assortment of dried leaves, benches, and brick adorning the trail through the Charles Bridge in Prague. My running shoes crunched under the last remaining memories of a fall season that, to some people, had lasted too long; while to others, namely me, it just wasn’t long enough. Though, even with the beautiful vision of the sun resting just over the city to wash over my nerves in a country I had never been to, I found no solace here. I hated the cold. Winter was beginning to come in strong and the cold air stung my lungs and tightened the muscles in my legs. The dropping temperature helped to numb the bruises of a cage match that still decorated my body, but I was still not accustomed to working out in anything other than warm climates. There wasn’t any time for pain though; there was never any time for anything other than training. It was my gift, an unrelenting need to be better than the man I was supposed to step into the ring with. But, to some it was viewed as my curse, the pride and devotion to a cause that would ultimately be my downfall. On schedule, the street lamps flickered on as the sun made its exit behind the Prague backdrop. My heaves of breath became visible as I ran on, trying to get those last few meters in. But, yet again my thoughts floated back to the business, to my friends, and to what seemed to be the most important part of me. Even though, the weeks leading up to NC17 had helped to form a firm connection between the Ascension members and myself, I was beginning to feel more and more like an outsider with them. I had known from the beginning that joining would be a challenge, but with the dwindling members, and constant attacks with no end in sight, this war was beginning to wear me thin. More than that, my own drive to succeed in this business was beginning to separate me from the rest of the pack. When the night’s matches were over, and the dust had settled, Tessa’s and Ciel’s work for the night was practically done, and they went home to their families. Their Families? I missed it. For the past ten years, Brack, had been the closest thing to a family that I had. But, even with him there I felt myself missing what others had? what I used to have, what my family gave up. It had been so long since I had been able to go home to one of my own, that I had almost forgotten what it felt like. My legs slowed, as I wrapped my body around a lamp post, and choked on my tired breaths. The more I struggled on the thoughts swirling around my personal life, the more I tried to push them out by focusing on my training; by focusing on the man or men I would be stepping into the ring with. But, once again my personal life and work intermingled in my mind. For the second week in a row Brack had seen fit to put himself in a predicament that both, helped to ensure my place in GCW while also putting him in the cross hairs of a war that he never wanted to be part of in the first place. I couldn’t stand to be around him knowing that I had pulled him in this battle. I could still see the smile on his face as Blaise and Lox had their way with him at Devil’s while I was stuck on the outside of the cage fighting to get at them. He knew what he had done, knew what it would take, and gladly accepted the consequences of his actions; helping to ensure Ascension’s victory that night, and helping to claim me a title match against Blaise McClancy. Yet, I knew more than anyone, deep within that rough exterior, the rude comments he made to others, and his loyalty to me, hid a secret that he was willing to die to protect. “What happened that night, Brack?” The words slipped through a pair of gritted teeth as I struggled to put the pieces of my past back together; a past that Brack had hoped I would forget. “What am I missing?” ~~~ September 14th 1996 1:15 AM I remember it was only a few days after my brother’s funeral as I sat in my room, staring at the ceiling. For the third night in a row, I found myself unable to sleep, awakened to the red and blue lights of police squat cars outside my bedroom, and thinking about Alex’s death. The tears were still fresh on my face as I struggled to imagine of a life without my brother at my side to see me through. I remember peering out the window to catch a glimpse of my father, standing there amidst family members, friends, and the stroboscopic red and blue lights adorning my parent’s front yard. I dared not to step outside to see what all the commotion was about; I was supposed to be in bed, sleeping. More than that, after Alex’s funeral, my father never looked at me the same, stopped coming in to see how my day was, stopped with the kisses to the forehead, and had stopped with anything the would’ve resembled intimate contact between a father and son; he, like the rest of the family, blamed me for Alex’s death. But, deep down, so did I. I buried my head in my pillow and squeezed, until only the dancing shadows of my darkened room were privy to my mournful sobs? and that’s where they stayed. ~~~ September 14th 1996 3:26 AM “Alex!” my voice bellowed out through the darkness of my room. It happened as it always did; with me waking wide eyed in the night, sweat pouring from my face, and the wind howling through a cracked window. My curtains whipped violently through the room as I struggled to recognize my own room. I finally managed to calm my heart as I laid in bed grasping my chest. I leaned over to my night stand reaching for a glass of water. Nothing. The last drop dangled from the otherwise empty glass, unlit it hit my dried tongue. I didn’t want to go downstairs to the sink for more, but I was beyond thirsty by now. I sat up, peering out the window as I did. “They’re gone.” I whispered to myself, searching for the crowded yard I had been greeted to earlier. But, it appeared that whatever had happened had been resolved and everyone had gone on about with their nightly business. So, with a sigh, I slid into some flip-flops and tip-toed my way out of my door and down the stairs. I remember I was half way to the kitchen when I noticed the lamp on in the living room. I poked my head around to find my parents on the couch, arguing; something that had become a common occurrence since my brother went on. “ENOUGH!” My father’s voice startled me and before I realized it, the glass had slipped from my hands and onto the floor. I watched, practically in slow motion as it smashed into the floor, into hundreds of tiny little pieces. I wanted to catch it, wanted to gasp, wanted to run, but I froze in terror. I remember hearing my father’s heavy footsteps stalking toward me as I stood against the side of the wall. I quickly dropped to the floor, desperately trying to sweep up the shards of glass with my hands as the sound of my father’s footsteps inched closer. “What the hell are you doing here?” bellowed my father’s monstrous voice in thickly accented Spanish. My head snapped up. I couldn’t speak, couldn’t breath, or even choke on my words. I just stared at him wide-eyed and silent. “A” I? I wanted? a glass of water.” I finally managed to murmur, pointing at the remains of the my shattered glass. “Leave.” He demanded “But the glass?” “GO!” He was angry; REAL angry. But, I couldn’t do anything but scuttle up the stairs. My father turned back to the living room as I folded into a ball at the top of the stairs. He hated me, I could feel it when I looked into his eyes. Yet, through my terrified whimpers I could hear my parents bicker back and forth. “I will not give up my last and only son!” my mother sobbed. “We don’t have a choice, Maria. We can’t allow this to happen again.” “But where will he go and who can we trust with him?” “Brack, will take him” far away from here.” ??And you trust him?” “More than anything Maria! He helped to train Julio and loves him like a son.” I had heard enough and slowly sulked toward my room. If there were any doubts in my mind before that, about how my father felt about me, they had been quelled by his admittance to not wanting me around. I remember thinking to myself that I only had myself to blame and perhaps things would be better this way. ~~~ September 21st 1996 5:56 PM It was a week later and I remember the heavy rain splashing across the hood of the car as my father cut the engine looking to the airport entrance. “I don’t expect you to understand what I am trying to do here, Julio” he said in an uncharacteristically soft voice keeping his eyes locked ahead. “But, there isn’t anything left for you here. Plus, Mr. Conway is a good man.” I didn’t wait for him to finish and, instead, opened the door, grabbing my suitcase, and made my way out the door into the airport. My father didn’t follow me in, but I could feel his stare on my back. Brack greeted me at the entrance to the terminal, but I said nothing to him aswell. I bowed my head, taking one last look at my home, my country? my Mexico and walked on through the doors. I shed no tears, because in the end, I only had myself to blame. ~~~ Present Time I finally managed to peel myself from the lamp post looking to the now-luminous moon standing directly over me. I wasn’t sure what Brack thought had happened on that night, but right now I didn’t care. I was tired of having my family torn from me. I was tired of letting people like Lox and Blaise have their way with me and mine? But Tonight I would show them, tonight I would make a difference? Starting with Lox!
