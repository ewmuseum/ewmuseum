Smile, Scott, Smile

27 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/27/10 I've decided to start a journal. People tell me all the time that I should probably write down my feelings or stuff. They said it might help me smile more. Apparently I don't smile enough. I really don't want to, but I have another purpose for writing this. "Man, I almost hope no one ever finds this" he muttered, putting the pen down for a moment. He rubbed his temple, allowing himself a moment to gather his thoughts before he returned the pen to its paper. That purpose is to be remembered. My name is Scott Drake, most people know me as Chris Storm. I am a professional wrestler, a husband, and a father of four. Even if you do not remember me for any of those, at least remember me for writing this journal. "…" Scott placed the pen down, debating if he should even continue writing or not. I realize that I may not be remembered favorably. I have made many decisions in my life that people would not agree with. I have threatened those once close to me, threatened their very existence, their very lives. It may not have been the right thing, but it is what I chose to do. I will stand by that decision until the very end. It may be stubborn but it is my way of doing things. I will never bat an eyelash over that, even as people scorn me for it, though those that do are close to me. I told Markus Stone I would break his neck. I really meant it. If he had not retired I would have broken his neck in our match. That would have casted my legacy in an extremely harsh light, if not completely ruined it. Just uttering the words have casted me in a negative light. I can deal with a negative light. After all, I just need any light to shine. "Scott!" Tessa's voice cried out. He laid the pen down next to his journal and quickly placed both of them into the bottom drawer. "Yeah, I'm in the den" he told her, making sure to lock away the drawer before looking toward the door. Seconds later Tessa Windsor stood in the frame, looking none too pleased as she held some papers in her hands. She waved them at him, her frustrations showing in every fiber. "What is the meaning of this?" He didn't even have to see what the papers were, he knew already. Giving her a shrug, Scott turned away from Tessa and focused on the empty space in front of him. "I already did what I had set out to do." "And I'm already upset enough with you about that!" Seeing that he didn't seem to have anything in front of him, she slapped the papers down onto the desk. "But this is just…just…I don't even know." "What's the big deal? You didn't even want me to return before." He grabbed the paperwork, getting it neat and organized before laying it back flat on the desk. "Now you're upset that I want to leave." She could only throw her arms up in the air in frustration, wanting nothing more than to slap him upside his head with a brick or other such large and solid object. "You didn't think to talk to me about it before you trying to quit?" Despite her harsh words and even harsher tone, Scott smiled wide at her. "I get what this is about, you aren't mad that I want to quit. You're mad that I didn't tell you before." "Oh course I am!" she bellowed, almost knocking him out of his chair. "We're supposed to be a team; you can't just go running off and doing whatever you want without telling me. We're supposed to be past this." "You didn't want me to join anyways, what was the big deal if I quit without telling you?" "Exactly!" Her arms again found their way into the air as she shouted. "You didn't even tell me you were going to rejoin either, I had to find out about that on the dirtsheets." He finally stood up from his desk, turning to face her. "I'm sorry" he told her, hoping that it would help in some way. He didn't mean it, and she knew he didn't mean it. He still felt like he did nothing wrong. She knew there was no chance of getting through to that thick skull of his, at least not for now. If she wanted to try and talk to him, she would have to do it later. "Fine, whatever" she muttered, letting him know he was off the hook for now. But her tone let him know that the conversation was far from over. Scott walked past her, trying his best not to make eye contact as he left the den. He wanted this to be over before she tried to draw him back into it. Thankfully she let him pass by her without another word said, though her eyes did weigh heavy on his back. "I'm heading to the gym" he weakly told her as he headed toward the bedroom to grab his gym bag. Expecting that from him, Tessa just rolled her eyes "of course". It just seemed like his answer to any situation, to escape to the gym where he didn't have to worry about anything. It was just like him. Scott allowed her comment to without a reply, knowing that nothing he could say would fix the situation. ~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~! Scott opened the door to the gym and within his first five steps he already heard Hershel hollering at him. "You didn't tell her, did you?" "Ah damnit Hershel, this is when you know you've gone to the same gym for too long" he muttered as he tossed his gym bag onto the counter. The old man running the gym just laughed, "A blind man could see that one coming." "Now don't tell me you're about to start up on me too" Scott moaned, hoping he hadn't just left a lecture for another one. With a wave of his hand, Hershel let him know he wasn't going to get lectured here. "Nope, I know you're a 'go-it-alone' kind of a guy, but I just thought it was funny considering you're supposed to be a team specialist, or whatever it's called." Scott shook his head, realizing he HAD been lectured, just in a much more subtle way. "Tag team, but yeah, I get what you're saying." He stopped, looking around the gym for a moment. "Hey, where that kid at? What was his name, Sam? Steve? Haven't seen him around." "It was Johnny, and he's been working out over with Bruce. Don't you remember?" Hershel shook his head disappointedly, knowing that Scott should have remembered that name better. When Hershel corrected him on the name, Scott started to rub his chin as if it was all coming back to him. "Yeah, he still hanging around Bruce? Figured by now he would realize what a bum the guy was and quit." "He wants to break into the business. Bruce has been in the business, probably figures if he works hard enough he might be able to make it too." Hershel looked over his shoulder, as if he expected someone to be behind him, but it was only the storage room to the gym. "Been giving him some work around here so he could continue to pay for the training." Scott just rolled his eyes, "Bruce was a hack who never even won a belt. I could teach the kid twice as much as Bruce in half the time." That was all Hershel needed to hear before he jumped all over Scott. "Then why don't you teach him!" He gave a laugh, "heck you been mentioning you wanted to train someone for years now. Just let him shadow you for a bit while you work out and gradually teach him some stuff." Like that Scott knew he had fallen into a trap. "You got some kind of interest in this kid or what? This is the second time, if my memory doesn't fail me, that you gave me his sob story." "He's my nephew" Hershel answered with a Cheshire Cat grin. Scott shook his head, "figured something was up." Hershel leaned over the counter, trying to almost lean into Scott. "So what do you say? Maybe let him shadow you two or three times a week? Maybe get in the ring with him every so often? He'd learn an awful lot from you, just like you said." He was buttering him up good now, really trying to play into Scott's sense of pride. It was so obvious that even Scott could tell, who had to look away so to give nothing away with his eyes. "You're pretty much the best wrestler we've ever had at this gym. You're known around the world as one of the best technical guys. And the kid got talent, I'm telling you." While continuing to keep his eyes away, Scott replied back "tell'm that I'll meet with him next week. I'll see what he got for myself and if I like what I see, maybe I might give it a thought. But I'm not some personal trainer and I'm not going to hang my name out there for some kids, got it?" "That's all I'm asking, just to give him a chance" Hershel told him, "boy's like a son of mine." Scott allowed a sigh to roll out, "You know I'm only doing this because you asked." "And I thank you for it, I really do. And best of luck with the stuff with the misses." "Don't remind me" he muttered as he grabbed his bag off the counter. Hershel placed a hand on his shoulder, "you're a head-strong person, but you gotta remember that she's rather head-strong as well. Gotta be to put up with you for some long." He gave another laugh, this time full and rich. Scott threw it over his shoulder and walked through the door toward the locker room area. Hershel watched him as he left, chuckling to himself at the inexperience of youth. In the locker room, Scott slumped onto the bench and allowed his body to lean back and used the lockers behind him as a brace. He understood what people were saying to him, about how he couldn't always do what he thought was best, or at least needed to check with others first. Maybe at Shockwave, after all, there wasn't any easy way out of the match. "Yeah" he mumbled to himself, "at Shockwave." After all, there is no better way to show you care than to beat the piss out of someone else with your wife. "After that…," he looked up at the plain white ceiling above his head, "…after that I'm quitting, Garbage Bag be damned." If only it was that simple. He would love it if he could walk away, to just return to his condo and to watch his children actually grow up instead of short phone calls from the road. He would love it to be able to train someone who would be able to carry on his legacy instead of always having to run headfirst into whatever challenge laid before him in an attempt to build up that impossible legacy on his own. He would love it if he knew his knees, back, neck, and hands were still be healthy by the time he reached the age of forty rather than feeling like he was eighty. But he also loved the crowd, the excitement he got. He loved the challenge, the violence of it all. He loved every minute of every day so much that it was all he could ever think about. It drove him crazy being away from it all and having to watch others while he sat in a luxury box up above. Retiring again? He loathed the idea. It made him sick to his stomach. But he knew he had to. It wasn't for him; if he could he would go until he was hundred. It was for her. He would do the impossible for her, even if he never got the credit for it. Before he did that, however, he had a match against Jay Terror and Clyde Fox. There would be no disqualifications, no easy way out of the match. He had to wrestle, he had to get violent. If you have to go out, why not go out with a bang? Scott smiled, he liked that thought. Garbage Bag may not have meant it, but he gave Scott a great gift. For his last match, he is allowed to go all out. Nothing, and no one, was going to stop him from having his fun. Tossing his bag into his locker, Scott pushed himself off from the wall and sat upright on the bench. The smile on his face was stuck. It was something that wasn't going to leave until he walked through the doors of the Aloha Stadium. Then it would be game face time. But for now, he could smile. Smile, Scott, smile.
