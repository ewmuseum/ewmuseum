A lesson in Spanksgiving

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/27 - 11/28/2009 A little information for the uneducated and unfamiliar… The “Siamese twin” cities of Saint Joseph and Benton Harbor both straddle the Great Lake in extreme southwestern Michigan. To the local population, they’re known as the “Siamese twin” cities because they are connected by a single bridge that is metaphorically made of skin. Reminiscent of earlier days when segregation was prominent, the towns are entirely divided despite the fact that they are separated by less than one thousand yards. St. Joseph is a quaint and prosperous town with a booming tourism industry. The municipality of Benton Harbor lies in economic ruin, has been mostly abandoned, and has an economy that is powered by corruption and illegal practice. Saint Joseph is known for being elected as “one of the most romantic cities in the United States,” as it prominently displays on a billboard at the town’s main intersection. Benton Harbor is known for bringing national news coverage to the area after a week of unabated rioting. The late Harry Gast, a retired politician (and closet racist) whom had several decades of prominence in state and local government had always said, “The town of Saint Joseph puts the ‘white’ in ‘White Christmas.’ The badlands of Benton Harbor puts the ‘black’ in ‘Black Friday.’ It’s like a god damned yin yang, I tell you!” As polarized as these neighboring cities are, there is one lone entity that synthesizes them. That entity is the local rock radio station: Rock 103.7, (The) PUKE. No one truly cares for their particular brand of classic rock. It’s all songs you’ve heard a million times before, some would say. But, every weekday morning from 6am to 10am, The Happy Hangover Morning Show would draw listeners from both sides of the bridge. The Happy Hangover Morning Show was comprised of three members. Bones, the main talking piece, was a balding middle-aged man whose inability to gain weight granted him his radio handle. He had been in radio for several years. Then there was Catfish, the sidekick and producer who was mainly responsible for adding sound effects, splicing audio, and decorating lame jokes with wheezing laughter. The show’s third co-host was Cheap Labor. Have you heard of him? Thanksgiving, Schmanksgiving… Shortly after winning the Television Title and foolishly trying to pawn it off, Cheap Labor drove 300 miles west to his hometown of St. Joseph, Michigan. He arrived to his apartment shortly before dawn and lay down in his bed as the sun rose up. Labor’s Thanksgiving Day was morose and uneventful. He shook awake and started drinking around the time that most functional families were sitting down together for a home-cooked meal. He plopped himself in front of the television in nothing but his boxer shorts. All of Labor’s immediate relations lived within a few minutes of his apartment, but he had strategically forgotten to tell them he would be home for the holiday. This Thanksgiving was lonely and, for the most part, that’s just how he wanted it. No fights over how to properly cook the turkey. No in-depth dinner table discussions about Uncle Jerry’s colostomy bag. No more of Uncle Ken getting hammered and throwing empty beer cans at the TV as if it would somehow motivate the Detroit Lions any more than it had the year before. Truth be told, dining with Labor’s family would show you where his childlike tendencies happen to come from in the first place. Yes, sitting alone in the apartment… This was peace. This was peace in its most tranquil and depressing form. Cheap Labor’s cell phone sat on a TV tray to his immediate right. He waited for it to buzz or chime or light up, but it never did. In his own mind, he just wanted someone to call him so he could act at ease and dismiss them with chuckling sentiment. He wanted someone to be thinking of him, and he wanted to know who it was. There was one thing that Labor knew for sure: his wife, who was actively pursuing legal action to become his ex-wife, wasn’t wasting her time on such thoughts. They hadn’t spoken in months. There were constant daydreams that he would get a call from her out of the blue. Time heals all wounds, you know, so it could happen. She would talk to him in her familiar comforting tones of voice. “Hi, honey. Someone wants to say talk to you.” And then his wife would put the phone to the baby’s ear and the baby, who was just starting to learn her first words, would babble to him and fill him with an immense swell of happiness and… Labor shook awake again. Did you know that eating turkey and drinking in mass quantities both have sedating effects? He checked his phone. There was a voicemail. It was from Bones, his old radio buddy and one of the only people who were ever informed when Labor was coming back into town. Labor didn’t have to listen to the voicemail to know what it said. He uprooted himself from his chair, turned off the television, and began to get dressed with renewed vigor. Cheap Labor was keyed up. It was time for him to do what he did best. The setup, or the set-up, depending on how you feel about hyphens… Cheap Labor had knocked a girl up and needed to get a real paying job to support his growing family. That’s why he had amicably split from The Happy Hangover Morning Show in the first place. The remaining duo oftentimes missed their stunt boy. You see, Bones and Catfish are lazy creative geniuses. This is why their material is compatible enough to pull together two wholly different communities. This is also why they will never expand beyond their local market. They hatch brilliant, hilarious, psychotic plans and never have anyone available to enact them with flair and bravado. So, whenever their former stunt boy is back in town, it is time for him to make a cameo. Labor had been informed of the plan about a week earlier and had agreed to it instantly. Being re-launched into relevance, if only for a sweet fleeting second, was a constant goal of his. The set-up was easy and all ready complete. On the eve of Black Friday, Cheap Labor’s automobile currently occupied the very first spot in the very first row at Wal-Mart. He was driven back to Bones’s house and an alarm was set for 3:30 in the morning. Ah, yes. Black Friday would be a very black day indeed. Wait for it… The Happy Hangover Morning Show crew, sucking down coffee quicker than Tiger Woods can suck his marriage down to the tubes (too soon?), dropped Labor off at his car at 4:30 the next morning. Wal-Mart’s parking lot was all ready bulging past its capacity. Folks were lining up at the door on a remarkably mild morning for the end of November in Michigan. “You know what you call all that white trash stacked in one place?” This was Catfish. He was used to setting up the jokes. “A landfill?” Labor always said things worthy of a guffaw and little else “An episode of Cops?” Bones was generally wittier than this, too, but it was early. After some instruction, Labor hopped into his car - occupying the most coveted spot in all of Wal-Mart’s parking lot - and waited for a call on his cell phone. The remaining duo was heading to the studio where they would call Labor to set audio levels and then let the frolicking began. Labor’s hands were clammy with an anticipation that continued to grow as the crowds grew larger. This was about the time he would begin second-guessing his decision to agree to this, which is something Cheap Labor always did no matter how good or bad of a decision it was in the first place. Ten minutes later, the cell phone buzzed in his pocket. The crew made their audio adjustments. They made sure Labor’s cell phone reception was full so they didn’t miss any precious detail. It was 5:15 and there was a torrent of people pouring in and out of the store. It looked the aftermath of a trailer park enema. Motorists drove aggressively through the lot rows trying to find a preferential parking spot still somehow left open. It was time. He does all his own stunts! Cheap Labor, head cocked to secure his cell phone between his ear and shoulder, brought the car to life. He tested the brake lights, watching for the dim red glow of them in his rear-view mirror. “Okay, guys. Let’s see how this goes!” The car was shifted into reverse. From behind Labor’s right blind spot, there was an immediate squealing of brakes followed by a tirade of honking horns. A rust-bucket SUV had abruptly stalled the traffic flow in expectancy of Labor’s next move. The stunt boy inched his car backwards, using an irresponsible amount of room to back up a sub-compact car, and straightened the wheels. “Alllllllllllrighty, we’re all backed up here… let me go ahead and straighten these wheels so I can…. Hang on. There we go. Yep, we’re going to park much straighter this time.” Then, as if he had never backed out in the first place, Labor pushed the car into drive and let the car drag itself back into the parking spot. He removed the keys from the ignition, placed them in his lap, and locked the doors. The SUV’s driver side window came down in a flash and a volley of spittle was launched in the general direction of Labor’s car. After much honking of horns and flying middle fingers, the SUV’s driver gave up hope and rattled onward. As the parade of held-up cars idled by, they all gave their own revolted looks of anger. What would follow next would be the Happy Hangover Morning Show’s best audio of 2009. Black Eye-day… John Young was a former body builder that hated his wife. He hated shopping and he hated getting up early too. Yet, somehow these three forces had descended upon him on this particular morning. It was a necessary evil. His wife would bankrupt him if she paid full price for all the foo-foo shit she wanted, so it was best he accompanied her to finding discounts. At least he could retain possession of his credit card this way. Mr. Young had circled the Wal-Mart parking lot three or four times trying to find a vacant spot. His wife nagged that they could park at a neighboring business and just walk over, but he’d be damned if he was going to have do one more fucking thing he didn’t want to do. He was once held up by Cheap Labor’s antics, stranded in a long line of unmoving automobiles. He looked ahead saw a car back out of the very first spot, straighten its wheels, and then pull forward again to occupy the space. Mr. Young assumed it was an elderly person and dismissed the injustice. After passing the car and examining, he saw some young smartass jabber-jawing on a cell phone and whooping with laughter. John Young’s grits were burned. He had circled the lot a couple more times before falling victim to the radio stunt once more. This time, Mr. Young happened to be the first car held up by Labor’s stunt. The kid was still chattering on his cell phone with a big shit-eating grin on his face. John Young put his car in park and felt his mercury start to rise. He waited for a few seconds, waited for Cheap Labor to predictably pull his car forward, and then waited for Labor to kill its power. This is when John Young snapped. Much to his wife’s verbal resistance, John Young ambled out of his car and opened his trunk where he retrieved a crow bar. One may think of this as an unusual circumstance to have a crow bar in your trunk, but Young was a man of short temper and always carried his “ass-kicking kit” with him. He started towards Cheap Labor’s vehicle. The occupant was oblivious. Labor was in the middle of a cheesy monologue when ball-bearing knuckles rapped against his window. “Oh… crap…” Mr. John Young had thick ropes of veins bulging from his neck and his skin had turned the aggravated color of Campbell’s tomato soup. “You think you’re funny or something, you little asshole?” HOLYSHITHOLYSHITHOLYSHITHOLYSHITokayokayokaycalmdown. Just stick to the stunt, Labor, stick to the stunt. “No, sir. I just wanted to straighten the car out a little bit; was just a little too close to this other car and had to take care of it. Sorry to get your hopes up, but this spot is mine all mine.” Good judgment has never been a strong point for Cheap Labor. John Young roared like a stampeding wildebeest, and Labor saw the shit hitting the fan. “Get the fuck out of the car!” “Can we be honest with ourselves here, sir? Why would I want to get out of the car when you’re acting so irrationally right now?” Bones and Catfish, from the comfort of their radio studio, were doubled over in laughter at this point. John Young continued to miss the humor of the situation. He lumbered towards the front of Labor’s car and jumped on the hood, using his bare hands to punch spider webs into the windshield. The car’s shocks, old and worn out, moaned and squeaked like a bad porno. Labor briefly contemplated using his glove compartment as a Panic Room, a la Jodie Foster. In the background, John Young’s embarrassed and fat wife shrieked in disapproval. “GET OFF OF MY CAR, YOU CRAZY SONUVABITCH!” And, as though it were an early Christmas gift, John Young did. He walked back towards Labor’s driver side window while Labor continued to make cutting comments into his cell phone. “My gosh, sir, this is supposed to be the most wonderful time of the year. Maybe you should go back to bed, GRINCH!” The crowbar exploded through the driver’s side window with the Barry Bonds-like velocity. Splinters of glass danced into every crevice of the automobile. Labor dropped his cell phone onto the floor and tried to pick it up, but was unable to move thanks to a pair of baseball mitts firmly wrapped around his neck. Labor tried to scream for help but lacked the proper tools, like oxygen for example. A molded piece of concrete that former body builder John Young called a hand smashed firmly into Labor’s right eye, which began to bleed and swell immediately. John Young then leaned into the car so that Labor could get a good look at him, busted eye and all. “Happy fucking holidays, you twat. Now, kindly remove yourself from this spot.” John Young returned back to his car and waited. His wife chewed his ear, but he ignored her like he always did. He had gotten what he wanted. Cheap Labor heard his cell phone before he found it; he just had to follow the sounds of laughter. Bones and Catfish were hysterical about the entire incident. Later, when Labor would approach them for potential reimbursement for the card window, they would hardball him like they always did. “Hey, dude, we didn’t make you say yes to anything.” And, of course, they were right. The tie-in… Black Eye-day 2009 is a day that shall live in infamy for The Happy Hangover Morning Show. The audio clips of Labor’s misfortune aired and were met with much laughter and overwhelming response. The response, of course, came from both the white folks of St. Joseph and the black folks of Benton Harbor. Meanwhile, Cheap Labor was busy taping a garbage bag over the unfilled area where his window used to be. He sighed discontentedly. Very rarely on Thanksgiving did Cheap Labor have anything to truly be thankful for. The (merciful) end
