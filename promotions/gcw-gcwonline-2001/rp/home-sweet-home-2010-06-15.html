Home Sweet Home

15 June 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06/15/10 Then… They weren’t really that much bigger than me, but there were three of them. Ok, they were a lot bigger than me, and still the monsters of my world. I was nine and just a couple of years removed from my father’s murder, the event that put me out on the streets of Hong Kong, I had not had the sort of life a growing child needs. I was small, thin, weak, a typical street kid. You’ve seen dozens of me on those Save the Children commercials. And that’s exactly why I found myself there, at the end of a pier in Victoria Harbor, facing three bigger boys, and in real need of saving. I was in no shape for a fight, but I was going to get one. I had slipped up. I was so hungry and so tired. I didn’t see them until they were almost on top of me as I scooped up the fish tails from under the butchers’ tables there on the docks. You see, when they dress the fish straight off the boats, they lob off the tails and there’s always a little bit of meat there just in front of the fins. Gather up a bunch of tails and you’ve got yourself a meal! In finer places, with a different cut, you call it sushi. I called it life; my only real source of protein. Now and then charities gave out rice. But you can starve on just rice. I hadn’t eaten anything in three days by the time I hit the docks that night. I was so desperate that I got sloppy and these little thugs spotted me. I got turned around as they chased me down the pier, past the sleeping ships and the waves that lapped gently at the pilings as if there were no struggle for survival going on a just few feet above them. I was trapped. I backed up until my butt hit a box over which some nets were drying. The air smelled of salt, motor oil, and fish stink. Might have been the boys, but it was hard to tell. They were all as lean as me, straight and shapeless in their too big clothes, picked out of a garbage can or stolen, just like mine. One of them didn’t have a shirt and a large bruise on the side of his jutting ribs showed me where it might have gone. My shirt, on the other hand, held the precious cargo of food tucked hastily into an inside pocket and tied closed with string. They formed up in a half circle around me, moving forward slowly. ‘Who’s the weakest link here?’ I thought, struggling to see a way out. My stomach, already so contorted with hunger was now roiling with adrenaline, a rush of fear and that whole fight or flight thing that was now down to one choice. There. The one on the left slurred out a curse in Cantonese and tried to advance, but he stumbled. He was stoned. Idiot. He must have spent what little money he begged, borrowed, or stole for some opium or maybe heroin. Maybe even gave himself away for it instead of some food. Now he wanted mine. No way. He was already on his way to dying, but not me. Not without a fight. In better times I had had lessons in various martial arts, mostly Kung Fu. Little did I know then how important those lessons would become to my survival. I was no artist. Not then anyway. There was nothing elegant about what I had to do. But it worked. It was the tiniest edge and it allowed me to conquer fear just long enough to stay alive. I caught him under the jaw with a swift kick as he stepped forward, and his head snapped backward sending him onto his back in a heap amongst a bunch of ropes and nets. The other two tried to plug the gap in their little line as I darted past, one catching me by the hair, the other around the waist. I screamed. I don’t know why because they clearly didn’t care and no one else was close enough to hear, if they even cared. On the ground I flailed wildly, squirming for all I was worth. It turned me just enough to spot the shirtless one and while he tried to bang my head into the dock I took a wild swing and popped him right in the bruised ribs. He screamed louder than me and nearly fell over, but most importantly he let go of my hair. It was longer than usual. I tried to keep it cut back then and pass as a boy. It was safer. Now that I was a little freer I grabbed the boy grabbing me and twisted his chin up. I caught a brief glimpse of the same desperation that drove me in his eyes, that edge of survival hunger that defies the merely physical and eats your soul when it runs out of body fat. I pulled back my right hand and slammed it into his face. His nose popped and blood flowed over my hand and his mouth. He gurgled and I slid up and partly out of his failing grip as he yelled to his co-conspirators. The one with the bad ribs lurched back slowly, but I was already half free, the other one’s grip having slid down to my thighs. I sat up and slammed both fists into the sides of his head, still kicking. That did it. I squirmed free and the other boys, just reaching me, weren’t fast enough. I scissored my legs around the feet of the shirtless one that had gotten the closest as he reached for me. He stumbled and I rolled over, pulling him down. He smacked the dock hard, but only the edge as he scrambled briefly before falling into the water below. The stoned one was just too slow and by now his system was overwhelmed. I rolled one way and evaded him then rolled up to my feet the next. The path was clear! So I ran and this time I knew where I was going and didn’t stop until I got there. It was several blocks, but I knew I’d lost them. And even though I was tired, I turned a couple different ways to throw them off, just in case. Clutching the smelly prize to my chest I climbed up the fire escape to the roof of the factory. It was still going at this hour and thus no one ever heard me. It was warm around the chimneys in winter and most street people were just too lazy or stoned or weak or all of the above to climb this far, so it was safe. Well, as safe as I could get, considering. Nonetheless I yanked out the fish and bit off a meaty end bit from the tailfins quickly. You can never really eat too fast out here. I swallowed without even chewing and bit off another. You never know who may be watching or who may come and take it away from you. I got away that night, but I didn’t always. Victory isn’t always glorious. Sometimes it’s just about surviving. Sometimes it just means taking it away from someone else, and not caring how badly they might need it. Sometimes it’s just this ugly and smells this bad, but you have to swallow it to keep going. I thought about home, my father’s house before he was killed. I thought about eating in our tiny kitchen, such as it was. It was cozy at that little table for two where I learned to read, and to work a computer and that people were good and worth saving, which is what he did every day. He was a doctor and little did he know, but his insights were still saving my life now that he was gone. I knew just how deadly opium was and what the drugs of these streets would turn me into because I watched him try to help people escape them every day. And I knew what to eat and to get good, clean water no matter what. I tried to keep my mind on that memory not only because it was pleasant but because I had to keep this meal down, no matter how disgusting it was. As my stomach tried to do back flips, I looked up and into that remarkably clear night. I breathed deeply, willing my body to calm, and stared up at the stars sparkling so bright and clean and so far away. So very far away from there… ~ Now… The stars shone in the sky, droplets of brilliance in an inky sea as Tempest jerked awake and looked around, cringing almost instinctively. It was calm and cool with a light breeze drifting through the open windows, playing with the gauzy maroon drapery of the canopy over the bed. At the foot of the bed, Trouble stirred and looked at her inquisitively. The Border collie rested on a soft bench that Troy had built for him as a compromise, having displaced him from his mistress’s bed. As Tempest’s breathing slowed, Troy sat up next to her. "Hey, what’s the matter?" he said, not as alert as she had suddenly become, but close. "Nightmares?" He pushed his dark brown hair back from his eyes and watched her curiously, even if he was all too familiar with the expression on her face. She shook her head, swiping a hand over her face. "Sorta. Not exactly. More like… memories." She didn’t need to see that look from him, the one that wanted to do something, anything, to make it better. But she was not above simple comfort. She curled back up against Troy and he held her tightly, stroking her hair. "I wish they weren’t the same thing," she said quietly. Troy kissed her forehead and said, "Hey, I know you don’t like to talk about that stuff, but you know you can, right? It’s not some burden you have to carry alone." He smiled slightly, as encouraging as he could be to the woman who, in some ways, he knew so little about. "I think, it’s just…" Tempest paused, her features clouded with uncertainty even in the dim moonlight. "Troy, I want to forget I ever had to live through those times. And in a way, it feels like if I tell you all about them then I’m making you live through them too. And I don’t want that." She snuggled into the crook of his neck and sighed, slowly relaxing again. They toppled back onto the pillows and just as Troy thought Tempest might be drifting back to sleep, she said, "I don’t want to go back to Hong Kong. There’s too much there I never want to see again." "Then don’t," he said gently, accompanying his words with a soft squeeze. "I have to." "You don’t have to do anything, sweetie." "I wish it was that simple. But I’m not giving up Rampage out of fear." "I know." It was never simple. And Troy kissed her cheek and neck, holding her tightly, and wished, as he often did, that it were. ~ "Are you ready for this?" Miyuki asked, smoothing her neat gray skirt. She smiled at Tempest and motioned toward the door of the plane. They were the last ones on board. Something about her passport, the security personnel had said. "Yeah. Ready as I’ll ever be," Tempest said, fidgeting as she stood in her casual, yet carefully chosen little red dress. "How do I look? Vaguely dignified at least?" Miyuki snickered and said, "Yes, surprisingly you do. It’s an odd look for you." Tempest rolled her eyes. "Thanks a bunch. It’s not every day I come back to the country that… well, so much happened here." Her expression seemed far away as she stared at the open door of the plane and Miyuki sensed her trepidation growing again. "Hey, come on now. It’ll be fine," she said. She grasped Tempest’s hand and smiled. "Look, you and I have both faced cultures that didn’t quite understand us. I know they’re going to be thrilled to see you. Tempest…" She moved into Tempest’s field of vision to bring her back to the here and now. "They love you here. I’ve been looking into it ever since the show was announced. Whatever you think is waiting out there to jump up out of your past…" She shook her head and spoke as calmly as possible. "It’s not going to happen. Now come on." Tempest nodded and took a deep breath as she stepped through the door and headed up the long tunnel from the plane to the airport. Miyuki tried to keep her distracted as they walked and said, "Listen the only thing you need to worry about is who you’ll be facing and how to deal with him. You have a good idea of what Lori Equinox is like, but Shaman and Mayhem…" She shook her head, a noticeable wrinkle of distaste marring her pristine Japanese features at the mention of the latter’s name. "Monsters, both of them. Although I think Mayhem may be the worse of the two, considering both his championship and his… reputation." "Reputation isn’t everything." Tempest said. "If I’m as welcome here as you say, then I will have truly beaten my own reputation. Maybe it really is over." "No matter how much it haunts you, the past can’t beat you here. Only one of those three men can," Miyuki said, nodding toward the exit that loomed ever closer. "You have to be prepared, as much as you can anyway." Tempest laughed, for the time on the trip seeming lighthearted. "There’s no preparing for this. Just like there’s no such thing as control. You have to always be prepared, every minute of every day. Because you just never know who or what’s going to happen to you." The doors opened at the end of the tunnel and before Tempest could even step out, she was buffeted by a wave of noise as cheers rose from the crowd gathered at the entranceway. Signs in Chinese and English greeted her, welcoming, encouraging, even one with an adorable cartoon Tempest with bright blue hair flying through the air in a missile dropkick. Her eyes lit up and she grinned and waved at the cheering throng. She leaned toward Miyuki and said, "See what I mean? I couldn’t have possibly prepared for this. But I’ll just have to deal with it." She winked and bounced out toward the waiting fans, ready to sign autographs, shake hands and be adored. She hadn’t imagined this reception in her most daring dreams, and it was far away from the stuff of her nightmares.
