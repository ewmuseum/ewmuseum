Hello, Goodbye

25 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/25/06 The scene opens with a view of Claudia Lox seated in what appears to be a waiting room of some sort. She’s tugging at her collar. She has never felt comfortable in pant suits, but the occasion requires a more formal dressing. She was in the office of a private investigator that Draven had hired for her. She was wanting to know more about her past. Ben had adopted her a age where, for some reason, she couldn’t remember anything. The waiting room is small, but elegantly decorated. She was seating on a plush leather sofa flanked on either end by mahogany end tables. A coffee table in front of her was stacked with several types of magazine. Something for everyone, Claudia thought as she skimmed the pile with an anxious smile. After what seemed like an hour but was really only about ten minutes, a door to her left opened and a short lady with blonde hair peeked her head around the corner. “Mrs. Lox” Mr. Dunham will see you know.” Claudia shot up and was through the door in a matter of seconds. The lady showed her to an office at the end of the hall. The large French doors were open and she could see a middle age man seated behind a desk, viewing some paperwork. Claudia cleared her throat, causing the man to look up. He stood to greet her. “Mrs. Lox, please have a seat. I’ve got some good news and some bad news for you. First, how is your husband” I am a big fan of his, you know.” Claudia took a seat, still uncomfortable in the pant suit. “He’s doing great. Right now he’s preparing for a match with La Sombra. Now, you said you had both good and bad news. Let me have the good news first.” Mr. Dunham shuffled his papers a bit and cleared his own throat. “Well, we located your birth mother, she’s in Ireland.” Claudia raised an eyebrow. “Ireland” Well, what’s her name, where in Ireland?” “Well, see now that’s part of the bad news. She’s deceased, buried in a small cemetery next to a catholic church just outside of Dublin. Her name was Lorelei Ryan. So your birth name was Claudia Olivia Ryan.” Claudia sat silent for a moment. She had hoped that her mother would still be alive, but she knew there would be a chance that she was not. “You said that was only part of the bad news. What is the rest?” “Well, while we were able to locate your mother. We still have not found out who your birth father is. That, I’m afraid, may take some more time and money.” His eyes dropped from Claudia’s. “Mr. Dunham, you know that money is not an issue here. Both Draven and I are willing to pay for your services as long as we need to. Now why do you think that it will be so difficult to find my father” You found my mother fairly quick.” “Yes, well it’s your mother that is the reason we are having problems locating your father. You see, your mother did not die a natural death. She was murdered. It was a hit, ordered and we believe carried out by the IRA. ? Claudia didn’t know what to say. She sensed there was more. “Why do I have the feeling that you aren’t telling me everything?” Again, Mr. Dunham shifted his gaze away from hers. She had piercing blue eyes, that he felt very intimidated by. “We believe that your father was in some way involved with this order to have your mother bumped off. For what reason, we do not know and the good people of Ireland are keeping their mouths shut out of fear. Mrs. Lox, who ever your father is...he is not a man to mess with. We don’t believe your mother is the only person he has had eliminated.” Could her family really be that fucked up? Was Ben right all those times, he told her that he was the best thing that had ever happened to her? Of course he wasn’t. Draven was. But if it had not been for Ben, would she be dead too? The thought sent a shiver down her spine that caused her to visibly shake. “Are you okay, Mrs. Lox” Should I call Mr. Lox for you?” “No, no. I’m fine, Mr. Dunham. Thank you so much for your hard work. Please call me again as soon as you find out more about my father. I do not want you to stop, do you hear me” We will pay you for your services as long as it takes.” Mr. Dunham was already from behind his desk and at her side. He helped her to her feet and walked her to the door. “I promise you, Mrs. Lox, that I will keep this case my top priority until we find your father. And we will find him, you can count on that.” “I hope that you do, Mr. Dunham, because when you do, he is going to have a hell of a lot of explaining to do. A hell of a lot.”
