A tempting offer

20 March 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/20/09 I pant as I continue to run along the sidewalk. It seems like it has been forever since I agreed to help Max with his mad quest to fight the mob, and my life has yet to return to normal. Lately I have been taking out my frustration by jogging; yesterday I ran for so long that I could barely stand when I was done. Its funny, but I am finally forced to fight someone who’s bigger than me, and I don’t like the experience at all. The street rushes past me and I am reminded that a few months ago I would have been having a good time in a bar right about now. Instead I keep watching the dark alleys as I pass them, certain that someone with a gun is going to emerge without warning. I know I am becoming dangerously paranoid, I just don’t know what to do about it. I round the corner and realize that I have reached the house that is serving as a hideout for Max and me. I hesitate when my hand reaches the doorknob knowing that I’ll hate seeing what is inside. But slowly I open the door and peer inside, then cringe at the sight. As a rule I like to live in a fairly clean home. Unfortunately, my unwanted roommates are more comfortable living in what can best be described as the aftermath of a frat party. There are beer cans strewn over the floor alongside the pizza boxes. There is plenty of furniture, but all of it has been dirtied by spilled coke, pizza sauce, and some sort of green fuzz whose origins I won’t even try to guess. I walk in and catch a glance of Max lying on the last clean part of the couch watching some football game on the TV. For some reason I am beginning to feel a deep, smoldering rage at the very sight of him. I am breathing deeply trying to calm myself, telling myself that Max is probably suffering just as much as I am. “Max,” I say quietly, “what has your investigation turned up?” During breakfast Max had mentioned that he was going to go out and try to find out who Greg’s partner had been. We figured that it was easier not to have to fight to enemies at once, and Greg’s scared partner is probably an easier target than the mafia. “Oh,” that he answers lazily and dispassionately, “I never got around to it. I was planning to but there was a House marathon.” I swear I blank out for a few seconds. There is a red haze in front of me, and I can’t think, I can’t comprehend what I am doing. Finally the haze disappears and I have Max pinned against the wall with my hands closed tight around his neck. I let go of him immediately, and he drops to the floor coughing and clutching at his throat. I know I should apologize, help him to his feet but I don’t. Instead I continue to lash out at him. “Look you lazy ass Bastard,” I hiss at him as he pulls himself to his feet, “I guess you can’t quite tell, but we are in a fight to stay alive here. For us to even have a snowball’s chance in hell we have to make sure that only one group of people want us dead at a time. But we can’t do that if you’re not even willing to find the other guy that wants to kill me!” Strangely Max does not respond with his usual assortment of quips. He just keeps looking at’or rather through me and goes back to sitting on the couch. I close my eyes and wonder if Max has finally become as exhausted as I feel, and then realize how truly royally fucked I am if he is. “Look Max,” I continue earnestly, “I understand that you probably feel like its hopeless right now. But I know there is no way in hell that you actually want to die, and that mafia guy blames you for his dau-? Max is starting to laugh. Not chuckle, but laugh so hard and hysterically that tears begin to come out of his eyes. I step back, slightly afraid that Max has actually lost it. But right when I am starting to consider calling an ambulance he brings himself under control. “Let me guess,” he says mockingly, “you heard the story of how I impregnated the daughter and she killed herself cause I wouldn’t marry her.” I nod hesitantly. “It’s a load of bullshit!” he explodes violently. He stands up and moves around in jerky erratic motions as if he’s itching to attack something. “Well yes,” I respond perplexed. “Let me explain something to you. I did not tell the girl I wouldn’t marry her, on the contrary I proposed the second she told me she was pregnant. But her dad decided that I was not worthy enough for his daughter and told her in no uncertain terms that she was going to have an abortion.” His voice is beginning to break and I can tell that he is on the verge of tears. He winces and visibly gathers himself before continuing. “She calls me and tells me what happened. Like the idiot I am I walk straight to his house and tell his guards they better take me to him. He decides to humor me, and I respond by telling him that I am marrying his daughter and if he doesn’t like it he can go suck on his own dick. He has his men beat the shit out of me, I literally only survived because they got tired. They left me for dead, and then Lyla kills herself. She dies three years ago today, and I only realized when I looked at the calendar.” I stand there shocked and speechless. I do not know what to do in this situation, so I tentatively reach out to try and give him some physical comfort. With a snort Max pushes my hand away and walks out the door. I let him go, hoping that some time alone with allow him to clear his head. As for me I need a nice, cold, and utterly alcoholic drink so I am on my way to one of my favorite bars. It’s a nice out of the way place, with a bartender who is more than willing to listen to his customers problems’as long as they order a drink every half hour or so. But tonight the bar is too busy for me to lay out my soul to him, so I just sit in the corner of the room nursing my beer. I must have been here for the last two hours, but I’ve drunk enough that time is slowly becoming nothing more than an abstract concept. “I suppose I should not be surprised that being associated with that hooligan has led you here,” a cultured voice heavily accented with Italian says to me. I look at him with bleary eyes and try to dispel the drunken haze that has begun to cast over my mind. He’s wearing a black suit, sunglasses, and his black hair is slicked back with the help of an enormous amount of hair gel. For some reason I feel like that’s really freaking significant, but I am not quite able to figure out why. “Who the hell are you,” I question as I rest my head on my hands. “I am no one of importance, “he responds. He lays an envelope on the table, and pushes it toward me. I clumsily manage to open it, only to find what seems to be a hotel room key card inside. Confused I look towards the man who is favoring me with a creepy smile. He attaches a surprisingly strong grip to my wrist and leads me out the door and down the street. “Where we gonna go,” I slur as I stumble next to him. Through the big glass doors, into the fancy looking lobby, onto a freshly made and obscenely comfortable bed? I am waking up to the familiar sound of honking cars. I barely remember what happened last night, though I do recall allowing what was probably a member of the mafia to take me to a strange building. Speaking of which, where the hell am I? I look around and realized I am still in the hotel. There is a note on the table and it simply reads, “Mr. Black, we are ready to make a proposition to you. You may stay in this luxurious hotel as long as you like, and we shall guarantee your safety from the people responsible for the fire that destroyed your home. In return all you must do is sever all contact with Max, if you do not we shall cease to treat you with kid gloves.” Every moral bone in my body is screaming that I can’t accept this offer. Max is a friend, and I can not leave him at the mercy of these cold-blooded killers. But every pragmatic bone in my body realizes that Max’s fight is a hopeless one, and that this might very well be my greatest chance to return my life to normalcy. My cell phone is ringing, I look at the screen and see that the caller is Max. I let the phone continue to ring as I stare at the note, then shrug and head into the bathroom. I’ll call Max later in the day, there’s no way that I can abandon him. I think.
