Break Stuff, part 1: Butterfingers

14 March 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/14/10 Break Stuff, part 1: Butterfingers *CRASH* Tempest’s head snapped forward and swiveled to the side as the sickening thud yanked her back into the reality of what was in front of her: a Ford Explorer. It was a remarkably solid and unmoving Ford Explorer; blue, and complete with a towing hitch that was just bumper height for Tempest’s MINI Cooper Convertible. Lady Gaga continued to pound from the speakers as Bad Romance filled the air surrounding the little car on this, the first warm day of the year. And this new scene before her was far less interesting and fun than the brightly colored shop window she had been admiring while still moving. “Just at a crawl, officer, I can’t imagine how I didn’t stop in time.” Why did that never work? She handed over her insurance information and license and apologized profusely to the man driving the truck. No really it was nothing, he insisted, smiling sappily. He didn’t know who she was or so it appeared and that was fine; even better. He would have been happy to take her card or better yet Miyuki’s, but the cop who pulled up in mere moments saw that that would not be sufficient. She never could talk her way out of a ticket, no matter how good she was at talking herself out of everything else. Perhaps it was the way she always looked at the officers, with that little smirk, so cute to so many, but so irritating if you’re an authority figure. It was as though she didn’t take them seriously, doubting their control over the situation. That’s because they don’t have any. Control is an illusion. Ok, so you give a cop the ability to write me a ticket. But did he stop me from running into someone? You give a cop a gun, but when he trains it on someone running away from a crime, what good is he really doing? Oh, he’s gonna catch the guy. Again, does that undo whatever crime he committed? Hardly. No control. None that really matters anyway. And me? Sometimes I wish I could control what happens around me. But I know I can’t. And when I try, sometimes what’s most important slips right through my fingers and comes crashing down, even though I don’t mean for it to. I can barely control myself, so what makes anyone think their chances are better? But it’s fun to watch them try. ~ “You what?!” Frank Valleta’s voice boomed across the vast expanse of the loft that was Tempest’s home, her office, and her gym all in one. She sighed and handed the ticket over to Miyuki Matsudaira, who sat at a conspicuously clean desk that had been moved in right next to Tempest’s. Miyuki sighed, rubbing her temple with perfectly manicured fingers and said, “While it’s reassuring that some things never change, I had hoped this one might.” “What do you mean?” Frank said as he looked back and forth between the two and peeled off his cap, rubbing his forehead. He was still covered in the dust of his day job as an electrician. Miyuki eyed him with a curious disdain and said, “You’ve never ridden with Tempest have you? I mean, not with her behind the wheel?” Frank shrugged and shook his head. “Well, if you had, you’d know what I’m talking about,” she said. “Tempest should be a registered road hazard.” She shot Tempest a look, but Tempest wafted her palms innocently and said, “I’m not that bad! And I’ve gotten better!” “How many tickets is this for you this year?” Miyuki asked doubtfully, pointing the document at Tempest. “Only four,” Tempest said sheepishly. “It’s only March!” Frank said, shaking his head as he paced. “My point exactly,” said Miyuki calmly. “Luckily the very first thing I did when I returned as your manager was secure legal counsel.” “Damn, she really does know you well,” said Frank as he grinned at Tempest. She rolled her eyes and threw up her hands as she flopped down on the nearby couch covered in a riot of multicolored pillows. She folded her arms and pouted up at the both of them as she said, “It’s not funny how I keep wrecking things. It’s not like I mean to.” “Aww, c’mon don’t take it like that,” Frank said, patting her gently on the head like a kitten beneath the paw of a Great Dane. “I’m just glad you’re all right. I told Troy I’d look out for you and let me tell you, that’s more of a task than I ever imagined.” “Thanks,” Tempest said with a little huff. “Well, I will take care of driving you ladies to dinner if you don’t mind me hitting the shower first,” Frank said, with his best conciliatory smile. “Come on, what ’dya say?” “Ok… and yes, shower. You smell,” Tempest said, playfully fanning herself. Frank laughed and Miyuki merely smiled the subdued smile of someone not entirely comfortable with the easy familiarity of those around her. Frank headed off and Tempest turned to her friend. “God, it’s good to have you back at times like this. Darren would have kittens. I’m pretty sure I was driving him nuts,” she said, nodding thoughtfully. Miyuki smiled and said, “You drove me nuts too, I just got used to it. Tell me, have you gotten into any other trouble? Say, with your internet escapades lately?” “No! Well… not much,” Tempest said quietly and twirled her hair, again trying and failing to look innocent. “Ah, then you simply haven’t gotten caught.” Miyuki said, leveling her gaze at Tempest with no sign of amusement. “Much…” Tempest said and bit her lip. Miyuki sighed and said, “Then that also hasn’t changed. Is anyone trying to kill you for any of it at the moment?” Tempest looked thoughtful and said, “I don’t think so. LeStatt seems to have gotten over his generous donation to Haitian earthquake relief efforts.” Miyuki rolled her eyes and tapped her slim silver pen on the pad she was taking notes on. “And how did you manage that?’ “I apologized!” said Tempest, looking scandalized. “I thought he meant for me to erm… well, make a donation on his behalf.” Miyuki narrowed her eyes and said, “This is precisely what makes you a challenge for anyone to manage, and I use the term loosely. Luckily I have always enjoyed a challenge. So LeStatt Knight has allowed you to get by with an apology?” She paused while Tempest made a little shrug with her hands in response. “Well, you’re cute, and you’re new. Maybe all of that together is sufficient. Thank god for those big baby blues. I suspect they’ve saved you from harm more times than I can count." Tempest giggled and said, “Oh come on Miyu, you love me and you know it.” Miyuki smiled, bemused and said, “Of course I do or I wouldn’t have come back. Anything else? I need to be aware of what you’ve been doing to put a bull’s-eye on your back if I’m to have any hope of diverting any more disastrous consequences.” She smoothed her neat burgundy skirt and Tempest chuckled at her fastidiousness. “I must be the only wrestler on the roster whose manager has to take damage control reports, especially after an event as successful as NC-17,” she said. “But at least you’re just as well versed as you always were in all the people and personalities in wrestling. I’m impressed.” Miyuki nodded, a touch of pride coloring her smile as she said, “Of course. I never lost interest in wrestling. I returned to Japan after…” She looked away, but recovered her stride quickly. “I worked for a promotion there and continued to follow U.S. promotions. I only took a short break from them after you left because I got tired of the sniping and complaining about you that inevitably follows when people know you will not be coming back to defend yourself.” Tempest nodded thoughtfully. “Yeah, I ignored all of that, obviously. But it looks like it’s all blown over. More than five years must have been enough time for people to just be excited about me again.” “Very true,” Miyuki said with a soft smile. “The good times may very well be back. But come on, what else have you been up to? I know you are your own worst enemy as far as setting yourself up for a train wreck.” Tempest seemed to have no argument for this and put her finger to her lips, searching her memory. “Hmm, well there’s all the picking on Jay Terror.” The pen that Miyuki had been wielding over the notepad hit the desk with a smack. “Jay Terror? Steven Caldera’s heir apparent seems to you a good person to needle mercilessly just for your amusement?” Tempest shrugged and said, “Yes. Does that not fit with your plans?” The two old friends looked at each other perfectly seriously for a long moment and then burst out laughing all at once. “Yes. Yes, that will be fine then,” said Miyuki as she dutifully jotted it down. “And… what else? That’s hardly enough to keep someone of your mischievous talents busy.” Tempest sighed sadly and said, “Well, you’re right, but I’ve lost some other people I enjoyed. I’m so bummed they fired Christian Zenith. I was starting to like him.” Miyuki peered at her with one eyebrow raised doubtfully. “Was that before or after he socked you with a handicap match? I know your definition of ‘like’ happens to cover a wide range of behavior,” she said. “In what way were you bedeviling him?” Tempest gasped in mock horror and said, “I was doing no such thing! I just hacked a few of his corporate files and such. You know, he’s not all bad like some people make out that he is.” Miyuki leveled a stern gaze at her and said, “Coming from you, that assessment is very much suspect. Christian Zenith, of Zenith Corporation is exactly as ruthless as he is made out to be. Just be glad that he’s gone.” Tempest stuck her tongue out at Miyuki playfully and said, “He’s still generous sometimes. Plenty of rich and powerful dudes never are. And still, the way Caldera just fired him and embarrassed him… that was just wrong.” Tempest shook her head. “I sent him an email, apologizing for some things I said that turned out to be pretty mean considering the way things went down. I didn’t want that just left out there.” Miyuki smiled softly at her and said, “As so often happens, Tempest, your sympathies are misplaced. I can assure you that he would have done the same to anyone else given the power to do so. I admire that you can see the best in people, even when they can’t. But sometimes, it does no one any good.” Tempest looked at the floor and said, “Yeah, didn’t do Cheap Labor any good.” Miyuki watched her friends’ crestfallen expression with concern. “You defeated him fairly. What is this regret?” “He’s gone though,” Tempest said her eyes wide and sad. “I liked him. I didn’t want him to go. I feel so stupid, like I should have seen it coming.” Miyuki waited, watching Tempest’s anguished face and then said gently, “Tempest, if he had a bad knee, you would have zeroed in it. If he had a bad back, you would have stretched it till he tapped out.” She turned one palm over, a small half shrug. “This man left his heart out there on the mat for you break. Do you really blame yourself so much for doing so? What could you have done any different?” Tempest sighed and pulled her arms around herself and said, “I dunno. I just feel like I should have warned him. I should have made sure he understood.” “You can’t make him understand if he really doesn’t want to and… you didn’t give him any real signals that he should believe you loved him back. Did you? You weren’t…?” Miyuki trailed off, her face full of questions born of experience with Tempest’s past problems with exactly this sort of thing. “What? No!” Tempest said, shaking her head vigorously. “No, never even close. I don’t go there with wrestlers anymore.” Miyuki nodded slowly, trying to hide a little smile that snuck across her lips. “What?” Tempest said, tilting her head toward her. “I’ll believe it when I see it,” Miyuki said. “You enjoy the men in this sport far more than most, even if not romantically. But when it is… Well, it’s a natural, if catastrophic fit.” Tempest threw up her hands and huffed at Miyuki, who blinked in surprise. “And how many times should I get my heart broken before I learn not to mix business with pleasure?” she asked. “I have grown up a little bit you know. And Troy’s way more awesome than any wrestler I’ve ever met. He’s sweet, he’s smart, he’s fun…” “He’s a pacifist who thinks what you do is stupid,” Miyuki tossed in, her tone gentle, but at the same time firm. Tempest sighed and said, “Just… sending the wrong message, I think. There’s nothing heroic or glorious in it to him. He tries, for me, but he has a hard time being proud of me.” She chuckled softly and said, “He was proud of me as a seamstress.” Miyuki raised her eyebrows. “A seamstress? Well, I knew you made your own costumes, but I had no idea you could make it into a career. But I don’t see how it could have satisfied for long. You need the adrenaline of the fight.” Tempest nodded. “And that’s what I can’t get across to Troy. It’s a sport for me, but also a life. It’s my high, it’s how I know I’m alive.” She looked at her clenched fist and opened it slowly. “Even when I’m in pain, getting beat on or when I’m the one dishing it out; it hardly matters.” She sighed and said, “I didn’t hate Cheap Labor. I didn’t need to. I can fight with a friend or even a lover and still come out of it happy. I didn’t need to break him. I just couldn’t figure out how not to.” Miyuki smiled sympathetically and said, “Well, you can break this new one…” Miyuki turned to her laptop and tapped lightly at its keys, “…Pazuzu with no such qualms. He’s apparently a complete freak and someone you have no ties to.” Tempest shook her head and said, “But I won’t and you know it. I would never want to take a freak out of action.” She laughed. “All the freaky people make the world go around. You know I’ve always believed that and I’m one of them too. Besides, he makes me laugh.” Miyuki smiled slightly and said, “Don’t they all?” Tempest bit her lip and said, “Right up until I make them cry.”
