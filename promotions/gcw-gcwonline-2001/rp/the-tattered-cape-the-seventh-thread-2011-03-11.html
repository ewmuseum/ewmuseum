The Tattered Cape, The Seventh Thread

11 March 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/11/11 He sat there in front of the machine. If you didn’t have any idea what type of machine it was, then one would probably guess whether or not he who sits in front of it, runs it, or if the machine is the one in control. It was a sewing machine. An old one. One that had been used many times over for many different reasons. One that had been used so much, that it showed welts and wears in places that a machine shouldn’t ever acquire. In fact, machines aren’t built to last this long. It could be deemed the ironman of sewing machines. No other sewing machine has sewed so much, or even existed for as long. Yet, the dreary man couldn’t help but sit in front of the machine and stare. Stare directly into its holes and widgets. Right into its welts, belts and cranks. Right into the worn and torn metal bracers. It made him feel old. But let’s be honest; he was an old man. Still, it’s not comforting sitting in front of a generationally-old sewing machine, with it acting as a reminder of just how ancient you are. The man claspes his hands together in a feeble attempt to gain some self-respect. He bites his lower lip and closes his eyes in reflection. Even after opening his mouth, no sounds escape. “...” His hands start to shake. The shaking slowly develops into trembling. The trembling finally settles. He tilts his head back and gulps the air inward. His head drops between his shoulders. “How are you today?” A voice overlaps the silence. Everything transitions over. The tall sky scraping buildings hide the bustling underworld of rodent-like humans. It’s another busy morning downtown in the large city. Clyde raises his head up and peaks from under his brow. He stares back at the African American man who is behind the counter of a cardboard-like newspaper stand. “I’m doing okay today, how are you?” Clyde replies. It’s rather brittle out as steam rises up from each sewer ventilation grate. Many people hustle by, with briefcases and bags in hand, eagerly on their way to work. “I’m managing. You know how it is,” The newspaper stand keeper answers. Clyde droops his head once more. He snatches a paper on the top of the stack right by his legs. He then propels his arm forward in an attempt to drop some spare change in the man’s hand in order to pay. “Thank you much,” The newspaperman says as he cups his hand and receives the coin. “Go fuck yourself,” Clyde sputters, “You’re lucky I paid.” The storekeeper is left confused as Fox meshes in with the blur of people. Clyde is wrapped in his now usual garb. Black denim jeans accent his silver studded belt and offbeat emo shirt. He looks rather festive sporting a full body black cotton trench coat. So, in essence, he really doesn’t blend in at all. “Excuse me... sorry... move please... jeeze...” Fox mutters to himself as he knocks shoulders with random pedestrians. “AAAARRRRRGGGGGHHHHH!” Back to the man sitting in front of the sewing machine. He screams extraordinarily loud. “Where is it?” He mutters to himself. In a panic, he starts shuffling all sorts of items around. After making the mess around him that much messier, he finally pulls out a bundle of black thread. “I found you!” He exclaims. The man glances across the room at a picture hanging on the wall. It’s an old photo from around the 1940’s. Just turning his head and catching a corner of it pastes a large smile on his face. The gold plated caption under it reads “Commander Skip Vignette.” The photo is of a man dressed in old fashioned pilot gear from the early 40s. His flight goggles are raised up, as if they were fastened to his helmet. He stands stoically in front of a small plane. Wrinkles and coffee stains inhabit the photo if it wasn’t vintage enough for you already. This made the old man smile. It made him remember a time when he felt proud of what he stood for. After all, that was him in the photograph. “I remember those days,” He said to himself as he looked down towards the black threads, “Now, now, Vinny, don’t get all sappy on yourself.” The former Commander couldn’t help but smirk at the fact that he was joking with himself. Clyde continued on his way, through the mass of sidewalk meat crawlers. What he lacked to see ahead was a rather large strip of stores with old cathedral-like apartments stacked on top of them. But what he also really didn’t see was the danger lying ahead. There was a robbery that was just about to commence a few blocks down the street. And Clyde was heading straight for the eye of the storm. Commander Vignette pushed his back against the chair. His laughter subsides after a few moments. He raises the black cloth into the light, in front of the sewing machine. His eyes scan it as if it were made of some sort of magical material. The light filters through the cluttered mess he holds high. His eyes remain engaged with the lush black threads entangled in his grasp. He has some sort of idea with what he wants to do with it, but he’s just not sure how it’s all going to fit together. The sewing machine sits there, waiting patiently for it to be called upon. Clyde paces ever so swiftly towards the heart of the downtown core. Commander Skip lowers the threading to his lap. He then flips the switch of the sewer on. It rattles around for a few moments before a smooth humming noise emanates from it. Just that simple. Who knew that a machine so old and so used could start up with such ease. The Commander smiles at the purring mechanism sitting in front of him. Even still, it amazes him. From a man who seems to be on the edge of despair; he’s certainly doing a lot of smiling. Clyde frowns as he arrives at a crosswalk. “Ugh, come on little walkie man!” Fox shouts thus causing the ladies beside him to shuffle away awkwardly. The light changes and Fox is on his way. “Finally,” Fox remarks. “Finally,” Skip says satisfied. As if it was a spry jaguar setting its sights on its pray, the sewing machine eagerly awaited Commander Vignette’s first orders. Yes, for the first time in known history, a sewing machine has been compared to a jaguar. Go figure. Skip clutches his free hand to his knee as he can’t nearly contain his excitement. Vignette thrusts his foot down on the pedal and pushes some yarn forward. Fox pounds his foot into the ground simultaneously. The sewing machine… Starts sewing. Clyde steps an inch closer to his confrontation, as Commander Skip Vignette creates a tattered masterpiece, one thread at a time. The steel cast sewing shank bobs up and down as Vignette continually feeds threading through the machine. He works feverishly to put the plans inhabiting his mind into reality. “Hey!” A voice screams adjacent of Clyde. Fox turns his head and notices a man in a black ski mask standing in the middle of the street. “But it’s not even cold out,” Clyde whispers to himself. “Hey fuckhead, what are you looking at?” The man challenges Clyde, who just continues walking. Skip can’t take his eyes off what he’s doing as he pushes the pace a little faster. “Hey! Hey you! Fuckhead! Yeah, you! Don’t keep walking away from me! I’m talking to you!” The man in the black ski mask points at Clyde. Clyde stops and looks over his shoulder. By this time, the man in the ski mask has made his way onto the sidewalk. “I’ll get your attention,” The masked man plots. Clyde spins on his heels just in time to see the man snatch a twiggy lady that was just walking by. She flails her purse wildly, but the man flexes his already large muscles. “What the heck are you doing?” Clyde questions. “Don’t play dumb with me man! My buddies and I know who you are!” The masked man hints, “You’re the self proclaimed superhero that goes around the streets solving crimes and mysteries... pretending to be a real live Batman or something.” Clyde looks off to the side. That was the old Clyde Fox. Apparently this crook never got the memo. You know? How Clyde kind of gets aroused by doing the WRONG thing now. “Ummm so yeah, listen there bub,” Clyde darts back, “I don’t quite care what you do to that little lady to be honest. Maybe I’d care a few months ago, but not now; not this Clyde Fox.” WHACK! WHACK! “OW, FUCK!” Skip pulls his finger up to his mouth. He just got pricked by the oldest machine on the planet. Oh, and Clyde got whacked across the back of his head with a lead pipe. Where there’s one crook, there are at least two of his friends and a midget gangster waiting by... ... “W-where... where am I?” Clyde mumbles. ... “My finger is bleeding! Dear lord that smarts!” Skip confides in himself. It didn’t matter, however. Because he was finished. Rather, it was finished. A trim, square, professional looking black silk cape lay right in front of him. It was the most perfect cape ever. It was almost too good to wear. “It’s a masterpiece,” Vignette comments to himself. He gently runs his hands up and down the material. He snatches it and goes to throw it into the wind just as he hears a dreadful tearing sound... “WHAT!?” Commander looks over in pure shock. He didn’t remember to fully remove the cloth from the sewing machine and therefore when he pulled, the cape suffered many stretches and tears along the edges. Vignette falls to his knees, as he can’t believe his eyes. What did he do to deserve this? He almost gave up a finger just to make the perfect cape and now it’s ruined. Now it’s tattered. Now it’s tainted. He shouldn’t have been so naive to think that a black cape could have been created with such pure intentions. Skip blurts out uncontrollable expletives before trashing his apartment. The very apartment that Clyde Fox would wake up in, in now more than four hours. The very apartment that would see the end to the sewing machine. In a frantic rage, Skip rips the machine from its hinges and smashes it to the ground. ... “Where am I?” Clyde murmurs. He wipes his face. It’s wet. He wipes his face again. It’s still wet. He then notices how he’s laying in a bathtub half filled with water. Clyde drags his boney ass out of the tub and can’t help but feel a pounding headache inside of his skull. He looks around and can only hear a few different sounds from a room nearby. Clyde tippy toes over to the bedroom. He takes a peak through the door’s crack and notices two hulking having their way with the woman that was taken in the streets just moments earlier. Clyde shrugs his shoulders and continues into the main living quarters. It’s a complete mess. Threads are everywhere; little itty-bitty machine parts plaster the ground. Clyde can’t help but stop and take a second to plan his escape route. The Black Tattered Cape. “Huh? What’s that?” Clyde talks to himself as something catches his eye. The Black Tattered Cape. Clyde lurches forward and notices a severely torn piece of cloth hanging on the edge of a broken wooden table. Clyde proceeds to unhook the frayed garb and hold it up in front of himself. The Black Tattered Cape. “It’s perfect!” Clyde decides as he tries to keep his volume level down, “This is exactly what I need.” Fox flings the material around to his back. He ties up a pair of loose strings around his neck. The black tattered cape settles around him, as if consuming every single inch. Clyde’s soul darkens as he stares back into the room with the strangers in it. He smiles evilly before walking over to the stovetop. “And this is where we part ways,” Fox concludes as he turns the burners on. The flame starts small but builds itself up reasonably fast. “This cape will signify my decent,” Clyde finalizes, “My decent to darkness.” Clyde viciously yanks the drapes from the kitchen window and places them over the burner. He is quick to make his exit as he can’t help but ponder what will happen to the strangers in the other room. Clyde jumps down one-story and lands feet first on the concrete. He sprints off into the distance, never to be seen in the heroic light every again. And there... Off in the distance... On a rooftop no less than a block away... Stood Commander Skip Vignette. Proud of his creation.
