What You See In the Setting Sun

16 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 16, 2005 Fade in. The people got exactly what they asked for. They got their monster’their cold, heartless beast. Although it seems what they asked for isn’t what they really wanted. There is a red fence, and just beyond that fence a playground. It’s a bright, comforting, sunny afternoon and several children are playing in the background. Toxic leans back in his chair and takes another sip of his drink. Of course it wasn’t. People never know what they want. They want a winner but not someone who will do anything to win. They want an underdog but not an overachiever. They want a monster but they don’t want destruction. A bigger child enters the playground. He is probably the same age as the other children. An early bloomer as the experts would say. Maybe that’s not true. Now that they have their monster they want to see me destroyed in the most painful way possible. And they’ll pay their hard earned dollars every week hoping to be on hand witnesses to the end of my career. They’ll watch every week as another one of their heroes suffers through a pain that is almost inconceivable to them. Secretly, on a level they’ll never be aware of, they’ll enjoy it. Why? Because it gives them yet one more reason to hate me. It’ll make it that much more euphoric once they finally see me fail. A sense that justice has finally been served. Toxic glances at the playground scene again. The big kid has begun pushing the smallest kid around. The typical bully, fulfilling a role that he was destined to fill. Of course, the other children sit their quietly, hoping not to be the next player cast in the role of victim. But after all it is a false sense of justice; like the death penalty for a serial killer. “An eye for an eye” won’t even come close to applying here. Suddenly, the small child lashes out and the bully drops covering his face. The other kids form a circle around the scene. The smaller child, however, hasn’t finished. This obviously isn’t the first incident of this nature he’s faced and his rage is apparent as he climbs on top of the bully and continues his assault. Their masochism is what fuels their most sadistic nature. The smaller child still hasn’t let up his assault on the bully. The kids in the circle have begun cheering and laughing wildly at the scene. Some are even taunting the bully in between chants of “Fight! Fight! Fight!” The chants are in celebration of a long strived-for victory. At the same time, however, they seemed more barbaric and haunting than liberating. It was like some twisted reenactment of “Lord of the Flies.” But it’s sheer Darwinism at its best. Only the strong survive, so the weak must find ways to adapt to match the strong. And you need pure adrenaline for that sort of accomplishment; the kind of adrenaline that can only be fueled through fear, hatred and pack-frenzy. Finally an adult, one of the children’s mothers, comes running into the scene, spewing vile filth that, while not meant for children’s ears, would clearly get the message across. Toxic frowned and took another sip of his drink. A cool breeze whispered through the air and the trees began to sway ominously. Then we have the Steven Caldera’s in the world; people that are so consumed by guilt that they will go to any lengths to see the underdog win or to make a conflict justifiable. From the playground, the mother turned her head towards Toxic and immediately marched towards him. The injured bully followed closely behind her. “Do you see what they did to my son?” She asked. “Yes. I watched the entire spectacle.” Toxic replied smugly. “And at any time did you consider stepping in and breaking up the fight?” “I suppose I considered it.” Toxic answered, leaning back in his chair. “And as a responsible adult you didn’t because?” Toxic pondered this for a moment. “Responsible?” He asked, almost rhetorically. The mother simply glared at him. “But it was so Darwinian. Survival of the fittest.” He continued, but barely acknowledging the woman. “What?” The puzzled woman asked. “Never mind.” “You’re sick a bastard.” She scolded him and then stormed off in the same direction she came. “Wait! I was going to lend you “Lord of the Flies”.” He called after her. The woman turned around and flipped him off. “That crude behavior can only have a negative impact on your child’s development!” He continued to heckle her. “Make sure you send me his cell number when he reaches San Quentin!” She was now out of earshot so Toxic decided to down the rest of his drink. “Bitch.” He muttered. Now what was I just thinking about? Toxic looked around him again. The sun was now setting and shadows began to cover the playground. The breeze had died and the trees now stood motionless. In his right ear Toxic picked up a barely audible conversation. Down the alley again three teens were smoking a joint. And there’s the other way people use to right the wrong, although, that particular kind won’t enhance any performance. This is the most pathetic. A junkie injecting himself with some strange pharmaceutical has no business being in the same ring with me. Yet, in a world where the underdog is king it makes sense in a dillusional way. The teens dropped the joint and walked out of the alley. When someone’s reached that point they are truly desperate. Toxic's brow curled and his eyes glowed. There’s no place in this business for the desperate. Fade out.
