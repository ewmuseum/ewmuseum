After The Fall

25 November 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 25, 2007 __Start What is success? What is failure? There is a fine line between them but where exactly do you draw it? Is success the point of achievement where you feel as if you're unable to be overcome? Or perhaps success is the idea that you've improved. It all depends on where the line is drawn. For instance, failure may be not improving enough. Failure could even be grouped with not being the best. Depending on where the line is and how loose you feel about the words, a person will fall differently in the categories. Where does Jay Terror stand though? Has his rise been enough to make him a winner? Maybe he hasn't climbed enough steps to make it to the point of attainment. Both could be true but the question diverges onto another path when you sit and think of who makes the decision. Is it an outside force that decides whether you've succeeded or not? Is it Jay, himself, that chooses if he's good enough? It's all part of perspective and depending on how you look at things; it makes a world of difference. After The Fall A fine spot If there was one perk that came with falling off ladders and taking steel chair shots to the temple it would be traveling. Every week, professional wrestlers are able to claim homage in a new location where all amenities are taken care of for them. They are kings among common people and if you ask them, they'll tell you it. If they want to feel like royalty and take baths in tubs made of solid gold they are able to. Then again, if they decide that they want to live the everyday life they once did, they are able to walk the streets casually. For Jay Terror, casual was all there was. Sitting in a typical internet cafe, The Outlaw leans inward looking over the GCW homepage on his laptop. Seldom, does he take advantage of his personal computer but in a time where you have no real friends, what's a guy to do? He looks over the articles, the news, and even LeStatt Knight's biography. His eyes widen and his teeth grit together as he stares at the dumb smile the hall of famer has on. Holding back his frustrations though, Terror leans back in his seat. A line of people on both sides of him talk through instant messaging and type up documents for school and work. Jay huffs to himself and shoves back to the desk. On his way in, though, he notices something. Take that back, he notices someone. A girl jumps into his peripheral vision, one with long flowing blonde lochs and piercing blue eyes. The stereotypical hottie fingers her way across her keyboard making motions that Jay mistakes for sexual innuendos. Her shirt, a green tank top with a unicorn on the front end, catches his attention and his eyes go even further to look at the bottom half of her. A pair of hole-infested jeans coat her luscious thighs ending with a set of green flip flops on her feet. He stares for, what seems like, hours until she notices him. Looking up, she caught Jay's head falling and his eyes jumping back to his computer screen. Her eyes remain on him for a second and Jay notices it. As he looks up, they dart down and that's when Jay knows he has her. Like a helpless fish, she's hooked and all he needs is to reel her in. The question at hand, however, is how? Given his luck with girls, he wasn't sure how to make one come home with him easily. Sure, he'd had some great luck with a few cheap whores in past cities but something about her told him a different story. He might have to buy her something or another. Taking a second to think it over, Jay decides that there's no debating it, he wanted her. He closes his laptop and stands from the chair. Oddly enough, the best route to the coffee corner, from where he stood, was around her back. Grinning, he formulated his plan and stepped around the tables until hers was beside him. In the ultimate sacrificial move, he allows his laptop to slip from his grip and crash onto the floor behind her. 'Shit!' He shouts, diving to the ground to save it. The lid flips open and keys pop loose from the board. The bottom end breaks loose and circuits become exposed. According to plan, this was the part where she turns around. 'Oh no.' She did. Looking at the broken pieces of his computer, Jay puts on his best distraught face he can muster. 'It figures, new computer and now it's a goner.' She drops from her own chair and gets to her knees beside The Outlaw. 'Here, let me help you.' 'Thanks.' Beside her, she's even more beautiful than he saw from afar. There is a twinkle on the corners of her lips and a shine in her eyes that make Jay want to melt. Her skin is a flawless tone of bronze and her voice is as soft as an angel's. 'You should have got a case for it.' She offers, looking up at him. 'Eh, I'm a guy. Enough said?' He smirks. She shakes her head playfully and laughs a bit to herself. They gather the pieces and Jay stuffs them into his jean pockets. He picks up the broken laptop and allows it to hang at his side. They both stand and look at one another, 'Well if you get it fixed, ask them about a case. Tell them it's for your girlfriend.' 'You're my girlfriend?' She laughs, 'No, I can't say I am.' Jay shakes his head, 'Oh, right. Sorry about that.' 'That means dates and alone time and all that stuff. Unfortunately, I don't think that picking up keys and computer chips translates to either of those.' 'Right, of course. And a name too, I don't even know what to call you.' Things seem to be easier than he thought. Perhaps it's his look or just the way he carries himself. Either way, he can feel her falling for him and he grins at the power it's giving him. Playing hard to get, 'I know. Odd right.' 'Very.' He turns toward the cafe portion of the room and begins to walk off, 'Thank you again for the help, maybe one day I'll take your suggestion.' She watches him walk away and in that time contemplates her decision. Confusion is in her eyes but sitting there, as well, is a sense of yearning. 'Caroline.' She says. Jay turns around, 'Huh?' 'I'm Caroline.' A warm smile emerges, 'Hi Caroline.' They both stare at one another for a moment dazed at the other's radiance. Jay holds as genuine of a smile as he can while he wonders how long it would take to get her clothes off. Images of her naked body pulse in and out of his mind followed by hopes of mounting her. She breaks the silence, 'You were off?' 'Eh, I was thinking about it.' He paused, 'I was thinking that you were too?' Her nose wrinkled in, 'You wouldn't be asking me out on a date, would you?' 'Only if you'd say yes, then surely. If not, it was only a guess where I was predicting you were leaving too.' He muses. She digs the comedic relief and nods her head, 'Where are we going?' 'Just follow me.' A set of hands from a neighboring table begin a slow clap. The clap catches on and soon everyone in the cafe are applauding for Jay's successful catch. He smiles to Caroline and grabs her hand. Together, they bow to them and walk off with a broken computer. Funny how things work out. __________ There is always that saying where "you can't be successful without failure". Could that be how you describe Jay? Maybe The Outlaw has already found his failure and now he is ready to succeed and make a true name for himself. Then again, what if that's wrong. Perhaps his biggest failure will come at the hands of a neighsayer. LeStatt Knight. Could Jay's aim be far too high to equate to success? That's a question turned over to time. Instead of asking if he will pass or fail though, maybe it would be brighter to find out if he is up for the challenge. Is Mr. Marlboro ready to topple a hall of famer and add him to his list of foes? Of course he is. A Bandit like Jay has never and will never be the guy to walk away from a fight. The odds could be stacked ten to one in the oppositions favor and the bandit will still swing until he can't. It's the lifestyle he chose to live. In sum, don't count out the smaller guy because he has a disability, look at it from the right stand point. Jay's a bad mother fucker. The other guy's a bad mother fucker. By any mathematic equation, that looks just about equal. __________ It had been an hour of dinner followed by a slow walk to her front doorstep. It was a romantic way to end a romantic evening and if his cards had been played right, which he believed they had been, then he would wind up staying for breakfast. With her hand in his and a smile on both of their faces, Jay leads the way onto the welcome mat in front of her door. He releases her fingers and brings his to his pocket. 'You have a good night?' He asks. She nods, 'Surprisingly, yes.' 'Surprisingly?' The smile on her lips could be explained over pages of a novel giving them a personality and certain look with every word, 'Yes, surprisingly.' She pauses, 'I was afraid that you were just looking to get in my pants.' Laughing to himself, Jay steps back. 'Me?' He pretends to look flabbergasted by the accusation but is challenged by the sadness. 'Yeah right. I'm just the guy that dropped his laptop behind you. That's all.' 'You didn't mean to, did you?' She questioned. Jay couldn't understand how she knew everything. It was like he was wearing a sign on his chest displaying his master plan. He looked down to check and was happy and shocked to see that there was no such sign, 'Why would I drop a laptop? That's just weird.' 'You're just weird.' 'You're the one in the unicorn shirt.' Jay points out. They both laugh and a subtle silence creeps up on them. Just like a movie, they look at each other, trying to read the other's thoughts, hoping that they don't have to make the first move. Caroline wants to talk but refrains leaving Jay to speak first, 'Well, I guess I'll be off. I have to get back to the hotel.' 'You have plans in the morning?' She blurts out. 'Not particularly.' He answers. Another pause between the two, 'I don't do this very often, because I mean - I don't want you to think I'm some kind of sleeze and all, but...well; do you want to come in and guzzle a drink or something?' Jay seems a bit shocked by the outburst but loves every second of it. Without the motion, he pumps his fist and nods to her, 'You have any Jack Daniels?' 'I was hoping you weren't a coffee guy.' 'That and tea - I can't stand either.' Caroline laughs and turns to her door. She shoves her key into the socket and twists it around. With a push, the door opens allowing them to walk inside. Jay slams it behind him and follows her down a hallway leading to the kitchen. He glances around the spacious area admiring the dishes piled high on her drying pan. The smell of eggs is still prevalent as he takes a whiff of the air making him feel more at home than ever before. 'Cheesy eggs if you were wondering.' He scans the room some more taking in all it has to offer, 'It did smell a bit eggy. If it wasn't for that Chocolate Meltdown at dinner I'd ask you to whip some up for me.' 'Good thing too. They didn't turn out too well.' Walking through an opening, Caroline steps toward a new room, 'Quit taking notes, I promise there won't be a quiz.' There was something special about her. If you asked him what it was, he'd most likely fumble around and draw blanks explaining the technicalities of it. In a broad sense though, he liked the way she was always smiling and the way that she had witty things to say other than memorized recycled lines. She was cute in a crush kind of way and that was something Jay had lacked since the days before wrestling. All together, she was a girl that he already trusted and wanted to know more about. He followed her into the neighboring room where he could see a bottle of Jack Daniels hanging sideways in the air. The contents spilled out of the spout and into a small glass that Jay assumed was for him. After finishing her pour, she looks up to Jay 'How much do you want?' 'Is that yours?' He asks pointing to her current glass. Caroline raises one of her perfectly crafted brows, 'D'you think I was going to watch you drink?' 'Fill ?em equal.' She fills his glass to the same height of hers and passes him it. He swirls the liquid around a few times before looking up at her. She raises her glass and speaks, 'To your slick palms for without them we wouldn't be sharing such a sweet drink.' 'Cheers to that.' They clink glasses and send the liquid spilling down their throats in a steady stream. Caroline shakes off the sharp taste but plays the role of a champion and jumps back into it as she tips the glass back again. It drains from both of their glass completely and, together, they place their glasses on the table. 'Aren't you something.' Jay says licking his lips. She grins and twitches, 'I can hang with the guys.' 'I'd say.' 'You want another?' 'Dumb question.' She pours another shot and a half in both of their glasses and hands Jay his; they tip them back and kill the contents leaving a tender smile on both of their faces. 'You trying to keep me here or something?' She smiles and stands. The full effects of the alcohol begin to settle in on her showing how powerful a glass and a half can be. 'The funny thing is, you're already stuck here. I'm not letting you drive like this.' 'Like what,' he stands feeling perfectly fine, 'I could drive well enough.' 'So could my unicorn.' 'You're not even making sense.' 'Let's go upstairs.' How can you argue with that? __________ So at equal stance, how does the fight play out? It could sway either way if you look at the match-up but how do you pick a winner out of the two. Who has more in them? Who has more to loose? On Jay's side, he can't go anywhere but up. It's not like he's someone special. His reputation, although formidable, isn't the best around. In those terms, he can easily fall and pick himself back up for another round. For his challenger though, say LeStatt Knight, things are a little different. He's been on top and now, arguably, he still stands on the top waiting for his prey. One loss and his fall is a whole lot greater than Jay's. So with that in mind, who will work harder? The guy with nothing to loose or the guy with everything to loose? Put simply, don't expect the obvious. __________ They didn't have sex, just to clarify; Jay wasn't the type of guy to bang an unconscious girl. Oddly enough, it didn't matter to him. There was something about the drool line hanging off her bottom lip and her crooked smile that made him feel satisfied without a physical climax. The feeling was a weird one and he could openly admit that he never thought it would come over him. There's a first for everything though, right? As he lay there, looking up at her beige colored ceiling, he wondered what to do. She'd been lost since twelve and by now it was three leaving a large gap of time allotted to simply staring and wondering. He was done wondering though and freed his arm from under her back. She rolled her face into the fluff of her pillow and stirred a little, 'Ugh.' She said roughly. 'You alive sweetheart?' Jay asked. She grunted and Jay laughed to himself. He scooted his body out from under the covers and found his pants on the floor beside his feet. Pulling them up by the belt, he noticed a picture on her side table. Two girls, one was her and the other unknown, sat together on a wooden dock smiling as wide as a day is long. Their happy faces match the sky's blues and golden yellow creating a warm feeling. Both were gorgeous girls and made him want more. He could hear Caroline rustling on the bed again and this time he looked over to see her fluttering eyes. Half squinting, wiping the spit off her chin, she mouthed, 'You leaving?' 'I was thinking about it.' 'Why?' Her body stretched to its full extent. 'I have stuff to do.' 'Stuff?' Looking over at the clock, 'It's three.' 'My day starts early.' A pause takes over and Jay almost walks away figuring she fell asleep. She awakes before he can step, though, and opens her mouth again, 'You think you could call me later?' 'I think I could do that.' 'Good.' With that, her eyes rolled into the back of her head and the alcohol seduced her once again. Jay walked out of her room, shirtless, with a smile etched on his lips. He had a feeling that life was done shitting on him and that it was his time to shine. He hoped at least. __End
