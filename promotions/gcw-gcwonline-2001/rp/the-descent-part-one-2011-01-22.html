The Descent (Part One)

22 January 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/22/11 The Descent (Prelude) You felt your grubby, brittle fingernails fracture as they clung onto sanity; a stamp crumbling them to mustardy dust. As you fell into Rasa’s black, bubbling tar pit, there were no hands reaching to pull you out, only to buckle the straps of its prickly straightjacket. This is how you fell. The Bartender‘s Dog Gus’ damp nose nuzzled your ear, before a slobbering tongue slapped down and plastered split-ended strands of copper hair across your cheek. He winced a little as his nostrils were assaulted by the sour tang of stale alcohol on your breath. Your eyes refused to open in denial of the new day. “Ugh, Jimmy, no…” you sighed, words not sounding wholly of the waking world. “Not while my bones hurt.” A piercing whistle perked the German shepherd’s ears, yet failed to penetrate the stodgy, ethanol smog which engulfed you. “Ge’down from there, Gus, ya don’t know where she’s been,” ordered Jimmy. “Let ’er sleep.” The Sister-in-Law Manicured nails pricked at the bed sheets as his fingers brushed Alice Wolfenden’s raven hair aside. She felt his warm, panting breath on her neck. “We don’t get to do this nearly enough,” he softly growled into her ear. “How does a conference every week sound? I‘m sure I could put in a good word with the boss; him being me, and all.” The unknowing cheesiness was part of his charm. The tightly bound ponytails, crisp skirt-suits and starched collars of Alice Wolfenden created a static which was only intensified by the curt demeanour within which she encased herself. Her waspish, prim-and-proper form was a ticking time bomb which could only be diffused away from the unrelenting pressure of being the perfect wife and mother. It was a necessity as much as it was infidelity. Alice slithered free of his groping grip and pushed him down flat. Snaking up his bare torso, she pressed her finger across his lips, melting the seal with a kiss from her own. “No talking,” she said. “Don’t think you mean anything to me outside of a hotel room,” she meant. “Don’t think you‘re the only one, either,” she really meant. The Bartender “Someone needs a fuckin’ job to go to,” Jimmy Franks muttered to himself. “If you’ve pissed on my pool table you’re barred.” The Sister-in-Law Sliding fingers through his gel-crispened hair, Alice guided his head to the other side of her neck, rewarding his obedience with a high-pitched, yelping gasp. Leaving her body at his thrusting mercy, Alice’s mind focussed her eyes on the bedside table’s clock. This was the only time she felt guilty; when someone other than her would pick Erin up from school. The guilt spreading like bacteria within the microscopic crack in her icy shell, Alice saw her daughter standing as classmates flooded by, leaving her alone at the school gates. Erin looked up, her haunting, helpless stare driving Alice’s consciousness back into her rocking, panting body. “She saw us…” trickled from Alice’s lips as her body tensed. “Huh?” grunted the sack of muscle, testosterone and hair product atop her. “Get off me.” The Disappointment The hands ran aimless laps when your existence gave the clock face no context. Your eyelashes, plastered together by mustard-coloured sleep, broke their seal. The noon sun, swollen in the sky with pride having conquered the morning, demanded your worship with a ferocious assault on your unprepared optic nerve. Screwing your eyes up and retreating behind a tufting mess of copper fringe, a numb, clawed hand chased the vague memory of a pillow down the plastic rabbit hole; or the pocket of the pool table upon which you were curled. “Uh?” you grumbled, only your primal functions having booted. “Huh?” A cough churned deep within you, expelling a malodorous cloud of stale liquor which hung in the dusty air above you. “Afternoon, Sleepin’ Beauty,” Jimmy chirped from behind the bar before a swirling cloud of steam from the glass washer engulfed him. “Hair o’ the dog?” “Am I on the fucking pool table?” You peeled your cheek from the static grasp of the blue baize and patted an arid tongue around your ashy mouth for any trace of moisture. As soon as you twitched, your numb hipbone set off a pulsating, dull pain which cascaded down your leg. “Yes,” answered the bartender in a blunt, not-amused tone. “Next time, curl up on there after we’ve closed.” James DeFrancesco - Little Jimmy Franks - had the grandstand view to your descent. He was clearing tables and scrubbing toilets when you used to drop in between foreign jaunts, with stories of conquering mountains in the Pyrenees or dethroning champions in Tokyo. Illegal high school drinkers and grizzled old-timers alike would hang off every word as you sipped at a bottle of spring water between stories. He remembered the vibrancy, the energy which fizzed in the air around you. He was working behind the bar when you began to lose traction on the steep slopes atop the pinnacle of the wrestling business, and your stories took on a bitter taste on the rare occasions you stopped in for a beer. He remembered the holes in the stories, as if you’d lost days on the road through apathy but didn’t want to disappoint your audience. By the time he’d raised enough to buy out the old owner, Jimmy had inherited you as a regular, exhausted of stories and with no early flight to catch the next morning. He remembered when your name wasn’t prefixed by ‘remember when…’. A vibration in your back pocket shuddered along your tender spine. Chin resting on the pool table’s cushion, you fumbled behind to excavate your cell phone. Wincing as your lethargic shoulder muscles were pulled apart like cheese-string, you dropped the phone beside your mouth. “Yeah?” “Kari?“ The Brother The winding minute hand tightened Max Wolfenden’s tie around his neck like a garrotte. Even within the frigid waft of the office’s air conditioning he felt beads of anxious sweat bubble up through the fragrant crust of his liberally applied antiperspirant. His body undergoing an allergic reaction to time itself, every cotton fibre of his shirt’s collar unravelled into itchy barbs which tormented his neck. The rat-a-tat-tat of clammy fingertips on laptop keys played a frenetic melody, spiralling off towards detuned anarchy. “Max,” barked over his hollow-tap backing track. Greg Inman, eyes bulging from his squeezed skull, pointed to the clock in a more dramatic fashion than was strictly necessary. “Fifteen minutes. Fif-teen.” His bloodshot eyes skipped from keys to screen to clock to iPhone, stopping off for a 5 second pit-stop, peering into the boardroom, before starting on another lap. “One of you please call me the heck back,” he grumbled under his breath, tongue far cleaner than that of his sister; disinfected by parenthood and responsibility. “Please?” Keys. Screen. Clock. iPhone. Boardroom. His fingertips became lightning rods for every idea he could pluck from the ether, the MS Word document mirroring his brain in Ariel size 10. “Max,” dampened the flow. “Twelve minutes,” he snapped. “I get it, Greg. Important meeting in twelve minutes. I‘m working on it.” iPhone. Keys. iPhone. Screen. iPhone. Clock. iPhone. Boardroom. iPhone. Gulps of scalding coffee lit up the nerve endings on its route down to his stomach. Heated by a molten, caffeinated core, his face flushed red under its sweaty sheen. His scalp itched. iPhone. Keys. iPhone. Keys. 10 minutes were swallowed up in what seemed like a keystroke. iPhone. “Max, you’re killing me,” wove anxiously between the rhythmic double-barrelled assault of tick-tock, tick-tock. “Two minutes. Then I need you and that genius of yours in that boardroom for as long as it takes to get a breakthrough. This is an all-afternoon-evening-nighter. Tell Alice you won’t be home for dinner, tonight. Hell, I’ve brought my sleeping bag and a bottle of scotch; with no wife and kids I might just get the best night’s sleep I’ve had in months.” “I need one minute,” Max calmly spoke. “I need one minute, I need peace and quiet, and I need you to not be in my face. Greg, go, I’ll be there.” As his printer whirred into life, Max’s body relaxed, like a steak taken off the grill; his blood settled back from the surface and his tense muscles began to unravel. iPhone. Printer. iPhone. Boardroom. Through the boardroom windows, Greg raised a single digit, ticking it round, clockwise. “Please… just this once don’t let me down.” Like an itch on the inside of his skull, he could hear Alice’s nagging, Not her. Anyone but her. Give a vagrant off of the street $25 and point him towards the school, just don’t not her. Slamming his phone against his ear, Max began to do something about his dishevelled appearance, managing to slick and straighten himself out before he got an answer. “Kari,” gushed from his mouth, riding the venting of relief. “I only have about 30 seconds, and I don’t even know if you’re in the city, but I need you to pick up Erin from school. Please? 2:30. I wouldn’t ask unless it was urgent… and it is. Alice is off at a conference, the sitter fell through, none of the neighbours or her friends’ parents are answering, and I‘ve just seen the clients who will determine the rest of my life walk in through the door. Did I say, please? 2:30. I’ll stop talking now. You sound terrible; you‘re not ill, are you? Sorry, stopping now.” The Disappointment “No, no, just, um, just a bit jet-lagged,” you lied through you furred teeth. “Glass of OJ and I’ll be perky. Yeah, of course, no worries, I’d love to. 2:30. Consider it done. Oh, and thanks, Max. Really, thanks. I know Alice is… well, yeah, you know. Thanks. Yeah. Bye. 2:30. Bye. Bye.” The Sister-in-Law‘s Boss “What the fuck?” Jacob Barker cowered as Alice slapped him away from her, nails hacking into his forearms. “You wanna stop just say, okay? Don’t go off on me like I’m a fucking rapist or something. Fuck!” Flinging himself back onto the rumpled sheets, he wilted as he watched Alice engulf her petite frame in a white robe. “This is… I can’t,” she sobbed, snatching her cellphone from the dresser before barricading herself in the bathroom with a imprisoning slam of the door. The Disappointment Your brother’s call had brought you off the bench and back into the game of everyday life; an act which dumped you off in Eastern standard time. The clock’s hand slapped you sober(ish). “Car keys?” you enquired, patting yourself down. “Jimmy, keys? And don’t say Gus ate them again.” “Your keys are locked in the register,” he stated, each word punching the groggy haze from around your head. “Where they’re stayin’ ‘til that breath o’yours won’t melt the windshield.” “Not now,” you warned, slithering off the pool table. “I need my keys.” “Girl, if Count fuckin’ Dracula tried to drink your blood right now he’d be askin’ for soda wit-” “Jimmy, give me my fucking keys… or I take them.” The Brother As his phone went off in his pocket, Max Wolfenden hastily flicked it on to vibrate. His hand went from his pocket to shaking that of the clients entering the boardroom, only the lining of his suit jacket’s pocket knowing it was his wife calling. The Disappointment Gus’ growl sawed at your ears before a fierce bark almost punctured the drum. You stared into his black marble eyes with enough intensity to neuter his protective instincts. As Jimmy’s flailing arms began to sink back down onto the tiled flood, a whimpering Gus shuffled over and licked at his curled palm. “Sorry about this, boy,” you spoke to the loyal German shepherd as Jimmy finally stopped twitching. “You stay with him.” Unfurling your arm from around his neck, you rested Jimmy’s head on the cushion torn from a bar stool. Gus lay at his side, his eyes loaded with judgement encased within obsidian. What are you doing? your sobering conscience asked. You’re not thinking straight. You stared into the mirror and tried to round off your rough edges. Half a lemon was squeezed into your mouth, pursing your lips and almost imploding your facial features. Zesty freshness scrubbed your breath squeaky clean. Tossing Jimmy’s metallic forest of keys in your hand, you tried to overlap your duelling visions to identify most likely suspect for the key to the register, only to meet over his car keys which hung beside the tip jar. The Brother Like a persistent fly buzzing around him, Max Wolfenden’s vibrating phone hummed deep in his pocket, his folded arms muffling it to the others around the table. Conscious of the noise, his voice edged up in volume. “The generally accepted course of action is t-…” he’d begun to pitch before a rat-a-tat-tat at the board room’s glass doors interrupted him. As all eyes turned to her, Polly Newman battled against the weight of the door, and the focus baring down upon it, as she snuck in. “I’m very sorry to interrupt,” Polly half-whispered. “Max, it’s the hospital.”
