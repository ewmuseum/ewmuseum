Date: 9/21 I take a deep sniff of the brandy bottle. It mingles in the warm Hawaiian air like something I'd forgotten about long enough ago that there'd be no point in trying to conjure it back up. Let it slide. Sand crunches under my feet, barely away from the lapping tide. I laugh, remember what I'd forgotten. Wasn't worth it anyway, so it's a shot from the bottle and a few more yards with the seagulls screeching in kamikaze waves overhead. Somewhere, there's a place I'd rather be. I can't name it right now and I'm not sure I want to. The sun sets in an orange-purple orgasm, its game played for another night. This is where I need to be--tracing memories so I can sell my soul for someone else to profit off of. Okay, someone. There's a small house, almost modest, nestled at the edge of the woods. I don't remember buying it, but I own it. Vagrants. Squatters. Trespassers. The sand can't carry me quick enough so I walk with a convict's passion and hang my head low. Stage flair because there's not enough brandy in the world. Behind me, there's fish being torn apart by a spasm of gulls and I'm trying not to find a shitty metaphor in everything I notice. The house. I miss a step, barely, and stumble forwards a few more to catch it again. The house wavers, mixed in the rising heat and the killing drunk. It's a destination, if not the shadow I'm building it up to be, another useless talk for a man in need of more intervention than I can deal out. Okay, a man. Climb the steps, Jordan. Push the door open and look around, let the aroma settle in the bottom of your lungs. Count the signs of life on the trash and the bundled clothes that dot the floor. Wade through the smoke and, hesitating because you like to think you're getting old, call out--once--a name. 'Trey.' Let it echo. Catch, on the breeze, the din of music. Look at all the closed windows and walk upstairs. Follow the ghost of Kurt Cobain and kick what-makes-sense-as-the bedroom door open and, again, call the name. Be almost shocked to find no one around. We can buy a house, we can build a tree. Christ; he's in my head. Rip the stereo from the wall and throw it out the window. Watch it roll off the awning to explode on the rock driveway below. Drink. Light a cigarette. 'Fuck.' Look around again, frustrated enough to drop your glasses. Look down at the tattoos starting to sag and expand. Romanticize this whole aging idea like it's actually happening; definitely use it to motivate yourself to 'save Trey.' Say, there's better shit out there. Say, this will destroy your soul and not call you back. Say, I'll kill you if I have to. Say, whatever works. Laugh at the idea of any of that actually being true--but never ignoring just how warm the gun feels. Again, emphasize the moment. There's no point in dancing with the lithe muses, drink with Hemingway and absolve yourself of sin. No, Trey's not your fault. You weren't the one misleading him at every step along the line. The strong survive and the weak shall perish. Let the meek inherit the earth after I'm gone. It is not their time. 'Jordan.' Your name. My name. I turn around, sharp in my delirium. 'You son of a bitch.' 'Didn't think you'd find me here, man.' 'I know. That's the exact problem I was thinking of when I showed up.' 'Shyeah? Shit must've got borin' in the office. So, how'd you do it?' 'Wasn't the first place I tried. One of Lexicon's old houses, looked like your kind of place.' 'Damn. I that predictable?' 'Always have been.' 'These are things you should let me know about, J. Shit, you probably knew this was comin' a month ago.' 'About the time I heard you were wrestling Knight. Too much shit going on for you to not snap. Simple arithmetic.' 'So, the bums lose?' 'Nothing against a week off. I've taken enough. Just, well, when's the game over?' 'What game?' 'The one where you stick around and just waste my money.' 'Figured that one out too? Damn, man. What's next?' 'How far you want?' 'Two months.' 'You're wearing a cast and laughing your ass off, still.' 'Before that?' 'It's worth waiting for. Entertaining, at least.' 'Damn. Gotta fuck around, don't you?' 'It's how I work. How do you?' 'What?' 'Work.' 'Little as possible.' 'Who told you that one, Johnny?' 'Nah, saw it on one of those office greeting cards.' 'Damn. I was close.' 'How drunk are you?' 'Not drunk enough.' I'm staggering too much, bobbing my head to some otherworldly rhythm. 'Should I just let Seymour slide, too? Knight was obvious, but this one's kind of tricky. I mean, this is one of those make-or-break things you have to deal with. You win and it's just another match where you're a bad-ass and a contender and, well, whatever the fuck. Lose and a lot of people are going to see a changing of the guard, just the final kick to put you out of the midcard and pushing towards obscurity. You had the world title shot and threw it away, and fucked yourself out of the US too. You up to putting that final nail in? Fuck, I am. Ought to be fun.' 'Was your little bitches that did it.' 'Hey, they're as much your little bitches as mine. You just didn't put them in their place when they bucked up.' 'And you don't have to give a fuck, since you got them to pick up when I fade out. Just a money game now.' Snort. Laugh at him for talking about money games. 'I just own some infinitely massive company; I haven't changed. A friend's a friend, and you did make sure I was set for life. I owe you something.' 'A kick in the ass? Wasn't worth your time comin' out here if that's your brilliant plan.' 'No, I save the brilliance for the good plans. The ass kicking's just ancillary.' I say the word wrong. Slur it. 'Whatever it takes to get the job done, you know?' 'What job's that?' 'Making everyone's life a little better.' 'I buy that, yeah.' 'Doesn't matter much. I'm their boss, I give them insurance, health, and a thousand other little helpful things to boost their careers. They owe me. I, on the other hand, owe you one. Paying that back just seems like the right thing to do and, you know, I'm feeling like evening out the universe. Now, to be fair, you did forgive everything that happened before GCW, so I'm not really counting it in my estimations. You realize, I had a personal helicopter ride on a company chopper to get here? That's because of your ass, and now you're trying to go all mopey, antisocial on me, like I don't get it.' 'Get what?' 'The reason you're going homicidal whenever you get the chance.' Flick the cigarette. Stage flair. 'Don't have to turn everything melodramatic just because you've got some questions to your goals in life. You might trick them, but I can smell it on you.' 'Shit ain't that bad.' 'Yeah. Totally. Radical. If you're gonna mope about what you do, then fucking quit and find something new. There's a whole world out there to dick around in, so you can step away from that tainted gash anytime you feel like it. But, if you're gonna wrestle, you should probably start wrestling. Find your prophet, or your muse, or your fetish, or your fucking holy grail and just ride on that. But fuck, do it.' 'Serious about this shit?' 'As serious as you are about blow.' 'I said I quit, man.' 'Yeah, 'said.' And?' 'Fuck it, man. You always end up being right about this shit, y'know? An', fuck, I guess I always played along because of the track record, but I've gotta do something by myself; an' fuck what anyone else thinks.' 'Better than doing nothing just for the fuck of doing nothing. That's my gig, you bastard. I'm not letting you steal that from me right after Lexi got The Marauder. My soul's not getting that raped.' 'No one's tryin' to rip your shit. How important you think you are?' 'Pretty fucking important, just for the wrong reasons. Isn't about me, though.' 'Well, shit, ain't that a first. Look, I appreciate the motivational speeches, but you gotta quit that shit before I lose my fuckin' mind. I'm just havin' a good time, unwindin', and tryin' to get some shit outta my head.' 'That working?' 'Yeah,' he lies. His voice is too sharp, almost cutting me off. 'Everyone needs time off now and then.' 'Nah, not the people that care about it. You can't hide from everything forever. You actually showing up at Worldwide, by the way?' 'Was plannin' on it.' 'That's a good start. Seriously, though--why?' 'S'my job.' 'Should probably be your life, by now.' 'Heh. Funny how shit works out, y'know?' 'No. Do tell.' 'Well, I mean, I've been goin' at this for a few years now, never actually stoppin' to think about what I'm doin', if I really like it. So, I take this break and almost get to the point that I'm diggin' on this idea of wrestlin', 'til your ass comes along and just fucks shit up all over again. You're kinda counterproductive, especially if you're doin' this to try and turn me into your pet beast.' 'Already were my pet. Worked out a lot better than this muck you're wading through now.' 'Yeah, you're right. Didn't like it though, kinda scary lookin' back on it.' 'You were having a good time then.' 'Doesn't mean it was the right thing to do, J. Shit changes, all the time man, an' that's about all I've got to rely on anymore.' 'What's gold worth, anyway?' 'Spendin' a life without ever bein' more than halfway sure of myself, I'd say. There's work, an' there's fun. Let one bankroll the other.' 'Nice philosophy. Working out for you?' 'Just picked it up. Without you around, don't get the luxury of someone else thinkin' for me.' 'What a shame.' 'Damned shame, man. Learn to survive with it, just like anything else though. Hell you been up to?' He pulls a bottle with a sagging black label off the counter, beside of where a stereo stood before some hooligan decided to take matters into his own hands, and collapses with a heavy chug. 'Same shit as usual. Shadowy background actions that'll come back to haunt everyone else in a few weeks.' 'Nice, nice.' 'Better question; what's pride worth?' 'What you mean?' 'How much is your pride worth to you?' 'I can swallow it if I need to. Shit, you gotta do what you gotta do.' 'Fucking Christ. You need someone around to keep your mind right.' 'Means a lot comin' from you, crazy motherfucker.' 'I try. But, shit, not like you haven't heard what people are saying. I mean, that could get to anybody after awhile.' 'Wait, s'people sayin' shit?' 'Oh, damn. Nevermind, didn't realize. Better to be blissfully ignorant of the world than to get stuck in a rut of piss and whine. Whine with an h. Crybaby.' 'You use that reverse psychology shit enough to know that you sorta suck at it, righ?' 'Oh yeah, yeah. Noticed that one a long time ago, still have fun with it. Apt analogy for your career?' 'Maybe.' 'Little down on yourself, eh? Hell, could've figured it. We fucked up a long time ago.' 'How's 'at?' 'Well, we never got nicknames for one another, something to cement the mentor and prot?g? thing.' 'Nicknames?' 'Yeah, for instance, I can be Gilgahash and you're Little Chuck.' 'Li'l Chuck?' 'For instance; I didn't say it was good.' 'Least you're honest. I'll let you think about that one, J.' 'That your smooth way of telling me to get the fuck off my property?' 'You're good, man.' 'Always have been--doesn't matter anymore, though. That being worth a fuck thing? That's all on you now.' 'Wait, what've people been sayin'?'
