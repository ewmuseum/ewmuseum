Sometimes Bad News Just Turns On The Light

13 December 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/13/10 The balcony explained Detroit’s nightlife in its view of dimly lit streets, smoking bar patrons, and the swish sound that cars made in passing. Outside the bar was an alley with a man sitting on the ground. It had rained earlier and he sat on his jacket playing a guitar. The song sounded like a Ben Harper cover. “I’m not mad. Just. I’m confused,” Jay said. “What’s confusing though,” Maggie wondered. “You never told me. Never bothered to call. I just figured. You know.” “You’ve been busy.” “You too apparently,” he said with conviction. Maggie drank her beer. “You’re mad.” “I said I wasn’t. Just worried.” “You think it’s too soon?” Jay nodded and smoked. He followed with a beer. “Maybe.” “It’s been over a year. I need someone. I need something.” “I just thought,” he started. “Thought what?” “Never mind,” he whispered. Jay smoked the cigarette again. His eyes found a car and they followed it to a stop sign. “He makes me happy, Jay. I want to be happy.” “We all want to be happy, Mags.” He was still looking into the distance. “I’ve never met a person who didn’t.” “You have the title. You have Steven’s blessing. You have everything you ever wanted.” Jay’s eyes moved to the guitarist. He took a sip from the bottle of beer. “I’m just worried about you. Worried he’ll pick you up and drop you.” “John’s not like that.” “Just saying.” “I appreciate the support. I really do. I’ll be fine though.” “I know you will. You always are,” he replied, finally giving her attention. “Just don’t cut us all out.” She sighed, “I think you’re doing that on your own.” “Serious?” “You never called me either. I figured you didn’t want help and,” she stopped as he started to cough. Even after another swig from her bottle, Jay continued to cough. “I thought you said it was just a cold.” He smashed his cigarette into an ashtray and propped his legs on the railing. “I don’t know what it is.” “Haven’t checked?” “You said it yourself. I’m busy.” “To call, yeah. Not for health care. Not for that cough. I thought,” He cut her off. “I’ll get it checked in the morning. Promise.” The strings of the alley man’s guitar sang into the wind as snow began to fall harder. Both Maggie and Jay finished their beer. “I’m sorry, Jay.” “Don’t worry about it.” --- I’ve climbed mountains, beaten monsters, and walked with my head held high more times than I can remember. I’ve never come to terms with the things that truly frighten me though. I just fight past them. I break the legs of people like Tessa Windsor, break friendships with people like Jordan White, and cut deep gashes into the foreheads of the self absorbed like Johnny Borealis. For all my life, it’s worked. Personally, I don’t see why it wouldn’t now. --- The doctor’s office waiting room housed four people. There was a woman reading fitness magazine, a man beside her filling out a questionnaire, and a couple holding hands in the corner. The room itself was plain and plastered with informational posters. Most of them dealt with fatal illnesses and the bright side of them. “I think I recognize that man,” said the husband to his wife. “I think I’d remember that hair,” she replied hastily. “From TV. I think he’s a wrestler.” “You watch wrestling?” “No,” he reassured. “I watch commercials unfortunately. His face was on the screen all last month. Some big show.” “I read in the paper that there’s a show this week at the Joe Louis. I guess that makes sense.” “I guess so.” “I wonder why he’s here,” she prompted. Their eyes were focused on him, but his attention was focused more on the woman beside him. She had placed the magazine on her lap. “Maybe he’s sick. I bet that’s not an easy job on the body.” “This isn’t just any doctor’s office though.” “People get sick outside of their profession too. Look at me. I just figured the conditions. The travelling. You know?” The wife placed her hand on her husband’s knee. “Of course. Yeah. You saw him on a commercial?” “Yeah. He was advertised for something important. They fight for belts I think. Like trophies. I think he was fighting for one of those.” “I guess that is good. Good for him.” “The commercial didn’t seem to think so though.” “No?” The husband shook his head and watched the wrestler smile. The woman beside him smiled as well. “No. They showed him off as some monster.” “He doesn’t look like a monster.” “I’m sure he’s not. You know how they stage things with that business. It’s all entertainment.” “I guess some are entertained, yeah.” “Sure. I bet he’s a guy just like me who drew a short straw.” “If he’s sick?” “Yes,” the husband replied. The door across the office opened and a woman walked out. She was wearing a white jacket and a practiced smile. “Mr. James?” The wife stood and she helped her husband, Mr. James, to his feet. Slowly, they walked toward the nurse. Each of them took a last look at the wrestler and the girl. --- Then it hits me. I’m Tessa Windsor, Jordan White, Johnny Borealis. I’m the wounded, the heartbroken, the scarred. There was a lot of emotion then, but I didn’t show it to anyone. Just the mirror, just the hotel bathroom, just the toilet. I couldn’t show it. I had an image to uphold. --- James Bryan sat backstage taping Jay Terror’s wrists. The world title rested on the top of the couch which was an arm’s reach away. “You know, what you did to Tessa Windsor was uncalled for.” Jay stared at the tile floor tracing patterns with his toes. “Jay,” beckoned Bryan. Terror tipped his chin up and flicked his brows as if surprised. “What’s that?” “Tessa Windsor. You shouldn’t have done that to her.” “Done what?” “The chair. You shouldn’t have gotten the chair.” Jay shrugged and James Bryan pulled his wrists close again. “Zenith’s the one who swung it. I was just thinking about it.” “Thinking about it is the same in my book.” His eyes slowly fell back to the ground where his foot was still swinging. The silence allowed him to move without thinking. “Something bothering you?” Jay continued to trace. James Bryan tugged the tape tight and tapped Jay’s wrist. “Jay.” Terror snapped out of his trance again and looked at his friend’s handiwork. “Looks good. Thanks.” “I don’t know what’s been going on with you lately, but it’s Johnny tonight. World champion or not, you’re gonna’ need some kind of focus to compete.” “Yeah, I got it. I’m focused.” “You see the doctor?” “Sure did.” “And?” Jay turned and grabbed the title off the couch. He smiled at himself in the reflection. “Everything’s going to be alright, James. Thanks for the help.” James Bryan felt his queue and stood. “Good luck tonight, Jay.” --- When you have days to yourself, free hours to stop and think of the world like I do, I guess you think of the things you hear and the people you meet. You realize what you’ve done and what kind of impact that you’ve made on the world. While some people may call me accomplished, I think it’s finally sank in that I haven’t done what I’ve set out to do. There are better wrestlers, there are better speakers, and there are better looking people than me. I’m not perfect and I never will be perfect, but I can be the most perfect of my peers. Wounded or not, I have time, however long they tell me it is, and I have drive. It’s time I use them to my advantage and show the world what it means to be the world champion.
