Lost And Found (An AXEL Action Story)

29 October 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/29/10 "AXEL, are you okay?" The lights swirl around high above me, I can just barely hear a voice above the calamity of the live crowd and what sounds like the shitty guitar riff of Scars Of Life. "AXEL, can you move your arms and legs for me?" Sounds easy enough. Turns out actually doing it is a different matter. I feel as if gravity is indefinitely multiplied as I give it a shot. Deep bruises, scrapes, and lacerations shoot pain through the muscles, overtaking the adrenaline and making every inch I move feel like a mile. As my vision goes blurry once again, I can see what appears to be a sigh of relief come across the man's face. So far so good. "AXEL, how many fingers am I holding up?" Trying to focus through the haze was something else entirely. Squinting my hardest, it still looked like trying to guess images through an opaque shower curtain. It was right there in front of me but... "AXEL, stay with me here. How many fingers am I holding up?" Suddenly for just a split second the world comes back into focus. I see the hand clear as day and a sudden rush of warm thick liquid forces me to look to the side and hack it onto the canvas. As a tiny stream of crimson flows out of the side of my lips, I press them together to give him the answer he's been so damn pushy for. "..." "AXEL, can you try that again? I couldn't quite make out what you said." I did as he said, straining my vocal chords to answer over this terrible, terrible music. Deep in my throat I could almost feel the number three vibrating through my box, but all that came out with a fountain of blood. I feel like a Pez dispenser as I turn to the side and hack one more gelatinous gob of plasma next to me, this one even bigger than the last. "We need a stretcher out here, ASAP. This isn't good." I try to express my deep seated hatred for that place to no avail. She had done exactly what she said she would. Not only had she taken my will to fight, my only belongings, and my pride... she'd taken my voice. For just a second I ponder what a better place this world would be like if I would've just murdered that heartless bitch instead of wrestled her. Then I realized the horrifying truth that I may never get the opportunity to speak those words to her face. I tried to scream, but it was at this moment when I truly realized what it was like to have absolutely nothing. ******************************************** As I opened my eyes on this bright and sunny afternoon, the first thought that rolled through my head was the same thought that always rolled through my head. "Fuck my life." Of course, no matter how much I'd wanted to say that out loud that choice had been taken away from me... along with what seemed like my only connections to humanity. Two weeks trapped in this sanitized prison cell full of adjustable beds and non-cable tube televisions, and it feels like solitary confinement. Not one person has stepped into this room to find out how I've been doing. Nothing but the impersonal attempts of caring printed on the hallmark greeting cards and company letterheads. My own flesh and blood hasn't managed to swing more than "Get Well Soon" printed in script inside of a one-dollar card in the shape of a teddy bear in a nurses outfit. The longer my life goes on, the more I realize exactly what it is to have nothing. Every day I seem to lose something I had the day before. In the back of my head one of these days I hope the thing I lose is the will to live. Maybe that would get these thoughts out of my head. Maybe that would erase those cerulean blue eyes that slowly strip me of my humanity... "Mister Action?" The squeaky and unconfident voice of a woman quicky drew my attention away from self-loathing, but a place like this gives you a lot of time to think. It wouldn't be long before I went back. She is new, I can tell by the way she acts, the way she holds herself. Understanding she probably didn't realize I was unable to speak, I made a motion towards the surgery scars around my neck and moved my lips to mouth the words "I can't speak." It had been so long since I tried, I'd almost forgotten how. "Sorry, I didn't..." She slowly moves her foot across the room threshold, holding a chart in her hand. It was the daily menu from the cafeteria, it doesn't take her long to realize I wouldn't be needing that. Her mouth opened but at first the words didn't come out, almost as if she was suffering from the same fate as myself. "There is someone here to see you. Would you like me to let them in?" With my nod the girl quickly turns around and races back down the hallway. Two weeks in this cell and I get a visitor now? Who in the world was it? More importantly... why are they coming at the ass-crack of dawn? Reaching over I attempt to grab a mirror, interested in just how much of a monstrosity I look like today but instead accidentally take hold of a white towel. Well, at one point in time it was a white towel. Now it's soaked in blood and face paint. A daily reminder of who I am and why I'm here. It was the towel I used that night when I got here, I wouldn't let the doctor's take it away. It was important for me to remember... "AXEL?" A familiar feminine voice echoes through the room as I hold the towel in my left fist. "Is that... you?" Shaking my head yes, I answer the only way I could. I'm sure I look quite different than I did before I came here. Long gone is the long hair that once was my signature with my face paint. They cut it off, said it was easier to cut it off than it was to pin it up. They didn't have time for formalities. "I didn't recognize you without the hair... or with a beard." She speaks hesitantly as she slowly steps into the room. Cautious wasn't something I usually associated with Ayake Sonoda, but I'll be the first to admit I'm not the most attractive man in the world right now. Some would say my appearance was even a bit horrifying, but simply being in the presence of someone I knew made me feel like I was human again. "I can't believe she would do this to you..." Her footsteps click on the white tile floor as she approaches. Biting her lower lip, it was obvious that she was shocked at what she'd seen, "...do you mind if I take a look?" Reaching out she touched the bandages around my neck, forcing me to jerk away. The wounds were still too tender, still too personal even weeks later. Guilt quickly spread across her face as she pulled her hand away... "I'm sorry, I didn't mean to..." Waving her off is the only way I have to let her know she was fine. I had so many things to say. So many questions to ask. The first and foremost asking why in the hell they've all abandoned me. "It's just one of those things I have to see to believe." She pulls a chair up next to my bedside, some much needed company. If only she could understand the way this made me feel. "She wasn't always like this, in a way I refuse to believe it's really her. That spark. That honor. That inspiration that followed her everywhere she went is just gone. It's like she's dead inside. Behind those eyes she's not even human anymore." The words cut deep. "She was really my only tie to you guys, she was my only friend. I never talked to anyone else and honestly adjusting to the way things are in this country has been harder than I'd ever admit. It gets lonely." Pausing for a moment she lets out a deep sigh, looking into her eyes I can see the pain. "I came here because I figure you felt like I did. I'm sorry to drop all this on you but..." I put my hand on her shoulder and she looks up at me. I force a smile across my lips, letting her know that even through the pain and misery, simply being in someone's thoughts again makes it worth waking up in the morning. "I had to see for myself. I had to see all this was real." Opening my lips I feel obligated to let her know that someone felt like she did. Straining what is left of my vocal chords, I realize that re-learning how to speak is much like trying to make wadded up piece of paper perfect again. It may be possible but it would never be the same... "Thank you." came out as a choke and not any kind of understandable phrase, but as she looked back at me it was almost like she knew. She could look right into my soul. She understands me.
