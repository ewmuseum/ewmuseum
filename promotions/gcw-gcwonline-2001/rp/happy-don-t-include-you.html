Date: WorldWide 105 “Why do you gotta turn everythin” into a fight?” I snatch up Alex, pressing him onto my hip, shaking my head as Morag scowls across the room at me. Her Scottish temper flaring incandescently beneath her skin and her eyes flicker with anger. Shaking her head, she points an accusatory finger across the room at me. “Don’t you dare make me,” she says, thumbing her breast to indicate herself, “feel guilty. You’re such an asshole sometimes, Craig. For once in your life take responsibility for your actions!” “Responsibility” Dammit, Morag, I AM takin? responsibility for my actions. I’m makin? things right. And right ain’t you and me together. You think the kids need to hear you on the high and mighty?” “You think that rollin” around a ring with men’ll make you happier, Craig? Is that it?” Try my best to stifle my laughter. She doesn’t understand what makes me tick and it’s moments like these I realise that she never HAS known what makes me tick. “Morag,” I sigh with discontent, “you just don’t get it. Do you?” Raising her arms in what appears to be disbelief and a plea for the details, she lets them slap at her thighs which was still to recover from the birth of two children. “What is there to get, Craig” You wanna be a faggot and I still want to be with you.” There’s a silence. An awkward pause in which our daughter Leslie steps forward to fill, she takes my hand and stares at her mother defensively. “Stop bein” mean to my daddy,” she huffs, with her bottom lip cascading down in a mask of her emotions. Kids wear their heart on their sleeves. Everything they feel is written all over their face and they don’t hide their feelings from you. You don’t hear an adult walk up to another adult and say “I’m sad because you shout!” or even express their feelings on their own face. Kids have this ability to be true to themselves and it is something I admire and wish I had the ability to be able to do. It’s something that I myself am striving toward now that the proceedings for freeing myself from this marriage are coming to fruition. Drop to Leslie’s side, with Alex resting on my knee so I kneel on the other. Wrap my free arm around her shoulders and turn her gently so that I might be able to look into her eye to speak with her. Kids appreciate eye contact, it helps them understand a lot about social interaction and it helps them learn to communicate with people. But kids kind of naturally do that already, it’s just drumming in the importance of being able to do it. “Darlin”, it’s okay. Mommy is just a little upset at the moment, that’s all,” I try to explain but Leslie won’t have a bar of it. Her tiny arms cross over her chest in a defensive fashion, her gazes fashions once more on her mother and she shakes her head. “Mommy told me that even if I’m cwoss to be good,” she advises, never breaking eye contact with Morag. Defeat is admitted and Morag kneels before Leslie, all of us on the same level, trying to speak to her in regards to the situation. Taking her hands in hers she tries to smile at the innocence that demands honesty. She nods at her. “You’re right. I’m sorry, honey,” Morag admits. “To daddy. You were mean to daddy. Wasn’t she daddy?” I run my hand over my neck, as if it my relieve the tension of being put into this predicament where I’m to comment on Morag’s veracity and anger. I stammer and stutter, trying to avoid the issue at hand but Morag, funnily enough, comes to my rescue. “I’m sorry, daddy. I’m sorry.” Morag’s gaze is strong and within her eyes, I can see the fire, which was fuelling thanks to my mere presence, has now extinguished. It’s replaced with a sadness deep inside of her and sullen emptiness at our situation. Was it sadness that she was finally understanding that I had to do this for us? Was it just because she was greedy and wanted to try and work it out so she wasn’t excluded from the comfort zone she’d grown to enjoy? Or did she still love me? Leslie reaches out, her body language calling for her mother to cuddle her and she snatches her up. Our daughter kisses her cheek ever so delicately and wraps an arm around her neck, clutching tightly. “Good girl, mommy.” “Good girl?” Morag replies, trying not to smile. “For saying “sowwy” to daddy,” she advises. “That’s a good mummy.” And she kisses her on the cheek again. . . . . . . . . . Light a cigarette as I leave my old home, the place which now houses my ex-wife and two children. Puff out the smoke and sigh heavily as leaving the kids behind always leaves it’s mark on my soul. Leslie and Alex are both at the window on the third floor, inside the curtain. I can hear Morag asking them to come away but neither listen. Alex leaning on it, using it to help him stand. He’s only been standing for a little over a couple of months and he still is unable to do it without some sort of stabilizer. His grubby little paws press hand-prints on the glass and he smiles and burbles as he sees me standing on the path. Leslie cries. She tries to be tough. Everybody always said to me “Craig, that girl is the spitting image of you inside AND out”. And they were right. Trying to swallow her feelings but failing miserably, Leslie watches me as I draw on my cigarette again. She watches the embers glow orange as the day slowly tries to switch over into night. The hues in the distance of orange and purple as the sun tries to set but leaves enough light to not worry about headlights so early into the evening. Her hand is pressed to the cold windowpane but she is not using it to balance her, she is stable enough on her feet at three years old. That tiny hand is trying to reach through the glass and out for her daddy. Longing for her father as he fights to not rush back in there, to not scoop her up and pretend everything is alright, playing “Happy Families” with her mother just for the sakes of being closer to her and her brother. I try to be tough. But I cry with her. The door opens to the apartment building and Morag steps outside. My free hand unmercifully whips to my eye and whips the tears from them. I try to blame the smoke rising from my cigarette, catching my eyes unawares but Morag does not believe me. She’s always been able to tell my lies and she tries to stifle her smile unsuccessfully. “You don’t have to go,” she says. “I miss you, Craig.” I cannot respond. Emotionally, I long to be with my children. I long for things to be the same between me and Morag again but I know in my heart of hearts that things wouldn’t be the same. Nor could they ever. “These kids miss you.” “I miss the kids, too, Morag. Alright” You think this is easy on me? To not be with them every single wakin? hour?” “THEN BE WITH THEM!” she screams, into the slowly darkening evening. The noise startles me and a couple of birds who had come for peaceful roost in a nearby tree. Draw on my cigarette and shrug. “This is why. This is why I can’t do it no more. We can’t communica-? “You never try!” she interrupts, pleading more than telling. My shoulders rise and fall again out of exhaustion. “We never could. As soon as things went outside of the ways we thought they’d go we’d” I dunno? it was like our words ceased to function. Like we fell back into some other us from like years and years ago. Cavemen, screamin? at one another. Like fucken neandethals, Morag. Neanderthals.” This time it was I that raised their arms out in exasperation. “We’re better than that. These kids?” I say, beckoning to the pair in the window, ??they deserve better than that. What’s it teachin? our kids if we stay together despite not being in love with each other anymore, Morag?” She shakes her, trying to avoid the question but more to the fact trying to avoid that I was right. And she could feel that deep inside her heart. “Want Leslie to think that coz she’s married to some big jerk that she’s gotta stay with “em” Want Alex to think that he’s gotta do what a man’s gotta do and ignore his own heart?” Shake my head. “I don’t want that for “em. It just ain’t right, Morag.” I could see the warm air hitting the chill of outside. It felt ironic that the air around us had dropped a few degrees by the chill of our moment. “You know it and I know it,” I tell her. Inside her own mind, she was clutching at straws, just to make me stay and try and talk things through but my soul told me that we were done talking and that no more talking could ever constitute the feelings that were there now. There weren’t any. “Craig, you can stay with us to do this wrestling! The kids would LOVE to see their daddy be a wrestler.” “They still can, Morag. Things are lookin” up for me. I got a big gig comin? up. Debut match on TV. This is a good thing for me. I gotta do some stuff that’s for me, Morag. I can be a better daddy to these kids when I’m happy. Bein? a wrestler makes me happy. GCW makes me happy. I almost forgot what it’s like bein? happy but now I am. Happy doesn’t include you, Morag. It just don’t.” “You don’t gotta leave us to be a wrestler, Craig,” she tries to tell me. Desperately pleading her case. I shake my head at her. “You just don’t get it, Morag.” “But I love you still, Craigy. Ain’t that nothin” to you?” “I know you love me. But you ain’t in love with me no more. And that’s somethin” altogether different. I love you but I don’t have no passion for you no more. I don’t got no feelin’s in that way, y’know?” Slowly, she nods her head. Her realisations are frustrating her as she begins to understand this more and more. “I can’t do this play house bullshit no more. One of us has gotta do what’s right for both of us, Morag, and if you ain’t gonna do it?” I thumb my chest slowly ??it’s gotta be me.” Turn to head toward the sidewalk, to head home. So Morag couldn’t see my tears.
