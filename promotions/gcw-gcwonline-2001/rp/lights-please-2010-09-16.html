Date: 09/16/10 I'm Johnathan Fitzwillis. It's High Stakes 2010 in Las Vegas. And I just threw up. That's called a setting. People vomit for a wide variety of reasons in show business. It's comforting to think of it as a unifying trait; whether musician, actor, athlete, or game show host, everybody pukes more often than you'd think. Models are bulimic, ensuring that they keep their figure. The party crowd has grown so accustomed to overloading their bodies with drugs and booze that regurgitation is accepted as part of it all. Few adjust well to stomach staple surgery, eating more than their body can handle and regretting it soon afterword. Then there are people like David Letterman; the man still leans over a trash can or sink before every show even though he's been doing it his whole life. Nerves. Knots in the stomach that tighten and twist and turn my insides inside out, bile mixing with memories blending with my lunch and rubbing up against some sort of fear of imperfection. Some people in show business get nervous at the drop of a hat, but they learn to hide it from the camera. I haven't been nervous in years. "And I knew what was best for my time... here... Now, it's not how I'd envisioned, When I made my decision. Now, I pull all my strength from my fear." The lyrics echo backstage through the empty entry way fifteen feet to my left. They're glued to watching the entry way over the TV screen rather than glance five feet to their right. Somehow everything just looks ten times better if it's shot through a camera, with the filters and the lights and the background of the crowd hungering for it. I guess it loses some sort of magic if you just see the big deal backstage. Puking in a trash can. "Hey Daryl," Daryl turns to face me again, surprised at seeing that a superstar has replaced the forgettable stagehand that was in my place a moment ago. I guess he's a major throwaway character. You see him more than once in the episode, and the main character even refers to him by name, and even though he won't be around after this he definitely means something. I'm sure he means something. At this point it'd be kind of stupid if he didn't. "Wanna see something cool?" He's confused, but he's the only one looking at me while the others gawk at the screen. Blind by their own choosing to see something that's right in front of their fucking faces. Something that, for the first time in four years, I have no problem saying is fucking awesome. I've got an entire casino, an entire wrestling company, an entire world on TV by the balls, waiting and ready to hear just what it is I have to say. The kicker is that right now they don't even have a clue that it's me. But I can hear the music reaching its crescendo and I know the pyro is about to go off. After the explosions, that's when the real fireworks are going to begin. A force of habit that's been dormant for too long, the Oakley's flick on and the smile is worth a million dollars. I leave poor Daryl to be lost to his back breaking job away from the spotlight, and it's as natural as breathing for me to walk out that entrance way and toward the thousands of fans. The millions if you count the TV. No more supporting roles, no more throwaway parts, and no more regrets. I'm Johnny Borealis. It's High Stakes 2010 in Las Vegas. And I am finally where I belong. This is called owning the spotlight.
