Operations 101: Introduction to Ops (Lilith de Leon, Part II)

18 April 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/18/11 Monday, April 18, 2011 12:39 AM Eastern Daylight Time The Empress Lounge at the Mandarin Oriental Hotel Washington, DC There once was a woman in a hotel bar. The 148th edition of GCW's WorldWide program was no more than ninety minutes gone, and just a few blocks away, the crew was wrapping up the disassembly of the sets. It had been a scene that had seen what may have been the 148th fight between Amy Campbell and Alexandra Pierce, and a dour part of the little redhead in the high-backed stool might have waxed eloquent about the parts of her heart being taken apart, too, but she was in too foul a mood to think of it. Amy let her glass drop onto the gleaming oaken bar top with a sigh and a slump of her shoulders. She hated being by herself, hated being alone. Not for the first time, she looked at the iPhone sitting on the bar. It had gone back to sleep, but a tap of the home button brought it to life, a picture of herself in happier times strumming a ukulele greeting her. Her thumb swiped across the face to unlock it. The text message program was still up -- it'd been there for the last hour -- with a text from Alex as the last message. "I'm sorry," the message said. "I love you." She hadn't responded, though she must have deleted a dozen ill-fated attempts. She waved the bartender over -- he was too young to be serving bar and too Hispanic to hold up his end of the conversation, but he was good about picking up empties -- and he nodded as he plucked up the tall glass, sliding down the bar. His eyes only flicked past Amy to the entrance briefly, but she knew from his expression that someone else had come in. She glanced to the clock on the phone face -- it was awfully late for a nightcap. She knew what Alex would have done. She would have snuck a look, been ready for a fight or with some bullshit lie. Amy would have mocked her for being paranoid; if they were there this late, then other people could come in, right? It would be ridiculous to imagine that they'd be coming after her. Being alone made it seem a lot less ridiculous, though, and Amy ducked her head, trying to get a look out of corner of her eye. She'd been part of Alex's world too long not to think it, and she'd seen the two worlds collide just tonight. Sure, it would just be some lobbyist looking for a quick drink before the bar closed, but with her luck, it could also be a hulking, tattooed Neanderthal with a bucket for a head and bad intentions. Instead, she found... a hulking, tattooed Neanderthal with a bucket for a head, but with good intentions -- or at least she hoped, and the sight of the man brought a grin to her lips. Roderick Ashe was big even for an occupation that favored big men. He stood probably six-foot-six and had to be more than three hundred and fifty pounds, but he'd trimmed down since the last time she saw him. Had it been a year ago? More? He still looked like someone had shrunk down the giant from Jack and the Beanstalk, with the kind of weathered old muscles that an old bodybuilder gets, his skin gone leathery, and he still had that damn military-grade crewcut (though it looked like the soldiers along his hairline were retreating at a rapid rate). A crooked smile clung to thick, chapped lips, and when he saw her and smiled, he still had a yellow snaggle-puss from too many years with too many cigars and too many Jolly Ranchers. "'Ey!" the big man rumbled as he wandered over, raising a paw. "Whatta... whatcha call, coinkydink to find yer scrawny ass in a bar." Ashe might have looked like he was as dumb as the box of rocks he looked to have been built out of, but he was smarter than he looked, and keen eyes took note of the fact that the bartender refilled Amy's glass from the soda fountain, not the tap. "Little bit cliche to be drownin' yer sorrows 'n shit, ain't it?" With a relieved sigh, Campbell smiled at the big man. "Who said I've got sorrows?" She chuckled. "You're a sight for... well, you're actually more of a sore, but we can make it work." "I hear shit. You don' figure these big fuckin' ears are for show, do ya? 'Sides, if y'all was onna same page, you'd be... whatcha call." He lifted the index and middle finger of each hand, interlocking the two V's. "Together now, wouldn't ya?" Amy just shrugged, drinking from the refill. "She's... y'know. On business, doing her thing. And I'm here! Having tons of... well, not fun. Where's your girlfriend these days, anyway?" "Who, Hawkers?" He swiveled his head to look behind him. "Parkin' the car or some shit. This'd usually be when he'd make a dramatic entrance, but I think he got slow inna last year. Old age, y'know." He rubbed the back of his neck. "What, you too good to gimme a hug or nothin'? I showered an' everythin', I promise." When she pushed off the stool, the height difference was quite pronounced. He wasn't just a head and shoulders taller than Amy; he was a head, some shoulders, another head, and some more shoulders. Finally, the little redhead grinned and slung an arm around his back, nodding her head into his chest briefly. "You still smell like... a mixture of beer and smoke and awful. Why's she got you guys sniffing around again, anyway?" He waved a thick hand. "Eh," he said. "Not workin' for her. Haven't seen her since... since before she won that big deal tournament shit. Well. I mean, I seen her, but only onna TV, an'..." Another hand-wave. "You unnerstand." "Nooooot really." She grinned. "I don't speak ape." "'Ey, I resemble that remark." Ashe scratched at his upper arm irritably. "Fuckin'..." He lifted his sleeve, picking at the edge of a nicotine patch. "I hate this shit." "Well, don't pick at it!" "I'm tryin' ta quit," he grumbled, glancing around the bar. "Y'wanna sit or somethin'?" Amy picked up her glass of soda, lifting the end of the straw to her lips. "Yeah, sure. Is something up?" The wooden chair creaked as he lowered his bulk into it. "Naw, nothin' for you to be worried about. So Allie's out or some shit?" "She's, uh, yeah." Campbell nodded, casting her eyes to the counter. "Yeah. She's out... somewhere. I dunno." She shrugged. "One thing. Uhh, if she didn't call you here, then..." "Mebbe it's better if we wait for Hawkers. He can explain this shit better'n me." "Yeah, because that doesn't sound ominous at all." She draped an arm over the back of the chair, looking back to the door. "I'm just saying, if I'm going to hate this, tell me now so I can have them slip some rum into this before the bar closes." Roderick lifted one shoulder. "Gonna leave it to Hawkers. He'll be along in..." The hotel lobby door swung open, an older man with a cane stepping through. Elijah Hawke (Amy was one of only a handful of people to know the man's first name) looked older than he had the last time they'd seen each other, his back curved a little more, the narrow lines of his face having multiplied. His dark hair was still slicked back, but its black had been joined by some gray. He wore a dark, turtleneck t-shirt without a logo and a pair of black pants, a locket no one Amy knew had ever seen inside hung around his neck. Ashe whistled, and the slight man with the cane stepped as briskly as he could manage towards the lounge, his cane still making his gait into the signature step-step-tap rhythm she was used to. He smiled when he saw Amy, a rare thing, thinner than Alex's got and -- as with the rest of him -- a little tired. "Ah, Ms. Campbell," he said, his low voice always terribly polite. He should have had an accent, but instead his words were scrubbed free of almost everything that could be used to identify him. "I believe--" "You owe me ten bucks, man." Roderick extended his hand, curling his fingers. "Fork it over." "You said we'd find her in the bar--" "What's this look like, the petting zoo?" "You said she'd be here with something strong." Amy watched the two bicker -- that was what they did for fun. "Well, I was thinking of having a Cherry Coke. That's pretty strong." "C'mon, man..." Ashe bounced the heel of his hand off the table. "You cheat me all the fuckin' time." Hawke didn't sit, not immediately at least. If Alex was paranoid, he was certifiable. He simply stood there at the table, his hands folded atop his signature hawk's-head cane. "It's been quite a while, Amy." "It has." She smiled. "And yes, drinking nothing more than sugar. How're you, anyway?" "Older." Hawke's laconic, self-deprecating charm prevented him from expounding. "I rather thought that we would never see each other again." He craned his head to look around the bar, his eyes lingering briefly on the kid behind the bar. "I take it that--" Ashe finished for him. "She's out doin' some shit. Aimers don't seem too happy about it." "Mm." Thin lips were pressed together. "I was given the impression that you never let that woman out of your sight." The corner of his lip quivered in what may have been a smile. "Might we trouble you for a few moments of your time?" "Hard to be a puppy dog when she's running around the world with PRIME," Amy shrugged. "And have I given you the impression that I've got anything better to do than give you a few minutes? C'mon, though -- if someone sent you to off me, wouldn't it be easier up in the room?" Another moment of thought had her shudder. "Or you're lying about Alex not sending you and I'm about to get a random strip show that she's sure will put me off men for life..." Again, she shivered. "And if that's the case, just cut my head off here, before Rod loses the pan--oh my God, you actually do have the perfect stripper name. That's disgusting." "Yeh, I did it in my other life, back before I discovered beer an' red meat." The big man grinned toothily. "I'm pretty sure I'd tear you up pretty bad, though. The size difference alone is just--" "The room would be fine, yes." Hawke had a knack for cutting in just when his partner was about to overstep his bounds. "We do not work for Alexandra or for any of her enemies. There is no offing planned. Nor..." He slid a side-glance to the other man. "Will there be stripping." "Please don't take this as any reason to show me, but I gather I've had bigger." She chuckled as she rose, scattering a handful of bills on the bar for her tab. "Let's go. You two had better have some answers, though." "I promise full disclosure," Hawke said. It was almost instinctive as they led her towards the bank of elevators. Amy was too polite to outpace Hawke and his cane, and Ashe kept his eyes on a swivel as he followed. She didn't fear for her safety (she fought people much larger than she was for a living), but she still had to admit it was nice to have someone the size of Roderick Ashe at her back -- and she'd seen what Elijah could do with that cane if pressed. The thin man pushed the up button on the elevators with the pad of his cane, glancing back only briefly. Ashe nodded, turning in place to fold his arm. "I'll take the next one," the big man said. "I really don't think all of this is necessary," Campbell began. "I mean, I know it's what you guys do, but..." "I think you'll find it is." Elijah didn't quite turn as the chime rang, the elevator doors opening. He held the door open with his black walking stick, and she stepped inside. "What's going on?" Amy demanded. "Why are you here? Elijah, if something's going on--" Her fists clenched. "I've had just about enough of people keeping secrets tonight." He didn't answer her as he stepped in. "What floor?" he asked instead. "Elijah--" "What floor, Miss Campbell?" "Goddammit." She bashed the number 8 with two fingers. "Now start talking." "I'm faintly sure you will not want to hear what I have to say, and we are in a rather confined space." "I'm not going to hit you." Amy set her jaw. "Is this where you make sure I'm safely out of the way or some stupid shit? Alex--" He turned then, his eyebrows climbing. "Alexandra Pierce is my oldest, dearest friend. There is no one else on this planet that I would trust more, and no one who has more earned that loyalty. I understand that her life is difficult for both of you, I do. But right at this very moment, I do not care. Our employer has asked that we keep you safe. We were not even supposed to have contact with you, but Roderick insisted that we have this meeting. I promise you I will answer every question that I am able, but full disclosure does not mean right fucking now." It was the most words she'd ever heard him string together in a row. "Please." "She hired you under a fake name, didn't she? There's nobody else who'd give a shit if I..." The elevator bell rang for the eighth floor, and they stepped into the hallway -- Amy before Elijah this time, only because she loathed elevators. Her voice hushed when people passed them. "If I was protected. How worried should I be, exactly?" Hawke hobbled out after her, nodding politely to the late night passersby. "I only know what I have been told," he said. "That's not an answer." "Amy." He softened his voice slightly. "We started our search at the National Mall. It's quite clear from some of the people deconstructing the GCW stage that you and Ms. Pierce had an argument. But please do not take your resentment at what she does out on me." "You don't think we've been through enough that you can drop the formal talk?" She sighed, clasping her right hand over her left in the hopes it would hide any tremors. "And it wasn't an argument worth mentioning, okay? I just wasn't inclu--I was kept in the dark about what she'd decided to do tonight, that's all." He glanced down for a moment. "Habits are hard to break. Harder still when you're an old man like me. And it may not have been an important argument to you, but I really rather doubt that Ms. Pier--that Alex feels the same." She jerked the keycard into the lock, not answering. "I'm not really mad. It's just--" "Just that there's this whole different part of her life that you feel like you're not a part of?" His smile drew new wrinkles across his weathered features. "That sometimes you think you're an anchor weighing her down, that you have nothing in common?" He followed her into the room. "I heard all the same arguments. Lost most of them, just like she has." "You know that it doesn't make me feel any better to hear that she and I have absolutely nothing in common, right?" The handle had been tagged for privacy instead of room service, so when they entered, the bed was unmade -- the blankets still sprawled out from waking that morning (or afternoon, as was usually the case with Amy), pillows bunched at the head, though one remained seemingly untouched, undoubtedly straightened when Alex had left. The only motion to tidy up was made when Amy quickly kicked a bra under the bed (that, undoubtedly hers -- she and Pierce were polar opposites when it came to clutter). "Sorry for the... well, this isn't really messy, but yeah. We both sleep better than we used to, somehow -- she just had press to do, so I was the last out to--" Anyone who knew Campbell was aware of her anxieties ruling her train of thought, and Elijah was no exception. "We really don't have anything in common, do we?" He shook his head gently, suddenly a kindly uncle rather than the oftentimes-cruel man he'd once been. "You very much do, Amy. You love each other. A blind man could see that. It doesn't matter if you enjoy the same kinds of music or movies -- though I rather expect they're not entirely dissimilar. She would move mountains to be with you. One only need look at the number of miles she travels simply to share a locker room with you to know that. I suspect the reverse is also true." He didn't move to sit down, and she wouldn't insult him by asking, even if she never knew just how dependent he really was on the cane. She sat on the edge of the bed, her elbows on her knees and her fingers in her hair. She bit her lip to avoid expressing her doubts, and was surprised when Hawke moved to her side, touching his thin-fingered hand briefly to her shoulder. "I know what you're thinking, Amy. But Alexandra is a kite. She can soar to unimaginable heights, but she often forgets to leave her feet on the ground. She needs someone like you to keep from flying away." "Just... it feels so much like I'm suffocating half the time," Campbell cringed alongside a sigh. "She's got so many plates spinning, I keep my real problems to myself because I'm terrified to tip her balancing act. I don't expect you to understand, but it--" The pounding at the door startled them both, quite frankly. "Cucumber," the gravelly voice on the other side said, and Elijah shook his head, hobbling over to open it. "Cucumber'?" Amy asked, brows lifting. She swiped her hand under her nose. "Izza... a, uh, whatchacall. A password or some shit. So's ya knew it was me." Roderick trundled through the door, a white plastic dangling from one hand. "We never arranged a pass phrase," Elijah said. "Yeh, I just thought'a it right now. Pretty smart, no?" He stopped by the bed, dropping his bag on the comforter. "Y'started onner a'ready, didn't ya? Got 'er all ready t'cry an' shit." He dumped the bag's contents -- an assortment of chocolate bars and a pint of strawberry shortcake ice cream -- on the bed. "Here," he said. "Take whatever. It's comfort food." "I wasn't crying!" Amy almost immediately took to the ice cream, unwrapping a plastic spoon. "And now I'm just suspicious. What the hell kind of argument do you think we had? Bringing me candy and shit, fattening me up..." She chuckled very slightly. "Like you're planning to eat me or something." "Ain't said you was cryin'. Said you was ready ta cry. Totally different. Izza... a modifier or some shit." He shrugged one shoulder. "An' no 'fense or nothin', but you're a li'l scrawny for my dinner." "Uh... thanks?" "Lissen, Aimer, them guys at the show, they said Allie looked pretty sad. I thought... er, maybe I thought..." He glanced at the man with the cane. "Shit, you gotta help me out here, man." Elijah shook his head gently. "We thought perhaps the stress of her being what she was, and doing what she was about to do might have..." "Overflowed the levees," Ashe put in. "...you thought I was breaking up with her?" "Uh, I, uh..." The big man stammered. "The thought had crossed our mind." Hawke lifted one hand from the cane to stay whatever vitriol she might be preparing. "We have been wrong before, admittedly. And we will be again. But something did happen back there at the National Mall, and..." Roderick stripped open a package of Reese's peanut butter cups. "An' we're guys, Aim. You might'a missed that, but we don' do feelin's real good. I hope ya ain't broke up. Y'all are fuckin' awesome together, the perfect storm of hot an' crazy. An', I, uh..." He cleared his throat, looking away briefly. "An' when you're not bein' crazy yelly at each other, you both seem happy." Amy's gaze fell to the wall, lost in thought. She nodded, still. "Yeah. We... I mean, at least on my end, she's the only person who makes me feel that... I dunno. The completeness? It..." She sniffled. "Seriously, you had to get me all fucking emo..." "Forgive me, but does she know that?" Elijah prodded gently. "Alexandra may be the smartest woman I know, but she is also very, very stupid. She has spent years waiting for you, Amy. Never wavering, clinging desperately to the path despite roadblocks that positively beggar the imagination." Hawke smiled, a sad, thin, ghostly expression that made him seem older than he already looked. "I never told Loretta. Not really. She didn't..." He cleared his throat. "Don't make an old fool's mistakes." "How could Alex not know--y'know, not having this conversation right now. Who's sent you?" "Ain't allowed to say," Ashe said dourly. "'Cept to promise it ain't no bad shit or nothin'." As ever, Elijah moved to smooth over the wrinkles. "When we heard about what was happening, we insisted we assist, but... frankly, were we to dwell on our employer, you'd be averse to us offering aid, and we have a promise to fulfill." "If you wanna help me, stop feeding me sugar and figure out where the hell Malcolm Girard is." "Uh..." Ashe glanced over to the thin man, then back to the redhead. "Your dad, you mean?" Amy grunted, wiping her hand on her leg. Her spoon dug into the pint. "Whatever you want to call him." She took a bite. "He has what Alex says is the only thing that can get her out of that world." "Maybe he's got his reasons to keep it." Hawke raised a hand. "That's not important right now. What's important is what Alexandra is about to attempt." "That's the second time you've implied you know what she's doing in town." Campbell shrugged, very carefully not looking at either man. "Which is funny, because I don't know what she's doing." "She's about to get herself in a lotta fuggin' trouble, is what she's doin'." "Then who's driving me down to stop her?" "Wait, that's it?" Roderick wadded up the candy wrapper. "Shit, if we'd known we coulda just said, 'C'mon, we're savin' the goddamn day', I coulda saved like eleven bucks at the damn corner store.'" Amy grinned a little. "Yeah, but then I wouldn't have had any ice cream." "So you still want to save her?" "I love her, dumbass." Ashe chuckled, his grin splitting his lips. "Yeah," he said. "Yeah, you fuckin' do." He reached out, ruffling Campbell's hair. She swatted away his hand. "Do that again and you're pulling back a stump." "Can't help it. Missed ya." The big man started for the door. Amy trailed hesitantly after. "So what kind of trouble is she in? Is it Bronze or--" "We'll tell you on the way," Elijah said, bringing up the rear. "But are you familiar with a man named Randall?" Campbell's shoulders stiffened. "We've met." "Guy's the worst kinda fucknugget," Roderick grunted. "Loud, proud, an' dangerous to know." "And distressingly good at his job," Hawke added. "He's the boogeyman for boogeymen." They seemed to be going slower on the trek back to the elevator, and while Amy knew it was only the sudden stab of concern that made her feel that way, it didn't make her any less crabby. "Uh, doesn't Alex work for him?" "She did." "Well, what happened? She never mentioned--" "You two broke Kathryn Shaw out of his custody." He jabbed the pad of his cane against the down button. Habit led Ashe to check the elevator before they got in. "Now he's pissed an' wants t'stick 'er in a cell fer the rest'a her life." "Alex or Kathryn?" "The one y'like." Amy's fists clenched as she stepped into the cab. "He can suck a dick. Seriously, fucking wannabe Walker, Texas Ranger and all that bullshit of his." She lifted her chin. "So your, uh... your employer found something out? And who is that again?" Hawke frowned. "I told you that's not important." "Yeah." The big man leaned his shoulder against the door. "What's important is she's walking into the laps of like a dozen feds, all armed for bear." Hawke hobbled past him and inside, using the railing to ease his weight off the cane. "It's cool. Hawkers has a plan." He beamed as he leaned off the door. "I'll take the next one." The doors slid closed, but Campbell slipped through just before they were shut. "Me too," she said over her shoulder, charging forward into Roderick. "Who the fuck are you working for?" "I toldja, Aim, I can't--" She had to reach up to put her hand around his throat, but that didn't make her grip much looser. "I know what you said, but I'm done with people not telling me shit and just expecting me to go along. So who is it? Bronze? Malcolm?" "Amy, I can't--" "It's your left knee that's messed up, right? Or the right one?" She looked down slowly, her eyebrows coming up thoughtfully. "Aim..." Ashe didn't fight back. "I... I fuggin' promised." "I. Don't. Care." Amy bit off the words. "I love her. You understand the meaning of those words at all? It means I don't always agree with her and sometimes we fight, but I'm not going to let anyone -- anyfuckingone -- hurt her. Not Bronze, not Malcolm, not Randall... nobody. Not even you two. So just... just tell me, okay?" She tried a smile, but it came out more of a snarl. "I don't want to do what we both know I'm willing to right now because you and I, I like to think we might be friends, yeah?" "Awright!" Ashe lifted his hands. "Awright, it's your dad! We're workin' for yer pop, an' he found out what Randall was tryin'! He let us come help! He ain't such a bad guy, you know. That Golgotha thing's--" The punch was swung with all her weight behind it, and it landed squarely on his jaw. What followed was an overemotional fit of epic proportions, not the precise fighting one would normally get from Amy Campbell. The altercation was full of scratches, flailing, growling and the general sense that a hundred and thirty pound kitten was attacking him. He never once swung back. Even before the knee injury effectively ended his career, Roderick Ashe became a meat shield. He was the one who took the shots from Alex's enemies so that she could live to fight another day, and he was doing it now because no one else would. "Amy!" he shouted. "Amy, quit... Amy!" "How dare you show your fucking faces and pretend to be my friends!" was all she could say when the fight drained from her. "Because we are." It wasn't Ashe who said it, but Hawke. She hadn't heard the thin man approach -- definitely didn't hear the elevator -- so he must have taken the stairs, which seemed impossible. "We are your friends. We're her friends, too. You may not understand what has happened, and I am sorry about that. I'm sorry that we haven't told you everything. There's so much that I wish I'd said. But she needs you. She needs us right now. We might be the only chance she's got." Campbell dropped her head. glancing behind her. "How can I trust you for this?" "We're the only choice you've got. The three of us are the only chance she's got." He stepped forward -- step-step-tap, step-step-tap. "They will throw her in a hole, Amy. Randall will throw her in a hole. And we will never see her again." She had to swallow her pride, but couldn't manage her nerves. "Just... let's go. I don't want to hear about... about Malcolm... we've gotta get her." Elijah only nodded, settling back into his typical posture: two hands on the cane, back ramrod straight. "I'm sorry," he said, and he meant it. "Yeah, I'm sure you are." Her tone was scathing, exhausted. She was stretched to her limit, blowing out a sigh as she glanced down to Ashe. Her assault had left the big man on to his side, and she took his massive paw in both of her own, yanking him to her feet as best she could. "Awright then." Ashe said, scrubbing a hand over his short-cropped hair. "Let's go be bad guys." * * * At about the same time, about an hour away at the InterContinental Harbor Court, Alexandra Pierce delivered a fast, snapping blow to the side of Drusilla Devonshire's jaw, knocking the other woman to the carpet. Only the presence of her seventeen-year-old daughter Quinn and her protege Donnie Bell prevented further violence. "I can help." Devonshire insisted, knuckling her lip. "What makes you think I need your help?" Pierce demanded. "Quinn and I can hand--" "No, you can't." Dru used the rail to get to her feet. "Look, she was impressive downstairs, I'm not going to deny it. But this isn't some front desk clerk you're talking about now, and speaking German won't get you in that room." Quinn's lips twisted. "It's two o'clock in the morning -- what exactly do you expect to be up there?" "That's why I'm here. I'm pretty sure you're walking into a trap." * * * Three floors up, a blond man checked the clip of his government-issued Glock for what might have been the fortieth time in the last hour. "Where are they?" Nicholas Lee demanded. "You said she'd be here twenty minutes ago." The man who turned away from the window could have been thirty-five or he could have been sixty, with a face like a roadmap. He smiled a thin, cold smile, his eyes a shade of blue that someone more poetic would call "the color of the sky right before a thunderstorm strikes." "She'll be here," said the man they knew only as Randall. "Just keep it in your pants for a little bit longer." He took a long, deep breath, inhaling the scent of it all. These were the moments he loved. That he longed for. Right before it all went down.
