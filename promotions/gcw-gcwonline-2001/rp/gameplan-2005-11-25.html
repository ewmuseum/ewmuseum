Gameplan

25 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/25/05 I stepped out of the limo and gazed at the magnificent hotel, shooting towards the stars, in front of me. That lazy, son of a bitch of a driver didn’t open my door! I made a quick mental note to fire the old bastard after he returned me to the airport on Sunday night. So what if the guy had shrapnel in the knee and six grandkids? If you can’t play, get out of the game. Upon further thought, I realized a valet should’ve been there to open the car door. I looked around and realized there was no bellhop stepping forward to grab my luggage. Suddenly, a young man came hurrying out of the entrance with an apologetic look on his face. “I’m sorry, sir. The manager was giving me shit for not?” I cut him off quickly. “I don’t need your excuses. Just take the luggage to my room.” I commanded him. He did as he was told and I headed to the front desk. The blonde receptionist at the desk had her back turned to me. She was happily blabbering about some airheaded issue into the phone, while some poor soul was forced to listen to this garbage on the other end. This is a Sheraton! I thought with exasperation. I noticed the bell on the edge of the counter. Casually, I picked it up and leaned over the counter. Once the bell was in its proper position, right behind the receptionist’s open ear; I gave it a solid flick. *BING!* The startled receptionist glanced at me, before briefly going back to her conversation. “I have to go.” She whispered it loud enough to make sure I heard. The chair spun back towards me and I was greeted with a “Made by Matel” smile. “How may I help you?” She asked, in what was probably the most insincere sincere tone I’d ever heard. “I’ll be checking in.” I replied with mock enthusiasm. She checked me in and gave me my room key. I had only taken two steps, when I heard her resume her previous conversation. The hotel lobby was nice. It was your typical plastic plant and nice painting set up, surely aimed at pleasing yuppies and conservatives alike. The furniture warned that loitering would not be tolerated. The first pleasant surprise of the night was the elevator, which arrived, in a timely fashion. There was nobody inside when I stepped in. Once the doors closed, I was left with some peace to concentrate on my thoughts; or so I thought. The elevator music quickly disturbed that, and I quickly realized I wouldn’t be left alone until I reached my room. I the door key, which was actually a door card, into the slit above the chrome door handle. A red light blinked at me, and the door refused to unlock. I let out a sigh of frustration and slumped against the door. Today had gone from bad to irritating. I fumbled around in my pocket for my cell phone and flipped it open. One 411 call later, I had the number to the front desk. I dialed the number, only to get the blood-boiling busy tone. Apparently Barbie wasn’t done with her meaningless phone call. “What next?” I wondered aloud. Indeed, tonight wasn’t exactly a stellar start to the weekend. GET UP! GET UP YOU SON OF A BITCH! I wondered how long those words would keep me up tonight. What had I done? Clearly it wasn’t to my advantage to draw Lexicon out from behind the mask, yet I felt a small sense of satisfaction for doing so. It wasn’t because I’d felt sorry for the “tortured” persona of the Marauder. I did it. It was all me. I beamed. Yes, it had been me who’d finally driven the Marauder to the desperate limits he’d feared to go. And maybe, just maybe, the thought of going into a brutal Last Man Standing match with an enraged Lexicon would be exactly what I needed to get up for the event. Up until tonight things had been looking way too easy. I finally had a reason to let the humiliation of the nights earlier events dissipate somewhat. Those feelings, however, would return soon enough. For next few hours though, I’d be in the right state of mind to get ready for Sunday. What do you care anyway? You beat him once, how hard do you think it’s going to be to do it again? That wasn’t the point. Never underestimate your opponent. This was my first big match in nearly six months and I’ll be damned if I let Lexicon take all of the glory. You’re the champ, just remember that. Maybe, but this is as personal as it gets. There’s no respect on the line. Belts wouldn’t settle a pure blood feud like this one. No, there was only one thing on the line’pride. I knew at this very same moment, somewhere, Lexicon was thinking the exact same thing. YOU MUST GET THE JOB DONE! Of course. Everyone knew that both Lex and I were going to be laying it all on the line come Sunday. There’d be no excuses for a loss. The better man will come out on top. Perhaps though, I had more riding on this than he did. In everybody’s minds, including my own, I was the favorite. This was MY type of match. Technically, though I was younger, I was the veteran. I had a lot more to lose than Lex did. A man with nothing to lose is the most dangerous kind of opponent. What did he have to lose? If I had any chance of regaining some kind of mental advantage, I’d have to figure that out. The only thought that comforted me was the fact that if he lost, it would mean he couldn’t beat, whether he was the Marauder or Lexicon. I flipped open the phone and dialed the front desk again. “Sheraton Times Square, Jamie speaking.” The Barbie’s cheerful voice answered. “Yeah I’ve got a defective room key. I’m in room 605. Send up a new one.” I answered and hung up the phone. I leaned back and closed my eyes. Leave it all in the ring. I opened my eyes again, looking towards Sunday with renewed confidence. Now I knew what I could use against him? “Desperation.
