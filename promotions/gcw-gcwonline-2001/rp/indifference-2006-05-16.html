Indifference

16 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 16/05/06 Then... McKail’s Apartment. Upper East Side. New York City. Hours after McKail’s wife died, he found himself sat in his worn-out, originally green armchair in complete silence, waiting for the shock to subside, which would inevitably allow the realisation of what had happened to be fully absorbed. The small portion of his brain that was still lucid, still open to the undeniable harshness of reality had warned him in advance of the agony he’ll likely be subjugated to and hoped this transition period would last forever. McKail fondly rubbed the arm of his chair and was immediately thrust backward in time, to the day when he’d brought the damned thing into the apartment. At the time it was very funny, there were arguments and tears spilled, but looking back on that time now, McKail couldn’t help but smile. Compared to now, those problems were nothing more than meaningless and for some bizarre reason amused him more that really should of done. Looking around the room he quickly came to realise that sooner or later everything and anything will ultimate bring back fond (or otherwise) memories of his wife and sooner or later those memories will begin to haunt him and torment him in a way that a human being should never have to experience. A beep emanating from the vague direction of the answer machine caught his attention for the hundredth time that minute and would continue to do so on a regular basis until he actively did something about it. That notion swiftly dissolved in his mind just as quickly as it formed. Both of his parents had died many years ago and Mary’s parents were with him at the time of their daughters death, so the messages on the answering machine were probably from other less intimate members of the family and possibly a close few friends. Their intentions to comfort him in his time of need were thoughtful and much appreciated, but the last thing he needed right now was to talk to anyone about it. He couldn’t think of anything worse right about now. McKail made his decision right then and there. He needed to leave this place, he needed to leave everything and everyone that reminded him of his dead love and he needed to use all of his energy to see to it that the one thing that now meant more to him than anything else in the world was taken care of. His need to fulfil the commitment he made to Mary, just before her essence left this realm of existence, just before she left him forever. Tomorrow he will help arrange Mary’s funeral and he will begin to make preparations to cut his ties to this place and everyone he knows. Nothing else concerned him. Not anymore. Now... Outside of Some Crummy Motel Room. Just Outside Columbia, SC. McKail hit some random button of the cigarette vending machine and waited for the satisfying clump of a full pack of shiny new smokes to fall pleasingly into the open dispenser. McKail reached down and extracted the packet, immediately ripping away the wrapping impatient to obtain a brand new cancer stick. He delicately placed the thing in his mouth and lit it with considerable ease, a monumental achievement considering such windy conditions. It was decidedly warm outside, but the renegade wind provided a pleasing coolness that at least made the near-summer night bearable. The machine was located just outside of the motel reception and so McKail simply leant against the wall to finish his smoke, before heading back for yet another sleepless night in his utterly depressing motel room. McKail winced when his left shoulder ever so gently caressed the brick outer layer of the reception area, another painful, yet completely gratifying, reminder that he was well on the way of accomplishing his ultimate goal. Another opponent, another victory notched up in his unblemished record and another successful defence of his Television Title. It was more than he could possible hope for in such a short space of time, but he had to admit that it was not unexpected. Not competing to get the crowds undying attention allowed him to focus more on actually winning the match and in this day and age of professional wrestling, that’s somewhat of an achievement that McKail never even considered a plausibility. Perhaps this is the new age the wrestling pundits are all championing. Perhaps it isn’t. He couldn’t really care anymore. He was just looking toward his next match, towards his next tile defence against Anthony Munroe. At this point in time, that’s pretty much all he cared about. “You know,” the female voice began. “Those things aren’t good for you.” By the confidence and ease laced within the woman’s voice, Mckail could tell that she was probably a beautiful woman and what’s more, she probably knew it. He didn’t even bother to look up, hell he didn’t even want to respond to her at the moment, instead he kept his attention focused upon his cigarette. “Yet,” he responded, taking another drag of the smoke. “I just can’t seem to care.” “I like your style,” the woman said. Something at the back of McKail’s mind flickered into life and he quickly came to realise that she was flirting with him, perhaps even trying for something more. For some reason, he found himself quite shocked by this prospect, but didn’t show it. Then she added, “Got another one of those?” “Yeah,” McKail told her, but made no attempt to reach for the packet. For the longest time she remained silent, obviously trying to figure him out. She got tired of waiting. “Can I have one?” McKail glared at her for the first time and confirmed his suspicions. She was very beautiful. He then fished into his jacket pocket, extracted the pack of smokes along with the lighter and then threw them to her. She took one of the cigarettes, lit it and then carefully handed the bundle back to him. At that point, McKail hoped that she would leave, but was disappointed when a external puff of smoke from the second cigarette crossed his path. “You know I’ve just split up with my boyfriend,” she told him. McKail shook his head, almost disgusted. She was just getting desperate now. “And I could use some company, if you’re interested.” He looked her in the eyes, he probably owed her that much. “I’m not.” She crossed her arms and batted her eyelids defensively. “Look, I’m offering myself up on a plate to you and you’re not interested?” McKail nodded. “Pretty much.” The woman developed a confused expression. “Why?” McKail threw the butt of his cigarette to the ground and began to walk away. He didn’t much care for the conversation anymore and now welcomed the dreary motel room. “Well, what’s in it for me?” The woman flicked the cigarette in McKail’s general direction and stomped off in the opposite direction. “What the hell is wrong with you?” That McKail didn’t have an answer for, but he didn’t much care. Not anymore.
