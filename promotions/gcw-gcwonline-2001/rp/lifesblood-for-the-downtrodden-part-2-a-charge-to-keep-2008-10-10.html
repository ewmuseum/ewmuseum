I Marvin Murray “Go on then!” a high-pitched, pre-pubescent voice cut through the chaotic playground background noise. “Don’t be a baby!” Marvin Murray’s head was spinning. He’d only been on the roundabout for a matter of seconds but the rate at which his peers had been spinning the ride had reduced his brain to jelly. “JUMP! JUMP! JUMP!” Peer pressure slowly began to grip hold of his better judgement as the chant broke out among his classmates. He didn’t want to leap from this out of control playground ride but his “friends” weren’t giving him much of a choice. Reluctantly he slid his legs down the side of the wooden frame and clamped his eyes shut. “JUMP! JUMP! JUMP!” Marvin allowed himself too much time to think. He clenched tightly to the handrail as the world spun around him at dizzying pace. Opening his eyes, he saw naught but a blur of the school uniform’s royal blue colour, unable to make out the faces of the circle of children gathered round like flies to excrement. “JUMP! JUMP! JUMP!” The chanting was beginning to get to him; Marvin desperately wanted to get off the roundabout before he threw up, but the kids were showing no sign of letting him off easily. So he did it. Eyes closed, he released his vice-like grip on the rail and propelled himself from the ride, using his feet to spring off the frame. His body seemed to hang in the air as he propelled towards the ground. Thud. The children scattered, dashing out of the way so as not to be struck by the flying professional wrestler’s son. Marvin hit the dirt like a sack of potatoes, sending a sizeable cloud of dust and dried mud into the air as his body connected with the ground. Unfortunately he landed chest-first, sending his glasses skittering across the muck. He was quite lucky that they weren’t trampled under the feet of the gathered mob, but the location of his glasses was the last thing on his mind at that moment. “AHHHHHHHH!” he screamed, as he rolled onto his back, flopping a limp, lifeless forearm across his stomach. Terrified, he focused his blurred vision down at the result of his awkward landing. A stunned hush descended upon the group as Marvin’s arm lay draped across his torso at a horrifically unnatural angle. He tried to move the dislocated limb but to no avail. “Kids! Her gravel-rough voice ravaged by decades of tobacco inhalation made it hard for her to sound sympathetic, but she was trying her best.
