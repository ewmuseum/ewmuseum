The Ties That Bind

1 April 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/01/11 Chapter I <><><><><> Sunsets in the Big Apple are different. Subtle appreciation at the end of the day is long gone unless it comes with a bell or profit margin. Stops to admire and bask in the warm sensation of the day’s final light are few and far between. The closest glimpse of the sun’s glowing amber is a reflection that bounces off of the pillars of steel and glass. Danny Francisco knows the only proper way to watch a sunset within city limits is from dangerously high places. He’s learned that through various sources, most of them dangerously high themselves. A few years ago he found out he could slip the elevator man or valet at an upscale highrise a few hundred bucks and get rooftop access. Since getting away from the masses is half the point, it’s well worth it. "What’re we doin’ up here, Sug’?" Lacy says effortlessly as her hair blows in the gusting wind seventy-seven stories above NYC. "It’s kinda… cold." Danny smirks, pulling out his cell phone, and refocuses on scrolling through his emails and such. He’s got a smartphone and he’s technologically savvy. He even Tweets. "Relax gorgeous, we’re waiting," he says assuredly. "Take my jacket." "Fer what?" she shrugs, picking up the brown leather jacket, and slides it over her bare, bronze shoulders. "You’ll see," he turns, smiling. "Say, I wanted to ask you something." "Okay," she shrugs. "Then ask." Danny looks up from his phone and waits for Lacy to figure out why he’s looking at her. Seconds pass by before he shakes his head. "I said I wanted to ask you something," he repeats. "I didn’t say I was going to ask you." "Nobody likes a wiseass," she sends a few solar flares in his direction with a feisty glare. "Are you kiddin’ me?" he spreads his arms out, palms up, and purses his lips together with bravado. "They love me. I’m the only reason GCW even has a female demographic." "Look, Sug," Lacy rests her hand gently on his shoulder. "I ain’t talkin’ about wrestlin’. In fact, I don’t know why you are mixed up in that circus anyway. All you do is put yourself through Hell ‘n high water, and for what? A lousy paycheck? I know plenty of other ways we can make money, more money, and be better off." "Other ways to make money," his brow peaks in the middle. "Less hazardous to my health than what I’m doing now?" Lacy stops to think but finds the answer out of her favor. She shrugs as slight frustration sets in. Danny recognizes the symptoms and quickly tries to quell an outburst; his experience with females and ‘looks of angst’ are that they usually lead to uglier, more verbal, and abusive ‘feelings’. "Relax kitten," he says confidently. "Momma didn’t raise no fool." Danny’s new partner in crime waits to offer him a curious smile. Every wrestler, deep down, wants to have all the power and all the respect of his peers. Danny needs power much the same way, coupled with an unnatural thirst for fame, and soaks in the few seconds of importance he has in Lacy’s world. "So…" Danny stops mid-sentence, eyeballing Lacy for any negative body language. "Are you ready to get to work?" When her chin picks up, Lacy allows her eyes to lock onto Danny’s without saying a word. He decides, from that small gesture, that the offer is genuine. A second thought passes through his mind but exits along with the smoke from his lungs. He feels the bitter taste of his own ego creeping back to life but kills the notion by pulling on the cigarette again. "I found your first job," he changes the tempo of the conversation in his head and in his tone. "A… test drive… if you will?" Lacy squares her shoulders to face the Prodigy, trying not to seem too eager, but says nothing. Her big brown eyes fill with hope, obvious to even the dense receptors in Danny’s heart. He takes another drag before glancing at the waning embers of his cigarette and flicking it off the top of the building. "Anything," she affirms over the howling breeze. "You name it. Whatever it is, I’ll get it done, Danny. I’m good for it. You want me to find Fujita? Teach him a lesson? I can -" His demeanor shifts, uninterested in empty promises, and offers Lacy a look that cuts her off before she can start. Danny knows the routine she was about to give him. It made his stomach twist, causing him to rethink bringing her into the fold, and ultimately causes him to light up another cigarette. It’ll give him time to make a sensible decision. "They’ve got me on a 3 o’clock flight to Baltimore tomorrow," his words are slow, drawn out, but very precise. "Want me to do some special dinner for the Rampage tournament, shoot some photos and shake some hands or something like that. I’m not sure I care enough to show up but I suppose the publicity won’t hurt." Lacy decides this is the part of the job where listening will go a long way. The last time she remembers listening to an employer was when she was 15, running pounds of grass over the Kentucky-Tennessee state lines, before running off with his $4,000 to start her new life. She smiles, pleased with the outcome from that endeavor, and wonders if Danny Francisco would find the same fate. "I think I’ll take the train," he ponders. "…fuckin’ sick of airports. All that bullshit that comes with ‘em...." "I rather enjoy flyin’," she interjects, under the impression her opinion matters. "All the nonsense forehand can be done away with if I’m to have any say but, still, I like it." Danny quickly proves her otherwise through a cold gaze and insincere smile, "Oh yeah?" The comment strikes an odd chord with Lacy. To emphasize her confusion, she lowers her brow and cocks her head to the side. Danny spots the international sign for ‘repeat that please’ and runs a hand through his unkempt hair when he notices frustration turn up his temperature slightly. "You’re gonna have to learn a few things if you want to work with me," he says mysteriously. "I might say this or smoke that but remember, I’m human, just like you. I make mistakes. You make mistakes. We’re just two birds tryin’ to get to the next telephone pole. My telephone pole just happen to come in the form of a girl with Daddy issues, copious amounts of drugs, and unconventional bliss, with a side of hand on hand combat, just to spice things up." His head rolls mechanically toward her, locking into place once he has a clear view, and lowers his shades. His finger pushes the sunglasses to the tip of his nose before offering her an empty stare. In that second she spots Danny looking past her off into the distant skyline. She recognizes the icy blue stare she was drawn to the night they met. That’s also when she notices something else in his eye but has trouble spotting it right away, almost like she was something that wasn’t. Danny feels her demeanor shift and quickly hides anything that might lead her to believe that somewhere inside this marvel of a man is a weakness. "What’s your telephone pole?" he says over the wind. "What keeps your going?" Her answer is to the point and instantaneous, "Love." The word rips Francisco’s eyes from his daydream, pulling his eyes toward hers, and takes any snap comebacks or jokes out of his mouth. The always sure and rock solid superstar is speechless, all words are lost, and he begins to look around. The first thing he spots is the cigarette in his hand. He uses it like a Twix, jamming it into his mouth, and inhales with full suction. His eyes float around aimlessly before settling on the ledge ahead. "You need to go see a friend of mine in Queens," Danny avoids eye contact but turns his head enough to remain clear. "I say friend in the loosest sense of the word. Regardless, I’m leaving for Baltimore tomorrow, so you’ll have to stay back until it comes in." Lacy’s eyes narrow and her jaw clenches. The wind no longer bothers her yet her hair drifts aimlessly in the gusting bursts. Danny’s hair does the same but at slightly less violently. She finally looks at Danny. "What is it?" her question is precise. "Drugs or money?" He shakes his head, hoping he’s not as predictable and corny as that sounds, and finds himself let out a surprising chuckle. "Even better…" his words trail off in the wind. Danny pinches the smoke between his teeth before reaching into his pocket with his free hand. As he leans back, the Southern belle watches his every move, and finally notices his lone eye lock with hers. She’s unsure, as if she forgot to do something, and quickly realizes she has. She forgot to keep an eye on what was in his pocket. Chapter II <><><><><> The first thing to seem off is the idea of a bakery having a back room. Bakers and pastry chefs don’t typically need a space to congregate or conspire but this bakery was the exception. Once she was inside the door, the musty smell of stale dough and burnt bread left as quick as her idea that this might have been a bakery. Red leather sofas with matching armchairs, polished and pristine silver décor, and a looming oak desk likely crafted before World War II. She has yet to learn the details but knows one thing that all the things in this room have in common. "Fancy," she can’t help but say in her Southern drawl. Behind the desk sits a man in a pressed buttondown shirt with a suit vest clinging to his heavy torso and blazing white hair. The strange accent lifts his head off the desk seconds before he offers a broad grin, standing politely with open arms. "So this is the lovely Miss Carmen…" his eyes run from her neckline to her ankles. "Your boss is a lucky, lucky man. What I wouldn’t give for a pretty little lady around here. All I have are… these two." Her eyes follow his before she finally spots two henchmen sitting on the sofa. One of the thugs gives the other an unpleasant look before she turns back to the desk. She smiles, indifferent on getting to know the helping hands, and looks straight at the Hoss from arms’ length of the desk. "Come," he says, slowly taking his seat. "Sit." "They can’t be all that bad," she says, offering the tall one with a cunning smile as she slides into the chair. He waves off any idea of saying something positive about the goons before his head dips below the desk. After fumbling around and making a few boisterous noises, he returns with a grunt, and sets a bottle of bourbon with two glasses on the desk with a thud. He gives her a less than inviting look which must be his generous offer. "Oh," she smiles politely. "No thank you. I was actually hoping we could make the exchange quick. I’m just anxious to get the package in the right people’s hands as soon as possible. In fact, I have a package for you, compliments of my employer." The old man smiles with a nod but says nothing when she pulls a thick, heavy-looking envelope from her handbag. His eyes stick to the oddly shaped gift as she pushes it onto the desk, watching her every move for anything one might call a red flag. She shows no signs of deviance though. He takes that as a chance to have a drink to wet his lips. "Five years ago I was running the books for the Family in Las Vegas," he leans back, kicks his foot over his knee, and takes a deep breath. "I was young, naïve, and looking for a good time. At first I did this because it was easy but then…" She feels the walls closing in. Not a lot, not yet, but they were closing in. The last time she found herself in a fancy room, hearing tales of the old days, and sipping Scotch was never. The main reason for that happens to be because the last place she wants to be is in these rooms, with these people, doing these things. Good thing for her he can’t see the sweat forming on her neck and chest. "Then things just…" he doesn’t wait for her answer. "Got fucked up." The girl he knows as Carmen looks at him with heavy brown eyes. He can tell she’s not all the way with him. Not many people are anymore. That’s what years of being in this line of work did to a man and how it made him seem to the world. He decides to save the young beauty the time and agony of going too far into detail. "What do you mean Mr. Green?" she whispers, more interested than she knows. "What happened?" If you’ve ever seen someone deny themselves of doing the right thing you know the motion the old man makes next. The head kicks back, the eyes narrow, and then the subject will most likely shake their head nervously. "I’m sorry," he whispers. "What were we talking about?" A polite smile breaks the spell cast by his past before she feels compelled to ease the tension. There’s no clear reason why people break down, they just do, and she wasn’t about to let the fact she was an imposter get in her way of being human. Her brow peaks at the middle. "It’s quite alright," her words wash away his excuses. "I guess I missed something though? I’m not sure what happened. You were talking about –" "Would it make me sound bad if I still don’t know?" he laughs at his own shortcomings. "I was in Vegas and got a phone call, asking me to come to head East for some work. My old friend needed some help with a runner, and the next thing I know… he’s dead." "Dead?" her tone is higher and caught off guard. "I thought I was coming here for –" "I don’t know if you know this but…" he pauses, making it clear he has more important things to say, and lets her focus. "I work with some very questionable people. My money is their money, and it comes with a very high... exchange rate. I don’t think I’m going to surprise you when I say these things you see around you come from hard work." The old man reaches for the top drawer of his desk. When his hands return, she spots a manila envelope in one hand, and a shiny silver pistol in the other. Small in caliber, the most damage it will do is at close range. The range she finds herself at right then. She swallows fear briefly before looking him in the eye. "Mister Green," her first idea is to run but stops before she leaves the chair. "What exactly is it that you do?" He remains silent and emits a stare that slices through the dim lighting from the desk lamp, much the same way it cuts her words off. As the conversation soaks in her head for a few more tense seconds she begins to play out how she walks out the door alive. Against that strategy, the old man lifts the pistol and slides the envelope across the desk. "Open it," he says without the initial friendliness. "Find out for yourself." Fingers trembling and heart pounding, the menial task of ripping open the package becomes a struggle for the young girl facing what looks to be an execution. She becomes even more nervous when she notices the thugs leave the couch. The minute the tall one stands over her shoulder the envelope rips and the contents spill into her lap. "I don’t get it…" she says before actually looking at the contents. The words trail off and silence comes over the group. Her eyes scan through papers and newspaper articles and finally come to a stack of photos. Something strikes her, lifting her eyes from the photos to notice the boastful smile Mickey is wearing. She continues investigating the photos. "So Carmen," he takes another sip before locking his eyes on the girl. "Or is it Janette? Or, what does Danny call you? Lacy, right? Does he know about you? Why you’re so… good at what you do?" He studies her reactions over the soothing sound of bourbon pouring into the glass. She offers no direct response but instead, simply drops the photos and papers on the office floor. He slides the half-full glass across the desk, where it lands inches in front of her face so she can smell the spicy aroma that comes from years of aging the fine liquor. "I’m guessing the fine people of Pierce County, Georgia know," he leans forward and leans on the desk. "Don’t they, sweetheart?" She lifts the glass through a clenched jaw and takes a deep, long look into the bourbon at the bottom of the container before spotting the old man’s ugly mug through the glass. She knocks it back with ease and sets it down on the oak with a loud smack. "So what now?" she says, squaring her jaw and fixing her eyes on the greyhair. "Gonna call me in? Send me home?" The seconds pass as he conjures up a way to remedy the situation. The major issue for Lacy is how little she knows about the situation. His eyes lift from the desk and lock onto Lacy before nodding to his henchmen. She doesn’t struggle when a one of them applies a strong grip to the back of her neck. For now, saving her strength and energy is going to be crucial. A sick smile spreads over the old man’s weathered face. "Where can I find Danny?"
