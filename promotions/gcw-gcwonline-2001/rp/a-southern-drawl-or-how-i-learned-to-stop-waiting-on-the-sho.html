A Southern Drawl or How I Learned to Stop Waiting on the Show and Love to Post

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Span of days between WW80 and NC-17. So, yes, the future. Jordan White was tired. Jordan White was on his way to Texas. Jordan White's defining feature was orbiting around a ball of hydrogen and helium as much as it was orbiting him. The dip of his globe. The sway of the star. His beer was warm. There was dirt between his teeth. His knees were stretched straight, the left leg of his pants tight to thick bandages. He was the only person in the room not laughing at reruns of a comedian on the motel TV. He was the only person in the room. He was free; that is, able to choose his obligations. The map had an inked line on it that canvassed space, yes. But time? The only thing it indicated on that scale was forwards’and even then, only because reading it as a backwards trek would just waste more of the inevitable march. He joked to himself about sober days and didn't answer his phone for anything. He was far below active thought. He was three and a half feet above a carpet of empty soldiers. Crushed and bent aluminum cans paved a path to the out door and to the bathroom door. The TV flickered. It was old and gave a second of blank screen between every channel. Those seconds added up as the days did the same. He built a wall of time. Behind it, he grew to wonder why’if it was all business’he, as Frank, had to pay for the sins of Jordan. Knots. Bandages. Shiners. Public humiliation. Did actors have to deal with that? Had Stallone limped away from his Rocky?, Brando been slaughtered for Kurtz? No. That was just a cow. He drew the comparison. It drew him out of bed with a gimp swagger to his suitcase for clothes that hadn't been sweated down by the Georgia swelter. It wasn't true heat. It had just caught him offguard, overdressed. He made himself presentable to the gravel parking lot out front, to the white Chevy truck that Sage had told him to fit in with’if he was so concerned with driving the whole goddamn way. Not a vacation, a break. The truck shuddered to a roaring start, tried to shake off the dust that carpeted like apocalypses. He pulled a blunt roach from the ashtray and lit it before letting his window down. The yellowing plume rushed out and he watched the road through streaked glass. The oncoming lane was empty. The sun was low to his side, blinded him against any traffic that may have materialized. He made it to a corner store without wrecking, regardless. It was in the gut of a shopping centre flung one story low; only a corner store out of some force of habit he'd picked up in his earlier formative years. There was still time to change. He nodded to the older woman, with bleached hair and lighter roots, behind the counter. He didn't keep track of how many nods he had given her; several a day since he'd fallen into town was enough remembrance. All that could be expected, really. He sat a case of Yuengling on the counter and laid a pack of cigars atop it. He ripped the box open and rolled the cans into the miniature fridge when the box wouldn't fit whole. With another doomed drink in his hand, he fell back to the bed; rolling to his side for the remote after his initial crash. His hand fell into the bedside stand. Something, or some things, clinked. Throwing his legs off the side of the bed with a black and white sitcom star screeching hysteric at his side. Hand on either side of the top, he shook the dresser and found the jangle again. Crouching down. He pulled the drawer out and shook it’nothing but the sound of shaking wood. He dropped it on the carpet and reached his arm into the hole it left. Something cold. He pulled the lamp off and laid it across a pile of cans before he tilted the nightstand to him. He laughed. A small changefall ran out, accumulated a pile between his knees. A few dollars, maybe. Nothing to get excited about. He lifted the mattress and stretched his arms into the crevice. He smashed another can and weighed the doublebagged lump of coins against himself. He wrapped a third bag around it just to be safe and, pulling a button up shirt on over his wifebeater, headed out to the pale day. Concrete blind, rendered deafmute for a head full of thick Georgian air. Musky. Rancid. Paper mills and peach trees; it staggered him. A cool breeze cut through the afternoon heat. He buttoned his shirt with one hand and, down steps, hit the sidewalk Groggy. Swaying. Distracted in his cause by the glint of the parking lot pool. The whine and rattle of passing cars. It was some minor mischief he carried, one of a string of little rebellions instead of any single great one. He needed the exercise. He needed to stretch his leg out. That is, his limp wasn't just drunken. Grimaces rose to the surface of his face and he wiped them away with a hand that ran from his chin to the back of his neck, keeping his hair out of his eyes just long enough for his face to contort again. His knee stabbed. He thought of Rollins. She stepped behind him at the cash machine. Silent under the rattle of change. "You bringing me some money, Mr Jordan?" He turned and looked down to her. Uncomfortably close; tight to him like city dwellers. Shrinking into the violet vest with the store's name branded across her chest. He didn't try to guess her age; he just considered how many years removed he was from trying to pull it. Four, maybe five. "Huh. I dunno. Looks like you got a job already." "Not much of a job." She smirked up to him. "Couple of my friends said theyd seen you around town." "So, uh. My reputation precedes me?" "Sure. Thought you'd be little better off than this." She nodded her head to the green machine. "Oh, uh. Hah. Wanna ask about it? Sure I got a explanation." Crossed her arms over her chest. "Whatve you got?" "Haha. So, Im shitfaced in my motel room, find out fuckersd left about a halfton of change. Didnt wanna go rest of my life not turning coins into paper again." "Cute." The voice of the store: "Erika. Theres a line." "Shit. One second." She snapped on her heels, debated her manager. Jordan went back to feeding the beast. The tally was past fifty then, nearing sixty. The tray full, he swung the bags by his side and waited to be caught up. Erika at her register, scanning a cart's worth of cans, faced Jordan and rolled her eyes. Then laughed. More baskets were pushed into her aisle; Jordan took the time to empty out the bags and tossed them in the trash can between the machine and the box for plastic recyclables. There was a receipt; he snagged it and headed through a closed checkout to the store proper. A hundred and seventeen on the side. That, he figured, was at least a night or two's worth of drinking money; longer still if he stayed in and did it. He'd recanted on his prior want to drive the whole way, had called for plane tickets with a mind to leave the truck at the motel until he got back. The room was rented on a JXW account; there was going to be enough money to leave it uninhabited for a few days. It would let him stay for longer, and it would let him stay wasted for longer. Rollins and Knight and the rest had broken him down into component pieces; he needed physical therapy more than he needed to train and both went ignored. If it hurt too much to exercise, then, why not just assume being healthy's enough to win a match? He recounted his odds and backtracked from the beer aisle to the entrance for a cart that had less sqeaking wheels than the others he'd tried. He looked over his shoulders until his neck went stiff and was, by then, already firmly in the heart of alcohol and began to load his cart; first the plastic gut, followed with the steel undercarriage. Eight cases, he managed to wedge a ninth beneath and balance a tenth up top. Leaving his crude pyramid where it sat, he grabbed a bottle of red wine. He swung it by his side, dragging the cart behind him. He had a longview of the situation; he saw no chance of survival for the cases. "Yo. E" She spun her neck slow to him, broke away from a standing argument with her manager’he had the air of a pederast to him. Jordan couldn't place it. "Thats a lot of beer." "Really fuckin is." He started unloading, wine bottle standing on the pad for signing checks. "You got company?" "Not yet." "Planning on it?" "Maybe. Be prepared." "Boy Scouts?" "Far from it." "Mm. Gotta see your ID for this." "Im flattered." He, after an awkward hunt, handed it over. "Frank Black?" "Jordans a stage name." "It sounds better." "Maybe." "Im gonna call you Jordan." "Okay." "That weeeird to you?" He shrugged. The manager indicated the people piling up as per her delay; Erika put on a fake snarl to Jordan and waved a hand behind her. Cart loaded, he paid for what the cash machine's receipt didn’t cover and felt he’d came out on top. She scrawled her number on the back of the receipt and he was in the parking lot before he considered how far away his motel really seemed with that much alcohol to move with him. He locked his ankles into the steel wiring of the cart and lit a Newport, waiting on a bench. He'd worked grocery stores. A shift change was due. He wasn't paused for an hour before she came out the door, still wearing the white collared shit and the pale khakis. "Yo. Erika. Think you could help me out?" "If it isnt the lustrious Jordan White." He swiveled his head from drink to a man with a farmer's tan and a drawl, no older than Jordan but with a brusqeness that belied an accurate assessment. "Sorry man, aint got a pen on me." "Dont want your autograph." "Oh. Well. Not offerin anythin else." "Sister said she saw you the other day. Said you were hitting on her." "You sure? Spend a lot of time in my room." "Dont get smart." "Wouldnt dream of it." "Think youre funny?" Jordan shook his head and, twisting his stool to the bar, went back to killing whiskeys as they came. "You listen to me. You got that?" "I dont even know your name." "Mike." "I dont even know why the fuck I thought Id listen to you if I knew your name." The man sucked his lips into his teeth and lifted his chin. He rolled his shoulders. The barmaid put a full mug in front of him’he didn't regard it. His conversation was buried under the din of the room. He eased towards Jordan. "Listen. Just cause youre this bigshot dickhead dont mean I want you comin around tryin to lay my sister like she was just some tail. Thats it." Jordan nodded and watched his reflection in the drink. "Fair enough. Not that its your place to give a fuck who your sister bones. Not that Ive got a fuckin clue who the chick is. We good?" The man wrapped a fist around the cold handle and sloshed the beer. "I said, we good?" "Shes 17." "Dont see how that relates to shit. Havent even gotten ass since I came here, so. Uh. The fuck are you gettin at?" "Erika." "Didnt fuck her." Jordan kept his eye on Sportscenter. The man bit his tongue, cheeks swelling. Both minded their drinks for a moment. "Where was she last night?" "She was at my room. Doesnt mean I fucked with her." "Uh huh." "Ive had a shit week. Are you gonna do something?" "Warn ya." "Yeah. Something." "Just stay away from her. You dont, I let her boyfriend know." "He as passive aggressive with it as you?" "Youre funny, asshole. Real goddamned funny." She was knocking at his door with coffee a half hour before school started. Jordan, still halftilted, was just getting into something that could be called a real sleep when he had to climb off of bed and answer the rapraprapping at his chamber door. "Its me. Lemme in." He slid the chain off and pulled the door, straightening out the white tshirt he'd fallen over in. His hair, tangled and on end, still adjusted to its most recent cut. She sized him up. "Not a morning person?" "Nuh." She shoved the coffee at him like he should've been thanking her. He drank it with the automatic handtomouth he'd practiced. It, flavor of burnt oil, scalded. She shook her head. "Thirsty, though." Jordan sat on the bed and wiped a coffee smear off of his growing need to shave. "Saw your brother last night." "Oh?" "Yeah. Ran into him at Sagebrush. Or he ran into me. Dont like him." "Whatd he say?" She posted against a wall. Jordan packed and opened a box of smokes and lit two, tossing one her way. "That I fucked you and that I shouldnt be fucking you." "Jesus. Hes an ass." "Your boyfriend that much of an ass?" "Oh. He told you about Jim?" "Halfway. He threatened me with him, some 'keep fuckin around and I sic her boy on you' shit. Hell do I care about Jim, though?" She kicked a can across the room, twisting her lips into her teeth. "Jims a little crazy." "Erika. Im a wrestler. Everyone Ive mets a lot of crazy." "Hehe. Maybe." "What makes James stand out so much?" "His names Jimmy." "Like, birth certificate name?" "Yup." "Dont see how that makes him so crazy." "Its, its. Its not that. Hes just schizo. Like, actually medicated, diagnosed, schizophrenia. Shit." She swayed her foot sidetoside. "Hes not my boy, though. Hes a friend of my brothers and he doesnt like the idea Id dump him for a younger guy." "Younger guy?" "Oh. Yeah, you dont know him. Jims twentyfive." "Oh, shit. So youre always tryin to get up on the older man." She blushed. Slightly. "Everyone my age is boys." "And Im in that 'younger man hes gettin left for' spot." "Maybe. Look, really, its nothing to worry about. He cant even drive for like, another six months." "DUI?" "Yup." "Damn. Sa lotta shit to hear cause of a chick Im not even fuckin." "Age of consents sixteen round here." "Not tryin to hear that either, girl. It aint just that, its my job and its my girl and. Shits, uh. Shits complicated. Feel me?" "Not yet. No." She looked at him down the bridge of her nose. Her lips curled. Jordan shook his head and rolled another lager out of a box. Almost room temperature; cold enough against the memory of coffee. He pointed one to her; ashing on the floor, she waved it away. "Exams today." "Fuck." "Its alright. You mind if I come by when I get off work?" "What time?" "Late tonight. Closing." "Thats word. I should be around." "Yeah?" "Yeah." "Good." She crossed the room and stopped in front of Jordan, her hands on the back of his head. He looked up to her as she leaned in to kiss his forehead. "Be good, boy." "Yeah." "Alright. Ill see you later." She left. Jordan wiped his brow, finished his beer, then his coffee, and slid the chain after her. He slept most of the day, woke up after dark and tossed a sandwich together for the oven. He woke to the cracking of glass outside and went to the window. He cracked the vertical blinds and looked down to the parking lot, full of raised trucks and beaten Buicks; all covered in mud, all with engines running and lights on. A very large man held a pipe to the sky in the center; the rest leaned against the hoods of their vehicles and watched the motel front. "Mother fucker." It bellowed. "Get out here, asshole." Jordan counted to himself; eleven. Bad odds for something he wasn't able to measure the worth of. Erika rolled over on the bed, eyes split slits. "Mnh?" He put a hand up towards her and left the window after tugging at the bracket the blinds were mounted on. Pulling his blanket off the couch, he tossed it over the rail and spread it wide. He stuffed a pillow in the top and, blind to any light outside, staggered to the bed and turned one lamp on. "What's going on?" She kept her voice low. "Jim, uh. Jim a big motherfucker, loud?" "Yeah." "Huh. I got a problem, then." Another window smashed out. Jordan hoped they were demolishing their own rides. "Fucking asshole. Let me talk to him." "No." "No?" "No." "Why? Hes my ex." "Exactly. Yknow what exacerbate means?" "Yeah." "Yeah. Thats whatd happen, and I dont want no escalation of this here shit." "So youre gonna hide out here and hope he leaves? Because, well. The law aint comin. His daddys the sheriff." "Doesnt even matter, Im leavin this fucked up town an this fucked up state with it." "What?" "Now, now, chill. Im not leavin you alone with a bunch of fucked up rednecks. Youre the one thing might keep me safe on the way out of here, so. Can you climb?" Jordan stepped out of his room. Jim shut up; the onlookers looked on still. "Yo. Up here’you lookin for me." "Less theres some other pole suckin, girl stealin motherfucker up in here. Get your faggot ass down." "Why dont you come up?" "Fuck you." Eyes peered out of windows. One door cocked open a crack. "I am a busy fucking man, tubs. You want some contact, you come and take it." "Fuck you." "No, fuck you. I was tryna sleep and you came along to wreck that shit, so fuck you. Not goin outta my way for shit, much less your bullshit." "Pussy." "Yknow it. Swhy I brought my whole fuckin crew to watch me get my ass beat. Wait, wait." He grabbed the railing, leaned over to stare down at Jim. "That was you’so, fuck you. Bitch." It was enough. Jim spat at Jordan and headed for the steps. The congregation fell in line behind him, crowding the walkway as they turned the corner to see a distinct lack of Jordan, and an overabundance of a single halfopen door. Putting a hand up, Jim laid his forearm against it and smiled a stained grin. The door slammed shut against him. He staggered to the railing and, collecting himself, launched at it. It swung open with him, swung shut behind him. He stumbled in the dark; crashed into a dresser and sent a lamp to the ground. His pipe rattled to the floor. "Turn on a fuckin light." "Thatd be fair. Clearly, this fight wasnt never supposed to be fair." And a chair crashed down over some rough estimation of the back of Jim's neck and head, back and seat splintering a few inches past impact. He slumped to his elbows and knees, tried to brace himself against the ground and found a beer can; prone to sliding as cans are, he planted to his face with a grunt. They were beating at the door, then. The breeze from the balcony ran through Jordan's hair and he looked down to the heap of Jim; vague, deformed, gelatinous in the dim light of a blocked out world. Jordan shook his head and brought the remnants of the chair down again, knocked the wind out of the heap before him and knelt beside it. "I had a real bad week, dog." He whispered. "Didnt have to bring it up." Before him: wheezing, gasping, heaving. He only figured on a minute before there was a fight again and went to the door. He swallowed and turned the knob, pulling it in. He conflated bravado with valor and value alike. "I'm fuckin up eryone out here." His voice was low; it rattled his ribs and tore at his throat. He swung the leg. "Back the fuck up. Back your fuckin bitch up." Affirmation preceded a cautious backtracking. "Good. Good." He nodded. "Were gonna play a game’I get paid to fuck people up. Back the fuck up!" He lifted the leg and bucked forwards. Blood trailed down to his fingertips. Crowd control; they kept a light distance even as they blocked the stairs down. Jordan advanced, clapping the snapped leg against his cupped hand. They all jittered; clenched and unclenched fists, ground teeth to dust and filled their shoulders out. He took another step. They didn't back any further. Below them, a big block engine revved. "This gonna be it, then?" One charged. Jordan held his breath and swung wild; the cheap wood gave one last time across a sunburnt face as Jordan's body went a sort of rubbery limp; momentum carried the swing over the railing and Jordan's mass followed it; he carried down an "oh shit" cut short by a one foot/one knee landing in a truckbed coated with glass dust. He cringed, bracing himself against abrupt reversal with one arm locked into the hole-n’e-rear window of the cab; he held a defiant fist up to the crowd in front of his room and turned it into a middle finger as an afterthought as they peeled out of the parking lot. They were thirty minutes out of town when Erika pulled over on a dirt road. Jordan hopped out of the bed and went to the front; he tossed his bags into the back to make room and shrugged. "Damn. Five cases made it? Word up, girl." Erika laughed. "Not the first time I had to sneak beer out of a second story." He checked his bags and pulled out a portion of a carton of cigarettes that he tossed Erika's way. She repeated him; lit two and walked around the truck to hand one over to a nodding Jordan. "Thats everythin." "I told you to trust me, boy." "Hah. Yeah. Shit. Think Jims gonna bring the law?" "No way. Too proud for that." Jordan fished himself another beer and let the tailgate down to take a seat. "What about you, then?" "What about me?" "Youre 17. I cant just take you with me." "And why not?" "Youre a fuckin kid." "Am not." "Come on." "Come on what? I saved your ass back there, I turn 18 in January, and I really want to get out of this shithole." "What about school?" "Uh, I just finished my finals and and, and! I'm on the early graduation program." "Your parents?" "I live with Mike." "Oh." "Yeah. I emancipated my way to that shithead." She sat beside him and faced the ground. "And I really really really dont want to go back there. Not right now. At least." "True." "So I can tag along with you?" His cellphone let out three knocks from the bottom of one of the duffel bags. He leaned back and slid it between the two of them, digging it out to check. Sage. "am leaving country stop contact me when possible stop." Erika read over his shoulder. "Sage?" "My boss." "Ohh." He slid the phone into his pocket. "You on winter break?" "Uh huh." "Youre takin shotgun." "Fuck yes."
