A change of heart

18 May 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/18/2008 Generally when the phone rings I strive with all my willpower to against picking it up. The person on the either end is usually someone with news that I do not want to hear and I see no reason to help them get their message to me. But for some reason I chose to succumb to the temptation on a lazy Tuesday evening as I sat watching a rerun of House. I had actually decided to forgo my usual strenuous workout routine and had taken the day off to sit on my ass. It was the reward to myself for finally paying off all the rent that I owed and for once not being the lone opponent against a large group. Do not misunderstand me, Chris Bagwell is an ass. In fact his entire group is composed of people that I wouldn’t even bother to piss on if they were being burned alive. But the fact of the matter is that the lone man standing up for what’s right has a tendency to get his ass beat more often than not in the world of wrestling. I am quite frankly done trying to be the “good guy” or even trying to entertain the fans. My only goal in the ring is to earn my paycheck with as little damage done to me as possible. Fortunately because of my association with Bagwell I don’t even have to pretend to care about the fans? enjoyment. They are perfectly happy to keep watching my matches and hoping to see me get beat or hurt. So while I was watching House I was beginning to seriously consider cutting my training regime down to three days a week, with the other three days devoted to rotting my body and mind in front of the television. But right when I began to seriously believe I had a feasible idea I picked up the phone. “Hello,” I said in the bored tone made possible only by someone who has lost all sense of ambition and enthusiasm. “Wow kid, considering the sweet gig you’ve managed to land I thought that I would be hearing a lot more happiness in your voice.” I sat straight up and smile began to spread across my face. Most people have something akin to a favorite uncle and mine was on the phone. “Igor” I said happily “how are you?” I hadn’t seen Igor for about five years when he decided to take an extremely long vacation in Europe. The whole family, including myself, argued that a 40-something year old man had no business backpacking through a continent but the stubborn goat left in the middle of the night explaining that he would be back when he was done “judging every whorehouse that I can find.” “Well as it turns out” he answered with a hint of embarrassment in his voice, “if you don’t pay you’re rent for five years the landlord is allowed to give your apartment to someone else. The old bastard didn’t even bother to store my furniture anywhere, he just sold it.” I couldn’t help but laugh, though I was surprised at how unfamiliar the sound seemed. There had been nothing for me to laugh about for a long time, and I decided that even though my uncle was about to ask me if he could crash at my place I was glad that he was back. “Get over here,” I interrupted before he could even ask. “I don’t have a spare room, but I do have a couch till you find something else.” Igor made it to my apartment in less than fifteen minutes, and he was asleep on my couch five minutes after that. As I studied his face I was shocked at out haggard and tense he looked, Europe had obviously not been as kind as he had hoped. But I shrugged and headed into my room to get some sleep. Now that he was back I figured it wouldn’t be long till Igor was restored to his usual self. I slept like a log, as I usually do. I should have awoken to my irresponsible uncle sitting in his underwear eating a bowl of cereal while watching tv. Not only should I have seen it, but I was looking forward to seeing it. It would have been a sign of a happy childhood that I had left a long time ago, and I wanted to relieve some old memories. Instead as I walked out of my bedroom I found that I had been completely cleaned out. Everything from my couch, to my laptop was gone; somehow Igor had even managed to take the fridge. A cold wave of anger swept through me even as I dropped to my knees in a daze. I had always known that my uncle was not the most reputable person in the world, in fact I was well aware that he was on the wrong side of the law more often that not. But I had always thought that I meant something to him, something more than just an easy mark. Mechanically I stood up and headed outside. As a child Igor once took me along to one of his “business transactions” and I had never forgotten where it had taken place. Most thieves are intelligent enough not to sell their ill-gotten merchandise at the very same place, but the fact that he openly screwed me over proves that my uncle is not a smart man. A deceitful one certainly, but I had a feeling that he continued selling at the same exact place for no other reason than convenience. I made my way there, though I borrowed a steel pipe from a group of construction workers on the way there. In my part of town there are two types of people when it comes to crime. There are the ones who futilely call the police, and the ones who take matters into their own hands. The police try their best but there are too many limitations constraining their movements. Vigilantes on the other hand have no such obstacles, and as such people tend to help those that plan to take on matters personally. For some reason it helps to lower the crime rate. The rest of the story is fairly predictable. I found the bastard and I beat him to an inch of his life, than I took all the money he had made off me before leaving him there. There was quite a bit of dialogue involved, mostly concerning his begging and my angry responses, but there is no point in relating it to you. The point of this story was its affect on me. Subconsciously I always believed that I was still a decent person. I had always thought that Bagwell would one day cross an imaginary line, and help me rediscover the “good guy” within me. But I frankly do not give a crap about that anymore. There is no longer any point in which I will decide I want to go back to being loved by the fans, they can go fuck themselves for all I care. Bagwell keeps me from getting in any of the idiotic position the fan favorites find themselves in, and in exchange I will do exactly what he says. His latest request is that I win decisively in my next match through whatever means necessary. I would apologize to Jack Reed in advance for the consequences of that request’but everyone knows I’d be lying.
