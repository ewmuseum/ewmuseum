Game Time

4 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/4/06 After three months of inactivity within the wrestling circuit, I get to go back on the road again and I can’t wait. This time it will all be different, I’ll be different. I’ll try my hardest and I will succeed despite failing so many times before. I’ll make sure it comes to pass. Before, I was a simple lower-card clown. Only interested in having a good time and getting a reasonable wage for it. There was no glory or honour in what I did; I was an entertainer more than the fierce warrior I always knew I should’ve been. It wasn’t important then. It didn’t matter whether I won or lost in any given match because I was having fun. I was a crowd pleaser and I just wanted my face on the television, mugging the spotlight for as much camera time I could get. No more. No more of that buffoonery. When I come back this time, I’m probably not going to please anyone. I’m coming to ruffle some feathers. Spice things up a little. Ascend the ranks of the GCW as quickly and as efficiently as I can. I’m not here to play or have fun. That’s not me, not anymore. This newcomer battle royal at this weeks GCW World Wide will provide the platform I require to get the ball rolling on this one. It’ll allow me to indicate to the management that I’m clearly head and shoulders above the rest of these supposed “hot, upcoming new stars”. It will show them I’m already primed for an opportunity to shine. Then... McKail sat beside her bed, holding Mary’s pale, weak hand, trying to shake off the hospital’s sterile smell that always managed to make his stomach wrench. He hated hospitals. Always avoided them like the plague. This time it wasn’t about him and he was determined to stay by her bedside no matter what. His dislikes didn’t even come into it. “Hon,” she began, startling him somewhat. She’d been asleep for a couple hours and the last time McKail checked up on her she hadn’t shown any sign of waking up. “You should go home. You hate hospitals.” McKail grinned and shook his head. “I don’t think so,” he told her. “The doctor came by earlier, you have surgery scheduled for this afternoon. I’m not going anywhere.” McKail could see her eyes suddenly glare back at him fearful, but Mary put a brave face on it. “Great. Time to cut this thing out!” McKail smiled. “It’s going to be okay...you’re going to be okay.” “I know but...” Mary trailed off, turning her head to look away. “What is it” What’s wrong?” McKail asked her. Mary turned her head back toward him, her eyes quickly welling up with tears. “It just feels like the last time we’re going to speak...I have so much to say.” McKail shook his head and laughed it off. “Don’t be silly,” he advised her. “I’ll talk to you right after surgery.” “No, Jacob,” she said, angrily. “Don’t laugh this off. There’s a very real chance I’m not coming back out of that operation room and I want you to listen to me.” “Okay,” Jacob replied weakly. “After this is all over,” she began, glaring directly into Jacob’s eyes sternly, “promise me you’ll go for your dreams. Promise me you’ll do everything in your power to attain them.” “What are you talking about?” McKail asked her, genuinely bewildered. “You’re wrestling career, Jacob,” Mary told him. “We both know you have the skill to go big time. To take your career to it’s logical conclusion.” “But...” McKail trailed off. “But nothing,” Mary mercilessly interrupted. “You’ve attempted to hide it from me, but I know. I know you have the ability to take your career to the next level, but I’ve been holding you back.” McKail shook his head. “How could you have possibly held me back?” Mary smiled. “Being higher up on the card would mean going on the road more. It would mean more personal appearances and more high profile matches. You don’t want to leave me alone on my own. I didn’t mind before, I was just happy to have you around.” “But now?” McKail questioned, not really wanting to believe what he was hearing. “Now I finally understand,” she enveloped my hand with both of hers now. “You have to do this.” “Okay,” McKail told her. “But only after you’ve fully recovered.” She smiled and released her grip. “Okay.” The doctor approached at the door of Mary’s private room, sensed he’d walked in on a special moment and coughed to make his presence known. McKail immediately rose from his seat, shook the doctors and nodded a greeting. “Well, Mrs McKail,” The doctor began. He took her chart from the foot of the bed and began to flick through it. “Looks like we’ll operating on you today. We’ll have to prepare her for surgery immediately, so you’ll have to wait outside sir.” McKail nodded and then kissed Mary on the cheek before leaving the room. Nurses with a cart full of preparation equipment were already waiting outside and he held the door open for them before he left for the waiting room at the end of the ward. That was the last time McKail ever saw his wife again. Now... Driving to Miami, Florida, McKail could do nothing else but while away the hours thinking about the most painful day in his life. It was a form of self torture his brain had decided to utilise in order to get the results it - he - knew it had to. Upon hearing the news, McKail overreacted. Drastically so. He freely admitted it to the hospital staff and the police afterward. That didn’t stop him from having the right to overreact. The hospital staff are used to people grieving, upset that the doctors or surgeons couldn’t save the life of a loved one, but he understood they weren’t much prepared for a two hundred and fifteen pound man overreacting and ultimately devastating the waiting room. The nurses were terrified; they ran for cover quickly. A few hero doctors decided to try and stop him, but they were shrugged aside with considerable ease. Hospital security arrived on the scene quite quickly, but they didn’t really pose much of a threat to him either. The whole scene was strikingly similar to a well-seasoned warrior facing part-time, inadequate mercenaries, in a blatant uneven battle. Then McKail simply stopped mid-tussle, fell to his knees and finally broke down in tears. He wasn’t used to showing emotion in public, but venting the anger allowed all of the pain and heartache to rush forward and quickly overwhelm him. In the end, there was nothing he could do to hold it all back. Looking back on it, McKail felt ashamed. Ashamed that he reacted is such an animalistic, violent way. Ashamed about all the damage he caused. Ashamed about all of the people he either scared or hurt. Ashamed that he found it easier to blame everyone else for the death of his wife than the true culprit - himself. He should have noticed it earlier. He should have forced her to go to see the doctor sooner. When the police arrived to cart him away, the hospital had every right to want to punish him, but ultimately McKail didn’t even have to spend the night in the cells. The hospital understood his pain and with their alleged culpability in the death of his wife hanging over their heads, they deemed he should see an hospital appointed counsellor twice a week. A “punishment” that McKail wasn’t particularly looking forward to, but accepted nonetheless. As McKail pulled up to the arena, home to this weeks GCW World Wide, he felt a tear well up in his eye, but he put a stop to that immediately. Emotion was meaningless from this point on. Only intense focus could aid him now. It was game time.
