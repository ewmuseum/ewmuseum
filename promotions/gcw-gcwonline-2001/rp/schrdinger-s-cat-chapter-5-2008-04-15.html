Schr’dinger's Cat: Chapter 5

15 April 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 15th, 2008 The pain woke him. It was not unusual for him to wake up battered and bruised as it was basically his job to give and take beatings but this pain was like no other. He reached with his right hand towards the source of the pain and found his armed wrapped in gauze and more pain. He couldn’t remember everything that had happened and it all seemed like a blur. The sounds of gunfire, explosions and the smell of gunpowder were still imprinted in his memory, though. He tried to sit up in the bed but the pain kept him down. He looked towards a window on the far side of the room and saw someone standing there. He grunted once more as he tried to take a better look at the person who stood silently looking towards the horizon. “Don’t move so much, you might reopen the wound. We don’t want you to bleed to death,” the person said in a very familiar voice. The individual turned around and he could see the sudden glow of the sunlight reflecting off of her short white dress. Her jet black hair was eye-catching when it was contrasted by the white of the dress. Antonio finally saw her features and recognized her. There was no way that he couldn’t recognize the person he had cried for and almost died for; the person that once in a lifetime had been everything for him. To see the face of Justine was something he would have never thought possible. “I” ah?” He had thought so many times about what he would have said to her if he had the chance to speak to her again. The words were in his head, if he could just reach for them. The dreamlike appearance of this person who he had long ago lost was a little bit too much for his brain. “You don’t have to say anything,” she said as she sat down besides him and put her hand on top of his. “I can’t believe I’m seeing you again.” It was the same for her. In this world that she lived in, Antonio Vargas had died and left her behind to raise her son alone. This was something out of a dream for the both of them. “I’m sorry,” he blurted out. He felt deep down that he owed Justine an apology. It had been his fault that she had died and it had been his fault that their unborn son had died. His past had caught up with them and there had been nothing he could do about it. Antonio had never been able to forgive himself for what he had brought upon those he loved, but seeing her that day was somehow his only chance. She smiled at him and tightened her grip on his hand. He responded. Antonio wished the moment would never end but he knew that eventually they would need to discuss what had happened. It hit him at that moment. The person in front of him looked so much like the Justine he knew, but it wasn’t. Or was she? It was all too confusing for him. Before he could keep questioning the moment, there was a rap on the door. Justine’s eyes shined in the dimness of the room. She stood up and walked towards the door. “I want you to meet someone.” She opened the door and a small boy of about six walked in. Antonio immediately tried to stand up but the pain of the bullet wound on his shoulder kept him down. Antonio released a moan of pain and fell back down on the bed. His eyes never left the little boy. He had always known the face of Justine, but back in his world, he had never met his son. It was a surreal experience. “Milo, say hello to your real father,” she told the boy in a sweet voice and gently pushed him towards the edge of the bed. Milo looked at the man in the bed and shied away. Justine had been telling the kid that Burton was his father in order to gain his confidence but she had secretly been showing him pictures of his real father. She gently pushed him again. “Go on.” The kid walked up to the edge of the bed and saw the hand that was waiting for him. He hesitated a moment and then went over and grabbed it. The touch of his son’s hand instantly sent goose bumps all around Antonio’s body and for a minute that they spent there in silence and holding hands, the pain was nonexistent. The moment was finally too overwhelming and Antonio felt tears welling up in his eyes. Justine saw Antonio’s eyes and called Milo over to her. Milo went towards his mother and she led him to the outside of the room. She came back inside and Antonio was sitting up. The pain had been bad but he needed to think straight about everything that was happening. Justine closed the door and went towards the bed. “I have to go now. Burton will realize what happened if I never get back. Rest. I will be back later and we will talk,” she said as she closed in to kiss him on the cheek. His good hand went to the back of her head. He caressed her hair and kissed her passionately. His eyes stayed open for a second before he couldn’t help but fall into the kiss and let go. --- Burton paced back and forth in rage. The plan had not gone according to plan and he had not gotten what he wanted. These days, he always got what he wanted. The death of Antonio Vargas had been carefully and meticulously planned and it failed. The anger in his eyes was almost murderous. “How did it fail” Did you tell him something about it?” he asked someone else in the room. “I want to see his blood running, damn it.” “And you will,” the other figure in the room said. The smooth female voice belongs to Sheila. “It was just a minor set back. We got the resources we needed to divide amongst the people and soon Antonio will be dead.” She didn’t much like the last part, but in order to survive in that dog eat dog world, sacrifices had to be made. Burton slammed his fist on the wall of his room and cursed once more. He not only had lost another good man, but he had nothing to show for it besides a few boxes of supplies. Not even the fact that his people would not die of starvation could alleviate the hatred he felt for Antonio. The door to the room opened and Justine entered the room with Milo in tow. He saw the look of frustration in the face of Burton and then saw Sheila as she handed a drink to Burton. He downed it without thinking and sat down. “Where have you been?” he asked suspiciously. “I went out. I couldn’t help but go out. The anticipation was too much to bear. By the look of your faces though, it doesn’t seem like you were successful,” she walked towards the bar and served herself a drink. She whispered a few words in Milo’s ears and he went to the back of the room where a door to the sleeping quarters stood open. He went inside and closed the door behind him. She poured another drink for Burton and gave it to him before sitting down besides him. Justine had been able to turn off the excitement she had been feeling the last few hours as she stood with the man she had lost once in order to be convincing. She had been playing a character for so long now though that it came naturally. “So, what exactly happened?” “Everything was going according to plan. I left Juan in the stop where Antonio would be going by according to Sheila here and then left to coordinate the attack on the convoy. It wasn’t that hard to pull off. Either Juan got sloppy, which I doubt, or someone helped him,” he said scratching his brain trying to figure out exactly what went on. “Is there any way someone from your camp in helping him?” he asked Sheila. “Not that I know off. He was there too short a time to get reacquainted with anyone. I had the most contact with him and I surely didn’t do it,” she said as she took a sip from her scotch. “I guess we’ll just have to investigate. He couldn’t have gone long. There was a trail of blood where I guess Juan shot him but didn’t kill him. It was lost, but we know he is hurt,” said Sheila. “Let the hunt begin,” Burton said with a sadistic smile on his face. --- Antonio had not been able to sleep much since Justine and Milo had left. Sleep was almost inconceivable at that point. Plus, the pain was too much. He had tried to get up and failed miserably which only left him feeling sore and in a bad mood. He gulped down what was left of a bottle of water Justine had left for him. There was a sudden bang as the front door of the house was closed hard. He listened intently, unable to do or move much. The door to the room opened slowly and a familiar face appeared with a gun in hand. “You!”” Antonio Vargas tried to dodge the bullet that was to come. Finally, death had reached him.
