Date: Various Apologies everybody, this sucks and I know it, I'll get better. She fell to her knees, all alone on a field that belonged to the dead. Daria Ngozi knew no conscious thought, no emotion, nothing but a sense of numbness that had become all too common in her life. As the sun set, she became bathed in an orange light, becoming more and more intense as those orange rays slipped beneath the distant horizon. She knew inside of herself that, soon enough, she had to move, had to leave this place, but for now, she found herself incapable of it. Fear, perhaps, or sorrow, on some level she couldn't consciously feel, kept her locked in place, the AK-47 lying in the mixture of dust and blood at her feet the only thing keeping her company. That, of course, and the dead. Both friend and foe. A slight breeze came from the south, blowing across the sweaty flesh in a way that felt refreshing, but it was both blessing and curse at the same time. The wind that cooled her, that made this awful situation just a little more comfortable, carried on it's breath the stench of blood, of sweat. Death breathed into the wind, and the odor nearly curdled her stomach. She knew many of the dead, those who lay in tangled piles or alone on the scorched and stained ground. Some of them she'd known back in the days of her innocence, some she had known only in passing. And still others she knew only because she had seen them through the sights of the rifle, or at the business end of the knife. They were like her in life, frightened, forced into this conflict or coerced, some of them too hopelessly addicted to the drugs that flowed freely in the camps to even consider the merits of escape. They had been all of those things and more, and now they were nothing. Now they were piles of freshly created carrion awaiting cleanup by Mother Nature. Carrion that Daria had had a hand in creating. She knew that, the feeling reinforced as she looked to her left and into the dead, staring eyes of a boy she had known, had played soccer with, ran with, and even fought with back in the village she had called home. He'd been a rangy, athletic young man, intelligent despite his lack of education, and she'd called him a friend. Quick to laugh, even at his own expense. That young man didn't exist in the last three years, he'd died and been replaced with someone else living in his body. A vicious, angry young man who hated everything in sight had replaced him, a poison that Daria herself shared. She swallowed hard, hating herself, hating this place, hating everything. Suddenly emotion rushed hard to the forefront of her psyche, a snarl echoing in the stillness as she looked at the myriad of insects that had ascended on her friend's face, beginning the process of breaking him down into nothing. They were the first on scene, those who arrived early to the buffet, and their crawling, snaking motions made his face look alive in a macabre way. The light was becoming more and more dim now, and she sighed heavily as the first of the tears flooded her high cheekbones. What was there to do? Where would she go? Many of the people on this field had been the closest thing she knew to family, to friends. And now, with them gone, she felt a surge of hopelessness tear through her. Slamming the stock of the assault rifle into the ground, she used it to pull herself upright, leaned on it for support. The wound to her leg was minor, a mere flesh wound, and she supposed that somewhere out there were others who were merely wounded. But the stink of death on the wind and the utter stillness was more than she could bear, more than she could deal with. Soon enough, darkness would fall, the sun fading so rapidly gone completely, and then the footsoldiers of nature's cleanup efforts would arrive, replacing the silence with the sound of feasting. They would clear this field, the scavengers, and by morning, nothing would remain but gnawed bones to bleach in the sun. Taking a single, wobbly step away from the nearest bodies, Daria looked toward the hilltop down which they had charged. She would seize the moment, would make her escape, even if she knew not what awaited her. Even if what ultimately was her reward was her own death, which was likely if she were caught, she could no longer stand this. This could not be her life anymore, this endless cycle of killing and bloodshed for no reason other than the lust for power of men. And now, on a field seemingly devoid of life, her opportunity to get away seemed to be at hand... ---- I woke up again to the dream, to the day I walked away from my old life, but as always, seemed to wake up before the really bad shit started. I knew it would be happening, it always seems to start again anytime I'm worried or stressed about something. Like going back to GCW, for example. Jesus, I had expected this to be something that I would enjoy, that I should be over the damn moon happy about. I was going back to the place to settle some unfinished business, to gain back what I had lost. I was going back to once again be somebody. Instead, I was rapidly making myself into a basket case of epic proportions, stressing myself out over things that should be easy. In a case like this, there was only one thing to do. Pick up the phone, dial a number, wait for the person on the other end to answer. Because I can't do this alone. Trust me, though, GCW hasn't seen the last of me. On the contrary, we've only just begun.
