Gravity

8 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/08/10 "He was the most crooked man I ever met. By that I don't mean that he was a criminal, though he certainly had been at one time. I just mean that there was nothing simple about him, nothing straight, nothing honest. His road wound so much that one could never be sure where it was leading. "I haven't seen him in some time. Last I heard he had gone back to Japan. Which is just fine with me, I sleep better at night knowing he's on the other side of the planet. But the man was so... large. I can still feel his gravity." Franklin Faulkner shifted in his seat and flipped to a new page in his notebook. "Was that a fat joke?" "No." Faulkner swallowed hard. He was starting to regret having asked for the interview in the first place. He thought a conversation with GCW's estranged owner might somehow be good for his career, but at the moment he was feeling out of his element. Caldera sat behind his desk, reclining slightly, his eyes now and again drifting to the window. They said he had become distant in recent weeks, quiet and contemplative. Faulkner marveled that a man so young might have so much to reflect upon. But sitting atop his dramatic skyscraper in downtown Columbus, Caldera seemed deeply affected by time. It wasn't in his face. It was in his eyes. They seemed weary, wise and cautious. As a journalist Faulkner prided himself in his ability to read people, but Caldera was a blank slate. He revealed nothing save the telltale signs of deep wells of thought to which Caldera would never grant another human being access. Caldera cleared his throat and continued. "No, Kimbusa had a strange greatness about him. It wasn't his personality, believe me, nor was it his size. I think it was his nature; his survival instinct was more finely honed than yours or mine. And how could it not be? When you look like that, when you're that well known, you can never hide." "You think he hated the fame?" "Oh, it's far more deeply seated than that. People like us, Mr. Faulkner, we take our ability to blend for granted. I've spent my entire adult life seeking fame and fortune, and I've never wavered in my ambition. And I've done well in that regard. But I can still walk into most places in the world and go unnoticed. For me, that just means I haven't reached my full potential. But for Kimbusa, that's the kind of freedom he's always craved. " Faulkner's face twisted, still having some trouble grasping Caldera's explanation. "So why sumo? Why come to America to be a professional wrestler? If he hated the fame so much--" "There you go with that word again. Fame had absolutely nothing to do with it. Yes, Kimbusa was known all across Japan. And yes, Kimbusa was a successful wrestler in America. But they didn't stare at him because they recognized his face. They stared at him because he was different. Truth be told, though, it wasn't just his size that made him stand out. It was his aura, if you believe in that sort of thing. He had this dark cloud that seemed to follow him everywhere. You couldn't escape it. The superstitious types in Tokyo remember a huge beast that roamed the alleys. They say it was made of shadow and had glowing red eyes." Faulkner couldn't contain a chuckle. Caldera nodded. "I know better. The eyes are the windows to the soul, and Kimbusa's eyes are black." Faulkner swallowed hard. "It sounds like you think he was evil." "No, Mr. Faulkner, I'm sorry if I gave you that impression. Kimbusa never tried to do wrong. In fact I think he wanted desperately to do good. But as you've obviously noticed, Kimbusa was quite contrary." "To what?" "To himself. To everything you think you know about the way people are. He hated fame yet he sought it out. Why? Because he knew he could never hide, so what difference does fame make? He hated violence yet he fought for a living. Why? Because violence was the only thing he knew. It tears at one's heart, Mr. Faulkner, to be trapped in a body and a mind you detest. Kimbusa's soul wasn't black because he meant to harm others. There was simply nothing there to see." Caldera paused. Faulkner, unsure of where to go next, sat in silence and waited. "Do you know how gravity works, Mr. Faulkner? It's a passive force. A body doesn't pull on you; rather, it warps the space around it, and anything that gets too close simply falls in. I don't think Kimbusa ever wanted to affect things, he just couldn't help it. It's gravity, Mr. Faulkner. Kimbusa's gravity was so great that wherever he went, things just sort of got sucked in. And try as he might, he was never able to get so far away from things that he stopped grabbing them. Kimbusa changed the world simply by virtue of being in it." Faulkner shook his head. "We should all be so lucky." Caldera nodded in agreement. "I used to think so, too." The room grew quiet again. Caldera raised an eyebrow. "Was there some reason in particular you came here today?" "Oh. I heard Kimbusa was back in the country, as a matter of fact." For the first time, Caldera seemed moved. "Where did you hear this?" "Sorry, sir, I can't reveal a source. But as you know, he's difficult to miss." Caldera slid his seat back and leaned forward. For a moment Faulkner thought he saw him perspiring. "Is this cause for alarm?" "Weren't you paying attention? It doesn't matter why he's back, Mr. Faulkner. If Kimbusa is here, you'd better believe the earth will move."
