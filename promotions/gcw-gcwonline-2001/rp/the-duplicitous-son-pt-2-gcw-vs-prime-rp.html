The Duplicitous Son, pt.2 (GCW vs. PRIME RP)

Date: Present Day 19th May 2008 Late at night iPay One Centre, San Diego. The door of Thomas James LeStrange's locker room in the iPay One Centre creaked as it tentatively opened, taking a considerable amount of time to open fully. From within emerged the familiar figure of the Englishman. A first glance from a passerby would have observed and confirmed the usual aspects of his appearance which GCW fans and staff alike were rapidly coming to expect. They first would have been struck by his athletic form. Whilst of average, arguably even small proportions for his profession (his vital statistics listed him as six-feet-one of height, with a weight of two-hundred-and-twelve pounds), compared to the remainder of the population, he was tall and broad. His noble head sat upon broad, strong shoulders, easily his most striking attribute. From these brawny, Atlas-like joints, the remainder of his upper body narrowed as it descended, forming the 'V' shape of the prototypical sportsman. Below his lean waist, sat strong, tree-trunk thighs, the second most striking aspect of his appearance, and the area of his body from which he derived his strength and explosion.Covering this Spartan physique was an exquisite bespoke suit of grey Prince of Wales check, complimented by shiny black brogues, a combination he considered to be the height of sartorial elegance. With a timidness incongruous to his usual gait, Thom edged down the corridor on soft, light feet, clearly trying to control and limit the squeaking of his shoes on the linoleum flooring. Rather than taking his customary route to leave the arena, through the main hallways, today Thom took instead the most clandestine route possible, stalking the darkest and most remote bowels of the arena. As he finally arrived at a rarely-used fire exit at the back of the arena, a diminuitive GCW worker stepped into his path. A smidgen of panic flittered across the enigmatic eyes of the Englishman, as they chanced across the headset and radio pack of the employee. Tools that could alert others to his departure. The worker, who sported a black GCW polo shirt tucked into cream-coloured chinos worn at an alarming height, opened his mouth to address LeStrange. Without even breaking his stride, Thom stepped past him without acknowledging his existence, depressing the bar of the fire exit and pushing the heavy door open with focused and determined force. The employee was left silent, the words he had prepared caught in his throat, the Englishman's haste denying him the opportunity to enunciate them. Moments later, they trickled out as a baffled, incoherent mumbling sound. Eyebrows raised, he turned and left. Whereas a swift first glance would have confirmed the aspects of LeStrange's appearance that now were becoming the standard, a second, more lengthy gaze at the English wrestler would reveal alarming differences. His handsome face, once full of colour and marked by a strong jawline and enigmatic, devious brown eyes, was a barely a shadow of its former splendour. His hair, once deep brown, voluminous and perfectly styled, was now lank, greasy and wild. The colour had long since drained from his features, his skin now sallow, wan and pale. His eyes were sunken and dull, and his stare was now reduced to a lifeless, glazed gaze. This sudden change had manifested itself in his physical idiosyncrasies too; previously he strode about the place with the unquestioned self-assurance and confidence one would expect of the aristocracy. Now he shuffled about nervously, unsure of every step, his eyes darting about as if expecting danger at every turn. It had been just over a month since the death of both of his parents. The inquest had decided that his father, who had been driving the Aston Martin DB-5 that he so often took out for a spin of an evening as a precursor to a hearty supper, had suffered a massive heart attack at the wheel, causing him to career off the road and into a tree, killing he and his wife immediately. Initially, the shock had cushioned the blow of the news, creating an impermeable membrane over his emotions which prevented them spilling forth. For a week or so, it was as if nothing had happened. He was able to continue life almost as normal, continuing the hectic schedule of his wrestling profession without thought. However, day by day the security of denial had been eroded; every time he found himself alone at night with precious few thoughts except for the yawning chasm now a part of his life, another chink in the armour of his refusal to confront this loss was exposed. His substance habits had previously been rigorously self-surveyed. He considered his intermittent indulgences in drugs and alcohol to be a frivolity that was justified both by his social status (the latest generation of an aristocratic line that stretched back for centuries of English history, he was hardly a dregs-of-society junkie who contributed nothing) and by the strict self-control he was able to exercise. However, he was beginning to spiral out of any and all control. Money was no issue to such an opulent young man, but his health and sanity were beginning to suffer. Any moment he found himself alone was rapidly devoted to his controversial form of self-medication. When he wasn't travelling or wrestling, he could undoubtedly be found rolling himself a joint, lining his stomach with scotch, or, in the evening, ensconsed in the bathroom of a nightclub, cutting lines of MDMA and 2C-B to insufflate or rolling the mixture in a cigarette paper to swallow, so that his mind would be distracted by the desire to dance and the strange, fluid distortions affecting his vision rather than the reality of his situation. Amongst all this, however, he managed to stay in shape, the intensity and focus of gym work representing the only sensible diversion of his routine. Besides his rapidly increasing substance abuse, the death of his parents was beginning to inflict serious emotional and psychological damage. Stripped of his mother and father, Thom was swiftly losing sense of his own identity. Now the only living LeStrange, he felt no solidarity or affinity with anyone; he was alone, the only one of his kind. At night, when he lay awake, he often cursed the beating of his heart, which transported blood which he now shared with nobody. In one bizarre incident, Thom had been escorted for a blood bank by armed police, and almost arrested, for requesting a full transfusion with someone else's blood, and refusing to leave the premises until his request had been granted. The papers had jumped all over the story the following morning. LeStrange ambled across the parking lot, towards an elegant black Bentley parked nearby. Inside sat his chauffeur, waiting patiently at the wheel, the radio murmuring in the background, the engine purring in neutral. Spotting the vehicle, Thom nodded to himself and muttered something unintelligble, and began to walk quicker, although in a far more ungainly fashion. As he reached for the door-handle, a figure stepped out from the gloom off to the left. As he came into the garish bright light of the halogen streetlight, Thom was able to cast his tired eyes over the man who had surprised him. If he had to guess, Thom would have placed his age as late forties, perhaps early fifties, largely due to the paunch of his stomach which was beginning to hang over his belt - his metabolism was clearly beginning to relent and slow down. He wore an all-black suit, complete with a wide-brimmed black fedora hat which cast shadows over his features and head his face from view. Had he been endowed with the swift mental faculties he was renowned for before his degeneration, Thom would have found his appearance somewhat absurd. Instead, he barely managed a shrug. "Mr. LeStrange, it's a pleasure to make your acquaintance." Thom raised an eyebrow, simultaneously intrigued and indifferent, his curiosity just about sustaining his interest. "I heard the news about your parents. May I offer my sincerest condolences." Thom's eyes narrowed, irked by the mention of the tragedy he was still fighting desperately to ignore. After several moments, he relented, and his shoulders sunk. "If I may be so bold as to be honest with you, you look a mess." This bold statement was met with nothing but silence from the Englishman. He raised an eyebrow, encouraging the stranger to elaborate. "You appear to have given up on yourself, to have abandoned your previous drive and determination. I'm sure there were many who felt your self-confidence, one might even say arrogance, was unshakeable. Clearly, they were wrong. I for one never had you pegged as being so easily shaken from bravado. But somehow, despite your worn appearance, I sense there is still some fire burning deep within you." Yet more silence. Thom's interest was beginning to wain. He sighed, his eyes beginning to dart about in a disinterested fashion. "Call it anger, call it denial, call it whatever you will, it's there. My only hope is that you'll harness it. Please, Mr. LeStrange, do not let me down." The stranger extended his hand in stern fashion. Thom eyed it quizzically for a moment, but with a rolle of his eyes relented, and shook the man's hand. As he did so, he felt the sharp edges of a small piece of card spike the soft skin of the palm of his hand. As the stranger withdrew, he looked down, turning the card over to find a few words printed in small type. It read "Phillips Arena Parking Lot; Atlanta, GA, May 31st, 12 pm." Confused, Thom looked up again, to consult the strange man dressed in black as to the meaning of the cryptic message. But the man was gone. *** 31st May 2008 Twelve pm Phillips Arena, Atlanta Thom LeStrange shook his head forcefully, his cheeks smacking from side to side, until his head began to hurt, wondering if when he opened his eyes again the strange scenario that lay before him would have dissolved. When he did, however, it had not. Before him stood a strange assortment of characters; Chris Bagwell, Zacharia Taylor, Brad Jackson, and the strange, middle-aged man dressed in black that seemingly had arranged this rendez-vouz. He considered each man in turn. Bagwell was a man with whom he held an uneasy truce. Previously had had no opinion of the man whatsoever; he was merely another brash buffoon who happened to be an employee of the same company as himself. This had changed to an attitude of superiority after having defeated him in a singles match, but this had rapidly been equalled out by Bagwell's victory over the Englishman in the Rampage Tournament. Now, LeStrange supposed he could considered him somewhat of an ally, in the vaguest sense of the word, in that they had a common enemy - Andy Murray. After all, Thom supposed, my enemy's enemy is my friend. Zacharia Taylor was another man for whom Thom had little regard. His opinion towards him was somewhat disparaging, both because he had beaten him to the GCW Television Title, and on account of his bizarre religious fervour. However, neither were of sufficient importance to truly sour his opinion of the chap. Lastly, Brad Jackson. There was considerable animosity between these two bull-headed gentlemen; they had butted heads on several occasions, but their nascent spat had not yet had the opportunity to boil over. Unfinished business, the soundbyte would undoubtedly state. Four men with little interest in each other, and most likely less respect. And yet, there existed an odd sort of kinship between them, in the current circumstance. All of them led blind to this strange location by the man that stood before them, who now spoke. "Gentlemen, tonight you must put your squabbles aside and unite. you have been assembled you here for one specific purpose; Prime wishes you to combat four of its best, four hearty competitors who wish to put your place. If you are men of pride, and I feel confident that you are, you will accept the challenge and defend your honour, and attempt to prove your federation to be the superior." With that, he turned and left. Instantly, there was solidarity amongst the four GCW wrestlers. A synergy surged through the veins of each man, and without the need to speak, without even the need for eye contact, they all felt the obligation, the desire for combat. And a certain Englishman, another distraction had been provided.
