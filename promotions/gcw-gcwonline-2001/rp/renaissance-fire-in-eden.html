Date: various .:: ::: ::. Scenting the Trail Outside Sapporo, Japan Frustration and pinkish-tinged sweat fought a race for ascension on Lia’s forehead. Around her, the forest whispered as the wind swayed the trees back and forth, but even the sibilant speech of the trees, mystic and calming as it would have been under another circumstance, was offering her no clues today. The scent was all around her, that scent of camphor, mixed unpleasantly with the unmistakable scent of decay. It was a disgusting, vile smell, made that why by its intrinsic nastiness as well as the fact that it was plainly not supposed to be here, not in this sylvan wonderland of thick pine trees, tangling together to create a shadowy canopy over the soil. Wearily, she trudged on, more weary of mind than she was of body. It had been wearing on all their nerves lately dealing with this feeling of something hanging over their heads like the blade of a giant guillotine. And after all that feeling, and the positive step of coming out into the woods to deal with whatever it was, she was starting to get frustrated because plainly, whatever was out here didn’t want them to find it. She’d passed this same damn pine tree eight times, she was swearing to herself? When all of a sudden, she realized that the scent had gotten stronger. Her body tensed, and her step slowed as she tried to take care not to stumble in the underbrush. Though it was probably useful to try and prevent detection, if this being was as powerful as everyone seemed to think, there was no use being stupid and crashing around in here like an elephant. Slowly, she approached the little clearing in front of her eyes, the stink becoming ever more prevalent and encompassing. She resisted the urge to gag. Left foot first, she breached its edge, where the trees twined together even more tightly to create a near-black inlet in the middle of the forest. Or perhaps that was just the pervasive feeling that seemed to grip this little cleared island, no more than twenty by ten feet, in the midst of all these trees. As her eyes adjusted to the darkness, she couldn’t help but gag. A feeding frenzy. That looked like what had gone on here- some creature had gone on an unrestrained orgy of killing. Carcasses, some with the hide beginning to fall from the bones, lay scattered all over the place. Scarcely could she have taken a step without falling over one, so she simply froze, standing in the middle of this disgusting makeshift garden where the only flowers stank of rotting flesh. Reeling, she almost didn’t notice the figure crouched in the shadows- almost. But as the curved back, the folded wings, the flowing hair, registered to her senses, she got a sense with it of awful power, power barely restrained. Slowly, with the sight, she registered a growling sound, and began to consider turning and bolting, but this was adversity, and Lia Vlaszy never ran away from it- but then she realized that the growling was her, coming from some deep recess within that registered danger, and responded to it without her consciously having to will herself to do so. And slowly, the figure began to rise. Leathery wings spread from its back, appeared to stretch, and then fold themselves back against the beings back. Just below the hairline of glimmering raven locks edging its forehead, a tiny pair of horns curved upwards. The being was close enough for Lia to discern that it was female, but she could also see that Mifune had been right’this wasn’t Lillith. The ground rustled as the being moved forward, but strangely, Lia found herself rooted to the spot. Her muscles were tense, prepared for a fight; instinct told her that no matter whether this situation seemed threatening or not, she needed to be prepared, lest she find herself missing a head. “Nazdar, little one,” came the voice, smooth as bamboo wood, as the creature approached her. Lia could see behind the creature a forked tail flicking back and forth as if in idle amusement. But she sneered. “Who the hell are you to talk to me like that?” The being drew herself up a little taller, her sparkling green eyes seeming to widen in surprise. “My apologies, that was most rude of me. Please allow me to introduce myself. I am Roshael,” she said, bowing deeply. “Is that supposed to mean something to me?” Lia’s lips still quivered with a snarl. Roshael shrugged. “It is to mean no more nor less than anything else. I’m glad you came. I’d like to speak with you. Will you accompany me to the shrine just ahead?” “Why there?” “Privacy. The trees have ears, you understand. Now that you’ve come all this way and followed all my tracks, I’d like to have a little privacy to talk to you.” Lia stiffened slightly, but found herself nodding in acceptance before she could really consider whether accepting such a proposition was wise or not. This was certainly turning out to be a lot different than she thought it would. .:: ::: ::. The Shrine The lone monk had been dismissed. Now, it was just Lia and Roshael, Lia seated on the windowsill of the room they occupied, and Roshael pacing the floor. Roshael’s eyes caught hers, and she felt a bit unsettled for a moment. “Has Japan treated you well?” Somewhat taken aback by the question, Lia shrugged, questioning the point of the question with a simple gesture. “As well as is to be expected, given that I am only here to wrestle.” “Ah,” said Roshael, offering something of a crooked grin, “But you’ve done a lot more than wrestle since you’ve been here, haven’t you?” Lia nodded mutely, allowing that to be the answer. Roshael didn’t have to be a psychic to sense her stubbornness, and she sighed softly. The sound was not annoyed, but seemed almost to be frustrated. “Why is it that you’re so adversarial towards me?” Lia’s hypnotic green eyes locked with Roshael’s, and she favored the seeming-demoness with a stony glower. “Why have you been stalking us through Japan?” “Stalking is a bit strong of a word, I’ve just been paying attention. And in the end, I only wanted to talk to you.” “So why aren’t you talking then?” Roshael’s face blanched as she gritted her teeth. Obviously, she’d had thoughts that this would go much more smoothly than it had. Instead of getting mad, though, like Lia would have expected her to, she spread her palms in a gesture somewhat resembling passivity. “Alright, then,” she said, sighing, “What is it that I can do to make you trust me?” “Tell me what your motives are,” Lia said, returning fire almost as soon as Roshael had asked the questions. “Ask me any question that you like.” Lia clenched her jaw, a bead of pink-tinged sweat rolling down her finely wrought forehead. “Why have you been following us around Japan?” Roshael sighed. “I told you, I wanted to talk to you-? “And now the truth?” Lia snapped, without really thinking that with a being like this, she might be doing the wrong thing, attempting to bully her into submission. “I wasn’t finished yet,” Roshael said, taking a step back away from the stationary vampiress, her tail flicking rapid-fire back and forth, “I was going to say that I wanted to talk to you about Dawn. She’s been my real concern, but I think that you have a lot of” potential, I guess is the word? to understand what she is, and what may happen to her in the coming months.” Lia could feel the ire rising in her again, though she could scarcely explain why that was’only that it smacked of something Lillith would say. “What kind of crazy story is this” What are YOU going to tell me about her? Are you going to say that she’s signed her soul over to some other power? That she’s only special because of whatever sort of blood she has running through her veins? That some day, she’s going to betray all of us because it’s just her nature? What are you going to try to do to break us apart now?” “Wha?” Roshael looked genuinely baffled, but Lia, in her anger, took it only as good acting. “Well” I hope Lillith’s compensating you well for going to this much trouble to spread her bullshit.” “Enough.” The word was uttered strongly, and Roshael held up a hand. And for some reason, Lia lost her compulsion to continue rebelling against this as a sinking feeling took hold of her from head to toe. “I’m not going to deny that Lillith is a part of this. But I’m not her agent. Just give me a chance, okay” I want you to know the truth so that you can understand, not so everything that you and Dawn have will fall apart.” The seconds stretched into eternities as the two women stared deeply into one another’s eyes. It could have been no more than ten seconds before Lia answered. “I will listen,” she relented’and felt surprising relief flood through her with that phrase. Perhaps knowing the truth would do all of them some good. “Then look here,” Roshael said, stepping closer to gesture towards her eyes. Lia, her stomach churning with anxiety, looked up into them, a stormy green with tiny threads of glimmering, glittering red flitting in and out of the iris. Before she could so much as offer a barely mumbled protest, she was swept into another world. .:: ::: ::. The Garden Winds swept across open fields, fields full of tall lush grass that swayed and ruffled as though unseen fingers were gently parting a lover’s hair. The scent wafting on the breeze was lush and heady, almost overwhelming at first. Upon looking for its source, Lia could see nothing’until, squinting closer, she saw the tiny purple and fuchsia flowers blooming in between the spires of grass. All across the field, they made miniscule dots of crimson-ish, splashed among the green. And she could easily see that the grasses weren’t the only thing moving. All over the field, creatures appeared to be darting in and out of the long strands of green, rising out of them in increasing fantastical detail. An iridescent wing there, a forked tail flashing here, incandescent skin gleaming momentarily in the sun before disappearing again. Even the sunlight here seemed somehow more golden, more shimmering. In awe, Lia turned her eyes to Roshael. “This is?” But the words wouldn’t form the question that she wanted to ask. “Look behind you,” Roshael said simply. And so she did, turning to see almost directly at her shoulder a gray stone wall, walled up so high that she could hardly see the end of it. Though that, at first, meant nothing, slowly, memory flooded back to her’the Garden that the Italian Gentleman had brought her to after she had rejected Sammael. Only now she was on the outside of it? “Now you’re beginning to understand,” Roshael noted, rather simply’but Lia had no dry, smart-aleck answer for her. “What is this place?” “I suppose that the easiest thing I can think of as a descriptor is “Outside Creation.” It’s not in there,” she paused to jerk her thumb back at the Garden, with a roll of the eyes, “Because that’s where the favored of God are kept. Out here is” well, it’s whatever you’d call the rest of us. Perhaps the mistakes that went awry.” And finally, a little bit of the old Lia came back, as she smirked rather wryly. “I thought God didn’t make mistakes.” “Don’t believe everything that you hear” We’ve been here longer than they have, only we get run over by their Good Book because they’d rather just imagine they’re the only ones here.” “Who’s we?” Roshael took a step away from her, beginning to walk through the field’but Lia didn’t follow. Patiently, a hand found its way into the small of Lia’s back, pushing her gently, as though Roshael was used to guiding children. Lia stumbled forward slightly before gaining her footing with a look down at the ground’she’d been so focused on the bizarre and fantastic scenery spilling before her that she hadn’t paid attention to anything involving her own physical state. “We is a broad term that I’m using to describe a lot of different creatures” but that’s not telling you anything that you’re not seeing with your own eyes. Out here, I guess you’d say there’s an assortment of all the various little devils and beings and sprites and fairies.. whatever name you want to use for them, really. The humans write in their mythology about them, but what they’ve forgotten in this so-called “Age of Reason” is that maybe their mythology isn’t as fake as they think.” To Lia’s right, a pair of big eyes peered at her from between the blades of grass. Big, dark eyes, the kind that looked like Cyrus, only the skin around them was a pale pinkish tinge instead of blue. Reflexively, she shied away, which caused Roshael to smile. “Nothing here can hurt you” nor would they if they could. In addition to this place not having the capability for violence, no one would really want to hurt you.” Lia didn’t respond to that, even though she probably would have found it quite the odd distinction had she noticed it. But her eyes were catching on movement here and there in this wild plain. Little fey eyes and wings, forked tails flipping to and fro as tiny devils skulked and hunted and pounced, lupine barks and yips as little pups played with one another in the clearing. “Why are they all babies?” Roshael smiled, a smile that seemed rather proud to Lia, but one that was backlit with a soft sort of motherly air. “I suppose you could say this is where their souls go” when they die, when astral travel is involved? or even just when things get too hard on earth-they come to us in dreams, and seeing THEIR Garden helps them forget their pain? but nevermind the philosophy, we can talk about that later. Come with me, there’s something I want to show you.” Roshael picked up her pace, and Lia was practically dragged along with her, even though by this point, she was going willingly. And she felt like Roshael was going to drag her for miles as they passed group after group of creatures that no rational man would have believed to exist outside the pages of a fairytale. On and on until they reached a fairly secluded spot in the field, so far away from the Garden that Lia could scarcely see the walls off in the distance. “This is my place,” Roshael said, practically beaming with pride, “Where my little ones grow.” Somewhat confused by the idea, still, Lia cast her gaze slowly around’and was a little surprised. The creatures that lounged on the knolls and hillocks were anything but childish, like the ones she had seen closer to the Garden’s walls. These were beautiful creatures, but not so much beautiful as they were horribly seductive. And it seemed like all of their eyes were on her-could she have run to each of them and spent a few wild moments in their arms, she would have, but for some strange reason, she could discern that this was a most unnatural pull they were exerting on her. “I” what is this?” Suddenly, she felt like she was in the midst of her own personal Gethsemane’only this was a garden of flesh rather than a garden of pain. “I guess you could say my personal project has been the women. The succubi, you might call them.” “Succubi?” Breaking free from Roshael’s grip, Lia walked forward, ignoring all the hypnotizing gazes that fixed on her as she walked. She was caught by one creature, one beautiful creature who was now standing, beckoning to her. Long blonde hair whipped in the wind, back and forth in a haphazard way’and from it, the intoxicating scent of flowers. Jade eyes fixed on Lia’s, and pretty coral lips curled into a welcoming, but feline smirk. Her fingers beckoned, and Lia followed, approaching the being with a hand stretched to meet hers. The woman stretched a hand out to meet Lia’s fingertips, and Lia could practically feel the heat of her skin? Until Roshael grabbed her shoulder, and pulled her back, with an arch little smile on her lips. “I think she likes you” but unfortunately, we don’t have time for that diversion.” “She looks like?” Lia could hardly tear herself away. “Dawn, I know.” But at the sound of that name, Lia’s attention suddenly snapped back to Roshael, whose tail was switching idly back and forth. She sighed. “Yes, I tried to break the proverbial mold with Dawn, as though there IS a mold for such creatures. For a long time, I thought I’d simply outdone myself, but I’m at no place in my existence to be so vain” a good deal of what makes Dawn wonderful comes from inside herself, regardless of her parentage.” “Her” parentage?” .Roshael nodded, the smile on her face widening. “Yes, I thought you’d figure it out after seeing some of her kind” I’m Dawn’s mother.” The shock on Lia’s face seemed to be all-encompassing. A span of time passed where there were no words, no movements from her camp. And then, the unexpected. “That’s ridiculous. I don’t believe you!” It was as though she had broken a spell woven to keep the world beautiful. The vision in front of her wavered, turned into water, it seemed, and then slowly began to fade from her eyes. ?” impossible?” The indignance in her voice had not faded, even though the scene was beginning to fade out, block by block, like static was consuming the soul of Creation. “Dear” this is definitely not how I wanted this to go?” Roshael’s voice faded into nothingness. .:: ::: ::. The Shrine Outside Sapporo, Japan As Lia shook her head, and snapped out of what seemed like a hellish and ridiculous vision meant only to anger her, she had a glower ready for the being still standing across from her. “What are you trying to play at, Roshael?” Seeming crestfallen, Roshael looked away. “I only wanted you to know the truth about everything. You’ve all been running around with such half-truths in your head because of what Lillith’s told Dawn. Can’t you accept the full truth from me?” Lia shook her head. “No! How can I accept any of this as truth when you’re all just all of a sudden popping into Dawn’s life to give her this great revelation that’s done nothing but make her miserable. Are you aware that before any of this mess with GCW even started, she didn’t want to try to have her friends because Lillith had already told her she’d betray them, no matter what her own wants were?” Soberly, Roshael nodded, seriousness replacing the emotional sadness that had been in her eyes moments before. “Yes, I am aware of that. Lillith is?” she paused to sigh, “Lillith is very much her own woman. She’s very aware of the way that she wants things to go” and most of the time, very aware of the buttons that she has to press to get there.” “So why the hell does she want to split us apart?” Roshael smiled slightly. “Well.. you were getting rather close to one of her most important allies, if you think about it. Perhaps it’s that she thought you’d compromise the war by getting involved” perhaps she wasn’t sure what side you’d take? or maybe she was just jealous.” Lia gritted her teeth. “Of Soma?” “Of the same,” Roshael rejoined, “Lillith wants to have absolute control over all her pawns when things line up” you weren’t part of that equation. It’s even more complicated now?” “How so?” And suddenly, it was as though the conversation came to a screeching halt. Roshael’s eyes shifted slightly to the wary. “I” don’t think I can tell you that.” “Why not?” And then, as though emboldened by the situation, Lia stabbed through with another question. “And why was I outside of the Garden when after I came back from Sammael’s bachelor pad, I was INSIDE it.” At that, Roshael raised an eyebrow. “Impossible” but our own perceptions can sometimes do a lot to alter what we see in front of us. Perhaps if you felt, on some level, like your journey through hell and your rejection of Sammael had absolved you of your sins, you might have pictured yourself back in the garden from whence you came.” “Why isn’t it possible?” Roshael ran her fingers through her hair, looking away from Lia for a moment. “Because you can’t ever go back there. Not once you were changed into one of the Kindred, and now, you’re doubly tainted.” Something of inexorable sadness began to descend on Lia as she thought of the Garden, with its lush flowers, and its peaceful solitude, and the chirping of birds like a clarion call of purity. And it was all gone, just because someone who randomly appeared to her out in the woods told her that it was impossible to go back? “What if I want to go back?” “Think about that. Would you really want to go back now that you’ve had a taste of what is beyond the pale” Your beauty, your strength, your time-weathered intelligence, your power? all gone in a flick of the wrist. You, as you are now, have more than you ever did.” Roshael paused, as though unsure of whether or not to go on. But evidently, discretion was not the better part of valor today. “You’re not just “a vampire” anymore, Lia. You ceased to be that the moment you drank Sammael’s blood and gave yourself to him as a student, however briefly. Now that you know the truth about who he really is, then you should know the truth about what you really are. You? and the other people who’ve drank your blood, that is. Perhaps that will give you something to think on.” Lia’s eyes narrowed. “What the hell is that supposed to mean?” No answer from Roshael. “Come on, you’re going to fill me full of THAT crap and then not even tell me what you’re trying to get me to do” You’re going to bait me and walk away?” Roshael shook her head solemnly. “I’ve said enough.” She turned gracefully, and started towards the door of the shrine’but Lia was never one to know when to drop a topic. “What is your affiliation with Lillith” Are you an ally? And if not, how do I know I can trust any thing you’ve said here today?” Roshael didn’t stop to answer her, but as she ducked through the door, Lia could hear the strains of one last sentence. “Lillith is the least of your concerns?” And as she slipped through the door without any more explanation than that, Lia suddenly found herself sitting cross-legged on the ground, palms facedown in the slightly moist dirt. The shrine, and everything that had come with it, was gone. .:: ::: ::. Toshiro Mifune’s School Sapporo, Japan Breathlessly, Lia pushed open the large front door of Toshiro Mifune’s wrestling school, which really had become her home away from home in the last month or so. Although now, it was significantly less comforting with so much weighing on her mind. “Lia! You’re back!” The embrace that she received head-on was practically a pounce. She would have known even without the vocal greeting that it was Dawn just from that. As her arms wrapped reflexively around Dawn’s slim torso, Lia suddenly realized just how exhausted she was. As Dawn pulled away, her face fell slightly. ?” Are you alright? What was out there? Gods, I’m so glad you’re okay. Virgil had me worried sick when he told me you’d gone to meet her.” “It wasn’t a problem.” “Wasn’t a problem” Lillith’s tried to kill you the past few times the two of you have met, how was it just not a problem? I swear,” Dawn said, shaking her head, “your face could be on fire, and you’d say it wasn’t a problem, and just go douse it in a bucket of water.” “I wouldn’t go that far.” A small grin had crept over Lia’s face. “Is Virgil around?” Dawn nodded. “You go find him” I’m going to ask Mifune Sensei if we can use his balcony for a meeting.” She turned and started trudging in that direction, leaving a more than confused Dawn in her wake. ??Lia?” Lia turned and looked over her shoulder. “What about Vivica?” Lia shook her head. “This is more of a closed meeting. Vivica doesn’t need to get mixed up in this.” .:: ::: ::. Mifune’s Balcony ??so it wasn’t Lillith?” Virgil seemed somewhat lazily attentive as he asked the question’like he’d been awakened from a nap, but Lia knew better than to believe that. He’d probably been on pins and needles ever since he’d figured out she was gone, and she didn’t suppose she much blamed him. “Roshael. Dawn’s” mother.” Dawn made a slightly strange face. “I don’t know if?” She trailed off, seeming to be more than a little bit uncomfortable with the notion. Lia nodded. “Yeah” it gets better than that. I guess I never really thought about this before, but Roshael brought it up-? “How do you know you can trust her?” Virgil cut in, gesturing a hand with much less ambivalence than he had displayed before, “What is the point in even discussing all this nonsense if we don’t even know whether or not we’re being played for fools again?” Lia smiled, an odd reaction to such a conversation-killing question. But by now, she’d had ample time to calm herself, and start coming up with a few ideas about it on her own. “I’ve thought of that. Perhaps we are, but she brings up a good point. May I?” Virgil nodded his assent. “There are plenty of creatures that are outside the realm of human. That’s well established. But what if two of those creatures were to sort of combine their powers” Say? combining a human and a succubus? or vampire blood mixed with the blood of something like? a nephilim?” There was a distinct pregnant pause. Looks were shared all around the low table on the balcony. “So you’re saying?” Virgil began. “That nobody can be pinned down anymore. None of us are what we were expected to be by those who’ve tried to use us as pawns,” Dawn finished. For a brief moment, Lia looked almost surprised that such an insight had spilled out unbidden from a woman who seemed to have much else on her mind to worry about at the moment. But it wasn’t at all surprising, given that Dawn had much more going on under the surface than was immediately evident by a cursory glance. “That’s exactly the point.” A brief but growing smile flashed across Lia’s face. “We’ve reached the turning point. It doesn’t matter what anybody says, and what matters the least is the perception of who or what each of us is supposed to be. We’ve blown all the naysayers and categorizers out of the water over and over again” and now we’re at a point where beings who shouldn’t exist? do. And other beings defy natural law to relate to us in a way that is exactly the opposite of what you’d expect’if Roshael hasn’t tried to destroy anyone yet, then that means they’re expecting us to all be stupid, still. To not realize what we can do if we combine our efforts.” She glanced between her companions again. “This is the beginning for us. A new life, a chance at something we never imagined. We play up to the world’give them that contentious, dissatisfied, disorganized face that they want to see from us, make them think we’re easy prey. But we’ll be giving them the lie the whole time. We play it this way, and no one can stop us” not even Lillith.” That grin was turning into something almost terrible in its finality. An era had ended for them’an era of being scared, not knowing what to do, and thinking that around every turn was a monster waiting to eat them whole. Now they were playing for keeps.
