Orchestra of Wolves, Part 5: Wither, Blister, Burn, and Peel

26 July 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 26th July A salient gust of Nebraskan wind swept the Dust Bowl's tawny sand through the churchyard's dying grass and down to the the hole in which her brother lay. Grains cascaded from the edge like a dry dirt waterfall, scattering across the varnished mahogany casket with a gentle rattle as Sean Henry Jarosz's family stood glum and mournful. All around her people were weeping, and she choked on her own jagged breath at the sight of her sickly father raising an old handkerchief to dry his weary eyes. She could sob no more; her ducts had run dry within the church's granite walls, and now all her round hazel eyes could do was swell and puff. Stood beneath a decaying tree, shielded from the unrepentant afternoon son, the pastor pulled his holy book from his black-robed body and began to utter words of absolute finality. He was a man of wispy white hair and weathered skin dotted with liver spots, shiny and uneven like a tightly packed ski slope. A frail man living on the wrong side of sixty years, sending away someone barely half his age to the world beyond. He spoke loudly with authoritative compassion, yet she heard not a word. Too transfixing was the sight of her brother's modest sepulcher, too dreadfully enrapturing was the knowledge that she'd never see it's occupant again. Stood close to her surviving elder brother, head bowed, hands by her sides. Saw the rolling plains beyond the church, passed the town. Realised how exposed she felt. Encircled by those she loved, yet never more detached and cut-off from everything else. She heard her name mentioned in the pastor's final tributes and closed her eyes. The church bell's dull ring resonated across town as a solitary black crowd crawled across the sky, hanging over the yard like a graven spirit and time seemed to pass glacially. She knew the ceremony would be arduous, but the burden had become almost Herculian. And there she was, alone. But she wasn't. She barely felt her partner's warm paw as it slipped it her own, but when she turned and gazed into eyes she hadn't beheld in over a fortnight she felt a suddenly warm glow rising from the pit of her belly. “Sorry I couldn't be here earlier,” he said softly, muted. Placed a hand behind her head and gently pushed it against his shoulder. Parted his lips to speak again, but she interrupted with a “shhhhh”. It didn't matter what he would say, didn't matter that he'd spurned his grieving wife-to-be for Just Wrestling's latest tour. All that was important was that he was there, and that she at last felt stronger than the death that surrounded her. * * * * * They peered at a man who'd soon be their relative through a dirty windowpane. Kayla and Roland; brother and sister, stood side-by-side as the wake rumbled on behind them. “Good job he turned-up this time,” Roland began in a soft midwestern drawl, “else him and I would've had to have words.” “Don't be stupid, Rolly,” Kayla said. “You know how demanding his schedule can get. He'd have been here sooner if he didn't have to go out and wrestle twice a week...” “Time-consuming his job may be, but that ain't no reason to be leaving you hanging, especially with Sean passing and all...” Not wanting to retread his brother's death, Roland found his voice trailing-off. “Don't wanna trash the man you're marrying here, but dude's gotta get his priorities right.£ “But he's doing really well,” she said, turning to face her brother. “When you're in as deep as he is, you can't just come and go as your please, whatever the circumstances.” “Surely even a pro-wrestler gets compassionate leave?” quizzed Roland. For a man of his age, his complexion was very rough and ragged. Tarnished by a lifetime of hard graft, Roland Jarosz's image was that of a man fifteen years his senior, although he was not unsightly by any means. A set of beady blue eyes penetrated from below his heavy brother, and his thick, brown hair always looked well-maintained and presentable. “If I was in your sho-” “Just stop, Roland. Please,” she said. “We buried our brother today, remember? Roland huffed, watching Andy as he paced back and forth outside, talking calmly into his cellphone. He'd always been protective of his younger sister and, as likeable as he found the Scot, found the issue hard to shake. “Alright, I'm sorry. Promise me you'll let me know if he ever does anything to hurt you, though.” Kayla managed the slightest of smiles. “I'm not twelve years old any more, you know. Me and Andy are solid as a rock, anyway.” “Hope so, 'cause you can't be marrying a guy who spends half his time on the other side of the country if you ain't completely head over heels with one another.” “Well, we are,” Kayla said. “I think we are, at least.” Roland caught the scent of doubt in her voice like a bloodhound. “What do you mean 'think'?” “Oh, it's nothing,” she waved her hand, dismissing his notion. “Just gets tough sometimes, looking after that big empty house on my own, taking care of his kid. Gets rough every now and then, yeah, but when he's there it's like living on cloud nine...” “I can tell from your tone that that's not all, Kay.” She sighed, unable to shake her brother's probing. “Maybe not, but I don't wanna talk about it right now. Okay?” As his sister attacked Roland with the slightest of scowls, a tall, dominating presence cut through the mourning masses behind him and made his way across to the window. Sliding his cell phone into the pocket of his suit jacket, Andy Murray paced slowly towards the siblings, awkwardness coursing through his body as the room's attention was inevitably drawn to the 6'7” wrestler. “Oh, uhhh. Hey Andy,” nodded Roland, noting the Scot's approach, knowing that there was no chance Andy had heard his despondent words, but unable to lose the thought that he had. “Roland,” Andy said, returning the farmhand's nod as he met the siblings. “Holding up okay?” “Well it ain't easy; just trying to keep it together for Pop and Wes' sake,” he said, signalling towards the other side of the room where his youngest brother sat crying among those who seek to comfort him. “Least he died doing something he believed in, I guess.” “Still, it's never easy. Is it?” “No,” Roland shook his head. “No it isn't.” And without a farewell or a goodbye, Roland moved himself away from the engaged couple and moved back towards the black-clad assembly of relatives and associates. Andy watched him for a moment, before looking down upon she he would soon be his bride. “Seems to be doing okay.” “He is. He's strong.” “Gotta be when things like this happen. Remember when my Dad died last year, I-...” Murray stopped himself. “Damn, there I go talking about myself again.” “No, it's okay,” she said, clutching his wrist. “It's just good to hear the sound of your voice in-person again, not through a telephone line.” “Just wish I could spare more than half-a-day. Shipley's got me stacked; I beat these guys and I'm in-line for a shot at Violence Jack. If I bail on this one he'll have me fighting bums in the car park. Something like that, anyway.” A half-convincing smile spread across her face and she asked herself when he was going to stop talking about wrestling. “Well, you're here. That's the main thing.” “Yeah,” Andy said, pausing. “Felt like I owed it to you. I know I haven't been around lately, and it's killing me that I've not been the shoulder you needed to cry on. Damn tour couldn't have come at a worse time, really, but I'm here now, so if you need to pour it all out, I'm all ears.” “I've done all the mourning I need to do, Andy,” she said, “and I don't wanna do no more.” “Are you sure? 'Cause I'm here fo-” “Yeah, I'm sure.” Kayla nodded, looking him dead in the eye, making sure he knew that she [i]had[/i] done all the mourning she needed to do, and she'd done it without him. * * * * * “You can't stay just a little longer?” she said over the crowd's murmur beyond the door and the shrill chirp of a million crickets rubbing their limbs together. Stood on the porch of the family's farmhouse, enveloped by the clammy night air, she took both hands and clung tightly, although she knew he'd never make Vegas for Sunday if he didn't leave now. “Wish I could, Kay,” he said. “Wish I could.” He knelt and pulled his bag from the dusty wood below his feet. Slung it over his shoulder and cracked a benign smile, praying that Kayla wasn't fishing for another “me versus wrestling” discourse that he didn't need to hear. “Coming straight back on Sunday this time, though. Spend some time with you and the boy.” It pleased her to hear this, even if she wouldn't be ready to leave her family home for Andy's Floridian retreat for a few days yet. Kayla leaned into him and let him kiss her forehead. “Okay.” “I'll phone you as soon as I get to Omaha,” Andy said, pulling away. Kayla felt herself weakening; trembling, almost, as he smiled, turned, and plodded down the steps. The heavy steps of a 275lb man hit the compact Nebraskan dirt with a [i]stomp, stomp, stomp[/i] as he walked towards his car and threw his bag into the back. Before he stepped inside the Benz and drove off into the night, Andy left her with a wave and a smile, and she knew from his face's earnest mask that he'd be true to his words. The car pulled away, it's wheels churning the dust and kicking clouds of it up into the moonlight sky. As Kayla watched two red lights slowly grow smaller and smaller, she heard the door behind her creak open and a set of footsteps gently pad out onto the decking. “There he goes again, huh?” Roland said, taking a slow sip on whatever clear fluid occupied his crystal glass. “Out on the road again.” She turned. Looked up at him. Tried to speak but could find no words to push through her parted lips. Her elder brother's face tightened. “You alright, Kay? Jesus, you're shaking...” She felt a knot tighten in her throat. Her tear ducts began to bulge, and the corners of her mouth tugged downwards with such gravity that the swell was unstoppable. Roland quickly lunged forward, catching her petite frame before it could hit the floor. Guided her towards Dad's rocking chair and sat her down. “Kayla, what's wrong?” Roland said, kneeling down, clutching her by the shoulders. “Come on. Talk to me...” Her eyelids clenched tighter than a vice, sending a single salty tear rolling down her olive cheeks and over her lips. Rubbing a shaky hand softly against her stomach, she tried to think of a way to tell her brother that Andy Murray wasn't the only one with something to feel guilty about as she tried to feel for a kick that wasn't there any more.
