Good God, Bad Karma

10 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 10, 2005 I need music. Something big and deadly and loud and fast to drown out the confusion. I've finally lost my mind, I think, and Trey's come along for the ride. It's all his fault, of course, because he asked me a very stupid question a few days ago, and since I could find out the answer, I was damned well going to. It was about the 'true nature of God,' whatever that means, and he refused to accept my explanation that the entirety of the godly pantheon consisted of a 19-year-old slacker and, well, not much else. Now it's too quiet--I can hear with great detail the scratching of pen on paper, simultaneous a thousand times over, coming from the next room. They haven't noticed us, yet, but I'm not too happy with the idea that they're going to. Trey hangs tight against the wall, looking over to me at routine intervals to see if I'm quite ready to kick the door in and reclaim my fate from the bastards. Okay, okay. There's some backstory here, other than Trey asking about the omnipotent potentate. After the question I'd called my lawyer and former manager, Sobias Torm. He is and, from what I can tell, always has been a very pissed off and very large Samoan man with a serious gambling problem, a fondness for controlled substances almost outmatching mine, and a connection in every smoky rat-infested corner of the world. Bad company that's very good to have around in times of a crisis, even if he's been boning my ex, Danielle, for awhile. We all remember that story, though! No? Oh. Doesn't matter to me. 'What the hell do you need?' He loves me so. 'You still know how to get up with that Moses guy?' 'The crazy preacher? I probably could, if I needed to.' 'Give it a shot, bastard might be useful to me.' 'You coming into town?' 'What? You're probably still plotting on killing me for Trey losing to LeStatt. It'll be years before you see me again, you bastard.' He laughed. 'So, Moses. I'll let you know when I find something out.' I hung up and went back to Trey's questions about the high exalted, or whatever. 'Was he a big dude?' 'Pretty tall. Not what you'd call grandiose though, more of a loom than anything.' 'He tell you his name?' 'God needs a name?' 'Shit. You're right. So, what the hell you plannin' on this?' 'Finding him so you can get answers to your questions.' 'Man, you lost your mind. God ain't wandering around earth and wastin' his time on you or me.' 'Fuck else is he gonna be doing? You can't give him but so much credit--just wait and see. This whole deal of life isn't as sweet as you might expect.' 'Shit, who said life was sweet?' He took another drink from a bottle of Cristal and slinked his arm around the neck of the supermodel next to him. 'Life ain't had much for me but gettin' fucked over, y'know?' 'No, I don't.' 'Damn, you grow up with your mom an' pops?' 'No.' 'That's harsh man. The world's fucked, an' this god kid's gotta have some answers. Don't you want somethin' back from him for your family?' 'You don't even know about my family, so why the question? Hell, for all you know my parents were international agents of espionage, which would keep 'em away from the house enough that I'd say that I didn't grow up with 'em.' 'Your parents were spies?' 'Hell no. Dad was a big tweak dealer--still is--and mom either died in childbirth or ran like hell after it. Fuck if I know.' 'See, man? World's fucked.' 'I think I've had enough time to figure that out on my own. Hell, I've already met the big guy and questioned if there was anything I really wanted. But no, your hopes are always a little too high. God can't be but so good.' 'He's omnipotent.' 'I wouldn't say that. He's smooth, yeah. Maybe. It's sort of confusing.' 'So, when's Sobias callin' back? 'Bout that Moses dude or whatever.' 'Whenever he finds something, or gets off his lazy ass an decides it's worth calling me back.' 'Cool, cool. Who's Moses?' 'Nice to ask after you start calling him by name. He was an old preacher Sobias knew, introduced me to. Involved with this group of people that were trying to hunt down and find the real God, number one force or whatever. Said that the capital 'H' He had to walk the earth, since they'd found Thor in Minnesota and Shiva in Baltimore. Only made sense that the Christian deity would show up too, right? It's a regular god clusterfuck on earth right now, so they're sorting out a lot of confusing shit. I don't think they're going after the same guy I am, but they might know something about him. Makes sense, eh?' 'Not at fuckin' all, man.' 'That's what I thought. Only problem is, the less sense something makes, more likely it is to be true. Like, fuck, did you know I saved George W. Bush's life once, and still voted against him?' 'Man, like I said. You're insane.' 'Yeah. It's beautiful.' My phone rang, so I answered it. Sobias, blabbering on and clearly on his way to half-drunk. 'Bad news, Jordan.' 'What?' 'Moses died; killed in a gang war in the Old City. I always told him he'd be fine until he started picking sides, but...' 'Old Bloods die hard? Yeah. Christ, that's bad. You know anyone else that was involved with that whole wild Yahweh chase?' 'You're getting into that tired shit? Fuck, Jordan, I didn't realize you'd gotten this bad off.' 'Aren't you Catholic?' 'Yeah, but I'm not stupid. I'll say my prayers, tithe my bit, and go to confession whenever I've done enough wrong--I just don't expect a good deal out of it.' 'Back-up plan? You really aren't right, Torm. The hell's the point of believing in that cruel old fool?' 'You're the one trying to find him.' 'Not the one that gave us baby Jesus. The real one, the bastard that's pulling our strings.' 'Into acid again?' 'Like you'd never goddamned believe. So, tell me, you useful or not?' 'Yeah, I know a few people just as insane as Moses. It might take some time.' 'How long?' 'Day or two.' 'Three hours.' He laughed short and deep. 'Fuck you, Jordan. There's a lot of shit to do that takes precedent over you.' 'Fuck the cases, you know where the fun is. It's been a long time since I got in so far over my head legally that I needed you to step up--why not enable my insanity one more time? Just think back to all the good times we had in our day.' 'I'm going to strangle you.' 'So, three hours?' 'Where are you?' 'Birmingham. England.' 'I'll see what I can do.' So, I waited and made small talk with the beautiful Indonesian girl that was next to me for some reason, and drank and smoked and enjoyed the shame of life until someone called back. I assumed it was a local number. 'Yeah?' 'Jordan White?' 'One and only.' 'Good. Sobias called me and said y'd be interested in finding a god or two. What specifically y' want?' 'Not quite sure how to order. Uh, not really a known deity so much as this guy that actually runs the show. Didn't get his name, but he seems to travel a lot--I've seen him around, mostly in the southeast. Of the U.S.' 'I,' she stuttered, 'I don't think I'll be much 'elp to you. Is there anything else?' 'Christ, yeah. Anything's better than nothing, so what's a good deity to find around here?' 'No deities in 'is area so much, though there's a Tallier's hall 'round.' 'What the hell is a Tallier's hall?' 'Where they tally up your worth, your karma debt an' the like.' 'They do that here?' 'Had to do it somewhere.' 'Why not do it wherever the tallies go? El Afterlife, to borrow a Spanish term.' 'Your worth's evened out when y'die. Karma only matters to the livin'.' 'So no reincarnation?' 'Not so much. Seems t'happen sometimes, wanderer spirits.' 'Huh. So, where exactly are they doing this tally thing at?' 'The hall? It's a manor out on the moors. I can lead you, but none've ever dared breach its walls.' '...then how do you know what happens in it?' 'They leave an' freely tell a person their measure. Durin' the day, it's the most lovely spot in the world--the lot of the Talliers sitting outside underneath boughs of roses an' lilacs an' writin' endlessly. A sight to see for those lucky enough to happen upon it, and the only hint of redemption in all the moor.' 'And at night?' 'The most ghastly haunt in all of England. Green to grey without so much as an as-you-may, and all the life dries up and withers away. Howls and screams echo through the forests, an' the only light's of candles that don't flicker even in the beatin' wind.' I thought for a moment. 'How soon can we be there?' 'If you can meet me tomorrow, I can lead you.' We decided on a spot in Birmingham to meet at, since I'd no clue what town she'd tried to give me directions to initially, and I hung up to share the good news with Trey. 'So... we ain't found that God kid, but we are gonna bust in on karma without warning?' 'In so many words, yeah.' 'That don't even make sense, man.' 'I know. It's fucking great.'
