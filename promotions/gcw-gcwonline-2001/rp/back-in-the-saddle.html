Back in the saddle

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: WorldWide 106 The doors part like Red Sea and I enter the 7-11 with one thing on my mind. Tootsie Roll. Making my way to the candy bar aisle I overlook the quality Milk Duds and a Skittles in search of my Tootsie Roll, yet on arrival I find that box is empty and my heart sinks. “Uh” no way!” I cry in disbelief. “What’s wrong?” asks a voice. A kid, around twelve or so, has rounded the corner, he stands not far from me, trying his best to be unimpressed, and I cannot tell why he is trying to cover up that emotion. It does not dawn on me in the slightest why this kid was trying to act cool around me. Dressed in a New York Knickerbockers t-shirt and a pair of track pants, he looks local and close to lower class. His cheap haircut and dirty face says this kid loves being a kid. An unfathomable feeling of awkwardness is apparent in the air as my mind flips through the reason why I was in disarray. I remember the empty box of Tootsie Rolls. “I wanted a Tootsie. They’re all out.” “I got one right here I was gunna buy. You could have it,” says the kid, reaching out with his grubby hand clutching the Holy Grail in his paw. Wave him away, not wanting to take the proverbial candy from a baby. “Nah, I don’t wanna take away your snack, kid-O. Those things are God’s damn gift to taste buds. You have it.” Kid shrugs at me, staring at the bar and then across at the rest of the treats on the shelf before pushing in my face again. “I was kinda tossin” up between this and some Skittles anyways. Go on. Take it,” he insists. “You sure?” “Sure, I’m sure.” My stomach growls as if on cue to urge my hand out to take the bar from the kid who seems to be suppressing a smile. He takes some sour Skittles from the shelf and grins now, as if everything in the world were right. I had my Tootsie Roll and the kid had some Skittles. The smile seemed to hold more in it than making some stranger happy by handing out a Tootsie Roll which he had been craving since ten that morning. “What’re you smirkin” at?” I finally ask. “Nothin”. It’s just?” he trails off, rubbing the toe of his shoe around on the ground and dropping his eyes to watch it makes it’s circles on the linoleum. “Just what?” “You’re a wrestler, aren’t ya” CJ Woods, right?” he says, almost bursting at the seams to mutter those words out. Finally, the downpour of sensibility arose cascaded on me and I remembered that I WAS in fact the wrestler CJ Woods. Flushing with the crimson of embarrassment I found myself toeing the linoleum, watching my own toes circle an intriguing stain upon the floor. “Heh, well, I guess I am. I almost forgot.” “Forgot” Man? I saw you on WorldWide. Watched you beat Shaman. It was SWEET! Four bell claps and then the Woody Woodpecker?!” That was awesome, man. I turned to Chucky-? “Wait. Who’s Chucky?” “My brother. My brother is Chucky. So I says to Chucky, I says, I found my new favourite wrestler and he says I know! He’s fucken swee-? the kid stops, hand bursts to his mouth and covers it in embarrassment. “Oh damn, I cussed.” Cannot help but laugh. It takes the kid by surprise, both my laughter and his language. Embarrassed, he runs a hand through his hair before glancing around fleetingly, as if his mother might bust him. “It’s alright, kid. I won’t tell nobody. Say, what’s ya name?” “Cam. Cameron Disco.” “Cameron Disco, huh” What’ve you got in ya hands there, Cameron?” “Some sour Skittles and a Cherry Coke,” he admits, looking down to reassure himself that he gave the right answer. “Whaddaya say you run along and get me a Cherry Coke and then I shout you you’re treats?” Cam’s eyes lit up like little Timmy in a Christmas Carol. Feet moving before he could he bursts around the corner, almost skittering an old man rounding it to get to the fridge to grab me a Cherry Coke. The old man raises his arms above his head, as if it made him narrower and harder to knock down. “Whoa, feller! Careful where you’re goin”!” he cries after him before turning to me. “Kids these days. Always in a hurry, eh?” A smile politely. His question seemed so rhetorical I didn’t even dare try to ponder an answer of any kind to his question. “You some sort of kiddie fiddler?” Aghast with shock I can feel my jaw slacken and fall open. Eyes widening as the question echoes in my head. “I beg your pardon, old man?” “You heard me. You fuck little boys or somethin?”” I can feel my jaw dropping has recovered as my top teeth dig into my lower lip angrily. An accusatory finger waggles in the old mans face as I feel my feet stepping forward toward him. “You got some fucken nerve.” “Whatchoo buyin” him candy for then?” “I’m bein” nice. Ain’t nobody allowed to be nice these days? Huh? Is it too much to be nice to some kid?” “Buyin” candy for kids you don’t know ain’t what it used to be,” says the old man. “You read the papers.” Nod at the bitter old fool. “Yeah, but I ain’t no priest.” “You leave God out of this!” “Where’s ya fucken God when his servants is fiddlin” with kid’s asses, huh? Get outta here ya fucken mook. I’m bein? nice to one kid is all. And I’ll keep bein? nice coz that’s the kinda guy I am!” The old man lets out a pfffft exhalation of disbelief and shakes his head. “See ya in the newspaper, ya weird-o!” “Yeah. Front fucken cover with a championship belt raised above my damn head, you old fool. Hope you break a hip, ya negative Nancy!” Shuffling away, the old man mutters to himself as Cameron comes back to the candy aisle. He’s armed with two cans of Cherry Coke and a packet of sour Skittles. “I had to get another one for me. I dropped mine.” The desperation of the disappointment in him looms overheard like a storm cloud. “Kids at school call me Clumsy Cam,” he admits with a sigh of malcontent. Frown and shrug, ruffle Cam’s hair and lead him toward the counter. “You tell them kids that if they wanna mess with you that they gotta mess with me, aight?” “Aight, CJ.” Hand over a ten-dollar bill and the man behind the counter hands me back my change. Pocket it and hand Cam his Skittles and Coke, clutching mine under my arm. “Thanks, CJ.” “You’re welcome, Cam. You run along now and be good to your mother,” I tell him. He smiles, dashing out the door and near putting the old man over on his ass again. Like out of a cartoon he turns and shakes his fist into the direction of the fleeting child muttering expletives beneath his breath. Begin to make my way across to the exit when the shop attendant calls out behind me. “Good luck against Face, CJ. Knock him dead, alright?” Frozen in my tracks I couldn’t help but feel a little ignited within in regards to the warmth the general populous had given me for my wrestling efforts in the last five minutes. Turn on my heels and smile at the shop attendant. “I’ll do my best, huh?” “You do that! Knock that stupid mask off his stupid head.” Laugh in disbelief, not at the comments, but at the adulation I was receiving after being on TV for five minutes. It felt good to be back in the saddle again. Felt damn good.
