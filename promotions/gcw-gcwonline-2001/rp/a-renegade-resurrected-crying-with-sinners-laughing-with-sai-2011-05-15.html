A Renegade, Resurrected: Crying with Sinners, Laughing with Saints

15 May 2011

Beneath the clouds, beneath the black, the storms will creep, the rains will seep on buildings felled, through highways cracked and something will awake to sleep. -Jayce (the Jayce) ~<>~<>~<>~ February 5th, 2008 12 days before the Death of Rollins Mercy Hospital, Chicago... There were many parts of Rich Rollins' body that didn't want to cooperate anymore, but thankfully his eyes were not one of them. As he sat in an antiseptic examination room, his perfect eyes allowed him to see every detail of the charts and scans situated before him like a modern art mosaic made up of everything that was wrong with his life. All his bumps, all his bruises, all his mistakes, all splayed out against a back-lit wall. Bits of red spider webbed around the usual places; his neck, lower back, and left knee all bore signs of serious damage. He knew what the doctor was going to say before he said it. "It's not good," said Dr. David Smith, indicating the area around Rollins' knee and neck. "In fact, it's about as bad as you can get without being confined to a chair. On a pain scale from one to ten, how does your leg feel?" Just rotating his knee sent bouts of pain up Rollins' side. "About an eleven. Give or take." Dr. Smith nodded and wrote something on his clipboard. "Your surgery last summer was just a temporary fix. You knew that going in, and the scar tissue didn't heal the way we wanted it to. You know what I'm going to say next, don't you?" He did, but Rollins didn't want to hear it spoken aloud. As if the truth wasn't true if it wasn't given voice, giving it tangible power. But he couldn't run from the truth any longer, and Rollins knew it. "You're gonna tell me I'm not cleared to compete against Ambrosi tomorrow," he offered, hoping that would be enough. Setting his clipboard down with a sigh, Dr. Smith leaned against a counter opposite the Renegade. "Rich, from these charts alone I could not, in good conscience, clear you to wrestle ever again. I'm sorry. It's just too dangerous, and the inflammation is so advanced that I'm shocked you walked in here under your own power." And there it was. Rich Rollins glared at the wall that damned him like a death sentence (something he knew quite a bit about), his chin quivering with a frustration he didn't know how to express. Well that wasn't true; he did know how to express it, but he liked Dr. Smith and didn't feel that hurting the young doctor would make him feel any better. He chose instead to clench his fists until he could feel his fingernails make friends with the second and third layers of his skin. "I can't accept that, doc." "Well you'd better start somehow," Dr. Smith said, making an earnest effort to sound sympathetic. "I've diagnosed athletes before, Rich. It's hard to tell someone that their career is over." "Oh yeah?" Acrid bile filled Rollins' words. "Try being the one told." ~<>~<>~<>~ May 13th, 2011 WorldWide150 Press Conference GCW Headquarters, Columbus, Ohio... The media room was crawling with activity as Rich "The Renegade" Rollins walked in, sparking a frenzy of talking faces and shutter clicks that caught the huge smile on his unshaven face. Reporters jockeyed for his attention, each one trying to talk over the next, none of them succeeding. It was all just as Rollins remembered; goddamn he had missed this. Scanning the crowd, the Renegade tried to find Steven Caldera's face. He was nowhere to be seen, which was surprising. Rollins' announcement last week had stirred a lot of interest in the upcoming 150th episode of WorldWide, and it wasn't like Caldera to be camera shy when big things went down in his backyard. Pushing the thought away, Rich Rollins followed the wall until he came to the head of the curious mob, taking his place behind a podium that had been loaded with more microphones than he cared to count. Behind him hung a promotional banner featuring both his face and Alex Pierce in profile with the number "150" in bold white letters superimposed in the background. Tapping his finger in the middle of the mic bouquet, Rich Rollins grinned. "This shit on?" Laughter. Motioning for his audience to have a seat, the Renegade continued. "All right, the Chief is here. Let's keep this focused on WorldWide one-fifty, understand? I'm not up here to tell my life story, and you assholes probably don't wanna hear it anyway." This was greeted by a few chuckles in the peanut gallery, which pleased Rollins just fine. "Okay, who's first?" A petite brunette from RoughKut.com made her move, shooting her question off immediately. "Would you say you're currently in better or worse shape than when you won GTT5?" A smirk tickled Rollins' upper lip as he moved his left knee into view, smoothly bending the braced appendage to demonstrate its condition. "I'm in the best shape of my life, thanks for asking. I'd just been released from prison before my match against Shanty Jones in the GTT5 finals, so I'd say I'm in a helluva lot better head space now than I was then, you know? Next question." Barry Goldstein practically jumped into his neighbor's lap, flapping his arm like an epileptic break dancer. "Rollins! Current odds put you as a three-to-one underdog in this fight due to your extended... absence from the sport. Are you worried that Alex Pierce might have the edge as far as conditioning is concerned? Also, what is your take on the only female champion in GTT history?" "She's a looker, I'll give her that," Rollins replied with a smug smile. "But she ain't no Renegade. She beat some big players to get that GTT win on her resume, includin' our beloved Commish, Garbage Bag John-boy. Violence Jack ain't no slouch, either. Is she worthy to be called a GTT Champienne? Bet your ass she is. But I'm not GBJ. I ain't Violence Jack, or Terrence Kingsley, or any of the other yokels she steamrolled in that tournament. I'm a different beast entirely, an' you better believe I'm ready to prove it to her next week. Next question." GCW columnist Franklin Faulkner spoke up, his meek voice somehow cutting through the white noise. "Some issues came to light prior to your return at NC-17, including the medical records from your faked autopsy reports... and their similarities to your brother's dental records. When did you last speak with your brother, Jason Rollins?" Fifty people held their collective breath as the question reverberated in the sudden silence. Rollins turned his eyes on Faulkner, causing the man to shrink back into his seat at the quiet fury he found there. Remaining calm, the Renegade replied in a voice that defied his burning blue eyes. "You wrote that article, didn't you Frank?" Barely a squeak, Faulkner replied, "Yes." The knuckles on Rollins' hands turned white as he clenched the sides of the podium. "My relationship with my brother is none, I repeat none of your goddamn motherfucking business. Did you hear what I said about the nature of this Q and A, Frank?" Again, more a breath than a word, "Yes." "Then get the fuck out. Next fuckin' question." ~<>~<>~<>~ February 8th, 2008 9 days before the Death of Rollins Downtown Chicago... The cityscape was a panoramic masterpiece of architectural excellence as seen through the bulbous fish-eyed lens of what locals called "The Bean." In truth, Rollins had always thought it looked more like a massive ass crack than a bean, but nonetheless the name stuck. Towering above Millennium Plaza, the smooth mirrored surface of the odd structure bent and contorted its surroundings like a fun house mirror as visitors walked around and under its seamless exterior. Children touched it and giggled as their faces expanded and the world around them blossomed along the outer shell. From any angle, the city seemed to close in around the viewer as skyscrapers bent and twisted their way to the sky, crossing the Bean like a jagged frown built of brownstone and glass. It was for this reason, this breathtaking effect, that the structure's actual name was Cloud Gate. Nope, Rich Rollins thought as he wove his way through the crowd of tourists. It's definitely an ass crack. The terrace below the Bean swirled with activity and falling snow. A large ice rink sat alongside Michigan Avenue, filled with laughing children and quiet couples enjoying the winter afternoon as they slid around in lazy circles. The Renegade shoved his hands deep into the pockets of his leather jacket as he descended a flight of stone stairs, joining the street-level crowd of skaters and patio tables. Tall lamppost-like heaters kept the outdoor eating space dry this time of year, letting the romantics enjoy the beating heart of the city in comfort. Rollins stopped at the base of the stairs and watched the ice skaters, the lovers, the mothers, the throbbing pulse of his home, until a familiar voice cut through the bleating haze of sirens and nostalgia. "You call this a medium rare lamb chop?" The speaker was insufferably self-entitled and grew more impatient as he spoke. "If only the Lamb Himself could see you now! This is charred like thick bacon, and I refuse to eat swine! My body is a temple, dammit!" The Renegade smiled. He found who he was looking for, all right. The irate diner continued. "You're sorry? Well I should hope so, you sinful spur on Satan's bootstraps! I come here for the service, but it appears that's just too much to ask. Fix this lamb or the RISP will hear about it, so help me Hoyt!" Climbing over the waist-high divider separating the masses from the Park Grill patio, Rich Rollins announced his presence with a slap on the startled man's shoulder. "Still talking about yourself in the third person, I see?" Hoyt Williams jerked around. He looked different since the last time their eyes had locked; gone was the scraggly Moses beard, replaced by a more economic goatee and long, straight blonde hair. He really does look like Jesus, Rollins mused. Hoyt, however, did not share this whimsical amusement. "You!" Hoyt cried. Jumping out of his seat, the Son of Duke knocked his plate off the table, spilling overcooked lamb across the snow. "Stay back, you blasphemous pill popper! I've got a lot of lawyers, and most of them are Jewish!" Rollins had expected this. After all, the last time he and Hoyt had been this close to one another they had ripped apart a PRIME parking lot (and each other) before gate-crashing the Dual Halo. Raising his hands in a call for peace, the Renegade maintained an amiable tone. "Relax, Hoyt. I didn't come here to cause trouble." Hoyt snorted. "Right. Like the viper who didn't mean to cause trouble when he tempted Eve with an apple. I know your game, Rich Rollins. Play it somewhere else." Collapsing into an empty chair with a sigh, Rollins shook his head. "It ain't like that, hoss." "Don't you 'hoss' me, hoss!" Hoyt's nostril's flared. "Do you think I'm some gullible liberal idiot? I won't-" "Hoyt," Rollins snapped, his patience fading. "Cut the crap. I need... I have to talk to you." He hesitated. He didn't know how to say it. His eyes cast down at the table and his cheeks became warmer than they had any right to be in the frigid winter wind. "I'm hurt, man. Pretty bad. Doctor says I'm finished, and I'm inclined to find a second opinion." That took the steam out of Hoyt's tea kettle. A bit of air deflated from the Williams boy's lungs as he regarded Rollins with wary suspicion. "You're hurt? Why should I care?" "I'm not sayin' you should," Rollins countered, bringing his eyes up to meet his Personal Jesus' face. "But you're tight with God, and the last I heard He was supposed to care about everybody. I need help, Hoyt. Please." The last time Rich Rollins had shown this much vulnerability was never, a fact that did not go unappreciated in Hoyt William's head. This uncharacteristic display of humility wasn't an act, Hoyt decided. It really was a cry for help. Straightening his lily white collar, the One True God's Champion felt a strange wave of pity for the broken man before him. Rollins eyes were dark circles of pain, the internal kind that Hoyt knew very well. God dammit. He sighed and pulled up a chair. "Okay hoss. I'm listening." Relief washed away the lines on Rollins' forehead. He offered a grateful nod, happy to see that there was a human being underneath all of that bombastic Williams bluster. Rubbing his palms across the bristles of his face, the Renegade let the tension in his body slip away until he was slumped back in his chair, emotionally and physically drained. They sat in silence, Rollins idly watching the ice skaters while Hoyt watched Rollins. Finally, the Renegade spoke. "They say I'll never wrestle again," he said. A knot of anger nearly closed his windpipe, but Rollins swallowed it. "It's my knee. Caldera wrenched the muscle right off the bone a while back, hung me off a forklift of all fuckin' things, and the cartilage is gone. That's why I quit PRIME." He shook his head, then added, "Well, there's a lot of reasons why I quit PRIME, but the fact was, I couldn't crawl outta bed anymore without feelin' as if my leg was dipped in molten lead. I could barely walk, and I damn sure couldn't wrestle. "So I took some time off. Against my doctor's advice I had them stitch what muscle they could back together, stuck it to the joint with spit an' sheer willpower, and came back home... to GCW." The Renegade's eyes began to glisten; he cleared his throat to hide it. "I've been workin' a few months with this piece of shit knee, hopin' I could just shrug it off, but..." Rollins didn't want to complete that thought, so he changed subjects. "Two days ago, I got beat by Lia Ambrosi. Next week Jay Terror just might finish me off." Not like that was any better. Hoyt was chewing his lower lip, nodding his head with grim sympathy. "That's a pretty rough rock and an even harder hard place," he said. "But I'm not a doctor, Rollins. What do you want me to do about it?" The words were out of the Renegade's mouth before he could stop them. "I need a miracle." He looked up at Hoyt then, his eyes filled with desperate pain. "I need you to ask God to fix me." Silence. "Rollins, I can't-" "Goddammit, don't give me can'ts!" The explosion was so sudden it took even Rollins by surprise. Slowly, calmly, he took a breath and let it out. "I'm sorry. That wasn't directed at you." "Then who was it directed at?" Hoyt asked. "God?" Shame and anger filled Rollins' face. He looked down at his hands and occupied himself with a wadded-up napkin before he could erupt again. Hoyt Williams shook his head, sadness creeping into his eyes. "Rollins... I think you need to come with me." The Renegade's head shot up as Hoyt stood and grabbed the coat off the back of his chair. "Where are you going?" "You know, there are in fact stupid questions, and here's my stupid answer," Hoyt replied, snapping the long coat around his messianic shoulders. "Just shut up and follow me." He then reached into his pocket, pulled out a wad of twenty-dollar bills, and absently threw them on the table. A thought seemed to occur to him which gave Hoyt pause, causing him to retrieve two of the twenties and return them to his wallet. Winding back to the concrete stairs that led up to the heart of Millennium Park, Hoyt Williams said not a word as Rich Rollins fell in line behind him. The people flowing around them began to phase out in Rollins' mind, disappearing like ghosts, becoming nothing more than incorporeal shapes and outlines. All that remained was himself and Hoyt Williams, and in less than a minute they had reached their destination. Hoyt had brought him back up to the Bean. "Do you know why I like coming here?" Hoyt asked as he gazed into the convex reflection of the sculpture. Rollins didn't respond, so Hoyt tried another approach, pointing at their tiny duplicates in the mirror. "What does this look like to you?" Rollins frowned, considering it. "A massive mirror shaped like a baby's butt crack." Hoyt turned his head slowly, fixing the Renegade with an odd look. "You need Jesus, guy. And a shower," he added, crinkling his nose. Shaking his head, the Son of Duke turned his eyes back to the Bean. "To me, it's like a drop of mercury fell from Heaven and froze before it could bounce back. The teardrop of God, reflecting the way the world truly is. It isn't the mirror that's warped, Rich. It's us, it's this city, this country, this planet. We're all flawed when reflected through the face of God. His sadness is our shame." The Renegade looked at the curvy version of himself, the city fading around him. It was just the two of them now, standing in the snow, looking at themselves looking back in an eternal loop of existential observation. Thinking of this big round piece of polished metal as a message from God sent a shiver down Rollins' spine. If this is how God sees us, he thought, then I'm definitely fucked. Hoyt's voice was softer now. All traces of Hoyt the character had slowly slipped away, leaving only Hoyt the man. "Look, I'm a huckster just like you," he said. "I can't perform miracles any more than I could perform one of Beethoven’s symphonies on a banjo with two broken strings. But I can pray. If I can do that, then so can you. I think He just might hear you." Flipping the collar of his coat up around his neck, Hoyt Williams clapped the Renegade on the back. Rollins was absorbed by his own reflections, both the one in front of him and the ones mulling over Hoyt's words in his head. Satisfied, Williams smiled. Rich Rollins never noticed him leave, lost in his own head as he was. When the Renegade finally looked around him, Hoyt was nowhere to be found. A solemn expression settled on his face as he looked to the ground and traced their path back from the stairs. There were only one set of footprints in the snow. ~<>~<>~<>~ May 13th, 2011 GCW Headquarters, Q&A continued... Legendary wrestling blogger Thomas Holzerman spoke up. "How is this match against Desade going to affect your focus concerning Jay Terror?" Taking a moment to tighten the knot on his bandana, Rich Rollins locked his jaw and trained a pair of unwavering pupils on the former (e) fed lifer. "Great question. An' I suppose you want my answer?" "Well, obviously," Holzerman said, grinning broadly. "All right," Rollins replied, finally getting comfortable on his soap box. "Here's what I gotta say about that bandana-stealin' shithead. He is my focus, no question about it. I made this match against Alex for two reasons; one, because the people wanna see it. Two, because I want Jay Terror to watch." A twisted smile formed on the Renegade's face, his tone taunting. "He's been runnin' around so long tryin' to be me, I figured it was time to remind him what the fuck Rich Rollins is all about. My War on Terror ain't even started yet, kiddos. I promise you that. Next question." "I've got a question," a familiar voice cut in. Chairs screeched and people tittered (Teatered?) with excitement as every head turned to stare at the man slouched in the doorway. Crossing his arms in a most irritated way, Steven Caldera let the noise subside as people settled back into their seats. "I know exactly how much damage that knee of yours suffered, Rollins." Much to Caldera's pleasure, the Renegade flinched. "After all, it was me who shattered it and stretched you like a piece of Laffy Taffy. You kicked sand in all our faces when you went to PRIME, Rich. You deserved what happened to you, and you know it." Swallowing the outrage that colored his face, Rich Rollins leaned forward. "I'm sorry Steve, did I miss your dumbshit question in there somewhere, or did you just pop in to stroke yourself in front of all these fine people?" Nobody was laughing anymore. Chairs creaked in the awkward silence. Caldera broke the reprieve with a snort, crossing through the crowd at his own deliberate pace. "Tell me, Renegade." He soaked the word in sarcasm, dragging it through a decade of bitter memories. "How long will it be before you disappear into obscurity once again? Exactly how long before you burn out with another bullshit excuse and expect the fans to accept you with open arms?" His finger found Goldstein without taking his gaze off the Renegade. "Barry, what are the odds that Rich Rollins sticks around past Rampage?" Shrinking from the sudden attention, Barry Goldstein cleared his throat. "C-currently?" His shoulders jerked in an attempted shrug. "Seven-to-one, maybe? I've got a spreadsheet on it somewhe-" "Thank you, Barry." Reaching the stage, Steven Caldera climbed up to Rollins' level and planted himself. Cameras flashed as the Chief of Staff and the President of the company closed in, squaring their shoulders in a showdown of pure stubborn will. They had danced to this tune a thousand times; they knew the choreography backwards, blindfolded and in Chinese, for crissake. Their bad blood predated the very wrestling empire it had helped create, forged in the fires of Meltdown and tempered on a WorldWide stage. This time felt different, though; it felt different, because it was different. This time, Rich Rollins knew, Steven Caldera couldn't do shit except annoy the ever loving piss out of him. And he's doing a damn good job of it, he thought. The Renegade yanked a random microphone from the bundle on the podium and rounded on his President. "How many, Steven?" Confused, Steven Caldera cocked a questioning eye and shook his head. "How many what?" "How many puppets have you used, abused, and thrown away over the years?" Switching the mic into his other hand, the Renegade began counting on his fingers. "Octavian. Stevens. Low – thought I forgot about him, didn't you? Jason Dread. Jordan White. You've been grooming World Champions to do your bidding for ten years, but where are they now? Did they even know, Steve? Did you tell 'em about all the failures that came before, how you tossed 'em aside when they had no further use to you? Toxic. Mike Hardy. LeStatt Knight, for fuck's sake! Where are they now?" "As I seem to recall," Caldera spat back, "even you were on my payroll, Rollins. I thought I had finally gotten through to you, you stupid son of a bitch! The Establishment-" "Is FINISHED!" Rollins roared. Caldera's face turned cherry red, which encouraged the Renegade all the more. "Enough is enough, Caldera. You've monopolized this company with all this Establishment horseshit for ten years too many. It's over. Jay Terror is the last of a dyin' breed-" "And you're already extinct, you're either just too stubborn or too stupid to realize it!" Caldera turned his eyes to the poster on the wall behind them, indicating the intense promotional profile of Alex Pierce. "At least she demonstrates something you seem to be allergic to – loyalty. She cares about people. You don't care about anyone or anything that doesn't immediately benefit you, Rich Rollins. You-" The shot came so fast that Steven Caldera wouldn't know what hit him until waking up on a sofa bench in the hallway ten minutes later, fawned over by a gaggle of on-site medical geese. Standing over the unconscious President, the Renegade kissed the "brass" knuckles on his fist, admiring the bright red letters now branded across Caldera's forehead. The angry bruise spelling "PWN" flared like a neon sign on Caldera's skin, courtesy of the golden knuckles made from the melted remains of Angelo Deville's 2005 PTC Wrestler of the Year trophy. "That was for John Reed, jackass." Turning back to a shocked-silent crowd, Rich Rollins put on a smile as if nothing at all had just occurred. "Next question?" ~<>~<>~<>~ Feburary 9th, 2008 8 days before the Death of Rollins JFK International Airport, New York City... He knew what had to be done, he just didn't know how to go about doing it. The encounter with Hoyt Williams the day before had left a decidedly bad taste in Rich Rollins' mouth. Questions bounced around inside his skull, and none of the ham-fisted advice offered by the self-proclaimed God's Champion had provided any answers. Self reflection and prayer? He might as well have told the Renegade to count all the rocks in Whales for all the good that Bible-thumping bullshit would do him. Happy thoughts did not repair broken tissue. It didn't change his situation, and it damn sure didn't make simple things like walking to the bathroom any less excruciating. The Renegade stepped outside of Terminal 8 and scanned the crowd for a taxi. Carrying only his shoulder-mounted duffel and a whole lot of bad attitude, Rollins wove through the noisy foot traffic with a purpose, pushing his way past anyone between himself and a means to get to where he was going. It had been months since the two had spoken, but Rollins knew that the man he came to see could help him. Jason Snow was a lot of things. Arrogant prick, dangerous ally, deadly enemy; all true, and the Renegade wasn't about to expect a warm welcome after his falling out with Fuck You. But Rich Rollins didn't have a choice anymore. If what he heard was true, there was a man out there who felt no pain. There was a man who knew a secret, a way to cope with the daunting trials of the industry and swat weakness away like an annoying housefly. As the only person to successfully defeat this man, Jason Snow had to know where Rollins could find him. Cutting the winter air with a sharp whistle, the Renegade caught the attention of an empty cab, climbed in, and flashed a hundred dollar bill at the driver. Barking an address and a desire to get there yesterday, Rich Rollins knew his time was running out. He had to find the man they called Iblis, and he had to find him now. ~Fin~ (Note: The use of Hoyt Williams was given the =w= seal of approval by Al Larcher himself.
