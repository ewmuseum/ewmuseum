A War Against All

29 August 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/29/10 One Heidi Rosenberg studied her bare, damp face through the steamed-up bathroom mirror. The dense, wavy mane of strawberry-blonde hair that usually bordered her face was heavy and sodden, imprisoned within the white bath sheet woven around her head. A naked face, coupled with nervous insecurity, made her eyes appear uneven; to her, at least. “Focus on the natural light,” Heidi whispered to herself, dictating her own mental notes. Her tone was tinged with fleeting hope quickly drowned by doubt‘s cemented shoes. “Light… good. Natural light and good storage. Accentuate the positives. The previous owner… moved to a retirement village? No. No. Went overseas for work. Yes. England. London. They most certainly were not found with rats gnawing on them and a needle hanging out of their arm.” Her cheeks were red and blotchy from the blisteringly hot shower she had subjected herself to. Or they may have been a remnant from the bottle of pinot grigio she’d sank the night before. The prospect of the morning had required the kind of encouragement that came with a percentage proof. “Please, Lord, just don’t let there be gunshots,” Heidi prayed, drawing a smile over her lips through the mirror‘s steam. “Not like last time.” Two Even the wrought iron stomach of Detective Niall Bonner creaked queasily as the sight violated his eyes. He winced; crows feet stamping themselves into his creased features. Somewhere, beneath the swollen, bruised and bloody death-mask was the face of a 21 year old girl. Not a woman; a girl. A woman was someone who had lived… and who wasn‘t 20 weeks pregnant, but not showing between her boney hips. “Fucking piece of shit still had her teeth stuck in his knuckles,” Detective Patrick Reeve announced with despairing bewilderment. He had long since stopped viewing the detritus of society as human beings; they were little more than depressing clips on animals do the most savage things. “Had her fucking name tattooed on ‘em, you believe? Motherfucker beat her so damn savage even God must’ve looked away.” “The Almighty don’t see down these streets,” Niall muttered. He wondered to himself how many times she‘d been wheeled into the E.R. with broken bones, weeping wounds and deathly-dark purple bruising only to be spat back out onto the street when she was too terrified to give a name and press charges against the man peering through the blinds at her. “He ever did, we better prepare our asses for judgment day soon as he managed to look away.” Either side of the late April Greer they stood, toes treading the tide of crimson which had spilled from her beaten, broken corpse. Tomorrow’s newspapers would relegate her violent death below concerns over chewing gum now being cleaned from the city’s park benches monthly, as opposed to fortnightly. “Apartments either side are both vacant,” Reeve reported. “Not exactly prime real estate no more. Floor below was in; didn’t hear anything. Didn’t hear me knocking until my knuckles were damn raw, either. Looked damn-near possessed… gotta be to live round here. Scar down the side of their face seemed pretty fresh. Dragged our guy hissin’ and spittin’ past ‘em in chains and didn’t even get a flinch, so looks like he kept his fists faithful to the wife.” “Same story as ever, Pat,” Bonner wearily sighed, finally peeling his mournful eyes from April’s swollen, mottled face. “Don’t expect no different; not anymore. Invisible to everyone but the guy pummeling on her. Silent to everyone but the guy making her scream for her life. One human being to give a shit about her… that’s all it’d take for her to be alive right now.” “Best thing they can do is pull down every brick in a five block radius,” snorted Patrick Reeve. “Make her death fuckin’ count for something. Otherwise, hundred bucks says we’ll be back up those stairs this year. Sign a lease in this neighborhood and it might as well be your death warrant.” Three As the door swung open, its rusted hinges let out an ominous shriek. “So…” Beside you, a fidgeting Heidi Rosenberg looked as if she’d just pulled the pin out of a hand grenade. You’d taken an instant dislike to Heidi, with her… not energy, but something a little - a lot - more grating. She was dishonestly perky, and you could picture her running through a pre-viewing pep-talk into her bathroom mirror every morning. She needed a transfusion of decaffeinated blood. She half looked you in the eye, half looked you in the scar. “… let’s go in, shall we?” What you were looking for in an apartment, beside location, were three things; walls, a floor and a ceiling. What you walked in on met those criteria; barely. The walls had peeled back to show five or six different colors where patches of festering damp had seeped from the walls. The bare floorboards were splintering and sprouted mangled, rusting nails. The ceiling was more cracked than not, and had been stained runny-shit-beige by cigarette smoke. A breeze whistled through the window frame where the wood had split and concrete crumbled. “It’s not much to look at,” Heidi conceded. You waited for her to tack on a forced positive; she was struggling. If you heard the words blank or canvas, you’d struggle not to punch her on her pinched little nose. “But it’s a blank canvas.” You fought your urges and strode to the window, nails pricking at your soles. The view was bleak and monochrome; all pale, characterless concrete broken only by the ferric red of old fencing and the flushed brown of withering, bare twigs. Twenty blocks away, past the barrier of urban wasteland, was what little family you had left. Social deprivation and a spiraling crime-rate made for an effective two-way mirror through which you could watch their lives. “Now, this neighborhood hasn’t had the best press,” she spewed in a limp warble. “But, regeneration grants are being approved at City Hall which wil-” “The location is perfect,” you interrupted, having tired of the whimpering, apologetic tone her voice was blighted with. “Not too close, and just far enough away.” “From?” Heidi pried, obeying her natural instinct to prod and poke for every last detail she could. Her wide eyes retreated back behind their lilac-shadowed lids at the sight of your dead-eyed rebuff. “Oh, I’m sorry, I just… it’s a habit. Let me show you th-” “I’ll take it,” you bit, sharply. Away from the window you walked, past Heidi and towards the door. “Someone from my employer’s staff will be in contact later today about the paperwork.” “Um, okay, yes, of course,” Heidi rambled, taken aback. “Aren’t you the lucky one; wish I had a boss who would cover my rent. What is it that you do?” Four This was what you did. “001101000,” Christian Zenith commanded. “010111000001011.” Your conversations with your employer were devoid of pleasantries. It was irrelevant how you were feeling, or if you were feeling (although the latter was preferable). They were information protocol transfers, nothing more. “0101100000100000,” he continued. “101110110000010.” There must have been a glitch in the data stream. You requested that he run a diagnostic and retransmit. “101110110000010.” Data content verified. “Fucking Garbage fucking Bag fucking Johnny?” you spoke right into his eyes; they’re glistening. You don‘t excuse your language. “Fuck. This isn’t going to end pretty.” Five On a old mattress, with your body contorted to avoid the loose and unfurled springs that stabbed through its surface, you endured your first night’s sleep in your new home. Your tired, itchy eyes traced the cracks in the ceiling; thin, dark lightning bolts breaking the tar-stained sky. Outside, voices erupted like howls in the night. Glass shattered. A car alarm blared. A dog barked, rabidly. A gun fired. Your iced spine shuddered. The dog whimpered. Tires smoked and screeched. The dull thud-thud-umph of a body rolling over a speeding vehicle rumbled like crunching thunder. Gunshot. Breaking glass. Gunshot. A muffled pop of a tire blowing. Brakes unleashed a stressed scream, unable to handle their workload. Gunshot, silenced by bodywork. The sprinkling shatter of headlight glass as it clipped the streetlamp was promptly followed by the concussive crunch of grill versus brickwork. An off-tempo pitter-patter of footsteps scrambling off gave way to silence; as if the night had taken a breath amid an over-stretched, rambling sentence. Your eyelids played pat-a-cake with one another, flirting but not embracing. Through the ceiling, you heard angry words floating down, just like the shower of plaster-dust that fell every time a door was slammed. She pleaded, voice breaking as it pitched high, for him to get her out of here. He ordered her back to bed. As she goaded him, his voice awoke angrily from its weary tone. From their verbal haymakers, you learned that she was a waitress, when she could get a shift, and his income wasn’t the kind he would be declaring to the IRS. Fucking whore cunt, seemed to be his pet name for her. “Cocksucker, hers for him. It was like a lullaby to you; which raised severe questions about your mental state. When his backhanded thrash connected and she collapsed into a heap on the floor, you joined her in deep sleep. Six Russell Greer’s rodent-like features snarled as he passed your front door. Greasy black hair was tightly slicked into a ponytail, leaving a sharp widow’s peak in its wake. His cheekbones could carve marble; the left sporting a scar which mirrored your own. He was wiry and sinewy, with gaunt features which cast sinister shadows on his unshaved face. “The fuck are you looking at?” were his first neighborly words, spat out through yellowy, uneven teeth. “Better get those eyes off of me before I fuckin’ stab ‘em out, bitch.” He led April Greer past you by the wrist, not the hand. A band of mottled purple peeked out from behind cheap, plastic bracelets. Scar tissue wove through the bruising. The stone in her wedding ring looked like a plastic bead from a cheap lampshade. Her face had been painted as an adult, but beneath the bruise-concealing plastering of foundation, blush and eye-shadow her face was younger - if not fresher - than her 21 years. Her thick, straw-colored hair was tightly curled, which helped disguise the missing clumps. She was the next generation of the toddler whose parents had dolled her up for a beauty pageant. She gave you a smile, and you saw through the oil-slick of shiny lip-gloss to the innocence which lurked under her painted face. Her lips engaged you rather than her eyes; his gravity holding her gaze down to the floor. He gave her a yank of the wrist and she squeaked through her stubby nose. “Yeah, you better fuckin’ walk off,” he boasted, ignoring the fact that you were leaving the apartment as he dragged his wife up the stairs. Russell Greer knew what you had heard last night; he’d gotten used to having no neighbors either side and directly beneath. “Keep walkin’, bitch. Keep walkin’.” You obeyed his barked commands, gritting your teeth through the frustration of playing the submissive role. Russell Greer would think you were intimidated. Russell Greer would think you feared his ring-wielding fists as much as his wife. Russell Greer did not know who you were. Seven A timid knock at the door nagged as much as dragged you from your sleep. You ignored it and dropped your head back onto the cleanest portion of the mattress. That knock, again. It was just loud enough that you’d hear, just quiet enough that nobody else would. Grumbling a 7-hit-combo of profanity, you heaved yourself up and grunted, “what?” That nervous little peck of a knock, again. The oppressive wail of the door’s hinges made April Greer hunch herself up, tort nerves recoiling in fright. Devoid of gaudy make-up, this was the meek girl hiding beneath when you’d first met. Twitchy, she checked over each shoulder before looking back at you, tracing the scar up to your sleep-crusted eyes. “Um, hi, I’m April, from upstairs, er… about the other night,” she began. Her voice was low and mumbled. “Sorry if it kept you awake. It was nothing… really. I’d jus-” “Come in,” you interrupted, sharp words hacking her weak explanation off at the root. After shuffling into your apartment with cautious steps, April took in all that you’d done with the place since moving in, which amounted to dumping a mattress in the corner of the living space and little else. She was polite enough not to comment on the décor. “So, um, as I was saying, about the other night,” she resumed Russell’s programming. “It was me starting an argument about nothing, really. It’s a little embarrassing, you hearing us air all our dirty laundry like that and all. I threw a few things and knocked a lamp over bu-” Your features grew more contemptuous with every false word she spoke. This wasn’t the first time she’d had to explain away the screams and the smashes, the pleas and the thuds; but it was the first time it had been all but crammed back down her throat. “Look, I just don’t… I don’t want Russell hurting you, okay?” she whispered, eyes darting back and forth from the window to the cracks around the door. “The guy who lived here before you - Jeff - he took me to the hospital this one time after I… after I collapsed. Two weeks later, they found Jeff in that corner there with a dirty needle in his arm. Jeff was a vegan, for Christ’s sake … he even bought all this expensive organic fruit from the market one time. He wouldn’t pump anything like that into his veins.” “I’m not scared of your husband, April,” you told her with steely assurance. “You should be,” April urged with as stern a whisper as she could muster. “He’s… he’s…” “He’s a man who picks his fights with people much, much weaker than he is.” You thought back to when your knees had almost collapsed the lungs of Ayake Sonada; how despite such physical dominance you’d never felt more feeble. Listening to the defenseless interviewer coughing and wheezing at your feet stabbed at your darkened soul more than any of your victor’s taunts ever had. You knew how weak Russell Greer was because you’d felt it. For the first time since she stepped foot onto the dusty, creaky floorboards of your apartment, April read your body language. Your fists were clenched as natural posture, with knuckles that were wrapped in a heavy bandage of scar tissue. Your arms were defined, neither sinewy nor bulky, while you stance was naturally wound tightly onto your back foot, ready to spring off of it. The scar which shredded your right cheek had been joined by splits on each dry lip and the bridge if your nose. Your eyes were flushed of color and in perpetual shadow. “He’s going to kill me… kill us one day.” Her words were weighed down by hopeless dread as she tenderly cupped her stomach. “I know it.” “April.” Your hands held her arms, softly, but still she squirmed as if it were the tight, clawing grip she were used to. “Next time he raises his hand to you, scream. Scream until your throat hurts, until your lungs collapse. Just scream… and I’ll put him in the ground before he can throw the punch.” For a fleeting second, you felt like a better person than you were. Eight Don‘t you dare back out of this, Rasa hissed venomously in your ear. You weak, limp, fleshy coward. No life, no hope, no friends… apart from a little white-trash whore upstairs. A family who want nothing to do with you, that treat you no better than that half-cousin of yours who fucked a thirteen year old. Nothing to go back to if Zenith peels that mask off of your face. Tempest’s words were still echoing around your head; Rasa knew this. Its spite began to erode them. Her confrontational reason was a virus which Rasa was intent on purging from your system; your black blood cells. Or do you think that when the mask goes, so will I? it cackled. Silly, naïve little thing. You’re the mask. You’re the voice in my head. You’re all broken inside, and there’s nobody that can fix you. The flimsy flame of Garbage Bag Johnny’s Big lighter gave birth to your grotesque mask from the darkness. Do it. Nine The sound of a screaming April Greer crashing through a glass coffee table was your shuddering alarm call. Off the tatty mattress you sprung, the fabric of your bedclothes plucked by the loose, barbed springs. As you bounded towards the apartment’s door, you heard Rasa’s serpentine tongue flicking poison in your ear. Where do you think you’re going? it lashed as you wrenched at the handle. Locked. Sorry. Can’t have you drawing attention to us, now. Mr. Zenith would not be pleased. “Fuck you,” you growled, rummaging through stacks of unopened mail and a bowl of change for your keys. Cold, Rasa teased as your frenetic rummaging bore no fruit. Colder… colder still… As you heard April’s chilling screams scratch through the thin membranes of your eardrums, you exploded a thunderous kick at the door. The rusting hinges and worm-ridden wood should have been flung down the hallway. Again you lashed out, actions amplified by effervescent vitriol. But your muscle fibers were infected by Rasa’s malignant presence, and they resisted you on its orders. Again you kicked, but the civil war waging within your tissues was being won by the forces of festering darkness. “Where’s the fucking key?” you seethed, teeth gnashing. “I swear to God, tell me where the fucking key is or I’ll kill us both.” Through the ceiling, you heard April Greer plead for her life. She said she was sorry. She said she didn’t mean any of it. She said nothing to dampen the ferocity of the punch which dislodged her front teeth and split her soft palate. Ouch, Rasa winced, taunting you. That’ll leave a mark. Legs paralyzed, you hammered on the door with your fists, splitting scarred knuckles and streaking blood down with chipped and peeling paint. When your arms defected, you used what little of your body remained your own and smashed your forehead into the door, splinters piercing your eyebrows. You’ll end a man’s career at a click of Zenith’s fingers. Dangling you from puppet strings, Rasa hissed halted your forehead an inch or so from the splintering, crimson-stained wood. You’ll attack everyone he orders you too… so why does one bitch’s life - one that nobody in the world will miss - matter? “Because nobody ordered me to save her life,” you growled through a clamped down jaw, teeth aching at the roots where they were squeezed so tightly. “Or to let her die.” The sounds seeping through the damp-blighted ceiling was inhuman; April’s mangled mouth warping her screams. Rasa had refused to let you blink, a subtle form of torture to punish your disobedience. Hmmmm…Rasa pondered your humanity, which disgusted it more than just a little. I’ll play. Let’s just say I was a little hungry while you were sleeping; the food you eat is utter garbage. Everyone needs a little iron in their diet. “You motherfucker,” trickled from your lips as Rasa surrendered its hold over your muscle fibers. Newborn, you flopped down into the nail-barbed floorboards and scrambled to the toilet bowl. Your index and middle fingers reached for the back of your throat. You gagged and spluttered, foreplay to your guttural heave’s bilious orgasm. “Pleh… hyuk… uheyck…” You cared little that the key may slash your esophagus on its vomit-y ascent. If you died, hawking up dark, treacly blood, you knew that Rasa would die in a toilet bowl; a better end than it deserved. The orchestration of violence conducted by Russell Greer upstairs reached its crescendo as the contents of your stomach erupted over your hastily retreating fingers. You felt your nostrils scorch. Leaving no mangled muscle or cartilage in its wake, the key spluttered out over your tongue, pinging off your incisors. Silly child, Rasa snorted. You don’t get to be a hero. “No… NO!“ you yelled as you felt your numb, vomit-soaked fingers grab a handful of hair. “Don’t you FUCKING da-” Your skull bounced off the toilet in time with April Greer’s thumping down on the floor above, her last breath seeping from bloody lips. Ten Heidi Rosenberg’s heels stabbed into the carpet’s faint halo where April Greer’s blood and bodily fluids had been shampooed out of the fraying carpet. “Regeneration grants have been approved just last week,” she chirped. “Going to be lots of young couples looking to raise a family here.”
