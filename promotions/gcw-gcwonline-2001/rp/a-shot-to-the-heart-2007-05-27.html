A shot to the heart

27 May 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 27, 2007 __Start Although there were four separate phones scattered throughout Jay and Daniel's home, Jay himself had never disturbed one. Naturally he'd heard it ring before. He'd even seen the red light flash when a call was waiting to be answered. As was said though, he would not be the one to speak over the line. That was better left to Daniel. With Daniel absent though and the annoying hum of the ring playing over and over, it was time for a change. Thus far the phone across the kitchen had played, stopped, played again, and stopped again. The cycle seemed endless as it began to ring again. By counting, he would have to endure six more dings until it stopped for a quick reprieve. To say the least, Jay had heard fifteen too many. He stood from the wicker chair and stepped away from his bowl of fruit loops to make the quest across the kitchen. That red light winked at him menacingly and lured him ever closer to his first run-in with a caller. He grabbed the phone off the charger and drew it close to his ear. Silence. Jay tapped his foot on the tiled floor and waited for the anonymous caller to make their voice heard. Three seconds went by...then five. After seven he went to hang up the line. 'Hello.' The voice was dry and seemed like it belonged to an elderly man. 'Hello.' it said again. 'Who is this?' He shot back with his boyhood charm. As if surprised by the brash words he responded hesitantly, 'Is Jamison there?' 'That depends on who's asking.' 'Dave Talley. I'm sure he'd be willing to talk to me. It's been a while.' Jay pulled the phone away from his ear at the mention of the name. He was the one man that he was ashamed to share a name with. 'Dave Talley is dead. Jay told me himself.' 'Far from it,' he spat out hastily, 'Could you just let him know that I'm in Philadelphia?' It had been nearly five years since he had felt the way he did now. The hairs on his arms stood tall and his eyes boiled red in disgust. Dave Talley may not have been dead in a normal sense but to his son, he was choking on dirt six feet under. 'I'd rather not ruin his concentration.' He went to hang up the phone. 'Please, I just want to talk to him....' The voice was loud enough for Jay to hear. He held the receiver down by his knees for a moment and found the wall as a good resting place for his head. Silence overtook the conversation and Jay liked it. His mind jumped from memories in the basement to bursts of anger in the streets. He had nothing good to remember of his father. 'Please,' the voice was lower this time empathizing a more sincere man. He drew the phone back up to his ear and spoke back, 'Find Rouge on South 18th, I can't promise it will be long....but he'll be there.' He slammed the phone back down on the charger and swung his hand at the wall. His knuckles cut into the plaster and scraped the paint off in four small circles. It wasn't something he was looking forward to but he knew he had to do it. __________ 'If it gets any hotter I may have to move again.' Daniel Franco was at the point where living in Philadelphia wasn't as fun as it had been in the winter. The sun seemed to target the city of brotherly love and extend its rays the furthest to all the civilians. To make it even better, the trees in the park were doing very little to shade Franco and Heather on their walk along the stony path. 'With or without Jay?' Heather was dressed well for the day with her short jean skirt and white strapless top. She sure was one to look at. 'That depends on whether or not he decides to keep winning.' 'Did he win against that guy last week?' Franco nodded as he loosened his shirt and jacket to let some air in. Even in the heat he maintained the prestige of a professional. 'He did. He did it quick too. They were speaking very highly of the amputee too.' 'He fought an amputee? What a ridiculous sport.' 'Depends on who you ask. Remember, Jay needs to know you love wrestling. The better you two are for one another the harder he'll crash.' Heather whipped her bangs out of her eyes and found a cigarette from the front pocket of her skirt. She lit the end and smiled after the first hit. 'You keep to managing, I'll do the acting.' 'I really wish you could avoid that habit.' He motions to the cigarette between her middle and index fingers. 'The better we are for each other the harder he'll crash.' She flashes a toothy grin his way after quoting him directly. Daniel shakes his head and puts his arm over her shoulders. The duo continues to wind through the park under the sun's watchful eyes. __________ Rouge, rated number three of Philadelphia's outdoor dining places by citysearch, sat comfortably overlooking the park. Surprisingly, people didn't mind the heat much at all seeing the amount of people around. Nearly every table was filled by two, sometimes three, people. ?Nearly? being the key word. Jay Terror leaned back in the black iron seat waiting for his Jack Daniels. He had fought with his waitress for a good ten minutes before she gave in and went on a quest for his favorite liquor. It was a good thing to get your way. 'Excuse me,' In the middle of his anticipation he felt a tap at his shoulder. It was light, nothing like his father would have done. He turned to his side and looked into a young boy's eyes. 'You're Jay Terror.' He rose his eyebrow up a notch and studied the kid. He looked to be about eleven or twelve by face and fifteen-ish by height. 'To some, yeah. You want a smoke or something?' 'My mom told me that they'd kill me.' 'Who's your mom to say anything. She does it after you go to bed.' The boy looks backwards, most likely toward his mother, and then back frontward again. 'I've never done it.' 'That's nothing,' Mr. Marlboro himself reached at the box on the table and found a stick for the little boy. At this point Jay Terror became the biggest threat to the TRUTH campaign. 'All you do is stick it in your mouth,' he did it, 'light the end,' again he performed it, 'and suck in the good stuff.' The boy coughed as a cloud of smoke surrounded his head. It found holes in all parts of his face and seeped in each to give the youngster a taste of Jay's life. 'Does it always make you cough?' 'Not always. After your first you hardly ever cough.' He extended the lit 27 down to the boy and waited for him to grab it. The boy reached up for it and stuck it between his fingers just as Jay did. While it was getting situated though a woman came up from behind them both and ripped it loose from his grip. 'Thomas Jeremy Luther Gianni, what are you doing!' By the name and tone of voice, it was obviously his mother. She threw the cigarette on the concrete and crushed it under her white heels. Her attention shifted to Jay. 'And you, who do you think you are teaching kids to smoke?' Terror lowered his sunglasses and showed her his deep gray eyes. 'Just a guy trying to make sure kids learn the truth about smoking.' 'The truth is it will kill you in a year. There's nothing good about the things.' Thomas, the boy, chimed in as any child would, 'He said you smoke them when I'm sleeping mom.' His mother looked appalled at the accusation and sent her son back to the table. He waved to Jay and followed her orders. 'Good luck with your cancer. Maybe when you can't breathe anymore you'll be more of a role model to kids like Thomas.' She wound her arm up and slapped her palm against his cheek creating a sick snap. Heads turned and voices quieted as everyone watched the aftermath. 'You owe me a cigarette.' It was all he would say to her. Maybe it was because it was all she stayed to listen to or perhaps it was all he had to say after being hit. It really didn't matter. Jay looked around at the people and they broke the silence continuing on with their afternoon. 'You haven't changed much I see.' The empty seat was now filled. His father looked across the black table with an expression he hadn't ever seen on his face. It was a mix between sympathy and apathy he presumed. 'People don't change.' Jay fixed his sunglasses and sat straight in his seat. Seeing Dave Talley felt the same as it would if he was staring at a ghost. Five years would do that to people though. He was wearing a red t-shirt and a pair of short khakis on his lower half. 'I beg to differ. Time does a lot to a person.' 'Time makes people wrinkle and lose their memory. It doesn't make them kind and loving.' 'I'm sorry Jamison.' He lowered his head. 'You're five years too late for that one. What do you want from me?' Dave popped his head up and watched the waitress return with Jay's Jack Daniels. She tried to offer him something but he declined courteously and she went away again. 'Hard liquor huh?' 'Shit happens. Just answer the question.' He took a swig of the drink and licked his lips hoping to enjoy every last bit of it. 'I just wanted to talk. I've been thinking about you lately.' 'Because I'm on TV? Because I'm starting to find success in wrestling?' 'No,' he paused, 'I always knew you'd make it big. I was just trying to speed up the process I guess.' Terror lit one of his cigarettes and took a heavy drag. He saw his father's disapproving eyes watch him as he took another sip of the Jack afterwards. 'I wish you wouldn't do that.' 'You're not someone I'm trying to impress here. You had your chance to be with me and help me with wrestling. Unfortunately, you killed that chance when you decided I couldn't have a life. Thanks to Daniel, I can have both.' Dave shook his head left and right. 'Is this Daniel character that much better than me? I'm sure I could work with you better and make us stronger.' 'Us?' Just as he had expected, his father had no intentions of making the situation between the two better. His greed and disability to show compassion for his own flesh and blood was too much to overcome. 'I told you people don't change.' That was the end of the conversation. As quickly as it had started, Jay had heard one thing too many. He stood from the table, grabbed his cigarettes and drink and walked off. Dave Talley watched his son disappear without saying a word. He knew there was no fixing this. As Jay exited Rouge he placed one foot off the sidewalk and prepared to cross the street. In a second he pulled that foot back and turned the other way. Seeing Daniel with his arm around Heather's shoulders was too much for one day. __End
