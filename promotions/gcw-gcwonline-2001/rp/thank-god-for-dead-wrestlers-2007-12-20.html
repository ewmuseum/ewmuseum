Thank God for Dead Wrestlers

20 December 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12-20-07 ~<>~<>~<>~ Sprint Center, Kansas City Outside? On any given day a man wakes up, throws some cold water on his face and a comb across his hair, straightens his tie, ties his shoes, pats his children on the cheek and heads to work. He whistles off-key to a song on the radio, stops to get a cup of coffee, looks at his watch, realizes he is late, walks back to his car, and gets hit by a bus. Figuratively speaking, of course; Rich Rollins would never wear a tie, and he hated coffee. And kids. As the thought ran through his head however, it pretty much summed up his feelings as he arrived at the arena of port, another day at the office, just in time to witness the most angry protesting mob he had ever seen. That was saying something, too; Rollins had seen a lot of angry mobs. Hell, he had been the cause of many of them, what with that whole debacle involving Frank Jacobs and the murder of his pregnant lover Cynthia O’Doyle. Vile names had been cast at his feet like stones straight from the New Testament, and still, what Rich Rollins saw as he arrived for this not-so particular house show in Kansas City made him smile in spite of himself. “GOD HATES FAGS!” roared a rowdy picketer, toting a homemade rainbow sign that screamed the same message. Nearly four dozen people voiced similar support as they gathered around the security gate leading into the staging areas, right where Rollins intended to park. After one good look at how close the mob liked to get next to every passing car, Rollins decided to pull over and park well outside of the danger zone. Locking up his favorite cherry apple red “69 Shelby Mustang, the Renegade decided to get a closer look. He wasn’t wearing anything that signified he was a GCW employee, a definite plus. He paused in amusement as he considered his options; a). he could walk right in and grab a fuel hose off one of the diesel trucks he saw nearby and have himself a little Baptist bonfire, or b). he could join them. Two seconds later he was walking into the rowdy rabble, screaming about how gay lovers were God’s gifts to godless people. He was obviously way more interested in the second option. Why the hell not? “Shun the non-believers!” Rollins cried, trying to sound as genuine as he could. A few nearby picketers tossed him a glance as he brushed by, obviously not recognizing him. That was good. “Shuuun them!” They obviously didn’t watch much YouTube, either. Finally worming his way to the mesh gate, Rollins had to swallow a serious urge to break into laughter at how the middle-aged Jesus freaks were carrying on. Did they really believe God hated gay people, or hell, that He even cared? The more Rollins watched the less amusing this shit was becoming. People just stood around, ignoring angry looks from the GCW stage crew on the other side of the fence. They didn’t seem to be violent, at least not that Rollins could tell, but they were numerous. It was the children that got to him most, though; there were a handful of kids barely old enough to recite their ABC’s sitting on their parent’s shoulders, holding signs proclaiming the Second Coming and the vengeance of a God hell-bent on destroying homosexuality. “Thank God for AIDS!” A woman, holding the hand of her daughter. An eight-year-old voice responds, “AIDS cures fags!” Rollins was physically startled when a firm hand clapped him on the shoulder and said, “You must be that poor fella Pastor Phelps was telling us about.” The man’s frame held about a hundred pounds more than it should have and his teeth were the color of crusty urinal cakes. Breath smelled like it, too. “Jerry Allan’s cousin, from Dodge City?” “Uhm, yeah,” Rollins lied, cracking the most pleasant smile he could manage. “Pastor who?” Shocked, the God-fearing zealot gasped. “Pastor who? Pastor Phelps, the founder of Westboro Baptist! You really must attend his sermon this Sunday, especially after Jerry told us what happened to your brother. How long had your family known?” “Known?”” Rollins was completely lost. “That he was a godless sodomite,” the man finished. “You must have known. Those fags all sound alike. Their lisp is what happens when the grace of God leaves their hearts.” “Oh yeah,” said Rollins. “That. Honestly, my brother can smoke all the pole he wants. We didn’t really talk much.” “It’s impossible to communicate with their type,” the man continued, apparently lost in his own rhetoric. “It is our job to put the wrath and fury of God to the lips of all heathens and liberal atheists and make them drink it. Forgiveness is God’s duty, even for a soulless faggot like your brother.” Rich Rollins had heard a lot of crazy shit in his day, had said a lot of crazy shit, but this? this was quickly becoming too much. Were these people for real? It was all he could do not to gape and stare. “Yeah, well we still love him. He’s still family, you know” Misguided and gay, maybe? but family.” The man made a sour face. “We’re your family,” he said very matter-of-factly. “I’ll see if Pastor Phelps can speak with you privately, it really sounds like you’re a bit misguided yourself.” “Excuse me?” The novelty of crashing this little Bible-thumping lynch mob’s picket line was wearing thin, and so was Rollins? patience. “I didn’t say I was a faggot, or a gay-lover, or whatever-? “I know exactly what you said, son.” A new voice rose from the cacophony of homophobic hysterics, stabbing Rollins in his right ear. “You don’t need to fear His judgment, since you appreciate and respect the way of the universe; one man, one woman. Don’t mind Christopher, he’s just very passionate about God’s work here.” “I’m sorry Pastor Phelps, I was just telling him not to give in to his sympathies for the sake of a sinner.” This disturbed man who had been speaking with Rollins, Christopher, simply shrugged. “Has to come to terms sometime.” “And he will,” the Pastor replied, coming around Rollins and into view. Standing a few inches shy of Rollins? nose, Pastor Fred Phelps was a simple-looking soldier of the Word wearing a pressed cardigan he likely bought back when JR got shot. He wore a face that Father Time had kicked the shit out of, but his eyes were not insane; they were powerful, convicted. “Give him time, Christopher. He will come around once he realizes that Heaven is eternal, and so is Damnation.” Rollins addressed the man, albeit carefully. “So, you’re the leader of this” church?” It was hard not to mock, especially when the target made it so easy. “Pastor Phelps?” “Yes,” the wizened old face said, “and I know who you are, Rich Rollins. You don’t belong here. Not until you accept the teachings of Christ and give up this violent profession with its ambiguous morality and the heathen homophiles who populate it. Professional wrestling is a blight, my blind friend. It’s a cancer of the soul, and it spreads the homosexual agenda as surely as I live and breathe. A certain man from a local promotion known as UCW began using a gay gimmick recently. This is why we are here.” “What the hell?” Rollins? eyes grew, flabbergasted. “Because of some gay character in a federation I’ve never heard of, you pull this shit” On my show?” Fred Phelps let his body wilt ever so slightly as if distressed by Rollins? lack of moral character. “You use that word so loosely, Mr. Rollins. Hell. Yet you know nothing of it.” “Sorry to break it to you Phelps, but I’ve been there.” Phelps just laughed. “My dear Rollins” you never really left.” ~<>~<>~<>~ Sprint Center, Kansas City Inside? “Fuck, just” FUCK!” The sound of crashing chairs and clattering cups filled the hallway, causing a technician in a GCW shirt to cringe as he scurried away out of a well-defined sense of self-preservation. Inside the communal catering room open only to The Establishment, Rich “The Renegade” Rollins wailed furiously against a rhythm bag. Every few seconds he would lash out, sending another empty beer can or plastic tray skittering across the floor. He was breathing heavily; he hadn’t been this worked up since? Since when? Cynthia? That thought sure didn’t help. The mere spark of her memory triggered a violent streak in Rollins that even he didn’t understand. He never loved her, not in the way she wanted him to, but she had been carrying his child. And yet he hated kids. Complicated? You betcha. So lost in his own anger was Rollins that he failed to notice the door open. Wearing a finely tailored black suit over a flared red Nordstrom’s undershirt, LeStatt Knight took a wary step towards the cornucopia of finger food, cautious of any potential airborne hors d'oeuvres. Plucking a saut’ed mushroom from a ridiculously large center piece, LeStatt spoke as he ate, not looking at the raging Renegade. “You seem to be upset,” LeStatt offered. “Have you tried these shrimp” Delicious.” Ignoring his strangely well-centered stable mate, Rich Rollins continued to focus his attention on the steady buhdumdududumdudum of the rhythm bag. LeStatt turned quietly, regarding Rollins with the kind of bemusement befitting a bona fide badass. “So, you talked to those homo-haters too, I take it” How did that go?” Buhdumdudum- Silence. “That well?” Rollins? voice sunk, scraping the bottom of his throat. He still faced the wall, refusing to look his unlikely friend in the eye. “I’m not in the mood, Roberto.” “You never really are, Rollins.” Strolling over to a frivolously ornate leather couch (one of the many lavish luxuries Steven Caldera had conferred to them, not to mention the personal on-call masseuse who traveled with his own goodie bag of lotions and acupuncture toys), LeStatt Knight sank into the soft suede folds of relaxation and sighed. “It makes me angry, too. You just can’t communicate with those kinds of people.” “Funny,” Rollins said, turning around. “They said the exact same thing.” Opening a bottled water he found in an ice bucket tucked into an opening at either end of the couch, LeStatt shrugged. “Olv’dese acerca del ignorante, porque ellos ignoran la raz’n.” Smiling, he took a sip and sighed reflectively. “You know what it means?” “Sorry, I don’t speak spic.” Grabbing a towel, the Renegade began to march towards the door. “Now who sounds like a mindless bigot?” That stopped him. Rollins? eyes carried an agitated, almost manic sheen as he spun around to confront LeStatt. For his part, LeStatt Knight just held his tongue and let Rollins simmer, waiting for the other man to speak. A handful of heartbeats passed between them in silence; it became apparent that the Renegade wasn’t going to make much of an effort to share, so LeStatt continued. “It means that you should ignore the ignorant, because they ultimately ignore reason,” LeStatt finished. “Those people out there, they’re just crazy. I read a local newspaper article about them; their “church” has less than a hundred members in the congregation, and almost eighty percent of them come from the Phelps family. They make a living off these public displays of ignorant hatred. Let them be angry, and let yourself just be grateful you didn’t grow up in the Bible Belt.” “Fuck that.” Rollins snorted. “They can be grateful that I didn’t put their little leader on his ass. They tried to insinuate that I was gay, or something. Hell, I don’t even know what they were talking about, it was all just?” He paused, searching for the perfect combination of words to bring his point across. “It was funny at first, watchin” “em rant and rave like that. But when I saw those kids, a buncha rug rats brought up to say some of the most hateful things I’ve ever heard and actually believing in their own bullshit, it just makes me wanna fuckin” hit something, you know?” LeStatt nodded in agreement. “Remember those faces the next time you meet Jordan White.” He winked. “Yeah, sure. Whatever.” Rollins turned back to the door, trying to push it all out of his mind. He had a show to put on, after all. Gripping the handle and turning it sharply, the Renegade headed into the hallway. Or he would have, rather, if the way hadn’t been blocked by a burley man wearing a Postal Service pin on his lapel. “Are you Rich Rollins?” he asked. Rollins nodded impatiently, prompting the man to whip out his delivery. A sealed envelope with a red label reading “Urgent” was pressed into Rich Rollins? palm. “Yeah, you look like Rich Rollins. Say, you think I could get you to sign-? “No.” Rollins snatched the envelope and swung the door shut, rattling the frame with the force. Rolling his eyes to the ceiling, he looked down at the envelope, then at LeStatt. “I never get mail. You think Caldera finally got wise and found himself enough time to sign my pink slip?” Having worked his way over to a weight bench, LeStatt Knight pulled off his suit coat and loosened his tie, only half listening as he sat down to stretch. “You wish, Rollins. He’ll never let us free, not now.” “Yeah, not ever.” Rollins finally cracked a smile as he traced his index finger under the envelope’s lip, tearing it off and removing the solitary paper within. Unfolding his mystery letter, he smacked his lips in anticipation of something he would no doubt care less about, walked over the couch, sat down, and began reading: Dear Richard Rollins, My name is Tim Davis, son of a man you used to work with earlier in your career, Samuel Davis. My father was always an avid fan and follower of your work Mr. Rollins. He used to tell me about the tag team the two of you used to run, back when he was calling himself Sammy Davis. The Rat Pack, I believe you called yourselves. He kept the belts you both won back in Primetime Wrestling, which if his memory is to be believed, was the first title you ever won. Rollins? brow furrowed in concentration, now fully engrossed in the letter. Of course he remembered Sammy Davis; the old man had given him one of his first breaks, hell, had given him the moniker he now cherished and made a living off of. Hunkering back into the couch, the Renegade continued: Two days ago my father suffered a fatal stroke. He went in his sleep, as painlessly as possible according to his doctor. He talked about you frequently, even when the public outcry was drowning out your accomplishments during GTT5. He never lost faith in you, Rich. He wanted me to give you one of these tag belts, sort of a memory captured in gold as he would have said. If you could come back to Chicago for his funeral on Saturday, I would love to give it to you. It would mean a lot to me. And to him. After all, my father was the first man to call you a Renegade. Sincerely, Tim Davis “Good “ol Sammy,” Rollins mused, placing the letter back in its envelope. He hadn’t thought about the grizzled old vet in ages, not since the days before GCW had evolved from the modest roots of the CWU. Sammy had taken him under his wing, shown him what wrestling was supposed to be. He was Rollins? first and most influential mentor; the man who had once said that Richard Rollins was a renegade of the heart. God damn, he felt guilty. All these years, and Rollins never once stopped in to see the old coot. Hell, they were from the same city. Too late now, he thought sadly. “Good news?” LeStatt ventured mid-curl, perking an eyebrow in the Renegade’s direction. Rollins shook his head. “Not for Sammy.” “Sammy who?” But Rollins didn’t answer. Instead, he collected his denim and leather jacket from an open coat locker against the back wall. Too many things were competing for his attention, too many pet peeves and regrets to focus. “Rollins” Where are you going? Show starts in half an hour.” He didn’t even turn back as he opened the door. “I’m going home,” were the only words Rich Rollins offered as he took his leave, leaving his confused Establishment mate to pick up the pieces for the evening. ~<>~<>~<>~ Resthaven Cemetery, Chicago? Steven Caldera had called, furious. It was the umpteenth time Rollins had skipped out on an un-televised event, but honestly, Rich Rollins couldn’t possibly care less. He had hung up the phone, cutting Caldera off mid-sentence. Way Rollins figured, losing his job wouldn’t have been the worst thing in the world. It would piss Jordan White off royal, and that was just fine by him. Besides, there were more important things in life than winning wrestling matches. Or, if recent history was any indication, losing them. A cold chill ran down the Renegade’s spine as he watched the mourning faces of Samuel Davis? family and friends; their grief was an absolute that felt so alien to Rollins he couldn’t stop fidgeting. The silent headstones of Resthaven Cemetery stood in welcome of the recently deceased, dusted with the last remaining piles of the season’s first snow. It was an oddly charming sight in the midst of such a somber occasion. The charm was still lost on Rollins, though. After all, his own mother’s plot rested not fifteen yards away, the location of which Rollins had taken great pains to avoid since the night Cynthia O’Doyle had been burned alive. He had shot two men in cold blood for what they had done, an act that had caused more sleepless nights for Rich Rollins than he would ever admit out loud. It all happened right here, on the same hallowed ground that now held the Renegade’s feet firmly in place. God, this place gave him the creeps. “I’m so glad you could come,” said Tim Davis, taking Rollins? hand firmly. “This would have meant so much to my dad, you have no idea.” Rich Rollins nodded grimly, stiffening in awkward surprise as Tim pulled him into a tight hug. The Renegade had never been big on physical interaction (at least not the kind that involved the fully-clothed) but he let the man have his moment, washing away at least a little grief in the comforting embrace of closure. Tim finally pulled away; Rollins’s lips curled in an oafish grin that didn’t touch his eyes. “Your old man was a helluva grappler,” said Rollins. “A hell of a man.” Tim glanced at the casket that hovered over a fresh grave, already buried in a mountain of flowers and velvet cloth. “He was a spirited old bastard, all right.” Tim laughed, stifling a sneeze that Rollins figured could just as easily have been a sob. “He loved his job, that was for sure. He always took pride in how successful you became, Rich. It was like, after he got too old to roll around in those tights, he could still live vicariously through you. He taped just about everything you did.” “Yeah.” Rollins didn’t know what else to say. Taking a deep breath, Tim turned back to face Rollins. “Well, I guess it’s that time. I have my dad’s belt in the car, I’ll get it for you when the ceremony is over.” “Take your time,” Rollins replied. “I ain’t goin” nowhere.” Tim offered his sincere gratitude once again and left Rollins to rejoin his family underneath the awning that sheltered his father’s grave. Most of the mourners began to gather close, waiting for the pastor to begin his eulogy. Rollins wandered closer, keeping himself slightly separated from the bulk of the crowd, not sure how he could possibly console a bunch of people he had never met. The minutes ticked by in hours as he watched them pay their respects, their voices muffled by the cold Chicago wind. Finally, the preacher took to the podium, and Rich Rollins? eyes popped. “Thank you all for gathering here today,” Fred Phelps began, positioning himself in front of his lamenting audience. “The pain of Samuel Davis” passing is felt by everyone here, even if he was a progenitor of the godless profession that is professional wrestling. His stroke was God’s will, a smiting of the wicked deeds his work helped propagate, but even in Hell his memory will be forever cherished by you, his faggot adoring family. Amen.” Confusion rippled across those gathered, underscored by the murmurs of shocked outrage. Rich Rollins was too horrified for words, too shocked to see the leader of Westboro Baptist Church to do much more than stand with his tongue sticking to the roof of his mouth. He turned around slowly, expecting a flood of sign-wielding warriors of Christ to pop out of the soil in order to reap their hate-fueled revenge against them all. And there they were; having just arrived in a caravan of vehicles marked by the church, the Phelps family began setting up shop across the street. They had apparently been busy over the weekend, because Rollins spotted a new multicolored sign in the hands of some young woman. It read simply: Thank God for Dead Wrestlers “I asked the Davis family if I might lead this service today,” Phelps continued, unperturbed by the growing unrest of the crowd. “My apologies to the closest of kin; my pretense for being here was false. I do not intend to eulogize this man, and neither should you. God’s wrath has been visited upon the wicked sodomites of this country, from the soldiers who fight and die in Iraq for a government who openly supports homosexuality all the way to the canvas-covered stage of evil that popular culture knows as professional wrestling. You need look no further than Chris Benoit, a man who murdered his own wife and child due to the evils of wrestling and its satanic hold over the minds of our youth. Samuel Davis is with him now, burning in Hell for his sins.” “What are you doing?” Tim Davis stood up, clenching his fists subconsciously. “That’s my father you’re talking about, you son of a bitch!” Voices began rising over the dumbstruck silence of the mourners; the Westboro worshipers had finally gotten themselves settled, timing their obnoxious blasphemy to perfectly compliment the arrival of their leader. Two men held up an American flag, the great stars and stripes of their own country turned upside down as they cried, “God hates America! Thank God for dead wrestlers!” “You said you were honored to eulogize my father,” Tim Davis seethed, his shock slowly dissolving into fury. “You lied to me! Your own God has a commandment against that, last time I checked! You hypocritical bastard!” Phelps remained unmoved. “I rejoice when the ignorant homophiles of this world show me their anger. It’s a powerful theme in the scriptures; when we preach the truth about God, people will undeniably hate us. You hate yourselves, and that is the real truth. Repent, and grieve not this man! He has been touched by the Lord’s anger, and so will you if you heed not His holy rage!” “Holy rage?” Rich Rollins called out, his frozen tongue finally thawed. “I’ll show you His holy fuckin” rage, you arrogant fuck!” People dove away as two hundred-fifty pounds of pure Renegade plowed through. Rollins launched himself at the psychopathic reverend, clearing the podium clean and tackling Phelps dead-center. Both men tumbled backwards, tripping over Samuel Davis? casket. Flowers and photographs of the immortal Sammy Davis scattered in all directions. Some of the elderly women in the crowd began to cry as Rich Rollins scrambled to his feet, his self-control completely severed. Phelps wheezed in protest as the Renegade put one knee in his chest, held him against the ground, and popped the blasphemy right out of his mouth. “You” you would strike a seventy-eight year old messenger of God?” cried Phelps, a healthy helping of terror finally finding sanctuary in his eyes. Snarling with contempt, Rich Rollins spat in his face: “You’re goddamn right I would.” Thirty seconds later the Westboro bunch came running, screaming with all the might of their twisted perception of a vengeful God. Members of the mourning family tried to stop them, sparking a confrontation of Biblical proportions. Women in black funeral dresses clawed and slapped at anyone carrying a rainbow-colored sign, some of them using said signs as ammunition against their owners. Westboro loyalists retaliated, swinging and swearing damnation on everyone and everything in sight. By the time anyone managed to pull Rollins off of Pastor Phelps, the old man lay mangled and unconscious. Police sirens stabbed the skyline. Families fought other families, children scrambling for safety, men and women yelling vulgarities that would make Jesus turn over in his cave if he had been there. Rich Rollins watched it all in silent reflection, his hands still shaking from the beating he had so justly delivered. It was pure anarchy. Just the way he liked it. ~Fin~ “God doesn’t hate them because they’re fags. They’re fags because God hates them.” -Fred Phelps Fun addendum - Fred Phelps and his crazy cultists are real people.
