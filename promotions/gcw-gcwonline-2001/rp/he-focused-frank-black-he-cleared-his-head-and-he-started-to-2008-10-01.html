He focused, Frank Black; he cleared his head and he started to take control.

1 October 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/01/08 Frank Black ignored a lot of phone calls. He stopped checking as to who he was disregarding after the hope for surprise was gone; he was busy, or he had better things to do. He'd become totally subjective, that is, and there were things that had to be done by those rules. It started red against the canvas. It wasn't the first thing that started that way; it wouldn't be the last. Sometimes he felt like nothing but a meditation on its properties and purpose. He quit thinking about what was Frank Black or what was Jordan White; fell into himself without the pretensions of either and he did what he wanted. An emergency removal of the world was required to save the patient's life. He was in the downtime, he was taking precautionary measures to keep his newly formed comprehension from shattering under the weight of whoever he really was. He had gotten used to his name. He'd thought it encapsulated him. He'd believed that the only acting he got in on was on television, was under his fake face. It was Caroline, somehow, someway, that pushed him over the slight dropoff--not to be confused with an edge. It was a staggerstep reaction. He twisted in blue to purple, defined shadows and pits in thick crests. It was Canada. He was with some groupie, lamenting internal about how his idea of relationships had become taking the positive from all the women he knew and having a fling with that gestalt, when he started to feel a shifting. Caroline nagged at the back of his conscience, and he couldn't convince himself that he understood why, or that it was maybe that he just hadn't figured out what he wanted to take from her to add to his collage. Or that she didn't have a place. There were options. He painted those in browns and greens turpentine thinned, those that ran in drops that smeared as they trickled. There were several options. He took his time considering them and that was that. He also took his time accepting that he was redirecting one want for another, and that he hadn't tricked himself into giving over fully. He took his time to wonder if he wasn't just smashing his way through life as a reaction to some void. He took a lot of time, frankly. He painted. He slept. He painted. He thought. So on. He was tired of his calls so he went anonymous to a bar, asked for a drink when he thought a waitress hadn't been busy, and watched her waste a splash when she got the time to pour it. He tipped her extra to cover himself and went on with his drinking until it was time to walk home. Half of a block from a public intoxication charge. He went into his apartment and his phone was still ringing, or it had stopped and started again but it carried the implication, or the effect, of having been a constant. He was very drunk and he thought like that. He went back to the easel, yellow touched faint to highlight red. He lifted a gas station glass bubbler to his lips and inhaled green in yellow smoke, took steps back from the canvas and studied lines, questioned contours. Something wasn't right. He switched out CDs in his stereo and poured himself a drink that he finished, that he let settle into his bloodstream. The painting was still incorrect. He fell into a recliner opposite it and smoked. He tried to discern a central form from the static and the noise. He knew what he was painting; he wanted to know which lines to come were predetermined by the canvas and which were demanded by the spaces he'd already filled and the forms he'd begun to imply, if not cement. He had ideas. He watched them play until, tired of comparing his options, he took to his phone, breezed past the bloated list of ignored calls, and dialed a number from memory. He felt comfortable calling the third number he'd keyed up--it was wrong, so he transposed digits back to his first idea and let it ring. Just a friend from back in the day. No one named. She answered. "Hello?" "Hey." "Whoa, Frank?" "Hah, yeah." "Holy shit. Todays been like, a parade of people I havent talked to in ages. How are you?" He scratched the back of his neck and he lied. "Im good, Im good. Shit--knew I shouldve timed it better. Now Im just gonna get lumped in with the other people you used to know." "Its alright. Theres only been three of you, I mean, and one of those is an ex that I didnt wanna talk to anyways. But hes a retard, so Id have to like, literally spell it out for him. Why do I date these idiots? Anyways. How are you?" "Hah. Same status as the last time you asked. Things havent like, degenerated any." "Sweet deal. Oh yeah--Im a wreck diver now. Started diving last July." "Shit, thats awesome for you. What I get for not keepin in touch--miss all the cool shit happenin. Guessin you like it, yeah?" "Yep, its preeeetty awesome. You ever get back to college?" "Nah. Slacked my ass out." "Naw, its cool. You still wrestling then?" "Yeah. Got hired by GCW, though." "Oh, thats cool. Ive heard of them before, at least. You liking it?" He lit a cigarette and told the truth. "Oh, god no. No, no, no." "Thats no fun. Whatever happened to smashing aliens upside the head?" "Childhood dreams gotta die sometime." "Sometime." She sighed. He paused. He had nothing; not even the intrinsic connection between old friends. "Guess you aint around Charlotte right now." "Hah, nope. Florida." "Same number, though?" "I still live in the, like, general vicinity, the job just brought me down here. The waves were pretty good this summer, though. I liked it." "Shit. Good to hear. Probably oughta let you go, though. Sgood talkin to you again--have to try and get in touch with you when youre in town sometime." "Okay, sounds good. See ya." She hung up first. He knew without figuring variations that it could've gone better, and that it didn't speak well for anyone else he'd drifted away from over the years. He'd discarded some and he'd neglected others, regardless of method, he felt safe in deciding that neither group would help him back up from a worst case scenario. Fair enough--he wasn't happy with it, but fair enough. He'd have to avoid falling; and he'd well enough measured the distance he'd climbed by the matches and by the sales figures and by any number of varying methods, and he trusted it would be a very long drop from where he was to the true bottom. People he'd known had found it but, even at his darkest, he had only imagined to be able to see it at the pit of the well, in the haze at the end of the tunnel. He had a job. He was secure. His phone was ringing again and, for the debts he'd built up with the world, he answered without checking. "Yo?" "So. The illustrious Frank Black finally deigns to answer his phone. Thank god. Id spent all day wondering if I just wasnt worthy of the privilege anymore." "Whoa. Wasnt like that, boss." Sage laughed; it was received as distortion, static. "I know. Or I hoped, slash assumed, slash knew in my heart of hearts. What are you up to?" "Painting." "Yeah? Whats the subject?" He tilted at easels. "Shit. I dunno." "Well. Hows it coming along, then?" "Its, its alright." "Thats good." There was a moment of silence. "And?" "This is difficult." "What?" "Asking a favor." "Shouldnt be. Just ask it--youre the boss, all of that." "Yeah, but Im having a hard time calling it a professional favor. You, uh, youd better be distracted by other things right now, and a little uneasy at the idea of having them disrupted by your crazy fuckoff employer." "Not exactly how Ida phrased it." "Ive got a way with words." "What is it, then?" "I need you to babysit Trey next week." "What?" "Did I lose signal?" "Nah, I heard you. Just didnt, like. Understand it." "Hah. Motherfucker. Okay. Heres what it is: Trey isnt under JXW contract, and I dont think he likes me very much at all beyond that, but hes been spending a lot of time around Torm, lately. Youre going to hunt him out and let me know if you think hes, well, a threat, anything like that. Heres what youre going to act like it is: Treys been getting back into some hard shit, thanks to Torm, and Ive sent you in to make sure hes not fucking himself over. You know--act like I give a fuck." "Do you?" "Maybe. I mean, Treys an old friend, and I dont want to see him just fuck his life up. Other hand--Sobias is an old friend and Id still rather he was off bloated and floating somewhere. Id like to bring old boy in under the JXW banner, but Id like to do it without that fat fucker as the go between, and Id like to know that Trey hasnt just, lost his fucking mind. Its complicated. All you have to do is let me know if its a viable plan, or if Torms got to him too much." "Sounds simple." "It probably isnt. Probably." "Hey, don't gotta like, try an dissuade me." "Better than underprepping you." "Maybe. Shit. This is next week, youre sayin?" "Yeah, after Worldwide. Im hoping Terror ends that silly fuck off gauntlet on 99 so this whole Trey thing doesnt become a major distraction or anything." "Would be nice." "So, yeah. You up for it?" "Shit. Aint like I got anythin better to do." And Sage laughed again. "Youre a real motherfucker, kid. You know that?" "Heard it before. Dunno that Ive bought in on it yet, though." "Give it time." "That us, then?" "Yeah. Thats it. Ill get in touch with you again, let you know where this is all going down at. You got everything ready for Scotland?" "Yeah. Passport, tickets, bags with shit in em. Its all together." "Alright. Im done keeping your mind off the job--for now, at least. Be easy, Frank." "You too, jefe. Peace." That was that. He hung up and, when he couldn't pretend to care about the phone call any longer, fell back to his earlier distraction. It wasn't a very good base for a painting: he decided that. He wouldn't give up on it, but it was beyond him then and he stopped critiquing it and he kicked back and he turned the television on. Nothing. Dead air. He thought about his job until it slipped and then he thought about a woman, briefly, before he thought about his job again, held to a different set of standards than before. He'd taken to blaming it for: the implosion with Leslie; his sleep schedule; how hungry he stayed; how often he lied; the scars and scabs decorating his face like an African war mask; his inability to make eye contact with mirrors; his separation from himself. He didn't give it credit for showing him the gulf between who he was and who the world saw him as, the divide between even Frank Black and himself--but he did realize that the separation was there, and it did illuminate certain things that had been on his mind in a form like regrets. It was late, almost very late, and he picked up his phone and, heart mimicking the Indian drums that mimicked hearts, held 1 down until speed dial clicked in. It rang. He wondered who would answer, how. It rang. If it was her, how she'd react. It rang. He didn't think her reaction mattered. Some part of him was certain that he just had to give the notion air to breathe so it would stop taking his own. It rang. He was losing faith. Nothing new. It ra-"Frank?" And Leslie's voice came down hushed: it was wavering and it was tired, and it hung the fine line between a squeak and a midnight bark. "Yeah, L. Its me." "Oh. God. Gimme a second." "Okay." He sat and he waited. He heard: the rustle of comforters and sheets, the slight creak of floorboards, the swing of a door, the rush of the city. "Okay. Be glad you didnt wake Adam up." He thought, in the timespan of a flashing image, about the human cycle of violence perpetuating violence, about blood feuds that drafted generation after generation, about personal conflicts escalated into homicide charges. It was all classed as cruelty and it did nothing to dull the idea of destroying that man, his life, his world; especially his face. "Always am." "So. Whats up?" "Theres somethin Ive, I dunno. Shit. Ive never said shit like this. I dunno how to phrase it, I dunno how Im supposed to phrase it. Shit." A horn outside of her phone rippled an incomplete transmission. He guessed at what she was doing. "You dont have to set me up here. If you wanna say something, go ahead." "Shit. It aint that easy." "Yeah, Frank. It really is. You just open your mouth and wiggle your, like, voice box." "Haha. I mean. You know what I mean." "I seriously dont, because Ive been asleep for like, an hour and arguing every last minute of the last fucking month." "You know what I mean." She didn't respond automatic. She breathed into the phone. "Oh." "Yeah. Leslie, Ive had this in my ribs for way too long to feel comfortable about it." "Are you sure? I mean. I. I dont. God, you know me, Frank. Do I want to hear what youre about to drop on me?" "Probably not, but tellin yous the only way Im gonna think straight again. Its about me, I mean, if you wanna get down to it." "Well. If youre going to tell me, tell me. You know Im here for you." "Leslie." He pictured her face twisted curious, button of her nose tweaked to one side, an eyebrow rising and her lips pursed as her head tilted a <45 degree angle. He thought about intricacies, about stitching onto his statement lace, filigree. He wasn't sure if he had none of if he just wouldn't allow himself to use it. He breathed oxygen, smoke, paint thinner fumes. "Alright. Leslie. I for real, seriously, from the bottom of my heart of hearts," he didn't imply that that was borrowed, "I. Fuck it, girl: I love you." She was silent. His heart did not proceed as anticipated. The rhythm was supposed to placate, not rise machine gun. "Yeah." He threw it up as a defense. A wall to distance himself from personal culpability. A cow catcher. She sniffled. It made him smile. "Whoa. Thats. Thats a messed up thing to throw at me, right now. I mean. Damnit, Frank." "Ive done nicer things in my life, yeah, but just. The fear of keepin that in my head the rest of my life, just wastin away on words unsaid? I couldnt deal with that another day. The pressure was. It was somethin." "Goddamnit. How long are you going to be around? Charlotte." "Leavin for Aberdeen in another day." "Huh. Ill give you a call as soon as Im free, okay. Tomorrow, like. Three to four in the afternoon?" "Good by me." "Okay. Good. Ive. Ive got a lot of things on my mind and. Im just gonna have to think about this." "Thats straight. I wasnt expectin like, a reply or anythin. Just, thought Id let you know." "I appreciate it. You dont know how long its been since I believed those fucking words." "But you believe me?" "Yeah. You didnt tell me anything I didnt already know. I just, I didnt expect you to ever actually say it. It was just, easier that way." "Wind outta my sails." "Hee. I didnt mean that. Look, just, Ill give you a call when I get off work tomorrow. Please answer, okay?" "Always answer for you." "You didnt earlier." "I. What?" "We can talk about it later. Alright?" "Alright." "Night, Frank." "Have a good one, Leslie." "Love ya." And she hung up. And he rolled his head back. And something in his heart had calmed down, and he didn't have to fight the twitch of adrenaline. He thumbed down through the errant calls, stopped on her name. 4:38 pm. Again, seven minutes later. He lit a new cigarette from his former, gap narrowed between himself and who he claimed to be. He thought about his job. He thought about time and he made the saunter to his bedroom. He thought about wrestling and about how easy it all was when he let passion steer instead of logic. He was beginning to come around. He could identify what he wanted. He could count how many chances he realistically had left if he failed, and he didn't have to count past zero to do it. He could feel a certain swell of pride when Jay Terror came up. He finished his cigarette and snuffed it out, a certain acceptance of potential insanity as an occupational hazard. And maybe, the big problems in his soul had been his blinders against the smaller ones. He wondered if that was his raison d'apathy, and he went to sleep.
