Dancing with the Devil (Part II of II)

3 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10-3-04 “So Richard, what can I do for you?” [The words spill out of Don Alexandro Dante with a practiced charm that could only be learned from a life of high society, the underlying confidence of power not lost on “Renegade” Rich Rollins. Sitting in a plush office outfitted with the finest furnishings money can buy, Rollins carefully considers the figure of Don Dante from across the polished black desk.] [He called him “Richard.” Normally Rollins would call him on it, but now was not the time for petty arguments.] Renegade: I need you to help me find somebody. My brother. [The Don merely folds his hands in his lap, imploring The Renegade to continue with his body language. Rollins complies.] Renegade: Do you remember the Slipknot Assassin, the serial killer who went on a rampage here in Chicago two years ago? Dante: I seem to recall the name, yes. Renegade: It’s not something I could forgot. For weeks, I received strange messages from an unknown source, items from my childhood popping up in the privacy of my home without reason. There was an old baseball bat I had my name inscribed on when I was a kid, random photos left on my bed while I was gone, things like that. It finally occurred to be that I might actually know the killer in some way, although at the time I didn’t know how. [Intrigued, the Don nods.] Dante: How did you make the connection between the killer and these random belongings? Renegade: A woman named Lacey Rienking. [Lacey.] [The only woman who has ever been close to Rich Rollins.] [The only woman he would ever allow to get close.] Renegade: You see, a woman who I care a great deal about - Lacey - was one of the victims. She was kidnapped in the middle of the ordeal, and I started to receive more pictures? pictures of her bound up in duct tape, crying. It was infuriating, because the police were content to sit on their asses and wait for the inevitable execution before acting on it. Dante: (amused) We seem to be in agreement there. Renegade: The media labeled me as some sort of vigilante as I went looking for her myself? and that’s when everything clicked. The personal belongings, the pictures, all of it. I knew who this killer was, and why he was tormenting me. Dante: So, who was it? Renegade: My brother. [Silence.] [His interest now completely captivated, Don Dante leans forward onto his elbows atop the organized desk, his eyes burning with fascination. Absently licking his lips, the Don responds.] Dante: I remember this now. The Tribune followed you like a hawk until you finally found this man, correct? Renegade: Correct. Dante: And the police caught the killer - your brother - also correct? Renegade: Yes. Dante: He was sentenced to death, wasn’t he? I mean, I assume you want me to find this estranged psychotic brother of yours, is that right? Renegade: It is. Dante: Then why isn’t he still in prison if he was convicted for these crimes? Renegade: His defense lawyer pleaded insanity, and my brother did a year in a hospital instead of life in a cell. There was no physical evidence he had ever touched any the victims, save for Lacey? but his defense lawyer saw to that, too. I was willing to let it rest, hoped he would fade back into obscurity? but of course he hasn’t. You’ve heard of the recent murders along the Chicago turnpike, right? [The Don’s eyes glaze over as all the pieces slowly come together. He leans back in his cushioned Italian-leather chair, mulling over the implications of the information Rich Rollins just gave him. His expression is grim.] Dante: You believe you brother is still in Chicago, and responsible for the murders? Renegade: Without a doubt. Dante: I have to say? (he laughs nervously)? I listen to many people’s stories signore, and I’ve heard every spin on jealousy and greed you can think up. This, however? I did not expect this. I’m not taken by surprise very often Mr. Rollins. Renegade: So you’ll help me? [The Don grows quiet, his attention suddenly turned inwards over some unsaid request. Rollins reads it all over the man’s face; the help is there, but for what price? Nothing comes for free, especially when it comes from a prominent family in the Italian mafia.] Dante: Si, signore. I will help you find your brother. But I ask you do something for me first. [His face an impassive fa’ade to the whirling mix of emotion within, Rich Rollins merely nods.] Renegade: What do you want me to do? [Don Alexandro Dante shoots a guarded look toward Armand, who as per usual stands against the fall wall, watching the exchange like a hawk. Armand nods once.] Dante: I want you to kill my brother. [Silence.] [Rollins hesitates, his eyes narrowing on the stern expression of the man sitting across from him.] Renegade: Is this some kind of joke? [The Don shakes his head.] Dante: I’m afraid not. You ask me to find your brother, I ask you to kill mine. It’s a fair deal, if you knew the circumstances. Renegade: Enlighten me. Dante: My brother Bernardo has been cheating on his wife for nearly a year. His mistress is a dancer at a club that I own, and for a long time I helped protect his secret love affair from the prying eyes of his family. I begged him to call it off, signore? I threatened him, and I threatened her. But instead of hurting them directly, I told his teenage daughter - my niece, Simona - what was happening,. She was furious, and ran away from her father. She was found two days later face down underneath the Navy Pier, raped and strangled. [Rollins remains motionless, his listless eyes conveying no outward sign of remorse or sorrow for the Don’s obvious loss. Instead, he contents himself with silence, pressuring Dante to continue. After a handful of awkward heartbeats, the Don obliges.] Dante: My brother drove his daughter, my niece, to her death with his infidelity. For that, he will suffer in damnation? and I am most willing to aid him along the journey. However, I cannot kill my own brother, signore, nor can I allow anyone within my business to carry out the action. If it were linked back to me, many of the other Families would consider me a turncoat, and anyone with Dante for a surname would surely be in danger. I can’t let that happen. So I will make this offer to you, Rich Rollins? if I help you find your brother, a man convicted of multiple homicide and a known serial killer, all I ask in return is for you to take care of my brother, also a murderer in his own right. I suggest you think it over. [Murderer.] [The very word sends chills down The Renegade’s spine. He’d lived in Chicago his whole life, and was accustomed to the gruesome reality that a metropolitan lifestyle brought along with it, but he had never killed a man. Rollins was a lot of things, but a murderer he was not.] [A cold-blooded killer.] [A promiscuous husband.] [A homicidal maniac.] [A dead little girl.] [Random thoughts assault Rollins? mind, twisting and turning the situation at every angle. His eyes close as he leans back in the expensive silk-lined chair the Don had provided, his head churning like a cog in a massive machine whose purpose was a complete mystery, even to its inventor. Finally, two simple words float out of the chaos and confusion of his brain, silencing the voices in his head.] Justifiable homicide. Renegade: I’ll do it. [The Don merely nods, spreading his hands in a gesture both of gratitude and relief.] Dante: I’m glad to hear it, signore? Renegade: On two conditions. [The Don stops, eyeing The Renegade cautiously.] Dante: Which are? Renegade: First off, you need to provide me with the gun. A six-shooter revolver, preferably small enough to tuck into a pocket or the front of my pants. Dante: Done. And the second? [The Renegade spares a quick glance over his shoulder, meeting the unblinking eyes of the massive Spaniard that is Armand. Turning back to face the Don, Rollins nods.] Renegade: As you know, I’m a professional wrestler. Dante: I did. Renegade: In less than a week, my federation is throwing the biggest event on our calendar year, a pay-per-view called Live and Loud 4. I have a match against two other men, both long-time staples in the organization and obvious favorites to win. I know I could take them on my own? but I’m not one to gamble. If you can help me in any way, I would appreciate it. [Don Dante sits back, clasping his hands over his stomach as he looks toward Armand. A huge smile is already spread across both their faces.] Dante: Did you hear that, Armand? He does want us to fix a fight! [They both laugh.] [Rollins does not share the special moment.] Renegade: If you’re both finished, I’d like to discuss business, if that’s all right with you. [The Don’s smile quickly fades, as does the laughter. The deadly look etched into Rollins? face gives the Don pause. The Renegade was far more serious than Don Dante had given him credit for.] [Much like another professional wrestler the Don knew of.] Dante: All right, Rollins. I know of a? (he hesitates)? a friend who might be able to help you out. I guarantee nothing mind you, only an opportunity. But before I do, I want to make absolutely certain you want me to go through with this. The man I have in mind isn’t a thug for hire, signore. If I contact him and he agrees? well, whatever happens is your responsibility, understood? I have nothing to do with this. [The Renegade nods once.] Renegade: Understood. [Allowing a moment to pass between them in silence, Don Dante studies Rollins? determined expression, coming to the conclusion that he was worth his word. After all, in Alexandro Dante’s line of work, a man’s word was all you had.] Dante: It is agreed, then. [Reaching under his desk, the Don pulls out a file drawer and roots through stacks of manila folders for a brief moment. He quickly returns, and tosses a heavy metal object across the liquored finish of the black desk toward Rollins.] [A revolver.] Dante: My bother is on the thirty-seventh floor, wrapping up his weekly poker game. Perhaps you should be a “gambling man,” just for tonight. [Rollins shows no hesitation as he scoops the revolver up, pops the cylindrical chamber and examines the six bullets already loaded.] [He snaps the chamber back into place.] Renegade: You’re a trusting man to give a relative stranger a loaded gun. [The Don grins wolfishly, tossing a nod to his confidante in the corner.] Dante: You’re awful na’ve to think you’re the only man with a loaded gun in this room. [The Renegade’s lips a crack a wry smile, the first hint of amusement he’d shown since he walked in.] Renegade: Touch?. [And with that, “The Renegade” Rich Rollins tosses a respectful nod toward Armand and strolls out the door.] [Nearly ten seconds pass in utter silence, neither man quite sure what to say.] Armand: You think he’ll do it? [An amused smile tickles the corners of the Don’s mouth as he watches the double doors swing shut.] Dante: We will see. ~<>~<>~<>~ Two Hours Later? “Do you know how to tell if a man is lying, signore?” [Sitting across a green felt card table from one another, Bernardo Dante casually holds his cards cupped in his lap, tossing Rich Rollins an assured smile that flashes teeth too white to be natural. The sounds of Chicago waft in from over the patio railing, filling the open-air space with the general noise of the productive city below.] [Alone with this man, The Renegade wonders if the poor fool even knew what was coming. Tilting his head questioningly, Rollins flashes Bernardo a fake grin as he studies his own cards, paying no particular attention to their value.] [Bernardo continues.] Bernardo: You see, most people have ticks, my friend. A man scratches his nose, adjusts his tie or shirt lapel, you know he’s got something good. It’s subconscious, signore. Only the seasoned players know how to hide their own imperfections, create that ultimate poker face. Renegade: So why tell me? You haven’t exactly been busting my bank tonight, Bernardo. [It was true. There had been four men including Rollins when he had first arrived an hour before, all of them Italian. Business partners, relatives? Rollins didn’t know, and he didn’t care who they had been. He had been practically ignored at first; a phone call from Don Dante before Rollins had arrived had made sure Bernardo was expecting him, but it was a sensitive half-hour out on the penthouse patio, playing poker with mobsters while trying to conceal the gun tucked into the front of his blue jeans.] [Rollins was good at poker, and the fact that he was the only sober man in the suite didn’t hurt his chances, either. The stack of red, white and blue chips at his elbow was testament enough to that.] [Now alone with his new poker partner, Bernardo Dante becomes quite serious.] Bernardo: Because you don’t have any ticks, signore. Like a blank page? completely unreadable. You are good at what you do - I’ll give you that - but don’t bullshit me. You’re hiding something, my friend. Why did my brother send you? [Silence.] [A face flashes into Rollins? mind, a picture of a girl he had never actually seen. Simona Dante, the sixteen-year-old victim.] [Bernardo’s own daughter.] [Rollins? personal savior.] [Meeting his unwitting adversary’s steady gaze, Rollins slowly reaches into the front of his pants. As he casually pulls out the revolver and sets it on the green felt of the poker table, Bernardo doesn’t flinch. It was no surprise; Rollins was sure this was not the first time somebody had pulled a gun on him.] [Little did he know that it would be the last.] Bernardo: Why? [A simple question, but it wasn’t Rollins? place to answer it.] [He tries anyway.] Renegade: I’ve been called a lot of things in my life, Bernardo. A lot of things. I’m a fighter by nature; I get paid to hurt people. Bernardo: And paid to kill them, as well? Renegade: No. For this, I wasn’t paid a dime. I’d kill you for free. [Bernardo didn’t expect such a strong retort, and his face clenches up in an expression of betrayal that strikes The Renegade as comical, seeing as how they’d only known one another for an hour, give or take. Regardless, Rollins doesn’t give him the chance to respond.] Renegade: How is your relationship with your daughter, Bernardo? [A direct hit.] [Bernardo’s face freezes instantly, the pupils in his eyes shrinking to the size of pinpoints. His cheeks start to redden, but Rollins is sure it isn’t from anger. Embarrassment maybe, but not anger. The demeanor that Bernardo has now adopted is one of frightened submission, a rat caught in a trap he saw coming a mile away but still couldn’t quite dodge.] Renegade: That’s what I thought. I shouldn’t have to explain myself to an arrogant, cheating bastard of a husband like yourself, and so I’m not going to. [Rollins quickly snatches the revolver from the card table, causing Bernardo to flinch once more.] Renegade: I’ve been called a lot of things in my life, Bernardo. [He pops open the chamber, exposing the six bullets encased within.] Renegade: A liar? [He pops out one bullet, ignoring it as it clatters to the marble deck.] Renegade: ? a cheater? [Another bullet clinks to the floor.] Renegade: ? a recluse? [A third bullet finds its way to the patio deck, rolling near the edge and the industrial abyss beyond.] Renegade: ? a curtain-jerker? [Bullet number four, spinning to the ground.] Renegade: ? and a main-eventer. [As the fifth bullet leaves its chamber, Rollins whips the gun sharply, snapping the chamber back into place with the sixth and final bullet still lodged within. Carefully watching the utterly shocked expression on Bernardo’s ghost-white face, Rollins sets the chamber spinning with a thrust of his palm.] Renegade: But I am not your brother’s fucking goomba. [The chamber stops spinning and clicks into place.] [Rollins angles the revolver straight into his temple, pressing the sight deep into the soft folds of his own flesh.] Bernardo: What the fuck are you?”! [He pulls the trigger.] Click [Stunned beyond words, Bernardo can only gape at the lunatic sitting across from him. All color has drained from his face, a startling comparison to The Renegade’s serene visage of pure confidence. Bernardo’s pale face sinks another shade, however, as Rollins sets the still-loaded weapon on the card table and shoves it across toward his elbows.] Renegade: I know you want to kill me. But a real man would challenge fate before taking the life of another man. It must be my lucky day, Bernardo? let’s see if you have what it takes to be a man of his word for once. For the things you’ve done, you deserve this? and if you don’t, I’ll beat you to death with the damn thing, never mind shooting it off. [A deep, thick tension shrouds the patio as Bernardo stares wide-eyed and open-mouthed at The Renegade, then the gun at his fingertips. A single tear fills the corner of his right eye, but it manages to suspend itself against gravity, an obvious attempt for Bernardo to maintain some sort of foolish masculine dignity.] [Russian Roulette. A game played by few, survived by fewer. Rollins had just offered Bernardo the chance to kill either of them, and Bernardo knew it. But for some reason, some unexplainable and significant reason, Bernardo knows that Rollins is right. If his own would-be killer could spin the chamber and come out on top, so could he.] [Rich Rollins watches with rapt attention as Bernardo takes the revolver into one shaky hand, his lips now the color of notebook paper. As if on command, the sounds of the city below seem to fade away in the heat of the moment, locking these two men in a deadly dance with the Devil himself, tempting fate beyond sanity.] [Bernardo spins the chamber.] [A moment of anticipation.] [The chamber stops, signaled with a click.] [A moment of truth.] [Bernardo finally sheds the waiting tear, placing the barrel to his temple and closing his eyes.] [A moment of?] BANG~! [Fade to black.] ~<>~<>~<>~ Rich Rollins could picture the conversation now. “No officer, the mobster was dead when I found him. How do I know him” I just met him today, actually. Funny, isn’t it?” Only it was anything but funny. Bernardo Dante was dead, a still-smoldering hole in the side of his head emitting curled white smoke into the crisp October air. Poker chips were everywhere; he had fallen on the edge of the table as he went down, spilling the contents in every direction across the once beautiful landscape of the private penthouse balcony. Blood mingled with playing cards, a poetic symbolism if Rollins had ever seen one. And to think it was all to further his own career. Somewhere deep down, Rich Rollins felt sorry for Jamie Shock and Malcolm Kennedy. He wasn’t in the mood to lose, not this time. ~Fin~
