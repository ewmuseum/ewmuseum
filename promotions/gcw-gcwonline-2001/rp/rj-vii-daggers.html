R’j VII- Daggers

Date: unknown .:: ::: ::. The hallways were cold, as she would have expected them to be. The Order of the Hammer's Headquarters were not meant to be pleasant; they were meant to be sterile, indicative of the discipline required to work within such a body. The walls were dark wood, the floors cold marble, giving the whole place a closed-in feeling that wasn't helped much by what was going to happen today. Today, she was 99.9% sure, she was going to get ejected from the Knightmare Brigade, and quite possibly imprisoned. Of course, there was the chance that Ian Childress would feel sorry for her and intervene on her behalf. But in the split second she'd seen him today, in the ice-blue glare she'd received, she didn't think that was much of a chance either. Lia Ambrosi looked every part the lady, dressed in a pair of loose-fitting black slacks with a slimming satin bustier and loose shirt underneath. Virgil, of course, was at her side. She'd tried to convince him that this wasn't any of his concern, but it was rare that he would listen to such things, rare that he wouldn't insist in interjecting himself every time she got herself into trouble. It grated on her nerves, at times, but she could understand why. The two of them had been dragged through hell backwards in the not so distant past. There was no sense in chancing going through it again if either of them could help it. The doors would open soon. And she'd be ushered in to the council room where she'd see a bunch of people she didn't know, plus a couple she did’though likely only Fey and Ian Childress and Aine Leanan. Blaise McClancy would not testify in her favor, nor would he testify against her. She would not be allowed to see Alexis Key one last time, to see how the baby was. It was all ludicrously unfair, but complaining about it was pointless. She'd done what any friend would have done’dropped everything and run to help Vivica find Markus. Helped her to find Markus because, by the gods, Markus was part of her blood and her brood as well, and he deserved something more than being given up to Zenith's power games. But there had always been a conflict between past and present for the KB. They wanted Lia Ambrosi wiped of past ties and past connections. They wanted her sterile, except for her connections to Virgil (which they knew they couldn't break). She was to be the left hand of Ian Childress, the masked assassin, vengeance in the night. Anything else didn't fit the mold. Lia had never been a good mold-fitter. She pressed her hands together, pursing her lips, which were plum with gloss. Clenching her own palms together did nothing to assuage the nervous energy inside, knowing that any moment, they would call her in. Knowing, too, that they had likely already decided her fate before she walked in the room, that they were possibly discussing it behind closed doors at this very moment before she even gave her testimony. Virgil seemed to sense her agitation (though it wouldn't have taken very much). He pressed his hand firmly into the small of her back, his fingers sliding around to the side of her hip and squeezing. She tossed her long black locks behind her shoulders again, smiled up at him. It was then that the door to the council room squeaked for a millisecond, then glided soundlessly open. Behind it was Aine Leanan, her face drawn and serious. It both perplexed and angered Lia for a moment, because she had started to consider Aine a friend. But Aine was all business right now. “The council will see you now, Lia, Virgil.” Virgil was a secondary dealing in all of this, but by protocol, he'd have to be judged as well. He'd stopped going to Knightmare Brigade meetings, refused to answer their communications, once he'd found out about Lia's suspension. Without saying a word, Virgil Ambrosi had sent a message, clear as day. Now, they were both going to be forced to answer. .:: ::: ::. Lia didn't remember, even only twenty minutes hence, what they'd said to open the case. It was all a blur, and it wasn't that they were talking over her head’she understood the ridiculous legalese with which they'd laid out her subordination just fine. It was that she didn't care’she didn't care about their rules. She was just angry now. Angry, and wanting to speak her own piece. Her arms were folded over her chest, and she was the picture of attentiveness. Behind that, her green eyes were seething. Ian Childress could see that well enough, and as he turned from the lectern, he gave her a warning look. “Mrs. Ambrosi, at this point in a hearing, we usually give the accused the chance to speak his or her mind. Would you like to take that opportunity?” “I would,” she said, nodding vigorously enough that her hair swung forward and dusted the tops of her fingers where they rested on the table. Ian nodded, shuffling his papers. “Well, I'll start with a few questions of my own, then open it up to the council. Standard protocol applies, of course, in that we have the right to end the questioning at any time to begin deliberation.” Lia nodded. They were treating her as though she hadn't even read the rulebook when she entered. For God's sake, they had tested them on the damn protocol before they'd even been allowed to be fitted for a training uniform. “To begin with, I'd just like to give you a brief opportunity to recount the story in your own words.” Lia laced her fingers together on the table, took a deep breath, and looked up at Ian, zeroing in aggressive eyes. “I completed an assignment that I had for you, Commander Childress. I was free of all Knightmare Brigade obligations, except in coming to our meetings, and the usual duties required of Captains. Then, I received a phone call from Vivica J. Valentine, a close friend of mine, who I work with in other ventures. Markus D'Artifice, a young vampire spawned in a genetic experiment involving my blood, had been kidnapped. The young woman was rather distraught, being that she seems to have developed feelings for him, and I felt it in my power to help her find him.” She took a deep breath, lacing her hands tighter together. “I attempted the standard leave procedures, and was told that I could not go. I asserted to my CO that this was, in fact, a personal emergency, and was still denied leave. Therefore, I felt I had no choice but to take the leave myself, as it was a personal situation that required I immediately act.” “The rules do say that a Commanding Officer can at any time choose to deny leave to his soldiers.” The woman who spoke had pale skin, nearly luminescent gray eyes framed by auburn hair. It was impossible to tell her age’plainly from the European stock. She continued. “And in any case, I fail to see how helping a friend is a personal emergency.” Lia's eyes met the woman's, snapped with ire that was barely concealed. “The people who took Markus made an attack on our property, and we were not aware how safe it would be to remain in the area, nor whether the individual had made further plans to attack the rest of us.” Silence reigned for a moment. It was a perfectly logical answer to a perfectly ridiculous question, but what it all came down to was her insubordination, in the end. She knew that. Whatever the reason, it wouldn't be good enough. “What if Commander Childress had an assignment for you” Personal leave can stretch on for months, and he would have had no way to contact you.” This time, it was a gruff yellow-bearded Viking, or so she imagined him to be. “I was given no assignments for two weeks' time, and told by several people that the KB might even be on hiatus until we knew whether or not our enemies were going to move.” “But the hiatus was never ordered,” Ian Childress finished, his stony look conquering the flippant tone that was starting to creep into Lia's voice. “I had to move. Time was my biggest constraint, and I couldn't stand around for another week to see if I might possibly be ordered to fill out some paperwork.” It was completely the wrong thing to say, and it was completely obvious that it was. “I'd like to turn for a moment to Mr. Ambrosi.” Virgil jumped a little bit, stirred out of his reverie. It had the appearance of composure, but Lia knew the look well enough. His steel-blue eyes were hard, closed-off, the look he had when he was having a very difficult time preventing himself from doing some act of violence. “Yes?” He hadn't used Ian's title. Lia could practically see them jotting notes about it, and she suppressed, with great difficulty, the urge to roll her eyes. “After your wife's suspension, I'm curious to know why you declined to show up for weekly meetings, especially as you know you were her second, and therefore, her replacement if she were ever incapacitated or removed from her post in any way.” The look Virgil gave Ian was quite clear, one that resembled the face that Lia presumed her husband would make if someone asked him why he didn't just shoot his gun without first opening his eyes. “It's quite simple,” Virgil said, his voice mellifluous and cool. “I don't even see the need to answer this question. My wife kept me informed of what was happening with her, and when I found that she had been suspended, I voluntarily chose not to continue my own duties with the organization.” “So you're saying that you chose to lodge some kind of protest by just not speaking with anyone from the organization and refusing to come to meetings?” “That is exactly what I'm saying,” Virgil said, switching a stony glance to Ian's red-haired wife. Silence again. Lia wondered if this was the most cut-and-dried case that the council had ever heard for the Order. She was sure there had been others thrown out of the Order before, but perhaps not others who would willingly tell the Order where to stuff it if they tried to emphasize protocol over the personal. There were certain things worth risking your post for, especially when you'd become meaningless without a knife in your hand. “I think we're ready to begin deliberations.” It was Fey that said it. Today, she was presiding over the group. Lia didn't know how, or why, or perhaps she'd blocked it out of her mind, but they worked on some rotating basis, each person taking a turn at the head of the council, and one person serving as inquisitor for each case they heard. Ian was the last to leave the room. He cast a glance back at Lia and Virgil that was almost regretful as he shut the door behind the five-member council. It was a moment before either Lia or her husband spoke. She didn't look at him. “Doesn't look good.” “No,” he responded. “But that depends on how you define good.” “Hm?” “We lose ties, certainly, we lose our role in whatever it is they're trying to make us do right now... but we have our freedom back. We can do whatever it is that we want to do without the Order breathing down our necks every time we take a millisecond longer to jump than they want us to.” A slight smile crossed Lia's features, fading almost immediately into obscurity. He was right of course, but there was something about being released from the Order after she'd done such service to them in taking out one of their most potent enemies without so much as a thank you from anyone but Ian. That was just how it was going to be, or so it seemed. Virgil's hand stretched across the table they sat at, reaching for her own, but he stopped halfway. They fell into silence. * It wasn't long until they returned. They took their places, seeming to take longer than was really necessary to shuffle robes and chairs back into their proper spots. It was all an act, an elaborate circus they were putting on in order to make sure they had her attention, and his too. “The panel has reached a decision,” began Fey Childress, folding her hands demurely in front of her on the table. It was a posture that did not suit her in the least, being that she was typically quite the firecracker. “Based on the evidence,” began the other woman; Lia was starting to wonder whether or not she was related to Fey somehow, “We've come to the conclusion that both Mr. and Mrs. Ambrosi were entirely aware of the Order protocol they were breaking, and did so willfully. Under our provisions, this is not only grounds for suspension of duty, but for expulsion from the Order.” “Or imprisonment,” came the gruff voice of the blonde-haired man. “It was the opinion of this counselor that the two officers in question could be a danger to the operations of the Order of the Hammer if they were let free. It's a well known fact that both of them have liaisons with powers that could prove a detriment to us’could even mount a counter-attack against our purposes. It is the opinion of this counselor that individual confinement is the safest option.” “Dissentin' opinion ran,” came Aine's thick-brogued voice, and when Lia looked up at her, her face looked thin and pinched, as though she hated this, “that the security clearance of Mrs. Ambrosi, the higher officer of the two, was not high enough to warrant our concern wit' imprisoning 'em. As it's also a unique circumstance, seein' as we've never had immortal officers in the Order before, I recommended against this action, citing a lack of precedent in such cases, and also citing the fact that neither officer was cleared past Adept-I level.” What little color there was drained from Lia's face. They'd made her an officer, made Virgil a second... but it was plain now that the Order had never trusted them, not even as they'd demanded their loyalty to the utmost. “The end opinion, in a 4-1 vote, is that the accused will go free,” said Ian, his voice heavy, but without emotion. “The accused are declared guilty of insubordination to a superior officer, and due to the protocol of the Order of the Hammer, and their own rank within it, are to be dismissed immediately, without honors. Standing orders are that neither of them will be trifled with, but their records kept in our databases.” Ian looked up from the paper now, zeroing in on first Lia, and then Virgil. “And strictly off the record, I'd like to add that you're lucky three of this panel know the both of you personally. We don't intend to track you, but if you become a problem, we have your records, and you're easy enough to find.” Virgil's jaw clenched, anger hardening into fury. Instinct told Lia that if he ever saw anyone from the Order, he wouldn't hesitate to kill them. “Closing statement,” rejoined the auburn-haired woman. “would simply like to remind the Order itself that there are reasons we have rules about what sort of officers we take in. We've heretofore been extremely careful about screening who we allow into our officer corps, and I believe this is a cautionary tale about why this has to be so. Plainly, in this case, loyalty to previous personal commitments, and to the species concerned, was much more important than the duty to the Order. This is something that we can't conscience within our ranks.” Lia's jaw clenched now too. The conclusions, the signing of the papers, all went by in a blur. .:: ::: ::. Virgil and Lia didn't speak to one another on the way out the door. She was hard-pressed to keep up with him, storming as he was on legs no less than six inches longer than her own. His hand was on the doorknob, presumably about to fling open the sturdy oak door, when a voice assailed them from behind. “Lia! Virgil! Wait!” Male. And sounding more emotional than it had in the debate room. Lia turned slowly to face Ian Childress head-on and alone for the first time in months. She had once considered him a mentor and a friend, but now... now the knife in her back was clearer than it ever could have been even if someone had notified her before jamming it in. Virgil stepped protectively in front of her, his eyes narrow and never once leaving Ian's face. But Ian gave them space. He held his hands up. “My apologies. There was really nothing I could do.” “Ian, it doesn't--? she began, but she was interrupted by Virgil's snarl. “Nothing. Nothing at all. Really?” They engaged in a brief staredown. Neither would let go of the other's eyes. “You were going to be released one way or the other. You should just be glad Aine argued so passionately that you not be imprisoned, and apart from one another at that.” “Oh, bullshit, Ian!” Lia spat, stabbing a sharp-nailed finger in his direction. “That wasn't her doing, nor anyone's doing here because none of you ever had good intentions towards us! Adept-I security clearance, Ian” I WAS A CAPTAIN OF YOUR FUCKING SPECIAL FORCES!” The bitterness seeped forward now, her voice ringing off the walls of the cavernous entry hall. And Ian winced. Something broke the stone on his exterior. “I know,” he said, in a somewhat subdued rumble. “I can't do anything about that. I thought... I honestly thought...” Suddenly, his voice took on a weary note, and he scrubbed his palms across his face. “The two of you were the perfect way to bring the Order into the new age. We needed fresh blood, a different perspective from which to view this conflict, and you brought it... I had to buy your entry by making the security clearance concession, but I knew that you were going to serve us well enough to make them forget about that. People were starting to buy it, to realized the usefulness of beings that we perhaps had not considered closely enough before... and then this.” Lia gritted her teeth. Half a step in front of her, Virgil spit on the rug, two inches from Ian's boot. Ian spread his hands apart, eying them with cold honesty much more fitting of his personality. “Old prejudices die hard. I didn't want to see you drummed out of the Order, but it was practically unavoidable. I did what I could.” “You did indeed,” Virgil said, his voice a rumbling growl. Ian didn't respond, and Virgil turned again to open the door, stepping in front of his wife. He had it open a crack when Ian spoke again. “I thank both of you for what you've done.” It wasn't expected, nor entirely appropriate. Lia, green eyes afire, turned over her shoulder to look at him. “No, thank you Ian... for showing me that nothing changes.” The flap of Virgil's trenchcoat as he slid his arm around her back and ushered her towards their waiting car. The mild February wind ruffling her black hair. That was the last he ever saw of them. .:: ::: ::.
