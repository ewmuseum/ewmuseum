Me Duce Tutus Eris

10 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10 April .:: ::: ::. The McFarlane Estate Early morning The sun was rising over the McFarlane Estate, a glimmering golden coin rising from the sea-water gray of the pre-dawn skies- the old rhythm, light to dark to light again, no chance at one ever usurping the other. Even centuries couldn’t shed clarity on what would happen during a given day; experience, nor plans, were guarantees. Sitting in a rattan recliner on the second-floor porch, Lia could hear the birds awakening for the day, could see the flowers begin to strain skywards for the sun’s touch, like reaching for a lover forever a whisper’s breath away. The cushion of the chair had seen better days, but as she looked down at it, her eyes were still drawn to its sun-faded pattern. Speckled red, blue, yellow? a sea of primary colours that must have stood out quite strikingly during the snow-drenched Canadian winters. With a perfectionist’s obsession, she picked a piece of navy lint off of the cotton-puffed surface and flicked it onto the reinforced wood flooring. It was a gesture of understanding, understanding how one tiny chink could make something imperfect, how one tiny blemish could make an otherwise stupendous total package into something that looked like a cobbled-together, last-minute mess. She’d been too lenient on everyone, she knew that now. GCW was the fresh opportunity that took them all away from the constraints of Global, knowing that you’d never get past a certain point with the higher-ups there. And, the truth was, Global and Las Vegas had turned into a rewrite of WMW and Cleveland- married to a title that meant nothing because you were the one who held it, dangled by a string of hope with a promise of the world at the end of it, only to consistently get put off. Nobody could work under those conditions, nobody who busted their ass consistently, but still knew painfully that they deserved more than they were getting. Maybe that was why GCW had seemed such a lark. It was “away.” It was “opposite.” It was all the opportunities that none of them had ever been given anywhere they’d previously worked, and it was promise, metric tons of promise, rolled into spectacularly dazzling balls dropped in their laps. And like transfixed kittens, they’d all run with the promise. Except her. Slowly, in bursts of painfully obvious Technicolor, it was becoming apparent to her that while her companions had been running wild, drunk off the promise of a new promotion, a new venue to showcase their obvious talent, she’d somehow fallen by the wayside. It was perhaps a ridiculous thing to say, what with the fact that she’d plowed through the Rampage Tournament all the way to the final, even beating Rich Rollins to get there. But this wasn’t all about skill, what she was realizing right now. It was far more mental a loss than she had given it credit for. Starting with the fact that while everyone else had been adjusting to the new place, stretching their wings and beginning to ascend the dizzy heights, she’d been attending to their whims. Making sure they were comfortable and happy. Trying to help them get along with one another when a square peg didn’t want to fit into a round hole. It was more work than she had realized at its undertaking. At its core, every individual involved in making their refugee group work was strongly opinionated, none of them quite desiring to let go their hold on their own personal future due to the fact that in a place like Global Wrestling, you couldn’t. If you forgot yourself among a mass of self-serving wolves, you were eaten. Really, she didn’t blame any of them, not Vivica for her self-assured cockiness, not Virgil for fraternizing with the enemy to hold a title, not Dawn for her lioness-starved hunt for self assurance. Among them, though, she was uncertain where she fit in. And the painful truth that she had to admit was that the uncertainty stemmed from the fact that she hadn’t realized all of them could be so fractious and self-serving. She’d told them to go out there and succeed, to make GCW sit up and pay attention by being the best, by being the people to beat? but perhaps she should have specified that doing that didn’t mean completely forgetting that one was also part of a larger group. This was the end of the line, though. No more screwing around, no more putting yourself before everyone else. Because it was time for long-held plans to come together. There was no sense wasting all the hours they’d spent over GCW’s break meticulously planning how things would go, no sense losing all that because they all wanted personal glory’at least that was how Lia worked it all out in her mind. The rest of the group would soon know her stance on it. Vivica J. Valentine, her tag team partner, would be arriving early this morning from her loss at Total J-Cup. The itinerary was breakfast for all of them, and then a prolonged meeting in the common room. Probably not a pleasant meeting, but she hadn’t made any promises that it would be. More than ever now, she understood Christian Zenith’s position- put in control of a group of sharp minds and impulsive, willful personalities- told to lead, but at the same time criticized for making any move that smacked of telling them what to think or do. But this wasn’t going to become Hunting Grounds Redux. There was no room for failure, and no room for not accepting her leadership. By the end of the day, Dawn, Virgil and Vivica would all have to face those facts, and decide whether or not they could live with them. .:: ::: ::. 7:37am Riiiiiiinnnng? Riiiiiinnnng? The hollow half-buzz of a ring wasn’t giving Lia any indication as to whether or not there was anyone on the other end of the phone to answer. She rolled her eyes impatiently, her gaze drifting across the rolling gardens of the Estate. Her mind, however, was in a half-flaming rage at the moment. Because it was 7:30, Vivica was half an hour late, and nobody else had yet made it out of bed for breakfast. Not that she expected them to be there early, but Vivica, of all people- her flight was supposed to get to Toronto at six, and then she was supposed to hop a short flight to the Estate. Ten minutes, Lia could understand. Maybe even twenty. But as the clock had ticked on to half an hour past Vivica’s intended arrival time, Shadow had started to get irritated at both the selfishness and irresponsibility of her tag team partner. “Hello?” Vivica’s voice sounded aggravated and half-muffled from the other end of the phone. Immediately, Lia forced a gulp of air into her lungs- forbearance, she told herself. “Listen, I don’t have time for this kind of bullshi-? “Vivica” it’s me.” “Oh.” For a few moments, there was nothing but a cacophony, a grinding, godawful noise that made Lia hold the phone away from her ear. “What the hell is going on?” There was a frustrated sigh. “Oh, let’s see” I’m driving a piece of shit truck through God-knows where, and I’ve recently had a tangle with border guards, and the clutch of this damn thing threatening to give out every time I shift gears. Overall, you know, great day.” Her laugh was a forced, sardonic one. Lia’s brow furrowed. “Driving” I thought you were flying.” Vivica paused. “Yeah. Well. Let’s just say that something came up that caused me to have to go to a whole shitload more trouble than this is worth. Didn’t Chris talk to you about it?” “Yes, he did” I don’t suppose that I thought it would come to a head so quickly.” “Yeah,” Vivica scoffed, “You ought to know in this line of work, anything that can screw you over will.” Lia nodded- to herself. Another series of ear-wrenching noises echoed from Vivica’s end of the phone. “Vivica” will you be here soon?” “GOD DAMMIT, SHUT UP YOU SON OF A BITCH! I’LL LEAVE YOU OUT HERE TO WALK HOME!” The line went dead after that, leaving Lia to presume that Vivica simply had other things to take care of. Whatever the case, there was nothing to do now but wait for her to show up. That wasn’t a pleasing idea. .:: ::: ::. 9:45am Virgil's eyes told the truth- that despite not having been up for very long, he was wide awake. His eyes glimmered with a very active sort of concentration, one which had him seemingly attempting to memorize ever swirl in the porch's woodwork at the moment. Or perhaps it was another one of those days where he decided to exist solely in his mind-created reality. It was strange, sometimes, a creature like him who could manipulate people's perceptions at will, and yet still choosing knowingly to live in a reality that was apart from everyone else. But that was Virgil. He didn't have to make sense- she cared for him even though he didn't, even when thousand-year-old prejudices got in the way of the truth. Everyone had faults, after all. 'Where's Vivica?' It was really the first substantial thing he'd asked her all morning. But of course, it was the only question that she really couldn't answer. 'I don't know,' she shrugged, 'I talked to her a little while ago, and she said she'd be here as soon as she could. Something about having to drive up from Mexico instead of taking a plane.' 'Mm.' He said it in a cold, stoic, suspicious tone. It let her immediately to believe what she'd been suspecting all this time- that he hadn't really buried the hatchet and truly agreed with his heart to 'give Vivica a chance' like he'd said he would. His definition of things seemed to be more akin to 'give Vivica a chance to inevitably screw up.' All his daily doings were centered around waiting for that I-told-you-so moment. 'You don't believe her.' It wasn't a question at all, and the tone of her voice bent it more towards an outright accusation. 'I didn't say that,' he countered, but his blue eyes flicked away from her gaze in a way that told her he just said that in order that he not have to deal with her anger if he'd agreed wholeheartedly with her statement. 'Virgil... it's serious now, alright? We've got to go beyond our misgivings and petty distrust if we're ever going to make something out of the chances we have. We're so close to what we want to be, and yet without a little more give from you, we won't have it.' 'I see.' The expression on his face faded into one of bitterness. 'So that's what this meeting was about, then? Calling me out in front of Dawn and Vivica, making an example out of your staunchest supporter for not giving his all and putting on a happy face every waking moment?' She looked surprised. And really, perhaps she should have guessed he'd have that kind of sentiment, but she hadn't thought of it. It was just, at the moment, the confirmation of her fears about their plans falling apart before they'd ever really started. 'Virgil, that's not what I mean. I don't want you to think that...' 'What do you want me to think then? Ever since we've gotten to GCW, you've been coming down hard on me.... that leads me to believe I'm the only problem.' 'Nothing could be farther from the truth!' she rejoined, 'Just listen for a moment. Ever since Rampage, I've racked my brain about what we could be doing better, about what could possibly be keeping us from mounting that final rung that would cement our place at the top of the heap, untouchable. It's not just you, Virgil, it's a problem with EVERYONE in the group making the steps necessary to give on their personal wants just a little bit so that everyone in the group benefits.' Deliberately, as though it was a practiced dance routine, he folded his arms over his chest and crossed one leg over the other. His storm-troubled-blue eyes came to rest on her, not wavering an inch, as she'd come to expect them to look. 'What is that supposed to mean?' 'It...' She sighed softly. 'It means that whether you like or trust Vivica or not... you don't deign to wear your heart on your sleeve and show it in the ring. It means that this week, instead of going out there with any intent to officiate hard against Vivica.' 'You are asking me to cheat?' His voice was flat, the question barely intoned. But she shook her head slowly. 'No. What I'm asking you to do is play for the team that you came here to play for. I don't know how you got into the position to referee that match, but I'm asking you to use that position to stand as a united front with two of your three teammates.' 'That's what this little meeting was about?' Again, she nodded, the confidence building in her voice, sweetly laced with Czech accent. 'Yes. Now is the breaking point- the point where we decide if this is or isn't going to work, but I won't accept a no without everyone trying their hardest to follow through on the plans we've made. Understood?' He stood from his half-slouched position, his eyes bearing down on her. 'Understood.' There was a sort of dignified strength about the way he said it, as he paced off the veranda and back into the library, no doubt to have a sitting session with Cyrus- one where neither of them would speak, only sit across the table from each other playing chess. It was Virgil's way of blowing off steam when he needed to- and Lia didn't care how he did it, so long as he had himself put back together, ready to become the fast-talking, cool-headed quick-thinker he was known for being by the time he stepped into the ring at World Wide. .:: ::: ::. 11:02am Dawn, being the latest riser in the household, was still a bit groggy at the moment. But Lia, perched ramrod straight at the end of her bed, one slender arm wrapped around the bedpost, was as lively-minded as she had been since very early this morning. Dawn found herself somewhat perplexed. Though Lia had awakened her with the usual affectionate niceties, there was something about the slightly smaller woman's manner today that betrayed something else entirely, something seething below the surface, barely concealed. She stifled a yawn. 'Did you need me for something?' 'It's eleven o'clock,' Lia said. And strangely, that was all she said, as though that was supposed to mean something. Dawn racked her brain quickly, searching for the hidden meaning, but nothing was apparent. Unless... 'Oh my God, Lia, I'm so sorry, I forgot all about breakfast this morning! It's just that I was up late last night, and-' Lia held a hand up. 'Pax. Vivica isn't here yet. She's run up on some... complications. I'm not sure when she's going to get here, but I decided to let you sleep in until you woke up, since she hasn't called me back since around seven forty-five.' Dawn tilted her head, perplexed. 'But you seem like there's something bothering you.' Dawn could be terribly insistent when she wanted to be- especially when it involved hearing something that she might not particularly want to hear. She wasn't certain if it did in this case, but Lia's distant, guarded manner had to mean something. She wasn't ordinarily so cool, so close-kept. If Lia had retreated so far into her shell... then that must have meant that she'd done something to cause it. Suddenly, Dawn found her own fingernails very interesting. 'I have a few things on my mind, yes. That was my intention behind gathering everyone together. Or attempting to at least.' A sort of wry bitterness slipped into the vampire's gestures. Dawn, in an effort to avoid whatever storm was currently headed in her direction, stood and took a couple of shaky first steps out of bed. Sleep was still heavy in her limbs, along with a growing sense of dread knotting in her chest as she sat down at her dressing table to pick absently through her sleep-tangled hair. 'I'm concerned, Dawn,' Lia went on, her dark head bowing slightly, 'I've been taking stock of things ever since Rampage, and I'm not certain that I like what I see.' Something cold crept through Dawn's limbs. Of all subjects, this was probably one that she would rather avoid. 'Oh?' Lia brushed her long black hair behind her shoulders again, though inevitably, thick locks of it would fall forward again soon enough. 'It's time to step back and take stock of what we have. And I don't mean titles, either. I encouraged everyone to go forward, to make an impression, but now that we've done that, it's time to ascend the next step. Plateaus mean complacence and complacence soon turns into failure.' 'I agree.' Dawn fingered her brush lightly before picking it up, not sure where else this conversation might be leading, but still not entire comfortable with it, no matter its seeming-innocence. 'What do you plan to do now that you have the title?' The question took Dawn a bit by surprise. And honestly, it wasn't really a question that you expected to have to consider a few moments after you'd been awakened from a rather sound sleep. She chewed her lip, her brush-hand arrested halfway to her head. 'I... don't know. I talked over a few things with Caldera when he called at the beginning of this week. He wanted me to show up for World Wide, even though I'm not booked.' 'Of course. After your first successful defense, I can imagine he would appreciate your presence at the taping.' Her voice was smoothly even, betraying no emotion whatsoever. That was usually when it was time to worry- when her true purposes and true feelings were completely concealed and unreadable. Dawn nodded almost imperceptibly as she began to work the soft bristles through her long hair. She winced slightly as the early-morning wounds pulled, seemed as though they'd pull every strand out by the roots if she weren't careful. But her hair was often stubborn in such a manner, and if she just kept brushing, the pain would soon equalize itself into something bearable, one of those things that happened every morning. A few more hard strokes got her hair smoothed down into a slightly-tamer version of frizzy bed-head. As she tilted her gaze towards the other side of the table, she caught sight of Lia in the mirror- and suddenly forgot what she'd been reaching out to get. The vampire's eyes were fixed clearly upon her, calculating every move that she made, almost before she made it, if Dawn had to wager. And even looking in the mirror at Lia, Dawn had the distinct impression that she'd been watching her for a lot longer than the past few seconds. It was one of those near dread-inducing moments, though for everything inside her, Dawn couldn't figure out why. At least not immediately. But as she set the brush gently down on the glass-topped vanity, it became clear to her. Guilt. And a lot of it. Guilt that she'd been carrying around since Rampage, despite any agreements that she and Lia had made that whatever happened in the ring at the event would stay there, and would be fair play, in a situation where the rules of war were required. 'I... I'm sorry,' Dawn choked out, her voice seeming tinny and hollow even to her, though perhaps that was the sudden dizziness that trapped her inside her own perception. She'd been holding her breath without realizing it. Quizzically, but with near-to-no emotion still, Lia tilted her head. 'What reason do you have to apologized to me? I told you that Vivi-' 'Not that,' Dawn interrupted, a slight hint of misery in her tone, 'I was apologizing because... because of Rampage.' The room fell silent. Lia's face set into a stony mask from which nothing could escape, even worse than the benign passivity that it had showcased only moments ago. Somewhere, a door had shut, and it was debatable as to whether or not that door could be opened again. Dawn looked down, not able to bear the thought of having upset her further by apologizing for something if it was truly nothing to be bothered about. But it seemed to her that this morning, everything she did was bothering Lia. She made a few motions to clean up the clutter on the dressing table, but they were ineffective ones, with fluttery useless hands that betrayed the uselessness their owner felt right now. The rustle of silk attracted her attention, and Dawn looked up to where Lia was now standing, straightening out a miniscule tilt in the alignment of her satin corset that was probably visible only to her eyes. Her pretty face was set into all-business lines- no room for emotion- as she stepped deliberately towards the door, her boots knocking holes in Dawn's eardrums for all they tapped gracefully against the wood flooring. And again, those verdant eyes so similar to her own dug into Dawn ferociously. 'It's alright, Dawn,' Lia said, her voice sliding easily back into that reassuring and carefree manner that she'd taken earlier in the morning. At first, Dawn thought that was all she would say on the matter. But as she reached the doorframe, her hand caught on it and she turned back, leaning her head back into the room a little farther than the rest of her, 'Some of us just weren't made to realize our dreams.' She was gone too fast to really note what sort of expression she wore when she said it. But Dawn could have sworn against her morbid imagination that what she'd just seen quirking those rose-petal lips was an extremely bitter smile. .:: ::: ::. 1:03pm There was still much work to be done today. But, as best Lia could figure, she'd laid the ground-work for it adequately, and now the only thing to do was wait until Vivica got here to make certain that everyone understood what she was asking of them. And for once, it felt good. Good to focus on business, and getting it complete instead of feeling like she was baby-sitting for errant children. Perhaps that was a cruel thing to think about teammates that were really much closer than mere teammates, but from a business stand-point, they were rather finicky: often changing their minds on what they wanted, running hot and cold on personal matters, and contentiously scrapping over silly little things. But no more. As she stepped back into the third floor guest room that was now unofficially dubbed 'Lia's Room' she noted that even here, she would not be alone. Cyrus made a sweeping bow from the waist. 'Milady,' he greeted her in his booming, yet subdued and elegant-sounding voice. Though she found the formality unnecessary, his constant and devoted friendship was flattering. 'Good day, Cyrus. I didn't expect to see you up so early.' He gave her what passed for a smile. On a face that was normally rather stoic, that wasn't much of a change, but it was one of the many subtleties about his manner that she had learned to detect. 'My hours have been tending somewhat towards the strange lately,' he said, almost sounding apologetic for interrupting her when she hadn't been expecting him, 'But, I've found something most interesting that I know would benefit you. Something for your search.' Lia's fingers clenched inadvertently, adrenaline shooting through her in a sharp burst. 'Something substantial?' 'Yes, milady. Something that I believe should set you off in the right direction.' From his inside coat-pocket, he produced a leaf of paper, neatly rolled so that none of the ink would be marred by folding. She touched it delicately at first, afraid of spoiling its quality and thus, its usefulness, as she opened it. The first thing that her eyes caught on was not the words splayed across the page in a cramped black writing, but the seal at the top. Her eyes widened. '...the Society of Leopold?... Cyrus, where did you get this?' He nodded slightly, one of those respectful gestures that meant he was about to decline to answer her. 'Where I got it doesn't matter so much as its contents do. There are thirteen more pages of this document alone, plus a good seven more pages from their annals that contain passing references that may aid you in your search.' She swallowed hard. 'Then he's not...' 'Dead? No, milady. As of the last of those official documents, dated September 17, 2005, your brother is still alive.' Hope shot through her, nearly violently shaking her small frame, small compared to the epic that was playing inside of her right now. Most uncharacteristic for her, she stepped forward, pressing Cyrus' hand between her own. Her eyes seethed with emotion, not last among them grateful thanksgiving. 'I cannot express how much this means to me.' He nodded again, as though he understood without her having to attempt fawning all over him. To him, of course, it was just an exercise in interest, something that he had enjoyed researching in his spare time, of which there was much to be had. 'With all due respect,' he said, gently and respectfully withdrawing his hand, 'The documents are fairly self-explanatory. You'll find all of them downstairs in the library for your perusal whenever you have the time to do so. But if you need nothing else from me, I believe I'll be going to get some rest.' 'Go ahead,' she said, 'You deserve as much rest as you need after helping me this much.' He didn't take long to make his exit, offering his obeisance with a bow one last time before he slunk off to whatever recess of the McFarlane Estate it was that he called his own. For the life of her, Lia hadn't been able to figure out where he slept yet, and he'd been here for months. She was only able to contemplate that for a moment, though, before she was off to the library like a shot fired from a cannon. Once there, her fingers lightly pawed through the papers. Though in number they were sparse, the tiny shorthand of the writing meant that a great deal of information would come from these pages. Cyrus had taken the liberty of jotting down a sort of key to their code on a sheet of legal paper that was ripped off its pad and lying to the side of the paper-clipped stack. As though opening a Christmas present, Lia uncapped a pen and flicked on the desk lamp, prepared to go to work until Vivica arrived. Her brow furrowed as she slowly absorbed herself in the task, cross-checking the key and the document several times to make sure of its meaning. She hadn't been at it very long, though, before a horrid cacophony disturbed her thoughts. With gritted teeth, her head snapped up to look out the picture window beside the desk... 'What in the hell?' A huge, unmarked truck was pulling up in the Estate's driveway, lumbering off in the direction of the parking pad. It reminded Lia of a refrigeration unit, or perhaps one of those trucks she had often seen on the road labeled 'Animal Control.' Her senses were on alert, or at least puzzled for a few moments as it trundled to a squeaking stop in a parking space, and there was a groaning sound as the driver yanked up the parking break. A door opened and shut and Lia could see a familiar honey-blond head underneath the stark white spotlights over the parking pad. All this, of course, meant that the opportunity to tie up loose ends and step forward with a new outlook was fast approaching. There was a lot of work to be done, but finally, there was no more waiting to accompany the antsy, work-ready feeling. It was time to begin in earnest. .:: ::: ::.
