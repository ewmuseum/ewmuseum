Stephen's Side

16 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 16th May 2006 Please allow me to introduce myself. I’m a man of wealth and very, very bizarre taste. I’m also, rather ironically, a saviour of lives. Quite a contrast to my previous lifestyle. But I’ve learned, over the years, that life, the universe and everything is a machine, some assembly required. To enjoy life, you simply have to perform that assembly. Of course, we all have our parts, parts to build with and parts to play. I suppose, be it part of a machine or a part in a play, I’ve inadvertently become a collector of parts. But enough from me? it’s always more fun when you hear more than one side of a story. ~ “Stephen’s side.” ~ My name is Stephen McDouglas. I don’t mind whether it’s McDouglas or MacDouglas, you’d be hard pressed to find someone who could spell it correctly in my line of work anyway. You get used to it after the 387th time someone calls you McDonald’s by mistake. My line of work? I’m the chairman of Stockman & Saki INC, an electronics manufacturer. I’m not incredibly wealthy. At least, I’m not anymore. I have a sizable home in Tampa, Florida, where I used to live with my wife, Bonnie. And that’s the end of the fairytale. The melodrama begins here. My wife has been sick since her teenage years, stricken with some disease that the doctors refuse to diagnose. I’d like to say that it’s just them being greedy bastards and milking us for the heavy fees, but I don’t think anyone could drag this out for so long. She was a quite girl when I met her, the kind who always sat alone at the dance and usually left alone too. I think looking back, it was probably the mystery that attracted me to her. But now I’m 31 and she’s 27 She only leaves her bed for the bathroom, even then, in a wheelchair I have to help her in and out of because she has no energy. She spends most of the day and night asleep. It’s? taken quite a toll on our sex life. But at the same time, I still love her? y’know? The sound of ice cubes clashing together inside the glass marked the exclamation point of it all. Here I was, the Rising Sun again. Sitting at the bar, drowning my sorrows in another scotch on the rocks. My unfortunate captive audience happened to be Derek Bale, an old friend of mine who I knew from college. I was the straight-laced guy who paid attention, he was the guy who has no respect for the lecturers and probably spent the early years of high school spitting on the teacher’s seat before it got old. Either way, he’d grown up to be quite the character, a computer criminal who had pretty much whatever it was you wanted if it could be burned onto a DVD. “Steve, I told ya, man.” He slurred over an almost empty Budweiser. “There this surgeon way off the west coast, some kinda faith healer that works miracles for the right price. Manse Island, I can tell you how to get there if you want.” I shook my head, knowing that Derek was most likely throwing me a lead he’d never checked out and for a high price. He slid over a piece of folded printer paper, along with enough money for one last scotch and slid his baseball cap back over his head, pulling his dirty blonde ponytail through the back and said his goodbyes. Even if it was only an hour out, it was good to get away from having to attend to Bonnie. I didn’t like leaving her, but the feeling of being chained down this much? a guy needs his freedom. Sadly, it was one hour too much. I came back to find her on the floor of our bedroom, fallen out after trying to get into her wheelchair on her own. Bonnie’s pretty? pretty enough so that even with the sweat and tears running down her face, even with that expression of pathetic embarrassment? all you gotta do is look into those baby blues and you know why you do this. But Derek’s offer was in my pocket? I could feel it as I lifted Bonnie back into bed. Her arms were wrapped as tight as they could manage around my chest and I could hear it crumpling in my breast pocket. It was like I’d been set up to look at it. And just like Derek’s offers and my own marriage? I had no idea what I was getting myself into. ? The flight was surprisingly smooth? the plane itself was practically a flying hospital, complete with relaxing music and staff on hand. It was probably the first night that I’d ever spent away from her, not in the same bed, since the first time she collapsed. I remember it like yesterday, biggest scare of my life. It really puts things in perspective when you carry your wife into a roadside diner, screaming for a phone and repeatedly pulling those long strands of black curly hair from out of her face to stop her choking on it as she gasps for breath. As we approached the island, I just had this feeling of hope. The sun was shining brightly and even Bonnie was stirring. Something inside me forced me to laugh. It was the first time in forever that I’d felt things are just? going to be okay, I guess. But as I approached the island, I think I was at ease enough to question why it looked like something from something Jules Verne would have made. From just off the beaches to the middle of the island, save for what looked like a gigantic smokestack and a tunnel from a smaller island, which appeared to be our runway, the thing looked like it was covered in a glass igloo. We touched down and at that moment, I almost broke down in tears as I felt my wife’s hands on my shoulders. She was standing? leaning on me, but standing. I was wondering, as we descended the staircase, exactly what could be making the change so suddenly. Maybe the dome kept in some kind of miracle cure in the air, or maybe it was mind over matter. I didn’t care. Whether it was technology, faith, Bonnie was standing. Walking. Moving on her own. “Stephen” let me walk there.” She said to me. It was like the birthday present I never got. I finally gave in and tears began rolling down my cheeks as we both walked the tunnel to the mainland. We got closer to what I guess was the entrance to the hospital? a massive set of gates, all decorated with flowing vine patterns and golden leaves. Behind them, a massive square building with a pair of statues at each side. I didn’t get a good look at who they were of? for a moment, I thought I’d seen him before, someone in a movie, but I didn’t have time to look at statues with spiked hair and smiles. The gates opened and we were greeted by a man in a black suit. He straightened up his little red necktie and cleared his throat, speaking in an accent I could only place in something as vague as Europe. “Welcome to Manse Island. I’ll be your attendant, Mr. Dyango.” “Hi.” I replied. “I’m Stephen McDouglas, this is my wife, Bonnie.” “Ah, the patient” But I thought she couldn’t walk?” “Well, she-? Bonnie interrupted me. She could hardly contain herself. “I couldn’t. It’s been years, but” I don’t know, ever since I got here, I can walk just a little!” “I see?” Mr. Dyango said, scratching his chin. He was exceptionally well-groomed, clean shaven, nicely done hair, everything about him looked like it had been done moments before we got here. “Well, I must insist we don’t allow you to overdo it.” And with a snap of his fingers, two more people in white jumpsuits that covered everything on their bodies except their faces came out from the lobby, one with a wheelchair, the other with a flat, book-shaped hunk of plastic with a few wires hanging from it. “This machine here will notify us of anything special, such as how her condition has suddenly changed. I am afraid that we cannot, however, allow you inside the hospital, sir. In the meantime,” he indicated towards a pathway leading around the hospital, off into the distance and holding out a card. “You may proceed to your accommodations.” I took the card from him. It was striped purple and white, with a logo on the corner, what looked like a snake trying to eat itself from the tail. I figured it was like those two snakes you see on medical symbols, or something. “What do I do with this?” I asked, looking as dumb as a brush. The guy hadn’t cracked a smile since we got here, just looked like some sort of tour guide on a bad day having to behave tolerant at best. “Just flash it if anyone asks you to see your card. It indicates you’re a visitor and frees you from the charges of any expenses. Also, we will use it to identify your room so we may bring you your luggage where you’ll be staying.” Wow? just, wow. I glanced up, looking at what appeared to be a multi-storey hotel. I could hear laughter in the distance and began wondering if I’d actually died on the plane and gone to Heaven. I stuffed the card into my pocket, then began to walk away. “One more thing, Mister McDouglas.” I was a little irritated that he waiting until I was about to leave, but patience is a virtue, after all. I turned to see him holding out a clipboard and a pen? gold plated and everything. I’m just glad I was free from expenses, touching that thing looked like a million dollar charge. “For the administrative purposes, please sign your name here and here” he asked, pointing to two dotted lines at the bottom. It was a single sheet of paper, no small print or print too small for me to see. The writing was big and bold, three bullet-points. 1. The patient must remain on the island for one year after initial treatment for security reasons. 2. Upon completion of the treatment, the fees must be paid in full. 3. For the first month after the treatment is complete, patients are not allowed to have physical contact with visitors. Without thinking, I quickly jotted my name down. Three simple rules in exchange for Bonnie’s happiness? I’d give away the entire company for her. At least, that was what I thought at the time? To be continued...
