Date: May 21th, 2008 They called him Gladius Marius because the locals said he was so talented with the sword that he had to be a descendant of the War God. Gladius certainly looked the part, with blond hair that dazzled like the sun and blue eyes that sparkled like the ocean. Women came from all over the region just to watch him buy bread with his wife Virginia. Each of them looked at her with jealous eyes, so deep with envy that by all rights she should have died a hundred times over. But Virginia stayed alive and in so kept Gladius alive, because everyone knew he would surly die without her. She was his pillar of strength and his battle cry. Gladius? opponent’s learned to fear her name, to tremble at its mention. But the small woman with brunette hair was far from scary. She was purer than the winter snow and smiled brighter than any candle. The only time anyone ever saw her frown was on days Gladius had to battle. She would be the only person in the entire city that did not attend the events. She simply could not bear it. Today she would not smile. She could not smile as she watched him lace up each strap on his boot, his fingers nimbly working the leather with great care. The leather was old, the first he had ever owned, and he knew it would cost too much to have it repaired should he break it. So he took each time with his boots, getting each loop correct and making sure everything would hold. Virginia stood in the doorway, watching him. She body pressed up against the wood and she hoped as she watched that today the leather would snap. Then, she thought, he would have to go to the tanner and get a new piece. He wouldn’t be able to compete, that would mean, and she wouldn’t have to hold her breath for three hours. When it didn’t break Virginia pushed herself from the wooden frame and made her way over to Gladius. Noticing her approach, he turned to her and smiled. Oh, she should kill him for that smile. “Please don’t do it” she asked, and then begged, “please don’t.” She ran her fingers up and down his muscular arm, her body locking up each time her nail passed over a scar. He had many scars and each time she would wince as if she had just suffered the brutal blow herself. “You could die” she says as her finger passed over another proof of his battles. Gladius rose from his seat, his massive frame now towering over her. He wrapped her in his arms, covering her like a blanket of comfort, and whispered “there is always a chance for death.” She pushed away from his embrace, unable to feel those horrid scars any longer. “But I would rather face death, staring him in his eyes, and know freedom than I would us live in a hobble as a slave.” Gladius grabbed his sword that rested on the table. It was barely more than a large slab of metal with a sharp edge on one side, the same used by the foot soldiers on the front lines. He hadn’t bothered to try and purchase a more elegant sword nor had anyone gifted him a fine weapon yet. But in his hands, that piece of steel was as glorious as any weapon in all of Rome. “With this,” he told her as he brought it to shoulder level, “I will buy your freedom and we will escape from Rome together.” Placing the sword in its home on his hip, Gladius grabbed the large metallic mask on the table and slipped it over his head. It was the finest thing he owned, and most likely the most expensive. He would have no doubt sold it off if Virginia would have allowed him. The helmet was in the shape of a roaring lion, blood stains covered it gave the cold metal an eerie living feel, one that scared Virginia ever so. Putting her hands over her face she turned away and left, frightened of and for the man standing in her home. Gladius? expression hid behind the mask as he turned away and walked out into the cold night. In the distance he could already hear the boisterous crowd warming up as they packed the amphitheater. Under the soft, pale moon he walked toward his next battle; Virginia watched from the window from their bedroom, her hands clutching tightly the silk gown he had given her as a wedding gift. “Please come home” they both said silently as the crowd again roared to life in the darkness. --------------------------------------------------------------- Scott Drake sat alone in his locker room, diligently lacing up his boots. He paid special care to make sure each lace was perfectly done. It was one of his rituals, spending so much time lacing his boots, as he gave himself time to visual the way the match would go. In his head he could see each move he would attempt along with all the various ways he might be countered or reversed. He tightened another lace and he pictured all the ways he would then counter that counter. It was a mental game of chess, one he felt dire to win. When he couldn’t think of a way to counter or reverse he would undo his laces and start back from the beginning until he got it right. Some called him obsessed. He saw himself as wrestling’s version of an Eagle Scout, only he had much better taste in an outfit. His prematch ritual was interrupted when Tessa Windsor walked into the room. Scott stopped with his laces and looked up at her, the women who’s hand he had vowed to take. “You don’t have to do this” she told him coldly, “it won’t accomplish anything.” When he said nothing and started going back to his laces she approached him, poking him in the chest with her index finger. “You don’t need to do this” she again said, this time enforcing it with her small finger. “You should be thinking about your obligations to your family instead, you know, the people who would actually like to see you.” Scott said nothing, instead going back to his laces. Tessa took a step back, shaking her head. “You don’t need to be running around taking matches you don’t have to, Scott.” She tried to catch his eye but he remained focused on his laces, most likely barely even paying her any attention. That didn’t stop her though, “it’s not like we even need the money, so I don’t know why you’re bothering.” Finished with his laces, Scott stood from the bench and turned to her, his fianc’e, and told her “it isn’t about the money.” “Then what, Scott?” she asked him, “what is it about?” “It is about pride” he said, holding his chest out, “it is about honor.” “And you don’t have that?” “No.” “And this will get it for you?” “I don’t know.” He paused, which was long enough for Tessa to let out a sigh. “So then why do this?” she asked him again, feeling like she had just ran in circles with him. Scott walked out to the center of the locker room and looked her dead in the eyes, “I cannot and will not just allow myself to fade away. I need to make a legacy for myself and prove I can stand on my own two feet. Once my legacy is intact, then I can retire peacefully and we can finally be alone as a family without any more matches or appearances or late night flights across the globe just to take pictures and greet fans.” He grabbed his championship belt from off the bench, holding it to allow the light to reflect off the gold plates. Tessa stared at it hard, stared at him, and walked away shaking her head. She opened the door to leave and looked over her should at him, “I’ve heard this song and dance before and frankly, my feet are getting tired.” Resonating from down the hallway was the roar of the crowd as they soaked up the action taking place before them. Scott slung the belt over his shoulder, watching as Tessa walked away. Again the crowd exploded with cheer and Scott looked at his own reflection in the gold. Staring deeply into his eyes he could see a lion ready to pounce, to stand talk and let roar with a blood curling victory chant. Turning off the light, Scott Drake made the long walk down the dim hallway toward his future, toward his legacy he was ready to embrace. --------------------------------------------------------------- Even without the helmet, Gladius wore a steely look about his face. His expression was cold and never changed, like it had been hammered on by a bitter blacksmith. Others did not have the same look about them; many were cheering and smiling graciously as he passed them by. They offered him congratulations and shook his hand as he headed down the hall in the soon-to-be empty amphitheatre. They smiled to him and patted his broad shoulder as he passed, even as he headed toward the door at the end of the hall. None slowed him, none fazed him. He kept walking toward the door. Pushing it open, he slipped in as soon as there was room, and shut it as soon as he entered. With his sword at his hip and his helmet under his arm, he bowed to the man sitting behind the desk. The man lowered his head just a bit for a formality and motioned that Gladius could return erect. On the desk are a litter of papers and a stack of coins, all neatly placed on top of each other in front of the man behind the desk. “Excellent job, Marius!” he cried out, “you have certainly earned this.” He counted off four of the coins and presented them to Gladius, who gladly took them with earnest. “If you keep this up, you may just catch the eye of the Emperor!” Gladius bowed again, “thank you, sir” he told him as he pocketed the coins into his helmet. They hit with a hollow clang that vibrated off the stone walls. Turning, he exited the door just as he had entered it, only this time there was no longer the gathered masses wishing him well. With his coins, his helmet, and his sword he made the trek back toward his home and his beautiful Virginia. The only people who occupied the streets at this hour were the drunkards, tossed from the bars, and the beggars. Gladius made sure to avoid each as he walked, taking care to keep to his own thoughts. Coming up to a very small shanty of a house, Gladius stopped and smiled, that cold steel melting away into a warm kiss. “I won.” Virginia nodded her head and allowed herself to seep into his arms, scars or not. “I know,” she told him as she enjoyed listening to his heart beat for her. Her eyes lined up with the amphitheatre, “they always cheer the loudest when you win.” Reaching into his helmet, Gladius produced the four coins and held them up for her to see. “Soon,” he told her, “I will be able to buy you your freedom. I promise, no matter what I must do, I will see you free.” She didn’t say anything, she just smiled to the man she loved so deeply and hugged him. Gladius pulled her in close to his heart, letting her warmth keep him from the cold Roman air that blew through the streets. She held him in her embrace, patting at his brilliantly golden locks that even in the darkest of nights seemed to light the sky like Apollo’s chariot. She felt his body heave up and fall, his head dropped onto her shoulder. Virginia felt a drop of rain fall from her husband’s face, hitting her on the shoulder. She said nothing but wrapped her hand around to the back of his head and comforted him. “I killed a man” Gladius said, weeping to the only person he knew he could. Virginia placed a delicate finger to his lips and moved him from the streets and into their small home. --------------------------------------------------------------- Scott Drake walked through the halls; his body was wretched with pain. He felt sore even in his eyes and hair. He didn’t even know his hair could feel sore, but at that moment, he was positive it was. People looked at him as he passed, the grim expression stretched across each face and onto the next. He got tired of looked at them, all of them with that same damned expression, so he kept his eyes turned forward and focused on the end of the hall. He knew what waited for him at the end of the hall, a 1976 Chevy Camaro. To the untrained eye it looked like a yellow and black rolling junk pile, but he knew its true beauty. With that car, there was more than meets the eye. Waiting for him with his car was a man in a simple dress suit. He adjusted his glasses when he noticed Drake approach, tapping in his right hand a plain white envelope. “With the kind of money you pulled in tonight, it should certainly soften the blow.” Drake wanted to punch him square in the jaw but he thought it might mess up his paint job on the car if he did. He didn’t need that on top of the night he had been having. Instead he took the envelope that contained his night’s pay and went on his way without saying a word. It took a few late night hours, but Scott Drake pulled into the driveway of a gorgeous Cape Cod home. Every detail of the home looked lavish, something that helped ease Drake’s weary body. Cutting off the engine, he examined the house and let out a sigh, noting that all the lights were turned out. “Figures” he mumbled as he reached across the passenger’s seat and grabbed his stuff. Walking inside, he found Tessa to be waiting for him in the kitchen. She wore her nightgown he had bought her last Christmas, but certainly did not look merry in it. As soon as she saw him pass through the hall and head into the kitchen she asked, “So was that pride out there?” “Just because I lost,” he said, “doesn’t mean I should give up.” He allowed his belongings to drop onto the tile floor, ignoring any noise it made in the process. “I’m going to fight on and prove my worth.” She just shook her head, looking at him in the eyes, “you just don’t get it” she said before turning away from him and leaving. Scott dropped down into the chair she had previously occupied and allowed his bones to rest for a moment. He suddenly felt very sore again, as if he had just relived the entire match. With what energy he still had from the fight, he used to slam his fist against the table. It would be the only good shot he got in all night. --------------------------------------------------------------- The table was set modestly, but with care and consideration. Gladius sat across from Virginia, enjoying the view. She would take a small bite from her fork, look over to her husband and smile. Gladius would return the smile and take his own bite. Their routine is rudely interrupted when the door swung open, crashing into the wall behind it. Gladius jumped from his chair, ready to reach for his sword. Two men in fine silk clothes walked into their homes, men Gladius recognized as assistants to Senator Claudius. One of them stepped up to face Gladius, “the honorable Senator Claudius requests your presences. You are to come with us at once.” Gladius looked to Virginia, and she nodded that it was fine. Standing up, she grabbed the plates on the table and started to wrap up the remaining food for later. With the two men, one at each side, Gladius is escorted out of his own house and headed toward the Capital. The Capital stood victoriously over the rest of the city, a thing of modern marvel for the people. Stunning ivory pillars stretched high toward the heavens, so high that it made Gladius? neck hurt to stare up at them. Following the two men, Gladius found himself in a room filled with expensive drapes and clothes, all wrapped around the room in splashes of color. To the back of the room, in what could only be described as a throne of sorts, was Senator Claudius. Gladius and the men bowed, as was the custom when meeting Claudius. It was always common for a man to bow to someone of higher authority, but Senator Claudius enjoyed seeing them bow down further when in his presences. The chair deserved extra mention, as it was so finely crafted that it would be a shame not to talk further about it. Hand carved of both ivory and wood, every bit of detail was paid to ensure the chair had no rival for miles. Small jewels adorned the chair to allow it a shimmer when the light hit just right. Every time Gladius had ever seen Senator Claudius, which hadn’t been many, he was always in that chair. Many of the locals speculated that Claudius could not rise from where he sat, though none publically stated such for fear of his authority. Once Gladius was before Claudius, things remained silent while Senator Claudius looked him over, admiring him. His eyes glistened, washing over every tight curve on Gladius? body. Senator Claudius, a man twice the age of Gladius, smiled down at him. “Welcome Marius!” he cried, grabbing a chalice filled with deep red wine. He poured the blood colored wine down his throat, wiping his mouth with the back of his hand, “I have longed to meet you in person!” Gladius marched up the steps to the throne, kneeling down before Senator Claudius and kissed the back of his extended hand, remnants of sweet wine teasing his lips. “I hear you are the man,” Claudius started, “that married the fair Virginia, is that so?” “Yes” Gladius confirmed, “It is true.” “Ah how marvelous!” the Senator exclaimed, his hands clasped together, “oh she is a beautiful girl. You are quite fortunate; she is a very loyal worker.” Gladius nodded his head, though grinded his teeth behind his lips, “and you are a most noble server.” Gladius stepped back, looking at each of the assistants that had leaded him here before he turned and looked again upon the Senator. “I must inform you though,” all eyes, as Gladius expected, turned to him. “I intend to pay you for Virginia so that she may be free. We start to start a family.” Senator Claudius thought on those words, chewing on them with a wrinkled brow. “That, I take it, means you shall not be competing any more in our games?” He took another drink from his chalice, this time much slower; his eyes stayed on Gladius. “It is a shame, I guess, I have not seen such a fighter as yourself in quite some time.” “You have” he went on, “the most glorious form when you bring the final blow down onto your opponent. It is a thing of beauty to watch you fight, a gift the Gods have surely granted you. Why I have told my men many times that I just love watching you.” “In fact,” Claudius motioned toward one of his men, turning his gaze over to him, “I was just tell them that I would love to have you on as my personal gladiator.” Senator Claudius smiled to Gladius, taking yet another sip of the blood colored wine. “I am sorry,” Gladius told him, causing Claudius to stop mid sip, “but after I have earned what I must to pay for Virginia’s freedom, I will leave.” Reaching into his pouch, Gladius produced the four coins he had just earned along with twelve others he had with him, and handed them over to Senator Claudius. “This is what I can for now, but I promise I will pay the rest when I can. Now I am sorry but I must go, I have another competition tonight.” Senator Claudius waved him off, letting him know if was fine to leave, telling him “if you must go.” Gladius turned and made his exit as he had come, though this time without any assistance. When he was gone Senator Claudius turned to one of the men who had brought Gladius before him and told him “make sure his next fight is his last one” with words spoken from blood soaked lips. Once arriving home, Gladius entered in a huff, and Virginia could tell instantly that his meeting with the Senator had not been enjoyable. Seeing her he ordered her to “grab anything of importance” and before she could ask why he answered the question, “we are leaving tomorrow, we will flee the city.” Gladius, gathering things as he spoke, turned to Virginia, “you will leave first and I shall follow after the next battle.” She stopped him, placing both hands on his chest, “After the battle I will pay Claudius the rest of the money and return to you.
