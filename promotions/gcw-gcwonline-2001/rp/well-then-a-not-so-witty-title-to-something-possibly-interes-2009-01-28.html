Well then (A not so witty title to something possibly interesting)

28 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: January 28, 2009 Say that again. They were on opposite sides of the bed when the sun poked its nose through the curtains of the third floor. Two tangled entities, one with a familiar shape, remained silent planted face first into poor quality hotel pillows. Blankets did their best to cover the true story of the night but bare skin and empty jeans told it all. Jay, taking over the left half of the king-sized mattress, turned over with a hefty sigh and quickly blinked his eyes open. The light brought his hands to his face but did nothing to stir the well-endowed woman beside him. Not even a knock at the door. Terror sat up and eyed the door with surprised eyes as he shook off the slight rigamortis. A check of his breath returned signs of whiskey but that was only a guess at this point. The knock came again, this time with a voice to follow. "Mr. Talley, a message." He turned to face the girl, certainly more girl than woman, and realized she wasn't moving. For a moment he figured she had died. Too much alcohol. Maybe the drugs. He was sympathetic but that lasted a mere second before he shook his head and pulled the twisted sheets off his legs. His feet sank into the carpet as he stood vertical and they continued to sink as he made his way across the room to the door. Another knock started. Jay opened the door as the man's hand came down for a third time and looked miserably at a well dressed gentleman in hotel garb. His nameplate said he went by Dan. "It's early Dan." Shaken by the realization that he was recognized, Dan remained quiet. Jay pointed at the badge bringing forth his not-so-thunderous voice. "I know and I'm sorry about the inconvenience. A man delivered this here a few minutes ago, said you needed to see it right away." His hand came out from his pocket holding onto an unwrapped letter. Jay took it. "Did he leave a name?" "Nothing, only a note. Said he needed you to see it." "Have you read it?" "Against hotel policy sir." "Fair enough." He passed it between his fingers and thought for a moment about the man. Who it could be, how they found him, what the urgency was. "Thanks." He didn't wait for the reply while shutting the door. Back inside, the girl was still out cold. He flicked his brows up and walked to the couch. The letter unfolded easily and read the same. In handwritten print it stated: Jay, Meet me at the pub on fourth. 4:00. The wall clock read three o'clock as he glanced its way. For a few seconds, he stared at the floor. A continuing cycle ran through his head as he tried to think of something, anything. Nothing was making sense to him. Not even why the girl wasn't awake. A minute had passed as he looked at the clock again, time was moving slower. He stood again deciding to take his mind off the note. He did have an hour. His attention turned back to the girl. In all honesty he couldn't remember what she looked like. Her eyes, hair, anything distinguishing about her. She was placed on the drunken side of his memory the night before and had been deleted before he could wake up and remember. As he approached, he could detect the smell of vomit. Her pants were the reason. A large stain coated the bottom soaking into the carpet. The closer he got, the less he remembered. She wasn't a blonde like he had thought, a red head instead. With his calloused hands he tapped her. It did nothing. He tried again this time flipped her onto her back. She was pretty. A few freckles covered her cheeks along with a long smudge of red lipstick. Her eye shadow was a crisp black to accent eyes that he pictured would be green when the lids moved. He shook her again, "Hey, wake up." Still nothing. Another minute of paranoia came by. Again, he was afraid she was dead. The drugs. The alcohol. All of it. She must have overdosed. Then came a glimmer of hope. She vomited. It covered the dent where he laid earlier and colored the sheets greenish brown. Her eyes flickered to life and just as he suspected, they were brilliantly green. As her head snapped back to the pillow she began to gag and cough. Jay stepped forward. "Come on now, you're ok." She continued to cough. "Let it out, yeah." After a solid two minutes she came to and laid silent. Her gaze was trancelike and pointed directly at Jay. After another minute of recovery, Jay spoke up. "You ok?" She was hesitant but was able to speak. "Where am I?" "My hotel room," he said reassuringly. "Who are you?" Oh shit he thought. "My name's Jay. I guess I was out with you last night." "Where?" "Good question." She stopped and blinked a few times. The vomit was beginning to become unbearable to smell. "How did I get here?" "I don't know. Walked maybe?" "I don't like this." Jay glanced away from her and at the side table. A tall bottle of half drank whiskey and the remnants of some powdery substance remained in the open. "I didn't like take advantage of you though. I mean, I don't think. Concentual and all. Yeah." "How do you know?" Her tone was on the defensive. "I wouldn't. Just not me." "Who are you?" "Jay." "Like who though. Why you?" He grinned, "I wrestle. Professionally. You might have noticed -" "No." "Oh." Another moment passed with no talking. Jay tried to keep the conversation up. "Who are you?" "Well if that doesn't classify one night stand." "I swear I knew last night. Must've just slipped. You know?" She rolled her eyes and coughed up more remnants of the night before. She wiped it on Jay's pillow. "Caroline." He froze. Like a bullet hitting point blank, he felt the name tear through his heart and pass out the opposite end. "Wait," he paused, "you're Caroline?" "Unless I got it changed last night." Jay stood from the bed and looked at her one last time. "Sorry." He turned and found his clothes, gathered them, and began for the door. She sat up. "Are you serious?" He never looked back. His feet carried him through the door and into the hall where he collected himself. Once his jeans were on he glanced at his watch, he still had twenty minutes. Jay made his way to the elevator and down the shaft until on the main floor. He wanted to be anywhere but there. __________ You're who? The bar was only a few blocks from the hotel and Jay stirred a beer as he waited for the clock to say four. Caroline was a distant memory by now as he focused on the letter again. His purpose was still unclear and he had a feeling that it would remain so until the mystery man showed his face. He wanted to say it was Caldera, it usually was. Caldera though never kept himself mysterious with Jay. Perhaps it was Murray messing with him. Perhaps. The clock turned to four ten by the time a new entrant arrived and that entrant was a woman. She took a seat beside Jay and smiled politely. Jay nodded. "Miller Chill," she said toward the bartender. Jay struck up conversation. "That any good?" "Better than Heineken I'd say," she said nudging toward Jay's drink. "You think? What makes it so?" She flipped her dark hair, "Just is." "You must have been in debate club." "Must have missed you there." Feeling the cold shoulder Jay nodded and began to stand. "Nice chatting with you." He asked the bartender about the bathroom on the way past and with a smile he pointed him around the corner and down the stairs. The stairs themselves were slim, very slim. As well, the light was out forcing the climb to be slow and steady. At the bottom, there was still no light. No indication of a door. "Hello." He felt a hand on his back and immediately spun around swinging his fist. It connected with something. Jay searched but it was still too dark to see. Within a second, another hand grabbed him taking him off guard. "What the fuck!" Two large arms grabbed him and suddenly the light turned on overhead. Jay stared straight at a man mopping up his now bloody lip. "Nice shot Jay." He balled up a tissue and tossed it onto the floor. "Who are you?" The man was in a black suit and had a graying goatee. His hair was longer and swayed as he moved closer to Jay. "I'm the guy you want to talk to." "I want to go to the bathroom, that's all." He tried to break free but the muscular man behind kept his full nelson tight. "Jay, do you want to hear what I have to say or do you want to go back upstairs and make a fool out of yourself?" He shook his head, "Who are you?" "I know something about someone. That's all." "I need a name." "No, you need to settle Jay." He was breathing heavy now and out of energy. Stopping the fight Jay went limp lowering his head for a second. "You sent the note?" "I wrote it, yes." "Go on." "Jay, it's about Caroline." He froze as he did in the hotel room. Flashes of an old school mobster movie played in his mind as he found a second wind and began to struggle. "What did you do to her?" "Settle Jay, nothing. She's still in her cell." "Don't talk about her like that." He grinned and moved even closer. His voice lowered to a whisper. "She's not meant to be in there Jay. You know that." "You better have more to say than that." "It wasn't her. She wasn't behind the burning of your place." He paused and grinned even wider, "She was framed." "Jordan White." "Close." "What are you saying?" "In life there are times where things must be done to reassure someone succeeds. Remember a month back, when you and White were fighting. Who was the guy in his ear Jay?" He thought for a minute before blurting out, "Murray." "It was a well crafted plan Jay. He took your head out of the match and we all know how that went." "How do you know this?" He backed up and allowed his voice to pick up again. "I witnessed it." He let Jay chew on the idea. "I was on that street that day. I saw him run out of your complex and I watched the flames color your home. Jay, Caroline was framed." "What was in it for him?" "Think of this. Murray gets White the title, Murray believes he's a shoe in against Knight in the rumble. Knight actually wins. Instead of fighting for the gold, he's fighting the very man he royally screwed." The more he heard the more he believed. The nameless man continued to ramble. "I don't know what you can do for Caroline but I know what you can do about Murray." A sudden realization came to mind, "Why are you telling me this?" "Let's just say that there are those that don't follow the Admiral. There are also those that believe in decency and judicial equality." "Fair enough." The larger man behind Jay begins to release his grip allowing Jay to stand normally again. "Thank you." "Use it well." The man in the suit pointed toward the stairs excusing Jay from the conversation. A newfound hate began to brew in the pit of his stomach as he climbed the narrow stairs back into the bar room. The woman from before still sat with the same bottle of Chill resting between her palms. She glanced his way. "Well look who's back." He ignored her and walked past. __________ How Stepping through the double doors of the police department was like every other time he had done so. The floor was tiled and the scent was a familiar blend of Columbian coffee. Jay shoved past two officers on their way out and made his way to the front desk. He leaned on the glass case. "I have a question." The woman behind the desk looked up from her book, "Yes." "What's the easiest way to spring someone from jail?" "Excuse me?" "Legally." "I don't think I understand what you're getting at sir." "Someone I know is wrongfully in jail. How do I get them out?" "You have to bring up new evidence at court. Possibly bail. Why?" "Thank you." Jay exited with a woman staring at him as her book shut on her desk. She shook her head and sighed. __________ Please "I need a favor Steve," said Jay into the microphone of his phone. Steven Caldera's voice could be heard on the other side. "What now Jay?" "It's Caroline." "Jay, I thought we were done with this nonsense. She burned your home down, she's out of your life and out of The Establishment's hair. We need to focus on -" "Murray." "Exactly." "I don't follow." "It was Murray Steve, Murray burned down my place." "Jay, I'm not following." Jay paced the street outside of the police station, "I talked to a guy, he saw him. He did it." "Jay, you focus on Murray and I'll do what I can for Caroline. Don't worry about it." "I need her out Steve." "I'll get her out. Murray though. Focus on Murray. He is the one who burned your house." The realization crept up on him completely as he hung up the phone. It wasn't Jordan that ruined his life. It wasn't Caroline that turned her back on him. It was Andy Murray and only Andy Murray. For once in his life, he was thinking clear. He knew exactly what had to be done. __________ Perfect The suited man walked out of the bar handing the muscular man an envelope. On the front, in thick black ink, read Steven Caldera.
