The Story Of Everything, Chapter Ten: Jenkem (Revisited)

Date: Spanning Across Time When I woke up, I had no idea where I was or how I had made it there. I remembered vague messes of bodies and attempts to retrieve some sort of collateral. Apparently, I had somehow achieved a modicum of success, and for some reason, I still had it with me; that jar with those two wrinkled pebbles and a droop of flesh inside! My surroundings were familiar, but I found it strange that I was to wake up here, in my bed, of all places. I can't remember the last time I ended up here in the morning, and something about the whole situation seemed to trigger a notion that something was going to go horribly awry. I stood up, grabbed the jar and waded through piles of dirty clothes, empty cans and plastic bags, unopened letters, the occasional syringe and various ephemerally used items that may have had some use to me at one point or another. I opened the door as far as I could, compressing a pile of dirty shirts and coaxial cables against the adjacent wall until I could slip through. The door snapped shut behind me. "Holy shit." The living room looked like it had exploded. Furniture was overturned. Multicolored plasmas stained the walls and the floors. The windows were broken, and the layer of debris that covered the floor was covered in broken glass. One of the windows was busted inward, but it was hard to tell if someone else was behind this ruthless vandalism or if I had reentered my apartment by hurling myself through a second story window. "That's ridiculous," I said aloud to myself, but then again, I had no idea where my keys were. I surveyed the wreckage and considered getting a broom to sweep a path to the front door, but it was really a job for a shovel, and the shovel was on the wrong side of the mess, sitting there next to the door. I cursed myself for not having the foresight to buy multiple shovels, and then my phone started ringing. It was Doctor Wayne. "Johnny Don't," I answered. "Huh?" Wayne questioned. "Johnny Don't," I repeated. "Yeah, I know what you said. Why are you answering your phone with your name like that?" "I don't know. It seemed like a reasonable thing to do." "Only pricks answer the phone like that." "What do you want?" "You still got that old man's junk?" "Yeah," I said, examining the jar, feeling its weight. "Well, we should probably get it back to Emery and cash in on our quarter million." "Quarter million?" "Fuck yeah," Wayne beamed. "Guy said he'd double our money if we got him the junk. We had 120,000 dollars, so that's 240,000 -- almost a quarter mil. You gonna be ready in five minutes?" "Yeah," I nodded, nearly speechlessly. I hung up the phone and began fording through the living room towards the front door. I jumped as I stepped on some pointed object or bit of broken glass, and I looked down under my foot for the culprit. There was nothing visible to indict, but a bottle caught my eye. Usually, there'd be nothing abnormal about a bottle, but there was something about this one that drew me to it. Its label was peeled off and rolled up inside. I picked the bottle up and turned it upside down, but the label had loosened and it was no longer rolled tightly enough to fit through the bottleneck. I tried fitting my finger into the bottle to tickle out the label, but my finger got stuck, and the bottle became a part of my hand. "Fuck." It was quite the predicament, but I was an intermediately skilled as an escape artist. I had been caught in a Chinese finger trap once, and sleuthed that the way to freedom was not to try and pull your fingers out, but rather to push them in! "Ah, fuck!" The plan had backfired, and now I was deeper in, further stuck. I forged forward towards a three-legged coffee table and tried smashing the bottle on the edge of the tabletop, but the table wasn't sturdy enough to work. My phone rang again, and I was only halfway through the room, Wayne wouldn't be pleased. I fumbled with the jar wedged under my armpit and the bottle stuck on my finger, and I flipped the phone open. I slowly brought it to my ear. "Johnny Don't." I answered, in an attempt to mock Doctor Wayne. "I know who this is," It wasn't Doctor Wayne. "Do you know who this is?" "Yeah," I lied. I wouldn't have answered my phone for an unfamiliar number if I hadn't been so distracted by the entrapped predicament I was trying to solve. "Good," he said shark-like. "I believe you have something of mine." "I don't know what you're talking about." I glanced towards the jar uneasily, finally recognizing the owner of the voice on the other end of the line. "It's in your best interest to be honest with me, Johnny. I can help you out if you cooperate." "I'm listening." "You know, I never would've taken you for a double agent, Johnny. I thought you were a lot smarter than that." "Get to the point," I gritted. "I'm sending over a trustee to pick up what belongs to me. If you hand it over, we forget about Andre Smiley and go our separate ways." "And what if I refuse?" I cowboyed. "You know that trustee I said I was sending over?" he asked, most likely rhetorically. "No," I used as a placeholder while my memory or attention span failed me. "He's not too excited about running this errand. Usually I don't send assassins to pick up my laundry, but I have a gut feeling that this might turn into more than just a trip to the Laundromat." I swallowed hard, trying to think of something defiant to say, but I couldn't think of anything. "He'll be there in about two hours. He'll say his name is Jack Brady. It's not, but that's no matter to you. The one thing I want you to remember is that you don't hand the package to anyone until someone introduces himself as Jack Brady. Got that?" "Got it." "And if he gets there and you're gone, he'll find you." He hung up the phone, and I just stood there, sinking down into the pile like it was a mucky patch of quicksand. The phone rang again. I was sure to check it this time. It was Doctor Wayne again. I picked up. "Hey," I humbly answered. "I'm out front. You ready?" "Not quite," I sighed. "Maybe I should just stay here." "I'll drag you down here, man." Wayne was adamant. "We're talking about a quarter mil." "The judge called," I stated flatly. "He's sending an executioner." "Then you better get the hell down here, and we should get going." "I have to hand over the jar," I said. "It's the only way they'll stop coming after me." "You think they won't kill you anyway?" Wayne reasoned. "And if you hand over the judge's skin gavel, don't you think Emery's going to start sending people after you?" "That's a good point," I agreed, trying to make progress through the squalor. "I'll be down in a minute." I hung up the phone and continued on towards the door. It killed me that Wayne was right. The only ways out of this mess were to give the jar to both of them or neither one of them. Of course, if I hid the jar too well, and without any clues, I still risked being assailed. I needed to give Curtis Westonbrook a chance at finding it to give Emery Island a reason to need me alive. Both of these plans, I recognized, were incredibly implausible. I made it to the door and fumbled it open, trying to balance the hindrances my hands were suited with, and I headed down the stairs to Wayne's car. He reached across the inside of the car and opened up the passenger's side door. "What happened to your hand?" Wayne greeted. "Don't have time to worry about that now," I waved him off. "We've got two hours before they show up and realize I'm not there." "We'd better get to Emery's then." Wayne turned the ignition and pulled out of his spot. "We're not going to Emery's. I've got a plan." "We don't have time for plans," Wayne rationed. "We've got to get the money and run." "Not if my plan works," I said, rolling down the window and lighting a cigarette as best as I could. "How fast do you think we can get to Matthais Henrik's farm and back?" "It's pretty out of the way," Wayne grumbled. "Why the hell are we going there?" "Can we get there and back in under two hours or not?" "That's cutting it close," Wayne summed, and it was a more accurate foretelling than he'd ever know. "We'd have to completely disregard all the rules of the road." "Then step on it," I said. "Because we have to stop at a hardware shop on the way." "For what?" "I'm going to need some hedge cutters and another jar that looks like this one." I got to thinking, "Oh, and don't let me forget to pick up a good shovel." *** We had made record time securing the goods from the hardware store and speeding onward to Matthais Henrik's farm. We had gotten there in short of an hour, and it looked like the place had gone untouched since we had last been here. Matthais was still at the same place I had left him, next to the half-dug hole. The discharging intestine that had been planted had built up to heap into an aboveground protuberance. "It smells fucking godawful," Wayne complained. "Roll up your window." This slow process did little to block the horrific odor. "So what's your plan?" Wayne prodded. "You see that swarm over there?" I pointed towards the small mound beside Matthais corpse. There was a tremendous buzz of flies above it, which made it difficult to see what was beneath it. "Yeah, that's a lot of fucking flies." Wayne squeezed out, holding his breath. "Matthais should be somewhere around there," I gasped for air and coughed. "We need a decoy set to give to the judge's guy. I figure one old man's collateral looks just the same as another old man's collateral." "I wouldn't be able to tell the difference," Wayne admitted, still holding in his breath. "That's not a bad plan." "Do you want to cut or hold the jar?" "What?" Wayne gasped out, reintroducing himself to the repugnant air. He coughed a bit before tightening his windpipe. "I don't want to do either." "I can't do both!" I exclaimed, showing no regard for the malicious stench. "We're on a truncated time table!" "Fine," snarled Wayne. "I'll hold the jar." Wayne grabbed the empty jar and popped open his trunk. I put the jar containing the judge's junk in the trunk, grabbed the hedge clippers and the shovel and slammed the trunk closed with my elbow. We sifted forward through the palpable atmosphere towards the swarm of flies, but the smell was starting to play games with my sensory perception. Wayne and I traversed the field with great difficulty, wobbling side to side. It seemed like it took forever to get there, and we were both running out of breath. "Get his pants down and position the jar underneath," I instructed. "We need a clean catch." I swatted the flies away with the shovel to give Wayne clearance to wiggle down and expose the deceased farmer. This was no easy task, and his fingers didn't appear to be working in unison. "Can you go any faster?" My voice started sinking. "I'm having a hard time with all these flies buzzing everywhere," Wayne droned, his voice reaching inaudibly perceivable levels. I swatted more with the shovel, which grew heavier in my hands. My aim wavered. "Shit, Johnny! Stop hitting me with that fucking shovel!" I stopped swinging the shovel, and Wayne continued on as the flies swarmed back in record numbers. I felt time pass, physically. It shook my bones. "I've got it!" I snapped my focus back with all that was still functioning in my brain and shivered myself into position. It was a shaky crusade, trying to get the clippers properly arranged. "Come on! Clip it already!" I couldn't even have begun to imagine how difficult this was. This was a further cry from responsibility than I'd ever imagined possible when I had first tried Jenkem. Here I was in the core and the hell of it, in multiple semblances, dismembering a corpse. Maybe I deserved to die if this was the only way not to. "Clip his fucking shit off!" I closed my eyes and pushed the wooden handles towards each other with one last burst of strength, and as soon as I felt the handles clack together, the sound of an explosion burst out all around me, as I was centered in a circumference of noise large enough to encompass my mind without penetrating my body. From there, everything happened so fast, and it eventually accelerated to the point where it all blurred to nothing. I remember a few things. I remember Wayne taking off towards the car with a supercharged sprint, bobbling the decoy jar. I remember yelling at him to back up the car and after having him do so, filling his trunk with shovel after shovel full of the densely fertile, intestinal expulsions as Wayne protested. There was no time to think, and as the acceleration grew, I blunted Wayne with the shovel and slumped him onto the passenger's seat. I really had no choice in the matter. I packed in as much of the earthy product as I could, leveling it in flat with the shovel. I slammed the trunk shut and commandeered the vehicle. As I sped off, the acceleration of the vehicle squared and cubed and further powered up the speed of time until all was light flashing against a black enclosure. I was screaming, and I could hear other screams, maybe they were but the overlapping echoes of my own. *** When I woke up, I had no idea where I was or how I had made it there. I looked forward, and there was a tree trunk where the hood should have been with half of the hood peeling away on both ends of the tree in what might have been the most convoluted indicators of symbolism I've ever born witness. A few more feet into the tree, and we would've been goners. I was surprised we weren't regardless. I stuck my head out the window and looked down. The bottom of the car was hovering a few feet off the ground, bobbing buoyantly. I concluded that I had reached such an alarming speed that the car lifted off the ground, and at the velocity it was going, the tree just split the car in two until friction jarred the car to a stop. I had heard tales of fence posts wedged through trees in high winds. This must have worked by some similar science. "How'd I get back here?" mumbled Wayne, processing a turbulent readjustment to consciousness. His eyes sprang open wide. "Holy shit! What the fuck did you do?" "I'm not sure myself," I jawed out, figuring Doctor Wayne would be none too keen on hearing my hypothesis. Wayne opened his door and fell out of the car, not anticipating the altitudinal difference between perception and actualization. "Fuck!" he shouted, getting up sorely and dusting himself off. "How the fuck is this even possible? And how the fuck did you end up driving my car?" "You don't remember?" "All I remember is that we were going to cut off that dead guy's junk and then you kept hitting that chair into me. Then I heard an explosion and things started to go too fast for me to keep up." "That's where I get fuzzy, too," I lied, figuring it was best not to let Doctor Wayne know that he's got a trunk full of freshly -- maybe even prematurely -- harvested organic Jenkem and a bump on his head where I had hit him with a shovel. The barren road beside us became host to two distant lights. "Shit," I remembered. "How much time do we have left?" "Does it matter? There's no way we're making it," Wayne pessimistically responded before hopelessly looking down at his watch. "We're an hour and a half late as it is." "Shit," I panicked. The lights grew brighter, slightly larger. I could see the car now: a black sedan with tinted windows. I looked at the decoy jar in my lap and took a deep breath. "This better work." I opened my door and hopped out of the car, noticing that glass cascaded down from my shirt as my body straightened upon dismount. I also noticed that my arms were lined with stinging cuts. I could feel them biting me in the face, too. I surveyed the incisions and noticed that the bottle stuck around my finger had shattered. I looked around on the ground and then back in the car and found the rolled label on the floor mats underneath the driver seat. I unrolled it and found that it was not a label at all, but a square of paper cut out of a transit system map. A crude treasure map had been drawn onto the other side. Three images were connected with meandering dotted lines forming three points of a wavy triangle. In the bottom left, there was a crude drawing of a house that snaked right and up towards an X. From the X, there was a straight line to a drawing of an island. Underneath, there was another name. Jeremy Forton-Heath. The car rolled closer. "I'm sick of these games with these words. Names scrambled into other names!" I cursed. "What?" Wayne questioned, walking around the car towards me. "Is that the decoy jar or the real deal?" Wayne pointed at the jar in my arms, his eyes wide again with renewed hope that we might still be able to somehow bullshit our way out of morose inevitability and squeak by with a fiscally victorious survival. Chances were grim due to an overwhelming amount of unforeseen obstacles and untimely events, but they were still chances. "It's the decoy." "You think this is going to work?" "I hope so." "What's that there?" Wayne pointed to the paper rolled out between my fingers. "It's a map on the back of another map." "Where's it lead?" "It leads to the treasure, I guess." The car was cutting in closer. "Don't say anything. I think this is the judge's guy." Wayne nodded, and we both looked on anxiously as the sedan rolled to a halt in front of us. The back door opened, and the suited pants leg of the passenger found firm ground, visible just beneath the fluttering flap of his long trench coat. An austere looking ghost of a giant of a man stepped out. There was nothing behind his eyes, and I could even see that through the dark sunglasses in front of them. "Running from something?" he boldly stated. "Are you who I think you are?" my voice came, trembling, just like the jar I held in front of me, like an offering. "Jack Brady," he introduced, with an equal boldness. He pulled a pack of cigarettes out and slid one between his lips, and it was as telling as a limp handshake. He smoked an inferior brand. Behind his tough, fragmented speech and imposing countenance, he smoked cigarettes for pussies. Jack Brady was a pussy. "Here it is, Jack," I pushed the jar towards him, hoping he'd accept it. "Little late." He took a drag from the cigarette and grabbed the jar, examining it from every angle, letting the light shine on its contents as I held my breath. "What do you say?" I offered. "Are we square?" "The deal was two hours," he didn't seem to regret to inform. Jack pulled the flap of his long coat back and unsheathed a blade. He ran his fingers down the sides. "Any last words?" "You know, if you want to make this interesting, I bet I could take you down with my eyes closed." He laughed in response and put his cigarette out against the sole of his shoe as he contemplated. "I'm interested." "Wayne, get me the clippers." Doctor Wayne, who was rendered speechlessly dumbfounded, obliged. I cut a strip off from my shirt and stuck the sharp ends of the clippers into the dirt at my feet. I tied the makeshift blindfold and pulled the clippers from the ground. "I'm ready whenever you are." I snapped the hedge clippers twice, and it was on. Jack Brady boldly charged at me with the blade and too much bravado. I easily caught it with the hedge clippers and tried jerking him back and forth to pull the sword away. Jack was strong. He heaved back, and the sword slid between my clippers. Jack lunged forward again with an insulted fury, and he wildly swung his blade. I knocked it away with the closed clippers. Jack tried again, whirling his blade around from the opposite direction, and I smacked it away with the clippers once more. Jack changed strategy and began lancing the sword forwards towards me as I swiveled back and to one side and back and to the other. I felt the raised car frame behind me, and knew I had nowhere to escape. He lunged again, but I ducked and rolled underneath the car to the other side. It was no use, as soon as I was up to my feet, he had circled the car, and he lunged at me and stabbed downward. I sidestepped, turned and rolled away as his sword pierced the car trunk. He struggled to pull it out, but it must've been engulfed in the mucky, organic Jenkem. I headed around to the back of the car and started smashing the trunk's keyhole with the hedge clippers. "What the hell are you doing?" Wayne disapprovingly questioned, looking on. I continued bashing the trunk's lock until it had been smashed out of functionality, and Jack Brady was making no progress with his sword. I wedged the clippers into the space created by the slightly open trunk and cranked the clippers open to push the trunk up. The hatch shot up and took the sword with it. Jack Brady started scaling the vehicle in an attempt to remove his sword, but with one foot on the ground and the other attempting to gain foothold on the tire hub, Jack left himself susceptible. With firm hand, I slid the clippers between his spread legs, just as he had gained hold of the sword. "Why don't you let go of that sword, and we'll call it even and go our separate ways?" Jack cautiously returned back to the ground as I kept my hedge clippers poised. "Deal," he muttered with a certain boldness even in defeat. I removed the blindfold and guided him by the bits between the clippers back towards his car. He took the decoy jar and got in. I watched the black sedan trail off, and let the tension in the air dissipate with it. "Why is the trunk of my car filled with shit?" Wayne seemed more than annoyed. "Does it really matter?" I reasoned. "Your car is trashed." "You still got the jar with the real collateral?" "Yeah," I assured him. "It's in the trunk." "It's underneath all of that shit, isn't it?" Wayne figured out. I could only nod. "You still got that shovel?" "Yep." "Dig it out and wipe it clean. We've got to collect on it." I got to work, trying to retain the collateral from the viscous mess I had made in Wayne's trunk; but what an auspicious purchase that shovel turned out to be.
