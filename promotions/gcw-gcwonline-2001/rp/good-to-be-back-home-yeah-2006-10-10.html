Date: 10/10/06 Welcome, chumps and charlatans and skanks alike, to Jordan's head. There's not very much room, no, but we'll try to be accommodating. Even play catch up for you. What do you do when you fade into mediocrity? What happens when obscurity's to your chin and still rising? You run. You pack all of your shit, and you run. There's no thinking about it, there's no weighing the alternatives, there's no words; just this middle of the night dash to a place far, far away with people that have been waiting for your return for a very long time. See, in California, oh shit. I should start from the beginning. See, I tend to react poorly to stressful situations. These people that I worked for tried to get me to a psychoanalyst, a publicist, and a style consultant. I formed a cult. I started wearing fur coats. Shit happens, dig? Time did its thing, just kept chugging along. Stuff happened, and I ended up having to get rid of my cult. Stuff being: I got tired of having them following me around all the time. They're stupid, uncharismatic fuckers. So I gave 'em a city. I bought it first, and it was very deserted then, but yeah. It's been a long time. They still send their cute little donations and all. We just don't talk like we used to. And that's why I dragged Danielle to the car in the first place. It went something like: 'look, just because you didn't do shit in your little sport doesn't mean you have to go back to Cali for those freaky. Freaks.' Followed by a: 'yes it does.' She argued her side for hours down the road, but I think she understood well. Some vital nerve for keeping this lovely calm smirk on my face just snapped under a world of strain and, well. She might think I'm scared. She could be right--it hasn't really come up, though, and she only argued her point, not that we should have actually stayed at home. She's scared of Casey. Maybe I'm scared of being this unremarkable shmuck after being stupid enough to take this quick heeltoe spin back into wrestling. I mean, I could've just slept instead of going out of my way to be random fodder. There's fun and there's stupid. Making people look bad in front of their fans, friends, and family: fun. Eliminating some sap I've never heard of before getting tossed out by some sack I've never heard of: stupid. And beyond that, there's this fancy little preening version of a gangwar going on, complete with monopolization and betrayal and more than enough thrashing to lose some singles scumfucker like me in the waves. Jordan White just isn't smiling of his own accord today. Anyone could appreciate that. What sounds better than slipping away from a decaying fame to hide in the valleys of western California, warm in the arms of your dumbfuck followers? I know, I could be overreacting here. I mean, it has become clear to me that professional wrestling--on the whole--is as legitimate as those old drawings of Minnie Mouse giving a rimjob to Jessica Rabbit and, if I'm gonna be speaking logically, I've gotta assume that odds are my career is as fixed as the rest of the schlubs and I just have yet to be informed. Maybe I was told, and maybe we just spend a lot of time not talking about it. It's a solid explanation for things, at least. It's a good way to explain some dumb fucker thinking scrawny bitch Jordan White would do well against the Towering Stupidity of Mayhem--assuming they weren't just serving me up as a treat to the grunt to thank him for not sodomizing any of the staff, or their vehicles, or their pets. And I'm thinking: yeah, once upon a time I beat a real wrestler and a giant shithead in a handicap match. And I'm thinking: I don't have near enough smoke bombs and nunchuks for it to work these days. So, maybe my life is a sham. Okay. Cool. So this failure--total, abject, and utter--shouldn't get me this far down or this far away. Fuck it; the varnish is already wearing off this piece of shit belt anyways’I’m thinking the value of coke done on it’s quadruple what this thing’s worth, ten times what they paid for it. I've got no room to complain. I made myself a commodity long, long, long ago. Only reason I'm out here is to check up, see how my franchising went. It's not that there are a hundred or so sets of arms waiting to smother me in hugs. Because I am not a little pansy. No matter what you say, brain. It's just, well, I think I felt one of the negative emotion things the other day, and. Here we are. Danielle's been asleep for a while, following night from timezone to timezone. She doesn't know how we got here and she most likely wouldn't care, and I doubt she wants me to wake her up for the arrival, so, well. No one knows but me. A quarter mile up the road stand the towers. Two, ominous like cyclops sentries shining their two glowing eyes on my car as soon as I've taken that last turn. So, someone knows. They might recognize the car, they might recognize me. No warning shots, no roars out of hidden speakers. The town just keeps growing, squat buildings sunk into the shadows of night; or I think that's a town, at least. It's hard to see much of the swampy nothing beyond this fucking spotlight. Kept the fence open like I didn't teach them to be paranoid fuckers. The lights break away from me, stare forwards to the tree line and die out. The road goes from rough gravel to smooth asphalt and my car stops jerking, stops jittering. This is progress. This is a town quiet at, I don't know, something like 2:30 am. This is a place without broken windows, without heavy taxes on property, without cops sniffing around to arrest teenagers for small possession. It's tranquil and quiet and calm and it's everything it ever wanted to be. Turn the music down just a little, listen to myself think. No stoplights, just antiseptic blue streetlights and the signs that shine in them. Downtown, I'm guessing by all the plate glass, by the banner that's waving over the street, asserting 'DOWNTOWN' in block letters that don't have much in mind for conserving ink. Short trees at intervals, maybe every fifth parking space if they were to actually paint them out. But, no. People understand the order of this place. They must not need stupid guides. Fuck me. I made a good community. It's not quite empty, I mean. There's this car with the lights off that's about a turn behind me. I'm keeping a steady pace for them. No need to make it hard on them. This is my home, too. These are my people. Wary, mainly. They're smiling in shadow cages, yeah. They've built tranquility using my drugged up ramblings as a foundation, so how long could it last? Not the kind of night to entertain all those notions of futility though--not the kind of place to start damning. There is chaos inside these two tons of steel. I can let there be peace outside and I can relish it and I can wallow in it and I can stare at it with this sort of glasseye longing. I just don't see it reveling too much in me, is all. A quiet acceptance of its founder. Maybe a parade. They really fixed this place up. I mean. Wow. I'm not even seeing cracked paint or wooden siding or weatherbeaten stoops. All brick, and glass, and plastic. It's not idyllic, no, but it's the small town of the new millennium; it's quaint like 4 grams of fat in a fast food meal. The residential flows out of the business sections, or the business flows out of the residential. A smooth transition from the street signs hanging off wrought iron over intersections to them resting on top of ornamental poles at every possible turn, all painted this patina green like I'm really gonna buy it as bronze. But, I mean, they're trying. Still following me, yeah, pointed out by the lamps that keep them from needing their lights on. Could be awe. Fuck, there's gotta be over a thousand people here now. This neighborhood's too nice, too cultivated and landscaped and sprawling. This all started with 250 stupid fuckers, and now I'm wandering through this verdant labyrinth of yards with fountains, doors surrounded by looming marble sculptures and telescoped columns three stories high. Progress, I guess. I remember when this whole town was just brown and abandoned, when these houses were dead or dying, when these roads were dustworn and splintered. It had potential, so I bought it and exiled these people--who listened to me and followed me and trusted me--to it so I could have my free time back. Because they were shit company. Who wants to hang out with cult members? Looks like they were better off abandoned. Give direction to passion and you've got yourself an army. And, in leading themselves, did they forget who the commander-in-chief of all this was? No. Paranoia. S'all. God damn, my balls itch. Hah. The intersection of Jordan Ave., Xavier Dr., and White St. If this isn't an interesting place to end up, a little monument born out of hero worship when the hero's me. Hm. Weird. It sort of tingles. Electric and waiting. Like: maybe I should do something to deserve this. I'm no Martin Luther King, Jr. here. Just another sap whose only major burden is having to live down all the undeserved attention. For some reason, I'm thinking about how disappointed dinosaurs have to be when they end up refined into gasoline. Maybe I'll catch up to the world when I understand now a little better. Okay, okay, the word I'm looking for isn't “understand”--maybe I can smash 'comprehend the full breadth of this crazy shit' into three syllables. Five if I want people to think I'm really bright. I should just talk to one of them, I guess. They're still back there. I pull over, halfway blocking off one end of this curved driveway that, fuck, that's just ivory white and twinkling. Let these cats be a welcoming committee, fuck it. I don't need a parade. This old white pristine Cadillac, miles deep and skyscraper tall, takes a long corner and rolls to a stop behind my car. I'm already out, waiting, cigarette lit and smile burning. Bring it on, fucks. The driver's younger, maybe my age or below. Shaggy blonde hair hides eyes I'd have to lean down to make contact with. The passenger stays inside the car, indeterminate haze of a person. And probably ugly. 'Hey,' says this kid. I give him a heads up, click my tongue against my teeth. He digs his hands into his pockets and looks at his feet, eyes rolling up out of black patches to try meeting mine through shards of dangling hair. 'Are you Jordan?' 'Yeah. Yeah.' 'Yeah.' He's shifting his weight--left foot, right foot. Left foot, right foot. I remember his face. It's been so, so very fucking long. 'If you could just leave, like, quietly, without buggin' anyone or anythin', yeah. That'd be good.' I don't have to say 'what' here. 'It's just, well. You know. Things is nice here and peaceful and. You're not. You know? Nothing personal, you're just. We don't want you here Jordan, and. And we don't need you here. We thought the checks would've kept you away, but.' Okay. 'Thank you for cooperating.' I can always go home, yeah. Fuck. Just. Just back in the car. Just back out. Just. Leave. And let it slide. 'Uh. You mind if I get one of those off you?' He's making a long invisible line at my cigarette with fingers loaded, not quite cocked. He fumbles, jitters. I could break every bone he's got so badly that they'd have to inject this fucker with concrete to get him standing again. I could wear his trachea as an elaborate leather necktie. I made this sonofabitch. I can crush him. Right? 'Um.' But: he remembered his lines. He's earned a cigarette. The passengerside door bangs open, and I catch a familiar wheeze. And out here--under this benevolent moon on a street paved out of my pocket in a town planted in my image--I'm twenty feet away from a man that was on the opposite end of a chair leg the last time I saw him. Telling me I'd let everyone down. That I was the sucker's bet. That this wrestling thing was going to get back in my head and destroy me. And he's stolen something from me again. He coughs. Oh, man. 'Is Mr. White giving you any trouble, Dan?' 'Well.' 'It was a yes or no question.' His voice is gravel, sharp marbles drenched in cheap whiskey. 'He's just. He's not talking.' 'Did you ask for a smoke?' 'Yeah.' 'God damn. I thought he'd do something like this.' It starts with these two clicks, swinging his heels out to solid ground. Next, he stands up. I mean. It shouldn't be anything. He's just a lawyer--a very good bull of a lawyer. I've beaten people that eat the people that eat people like him. He's only big like mountains or planets; he's only terrific and terrible and thunderous. 'Jordan.' 'Torm.' He's clean, smiling. Dark sunglasses, darker suit. No tie, but his shirt's not hanging open to his navel and drenched in some piss beer that's a dollar a case just south of the border. He's taking his time. 'So. You're the mayor of Jordanton?' 'I was thinking Whitesdale, myself. But, you know. It's barely a town right now.' 'And when'd you get elected?' 'A few' 'Fuckface.' 'What was that?' 'Fuckface. Go on.' 'You're a helluva guy, Jordan. But, It wasn't long after you founded it, remember? You told me to stop by, said I should keep an eye on it for you. Haw, you know? I just saw how badly you'd fucked up. You really thought these dumbasses could survive on their own? Or, well. Did you even give a damn?' 'Let's go with that last one for now. So, what, you kept it running to fuck with me when I finally came back to see what was up?' 'How petty do you think I am, Jordan? I helped these people out of an infinite kindness.' 'And my wallet. Fuck. Fuck me running, Robin Hood.' 'Nice offer, but you're not my type. A little too unkempt. Speaking of, how's Danielle?' 'Asleep. Few yards away.' 'Sounds like she's excited about your crazed fetishes.' 'Very.' 'That's what I said.' 'When's the time you tell me I should leave?' 'Well. You made me see you long enough that I couldn't call it a day unless I saw you sniveling and beaten.' 'How's your face?' 'It's healed up nicely. Faring better than your career.' 'Hm?' 'Come on, Jordan. I can't be the only person noticing that it's finally safe to start betting against you. Jesus. You beat Mayhem and I'll have a million dollars to wipe my ass with.' 'Ouch.' 'A hell of one. I couldn't have rented a timeshare with what’s riding on you, boy.' 'Odds that bad?' 'They always were, one way or another. But, I mean. You're clearly taking it well, Jordan. It's not like you drove across a country to refill your ego through the love of a cult you left to fend for itself years ago. You're laughing about it with your beautiful wife, watching the sunrise off of your deck--you do have a deck, don't you? You always built a goddamned deck. But, man, it's because you're doing this for fun. You enjoy your work. You don't care if you win, or if you lose, or any of that. Right? 'Or is how bad you suck burning you up from the inside?' 'I really really dislike you, Torm.' 'Call me Sobias, please. We're friends.' 'And I thought you kept sayin' my name so you wouldn't forget it.' 'Haw. Haw.' Snortinglaughing. 'Forget Jordan? You don't make that one easy.' 'To be fair, you do seem to obsess yourself with inconveniencing me. Shit, you spend all your time waiting on me to show up here?'
