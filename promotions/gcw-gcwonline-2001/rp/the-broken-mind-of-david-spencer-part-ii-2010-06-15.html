The Broken Mind of David Spencer, Part II

15 June 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06/15/10 The Broken Mind of David Spencer Part II: Sanitarium, Let Me In It's the night of Worldwide 131. The main event is roaring onwards as the crowd can be heard cheering loudly for their hero, Andy Murray. In the back a man no one considers a hero, David Spencer, is about to make his exit for the night, his head shaking in disbelief after how he acted earlier on in the evening. To him, it feels like the lighter flicking on and off in his hand is the only thing he can control tonight. And so, Spencer looks to make a quick exit, before he can dig an even deeper hole for himself. However, unbeknownst to Spencer, Sally Ford is lurking nearby for a last minute scoop. When Spencer gets within ten feet of the exit, Ford spots him and jolts after the masked man, calling at him as she does. "Excuse me! David Spencer!" she yells down the hallway. Spencer slows his pace and eventually stops just before the door out of the building. He sighs, his mind frantically working for an answer to the question he already knows Ford will ask. I wasn't properly medicated. was the first thing that sprung to mind. This was technically correct and would provide a reasonable answer for the reporter to chew on and spin as desired. Of course, the medication only serves to numb the real problem and David knows this quite well. The time for deliberation ends, however, as Sally Ford's clicking heels come to a rest near the recently returned GCW superstar. For a moment, Spencer is reminded of a similar thing happening to him in the past, albeit in much more dire circumstances. . . . It's 9 AM on July 27, 2009. Face looks up, not having spelt a wink all night, as the guard to his cell in this local Philadelphia police station opens his door. "Rise and shine," the guard pointlessly says to Face, who pushes himself up to his feet. "And what, pray tell, do I have the pleasure of your company for this fine morning, Officer Stevens?" asks Face, practically drowning in his sarcasm. "Someone apparently gives a crap about you," responds Stevens. He steps to the side of the doorway, revealing a tall man in glasses. The man, who carries a clipboard and a pencil, looks at Face with in an endearing, yet inquisitive manner. A shrink... this should be fun. thinks Face as the man takes a few steps forward into the cell. "Hello. My name is Doctor Jeffery Lyons," says the shrink, paying particular attention to Face's mask. "I'm a psychiatrist doing some research here in Philadelphia." "Are all shrinks in the area paged whenever a man in a mask is arrested?" inquires Face, tilting his head in mock curiosity. "I'm assuming you all fight to the death for the chance at getting the interview." The doctor chuckles. "Well, you appear to still have your wits about you. You're prime real estate for us." "Well, let's just skip ahead to the diagnosis then, Dr. Lyons," says Face, crossing his scarred arms on his chest. "I have Antisocial Personality Disorder." With that, Face steps forward and shakes the doctor's hand. He then walks back to the wall he was sitting against earlier and sits down against it once more, adding a parting comment. "Spot on diagnosis, Doctor Lyons. You really are a genius in the field." To Be Continued...
