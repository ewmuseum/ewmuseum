Butterfly Migrations, Part 1 (Backstory only)

31 July 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/31/2006 (OOC: For backstory purposes only and to set up the WW 51 RP.) Sunrise over County Kerry was the highlight of the day. Many a proud Irishman and Irishwoman rose willingly, even eagerly, at the very break of dawn, even when dawn came as early as 4 in the morning... just to be able to watch a beautiful sunrise. All about the whole of the county, families sat together on the porches of their houses and cottages, mugs of hot coffee or tea in hand, chatting happily among themselves as they watched the sun come up. It was no different at a quaint country inn nestled proudly between a crystal blue lake and a small group of gentle hills. The Rotton Apple, as the inn was called, had rooms for up to eight guests or couples... and on this warm summer morning, five guests were staying at the establishment. All five rose at dawn along with the proud innkeepers, and gathered on the porch with coffee and scones for all of them. “Just as beautiful as you said it would be,” said the eldest of the five guests, who’d come all the way from Istanbul. Percival Quincy Rottonbottom nodded his agreement and sipped his coffee. “Mary and I never miss it,” he said. “How long have you been wed?” asked the Turkish gentleman. “Sure and the end of July, it will be our third anniversary... and we still feel like newlyweds.” Mary Mulvane-Rottonbottom smiled a little mischievously and clasped her husband’s hand. “And you are both happy as innkeepers... content to leave the cameras and the neon lights behind?” the Turkish gentleman inquired curiously. The Rottonbottoms nodded in unison. “I would never trade the time I spent as a wrestler, for anything,” Mary said resolutely, “but I have everything I could want here. Me family is the most important thing there is.” She smiled and nuzzled the cheek of the sleeping infant boy in her arms, as Percival stroked the baby’s head. Sunrise in County Kerry was the highlight of the day... but John William Rottonbottom was the highlight of his mother’s life... and his father’s as well. The thought of returning to wrestling wasn’t even a thought for either Percival or Mary; being a family and running their inn was everything to them. Not that the Turkish gentleman nor any of the other guests minded one bit. The Rotton Apple had a reputation as a quality-service, homey-atmosphere bed-and-breakfast inn with a long list of satisfied guests from all over the world. In fact, quite a few guests wrote letters back to the inn after returning home, to express their gratitude and pleasure at their stay. Some of these letters were proudly placed on a wall in the common room. Each day at noon, the postman arrived with the day’s mail, and some of the mail was read aloud to any guests that might be staying on that day. The rest of the mail was bills, bank statements, reservation requests... and on occasion, a letter from the U.S. Probationary Board. The darkest time in Mary Mulvane-Rottonbottom’s life was during her wrestling career... another, less-talked-about reason she was disinterested in returning to the business. Her most bitter feud had pushed her past the brink to the point where she actually made several attempts on the very life of her opponent... and she was now serving a seven-year probation sentence for it. She had four years to go on this probation period... and it was getting to be time for her to return to the one-bedroom apartment she rented in Burlington, Vermont, to meet with her probation officer. The terms of Mary’s probation required that she maintain and continue to pay rent for this apartment, and tenant it one week out of each month, to facilitate meetings with her probation officer. So it was no surprise that afternoon when a letter arrived for Mary from the Probationary Board. Presumably it was a reminder for Mary to return to the States and prepare for her monthly meeting. She slit the envelope open with a letter opener as she sat in the inn’s office room, and glanced it over. “Nay....” she whispered, putting a hand to her mouth as tears suddenly began to well in her eyes... “Oh nay...” Percival turned around, struck with immediate concern. “Mary, what’s wrong?” he asked, quickly putting an arm across his wife’s shoulders. Mary looked up, her face a mask of pure misery. “Percival... dear merciful God...” Percival snatched the letter out of Mary’s hands and began to read it aloud. Dear Mrs. Rottonbottom: Please be advised of new federal probationary laws and regulations going into effect on the beginning of August 2006. In an ongoing review of the probationary system, the board has approved the following changes that apply to you directly. To better address recurring problems with probates that miss probation meetings, probation officers are now charged with more closely monitoring their clients. They are charged with keeping more regular contact with their clients and screening them more thoroughly to remain apprised of continued good conduct. We regret to inform you that this need for more regular contact with probates, and more thorough screening of conduct and behavior patterns, means that provisions for probate clients living abroad, outside the borders of the United States and its jurisdiction, are discontinued effective immediately. Probate clients are now required to return to the continental U.S. to live full-time so that probation officers can more closely monitor their conduct. You have until August 10, 2006 to contact your probation officer with proof of compliance with this new regulation. Failure to comply will place you in violation of the terms of your probation. This can result in an international warrant filed with Interpol and the Irish government for your arrest and extradition, and imprisonment for the remainder of your probationary term. The context of this letter is not meant to threaten or intimidate anyone. We are required to inform all probate clients of new probationary rules and regulations, and ensure timely compliance with the same. Please contact your probationary officer with any questions. Sincerely, Tess Wilson U.S. Probationary Board General Secretary Mary wept quietly with her face in her hands as Percival finished reading the last half of the letter. Percy felt his stomach tie itself in knots as bile rose to his throat. Disgustedly, he cast the letter aside and took his wife into his arms. “I promise you, Mary... we will get through this. We will be okay. I promise.” The conviction in his voice was unshakeable, though it provided neither of the Rottonbottoms with more than minimal comfort. Mary laid her head heavily on Percival’s shoulder. “Sure and I love ye so much, Percy,” she whimpered sadly. “I know not when I can hold ye like this again...” “Sooner than you think,” said Percival. “My place is with you... whether that be here or in Burlington. I’m going to call Stanley... while I’m doing that, call your cousin Jessica. Ask her to come here with her fianc” as soon as they can. We have business to discuss...” (TBC)
