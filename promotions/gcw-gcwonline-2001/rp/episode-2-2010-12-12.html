Date: 12/12/10 “You thought? There’s your fucking problem Erazo, you don’t get paid to fucking think. You get paid to arrest people.” – Captain Phillip Toms 1 “What the fuck do you mean you’re not ready to go under?” Rios opened his mouth to respond, but he found no words. He allowed his look of stunned silence to last only a moment before simply lowering his head and shielding his face from the glare of his supervisor. He was stalling, trying to buy himself some time to come up with some sort of reasonable excuse. He found nothing. Taking his subordinates silence as a cue to continue Unit Chief Erwin obliged him. “I’m the one that pulled you out of that white-collar corporate bullshit Rios, if it wasn’t for me you’d still be sifting through donation receipts to charities and election campaign budgets!” Rios was still racking his brain for an answer, but the truth was he didn’t have one, at least not one that was going to be sufficient for his Unit Chief. In the brief amount of time that Steven Rios had worked with Kevin Erwin he had never known him to be an understanding man of compassion. No, Erwin was the kind of man with ice in his veins a man of action, one of a dying breed left in the FBI. Erwin craved big cases and big arrests and right now Rios was preventing him from both. “This is fucking bullshit Rios,” he said, his voice finally lowering since the beginning of their conversation. “I’m handcuffed here, I don’t have any other options and the meeting is set up for tonight. Either you give me a legitimate reason why you can’t go, or you’re getting sent in.” Erwin’s last words lingered in the air, enough to draw an intense stare from Rios, who looked partially shocked and partially appalled. Erwin knew why, it was horrible practise to force an agent into undercover work. It was highly dangerous, it was highly risky and it was highly against FBI protocol. But the one thing that both men knew was that Erwin would rather deal with the consequences of sending him in, than risk blowing a potential career case like this one. “Fine,” Rios finally spoke. “Fine?” the Unit Chief pressed him for a more complete answer. Rios nodded before speaking. “Fine, I’ll do it.” “I knew you’d see it my way,” Erwin said with a grin. “Now saddle up cowboy, we’re going to meet at 1900 hours to go over the final plan.” Rios said nothing, he was done speaking to his Unit Chief. He instantly regretted not telling him the truth, not telling him the reason he didn’t want to go undercover anymore. Even a man as heartless as Kevin Erwin would have understood his reservations about going into a dangerous undercover assignment if he had known that his wife had just been diagnosed with breast cancer. 2 Tommy was lighting his fourth cigarette for the past half hour and the constant smell of tobacco and looming blue haze of cigarette smoke were finally starting to bother his partner. Miguel rolled down his window, allowing the blue smoke to escape through the crack in the window. “Not a good idea partner,” Tommy began to say. “If they see smoke coming out of the car, they’re going to know that someone is in the car. And if they know that someone is in the car, they’re going to wonder what that someone is doing. And if they wonder what that someone is doing, they’re going to get suspicious.” “And if I have to breathe in any more fucking nicotine, I’m going to develop lung cancer, or drop dead on the spot,” Miguel interrupted. Tommy simply shrugged his shoulders, his partners answer seeming to satisfy him. Or more than likely it was just that he didn’t really care. It’s not like there was a whole lot of action going on anyways. They’d been watching the same bar for the past two hours or so and they had yet to see anything exciting. “This is fucking bullshit,” Tommy said between long drags from the cancer stick. Miguel looked to his partner but said nothing. Deep down he agreed with him, but he wasn’t ready to give up. Besides, bitching about it did nothing to make it any better and Miguel wasn’t one for whining. There had to be something going on, an hour earlier they had seen several bikes, many of them ridden by people they had assumed had at least some kind of minor connection to Los Aztecas. But they had been inside for a long time, and they had yet to see any of them do anything interesting. “Let’s just bust in there and take one of them with us,” Tommy said, finally extinguishing the cigarette by smothering it in the ashes of the half pack he had already smoked. “What are we going to just ask a potential prospect or member of a biker gang to come along with us nicely?” Miguel mocked. “These guys would tell us to go fuck ourselves, you know that Tommy, we need a charge, or at least some reason for taking him in. Don’t worry, one of them is going to fuck up, I know it.” “Let’s just plant something on them, drag them in for that,” Tommy suggested. Miguel looked at him sceptically. Apparently his partner wasn’t handling the lack of action over the past few weeks quite as well as he was. “They’re all riding sport bikes, none of them even have carrying bags or side pouches, what are you going to do? Stash some coke on the seat of the bike and hope it doesn’t blow away by the time they come out.” Tommy was about to say something back in reply, but thought better of it. Instead he reached into the inside of his jacket pocket for his pack of cigarettes. “Oh for fuck’s sakes Tommy, would you give it a rest,” Miguel said. “Shhh, shh,” Tommy interrupted him. “They’re coming out. Get the camera.” Miguel was already two steps ahead of him. He already had the digital camera open and was powering it on as Tommy spoke. He aimed the camera towards the parking lot of the bar as several members of the crew they had been waiting for walked out of the bar. They all seemed quite happy, joking and laughing. [i]Laughing at us.[/i] Miguel thought to himself. He quickly began to take snapshots of the entire group. There were no heavy hitters here, he knew that. Mostly low-ranking soldiers and a couple of prospects. But that was exactly who they were after tonight, the less time involved, the less loyalty they would have. “Come on you sons of bitches, give us something, anything...” Tommy begged, for the moment preoccupied with something other than his nicotine addiction. Miguel was thinking the same thing, but was beginning to lose hope. For the most part the riders seemed to be saying their goodbyes and getting ready for the ride home. Most of them were putting their helmets on, shielding their faces from the watchful eyes of Miguel’s camera. Miguel didn’t stop however, continuing to take pictures, hoping for one of them to slip up. “You know, we could probably ding one of them on a drunken driving charge,” Tommy suggested. Miguel considered it. It wasn’t a horrible idea and was by far the most productive thing that Tommy had come up with since they began staking out the bar. He was beginning to think that might be their only chance as some of the suspects were beginning to climb onto their motorcycles. That’s when one of them slipped up. One of the younger looking members of the group reached into his pocket and pulled out a small vial. The man dressed in bright orange riding gear and standing beside a matching orange Kawasaki Ninja might as well have been a signal for their attention. He lightly shook the vial’s contents onto his gloved hand and lifted it to his nose. “White devil, white devil,” Miguel muttered to himself. “Indeed,” Tommy replied. “You got pictures of that?” “Sure do, got a picture of his tags too, why don’t you go ahead and run the plate number Thomas,” Miguel said. “Don’t mind if I do,” Tommy said and began typing away furiously on the in-board computer of their squad car. Miguel finally put down the camera and reached into his jacket pocket. He pulled out his cell phone and quickly hit the first number on his speed dial. It rang only once before the familiar sound of his Captain’s voice came over the phone. “Erazo, what do you have for me?” “Captain, we’re at Gringos Tavern, checking out that crew you told us to keep an eye on,” Miguel reported. ”And?” “And sir, we believe we may be able to apprehend one of the suspects tonight, maybe bring him in for questioning, and see what we can get out of him?” There was silence on the other end of the line for a few seconds, seemingly the Captain was weighing his options. “What have you got? How strong is it?” “Narc charge sir. Possession of cocaine. Not a lot, but easily enough to get him downtown. And we’ve got photographic evidence to back us up.” Again there were a few moments of silence before Captain Phillip Toms answered. “Alright good, bring him in. But don’t take him to those schmucks at central. Take him to our shop, get him into one of the interview rooms. We’re not charging him on this, I just want to see what he’ll give us. I’ll meet you there.” “Yes sir,” Miguel said and went to shut the phone, but was interrupted a final time by the Captain’s voice. ”Oh and Erazo?” "Yeah Captain?" "Don't you fucking dare, start without me." Miguel allowed a smirk to cross his face. Tommy looked over to him and looked slightly confused. “What the fuck are you smiling about? He offer you a bonus for this shit or something?” Tommy asked sarcastically. “He says not to start the interview without him,” Miguel answered. “Phillip fucking Toms, still doesn’t trust his best two detectives to do their fucking jobs,” Tommy said with a chuckle. In the parking lot the crew of riders was finally beginning to depart. The riders hopped onto their motorcycles and their engines roared to life, the sounds of their engines roaring like thunder, echoing across the streets. One by one they began to pull away, disappearing into the warm San Diego night, some in one directions, others in a completely different direction. They waited for their target, luckily for them he was the last to depart. As the man in orange watched the other riders power their motorcycles into the night, he took one more bump of cocaine before lifting the kickstand of his motorcycle and lurching it forward and onto the street. He made a left and began to push the throttle of his bike, pushing the motorcycle speeding off into the night. He was so focussed on the euphoria and adrenaline running through his veins from the drugs that he didn’t even notice the dark blue Crown Victoria pulling out behind him. In a few seconds though he recognized the familiar whooping sounds of a siren going off and the much too familiar sight of red and blue lights spinning behind him. 3 “Hey, it’s Toms. I’ve got bad news,” the police Captain said into the cell phone. He didn’t even bother waiting for the voice on the other end of the line to respond. He knew that he wouldn’t, his silence was enough of a sign to move on. “Two of my guys were at Gringos tonight,” he began. “They were watching a crew that they thought was associated with you.” He waited for a second as the voice on the other end of the line finally spoke. “No, I have no idea who they were watching. But these are two of my best guys, they know what’s going on. If there’s a connection there, they’re going to find it.” Again Phillip Toms waited for the voice on the line to respond. “Yeah, fine. I’ll take care of it.” And with that he snapped the cell phone closed and placed it on his desk. He sighed to himself as he began to massage the sides of his head. This investigation was beginning to become a giant pain in his ass. Not more than fifteen seconds after hanging up the phone, he heard a faint rumble as it began to vibrate on top of his dark maple desk. “What now?” he muttered to himself as he reached for the vibrating phone. The small envelope icon was notifying him that he had a new e-mail message. This phone was hooked up only for his work information, so he knew that it must have been something else about the case. He clicked the [i]Read Message[/i] button and began to scan the new message that flashed on his screen. Two minutes later he had finished reading the e-mail and it had done nothing to improve his already foul mood. He stood up and slammed the rolling leather chair against his desk as he slammed the cell phone into his pocket. Frustrated, he grabbed his car keys off of the desk and made his way towards the door. This was going to be a long night. 4 “What the fuck do you want with me, I aint fucking done nothing!” Alonzo Silva yelled as he was dragged through the front door of police Headquarters. He had been screaming since they pulled him over and took him into custody twenty-five minutes ago. On both of his flanks were Tommy Chase and Miguel Erazo. Both men held onto an arm firmly, and guided him through the passageways of the San Diego Police precinct, heading towards the holding cells. Captain Toms had told them to wait for him, they would interview him together, try to squeeze something out of him, but Toms wasn’t hear yet and they were sick of hearing this guy’s bullshit. “I said I didn’t do nothing, you fucking pigs!” Alonzo yelled. This last insult only served to infuriate the already frustrated Miguel Erazo, who grabbed Alonzo by the scruff of his neck and used his face to open the next swinging door. Alonzo cried out in pain, and Chase looked over at Erazo with a mild look of disapproval, but the sight of Erazo’s cheshire grin, brought a smile to his face as well. “That’s police brutality! I have rights!” Alonzo wailed. “You have the right to remain silent, you remember that one fuckstick?” Erazo asked him. “Yo, fuck you. Y’all be the ones pulling me in on some bullshit, just let me go man,” the suspect pleaded. “We found you with several grams of cocaine. That’s possession, possibly with intent to distribute, and I’m guessing this wouldn’t be a first offense for you. We don’t give a shit about that, we just want to ask you a few questions, so until then keep your mouth shut dickhead,” Tommy said. For the first time since they had picked him up, Alonzo Silva was silent. He simply nodded his understanding to Chase and Erazo. The two officers ushered him through the rest of the precinct before finally arriving at the interview and holding rooms. They took him into the first open interview room and removed his cuffs. They sat him down at a table, and reattached the handcuffs, one attaching his left wrist to the table and the other attaching his left ankle to the floor. “Coffee, water, food? Anything we can get for you Alonzo?” Tommy asked him. “I’d take a smoke,” he said looking up. Erazo pulled a pack from his back pocket. He threw the full pack and a lighter onto the stainless steel table in front of their prisoner. The man nodded his thanks, and quickly reached for the pack. He was changed from the outside only a few minutes ago. Erazo wondered if it was the chastising that he got from Chase, or if it was that everything outside the doors were simply for show, and now the real fear was washing over him. Either way it didn’t matter, they finally had some peace and quiet. “Let’s go find the Captain,” Erazo said, looking at his partner. Tommy only nodded his response and the two men turned, leaving Alonzo Silva locked up inside of Interview Room Three. 5 “This is Rios, checking all communication channels,” Rios whispered as he walked slowly towards the bar. “We’ve got you Rios,” a voice replied to him over the wireless earpiece buried inside his ear. “But stop talking out loud, they’re either going to be suspicious of you or think that you’re fucking weird.” A smile spread over Rios’ face and he did his best to suppress a slight chuckle. “And stop smiling, you look like a homo,” came more playful banter over his comm. unit. Sanchez’s was a bar in the downtown industrial areas of San Diego. It was mostly populated by shift-workers on break or who just got off. The place was a full out dive and it was rarely full. The bars grimy brick walls and faux-steel interior housed some of the loneliest saps in California. That’s what made it the perfect place for meetings like this. “This is Erwin, shut down the bullshit, the agent is entering the rendezvous point,” and with that the waves went silent. Rios entered the bar and immediately felt nervous about his surroundings. Loud hip hop music blared inside the bar, the bass thundering and trembling against the walls. The bass was so loud, it sounded like it could have brought the walls down around the entire club. He looked around, scanning the bar for his contact. He spied him, sitting at the end of the bar, conversing with a young Asian woman, Rios thought about letting the scene play out, but he knew how these guys were, they were animals and they didn’t give a fuck about women. He strolled casually towards the end of the bar. He had never met Luis Rodriguez in person, this was setup through a number of different channels. A couple of IOU’s were called in at the request of the FBI and ATF and they had arranged this meeting. Although his reputation preceded him, Rios thought that Rodriguez didn’t seem to be too much of anything. He definitely did not look like a high ranking officer in an outlaw motorcycle club. Rodriguez was dressed in a pair of blue jeans and a bright blue leather racing jacket. Unlike most clubs, Los Aztecas didn’t wear the tradition cuts. Instead, they chose only to show their loyalty and association to the club with decals on their helmets and bikes. Rios was dressed similarly, being instructed that his cover was a low-level thug gang-banger from San Diego, who had a hard on for two things, speed-racing on bikes and running guns. Rodriguez turned and saw Rios coming towards him and offered him a nod and quickly sent the lady on her way. He gestured for Rios to sit down as he walked up beside him. “Alex Vasquez,” Rios said, reciting the name he was given by Erwin. He extended his hand for a handshake, but when he was met with only a nod, he had the good sense to withdraw it. “I hear you’ve done some work for Enrique...” Rodriguez began. “You’ve been doing deliveries for him for a little while.” Rios nodded. “That’s right.” “Enrique’s a good guy, sometimes he’s a little bit shady, but he’s good with us. We get shipments from him every now and again.” Rios said nothing. He wasn’t here to give everything away this early, but this guy was testing him. When you’re nervous you either say too much, or say too little. The key is to find the sweet spot in the middle. Rodriguez let out a soft chuckle before he continued, “Don’t say too much, I like that in a soldier. He says you do good work, says you might be useful to me.” “Is that what he said? What kind of useful could I be?” Rodriguez sat in silence for a few seconds, staring straight at Rios, sizing him up. Rios wondered if it might have been a bit of attitude a bit too early in the meeting, but a soft smile quickly returned to Rodriguez’s face. Rios breathed a silent sigh of relief. “You’ve got balls kid. Bit of a fucking attitude, but big balls. I like that too.” This time Rios didn’t chance it, he said nothing. He simply waited for the man to continue. “You know who I am right?” Rodriguez asked after nearly a minute of silence between the two. “Yeah, you already told me your name earlier, and Enrique told me who I was coming here to meet.” “No, no. Like, you know who I am....” Rodriguez pointed to the Aztec sun painted on his bike helmet. “You know who I ride with, who I represent.” “I do now,” Rios said firmly. “That doesn’t bother you?” Rodriguez asked. “Should it?” “Naw,” Rodriguez said with another smile. “Naw, it shouldn’t at all. Let’s have a drink kid. We’ll talk business tomorrow.” 6 “Fuck this, I’m going to talk to him,” Miguel said. “You fucking heard what the Captain said Erazo,” Tommy replied. “We gotta wait for him.” “Fuck him,” Miguel retorted. “It’s been almost a fucking hour and he’s still not here, clearly it can’t be that fucking important!” Tommy let that last thought stew in his mind for a few seconds, before finally relenting, Erazo did have a point on this one. He nodded towards the doorway and then followed his partner into the interview room. Alonzo Silva didn’t look a whole lot different than when they had left him an hour earlier, except for significantly more stressed and slightly more sweaty. The small ashtray in front of him empty when they left, was now completely full of cigarette butts and ashes. A thick blue nicotine haze still hung in the room. “You ready to talk to us Alonzo?” Miguel asked, as he pulled up a chair and slid next to the suspect. Alonzo looked nervous, more nervous than the cocky gangster attitude they had gotten from him on the way in here. Certainly the tables had turned, and it appeared that Alonzo knew it. Tommy felt a tinge of excitement in his stomach, usually these guys wouldn’t break for a normal drug charge. Anyone with any amount of loyalty would just do the time, or hire some hotshot lawyer that was on payroll with the Motorcycle club and get out of it. “What do you guys want?” Alonzo asked. It was like music to the two police officers’ ears. This guy was ready to sing like a canary. Miguel knew that it had been a good idea to go after the associates and prospects first. Those who were knew were the least likely to have a strong bond to the club and to the members of the club. It was like a lion hunting a gazelle, go after the weak ones first. “We want to know everything that you know about Los Aztecas Motorcycle Club,” Tommy stated, slipping into a chair of his own. “Everything...” Miguel added. “Man I don’t know shit, I don’t even know who the fuck that is you’re talking about. You got the wrong guy, I ride bikes because chicks like it. I’m not out here playing Hells Angel,” Alonzo pleaded. A smile spread across Miguel’s face as he turned and chuckled to his partner. Chase allowed a brief smile as well, maybe this wasn’t going to be as easy as they first thought. “If you don’t tell me what I want to know,” Miguel started, staring a hole through the suspect. “You definitely won’t be playing Hells Angel, the only thing you’ll be playing is Hide the Sausage with Bubba at the County Jail. Would you like to try again?” Alonzo looked back and forth, from officer to officer, they could both see the wheel in his head spinning. Spinning as he tried to figure out an excuse, tried to come up with a plan, but the coke was starting to wear off, and everything was beginning to get fuzzy. Alonzo was coming down off of his high, and things were about to start to get really messy for him. “Alright, I don’t know much,” he started. “I only know a couple of the guys who ride with them. I’ve never ridden with them, I’ve got no idea what they’re into or where they hang out.” “Except for the guys that you ride with,” Miguel answered for him. “Who are they, what do they do?” “They’re low-level thugs. They’re gangbangers with a motorcycle license, these guys are twats. They wouldn’t know what to do if a Hell’s Angel rolled through San Diego with a gun.” “Names, Alonzo. What are their names?” Tommy pressed him for more information. “It doesn’t matter what their names are. You’re not going to get anything out of them. They got accepted for three reasons, they’re big, they’re tough and they’re stupid. They can’t think on their own, so they’re loyal to the one thing that shows them the way, and right now that’s the higher-ups in Los Aztecas.” “Where do they hang out? How can we get inside the club?” Miguel asked. Alonzo laughed, it was the loudest they’d heard him since they brought him into the building. He looked at the two blank stares of the officers and realized that they weren’t kidding. “These guys? They hang out at a bar downtown, a place called Sanchez’s. That’s where they do a lot of their entry level business. That’s where they talk to possible recruits and possible allies. It’s secluded, it’s dingy and on most nights, it’s nearly completely empty. It’s just the way they like it.” “You said new recruits, they’re patching people that quickly?” Miguel asked. “Patching?” Alonzo began. “Naw, they aint bringing them into the club right away, they test them out first, send them on small time shit. Burglaries, protection runs, collecting debts. A bunch of bullshit like that, word is that some of these guys go for months, even years before they even get considered to be accepted into the club.” “Alright,” Tommy said. “What can you give us, on actual members of the club? Who can you give up? What can you give up?” “I told you guys,” Alonzo pleaded. “I don’t know shit, I’m giving you everything I got.” “Well it’s not enough Alonzo,” Miguel prodded him. “It’s not nearly enough, if you want to play ball, you’ve got to play all nine innings with us, and right now, you haven’t even gotten to the seventh inning stretch.” Alonzo was silent now, his mind was still racing and the two partners could see his mind racing along, a mile a minute, trying to assess the situation he was in. Trying to figure out the best course of action. “What do you want? You want to meet them? You want dirt? You want drugs? What the fuck do you want from me?” he pleaded, hoping for something to help him along. “All of it,” Miguel said with a smile. “Tommy, go get this man a pen and paper.” “And more smokes, fuck, please more smokes,” Alonzo added. Tommy stood out of the chair and turned to leave the room, just then the door to the interview room burst open and slammed against the steel wall of the room. Captain Phillip Toms burst through the door like a hurricane, he was raging, nearly foaming at the mouth when he entered. He stared at the two officers in front of him, seething, his breathing heavy, almost panting. He pointed at the two of them. “You two, outside. NOW!” The two said nothing, they simply lowered their heads and exited the room. They knew it wasn’t a great idea to go against the Captain’s wishes, it never was, but this was the angriest that they had seen him in a long time, and they couldn’t believe it. Why the fuck was he so angry? It’s not like it was that bad that they were talking to this guy. They had done interviews before, plenty of them and hell a couple of weeks earlier Captain Toms was telling them to hide information from the FBI so that they could work it privately. Why the hell would he be so against that now? “I told you two fuck-heads to wait for me!” Toms erupted on the two members of his team. “I gave you specific instructions and you disobeyed them! Give me one good reason why I shouldn’t just have the two of you suspended for insubordination!” Miguel was the first to speak up. “Captain, you told us to wait for you and we did, we waited nearly an hour before we finally went back to talking to this guy. And I don’t see why you’re so angry, we’ve got this guy ready to sing like a bird.” Something changed in Toms’ demeanour then, something big changed. The anger seemed to leave his body, instead it was replaced by a strange sense of calm and intrigue. Indeed, he was more interested in what kind of details they had than the actual insubordination as he put it. “What’s he saying? Who is this guy? Is he a member?” Toms asked. “Naw, he’s not a member,” Tommy answered. “This guy is fucking soft Captain.” “He’s just a guy that knows a couple of people,” Miguel added. Toms took turns showing both of his officers his attention, waiting for the next to continue where the other left off. “And who does he know?” “No one of importance, unfortunately,” Tommy said. “He knows a couple of street level thugs. He wasn’t too keen on giving them up, but it seems like friends aren’t worth as much as jail time to this asshole.” “Street level guys? What the fuck is that going to get us? If you were going to put the screws to somebody, why the fuck wouldn’t you go after someone who knew something. Someone who was important, someone who these guys actually might give a fuck about!” “That wasn’t our plan, Captain,” Miguel interjected once again. “We went after the prospects and associates first. We assumed that these guys would have a lower set of loyalty and allegiance towards the club and its members. We thought that this would be the best way to get access to the club and its members.” “You thought? There’s your fucking problem Erazo, you don’t get paid to fucking think. You get paid to arrest people. And this dipshit isn’t going to get you any closer to an arrest, now is he?” Toms asked. Miguel stared at his Captain, not used to the amount of sarcasm and seemingly fear in his voice. It was unusual behaviour for a man who was usually as resigned as Captain Toms. He had nothing to say, he wasn’t sure what the Captain wanted from the two of them, but he didn’t have whatever it was. He simply shrugged his shoulders. “Well isn’t that fucking grand.” Toms began sarcastically. “Sir,” Tommy blurted out. “We could use this guy and some of his information to arrange a meeting. To get something set up with Los Aztecas, we might be able to think about undercover. Just like the feds are talking about.” That seemed to set something off in the Captain’s head. He looked at his two officers for a few seconds, his eyes lingering a little bit longer on Erazo. Finally he relented. “That might be true. How long were you on these guys?” he asked. “A few hours, probably been following leads and possible members since around noon,” Miguel answered. “Noon? For fuck’s sake, it’s nearly midnight you two,” Toms continued. “You know I can’t authorize you OT for this. You’re going to have to count this as a normal day.” “But Captain,” Tommy cried out. “Don’t worry, I’ll do what I can,” Toms replied. “But for now, you’re off duty. Head home, see those beautiful women of yours, or go find a hooker, I don’t give a fuck what you do, but you’re off duty.” “What about this piece of shit?” Tommy asked, nodding towards the interview room. “Get me that pen and paper, and then get the hell out of here,” Toms answered. “I’ll book this piece of shit myself.” With that Tommy and Miguel nodded and saluted their Captain before hurrying out of sight. Toms waited a few seconds, waiting until he was sure that they were finally out of sight and out of earshot before he turned his attention back to the door of interview room three. 7 Captain Toms entered the interview room slowly and casually closed the steel door to the room behind him. Alonzo Silva looked at him, a slightly confused look on his face. “What happened to the other two?” he asked. “They’re gone. It’s just me and you right now, Alonzo,” Captain Toms replied. “How do you know my name? Who the fuck are you?!” Alonzo demanded. “My name is Captain Phillip Toms. And I know a lot more than you might fucking think shithead, so listen up closely,” Toms said leaning over the stainless steel table. “I know people, some of the same people that you know. And they’re not going to be too fucking happy to hear how quickly you were ready to turn your back on them.” A look of realization began to wash over Alonzo’s face. And it was quickly replaced by a look of fear. “Yes, you know what I’m saying now, I can see it in your eyes. Now, you’re going to need this,” Toms said, and he reached his hand across the table to the handcuffed suspect. Something metallic and shimmering even in the dim light of the interview room dropped into his hand. “What the fuck is this?” Alonzo asked. “What does it look like? It’s a fucking razor,” Toms stated. “And what the fuck am I supposed to do with this?” he asked. “I’m having you booked and transferred to County in the morning. But in the meantime, you’ll spend the night here, in a local cell. Now, you’re going to fill out this paperwork that my officers talked you into, but it’s going to disappear. And in the middle of the night so are you.” “You... you want me... you want me to kill myself?” Alonzo asked. “Precisely.” Toms said coldly. “Fuck that! Fuck you! I’m not doing anything that you say! You’re a fucking turncoat, a fucking dirty cop. Fuck you pig!” A fire erupted in Toms’ eyes at that moment and he reached across the table, he grabbed the back of Alonzo’s head and grabbed a handful of hair. He slammed the suspect’s defenceless face into the cold steel table. There was a sick thud, followed by another and another, as Toms slammed his face off of the table. A crimson red smear appeared on the table, as blood began to flow from what was surely a broken nose. Toms could hear footsteps outside now, Erazo and Chase were back. He chose his next words carefully. “You either do what I say, or I’ll find someone in County to do it for me tomorrow. Now I need one last thing, where the hell is Rodriguez tonight?” “Rodriguez?” Alonzo asked confused. “You would know him as ‘El Guerra’” “War?! You fucking want War? He’s meeting a new guy tonight, I don’t know where. But some new guy that runs guns in Cali. Some new guy named V something. Velasquez... Vasquez... Valentino... Some shit like that,” Alonzo replied. “Perfect, now remember what the fuck I said.” And with that Toms turned and exited the room. He met Erazo and Chase outside the room, making sure that they were unable to get back inside that room. Alonzo Silva was going to need some time by himself, he had a lot to think about. 8 A few minutes later and Chase and Erazo were on their way home, or to the bar, or to suck each other off, no matter what the case it didn’t particularly matter to Phillip Toms. He had to finish wrapping up this bullshit. Fucking new guys, they had no fucking loyalty. They always fucked things up for him. While he waited for the paperwork on his suspect’s stay overnight in the local containment cell, he reached into his pocket searching for his cell phone. He opened it up and quickly hit the redial button. The phone rang only once before there was a soft breathing on the other end of the line. No words. Only breathing. “Yeah it’s me. I’m taking care of the problem right now. But you’ve got another one. You’ve got to find Rodriguez. There’s a problem with his new prospect. Vasquez. There’s a big problem with him.” There was a barely audible whisper on the other end of the phone, and even though he knew they couldn’t see it, Toms nodded his head. “Yeah, it’ll be taken care of.” 9 Rios was just settling into bed at the cheap motel that he was assigned to sleep in, as part of his character role-playing. The meeting today had gone well, there were no problems, and the fact that they had shared a few drinks before going on a midnight ride had told him that he had at least earned his contact’s trust a little bit. Sure as shit it had pissed off Erwin. He always hated bending the rules, he hated playing the criminal’s game and drinking and driving was a big no-no for him, ever since his son was nearly killed by a drunk driver years ago. But, both Rios and Erwin knew that it was a necessary evil for cases like this. It’s what these biker scumbags did, they drank, they did drugs, they did hookers and then they hopped on their bikes and joyrided around afterwards. Rios had to keep his character up, and Vasquez was a low-down scumbag biker, gun-runner who enjoyed the occasional beverage and joyride. There was a message on his phone, and the number was hidden. He had been expecting this message, but not nearly so soon. There was no name and there was no call-back or return number. But Rios knew who it was. He began to get himself ready after quickly scanning the text message. The message that read... [b]It’s time. We need to meet ASAP. Head out to Harbour Road, out near the old Airstrip. I’ll meet you there. 3 am.[/b] Rios threw on his clothing and began to contact his supervisor. He needed permission for this meeting, and he would get it. But no one knew the real danger that was going to be waiting for him.
