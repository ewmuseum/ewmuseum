Home Sweet Home, Reprise

26 July 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07/26/10 It should have hurt. It should have been the last thing on her mind after she dragged her suitcase into the loft like one more broken dream dragging behind her with the squeaky wheel of a broken friendship in tow. But when the door closed behind her, the suitcase was dropped. Clothes flew, animals ran for cover, and Tempest pounced on Troy like she had been gone for years, not weeks. It was long enough. If her body was battered, she didn’t seem to notice. Or maybe she just didn’t care. She was alive. She was free. It was easy to let go of the feral dream of dominating another person for three seconds when summer was drifting in the windows on a lazy breeze. Its touch was a caress all its own and made it easy to forget the last few weeks entirely. Complication resolved into simple pleasure, confusion into unclouded passion, resistance into surrender. The city even smelled clean after a late afternoon squall and now the moist air that washed over the bed where Tempest and Troy lay seemed to her to wipe away the last of the taint of Hong Kong, of betrayal, of loss. And they rolled off her in beads of sweat, soaking into the sheets, toxins cleaned from her system like free radicals of the soul. "Missed you," she whispered. "I noticed," Troy said, retrieving a far flung pillow for them. They curled up in a single ball and Tempest only had to move his arm once for her shoulder, still tender after too many encounters with Tim Shipley’s boot. "Tell me something," Troy said softly. "Are you really ok? "Of course I am," Tempest said, chuckling very slightly. "You couldn’t tell?" "That’s not what I meant." She peered at him curiously in the half light. "I want to know what happened in Hong Kong." Tempest started to speak, but found Troy’s fingers placed lightly over her lips. "Not right now. Rest. You’ve had enough. But tomorrow we need to talk. And you need to stop believing that I’m gonna break in two if I see what you’ve seen or know what you know." Tempest smiled. It was good to be home. There’s a myth about me – that I want to be the center of attention. Well, I want to be the center of attention when I want to be the center of attention. The rest of the time? Not so much. Celebrity grates on me. It gets in the way when I just want to go out and have fun, to connect with people one on one. It gets in the way when I need to rejuvenate, and when I’m not as proud of who I am. And while fame has in fact been the best cover for some of the more private trouble I’ve gotten myself into, the spotlight is still harsh when you’re hurt and don’t have all the answers. Luckily, there’s one person that really understands this. "Here we go," said Troy with a relaxed smile. Tempest nodded in approval as they stepped into the crowded deli. Built sometime in the 1920’s it still didn’t have air conditioning. But its high ceilings, covered in ornate stamped tin were dotted with wide ceiling fans that hummed lazily as they kept air moving through the place. And what air it was, laden with the scents of fresh peppers of all varieties, being roasted on the grill while bread baked fresh in the back. The front of the deli opened almost entirely with floor to ceiling doors that opened onto the sidewalk where most of the tables were set in a shady clump of trees. A "Campari" umbrella here and there took care of the rest. "Feeling more at home yet," Troy asked, squeezing in behind Tempest as the jovial man behind the counter came forward to wish them "Abbondanza" and take their order. "I already was," she said softly, smiling up at him with a comfortable satisfaction that left little doubt. She ordered a sandwich with sweet peppers. He ordered hot. They laughed over which beer would go best with each. It was a shocking return to normalcy and Tempest breathed it all in, donning her shades in the speckled sunlight out on the sidewalk at their table. After a swig of Legends Golden IPA, Troy pondered his girlfriend, seeming so casual, so normal, so far removed from the world he knew she thrived in. He pushed back his dark brown hair and said, "So this Atken guy… he gonna be any trouble for you?" Tempest smiled and chewed her sandwich thoughtfully. A swig of Blue Moon Honeymoon Summer Ale later she spoke. "Don’t know. He’s not going to be much fun though. He doesn’t like girls." "Really?" Troy seemed taken aback. "Seems like that might be a death sentence as far as image goes in your line of work." "What?" Tempest shook her head and then seemed to catch onto his words and laughed. "No! I didn’t mean he doesn’t like girls THAT way. I mean he’s one of those guys who’s all like, ‘Girls shouldn’t wrestle with guys. They should wrestle each other in bikinis." "Oh!" Troy grinned his best smart-ass grin and said, "Well, I’m not sure I disagree about the bikini part, but I follow." Tempest looked aghast and said, "Troy! That’s hardly fitting. What are you talking about?" He shrugged and looked like he might be trying to dig his way out of this one, but the answer really was fairly simple. "Look, I have dutifully watched X:TV ever since I found out you were a part of it, even though I find some of it a dumb, pointlessly violent spectacle. And I have to tell you that watching a bunch of guys roll around in spandex just isn’t my idea of entertainment. I’m just speaking as a straight guy here, not that I think you shouldn’t have the same opportunities." Tempest giggled as he smiled and held out his hands. "Fine, ok, I get it. Anyway, I need to plan Tessa’s party too." "Now that’s the Rachel I’m used to. But on the show? Isn’t that a little odd. I mean, does anyone else plan celebration parties for other wrestlers?" "I don’t think so," Tempest said, stirring up the last of the pepper’s juices with a hefty steak fry. "But they’re selfish. This is me and Tessa we’re talking about." Troy chuckled and looked at her, an incredulous glance. "The whole enterprise is selfish. I mean, I’m not trying to be hard on you about it, but just the constant competition, struggling for place and titles and such can’t really be called anything else." It wasn't something he said with anger, meant to start a fight. Tempest mulled this over with a sip of her ale and said, "Well yes. It’s selfish. But that doesn’t mean I can’t have friends who I still care for and have faith in. And there’s no reason I can’t do nice things for them." Troy nodded and said, "And those who you’ve… lost faith in?" It was a simple and pointed question. Tempest smiled and peered over her sunglasses with a bright blue eyed look of mischief. "Don’t worry, I have plans for them too." "I was afraid you’d say that." Tempest just laughed. ~I didn’t have the heart to ask her about the dead man back in China. I’d read it in the paper. It was funny the way they wrote the whole thing up. "An unfortunate accident," they said. And it said she was released. Well, I know for damn sure she wasn’t because she barely showed up in time for the show in Tokyo and when she did, she smelled like salt water and ate about four sandwiches from the catering table before doing anything else. I want to ask her about it, but she’s smiling and happy and she deserves to be. It’s what I do best.~
