Hello Darkness My Old Friend

8 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/08/10 Becauseavisionsoftlycreepingleftitsseedswhileiwassleeping Morning. Weekday. Any city in any state. You woke feeling like you’d been roughly fucked by a thousand strangers, but not one had been so kind as to toss even a stained and crumpled twenty dollar bill onto your dresser. The gaping window by your feet frostily pinched your toes as your nostrils sucked in the first breath of the day. Your ribs felt like an iron maiden slamming around your lungs. The storm which raged around you last night purged the impurities from the atmosphere. The rich, burnt scent of ozone wafted on the breeze where forks of lightning had ploughed into the ground outside your window. Each crisp lungful fought a futile crusade against the staunch battle lines of lethargy. The bombardment of oxygen molecules failed to bring down the sturdy walls of aching, unwilling muscles. You didn’t want to live today. Exhaustion had been your sedative five short hours ago, but now you’re all out. You’re exhausted in all but the physiological sense. You’re tired of so many things, in so many ways. In thirty minutes, your cellphone will ring to say you’re late for a signing session. Then every two minutes thereafter. Reminders so polite that they offend in ways a spat cuss word never could. In three hours, you’ll be late for your flight. It won’t be the first time. It won’t be the second time. You’ll be reprimanded. You’ll be de-pushed. You’ll be everything but fired. So next week, you’ll do it again. Like an inmate filing away at their bars, grain by grain, you’ll free yourself. One final push, your knuckles plowing into the nose of a road agent, fragile bone splintering into tender flesh, will set you free. You’ll tell yourself that you’re done. You’ll tell yourself that you’re still young-ish. You’ll tell yourself that you’re still healthy-ish. You’ll tell yourself that you’re still wealthy-ish. You’ll tell yourself, maybe even convince yourself momentarily, that you know anything different; that you know other smells, tastes, sounds. Nature will debate nurture. You’ll lie to yourself. Over and over. But you’ll never truly believe. You are a professional wrestler. Yesterday you were a professional wrestler. Tomorrow you will be a professional wrestler. Again; more conviction, less shame. You. Are. A. Professional. Wrestler. You’re not a message. You’re not a philosophy. You’re not an artist. You’re a medium, though which you perform another man’s production. You’re the voice that carries another man’s words. You’re a puppet, strings pulled at a whim. You’re the donkey that carries your career’s baggage. Your tired bones splinter under the burden of expectation. All you’ve achieved, piled mercilessly across your shoulders. From up on your pedestal, gravity claws at your feet. Every belt and every accolade devoid of buoyant glory, now just deadweight. The higher you climb, the more perilous, the narrower the ledge, the more unsure your footing. At the peak of the wrestling industry, space is at a premium. All around you, dethroned champions and toppled legends plummet. Your swollen reputation only grows heavier. You feel your career crumbling underfoot… and you look down. Never look down. So many years, so many matches. Such a height to fall. Whenmyeyeswerestabbedbytheflashofaneonlightthatsplitthenight Way past your bed time. Bad part of town. Rain; easterly wind. Dense rain left you sodden ten seconds after the bar had finally belched the last of its patrons back onto the night. Staggering and swaying bodies flood out into the narrow streets, their feet tripping over one another, shoulders brushing; an alcohol induced static playing out the sparks charging up the looming charcoal clouds above. Peering out from under a hood, your vision blurred inches from your face; gaudy, blinking neon dying the gushing water. “So?” you asked. He looked back at you, furrowing features stained and streaked by the grimy rain. Tick had followed tock as beer had been followed by shot. He knew you offering to pick up the tab - no strings - was not a random act of kindness. “You ain’t got enough on that credit card to get me drunk enough to make sense of this,” is what he valiantly tried to say, words slurring into and over one another. “But can’t say I haven’t thought up shit just as crazy.” You’d told him that you were going away. Not to sunnier climes with bleach-blonde beaches and cobalt blue waters, but under a shroud of anonymous black. You’d told him because your career was as much his as yours. You’d told him because he might just have been the one person on the planet you could trust. You’d told him because you didn’t trust yourself. You’d told him because you didn’t know how deep into nothingness you could burrow if there was no longer anything for you on the surface. You’d told him because, one day, he might need to remind you who you were. Andinthenakedlightisawtenthousandpeoplemaybemore Almost past your bedtime. Condemned dive. Transplants on the jukebox. The bourbon was cheap; watery on your tongue and fierce on your throat. “I tried,” your stripped throat gasps. “I really fucking tried. But no amount landing in my bank at the end of the month is worth whoring myself out for them one night longer.” Your words made the last shot of bourbon sooth with a mellow warmth in comparison. In the darkest corner, where the burnt out light bulb above still fizzed, you slid the empty shot glass back and forth between index fingers. You looked down at the right, only a splintered shard on nail remaining at the end of a thrice-dislocated finger. A knuckle-duster of scar tissue connected fingers to hand, in turn a twice-broken wrist connected hand to pinned-together forearm. An elbow joint peppered with bone fragment shrapnel lurked down-arm from a shoulder which seemingly spent more time dislocated than where your maker had intended it to comfortably nestle, in its socket beside a once-broken collarbone. “After all I did for them… the injuries I wrestled through, the way fucking stupid spots I pulled off,” your bitter tirade continued. It was a hard break-up. “After all I did to shuffle myself to the top of the pack, I was still just another name on the roster. There was a fucking message in what I was trying to do, y’know? Their message was a bit louder; work with whatever charisma-vacuums and lumbering robot-speakers we think you could make look anything other than painfully generic, if you spend a night getting bandaged back together after you’re crushed flat trying to carry them.” The burnt amber flame of the dying lighter painted unflattering shadows onto your tired face. Blowing 60% proof breath through the folding cloud of smoke, you looked him dead in the eye. “I want to be something pure again.” Butmywordslikesilentraindropsfellandechoedinthewellsofsilence At the birth of the child who‘ll most likely kill you. Your body tried to reject the soulless persona you’d grafted to it. Skin itched feverishly and pores gushed icy, prickly sweat. With every zip and buckle, you encased your old self deeper within a fascist, black leather and Kevlar sarcophagus. Inside it, flair, ego and eccentricity would wither, starved of the worthless attention which fed them. Zipping the bulked-out suit up to your throat, the leather, yet to be broken in, refused to yield as your chest heaved in a wheezy breath. You squeaked mournfully with every movement. As the leather gloves took tight hold of your fingers, the tides of black met at the your wrist, imprisoning the skin under the surface. You could barely stand, body weighed down and contorted by your new identity. Fighting against the straight-jacked of unworn leather, you pulled your suit’s hood up, condemning your face to shadow. Senses trapped beneath the stifling surface, gloved fingers fumbled your first mask between hands as you brought it up over your cloaked face. Your peripheral vision bid you farewell, world condensed into all the suit would allow you to see. You told yourself that it was not you inside the suit. You told yourself that this was not a costume; not a decoration. The suit and the mask were all that mattered. They were who would walk down to the ring, who would fight, who would not be enslaved by marketing or gimmick, who would not be corrupted or conflicted by directions and edicts. You told yourself that it was not you inside the suit. It was empty. Rasa.
