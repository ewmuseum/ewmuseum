Imprisoned in Paradise: 21

12 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Oct. 12, 2005 Seymour Almasy was pretty sure that he had made a grevious error. Company wasn’t something he was used to at his apartment, especially if that company was the sort who called him at all hours of the day with minor quibbles. But this time, it wasn’t Jonathan Calver, VP of Talent Relations, who had asked for the meeting. It was the Final Fantasy himself. Sure, Jonathan was a bit obsessive, but Seymour owed the man a thank you. He worked tirelessly on Seymour’s behalf, arranging and juggling Almasy’s matches and public appearances. Without the Talent Relations guru, Seymour would have been utterly lost. And so, the Final Fantasy simply braced himself and waited for the knock on his door. He didn’t have to wait long, as the immistakable sound rang throughout the apartment. “One second,” he called, to the man presumably waiting at the door. “I’ll be right there!” Taking a deep breath, Seymour opened the door. Standing there was the Vice President, and he was bearing gifts. He raised the box of Dunkin Donuts, nodding to Seymour as he stepped into the apartment, revealing the Box O? Joe in his other hand. “Breakfast,” Calver said, simply. “Didn’t have a chance to get anything in the office, so I figured I’d pick up something for us.” Seymour looked at the gigantic box of coffee skeptically. “Geez, Jonathan,” he said, blinking, “that’s a LOT of coffee for the two of us.” “I drink the stuff like water,” the veep replied, with a casual shrug of his shoulders. “When you manage as much as I do, caffeine’s a virtual necessity.” Almasy nodded. He knew the feeling. As ragged as he had been run at times, he was sure that Calver was running around just as much, if not more. “I’ll get some mugs,” Almasy acquiesced, as Mr. Calver placed the coffee and donuts on Seymour’s table. “And some plates,” Jonathan reminded Seymour, who promptly pulled out some paper plates from one of his cabinets, “donuts can be messy. Jelly or chocolate frosted?” “Ooh, chocolate frosted, please!” Seymour’s trepidations about the meeting were fading somewhat quickly. Somehow, Jonathan Calver seemed less mechanical like this. Outside of limousines and the constraining confines of his job, he was a person just like Seymour was. As it turned out, he was a person who liked jelly donuts, considering the fact that he was enthusiastically chomping into one as Seymour returned with the plates and mugs. “So,” Calver asked, handing Seymour a frosted donut and taking a mug from the Final Fantasy, “why did you invite me here?” A fair question, really. Seymour took a bite from his donut, pulling up a chair across from the GCW executive. “I just wanted to thank you for everything you’ve done. Without you, my schedule would be a complete mess.” Calver shrugged, as he downed a small mug of coffee in what appeared to be a single gulp. “Don’t mention it. It’s what I do, after all, and I get paid rather well for it. You know, on that note?” Uh-oh. Seymour knew that look. It meant business was coming. “I don’t mean to pry, Seymour, but considering what we’re paying you’why don’t you have a bigger place?” A personal question was a relief in comparison to the spectre of business. And it wasn’t even a question he felt bad answering, so that was a further plus. “I live alone, Jonathan. What do I need a bigger place for” Besides, I’m on the road most of the year anyway. Everything that’s important is here.” Seymour smiled, taking another satisfied chomp out of his deep-fried, doughy breakfast. Jonathan nodded, his curiosity seemingly satiated. As he filled up another mugful of coffee, he suddenly remembered something he’d forgotten. “Agh! I forgot to tell you your match for this week! How could I be so--? “I already know it,” Seymour said, gently cutting him off. “Aiden Jackson, for the Cruiserweight Title.” The adrenaline had already begun to pump through Seymour’s body, as it always did at the mention or thought of a title match. His right foot drummed against the floor seemingly of it’s own accord, and Seymour’s body shook with energy that hadn’t been granted to him by the donut. “You okay, Seymour,” a concerned looking Calver asked, looking at Seymour curiously. Almasy nodded. Of course there was no way Jonathan Calver could understand. He’d never stepped in the ring before. He had never been announced as the challenger in a title match. Calver would never be able to understand COMPLETELY’but perhaps a look at Seymour’s favorite room in the apartment would explain partially. “Pre-title match jitters,” he explained. “There’s nothing I find more exhilarating in this sport than competing for a championship belt.” Calver, as expected, quirked an eyebrow. “Not to demean the GCW Cruiserweight Championship, or anything, but it’s not the biggest belt in the company. I could understand being nervous going for the United States, or for the World’but the Cruiserweight” After all, you’ve held World titles before. You’ve been the PTC Global Champion twice!” It was, Seymour thought, a rather typical response. And a fair one, really. Most people didn’t quite understand what drove Seymour so much. But the fact was that the man collected title belts like some collect rare stamps, and he treasured each and every one. “I’ve never been a Cruiserweight Champion before,” he said, to something of a gasp from Calver. “I won PTC’s Cruiserweight Tournament One, but that didn’t have a title belt. To be honest, I’ve never been in a fed with a cruiserweight title for any length of time. Now I am, and now, I have a shot to add that honor to my’heh. I should show you my game room. That’s probably the best way to explain, anyhow. Come with me.” The Final Fantasy stood, beckoning Calver to follow him. Jonathan did so, toting his coffee mug with him, as Seymour led him down the apartment’s only hallway. A short walk later brought Seymour to a door, upon which was taped the poster for UWF Coronation, the event at which Seymour won his first World Title. “I keep everything in here,” he explained, hand on the doorknob, body moving with nervous energy. “My videogames, my championships, my trophies’my reminders of who I am. After a bad day, I can always come back here and remember what I was, what I am, and what I will always be.” He turned the knob, and pushed the door open, revealing a room about the size of the average bedroom. What made it unique, however, was the d’cor. Every video game system in recent memory lay in one corner, with a 32? TV/VCR/DVD combination perched on a stand. The entire thing was plugged in by what appeared to be six or seven outlet strips connected together, with some plugged into others and the entire thing kept together by a tangled mess of duct tape. “You wouldn’t believe my electric bill every month,” Seymour quipped, seeing Jonathan’s eyes fall on his entertainment center, and the comfortable leather chair Seymour played his games in, “but look up. The walls are the reason we came here.” Calver did so, only to have his mouth drop open in astonished shock. Plaques adorned the walls. As did title belts. As did trophies in glass cases. Nearly every inch of the wall space was covered with awards and honorifics. A gaze upwards at the ceiling brought what appeared to be dozens of fan-drawn pictures of the Final Fantasy. At Calver’s side, Seymour beamed, looking up with the Vice President at the drawings. “Yeah, I thought that was a nice touch, too,” he said, pointing to some of his favorites. “I get so many pictures in my fanmail. It would have been a shame to let them just sit in envelopes.” The VP of Talent Relations let his eyes move back down to the walls. He had heard of Almasy’s success over the course of the recruiting process, but seeing the fruits of Seymour’s career put things in an entirely different perspective. “How many titles have you held,” he asked, quietly, unmistakable awe in his voice. Seymour gave an almost embarrassed shrug before responding with a simple number. “Twenty,” Seymour said, no hint of arrogance in his voice. “And all it takes is one look at each belt to remember how I won it.” It was nearly unimaginable to Calver. He had worked for GCW for a long time. Seen men such as Octavian, three-time GCW World Champion. He had seen prolific title holders. But as far as sheer volume of gold was concerned, no one could hold a candle to the Final Fantasy. “That’s, err, a lot of titles,” Jonathan said, really lost for any other words. The sheer enormity of what lay before him prevented him from saying anything else. “But there’s always more out there,” Seymour said, knowingly. “To me, a title belt’s not just a trinket, something to hold up when I go out to the ring. It’s a symbol of accomplishment, of achievement. They embody every drop of sweat, every single tear, and each and every injury I have ever suffered in my entire career. You see that first PTC Global Title up there” I nearly had a ligament in my right arm torn in the process of earning that.” He flexed his arm for Calver, and for a few moments, he could almost feel the overwhelming agony that Roulette had put him in with her relentless assault on his arm. Seymour’s practiced eyes scanned the line of titles, picking out another championship belt on the wall. “That one over there’s my third UWF Television Championship. I went up against Hot Mask, an undefeated rookie who’d just won the UWF’s Contest of Champions. He was on the hot streak of a lifetime, and I was a miserable shell of myself. But for that one match, everything clicked, and I beat him. It was one of the greatest nights of my career’a night I proved to myself that I still had it.” Jonathan nodded. Really, in the face of all of this, there was only one question he could ask. “What will the GCW Cruiserweight Championship mean to you?” Seymour couldn’t help but chuckle. It was obvious that Calver was overwhelmed. On the other hand, Almasy was not. One of the things he had vowed to himself when making this room was that he would never allow himself to become complacent, never allow himself to simply expect titles to jump into his lap. “Let’s not go putting it up on the wall just yet,” Seymour said, with quiet intensity. “Aiden Jackson’s no pushover. I’ve been watching him since I came to GCW. He’s got a dangerous arsenal of moves, and is almost unfairly well-rounded. Unlike some guys who only use bits and pieces of all styles, Jackson can work them all fluently. He’s going to be a tough, TOUGH opponent. But that’s why I prepare myself, body AND mind. Come. We should go back to the dining room. You probably have to go soon anyway.” Jonathan was too fascinated to worry about work at this point. Questions popped up in his mind like flowers blossoming, and it was all he could do to narrow the list down. “What do you mean, body and mind?” Seymour broke into what could best be described as an incredibly slow jog, raising his knees up to his chest with every stride. Calver followed, looking on questioningly. “You see this,” Seymour asked, as he maneuvered over to the table again, jogging in place by his chair. When Calver nodded, Seymour smiled. “Picture doing this for ten miles, straight, every day, and you get an idea of what I put myself through.” He thought for a moment, before clarifying his statement. “Faster than this, of course.” Calver’s jaw dropped, prompting a hearty chuckle from the 5’7?, 181 pound superstar who had held titles across the globe. “I’m never going to be stronger than most of my opponents. So I simply have to be faster. I have to have more endurance. I’ve gone sixty minutes with some of the greatest wrestlers of this generation. Some guys you’ve heard of, some guys you might not have. I’m prepared to do that each and every night if I have to. I’ve already done my running for today, and I’ve got a 3:00 PM flight to Phoenix out of LaGuardia. Everything from now up until bell time is mental preparation. Studying Jackson.” Seymour grinned, jerking a thumb over his shoulder, back to the game room. “I have tapes in a closet back in there,” he said. “Hundreds of them. Thousands of them. Of various wrestling shows, from federations all over the world. I use those to help scout. I look up win/loss records online, internet recaps, anything that can give me information about how a guy wrestles. Contrary to popular belief, I’m not playing RPGs all day. Only half of it.” Almasy gave a silly, lopsided grin. After all, all work and no play wasn’t good, either. He took his career far more seriously than most would give him credit for, but at the same time, he did enjoy a good time. “Shoot,” Seymour exclaimed, looking over his shoulder. “It’s eleven already! I hate to shoo you out of here--? “Say no more,” Calver stated, waving his hands. “You’ve got to get ready. It’s a big match for you this week, and GCW’s looking forward to seeing what you can do. I’ll leave you the coffee and the donuts, and--? “No,” Seymour interjected, interrupting the interruption, “they’ll go bad. Take them back to the office. Make some new friends.” Jonathan nodded, extending a hand to Seymour, which the Final Fantasy gratefully shook. Mr. Calver gracefully gathered up the coffee and donuts, and gave a final, cordial nod of his head to Seymour while letting himself out. The door closed, and Seymour gave a relaxed sigh. That had gone about two hundred times better than he’d ever expected. “As long as he’s not pestering me about triviata,” he said to himself, “Mr. Calver’s a pretty good guy.” With a nod of his head, Seymour returned to his seat at the table, moving a pad of paper, several printed out sheets, and a pencil to center stage. The sheets constituted Aiden Jackson’s GCW biography. On his moveset, dozens and dozens of notations were scribbled in of possible counters and avoidances, and any other strategy that Seymour could come up with. Poring over his scribbling, Seymour turned his attention to the pad of paper. Written across the top was simply “Seymour Almasy vs. Aiden Jackson”. Below this were several bullet points on what he thought he’d need to do to win. - BE PREPARED TO GO ALL NIGHT. - AVOID HIS SIGNATURES, HIT YOUR OWN. - CACTUAR CROSSFACE = THE END He would add many more such things to his list on the flight to Phoenix, but for now, he needed to remind himself of his mission. The bottom of the page had a picture of the GCW Cruiserweight Championship on it, lovingly recreated by Seymour’s steady hand. Its nameplate was empty, and would only be filled in were Seymour able to reign victorious. Wordlessly, he scrawled something just above the title belt, circling it three times. For Seymour Almasy, it was the ultimate motivation. 21
