Date: 03/14/12 (Note: Events occur following "All For One," the Aimz RP for Dangerous Games.) Donnie Bell ran. His Italian leather loafers were slick on the asphalt, and he struggled to find purchase, but still he ran. He ran so hard that his knees ached, so hard that his lungs burned with the need for air, and then he ran some more. He ran like someone was chasing him, which was good, because there was. He ran like his life depended on it, which was good, because it did. They were still after him. Always there. Indomitable. Indefatigable. Inescapable. Always coming, never stopping, always just out of sight. He'd think he saw something at the corner of his eye, but when he turned, there was nothing. He might as well have been trying to outrace his shadow. Donnie fetched up against the long trailer of an eighteen-wheeler, his pulse thrumming in his ears. He chanced another look back, but still only saw the late evening fog. But he knew -- he knew -- they was still out there. Still coming. He slowed, wincing at every kicked rock, every skritching step as he circled around to the front of the truck. He crouched as low as he could, fishing in his pocket for his cell. Maybe he could still call in the cavalry. Maybe he didn't have to escape, just evade long enough for help to arrive. Maybe-- "No bars," he hissed. "No bloody bars." He fought down the urge to pitch the thing across the lot, shifting his stance to peer around the truck. The lot was still empty, but it was as if he could feel his hunter waiting, watching for him to make a break for it. Donnie sat there, paralyzed with indecision, and just when it seemed he could wait no more, it was like fate finally smiled on him. The hollow, metallic thoom of a sleeper car opening and closing again nearly gave him a heart attack, but the voice that called out was so uncertain that he couldn't help but grin. "Hello?" the man called. "Somebody out there?" Despite his excitement, Bell forced himself to remain calm. The newcomer was a variable his pursuer could hardly have predicted, and he knew they'd move quickly to move the man from the equation. Donnie straightened as much as he dared, hurrying towards the sound of that voice. "Yes, sir," he said, scrubbing his accent from the words. "I'm sorry to trouble you, but I've run into a bit of a mess, and I was wondering if I could bother you to--" He came around the truck cab to where he figured the man was, only to find his would-be savior slumped on the ground, bleeding from a cut near his scalp. Donnie froze briefly, spinning in place. "I know you're there!" he shouted, though he didn't see anyone. "I know you're--" Bell never saw the arm emerge from beneath the cab, never saw the crowbar clutched in the woman's hand, but he certainly felt it slam into his lower leg. Donnie cried out, stumbling forward and collapsing to the pavement. Both of his hands clutched at his leg as his assailant rolled out from under the vehicle, coming to her feet. He tried to crawl, tried to scratch and claw at the ground to get away. She stood over him, her face obscured by shadow. Short, dark hair hung in her face, broken loose from a ponytail, and her suit was the next best thing to government issue, made for intimidation, not style. Donnie rolled over, kicking out with his good leg at the woman's stomach. It was useless, he knew, but he'd be damned if he let her catch him, not like this. The blow drove her back against the wall, and Bell scrambled awkwardly to his feet, staggering away at a pace that could certainly not be called quick. He didn't get far. She hit him like a freight train, like a hundred and thirty pound missile, and she aimed her foot to his wounded leg. He cried out again, fell awkwardly to the bedraggled dirt and hardscrabble grass that surrounded the lot. This time, Donnie looked up, pushing himself to his hands and knees. There was a car parked in the dirt, a forest green sedan with a crumpled front fender. He allowed himself a smile even as she climbed atop him, straddling his back. Now he had a goal. A target. A means to make his escape. "What now, then?" he taunted, trying to buck her off. "Gonna haul me away?" She didn't say anything, leaning into a forearm across the back of his head. Donnie pushed his head up again as she wrenched his arm behind him. He caught sight of a brunette in a tattered dress, a pair of handcuffs dangling uselessly from one arm. She watched wide-eyed as the woman cinched a zip tie cuff around his wrist. "Kathryn!" he shouted. "Kath--" She shoved his face down, and all he earned was a mouthful of dirt. He turned his head to the side, spitting it out. "Alright, y'got me. Congrats on that, but--" It was when he heard the hammer of the pistol cock that he knew he wasn't headed into custody. "What're you doin'? You got me. I surrender. You hear me? I surrendered!" Donnie felt the barrel of the gun dig into his scalp. The woman's breath came in quick, angry hisses; in another time, he might've joked she was about to hyperventilate. Her knuckles whitened around the guns handle, and she ground it into his skull, hard enough to make his eyes water. "Don't do this," he said, but he was unable to keep his voice from quaking. "Do not do this. You are a good person. I wasn't gonna hurt anybody, I swear. I wasn't gonna hurt--" The first shot echoed oddly against the asphalt, making the woman in the suit's ears ring. The bullet tore through Donnie Bell's cerebellum, shattering against the pavement. Another followed quickly on the first's heels. The third one she fired almost negligently as she stood, and it buried itself between his shoulders. Her breath slowed, evened out.
