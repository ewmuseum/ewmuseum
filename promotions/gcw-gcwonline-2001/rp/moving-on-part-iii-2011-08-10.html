Moving On: Part III

10 August 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/10/11 The last few months had played with Aaron’s emotions. A couple of his students had gotten jobs within the meteorology field just months after completing their Bachelor’s degree. Aaron was stuck in graduate school with little to know job prospects. Weather was Aaron’s first love and his true life passion but things were going less than well. On the flip side to that was his second love; wrestling. Aaron’s GCW career had taken off in tremendous fashion. Aaron was now the American Dream; a man with a shot at the United States title banked for the future. To say Aaron was at a crossroads would be an understatement. His GCW contract would expire at the end of August and an extension had been offered to him nearly a month ago, but Aaron left it sitting on his coffee table. He was unsure of what he wanted. He wasn’t sure of what direction he wanted to go. Saturday – 10 P.M. “Let me get another one, man.” Aaron spoke with slightly slurred words. He sat at the bar next to one of his friends, Brad, and took the last pull from his Miller Genuine Draft. Aaron reached into his pocket and pulled out his can of Skoal. He removed a pouch and placed it in his lip as he turned back to the bartender to receive his next beer. He took a long drink from it and then spit into his empty bottle. He looked at Brad who was nursing his second beer. “I have no clue what to do, man,” Aaron said. Aaron chuckled a bit and took another drink. “All I wanted to do when I was a kid was weather. Now that I’ve got a degree and am paying the bills with wrestling, I think all I want to do is wrestle.” He chuckled again before spitting into his bottle again. He turned to Brad looking for his advice, but he was slow to answer. Brad wasn’t the most obvious choice for advice, but Aaron had learned that his friend could offer up words of wisdom in almost any situation. Everyone thought Brad was a misfit after getting his DUI a year and a half ago, but Aaron got to know him and saw the good in him. He wasn’t a trouble maker; just a guy who enjoyed having a few drinks numerous times a week. “You have to factor in the fact that you haven’t gotten any positive responses to weather jobs,” Brad offered. “Think about it; weather is still your passion deep inside of you, but the fact that you haven’t gotten any offers is weighing you down. It’s your mind trying to choose what’s best for you at this moment.” Aaron nodded slowly. It was something he had never considered. His wrestling career was going great. It was inherent that he would turn to success as a way of life as opposed to continuing the failure that his weather career had become. It tore Aaron up inside to think that nearly six years of his life could be on the verge of being considered wasted. “I can see that,” Aaron said. “But wouldn’t I be betraying myself if I forgot about weather? I mean that’s just what I’ve wanted to do for twenty years. I don’t even know if I want to sign the new GCW contract. I haven’t even looked at more than the first page.” Aaron shook his head as he took another pull from his beer. After receiving an initial letter from James Bryan, he had received a new contract in the mail two weeks later. He opened the package and scanned the front page before sliding it back into the Fed-Ex envelope and dropping it onto his coffee table. It hadn’t so much as moved from its position on the glass in the last two months. “Then you need to look at it,” Brad said. “You need to go home and read the entire thing; front to back. You need to make the decision that makes the most sense to you. If I tell you what makes the most sense to me, you could turn out miserable down the road.” Aaron stared straight ahead at his reflection in the mirror behind the bar. He saw the weary eyes of a life on the road and a life as a graduate student. They both had taken their unique tolls on Aaron’s body both mentally and physically. Brad was right; Aaron knew he needed to go home and divulge his attention in the contract offered to him. “You’re absolutely right Brad,” Aaron said as he reached into his pocket and pulled out a twenty dollar bill. “I’m going to head home.” Aaron set the twenty on the bar and took a long last pull from his beer before patting Brad on the back, as if to thank him non-verbally. Brad nodded and took another sip from his beer as Aaron headed to the door and began the walk back to his apartment. Saturday - 10:30 P.M. Aaron pushed through the front door of his apartment and removed the ear buds from his ears. After he dropped his keys onto the counter Aaron made his way towards his stereo and set his iPod down into a docking station. With the remote in his right hand Aaron walked to the couch and plopped down onto it, a bit exhausted. He sighed and reached forward, retrieving the envelope that held his new GCW contract offer. For a moment he just stared at the envelope and took a deep breath. After a minute he told himself he had been putting it off for long enough and slid the twenty page document from the envelope. “Page one.” Aaron said as he leaned back into the couch and feverishly began to read. Sunday – 3 A.M. Aaron stomped his foot on the mat as Karina Wolfenden staggered to her feet across the ring. Aaron slapped the top turnbuckle with intensity as his prey finally turned around to face him. He charged forward and lifted his boot into the pretty face of the United States Champion as she propped herself up in the corner. The crowd groaned at the impact as Fujita freed his foot from the corner and grabbed Karina’s arms in a half Tiger, half Dragon hold. Bryan: Fujita is really rolling now! He’s looking for that half and half suplex! This normally leads right into that QG-Theory piledriver! Aaron lifted Karina up and over, dumping her right on her head and neck. She landed so high that she rolled right to a standing position, out on her feet. Aaron got right back up and took his place behind her. He threw her left arm over his shoulder and lifted her into the air. As he did the building began to creak and whine unexplainably. Aaron looked at the ceiling and saw pieces begin to rip from their frame. Aaron’s face was filled with confusion. His attention was drawn to the ceiling but his wrestling instincts were probing his brain all the while. Aaron dropped his gaze from the ceiling and lifted the United States Champion into the air. Without any hesitation he dropped to the mat and nailed his QG-Theory piledriver. The roof continued to rip apart above their heads until the dark mass above them loomed large. Aaron stared up at the vortex of swirling winds as they imposed themselves upon the arena. GCW fans that had been unlucky enough to get seats in the nosebleeds began to be swallowed by the tornado as it came closer. Debris began to flutter about; wood, glass, cars, animals and human beings all swirled around the black mass. Aaron couldn’t draw his attention from the twister as K-Wolf remained motionless next to him. He caught himself trying to guess the storm’s intensity but shook himself from the trance and looked back to the United States Champion. Aaron dropped down and hooked her leg as the building began to shake violently. ONE! TWO! THREE!!!!!!!! DING DING DING Andrews: Here is your winner and the NNNNNNNNEEEEEEEEWWWWWWWWW GCW United States Champion; AAAARRRR--- At that moment Aaron Fujita reaches for the title belt, but it is sucked upwards by the tornado. Aaron looks to the sky as the winds begin to exceed what he can withstand. Frantically he tries to clench the ring ropes, but before he can find a good hold his feet lift from the ground. Aaron screamed in horror as the storm gobbled him up and began to whip him around in its swirling winds. Then suddenly, darkness. Sunday – 3:10 A.M. Aaron awoke in a startle. His breathing was labored and sweat dripped from his brow. It took him a second to realize where he was, but soon his mind cleared and he recognized the living room of his apartment. Aaron reached for a nearby bottle of water and took a swig of it before looking to the floor. The GCW contract he had read laid there, folded open to the final page. The signatures of Steven Caldera and James Bryan filled two out of three lines. The third was the place for Aaron’s signature. He picked up the document and set it down on the table in front of him. He sighed loudly and picked up a nearby pen. He paused only momentarily before scribbling his name down onto the blank line. His re-occurring nightmare had to mean something. It had to mean it was Aaron’s destiny to be a professional wrestler. Aaron set the pen down next to the contract and stood up with his bottle of water in hand. He turned off the music playing from his iPod and then headed for his bedroom with his mind at ease for the first time in months. Too bad it wouldn’t last long. Sunday – 10 A.M. Aaron opened the front door to his apartment and carried the signed and sealed envelope that contained his new GCW contract to the mailbox at the front of his apartment complex. He was still tired from a rough night of sleep, but couldn’t afford to waste any more of the day to sleep. Aaron made his way down the sidewalk and opened his mailbox to leave the envelope for the mail man to take the next morning. “I guess I never got the mail yesterday,” Aaron said to nobody but himself. He pulled the five or six pieces of mail out of the box and filed through them. The first four were junk. But the fifth took Aaron’s breath away. It was addressed from a private forecasting firm in Des Moines, Iowa. Through all the hardship that Aaron had found from other rejections, he had forgotten about the application he had put into “The WX Experts.” Aaron quickly scanned the letter and almost screamed for joy as he read the final sentences. Please call our office at your earliest convenience to set up an interview with our Human Resources Coordinator. We hope to have you a part of our team in the near future. Aaron’s smile widened to an alarming size until he looked back into the mailbox and saw the envelope containing he GCW contract again. He took a deep breath and looked around, hoping to find the answer in the passing traffic or the bird’s morning chatter. He was back to square one.
