Chapter 26: The Rise and Fall of a Leader- (Part VII)

1 November 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 1, 2007 __Start You ever get that feeling in your stomach? You know, the one where the acid seems to be jumping a little too high for comfort and all you can think about is whether or not you'll have a hole through you in three minutes. That shit hurts no doubt but that's not the pressing issue. What's actually important is the question of why. What has made your insides go so sour that they're planning their inevitable mutiny? It could be anything, sure, but what about with Jay Terror? What has him smoking an extra pack of cigarettes a day just so he doesn't have to feel the rumble in the pit of his stomach? Tag teams. What were they anyways? He had to not only team with someone else but he had to find a new level of trust and hand it out. How could that work? Terror wasn't the kind of guy to run into a match and save another guy. He needed a reason. He needed something. Anything. Leave it to Caldera to fuck things up. Chapter 26: The Rise and Fall of a Leader Part VII The Official Boston Massachusetts "Quit Smoking Now!" Convention 'Man, you should have seen it man. It was like, man - seriously, I just woke up and said that's it, I'm through! I just knew it man, I was done killing myself man. Shit man.' He wasn't that fluent when it came to speaking proper English but Jay understood the man's point. Judging by the way the speaker's hand was trembling, Terror estimated that he was new to the smoke-free world and he still had that urge inside his head. They all had it - every smoker that is. It's like there's some guy hanging on your cartilage by one hand whispering in your ear. He wants you to keep smoking, he wants you to keep buying those cigarettes. The guy's like an inner devil or something. Anyways, the speaker still had him. It was so obvious. The way his breath still smelled like a rotten apple and the way that his hair was all messy thanks to a case of the itches on his scalp, everything pointed in that direction. 'You don't even know man, I feel free! I'm alive man.' He continued babbling. Jay nodded and whipped his feet onto the surface of the table. He shared glances with a few of the other people around him before looking back at the man with the yellow teeth. 'You're delusional man.' 'I'm not delusional man, I'm finally thinking right!' He argued. Jay continued, he couldn't miss out on a good argument, 'Oh come on, who says that you're thinking clearer after you quit smoking? Honestly. You think the Marlboro man would have created his stuff if he quit smoking it? The man was a genius when he had the paper in his mouth. Now tell me again how you're not delusional.' The man with the yellow teeth shut his mouth and allowed Jay to breathe in a patch of fresh air for a second. He seemed to be perplexed by the statement leaving Terror to realize that he was ultimately succeeding in breaking him. With anyone, there are certain steps you have to take a look at when you want to shift their point of view. To start, you have to make them stop. A guy won't quit making his argument if you don't shove a cork in his mouth to shut him up. Second, you have to make him think. When the gears in his brain start to get clogged with other ideas, that's when you have him snared. Finally, you have to make him believe. Once he believes, you've got yourself a fresh fried catfish. What a sweet taste. He shook his head and opened his lips again, 'I'm free. They had me captured man and now I'm free from them. Delusional is being captured man.' Terror laughed, 'You've always been free. Look at it this way, what does a boy buy first on his eighteenth birthday?' 'Porn?' 'Sometimes,' He pulled out a cigarette from the quick-draw box on his side, 'what about one of these?' Immediately he saw the faces turn toward him. It was like he said the magic word and all the puppies in the world were looking at him to throw the ball. The man with the yellow teeth, especially, studied the white stick between Jay's fingers. 'Cigarettes?' His voice was monotone. That devil in his ear started to work his magic again. 'Exactly. A kid is told that he can't smoke them or buy them 'till he turns eighteen. It only makes sense that he purchases all the flavors and types known to man because he can. Right?' The outlaw couldn't help but smirk at the audience he had attracted. Considering the location and type of people around him, it was amazing to see how powerful one tube actually was. 'I'm feeling delusional man.' 'See, I told you. Here,' He reaches across the table and teases the man with the yellow teeth, 'have this.' His eyes follow the filtered rocket ship along its path in the air making sure he doesn't blink. Under the table Jay knows that his palms are sweating and his nerves are tingling. It takes everything in him to fight the conniving words of the devil in his ear and his zombified brain from taking over completely. 'Man, can I?' 'I have plenty.' Struggling the demon, 'Nah, I can't man.' 'Think of it as a gift.' Jay twirls it like a fan between his index and middle fingers and casually glances toward the other onlookers. He felt the power. 'I like gifts.' He said letting out a gust of rotten apple. Coughing, 'You don't even have to unwrap this one.' The man with the yellow teeth shut his eyes trying to fight off the devil still. What he failed to realize was that by now he had already lost control to an army of brain chunks. The devil was slowly sinking into his core and soon enough the man with the yellow teeth would be identical to the little guy hanging from his ear. Odd, yes, but also true. He opened his eyes and a smile flashed across his crusty lips, 'I can't turn down a gift man.' Jay extended his arm and the man snatched the cigarette from his fingers. With a simple nod, he stood and walked off. 'Too easy.' Terror turned himself toward the front of the room and watched the eyes scatter. Up on stage was a short pudgy man dressed in a gray suit split down the center by a red tie. He was missing a patch of hair on the top center of his head and Jay felt as if he knew where it disappeared to. The thick mustache hanging over his upper lip seemed quite full for a balding man and therefore he formulated his thesis off those two matters together. Jay watched him fidget with the microphone and try to lower it. What a sight. 'Could you take your feet down please?' A voice came out from the crowd behind him. Jay turned and hoped to find the voice's owner, 'Huh?' A tap on his left shoulder and Jay spun again - this time toward the front. A woman was walking past with a pass hanging from her neck like she was important or something. 'Your feet, take them down please.' She was slightly agitated this time. It's funny what authority does to people. You get a laminated pass with a lanyard and all of a sudden you're Queen Elizabeth navigating the English. Real funny. Nevertheless, Jay dropped his boots down to the floor again and slid inward to the table. His attention shifted back up to the stage where the pudgy man was shuffling his feet and clearing his throat. He stepped inward to the stand and spoke slowly into the microphone itself, 'Good evening ladies and gentleman.' His voice wasn't fitting for the man Jay figured him out to be. 'It is wonderful to see all of your faces here today joining us to aid in the growing epidemic known as smoking.' Mixed reaction. 'You see, years ago there was a certain trend sweeping the nation. Someone had come up with the idea of rolling tobacco inside a piece of paper in order to smoke it. From there we saw innovations such as the assembly line and filters that made Smoking, not only more accessible but, "safer". People flocked in and snatched up this product believing that they would end their depression and change their mood patterns in general. A few statistics and commercials later, we get to where we are today. We get to the point of enlightenment so to speak.' Jay watched him create the story with his hands and laughed to himself a little at how ridiculous his voice was. At first Terror had expected it to be deep and grandiose but when it came out he found the complete opposite. It reminded him of the squeak a person makes when they catch the bottom of a fresh sneaker on the face of a gym floor. Mix that sound with a pair of clogged nostrils and you had something unexplainable. Funny even. The man continued, 'Learning began to occur and people were catching onto the affects smoking brought about. Heart failure, cancer, lesions, the yellowing of the teeth, etcetera. People looked at the pictures of a person who had lost their jaw or had half their tongue left and something about the gruesome images opened eyes. It opened all of your eyes.' He looked away from his note card showing that he was confident with the next part of his speech. 'And now here you are, quitters in the best way possible!' Cheering. The pudgy man smiled a little and scanned the crowd briefly. 'Who in here has a story for us?' Right away the hands shot up and what do you know, Jay was one of them. He stood and fluttered his hand around making his best effort to get noticed by the gentleman on stage. Like some act of fate, he was chosen. In a few seconds the same woman who had chastised him minutes ago came to his side and grabbed him at the wrist, 'Follow me.' Jay obliged and made his way through the swamp of anti-smokers wondering if he'd get the microphone by any chance. They walked up a set of six stairs and onto the stage where the woman turned around leaving Jay to make his quest to the pudgy man alone. 'Hello sir,' He chirped, 'What's your name?' Jay finally reached the microphone and stopped. He looked around at all of his former smoking friends and realized that he was beginning to slip into minority status. 'I'm Jay.' The pudgy man nodded, 'Very well Jay, you have a story for us?' 'A story?' 'Yes, that's what we're looking for.' Terror smirked, 'Yeah, I got you a story. It's to die for man.' Should he refrain from speaking? Should he blend into the crowd? 'Ok,' he started, 'so I'm going to tell you all about something that I have grown to love. It has a special place in my heart - take that back - it's the only thing in my heart that I feel deserves to be spoken about.' He tried to think but what was the point. He was Jay Terror and censor wasn't exactly a word you'd find in his dictionary. 'Cigarettes.' 'You mean used to be the only thing in your heart?' The pudgy man tried to correct him. 'Nah, my heart doesn't kick things out like that.' He grabbed the microphone from the stand and walked away from the center of the stage. 'I want to share with you all a little secret that you seemed to have tucked away and forgotten. Sit back and let me help you find it, huh?' 'This is a story right?' The pudgy man persisted. Jay turned toward him and raised his right brow, 'You'll never know if I can't get complete silence.' The pudgy man shut up and dropped his microphone to his side. He tapped his foot like he had somewhere to go and Jay turned around to look at the crowd again. 'Not too long ago you all developed a certain craving. Cigarettes. These things took you over I bet. You probably were so deeply involved that you needed some kind of hook to get you out of the hole, right?' He paused. 'Well my only question is, why'd you leave?' The pudgy man lifts his microphone again but Jay cuts him off. 'You left a good thing behind when you killed your urge for cigarettes and now look at you. I see some of you with wine or something where your favorite brand should be.' 'Security.' The pudgy man tried to seize control but Jay was a lot stronger than him. 'Look,' heavy breath from the struggle, 'Take it from me, you'll never be as peaceful as you should be without a drag. Make the right choice.' He flashed his teeth for the crowd and found that the members of security were a bit bigger than the pudgy man. They lifted him into the air and the three of them carried him backstage and away. In his last seconds he could hear stirring in the seats and a disgruntled sigh from the pudgy man. __________ So what happens when the rebellious one decides that he will put his guard down and share sides with the other man? It's a big risk, yes, but a risk that could very well pay off. Look at Jordan White: fresh, talented, already a world champion - he's the kind of guy Jay could see himself siding with. Yet something isn't completely right about it all. Can trust be open to a former member of the establishment? Can Terror really extend his hand and slap it with White when he knows what he stood for? That's when it gets tricky. So with two options in mind, it's deciding time. The Outlaw can choose to take sides with White and give him a shot at decency hoping that he truly is a changed man. Then again, he could walk in like he was wrestling any other guy and forget about White. Tempting on both sides, but which would provide a helping hand for Jay's climb to the top? __________ 'So let me get this straight, you preached about smoking at a "Quit Smoking Now!" Convention?' Anita Baker was baffled by her patient. It seemed that punching best friends in the face wasn't enough for Jay Terror and that he had to play the role of the KKK at black rallys. 'You should have seen them, they needed saving.' 'Saving? You're advocating cancer and death.' He laughed, 'You guys and your ignorant theories.' 'Jay,' She paused to gather her thoughts, it wasn't often that she came across a man like Terror, 'You will need to stay away from scenes like that from now on. Confrontation hasn't seemed to have been working out for you lately.' Jay leaned back in his new favorite chair and looked up at the ceiling. The light caught the silver pieces of it and he watched it sparkle overhead like some big patch of stars. The best part of it was that it was inside. 'Says you.' Anita shook her head and jotted down the notes in her book. She felt it would be best to switch topics and go back to the fight, 'So Jay, have you spoken to Phillip?' 'Tried to.' 'And...' 'And I didn't.' 'Right,' Like picking teeth with him, 'What happened.' He closed his eyes and tried to erase every memory that came to mind when he thought of Phillip and Art. 'I tried to see him, and he wouldn't see me. That's all.' 'So he is still upset with you?' He wanted to avoid the talk at all costs, 'You mind if I smoke one?' 'Go ahead. I want to hear about Phillip though, go on' Jay found his pack of 27s in his pocket and fetched a single stick and his lighter. He burned the edges of paper and poked the filter between his lips. Ecstasy. He let out a gust of smoke and got himself comfy. 'I suppose he hates me. So it goes though, right?' 'You're willing to dump a friendship that easily?' 'I haven't dumped anything, it's on him.' She couldn't figure him out. No matter how much she tried to dig, nothing seemed to break free. She wanted to hear emotion from him, she wanted to see him get upset, she wanted anything. Instead, Jay didn't crack. He held his feelings in and puffed them out with each drag of his cigarette. 'So everything is fine then? You're done being angry I suppose and you're ready to find new friends?' 'Friends?' He paused again and watched the ceiling shimmer. It's comforting. 'I got my best friend with me now. He's never left me. Not once.' 'Oh yeah?' Jay sat up and looked at the doctor. The smoke seeped out of his mouth slowly and made its way into Anita's blouse and hair. He pushed the cigarette close to her and made sure she got a good look, 'See this, it's all I need.' 'A cigarette won't compliment you or talk to you when you need it Jay. This is the problem. I think it's time to quit smoking.' He laughed and coughed all at the same time finding enjoyment in her idea. 'I'm serious Jay,' No smiling for Anita, 'It's taken over you. You lack emotion, care, desire - you lack everything. Jay, I'm going to recommend you leave the cigarettes behind and find something new to calm you down.' He could see that she wasn't joking. Her eyes were wide open and her mouth left no room for interpretation. Anita Baker was being as genuine as a person could be and all Jay had in mind was when he could leave. Was that human? Was she right maybe? Of course not. No way. 'Look, I let you tell me that I needed help with my social life, I let you into a few of my thoughts even, but now you've crossed the line Miss Baker. I'm not going to quit because a few of my friends turned their back on me. I'm not going to start eating cherries or some bullshit like that. I'm going to smoke until the day I die and there's nothing you can do to stop that.' Her eyes opened and closed. 'But Jay,' 'No.' For a few seconds they sat in silence looking at anything they could in order to avoid the other's eyes. Jay found the clock on the wall and noticed that he was almost finished. The silence went on until Anita found something to say. 'They told me that you had a maximum of three sessions to do. So far, we've done two and I would recommend a third. My question to you is if we do another session will you listen to what I have to say and help me understand you?' Jay finished off his 27 and tossed it into the ash tray beside the chair, 'No one understands me but me. That's just how it goes.' 'This is a first then.' She extends her hand and waits for Jay to grab it, 'It was great meeting you and I wish you luck in the future. Hopefully your habits find a way of telling you something.' Jay shrugged and shook her hand. He offered a loose grip and knew that she would have some kind of interpretation for it. He didn't care though, Jay Terror was free again. 'See ya'.' With that he stood and exited the room just like last week. Anita sighed and tossed her notepad onto the floor. Frustrated, 'That's how it goes I guess.' __________ He could only hope that Jordan would give him that boost. __End
