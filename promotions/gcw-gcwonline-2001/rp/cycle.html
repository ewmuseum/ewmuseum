Date: Yesterday, Today, Tomorrow Sometimes just rolling out of bed can be the hardest thing you’ve ever done. Joints stiff, everything hurts, you couldn’t sleep well, so you’re still tired. Problem is you know you’ve got to get up, if you stay laying down much longer, you’ll never get up, the stiffness will sink in too deep, sap every bit of strength and motivation you have. It hits you like a ton of bricks when you stand, and all the little aches and pains flare up for effect. You suck in a breath only to remember your ribs are bruised and even breathing is painful. That’s when you start to reconsider a lot of your past decisions. You spend your entire life doing something as dangerous and physically taxing as pro wrestling, and yet, one bad night at a bar can almost wreck you. Perils of the road they call it, just something that happens when you’re not smart. Usually it happens because wrestlers are notorious for trying to bully the locals. In a sport that emphasizes physical dominance, it’s only natural some of that would bleed over into your personal life. You feel the need to show everyone just how tough you are, and it ends up getting you jumped. That’s why the vets tell you, if you’re going somewhere, and going to be drinking in public, you should always have someone there to watch your back, just in case. Usually it happens because you’re trying to look tough. But sometimes like last week, you just end up being in the wrong place at the wrong time. Sure maybe you did something stupid like flash a little too much money, but for the most part you were just minding your own business, drowning your sorrows. Why it happened though doesn’t matter, the end result is the only thing that’s important. You get the shit kicked out of you, and you know it could’ve been a whole hell of a lot worse. In fact you start to think if you hadn’t been in such good shape, they probably would have killed you. Or at least taken you out of the game for a good long time. That would’ve been pretty pathetic, having your career ended in some dive bar, by a bunch of goons. Yeah it’d be one hell of a sorry end to the story, you spend your entire life taking more punishment than the human body should ever sustain, but in the end it’s not all times you got dropped on your head, it’s not all the times you hit the ground the wrong way that stopped you. No, it was a bar fight over something really stupid that did you in, just pathetic. That’s when you start thanking god it wasn’t worse. They could’ve just kept kicking, could’ve just beat you into a bloody pulp, but they didn’t. No, they were nice enough to just steal all your cash, and roll you out in the gutter once you passed out. Hell those damn humanitarians were great, they even made sure to have the cops pick you up for causing trouble in the bar. Yeah it could’ve been a lot worse. Before they arrested you, the cops dropped you off at the emergency room. Get you all patched up, you don’t remember even being there, but you know it must have happened. The cast on your wrist tells you that much, so does the wrapping on your ribs. You remember waking up in jail, with that feeling of being hit by a truck that tells you something bad happened the night before, and it all comes flooding back. A week of your life wasted on booze, and all you got in return was some bruised ribs, and a fractured wrist. Oh, and you’re in jail, you hope you had fun. So you make your phone calls, the movies lie, the police will give you more than one call if you need it. A few hours later you’re rushing to be at the show, and you just barely make it. Thank god you didn’t have to wrestle, and you start wondering if you should take a couple weeks off, but a real big show is coming up, and you’ve pissed off the boss big time, so you know a vacation to recover isn’t in the cards, and even if he did like you, can’t lose your spot. You keep going, and next week you’re in Greece, waiting. Another match, one last one before the big show, and you’re tired. You start rethinking your choice not to ask for time off, but you’ve made your bed, and now it’s time to lay in it. The only problem is getting back up. Once you’re up and active, the pain doesn’t go away, but at least it dulls, you don’t give it a chance to settle, you do the stretches, everything you’ve ever been told to work through the pain. Stay moving, keep active, it’s the only way to recover, you know this, doesn’t make it any easier though. When you do sleep, you dream of Morphine, Vicadin, Kodine, anything to stop the pain, a twisted perversion of the dreams of youth, instead of the sugar plum fairy, you have the chorus line of dancing painkillers. Still you don’t take anything for the pain if you can help it. You’ve already got enough vices, don’t want to add addict to the list. Sure maybe you drink too much, smoke a few cigarettes, but at least you’re not a junkie, and you won’t let yourself be tempted to become one, so you let yourself hurt. Movement in the only substitute you can find, so you exercise like a man possessed. At the end you’re exhausted, and you hurt worse than when you started, but at least you know you’re getting better. You take a hot shower, eat, make some phone calls, get ready for bed. The skin under the cast itches so bad you want to gnaw off your arm at the elbow, but you try not to think about it. You close you eyes, and lay down, try to sleep. You start the cycle all over again.
