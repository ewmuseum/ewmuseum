Finding sunshine in a world of black and white

11 August 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 11, 2008 Somehow he had managed to smoke a box of cigarettes in only an hour - no small feat for anyone. As he unwrapped the new container and packed them into his palm, he could feel his body moving. Shaking. He was steady, yes, but he could feel the light sway of back and forth movement. Like an addict, he gently rocked. Perhaps the cigarettes had worked like hard drugs? Maybe if you have too many they become a lethal dosage? Either or, Jay swayed and ticked with his mouth as he stared off the hotel balcony overlooking the streets Of Toronto, Ontario. The city had done no good for him. It brought him nowhere. It was a shit fest at Warpath and the only thing he could think about was the fact that his shoulder was bare. He wanted that title. Lighting a new cigarette, he inhaled a hefty amount before releasing a cloud to the atmosphere. Images replayed in his mind. He remembered Vivica walking up the ramp way with the belt on her shoulder. A smile on her face. That was his smile. It was his moment. He deserved that title, not Vivica. And then the image switched to Jordan White. He lied motionless on the mat, easy prey. Jay rocked harder as he listened to the way his bones snapped after the chair bit his ankle. Over and over, the snap played like music to his ears. Finally he smiled. The shaking continued. It was becoming a habit that wouldn't easily be doused out. Over and over, he rocked like a metronome. The spirit in his veins, the nicotine in his blood, he had so many thoughts and feelings flowing through his mind that acting normal was about as easy as beating Vivica. His cigarette had been smoked to the filter and he pulled another one free from the box. Before he could light it though, the phone rang. Pulling the Zippo out, he flicked it open and lit the tobacco. His phone was on its third ring when he picked up. 'Yeah?' 'Jay, hey. It's Leanna.' 'Yeah?' 'Hey. I was wondering if I could come over or something. You know? Like stress relief maybe? Or just to talk. We haven't talked in days.' 'I don't want to talk.' 'We could go somewhere. Do something.' 'I'm staying here.' 'Can I at least come over. I want to see you.' 'You're attached. I thought I told you not to -' 'Please Jay.' 'You know the address.' He flipped the phone shut and shook his head. He could feel the swaying returning. The addict's huddle he called it. The fact of the matter was that having Leanna was the furthest thing from comforting. She says relief when really she means fuckin' the shit out of him until she gets off. Once climaxed, it turns back into one of those love fests. The kind where you exchange thoughtful sayings and tell each other how perfect the other is. She was hooked to Jay like Jay was hooked to Marlboro and that fact made him wince. He wasn't looking for a companion. He wasn't looking for a good time. He was looking for gold. If anything had been proved by Caroline, it was that women get in the way of dreams. They nag. They whine. They want attention. They just don't understand that they don't get attention when there's a chance for gold. Still, she appeared at the door twenty minutes later while Jay was still outside. Opening the sliding door, she poked her head out and eyeballed the back of Jay's head. 'Hey stranger.' Her voice was soft, wanting love. Jay didn't turn, 'Hey.' 'You alright?' She must not have been watching. 'Been better. What's up?' She found the chair beside him and turned it to face his way. 'I just missed you. It's been a few days with no word, I was afraid something happened.' He laughed it off, 'Something happen? In Ontario?' He turned with spite written on his lips, 'Nothing happens in Ontario. Nothing at all.' A venomous after tone rang in the stale air and he took another drag from his cigarette. 'Ok. Is this the wrestling?' 'It's nothing. Just nothing.' 'Jay. I'm just trying to help.' She paused, thinking. 'Is it Caroline?' He turned her way again, this time his eyes squinted a bit. 'If you're here to guess what's going on in my head, you can leave. I don't need this.' Frustration reigns clear giving Leanna the sense to knock it off. 'I'm sorry.' A pause before, 'You wanna' have sex?' He knew it was coming. She always asked it. Leanna was one of those nymphos you keep around because sometime you may want to fuck a big breasted girl. Beyond that, she was straight poison. 'Let me finish this cigarette.' He smoked in silence, still rocking. Leanna caught glimpse of the nervous habit but figured she had already asked more than enough questions. Instead, she watched him inhale and exhale. He was thorough with it, meticulous. The smoke he released came out in perfect clouds and ascended from his lips to join its larger counterparts above. Jay leaned back for his last drag and flicked the butt off the balcony. He stood, looked at Leanna for a minute, and shoved the sliding door open. The air conditioning had made the room cold but Leanna didn't care. She still stripped down to her panties. Jay pulled his shirt off his body, a large bandage wrapped along his side. It matched well with the one across his forehead. Leanna said nothing about it, only thought of ways to avoid the injury. He climbed onto the bed with her and got close. Close enough that she could smell the pack and a half of nicotine coating his teeth. She kissed him and nearly drown in the taste of Marlboro. Still, she let him do what he did. Jay's eyes examined the headboard as he shoved his weight back and forth. His head played Vivica's smile again and again. Thrust after thrust, her grin grew wider. Leanna's screams came through but only made light thuds in his mind. Overpowering them was the sound of cracking bone and torn flesh. Jordan White cried louder than she screamed and he used it to pick up the pace. Harder she called and he delivered. He could imagine himself driving the steel chair harder into White's back. Harder over his ankle. He could imagine spiking Vivica's head harder into the mat and holding her tighter. Leanna reached climax early leaving Jay to himself. He knew he could fake it but what was the point? It wasn't like he was truly into the act at all. His mind was elsewhere. The title was the only thing he could see. He finished ten minutes later and rolled off. His eyes traced the ceiling as Leanna's arm crossed his torso. 'Thank you.' She said, as if Jay had done her some kind of big favor. 'Yeah.' 'I could lay here all day like this. Just laying. Looking around. It's so easy to get lost like this.' He didn't answer. He wanted another cigarette but knew the box was across the room. He didn't feel like moving either. 'What are you doing today?' 'Not sure. Maybe walking. Maybe flying home. Haven't decided.' 'Are you taking me with you?' 'To home?' 'Sure.' 'I could take you home. Not to my home though. I need time away. Time to do things.' 'Time away from me?' 'Yes,' he exclaimed rather bluntly. She was shocked and bit her tongue. 'Am I that bad?' 'I just have other things I need to concentrate on.' 'The wrestling.' 'Yes.' 'Why don't you start a new career? Something easier? Something less stressful?' It was his turn to be shocked. 'You don't get it.' He found the strength to move and grabbed his cigarettes up from the table under the no smoking sign. He lit one and found a chair. Leanna sat up, raised her voice. 'You're obsessed with it Jay and it's messing with you.' 'What do you care?' 'I care about you. You know that.' 'You're the only one.' The shaking starts again, his mouth makes a tick sound. 'Jay, you need help.' 'Unless you're going to help, you can leave.' 'Jay.' 'You don't know what I would do to carry around that belt. You don't know the pain I'm feeling right now. Leanna, you don't know shit about me or my situation. Just leave and let me work on it myself.' The outburst took her by surprise and went straight to her heart. Jay was beyond fixing. He was a crazed animal she noticed. She slipped off the bed and pulled her clothes back on. Her walk to the door was slow and quiet. With one look back at Jay, she would remember him for the way he rocked back and forth. The way he sat so uneasy and so on edge. Leanna walked out the door and let it slam back to its place. Jay continued to smoke his cigarette. __________ How could this happen? Why did things not go to plan? I was supposed to win. Supposed to walk out as the new champion. Not the guy who almost did it. Fuckin' Vivica. Fuckin' Destiny Driver. Ugh. You know what the most fucked up part of it all is, not even Jordan won. Of course. Now I have to forget about the son of a bitch and what he's done if I want to pursue my belongings. You know, this couldn't have gone any worse. __________ The bar was one of those places that anyone could go to for any reason. Just getting a drink or getting wasted, you could go and take a seat at the wood countertop. In Jay's case, he found a pleasant buzz swirling through his system as nicotine and Whiskey frolicked with one another. They danced circles in his mind bringing back the awkward sway that other customers obviously noticed. 'Jay, let's just hold off for a little, yeah?' He was back in Philadelphia, thousands of miles away from Canada, sitting at a barstool he'd sat at before. The bartender, Sam, understood the circumstances. 'Hold off?' He quickly turned his glass over and let the remnants pour down his throat. 'I don't have a drink. I need a drink.' He took another drag. 'Jay, c'mon. You know this isn't how you fix things. Do it in the ring, not a place like this.' He laughed it off, 'The ring? That ring is cursed - set-up so the fans decide who wins and dies. Fuck the ring.' 'And the ring is saying fuck you Jay.' He paused, thought gathering. 'You think you can't beat her? You think that this was destiny that gave her the win? It was shear luck Jay, that's all.' 'And I'm Rich Rollins.' 'Fine. You're not getting another drop from me though.' Jay shook his head and took another drag. He let the embers burn a hole into the bar as he set his hand down. 'I'll get my own drinks then.' 'Just get out before you cause any damage,' he examined the burn mark, 'you fuckin' changed Jay. Fuckin' changed.' Sam was a kind hearted guy, well liked by the Philadelphia public he knew. Seeing Jay though was not something that he ever expected. He remembered a young, jovial version of the boy sitting in his bar. He wasn't the best then and Sam liked him for that. Now, he wanted nothing to do with him. Jay flicked his cigarette onto the floor and stumbled out of the bar and into the street. He whipped his cell phone out from his pocket and dialed. 'I need the car. Yeah, still at the bar.' He closed it and found a nearby bench. The air was noticeably colder but Jay couldn't feel it through his numb skin. His eyes focused on a building across the street, a very tall building at that. He watched it ascended into the sky higher and higher, higher than any building he knew of. As he followed it back down, he noticed that he had found a seat next to a familiar man. Daniel Franco. 'You alright Jay?' He was too dumbstruck to speak and instead eyed up his former manager. His head started to pulse and he could feel the chills the air sent. 'What're you doing here?' 'Sitting I guess. You?' 'What the fuck?' 'Yes?' 'You. Here. Me. Here too. What the fuck man.' He nodded, knew he was drunk. 'Things happen sometimes, I don't know. Not like I picked this bench for the sake of your being here.' Jay was still spinning, his head still throbbing. He pulled a cigarette as his best bet for relief. 'Want one?' 'You still smoke those?' He lit it; felt no answer was in order. Franco laughed aloud and leaned back on the bench. 'Makes sense then.' He had a smug look from behind those glasses he wore, a look that made Jay sit up and wonder. 'What's that?' 'You Jay. It all makes sense.' Jay took a drag, unintentionally blew smoke into Franco's face. 'Huh?' Daniel grinned and nodded, 'The match Jay. I watched you know. That little devil's fast out there. Real fast.' He connected into his wave length but still cocked his brow. 'How do I figure? I figure?? 'She's a heck of a lot faster than you. Coincidence maybe or just those things you seem to keep lighting?' 'She's fast. Yeah. Not these though. Not even why I lost. Not by a long shot. Luck man. All luck.' 'Call it what you want Jay, I'm still attributing her being there to her speed and endurance. She could keep going. You? You were just about done either way you look at it.' Jay changed the subject unable to accept the criticism. 'Why were you watching?' 'C'mon, it's you Jay. I had a hand in shaping you, remember? You'd think I wanted to see how well I did?' He took another drag and began to sway again. His world was spinning with him, everything began to move. His vision started to blur and his head started to pound harder. Jay tried to shake it all away, 'I need that title Daniel. I need it.' 'I know Jay. That's not my thing though. Talk to Steven Caldera about it.' Jay could feel himself losing control over his own muscles. His arms lashed forward and his hands grabbed Franco around the collar. He shook him and looked hard into his eyes. The mixture of nicotine, alcohol, and paranoia had combined creating the most unstable version of Jay imaginable. 'Not your thing? Am I not your pet? I thought you were a trainer!' 'I was.' He tried to break out of his grip but found it quite hard. 'Let go of me.' 'Help me Franco. You know how to beat her. I know you do. You must. How Franco? How?' 'Get the hell off of me.' He shoved him to the far side of the bench and stood. Jay followed. 'You son of a bitch.' He lunged and grabbed him around the back. As he wrapped his hands around him, Jay felt a hard thud resonate in his back. He released his hold and fell to his knees. A man in uniform looked down at him with his nightstick ready. __________ Jail. Yeah. Steven's going to be pleased. __________ He sat in the holding cell for all of six hours before Octavian touched down in his plane and walked through the doors. The look on his face was easy to read, uneasy and confused. Jay watched him slide in and speak with the officer. They brought him to the cell and opened it for Jay to leave. 'You're free to go.' 'Thank you' said Octavian to the guard. Octavian had nothing to stay on their way out and Jay kept the same way. As they exited the station though and walked down the steps, a serious, yet drawn back voice appeared. 'Have a good time?' 'You don't understand Octy. You just don't get it.' 'No Jay, I think I do.' They stopped before getting into the car, Octavian looked over Jay. 'You lost, yes. There are others things in the world to worry about. You know, like your reputation.' 'What reputation. The one where I lose big matches?' 'Just get in the car. We have a plane to catch.' 'I'm not done. Not yet. Not till I have it.' Octavian climbed through the open door and sat onto the leather seat. Jay started to follow but muttered. 'That belt will be mine.'
