He had a heck of a lot more respect for the man’s gladiatorial abilities than he would ever admit to on GCW television, and he knew that his imminent clash with the former “Outlaw” at NC-17 would be no stroll in the park. The kid lacked the technical proficiency of Chris Bagwell and the psychological dominance of LeStatt Knight. His style was scrappy, it was all over the place. And he had power, he had pull and swing. In Steven Caldera’s absence, the only man more powerful in the company was Christian Zenith, and even then the margin was miniscule. The runt could sit with his feet up on Caldera’s desk and do as he pleased without any fear of repercussions. “155” 156? 157?” Bottom line, Jay Terror had a lot going for him. “158” 159? 160?” But then again, Andy Murray wasn’t exactly much of a slouch either. He was arguably the most popular man in GCW’s current age, and the most consistently dominant wrestler to grace the company’s rings in the past twelve months. He was experienced; a tenured veteran who’d been working the ring when Jay Terror’s age had yet to reach two digits. He’d recovered superbly from his Dangerous Games disappointment with big victories over Tessa Windsor, Brad Jackson and Vivica J. Valentine. With haste he swiped the back of his hand across his brow, sending a warm splattering of sweat across the concrete floor. Picking up his towel, he dabbed his forehead carefully and took a quick glance around the room. It wasn’t much, his basement gym, but it did the job. Plain white walls lined with a multitude of wrestling photos and a plain concrete floor broken up only by an old wrestling ring and a couple of thick blue mats, it was hardly the lavish paradise you’d expect a global superstar to keep his finely-tuned body in tip condition, but that was the whole point. You don’t work out to relax; you do it for the grind. You push yourself to the limit, you work until you can barely stand on your own two feet. In the end, you want to sweat like Michael Jackson in a kindergarten playground. You want your muscles to ache and your eyes to blur, which was exactly why the noble Scot kept decorations to a bare minimum. Regaining his composure, his arms beginning to full the burn, Andy steadied his balance and watched the action unfolding on the screen. Bryan Mayhem charged and clobbered the pixelated Scot with an overly stiff clothesline. Subconsciously Andy winced and rubbed the back of his neck, perspiration lubricating the movement. Swinging his arms back down, Murray was surprised that they didn’t fall right out of the socket as he dropped them, such was the strain after over an hour’s worth of hard graft. Every day it was getting harder and harder, and someday he’d have to face up to it. Andy wasn’t exactly a spring chicken and at thirty-one he was at least a couple of years senior to 90% of the professionals doing the rounds in the wrestling business these days. He’d been doing this for over a decade, and every day it seemed to take longer and longer to recover from the occupational hazards of a professional fighter’s life. It wasn’t just the physicality that was draining the King. He found himself a stranger in a strange land. Surrounded by egomaniacs, despots and psychopaths, he truly felt like he was the last of the good guys. The old-guard, the entertainers, the gentlemen. One-by-one they’d all been driven from the sport by their younger, blood-thirsty contemporaries, and the wave of alienation that swept over Andy every time he stepped into the locker-room was getting harder to deal with. It wasn’t as if he was alone, but the fact that he counted a man of Jordan White’s questionable character as his closest ally in the company speaks volumes. He was the last of a dying breed. The last of the great showmen. Chris Bagwell. LeStatt Knight. Jay Terror. Jacob McKail. These men were parasites, bloodsuckers. They’d taken a once honourable business and bled it dry for all it’s worth. Ten years ago, such men would have been driven from the industry without a moment’s hesitation, and now they were flourishing in it. If Andy were of a lesser disposition, it might have been enough to drive him from the diseased sport of professional wrestling once and for all, but he was in far too deep for that. He’d been doing it for over ten years, and he certainly wasn’t going to give up on account of the lack of scum that had attached itself to the profession over the years. Kicking against the pricks; that’s just what he did. Andy Murray would continue to thwart the bloodsuckers until his body could take it no longer. And there was still so much for him to accomplish, so much for him to do. He hadn’t given up on professional wrestling just yet. Slowly he shambled across the gym and picked up a half-full bottle of water. After unscrewing the cap and shuffling back across to the scream, he parked his backside on the ring apron and continued to watch. The fans popped as he nailed a big move, drawing a smile from the exhaust Briton. If there was one other thing keeping him going, it was the fans. Without question. David Yale said that Andy Murray would never reach his full potential pandering to the masses, but his dominant in-ring performances suggested otherwise. He was their hero. Their bastion of invincibility. The unflickering light in the tunnel of darkness. ? if only his success had the same effect on his family. Professional wrestling is a terrible career choice for a man who wants to raise a family. Always has done, always will be, and the case of Andy Murray was no different. He might have ceased legal proceedings against Jane, but in true Murray style there was always something else right around to corner to distrupt his harmony. They’d reconciled somewhat, they were getting along. They’d come up with a feasible plan to ensure both had adequate time with their son, and for the first time in over a year they were talking about. It didn’t make sense for Andy to continue fighting with his ex-wife, and he was glad to finally bury the hatchet. But the kiss? That was too much. He shouldn’t have entered her house when invited; he should’ve turned and drove away. But no. He couldn’t. Sucker. It could have been worse, he’d told himself, but it brought him no solace. His lips had met with those of his ex-wife, a woman who had sought to bring nought but strife to his livelihood during recent times, and it was cutting him to pieces. Poor Kayla. The mere thought of her getting caught up in all this mess made him feel free from the indestructible warrior that he portrayed on television. If she found out? well, he didn’t even want to think about that. She’d brought him so much during his darkest hours that the thought of betraying her would kill him if he dwelt on it for too long. But things were far from harmonious in that department, either. They’d been arguing, bickering. For the first time since they’d met it seemed like things might be starting to snag. Neither party was blameless, and Andy knew that his overbearing determination to his career would eventually have an adverse effect on the relationship, but the growing distance between them was as much on her as it was on him. They was growing argumentative, snappy. Perhaps growing tired of Andy’s continued extended time on the road, or perhaps jealous at the amount of time that he’d been spending around his ex-wife recently. After all, ever since she’d known him he’d virtually been at war with Jane, and now they were apparently getting along once again? well, the alarm bells are understandable. He might not have been able to pinpoint the exact reasoning behind the growing strain, but he knew that it must come to an end sooner rather than later. His domestic turbulence was following him from home to the workplace. It was exposing a weakness to his opponents; a weakness that Jay Terror in particular would have no trouble exploiting whatsoever. Andy Murray could not allow that. What he was going to do about it, he didn’t know. But he wasn’t happy. Kayla. Jane. He couldn’t go on like this. Coexisting with the mother of his child and his more recent squeeze would drive him only to further dysfunction. Something needed to be done. Something drastic. Somewhere there was a poison in his life, and it needed to be sucked out. Immediately.
