Date: 5/1/09 .::Disclaimer- May Contain Strong Language and Violence::. .::Viewer Discretion Advised::. ---- So sick of all the lies... ---- Mechanical Rhetoric Things change. As much as I want to ignore that fact, it's true. I saw a guy today that I haven't seen since college, probably more than thirteen years. He came up to me on the street, grabbed my hand and shook it. Blast from the past. It was like seeing a ghost. We're both nothing like we were back then. I'm a lot harder. He was a lot softer. 'It's good to see you, man.' He said to me with sincerity brimming in that earnest expression. 'You probably don't remember me…' Funny thing is, I did. He was a good guy, despite the adolescent failings we'd all had back then. Chasing skirts, getting wrecked and pretending to be rockstars. I played bass that year, and he stood in as one of our many drummers. We never got a real gig, just local parties and shit, the college special. We were minor celebrities, at least in our own minds. Somehow, then, I'd always known he would make it. He would be the stable one, the bread winner family man. Nice to see I'd been right. Power suit, cornflower blue power tie, and a precisely folded Wall Street Journal under one arm were enough to tell me that. Inane conversation, and somehow it's more real because of that- always. It's better than connecting on a personal level. Keep the shit surreal, and light. 'God man,' he said, still pumping my hand up and down, 'it's so good to know you're still around. Still giving 'em hell?' I shook my head. Nah, too old for that rebel shit. 'No,' he argued, 'you are. I saw you on TV, wrestling in that match at some Pay-Per-View they had on at the bar... couple weeks ago, I think. Shockwire or something. What's it like?' It was more than a couple weeks ago, now. I don't bother to correct him, instead I shrug. Can't really explain that, not to someone who's never been there. It's good, I tell him. I like my job. 'It's good to know someone like you, man. The guys at the office don't believe me, that you an' me passed out at that kegger. Remember?' I did, the past seemed so close right now, and the nostalgia factor was killing me. 'You're real man, fucking real. Don't ever change.' He pressed his business card into my palm and moved away into the sunset. He was wrong. I am the antithesis of real. Don't get me wrong; I'm not saying I'm living some grandiose lie. I'm just saying, what you see up there on the screen, that's not me. That's the overblown, extended version of me. The DVD extra, deleted scene and right in your face, ready to rip your heart out asshole. Heh, not that I'm going to debunk my own shit, and break that fourth wall. Truth be told, the hatred between myself and Garbage Bag Johnny is REAL. Four years ago, I signed with PCW. Nearly three years ago, I torched my WCWF contract on their Monday Night Revolution broadcast, a week after winning the PCW World Heavyweight belt. Riding the waves of invincibility. I was untouchable, unstoppable. Chalk it up to blood loss, and the twenty-seven stitches that put my ankle back together. I was adrenaline high, after surviving the holocaust that match had become. PCW closed down, a month and a half later. I defended that belt ONCE. Celebrity status was never my desire in all this, but I got it just the same. It's like a drug. You crave it. You want it more than anything else. I guess, somewhere along the line, I just wanted to be loved. Yeah, sad, isn't it? In a way, I suppose that sums up the basis for all of our actions. Wrestlers. We exist in the moment, riding the waves of adrenaline, thriving on the cheers, and the jeers. There's a little bit of madness in that. Not that we require any sort of counseling for that, or a prescription to mellow out the fire that burns in our veins when our music explodes over the sound system with that echo. There's just a moment when you need to recognize that we're not those nine to fivers who get off on the power lunches and closing sales. For us it's a little more old school. I guess I find that funny sometimes. I look around, and so much has changed. I haven't, but maybe there's some sort of hope for me yet. Don't hold your breath, though. Leopards are born with spots that never change. So are assholes. Doesn't matter, because the fucking buck stops here. Free rides are OVER. ---- Friday, May 1, 2009 somewhere in Maryland He stood and walked slowly towards the railing, feeling the wet wood slick against his bare feet. He leaned down, resting his elbows on the balustrade; swollen fingers laced together, hands and arms out in the warm rain as he looked over the sleepy town. "Buttfuck nowhere," he muttered, cocking his head as he listened to the silence around him. Just the sound of the rain, rattling against the metal awning above his head. With his eyes closed, he could imagine the patter of little animal feet, running hastily towards some destination. He bent his head, letting the rainwater soak his hair, washing away the sweat, and grime from the long drive. It felt nice to be quiet, a still moment of introspection that wasn't ruined by playing a role, or listening idly for his turn to speak. The screen door creaked behind him; rusted hinges moving to allow someone passage onto the upper veranda of this small bed and breakfast inn. He didn't move, content to remain there with his hands clasped as though in prayer, rainwater dripping down his nose. "It's air conditioned inside," she said softly, her hand snaking around his waist, fingers splaying against his clenched abs. She pressed against his back, feeling the familiar contours, despite the fact that he was a lot leaner than he'd been all those years ago. "We can't keep doing this," his voice was a low murmur, almost lost in the sound of the rain, hitting the roof like stones. Harder now. His voice shook slightly, not something that was terribly noticeable, but she heard it, and cocked her head, listening to the sound of the rain pattering down as she watched his nostrils flare. "There's got to be a line drawn somewhere." He yawned, feeling the leaden exhaustion in every fibre of his being. He'd never even made it to the bed, instead he'd passed out on the couch after drinking everything in sight, including the mouthwash. "We need a fuckin' barrier... something." The sigh that came from between his lips as he lifted his head was filled with weariness. How many times had he let her back in, only handing her the means to destroy him again? Weakness, for sure, but once upon a time, it had been love. God knows, the blame didn't rest solely on her shoulders, or his. Like lightning striking in the same place twice, they were bound by fate. "Why?" She snapped, her voice rising in challenge, matching the wind driven rain for intensity. "I don't fucking understand you. You want to fuck, but you don't want me around... you give me money, and then tell me to hit the road. What the fuck is it with you, Brad?" "I hate you." He turned in her embrace, hands closing over her shoulders. Strong fingers dug into the bones, but she made no sound. Defiant. "No, you don't." She countered, her eyes flicking back and forth as she studied his face. Rainwater matted his hair down, turning the grey strands to black, peeling away some of the years that had been unkind. "Besides, you need me. You know I keep you level, Brad." "Level? Yeah, that's a joke. I haven't been level in years." His eyes narrowed, features transforming into that glower that she found so amusing. She was a cancer, and if he let her get that foot in the door, let her take root again- he'd need to slice her out, forcefully excise her. The thought of cutting her excited him in ways he didn't want to think about right now, not with her hands still wrapped around him like that's where she belonged. She still had that slim body, but she seemed more toned than the last time he saw her. "Stop it, Kaitlynn," he ground out, shaking his head slowly. "This is not where I want to be right now, arguing with you about shit that's dead and buried." He pulled away from her, returning to the railing. He leaned over again, letting his hands dangle free in the rain. He'd been trying so hard to steer clear of the bad nights like this. Moments of sheer insanity, trying like hell to tamp down that angry fire that threatened to turn into a blaze. "I can't let you fuck with my head again," he growled, eyes fastened on the rain-slicked streets below. "In the morning, we'll go to the airport. I'll put you on a fucking plane back to Canada, and we'll pretend this little interlude never happened, ok?" One hand stole up to the back of his neck, squeezing the base of his skull where the headache loomed. It was always lurking when she was present. She infuriated him to no end. She said nothing, bowing her head as she moved closer to him, reaching up to pull the elastic from her messy ponytail. She tossed her head, knowing that he could sense what she was doing as she dragged her fingers through the loose strands. "No," she said softly, resting her hand against the small of his back. A moment later she joined him at the railing, leaning against him with a familiarity that should have been unsettling. She rolled her head against his shoulder, jade-colored eyes opening, and fixing on him. "What do you want from me?" He lamented, his voice cracking slightly as he looked down at her. "Try honesty." The smile that curved her lips was more knowing than the Mona Lisa's. "Look me in the eyes, Brad. Tell me that you don't want me here, and I'll go. Tell me with that steel in your voice, and fucking sincerity. Can you do that?" "We need space." His words were a tortured growl as she slipped behind his back, pushing her hands up beneath his shirt. The shirt was bunched up now, dragged up against his chest, so he grabbed it behind his head, and pulled it off, letting it drop to the wooden planks. In the dying gray light, she traced the contours of the tattoo that covered the skin, feeling the old scars beneath the ink lines. She remembered when those were fresh, still red and raw. She remembered when he didn't have the tattoo there at all. Memories were comforting to her as she let her hands explore his warm skin. Silence spilled out around them, and for a moment she'd forgotten that she'd baited him. She'd forgotten there were questions, let alone a challenge between them. All that mattered was the rediscovery. "I still love you, you know." She said finally, as though the confession had been a long time coming. Search and Destroy was tattooed across his broad back. It wasn't just a clever mantra, or a way of life. It was a simple fact- something he was born to do. Her fingers grazed his chest, and he tensed involuntarily as they slid over the scars without pause before touching his neck. His pulse beat against her palm, strong and steady. "I know," he replied. "But we're still not doing this." "…talk to me," she said, lifting his arm and cuddling against him as she returned to her former position. His arm fell woodenly against her, and she sighed, rolling her eyes. She leaned against him for a moment, feeling his solid weight against her. She'd always loved his body. "Don't push your luck, babe." He said flatly, feeling no need to explain himself. He could feel the sword of Damocles hanging over his head, and the need to get away was gnawing away at his insides. Fight or flight. He could go for either right now. Familiar thoughts. Somehow it always ended up this way. ---- Mechanical Rhetoric Fuck. Hands are clenched so tight my knuckles ache. Itching for a fight. I wanted to hit her; I wanted to break her pretty little face in for making me revisit the past. Filled with spite. I'm pressed up against the wall with her. She knows it; she knows that I won't follow through. I'm trying to push it aside. Focus. My head is killing me, and all I want is a fucking drink. A little Houdini moment of escapism. Slip the locks, and pretend I'm not hunched in a corner, licking old wounds. Trying to hide from myself when all I want to do is bust some heads. I want to fuck something up that was beautiful. Instead, I walk. Alone. Misty rain falling now, lightning flashing overhead, lighting up the sky. Glorious pyrotechnics. I'm walking in the fucking rain, looking for a sorrow to drown in. How sad is that? I don't really have any more points to prove. Not here. I know that I am garbage here, destined to always be buried somewhere in the middle of the pack. Didn't take a genius to figure that one out. Nah, it was driven home pretty clear last week, when Terror let that idle comment slip. Whatever. I am what I am. Simplicity, and that's something I am more than willing to embrace. All you want is too much. Fame is not something they want you to have here. Get the fuck over it and move on. I am the soul of truth, the last bastion of veracity in this fucked up lie we call a world. The best fire, the truest fire is the fire of hate. It burns clean. Hotter and hotter. It makes me feel good to be here. Back with the anger. Back with her. I can be honest with myself now, alone in the rain. I can't fake it with myself. I can't lie. This is not about me reclaiming the past, as much as it feels like that. This is about something I should have done a long time ago. Something for the pure pleasure of it, without obligations and all that other shit attached. This is my middle finger to all of you. Take what you will from that.
