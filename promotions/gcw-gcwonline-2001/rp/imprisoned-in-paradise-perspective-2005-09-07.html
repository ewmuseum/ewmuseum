Imprisoned in Paradise: Perspective

7 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Sept 7, 2005 It was a weary Seymour Almasy that sunk into the back of the limousine that GCW provided for him. Despite only being a few minutes after noon, Almasy had already done more than most men would do in an entire day. A breakfast with GCW investors at 8:00. An autograph signing at a grocery store at 10:00. And still there was more to do. The former PTC Global Champion nibbled on a granola bar, looking at the well-dressed figure next to him in the limo. “Ah, welcome back, Mister Almasy,” the suited man said, “how was the autograph signing?” “Fine, thank you.” Seymour cocked his head to one side, looking at the man Steven Caldera had assigned to him. He’d introduced himself as Jonathan Calver, GCW’s Vice President of Talent Relations. The man had a handshake like a steel trap, Seymour recalled, slight remembrances of pain going through his right hand before he finished his thought. “So what’s next today?” Mr. Calver looked down at his legal pad, where Seymour’s schedule was written in immaculate penmanship. He scanned the sheet for a few moments, before nodding. “The local hospital, on behalf of the Make-A-Wish Foundation. You’ll be meeting Randy Wilkins, nine year old leukemia patient.” Seymour nodded. The visits always did him good, and were among his favorite things that he did for the various federations he’d worked for. It still flattered him immensely to this day that people would wish to meet him. “Do we have the things I asked for,” Seymour questioned the veep, who responded with a nod in the affirmative. “Yes, indeed Mr. Almasy. One Game Boy Advance SP with copy of Final Fantasy Tactics: Advance, one PTC Global Champion Seymour Almasy action figure, and one GCW Seymour Almasy T-shirt, youth large. I apologize for not having a GCW action figure for you yet, Seymour, you’ll be in the next seri--? The Final Fantasy waved his hand dismissively. “Don’t worry about it,” he replied. “After all, I just GOT here a week or two ago.” “I know, Mr. Almasy, but it is my job to make sure that you are as happy here as possible, and that your name is projected as it should be. That’s why I’m the VP of Talent Relations.” And by God, Seymour knew, he meant it. The hand of Calver was seemingly everywhere he went, assuring that everything was correct. His omelette of choice at the breakfast, for example (three eggs with mushrooms, green peppers, and cheddar cheese). Making sure that he had bottled water to drink at the autograph signing. He had even provided the granola bar that Seymour was happily noshing on. In spite of Seymour’s constant protests, the businessman had always shrugged them off. “It’s my job,” he said, simply, both repeating himself in the present and in Almasy’s mind’s eye. “Alright then,” Almasy said, with a shrug. If Mr. Calver was going to be so helpful, why not take advantage of it? “Am I booked for this week on WorldWide?” Calver flipped through the pad like lightning, looking for the pertinent information. He found it with a quirk of his eyebrow, before giving Seymour the answer. “Surprisingly’no. Of course, Mr. Caldera will want you at the arena for an interview or something of the like, but I’m honestly shocked that you’re not scheduled to compete.” So, for that matter, was Seymour. Based on his realization last week, he’d half-expected Caldera to have him booked in three matches over the course of the evening to maximize Seymour’s visibility. But’no. He almost wondered what the catch was. “We’re almost there, Mr. Almasy,” Calver announced. Once you get there, you’ll have two hours. At 2:30, you’ll come back and meet me down here, and we’ll take you to the airport for your flight.” Seymour nodded, as the long black limousine pulled in front of the hospital building. Tearing a sheet of paper from the legal pad, and handing Seymour a plastic bag, Calver spoke again. “Take these. They contain all the information you’ll need to find the proper room, as well as the gifts you requested. Good luck, Mister Almasy.” The Final Fantasy took the objects gratefully. Calver was a very nice guy’but there WAS something about the VP of Talent Relations that grated on Seymour’s nerves. “Thank you very much,” Almasy replied, “but please, call me Seymour. I’m not used to being called Mr. Almasy. It sort of makes me feel uncomfortable.” Jonathan Calver blanched almost immediately, shuffling his legal pad in his lap as he looked apologetically at Seymour. “I am SO sorry, Mis--err, Seymour,” he corrected. “I will remember in the future.” Seymour winced at the utterly upset look on the VP’s face. “Don’t worry about it. It’s not really that important.” Out the window, Seymour could see the hospital’s front doors pull into view. The limo came to a stop, and the driver exited, opening the door for Seymour. The Final Fantasy exited, still feeling somewhat awkward about all that GCW stardom had brought him. He was a young, 24 year old man. Seymour was perfectly capable of opening his own doors, gathering his own gifts, and transporting himself where he needed to go. But Seymour Almasy was, for better or worse, a star. He had people to do such things for him, whether he liked it or not. While it WAS nice having a great bed on the road, for the most part, the answer was “not”. -- -------------------------------- Thirty minutes later, Seymour was quite happily settled in the room of one Randy Wilkins. Things had gone perfectly so far. Randy’s eyes had gone wide as saucers, Almasy had presented him with the gifts, and now the two were happily chatting away about what the conversation always turned to after the well-wishes and pats on the back. Wrestling. “You were AWESOME last week!” the young man said, grinning ear to ear. Seymour could only smile in response. “Thanks,” he said, “I appreciate that.” In truth, he’d been sort of worried that he was going to lay an egg on national TV. It had been so long since he’d been under the watchful gaze of Primetime Central that he was afraid he’d gone rusty. But when he stepped into the ring with Borealis, it was as if he had never left to begin with. That made him grin. “I’m glad it went well,” he told the youth, nodding. “Borealis wasn’t an easy opponent. Took a lot to beat him.” “Yeah,” Randy said, in agreeance, “but he wasn’t your toughest opponent.” “Oh, really,” Seymour asked, eyebrow quirked. “Who was?” The youngster’s response was immediate. “Tsunami Oni, right?” Ah yes, the Devil From The Deep Blue Sea, Seymour’s arch nemesis during the early days of his career. Indeed, Seymour had battled Oni on multiple occasions, with Tsunami emerging as one of the few men who could claim a winning record against the Final Fantasy. “Tsunami WAS tough,” Almasy replied, a small grin on his face, “but not the toughest.” Randy took a few moments to think, before trying again. “Karina Wolfenden?” Again, Almasy shook his head no. “Don’t get me wrong,” he replied, “she kicks harder than any human being on the face of this earth, but she’s still not the toughest opponent I’ve ever faced.” The young man was forced back to the drawing board. If those two, among the most feared superstars in wrestling, weren’t his toughest opponent, who was? “I give up,” Randy managed, looking intently at his favorite superstar. “Who WAS your toughest opponent?” Ah, good, he’d given up guessing. Seymour supposed that it wasn’t really a fair question, all things considered. It was a trick one at that. Tsunami Oni wasn’t his toughest opponent. Nor was Karina Wolfenden. Nor would Rich Rollins be. “Myself.” It was Randy’s turn to quirk an eyebrow. Before he could flounder too much, however, Seymour explained himself. “You know I’ve won a lot of matches, Randy. You also know that I’ve lost a lot of matches. Once you get to the top of what you do’everyone’s incredibly skilled. Oni, Wolfenden, myself’we’re all pretty much equally good at what we do. We have different styles, sure, but we’re all relatively evenly matched.” The boy nodded in response, waiting to see where the Final Fantasy was going. “So, in any given match, it’s not our physical abilities that determine who wins, but our mental abilities. Our state of mind, our belief in ourselves. There have been stretches of my career where I haven’t had that. That’s why I was gone for so long.” Randy looked at Seymour. That explained a lot, actually. Why Seymour hadn’t been seen in Primetime Central for so long. In truth, it was one piece of a slightly more complicated answer, but it was the most relevant piece to the current situation. Seymour reached a hand out, patting the cancer patient on the shoulder. “Believing in yourself isn’t something that can be taught. From what I read in the letter from your parents, you believe in yourself 100%.” “Well, yeah,” came the nonchalant reply. “Under the circumstances, I sort of have to, you know” It’s a matter of getting by from day to day. Each one’s a victory.” The Final Fantasy looked at his fan with new respect. The boy was strapped to various machines, and had multiple IVs in his arms. Yet despite this, and the treatments that he went through, he still kept a smile on his face. “You’re a brave guy,” Seymour said, unable to imagine what the youngster went through on a daily basis. It made being Caldera’s media whore the most mild of concerns by comparison. Randy, though, managed to wave his hand slightly to dismiss the statement. He had something else on his mind. “Enough about that, Seymour. Tell me’what’s it like?” There was an impish grin in Randy’s eyes, though why exactly Seymour couldn’t be sure. All doubt was erased, however, after Seymour queried the youth. “What’s what like?” “You know, being a SUPERSTAR!” Oh. THAT. Of course. While to Seymour, it was becoming sort of annoying, there was no way to explain that to a nine year old boy with stars in his eyes. No way he’d want to, either. Besides, even SEYMOUR had to admit that, as annoying as it could get, there was still something really cool about riding places in a limousine. “Well?” he began, trying to figure out the best place to begin. So he just let it all pour out. Stories about limousines and five-star hotels, of wrestling in front of 60,000 people. When he actually sat down and talked about it, it didn’t sound half bad at all. And it wasn’t.
