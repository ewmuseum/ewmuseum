Rehab

19 September 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9.19.06 I remember nothing from that night; it is all just a bad nightmare from what people have told me. My only recollection is from the information my friends who were with me at the time and the array of doctors and nurses have pieced together for me. Never in my life would I expect one single night to change my outlook on life so much. As much as I never wish I had gone through that one night, I think at the same time it may have been a blessing. If it wasn’t for that night over six months ago I probably would not be breathing right now. Things change everyday in our lives; it just so happens that most influential one in mine was for the better not the worse. That moment in the history of my life is the single most depressing and amazing moment in my life. After I flat lined on the operating table for almost a good 30 seconds and was brought back to life by those wonderful doctors whom I owe my life to. At that point the few I could call my friends were there to help me along, make me get away from the drugs, help me become me again, not the man addicted to these foreign substances the my mind had told me so many times that I need to just to be happy and succeed in life. This was the point I had to make a decision, one I would have to live with for the rest of my life. Drugs and the so called happiness I found with them or the chance at real happiness in life. Was I just delusional? Maybe there is no real happiness in life, I couldn’t be sure. But the one thing I was sure of was the fact that if I was still on drugs there was no chance of me ever finding out. At that point with the help of my friends and some ex-co-workers I made the decision to check myself in, no I wasn’t famous enough to enter the Betty Ford Clinic but I went somewhere low key, away from the world where I could deal with my problems and not the world. Although being at this facility was never mandatory or hell I was not even told to be there by anyone expect friends, I took my time there serious. If I wanted to find a better way I needed to get help, and I obviously couldn’t help myself. Throughout my whole six month tenure at the facility the various people I encountered shaped me into whom I am today. If you take the old Peter Vetra good Peter Vetra, and you mix him with the different types of folk he ran into you get a great new concoction, the new Peter Vetra. The main thing if not the only thing that led me to become the new me, was the exercises. The strategy they used to get me clean. The one that till the end of my life I have decided to continue in hopes of me staying clean and happy, such a simple solution but at the same time so effective, keeping a journal. Now who thought that such a thing from your childhood could help the road to recovery? I for one was very skeptical, I felt like a third grader the first couple of days, hell I wasn’t sure if I was still all messed up on the drugs still maybe this was just some kind of a horrible’horrible trip. But with each and every day, with each and every different activity my new mentors had given me, writing in the journal become more a hobby and the enjoyment of my day, not something I dreaded. I had finally found a way to capture the demons and with this advice and the help of my friends I was on the fast track to recovery.
