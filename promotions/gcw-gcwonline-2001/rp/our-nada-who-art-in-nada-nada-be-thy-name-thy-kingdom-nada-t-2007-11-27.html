Date: 11/27/07 "Oh my god, I love this song." When she told him that was when he finally understood that’regardless of the money, free press, and amazing sex’he had to get rid of her. It's not that he actively disliked Nickelback, because it is hard to really get into hating anything that bland; it's that he wasn't ready to fall into the arms of such banal people just because they were on some higher social level than he was used to. Mediocrity, as it stands, always offended him on some underlying level. It didn't leave strands of bile clinging to his uvula like some things might, but it inspired the kind of consistent and slow climb that built up a very definite, very fatal pressure in the space between his brain and his skull. He also didn't like granola. He eased his foot against the brake, a minor up and a slow down, with a hand moving tentative toward the radio. A white guy with Jesus hair complained in stereo to no one in particular. Ashlin, daughter of some ancient publishing magnate that smoked treetrunk cigars and drank snifters of brand, bobbed her head several years beyond the point of it being socially acceptable. Taste wasn't for sale when she went to have her nose broken in three places and reset to curl up into the retrouss? tip of the Irish; wasn't in stock when she had saline sacks shoved into her breasts. Sniffling, she pulled a ring away from her manufactured nostrils and pointed it at Jordan. "Want a bump?" He waved it off with a mentholated cigarette in his hand, the primitive radio degenerates still howling soulless. He had friends that had disconnected their antenna entirely, and it was only then that he seemed to understand why they would outright remove the option. The light went green; he pushed on into that good night down another city street with nothing much to really do. Someone mentioned a club over a dinner so expensive he hadn't been able to pronounce it properly; neither of them was convinced of the idea beyond having a place to pass their time idle in the one city in the world where people looked at them as a celebrity couple. She sang along as they weaved through and against traffic, switching stations to regulate the hints of respite allowed by commercials or the occasional block of dead air. He tightened his lips and her voice kept rising; she kept turning up the volume to have the speakers almost loud enough to drown her out as they drifted from bad old song to bad new song to bad song somewhere in between. Rock music with lyrics that everyone could relate to’like relating to music was the only way to appreciate it’didn't seem to hold anything special as far as he could tell. At the very least, they were the whores of the artform and kept their sound and voice as open and nondenominational as they could’offerings for the almighty, one eyed dollar. He rubbed his face as almost empathy for the powder going up Ashlin's rebuilt nose. He'd been resisting for weeks in the haze of paranoia that followed his ejection from the good graces of Caldera. He'd also taken up smoking herb again like it was going to be easier to hide in the case of a piss test. Then again, crazed fear has never made for rational reactions and he wasn't putting that much thought into it anyways. It was like drinking to him; it was like high school again and all he needed to do was find a spot in the crowd to blend in. Maybe he was frustrated already and the deluge of music devoid of charisma just hammered him further into a hole he'd been trying to ignore. Either way, there were lines that he couldn't just backtrack once he'd gone over, at least without lightening his load some. It was all about the pressure, it was always about the pressure: the kind of pressure that can make a man wonder just how much he has to have to drink before swerving into a building becomes an accident instead of a suicide. Not out of any innate want to slam headlong into brick, of course; just in the name of sating a curiosity towards how the world really worked. Truth was: facile; mutable; accommodating to a fault. He wasn't friends with Terror anymore than he was enemies with Rollins, but he found it hard to argue with the television screen. Not that he saw himself in that exact shade or with quite that posture; not that he found much correlation between the way he seemed in his head on one side of the monitor and the way he looked from the other. He'd come into the job knowing that none of it would ever personal and that everything was just an extension of business. It took all the effort he had to maintain that belief in between the steel chairs and the sneak attacks, the fire in the eyes of people that were maybe just better actors than he was; maybe just followers of Stanislavski or maybe just redirecting. It was either that or the worse conclusion: that he was the calm center of people that hated and loved like mythical gods and had no mind for anything in between. It doesn't really matter what he was thinking; it's not like he talked about it or hinted at it or did anything but carry his shoulders a certain way or drag his heel when he switched from the gas to the brake. Muscles firing in routine order on routine procedure; turn signals flaring with all the conscious effort of blinking or breathing: it doesn't matter what he was thinking because he was trying not to think, and that deserves some respect. He was celebrity; thinking would interfere with that job on the worst possible levels and, besides, the famous always had something to fall back on. There had to be some benefit to the hounding, the touring, the interviews, the fighting, and the sudden introduction to a new tax bracket’even if it was just something as simple as the ability to intrude on anyone that had yet to officially abandon him. If the night turned out badly, there was always the chance of hunting down the old friends that hadn't turned their backs on him in keeping with their assumptions that he'd turned his back on them first’not that there were but so many of those left, and not that any of them were the kind of people he'd want to go to in the name of escape. There was John (who had used Jordan as a test subject for a lie about an ex and an abortion’neither true’in the name of making sure he could impress some ditsy chick that wouldn't find it interesting to learn he'd more or less burned a year on the couch) and David (who just wondered why women weren't awed by his having spent a year on the couch) at the top of his list; the others flowed in descending importance from there and made the whole idea seem flawed in comparison to just finding a clean, well-lighted place to get drunk. If the night turned out badly. Nothing said it had to; he'd spent years tolerating people that had even less to offer than Ashlin. Not to underestimate the value of a friend with a good CD collection, and not to overestimate the value of having all of his free time leeched away by a debutante. If he had to choose between her and Sage's bloated lawyer, well, then the world had already forced him into the worst kind of false dichotomy. At least it had a simple answer to it. So he drove, stitching together test runs of conversations and following the streaks of orange and white and red glare blurring out against his windshield. Ashlin maintained paradox, the singing and uncultured elite; the white Charlie Chaplin mustache of wasted cocaine the only visual cue of her ties to the influential. She stole his phone from the dashboard, keypad clicking back to her presses and obeying the hint of coo in her voice. He kept his face ahead as she found the camera option and aimed it at him, hairtriggered. It popped fake white against him, immortalized in pixelated digital’immortal being a condition relative to the longevity of the information, and the hypothetical conditions wherein it lasted onto a second generation of copies, then into a fifth, and so on, and so on. She crooned at a scattershot dirge pace: "what a wonderful caricature of intimacy," if he even strained to find anything in the bilge other than a quiet comfort at her ability to drown out the original vocalist. Enough; he jerked that twitching hand to the stereo and smacked play without a shred of stoicism clinging to him. Dinosaur Jr cracked out, a CD Sage had thrown his way the last time he'd gone to Blackton, or Sageville, or whatever that tattooed maniac had taken a liking to for any given stretch of time. Ashlin rolled gray eyes to him. "When'd you start listening to rock?" He didn't grunt or shrug or give anything away. "Thought you were all urban, and shit." "You been listenin' to it for the last hour and a half. Guess I started somewhere in there." She grunted. "Where's your head at, Franky baby?" "Put the phone down, Ash." Snap, flash. "Why? You're fucking cute." "I don't like cameras. Always feel like I gotta pose, always end up posin'." "Smile." Interception. His phone shot three synthesized drum knocks that knocked her away from the camera and into a text message that she opened with an air of authority in her interference. "Who's Zoe?" He looked over his shoulder, eyebrows cocked. "Zoe who?" "Uhhuh. Zoe 'heard you were back in town and wanted to talk,' Zoe." "Weird name." "Don't fuck with me, Frank. Who is she and why does she have your number?" "Had the same number for like five, six years now. Lotta people know it." Which was true, aside from the period of time where he'd used a phone GCW had given him for promotional purposes. It found its home under a train; GCW's database had to go back to his classic number. "And what kind of people is she, then?" "Shit if I know. She leave a last name?" "How many Zoes are you fucking." "Chill out, Ash. I don't even know for sure who chick is." "Then who do you think she is?" "I mean, I went to high school with a Zoe." As expected, the conversation ended with a frustrated Ashlin walking back to her’very well outfitted’apartment, and Jordan driving directionless, if not aimless. He'd done his best to put up a thirty minute front to assuage her complaints, and she'd said she was tired and that it was too late already, under what could be understood as the first step of a plan to call him back shortly to see what he'd found to get into as the night progressed. He kept the phone on the dashboard?"I've seen enough to eye you, but I've seen too much to try you"?and lit another cigarette as he found himself singing along. But he was alone and it was socially acceptable to be socially unacceptable when in such accommodating company. He knew the city, or he knew the streets he always found himself following in his year-to-year routines; he trudged those same tracks down a crowded downtown, past the open air caf? and the bus stop parallel that his exgirlfriend Alyssa had tried to assault him at when his GCW career was fresh. He hadn't even taken a bus since he came back from Australia; he'd moved to rented cars to a single owned one, with the heavy intonation of vehicles Sage would supply in some venues. His ticket price had increased, increased again when he left Caldera's wing. Yet he drove around his hometown in a Caprice on low profile tires; he'd known drug dealers with nicer cars. He could have assumed he'd funded several. The hour mark found him stopping for gas and a bottle of soda at a corner store he'd passed by at least five times already. He'd recognized a group of guys on the beer aisle and forwent the soda in the name of getting away before they managed to recognize him. His gambit paid off; he hadn't been very thirsty to begin with. Coming back down the block his phone rang, a thirty second loop of an old Nas song that didn't get the chance to wander into its first repeat. Ashlin. "Sorry about that, Frank." "Don't worry about it, girl." "I drank too much, and I shouldn't have started fucking around with the coke. I'm sorry." "It's cool, it's cool. So, you tryin' to meet back up, or?" "I think I'm just going to lay down. I'll call you tomorrow. Alright?" "Word." "Love you." "Night." "Night, Franky." Her voice dropped him back into his focused lack of focus, that preternatural manmachine bond that allowed high velocity travel with a minimum of thought: no more inhuman than any other modern habit. He crept stupid from heat source to heat source like a cat on the scrounge: he wasn't hungry or cold or even looking, he just operated on the instinctual awareness that warmth meant something. But his old haunts were repopulated by new blood, and the old ghosts had left no forwarding address. He dialed and waited until one of the few Zoes he'd known over the years answered’truthfully, the only Zoe he'd known to contact him at random points in time. "Frank? Holy Hell, thought you'd gotten too famous for me." He shrugged, for all it mattered. "Nah. Hasn't gone to my head yet." "Well, look’are you still around?" "Yeah, shit. I'm on North College right now." "That's word. I stay on Pegram now’a couple blocks down from Cordelia." "You mean the park? Yeah, yeah, I know where Pegram's at." "I'll leave a light on, alright?" "Hah, you tellin' me to swing by?" "Thought it was assumed. Heh. You too busy?" "Nah, nah. I'll be by there." "Good." He put "Freakscene" on loop, "I've seen enough to eye you, but I've seen too much to try you," a little admission to what relating could add to a song. He wasn't cheating on Ashlin; empirical evidence put it somewhere near the exact opposite of cheating on her. He followed the curve of the road until he left it for another and another in quick succession; a stairstep line across the map with J. Mascis on his speakers and the appropriate chip on his shoulder. A few houses had their lights on when he got there; it was her old BMW that gave her away and not even the new rust on the bumper or crack in the taillight could disguise it well enough. He parked behind it in time to spot the blinds crack open for a second; he stepped out of the as she watched in the angle between the glass of the storm door and the oak finish of the real door. She shivered, arms wrapped around themselves. She was; in fact, the same girl he'd always entered the same cycle with. Her hair was still short, she was still a yellow sort of pale, her chest was still small, her ass was still flat and wide. She was cute but, as she said, he was famous’that had to mean something. They'd never fucked, barely come close to coming close to it. They knew each other in high school; he knew her without much afterthought until someone’postgraduation’claimed that it was obvious she had a thing for him. This, as it went, didn't work out in his favor and started the cycle. After months of sporadic talking, one would be compelled to start spending more face time with the other. This lead to parties, movies, smoke sessions, and the realization that they were each too aloof, too selfabsorbed, and too concerned to ever be able to stand the other's company for much longer. It was eternally mutual. He couldn't help it; he wasn't too stupid to notice the pattern and he wasn't dismissive enough to ignore it. It always crossed his mind when he saw her, and it never faded before she did. He'd wager they were friends, and he knew exactly why he picked the lines that he'd sing along to. Always under his breath, though; never boastful. She let him inside with a shaking head. "You cut your hair." She was right; it had seemed to get shorter from show to show. Not that she had seen them; not that she hadn't. "Yeah. Yeah I did." He nodded as the first step of inviting himself to a seat. He didn't have any melancholy memories to go wistful over; his relationships had ended because they needed to end, and he never fought that’given that vocal opposition and fighting are not the same. This was what he had to come back to, the never had and the never would. They'd made no pacts about marriage if they ended up single at 30, at 40, at 50. They'd made no truces; they made few if any concessions to the other's presence. They'd spent weeks of their lives, cumulative, on couches next to each other; nothing to say and bad movies to watch. Another night was nothing. "You, still, y'know?" His torso laughed. "Stay fucked up?" "I've been clean for a year, Frank." Her gray eyes washed white in the amber of the room; his rock of solidarity failed and fell, battered by waves. All things erode if they last long enough to get the chance. He just turned his head’not to her and not away from her. He just turned his head as the present again betrayed the past that birthed it’but that was the cynical way to see things. "For real? Like, don't drink, don't smoke?" She shrugged under her thick coat, crumpling fake leather. "Clean." "Shit? Shit. Any reason?" "Can't be in high school forever. I guess." Her head wandered off, eyes cast sidewise. "That's, that's true, yeah." "What about you, though? I haven't even seen you since you dropped out, and now Mike's telling me you're like, a superstar or some shit now." "Mike, fuckin', Mike Hall, dealer Mike?" "Well, yeah." "Dunno how you can stay sober hangin' around him." "Hanging around him's the best reason I've seen. Hah. Seriously, though, wrestling? Isn't that like, kinda, you know’weird?" "Weird as fuck. It's just a job, though, and shit; not weird enough to turn down that kinda paycheck. You know?" "Well, considering I stayed in and got my degree to waste it working at a museum, um. No I don't even have a hint." "It's. It's rewardin'." "That's good to hear. I just, I just have a hard time believing shy little Frank could be like, famous. I," she laughed. "Fuck. I didn't mean to sound like that." "Don't worry 'bout it." "It's just. Whoa. When'd you start being that kind of social?" "Came to me, I guess." "That's stunning. Really. Shit, and I'd kind of thought we'd be getting our degrees together. Looks like you made the right decision’too many art majors out there already." "You cool?" "Yeah." She nodded tight, conserved motion. "I'm cool." Not entirely truthful or not entirely clean, she fiddles around until she's got a glowing Marlboro Light to her lips. "So, like. Holy shit. What's it like?" "It's just a job, I mean. Straight up, it's like wandering into this weird TV set and smiling for the cameras if they're lookin' your way. It's fake as hell." "You mean you're fake as hell when they're filming you?" "Don't see any other way to do it. They don't even call me by my name, shit. I'm wrestlin' as Jordan White and, far as any of them care, that's who I am. They make the, uh, the disconnect pretty fuckin' easy." "I guess. But don't you like anybody there? You can't just fake the whole thing. Right?" "They're alright, I guess. It's just, okay. Where you work, people probably have their real names, and they probably act like just, normal motherfuckers. Wrestlin', it's like, everyone's in spandex, everyone's got a gimmick, everyone's dressed up like a fuckin' idiot’it's hard to like somebody when you don't even know what the fuck their friends call 'em. Y'know?" "Okay, yeah. You've got me there." "Smooth on, then." "So, why'd you get back in touch with everyone you knew except me?" She smiled; it was loaded and cocked. "Not as easy to predict as the rest of the old crew. Besides, I didn't get up with everybody." "No?" "I mean, I ain't even heard about like, what Leslie was up to." "You've still got a thing for her? Damn, maybe you haven't changed that much." "Good chick. Ain't too many of 'em in the world." "Speaking of, what kind of trash are you hanging around these days?" "Upper class trash, shit." "Any specific upper class trash, or?" "Yeah. Name's Ashlin, dad's some publishin' fatcat, or some shit." "Moving on up. You hear, Leslie's married?" Stabbing her elbow into the back of the couch and pivoting to it, she threw the question at Frank with ice in her eyes. "Heard somethin' about that." "Yeah. She got pregnant and hitched, god, it's been awhile now. Think it was right after you two split up." "Ain't that some shit." And Bob Dylan, tangled up in blue, had gone 33 years since he'd wondered if she'd changed at all; if her hair was still red. Frank's life, then, could be read as a collage of appropriate things that other people had said with no mind to a reformed cokehead that’in some cases’wasn't even considered yet, much less reformed. And time would always keep on keeping on, wasted even if well spent. He froze like a 30th century man; he always froze like a 30th century man. At least he wasn't that 20th century boy anymore’and neither was but so applicable. He didn't have time to save anyone from themselves, or he was already too dissuaded to see the reason. He just moved along, vaguely defined by the words of others.
