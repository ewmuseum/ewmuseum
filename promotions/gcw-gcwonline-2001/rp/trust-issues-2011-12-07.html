Trust Issues?

7 December 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/07/11 The apartment felt empty. Xander sat on his couch, reading the note that was left for him. Kirsten had to go back to New York, but she couldn’t wait to see him again. He thinks to himself that she may have used him again, just like she had when she in league with Alex. But there is a part of him, that same young man that was pulled from a line in a concession stand not too far from where he lives now, that wants to believe something different. He gets up and walks over to the bathroom. He looks at the bruises on his face and neck, still fresh from his return to the ring, the result of a flashlight that was used during his first match back. He doesn’t think to himself how it was that he allowed that to happen. It wasn’t something that was worth trying to figure out. What he wants to figure out is what he intends to do about it. That isn’t very easy. His cell phone rings. Without hesitation, he flips it open and answers. “Hello.” His voice was still a little rough; the handle of the flashlight went across his larynx. He pulls the phone aside and tries to clear his throat, but to no avail. Xander listens for a moment, and responds “It looked worse than it was. Mom, you don’t have to worry about me. This is what I’ve been train…” Xander’s mom cuts him off, like any good and caring mother would do. Probably telling him that he should be even more careful, or find a safer line of work. “OK, I’ll come.” As Xander hung up the phone, he felt trapped. He knows the moment that he walks into the door of the house he grew up in, he’s going to be hit with the deluge of questions like “How could you wrestle again?” and “Why don’t you go back to school?” He thinks about it for a moment and knows that if he doesn’t head there tomorrow, he will never hear the end of it. The house smelled of pot roast, vegetables and coffee. Xander’s mother, knowing the appetite her son used to have, went all out cooking. She prepares the table with Xander’s sisters Kara and Suzanne. Suzanne’s husband Rick and Kara’s boyfriend Owen are in the living room watching the Browns play the Ravens. “How the hell could they let Rice get that run off?” Rick gripes as he takes a drink from his beer. The front door opens, and before he could enter, Xander is mobbed by Kara and Suzanne. “Where were you Thanksgiving?” “I haven’t seen you in a while.” “What happened to your face?” “Mom is gonna kick your butt.” “You should go back to school.” Owen gets up and walks over to Xander, and gives him a big hug. “Man, it’s good to see you.” His breaking into the verbal barrage from Xander’s sisters very much welcomed. “How are things in the…entertainment business?” One could tell that Owen is trying to avoid saying wrestling. “I’m not an entertainer.” Xander retorts. “I’m a wrestler.” “You tell ‘em.” Rick yells from the other room. “Thank you, Rick.” Xander replies with a hint of sarcasm. “No problem.” “Where are your bags?” Suzanne asks as she starts to look behind him. “I’m not staying. I don’t live that far, I can easily drive back home after dinner.” The look on Suzanne and Kara’s faces drop. They hadn’t seen their “little” brother in over a year, and he was only going to stay in Republic for dinner. They hoped to be able to spend more time with him, talking about years gone by, how they all missed their father, trading stories about what they are doing in their lives. This wasn’t in Xander’s plan though. “But Paulie, we didn’t get to see you at Christmas, we didn’t get to see you at Thanksgiving.” For someone that was a couple of years older than he, Kara was starting to act like a whiny little sister. “I would love to stay, but I have to get back to Cleveland and train.” One thing Xander is good at is not telling his family the whole truth. “After all, I do have to stay in pretty good shape.” “Well, Paulie…Owen runs a gym. If you really need to work out, you can work out there.” Kara is now involving her boyfriend in trying to trap Xander here longer than he wanted to be. “Come on. Please?” Xander looks at Kara and then Suzanne and knows that it would only take one person to keep him there at this point. Unfortunately for him, she is in the kitchen. “Alexander Paul Searle Junior, you can stay the night.” There it was, the secret weapon that mothers all over the world that works well against their kids once they are already weakened. The dreaded full name assault. Xander just rolls his eyes and responds, “Yes, ma’am.” The full name works its magic again. “Now come in here and help me out.” “Yes, ma’am.” He knows that he’s done for now. After the dinner was over, Xander was sitting in the backyard with Rick and Owen, drinking whatever manner of frosty cold beverage they wanted. Xander, knowing that he will have to be up early to hit the gym for some private workout time, is sticking to a bottle of Pepsi MAX while the other two are imbibing in cold cans of Coors. “So, what’s it like being a wrestler? Lots of women everywhere?” Rick asks. Not very slick on his part, especially since Xander has only one match under his belt since joining GCW. “Man, Rick. You just don’t know. All we do when we aren’t in the arena is fuck groupies like rabbits. It’s pretty hardcore. One guy brings a suitcase filled with nothing but rubbers everywhere we go.” Rick’s eyes open wide. “Really?” Xander just laughs. “No, you dumb fuck. I usually arrive the day before the show, workout, do interviews to promote the show, go to my motel room, sleep, wake up, workout, go to the arena, do what I do there, then leave for the airport to come home. There are some guys that like to party after the show, but that’s what they do. Me, I’d just rather get home, soak in a hot bath with Epsom salt, relax and worry about my next match.” “Why don’t you party with them?” “Did you watch my last match? I’m a curtain jerker. I don’t get paid enough to party after the show. I get paid enough to get my ass kicked every time I step in the ring.” Owen interrupts. “I see guys like you in the gym all the time. Workout all the time, hoping to get in with a company that might not even be on TV. So I see how it is.” Xander stands up and walks closer to the fire. He stares into the dancing flames as he thinks about things. “I guess I’m lucky. Every match I’ve ever had was on TV. But it sure doesn’t hurt any less when I get dropped on the mat, or have a flashlight smashed into my face.” Owen stands next to the man that he hopes will one day be his future brother-in-law. “Why do you guys do it? There are better ways to earn a living. Growing up, I never remembered even seeing you watch wrestling.” Xander continues to stare into the flame, almost like he was mesmerized by the almost fluid motion of the pyre. “Remember how it was when I was in High School. I would get that rush every time I would dunk over some kid, especially at home. That’s the rush that I get every time I go out there. Every time I get the fans either to cheer me, or boo me, it fills me like it did in High School. You know that after Junior College, my chances of getting into a bigger school for basketball were slim-to-none. And forget the NBA, I wasn’t that good. “But I’m making a living, even if it isn’t that great of one, getting that same rush. That’s why I do it. That’s why I beat myself up every day, whether it’s in the gym or in some arena. I think that’s why my dad quit his dream of making it off some practice squad and actually play in the NFL. He didn’t think that was possible for him. He got that rush as a coach though.” Owen seems to be moved by what Xander had said. “Paulie, I know your dad would be proud of you. My parents, they were always saying that there was more of your dad in you than you let people think. He gave up that dream not because he didn’t think he would make it. My parents told me that he gave up that dream because of his family, that he didn’t want to move them anymore. You all grew up here because of that.” Xander turns to Owen after those rather profound words, “What the hell are you talking about? I know why he gave up that dream. I’m just saying that the rush that he got coaching at the high school is the same rush that I get when I step into the ring.” “Oh.” “Seriously, if I’m that fucked up that I’m a wrestler because my dad couldn’t make it off the Brown’s practice squad, I would have sought therapy. Fuck, it’s getting late, I’m going to sleep.” “OK, man. Kara will be at the gym early to let you in.” “Thanks.” Owen sits back down next to Rick, who offers him another beer. “Are you sure you want to be a part of this family?” It’s 6am and Xander is already up. Wearing nothing but the boxers that he picked up yesterday, he walks back to his old bedroom. He was a still a little sore from his match, and the bed that he slept in didn’t help any. He pulls out a pair of gym shorts and a t-shirt and starts to get dressed. This will be the first time he’s ever been to Owen’s gym, so he didn’t know what to expect. The drive to the gym helps to clear Xander’s head, as does the coffee he picked up from the local donut shop. Not a lot of sleep, but this was something that he was used to from his first tenure as a wrestler in SCCW. He pulls up to the gym and sees only one other car. Recognizing it from the day before, he assumes that it’s either Kara’s or maybe even Owen’s. He didn’t care, as long as someone would let him in to get a workout going. Xander gets out of his car, and walks up to the front door. Just as he grasps the handle, the door swings open. “Come on, you big lug. I had to be here this early to let you work out, so you better make the most of it.” Xander just shrugs his shoulders. After working out for a couple of hours, Xander notices that some of the regulars are starting to show up. He didn’t get here as early as he wanted to, he wanted to have the gym all to himself. But that doesn’t matter. Most of them probably don’t know who he is, so he doesn’t have to worry about talking to anyone. Well, all but one person that is. Deciding to use the machine that was next to Xander’s was a rather attractive young blonde. Xander doesn’t notice her until she starts to talk. “Well, Xander, for someone as big as you are, you have a hard person to track down.” smoothly comes from her lips with a hint of her original British accent. Xander just smiles as he continues to work out. Maybe this time, he thinks, he can trust her.
