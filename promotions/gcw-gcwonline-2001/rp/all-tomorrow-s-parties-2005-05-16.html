All Tomorrow's Parties

16 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/16/05 Wrestling. Some people love it. I've seen grown men with exact replicas of Goldberg's tattoo, grown men that showed no sign of regret or shame in wearing it proudly. I've drank with the whores that used to fuck Ric Flair and Lex Luger and Randy Savage back in their day. Hell, grabbed DDP's haggard little bitch Kimberly's ass at an autograph signing. There's a draw to this game that just doesn't exist the same for any other that I've seen. It brings in two types--the deluded and the ironic. The deluded watch it week in and week out without a passing question as to why we do missle dropkicks and hurricanranas instead of things that actually hurt people; never figure out what showmanship means. It'd break their hearts to figure out that we usually don't go out dead set on ripping the other guy's head off and forcing it up his ass, all while screaming and beating our chests in some primal outburst. They're the ones that bring the big money at any event. They want legitimate autographs instead of chances to molest; to be able to say that they'd been in the vicinity of their make-up stained and spandex clad warriors fulfills a bigger dream than if they walked in on a fifty-lesbian orgy. And all the dykes were getting tired of twat. Now, ironic fans are the bastard offspring of the lot, citing that every victory's preordained and that wrestlers are artists, not athletes. They appreciate our mastery of psychology and our a control of timing and our ability to build epic matches. Found calling all the confused fans 'marks' and constantly hammering home how bad the booking is in everything, at every current point in time. Basically, they're assholes that like to dress up the truth: they love the sport just as much as the stupid. I fucking hate it. It's paid my bills for the better part of a decade and bought me more fun than I ever deserved, and I've really come out on the good end health-wise. Never had a major injury, only racked up a few concussions, and I can still foxtrot circles around the Dynamite Kid--who was a thousand times the wrestler I was, yet hideously underpaid and unimportant to a lot of fans. There, that's what's to hate about the sport. A cocky little piece of shit like me can not only attain success for himself everywhere he goes, but he can assure others this sort of fame and fortune too. It's sick, and it's funny, and it's life, and it's not even the worst part of everything. Somewhere in between pissing on people's dead idols and tipping sacred cows, I got a bit of a following. People out there cheer for me, and they cheer for Trey, and they'd cheer if Ragnarok showed up on their doorsteps. Why not? It's ratings. Poor bastards. I'm not exactly the best role model in the world, and I'm only slightly better at being a leader. Fuck, I can't do anything if people are stupid enough to listen to me. It's Trey's fault he's still here, nursing that wounded eye and wearing his eye patch like another piercing or tattoo. It's Tulz's fault that she threw away an eternal job to hang around with me on a hunt for deity boy. I just have a few good ideas that resonate with people, and they latch on like I'm smooth enough to carry 'em to completion. Then, in another one of those fucking cycles that life's full of, I force myself to come as close to the target as possible every time. Let's hope, huh? She looks to me like she's falling in love, but only because she's never been drunk before. We're getting cocky, Trey and I, and we're celebrating before the match even comes because both of us need a break after the last few weeks. There's a lot of people I know here, and a lot of people that I don't, and none of them are very fun to be around. They're old friends and the friends of old friends, coming because the great Jordan White decided to throw a party and he accidentally told a few people. Haha, what a mistake--they swarmed en masse to a completely out of the way club, two hours from Greece, and congratulated me on a thousand jobs well done. I found an alcove a few hours ago, and I'm staying here until someone cons me and Tulz out. Trey's given up trying and come inside, bringing tidings of good will in the form of pills and vials and baggies. 'Yeah, some chick with green hair said to give this shit to you, so yeah.' 'Tell her thanks.' 'Yeah. So what's up with you man?' 'Huh?' 'Why ain't you partyin'? S'your party.' 'Nah, it's your party. I'm just a host.' 'These people don't care 'bout me, they came here for you.' 'Maybe. Who gives a fuck? They're all dipshits--wouldn't call you out if you outright claimed to be me.' 'Shit. You know what I mean, man, shit just gets bad when I'm tryin' to get hyped up for that match with that Lexicon shit on my back, and all those bitches askin' me 'bout you. Fuck, you seen Toxic? I'm gonna need to get some respect and some play if I'm gonna feel up to beatin' that mug.' 'You got it backwards. Everyone's seen 'Scarface.'' 'I haven't.' 'No fucking way.' 'No shit.' 'Well, to the victor go the spoils. You're going to come out on top if you just give it a little time and let me work things out. That shit with Lexicon is completely handled. You'll get that match and you'll beat him, blah blah blah. It's all bullshit, Trey, since we both know you're damned good, which is better than these cats.' 'True, true.' 'So quit worrying and have a good time. And remember, be home by 8.' 'Haha, I hear you man. You comin' outside anytime or not?' I look over to Tulz, innocent and eager and really fucking loaded. Trey's asking questions because he's stupid, because he's fucked up, and because the music's destroyed some brain cells, probably. 'How expectant are they?' 'They want you, man. You'll be a god in there.' Tulz, how much do I want to be a god? She whispers in my ear, 'a lot,' and laughs to herself. It's the only impetus I need. Out, Jordan! Out from your fluorescent box and into the roaring cavern of delight, drugs, dames. He wasn't joking; they're on me hard, already latching on before I've made it dueling distance from the door. They want to know things about where I've been and what I've been up to and why I never returned their calls and not about my managerial status, or about Trey. A few ask when I'm returning, and I tell them I'm retired like it'll ever be a satisfactory answer. It's an awkward mix between the more casual of the industry connections and the more rugged of the addicts I've found over the years. Most of them are here because they knew there would be an event, and that anything I'd put my name on would be worth it. They enjoy the show for the spectacle it is, without pretense or pretension. There's others that're here because they haven't seen me for however long, and need to talk to me about whatever. I know I'll be in debt to a few, and I don't want to party with them. Even if they're casual debts, a little bit of owed money can seriously take a person's head out of the fun. So fuck them, and fuck their lame debts. Nothing will interfere tonight, not old enemies or old accountants or-- 'Yo, Jordan. Get over here.' Trey's voice is serious enough to listen to. I cut through the sea and find him, lingering near the steps down to the lower floor. 'What's up?' He shoves a drink into my hand and then towards my mouth; I finish the motion and he starts what's gotta be really good news or really bad news. 'Some chick's here, sayin' that..' Okay, fuck it. I try to run and he grabs my shoulder as tight as he can. 'She's got my kid? I clearly figured that out, as evidenced by my running.' 'Nah, nah, not that. She's sayin' that she's your sister, man.' I stop. No, this isn't happening. 'Bullshit. Just, uh, send the psycho away.' 'I'm tellin' you, she looks exactly like you.' 'And? I don't have a sister, so her logic's pretty fucked. Maybe if she'd said she was my half-brother--at least I have one of those.' 'You do? Shit. Never met him.' 'Yeah, you have. Know him too well from what I can see.' Trey stops and thinks for a minute before he asks who it is. I smile and nod and make devil horns on my head. 'Casey?' 'Yeah. So really, that's more than enough siblings for me. Get rid of her.' 'I dunno man, I think you oughta check it out first. Shave her head and we'd have another Jordan.' 'Poor bitch. Poor, delusional, bitch.' 'If you don't wanna talk to her, I will.' I know what that means. 'I'll handle it.' 'You sure?' 'Yeah. Might as well give her who she came to see.' 'Wait, Casey's your brother?' 'No time, I'll explain later.'
