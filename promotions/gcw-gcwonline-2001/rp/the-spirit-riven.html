Once upon a Time Zdice, Czech Republic She couldn't tell exactly what was wrong with her brother, only that it seemed to be a very profound disorder. His green eyes were flashing troubled sorts of looks her way, but only when he thought she wasn't looking. She, at her dressing table, ran her brush through her long black hair as though she didn't see him doing it. Finally, his half-annoyed sigh became too much to bear, and she set down the brush carefully, before turning to him. 'Laszlo, what is it?' 'Hm?' He looked up at her with a feint at innocence that wasn't really convincing. She could feel the annoyance bleeding through to her face. 'You've been sitting over there for nearly half an hour, grumbling and sighing, and... what's wrong?!' 'Oh, that...' He turned his eyes away from her, reluctant to say anything, but now that she had observed a problem, she'd be like a hound on the scent of a rabbit. He was caught, 'I've just been thinking about things... about you and Jaroslav, mostly.' She raised her eyebrows in surprise. 'What about him?' He sighed, clasping and unclasping his hands. 'This whole marriage thing. I don't know if I like it very much.' At that, she couldn't help but laugh a little, which elicited a very frustrated glare from him. 'Is that is? Come, Laszlo... you know that my marrying him won't mean anything... we'll still be as close as we've always been, only I won't live in the manor house anymore.' He made a face, as though he'd just bit down on an apple that had been harvested too soon. 'It's not that... I don't mind you being out of the house. We've known, both of us, and all the family, that we'd all be split up when the girls got married. There's nothing to be upset about, it's just that I-' Abruptly, he stopped himself. She shook her head, throwing her hands out to the side frustratedly. 'Just that you what?! I can't read your mind!' He shook his head, sealing his lips together in adamant refusal. 'I can't say any more to you about it.' Her jaw dropped. 'Laszlo!' He held up a hand, cutting off whatever protest might have followed-- because now she was irritated, and he could tell. 'Lia, I'm not going to say anymore, and please don't ask me to. But... if you need anything after you get married to him, just remember that I'm here. Please.' She looked at him for a moment, bemused. She smiled slightly as she turned to pick up her brush again. 'Is that it, Laszlo? We've been so close all our lives, so why would that change? Of course I'll remember!' Shaking her head with a slight small on her slim pink lips, she turned back to her task, brushing through the ends of her hair once again. Her brother's look, behind her, flashed again with a dark knowledge- with the warning that he could not say. .:: ::: ::. McFarlane Estate 13 May Mornings had always been the worst time for Dawn, the periods of absolute silence while she waited for others to awaken or arrive. Time spent in such quiet was never good for a mind that was always working, always thinking or fretting about one thing or another. Even the sounds of animals both outside and inside the estate did little to soothe her, and so she'd taken to sleeping as late as possible, so as to avoid that almost terrifying feeling of being alone with her thoughts. Today was a first then, when she'd awoken before her lover, indeed before anyone else in the house had so much as stirred. For a long while she lay on one side, staring at Lia's slender form where it slumbered beneath dark sheets. Absently she stroked her fingers along the raven locks of her hair, enjoying the closeness as much as giving her mind something else to focus on aside from the gaping silence all around. Gently she tugged one finger through an errant knot, only to find another snarl not far from there. Kicking her legs out over the side of the wide bed, Dawn tiptoed over to her dresser and fished around for a moment before emerging with the wide headed brush she'd used to brush her own hair when it was long. Returning to bed she tucked her long, bare legs up underneath her and settled next to Lia's head. Slowly she spread the other woman's hair out like velvet curtain, fanning it out until it resembled nothing so much as an ornate headdress set above her lover's pale and strangely peaceful face. Taking up a few loose locks she began to methodically run the brush through it, a soft smile creeping onto her lips as she did so. It was that soft, swooping motion, so similar to the one she had been making in her dream that caused Lia to stir slightly, opening her eyes halfway before they shut again, still heavy-lidded after such a difficult past few weeks. “Mm,” she mumbled, the noise a little muffled by the pillow that her face was still buried in. Dawn smiled, unable to keep the genuine pleasure from her face at the sight of what she felt was one of Lia's more adorable habits. Pausing her brushing, she stroked the backs of two fingers lightly along the cool skin of her partner’s cheek.  'Did I wake you?' she asked softly. “Yes,” she said quietly, “But I feel like I overslept anyway” Ugh.” She twisted her head slightly, craning it at a slightly painful angle to glance at Dawn before letting it drop back to the pillow as her senses slowly came to her. Feeling sheepish, Dawn returned to her work, gently stroking the soft bristles through Lia's long hair.  'Sorry, I didn't mean to. I was awake a little early and just sort of...felt the urge.' She made a little face, sticking her tongue out impishly. Lia shrugged slightly, and let out a soft sigh. Slowly, the veil that had been between them all this time was beginning to float back down, obscuring whatever waking-up closeness might have been there. “Something on your mind?” she asked, her tone a little gruffer than it had been before. Dawn paused in mid-stroke, dread slowly seizing her at the thought of the ease of their conversation just a few moments ago slipping away as Lia became more conscious of what she was doing. But then, she remembered the promise that she had made to herself--she wasn't walking out of this room until she had answers in hand. 'Yes, actually,' she responded, continuing on with her task as though it was just one of those every day things, nothing more serious than passing conversation about the weather, 'I... can't help but notice you've been preoccupied lately, Lia. And I know you don't want me to worry about it, but it's my job to worry about you... I came to talk.' 'Mmm...' Lia sighed, unable to resist the heady, happy feeling that was coming over her right now, 'But there's nothing to talk about, Dawn.' Dawn resisted the urge to comb a little harder. 'Come on... you don't honestly expect me to believe that, do you? You've been pulling away from everybody and I'm not the only one who's noticed it. You can't honestly tell me that GCW's the only thing you've been thinking about lately... you've just been keeping the rest of it from all of us.' 'It's not important to the rest of you... things I need to deal with on my-' 'Lia...' Lia stiffened slightly, more than a little taken aback that Dawn actually interrupted her parry. It was not like her to do so- no matter how much of a lioness that Dawn seemed backstage at shows, for quite a long time, Lia's word had been incontrovertible to her. 'It's not fair of you to expect everyone... to expect me,' she corrected, 'to just be okay with this. I want to know what I can do to help you.' Lia grunted, an unusually inarticulate response. Then, it seemed, something broke within her, and she relented. 'Dawn... don't you ever miss your family?' Dawn paused, confused at the seeming non-sequitur, but warily nodded. 'Yeah. I guess even though I don't exactly get along with them, it'd be nice to have somebody related to be proud of me now and again... you know, to care whether or not I'm happy. It's one of the reasons I miss my dad so much.' Her eyes were suddenly downcast at the comforter, finding its high thread-count very absorbing. 'I have to find Laszlo,' Lia said starkly, 'It's driving me insane to not know what's happened to him, to not know whether Sammael lied to me or not about him still being alive. And I'm so frustrated about all this...' Her fingers, sharp-pointed black-tipped fingernails standing out in sharp contrast from her pale skin, grasped at the comforter and clenched. 'I get empty lead after empty lead, nothing points me in the right direction, and every second that goes by that I don't find him is another second that he could be dead without us ever meeting up again.' 'You were... close to him?' Lia nodded, closing her eyes so that her eyelashes made a delicate black fringe just atop her cheekbones. 'It's something about twins, they say. The family thought we were a good omen as well, like we would be the ones to lead Zdice to a better future when we got older. They called him Laszlo Jarek because of that... it means something like 'hope has come to us in January,' as best as I can translate it...' 'I don't guess it's easy for any of us to understand that,' Dawn admitted, fiddling with the brush, handle pressed in between her palms as she twirled it. 'It's become an obsession. I dream about my past, events that make it impossible to distinguish prophecy from memory, but I don't suppose that it really matters in the long run. I just know that I have a promise to keep. If our family can be put back together, then we must be.' Dawn sniffed. 'I guess that's a noble task for anybody to undertake. I don't think I could put mine back together, and I know where they all are.' Lia turned over on her back and sat up slowly, drawing her knees up to her chest and curling her back inwards to stretch it. 'It isn't noble, it's just a necessity... but one that is very personal. I'm not trying to shut anyone out, there's just very little that anyone else can do to help me hurry this process.' 'But...' Dawn rejoined, sensing that she was losing control of the situation, 'We're here for you, even if you're only wanting to vent. Don't shut us... me out.' Lia nodded, finally turning her eyes back to Dawn. For a moment, it seemed as though something electrically sparked between them, which elicited a small smile from Lia. 'Fair enough.' Dawn allowed a small smile to come through, a leap of happiness in her heart- compared to the way things had been, this was a breakthrough. 'And... that goes for everything else too,' she said, seriously, 'Are you sure there's not anything else on your mind?' Slowly, through a minute shift in her eyes, Dawn realized that she'd pushed as far as she was going to be able to this time. 'Nothing. Finding Laszlo is stress enough for me without having to involve anything else.' It wasn't true, of course, and it was eminently obvious to the both of them. Though the wall had crumbled slightly, it was still not broken down enough to do much more than stand on tiptoe and peer at the corner of whatever it was that was behind it. Dawn nodded quietly, and stood up from her bed, the couple of steps between there and the dresser, where she set the brush down, feeling like a yawning gulf. When she turned back to the bed, Lia had slid into a red silk kimono that Dawn had given her for Christmas, and was flipping her lustrous, thick hair over her shoulders. 'I know you'd probably like to be alone for awhile this morning,' Lia said quietly, and Dawn wanted to reject the idea, but she couldn't force herself to, 'I'll be down for breakfast once I wake Virgil.' 'Lia, wait,' Dawn said rather suddenly, her fingers circling around Lia's wrist, 'I have something for you.' Lia's attention thus caught, she turned for a moment, sliding open the top drawer of her nightstand. In between them was a delicate glass vial, filled with a liquid that could only have been blood. 'What...' 'Mine,' Dawn said, smiling a bit tensely, 'It... it's been so long, and I just wanted you to have it. You don't have to ta-' 'Nonsense,' Lia interrupted, waving her hand dismissively. Her fingers brushed over Dawn's as she accepted it, 'I would never turn this down from you. A gift so precious should not be scorned.' Softly, Lia pressed Dawn's fingertips to her lips... and it was with that ambivalent goodbye that she left Dawn standing in her bedroom, heart pounding, and entirely unsure what she was supposed to think. .:: ::: ::. Once upon a Time Zdice, Czech Republic She found that she could hardly look into the eyes of Vaclav Ionescu without being possessed of the unbearable urge to look away, or to bow her head in subservience. She could not explain what it was about him that made her so nervous- certainly, he was handsome enough, a gentleman impeccably dressed in black, with a long black cotehardie embroidered with a lavish dragon, his long black hair spilling out of his black hood. He regarded her with his deep, dark eyes, sending a chill threading its way down her spine. 'My child,' he said, in his elegant, deep voice, 'You must tell me of what has happened--the truth, mind you. It is only the truth which will save you at this juncture.' She swallowed hard, but found that she was trembling all over, the words refusing to come out no matter how well she wet her lips and attempted to say them. He looked at her with what seemed to be pity, and softly rested his cold, pale hand atop hers. It was a strange feeling, but one that seemed to give her courage, rather than unsettling her further. 'Tell me the story, Lia.' 'I-I...' she stammered, fumbling for words, 'I saw Jaroslav in the barn with the servant girl some weeks ago. He... he...' She shuddered, squinting her eyes shut. 'Which servant, my dear?' 'Petra, the one with the golden hair, and... he made her swear not to tell! And then two days ago, we were to go walking in the woods, and I... I was foolish, I tried to confront him with what he did to her, and said to her, and he threw me into the snow and--' A yip of fright came from her throat, tears swimming unbidden in her pretty green eyes. He squeezed her hand, attempting to persuade the rest of the story out of her fear-wiped mind. 'I knew that he would try to make me with child. I knew that he would try to make it seem as though I was a tramp, to shame the family and try to spoil our marriage.' Vaclav chuckled slightly- she was a clever little thing, for fifteen. The noise seemed to throw her off and she looked up at him, her eyes wide, round, glimmering discs. 'And then what happened?' She squeezed her eyes shut for a moment, inadvertently squeezing the hand of the man to whom she had been taught to pay such deference. 'I don't know... I don't remember. The next thing I remember is my dagger, and... and pulling it out of his chest, and there was so much blood-' The tears finally spilled over, cutting off the end of her sentence, and the end of the story with it. 'I see,' he said, sounding rather pensive. The silence hung heavy in the air for a few moments, and she knew, just knew he was furious with her. That she would disappoint her family and lose all that she had worked for. Finally, he broke the silence with a pat on the hand, 'Stay here, my little one. I will speak with your father.' He stood up, and she grabbed at his hand, terror reflected in her eyes. He smiled, and for the first time since they had begun talking, she saw the gleam of his razor-sharp eyeteeth. 'Do not worry, Lia,' he said, stooping to kiss her on the forehead, with a breath of air so cold it seemed as though it would freeze her skin, 'I will keep you safe.' He left her there, letting go of her petite fingers the last of all, as he turned back to her father, who was standing in the doorway, his face a serious mask. As Vaclav approached, he motioned for Vasek to keep his words quiet so that his child would not hear. 'What has she done?' he asked, his eyes somewhere between worry and anger. 'Nothing for you to be angry at her for, she was only attempting to protect herself from that filthy swine's unwanted advances, and in so doing, protect your family's honor.' Vasek's eyes flashed with horror first, then fury. 'Why that... I will speak with his father!' Vaclav held his hand up. 'Silence that nonsense,' he hissed, 'Your primary concern as of this moment should be her safety, and only that. She has killed a man, and not just a man, but a lord's son. She cannot stay here in Zdice.' 'She belongs here,' Vasek said, straightening his posture to where he nearly matched Vaclav's height. Vaclav drove a sharp fingernail into his chest, snarling. 'Vasek, I value your viewpoints, but this is not the time to test me. Do you want her to live, or do you not?' As though deflated, Vasek Vlaszy cast his ocean-colored eyes down at the ornately tiled stone floor. 'That is what I thought. Have you any allies?' 'The Cerescu clan, in Brno.' Vaclav shook his head. 'They must live farther from here than Brno. Think farther than that, Vasek.' Vasek's face blanched. 'There is the Sterescu clan, but they live in Iberia...' Vaclav smiled slightly. 'And their sons?' 'Two of marrying age the last time that I spoke with Marius.' 'Then you have saved her.' Vasek's head hung low for a moment- and it was that moment that Vaclav knew that he would protest no more. 'Write Marius a letter now. I will send one of my retainers as a courier at once.' 'Yes, Lord.' .:: ::: ::. May 1, 2006 Bessemer, Alabama Bessemer Airport The Jaedr taxied to a slow stop, and Lia's hair blew backwards from the wind that its propellers were still creating as they slowed to a stop. She unfolded her arms from where they were crossed over her chest and started towards the plane with a brisk step. As soon as Rune, Virgil's pilot contact and what she would have considered a friend, opened the door, she climbed up into the plane with a couple of light steps. A smile came slowly over her face. 'Nazdar, Rune. I'm glad to see you this evening, it's been too long.' Rune turned from the cockpit with a wan half-smile, the same kind he was always wont to giving, as he wasn't a particularly happy person. Lia supposed she could understand that, what with the kind of life he must have led as a smuggler, not to mention the wife and baby he had just lost to divorce. He looked more haggard than usual at the moment, something which was worrisome to her, despite the fact that he more than likely would have been surprised to know that she cared. 'How are things?' she asked. He shrugged the question off. 'A little worse than usual, I guess. Business has been slow lately, and I don't much like sitting around Norway with nothing to do, nobody to talk to... kind of sucks to go home when you got no family, you know?' Lia smiled at him, reaching across the empty space to press a cold hand to his forearm. She could feel him try to pull away- but he didn't, which was certainly a first for him. 'I understand. Is there anything I can do?' He looked at her, with hope in his light blue eyes, for just a moment, before casually and uncomfortably shrugging off her question. 'I don't guess you can find me a line of work that will make Heidrun bearable and get my little girl back.' 'I'm no relationship therapist,' she said with a rueful smirk, 'my advice probably wouldn't do you any favors.' 'Neither would anybody else's,' he said, sighing, and looking through the slightly smeared windshield of his plane, often the only place he felt at home looking out of. After a moment's uncomfortable silence in which he could feel her eyes digging into him, he jerked a thumb towards the cargo hold. 'Your boy's back there... been asleep most of the ride because he says he gets airsick.' She rolled her eyes. 'I suppose he'll have to get over that, won't he?' 'If he pukes in my plane, he'll be getting over it real fast,' Rune said, smirking for a moment, and shaking his head. She had already stepped over to the hold and pulled open the hatch, underneath which Markus was already peering at her through dark, and slightly irritated looking eyes. She ignored that- Markus Eaux D'Artifice had been irritable ever since he'd received the truth of what he was, and she didn't suppose that she blamed him... but he'd have to get over it, because there really wasn't much to be done about it now. 'Are you ready to go?' she asked. 'Yeah, I'd do just about anything to get out of this tin can,' he said, hoisting himself out of the cargo hold with his powerful arms. Rune glowered up in the front seat, and Lia could hear him mumbling something about how perhaps he should have awakened him for the sunrise- it made her smile, something that she didn't let Markus see as he turned to pick up his bags. He was first out of the plane, and Lia turned to Rune. 'Your payment?' He shook his head. 'Virgil already settled with me, you don't have to-' She tossed him a small pouch, which he caught with one hand, before curiously opening and pulling out the small glass bottle that was inside. He shook his head again, with more vitality this time. 'No way, Ms. Vlaszy. I can't take that.' 'Plenty more where that came from... if you don't take it this time, I'll give you more than that the next time I see you.' He sighed, looking down at it. 'Alright...' 'Have a good night, Rune.' With that, she hopped out of the plane to find Markus waiting, with that typical irascible expression on his face. He had that in common with Virgil, at least. 'What took so long?' he grumbled. 'I was speaking with my friend inside the plane,' Lia said, her tone firm, and bordering on irritated. 'You mean having a snack?' She rolled her eyes. 'You're a very nosey childe. No, I was not, and I'd caution you to hold your tongue before assuming that all humans are food. Let's go.' She started down the runway to the car where Virgil was waiting, moving surprisingly quickly for all that she was still shorter than him. He picked up his pace to catch up, slinging his bags over his shoulder. and trailing along after her. 'When am I going back to Chicago?' he asked. She turned back to him, her eyes still looking hard and annoyed. 'I wouldn't put many eggs in that basket. Christian has placed you in my care indefinitely.' 'Why?!' 'You still have a lot to learn, that's why.' 'But he needs me there to do my job!' Lia rolled her eyes, only halfway caring whether or not he saw. 'He had no one to guard his estate before you came, and I am sure he will be fine for a few weeks, or months, however long it ends up taking. Furthermore, in your current state of ignorance, you'd be a liability, and both of us agree on that. You'll go back when I say you're ready.' Markus narrowed his eyes, and opened his mouth for a hasty retort, but one last look from her silenced him... for now. .:: ::: ::. Once upon a Time Iberia The clouds had finally cleared from the sky, though they had earlier been threatening rain. That made her bones resonate with excitement from deep within, because it meant that her husband's troop would continue its journey home, and would be here in a few hours. She paced in front of the door, staring off into the garden and wondering how exactly she was going to tell him the news... her mind was lost in thoughts of that for a few moments. But it was just as quickly jerked back out of imagination by the sound of horse hooves. Her dark hair whipped out behind her as she snapped her head to the side. The dark head of her husband's Andalusian Armando poked his head around the gate, trotting a little faster as he reached the familiar confines of the manor. She burst through the front door of the estate, leaving it hanging open without a care as to whether or not any of the flies got in. The flies could be killed... this was a far more important matter. Her legs carried her quickly out to the front path, her skirts streaming behind her. He cut a majestic figure on his horse, still dressed in his half-plate armor, with the tabard covering his chest in his family's colors. His chainmail hood was pushed back, his long, dark beautiful hair flowing down his back over it. He pushed Armando to a canter up the walk and practically leapt off as soon as he was even with Antonio, who would take the horse back to the stable. He spoke a few words to him, but all the time, his clear and bright eyes were stealing glances at her. A smile broke over her face, and she sprang into a run down the walk. He met her halfway there, his strong arms lifting her and spinning her around in two dizzy circles. He was such a giant compared to her, and she felt practically enveloped in him as he set her down and set her world spinning again with a deep, romantic kiss that had her swooning in his arms. The two of them both seemed unable to stop their smiling. 'You're soaked,' she said, laughing. His grin continued. 'There was a storm... I rode ahead of the troop, because I couldn't wait to get home... My God, I missed you so much.' She found herself enveloped in his arms again, before he pulled away. 'How have things been? Elena? How is she?' 'Wonderful... I think she will speak soon,' Lia said, practically glowing as she spoke of their daughter, their first child. But then, a softer glow spread over her cheeks, 'Mi Senor, I have something else to tell you...' He gave a slight inclination of his head. 'What is it?' 'We're going to have another child,' she said, unable to suppress her beaming smile. His surprise was immediate, his light-colored eyes lighting up. He tried to hug her again, but found the plate mail too cumbersome- it was then that he pulled off his tabard himself, and squirmed out of his armor, a task that usually would have taken a groom's help, had he not been motivated by his immense joy. Wearing only his long cotehardie and the boots that he rode into battle with, he pulled her close again, kissing her with the same mad passion that he always did, the bright love between them that had made their surprise marriage and their surprise first child such happy occasions. Never before had she been so assured of her place in the world. .:: ::: ::. McFarlane Estate 13 May The knock came lightly on Alexis' door, and she looked up from her textbook with some surprise, as it was rare that she got visitors these days. 'Come in?' It was Lia's dark head that poked around her door with an anxious-seeming look as she stepped in. 'Good evening Alexis,' she said, bowing her head. 'Ah, hey...' she returned. She'd never really gotten comfortable with how she was supposed to address Lia and Virgil. 'May I ask you a favor?' she said, her eyes practically boring holes into the younger woman so that she nearly found the need to look away. 'Yes, er... of course.' Lia closed the door, and the click was so final that Alexis wondered whether or not she should be nervous. 'I have a problem... Virgil and I do, that is,' she started, then sighed softly, 'This is... very strange to explain, as I am not entirely certain how it could be possible, but there are great gaps in our memories...' She trailed off and Alexis ran her fingers through her half-blonde, half-red hair. 'Are you sure that isn't just because the length of your lives? I mean when I took psychology classes, and they taught us about memory, they said that we start to forget the things that are no longer important to us, because we have to make room for the new things.' Lia shook her head, perhaps the most animated motion Alexis had ever seen her make. 'No, that's impossible... Our kind has never had a problem with that, we have memories like steel traps, otherwise we wouldn't be able to hold these centuries-long grudges like we do... and these are important details, details that we can feel are palpably there, but are unreachable. They just... stop at some point.' Alexis was a good deal baffled by this, but her analytical mind was already beginning to leap to conclusions, trying to rule things out. 'I know that you can't really make conjectures, but I know that with what you've studied, you might be able to help us. Virgil more so than me, it frustrates him so much more...' She knotted her hands together, staring down at the carpet with an air of sadness that Alexis felt hovered around her very being almost all the time. 'I want him to remember, it would mean so much?' 'It could be genetic,' Alexis said, speaking up softly. Lia's eyes flicked up to hers, and Alexis was finally able to hold her gaze, 'and in that case, I would need cell samples from both of you? a good lab to work in?' She trailed off, then winced. 'And ideally, I'd be able to see a database of normal vampiric cells to compare yours to to see where, if any, the anomalies are. But I guess that's not an option.' Lia's eyebrows furrowed downwards in thought. 'It may be,' she said, folding her arms over her chest, 'but I'll have to get back to you on that after this week. As a matter of fact, maybe as soon as a few days. Which means I should probably get going.' 'Where are you going?' Alexis said, perking up at the idea of getting away from the confines of this lovely prison cell for a little while, but knowing that more than likely, she wouldn't be included on this trip, not if it was Shadow's official business. 'Virgil and I are going to meet Blaise and Gray of the Knightmare Brigade? they've called us with some information regarding? well, they didn't say. But they have been working on a few things for us.' 'Can I come?' She knew almost as soon as the question fell out of her mouth that it was a stupid one, and Lia was probably going to tell her no. The look on her face as she considered the offer was about to prove her right, she knew, but then-- 'Yes, I suppose you can,' Lia said, looking somewhat thoughtful, 'But you'll have to hurry. And pack light--we're only taking the bikes, and they can't hold a suitcase. Twenty minutes, downstairs.' 'Sure,' Alexis said, breathless at the rapid-fire orders. Just as quickly as she'd come, Lia disappeared back into the woodwork. .:: ::: ::. Once upon a Time Iberia She stepped out of the little sickroom, her eyes rimmed a deep crimson from tears and lack of sleep. His eyes shot up to meet hers, in much the same condition as hers. Damek, the large male dog, raised his head from the floor, staring at her with those all-too-close-to-human eyes. 'Elena? is?' he trailed off, terrified to ask and terrified not to know. She bit her lip, but the tears began to come anyway. 'The healer says he does not know? he says?' she swallowed hard, 'He has tried to ease her pain, but if she does not show signs of improvement in a few hours' time, there is little we can do? she is so warm?' He ran his fingers through his hair, his face a mixture of anger and agony. 'Can I see her?' It was then that she bridged the few steps between them and flung herself into his arms. 'Mi Senor, the healer says it must be poison, wormwood, but it is impossible? we haven't let her out of our sight.' His face contorted, reddened deeply even under his usual tawny hue. 'I know what it was? it leaves only one option--those bastard, landless, shiftless Roma,' he said, and his tone took on a frighteningly fierce and feral growl. Tears spilled over as she desperately pressed her fingers to his lips. 'No, don't say that, think about Elena, we have to think about her.' He gritted his teeth so hard that she could hear it, his free hand clenching into a tight ball. 'Senor! Senora! Come quickly!' She was up on her feet as though launched from a catapult, her Senor on her heels, Damek close behind. The children's servant, Raquel, was pressed against the wall in horror, the healer looking frantic. 'Mama! Papa!' The words came from Elena's lips so tiny and pitiful. Lia practically fell to her knees to take the child's hand, her husband stooping to cradle her head. The little fingers clenched tightly around her mother's thumb. Lord Sterescu's lips pressed into a thin, bloodless line, and Lia's parted, murmuring a soft Hail Mary in her native tongue. For a moment, she thought her desperate plea had worked, as the tight grip of her daughter's hand loosened slightly. But as she looked down, she saw that glassy-eyed blank look in her pretty blue eyes that meant death-- that Elena was gone to them now. The howl that nearly split her chest in half was fiercer in it’s agony than any Gaulish war cry he had ever heard. Something within him twisted and snapped, a vile monster clawing for purchase inside him. 'Proč?' she gasped through her tears, 'Ay, proč, proč?' Though he knew little of her native tongue, he knew from his soul the desperate need of that word, knew implicitly what she was daring their God to answer. He knew there would be no answer, save his own. The tears that clouded his vision did nothing to quench the dark anger that clenched his chest in its fist. What seemed like hours later, but might have been only moments, he emerged from the room, barely able to walk without staggering. His eyes immediately hung on the first servant he saw, his footman Juan. 'Juan,' he said, his voice raw, and with a look in his eyes that terrified the footman, 'Send a message to the lords to amass their armies.' Lord Sterescu turned away, and started down the hall towards his bedroom, as though his soul had been somehow mysteriously extracted by the foreign healer, strange in his speech and ways, that had come with Senora Sterescu. But this order made no sense? 'But why, Lord Sterescu? We do not go to the wars for another three-score days.' The Lord's shoulders tightened as Juan had often seen them do before striking a killing blow with his sword. He turned slowly, and the look on his face was so frightening that Juan had to look away. 'The Roma have killed my daughter? I cannot let so bold a move go unpunished.' The tears came again, and he wondered if he would ever be able to dam them. His own tears mattered not, however; his wife's would not be in vain. They would pay with their blood tenfold, and slowly, for each one she had shed, and he would raze their retched hovels to the ground for the daughter they had taken from him. That was the only way to quell the enraged demon that had taken over his soul the moment he had watched Elena breathe her last. .:: ::: ::. Rural South Carolina 14 May The thick Southern pine formed a canopy with the myriad thick and ancient oaks; the path that Lia traversed, springing doe-like along its rocks, was shaded, save a few rare patches where the sun peeped through the green. She had never seen a place such as this, but today was no day for sight-seeing. Two days ago, she had received an urgent message from Blaise McClancy, who had perhaps become her favorite KB contact of late. They had coordinated another quite private and neutral meeting place here in the South Carolina foothills, with the same rules as before--one lieutenant, no silver arms, no stakes. And in fact, they'd left all their gear with Alexis, who was waiting for them at the edge of the woods. She turned back to Virgil as they approached the clearing, motioning for quiet. They paused in movement, blending into the sylvan silence, and both scenting the air--lupine and nothing else. No surprises, and no one who had unexpectedly tagged along. With her typical unnatural grace, she parted the trees where she could see Blaise's and Gray's footsteps? or pawprints, rather? and stepped into the clearing, Virgil close behind. Blaise and Gray were already looking in their direction, and upon their entrance, all confirmed the proper deference with nods all around--their alliance was a comfortable, but not yet friendly one. 'I apologize if we are late,' she said, smiling slightly, 'We got turned around once on the way.' 'I thought Virgil had an estate in South Carolina?' Blaise queried, raising an eyebrow. 'Not in the middle of the woods,' the taller vampire rejoined, which elicited a chuckle from Blaise. Virgil wasn't usually one to crack jokes. 'Had you my nose, you'd probably not have the trouble,' he responded. Lia smiled, and Virgil rolled his eyes, though in a much more good-natured fashion than he was normally given to do. Gray, however, stoic as ever, cleared his throat. 'Business is what called us here, I believe? We have these for you.' He held out a pair of folders, stuffed to the gills with papers. Blaise shrugged. 'Fax machine at the estate wanted to give us some mouth, so we thought we'd deliver these personally.' Lia stepped forward, taking the files from Gray, but nodding her thanks to Blaise. 'We do appreciate this,' she said, tucking the files under her arms. But there was something still hanging in the air, and she tilted her head, 'Could this not have waited until backstage at WorldWide? Or am I to presume that there is something else?' Blaise's eyes switched to Virgil. 'He asked me for some information a few weeks ago about someone you were looking for, Ms. Vlaszy,' he said, pulling a thinner folder from his jacket, 'And here's what I've found.' She took the folder, her hands trembling slightly. Perhaps it contained the break she'd been waiting for? but she knew better than to get her hopes up. ~~~ The foursome burst through the woods loudly enough for Alexis' head to shoot up from the book she was reading. You're going with Blaise and Gray,' Lia returned breathlessly, gesturing for the young woman to hop off of her bike. Alexis, caught quite breathless by her frenetic movements, scurried off. Down the stretch of road, Virgil almost found it hard to keep up with Lia. And Lia herself was not at all concerned about the awkwardness of Alexis meeting two lupines for the first time? there was only one thing on her mind, as rubber roared over concrete at deafening speed: finally, all the pieces were starting to come together. .:: ::: ::.
