February 2006 Following WW39 Japan The depths of Lia Vlaszy's green eyes were still for once, her gaze focused outward over the trees that rolled over the hilltops of the scene before her. She couldn't say that she was at all familiar with this landscape, and perhaps that's why she found it so calming. There was enough new here to focus, to keep the ennui from interfering in her thoughts. The thoughts of the moment were cascading round in her head because of a rather interesting little missive Virgil had delivered to her when they had returned to Master Mifune's school after the last Worldwide taping: Gray Brand wanted to talk. Someone saying the name 'Gray Brand' was enough to curl a snarl of overt aggression across Lia's face. Gray Brand had been a thorn in her side for so long that having the opportunity to talk to him in a more remote setting should have meant 'an opportunity to carve him limb from limb and make his ribcage into a hat.' Alas, she had forced herself into diplomacy mode- and for a number of reasons. Foremost, of course, there was the issue of Dawn. The way things had gone last week, Dawn had pretty much left it up to Lia to deal with Gray and his continuing overtures of 'friendship' which Lia saw as scarcely more than shoddily disguised passes at one Ms. McFarlane, the elusive apple of so many people's eyes. Lia had a couple of options: ignore Gray, and let that be the answer, or go through with talking, with no promise of a happy resolution for Mr. Brand. The first option would have likely meant, at some point, an open declaration of war between the Knightmare Brigade and the little group that Lia had so painstakingly groomed for the task of taking GCW by storm. The latter option, while more distasteful, meant a chance at something of a ceasefire. Lia's practical side could hardly dissuade her from the chance to make a few allies- it made good sense, in an unfamiliar place, to hedge one's bets until the footing was firmer. She sighed gustily. That didn't mean that she had to like the idea. At all. Further complicating this was the remoteness of the location, insisted upon by Gray because these were 'private matters.' At least Gray had had the sense to hastily add that Lia could bring one of her number along. Had he not done so, there was no doubt that Lia would have refused the meeting. There was no sense being stupid, after all. Even now, she was wary, but faced with a decision: who should she take? Cyrus would have been an impartial party, but he was back in Toronto, keeping an eye on the McFarlane Estate while none of them were home. Alexis Key, the young mortal woman who had been accompanying them on their journey for some time now, would not do simply because she was an outsider, and Lia couldn't see Gray being open and trustworthy if there was an outsider involved, whether she had supernatural experience or not. That excluded Vivica as well, and, grudgingly, Shadow admitted that that was probably for the best, given Vivica's innate talent for saying incredibly cutting, observant things at the worst time possible. That left Virgil Ambrosi and Dawn McFarlane herself. Most of their number seemed to look at Virgil as sort of her lieutenant, her second. Since they had rescued her from the Hell's Mouth in Nevada back in December, Virgil had hardly left her side. She didn't blame him, nor did she mind. Plainly, their world was a bit too dangerous to go on alone, and she wasn't sure she would have wanted to go on alone in any case. Taking him along would have been the natural choice. Yet, at the same time, Lia knew that Gray would probably choose to bring along Blaise. And having three rather territorial, strong-minded men in the same area would likely not be a wise decision if the words got heated. Even just having Virgil around would likely make Gray and Blaise a little standoffish and guarded. She wasn't sure what sort of secret they were hiding, but she could sense that they were not quite mortal. And that was an understatement. Dawn seemed like a logical choice. Partially, Lia knew that she would be playing quite a potent card to bring along the object of Gray's desire to the meeting. It would force him to behave, but more than that, it might force him to admit something rather personally embarrassing in front of a woman he seemed to put a lot of stock into impressing. The thought of that was pleasing, in a rather emotionally sadistic way. But more than just wanting to get cheap thrills out of making Gray look like an ass if at all possible, there was the possibility that things would get hostile. Dawn being there would almost certainly diffuse that. It was a consideration for the safety of everyone involved; Lia couldn't promise to behave if tempers got heated because she was only what she was, half-beast at least, and guaranteed to defend herself and her kith if she felt it necessary. So, Dawn. But slowly, an unease began to grow in the pit of Lia's stomach. Choosing Dawn would mean that Dawn would have to be around Gray for an extended period of time. To Lia, even a few moments was long enough. She could see the desire in his eyes when he looked at her, sense the way he would love to snatch her away from what he at one time had regarded as a bad influence. She sneered visibly. As though the Knightmare Brigade would really do any better by Dawn. They didn't even have any clue of what she was, and had no appreciation for her as a person. They hadn't fought so hard to make everything right with her, they hadn't fought so hard to make love work, not like Lia had. Virgil it was, then. Lia turned slightly to the monolith beside her, who had been still and staring out at the snow-capped hills just as long as she had. By the innate and experienced senses inside her, she could tell that it was close to the appointed time when they would need to leave the school and take the train to the resort town where the Knightmare Brigade was staying. Then they would make the trek into the woods by foot, to the meeting place. 'I'm going to go talk to Gray about... whatever it is that he wishes to speak to me about.' Virgil grimaced. 'You know I'm not fond of this idea, I presume.' She nodded solemnly. 'Of course. But there's no harm in making an attempt to discover their motives in this little game. I'm curious as to what they will put out in the open, and what they will choose to hide.' 'It's too dangerous.' She smiled almost absently. 'Then come with me. Gray wished me to bring someone to accompany me, as a show of good faith. Presumably, Blaise will accompany him.' Virgil's look soured. 'I don't like the sound of this.' 'Think of it as a military-style negotiation. The first for us. There is no harm in attempting to procure allies, and I think it will be clear enough fairly quickly whether or not we're wasting our time.' Virgil nodded gruffly, turning his head away from her piercing gaze. That meant, of course, that she was about halfway there. As the wind began to gently run its fingers through his hair, working loose several long strands that turned over one another behind his back, she laid the last card. 'You're the only one I trust to do this. I need you to be there to back me.' He turned to look at her, his blue eyes almost resentful. He knew very well that he was being taken in- how could he refuse her when she asked him like that? 'Then I'll be there.' But he could only be resentful for a few moments. His arm slid around her waist, pulling her closer as he accepted his defeat. In the very least, it was not a bitter surrender. .:: ::: ::. December 2005 Below the Hell's Mouth Nevada Lia could no longer feel the icy winds gripping every inch of her flesh. And no more was the frightening visage of Leinad Soma/Sammael leering before her, threatening to force her into servitude because she supposedly owed him something. Instead, this place was verdant and green, foliage swaying gently with a wind that was like a warm, humid kiss. Trees dotted the landscape, and in the distance, she could see a gate, which meant that this was a walled enclosure, whatever it was, even though the walls were far distant- so distant she could hardly see them. At the gate, the only evidence of enclosure that she could see, stood a figure, stone-still and rather intimidating in bearing, who appeared to be wielding a sword. 'Well, count that as the wrong direction to go,' she said to herself, and turning away, she started towards the small hill in front of her. Once atop it, she was nearly rendered breathless by the spread of the garden before her. The flowers that opened their spray in the landscape below were vibrant of hues- unmistakable fuschia, flaming red, cheery yellow, elegant lavender, and delicate periwinkle. For a few moments, all she could do was stare. 'It is quite beautiful, eh signora?' The half-dulcet voice was mellow and calm, not quite so dispassionate as it had been when... She turned to the side, coming face to face with the Italian Gentleman once more. He bowed his head to her in respectful greeting, though she could still see no more of his face than before. The shade which clung persistently to its outlines seemed to be draped underneath the hat like a mourning veil. 'The get of Seth have been thrown from this place... you should consider yourself lucky to see it.' Suddenly, it was all beginning to make sense. 'The angel with the sword...' she half-whispered. 'Yes,' he confirmed, 'This is the place that the Creator deemed worthy of his children. I'm sure you can see that he worked terribly hard on it. I'm afraid you know the story- they didn't appreciate it much.' 'It's beautiful.' Again, the brim of the hat bowed. 'Of course. Any less would not have been acceptable.' A soft silence clung to the air around him, to the very dewdrops on the flowers. The breeze blew, and Lia's eyes closed gently as she drank in the kiss on her skin as though it was something she had been thirsting for. 'What was your reason for choosing not to accept his proposition?' The Italian Gentleman was still looking at her when she opened her eyes. There was only one way to answer his question with any accuracy, and she shrugged, palms to the sky. 'Non serviam.' (I will not serve.) At that, for the first time that Lia could recall, a warm chuckle came from the Italian Gentleman's throat. 'I suppose he was not amused at having his own reasoning turned against him.' 'I should say not.' Lia could not hide the small amount of pride in her smile. 'Brave... or perhaps stupid.' She frowned, but before she could interject a comment, he went on. 'I confess, you are the first of your kind that I know of to reject one of Sammael's offer. The amount of power he can give is usually staggering enough that those especially of your age are loath to reject it.' She shook her head. 'Power is not all there is to this existence.' 'Easy for you to say,' he half-scoffed, 'You have what is necessary to believe it, though.' 'And that is?' He shrugged non-committally. 'Love. Connection to others. People who genuinely care about you. What is about to happen is proof enough of that.' 'What do you mean? What's about to happen?' Lia's grip on the situation was rapidly beginning to fade, and panic coming to take its place. Hadn't she already been through enough? 'Hmph.' The noise he made was one that Lia had gotten used to over the course of their journey. Amusement at her weaknesses, the fallacies of her emotions. 'You are about to be released from your bondage. You'll understand soon enough.' In the meantime, he had started to retreat from her, and she fought the impulse to chase him down, to beg at his feet for the answers. She wouldn't be that pathetic. 'What does all this mean?' He turned back to her, and tilted his head, as though he didn't understand the question. 'Being here... why am I here if Seth's Childer have been rejected? Cain was rejected just as they were.' 'You're a curious exception.' 'What am I supposed to do?' The Italian Gentleman again gave that noncommittal shrug. 'Whatever it is that you wish to do. Only don't expect succeeding at any of it to be particularly easy- you'll never have it easy, especially now. Why don't you go find your brother Lazlo, for starters?' She shook her head. 'I dreamed that he was alive, but I saw him here in Hell... Sammael...' She trailed off. Surprisingly, the Italian Gentleman chuckled. 'Sammael makes many things seem to be what they are not. Don't trust him, Lia, his world is a lie, and he will lie to you if he sees fit to test you, you've seen that yourself. But you're beyond his tests now. You've passed them all. Trust your instincts when you ask yourself whether this is a fruitless quest or not. That is all that I can tell you.' Already, as he finished his sentence, the scene was beginning to fade. It ripple in iridescent colors, shimmering in waves as it started to seem more and more like an imaginary dreamscape. 'No!' she wanted to shout, 'I want to stay here!' The ground suddenly seemed to be trembling, and she reached for a tree to steady herself, but it was no use. Flat on her back like a turtle spinning on its shell, she was forced to consider the sky, rippling and twisting with colors of its own. Her head thrashed from side to side, and a flash of sky seemed to be broken through by a vision of a white tile wall, and the feeling of being strapped to a cold steel surface. .:: ::: ::. February 2006 Following WW39 Japan The solitude of the woods was almost frightening in its thoroughness, as though the entirety of Japan, the world, beyond its borders had been completely swallowed upon their entering. From the descriptions that Gray had given her, Lia knew that they were close to the meeting place, and her feet carried her forward almost eagerly with every stride. Mostly, she just wanted to get this over with, and she could sense that Virgil, at her side, had the same sentiments. He'd insisted on carrying his pistols with him. One armed with iron bullets, the other with silver. She'd chastised him for being so superstitious- and had very nearly asked him why he didn't pack some extra stakes in his pack pocket, just to be prepared. But really, being flip would only have made the situation more tense and unmanageable. 'I think we're close-' 'Shh!' Virgil nodded his head in the direction that they were walking to indicate that he thought something was amiss. She was about to roll her eyes at his overprotectiveness when she heard it too. Wolves. The yipping, half-howls and barks of wolves at conversation. And wolves in the area where they were to meet Gray and his accompanying 'second' could mean only one thing. 'Lupines,' Virgil spat, 'I can scent it from here.' She nodded, taking a cautious step forward towards the clearing that she could see not twenty yards in front of them, all senses alert. 'Lia!' he hissed, 'Forget this, it's obviously a setup!' She shook her head and half-called back to him, unwilling still to turn her head from watching in front of them, towards the clearing. It would have been stupid to drop her guard. 'Listen, Virgil, there's only two of them.' 'What does it matter? You know what happens to our kind against lupines.' She sighed. 'Well they've already heard us in any case, so we might as well go.' Tromping stubbornly ahead, Lia knew that Virgil would be forced to follow her, if only because he feared for her safety. It was brash, perhaps stupid, and a dirty trick to pull on someone she cared about as much as she did him, but it couldn't be helped. Things such as this weren't always pleasant, she thought to herself, as she barged through the clearing. Two wolves turned their heads simultaneously to regard her with piercing, abnormally intelligent eyes, one gray and the other a solid white with not a mark on him anywhere. But the standoff lasted only a few moments, taut and tense, before their forms began to morph. And then, there were not wolves in the clearing, but Gray Brand and Blaise McClancy, getting to their feet and looking across the clearing at two beasts similar to them, yet different enough for there to be palpable tension in the air. 'Good show,' Lia said, instantly on guard. Gray stood up a little straighter, his dignified bearing pushing out his strong chest and stubborn jaw. 'I'm glad you could join us, Ms. Vlaszy. We were starting to worry that you weren't coming.' Lia's face faded into complete seriousness, her green eyes flashing strangely metallic and cold. 'I do not break commitments.' 'This is my superior officer in the Order of the Hammer, Blaise McClancy,' Gray responded, simply ignoring her hostility as he gestured to his friend, a man who had always seemed quite wild of spirit to Lia, even now as he stood before her brushing snowflakes out of his shock of hair and looking slightly harried. Lia gestured daintily back to Virgil. 'I'm sure you know Virgil Ambrosi, my lieutenant of sorts.' Gray affected a respectful bow. From Blaise there was a nod. From Virgil, nothing at all, save a cold appraisal of the two men before him. He was still quite on edge, and to be truthful, so was she. Her arms folded over her chest as she glanced back and forth between the two men, before turning her piercing green eyes to Gray. Well... if it's business we have here, then let's get down to business. What did you have to say to me?? 'I never said it was purely business. I've asked for an audience so that I could apologize to you for some of my actions, and one other's, from a few months back. That would make it personal. The business can wait for a bit. But I figured you of all the others involved deserved to know why I said some of the things I did.' Lia nodded slowly. 'Even guilty men have a chance to plead their case.? Despite that somewhat discomfiting pronouncement, Gray carried on, taking a deep breath before he spoke again. 'As you know, this is mostly about someone we are both acquainted with. A few months ago I did make some comments to you about putting Dawn in danger. I'm sorry. In more ways than one. I'm sorry that my brother undeniably made it worse. But also for underestimating how much you care about her. And for underestimating her and her strength and resolve to stick by the side of her friends. I've already told her this, but you were also slighted. Unfortunately, when I first met her, I thought she was... only human. And that to me meant she was fragile.? A smirk came over Lia's face. A bit of a wry one if the truth were told, somewhere between a smirk and a baring of teeth. 'Yes, well... appearances are quite often deceiving, even among those of us who think that we know what we sense around us.' 'Some of us don't have more then the normal physical senses, even if they're enhanced. Some of us are still blinded by the veil until we step through it. I should have known from reading her file that was the case, but I was an idiot when it came to that. The fact that I had this situation dropped in my lap disarmed me. Guess that's why I'm not the one in charge anymore. I should have also read a bit more on your file, then I would have known what I was up against. Ms. Vlaszy, or should I call you Lia?? Lia gritted her teeth, the look on her face shifting more to threat than amusement. 'Your files may tell you quite a lot but they obviously don't tell you that my surname is a secret I've chosen to kept for my own safety. If you persist in calling me by it, then I will quite rapidly change my opinion of your supposed goodwill.' Virgil shifted uncomfortably behind Lia, almost physically registering the irritation that was beginning to emanate from the female vampire. Lia's arms folded over her chest, and her unblinking gaze bore into Gray. 'What is your interest in Dawn?? Gray cleared his throat. 'Dawn and I have had talks. Some from my aspect of my job in the Order, of being a trainer for the sport we take in. I originally saw so much potential there, I couldn't help myself in giving her some pointers for certain opponents. Then we had an opportunity to have some other talks, real conversations. Even one after a show where we hit a late night dinner and had an early breakfast and some coffee. That's when I realized that she was someone I could trust. I had already shown her what I was. She's known since the second time we met. It's not often that someone like you, or any of us here, meet someone that we can talk about anything with. However, the actions of myself and my brother have got in the way of that. And with him buying her the roses for Valentine's Day, and then sending them in my way... yes, Dawn came to me and said that if I wanted to continue talking with her, I had to set things straight with you. I don't know if she was hoping that I'd either run away.... Or show the initiative in actually opening a dialogue with you to fix things.? The vampire looked rather non-plussed as he finished speaking, and fired off her question point-blank. 'Are you sure your interest in her is as innocent as you say it is? To go to all that trouble to make her feel as though I was simply using her for a pawn is... perhaps a little suspect. Forgive my old-fashioned notions.? A smile spread over Lia's face, one that was still rather bestial and cold in nature. Gray gave her a stoic stare. 'I know about Marcus. The order has known for some time, since I read her file. I know about Corvus. She's had her innocence ripped from her. And I didn't see that in the case of you and here, there was true emotion. Something your kind should not be able to feel. For that I have to thank you. Because so far, she's only been abused by those of your kind she mingled with. And I don't think I have to remind you that you're almost considered a rebel to some of your kind. You're open , you've violated some of their rules on a regular basis. I saw that as a potential threat to her. If I would have read into it deeper, I would have seen that she's not a as fragile and in danger as I thought. To be honest, with you Lia... I was showing concern for her. I don't truly know why, but I was.? The smile faded, and for a few moments, tense silence reigned in the little grove of trees. Lia could hear Virgil behind her shifting position, no doubt putting a hand on the pommel of one of his pistols. But slowly, she nodded. It would not come to that today. 'Very well, then. Your apology... and concerns... are accepted, and appreciated.? Though her words sounded forced, her arms resumed a non-confrontational position at her sides, head still held at that proud bearing, wondering what else Gray might have to say. Gray shifted some and then looked up at her. 'You do realize what it is that I missed right? Between her other two paramours of the Vampire kind?' 'What is that?? Lia rejoined, nearly impatiently snapping the question at him. She had no time for games today. She gave no indication of whether she knew what he meant or not, but simply continued to hold his gaze. 'By all rights, Lia, she should be one of you. She's willingly consumed the blood of a vampire before. On more then one occasion. You were sired a long time ago, you know how it works. She didn't do the small amounts it takes to make a minion either... by all rights, she shouldn't still be in a normal living state. But she is. I should have guessed that right away. And you can't tell me that you didn't know about it. There has to be a reason you've never even attempted to turn her. I guess you realize that you probably can't. ' Among most of her kind, that would have been considered a challenge. A slap in the face for not being virulent enough in blood to turn another. Lia scoffed, disdain taking over her expression. 'Your files must be incorrect. A fairly important prerequisite to becoming one of us is being drained by one of us. Surely your files should tell you that's never happened to Dawn. It is a moot point in any case, because I would not force my existence on her. She has suffered enough of the abuses of my kind. I owe it to her to show some forbearance.' Gray laughed. 'Is there a chance that after so many centuries, you have a true beating heart? I mean that not as an insult. But in a commendation for how you've treated her. But you can't tell me that you've not suspected it for a long time. If it's true though, it means that working with our own charge has prepared us for if or when she would awaken. She's got to be something special. It's the only explanation. The unfortunate thing is that I don't know if I'd be ready to see her go through the same pain that I saw one other go through.? Lia shook her head violently. 'She is not a science project to be stared at as she goes through the process. And in any case, she's strong enough to handle it. I believe that she partially has a sense of what she is. It will not break her to know the whole truth.? 'We can hope. Part of my concern is also what would appear to be her getting close to your intended target right now.... Almasy.' An arch look came over Lia's feline features. 'What exactly do you perceive as getting close, Gray? And why do you presume that he is our intended target?? Gray didn't smile. 'Well you guys jumped him. And you and Virgil still owe him for what he did in Global. Other then that, did you not pay attention when you appeared on the 4WW holiday show? Cause Dawn and Seymour were there too.' Lia rolled her eyes. 'Anyone can make a mistake, drink too much eggnog, and kiss a jackass instead of a prince.? Gray was still stoic. 'You know as much as I do, that Dawn doesn't drink that much. You know it even more then I do more then likely. I'll admit that I agree with your take on him, but there is more to it. If she wanted to pursue that relationship, she's in for being hurt more then any of your kind ever has...' She doesn't,? Lia cut in, ?I can assure you that whatever that was, it meant nothing. I know more than anyone where Dawn's loyalties lie.? For a few moments, there was a tangible tenseness snapping between all parties, even as Blaise and Virgil just stood by watching the conversation. It was a challenge, clear enough, but what would come of it was up in the air. Lia's face was rather impassive, but within it, her green eyes blazed like verdant fire opals. Whatever else was said in this meeting, she only knew one thing: she didn't like the way they were already treating Dawn like their charge. She didn't like it at all. .:: ::: ::. The weak, kittenish moan came through cracked lips, lips whose pain was beginning to register again, a throat that felt like it had been dry for some time. It was with a noise of her own that she began to realize that the room was full of noises, and though at first they sounded as though coming through a funnel, slowly but surely they began to clarify themselves one from another. Fighting. A fight. Clearly, the noises of a scuffle were in the room all around her. Her senses screamed danger, and she realized that whether she could get her eyes open or not didn't matter- she'd have to. And she'd have to get up as well, up from this supine position, because she was obviously in no condition to fight, so fleeing was the only option. But suddenly, it was as though those bonds were freed. Cut free. Willing her muscles to work, she struggled to sit up, but her hands didn't have the strength to push, nor her abdomen to pull. She felt stiff all over. Herculean will and the continued shouting in the room caused her to force her eyes open in time to see... White tiled walls... Dawn tossed her empty pistols to the side, and draws the short kodachi she'd carried for an emergency. 'Dawn, God no, not Dawn... what is she doing here, and what the hell is that attacking her? You know damned well what that is attacking her Lia, one of your own, and it's your fault for putting her in this kind of danger... If I could just get up off this fucking table I could heal her...' It wasn't to be. No matter how she struggled, she couldn't get her muscles to do much more than twitch in an agonized and pathetic fashion. She whimpered, trying to force her eyelids to stay open. Her head dropped to the side, so forcefully that her vision blacked for a moment. Then, the colors cleared... White tile... it swings around with a spiked arm, jabbing Virgil in the side, and pulling back, opening him up reasonably severely. Virgil doubles over from the sudden pain of the wound... The scream choked and died in her throat. The frantic, paralyzing horror that seized her manifested itself only in the feeble gesture of her hand, straining towards him. February 2006 Following WW39 Japan The wind had begun to whip up from the south upon the return trip from the meeting. As Virgil and Lia arrived back at the doors of Toshiro Mifune's wrestling school, it was screaming like furious phantoms all around them. It had begun to seep into her bones on the walk back through the woods towards the bullet train station, and now, she was ready to relax a bit, away from the elements. Relax in the sense that she wouldn't be out in the cold any longer, that is. Certainly, there was plenty on her mind at the moment, plenty of things that Blaise and Gray had seen fit to bring up that warranted further consideration. As they started up the steps of the dojo, the big doors were suddenly thrown open, and a buoyant Dawn came darting through them as though she hadn't seen them in years. For a few moments, Lia saw her from an outside perspective, saw the new light gleaming in her eyes now that everything was back to normal and wondered what it was about Japan that revitalized her mortal lover so... but those were questions for later. Suddenly, she found Dawn's arms around her waist. 'Welcome back! Gods, it took you so long. We were afraid that the storm had caught you.' Lia embraced her back, perhaps a little more awkwardly than usual. If anything, Lia felt that this whole issue with Gray had driven a wedge into all things interpersonal. It was becoming a contest of who was best for Dawn between Lia et al and the Knightmare Brigade. A grimace came over her face as Dawn turned away from them, pulling her by the hand. 'Come in! I've got sake for you to warm up with, and fresh kimonos if you want to get out of those wet clothes...' But as she turned back to Lia and Virgil she saw the frigid look on Virgil's face- typical enough, really. But it was matched by a half-mirror of the same on Lia's. She nearly stopped mid-step, glancing back and forth between them worriedly. 'Um... What's going on? What aren't you telling me?' Lia and Virgil shared a look for a moment, Virgil's an obviously stony one, half-distrusting. Of course, as difficult as Dawn and Virgil's relationship was to maintain at a livable level day-to-day, Lia could only surmise that Virgil was looking for a reason to distrust her, if only to get back to the typical grind he'd had going before they'd rescued Lia together and he'd at least been forced to respect her- a little. No one ever said that the Kindred weren't creatures of habit. Lia sighed. 'The meeting was rather trying.' Dawn's face fell slightly. Lia wasn't sure whether she should presume it was because she was upset, or because things hadn't worked out well with Gray. 'Hush, Lia, there's no sense in thinking like that.' 'What happened?' Dawn asked, her face suddenly restored to its elegant strength, 'I want to know.' At that point, of course, there was no option of lying because Dawn wasn't going to stop until she knew everything, whether it was a pretty truth or not. 'Oh, let's see... I walked into the meeting a territorial vampire, and spoke with a territorial lupine who is absolutely convinced that he and his organization have some special ward over you and your awakening. That doesn't sit too well with me, I'm sorry to say.' Dawn winced, but underneath the wince was a hint of resentment. 'I... I'm sorry for putting you in that position, Lia.' Lia shook her head. 'You put me in no position, Dawn. I'm simply displeased with the idea that they don't seem to think you're strong enough to handle being what you are. They mentioned something to me about how their charge equipped them to deal with your awakening and its consequences.' A mulish look crossed Dawn's face. 'I didn't ask for their help. I hope you know that.' Lia nodded, but the gesture was hollow, made into a thoughtless instinct by the strain of the situation. 'I didn't think that you would have.' 'Is that all?' Dawn asked, her face beginning to show a significant bit of irritation, 'They just sat there patronizing me the whole time?' At that, it was Lia's turn to grimace. She shook her head. 'No. Although I admit that a significant portion of the meeting was taken up with the general sentiments that you need your hand held, it wasn't all about what you are.' Dawn only looked at her, presuming she'd say what else it was about. At the very least, the way her lips were pressed together into a thin line, Lia guessed that she'd already heard enough about the meeting to find it a bit dissatisfying to say the least. Lia met her consternated gaze with near-indifference. 'A warning. They misread our tactics fairly blatantly, and seem to have almost no idea of what we're really doing here, other than what they assume our surface workings mean. But they've told me sufficient information to warrant concern over a particular aspect of our plan.? A note of icy determination was slowly beginning to seep into Lia's voice, strength for the siege ahead. 'Oh?' Lia nodded grimly, her voice dropping in volume as she continued. 'Yes. The main objective isn't going to be as easy as we thought. We may very well find that our targets are not as easy to hit as we would like. I'm not willing to jeopardize success or safety, even if Blaise and Gray are blowing the danger out of proportion. Our tactics have to change.' Of course, that was only a bare-bones outline of what was going on, but the need for secrecy about such things went without saying. Slowly, Dawn nodded, and pasted over whatever emotion she might have had with a plain-faced, business-like look. 'We can talk in Master Mifune's office undisturbed.' ?Perfect.? Dawn was already a few steps ahead, slipping through one of the few Western-style doors in the building, the one that would lead them to the hallway that contained Mifune's office. 'Find Vivica, please,' Lia said as she passed through the door that Dawn was holding open for her. Dawn nodded curtly, and shortly thereafter disappeared down another hall to go get the last of their quartet. Once in the fortress that was Mifune's office, Lia felt free to sigh distractedly. This was rapidly becoming much more complex than she had anticipated, and the failing of her kind had always been an inability to adapt when well-laid plans went awry. Whatever Lia Vlaszy's failings might have been, that wasn't one of them. She had known from the start that the only way plans as comprehensive as theirs ever worked was if everyone was prepared for the worst, and armed to the teeth to blow the worst into oblivion. Outside, the wind howled around the corners of the building, the hissing and spitting of the squall but a small portent of the storm that was to come.
