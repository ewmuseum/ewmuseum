Taking it to the extreme

12 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/12/05 There are times in every wrestlers life that they'll never forget. Their first match. Their first main event. Their first title. Their first match where they realized they've 'made it'. The match that changed their lives forever. For me the match that changed my life happened during my days in the IWO. I was into my fifth reign as IWO Champion, and had been feuding with a guy named Darqside for the past six months. We had gone through hell and back in hardcore matches, tag-team matches, last man standing matches, even a triple threat match with another number one contender, a guy who went by the name of the Golden Hulk thrown in for good measure. But we had been at it for six months and I wanted it to end. I made a deal with John Steele to have a feud ender with Darqside. It was going to be the most unique match anyone had ever seen. It was to my knowledge the first (and only) Extreme Death Match. Take a Hell in a Cell match, toss in a hardcore match, and just for fun add a ladder match and you've got it. Oh yeah, and there was no three count in this match. To win this EDM you had to hold your opponent down for a five count. Shades of King Kong Bundy. Did I mention Darqside was three inches taller than me and weighed 300 pounds? Basically the weapons are hanging from the top of the cell and the only way to get them down is to use the ladders. There were two ladders inside the cell in opposite corners, hanging from the top of the cell were kendo sticks, baseball bats, 2X4's, street signs, chairs, bats and 2X4's wrapped in barbed wire, and garbage can lids (the cans themselves were under the ring along with the tables). We went into the No Rules PPV with the intention of tearing down the house. I can still see the match in my minds eye as clear as if it were yesterday. Darqside, being the challenger came out first to a rousing chorus of boos. Yeah, I was a face again at this point. I thought the roof was gonna come off the arena when I came out as the cheers from my Machineheads was deafening. There was no caution as the cell lowered over us, hell we didn't even wait until it was all the way down before we tore into each other. I got a ladder first and started using it as my tag-team partner hitting Darqside with it as I attempted to get to the weapons hanging from the top of the cell. Okay, I won't bore you with the details, suffice it to say after 45 minutes we were both bleeding, tired, sore and barely moving. We had hit each other with everything we had and then some, we had both gone through 2 tables, used every weapon hanging from the top of the cell, and hit each other with every high impact move we could. Darqside had chokeslammed me 3 times, piledriven me three times (once on a chair), tossed me like a javelin into the side of the cell once, bashed me in the face with the stairs, and hit me with his Darqness Falls finisher twice and I was still somehow on my feet. Not that he was in any better shape, I had hit him with a total of three chairs, DDT'd him four times (once on the floor), catapulted him into the side of the cage twice, hit him with two piledrivers of my own, hit an Arabian Face buster, hit an Arabian Face buster with him sandwiched in the ladder, and two Mean Machines. He was still on his feet. Of course my legs felt like rubber bands...no wet noodles was more like it. I was guessing Darq's legs were about the same. I hit a Machinehead on a chair and literally knocked him out. We had both lost a lot of blood and had hit each other with everything we had for 45 minutes. I sat there on my knees, and then using the last of my energy threw myself across his body for the pin. I don't remember actually getting the win because I passed out as well. I had to watch the match later to see the referee issue the five count and raise my arm signifying me as the winner, and then realizing that both Darq and I were literally unconcious. The arena fell silent and the cage was raised as the EMT's came down to the ring to check on us. We were both wheeled out, I had retained the title but I was lucky to be retaining my life. I had a mild concussion, a sprained neck, two broken fingers, a fractured wrist, a broken nose and a six inch long gash on my forehead. Darqside had fared no better, suffering a broken foot, a fractured collarbone, a fractured elbow, a broken nose, and like me had suffered a mild concussion and a sprained neck. The gash on his forehead was only four inches long. That was, to date the most damage I'd suffered in a wrestling match. I'd like to hear someone tell me wrestling is fake after witnessing that match. It was a good three weeks before I was ready to return to the ring. But I did and continued to put on high quality matches until the IWO folded several months later. Why am I telling you all this? Because the wrestling sites were procaliming that my Parking Lot Brawl with Johnny Lexicon at FallOut was the most brutal match I was ever in. Bullshit I say. Don't get me wrong, it was brutal, and I'm still hurt from what Johnny did to me, and I wasn't 100% when I fought Chrissy-Poo last week, but I'm the TV champion and I'm a fighting champion. And I'm feeling much better, thank you very much, have a nice day, drive through please. Oddly enough though, the match were I lost the most blood was when I won the IWO title for what would be my sixth and final time, against a monster of a man called Roadkill. I bled buckets in that match, having been busted open early in the match and then repeatedly having my face smashed into, ground against and generally fucked up by the cage. But the Extreme Death Match had changed my life and I had never suggested one again. What? I didn't mention that it was my idea? I could have sworn I did. Well it was, and I will never, ever suggest it again. Unless of course, I'm not in it.
