Date: 03/26/10 He sat uncomfortably on his throne, rubbing a thumb and index finger into his temples as burdensome clouds formed over his head. Eyes-closed, he rose his head and groaned. “How do we solve this problem, Umi?”. “It's hard to tell, sire,” replied half of the king's counsel. Though he was a tall man of over six feet, Umi's neck was strained from staring up at his leader's lofty perch. “Parts of the medina will remain loyal to you until the day they die, but whispers of discontent continue to rise from the desert's farthest corners. We have no reason to panic as of yet, but I would suggest increasing surveillance, particularly outside the capital.” “Umi is right, my lord,” piped the man at the other side of the chamber's thin carpet. Clad in identical white robes, but shorter and carrying more weight than Umi, he too looked up to the throne's raised platform with austerity. “You have reigned over these lands for an unparalleled number of years. A Pharaoh's enemies only multiply as time passes, and you have sat upon that chair for longer than anyone before you. Our foes numbers are not yet insurmountable, but we must act now to prevent that from becoming a certainty.” The king opened his eyes to gaze upon his advisers. Their words were true; never before had one man reigned over the Nile for so long, but his position was growing increasingly perilous. Those who hold power will always attract the envy of others, and though the was still exalted by many, those who coveted his throne only grew more dangerous with every passing day. “Then we must increase our garrisons,” said the ruler. “An extra guard at every outpost. I want our patrols to leave nowhere untouched; they will cover every grain of the Sahara's sand, and we will find our enemies and eliminate them. Though my rule has been long, there is still unfinished work to be done in this empire. I am not ready to relinquish my position just yet.” Prickly heat began to spread around the Pharaoh's white and blue headdress's headband. Though the torch-lit chamber lay deep in the heart of Luxor's grandest pyramid and the air was cool, he could not help but feel the heat beneath his silk fatigues. “A wise move, sire,” Umi said, punctuating his confirmation with a nod of his long face. “I shall send word to the military immediately.” “And you to the police, Ashai,” the Pharaoh said, looking at Umi's compatriot. “Their help will be invaluable in rooting out the usurpers, whoever they may be.” “They say that Caldari grows restless,” Ashai said. “He may have dispatched the pawn Jamison against you.” A smirk grew on the king's storm-beaten features. “Caldari is no longer a threat, Ashai. His power weakens with age, and his stooge is too narcissistic to aid his cause. I see no challenge from such peons.” “Perhaps not, bu--” Ashai found himself interrupted as the throne room's giant doors swung open quickly. Startled, the Pharaoh looked up as a robed figure burst into the chamber and dashed towards the throne as quickly as his ageing legs would carry him. He stopped between Umi and Ashai, pausing to bow before his ruler before rising. He was met with a frown from the king. “Zahur, what is the meaning of this interruption?” the Pharaoh enquired sternly. “Excuse my suddenness, sire, but I bring troubling news from the desert.” The old man was wheezing, struggling for breath. Beads of sweat trickled down his sunburnt dome and his speech was laboured. He waited for his leader's confirmation. “You do realise you are interfering with a matter of state security?” the Pharaoh said, leaning forward on his gold-painted chair. “This had better be important.” “It is, my liege.” Zahur struggled not to pant. “The tomb, Pharoah. The seal has been broken.” “What? Which tomb?” “The tomb at the Valley of the Queens,” Zahur said. “Knight's tomb.” Alarm spread through the king's veins. Damp sweat gathered in his headdress's cloth and his pulsed quickened, though he found no words. “Knight's tomb?!” said Umi, grabbing Zahur's shoulders. “Are you sure of it?” “Regrettably so,” said the old man, bowing his head. “Word comes from the garrison by the river. I came as quickly as I could.” “Then we must act immediately!” said Ashai. He turned, facing the Pharaoh. “Sire--” “We should never have let him sleep,” said the king, ruefully. Slowly, he rose to his full, imposing height, and began to tread down the platform's five stairs. “We should have taken his soul when we had the chance. If someone gets to where the beast lays and--” “With all due respect, my liege,” Umi said, “we must act now.” “He is right,” said Ashai. “This is no time for regret. If Knight's tomb has indeed been compromised and those who have broken the seal find where he lies, then we are all in great danger.” The king reached the bottom of the steps, nodding silently towards his advisers. His heart tightened and veins bulged as blood coursed through his body and a thousand thoughts of worry passed through his head. “Come, all three of you” he said, starting on his way to the door. “We must finish this.” * * * * * The young adventurer stumbled through the darkness with one sweaty hand clutching to a wooden ankh and the other pressed against a wall. “Don't worry, Ankhy,” he said in a childlike soprano that shook with trepidation. “Not far now.” Though the path was long and the course uneven, the explorer could see the flickering glow of orange torches ahead. He dared not quicken his pace for fear of tripping on the floor's jagged slabs, so the journey took him considerably longer than it needed to, but eventually he made it. Sweeping back clumps of sodden blonde hair, the boy's face shone brighter than the torch's flames when he entered the room. Though not exactly stacked from floor-to-ceiling with riches, there were enough good and jewels in the vault to draw a mile-wide grin and an excited clap from the youthful explorer. He pulled his ankh – which hung loosely around his neck on a black cord – to his face. “We did it, Ankhy!” he said to it. At about four inches tall and two wide, the hole in the trinket's loop had been filled-in and a crude smiley face had been etched into its wood. “We found the tweasure!”. Skipping gleefully into the room, blissfully unaware of the jagged spikes that shot up from the floor behind him, the treasure-hunter marvelled in his surroundings. Drowned in shadows cast from the thick pillars that ran from the room's floor to its lofty, unseen ceiling, the room wasn't the endless sea of gold he'd imagined but there was more than enough to satisfy his lust. He trotted towards a raised platform that housed a jewel-encrusted skull and quickly pulled it from its moorings, narrowly avoiding the plume of noxious gas that shot up beneath it. “Look at this one, Ankhy. So sparkwy and pwetty! I think I'll keep this one for myself,” he said, sliding the skull into his satchel. A glimmering weapon rack caught his eye. Sitting at the other side of the vault, it held no fewer than six swords and maces, all as bright and pristine as the day they were first cast. “Ohh, looky there!” He stepped away from the skull's platform and towards the weapons. Standing on a pressure pad, he moved with such excitement that the blade swinging down from the rafters swoosh harmlessly passed his backside. Wrapping his sweaty hands around one of the swords with visions of swinging it around his head like a mighty gladiator, the adventurer found it hard to remove from the rack. His skinny arms were unable to support the weapon's full weight, and when it dropped to the floor with a heavy clang when he finally did manage to pull it free. Red-faced, he looked away from the sword and back to the room's remaining treasures. A pile of coins lay around the base of an unopened chest in one corner, and a giant golden goblet overflowing with jewels and stones sat atop another platform. The youngster smiled as he pulled Ankhy to his face again. “Oh, Ankhy; how are we gonna get all this stuff--” Suddenly the treasure-helped yelped. From behind a hand grabbed his forehead and cool steel pressed against his neck with such pressure that his skin would break with the slightest movement. “Ahhhhh!”, he shrieked as his unseen assailant tightened his grip and pushed him face-first against one of the dusty pillars. “Quiet, or I'll bleed you like a stuck pig,” growled a baritone ravaged by years of hookah-abuse. The treasure-seeker felt the aggressor press a knee into the back of his thigh. “What are you doing here, Fawkes?! I thought I told you to stay away from these parts.” Fawkes remembered the voice through waves of panic. “Jamison, pwease,” he said through quivering lips. “Let me go--” “Not until you tell me what you're doing sticking your nose in my business again.” “I'm not! All I wanted was some tweasure...” “And you expect me to believe that?” Jamison snarled, pushing Fawkes harder against the stone. “I know you're not as innocent as your conduct suggests, Fawkes! You've got ten seconds before I stick this knife behind your windpipe.” Fear permeated Fawkes, spewing salty dears from his ducts as Jamison pulled his leg up and pushed it into Fawkes' back. “I...,” Fawkes stammered. “I...” Jamison felt something spread across the bottom of his leg, warm and damp. A slow dripping sound echoed throughout the hollow chamber and Jamison recoiled with horror when he figured it out. “For the love of Horus!” Furious, Jamison pulled away from the trembling Fawkes. A wet patch had formed on his right trouser leg, confirming his fears. He looked up to see moisture spreading across Fawkes' waistline and a puddling growing at his feet. Jamison saw read, grabbing Fawkes by the shoulder and pulling him forward, before slapping him hard across the jaw. Fawkes fell to a whimpering heap after a shrill wail, and Jamison stared at him, snarling. “Pathetic, kid. Pathetic.” He sheathed his blade and adjusted the band holding his straggly black locks back. “If you're here by the time I come back, you're dead. Hear me?” Fawkes looked up with puffy red eyes and managed to nod. Jamison said nothing, just frowned once more at the fallen adventurer and turned. He left Fawkes where he lay and headed on his way; he felt no threat from a man who'd wet himself. * * * * * Surrounded by guardsmen, Pharaoh Murrr and his entourage stood in the shelter of the stables as a royal armourer fastened leather protection around the king's torso. “Are you sure about this, sire?” a worried Umi enquired. “You do not have to do this. T'would be much safer for us to dispatch a battalion of men to the tomb instead, and it would save you the danger.” “I appreciate the concern, Umi,” the armoured Pharaoh replied, stepping away from he who'd decked him out, “but this is something I must do myself. I must see to it that whoever has broken into that tomb cannot bring forth the evil of LeStatt, or it will be the destruction of us all.” “What are you going to do?” “I'm going to do what I should've done a long time ago, Zahur,” Murrr said. He swallowed hard, not relishing the prospect of what awaited him. “I am going to destroy the soul of LeStatt Knight forever. For too long the threat of his awakening has loomed over these lands; now it's time to put an end to it, and to those who dare disturb the sleeping giant.” “Surely no mortal man knows how to animate the fallen Knight, my liege?” Ashai said, swatting a mosquito as it landed on his arm. “And even then, the tomb is heavily trapped. Unless whoever broke in is an expert survivalist, their chances of reaching LeStatt's casket are slim to none.” “I cannot take any chances,” the Pharoah said. Turning around briefly to pick-up a short sword and circular shield, the now fully-armed king sighed. “This is my charge to keep, gentlemen. I should have purged this land of his evil after the last war; this was my wrong, and only I can right it.” Murrr stepped through his audience and walked towards one of the stables. One of his servants stood behind a heavy gate, making final adjustments to a camel's saddle whilst struggling to ignore the stench of animal faeces that rose from the hay-covered floor. “Is she ready?” the Pharaoh asked, pushing through the gate and walking towards the camel. “Yes, m'lord,” the servant said, bowing before his king. “But lord, the opportunity to vanquish Knight passed by many years ago,” Umi said from behind. “Surely you can't take his soul without...” “... reviving him?” the Pharaoh said, turning. “I shan't lie, gentlemen; it is a risky task, but one that must be done and done now. Above Knight's casket sits a vial of holy water. One splash of the fluid will bring the beast to life, and it is then that LeStatt is at his most vulnerable. Before he has reached his full size, a blade through the heart will drain his life-force forever. I must be quick, though; the window between splashing the water and piercing the heart is but a matter of seconds.” “Then send one of the soldiers. Please!” pleaded an exasperated Ashai. “If you revive the beast but can't kill him in time, he'll destroy you and this land will be without a ruler.” Shaking his head, Murrr stepped onto his bowed servant's back and threw a leg over the camel. “No mere soldier can destroy Knight, Ashai. His evil will consume them. I've fought the best before; I've looked into his eyes, and I know I can resist his bewitchment. Only I can do this. Fear not, though. I do not travel alone.” The Pharaoh's steed strode out from the stable. Two spear-wielding guards, also perched on camels, flanked him as he stood before the three robed men. “Forgive me, my aides, for I must depart.” Though they were sure their leader was riding to his doom, Umi, Ashai, and Zahur stepped aside and let the king past. Murrr and his guards rode slowly towards the building's exit, before the beast they rode galloped off into the desert, eventually disappearing behind a giant sand dune. Deflated, Umi used his robe's sleeve to wipe the perspiration from his forehead and glanced at his colleagues. “We'd better pray he is successful, else we are all done for.” * * * * * Meanwhile, the baking sun shone down upon a veiled figure as she slid down from the stable's thatched room and hopped onto her camel. She'd heard every word. * * * * * Jamison had been squinting his way through the tomb's labyrinth for almost an hour and his patience was beginning to waver. Though Caldari's intense survival training had made him able to detect and disarm almost any man-made trap that had ever been built, doing so in the tomb's dim light was no easy task. Part of him wanted to turn his back and walk away, and that urge had never been stronger than when he'd stumbled upon the tomb's fifth treasure chamber. He could stuff his pockets full of gold and live like a king for the rest of his days, and if he lived smart enough, Caldari would never know a thing. He'd write Jamison off, suspecting him dead, and groom another pawn to help him quench his thirst for power. It was an easy route out, but not one Jamison would take, for rubies and pearls were not all that motivated Jamison. Thought their allegiance was poisonous, Jamison knew his benefactor was a man who's power was matched only by the Pharaoh, and with Murrr out of the way, there would be nothing stopping he and Caldari. He'd dedicated years of his life to serving his master, and thus far Caldari had held true and delivered on his promises. He'd taken Jamison from a broken down street rat to one of the most notorious figures in the Sahara. Detractors called him a puppet, but Jamison knew he was much more than that. Caldari wasn't using him, he was helping him. With Caldari's help, Jamison would learn to channel the rage he'd built-up from years fighting for scraps on Luxor's poverty-stricken streets. Soon he'd rise from street rat to one of the most powerful men in Egypt, and that was not something he was willing to sacrifice for the sake of a few pieces of gold. So he continued on his way, avoiding traps and ignoring treasures, praying that his destination would soon present itself to him. The journey was one of the most perilous he'd ever undertaken and there was still some distance left to travel, but if he can his benefactor were to claim absolute power then the journey was necessary. They could not overthrow the Pharaoh on their own; Murrr's palace was almost impregnable, and his armies were vast. Their number was only two – tackling the king head-on was not an option, and tens before them had tried to sneak inside the palace for an assassination attempt, only to be slain in the process. No, if they were to achieve their goals then they would have to take drastic measures. They both knew the dangers of summoning an ancient evil well. For centuries, LeStatt's shadow had plagued the country until eventually he was slain by Pharaoh Murrr at the conclusion of the second great war, but the king had made a grave mistake in not taking his soul too. Somewhere in the tomb, the body of LeStatt lay, waiting to be revived. Jamison knew the risks, and knew that he may very well be consumed by Knight's evil in the process, but he and Caldari had done their research. For years they'd read up on LeStatt Knight and his immense power, trying desperately to figure out how to put the reins on the beast. Though the possibility that Knight would rise from his tomb and slay them all existed, Jamison knew that if he can Caldari could harness his power and use it for their benefit, they'd be unstoppable. All he had to do was slip a few drops of the potent green elixir he'd tied to his belt between Knight's lips, and the monster would be under their control. Jamison was getting closer; he could sense it. He slowed his pace as he came to another long, thin corridor that seemingly came to a dead end. Recalling the instructions Caldari had given him, Jamison dropped to his knees and pushed a stack of crates away from the wall. Behind them was a gap of no more than twelve inches in height – a gap that he must pass through. Pushy himself to the ground and coughing as clouds of dust shout up his nostrils, Jamison clawed at the rough stone floor, pulling his body along. By the end of the passage every one of Jamison's muscles were aching and the tips of his fingers spewed crimson blood, but when he rose to his feet and dusted himself off he knew it was worth it. There lay the stone casket of LeStatt Knight. * * * * * Her camel carried her quickly across the desert's shifting surface, and though she kept a safe distance away from the Pharaoh and his guards she never let him leave her sight. Eventually the pursuit took her into the Valley of the Queens and deep into the vast necropolis of Thebes. Dug into the mountains that flanked her were tens upon tens of tombs, occupied mainly by Princes, Princesses, and Noblemen. If her recollections were correct, the tomb her target was heading for was at the Valley's other end. Eventually made it, and though the intense heat and discomfort of riding upon a camel's back had fatigued her, she still had plenty energy to hop from her steed and hide behind a pile of limestone rubble as the king dismounted. Peaking cautiously over the mound's peak, she watched Pharaoh Murrr as he turned and barked orders to the two guards who'd attempted to follow him into the tomb. Seconds later and he'd disappeared into the darkness, leaving his aides behind. Now was the time to strike. Grabbing a small rock from the valley's dusty floor, she flung it blindly overhead. It must've landed close to her targets, as she heard the guards grumbling to themselves and then the crunch of heavy footsteps on the ground. The footsteps drew closer and closer. She heard one of them unsheathe a sword, but that would not save him. When the guards were close enough so that she could hear them breathing, the assassin hopped over the limestone. The first guard was dead before he could feel the blade push between his shoulder blades, and the second was struck in the chest with a flying dagger quicker than he could turn around. Both fell harmlessly to the ground, kicking up clouds of dust as they hit the deck. Quivering, but very much deceased. She made her way towards Knight's tomb. * * * * * Cold, dead air kissed the Pharaoh's clammy skin as he walked into a place he'd hoped never to visit again. The first thing he did was pull a torch from the wall; though he still had vague recollections of the route he was about to travel he knew that it was dark and trap-laden. It would have been a cruel twist of fate for him to fall to a trap he'd set with his own hands years prior. Fortunately, his journey to the first treasure chamber went without incident. The torch's light helped him pass through the arid corridors safely. He dodged traps that whoever had broken in managed to avoid and sidestepped those that remained untriggered, before stepping into the vault safely. There was little time to waste. Pharaoh Murrr strode across the room purposefully, and though he dreaded to think what the warm squelch beneath his sandal as he passed one of the pillars was, he had no time to investigate further. Passing the old weapon cache, the king hauled his giant frame up onto a nearby ledge, and began thumbing away and the walls bricks. Soon enough he came across one that was loose, and with all his might he pushed it into the wall. The floor behind to shake and dust fell from the ceiling as Murrr jumped down from the ledge. The wall before him began to slowly grind forward, presenting him with a gap of about two feet to slip through. Drawing his sword, the Pharaoh hurried through the secret passage and sprinting into the heart of Knight's tomb just as Jamison dropped the heavy casket lid to the floor. The limestone hit the ground with an almighty thud, cracking in half as the Pharaoh thrust his blade towards Jamison. “Away from there, fiend!” Murrr used his blade to shoo Jamison away from Knight's mummified body and forced himself between the opened casket and his foe. “Why, Mr. Pharaoh,” Jamison began with a wry smile. “Fancy seeing you here.” “What in Horus' name do you think you're doing?” Murrr snapped. “Do you have any idea what you are meddling with?!” “Oh, I do. I do.” Jamison wrapped his digits around his own sword's handle. “LeStatt Knight. A blight upon the people of Egypt; the greatest evil these lands have ever faced, right?” “Back off! I'll kill you where you stand!” “Temper, temper now,” Jamison said mockingly. “Y'know, I had come here to use Knight's power against you, but seeing as you've made things easy for me, I don't think I'll bother now.” Quickly Jamison pulled out his sword and stepped menacingly towards the king. “You fool! You think you can control the beast?! He will murder us all!” “Huh. Really?” Jamison swung from the king without warning, but Murrr drew up his blade just in time. Clutching his sword with both hands, the Pharaoh battled against Jamison's strength, trying to force his foe's sword downwards when an arrows swooshed by his face, missing his nose by a matter of millimetres. “Whaddaya know? Two birds with one stone,” said a female voice. It's source stepped forth from the secret passage, taking two arrows from her quill and holding her bow horizontally. She placed both arrows on the bow's wooden frame and pulled back. “Any last words, boys?” Though the lower-half of her face was veiled, Murrr recognized the red streak in her hear immediately. “Julian! You were imprisoned three months ago!” “Next time, Murrr, trying putting me somewhere with thicker walls.” Glancing frantically from Jamison and back to Julian, the Pharaoh felt desperation overcome him. He dared not make a drastic move, though; all it would take to end his life would be for Jamison to strike or for Julian to let go of her arrows. He could take his eyes off neither. “For the greater good, cast down your weapons!” Murrr called. “NOW! Your Pharaoh commands it.” But neither would listen. Though a bow was bearing down upon him, Jamison stepped forward and Julian let him. The Pharaoh felt his back hit the wall as Jamison came closer towards him, pointing his blade towards the king's belly. Suddenly, Murrr swiped the sword aside with his own. Jamison's weapon arm splayed wide and the Pharaoh lunged forward, kneeing him in the gut. He rose his sword above his head, ready to slice Jamison's head from his shoulders, until a noise from above shook him. Footsteps, then a sudden clunk. “Oopsy,” came a young voice from above. Fawkes peered his head over the edge and looked below as Murrr's eyes filled with terror. “My God! What have you done?!” Dropping his weapon, the Pharaoh raced towards Knight's casket. His fears were confirmed. The vase that had housed the holy water lay in pieces on the beast's chest, and LeStatt's decades-old bandages had been soaked through to his rotten skin. Beams of light suddenly shot from the body, almost blinding the Pharaoh as he, Julian, and Jamison were flung to the floor. Landing near Jamison, he picked up a sword and staggered towards the casket. Now was the time to strike. Now was the time to destroy LeStatt Knight once and for all. But it was too late. A dead, bandaged hand reached over the casket's edge. TO BE CONTINUED!
