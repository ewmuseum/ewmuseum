March 5, 2010 Flashbulbs pop in my face to the point I can hardly see for the dancing spots in front of my eyes. I’m drenched in sweat and I stink like Hell, but I feel like I just stepped out of the shower; fresh, clean, new. I’m sore, tired, but every fiber and nerve in my body hums at the same frequency as the crowd’s roar. I stand on top of the turnbuckles and it might as well be the top of the world. I’m a champion again. After more than five years, I have a handful of wrestling gold that doesn’t even fit right around my tiny little waist, but it means more than its clunky appearance would have anyone believe. It means I’m back. It’s for real now. And out in the crowd a mix of faces greets me: those who know what it means and those are just beginning to see, who have just had their first glimpse of something they know is special and can’t put their finger on why. Suddenly, in this moment, it’s a whole new world for me again. ~ March 8, 2010 “Oh my god, tell me you are NOT hacking into PRIME’s web server!” The shriek of her manager, at least one octave higher than usual, brought Tempest out of the trance in which she was staring at the monitor. She popped her head up, peering over the top of it, her tumble of jet black hair with its streak of blue bobbing as she shook her head and said, “Darren, will you stop being so paranoid?” She stood up to her full height, a whopping 5’2”, and put her hands on her hips, clad in fuzzy black knit pants and a hoodie to match over a tee shirt that depicted a sea of Chinese characters in silver over red. “You always assume the worst,” she said, looking exasperated. “Don’t you trust me?” Her rotund manager sighed and set down a ream of papers on the flat part of the desk nearest him and said, “I trust you to be up to the very worst I can imagine. And you seldom disappoint.” He came around to her side of the desk to peer at the screen and started as he saw her pets, the stunt ferrets that danced and played pranks on GCW television, sitting back on their haunches calmly watching the screen as if they understood exactly what was passing before their eyes. Emil, the white one, looked up and chittered curiously at him, but Fernando, the darker one never turned his attention from his Cheezit. “That always freaks me out that they seem to know what’s going on,” he said, folding his arms. “I know they don’t, but still…” “Don’t bet on it,” Tempest said with a wink and reseated herself, taking a moment to scratch each of the ferrets gently on the head. Darren looked over her shoulder at the screen and waved his finger in agitation at the unfamiliar code sprawled across it in addition to the normal homepage for PRIME. “Ok, then what’s all that shit on the screen? That’s not what I normally see going to their website,” he squawked. “You are up to something!” “Relax,” Tempest said with a mischievous smile that did nothing to reassure him. “I’m just having a look around. I’m not going to hack anyone’s profile, misappropriate funds, make anyone donate large sums of money to charity, replace their gallery photos with dancing ferret animations or cause their network to be shut down for decency violations… this time.” She tried and failed to look innocent and Darren groaned as he sat back in a nearby chair and said, “I don’t know what’s going to give out first - my journalistic credibility for being your manager instead of editing my magazine or my blood pressure for what the duty actually entails.” Tempest laughed and tapped lightly at the keyboard, her fingers dancing across its glowing surface. “I’m sorry. You know I’m not trying to ruin your health but it’s just that there’s not anyone else who really understands me,” she said, looking at him with the smallest pout. “That last guy we interviewed to maybe be my manager seemed to think I was a toy ready to be packaged and marketed, and that other girl was so busy trying to be my best buddy that she had no professional presence at all.” She sighed and tapped lightly a couple of times on the mouse as she narrowed her deep blue eyes intently, looking at the screen. Darren smiled crookedly at her and said, “They can’t all be Miyuki you know, or Ivy McGinnis or…” “They don’t have to be,” Tempest interrupted, looking away from the screen. “They just need to really understand me, to grasp where I’ve been and how I work and what this is all about for me. And above all, I need someone who’s truly loyal, who I can trust.” Tempest’s eyes spoke volumes to the seasoned editor as she fixed him in her earnest gaze. He’d seen it all in this business and he’d seen most of it happen to her, from highs and lows, friendships and betrayals, loves won and lost, championship wins and crushing defeats. “Sure you’ve reported my life from up close and personal, but you still always went ahead and believed, you know? You didn’t just stand aside and watch.” she said. “That why I trust you.” She turned away, playing with Fernando, who had decided to pounce upon her hand, and turned him over rolling him around like a like a ball. “You know what I realized during Fanfest, Darren? While I was entertaining all those kids and signing autographs for them, I realized that most of them weren’t even born when I disappeared from the ring.” Darren nodded and rubbed the early stubble that covered his chin as he contemplated his client, his charge, his friend’s words. “They weren’t alive the last time I held a championship,” she said, nodding toward the GCW television title, where it sat propped against the glowing tower of her computer. “And even some of their parents who are GCW fans, have only heard of me before as some vague rumor. How many of them came of age while I was gone and look at this as the beginning of my career? If they find out anything that came before this, it’s through Youtube or PTC’s Museum project video library.” Tempest shook her head and laughed. “Can you believe that? I’m in a museum, like a stuffed dinosaur or caveman bones.” “Or the Hope Diamond,” Darren chimed in, grinning. Tempest smiled at this and shrugged, mulling that thought over. ~~ March 3, 2010 The light shimmered brightly on the surface of the pond as the water burbled gently over the rocks at the base of the tiny waterfall and seemed to gather in the brightness of the mid day sun. Its warmth permeated the stones on which Matsudaira Miyuki knelt beside the shaded patio on which the tatami bedroll was laid out. She held the hand of an old man who lay there and smiled at her with a calm that she seemed reluctant to absorb. “Grandfather, I don’t want to hear you talk like this anymore. You are certainly not dying.” The old man laughed a thin little laugh and patted her hand as he said, “Oh but I am. And I have been for some time. Are we not all dying? Some of us are just doing so more quickly than others.” He breathed deeply and continued, “Why do you think I have come here? No… I am most assuredly dying. But you should not concern yourself with this, for it is as it should be. I am happy here in this place, where my last sight and sound and smell will be blessed.” He pressed her hand gently between his and beamed up at her through crinkled eyelids. “And my family has come to bid me farewell. It is all right then, I think.” Miyuki seemed far less comforted by these words as her gaze dropped to the floor, but she nodded, nonetheless, her dark hair falling forward into her face. She smoothed it back and said, “You sent for me before my father. I’m not sure I understand why.” “Ah, a break from tradition to be sure,” he said, sounding almost mischievous. “But then we have this habit of doing so, yes? Renegades throughout our history, even as we embrace the spirit of our traditions. So it has been with you,” he said, and raised from his side a short sheathed sword, the samurai’s signature wakizashi. Its deep russet sheath was cut in with an intricate design depicting the legend of Amaterasu’s battle with her brother, the storm, Susanoo and the subsequent involvement of Ame-no-Uzume, the kami of merriment, whose dance brought the sun goddess out of hiding. Its gilt handle and delicate carvings of tigers overlaid with silken bindings shone softly in the half light as he passed it into her hands. “It was my father’s,” said the old man softly. “And his father’s before him and so on. How far back I do not need to tell you, for that is a matter of history as much as family. But it was brought to the room when you were to be born, just as with any male child previously. We did not know or care that you were a girl child because we knew that you were to be the only child in our line.” Miyuki held the sword, her eyes fixated on it in a reverential stare. She had only seen it once before, this artifact from a bygone age in which a code was born that had shaped a nation, even the world for a time. Its presence here, now, was not to be taken lightly. “And although your father has upheld the family name in many respects, it is you who has most fully understood the old ways I think,” said her grandfather with a smile. “Even if your interpretation has been unusual.” Miyuki tore her gaze from the beautiful sword to her grandfather’s face and said, “You mean my penchant for following wrestling?” “I mean your declaring loyalty to a lord, so to speak, who is a wrestler. Well, a lady, but then as I say, it was unusual. But not at all inappropriate I think,” he mused. Miyuki sighed and the silence hung in the air like the scent of the orange blossoms, fragrant, but more bitter. “She’s back, you know,” she said quietly, lowering the sword gently to her grandfather’s side. “After all that happened I thought I would never hear of her again, but she has resurfaced and even excelled again.” He nodded slowly, a tiny smile coming to his face. “I do know. You were elated when you first heard as I recall. Is it now, after thinking on it, that the memory becomes bitter? This is not the stuff of loyalty you know.” Miyuki looked up swiftly and said, “Loyalty? I’m not the one who left. She threw me out of her life when she needed me most and I could do nothing about it.” “This is true,” said the old man. “And that is why you must go to her now. The true test of loyalty is not going to someone when they call. It is going to them when they need you. This is the true test of honor. This is what you swore to do.” Miyuki stared a moment until he placed his hand on hers and smiled. “She will need you, and sooner rather than later I think.” ~~~ 1982 “Daddy, who is that old woman with that family?” Whether it was the question itself or the tug on his arm that pulled Dr. Ken Tyrell’s attention away from trying to negotiate the price of fish in Cantonese, which was not his strong suit to begin with, he turned his head and said, “That’s their grandmother, Rachel.” He nodded to the group of people his little girl indicated, a couple with two boys and an old woman between them. His own daughter looked confused, her brow furrowed beneath a tumble of black hair as she said, “What’s a grandmother?” With one handful of fish and his money leaving the other, Dr. Tyrrell froze with sudden realization. He paid rapidly and took her by the hand and said gently, “Tempest, sweetheart, a grandmother is your mommy or daddy’s mother. And a grandfather would be their father.” He watched this sink into her six year old mind as they walked and she nodded. Then came the inevitable. “Daddy?” “Yes, love?” “Where’s my grandmother and grandfather?” asked little Tempest, peering up at her father with keen interest lighting her big blue eyes. He swallowed uncomfortably, unable to relate the intricacies of family disgrace into a language a child could understand. He wouldn’t want her to anyway. “Well, they’re a long way away. You know how I told you I came from a long way away from here?” “Uh huh,” little Rachel said and nodded vigorously, her ponytail bobbing. “Well, that’s where they are still and sometimes, when one goes a long way from home, it’s hard to keep track of people and stay in touch. Sometimes, you lose people that way,” he said, trying to keep the sadness of that very loss out of his voice. “I’m not gonna go far away Daddy,” Tempest said. “I don’t wanna lose you.” She stopped and hugged him around the waist and he stooped to hug her as well. “Don’t you worry about that sweet pea. You’re not going to lose me, no matter what.” It was a lie. But not one he could have possibly foreseen. ~~~~ March 9, 2010 Darren fumbled to dig his ringing cell phone out of his lap top bag while trying to balance his hot dog in his other hand and a cup of coffee to boot. “Hello?” He listened and there was a pause that made him question whether this was one of those automated calling machines. He sighed and repeated himself, half expecting a mechanical voice, probably in another language, to chime in. “Hello?” “Hello, Mr. Stearns?” The voice was far from mechanical. It was a silky and smooth, with the faintest of accents, Japanese he thought. “Yes, this is Darren Stearns. How did you get this number?” He guarded this one pretty closely. The trash line for the magazine was routed to a voicemail system he could go through at his leisure. Only real people had this number. “Obviously you have not changed it. I have an exclusive for you. The one you’ve been waiting for.” “What? Who is this?” Darren stopped, having decided not to cross the street before resolving his precarious arrangement. “This is Miyuki Matsudaira.” The pigeons were overjoyed as Darren’s hot dog hit the sidewalk. ~~~~~ I still remember what it was like walking into fWo and later OSW. I was a novelty. I was fun and cute, like a fubsy new kitten everyone wanted to play with. Fans took to me because of that, but no one saw me as a threat. Not until later when I proved to them that looks could be deceiving and “cuddly” could lead anyone into a false sense of security. After my return a few months ago it was much the same. I was seen as a bit of nostalgia. I was a fond memory to be cashed in on while a whole new generation of stars who don’t even remember me passed me off as a silly little sideshow to their big plans. That’s not all bad though. Forgetting about me is the best thing the wrestling world has ever done for me. If they don’t remember what I’m capable of, then they’ll never see it coming. It was beautiful to remind them once. It will be more beautiful yet. ~~~~ March 3, 2010 “Oh my god, are you kidding me?” Tempest gasped as she looked up at the streamers and the banner hung over the deck and the tables and chairs decked out in blue and silver. She grinned up at Jeremy Rivers who ran a hand through his gray hair and said, “Yup, I’ll have plenty of folks bitch about the Cowboy’s colors, but they’ll just have to deal seeing as that’s where the event’s coming from, right?” Tempest clapped her hands and hopped up and down excitedly. “I can’t believe you actually know people who want to see it. I mean Troy’s so… well, just not into what I do at all.” The older man smiled down at her sympathetically and said, “He’ll come around. You just threw him for a loop is all. We’ve got plenty of friends who will love watching NC-17 and even more so knowing that’s my son’s girlfriend kicking that little weenie’s butt for the TV title.” Tempest smiled sheepishly and said, “I hope you’re right, on both counts.” “Aw, don’t worry, on both counts,” he said and chuckled. “I’m sure you can take care of your opponent. And Troy… well I’m glad he grew up and didn’t pick a bunch of stupid fights or try to be a tough guy for no real reason. He’s got his reasons for thinking violence is stupid and I can’t say I disagree.” He chewed his bottom lip thoughtfully, with his thumbs hooked into his belt loops before his considerable girth. “I came up on the south side and that was the wrong side in those days. People got shot or knifed over looking at somebody funny. I never wanted that for my kids. But I get the difference between that and what you do. It’s entertainment. And I think Troy will too if you give him some time, ok?” He smiled reassuringly and patted the tiny Tempest on the shoulder just as his wife came out onto the deck. “Don’t you think so, Nora?” “Hmm?” she said and turned toward them with a smile, having surveyed the layout to her satisfaction. “Oh, Troy? Yes, he’s been happier with you than he’s ever been. He’ll get through this, honey.” She came over and hugged Tempest gently. “But just because you’re so tough doesn’t mean you shouldn’t be careful. You keep your eyes open. I don’t know a lot about wrestling, but I hear there’s always someone waiting around the corner ready to sneak up and take you down. You just be extra careful. I can’t imagine any of those big guys are happy about a woman taking their titles or their thunder.” Tempest giggled and said, “Don’t worry, I’ll be extra careful. It’s easier knowing there’s family rooting for me.” ~~~ March 8, 2010 “That’s not a choice, that’s grounds for a suicide watch!” Frank Valleta snorted as he climbed up a couple of steps on the ladder to shoot another nail into the 2x4 supporting a ledge of boards above a mat inside the warehouse loft that Tempest called home. “I know, right!” Tempest said as she scaled the opposite end of the contraption made of scaffold and stepped carefully onto the boards. “Like ‘marry, fuck, kill’ are doable options when Jorge Samuelsson, Bishop Steele, and the Wolfeinator are the choices. Go Eddie Fowler!” “Seriously,” said Frank. “Ok, so is that the right height?” Tempest nodded. “Yup, that’s the height from the Halo down to the ring anyway. To the floor is another matter, but that’d be harder to arrange considering how this looks to be laid out,” she said, consulting an enlarged printout. Frank shook his head and whistled. “I wouldn’t want to have to fall from that high.” Tempest sighed and said, “The idea is to jump, my dear protégé, not to fall.” “Yeah ok, if you say so,” he said with a shrug.” It’s still not gonna be a soft landing, with just normal ring padding. “Of course not, that’s why you land on your feet, you roll, or you land on someone else and let them break your fall,” Tempest said and tapped her temple with a wink. “Oh yeah, that would do it,” Frank said and gave her a thumbs up as she walked across the boards, testing their strength. She turned her back and backed up until her toes were just at the edge. Behind her the door opened and she could hear the sound but simply said, “Hi Darren!” Ignoring the silence behind her, Tempest leapt into a shallow moonsault, avoiding the beams in the ceiling and easily went over and back, landing lightly on her feet in spite of the height. She turned and stopped, with stunned gasp as Frank peered back and forth between her and the well-dressed Japanese woman that had entered silently with Darren. Tempest’s mouth hung open for a moment as a dozen different emotions took turns seizing hold of her features, not the least of which was utter shock. She stepped forward slowly, as if in a daze, her eyes frozen wide as she stared at her former manager, who for all the practice that must have gone into this moment, looked as shaky as Tempest. Tempest inhaled sharply as moisture threatened the edges of her eyes and she stepped forward and said, in a voice just barely audible, “Miyuki? You…. You’re…” “I’m here,” Miyuki said softly, summoning a brave smile. Tempest half laughed, half sobbed, but practically leapt the distance left between them and threw her arms around her. “I’m sorry,” she said. “I don’t know if I have all the words to cover the stupidity of all the things I’ve done.” “You don’t need them” Miyuki said and hugged her friend back, trying to fight back a few tears that strayed their way from her eyes. “That’s all I’ve ever wanted to hear.” ~~ “I never understood why she left,” Frank said, peering anxiously at the two women sitting close together across the loft and out of earshot. Their expressions were fraught with an expectant tension, as if years of pent up emotion threatened to burst forth without rhyme or reason, but each kept it in check because being together again was enough to assure that they could work things out. “She didn’t leave,” Darren said nervously, digging in the upper cabinets for coffee mugs. “Tempest sent her away. She lost Miyuki like she lost everything, everyone, back then. She lost them to her own madness and paranoia. ” Frank looked at the older man, surprised by his agitation and said, “Except you? It always seemed to me she never got a fair shake out of all that.” Darren stopped and stared at the young indy wrestler, still so much on the outside looking in and said, “Fair’s got nothing to do with this business. I was just far enough away to keep from getting burnt, but close enough to see what was happening.” He eyed the two women in the other room warily and Frank seemed to pick up on the fact, his brown eyes narrowing in confusion. “Why did she come back then?” he asked. “I still don’t know,” Darren said as he leaned on the counter, peering across at the women deep in conversation. Frank eyed him and said, “You’re scared for her. What’s up with that?” Darren shrugged and said, “A little. Maybe that’s why I agreed to manage Tempest in the first place, so I could keep an eye on her. Miyuki coming back is the very best thing for her. But I’m mostly scared for everyone else. The wrestling world has forgotten what Tempest is capable of, but I haven’t.” Frank’s raised eyebrows said all that was needed as far as the strangeness of Darren’s reply to him, but before he could ask him about his ominous words, a knock was heard at the door. Frank turned and answered, receiving a delivery from a Fed Ex driver of a long, thin box. He turned back inside the loft as everyone gathered around him. “What the heck is that?” Tempest asked, standing on tip toes to peek at the label as Frank held it at a convenient height for him, which was well over her head. “I dunno,” Frank said, looking utterly floored. “But it’s for Miyuki.” He held out the box to her and she accepted it with a somber expression. “How’d they know you were here?” “If this is what I think it is,” she said softly, “There was only one who would know.” Tempest eyed her newly returned friend with concern as she laid the box on the end of the counter and opened it with a knife handed to her by Darren. Miyuki’s eyes fluttered and then shut as the cardboard parted and gave way to plastic, then paper, and finally revealed the delicate carved russet sheath of her grandfather’s wakizashi. She swallowed against the rising lump in her throat and while Darren and Frank looked at the weapon in awe, only Tempest seemed to grasp what its arrival here meant. She laid a hand on her friend’s arm and said, “Is it… his?” A short nod was her response and then Miyuki said, “We now have something in common. Those we love have gone and left us with only traditions to uphold in their memory.” ~ Today, now, staring up at the Dual Halo What does the future hold? Here where the past and the present collide and the explosion of unforeseen consequences dictates one simple truth: all we have is right now. The noise, the screams of the crowd, the looming Plexiglas structure which is nothing like I’ve ever seen before is all I’ve got. And you. You’re out there… a whole new generation, a whole new group of people flipping through the program right now looking for my name. Or not. But you will be. In just a few minutes you will be. Watch.
