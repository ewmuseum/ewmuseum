O’Conner’s Pub

22 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/22/05 For certain Boston-ites, O’Conner’s Pub is one of the best places they’ve ever been to; fully-loaded with alcohol and television. It’s owner, Patrick O’Conner, has been running the joint for about 18 years now, though it has been around since his grandfathers time. So far, the business has had no sign of closing, only being richly successful throughout it’s existence. But, there is reason for that. Patrick O’Conner isn’t as innocent as he seems when he sits with customers and has his shits and giggles. In fact, he is in charge of one of the biggest Irish mob families in all of North America. Everyone of the locals and pub regulars know about the O’Conner’s, but they ignore all of the tell-tale signs. Besides, it might be the smartest thing that they can do. Finally hearing that his son Coyle had become a professional wrestler, Patrick has endlessly tried to contact him. Despite getting the answering machine every single time, Patrick still tries to call him; all he wants to know is that his son is the superstar that he sees on television every Thursday Night. It’s one of those things where he knows, but he doesn’t. Even though he’s a tough guy and has a tough job, he feels that if he finally talked to his son after all of these years that it would be something so grand for him. It’d definitely be so meaningful to him, and would not fail to bring Patrick back to life (whether or not he’s in his mid-50’s). Tommy, “Patrick, quick, another commercial with Coyle in it!” As he heard the news, Patrick rushed out of the back room and into the bar. The screen showed a promotional commercial for FallOut. For a quick instant, it showed clips of X and clips of Coyle, bringing a tear to his right eye. Patrick, “I know he’ll do well too?” Wiping the tear away nonchalantly, Patrick started his way over to a stool. Shamus, “Hey you, cheer up, he’ll call eventually.” Patrick did nothing but looked down. He explained to them how his only son ended all ties with him, and how disgusted he was over that. Shamus and Tommy tried to comfort him through the process, but it just didn’t help. No one was in the bar yet, it was still morning. To be more exact, the bar had just opened, and these three strongly Boston-accented gentlemen were alone discussing Coyle’s childhood. Then someone walked in, despite the wee hours of the day. It was only a regular, but it did alarm the three guys. Tommy, “I’ll take care of him Patty?” Patrick just nodded his head, and then looked up to Shamus. Trying to fake a good mood, he started talking about football lines, wrestling predictions, and some other sporting events. If it wasn’t Coyle he was thinking about, it was definitely sports that would be on his mind. Shamus, “Call me crazy, but I have an idea. Become a bookie for this match, you know, take all bets for your sons match like the good ol” days.” Patrick, “Eh, I would, but after last weeks loss, I have no idea whether or not he’ll be able to perform with all eyes on him. This is a big event for him anyway.” Shamus, “Well, he’ll most definitely win if you know what I mean.” Patrick paused for a second, then chuckled. He shook his head in a no-motion, and stared at Shamus with a grin. Patrick, “The last thing I would need is for to Coyle absolutely regret being my son. I know how he is, he likes competition, he would rather win through talent than through scare?” Shamus, “If you say so Patty.” Shamus walked off into the back, leaving Patrick alone. A phone rang in the back room, but Patrick didn’t even notice it. Instead, he thought about what it would be like if things became good between him and his son again. He thought about Coyle being a grand champion, and being recognized throughout the world. He knew Coyle wasn’t set to be a mobster, but instead that he was set to be an entertainer. Patrick was so willing to drop everything he had in order to travel with his son, but again, he hasn’t talked to Coyle in years. Perhaps it was from that incident on Christmas of 86, Patrick always thought. Perhaps if Coyle didn’t see his father committing such an unnecessary crime, that Coyle would still be around. If only Tim McCannis didn’t have his way with Patrick’s wife, there would be no reason for such a murder. Shamus came running out of the backroom, and yelled to Patrick. Shamus, “Patty, you’ve got a phone call! You actually?” As he talked, Patrick zoned out. All he heard was that he got a phone call, and it alerted him. He thought, maybe Coyle finally returned a phone call. And maybe they would finally talk. And just maybe, they would become friendly with each other. Perhaps, Coyle would explain what he has done to get prepared to fight against X, or just how he has lived his life since 86 as a whole. It was all uncertain, but it was all something that made Patrick excited. Without letting Shamus finishing what he said, he rushed to the backroom and slammed the door shut. He nervously picked up the phone and answered. Patrick, “Hello?””
