All my pasts remembered Part 1

31 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/31/05 When I was eight years old my life changed forever. I saw a big blonde 300 pound man from Venice Beach, California defeat the hated WWF champion from Tehran, Iran. My dad sitting beside me, laughing as I cheered along with the other 19,000 fans in Madison Square Garden as Hulk Hogan won the title for the first time. From then on I was obsessed. I used my G.I Joe figures to wrestle with, I bought the first WWF action figures that came out (but oddly they had little action as they were solid rubber figures that didn't move), Hulk Hogan of course was the first one I got, then later 'Rowdy' Roddy Piper, Jimmy 'Superfly' Snuka, Andre the Giant and Big John Studd. Later the AWA figures that came out (that had slightly more movement), figures that included The Road Warriors, Baron Von Rashke, Rick Martel, Curt Hennig and Greg Gagne (they came as a set), Michael 'P.S' Hayes and Baby Doll (Hayes came with a partner, but I can't remember who), and the Long Riders. I'd spend hours each day playing with them imagining title changes and matches that would only happen in my fevered imagination. My friends and I would practice wrestling moves on the mats in my friend John's basement. Tossing each other around and jumping off of chairs. Today they call this backyard wrestling. Back then our parents called it a pipe dream. Maybe to the others it was, maybe they sat around and watched the saturday morning WWF show and dreamt about it. Maybe they sat around and watched all the wrestling tapes we could get our hands on and dreamt about it. But I knew that one day, I would live it. Even back then when I perfected my 'Well ya know Mean Gene...' Hogan impersonation, or my 'Macho Man' Randy Savage, 'Ooh yeeeeeeeeah...!' they thought I was just screwing around, but I was prepping for the day I would be cutting promos on some schmuck who harbored a hope of beating me. As a teenager I took to using my dad's weight set and building myself up. Getting into shape and still watching wrestling, but now using the slow motion button on my VCR to break down moves and practice them the best I could. It was hard to do without a partner, but not all my freinds were as willing to risk their bodies anymore as they were afraid of getting hurt. When we were little it was all fun and games, when we got older, our interest turned to other things. Namely things that were starting to sprout breasts and to our teenage minds looked damn good. Fast forward to Walter 'Killer' Kowalski looking over me and the other hopefuls that stood in his school. He walked down the line his careworn and road weary face looking all of us over. We all stood there shoulder to shoulder staring at the wall opposite us, standing stock still like Army recruits on the first day of basic training. And in a way we were in basic training. 'I want you all to know that this is going to hurt.' he said, stopping and then circling around us. 'Every single second of every single day that you are within these walls is going to hurt. If you aren't ready for that. There,' he pointed, 'Is the door. I have no time for bullshit, no time for games, no time for people who are here just for glory. This business is not about glory, it's not about money, it's not about being fancy. It's about working hard, earning respect and proving that you have what it takes to follow in the footsteps of Hockensmidt, Gotch, Gorgeous George...' he stopped in front of me and looked right into my eyes, '...and me.' He moved on and I let out a breath I was unaware I was holding, 'So if you're not willing to make the sacrifice, if you're not ready to give me blood, sweat and tears then get out now.' He paused, his words hung heavy in the air. I stared at a brick on the opposite wall. No one moved, I think most of us had stopped breathing again seeing if anyone was going to suddenly bolt from the withering gaze of this living legend. Not one soul moved except for those of us who remembered to breathe and blink. It was a month before Kowalski let any of us in the ring. The first month consisted of early morning workouts, cardio, weight training, endurace training, afternoon workouts, a half-hour break to work out the kinks and then more working out. Once we hit the ring he taught us how to take a bump, and then from there it was learing how to throw another man around. I remember after taking my first body slam I thought my back was broken. Then I took another, and another, and another, and then I lost count. The suplexes, the piledrivers, the backbreakers. The rope burn was the worst. I had welts on my side for a month. Fast forward again to my first night in the Independent Wrestling Association. I stood in the Gorilla position and the ring announcer announced me and I stepped out to a mixed reaction (mostly boos) to a crowd of maybe 80 people. They didn't like newcomers, but I remembered everything the Killer taught me and my opponent did the job for me, falling down for a cross body off the top rope, and I think a few people actually applauded as I walked back from the ring. Fast forward to a packed arena and me standing behind the curtain, jumping up and down on the balls of my feet, cracking my neck and stretching my arms. Then I heard the ring announcer's voice booming through the arena. 'Ladies and Gentlemen, the following contest is a cage match for the IWO World Heavyweight Championship!' In just a few seconds I would step through the curtain, and into my destiny... (To be continued...)
