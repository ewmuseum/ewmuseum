The Tides of Change [part III]

11 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 11th, 2009 This is ten percent luck, twenty percent skill. Fifteen percent concentrated power of will. Five percent pleasure, fifty percent pain. And one hundred percent reason to remember the name! Fort Minor's "Remember The Name" It is fair to say that the Irishman known as the Tribal War Machine has been undergoing several notable changes in his personality since his return from his latest injury lay-off. The injury he sustained at Rampage on his Global Championship Wrestling debut had set him back by two months, and had killed any hope he had held of making an instant impact. And rightly or wrongly, he has made no secret of the fact that he holds the rookie Englishman, Lucas Quinton responsible for causing the injury. To truly understand the driving force behind this blame being apportioned to Quinton, one must open ones eyes to the larger view of his accuser. Seán William O'Neill, SurReal to you and I, has one real passion in his life, that passion of for the sport of professional wrestling. His early years were dominated by one television slot, each and every Saturday, ITV put out their legendary World of Sport program. World of Sport was perhaps the most diverse and open-minded piece of sporting television ever made. A champion of sports such as such as Women's Hockey, Netball, Lacrosse, Water Skiing and Stock Car Racing. It was also World of Sport that first properly introduced British and Irish audiences to American sports such as NASCAR and Ice Hockey. But if there was one sport that World of Sport was best known for, it was professional wrestling. For the twenty years that World of Sport was broadcast in the United Kingdom its real mainstay was the forty-five minutes of wrestling between four and quarter to five in the afternoon. It single handled thrust wrestling into the public spotlight and made national heroes of its competitors, featuring endless bouts from the now legendary Big Daddy and his greatest rival Giant Haystacks. Whilst wrestling had become immensely popular part of the culture of Great Britain, for Seán O'Neill it was so much more. It had become his passion and frankly, it had become the most important thing in his life. He ate wrestling, drank wrestling, breathed wrestling until nothing else in his existence could compete with it. When World of Sport stopped broadcasting in 1985 it left a void that wasn't addressed until 1991 when professional wrestling returned to terrestrial television in the form of World Championship Wrestling. Now in his late teens and starting to reach the stage of life where you are expected to start marking decisions on what career path you wish to follow, the return to his life of his beloved sport had made the decision for him. He was going to become a professional wrestler, and nothing on this earth would stand in his way. His passion took him to every corner of the globe, initially working for Irish Whip Pro Wrestling and touring his native country with that promotion before getting an opportunity to perform in England. From there he spent years working for every promotion that came knocking. He spent years travelling all across Europe, eventually working in Japan and Mexico before finally getting his chance in professional wrestling's spiritual home in the United States of America. By this stage his eagerness to learn and overwhelming drive to become the best at what he did had built him a solid reputation as one of the best up-and-coming wrestlers working. By the turn of the millennium he was being talked about as a shoe-in for a future hall of fame spot. To O'Neill, this was all due to the way in which he had gone about learning his trade. He had always been respectful, hard working and appreciative of every opportunity, every piece of advice, every story offered up by the veterans he worked with. His willingness to work with anyone and everyone had reaped the benefits of a much wider skills base than the usual training a wrestler would provide. The important part there, is his perception that he 'done it properly'. As the years have gone by, he himself has nurtured several young protégés as he has established himself as a veteran. In those passing years though, he has also witnessed more than a handful of young bucks that burst onto the pro wrestling scene, bounce straight to the top of the card, and almost always burn out and walk away from the sport. Young buck that certainly did not 'do it properly!' You could say, that this has become a personal gripe. But now, more so than before, having been injured at the hands of such a rookie himself, that gripe is starting to take a real hold over him. It is bordering on obsession, and seemingly taking him to a place he has not been in a very long time. He is of sound enough mind to look in the mirror and see the transformation, he knows his own thoughts and their consequences. He realises that he is slowly becoming 'the bad guy' once again, an element of his past he had believed for the longest time that he had eradicated. Hopefully this insight into the man himself provides some enlightenment on his current obsession with rookies. - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - The scene opens on two men sitting opposite each other in a run down bar, in the background a rowdy group of young men are swilling beer and hollering at the large television on the wall. Presumably their NFL team of choice isn't quite living up to their expectations. One of the men at the table glares round at the back of this group, looking more than a little fed-up, turning to his companion he mutters. "I swear these idiots can't keep their mouths shut for 2 god-damn minutes?" His friend smirks, knowing that the pot has once again called the kettle black. "Yeah, 'cause you never get vocal when you're beloved Soccer is on the TV O'Neill?" "That's different..." "Oh yeah, I forgot, soccer is a real sport and NFL is just rugby with shoulder pads?" "Got that right!" Seán O'Neill takes another swig of his beloved Bushmills, Ireland's finest and the world's oldest whiskey. The stress so evident on his face just seconds before evaporates away as he enjoys the taste that reminds him of his home. His friend opposite was once a professional wrestler himself, currently retired after the collapse of the World Wrestling Alliance where he had performed as Komodo Dragon. But for now, they were just Matt Beoulve and Seán O'Neill, two old friends catching up. "So I see your friend Quinton dropped his title on the first defence, you gotta be happy about that, right?" "Matt, did you see Worldwide? You really think that Lucas Quinton is someone I'm concerned about right now?" "O'Neill, every time we've spoken in the last three months, Quinton has been all you've talked about, for a while I was worried at how deep the obsession was going to run. I've seen you this way with people before, and we both know how those scenarios played out. So, I assume that these mind games you've got caught up in are what's on your mind? Funny that, I remember when we first met, it was you who played those sort of games, I guess I never thought I'd see you let them affect you." "It's not the mindgames, Its just... him!" "So you think it was Equinox then?" "How can it NOT be Equinox? You saw everything that son of a bitch put me through when I was fighting for my career. Then last week I get knocked out, hung on a cross with a nice big cryptic message about the past catching up on me and then what-do-ya-know! Lori fucking Equinox just HAPPENS to have been at Worldwide signing a contract. Let me tell you Matt, he thinks its all going to happen over again, well not this time Matt! This time he isn't going up against a man lacking in the confidence of not having competed in five years, he is not going up against a man worried at his ability to still execute moves and holds that seem like distant memories. No way, not this time. I'm fitter than I have been for as far back as I can remember. I am back in control now, and if Lori Equinox thinks he can climb the ladder of Seán O'Neill's weak back once again then he is very much mistaken." SurReal slams his fist hard down to the table, a fiery passion has ignited in his eyes. The confidence, the arrogance, the pride resonating from him with each passing breath. It was true, he had been wrestling again for close to a year now, he knew everything he could and couldn't do. And more importantly, he had rediscovered his drive, his focus, and Lori Equinox would simply be another hurdle to overcome. "I know that brother, I see it in your eyes, and I believe it. I have known you for nine long years, I know what Seán O'Neill in this frame of mind is like, neigh on unstoppable! BUT and there is a but Seán, I have to say it. Lori Equinox is NOT the only man from your past with a grudge to bear!" Signalling the waiter that his whiskey was done and another would be required quickly, O'Neill sighed long and hard before bringing his eyes up to meet his friend's gaze. "I know, but what do you want me to do Matt? Think back over almost two decades to every opponent? Everyone who I might have upset? Don't you see? That is EXACTLY what Equinox wants me to do, if I do that, then I am not concentrating on him, and that is precisely the opportunity he wants. I won't let him dictate my career again Matt. I just won't let him!" As the waiter approaches their table, SurReal takes the whiskey from him, sending the entire contents of the glass down his throat with one swift gulp, he places the glass back on the waiters tray and turns his attention to him. "You'd better bring the bottle!" As the waiter scurries off to comply with his latest request, SurReal turns to face Matt who he finds shaking his head and laughing. "This is going to be a messy night isn't it O'Neill?" The question is met with a wry smile from the Irishman, of course it is!
