This was going to be a lot tougher than she had bargained for. ~~~ July 7th, 2008 Casa del Murray Tallahassee, Florida Being me is absolutely fantastic. It’s the first thing that pops into Andy’s head every morning when that oh-so-irritating radio alarm clock flickers into life at 08:00. He rolls his giant frame from the king-size bed and feels the soft, puffy carpet tickling the soles as he makes the short journey to the bay window to gaze out at another magnificent Floridian morning. He’s right. It is absolutely fantastic being Andy Murray. Things have been coming up roses for the Scottish King of Cool. His stock and reputation as a professional wrestling has never been higher as he continues to throw himself towards the Global Championship Wrestling main event scene, his home life has settled down after a turbulent couple of years, and, most recently, he’s finally bagged the rematch against Chris Bagwell that he’s yearned for since High Stakes. All these factors contribute heavily towards the noticeable spring in his step as he pads across the carpet ? which he and his elder brother Sid and laid themselves ? and heads for the en suite bathroom. His morning ritual takes him fifteen minutes tops, and before you know it he’s washed, dried and shaved and hopping into a pair of bootleg-fit blue jeans and a black t-shirt advertising the logo of some long-forgotten cock rock band from the eighties. No socks though; that carpet just feels so damn good on bare feet. His daily expedition through his far-from-humble abode takes him from his bedroom and down a grand, curved staircase to the grand yet tasteful hallway, from which he takes a left into the great room. Andy has stepped through this door every single morning for almost a quarter of a year and still the brilliance of this room left him awestruck. It was this kitchen/lounge hybrid that sold him on the house in the first place; large, open plan and extravagantly furnished, it looks every bit as awesome as you’d expect the living room of a man of Andy Murray’s wealth to look. And then some. Plus, it kinda reminds the Scotsman of Tony Soprano’s house, and James Gandolfini, Andy thinks, is the ultimate badass. His first reaction, as it is just about everyone he’s home, is to grab one of four remote controls from the glass-top coffee table. Blindly, he points the remote in the general direction of a partially-concealed hi-fi system, and soon enough Lynyrd Skynyrd’s Second Helping album is pumping from the speakers. One of the sweeteners of being the sole owner of a home, Andy thinks, is that you can play classic rock at eight thirty in the morning and not really disturb anyone. Sure, he might wake Cayle up, but that bum could probably do with a wake-up call anyway. “Morning handsome,” Kayla beams from the other side of the room, catching Andy off-guard. Surprised that he didn’t see her when he first walked in, Andy turns round as his partner places two plates of breakfast down on the dining table. The Scottish King of Cool points the remote at the CD player again and lowers the volume to “background music” level. “Good morning sugar,” he says as he aimlessly tosses the remote onto the surface of one of his black leather sofas. Andy watches her retreat back to the kitchen and ponders how he could just sit and admire that physique for hours on end. It has been almost a year since they’ve been together and he still finds her every bit as attractive as he did when they first met. The near-skin tight jeans black tank top that adorn her curves fill his head with the kind of dirty thoughts that most men usually abandon as they exit their teenage years as she saunters back across with two glasses of fresh orange juice. “Jesus, Kay” are you trying to kill me?” he asks with a smile as he examines the high-cholesterol foodstuffs that populate the plate before him. Sausages, back, fried egg, hash browns? not exactly the lifesblood of a professional athlete. “If you don’t wan” it I can easily pile it up in Winston’s bowl,” responded Kayla, referring to the chocolate brown daschund puppy that they shared their home with. “I jest, I jest. Since when have I been one to complain about a free breakfast?” “Good boy.” Kayla playfully patted Andy on the top of his head before sitting herself down. “What’re the Scottish King’s plans for the day, then?” she enquired before slipping a forkload of egg and bacon between her lips. “I dunno. Usual I guess; work out for a couple of hours, plot the demise of Chris Bagwell” you know, all kinds of ill-shit. Figure I should probably head over to the gym too, set about starting work on that rudeboy.” “How many times do I have to tell you” You can’t call an inanimate object a “rudeboy”; it doesn’t make sense!” “Can it, wench. Eat your bacon.” That line earned Andy a teasing slap on the arm. These kind of moments are exactly what the Scotsman longs for during his time on the road. You learn the value of everyday small-talk and time with your loved ones when you typically spend three or four days of every week as a professional drifterwrestler. No amount of cheering fans or big match victories can ever compare to a simple chat over breakfast with the girl of your dreams. It doesn’t take long for Andy to clean his plate; a single plate of food is nothing when you stand at 6’7? and weigh 275lbs. After cleansing his palette with a final sharp burst of citrus, Andy wanders over to the kitchen and places his dishes into the dishwasher. He then makes his way across to the lounge, and turns the morning news on after killing the music. Almost as soon as he plonks himself down on the outrageously comfortable couch a cool, wet feeling emanates from his right foot. “Ahhh!” Murray recoils as Winston laps his tiny tongue against Andy’s ankle. “What’s wrong?” Kayla enquires from the dining table. “Nothin”. Just the wolf,” he says as he reaches downwards and scoops the puppy up in his arms. Kayla giggles, still unable to get over Andy’s horrendously inappropriate choice of nickname for the dog. Wagging his tail enthusiastically, Winston tries his best to climb up the Scottish King’s gargantuan torso, but finds it too great a mountain to scale. Smiling, Andy gently rubs his hand against the pet’s head. “This is the life,” he mumbles as the day’s stories completely pass him by. ~~~ July 7th, 2008 Starbucks Coffee Shop Chicago, Illinois She has been trying for a couple of days now and she’s hardly made a dent in her list. Even Jane was amazed by the amount of contacts in the business that she has amounted over the years. One of the many benefits of being wife to one of the wrestling industry’s most charismatic competitors. Well, ex-wife. She sighs as she pulls a small, black notebook from the expensive leather handbag that Chris Bagwell had bought for her just a couple of days ago. With a well-used pencil she scores another name ? James Rande ? from the list, before sizing up her options. Jane thought that it would have been a lot easier than this, but dealing with men and women actively involved in the “sport” of pro-wrestling has never been the most enjoyable of his tasks. How could any of these vultures afford to turn something like this down? Jane wonders as she sits sipping on a vanilla latte. She has a point; she’s been dealing with agents, public image consultants, wrestlers, managers and journalists all morning, none of them are exactly the most noble professions in the world. Most of them just had too much respect for Andy to carry out the tasks that she had been asking of them. In a career spanning over a decade, the Scotsman has developed a reputation as being the consummate professional behind the scenes. While he may not be the most technically gifted of in-ring performers, Andy’s attitude has never been anything short of sublime. Of course, it is completely impossible to go through life without ruffling a few feathers and bending a few noses out of place. Andy Murray is no exception, but even still Jane was having trouble convincing them to aid her. Trevor Kent, a hated foe with whom Andy has shared many bloody battles, has long retired and has seemingly vanished into thin air. Robert Boughton, a man who found his career cut short in 2000 courtesy of a botched Murray powerbomb (thus giving him every right to want to bury the Scotsman), had absolutely no interest whatsoever. Even Don Cochran, internet smark, radio show host and one of Andy Murray’s fiercest critics, wasn’t interest in jeopardizing his own career by promoting something of this magnitude. She scores out a lot of the names without even calling them; heck, if a guy who Andy had left paralyzed during his rookie years didn’t want to bring the man down, then why on earth would a lowly internet wrestling journalist want to? All her options seem to be running out. However, one name sticks out from the sheet of paper like a sore thumb. Jane grabs her expensive Samsung phone from the table, dials in a number, and holds it up to her ear. Within a few moments the call is answered. “Rolash.” “Ummm, Mike?” “Speaking. Who’s this?” “It’s Jane. Jane Murray” you might not remember me, I used to be married to Andy?” Jane can almost see Mike Rolash sneering at the other end of the phone at the mention of Andy’s name. “I’ve got a proposition for you, and something tells me you might just be interested?” ~~~ July 7th, 2008 2056 West Pensacola Street Tallahassee, Florida “Shit, man, never knew this place was so big. Sure doesn’t look it from the outside,” Sid Murray says as he gazes around the interior of his brother’s newly-purchased $80,000 gym hall for the first time. It’s not exactly massive, but if Andy plans to follow through on his wrestling academy idea then it will be more than fitting. Clearly it’s been at least a couple of years since anyone’s used this place; the paint on the walls has started to crack, a thick layer of dust lines just about everything, and the windows are absolutely filthy. Natural light peeks in through the tall, old-fashioned windows illuminating a wooden floor that was probably nice and shiny at some point in time. At least enough room for a couple “a rings in here, thinks Sid as he looks around the place. “Tell me about it,” grins Andy. “This is just the main hall; there’s some changing rooms, showers, a couple of offices, everything I need to get this badboy up and running. Thing is, as you can no doubt see, there’s a lot of work to be done.” Andy’s right. If he were to just plonk down a wrestling ring and a couple of benches in this place in its current state he’d soon find Health and Safety on his ass. “Hell, I’m off the road for the next couple of months anyway,” says Sidney, himself a seventeen year veteran of the ring. “Need an extra set of hands?” Andy smiles. “I was hoping you’d offer.” “Cool,” Sidney nods before pulling a Marlboro from underneath the red bandana that covered his shaggy dirty blonde hair. “Mind if I light one up?” Andy parts his lips to respond but Sid already has the damn thing lit before he can say anything. Instead, he opts to change the topic of conversation. “So I was thinking, when I get this place up and running, I’m not always going to be around to keep things running smoothly, what with GCW picking up and all.” Sid raises a curious eyebrow. “Go on?” “So I’m not always gonna be around to help the kids out. I need an extra set of hands?” “Sounds a lot like yer trying to offer me a job here.” The Scottish King of Cool laughs. “You know me too well, bro. Assistant Head Trainer, or something equally important sounding.” The elder Murray brother merely nods, never one to show too much emotion. Andy can tell from the glimmer in his eye that the prospect has got him excited through. “Sure, why not?” Typical Sid response, Andy thinks. Suddenly the youngest of the Murray brothers, Cayle, bursts from the changing room area gleefully. Having already been offered the chance to help Andy get the place back into shape, Cayle was clearly looking forward to getting up a rung on the professional wrestling ladder. Renovating a gym? not exactly headlining a pay-per-view, but after two stints behind bars and a disastrous FUSE career Cayle Murray is just happy to be involved in the sport again, no matter how menial the task may be. “Shit-fucking-hot, Andy!” the long-haired twenty-five year old beams as he bounds back towards his seniors. “So, what do we get started?” “Damn, Andy, you never said Pipsqueak was gettin” in on this action, might not’ve agreed if I’d known that,” Sid jokes with a wry smile. “Fuck off, old man!” The Scottish King of Cool can’t help but hide the shit-eating Cheshire Cat grin that etches it’s way across his features. At last the Murray brothers were united again. The last twelve months had been a struggle; with Cayle’s prison time, Sid’s intense schedule as an independent wrestler and Andy’s divorce proceedings with Jane, the relationship between these three brothers had never been more distant. Now, however, things couldn’t be better. The gym had become much more than an opportunity to help bring through a new breed in sports entertainers; it had become a vehicle for the reuniting of the Murray clan. And Andy, the self-appointed patriarch, couldn’t be happier. ~~~ July 7th, 2008 The Office of Mike Rolash Chicago, Illinois “In you come, in you come,” says Mike Rolash, repeating himself as he ushers the sharply-dressed Jane Murray through his office door. “I’m gonna have to keep this pretty quicky, lotta shit to get done today. Please, take a seat.” He points towards the chair at the other side of his desk without carrying out the standard gentlemanly task of pulling it out for her. There are a few things you need to know about Mike Rolash. 1. His dress sense is better than yours, something that is proved today by the impeccable pinstripe suit that he wears, carefully tailored to fit every counter of his body. 2. In the sports media industry, he is the lowest of the low. A wrestling journalist with mainstream exposure and no qualms about getting his hands dirty, Mike Rolash has built a career out of building up superstars and tearing them down again. The list of names that this man has buried through creative reporting reads like War and Peace. Put it this way, if Mike Rolash wants to destroy your reputation as a professional athlete, you might as well save him the trouble and retire already. 3. He fucking hates Andy Murray. He actually worked for the Scotsman back in 2002 as part of the short-lived Championship Wrestling Federation promotion. As the majority shareholder in the Pennsylvania-based federation, Andy fired long-serving colour commentator Mike Rolash in a cost-cutting measure. A venomous argument exploded between the two that soon escalated into violence. Since moving into mass media, Rolash had always been looking for a reason to destroy Andy Murray. He’d just never had the opportunity. Until now. “What have you got for me then, Jane?” quizzes Rolash as he sits down behind his desk. “I know you and Andy never got along,” begins Jane, choosing to skip the bullshit and cut to the chase. I’ve got enough ammunition to put my ex-husband away for good. With you’re help, we could end him?” Raising an interested eyebrow, Rolash leans forward in his chair. “Sounds like we might be able to do business here.” “Make no mistake, Mike, I want my ex-husband to drown.
