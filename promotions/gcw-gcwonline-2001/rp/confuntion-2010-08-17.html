Confuntion

17 August 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/17/10 Wrestling careers can hit rock bottom in a variety of new and exciting ways. Some of them are a rapid spiral, others a slow decline. Some have surprising peaks; some have horrendous valleys (those ones generally involve a dead hooker found inside a cake). Despite the amount of wrestlers who have hit rock bottom, you see very little retirement from the industry. There’s nothing sadder than a run-down veteran wrestling for hot dogs in attempt to approve to the capacity crowd of Little Melisa’s Princess Party that he’s still got it. Actually there may be one thing sadder than that… WrestleContacular 2010 was in full swing. Well, as full swing as you can get for a rundown convention centre with around eight booths, one of which dealt exclusively in marketing wrestling theme porn and a crowd reaching the dizzying heights of around 200 people (most of them unwilling parents and partners dragged along). Wedged between said WrestlePorn booth and one that dealt in selling microwaved damaged wrestling figures from the 80s sat Phil Atken and Dirk Dickwood under a banner heralding them as “Wrestling Superstars”. Atken: A “Wrestling Superstar” banner? Really? You really thought “Hey, I know what’s a good idea, let’s copy that banner that’s now an internet meme for wrestling failure, that’s the ticket!” Dickwood: Nah, I just found it in the dumpster outside and thought we could put it to use. Atken: Great, I’m sitting at a wrestling convention with the scum of the earth under a dumpster banner. My career truly now feels revitalised. Dickwood: Don’t worry, once things get going that frown will get turned upside down. This Phil used all the restraint that a human could possibly ever have to avoid punching Dirk directly into the face after the Dirk spat out said cliché. He also wondered how a convention such as this could ever be deemed a party by anyone with an intellect above that a newly born baby dropped promptly on its head. Atken: Do we have to do this? I thought it very clear that I detest poor people. Dickwood: These people have money. That’s why they’re here. Atken: I thought I made it very clear I detest people. Dickwood: Look, GCW told me that our serves weren’t required this week and I thought we could make a bit of extra money doing the whole convention thing. Atken: Ah but you forgot one important thing. Conventions are soul destroying. Dickwood: Do I even need to say it? Atken: An implication that I don’t have a soul, a true nugget of comedy gold there. Before Dirk and Phil could enter into one of those hilarious exchange of words that everybody loved them for, a man in his mid to late 20s approached the booth. He was wearing a fedora which should indicate just how douche-like this human being was. The “ironically” racist t-shirt just being the feather in the fedora to prove such a thing. Let’s call him Guy. Guy: Hi there! Dickwood: Hello friend, that sure is a swell t-shirt you are wearing! Guy: Oh yah, I’m not really racist or anything, it’s y’know to break down barriers and mock the real racists y’know. Atken: No I don’t know, what the hell are you talking about? He can you be ironically racist? If I punch a woman in the face and then giggle about it am I being ironically misogynistic? Dickwood: Ignore Phil, he gets cranky if he doesn’t get his five o’clock whisky. How can we help you on this fine day? Phil turned away from Dirk, muttering under his breath. Atken: Are you kidding me? But let’s return to conversation A! Guy: Yeah, like, I thought I knew you guys from somewhere and thought that I’d y’know come up and chat and try to figure it out. Atken: It’s a wrestling convention, WHERE COULD YOU POSSIBLY KNOW US FROM? Guy: Oh I know that but I thought that you guys, y’know you guys had been on television before or something. Don’t worry it’ll come to me. Dickwood: Well we… Guy: Oh I know! Yeah, you’re that jobber guy from GCW right? I mean I love jobbers, I think they’re incredibly underappreciated, y’know. Guys like you, Barry Horowitz, that fat guy who fell down a lot... Dickwood: I think that guy had a heart attack mid-match. Atken(once again mumbling in a passive aggressive style): That’s what you choose you take issue with? Guy: That’s sad, a real bummer. Anyway, what are you guys doing here? Yes, he did switch topics from the tragic in ring death of a wrestling that quickly. Dickwood: Meeting fans, signing pictures, that sort of things. Guy: Oh cool, cool. Guy continued to hover around the booth causing mild discomfort to Dirk and general irritation to Phil. After a few moments of intense awkward silence, Dirk finally spoke up. Dickwood: So can I help you with anything? Guy: Oh yeah, um, a picture? Dickwood: Pictures are twenty dollars. Guy: Sure, sure, no problem. As Guy handed over his hard earned twenty dollars, Phil grabbed a polaroid and began scribbling on it. He tossed the polaroid to guy, going out of his way to actively avoid eye contact. Guy: This is a picture of a potted plant. Dickwood: A signed picture of a potted plant. Atken: I’m heavily into photography at the moment. Guy: You guys ripped me off! I want my money back! Dickwood: I told you what you were buying, I can’t see any reason to give you a refund, buyer’s remorse isn’t a justification. Guy: You told me the picture would be signed. Dickwood: It is signed. Guy: By a “Butts McDong”. Atken: Yeah, that’s the ironic name I go by in my photography career. Now piss off! Guy: I’ll be back. Atken: I won’t. As Guy stormed off Phil began work on taking down the “Wrestling Superstar” banner. Dickwood: Stop that, come on, that was one guy. It’ll get better I promise. Atken: I’m not going to sit here and smile while people piss on my career. I’m not going to sit here and let an endless line of idiotic hipster assholes call me a jobber. This kind of thing is for people at the end of their career. Dickwood: Well if we’re honest, you may very well be at the end of your career. Atken: Oh fuck off. I’m having a little lull that’s all. It’s easily fixed and I know how to go about fixing it. Dickwood: Three years is considered “little” now? Face it Phil, changes have to be made and you know deep down you can’t trust yourself to make those changes yourself - you need someone else to do that for you. Atken: But… Dickwood: And maybe, just maybe, you should start taking your career seriously again. You already how much of a locker room joke you are, I don’t need to go around repeating it. Those stupid comeback speeches of yours, your worthless schemes, they don’t matter if you can’t prove it in the ring. Atken: Maybe if you done your job a little… Dickwood: But that’s exactly the problem. I’ve excelled at my job, I’ve managed to get you title shot after title shot and you’ve lost them all. Atken: In close cont… Dickwood: It doesn’t matter - you still lost each and every one of them. Atken: Blowing up at me isn’t going… Dickwood: But I’m not blowing up at you, I’m trying to get you to realise that right now, backstage, you’re viewed as a joke. You’re a commentary punchline. The reason we’re at this fucking convention is because of how little you are thought of with GCW management. Seriously, would ANY other GCW talent be caught dead in the place like this? No! But this is the best I could do for you. I’m serious, this is the best booking I could manage and I tried, god knows I tried. Atken: Are you done? Dickwood: Sorry, I think we both know that it needed to be said. Atken: I don’t disagree that something needs to be done. I’m too young for the convention circuit. Dickwood: Well what do you propose. Atken: Think you can manage to negotiate a match at Worldwide for me? Dickwood: I can try. Atken: Then I propose we take this backstage joke issue and tackle it head on. Dickwood: You mean… Atken: Get me the instigator. Get me Bagwell.
