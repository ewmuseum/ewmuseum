House of Genesis: Part 8

14 February 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 14th, 2008 In as many times as he has gone to the gym, he had gotten into the rhythm of thinking it would never change. But all things changed, a shocking reality that caught Scott Drake off guard as he laid on his back and looked up at the lights in the gym. It took fifty-three tries but his student, Cleveland Paige, had finally managed to stop him from easily taking Paige off his feet. That was the deal. He had to stop Scott from catching him with a Takedown. If he could do that, Scott would train him. He did; which meant that now Scott had to train him. It was a reality that Drake had not prepared his self for. Now he had to face the music; he had to tell Tessa. For hours he kicked around what to say in his head. He could try explaining it rationally to her, saying that he never expected to him to get past the test. He could tell her that this would be good, because he wanted to retire down the road and have someone to carry on after him. He agreed on one thing though; no matter what he told her, she wouldn’t be happy. “Tessa” he said as he rubbed his foot on their tile floor. Scott looked around the kitchen for anything to keep his eyes on, just so he would not have to have his eyes locked with hers. He didn’t need to look at her. He could already pick the looks she would be giving him in his head. “Tessa” he again said, trying to get her attention as she sat at the kitchen table. Tessa turned toward him, her head stumping in her palm. “Hey, I wanted to talk to you about the training I’ve been doing with the kid.” “You finally giving it up?” she asked bluntly. So bluntly in fact, that it caught Scott Drake completely off guard. Scott had to stop and recollect his thoughts, having completely forgotten what he was going to say. The expression he had almost brought a smile to her face, but her lips refused to curl. Blinking twice to shake off his shock, Scott replied back to her “no”. He paused just long enough to watch her response. “Actually, it’s just the opposite.” As Scott expected, Tessa let out a sigh and rolled her eyes upon hearing the news. “You know, you never should have done this, I don’t know why you even bothered.” Scott wasn’t in the mood to hear her nag on him about the decisions he made. He had heard that song and dance many times from her. That still did not stop her from doing it anyways. “I swear, Scott” she said with the tone that let him know he would be here for a while. “If this becomes another one of those times where this business becomes more important than your family, well” she had to pause as she thought of a good enough threat to throw at him. “Well I don’t know what I will do. But I am certain you won’t like it one bit.” “Look” he begged, hoping she would pause for long enough during her rant so that he could get a word in. “Let’s not forget why I’m even doing this in the first place.” Tessa just rolled her eyes. “Yes, so you can retire with an apprentice to take over your legacy as the blah, blah, blah, blah.” She looked him dead in the face, “but you fail to look like you are retiring anytime soon.” Scott had a perfectly good reason for that. At least it sounded perfectly good in his head. “Well that’s because I haven’t started his training yet.” It took all her will-power to prevent herself from choking on her reaction. “WHAT?” she yelled out, “then what the hell do you call all this crap you’ve been doing?” “I’ve been testing him” Scott said, clearly not knowing when to shut his mouth. “I can’t just train any wrestler. I want someone who has all the right tools.” Tessa looked like she wanted to choke the very life out of him. “Testing him?” She didn’t want to believe him and she didn’t want to kill him, so she did the only thing she could think of. She went off to her bedroom to allow herself some time to cool off. At this point, having watched her leave, Scott Drake had to decide what would be more important. Should he hurry off to the gym to make it in time to train Cleveland Paige and allow Tessa to calm down, or should he check in on her to try and smooth things out and possibly be hit with a vase or worst. He just hoped there wasn’t a vase in the room. Taking a deep breath, Scott nudged his shoulder against the door and walked into his room. He had made it one step into the room without anything being lunged at his head, which was a good sign. “Tessa?” he called out, trying to look around the room for any sign of where she was. Spotting a rather large lump under the bed sheets, Scott headed toward the bed and sat down on the corner. “Hey” he said as he tried to get her to sit up. “you ok?” Tessa only shrugged her shoulder under the blanket to let him know she didn’t want to talk. Scott didn’t leave though, but he did stop rubbing her. “Look, I just want to talk, ok?” Finally she popped up from the blanket, though looking plenty annoyed. “What Scott” What do you want to talk about that we didn’t already cover?” He was taken aback by her snappiness, though he can’t say he was surprised by it. “I just wanted” he stopped, trying to think of just what he wanted to say, “I’m sorry.” She just rolled over in the bed, pulling the covers up higher on her. “If you were sorry you wouldn’t be doing this” she shot back sharply, letting him feel the venomous poison dripping from her words. Scott couldn’t say anything back to her, not when he knew it wouldn’t do any good. He started to wonder why he even bothered coming in to see her. He hadn’t accomplished anything he had set out to do and now he would be late for the gym. If this wasn’t the start of a bad day, he didn’t know what was. “Just get out” he heard mumbled from under the covers. “What?” “Just get out!” It came through loud and clear that time as Tessa threw the covers off as she yelled. Just as quick as they were off, she pulled them right back on to cover herself. Scott knew it was over with, that any further discussion would be met with the same result. Rather than continuing to argue with her, Scott Drake headed back out of the room as quietly as he could. Waiting for him right outside the room was Tessa’s adopted daughter, Christa. With her hands on her hips, it was obvious she wasn’t happy about something. Scott had a feeling he knew what it was. “Fighting again?” He looked around; as if he was making sure she had been talking to him. “Fighting?” he said, now looking at her, “no, of course not. We were just having a disagreement.” He smiled, acting as if everything was actually just fine between him and Tessa, even though he had a feeling she knew otherwise. The thirteen year-old twisted a lock of her hair with a finger, “you know, I’m not that young” she told him sarcastically, “I know what a fight sounds like.” Scott couldn’t help but laugh at the teen despite her assessment of the situation. “I know” he assured her, “Tessa just doesn’t like me spending so much time in the gym.” He stopped and thought about it for a moment, making sure he added “especially when I’m training the kid.” In her infinite teenage wisdom, Christa asked Scott “well if Tessa doesn’t like it, then why do you do it?” Stopped in a moment of shock, Scott had never expected that kind of question. Truth be told, he himself had never given that question much thought. It caught him off guard and left him searching for words to respond. There was just something about the look in her eyes that made it sink deep inside. Was it really that simple? Scott looked at her, his heart suddenly sinking, “do you think I am a bad dad?” It didn’t seem like a fair question to ask of a thirteen year-old girl. To assess his progress as a father despite only being in her life for the past few years? She could still remember her actual father, despite how many years it had been since he had passed away. “You don’t need to worry about that” she told him, sounding more like the parent than the child. “Tessa’s been feeling sick lately, so maybe she’s just taking it out on you.” Looking back at the door to the bedroom, Scott rubbed his chin. Could it just be because she’s sick? Why didn’t she say anything? Christa must have been able to sense that he was planning to go back into the room because she cut him off before he could. “Don’t worry about her, I’ll bring some soup for her later.” A smile cracked his worried face. Despite her age, Christa seemed like the most mature in the house at times. She certainly wasn’t his kid that was for sure. “I’ll leave you kids some money to order a pizza” he said as he reached into his wallet. “And I’ll be back soon, I won’t stay out long.” With that, he walked out of the door and again out of their lives. Each time, they only hoped he came back to them, for they feared that one time that he might not.
