Stuck inside of nowhere with the region-free blues again.

Date: Week of February the 10th He watched the embers fly. That was that: orange twitched on the highway far behind, and Frank was done with smoking. Again. Nicotine just wasn't his scene’again. He was losing the dry hack when he went to sleep, the wet hack when he woke up; most important, he was losing eternal stage business. He needed something to do while. While everything, really. When he was listening or when he was talking or when he was driving or when he was just killing time, letting the hours die around him. Just something to keep busy while the world kept momentum. Actors figured it out; he could too and he let it slide like it wasn't even a decision. A natural reaction, expected even. He took an exit ramp, slipped lower behind the rim of the dashboard and shot long headlamp stares up the hill. "Fuck it." He lit one more’didn't call it his last’and pulled away from the stop sign with his creep move potent and his radio low. On the freeway, back to the interstate even, it was only him and trash and speed limit signs’the chicken truck he'd spotted behind him was already under the bridge and gone, lost somewhere behind trees. He could think of, could imagine things worse than driving alien roads on empty nights; he just didn't, he just kept his head full of tissue and bone and distracted by rolling the stereo back and forth through the stations with no qarter sought. The returns were predictable. He stopped it at the first beat that didn't piss him off, turned the bass up and the volume down, and drove. He wouldn't have blamed Erika if asked about it; he wouldn't have mentioned her by name at least. "Some chick," maybe "this chick" depending on where his head sat; would've chosen "complicated" as the verb instead of "fucked up." Minor things. Conscious decisions, though. He'd catch them before they slipped out and try to explain the why later. All hypothetical, still. No one was going to ask Frank what he was doing, was even less likely that they'd ask why. He was well isolated; compartmentalized in two tons of borrowed steel and hurtling headlong down barren stretches of nothing. Sage, too. He'd have wanted to blame him too, but kept qiet’it wasn't anything, it was nothing. And Frank rehearsed as he drove, to make sure he was properly prepared for any other nothings. It wasn't his car, anyways; wasn't his stereo, weren't his CDs, weren't his ashes in the tray. He understood who he owed, understood that he didn't get to avoid responsibility just because his things never felt like his things. Autopilot was heavy around his neck; he caught himself wondering what "borrowed time" really meant, what would happen after everything settled down and he still had to live the rest of his life. He needed a bar like he needed oxygen, like he didn't need to think about what could happen. It bogged his head down Wrestling, or Rollins, or something in that mix, was too much. He gritted his teeth. The line had been bleeding, wouldn't tourniqet no matter how much he'd spewed his agenda, no matter how much he believed in the division. Everything in his new life lead back to one central point; he always hit the same hard earth when he tried to dig for fire. But it was: fuck it, defiant, and maybe he didn't even want answers and what would the world think of that, then? The idea of an outburst more than an explosion itself; he stayed primal, aimed for a light in the dark and drove with the occasional amble of traffic passed or passing or running away from where he was headed. It bled into his packed skull and took no purchase, melted away forgotten as the next in line replaced it. They were all lights. Less than people, less than cars. He found somewhere to be, a town or a city’he couldn't find out without more investigation than he really needed. He hadn't spotted the sign that named it, at any rate, and just turned off at the logo of a halfkitsch steakhouse franchise joint that he'd have trusted more than a dimly lit dive with a gravel lot. He swung the company BMW into a spot and checked himself in the rearview: he looked rough enough. He'd taken to accepting his face when they'd smear him with makeup to cut the glare of the lighting, took a growing second of resentment every time he had to remember who he was in flesh and not pixels. He fixed a knit cap down to his eyebrows and swiveled out and up from the car, trying to keep his hoodie from knotting the shirts underneath. He unzipped it and left it open, pushed inside real doors and the saloon doors just inside them and cut around the main counter to head straight to the drinks. Everything wooden and polished, an orange glow in a brown world. The restaurant wasn't crowded, halfpacked or just pushing it; the bar was swarmed. He hung back, posted up against a halfwall and waited for a spot to clear out. Frank hung his head and pulled the cap over his ears. "Aint ready t haul these assholes out yet." He'd made moves to avoid the town's dirty drunks, and maybe they'd workedt: there was no immediate comparison, so he damned one end the bar into a single beast, a shitfaced entourage that stemmed hydra out from one base mind. He counted five heads; a woman on the opposite half got up and left with a bag of food and Frank replaced her. A one man buffer zone between Frank and the howling nodded, went back to his beer. It didn't feel like recognition. He stared past the bar, past the rainbowed rows of gimmick liqors in front of him; watched his reflection in the mirrored rear wall and hunted out the creases and the curves, the scar here or the first dent of a wrinkle beside. Nothing surprised him but, even then, he struggled to find familiarity, to see himself in those dim green eyes’and in struggling found none, saw nothing. The public would never recognize him; he couldn't recognize himself. "What you want?" He looked to her, blonde and as psuedobeautiful as any bartender he'd ever known. "Rum and Coke." "What brand?" "Uh." He scanned the drinks instead of himself and shrugged. "Whatever." She turned, asked the man beside him if he wanted another draught and took his mug when he nodded. There was a rattish qality to the man that seemed to radiate from his eyes: the way they just barely bulged, or how he darted them paranoid when there was nothing to rest them on. "I been here an hour. Since bout nine. Hour and a half’they been drinkin for six hours." And something that ended with "fucked up." Frank laughed. "Shit." "Yeah." He kept his voice low, almost static against the din of the building. "Buncha drunks." Nearest stood, disappeared to Frank's left. "Thought Id have to fuck him up." "Hmh?" "Too many of em, though. Shit. Thought he was gonna try to kick my ass for my hat." Football team; college level and not even big enough that Frank knew it as a name. He imagined rivals, let the man go on. "Sa drunk bastard, though." "Yeah." "Not as think as you drunk I am’even, I swear to drunk, Im not God." The man in the hat, it was, the central snarl of the hydra, exploded at his own joke, let his laughter go a mile above his friends'. It drowned out the rat faced man. He rolled his eyes and set to fixing his jacket. Frank watched the TVs mounted above the bottles, old box sets running news and ads for pay-per-views. He caught a few shots of himself somewhere in a minute long GCW spot between the boxing match and some romantic comedy. He laughed. He drank. He ignored the old man and his wandering glare, once he'd made it back to the bar. He cut off for what felt like decades at a time, only to be drawn back in perpendicular to the grain. "Just talkin about breasts." "I dont wanna hear about em." "Youre gonna have to take my word for this, whole titty bar and you couldnt sqeeze together one breastess out of all of em together." "I didnt wanna know that." "Aw, I bet you got nice ones." "I think youve had too much to drink." She threw it at him without looking over from the sqat glasses she stacked. Frank watched out of the corner of his eyes, peripheral blurred by the rodent that had taken him on as a drinking buddy. "Dont tell me that, baby. Im drivin the bus. Now, Ray? Hes had too much to drink. Gonna have to put him in the truck bed, haw." "Fuck you too." "Nothin personal, Ray." So he dedicated himself to the news and ordering another drink; glass to the counter. The man beside him left in the same direction Ray had. Frank kept his voice low on the bartender’s approach. "You always gotta feel like a sellout to hold a steady job?" "Grin and bear it. You want another?" "Yeah, Yuengling on the draught." She set him up, a cold mug on a coaster. He kept watching ads; Ray kept staring. Frank had taken to halfreturning, to trying to affirm whatever the man's curiosity was. It was a concession, he'd decided; every bar has someone that's going to stare at you. So be it. He kept drinking. Through the beer, onto another rum and coke: he watched her pour Bacardi at her own discretion, wondered what "whatever" legally obligated him to pay for. The drink washed it from his head, the GCW promo reminded him that it was never a concern. He'd just forgotten himself. Nothing major. "I dont care if you look. I cant control your eyes." "Wish Ida known that few hours ago." "What, now youre seein two of me?" "Uh huh. And they both look good." Her face went stoic as mountains: "Last call." "I. Fuck with your soul like ether. Will. Teach you the king you know you. Not. Gods Son across the belly. Lose. I prove you lost already." And he glanced at the name, answered his phone after the first repeat. "Yo, Sage. Gimme a minute’loud as fuck out here." He pushed away from his stool and cut to the side, nodded to his neighbor on the return, and headed through until he found a spot in the corner of the bathroom. "Aight then. Whats good?" "Absofuckinglutely nothing. Theyre killing the Red Light district, and shrooms are going illegal within the year. Fuckers. Fucking asses." "What?" "Amsterdam, man. Conservatives took control and now theyre turning the city into, into. Fucking Zeus, Frank. Theyre turning it into every other worthless little European city with nothing to offer. Theyre killing it." "That sucks. But, uh, why you hittin me up about it? Not like I got a vote on the matter or anythin." "Im letting you know. Im gonna be there for. For awhile. Thought you might want to swing by if you get the time’everyone needs to see this place before they Las Vegas or Times Sqare it up. Fuckers dont realize how many people come just for the grime. Thats the kicker, man. I think theyre doing it for tourism." "Fuck do people go there for if it aint bud, hookers, and shrooms?" "Nothing. Its not a bad place, but its got its niche. Some, some assholes just took over the government and decided they didnt like it. I am, Im trying to be rational about this, though, so. Dont ask me anymore qestions about how bad these cocksuckers are fucking up one of the few nice places on the planet. Just’give me a call sometime. Ill get you tickets sent over, all that bullshit. You just have to come before they sanitize it." "Thats word up." "Fuck you up to, anyways?" "Drinkin." "Drinking, like, youre getting smashed and missing your flight tomorrow, or drinking like, “this is how I kill time??" "Second one." "Good call. Erika with you?" "No." "Dont sound too happy about it." "Nah. Im not." "Well. Ever happened with Ash?" "Hit her up, told her to fuck off. She fucked off." "Easy enough." "Yeah. Sage, can I ask you somethin?" "Fuck it, sure." "Aight. You, uh. You give a fuck about the Establishment?" And his voice dropped, and his pace slowed. "In what sense?" "The sense that you either give a fuck about em or you dont." "Give a fuck about everything, Frank. How much of a fuck do I give, though.” "Yeah. How much you give a fuck?" "Theyre there, kid. Right now? Barely even got that much. Its not an army now, its more of an institution. More of a museum. Heh. But Caldera is. Calderas a hell of a stone cold motherfucker. Ill put it to you like this: Valentine isnt standing between you and the title, and Jay Terror sure as fuck isnt. The only possible hurdle is the Establishment’even if theyre laying low and looking weak. Caldera isnt going to want you to succeed without him if you couldnt do it with him. Dig?" "I get you, yeah. But youre talkin about me, and not you." "The product reflects on me. Theyre fucking wrestlers, Frank: they cant touch me. Personally, they dont worry me at all, ever. Why would they?" Frank breathed. "I care about them as it relates to people I employ. Your threats are my threats; they just never actually threaten me." "Thats big of you." "And thats sarcastic as fuck of you, Frank. Doesnt matter: I want this clear, I dont want you bugging me with that shit again. The Establishment, hell; everything in GCW is up to your final discretion. Its not my place. You are, in regards to JXW, the ultimate decision maker for GCW. We dont qestion you. We dont argue with you. Its your war. You tell us how you need to fight it and we will do the best we can to support you’but Im not going to let you throw this shit back at me. You wanted free reign, man. You fucking have it. Why are you asking me?" "Its a lot of shit to deal with." "No, its not. Its a lot of shit to deal with in relation to how much time youre wasting not dealing with it. I can call you on this if you want’I just, I dont want to. Id much rather see how you work it. I dont want hostilities." "Aint gonna be any, taken or given. You got the experience here, just wanted to see your side of shit." "Fuck experience. I never had talent’so, alright. Youve got your reservations and everything ready for the Japan tour?" "Yeah." "Youre coming to Amsterdam as soon as Worldwides done, then. You dont even have a match, so just, take your face time and get the fuck out of there as qick as you can." "Lemme get my match with Rollins first." "Thats what, show after this?" "Yeah." A break. A pause. Bad reception? "Fuck it. Go ahead. Probably would be a bad idea throwing you against him with a week of Amsterdam in your lungs, isnt it?" "Just thinkin about jetlag, man. That shits killin me." "Either way. After Rollins, Amsterdam." "Word." "Let you go, then. Take it easy." "Peace." Sage had taken to ending phone calls with actual goodbyetypethings, Frank noticed. Something about business etiqette, he figured it, and tried not to admit he didn't hear any concern in Sage's voice on the subject of Rollins. It was enough deciding how much he really cared, sometimes; he wasn't even tempted to guess at what sort of pathos the rest of the world could muster for him. He slid his phone to his pocket and took the time to piss, tried to read faint vandalism under a coat of paint and no primer. The remnants of a phone number. The hieroglyphic fragments of a pornographic haiku; its shadowed limerick response. There was nothing fresh, nothing new. He shook and zipped before digging in his pockets for a pen that he expected he wouldn't have, much less find. The world, again, fielded no surprises; he washed his hands and left without bringing the wall's dialogue back to life. To the bar, again, the man was gone and left only an unoccupied stool between Frank and the legion of a heavy drunk. He slid his coaster and glass to the far rim of the counter, lit a cigarette without hesitation and went to waiting on the woman to ask if he wanted a refill. Staring blank, prosaic in the downtime; he tried to ignore another glower from the nearest of the lot and contented himself on nodding his head near the rhythm of whatever was playing: one of the flood of Zeppelin ripoffs from the 80s, he paid them no more mind than he paid the originals’or the ones that preceded them. "Another?" "Yeah." He watched her; still Bacardi. She brought it his way, almost overfull. He stirred lazy, twirled the thin straw in loose circles and tried to approach without spilling. He came close enough. "I dont give a shoot if you got a boyfriend." He'd missed something. "And I dont give a shit that you dont." But he'd always admired the college age bartender. "Wheres my other drink?" Another of the group shot it at her. "I told you five minutes ago, youre wearin it." She didn't miss a step; she tended the register and handed an empty bottle to a waitress near her. "Shouldnt have to pay for a drink I didnt drink." He was laughing with it. And she gave the group a round of replacements without a word, without any regard. "Hey, this aint on the bill." "You paid for em." "I dont remember that" "I do." "Oh." He smiled and nodded. Frank decided he could like that man, the central one of the group, under the right circumstances. He just seemed too drunk for his own good; Frank didn't dislike him anymore than he disliked anyone else at the bottom of a few hours of drinking. Nothing in how the man hung his head was detested; if anything, it was how Frank held his own that shaded the world in the colors it fell to. He wasn't trying to blame anything. Blame just came about as a natural product of thinking about it; he kept at trying to eschew thought in favor of, well. He had no alternatives. Action wouldn't solve anything. He'd learned he couldn't punch his malaise away anymore than he could fuck or drive or smoke or drink it underground. It would be delayed, postponed. It was new to him. He didn't know how to react. He hadn't been so tired minded before he joined JXW. Maybe he could accept the idea of renting his head out to dirt for the paychecks, but it didn't lessen the impact and it sure as fuck didn't buy him more room for his personal life. He was always something of a qick drinker, on his third when his friends would hit their second if he didn't pace himself. He had nothing to pace himself by, given, but it was last call and he took to enjoying the flavor instead of the burn. Not that he didn't find some comfort in comparing it by the others he'd drank, but there was always a line that divided him from the connoisseurs that lived off of it; he'd only ever skipped it when he needed distraction. So he drank without remembering, and he finished without a reason to. His world had gone septic. He paid the bartender more than he'd thought about and made for the exit. He had a plane in the morning; he'd find a hotel and crash out just long enough to get woken up by ringing phones. And Erika: christ, Erika. He'd have time when he got back from Japan’not that he was making any promises; just delays. Rollins? It hung at the back of his skull, mixed with the mortar of his spinal column. He unlocked the doors and cranked the car on his way outside. In a world so full of nothing, Rollins was something; something like a beacon. Decrepit. Worn down. Still glowing. He sat behind the wheel and closed his eyes. There would be time. There wouldn't be a high order of distractions. He needed to wrap his fists around the throat of permanence and watch it struggle. He shifted into reverse.
