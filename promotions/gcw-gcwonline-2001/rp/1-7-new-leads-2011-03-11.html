1.7 - New Leads

11 March 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/11/11 “Sometimes, great things also come with great costs...” - Miguel Erazo 1 There were so many thoughts running through his mind as he prepared himself for the upcoming day. Miguel Erazo was already sitting in his patrol car, sitting in the somewhat heavy early morning traffic of downtown San Diego. It was his first official day back to work after Unit Chief Erwin had forced his entire team to stay home after the shooting at Agent Rios' funeral last week. So many things had come into focus clearly for him since the shootings. But still, things had never seemed so complicated. He felt confused and lost, like a stranger in his own skin. His mind began to race over the events of the past few days. The accusations and betrayals of trust, the lingering sense of danger, the hanging stink of death, these were all things that were not entirely foreign to him. Hell, you don't become a police officer and think that it's going to be all sunshine and rainbows, you know about the dirty side of the job. But, even though you know it's coming, it never quite prepares you for when it does. The sound of a horn behind him brought him back from his momentary break from reality. As traffic slowly began to creep through the streets he followed along. It didn't take long for the flow of traffic to slow again and for him to stop, once again allowing his mind to wander from the road. Ever since he was a young boy, it was never a question of what young Miguel Erazo would do when he grew up. His father was a police officer, as was his grandfather, and his great grandfather before that. Being a member of La Policia was always written in the cards for Miguel. He had always had his doubts, usually little things. He was mildly successful as an athlete in High School, but it was always seen as a distraction. It was always the thing keeping him away from his goal, at least according to his father it was. Despite a few potential scholarship opportunities, Miguel did what his family expected of him and he enrolled in the Armed Forces. His time in the military being the perfect start for his career of keeping the peace and protecting the innocent. The innocent.... That was always what made it worthwhile for Miguel. Despite the constant danger, the shitty hours with even shittier pay. There was always that sense of worth, for protecting the city. For standing up for those who couldn't stand up for themselves. As a young child, he remembered reading the comic strips from the USA, all featuring great super heroes like Spiderman or Captain America. And despite not having any super powers, being a cop allowed him to protect the city, just like the heroes in the comics from his youth. Another honk from a car and the sound of shouting from behind him and he shook those thoughts from his mind. He creeped along slowly, following the tail lights in front of him. In the past few weeks though, his job had become much more complicated. In these past few days, the constant danger that he was putting himself in, suddenly seemed so much more real. Members of his team had died, other members were being targeted. It was clear that the biker gang known as Los Aztecas was onto the fact that they were under investigation by local police and by the FBI. And apparently the bikers from below the border were uninterested in being put under the microscope by anyone. And then there was Unit Chief Erwin. He had been getting closer to Miguel, confiding in him. It was him who suggested that Los Aztecas might have a mole on the inside of the police force, and that mole might be his mentor Captain Phillip Toms. Those accusations, accompanied by Toms strange actions of late seemed to be painting a poor picture of a man who Erazo had grown to respect deeply in his time working for the San Diego Police Department. Could Toms really be the monster that Erwin was painting him to be? Or was it simply venom spit from the mouth of someone who had been hurt personally and professionally by the death of Agent Rios. That seemed to be what everything revolved around lately; death. Miguel remembered the pained faces of Rios' family. The tears of agony and sorrow streaming down the face of his children. The desperate and helpless look of his wife. He had been unable to shake those faces from his memory in the days following the funeral and somewhat selfishly, he wondered if he could put his own family through something like that. Angelina was pregnant now and soon they would have a child of their own. Was it irresponsible of him to allow the chance that his child would grow up without a father? Traffic continued to move slowly and Miguel's mind continued to race. But no matter how much he thought about it, despite all his thoughts about protecting his family, he always remembered words that his father had said to him when he was just a young boy. 'You must always protect your family Miguel, family must always come first. But when you put on that badge, the entire city becomes your family. Protecting the city is a great honour, greater than any other in this world. But great things also come with great costs sometimes.' 2 FBI Unit Chief Kevin Erwin was sitting behind his desk. Very few members of the local police force had shown up for work yet, but that was to be expected, it was still quite early. There were some things on his mind, some things that he needed to take care of. And the firs item on his list was the mysterious phone call that he received the night before. Someone calling claiming to have information about the shooting at the funeral for Agent Rios. He had called immediately for a trace on the call, but he had received very little information as of yet. The best that the local field office had come up with was that the call came from a pre-purchased cell phone with limited minutes. A fucking burner, a throwaway phone. No help there. He was told that they would investigate further to try and find out where the phone call originated from. The telephone on his desk began to ring loudly and it shocked Erwin so much that he nearly jumped for his weapon. He took a couple of deep breaths, taking a moment to calm himself, before he reached for the receiver and answered the call. “Unit Chief Kevin Erwin speaking...” 'Yes hello, Unit Chief Erwin. This is Technical Analyst Rodriguez from the Los Angeles field office. We have some information about the trace you ordered last night.' The voice was soft, definitely feminine. Which was somewhat strange coming from the field office of LA. He had heard that most of the women that came from the LA Office were more manly than their male counterparts. Perhaps it was the fact that she was a technical analyst as opposed to a Field Agent. “Yes, just one moment please,” Erwin said calmly into the receiver. He reached for a pen and a piece of paper. “Okay, go ahead.” 'Yes sir. I've been informed that you already know that the call came from a disposable cell phone, that can be pre-purchased with voice minutes from any local convenience store. However, we have been able to locate the cell phone tower that carried the call. My office is faxing over the information as we speak.' “Excellent. If you could please give me the location of the tower right now as well, I'd like to send one of my Agents over to check it out,” Erwin said calmly. 'Of course sir. The cell phone tower was off of North Harbour Drive and West Broadway, right near the water. That cell phone tower carries the majority of calls that come from the Northern Harbours and waterfront area. The full map of coverage will be included in the fax.' “Thank you, Ms. Rodriguez.” 'No problem sir.' And with that the line went dead. No farewell, no goodbye, all business. Just like they were taught at the academy. Erwin put the receiver down and immediately his mind began to race. From the waterfront? The area was mostly industrial, there were a number of warehouses and storage spaces in the area that the caller could have been hiding in. However, a location like that seemed to point to some kind of trap, more like some kind of set up than an actual anonymous tip. And if it indeed had been a tip, then how did they get his personal cell phone number, instead of using the FBI Tip Line. It all smelled funny to Erwin, something about this wasn't making complete sense. Luckily for him, he still had one of the FBI's best Analysts at his disposal. A former hacker, Robert Magalas was caught by the FBI and ended up defecting to the good guys. Erwin could use that kind of intelligence now. Perhaps Magalas would be able to find something that the Field Office didn't find. He reached for the receiver once again and began dialling right away. 3 He was only a few minutes early, but the police station seemed nearly abandoned when Captain Phillip Toms walked through the front door. He began to look around and was greeting unenthusiastically by the officer at the front desk. As was protocol Toms flashed his credentials, even though the officer at the desk seemed more interested in the morning newspaper than actually checking on security credentials. Toms quickly pocketed his badge and walked through the double doors and into the area of the station that was currently dedicated to his Ward. And the current home of the FBI Team, who still remained in San Diego despite his as well as War's best efforts to send them back home. Toms scanned the room as he walked through the bullpen and towards his desk in the corner of the room. Very few members of his team had arrived, although that wasn't overly surprising. He had never really enforced a strict punctuality policy. Instead he chose to offer some leeway to officers, learning a long time ago that happy officers were better police officers. It also allowed him to use poor punctuality as a form of leverage when he needed to influence an officer in some way or another. He made a point to quickly check on the open conference room that was the current home of the FBI Team, currently investigating Los Aztecas Motorcycle Club. Despite heavy losses in personnel and public support, the FBI team was still in town. But at the moment it appeared that Unit Chief Kevin Erwin was the only member of the team at Police HQ. And judging by the bags under his eyes and the dishevelled look of his face, he hadn't been sleeping well. Toms allowed a sinister smile to creep across his lips as he hurried towards his office. He quickly slid through the door and closed it silently behind him. He hung his jacket and shuffled behind his desk, immediately beginning to look over the files that were on his desk of ongoing cases and memos from the weekend. He was just beginning to sort through the files when he was interrupted by a soft knock at the door. He craned his neck towards the door, trying to see who was there. But the blinds were still drawn closed and he was unable to see anything other than a silhouette at the door. “Come in,” he called out. The door swung open slowly and a familiar face came into view. Toms smiled as he saw one of his longest serving officers Tommy Chase enter the room. He was carrying something in his hand, but otherwise appeared normal and well-rested from the unscheduled long weekend. There was a smile on his face and in his voice as he entered the room and closed the door behind him. “Hey Captain,” Tommy said cheerfully. Captain Toms responded with a nod and a smile, before opening his mouth. “Good morning Officer Chase, I didn't see you when I came in.” “Ah that's alright, I was in the break room. God damned long weekends kill me, I feel like I've been at work for a full day already, even though it's barely been half an hour. I was getting another cup of coffee,” Tommy replied. “Very good, what can I do for you Tommy?” Toms asked, dropping the formality. Chase stepped forward, extending his arm with the envelope. “Someone dropped this off earlier this morning, not too sure who the guy was. Some kind of courier maybe? Either way, it's addressed to you.” That was somewhat surprising to Toms. Usually nothing was addressed directly to him unless it came through the inner-office mail system. All of his personal items were sent to his home address. He extended his arm and took the envelope. He studied it for a few seconds, the simplicity taking him by surprise. There was no postage, no sign at all that this had even gone through the postal service. Instead there was simply his name. He wondered where the envelope came from. “Where did you say this came from again Officer Chase?” his confusion and intrigue bring the formality back into the conversation. “Someone dropped it off for you earlier, maybe twenty minutes ago. They said it was important you got it. Something about some case that you'd been working on. I honestly didn't ask questions, it was early and I was the only one here. Sorry Captain,” Tommy offered. Captain Toms waved his hand, dismissing the apology. “It's no worry...” “Maybe it's something from the bikers? Some kind of warning or some shit,” Tommy offered with a chuckle, before turning on his heel and heading towards the door. “Yeah....” Captain Toms began, as he carefully opened the envelope. “Perhaps it is.” 4 Miguel's mind was still racing with thoughts of the past few weeks as he entered the bullpen of the Police Station. He was finally beginning to settle into his usual groove, trying to dismiss the negative thoughts and concerns from his drive to work and replace them with thoughts about moving forward on the Los Aztecas case. He was unsure what the game plan would be, but was certain that FBI Unit Chief Erwin would have a plan of attack. Surely, they wouldn't be allowed to get away with the shooting, especially with the amount of media coverage that it had received over the weekend. Miguel quickly flashed his credentials to the officer behind the desk, who appeared uninterested, but who nodded his acceptance of Miguel's badge and ID. Miguel pushed through the large double doors and was somewhat surprised to find the bullpen mostly empty. Despite being almost twenty minutes late, he seemed to be one of the first officers to arrive to work. He walked towards his desk, the pair that he shared with Tommy. He saw that Tommy's jacket was already slumped over his chair, so he was around somewhere, but he couldn't see him. Miguel ignored his momentarily missing partner and slid into the chair behind his desk and punched the button on his computer, bring the PC to life. There were a couple of folders that were on his desk, most of them surveillance files. Photos, notes and information that needed to be logged from some of the scouting work he had done before the shooting last week. The reminder of Agent Rios' death depressed him almost immediately. But for two very different reasons, firstly because it brought back the negative thoughts of death and pain from his drive to work. Secondly it reminded him that despite the special attention he had been getting lately from Unit Chief Erwin, he was still mostly a gopher for the FBI. Doing simple surveillance missions, far from the action. He had been hoping that would change but the events of last week had made him rethink his position in this war. The phone on his desk began to ring and Miguel casually reached for the receiver. He was somewhat surprised to be receiving a call this early in the morning, but perhaps it was Angelina calling to check in. She was supposed to be heading home later today, maybe she was calling to confirm her plans. Miguel brought the receiver to his ear and spoke quickly. “Officer Miguel Erazo,” he said into the receiver. 'Miguel, it's Erwin. I'm in my office, we need to talk. Come by, be discreet if you can.' “Yes sir,” Miguel said and hung up. He knew that there was surely a look of confusion painted on his face, but there was also a bit of intrigue. Maybe Erwin was already setting that plan of action into motion. Miguel stood up and began to head to the conference room where the FBI along with Unit Chief Erwin were currently situated at the station. He stopped in his tracks when he saw the door to Captain Toms' office open and saw his partner Tommy Chase step out. He felt a sick feeling in his stomach and the colour drained from his face, when he thought back to the pairs conversation at the bar on the weekend. There was a smile on Tommy's face as he walked back towards the two men's desk. He slapped his partner on the shoulder and gave him a quizzical look when he saw Miguel. “You okay man? You look sick. You haven't been at the bar all weekend because Angelina was away have you?” Tommy asked. “Fuck you,” Miguel said with a laugh that sounded every bit as forced as it was. “What was that all about?” “What?” Tommy asked, playing stupid. “That visit with Toms?” “Yeah, that fucking visit with Toms. Anything you need to tell me?” Tommy let the carefree look slide away from his face and he got very serious, very quickly. He closed the distance between himself and Miguel, getting right into his face. “Give your head a shake,” he started, his lips quivering as he spoke. “I'm your fucking partner! I told you to keep me out of your shit with Toms, I'm not going to go inserting myself into it. The Captain got a letter and I delivered it to him. That okay with you Erazo?” The pain and sarcasm was evident in Chase's voice and Miguel couldn't really blame him. He took a step back and raised his hands in mock surrender. “You're right, sorry Tommy.” Tommy nodded his head. “You ready to head out? I don't really have any orders, but I'm sure we can hit the streets, knock some heads, see what we can find out about that shooting,” he said. “Yeah, just give me a few minutes,” Miguel started. “Erwin just called me, he wants to talk to me about something.” Tommy chuckled slightly to himself before responding. “You give me shit about talking to the Captain and now you're heading to some secret meeting with Erwin. What are you blowing him or something?” “Why? You jealous?” Miguel asked with a laugh turning away from his partner. “You wish lover boy,” Chase called over his shoulder as Miguel walked casually through the bullpen and towards the conference room. 5 Phillip put the letter down and immediately began to scan his office. He stood to his feet and strolled towards the large windows of his office that stared out into the bullpen. He carefully slid a finger through one of the blinds and pulled it apart, scanning the area. It was still empty, apparently many of his officers were taking their sweet ass times getting to work this morning. But it actually made things a little bit easier for him. He closed the blinds and turned back to his office. He took one last look at the note, before reaching into his pocket. He pulled out his lighter and sparked the flint, bringing a flame to the end of the lighter. He touched the orange flame to the edge of the letter, the flame immediately jumping to the paper and beginning to consume the letter. “A meeting, in the middle of the fucking day. What the fuck do they think I am, a fucking servant?” he mumbled to himself as he watched the letter turn to ash. He stood and dropped the letter into his metal waste receptacle. The flame continued to destroy the letter as Toms grabbed his jacket from the hanger at the back of the office. He waited casually for the flame to dissipate before opening the door and heading towards the exit. He looked around cautiously trying to ensure that no one would see him leaving. When he was sure that no one would notice his exit, he made his way quickly towards the double doors and to the exit of the building. As he walked by the officer at the front desk, he was still busy reading the morning paper, but Toms needed to notify him of his exit. It was protocol for all commanding officers, Captains and above to report their whereabouts when they were on the streets. He calmly placed his identification and badge on top of the newspaper, interrupting the officer's reading. “Captain Phillip Toms, heading on patrol to investigate a tip in the North Harbour area,” he told the officer calmly. The officer sighed, but wrote a quick note of Toms' notice before quickly turning back to his morning paper. By the time the officer was back to the latest Calvin and Hobbes comic strip, Toms was already out the door and into the morning sunshine. 6 Miguel entered the office without knocking, Erwin barely even bothered to look up as Miguel closed the door behind him. He walked over to the Unit Chief's desk and took a seat on the other side. Miguel sat silently as he studied Erwin, who looked tired and unkempt. He wasn't exactly sure what it was that Erwin was studying but whatever it was, it had all of his attention. “Thanks for coming,” he finally said, before looking up and smiling at Erazo. Miguel nodded but remained silent. He wasn't exactly sure where this was going to go and he wasn't sure that he should be speaking yet. Erwin didn't seem to mind as he continued to talk. “This has been a bit of an embarrassment for us Miguel,” Erwin began. “Not just for the FBI, but also for me. These guys are smart and they are onto us. Although, I've already told you my opinions on that.” Again Miguel said nothing, he chose instead to sit in silence. “I got a call about the shooting at Agent Rios' funeral,” Erwin said flatly, his voice lacking any emotion, instead sounding strained and tired. “An anonymous tip, but it came on my personal cell phone. Obviously I'm skeptical about the whole thing, it feels like a set up to me.” Finally Miguel could be silent no longer, the thought of a lead, any kind of hope brought a fire back into him. The prospect of doing some real police work and seeing some real action in the field brought his mind back into it. It reminded him about the thrill of this job. “Still, a lead is a lead. What did it say?” he asked. “Nothing,” Erwin deadpanned. “At least nothing important. The caller was male. But left no name, no address, no way to contact him. He simply said that he had information about the shooting that was on the news, nothing else.” “Did he mention why he contacted you directly?” Miguel questioned. “No, strangely he didn't,” Erwin answered. “He actually didn't call me by name either. He may have known who he was talking to in advance, or he may have simply thought he was on the FBI Tip Line. Either way, I'm not taking these shootings lightly, and I'm definitely not taking any potential lead lightly either.” “Of course not, sir,” Miguel said. “Would you like me to check into it?” Erwin looked out of the office, simply removing his stare from Miguel's. He seemed to be pondering that idea for a few seconds, before he spoke again. “Perhaps that is an idea, but that's not why I wanted to talk to you,” Erwin started. “I've requested and received permission to make another attempt at infiltrating Los Aztecas.” “What?!” Miguel said, somewhat surprised by the news. “They're going to let you send someone else out into the ranks of those monsters after what happened to Rios. They found him out pretty quickly. And it got him killed. Who the hell in their right mind would sign up for that after knowing what happened to Rios. Do you FBI guys have a never-ending stream of Mexican-Americans to send to the slaughter?” “That's more of an immigration thing, slaughtering,” Erwin said, pausing for a few seconds before laughing to himself. “I'm kidding Erazo. Actually, the FBI is relatively short on Mexican-American Agents.” Almost as if a switch had been flipped, a light bulb went off in Miguel's head. He stood to his feet and put his hands up in the air, stepping away slightly. “You want me to do it?” he asked, already knowing, but afraid of the answer. “Yes,” Erwin said, pausing for several seconds before continuing. “I've told you my thoughts about what happened with Agent Rios, and no matter who it is, my superiors are in agreement that there is an informant within the local Police. I don't believe that it's you Miguel, I trust your loyalty. I know that your father, and your grandfather were police officers...” “And my great grandfather...” Miguel interrupted. “But still, if you think there is an informant, how exactly do you expect me to get into Los Aztecas without getting killed.” “I don't...” Erwin said with a smirk. “I have a plan.” “Oh great, a plan that involves me getting killed,” Miguel said his voice getting louder. “Seriously Erwin, when you guys first showed up, I was interested in going undercover, it's always been something I wanted to do. But I have a fiance now, and a baby on the way. After what happened to Rios, how can I endanger them like that?” “I understand your concern Officer Erazo,” Erwin began to explain. “It will take a lot of commitment from you, and I give you my word that your family will be completely protected, but it will take great sacrifice from you. This could be a landmark case for the San Diego Police Department and for the FBI, but we need to get inside these guys.” “That's it?” Miguel laughed. “That's the speech that's supposed to convince me to put my life and my family's lives at risk?” “Many lives have already been lost, but we can take these guys down,” Erwin said. “I can't ask you to decide immediately, but we may need to act quickly. I need to know if I can count on you when the time comes.” “You haven't even told me this so-called plan of yours, the one that, you know... involves me dying!” Miguel was furious now. “The plan will come in time, but right now this city needs help,” Erwin said. “You can be the one who protects this city from the terror of Los Aztecas, but it will take sacrifice.” Miguel lowered his head, staring at the floor. He took a few deep breaths, his entire body slumped over, like a defeated fighter. He turned towards the door and walked towards it, his eyes never leaving the floor tiles. “Sometimes, great things also come with great costs...” Miguel mumbled more to himself than anything. “My father used to tell me that when I was younger.” With that, Erazo finally lifted his stare from the floor and stared right into Erwin's eyes. He wasn't sure what it was about the silver-haired Unit Chief, but for some reason he trusted him. He offered a nod before turning and stepping out of the office. 7 The engine idled and music played softly over the speakers as Toms sat in his car in the parking lot of the small diner near the North End of the San Diego Harbour. The classical music seemed an odd-choice for the Police Captain and he had received several confused glances from members of his team. But there had always been something about Beethoven and Mozart that moved him. The sound of Mozart's Symphony No. 36 was interrupted by the roar of a loud 4-stroke, 998 cc engine, the sound of a roaring Kawasaki Ninja. Toms adjusted his rear-view mirror towards the street so that he could see the biker pulling into the parking lot. After the shooting, he couldn't be too careful, he had no idea what these guys were capable of. And despite being a member of their payroll since they set up shop in San Diego, Toms had no idea of his current use to the bikers. The pure black Ninja and it's somehow more shadowy driver pulled into the parking spot beside him. With a quick flick of his wrist the rider killed the engine of the super bike and the roaring of the engine was replaced once again by the soft, melodic piano of Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart. Toms rolled the window slowly, but didn't turn to face the rider of the bike. “El Guerra has a job for you,” the rider's voice was raspy and thick with a Spanish accent. “What does he want now? I'm a busy man, is this why I was called out here?” Toms asked, a hint of anger rising in his voice. There was no reply from the rider, and when Toms turned to see why the rider was being so quiet, he was staring down the barrel of a shimmering Desert Eagle pistol. With his point made, the rider returned the gun to it's holster and continued speaking. “He is losing patience with you, as am I,” he began. “I have no use for your complaints, I am here only to deliver the message.” “What is this task that War has for me now?” Toms questioned. “He wants you to bring him the FBI Agent in charge of the investigation into Los Aztecas,” the rider answered. “He feels as though they are not getting our message to leave. He wishes to send the message personally.” War wants to deliver a message personally? Toms laughed at the thought. War didn't deliver messages, the Sergeant at Arms of Los Aztecas was good at only a few things. They mostly consisted of drinking tequila, riding a motorcycle at insane speeds and ending the lives of others. Surely any message delivered by War, would be no warning. “Is he insane?” Toms asked. “This isn't how things work. You've killed an FBI Agent and then committed a drive-by shooting at his funeral. Do you guys really think that killing another Agent, a Unit Chief for that matter, is going to make them go away. It's just going to put an even bigger target on the club. You need to let me handle this from the inside.” The biker laughed. “War told me that you would say something like that,” he said. “That's because he knows that I am a sensible human being, apparently asking the same of you is too much,” Toms replied sarcastically. “Do not raise your voice with me, Toms. You may be protected by El Guerra right now, but one day that won't be the case, and when that day comes...” “Yeah, but that day isn't today,” Toms interrupted. “So let me do my thing from the inside and try to slow some of this heat.” “I have no time for your bullshit,” the rider said casually. “El Guerra wishes to meet the Agent in charge and expects you to set it up. If you decide not to do so, than that is your decision. But, if I were you, I would set up the meeting.” The rider pressed a button on his motorcycle and once again the engine came to life with a thundering roar. He revved the engine a few times, before backing out of the parking place. However, before he backed completely out, he pulled something from his pocket. Toms immediately began to panic, but when the rider pulled out a manilla envelope from the inside of his leather jacket, Toms relaxed, albeit only slightly. The biker tossed the envelope through the window, it landed in Toms' lap. And with that, he threw the bike into gear and tore out of the parking lot and quickly disappeared from sight. Toms quickly picked up the large envelope and begin to search through the contents. There were several photos, all of them black and white and all of them depicting him meeting with several different bikers and strangers. None of the photos were damning by themselves, but the investigation that they would cause, certainly would be. “Fuck, fuck, fuck!” Toms screamed, slamming his steering wheel. “Motherfuckers!” 8 The traffic had cleared significantly since his morning commute, but still Miguel found his mind racing. From the passenger side of the patrol car, his mind continued to contemplate the offer that Erwin had made him earlier in the day. He and Tommy had been on the road for an hour and it was unusually silent between the two of them. Tommy hadn't been saying much and Erazo had found himself pre-occupied with his conversation with Erwin. “Alright man, what the hell is going on?” Tommy asked as he steered through the traffic. “You've been quiet all morning, what the hell did Erwin say to you?” “Nothing really,” Miguel lied. “Bullshit,” Tommy called the bluff easily. “Well, he was talking about a potential lead he might have,” Miguel began. “Into Rios' shooting and the drive-by's at the funeral.” “Well that's big fucking news, what does he have?” Tommy asked. “He got some kind of anonymous call,” Miguel began and seeing the skeptical look on Tommy's face, he continued to explain. “Yeah, I thought it was strange too, so does he. But, he got his team to do a bit of research, they found the cell tower that hosted the call. It's in the north harbour area.” “Well what the hell are we sitting out here with our thumbs up our asses for?” Tommy said, his enthusiasm rising. “Let's go check it out!” “There wasn't anything,” Miguel tried to slow his partner down. “It was anonymous, left no name, no contact information. There's nothing to check out. It's just a cell phone call.” Tommy stared at his partner, somewhat confused. Two weeks ago the Miguel Erazo he knew would have been all over something like this. Raring to go check out any potential lead, anxious to get his hands into even the slightest bit of action possible. Something was amiss with Erazo, and Tommy told him so. “What the fuck is wrong with you?” he questioned. “You used to live for this kind of stuff, making something out of nothing. Getting involved just for the sake of action, what the hell has changed?” “I don't know man...” Miguel started. “I guess all this death and danger has brought about some changes in my priorities. Thinking more about what's really important, you know?” “What used to be important to you was catching the bad guys and protecting the city,” Tommy answered. “Yeah, but at what cost? The safety of me? Of my family? Tommy I've got a family on the way, I can't just run headfirst into this like before. I've got to be smarter than that.” Tommy let a smile cross his face. One of the best I know, you've got great instincts, and if you're going to let something at home change the way you do police work then you might as well get yourself a desk job, because you're useless to me out here.” Miguel stared at Tommy. The words hurt, but he could see the truth in his partner's statement. This wasn't the kind of job that you could show up and give 50% for, if you were going to be in this, you had to be either all the way in, or you might as well just get the fuck out. “Fuck it, you're right, let's do this,” Miguel said. Tommy punched the steering wheel and let his warm smile return to his face. “That's what I like to hear,” Tommy said. “Partner.” Miguel let that last word linger in his mind for a few seconds. Partner. No matter what Tommy had always been there for him, and he assumed he always would be there for him. Still despite the newfound enthusiasm, he still couldn't shake the conversation that he and Erwin had earlier. He still wasn't sure that he had the same enthusiasm stepping into a job that had killed someone less than a week ago. “Hey Tommy,” Miguel stared. “You ever wonder if all of this is worth it? Worth the risk, worth the danger? I mean, after seeing what Los Aztecas did last week, you ever think that maybe we're not making a difference?” Tommy's serious face returned and he stared straight out the window, his gaze not meeting his partner's. “I used to wonder that all the time. I used to be in this job for the thrills and the pay cheque, nothing else. Hell, it was a great pick up line at the bar, and the badge got me cheap drinks at every bar in town. But, the first time I attended a Police Funeral, that was all that I needed to see. I didn't even know the guy really, but he was a patrol guy in my Ward. He died off duty, heart attack. They had a public funeral for him and the support was incredible. Complete strangers were coming up to this guy's wife and hugging her, supporting her. Seeing a community come together like that, that was all I needed to know this job was worth it. We get to protect those communities every day, that's how we give it back.” Miguel let those words sink in. Paying it back to the community. He thought about his partner's story. The community giving back to a mourning wife. Right now, that same community was paralyzed by fear and terror. The source of that fear was Los Aztecas, and the community needed protecting. In that second, Miguel was pretty sure what his answer would be to Erwin. “You're a great guy Tommy,” Miguel said. “Thanks man.” Tommy looked at him quizzically, the mischievous look returning to his face. “Yeah whatever you say faggot, just don't try to kiss me.” 9 Erwin was still sitting at his desk, signing forms and trying to plan his next move. There was a rat inside the Police Department and he had only two choices, to leave the rat out of the loop, or to smoke out the rat. And while Erwin had an idea about who the rat might be, he didn't have any concrete evidence to back that up, so smoking him out was out the window. Instead he would have to leave him out of the loop and out of the planning process. Erwin was interrupted from his planning by the sound of his cell phone vibrating. He quickly shuffled a few forms and papers, searching for the hidden source of the vibrating. He found the phone and checked the caller ID, somewhat unsurprisingly it was a private name and a private number. He flicked the cell phone open and brought it to his ear. “This is Erwin speaking.” 'Warehouse 23A, North Harbour Port. Midnight. Come alone.' “Who is this...?” Erwin asked quickly. There was no response. There was a click and the sound of dead air. “Fuck,” he said to himself. He knew things were going to move quickly, but even he didn't think it would be this quickly. He hoped Erazo was in, otherwise he was going to need to come up with a pretty quick Plan B. He quickly reopened his cell phone and began to dial a number. He pressed the Send button and brought the phone to his ear once again. The phone was answered quickly. “Erazo,” Erwin began. “It's Erwin. We've got to talk.”
