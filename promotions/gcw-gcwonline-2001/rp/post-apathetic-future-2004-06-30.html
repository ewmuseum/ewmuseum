Post-Apathetic Future

30 June 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/30/04 'So, do we just sit in here and wait to die? Or do we go out and not let anybody stand in our way? Do we give up and bury our heads in the sand? Or do we pick up and face it head on? Do we act like cowards? Or do we act like people who have nothing left to lose, nothing left to give, and nothing left to go for but their very survival! And if we die, we meet death here! And now! On our terms! And...if we live...oh, my bretheren, if we live... The fruits of our labor will shine forth for the rest of our lives!' The deafening echoes of the cheers of dozens, maybe hundreds of men and women shoot up and resonate within the hollow, merciless structure of cold steel and stone that surround them. The metal panels shake and the pebbles and rocks on the floor quake under the fear of the force that these people let out in one loud cry. Except off to the side, there are a handful of people that watch the chaos of a stirred up bunch of dreamers. People who think they have a chance to survive to tomorrow night because they now have a drive and a purpose. And this handful of people are wallowing in their own self-pity, because it's happened to them once before where they were the only survivors among all the people around them, and they have no wish to repeat that instance. 'He feeds it to them. And they soak it all up like sponges on piss.' 'Well what can you honestly expect from them? They've been hoarded down here in this hole for months and months now. Half of them would kill their child for a fresh piece of meat if they were promised no ramifications.' 'And we call them the monsters.' 'Monsters? They're people, Toku. People just like us. People that also have families and hopes and dreams. People that have entire lives that we might have cared about a few months ago. Now...' 'Now they're people who contest one of the last food supplies on the planet. They try to feed their children and themselves by taking the food that we try to feed ours with. And for that, we call them monsters.' 'It's not like we let them into our society.' '...I know, Keith. But they asked for too much. We couldn't house them all and with them added to us as fast as they wanted to be added, our food and water would have been used up before we could have turned a decent crop yield. We would have died anyway.' The larger silhouette, Keith, turns away from the roaring crowd and the hopeless hopefuls, half out of disgust and half out of heartbreak. He can't hear the words of Terrance Slauson, Prime Director of HOPE ONE, without becoming physically ill. The man makes promises that he has not the means nor the intention of keeping. He makes them so that he can have the masses on his side, and he brings a long-term death to all by leading them while waving a banner of short-term survival. Toku, a rough, scarred, but by all means good looking girl, followed after Keith swiftly. The two had spent a long time working very hard to keep Terrence Slauson's word from affecting the people. Realizing that they'd failed completely, they gave up. The remaining group of around a dozen slowly dissipated from watching the hypnotized crowd and started milling back to their shelters. But Toku and Keith had decided to go somewhere other than their fabricated homes. 'I'm really surprised that Jack wasn't there, Toku.' 'I'm not. He gave up on those poor bastards a long time ago. He knew his urges to pack up and move east, to leave what we so nostalgically call 'Columbus,' would fall on deaf ears.' 'Well, the bombs did hit the coast. It doesn't make much sense to move toward the place that the bombs hit, does it?' 'What makes more sense? To move to where the destruction struck months ago, or to head straight for destruction now? These people that our friends, our family, are going against are larger in population, they have vehicles and better weapons, and they're driven savage from the harsh outside and near starvation. I'll take my chances with a ghost town.' 'And the radiation?' 'We have precautions for that, Keith.' 'I just think that whether or not it's a good idea, Jack didn't do an amazing job of telling it to the people. He made it seem like it's the only choice instead of the best choice. And they responded like they have.' The two have reached a shelter door. Thick metal plating that doesn't match the rest of the shelter itself, but is more for the fabricated security that the shelters themselves bring. With a cold, hardened hand Keith bangs on it a couple of times, creating a dull thudding noise that only the most alert of ears could hear. But Jack hears it. And with the turning of the lock, the door opens. 'Wha?' 'Jack, they're rallied. They're going to try to move against the incoming group of survivalists. And they're going to get completely slaughtered, and then the survivalists are going to move in here and decimate everything that's left.' Jack looks like he just woke up. The man was in shape once. Used to be fit and lean and ready to take on anybody that came at him, and all of life for that matter. Jack Fitzwillis used to be known as Johnny Borealis, cutlass supreme wrestling superstar. With muscles and drive and determination and heart. But since the fallout, that's faded and gone. Rather than have his stylish, moptop hair, it's easier to just shave it off and not have to worry about it. One less thing to have your mind on in this hellzone that we call life. Muscles have faded in time because of a lack of working out and a lack of high protein food. He's still relatively in shape, but now concentrates much more on trying to just get through every day. And he looks like shit. 'They're doing what-now?' 'They're planning to attack the incoming group before they hit us. Terrence just gave them all this big bullshit speech, and me and Toku were wondering what you might be able to do about it. We have to be able to do something!' Jack takes out a cigarette and lights it, then looks over the shoulders of the two younger, hopeful survivors. They were born in the 90's. They had just graduated high school, fuckin high school, when the bombs hit. The two of them found their way to Columbus, Ohio, one of the places that the devastation was the lowest in North America. Keith, from Kansas City, was with a group that had holed up in some inner city school for a few weeks before deciding that they needed to find more survivors. Toku, meanwhile, was part of an Inuit tribe. She was way the fuck up in the Kodiak when the bombs hit. She saw it from the only distance that could be considered 'safe,' and she knew that there was no hope in finding her tribe alive. Turns out they were two miles within the radiation limit. Two fucking miles, and they would have lived. She found out how to live in not just the wilderness, but the post-atomic bomb wilderness. And she found her way to Columbus after a long, long hike through what was once called Canada. Jack, meanwhile, had a simpler story. He was in Columbus when the bombs went off. At a show, doing this or that. Everybody heard the sirens. A few people tried to run. You can't outrun an atomic blast. Idiots. That was the first thing Jack thought as he headed to the nearest bomb shelter. And then, when food started running low, Jack and Terrence were the two to start taking the lead and taking control. Some twenty months later, Terrence is the one that has fallen completely out of his gord. 'Jack...you know those things'll kill you.' Jack held his cigarette and for the first time in a long time, he gave a good, hearty laugh. 'You're right, Keith. Absolutely right. Heh...' Jack thought of Don. Don was the devil incarnate. That meant that Don was living in Hell. That meant that if Don was living in Hell, but he was always on the Earth in a form, that the Earth was in some way Hell. It took quite a few times for Don to ever explain this to Jack. But one time it actually got through. 'Think of it this way, Johnny-boy... You see before your eyes all of eternity. You are able to go anywhere, do anything, and have no consequences about it with the speed of thought. And, more than anything, you are complete. Your life is complete, your every waking moment is complete, and with nothing else to say about it you are truly, utterly, completely, whole-heartedly happy.' 'I follow.' 'Now imagine that you know of this. Not in the way that idiots here on Earth 'know' stuff. I hear it all the time, 'Oh, I know Jesus!' 'I know Muhammed's way!' 'I know what the Buddah knows!' None of that shit. But you know, you actually see, touch, feel, taste, smell, hear, and know that this paradise exists. You were there. And now, no matter how hard you try, you can't go there.' 'That's Hell.' 'No. Hell is made-up. That's life, Johnny-boy. That's my life. And that's the story of my life.' 'So you can't die and get out of this prison?' 'Oh I can die. I have. Several times. But it doesn't matter. I die and I just become somebody else. I guess the idea of Reincarnation is a pretty accurate one for what I go through. Except I remember every fucking thing that I've ever gone through-' 'Even the nights you get so drunk you piss all over yourself?' '...ok, I remember most of what I go through. And I know that I'll never, ever get out of it.' And with those words echoing in Jack's mind, along with Don's theories on addiction, Jack holds his cigarette, his new precious hobby, and wonders where his friend is in this new world. And if the human race dies, and there's no physical way for Don to be reborn, what will be be on Earth as? But right here, and right now, Jack has two young survivalists looking for help in front of him. And they already knew what his answer to them was going to be. 'The choice is yours, kids. Right now, there are a few things we can do. We can head east. We can see if there are any people out there better off than us. Take the few vehicles that we can get our hands on along with some of the suits that have been made, and we can travel. And possibly find nothing. But we can try.' 'Sounds promising.' 'We can also join our friends, our new brothers, and take on the newcomers. We can fight them and maybe win. But most likely we'll all die and they'll move in. And the strong will survive.' 'Sounds even better.' 'Or...you can choose to do nothing. You can choose to remain here and accept whatever life gives to you.' The look on Jack's face told them how he felt about that. 'That's giving up. You're saying we should give up on these people?' 'These people gave up on us a long time ago, Keith. They've chosen what they're going to do. You can't choose that for them. Now, I'm going to go inside my house. And I'm going to finish off this cigarette, and then I'm going to eat dinner. And I'm going to put this hellzone that we call home out of my mind for a little while.' Jack closes the door on the two of them. Not harshly or coldly. But definitively. Keith and Toku are outside, and Jack is in. His big metal door protects him from anybody coming in and interfering in his world. And with a sigh of comfort, his shoulders sag just a little bit. He takes one more drag on his cigarette and throws it out, then lays down on his bed. Not wanting to think about the people that are about to march to their deaths, he closes his eyes and knows that there's still something that he can do... '...might as well. I got nothing better to do.'
