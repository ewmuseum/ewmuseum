Viva Las Vegas? (Dangerous Games RP)

17 September 2006

Date: 9/17/2006 Bright light city gonna set my soul, Gonna set my soul on fire... Got a whole lot of money that's ready to burn, So get those stakes up higher. "He had never slept in a better bed, Rainsford thought." Percival closely studied Katie’s face as she finished reading the last line in ‘The Most Dangerous Game,’ by Richard Connell. He wasn’t overly fond of any of the time that Katie had spent as a member of the "New Breed" faction during their old SAWF days. Most of her darkest days in the fed had been during her time with them. Katie turned to glance at Percival and smiled gently, running her hand lightly down his cheek. "Worry not, me love," she reassured him. "Sure and I have nay desire to see another five years added on to me probationary period." Percival nodded and squeezed her hand, holding it against his face. "Nor I," he said. "But do you really think that boning up on the philosophy is what’s going to help you the most? You’ve fought in a few battle royals before... what makes this one different?" Katie contemplated the question and shrugged in quiet concession. "The name, I suppose," she said thoughtfully. "The gimmick it’s built on. Dangerous Games... sure and GCW calls it one of the biggest of their pay-per-view events. Perhaps even the biggest one of all. Sure and how more dangerous a game can be played than ‘The Most Dangerous Game’ that Richard Connell wrote about? In a way, I think that all of us in that battle royal shall be playing exactly that... The Most Dangerous Game." There's a thousand pretty women waitin' out there, And they're all livin' devil may care. And I'm just the devil with love to spare! Viva Las Vegas, Viva Las Vegas! "I suppose you’ll want to talk to Vandal Mallory over the phone, for a long discussion on your old faction philosophies of ‘The Hunt,’ for a refresher course," Percival quipped lightly. Katie giggled for a second with a look that showed Percival wasn’t that far off. "If I knew how to contact him, perhaps," she admitted. "But I was not deemed part of the inner circle of the faction, and only they were given proper instruction on how to reach him in any matter. I shall have to settle for calling Liz. Sure and I am overdue to thank her for the wonderful present she sent me for me 32nd birthday almost two weeks ago." Percival lightly kissed the top of Katie’s head. "Give her my regards, then," he said. "I need to finish this contract analysis, and then I’m going to retire for the night." Katie nodded and fished Liz’s number out of her cell phone’s ‘address book.’ Her voice was a bit giddy as Liz answered. "Sure and top of the evening to ye, lassie," Katie said genially. Liz Ryan was Katie’s best friend in America... probably her best friend, period. They began as rivals in the old wrestling fed... and put aside their differences to chase after tag-team gold in order to bring respectability to women’s wrestling in the testosterone-driven, male-ego-ruled SAWF. Their tag-team, Yin/Yang, was one of the most memorable in the fed’s history... they were the only two-woman tag-team to win the championships. Liz had also been a longtime member of the faction called, ‘The New Breed,’ well-learned on its philosophies of ‘The Hunt.’ "More like middle of it," Liz said slyly, referring to the time difference between them. Vermont was on Eastern Time, and the time in Vermont was currently 10 PM. "How’d you like the crate of pears?" Katie took a big bite out of one of those pears and chewed a little more mechanically and noisily than was normal for her, in an exaggerated gesture of just how delicious she considered them. "Turn on ye image receiver," the diminutive Irishwoman said with a bit of a giggle. Liz pressed the proper button sequence on her phone and received the images Katie took, and then sent, of herself eating the pear. "Glad you like them," she smiled. "So... Dangerous Games. Sounds like exactly the kind of pay-per-view event I’d have loved to take part in." Katie nodded in fond reminiscence. "Sure and I know ye’d take names and kick arses," she said. "Perhaps we can chat about a little short story I’ve been reading, to get me into the right mindset..." How I wish that there were more Than the twenty-four hours in the day! But even if there were forty more, I wouldn't sleep a minute away! It was a full hour later when the conversation was winding down. Both women could hear the old edge, the old hunter conviction coming back into Katie’s voice. "Lock and load," Liz teased. "Going to be a lot of victims at Dangerous Games." "Count on it, lassie... count on it," Katie smiled. "And aye... ye can call me Rainsford if we talk again between now and Dangerous Games." "I probably will," Liz replied. "No stopping until the last enemy is demolished." "Thank ye for the talk, Liz," Katie said. "Sure and let’s get together for dinner some time in October. Perhaps at me place? Bring Rickson and Paolo." "Paolo I’ll have to ask, he’s always been pretty loyal as far as keeping the ranch running," Liz said about her faithful manservant. "But you’re on." Oh, the black jack and poker and the roulette wheel, A fortune won and lost on ev'ry deal! All you need's a strong heart and a nerve of steel, Viva Las Vegas, Viva Las Vegas! "Is the city truly that bad a place, Katie?" Denise asked innocently the next morning as she helped the Monarch Butterfly pack her bags. Discussion among the Rottonbottom family throughout breakfast had shifted subjects to the city of Las Vegas, the site of the Dangerous Games PPV. Both Katie and Percival had been especially adamant that the family not go with her to Vegas. Katie looked thoughtful for a second before answering Denise resolutely. "No... ‘tis worse. For I to even speak of half the things ye can find in Las Vegas, in front of ye, Denise... me own Da would take the strap to me as bad as that morn I got distracted while watching baby Nancy." Denise shivered delicately and acquiesced to her adoptive mother’s wishes without further debate. She remembered well the family’s stories of baby Nancy, a young colt that had kept wandering too close to a patch of poisonous mushrooms that had started growing on the outskirts of the farm. The day before the necessary fungal remover agents arrived at the farm for Lucas and his farmhands to use in destroying the mushrooms, Katie had been watching the colt one morning... and became distracted by the sight of a small ant colony industriously bringing food into their stores. Nancy had eaten the small patch of mushrooms, and died a few hours later, despite the best ministrations of a veterinarian. Nancy was to be given to Katie as a Christmas present that December... what she got was a beating that would leave her unable to sit comfortably for the whole of the next day. It was two years before she was again trusted to even handle a horse on her own, without supervision. "What makes it so bad?" Denise now wanted to know. Percival sat down on the bed and motioned for Denise to sit beside him. "Most Americans are quite proud of the city, actually," he admitted. "But remember that Katie, and both of your parents, and your grandparents, are all devout Catholics. Most of the family is, in fact. Las Vegas is a city that freely embraces a great many things that the Catholic Church decries most adamantly... including a number of, shall we say, activities... that are illegal by state law in any of the other 49 states." Viva Las Vegas with your neon flashin' And your one-arm bandits crashin'! All those hopes down the drain, Viva Las Vegas turnin' day into nighttime, Turnin' night into daytime! If you see it once, You'll never be the same again... Denise nodded soberly and accepted the wishes of her adoptive parents without further debate. Embracing Catholicism was the one thing Percival had never done for the family he had married into. But the clear respect and support he gave for the values the Mulvane clan held so dearly, was all the young girl needed to hear. Still, it was not yet a minute later when Denise got a suddenly mischievous look on her face and lightly poked Katie in the ribs. "I know a sure way for ye to leave everyone else at Dangerous Games staring up at the ceiling," she said with a big grin. Katie quickly squatted down to get to eye level with Denise. "Sure and let us hear it then, me lassie," she said quickly. Denise’s grin became very sly. "Just imagine that each opponent in Dangerous Games represents one of the things about Las Vegas that the Church condemns so much... and they be standing between ye and the airport that will bring ye home!" Katie laughed appreciatively and ruffled Denise’s hair. "Good idea," she praised her adoptive daughter. The last of Katie’s things were packed and she zipped up her valise. "Sure and I have an hour before we get on the plane, how would ye like a little sing-a-long on the radio?" Denise grinned eagerly and turned on the radio station. Katie was a fan of American ‘oldies,’ as the genre was called, and often tuned into a station that played them. The first song that started playing left both Katie and Percival blushing bright red, making the diminutive Irishwoman look like she’d gotten a very bad sunburn. Denise listened to a few verses, then obligingly shut the radio off. "Maybe a game of Chinese checkers, then," she offered. "I take it that man singing that song wasn’t a Catholic." Katie tugged lightly at her blouse’s collar and smiled a little sheepishly. "Ye could say that," she acquiesced. "1950’s radio wanted to ban him at first, to tell the truth," Percival said, taking the Chinese checkers set off the closet shelf. "Values were different then, and radio hosts thought that his hip movements when he sang... were a little obscene." I'm gonna give it ev'rything I've got, Lady luck please let the dice stay hot! Let me shoot a seven with ev'ry shot! Viva Las Vegas, Viva Las Vegas, Viva Las Vegas! Viva, Viva........ Las Vegas!
