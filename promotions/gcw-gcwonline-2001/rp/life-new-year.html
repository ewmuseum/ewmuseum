Life: New Year

Date: 12/31/93 6 Years Old Fondest Memory: New Years Party 11:00 pm It had been a rushed day. Nothing that set it apart from any of the previous three hundred and sixty four that preceded it. It was somewhat unsettling to go into something blind? just like I’ve done many times before, but this was different. I didn’t quite grasp the concept of celebrating a new year. Nor have I ever managed to stay up for it, let alone be allowed to. 1994 promised to bring me much more excitement and growth than “93 did. My Mom signed me up for swimming lessons that started in the coming summer. Needless to say, I was committed to seeing through this whole New Years thing. This will be my year. I’m so prepared to stay up for it too. I have my juice by my side, my Teddy Grahams as my snack of choice? I’m set! I sat comfortably on the house-famous furry brown carpet. The idiot box was on and I had already made it to eleven in the evening. I wanted to see what all the commotion was about with this ball dropping business. My parents were busy hustling around the house for some reason. My Mom would pop in on occasion and snatch a few veggies “n dip from the assorted snack platter on the coffee table. I remember trying to get their attention. “Mom” Dad?” I turned and blurted. No one bothered to respond so I posed my questions anyways. “What is the ball and why does it dwop?” I asked curiously. Again, I was ignored for the sake of something unknown. If only I had a Yen for every time that happened; I’d have at least one American dollar. I sighed and turned my attention back to the television. My little brother Clarky was firmly strapped into his baby rocker thing-a-majig off to the side. I thought I’d engage the little champ in a conversation even though he’d been seemingly sleeping for the past dozen hours. “Clarky. Hey, Clarky. Are you awake?” I whispered, “What this ball dropping business” I hear it works it way down the shaft. You know anything?” Of course all I got were coos. I always knew I could put a crowd to sleep, but never this quick. Ell oh ell. Anyways, I unhappily sat there. No one was paying attention to me. What was I to do? I ate some Teddy Grahams as I pondered to myself. Why do people make such a big deal out of the New Year? Do they really think it creates changes? I mean, all that happens is we add one more number to the end of the count. Nothing really changes. It isn’t really that special. I think it’s just an excuse for people start fresh? again. “Clyde?” My Mom shouted as she rumbled down the stairs for about the billionth time that evening, “Clyde” Oh there you are.” “I haven’t moved from this spot for like the last hour Mommy. What’s up” Is everything otay?” I asked. My Mom started loading dishes into the dishwasher as she instructed me. “Seeing as it’s the first time you’re staying up for the New Year, your Father and I have tried to make it extra special. We think you should join the family tradition,” she spoke, “That’s why we’ve been rushing around getting ready.” I bit my lower lip and glanced over at Clarky and then the clock. It read 11:30 pm. I was curious as to what exactly my parental units were planning, so I went along with it. “Otay. What is it” I wuv surprises!” I poked. My Mom waved me over into the dinning room. Yes! The dinning room. The one room in the house that has nearly never been lived in. In my whole life, I might have only gone in there say two or three times. So I got up and followed her into the dinning room and noticed my Dad sitting at the head of the table. Two chairs accompanied each of his sides. My Mom walked over and sat to his left and I walked up and sat in the only free spot. “Hi Clyde,” My Dad warmly welcomed, “Now” every year in our family, all the grown ups in the house have a New Years toast.” “They eat bwead?” I asked naively. “Haha” not quite my son,” My Dad joked, “We have a glass of fine wine, or something that’s equivalent to it. It all started back when I was a teenager and my Grandfather died on New Years Eve. He would always request rather expensive drinks when he went out to dinner with my Grandmother. So it’s just a sign of remembrance and tradition. And we want to include you Clyde.” Little did I know, but it was also commonplace for many families, couples and partiers to do this for New Years. But I always thought our toast was extra special because we were remembering a loved one. But this would be the first time in my whole entire life that I drank alcohol. It would also be the last? “Grab the wine honey,” My Dad directed. My Mom slid out of her chair and walked over to the glimmering mahogany display case at the end of the room. She opened the window door as it creaked like an old wooden floor. My Mom returned to her seat with a bottle as my Dad set up three drinking glasses. I feel like a rich boy sitting atop a throne with his two servants about to toast to the victory our army secured. I’ve always wondered what alcohol tastes like? My Dad ripped the label off and popped the cork. The steaming scream of mist poured out from the tip. Never before have I felt so distinguished; so important. I actually thought for a minute that I was more important than my baby brother. “Here you go hun,” My Mom said as she handed me a glass of a peachy colored liquid, “To nineteen ninety-four!” “To nineteen ninety-fow!” I shouted as we all raised our glasses. CLING! And with a quick swig, the stingy syrup went down the hatch. “Hu-ka-ka-ka!” I coughed radically as I swallowed the goop, “That was terrible” can I have some more?” My parents laughed sarcastically as they sipped from their golden-rimmed glasses. “You’re suPpose to sipp itttt siLLLLly,” My Mom said. Whoa? whut?”” Whut dud you jussay? Man that wasss just dreadful. I’ve nuvur tasted anything like that before. Whut’s happening? Everything became wavy. I couldn’t see straight for some odd reason. It wasn’t really my parents? fault. No one really knew what alcohol would do to me. It turned into one scary night, that’s for sure. BLIP! “Urrrrrrrrrgh” where am I?” I questioned. I looked around. Everything seemed so red. I was standing just outside of the dinning room. I wobbled forward a slight bit. I noticed something shiny in my left hand? the bottle of wine? What the heck was going on? BLIP! I was right by the television. It read 11:52 pm. Eight minutes. Only eight to go. But I just could not identify with what I was doing. I stood by my brother Clarky’s baby swinger. I drooled on him when I looked down and noticed him crying. I couldn’t hear it though. Everything was muffled and hot. BLIP! Sirens? Police sirens? I hear them. But why? Ohhhhh, I don’t feel so good. I was so cold at this point. It probably didn’t help that I was out on the back porch holding my brother by his legs past the railing. I remember that a sudden wave of heat poured out of the house and then it happened. My Dad came roaring through the flames and grabbed my brother and I. He dragged us through the flaming house and out through the front stoop. I couldn’t focus. I wanted to know what happened so badly? and why I couldn’t handle myself. BLIP! “Son” You alright son?” A strange voice echoed, “You inhaled a ton of smoke. Can you speak?” I slowly opened my eyes and saw a man in front of me wearing a bright read fire hat. I touched my face and nothing but black crud came crumbling off of it. “Wh-what happened?” I asked. “Well son, your parents illegally gave you alcohol and from what they saw, you went on a rampage and started a fire,” The fireman informed me. “What” But? how?” I asked confused. The fireman took a step back. I looked from where I was lying, and I saw my parents coupled together. My Dad was consoling my hysterical Mother. I felt so bad. “Son” you might not know this, but you have a certain medical condition. And little did your parents know, but the alcohol that you were given triggered something inside of you. You were thrown into a fit of rage and somehow used the stove to make a fireball from the wine bottle. I’ve seen a lot of strange things in my day, but a six year old son taking his family ransom is on a whole other level,” He said. “Is everyone otay?” I asked. “Your brother” we’re not sure of,” He replied. I laid my head back down. What have I done? Why did this happen to me? Questions swirled my head but none bigger than this one? “What time is it?” I asked. “12:30 son,” He said. Happy it wasn’t; New Year, it was.
