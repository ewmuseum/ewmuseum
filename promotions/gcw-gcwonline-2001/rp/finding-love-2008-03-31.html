Finding... love?

31 March 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 31st, 2008 As selfishness and complaint pervert the mind, so love with its joy clears and sharpens the vision Last Time In his third visit to his psychiatrist, Chris Bagwell unveiled the truth about what happened after his father's passing. In a sudden turn of events, he witnessed the tragic passing of his aunt. His aunt's death came as a surprise not only to him, but to the entire family. After the autopsy was done, Chris learned that his aunt had been suffering from cancer throughout her entire body for over a year. She, however, never wanted to suffer through chemotherapy and thus never acted upon it. Chris later learned that she never told anyone of her illness because she didn't want anyone to live life any differently. Her sincerity has sinced lived on in Chris' mind. In what seemed to be a downward spiral in Chris' life, he also unveiled the ill fate of his grandmother. At the funeral, she'd suffered a heart attack when seeing her first-born in the casket. It was her third of five children that she had to bury. Her heart merely couldn't take any more. After returning home from the hospital where his grandmother was taken, Chris walked in to his mother ... hanging. She, too, had grown tired of the pain and the aching and decided that she wanted to join the rest of her family in eternal rest. Chris, beside himself, brought himself to a near suicide. He lifted a gun to his head, with all intentions of pulling the trigger. But he never did. His parents were gone. His brother went missing. And his grandmother joined hands with God, after dying of (what he calls) a broken heart. ---- Psychiatrist Office, with Dr. Erin The large hand on the clock had just struck five when Erin let out a huge sigh of relief. Her day was finally over, and the weekend was about to begin. "God, I'm so glad this day is through," she mumbled to herself. "Rough day?" Startled, Erin jumped up from her paperwork and glared at the doorway to her office. She locked eyes with Chris Bagwell, who'd just stepped in. "Am I too late," he asked. She smirked at him. "You know, my office hours do end at five o'clock." Chris turned his head around, looking for a time schedule posted somewhere. "Where's it say that?" Smiling, and shaking her head, Erin replies, "Oh shush. Just come in and shut the door." Chris complies and gently closes the door behind him. He walks towards the sofa in the center of the room. The very sofa that has served as the stimulant for his recollection of his past memories. He contemplates taking a seat but quickly convinces himself otherwise. "You know, I can come back another time if you were on your way out..." he says with concern. Erin shuffles through some paperwork, trying to organize everything for Monday morning. She doesn't respond right away. "Erin?" She snaps her attention towards Chris. "Wait, what?" Laughing, he says, "I'll just come back another time when you're not about to leave." Erin tosses her paperwork aside and quick steps past the desk, extending her arm as if she were reaching for Chris' arm. "No wait!" He turns back towards her and she throws her arm down, slightly embarressed. "Really, it's not a big deal. Sit. I'm really in no rush." Chris nods his head and smirks. He brushes by Erin and sits on the sofa. She glances back towards her desk, staring down all the paperwork. She sighs and makes a face of worry out of Chris' sight. She knows she's got a ton to do, but seemingly can't find it in her heart to send him away. Erin pulls up her chair and takes a seat. "So I caught a glimpse of your fighting the other day, on television," she says with a sparking interest. "Don't poisen your mind with that stuff," commands Chris. Erin rolls her eyes. "Oh stop. I didn't realize how intense that kind of stuff gets. I'm going to go out on a limb and assume that its been where you've channeled all your anger over the past several years?" Chris shrugs his shoulders. Erin continues, "So what are you going to do when you're done with me? I mean, you and I have spoken about a lot in only three sessions. I can't imagine that there is too much more bottled up inside of you. Won't that ruin your drive in the ring?" Chris starts shaking his head, "Since when did you gain such an interest in me?" Erin stutters, trying to think up a response. Chris interrupts, "Life will always throw curveballs. There will always be pain. There will always be suffering. And there, for damn sure, will always be death. I'll just continue to feed off of the misfortunes of our society to fuel me in the ring." "You've got a very pessimistic view of our society's progression." "Should I not?" Chris rebutts. "Its true that our country isn't at the highest of times, but its not to say that things aren't going to get any better. Look at all the technological advancements we make daily. Or even the cures for different diseases that are constantly being discovered," Erin argues. "The technological advancements aren't necessarilly making our society better, Erin. As a matter of fact, kids are becoming fatter and lazier. People, in general, don't know the values of walking to the market or spending time outdoors. Hell, there's a new generation that can't figure out how in the hell to add or subtract without having a calculator in their pocket." Chris pauses, giving Erin a chance to speak. Just as she parts her lips, he continues, "People's lives revolve around cell phones and instant messages. Without those things, nobody has any indication what their best friend's phone number is or even how to get to a local hospital. When's the last time you looked at a map, other than the snipit they give you on mapquest?" Erin again remains silent, assuming the rhetoric in Chris' question. Seeing that she isn't saying anything, Chris goes on. "And as for disease and finding cures. That's just a bunch of bullshit. How many millions of dollars a year are donated to cancer research institutions so a bunch of doctors can research a cure? And you mean to tell me that they haven't come up with ANYTHING yet?" Chris' face starts glowing red and his eyes hot with passion. "These alledged researchers are driving around in Ferrari's and just fucking with the American people. They're taking our money and whiping their asses with it. How is it that everytime some national figure or some primetime celebrity gets diagnosed with cancer, they get cured instantly with little to no side effects?" Erin lifts her hand trying to butt in, but Chris ignores her. "Yet I've sat there and watched half my family suffer and drop dead from the same GOD DAMN disease. We've went to the best cancer hospitals in the tri-state area. But because my father was a retired cop instead of some superstar basketball player, those super cures were nowhere to be found. You tell me what that basketball star did to help America? He didn't do shit. He got paid multimillions to play with a rubber ball for a few years." Erin finally fires back, "But Chris, he probably also had the money to travel across the country for cures and to try all different types of medication." "Don't be so naive, Erin. The second one of them are diagnosed, the doctors fly out to wherever the hell these so called 'stars' are and treat them. Its just a sick ploy to make Americans think that we're making all this progress on cancer treatments. Next thing you know, you've got all these celebrities endorsing all these foundations to milk us for more money. Meanwhile these research foundations are doing NOTHING for the average American. NOTHING!" Chris finishes his rant, flopping himself back in the sofa. He sinks his body into the pillows and gleams up at the ceiling. Erin just patiently stares at him, waiting for him to explode again. An uncomfortable silence encompasses the room. He tilts his head towards Erin, locking eyes again. "I'm sorry for blowing up like that," he says softly. "Don't be sorry, Chris." "I didn't mean to just explode on you like that. I'm just so fed up with the lies that surround our country and our lives." Erin lifts herself from her chair and takes a seat next to Chris, on the couch. She lays her right hand on his thigh, and looks into his eyes. Quietly, she says, "I'm here for you to vent your mind, Chris. If I wasn't willing to listen, then this wouldn't be my career. And I definitely wouldn't have let you sit down at five o'clock and make a dent in my weekend plans." Chris nods his head, showing understanding. "So," Erin says, "what else do you have on your mind? You never got to telling me what happened after you put the gun down..." Chris turns his head away from Erin, breaking the gaze between the two. He stares back up at the ceiling, gradually closing his eyes. As he seals them shut, he mutters, "life actually started to get better..." ---- Perspective Shift. Flashback. After my mother hung herself, I was a complete mess. As one would expect, I wanted nothing to do with life anymore. I'd lost both of my parents in a matter of months. And, to top it off, I lost another aunt and my grandmother. My brother was missing. To this day, I've yet to see him. I haven't the slightest indication as to where he went or what he's been doing. I always knew he was an emotionally guy, but I couldn't imagine how he'd survive on the streets alone. He could be dead, for all I know. But I've got this inkling inside of me that keeps telling me he's alive. It's like a bond that you share with your siblings. Its almost as if a part of you is in each of them .. and vice versa. There are still nights that I can feel a heart beat inside of me. A heart that isn't mine. Sometimes I even have dreams that I'm walking the streets along the other side of the country. Doing things I can't remember doing and seeing places I can't recall ever visiting. I've come to believe that its some supernatural way for my parents to tell me that my brother is still out there. He's still alive. He's still well. I had a closed casket service for my mother and grandmother. It was extremely toned down version of the previous two funerals, and I made it a point not to be at the viewing. I shared my final moments with each, alone. I couldn't bare to see anyone else in the family in suffering. I couldn't bare to handle the questions and I certainly didn't want anymore sympathy. I was sick of hearing people tell me they were sorry. I was sick of hearing that my family was in a better place. If they're in a better place, why couldn't I be with them? What made me so bad that I had to stay here, by myself? I was tired of the empty promises of people telling me to call them if they could do anything for me. Ninety eight percent of the time, they'd just ignore the call anyway. Nobody wanted to hear of my troubles. Although they may have all felt sad, nobody actually cared what was going through my head. Why would they want to be bothered with even more grief? I was done with the whole death thing and swore to myself not to allow anyone to ever get close to me again. Thankfully, my apathy made the process tremendously easier than I would've ever anticipated. I was the man of the house. But I let the house fall apart beneath my feet. The big bad wolf huffed and he puffed... and I watched him blow my house down. I went from the man of the house to a complete and utter desheveled mess. The next several months of my life were painstaking. I was strapped for cash and loaded with bills and a mortgage payment that I wasn't in any position to take on. I spent several days without sleep, trying to figure out what I was going to do with everything. Would I sell the house or keep it? What about all their stuff? And their cars? It wasn't until four or five months later that I got a phone call from my father's long-time friend and his accountant. He'd just heard the news about my entire family and seemed just as heartbroken as I was, myself. It was then that he shed some light onto my father's hidden fortune. His words replay through my mind every single day. "Chris. You're inheriting two million dollars." It was the best thing that had happened to me in my life. Apparently, my father had held on to an apartment complex he had owned and had just sold it off months before the housing market really started to plummet. The money had been stashed away in a seperate high yield savings account. Two million dollars, after capital gains tax, doesn't equate to nearly that much. Nevertheless, it was a glimmer of hope in my otherwise soggy life... ---- Perspective Shift. Chris breaks his stare at the ceiling and peers back over towards Erin. "And that's my life." Erin's face goes blank. "That's it?" "Yep." Unbelieving, Erin pushes for more, "So you inherit money from your father and ... life is grand?" "You got it." "Chris, you're doing exactly what I worried about you doing from the beginning." "What would that be?" he questions. "You were doing so well ... and now you're dancing around your story." "Listen Rebecca, I've told you a great chunk of my life. Things I've never revealed to anyone ever..." Suddenly, Chris' expression drops. Erin says, confused, "Rebecca? Chris, my name is Erin." "Yeah," he mumbles. "I know what your name is. I just got worked up and said that accidently." "Who is Rebecca, Chris?" inquires Erin. Chris throws himself back against the sofa once again. He remains silent, ignoring Erin's question. "Chris," she insists. "Who is Rebecca?" Chris squeezes his eyes shut, as if he were trying to erase her memory from his mind. "I don't want to talk about it," he responds. He begins to lay his hands over his face, his breathing quickly increasing. Erin reaces out and pulls his right hand away. A single tear rolls out from Chris' eye and down his cheek. Erin's face explodes in shock. She leans in close and whispers, "Please Chris. Do it for yourself..." Chris slowly cracks his eyelids open, titling his head towards Erin. Their faces come close, noses nearly rubbing together. With his eyes barely cracked open, he whispers back to her, "She's the reason I thought I could never fall in love again..." There's a momentary silence. "...until I met you." The words passed through his lips with an immaculate passion. In a single moment, Erin's eyes closed and her head feel forward, only to be caught by Chris' lips. His barely opened eyelids shut as he leaned in, reciprocating the kiss. Chris gently wraps his hands along Erin's cheeks, embracing her face for a more intimate kiss. She runs her hands softly along his inner thighs, touching up into his lower abdomen. Their mouths open and tongues slow dance to the sound of a popular love song. Erin finally pulls her head slightly back from Chris. A small smile sneaks onto her face, giving her a look of satisfaction. Both Chris and Erin slowly open their eyes, locking in a gaze almost immediately. "There's never a right time," Chris says calmly, "to say goodbye." Erin nods her head as Chris stands from the couch. Her hands run down his leg as he steps away and walks towards the door. Erin doesn't move, but instead stares off into the empty space where Chris once sat. He begins opening the door, but abruptly stops. Turning his head towards Erin, Chris says, "Thank you. For everything." Without looking at him, she nods her head. He continues, "You've done so much to help me. You've changed my life. I don't know how I could ever repay you." Chris walks through the door, closing it silently behind him. The camera pans back to Erin's face. Tears roll from her eyes as her song of love slowly fades to silence. ---- Scene Shift: Outside the Building Chris walks up towards a black 2003 Dodge Viper with black tinted windows. As he gets near, the car starts automatically. But instead of walking to the driver's side door, he walks up towards the passenger side door. He climbs inside the car to be greeted by the driver. "Well? How'd it go in there?" Chris smirks and responds, "One more visit and I can bag that bitch like your afternoon groceries!" The driver turns his head in Chris' direction, laughing.. "Did you do your homework on the information I gave you?" asks Chris. "Ohh boy," sighs the driver. "Did I ever." "And?" Chris questions. "Kincaid should be over there now, marking her out," the driver explains. "Perfect. Its time to hit this Scotsman in every way we can." The two laugh maniacally to themselves as the car speeds off. The license plate reads "WESLEY E". ---- Scene Shift: Tallahassee Kiljadin Kincaid is shown in a black hoodie and a pair of cross training lightweight sweats standing outside the door to an industrial office building. He glances up at the clock, which is fixated in the building, and then back at his cell phone. A sudden beeping informs him of a new text message. Kincaid flips open the phone to read the message. A grin immediately develops on his face as he changes his focus to a directory board of offices. The camera zooms in to one particular office slat on the board. The name on it reads "JANE MURRAY". Fade.
