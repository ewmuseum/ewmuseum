Home for the Holidays

22 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11-22-06 'Yeah, Lo, you missed it girl! Show was kickin'! Not exactly The X, but I'd say the budgets all thrice over, ya know? Caldera dude ain't half-bad a slice either. Gonna like it here I think.' Reivyn sat before the gates of the massive Ft. Lauderdale estate, her powerful, bare legs holding up the large bike beneath her. It was quite warm for the day before Thanksgiving, even, and Rei, dressed in her stock short-shorts, halter top, and leather jacket, had no problem with that. Sighing, wit the phone still to her ear, she tapped the heel of one of her black biker boots impatiently, suddenly yelling. 'HEY, YO! ANSWER THE DAMN 'COM, JERKS!' She leaned over, slapping a gloved hand down on the metal intercom stand, before suddenly cringing as a pitchy yell came across the phone to her ear. 'Ow, damn, Lo,' Rei exclaimed, glaring at the phone for a moment. 'Nah, was yelling at the security at this place. As if seeing my pops the first time in years since I ran away is going to be bad enough, I'm getting a damn hassle now. Need to say my peace and get back for training so I can whip this big bastard they're making me fight.' 'Excuse me, ma'am?' A man's voice suddenly sounded from the intercome, causing Rei to roll her eyes and mouth the word 'finally'. 'Yo, Lo, I gotta jet. I'll call ya later, girl. Take it easy.' 'Hey, Scotty? Issat you, brah?' Reivyn asked with a laugh, turning to face the camera set into the intercome as she removed her shades. A bit cautious, the voice responded, 'Yes ma'am, this is Scott, how may we help you?' 'Dude, c'mon, lemme in. It's the holidays and I gotta couple of parents to visit!' There was a long moment of silence. Reivyn blinked, then glanced to the gate, expecting it to open, and then back to the intercom. With still no answer, she sighed and began to speak, but was soon cut off by Scotty's voice. 'M...Miss Reila?' 'Haha, yeah,' Rei responded with a light chuckle. 'It's Reivyn now, but you can call me Reila still, dude.' 'Oh, wow. Cody's going to flip, Reila. It's been nearly five years. I..I..Yes, come in. You remember the estate, right?' 'Like it was yesterday.' There was a light buzz at the gate as it began to slide open. 'Good, good... Your father is playing a game of pool in the recreational hall, shall I alert him of your presence?' Reivyn fired up the noisy Texas Chopper, smiling lightly and shaking her head. Despite the butterflies fluttering in her stomach, she kept her composure and responded over the noise of the bike, 'Nah dude, gonna surprise 'em!' And with cautious speed, she began up the long drive, her eyes taking in the familiar scenery of her childhood. It only intensified the butterflies in her gut, and for a brief moment, the fleeting thought in her mind passed in which she doubted her reasons for running away from all of this. She was a success of her own now, though. A famous auto garage in Tokyo, a prestiguous kickboxing career that was far from over when she left it behind, and now a growing career in the wrestling industry. Of course, her poppa wouldn't have to know about the whole Saikyo System/Yakuza thing, no matter how much it was making her. 'No regrets, girl,' she mumbled to herself as she hopped from the bike, starting up the stairs past the unfamiliar security. 'He'll respect me, he always respects independence...' ------------------------ The inside of the large estate appeared largely unchanged. Cody Mikona was probably the only man in the world who would take a celebrity home and decorate it from top to bottom in Native American influenced art and furnishings. A small smile crept over her lips, as she was sure her room was still untouched. If only Lotus could see it; the stuffed animals, and cute posters, right next to a framed Table of Elements.... Reivyn would never live that one down if so. She made her way to the back side of the estate, a rather large, wide open room filled with all sorts of childish entertainment from arcade games, to pool tables and a jukebox, which coincidently, was playing a few country tunes, the preferred music of her father. Speaking of whom... A middle aged man, tall and dark, stood over a pool table, aiming his shot. his shoulder length hair was well kept, and the toned arms of the native-american man showed he was prone to keeping himself in good shape. Reivyn's heart sunk into the pit of her stomach. Slowly she stepped forward on the carpetted floor, and had attempted to keep herself as quiet as possible. She did well in closing the gap, and just upon the verge of speaking, she found herself unable; confronting her father was simply not as easy as she had figured it would be. 'Been there long?' His voice suddenly questioned, forcing Reivyn to nearly jump from her boots. Slowly he walked around the table, as if he had no care of her presence. He readied his shot, and then glanced up at her, but was unable to continue. He stood up straight and leaned against the game, the sigh passing his lips almost seeming to be one of relief. 'Almost five years, Reila. At least you're still in good health.' Reivyn just nodded her head, and took in a deep breath. She was about to cry, and was doing everything she could to hold it in. 'H..hi... Daddy...' She quietly whimpered out, but said little else, pretty much unable to. Cody had a smirk etched into his features - as if he knew straight up that she was about to pass out from the nervousness. His coming words were sure the change that pace however, but to him, they had to be said. 'Hi, daddy? That's quite the weak statement coming from Yakuza muscle, wouldn't you say?' Reivyn blinked, and was taken off-guard by her father's statement. 'H..how did you?' 'I don't think the question is so much, how did I, as much as it should be, 'why did you?'. But I understand, sometimes a young woman on her own has to make difficult choices of right and wrong, and the choice you made wasn't all that bad. You did get a really lucrative business from it all.' Cody's thoughts were correct. Reivyn didn't care how he knew about her current life, but she was rather upset that a man with a not-so-clean past would dare judge her for her current choice of a lifestyle. 'I'm being lectured on my morals from a man who underhandedly deals with the mafia,' she flatly questioned. 'Good game, poppa, good game.' 'I understand what I'm doing, Reila,' he sharply retorted. 'I have the financial backing and manpower to handle a mistake. God forbid, you mess up once, and they'll be taking your fingers, or even worse!' Reivyn sighed, and gave a shake of her head. After making her way over to a highly-oversized totem pole decoration and leaning upon it, she glared back at her father. 'Apparently, I do know what I'm doing, poppa. I'm in great health, and very successful now, and i did it on my own.' Cody laughed, sharply, 'Oh yes, I forgot. My daughter, the VALEDICTORIAN of her graduating class, put all her smarts into the pot and is now a successful gangster, because some Yakuza had a hard-on for her and tossed a god-damned bone her way!' 'I EARNED MY KEEP,' she suddenly shouted in protest! 'I got myself out of the bad hole I fell into when I left here! A..And I asserted myself! I got a job with The X, and then began working with the Saikyo for really good pay! And I helped, I adapted and became one of them! i put my smarts and my health to use - and I helped them earn what they're upon today!' She shoved off of the statue and had began to pace the floor, her eyes red and flooding with tears. She didn't expect him to be happy with her, but she had at least hoped he might commend her for her success. 'Everything I have is something I've earned! I own my garage, clean and clear, it's legit! My fight career was all earned, I didn't slam no head like a bitch to get my title, and I'm not doing it with the GCW now! Don't lecture me on good business, poppa, you're not clean at all! You know why mom left!' She groaned and closed her eyes, 'Man, fuck this, brah. I'm out of here. Saikyo's my family now.' Cody stared, blank in expression. She had definitely grown way more brave and independent than she was come the day she ran away. She was right, though; he was never the cleanest of business men, and his only right in lecture was fear of her ending up in a predicament he had seen before. Almost failing, as she turned to stomp away, he swallowed the lump in his throat, and spoke. 'We're back together, actually, Reila...' Reivyn paused. The worst memory of her life was the the day her mother got in that cab, moving to a home on the other side of the city, away from her husband, and more painfully to both, her own daughter. Rei had slowly turned to face her father during her thoughts, her reddened eyes squinted as she stared him down. 'Reila,' he quietly sighed. 'I know you're a success. But, damn it! Nearly five years, and you've not once even called or wrote home to let anyone know you were ok! I think it's my right to be a bi tupset, and biting on your choice of lifestyle, no matter how much it mimics my own. Now just.... Come here, give me a hug worth five years of missing them.' A sharp snort of breath passed from Reivyn. She was upset, and highly confused. She wanted to continue being upset with him, but knew he had every right to be the upset one. This was the perfect chance to make amends however, and she'd be foolish to not do so. After all - she wanted to. 'Daddy...' She whimpered quietly and dragged herself across the floor, sliding her arms around him in a hug powerufl enough to make up for years lost. He held her there for what seemed an eternity; he had missed his only child so much, and wasn't about to let her walk away mad a second time. 'Your room is untouched, and you're welcome there any time, Reila. At least spend the holidays here; the gym can suit you for your wrestling, or whatever that place is. Just don't leave again yet.' Reivyn sniffled, and nodded against Cody's shoulder. No one would mind her being gone a month or two at all. Especially not the Saikyo, who stressed all too often, the importance of blood. ----------------------------- It was nearly an hour that the two had hugged, and spent time discussing the past few years apart. Reivyn was ecstatic to hear that her parents were back together for well over a year now, and happy. She was all to anxious to see her mother again, her last memory being her having to visit each one seperately after giving her graduation speech. Cody soon after had to leave, though, business at the Casino as usual, and Reivyn was left alone in the large estate. She fooled around in the rec-room a bit, and then in her old bedroom, rearranging a few things for her current tastes and going through her old 'dorked-out' wardrobe from back in the day. She did find something that brought a huge grin to her lips, however, that being her old muay thai training shorts and sports top. And that was where she would be now, her hair bound back in a tail, and the outft, albeit slightly smaller on her now, covering the unmentionables of her form with decency, before the large kick-bag in the upstairs gym of the large estate. 'Fsh! Fsh! K-YAH!' She let her breath out with each punch, and then screamed as she slammed a powerful kick into the side of the bag! Her entire form was drenched with sweat and her breaths were deep - she had been in her trance-like state for nearly two hours now, and the pump in her muscles showed that very much. When there is no fear, my judgement will not be clouded. Her thoughts echoed in her mind as she slammed a forearm, a hook, and a knee into the bag in consecutive motions. My opponent is larger, but my strikes are true. I can kick as high as any man stands. 'Fsh! K-YAH! Fsh! RAH!' She slammed her limbs into the punished bag with force, the creed she kept in mind before every fight playing over and ove rin her mind. Her trainer taught it to her during her career training, and every time, it staved off all doubts and fears within her mind, usually leaving her on top of her opponent, and never once having a bad showing otherwise. My mind is at peace and my body does follow. There is no opponent I can't overcome. She took a step back and lunged, spinning and kicking the bag with enough force to snap the grounding cable, sending it into a full sway. Many of the old masters say your mentality is over two-thirds of your success. It's something Reivyn had always practiced and asserted. Confidence is not arrogance. Arrogance is to lose, confidence is to succeed. I am confident in my ability. She danced around the swaying bag now, treating it as more of a moving opponent than a bag in full sway. She continued to shoot out sharp breaths with her swift punches and brutal kicks, finally beginning to feel the wear on her muscles as she knew her session was coming to an end. 'My, looks like you won't be getting that stun-gun for Christmas.' Reivyn paused, and a smile came almost instantly to her weary lips. Side-stepping the swaying bag, she turned to the source of the voice, eyeing the short, pretty, African-american woman at the door, dressed in a rather cute black dress. Exhaling sharply, Reivyn undid her bag-wraps and stepped forward, speaking through heavy breaths. 'Might wanna get me a stretcher instead, facing a pretty big guy this week.' The woman arched a brow, folding her arms, 'Better be for him, and not my little Rei-Rei.' 'You know us Mikonas never give up, momma.' The two shared a smile that was freakishly similar. Reivyn hesitated to hug her, it was such a pretty dress. Her mother was having none of that though, and forced the sweat-drenched girl down into what could very well be another hour long hug. 'We've so much talking to do, Rei-Rei. You hungry?' 'Still have a pound of beef and a bag of veggies to eat, momma, let's go.' Mrs. Mikona closed her eyes and chuckled. She was never too sure of her daughter's reasonings behind such physical fitness, but was more than proud of her daughter - soon revealing that night that she had every single fight of hers recorded, even those few agonizing defeats upon the scorecard. 'Now what's this about a car garage in Japan, I always wanted to go to Japan, and you never....' The mother's voice trailed off as she kept her hug upon her daughter, the two disappearing down the long hallway of the house. A disaster averted for Reivyn, her family life repaired for the time being, with an understanding father, and a mother who didn't even care, she was just glad to see her baby girl again.
