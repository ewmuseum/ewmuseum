Grace, too

1 April 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/01/11 The night welcomed thousands as they piled out of the Madison Square Garden. The moon over New York, in between shifting overcast, beamed its silver light to the mass of humanity shuffling off to the subway. There was plenty of shoving to be found, but more chatter over what they had just watched. They talked about how awesome it was to see Murray hold that title high, their conspiracies around Rich Rollins and the ever more general, "that was the best. Show. Ever." Yes, it would seem the city that never sleeps was active again tonight, but there was one in particular that wasn’t to be counted among that crowd. She stood on two and a half inch heels that clacked as she made her way down the corridors, deeper into the labyrinth. Around her were busy stagehands making hurried work of the deconstruction and compartmentalising of all kinds of equipment. It was a slightly worried look dawning beneath those blond bangs when she realised that beyond the simple determination she carried, she had little to no idea where it was she was headed. Her eyes stayed wide as she scanned her surroundings, looking to find someone not too busy lifting or dragging hundred pound boxes. No such person appeared on the horizon, so she made sure to talk with the one rolling something on wheels. "Excuse me?" she begged in a soft, feminine voice. "Hrmm?" he grunted as he came to a halt. Standing back upright, he turned his head to meet her blue eyes. "What… what could I help you with, Miss?" "I was wondering if you’d be able to point me in the right direction. You look like a guy who knows his way around these hallways." He’d gotten lost three time already tonight, but he wasn’t about to go and tell her that now. "I’m sure I can help you get where you are going, Miss…" And the lengthy draw at the end of that last sentence prompted her to say, "Grace. I was looking for my brother. He was one of the wrestlers in the show tonight." "And which one of them might he be?" he asked. He wasn’t about to get the heat for letting in some crazed stalker to the locker room area until he knew who she was. At least, that’s how he hoped this was coming off. And that his glances had gone unnoticed. "Redding. Alex." "Alright, you’ll want to head down this way" he outstretched his finger, "take the first left. It’s a right on the third intersection after that," he said with a subtle smile. "Thank-you," she offered as she started on her new directions, even after thinking that she had just come from there. She could feel his beady eyes follow her as she walked - just as sure as she had noticed them wandering moments earlier - until she turned the corner out of view. Seeing the cinderblock painted blue with red and white stripes reminded her of the arena she skated in when she was a kid. "One, two," she counted the T intersections as she walked. "Three," she turned. The door she was looking for was a few yards in, complete with a sheet of scrap paper marked with ink: ‘Palmer/Redding’. The red painted nails curled into a fist as she stuck her knuckles three times off the oak. She fidgeted with her overcoat as she waited, until the door finally opened. "Ale… you’re not Alex," she greeted Theo Palmer as he stood in the doorway. "And you’re mighty perceptive." And although he smiled, it did more to alarm than calm. She froze for a moment when he reached out. "Ah, if you don’t mind, you are kind of in my way. So, either come inside, or step aside, I don’t quite care which." "Oh, sorry," she said as they started an awkward game of mirror. It was five tries before she stopped and walked a little ways down the hall. "I will see you gents next week. Lawrence, let’s go," were the words that trailed behind as Palmer made his way to the parking lot, followed shortly by his older brother Lawrence. "Who’s there?" came a voice from inside the room. Content that she was finally not a roadblock, Grace stepped up and filled the doorframe once more. "Oh, hey, Gracie!" the surprised reaction of her big brother. "I see you have met Theo," and there was a pause. "Come in here," he stood with bare arms outstretched. His hair was still wet from the shower, but he was decent enough to have pulled on his blue jeans. "Should I…?" she asked, enquiring if maybe he’d want some time to throw on a shirt. Only, he still stood with his arms wide open. With a heave of the shoulders she walked toward the hug. "And what brings my little sister all the way to New York City? Just to she her rotten brother?" he managed to spit out without biting the tongue in his cheek. Pulling away from the five-seconds-too-long hug, Grace fixed her hair before responding. "I was in town for a fashion show and I thought I’d come by, visit you at work." Taking a few steps back to a comfortable conversation distance, she took the opportunity to compare these locker rooms to a backstage of a runway show. She noted how empty it all looked, just benches, lockers and a CRT TV hanging from the ceiling. How could she have known that it had to be this empty to fit in all the egos? "So, what did you think?" he says with a stutter with his head jostling between towel strokes. "It was a lot more exciting than any fashion show I’ve ever been to, that’s for sure." She bit her glossed lip for a minute before continuing, "a lot longer, too." "You’re only saying that because they left the best part of this company off the show," piped up Grady Patrick, who had been routing through his bag for God-knows-what, but had giving up. "And where are my manners?" Alex says through a grin. "Grady, this is my sister Gracie. Gracie, this is the greatest swindler and scumbag you’d ever hope to come across. And I say that with the outmost of affection." Alex peered over toward his manager to note Grady’s look caught between anger and pride. "Definitely the type of guy you want on your side." "Enchante," Grady said as he took Grace’s hand in a delicate shake. It was the extent of French that he’d feel comfortable using around a girl he wasn’t paying by the hour. "I just need a few moments to gather everything together here. Grady, earn you keep for once; try to keep my li’l sister entertained? And, no. Even think anything like that and, after your funeral, I’ll have to find myself a new manager," Red warned before turning his back to the small disaster of clothes and gear piled around his gym bag. "Come on Gracie, let’s leave the boy to his messes," Grady took the lead out to the hallway, dragging his luggage in tow. Grace followed shortly behind. "So Grady, how long have you known my brother?" she got the dialogue started. "Shit, it must be," he started, unrolling his fingers to count the years, "five? Yeah, five years now. You know I was the one who trained him?" "No, he never mentioned it." The scruffy beard pulled down into a frown had her quickly follow up with, "but it’s not like he talks about his job much. Or maybe he does? I can’t quite say. We," she swallowed the bitter words first to her tongue, "we don’t see each all that often, you know?" "All too well," he assured. "This business has a way of stressing relationship with distance. Perfect for only children with deep seeded fears regarding relationships, but for most, it’s a killer. You put all this time and effort into this job, but when you are done, you are just a broken man, with no one to lean on," he soothsaid, staring ten thousand feet past the floor to the core of the earth. "I," she started, trying not to picture her brother as that lonely, broken man, "I’m sure you’ll all have someone waiting for you at the end." It was an awkward ten seconds between subjects. "So, how have you liked the Big Apple so far?" he changed the subject. "Oh, it has been fun. Plenty of shopping done, just haven’t been out to any good parties," she said with a sigh of regret. "Party, huh?" he chuckled to himself. He revelled in the conversation he knew Alexander would never hear about. "I should have figured if you were related to Red you’d be into the bar scene," he said with a look in his eyes that begged her to ask for any of his half dozen stories regarding Alex and his adventures in the taverns of North America. "Not much of a drinker, really," she countered. It half knocked him for a loop. Maybe they were step-siblings. Maybe Red was adopted. It wasn’t until Grady opened his coat and fished through a pocket that he threw the counter punch. "You seem like a good kid," he started. "Wouldn’t want you having to deal with any of the scum you’d find on the streets here. Take this," he offered as he popped the cap to his prescription bottle. In among the dozen pills was one hand rolled marijuana cigarette. "What, really?" she said in a surprised voice. She didn’t bother to ask how he knew; only saying "thank-you." "For Mr. Redding’s sister? Think nothing of it. But you have to do me one favour," he paused to see her look back up from her purse, "if he finds you with that, he cannot know that I gave it to you. He already bitches enough at me for doing the stuff, if he found out that I gave you a joint? I’d be pushing up daisies for sure." She gave a wink, her gesture of consent, before pointing out that Alex was approaching. "Sorry about that," he said rejoining the group. "You hungry, Gracie?" "I think I have regained my appetite after watching that fat guy gorging himself on nachos and foot-longs, yeah. You know any places around here?" "Grady, what was that bar slash restaurant we ate at last week?" "Harrington’s?" "Yeah, that was the one. Sis, it might not be the fanciest place, but the food there was great. Did you want to come with?" "Me?" started Grady. "I can’t. I’ve got a red-eye to catch back to Toronto, plus I wouldn’t want to interrupt your time with family." "So, what scam is it this week?" Redding called. "The same one as always, my friend – a profitable one! Anyways, you two go, have fun. I’ll be in contact. It’s been a pleasure finally meeting you, Gracie," he turned before giving one final delicate handshake and a knowing wink. With all that accounted for, Grady walked away in the opposite direction Alex and Grace would take. ----- "Is this the place where all the wrestlers are hanging out after the show?" she asked not looking at him, but keeping her eyes on the sidewalks and the people they drove past. For his part, Alex inhaled slowly, dancing his eyes between the bright lights that lit the way. He made an effort to change the radio station before answering the question. "Ugh. Who programmed this thing?" he asked cycling through the presets. "What was wrong with that last one? And you still haven’t answered my question," she pressed on. "For one, I refuse to listen to this manufactured pop bullshit. Give me the soulful voice of a guy who’s been through some real shit in his life and a jazz guitar. I mean, what the fuck do I care if you waive your hair back and forth?" And finally satisfied that enough time had passed between the question he was asked and his answer, "No." "What?" she spoke before figuring out its context. "Oh! Why?" This time she tore her attention away from the pedestrians to read her brother’s expressions and sell her own intentions with a pout. "Well, you know how when you are around someone so often that you just want to fight them?" The red light allowed him to make eye contact for this next bit. "Now imagine that it is your job to fight them, too. It doesn’t really make for many friends." "But you hang out with that Palmer dude?" "Who said we hang out? Part of the reason we work so well together is that we stay apart in our down time. Something like how the key to a good marriage is to sleep in separate beds." "And that’s all?" she said sensing he was withholding something from her. She hated when people lied to her. "That’s it." He said, turning his head back to the roads as the break lights in front of him died out. "That, and girls seem to have this strange fascination with mentally fragile guys with decent bodies." "Ah-ha!" the sleuth remarked upon the admission of withheld evidence. "And what makes you think that I’d be one of those girls who want a make-work project of a boyfriend?" "I’m not saying anything. I know you’re a strong kid, but you got to trust when your big brother tells you that you haven’t always had the best taste in men. I mean, weren’t you still going out with Darryl?" "Who?" "Darryl, you know, the guy that you were dating the last time I saw you." "Do you mean David?" "I think it was Darryl," Alex said with the confidence of a photographic memory he didn’t expressly have. "I’m pretty sure I know my own boyfriend’s name. Well, ex-boyfriend, now," she said with a softer voice. "What happened? I don’t have to go beat up this guy now, do I? Because you know I will if I have to," he played the part of the overactive big brother. "No, it’s nothing like that," she assured him. "I was going to be doing more travelling and we," she stopped herself. "I decided it would be best if we broke up." "I think you made the right move, Sis. I mean, what type of person doesn’t correct a person when they call him by the wrong name? Stupid Darryl," he closed with a laugh as he flicked on the right turn signal and made his way into the parking structure. "Oh, are we close?" Grace asked. "As close as this will take us," he answered pulling into a spot and cutting the engine off. He shifted himself about to undo the seatbelt and met Gracie’s unhappy face. "It’s only three blocks from here. Don’t tell me that as a Canadian you are going to complain about above-freezing temperatures for a two minute walk." "I wasn’t thinking about that. I was just wondering why you’d bother to rent a car in a city where there is almost never any parking where you want to get to," that last bit a little muffled as she stepped out of the car. "You know that I get hit in the head for a living, right?" "Uh, duh? That’s becoming a little more obvious with each passing minute," they shared the same sense of humor, and the same silly face they made when they thought they had said something funny. "Yuck, yuck, yuck. Alright Ms. Colbert, let’s get going shall we? I’m getting a bit parched with all this talking." He wouldn’t turn his back to her, wanting to protect her from every scary thing he’d ever heard about taking place in this city, and even the things that didn’t actually take place. All of that played on a loop in the front of his mind. "Hey, let me ask you one thing though." "If you promise it is just one thing, go ahead and ask." "What’s the use of having my big brother be a wrestler if I can’t even meet that cute guy who won the title at the end of the night?" "He’s married. And besides, I’ll tell ya right now Gracie: every guy in that locker room is either a drug-riddled fiend or an outright asshole," he said knowing this to be a plain and simple truth. "But then what does that make you, Alex?" "I don’t do drugs, so you tell me." ----- The restaurant wasn’t all too large, but just spaced well enough that it took a focused effort to eavesdrop on the conversation at the next table. They had a satellite radio station playing lightly and it carried well off the walls of wooden panel and stone. It had a street front view and was filled with wafts from the kitchen carried the odour of various orders. The brother and sister duo only one of ten occupied tables on the second floor that night. A pleasant gentleman named Jack had took their order five minutes ago, and the conversation had stalled as Red was drawn to the Sens/Sabres game on the flat screen and Grace did her best to find interest in looking out the window. The dark streets did little to entertain, only to lead her deep in her own thoughts. ‘I make the effort to see him, and he takes me to a sports bar. Great, just great. I mean, it’s not like I haven’t seen him in a year, or maybe only a handful of times over the past ten years. In ten years there ought to have been something worth talking about. I’ve got stories, just ask me. Oh, come on? A hockey game? As if there aren’t eighty-two games anyway. Senators? Really? The only team more pathetic than the Oilers has stolen your attention away?’ her mind wandered in this type of torrent. It was by the time that she looked back from the window to his reddened chin that she caught him glancing away from the game and back at her. Only, she could never have been sure if he was looking at her, or just in between looking at his draft beer and back to that Godforsaken game. He hated the look on her face. He just didn’t know what to say. It wasn’t like he could boast about the many pranks he’d played on people she’s never heard of, or tell the story of the ever growing collection of scars that marked his body. The game was well into the third period and Miller wasn’t about to give up a two goal lead. He would say something, but he was just so happy in the silence. It was a rare thing to be around her and be able to sit in silence, not feeling rushed to give an update of his life. It made him sad to think that he’d forgotten what she was interested in; Sadder still when that second thought came, ‘did I even know to begin with?’ It prompted a long draw on the draft. "So," Grace finally broke the silence, only to struggle with any words to follow that. "Yeah, listen, I am sorry, it’s just that," and that was when Alex was cut off by the return of Jack, hands full with plates. "Okay, so here’s the Chicken Sandwich," he narrated as he placed the plate in front of Alex. "And the Cobb Salad for the lady. Let me know if you guys need anything else, and enjoy." He turned away with such speed that Red doubted how sincere he was. And sincerity affects the tip. They would have said "thanks," but it seemed vain at this point. "Well," Alex looked down at the oversized bun and full chicken breast. "Yeah, it looks good," Grace replied. "Bon Appetite." And they went about the business of eating their money’s worth of the meals, even if they weren’t that hungry. In breaks between mouthfuls, Grace was sure to lead Alex in small talk, at least. ----- It was an hour and sixty dollars later, but the temperature hung on to its few degrees Celsius, the Atlantic Ocean persistent to bring this city into spring at the resistance of a wonky jet stream and subsequent arctic airflow. Persistence followed the pair as they walked away from the restaurant, and quickly caught the attention of Grace. "Excuse me," he started his introduction. "I think that you might have dropped this leaving the restaurant," he explained with an outstretched hand and piece of paper between fore and middle fingers. It was the boyish grin and gelled hairdo that hinted this was more than just an act by a Good Samaritan. And for what it was worth, he was brazen enough to pull it off in front of Red. "Oh? Thank," she took the piece of notepaper and opened the fold. Inside was a ten digit number, and it didn’t take three guesses as to who’s digits they were. She was quick to slip the note into her purse and give something of an affirming look toward the hazel eyed stranger. "Thank-you," and she left a space, waiting for his name. "Danny, and think nothing of it, Miss." "Gracie." "Yeah, alright, thank-you and all that shit. Grace, you’ve got your mysterious piece of paper, now let’s get going." They could have only kept their game away from Red for how long? "But," she started to plead some. Maybe if he had been a bit more interested in giving her his attention, she wouldn’t be so apt to receive a stranger’s. "No, hey, listen. It’s cool, I didn’t want any trouble. You’ve got your thing back, and now I’m just going to go," and he slowed his pace, falling behind a few yards. "But you know how to get a hold of me," he said through that bleached white smile. And as he turned away Red stared a hole through the back of his head, right where he would have thrown his fist. "What was that about?" she greeted him with the question as he turned back around. "What do you mean? Sis, listen, I’ll be the first to admit I don’t know much about nothing, but when some Guido asshole is hitting on my sister, my guard is rightly raised. Is this standard fare in Calgary?" He didn’t mean to be curt, but some of the adrenaline from earlier in the evening had perked back up, bringing with it his fighting spirits. "No, but I don’t need you to defend me from every guy." There was something to her voice too, Red couldn’t quite tell where he’d done it, but he’s struck a nerve. "Look, I am sorry, I am just trying to do what is best for you," he raced out, just barely making it before she started again. "You mean what’s best for you. If it was up to you I’d be locked away in a castle guarded by a dragon," she said in a louder voice, unaware of her Disney childhood sneaking its way into her argument style. "You don’t get it." "Then explain it to me," he pleaded. "You don’t get the right. You don’t get the right to show up once a year and expect to play the hero big brother. You seemed to think I would fare well enough as an eleven year old when you left the family." "I didn’t leave the family, you abandoned me." "Oh, that’s right, poor Alex. Poor Alex, so screwed up his love life that he can only think it is best to kill his sister’s too." "And what is that supposed to mean?" Only in New York, he would later reflect, could two people be walking down the street yelling at each other like this and draw no notice. "It means, just because you fucked up the one good relationship you had in your life doesn’t mean you can tell me what to do." "Okay, I fucked up. I am not about to let you say that I am doing anything other than looking out for your best interests." "I think it’s in my best interests to catch a cab back to my hotel," she started looking for a cabbie, waving her hand. "Okay, yeah. Bum a dinner off me and run away. I’ll see you in a year." The anger had reached a point where he could not watch her get into that cab and he would have really appreciated something soft to throw his fist at, or even that eye-talian. ----- The ride was only a few blocks old when she dove through her handbag in search of her smart phone, past the note and past the joint. She was looking to dial the number she always called when she was upset. Only that when she got to Lee’s contact listing her heart fell a little more. She sat through a brief sadness that brought a clarity to her thinking. "Stop," she asked the driver. "Alright," he waited for further instruction while tickling the gun he kept under the seat. "Could we go back?" "You seemed pretty angry back there. You sure about this?" he split as a psychologist too. "Yeah, but I can’t leave my brother alone in a city this big." "You’re the boss," he said before making the three left turns and traversing back nearest to where she was picked up. "Here ya go, five dollars." She handed him the money and hopped out of the cab. Her eyes were wide as she followed the path back to the parking structure she remembered them pulling it. She didn’t get very far before she heard the harmonica and as she walked closer she could see Alex sitting on the hood of his rented Malibu. "Long may you run," she began to sing along with the music. He opened his eyes to see the mascara running in lines down from her eyes. They finished the song and he slipped the instrument back into his pants pocket. "I didn’t know you played." "Dad gave this to me before they sent him away, as something of a last gift." It was such a heavy pain in his heart think that he’d hurt her and it was the least he could do was to take her in his arms and tell her that everything was alright. "We missed you," she said as she pulled back. "At the funeral," she struggled, "At Lee’s funeral." "I know. And I do wish I could have been there. He was my brother just as much as he was yours. But I, I had my chance to say goodbye." "It’s not fair," she pouted. "What can I tell you? I know this sucks, but life isn’t fair. I’m not trying to preach some life lesson to you, it’s just," and he couldn’t finish. "Yeah, I know," she tried to show the toughness they both needed in that moment of commiseration. "You know I just want to see you happy, right Gracie?" "Yes, I know you were just doing your job as a big brother," she chuckled before continuing. "That’s exactly what Lee would have done." "You know how you said I messed up the one good relationship I had in my life?" "I’m sorry about that," she offered. "No, you just don’t know the whole side of it. The mistake I made was putting a ring on the finger of a girl who didn’t share the same ideas of exclusiveness as me." "You mean she…?" He nodded. "I am sorry." "Don’t be, I was just over reacting." "No, I am sorry that I stayed behind when Mom moved you out to Alberta. I, I didn’t think that by cutting her out of my life that I was cutting you out, too. I was a stupid, selfish seventeen year old. I’m still just as stupid and selfish at twenty six. But that’s has got to change. You didn’t lose your only brother, and I know I’ll probably never stack up to Lee, but I’d like to try." "You mean?" "I mean, I don’t want to go another year without seeing you. I don’t want to feel like we are strangers. I want you to trust me enough to let me help you." It took ten years to realise his mistakes. It took his brother dying to remember that he had a sister. It’d take a lot of work to fill in the missing pieces, but if he knew one thing, it was going to be worth the effort. "I think I would like that," and they shook hands as if to make it official.
