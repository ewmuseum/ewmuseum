Waiting for Redemption

26 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2-26-06 Rich Rollins knew the public outcry was going to be severe. “Fucking baby killer!” He knew his image was about to get dragged through the mud. “Murderer! Cop-killing monster!” He also knew that the backlash against the business itself wouldn’t be pretty. “Professional wrestling is Satan’s sport!” But if the Renegade knew anything, he knew that he would survive it as he walked through the horde of angry parents, police officers and reports, refusing to bow his head in shame or remorse as the flashbulbs demonized his face on the cover of a hundred newspapers. ESPN had boycotted his most recent PTC commercial. In the forty-eight hours since the bloody battle that was now on the lips of every news anchor and journalist in the country, ten thousand angry letters had already been stacked up and burned in the lot behind GCW headquarters. The public was a fickle mistress, and once you rub society the wrong way, there was no going back. A security barricade held together by an unwilling Chicago police force kept the fuming mob at bay as Rollins ducked his head into the waiting limo, eager to catch his flight to Japan for the next WorldWide. After that it was back to the States for God knew how long. The trial was set to begin next week. His public relations nightmare had only just begun. ~<>~<>~<>~ A private jet, somewhere over the Pacific Ocean? “You’d better be worth the investment, Rollins.” As the Renegade had expected, Steven Caldera was livid. “I don’t shell out millions of dollars for nothing, so you’d better pray this thing can be settled in a real hurry.” They sat in the comfy passenger fuselage of Caldera’s private jet, drinking heavily and trying to make the best of a bad situation. Rollins took another gulp of his whiskey sour, shaking his head sadly as he watched the clouds drift by outside the viewing porthole. “I killed two men, Steven. I can’t change that, but I can tell them the truth.” “They think you murdered a pregnant woman, Rollins!” Caldera had to clench an armrest to keep from ripping his hair out by the roots. “You’re a walking train wreck, for goodness” sake! A tabloid editor’s wet dream! How the hell am I supposed to spin this without turning you into every mother’s worst nightmare?” “It’s simple,” Rollins said as he looked back at his boss. “Don’t.” “Pardon me?” “Don’t try to spin anything,” he continued, finishing his drink only to pour himself another. “If they want to vilify me, let them. I was there, Steven. I’m keeping my mouth shut as it is, because I’m sure you don’t want it leaked out that Almasy blew the cop’s hand off, do you?” It was new to Caldera, and Rollins relished the shocked look on the man’s face. “Yeah, that’s right. I didn’t kidnap anybody; I was the victim for fuck’s sake! Frank Jacobs and that fucking felon with a badge killed my baby, man. Both of them.” He had to take another stiff swig to keep from breaking down. He hadn’t slept in two days; every time his eyes closed he could see the burning van, could hear Cynthia O’Doyle’s mortified screams as she burned alive. It was an image that would remain with him forever, and there was nothing he could do to change that. All he could do was drink it away, dulling his pain by getting too drunk to cry. Caldera softened only slightly. “What in the name of God were you doing there, Rich?” Rollins smacked his lips, his eyes burning from more than just the liquor. “I had a gun to my head when they threw me face to face with my mom’s corpse, Steven. I didn’t have much of a choice about the location, and believe me, if I had it would have been anywhere else.” Silence filled the cab as Rollins turned to look out the window once more. “We’ll get through this,” Caldera offered. “We always do.” But the Renegade had stopped listening. ~<>~<>~<>~ Tokyo? At least they love me somewhere, Rollins thought sarcastically as he stepped out of the loading terminal and into a throng of rabid wrestling fans. The news must have traveled to every corner of the planet by now, but it didn’t show on the faces of the eager Asian assemblage that surrounded the Renegade upon arrival, throwing posters and trading cards and the other usual assortment of memorabilia just waiting to be signed in his face. Without the energy to indulge them, Rollins was helped through the mob by airport security to another waiting limo that waited to whisk him away to his hotel. Caldera had other business to attend to, thus Rollins found himself alone as he plopped into the spacious back seat with a sigh. Then he jerked in surprise, as he wasn’t actually all that alone. “Pleased to meet you Rollins-san,” a surly Asian said with a deep Japanese accent, his English slightly broken. “I’m Yohiro, a representative of, shall we say, mutual friends in Tokyo.” When the man extended his hand in greeting, it didn’t take long for Rollins to figure out who “mutual friends” was referring to. The man was missing a pinky. Not wanting to offend the finely-dressed mobster, Rollins shook his hand firmly. “To what do owe the honor?” he said, trying his best not to sound demeaning. Yohiro turned around and snapped at the driver through a tiny retractable window as the limo began to move. Lounging comfortably in the extended seating, Yohiro flashed Rollins a wry smile. “You honor my organization with defeat of Kimbusa,” he said. “I know what happened to you, Renegade. I offer you protection while traveling in Japan.” “Protection?” Rollins released a grunt. “Who said I needed any protection” Haven’t you seen the news recently?” “Exactly my point,” Yohiro replied. “Many of our Families have taken offense to your presence in Tokyo. Killing women with child” not looked upon with much respect.” “I did not murder her,” Rollins spat. “The men I killed, they burned her alive while I watched. I couldn’t do anything about it.” Yohiro nodded. “Be that as it may, we enjoy your success over Kimbusa last week. He has been a rather difficult person to deal with over the years, so in shaming him on nation television, you earn our protection.” Was this guy for real? The Renegade’s head tilted quizzically, studying the Asian goomba carefully. The man looked serious enough in his Armani suit and tie, but then again Rollins hadn’t really had many conversations with Yakuza mobsters before. Or any at all, for that matter. Still, Rollins figured that when your reputation preceded you it was foolish not to take an ally by his word. He need all the allies he could get at this point. “Fair enough,” the Renegade decided. “I’m not going to be asked to hack off my, you know?” He wiggled his little finger, cracking a grin. Yohiro didn’t smile. “You are not Family, Rollins-san,” he stated very matter-of-factly. “You are friend.” Rollins nodded, trying to keep from offending the man yet again. “No prob, bucko. It was just a joke.” ~<>~<>~<>~ Two tournaments. Two bodies on my conscience. Two opponents on the card. Man, I get all the luck. The world hates me, but I had a sneaking suspicion that they always did. Caldera keeps leaving me messages, giving me the private numbers to dozens of lawyers he knew from law school, but I can’t even think about that just yet. I’m a warrior at heart, a gladiator. A renegade. I came here for a match, and I’ll be damned if some little stoner fuck and a walking meatbag-whatever gets to make my week any worse by eliminating me in a tournament on my own turf. The legal shit can wait. And so can I. ~Fin~
