Do Not Enter Pt 4

10 July 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07/10/10 Dr. Landis with the syringe still in his grasp watched as Ellen took her final deep breathes. Her heart monitor flatlined, the rhythmic beeping turned into a steady drone of a beep. Nurses, watching Ellen’s monitor from their stations came running in but stopped as they see Dr. Landis covering her face with the sheet. He shakes his head and walks away, making his way through the sea of nurses now crowded around the room. “Doctor?” Landis looks back at the nurse. His face is sunken and pale. Never in his life has he ever performed such an atrocity. He’s here to save and cure, not kill. “What happened?” He shakes his head. “I don’t know, she just slipped away.” “CPR?” Shakes his head again. “Unnecessary. You saw her condition.” Landis leaves the room, leaving his nurses in complete shock as one of Belmont’s most respected doctors just gave up on a patient. Ron and Margaret Beamer are flying down M2 towards the hospital. Ron in the passenger seat grabbing whatever he can as his wife tears through traffic like a bat out of hell. “Slow down!” “Our daughter needs us! Fuck you and the slowing down!” Margaret’s brain is being overcome with the worst possible thoughts ever. They told her that Ellen was in a serious accident and that her boyfriend brought her in. What boyfriend? What kind of accident? “Marge, I know, but if you don’t slow down we might be joining her in the bed next to her!” Marge made no reply. She tuned her husband out, and her motherly instinct has started to take over. Visions of Ellen has a baby flood her. She remembers the day she was born, the first time she fed her from her breast. Ellen’s first word, first step, and first boo-boo tackled Marge. Tears begin to well up in her eyes and Ron see’s them start to form. “They said she’s talking and responsive. Don’t cry love, she’s fine, really she is.” Landis crawls back to his little office, it’s hot and stuffy, and cramped with books, files, and more books. His desk is littered with sculptures of different bones and body parts, a messy stack of papers, and pictures. He grabs one, rubs the front glass plate and stares at it. Tears drop from his eyes and splash on the glass frame as he looks at the image of his daughter Tabby. Its her Senior picture, the last picture she ever took. Tabby was beautiful. Long brown hair, deep dark eyes, great complexion, tall and thin. She was an honors student, Head Majorette, with a scholarship to Marquette University in the United States. None of that will ever happen however, none of that can ever come true. Bryan O’Neil ruined that 15 years ago. Landis puts his head on his desk and sobs uncontrollably. He never thought he’d ever hear the O’Neil name again. Sure there’s the “anniversary of the O’Neil murders” but that’s all media hype. He never thought he’d ever have to listen to someone blame another incident on Bryan O’Neil. The very name makes him cringe. He butchered his little girl, in the most heinous crime in history. April 11, 1995. Three girls were walking home from school. The weather was unusually warm. The woods just beyond the Belmont Mansion was always filled with kids on a nice day. Most chose to ignore public transportation to take a nice walk home. The kids in the neighborhood were told to steer clear of the house. The O’Neil family would get you and eat you was the ghost story told and passed down from generation to generation. Although many have seen the O’Neils out and about, not once have they ever made an attempt to eat one of the towns residents. There were stories about disgusting acts of violence at the hands of the father and his oldest son Bryan, but none have ever been proven. Like all towns, this was its ghost hose. And like all ghost houses, this has its very own ghost stories. No one ever expected what happened that afternoon. Tabby and her good friend Carla began their last days of their lives in the woods just beyond the mansion. They stopped, found a fallen tree and sat on it and began to smoke cigarettes. Tabby’s parents would freak if they ever found out their “princess” was smoking. They sat down and started to talk about their day, and mostly boys. Tabby just lost her virginity last weekend at a party and was asking Carla who seemed to be an expert, different questions. They never saw or heard it coming. They sat alone as Bryan O’Neil caved Carla’s skull in with a large rock. The impact was so violent that the rock cracked in half and Carla’s skull spit out parts of her brain. She was dead immediately. Tabby fell off the log and screamed only to have a giant boot planted right in her face. She felt her facial bone crack, her nose shatter and her teeth slide down her throat. Her screams turned into a gargled mess of blood, pus, and snot. Her murder will forever live in the annals of Belmont’s history. Tabby’s body was found two days later deep in the woods. Her body was cut from top to bottom, her insides removed. She was strewn about two trees. Her friend Carla’s head was found but the rest of her remains were never recovered. EMT’s and other officials were witnessed at the crime scene gagging and vomiting. Belmont has never been a part of something so graphic and disgusting in their history. The Bathory murders paled in comparison to what happened that Spring day. Bryan O’Neil was accused of the crime because of his face paint found all over Tabby’s body. Armed officials rushed into his house and found the giant man sleeping in a coffin of all things. They arrested him and took him in for questioning. After searching the house, officials found the remains of atleast 6 other people. The mansion on Belmont Hill became known as “Belmont House of Torture” or “The Devil’s Playground.” After a long and grueling trial Bryan “Mayhem” O’Neil was sentenced and executed via firing squad. It was the first public execution in over 100 years and will serve as the last. The town gathered around the square and watched as Bryan O’Neil’s body was pumped full of bullets. The boogeyman of Belmont was dead, and good riddance. Some even say after he took his last breathe the sun peaked through the clouds, a sign which Belmont’s citizens viewed as a sign of better things to come. The house was never destroyed or knocked down because of a loop hole in the law. The Bathory family never fully gave up full stake of it’s property. A strange 6% of the house still belonged in that name, and since the name ceases to exist and the family has either vanished or was extinct they could not knock it down or condemn it. The O’Neil family claimed the rights, and the remaining family members refused to lay the house to waste out of spite to a town that they claimed falsely accused their relative and their name of such atrocities. Landis just kept staring into the picture of his beautiful baby girl. It may have been years, but these wounds will never heal. He stood up front along with the parents of Carla as Bryan O’Neil died. He even asked to check for the pulse since he was a revered physician in the town. Donald Landis remembers standing up, looking at the open eyes of a Lunatic, put his finger to his throat, and smiled when no pulse was found. He turned his head to the crowd and yelled, “The beast is dead!” He remembers the roar of the crowd, he remembers the tears of joy. The Lunatic of God’s Creation would no longer wreak havoc on this simple minded town. Or would it? Landis can’t believe what he heard come from Ellen’s mouth. He’s heard of copy cat murders, but this is ridiculous. She claimed the person that murdered her friends was enormous, had black hair and a painted face. Sure that fit the description, but could it really be? “Dr. Landis? Mr. and Mrs Beamer are here.” Boomed the voice box on his desk. He pushes the button and informs his nurse he will be right there. Now comes the hard part. Dr. Landis walks quietly yet briskly out of his office into the hallway. He approaches the elevator, pushes the up arrow and waits. The ding comes within seconds and he steps foot into it. He feels no guilt for “pulling the plug” on Ellen Beamer. He lost all guilt the day his daughter was spread open like a pig. The elevator stops, door opens, and Landis puts on his sad face. Marge sat up from the chair, saw his face and immediately collapsed into her husband’s arms. She screams no at the top of her lungs that sends the entire floor into a deathlike silence. Ron’s face crinkles, his lips quiver as he attempts to hold back the tears. He shakes his head as Dr. Landis puts a consoling hand on his shoulder. “Mr. and Mrs. Beamer there was nothing we could have done. The injuries were too severe, she could not fight any longer. She did go peacefully, I will assure you of that.” Marge pulls her face from her husbands collarbone, her face red, tears smearing her makeup, “Why couldn’t you tell us this on the phone? We were thinking she was just hurt, nothing more! Why did you wait to tell us now bastard!” Ron pulls his wife back as she makes a leap towards Dr. Landis. Dr. Landis, already a pro at this, looks both of them in the eyes, “We are not entitled to tell family of the severity over the phone due to the fact if we did, you adrenaline would kick in and who knows what would have happened on the road. It’s for your safety and the safety of others. I can give you guys some time alone, if you need to see me, I’ll be in my office, have one of the ladies at the counter…” “Did she say anything at all?” Dr. Landis stood frozen for a second. “What do you mean?” Ron, running his fingers through his wife’s hair, “Did she say, well did she talk. Was she able to?” Dr. Landis stood quiet for a brief second, than spoke. “Well yes, but it could have been the medication or the shock she was in. She claimed her friends were murdered and she was left to spare by…well Bryan O’Neil.” Marge pulled her face away again and looked at the doctor. “The Lunatic?” Landis nods, “Yes that’s the one.” Marge looks up at her husband, “Isn’t he dead?” “Yeah, been dead for a while.” Dr. Landis interjects, “That’s why we feel it was either the meds or a nightmare she had while in a comatose state. Her friends however, have not been heard of yet. I told the authorities to check out the old Belmont house where Ellen said they were, and they told me it’s still boarded up and there was no evidence surrounding the house.” A voice booms over the PA system, “Dr. Landis, ER please, Dr. Landis ER.” “Folks if you will. If you have any other questions feel free to ask. I’m sorry about your loss.” With that Dr. Landis rushed down the hall. Ron pulls his wife away, his eyes welled with water, he grabs her arm kisses her and rushes away. Marge chases him down the hall. “Where are you going?” Ron stops, his body quaking, “His daughter was killed by O’Neil. There’s something he’s not telling us!” Marge her sobs are becoming uncontrollable. “W-What do you mean?” “Exactly that Marge. He knows something!” “Where are you going Ron?” “Stay here, do what needs to be done, I am going for answers!” Marge yells again, stunning the already quiet floor. “Where?!?” “The house on the hill!” Ron rushes off, without even acknowledging that fact that Marge began a protest. Ron’s mind is racing the Daytona 500. 200 miles an hour and very little braking. What the hell was Dr. Landis holding from him? Was his daughter really that doped up that she thought a dead serial killer murdered her friends? Or was Bryan O’Neil really still alive? He raced down the road, the sun still shining over his head. He had to know the truth, but why the hell was he driving to the house? His car came to a screeching halt at the bottom of the hill. Pervert’s Path(named for Anton O’Neil the patriarch of this family) was old and worn. A car would most likely fall right through it into the cold river below. He shuts the car off, leaving the keys in the ignition. He slithers out of the driver’s seat and stares at the monstrous house of horrors only a half mile ahead of him. Ron stares in awe. The house was enormous. Someone once told him it has 9 bedrooms and 5 bathrooms. This person also said they use to heat the house with the bodies of their victims, so a grain of salt is definitely needed with some of the stories. Ron use to drive past here everyday on his to and from school. He remembers the youngest of the O’Neil boys being constantly bullied. Ron remembers nothing of Bryan because Bryan was never to be seen. The house looks exactly as he remembers it. Eerie, ominous, and unsettling. This is truly Belmont’s Haunted Castle. As he makes his way up the Highway to Hell, aptly named in the 70’s by a bunch of hippies, he realizes he has never once been this close to this house. Ron was too into sports to even give a damn about this place. Everyone wanted to break in and see what the place was all about. Was there really bodies in here? Was this a place where Satanic-Cannibals lived? None of this ever intrigued Ron. His pace was brisk, yet something held him back from running. Something supernatural, almost nonexistent yet stronger than hell kept him from charging the hill and blowing the doors down. It took about 10-15 minutes or so for him to make the half mile trek up the hill. The house was much more massive up close than anyone could ever imagine. It was old and decrepit. The doors and windows were boarded up. Remnant of police tape was still tied to the banister of the steps. Three floors of hell someone once told him. He stands at the bottom of the front porch and looks up at this massive structure. It reminded Ron of something from a horror movie. He makes his way up the four steps and stands on the front porch. He remembers walking home and seeing the youngest boy sitting on here after school playing with a cat or just staring at everyone that walked past. Some may ask, why would anyone ever walk by the structure, why not go around it? This house is built directly in the center of Belmont. Churches and cemeteries were to the west. Schools and shopping centers were east. South was the town. North was the woods. No matter where one went, you had to pass this eyesore day in and day out. Ron walks towards the door and tries to peer inside the 8 inch gap left between the boards nailed to the door. His view is obstructed, so Ron puts his foot on the bottom board, puts both hands in the gap and heaves. It takes two strong tugs before the board comes loose, sending Ron crashing to the ground, scuffing his shoulder in the process. He repeats this process with three more boards, this time catching himself before he falls. Half the door is exposed, giving Ron more than enough room. He checks his surroundings, grabs a piece of board, and busts the window out to a deafening crash sound. Once again he checks his surroundings and begins to kick the wood door beyond the glass one he just broke. He starts to kick the door with all his might, every curse word exiting his mouth like lava, until he feels a sharp searing pain in his lower back than quickly spreads to his stomach. Ron looks down, jetting from his stomach is a shiny silver rod, the end dull with a chunk of something sliding from it plopping to the floor. He watches as the rod is than thrusted backwards, exiting his body. Ron turns around, standing no more than five feet behind him is this giant man his daughter spoke of. Painted face, black hair. But this time holding the end of a metal fence post. Ron looks at his stomach and watches as blood begins to leak out. Its black and thick. He covers the hole, but his fingers begin to grow numb. He looks up, blood starting to cough from his nose and mouth. The giant man is still staring, almost smiling. Ron points at him, “Y-y-ou….B-But you are D-Dead.” Ron begins to grow cold, as the giant man steps closer towards him. “No. But you are!” the giant exclaims. Ron knew it happened, yet with his body going through states of shivering and numbness, he never felt the fence post smack him in the face, folding him like an accordion. The giant, whom Ron realized, too late that it was Bryan O’Neil, grabbed him by the leg and started to pull him around the house. Ron’s face was scraping against the rough terrain surrounding the house. His one eye shut to avoid anything entering it, his cheeks wet and rough as blood continues to spew from his mouth. His open eye saw his car at the bottom of the hill, the cellar entrance, than a doorway. That doorway would be the last thing Ron Beamer would ever see, well besides the split second the rusty meat hook entered his mouth….
