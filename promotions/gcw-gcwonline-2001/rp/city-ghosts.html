City Ghosts

Date: 11/04/2007?was meant as a Halloween thing, but time makes suckas of us all. He walks at night. Faces haunt him, make him a hypocrite that bleeds at a paced angst to the dust-shrouded floorboards. He flicks his lighter under the guise of lighting a cigarette’really searching for a switch to flip under the faint hope that power still flows to the carcass. If he'd just brought a flashlight. Then, he'd have known he was going to break in; which he wanted to feign ignorance of, like it was the spontaneous gesture of a vivid life. Something in the air’miasma or malaria or mildew’brings him back to his childhood, when the new was always bright and fresh. Happiness had been an assumed associate of change, a fallacy that faded in steps, until it existed only as a guideline for how life had ought to be. Now, finding a switch halfway down the hall, the world is painted in every color's gray’beige, ash, patina, rust. Floors, walls, floorboards, doors. The glowing hues of his memory blur, stop falling within the lines, leave him to walk through nothing, up nothing, into nothing, and to nothing. "I'm here." Now what? Reason, justification. "It's just me. Promise." It's quiet, except for his voice and except for his feet and except for his doors and except for him. Dust collects on the toe of his shoe. He inspects and finds it clamoring up his pants leg, now to his shin, now his knee, collected in strong lines where creases once were. It fails yet to claim his thighs. He checks another room, a door still locked. Instantly it becomes a prize to return to, a holy grail to cap his night off when all other rooms have turned into a dreary stream too eager to share their nothing space with the world. "Most definitely the coolest." They scream to him with nothing voices. Outside of an almost opaque window, the city thrives. Traffic lights outside give a lie of red or yellow or green’usually red, less often yellow than green. A silhouette that might be the skyline is faintly visible, but the years of grease and grime and dirt will not yield to his limply wielded rag. He, then, lets it be a blood-stained skyline and moves on. The city will not distract him again. A few steps from the window he notices a hole through to the next room. It is bleak; he assumes curtains drawn, door latched. Poorly protected, an already surveyed treasure. He argues with himself, on if it is nothing or if it is peace, to no conclusion. He is no linguist, no analyst of semantics. If there is a difference, he will not find it. He is, however, alone’which is free to many ends, all unsociable., all deep within his own head. Breaking & entering, vandalism, a wealth of crimes he considers victimless tint his progress. He passes over trinkets, a child's amputated doll here or a shattered jewel case in front of the broken speaker there or the splintered echoes of what was that fall between. There is no "why" for this abandonment that surrounds his search., no regards to such abrupt upheaval in the heart of the city. It is only a place that is there to create the now, to allow for the moment. in a graceful and unobtrusive manner. It bows into the scenery unnoticed, a pattern on the wall’small blue diamonds in a sea of white’or the monogram on a towel’DSCF. Steps. A door resists, unlocked but jammed, and he resorts to shoulder charging it with the three steps he can manage as a build-up in the cramped corridor. Something splinters, cracks echoing down the hallway. It encourages him; he attacks at an angle to get more speed. The door bows in this time, middle hinge coming loose from the frame. The next charge, weaker than the second, cracks it in half. He steps through. The something in the air of the building becomes a smell inside, becomes the only thing inside. His hand smacks to the wall and fumbles for the switch, finding it on an opposite side to the rest of the rooms. The bulbs, fluorescent tubes, hum and crack and tease until they decide to let out their unearthly glow. The room, aside from the door, is the most well-kept of any. He searches for what had blocked him; finds no signs of anything that could've possibly had a chance of explaining it; shrugs. To the rest of the room, he gives a string of cursory alien glances. A modest television sits on a wooden table, dusty but clean enough. In front of it is another table, a burgundy couch behind it. The walls are white, the carpets beige. On the table are a set of postage scales and a box of sandwich bags, atop a shallow stack of newspapers and psuedonews magazines. There are framed reprints of almost recognized works’he recalls the styles, even an artist or two, but he can't title a single painting. He approaches one, a languid piece of frustration’Goya by way of Dali, or Dali by way of Goya’made in loose lines with more details implied than actually painted. It looks unfinished, two skeletons (circles for heads and torsos and feet and hands, connected by wispy lines half-painted over) standing in a row, a tiny portion of a great field of dust. (umber blended with much more sunflower than rose), surrounded by hellish mountains and an dead sky of black, red, and perhaps burgundy/perhaps black blended with the red. The contrast says burgundy paint. He remembers, a cigarette that needed to be lit to cover for the earlier lie he told himself, and quickly reaches for his pack with both hands deep in their respective pockets. It takes a second, the lighter longer, but he succeeds, free to draw in a refreshing lungful of refuse, byproducts, chemicals, and almost-addiction. It tornados inside of him; he takes a seat on the couch and stares at the TV screen, or at himself in the TV screen. A few minutes pass at the speed of one drag per; he centers in on a concept of a definitive Now that exists now, and forever’but only now, since forever's a term defined by understanding. As such, this forever exists solely in the Now, as defined by the Now. The Now itself is a singular entity that, contrary to common sense, is in fact as infinite as anyone around here's thought of so far. "God, baby." "What?" "You're so cute when you smoke." "Stop it." "Seriously." "Come on, don't encourage me. I need to quit." "Why quit? I bet you'll have the sexiest little stoma." "Frank." "What? All the other patients in the cancer ward'll be jealous." "Frank." "You know I'd fuck the shit out of your goddamned stoma." "That's. That's weird." "You know I love you." He picks the box of bags up, weighs them against the scales, and drops both to look under the couch on what is commonly known as a "hunch." The carpet is rough against the back of his hands, the cheapest version of that burlap-and-barbed-wire beige texture that seems to have gone out of fashion. There is a wallet, still oily and sleek leather, and inside it are bills’a heavy chunk of large ones hidden in long runs of fives and tens and twenties and fifties’that he passes up with a quick smirk, almost imperceptible. He looks to the painting again, studies it at a distance to keep from letting the little details lead him astray. There is nothing: no motion, no energy, no progress. It is a singular dance step that cycles from itself, through itself, and finally back to itself with no appreciable change in appearance. A siren calls out to him, boisterous and almost laughable in its constant bellowing. It fades into the distance, slipping down towards amalgamation with the rest of the white noise. He leaves the room to the building and keeps through the hall. Glancing down or staring at his feet, he notices a gray smear across his shirt and wipes it off quickly in the open door of a laundry room. The old machines sit, painted in a shade of yellow that hasn't been used in home appliances in thirty years and none quite rusted, open or closed or without doors at all. "High Falls, yeah. It's off of Elm. South Elm. Aight. When? Cool, cool. 296. Yeah, well I guess you. Yeah. See you then.. Yeah, later. Yeah. Take it easy." Strips of wallpaper are missing, others lay draped over the machines. The wall beyond is unspectacular, white turned dark and covered in gunky clobs of tack. A girl, petite with curt red hair, a drab green skirt and an oversized denim jacket pushes up and sits on the first washer in the row. She snorts playful, almost a laugh; her hair bounces in a slow oval. "Tt. Bueno. Muy." She laughs again, leans back and presses her shoulders against the wall, jacket spreading from the neck at 37.5 degrees. Underneath is gray’not solid gray, that speckled white and black and gray’sleeveless top with something like a horizon on it. It is a line, that is, underneath a black circle with a radius of almost exactly 12 centimeters. His heart races. A dollar and twenty-five to wash his clothes. Another two for drinks. A wisp of smoke comes from behind him, twisting up his neck as he shovels another load into the machine. "Tt." Her chest heaves against his back. "Make me wait. See how you like me frustrated." A laugh. His abdomen strains. He steps out of the laundry room and continues, the carpet staining brown beneath him. A placard, varnished maple, hangs acute. The numbers on it are faded and unimportant. He lets it swing as he stumbles from haunt to haunt, old dreams and faded memories left behind like peeling paint and cracked windows. They lay somewhere under the graffiti tagged walls and the creep of mildew. Trudging awkward, even his feet are aware at how far away this is from the sort of thing he does. It's archaeological voyeurism, historical masturbation on urban decay. There were a thousand things to do tonight. He just felt like none of them; or felt none of them like he feels the draft sneaking through holes in the structure. They carry soft reminders of the world outside the walls’his job, his girlfriend, the warm musk of malaise late at night. He's too young for ennui and he doesn't know what it means anyways, but something its exact temperature settles in the base of his spine as he pushes his shoulder into an open doorframe’ostensibly, in the name of collecting whatever thoughts he can find. "How's it go?" "Good," she says. "Good. Never better. You?" "I'm alright, I guess. Just feelin' kinda lost." "Again? Come on." "Well, what if heaven runs out of souls?" "What if the old man keeps the fish? It doesn't happen. That shit's not in the book." She sucks her stomach in and turns sideways to a mirror. "Look, this whole losing control thing. You should tell somebody. Soon. Trust me." He shakes his head, staring at his hands. "Smell that?" "What?" "Smells like burning hair." "Oh, yeah, that. You'll get used to it. Thin walls between the apartments, and, uh, bad neighbors." "Sucks. The last ones were all quiet and easy to get along with." "Too loud for 'em, though. Drowned 'em out." "Maybe. Did seem to complain a lot. Whatever happened to them?" "Things change. Who knows why?" She adjusts her jacket over her shoulders, pulling it forwards until it's tight to the nape of her neck. "You've just gotta take control." Dropping into the room, hitting the lights and never wondering how the building still has power, he crouches to snatch a notebook from the floor. Chickenscratch and frames of sketches of actual drawings, words that flip from aping Cummings to Kerouac and miss the whole point of the soul they carried. They don't tell him anything he doesn't already know about himself. They're barely even about him for that matter; which is rare considering the egotism he's barely able to keep in check. The losses at least tend to help with that more than any string of selfdegradation he's come up with over the months. He still feels right, he's learning slow the exact amount of pressure he can take before everything starts to feel wrong. Or maybe he's paranoid about everything. Too paranoid to answer his phone, too paranoid to find his old friends, too paranoid to stick his head outside a window and breathe in the hundreds of thousands of people hived up in his city. The longer he scans, the closer the words come to being about him; he drops the book and backtracks from the room to plant his forehead against the wall and his cigarette in the carpet, grinding it out with the toe of his shoe. 154 pounds of pressure PSI and rising. Heading to a window opaque with grease and dust, he moves until he has an angle at his new car down the street. His Charlotte car, he called it, a powder blue Caprice raised to fit rims that cut through the dark like Venus above. He wasn't raised for this; he's never been sure what he was raised for. Was it the cold disconnect of being a celebrity? That plastic sheen the skin takes on after enough years of overexposure through the television, where the makeup cakes in the pores long enough that the first few inches of flesh turn rubber? The intense existential angst of laying in a hotel room and watching yourself talk in miniature on a big screen? All those millions of eyes? He shakes his head, eyes rolling lazy ellipses to keep his stare on the Caprice without a tinge of acceptance on the corners of his mouth’just the dried remnants of a day that chapped lips and peeled them back to leave just the raw pink. It matches the reds-n’e-whites of his eyes and the flush of his skin. He doesn't need to find himself, he needs to find where he stands in relation to everyone around him and come to a conclusion from there. He knows himself as well as anyone can, he figures; he knows the name he was born with as well as he knows the name he took for a contract, he remembers being habitually late for the better part of the fifteen years he can recall off the top of his head, he hasn't forgotten that he's never lived up to expectations. It's just that none of those things have precluded him from getting to where he is; or, more appropriately, where he is for a couple of hours on most weeks. Pulling the window open, squealing it against its frame, he stands on the fine line between having a career and being defined by it. No difference between him and any of the friends he lost in the name of his paycheck. Fair enough; jealousy's the leading killer among friends anyways. "Whatever happened to MC Serch?" "He's got a show on MTV about white rappers." He kicks the walls like it's what twisted his childhood into mass marketability and desperate pleas for relevance at the cost of artistic importance. Is this how we treat all of our idols?
