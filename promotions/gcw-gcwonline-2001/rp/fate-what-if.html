Fate. What If?

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Two Weeks Ago It’s it what if’s that hurt the most. Fate, to me, is an excuse people use so that they can lay the blame somewhere else. I don’t believe in fate, I think it’s more than likely that we have more control than we actually think, and that ultimately our actions spark reactions and so on. But when a girl runs up to you, mascara streaming down her face, her arms flaying all over the place as she desperately tries to tell you something. In such a hurry that her voice trembles, her top lip quivers making her almost incoherent. The shrill of her voice as she gets more upset and frustrated causes everyone around you to stop what they’re doing and look and stare. Basically, she’s a fucking mess. Well, the first choice you make, the decision you come to in those vital moments, might just be the wrong one. “I did something today that I haven’t done for a long time. I asked God for a favour. And I think he actually delivered.” She says this in a low whisper, her head close to the table as she speaks to me. This is Katie O’Sullivan. I’m sat talking to her because I couldn’t get away. An excuse didn’t come across my lips quick enough. Even though I had the ultimate one, that I needed to say a goodbye, board a plane, and go and help people far worse off than herself, as they hadn’t chosen what life had just dealt them. Not that I was given much choice in the matter, she’d come up to me a hysterical wreck. She sits with her face cupped in her hands, leaning forward, her shoulders tense. She’s been sat opposite me for about an hour now, although she’s only been talking for the past thirty minutes. She’s a few years younger than me, and it’s doubtful that she’ll even make it to my age. She smells of alcohol, so much so that customers at the other end of the room give the odd disgruntled look. Her sleeves are rolled up her arm, allowing the wounds from her self harming habit to be taken in by everyone. While I’ve considered myself to have hit rock bottom, the life Katie’s living is something I can barely get my head around. I’ve hit rock bottom in that there is only so low I can allow myself to go. Katie, she’s at the very bottom. She’s at a place I can only imagine, and even then I’m probably a long way from understanding. “Don’t change the subject” I say, preoccupied with stirring my coffee. Or trying to be. Katie looks at me with that same powerless look on her face. I gave her a napkin to wipe her face with, but it’s still more than evident that she’s been crying. Her eyes are red and her face is puffy, it’s a look that would take the beauty right away from anyone. “You mean how do I know I’m going to die?” she asks in a quiet hopeless voice. I nod. This is after all the reason I’ve stayed. I’m on a tight schedule, with a plane to catch and before that I promised to say one last goodbye to Cal. Katie lets out a long breath. “I just know”. I can’t help but feel insecure at the sound of the certainty in her voice. It’s hard to accept the helplessness, the look in her eyes of assurance. “Is someone going to do something to you” Katie, is.. is someone going to hurt you?” my own voice has dropped to a whisper, an urging whisper across the table. She shakes her head. “Tell me then, how do you know?” Silence. The reason she ran into the small cafe was because she was running from her boyfriend. He’s the reason she was so scared and breathless. I was the first person she saw, she had no idea who I was, but she saw me nonetheless. She collapsed at my feet, crying and trying to catch her breath. Her boyfriend never showed. She’d been running for days. “Is he going to hurt you” Come on? I snap. “I can’t help you if you don’t tell me” Katie shakes her head as she sits back in the chair, she swallows hard, looking out of the window. It’s still early. People are still streaming past the window, dealing with their own little lives. It doesn’t matter what’s going on in other places of the country, it doesn’t matter what people in other countries are dealing with, the world keeps spinning. It’s true that after a disaster, when something terrible happens, people offer their sympathies, they will see footage on their television and say “Oh my God, that’s awful” and then they will continue with their ordinary lives. “There is nothing you can do anyway” Katie sighs, looking back at me. “You’ve done enough” she smiles. Done enough? Not likely. “I can’t believe you’re just going to let this happen” I press a hand to my forehead. My grandmother used to do this, often in long queues while waiting with her grocery or while waiting for someone at the bank, or waiting on the phone while being put on hold. When she was agitated and her patience was drawing near. “There is nothing I can do.. it’s just going to happen” Katie nods, still sure. My chest heaves and my breath escapes me. She’s so calm and sure, seemingly unnerved, as though she knows she’s powerless and she’s accepted it. “You’re going to let him win?” It’s more of a statement than a question. I can’t shake the feeling that I’ve already been defeated. Katie rocks forward in her chair, keeping her eyes on me. They’re so blue. I can see myself in her eyes, desperately losing this battle. “Who’s the guy in your life?” she asks, her lips curling into as much of a smile as she can muster. “There is no guy” and for this I’m almost grateful. It’s a guy that’s driven Katie this far to the edge, of that I’m sure. “But there is someone?” she asks, and for a split moment, that teenage girl inside her appears, as clear as the sky above. A moment of hope catches her. “There is” I lie. It’s my desperate plea to keep her. As quick as the lie falls out of my mouth, everything becomes dark again. Katie looks down at the table, she looks at my cup and then back up at me. “There is someone” she nods. Maybe there is someone. But it’s never the right someone. It’s like I have an ideal no man can meet. I lose interest easily. I don’t tell Katie any of this, because it’s in no ones best interest to get into such a tangled web. I just nod. “You know it” Katie says, staring at me. I don’t say anything. I can’t see how any of this is her business, it’s not going to help matters any. I watch as she reaches her bony hands down into the pocket of her jeans, she pulls out a broken cigarette and a box of matches. She rolls her eyes as the people on the table behind me give her an unpleasant look. It’s a popular habit, but it’ll never be one that meets everyone’s approval. Not that Katie cares. She brings the match to life, cups her hand and lights the cigarette, taking a long drag she waves the match before throwing it on the table. When I was twelve years old my father set the house on fire. Well, one room. A room I happened to be sitting in. He denied he’d done it on purpose, but I swore he dropped the match deliberately. It didn’t take long for the fire to get out of control. By the time my father had returned from the kitchen with a bucket of water, the fire was already too far spread. I smile at Katie, picking up the match and dropping it into my warm coffee. Even now I run spent matches under cold taps.
