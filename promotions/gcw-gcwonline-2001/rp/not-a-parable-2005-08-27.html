Not a Parable

27 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 27, 2005 It's the smell, more than anything. Smoke drops the room into a murky haze that lends more mysticism than the party ever needed, gives it that elite sense of satisfaction and lung cancer. Yellow clouds roll along the floor and dance to the golden ratio over what furnishings there are: a futon, a nightstand, a television, a desk, two couches, and two losers that had won big. Trey stretches his feet along the carpet, vaguely aware of a consistent clicking that yields to no ideas of structure or pattern, but more aware of the eternal Jordan across from him. Always there, the recurring nightmare that ends up controlling the days, and wearing nothing cheap and nothing tasteful. His red fur coat--existing without logic--stands out through the twisting fog. It's not worth something because it was expensive, it's worth something for how he wears it. Pride giving value. 'Stripper fucker.' Trey shakes his head for the thousandth time tonight, voice coming out low and whispered. He rubs his left nostril. 'Wasn't like that, man.' 'It was. A night before Worldwide, and you were fucking a stripper. Good evil to confront, I figure.' 'Man...' 'No, seriously. You honestly believe you could even help the world the way your head's been? It's a long march to redemption. Trust me.' 'The hell do you know about redemption?' 'That you're not man enough to forgive yourself.' 'For what?' Jordan shrugs. 'Never told me.' 'You never asked.' 'Well, consider that a question.' 'Give a fuck?' 'You're my number one investment.' 'Bad pussy.' Jordan laughs. 'Always over bad pussy.' 'Don't get it man, this chick--I dunno, s'hard to say. It was a crazy ass night, lot of partying.' 'Oh no, a one night stand. The world's fucking ending.' 'Not that. She was an artist, showed me this sculpture that just changed things--I saw myself in something, and I was ugly as hell. Been trying to make it all make sense, get what I thought of myself back. Shit, just life.' 'Quarter life crisis.' 'Shyeah.' 'That's art for you. Good lay, at least?' 'Man, she put me on powder. I was a fuckin' machine.' 'So that's where you got the itch.' 'Yeah, some good shit. Ain't easy gettin' a hook-up everywhere though, been carryin' and ridin' buses a lot more. Shit, man, I'm actin' like a fuckin' junkie.' 'Victim of fate, just like you always were. Never been able to take control of your own life, at least without turning into this crude joke of your image of a hero.' 'You don't gotta talk shit, J.' 'Yeah, I do. You saw something ugly in a loser art chick's work, and decided to turn cokehead? Nice deal. Don't fix it--Christ no--fucking wallow in your own misery and forget to figure out what was so fucking disgusting about yourself there.' 'Chill, man.' 'Fuck you. Get this through your head, Trey. You're not me and you never will be. You're a good guy, not a degenerate.' 'Man, nobody I've met's helped me out with shit more than you.' 'You're fucking crazy. I've ruined your life more often than I wanna think about, and you're looking to me as a good guy?' Pause. Breathe. 'Fuck. No wonder. You had a pretty picture painted of what a great and noble hero I was and you shot for it and missed--ended up actually like me. Fuck. I'm not sure if that was ironic or obvious.' 'It ain't like that.' 'Yeah? How is it? What's your excuse for fucking up every time you get the chance?' 'Don't care like I used to.' Trey's fingers roll into his palm, popping them as his fist clenches to the clicking. 'See, fucking bastard. I'm the loveable slacker that's better than anyone; you're, well, fuck. You're Trey. Tremendous. Everyone loves you, and none of them have a clue how big of a shithead you really are. You ever think about how being fake on that scale felt? You're a celebrity, pure and simple. A vapid hole for the masses to abuse.' 'Man...' 'No, fuck you. I'm getting to a point here.' 'Yeah?' 'When I met you, and ever since, you've been this hollow little twerp that couldn't do shit unless he completely lost his sense of self. There's a thousand things going on in your head, and you've been happy to just smile and nod and play up to whatever image you'd been shackled to. Fuck, when are you going to do something smart? I let you loose for a month and I come back to a jackass that can't win a match for bitching out his friends every time he's fiending. This the image you want, a twitchy clucker that's dangerous to be around? You can live like Rick James, or Sid Vicious, or Jim Morrison! Your dad would be proud.' No words. Trey stands and cuts a swathe through the smoke. His shoulders rise and fall slowly. He breathes deep enough to save millions. 'Hey, remember how crazy you jumped when Casey got a hold of your leash? Couldn't even beat me, nearly lost an eye for trying. And you're the hot commodity? You're washed up after three months back, and you deserve a record for that. No man has fucked himself so severely and so swiftly as Trey, and none shall so long as man walks the earth. But yeah, you were something. You were a monster, after we trained you. Fuck. Why don't you train yourself? Are you scared to own what's in there? Worried that anger makes you one of the bad guys?' His footfalls quake mountains and tremble the gods on Olympus. 'No, being nothing when you could be everything is what makes you one of the bad guys. It's what makes you the runner-up, it's what makes you the eternal background. As much as they love you, they'll never like you. They'll never spend an hour with you and still find enough delusion to cheer you on. Give up while you're ahead. Borealis is on our team too, and at this point, even he's got you beat on stability. Might make a better representative, and you can luck out enough to win the television. If you're dedicated.' Fingertips tattoo his palms. 'Do something or sit down. Be a man or be a dog--whichever feels right to you by now. Act and accept whatever comes next, do what you want for just fucking once in your waste of a life. I mean, if someone's taught you how.' So Trey does something. He twists his torso, plummeting out a right forearm that collides against Jordan's forehead with an impact to weak-knee the devout and the desensitized alike. It tips the couch backwards and rolls Jordan out to sprawl across the floor. A cigarette burns. Trey blinks twice. He wipes a shroud of sweat from his brow and Jordan smiles like Buddha. The only sound Trey hears before he's outside are the clicks, eternal like the fires that destroy. There is no quick burst of insight, no overwrought symbolism, no peace of mind in their psychotic gibberish. It's a rhythm for the deranged to keep pace with, five quick pats here and a long pause and ten more quick beats in succession, and back into the nothing until a longer spree begins. He thinks in between, standing still in the night and burning a Marlboro. The Pacific pushes in the humid night air, thick with the taste of salt. It bathes Trey in his patient progress towards a vague goal that he's winging as he goes along, mad drunk on his own power. His muscles flex/twitch unconsciously and his right foot slaps against the grass a thousand times for every stop he makes on the way. Inky waves lap up onto the beach that glows phosphorescent in the moonlight. 'Fuck.' He sits on the in the sand, legs splayed and body leaning back towards the stars. Smoke makes a vortex as it rises into the night, to maybe lend dust to a comet or a star or a world. He doesn't think of that, just lets his head slip to places it hasn't wandered to lately. Pacing the own annals of his mind, for once. 'Fuck. Fuck. Fuck.' His head slips down into his palms, cigarette dangling limply towards the sand. '...I know you.' 'No shit. Let's hear it for fate.' 'Yeah--to fate. For throwing me back into your goddamned-' The ocean rises to his boots, their calm ruined by the wind. A light in the distance blinks on and off, and the bellowing cry of a freighter follows it. His eyes trace it until the fog rises and the clouds begin to fall in rain drops. 'My goddamned hostel?' 'Your goddamned path.' 'I've been a good friend.' 'To who?' 'Well, I mean, you're in one piece right now. Even if it's an ugly piece, it's not much worse than the last time I saw it.' 'Fucker.' He tried to snap, but changed his mind as the muscles coiled around injuries not worth thinking about. The water thrusts out again and fills his shoes with the sea, soaking up into the legs of his jeans. Unaware or unconcerned or still faking both, he flicks his cigarette end over end to the water and quickly lights another. 'Calm down, man. I'm your savior here.' 'Still looking for redemption? I've already forgiven the kidnapping, mutilation, and brainwashing, man.' 'God damn. I never noticed how ridiculous that plan sounded. Blame the drugs. The past is the past though, you know?' He sneered, left nostril perked up and a few bloody teeth shining through busted lips. 'Heh, guess you do. Things have changed.' Yeah, they have. He looks for comfort in the twists his life takes and finds only a string of regrets that add up until they crush every dream he's kept around over the years. Cheap fantasies topple as easily as the life-long hopes, and none of them on the spectrum make a sound other than the clicking. Inescapable. His infrequent thoughts go straight from brain to tongue to the ether and never matter much. 'Cold' or 'yeah' or 'fuck' or another quick exclamation to keep himself entertained as he self-destructs. The water level rises to his shins and his stomach and his wrists, too ice cold to still believe in summer as the clicks begin to make an obvious pattern. Somewhere his fate's been decided and he's acting it out as best he can, and it involves the ocean consuming him. His Red floats to the surface. He dives, keeping near the sand in the massive nothing. Moonlight wavers down in shifting beams that shimmer until they fade to away. It only takes a few yards. Suddenly concerned with breathing and ignorant of his goals, Trey surfaces. He gasps for air as his lungs feel like ripping apart, eyes darting to the debris that floats alongside him. His lighter, his pack of cigarettes, his coke ring circle--all useless and wasted. Let 'em corrode. 'Trey.' Like a lighthouse, a gentle voice calls to him from the coast. It's soft, feminine, drifting to him from a half-mile north of the house that peers down from the hill. Another follows it, and another, until an entire ill-timed chorus chants his name with the reverence of a dreaded, ancient incantation. It tears through his head, drilling a hole straight from ear to ear with a melancholy he can't shake--so he fills his lungs and dives again. Blind, out of his setting, things like 'up' or 'down' lose relevance. Something brushes past him and lets out a squeak, followed by the clicks. He latches onto it, grabbing at a fin. The creature thrashes out, clicking and squealing all the way, until another shade breaks through the sea and hits Trey in the ribs at full-force. His body jerks backwards, floating aimlessly upwards, until a third slams into him. He feels something give in himself, a rib or a will, and smiles as he bounces from strike to strike. The world rings in his ears, a heavy overlay of static clouding his vision until he just closes his eyes. Gives in. Pretends it was his decision. Teeth clench around his ankle and drag him down through the swarm of beasts, bubbles and blood rising from his half-open mouth and nose even as he's still used for target practice. And, it stops. His boots hit on sand and he opens his eyes. He takes a plaintive step and doesn't fall into a trench, so he takes another. Small fish, bright blue bait in the moonlight, glide past him in schools. He grabs one and eats it, taking it down in two bites, before sitting on an outcropping of coral to look around this brave new world. His eyes adjusted, a thousand insignificantly small life forms dart and linger in the wonderland. Everything glows like a movie set, fluorescent like an electrical charge. Awed, he lets the current take him and drifts further down the sloping ocean bed. Life hides as he passes, throwing itself into a maze of coral or changing its hue to blend in or simply swimming away faster than Trey can. His stomach's full, they waste their time and he wastes his. 'Trey.' He snaps his head around, looking for the source. It rises from the deep, none of the ghosts of the shore having followed him down. Curious and out of options, he kicks his feet and struggles into the murkiest ends of the earth. The voice calls out again, louder and breaking out of more throats than before. It's an uneasy call, his name from vocal chords not made to speak English, and it lures him on with a blank mind. He takes a deep breath and lets the cacophony linger just long enough to try and figure out where he is. Of course, it doesn't matter--he has no plans of coming back once the mystery's solved. An octopus shoots itself past him, changing its color to a glowing red. 'Trey.' 'What?' The octopus stops and reorients itself to stare at him before shooting off again. He thrashes desperately to follow it, gaining and losing on it in infrequent bursts of speed. His muscles readjust to the new form of movement, drawing his legs tight together and his arms straight out. The wide sleeves of his jacket bubble into wings, somehow stiff enough to move enough water that he finds himself again beside the octopus. It flashes from red to yellow and through the rainbow, then lets itself fall back to red. Then, with an orgasm in ink, it shoots straight downwards. Trey follows it and scrapes though a narrow hole, forgetting the surface forever. Brave or childish. The octopus, eight legged beacon, guides him through a tight maze dug in a hard rock formation. They circle for hours, dancing in true darkness, until the passages open up into a tremendous chamber, a hundred feet by a hundred feet by a hundred more. Its rock walls are smooth, save an intricate verse carved deep into the face. The letters, no more than a half of an inch tall and in no language man's seen, spiral down the walls. He traces his fingers along and dives to the floor. The verse ends abruptly on its last line, a foot away from filling all walls entirely. Bathed in the soft red glow, he fishes his hands through the sand for something hard and sharp and finds two rocks: one blunt, one chiseled. Putting the chisel to the wall, he stops and stares Minutes pass and he can't summon the voice to complete. 'Trey.' 'God damnit, what?' He looks again to the octopus and finds it baring a string of code down the center of its bulbous head. He puts the chisel to the wall and begins hammering in the characters, unintelligible but vaguely menacing, until at last his coda is finished. At peace, he rises to the top and begins reading the strange song as best he can. Erosion has worn some spots smooth and useless, but others stand out as fresh. It weaves a long tale set in time immemorial, a story of the old owners of the earth. Massive beasts, forces of nature as much as they were actual creatures, roamed the land and made sacrifices of all other life that spawned from the primordial ooze. It names their names, gibberish to a human ear, and tells of their ways in detail. They ravaged the world for eons, until they left into the ocean for reasons that aren't mentioned. Life spread in their absence, infant life without any trace of the elders' existence. While they dreamed, civilizations rose and fell. Species were hunted into oblivion. Man killed man and beast; beast killed beast and man. Religions rose and gave dim hopes to the world, ignorant of what slumbered below. Trey, yeah, he takes it all in like a child on his father's knee, too awed by the fantasy to question the reality. Several times around the room tell him of his world, winding slowly to the lower half. He reads of wars and warriors and gods and goddess, great nations that almost conquered the known world before fading away without even a name. He reads of his life, of Jordan's life, of every minor detail in the world. He reads. The spiraling narrative dropping lower and lower, until finally his chest is against the sand as he circles. 'All dreams end,' it tells him, 'and none sleep forever.' He can't help but laugh. 'The waves shall part and the elders shall return when their night has ended.' It ends untranslatable, the alien tongues he had carved in. 'That it?' 'No.' 'What next?' 'Aloud.' A tentacle stretches through the water and slides along the final line. Trey clears his throat. 'H'turt eht g'nirb,' he begins in a guttural spasm of language. 'Noitavlas g'nirb, eugalp g'nirb, ecneliyd'rp g'nirb. Uhluhtc, erom on me'ard.' Finished, he stares and questions his own words. 'So, what's it mean?' Darkness. Alone. He listens in between the clicks and the pained sound of his own breathing. His body panics, stupidly damned into a hole at the seafloor. 'Fuck,' he curses nothing but himself. 'Fuck.' A quaking from beneath brings an end to his whining. Another follows and he feels the cave rumbling and quaking apart. He kicks off of the bottom and fights upwards, rocks and boulders tumbling slowly down past him. A sudden jolt, and quiet comes. The moon streams down from a long break in the ceiling. Trey dashes up, stopping when another rumble begins. Slowly, he comes to understand it as not a quake but a voice; a low and terrible thing not of this earth. He looks around in desperation as the floor of the room cracks apart in massive chunks. Another wave of the ceiling begins collapsing, and he darts around in the shadowed edges of the room to avoid even bigger problems. With a mighty rumble, the floor explodes out. Sand and dust swirl in chaos, creating a whirlpool that the chamber begins emptying into. He claws desperately, sinking his fingers into a rough outcropping of rock that hangs close to breaking from the wall entirely. The water cuts and tears at him as it rushes past from the labyrinth beyond and the ocean above. Then, equilibrium is found. He lets loose from the wall and turns, opening his eyes carefully into darkness. A labored breathing, just off-step from his own, fills the room as the stench of a rotting ocean rises to his nose. He fights back vomit, overcome with nausea and a slight bent of madness. He can't blink for fear of his own creation, brought forth in ignorance and blind rage. Light falls in small beams on a rough surface, playing in hard scales and high contrast. It grows and falls in slight twitches; the bulk of it nearly filling the room. Trey takes an uneasy push from the wall and drifts in closer to whatever's risen, playing an aquatic green in those thing respites of light. The room vibrates again, knocking from it a few more heavy boulders to sink to the bottom. A tentacle crashes through the water towards him, and he dodges at the last moment. Another roar shakes the foundations of heaven and hell, and Trey swims in again, constantly dodging the thrashing beast. He comes within ten feet of it, barely able to make out a rubbery body beneath the tentacled-head when he hears that soft, gentle voice calling his name again. A thousand yellow eyes open at the same time.
