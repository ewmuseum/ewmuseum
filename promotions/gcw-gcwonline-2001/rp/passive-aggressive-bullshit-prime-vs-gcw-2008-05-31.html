Passive-Aggressive Bullshit (Prime vs GCW)

31 May 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/31/2008 ---- "You say your name like you're no longer convinced that's who you really are." ---- I'm in a bad place now, hands fisted at my sides, throbbing under the tape. Figure when I peel it off, I'll find the knuckles swollen and bruised, just this side of busted again. I went too hard, and I haven't forgiven myself that loss. I went down to the gym after losing, after meeting the stares of my peers. Avoidance of those condolences that seemed to be on everyone's lips- and the looks of smug reprisal from guys like Bagwell. Cocky prick. Seventeen rounds with the speedbag, in silence. There's my penance. Sitting in the dark, in a puddle of my own sweat, escapism at its best. Head back against the wall, feeling cool cinderblocks behind my head. Bottle of water in hand, and some backlight, bad action movie glow of the single lamp in the background that throws shadows across my lap. I'm not striving for something here, I'm just breathing. Feeling the ache. Feeling the burn in my lungs as I draw another breath. Zero hour has come and gone. San Diego will never be the same. I lie to myself because it's easy in this moment to feel like I've done something good. Beaten low, submission imminent. I know that I am not a God, but I can pretend now, with the roar of the crowd still in my ears. I feel lucky. I am doing what I love. I am a modern day gladiator, except I don't fight for my life out there. I don't fight to the death, although right now I feel a little like I did. Balls to the wall, full throttle. I know no other way. I dig deep, pull it all up from my guts, and throw myself into things with the frenzy of that eager mailroom intern that wants to move up in the company. Guts and determination, and zero self-consciousness. I don't care what you think, honestly, I just give it. I hear my name called, and through the rushing roar in my ears, it sounds far away. Like I'm lost at sea, drowning. Throw me a lifeline, build me a raft. I'll cut the cord; I'll burn the sticks you've lashed together for me. I like this feeling. Hollow and empty, the void inside me as I wait for something to fill me back up. I poured it all out inside that ring tonight against Seymour Almasy. I lost, and I am not surprised. If you examined my GCW career thus far, you'd see more of those than wins. But in these pure moments, when I am hollowed out, waiting for the world to fill me back up like an empty vessel, I feel that. Unease. Unrest. A shadow moves across the floor, drawing towards me like the night falling over me. A lifetime later she crouches down in front of me, taking my aching hands in hers. Slowly, gingerly, she unravels the tape, letting the sweat-logged skin breathe. I can feel the throb now, pulsing like the waves on the shore. Flex my fingers, feel the blood flowing now, bringing the pain with it. Pain centers me, and pushes back the hollow. The emptiness fades as she lays her hands on my knees, leaning forward to look into my eyes. She looks as tired as I feel. Hair coming loose from her ponytail, she blows it from her face with violent motions. That hint of a wry smile at the corner of her lips as she looks at me. She knows this void, and she trips it with me. It's all good. We can breathe without thinking about breathing. We can exist in each other's eyes for a moment. In that second, we know just enough to know that we know nothing- remember nothing. Not really. We know each other, and that's all that matters. Automatic. Essential. Pure and simple. Kindred rebel spirits. This is the aftermath. ---- Wednesday, May 21, 2008 1357 hours, local time somewhere in California "San Dimas High School football rules!" Jackson shouted the words, getting a groan from his girlfriend Georgie Nickles as they passed the sign announcing the 28-mile marker to San Dimas. Just cruisin', like the horrible duet between Huey Lewis, and that ugly chick that married the guy from Coldplay. Really no destination in mind, and no real reason to head back to Chicago just to bake in the heat and humidity, they were making their casual way to Las Vegas. Chuckling softly to himself, Jackson cast a sidelong glance at her, a smile curving his lips. "Humor me, babe. I'm in a good mood?" She leaned back in the seat, her feet against the windowsill, bright pink painted toenails drawing his gaze as she wiggled her toes in the wind. Long, dark hair was secured in two identical braids, making her look just a little like an extra from the remake of Dukes of Hazzard. The only sign of life besides the wiggling toes was the slight rise and fall of her chest beneath the ancient, moth-eaten hoodie that hung off her slight frame. It used to be his, and perhaps that was why she wore it. "A good mood?" She echoed, incredulity in her voice. "I mean, not that I'm knocking it? but? really?" The sun beat down on the old highway that would eventually lead them to their destination. Grinning like a madman, Jackson jerked the wheel, merging onto an off-ramp and startling her. Heat waves shimmered- it was hot on the blacktop as the car sped down, leaving a dust trail behind it. The sun was high in the sky, and the laughter came easy for once. The Torino seemed to be gliding on the pavement, sleek as a panther with that low growl. She reached for the cigarettes on the dash as they slid across to her side. She shook one out, and lit it, taking a drag and blowing the smoke towards the roof with an irritated sigh before passing it over to him. She didn't smoke, but his lighthearted mood made her feel edgy. "You could probably slow down," she murmured, watching his reaction. He slipped the Camel cigarette between his lips, pulling the sharp Turkish blend into his lungs. Smoke wafted in his face, forcing him to squint his eyes. Clad in a pair of black jeans, a black wifebeater, and a Hawaiian shirt adorned with dice and flames- a Vegas bent, for sure, he seemed to be nothing but freedom and rebellion. He took a long drag, enjoying the bite of the smoke, the pain in his sore throat. Too much yelling. He always got a little too high in the ring. A little too intense, it sometimes took him days to mellow back out. She turned to look at him, reaching to pull the elastic off one of the braids before dragging her fingers through the freed ebony strands. The freedom of the open road spoke to her on some elemental level, and she giggled suddenly, feeling playful. The spaghetti strap of her tank top crept down one slim shoulder as she turned her attention to the other braid. Jackson took the final pull off the cigarette between his lips, and flicked it out the open window. "You actually want me to slow down?" Open challenge was in the words as he cocked one dark brow in her direction. Raising her head up to look at him for a moment, she turned her body at the waist to look directly in his eyes - a slow grin spreading across her lips. "Oh? You mean you call this fast?" The challenge met in her own words, the devious, impish gleam in her eyes meeting his own. She tossed her head, the damp, and curly hair spilling around her shoulders as she blinked up at him. "Them's fightin' words, pudding." He released the wheel, draping his right arm across the back of the bench seat as he put the hammer down, pouring on more speed. The engine was purring- more a throaty growl- and Jackson's laughter matched it in tone and timbre. Georgie's head fell back as she laughed, overcome with a feeling of liberating joy as her eyes closed. Feeling the machine pick up in pace, going quicker and quicker now - in a moment she'd swear they were flying. "Mmmm, no but I can think of a few fighting words there, Brad." Teasing him, barely heard over the purr of the car as she leaned against him even further, nestled against his arm, her black hair flying wildly in the wind. "Oh, like what?" His eyes flicked to the rearview, and the empty road behind them. Not another car in sight. "You know, I like it fast." He let the words hang between them, allowing the tension to grow as he looked down at her. His fingertips brushed against her exposed shoulder, drawing patterns on the pale skin. "You sure didn't seem to mind it slow last night." She sidled closer, pressing her entire upper body against his as she turned towards him. She brought her hand up, idly running her fingers through his graying hair. The tongue in cheek comment was accompanied by a devilish grin, and an impish snicker. Leaning into him, she whispered into his ear, cupping her hand over it as if to make sure he heard every single word before settling back down beside him with a somewhat triumphant look on her face. "You're evil," he replied, casting a sidelong glance in her direction. The smile that curved his lips was possessive, a demonic leer. "Besides, you wouldn't dare. In broad daylight? Yeah, that'll be the day I win the lottery, let alone win a match against the likes of Seymour Almasy. Little miss prude." Raising an eyebrow, she fired back a reply. "Prude?! Prude?!" Snorting, she folded her arms in front of her, with a pout on her lips as she pushed away from him. Returning to her former perch, she pulled the hoodie around her, covering up her assets, and freckled shoulders, glaring out the window. "Hmph." Georgie was a wonderful actress, a fact that he hadn't quite realized. It seemed so serious, as if he had actually upset her. "Hey," Jackson eased up on the gas pedal, turning to look at her as she slumped against the door. "Hey, I'm sorry, baby. It's just been a rough week..." "Not as much as you're going to be in a few minutes." Moving back over to him like a bullet, her hand moved to his inner thigh, and her lips pressed against his neck. Whispering against his ear, her breath tickled him, and made him grin. "I think I will dare. Whatcha gonna do about that, hotshot?" "You..." he clenched his teeth, grinding them together, "are incorrigible, Georgette." He almost growled the name, playing the role as well as she did- pretending to be angry and annoyed. "But you like it." Whispering against his flesh still, her hand slid down his thigh and between his legs as she giggled softly. "Mmmmm," she crooned, as her lips wrapped around his earlobe and suckled on it for a moment before pulling back. "Don't you?" "I..." his voice cracked, faltering as he tried like hell to keep his focus on the road, and not what she was doing to him, "yeah." Grinning softly, Georgie bit his neck before pulling back and then settling against him once more. "I should behave, so we don't get into a car accident though?" Jackson looked at her, and then at the rear view mirror. Still nothing but empty road. Up ahead there was an In 'n Out burger joint. At this time of day, it was completely deserted, just a single car in the lot. "Screw that," he snapped, jerking the wheel. In a spray of gravel, the Torino fishtailed into the lot, rattling to a stop. Georgie raised an eyebrow to him, pulling her feet back into the car. "Oh... yes! I'm starving, Brad!" She said innocently, far too innocently as she batted her eyelashes at him. "Damn right," he snarled, freeing himself of the seatbelt and crawling on the seat towards her. He braced one arm on either side of her head, one hand gripping the doorframe, the other digging into the leather seat behind her. "Screw the games... I... want you... right now." Even then she could feel the trembling in his arm, radiating through the seat as she looked up at him. The fine edge of control was about to snap, and she could read it all over his face. Her arm draped around his neck as she rose up to meet him, looking into his eyes, whispering softly against his lips, "I'm right here." ---- The inside of the restroom was red and white, some twisted checker-board pattern from the depths of hell. Didn't matter, because it was cold, and he needed to take a leak like his former namesake. He was washing his hands when the stall behind him opened, and the bespectacled man in black stepped out. Their eyes met in the mirror, and Jackson immediately looked away, feeling repulsed by the strange smile on the man's face. He cupped his hands under the blessedly cool water, and tensed, figuring the weirdo would join him at the other sink at the end of the long, trough-like counter. Instead the man held back, still watching him intently. Jackson splashed the cold water on his face, washing away the sweat, still feeling her lips all over his body as he closed his eyes. When he opened his eyes, the man was closer, and was wringing his hands. "I don't swing that way," he said softly, his voice vibrating with anger, "and the name's not George Michael." "Oh, I know," the man replied, his mellifluous voice in perfect discord with his strange appearance. "I know who you are, Brad Jackson." Interest piqued, Jackson turned, facing the man as he wiped his wet hands on his jeans. The man was chubby, dressed in a rumpled black suit. It wasn't the suit that rendered him both ominous and absurd, no that honor was bestowed solely on the ancient black fedora that looked like something Bogart would have worn in Casablanca. Eyes blurred behind the glasses, features shaded by the brim of the nostalgic hat, the man nevertheless peered up at Jackson. "You know me, huh? Then you probably know that getting this close to me is probably not a wise choice, right buddy?" "On the contrary, Mr. Jackson. I find myself in the most precarious of situations on a daily basis." Jackson snorted, rolling his eyes at the man in black. "Yeah, well eating at a place like this'll do that. Flirting with food poisoning. Listen, weirdo. If you want an autograph? I'm happy to oblige, but if all you want is a sympathetic ear for some stalker moment, I'm afraid I'll have to pass. There's a beautiful woman out there waiting for me, and she's probably finished her food, and started in on mine by now." He shouldered past the man, moving towards the door. The hand falling on his shoulder made him stop, and turn around so quickly he almost knocked the man over. Before he could open his mouth and unleash some scathing cut-down, the man pressed a black card into his hand. Jackson looked down, finding the front blank. Grunting softly, he turned it over, and found the following words on the back, scrawled precisely in silver ink: Phillips Arena Parking Lot. Atlanta, GA, May 31st, 12 PM. When he looked up, the man was gone. ---- I arrived on time, annoyed at the intrusion on my personal downtime. I'd flown from Vegas to Atlanta, finding it next to impossible to rent a car on such short notice. Instead, I had to slum, and hail a yellow cab. As the cab circled the lot, I was surprised to see a black limousine, and two other cars arriving there as well. We all stepped towards the limousine at the same time, and I touched the hood, mocking the opulence as I lit my cigarette. Surprised when I found myself standing face to face with Thom LeStrange and Zacharia Taylor, I was even more shocked when Chris Bagwell stepped out of the limo. Wheels were already turning when the black Lincoln pulled in, stopping in front of us. I wasn't surprised when he stepped out, that strange man in the black fedora like some extra from a Raymond Chandler novel. He launched into some Uncle Sam speech, about how we'd been selected to represent GCW- about how special we are. I know who I am, and I sure as shit don't need some weirdo in a Sinatra hat to remind me. Most of it went over my head, white noise. Whatever. I heard a couple names, some that were familiar. Jason Natas. Simply Beautiful. Union Jack. Jimmy Bonafide. Guys I knew were wrestling with Prime. I heard him tell us this was an opportunity of a lifetime, but I was too busy staring at LeStrange, and watching as the energy seemed to flood into each of the men I'd been chosen to stand alongside. Chris Bagwell, the brawler, the badass, loudmouth who'd done so much in such a little time. Zacharia Taylor, the stalwart and vigilant man who'd turned to religion as an answer. Thom LeStrange, the annoying British upstart who'd provoked me at every turn. All men who were the best and brightest in GCW. And then, there was me. The guy who'd choked at every title match thrown his way, or been screwed out of a win by outside interference. The guy who'd walked away on nothing more than a whim. Part of the cause? Solidarity among like-minded men? No, perhaps not, but I did feel a grin on my face as I looked around the loose circle we'd formed. With these three guys, we could sure kick some ass. And really, went it came right down to it? isn't that what this industry is all about? I'm in. I'm game. Bring on the bad boys from Prime, and let me do what I do best. Break them, rip apart their egos, and leave 'em crying for mommy. That's what being The Machine is all about. Not some clever namesake to make you wet your pants. I'm talking serious as a heart attack. Reality, boys. Ever heard of it? It's the place where we come from, the place where GCW is the best, and Prime is a joke without a good punchline. Hah, I'm a non-stop laugh riot, I know, but put aside the pedantic insults, and step into the ring with us. I know who'll be walking away under our own power, heads held high. Won't be you couple of botards, that's for sure. Choke on that, sunshine. Simply beautiful. Yeah, our victory will be just that.
