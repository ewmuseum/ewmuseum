Our Darkest Days, Part Five: Angels With Dirty Faces

June 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 2007 “I never wanted your heart to break but we still knew it would, Impossible to save, but I did what I could.” - The Bronx, Dirty Leaves JUNE 2007 TALLAHASSEE, FLORDIA SUNDAY 17th 12:30am He killed the engine with a flick of the ignition; a dark shadow enveloped the land in front of the black Mercedes as his eyes regained focus. It was the first chance he’d had to take the car out for a spin on the open country road since he’d bought it a couple of days ago and he wished it could be under better circumstances. As his eyesight began to adjust to the night he could see the moonlight shimmering over the tranquil surface of Lake Jackson. At this moment he yearned for the conflict in his heart to give way to the peacefulness of the lake, but there were far too many contrasting thoughts bouncing around in his head for that. While at first glance he may have looked like a man at peace with the world, if you took a look in his bloodshot eyes you’d soon realise that things weren’t quite as they seemed. This place was perfect. The single carriageway road was dead, and he was on the opposite side of the lake from the endless rows of lakeside holiday homes. There wasn’t a building within at least a one mile radius, and there wasn’t a person anywhere in sight. The only thing that worried him slightly was the car pulled up on the roadside a couple of hundred yards away, but the lights weren’t on and there were no other signs of life emanating from it. Nobody would catch him here. He pulled his hands from the steering wheel and held them out in front of his face. Even in the dark of the night he could see that the blood on his hands at dried up completely. A thin crimson lining had crept its way around the outside edges of his fingernails; that would be a bitch to clean. Cupping his left hand, he made an attempt to scratch off some of the blood on his right; a plan he soon abandoned after the act proved itself to be not as successful as he’d thought it would be. Besides, no point in covering the floor of your beautiful new Mercedes with flakes of dried blood. He’d wash it off when he got home. At last he was alone with his thoughts. He’d hurtled through the past few hours like a freight train and if he didn’t have this cooling off period he thought he would probably go insane. With the car’s air conditioning turned off he soon felt the thick, muggy air of the Floridian night as it crept into the car. He felt a build-up of heat in around his head so he pulled the black and white trucker cap off and stuffed it haphazardly into the black holdall bag that sat on the front passenger’s seat. As he wiped the perspiration from his brow the moisture of the sweat mixed in with the dried blood, leaving a thin residue of crimson on his forehead that he was oblivious to. He did, however, notice the blood on the back of his hand become soluble as he pulled it down, but he didn’t care; he had far more important things to worry about. He began to question himself if what he’d done was the right thing. He’d never done anything like that before, he always thought he was “above” the crooks and criminals of the world and the thought of being lumbered within that group sickened him to the core. What if it caught up with him? He hadn’t exactly done things meticulously, and it was doubtless that he’d left a nasty mess back at Grunwald’s house. His heartbeat began to quicken and he felt himself began to perspire, but just as it looked like he was beginning to lose things he spotted a couple of headlights in the distance, crawling at a moderate pace towards his current position. “Shit, stay focused!” he mumbled as he grabbed hold of the steering wheel for support. He took deep breathes, attempting to slow his heart rate down, but it wasn’t working. As the car drew nearer and nearer to his Mercedes he figured there was only one thing for it. WOOSH! As the car swept past he quickly dived to the side across the passenger’s seat, hoping that nobody would see him. It was probably a good decision when you consider what the likely reaction of the average human being would be to seeing a nervy, sweating man with a small smear of blood across his forehead sitting in a lay-by at half-past midnight. He felt his composure coming back to him. “Of course it was the right thing to do. Motherfucker had it coming.” ---- FRIDAY 15th 1:30pm BZZZT! BZZZT! BZZZT! BZZZT! BZZZT! BZZZT! Andy felt his mobile phone vibrating in the pocket of his Nike sweatpants as he slapped the final slice of bread atop his freshly prepared double-decker club sandwich. It had been a tough couple of hours in the gym and the last thing he desired was an unwanted phone call to shatter a rare moment of internal peace. “Alright, alright?” the frustrated Scotsman mumbled as he sucked a small splodge of low-fat mayonnaise from his thumb. “What is it?” he hissed after answering; he didn’t even check the screen to see who was calling. What with his brother’s recent arrest and suspicions regarding the behaviour of his wife Andy had found himself becoming careless, and as soon as his finger punched the “Answer” key he inadvertently hit one of the numbers on the keypad as well. “What the hell?” was the response on the other end of the line as the recipient heard the faint “beep” from Andy’s unintentional number pressing. “Uh, hi Mr. Murray, it’s Wesley Easterbrock here?” Fuck. The private investigator was pretty much the last person Andy wanted to hear from this afternoon. He rolled his eyes. He was still struggling with the mental weight of knowing that he’d hired somebody to follow his own wife around all day. Murray didn’t think he could deal with another one of Wesley’s identikit progress reports, especially when his body was full of aches and pains after a particularly intense workout session. Why the hell was he calling anyway? Easterbrock knew he was only supposed to call Andy at a set time; once in the morning and once in the evening. This was 1:30pm, this didn’t fit the criteria. But then it clicked. Something must be up. “Something to report, Sherlock?” the Scotsman enquired as his teeth sunk in to the first triangle of the sandwich. His tone came over a lot more sarcastic than he had intended, but Murray didn’t feel the need to apologise to a guy whom he saw as a kind of “professional voyeur”. “Yes, this is important Andy,” Wes replied. Andy could hear the anxiety in his voice. “This is big, I’ve been trying to call you since last night but your cell’s been off.” “What is it then?” asked Murray. He completely disregarded Wes’s comment about his phone being off; heck, Andy mightn’t have even heard that part. “You must meet me immediately, Murray, I don’t like to divulge such sensitive information over the telephone line,” the PI said. “I think I might have made a “breakthrough” here, if you pardon the word choice. You’re not doing anything are you?” The mouthful of sandwich that Andy was attempting to swallow felt like a big lump of concrete as it lurched down his throat. This was it? this was really it. Her unannounced departures, her irregular schedules, her meetings with an un-named man? the truth behind all of it was about to be laid before him. And yet Andy wasn’t sure whether he wanted to really find out. Maybe he was better off when he was ignorant to all of his; if he didn’t know about it at least it wouldn’t eat away at his conscience. “Ignorance is bliss,” they say. Andy bounced that statement through his head a couple of times. Fuck that. He needed to find out, good or bad. This thing had eaten away at his brain for far too long; it was time to lay it to rest. Besides, who’s to say that Wesley wasn’t calling to clear Jane’s name? Maybe she hasn’t been up to anything underhand after all? That hopefully idea soon dissipated from Andy’s mind. Wesley wouldn’t have called like this if it was just to say “coast’s clear”. “Andy?”” “I can meet you in thirty minutes?” ---- SATURDAY 16th 11:05pm He ran through a mental checklist in his head as he sifted through the various items that were laid out on his bed. Spare jacket? “Check.” Spare trousers? “Check.” Spare shoes? “Check.” ? small claw hammer? “Check.” One by one he stuffed them into the holdall. His hands trembled as he slowly zipped the bag up; his mental health was hardly a pretty picture at the moment. Throughout his life he had always regarded himself as being incredibly composed; he couldn’t remember the last time he had lost his cool. It wasn’t every day that he was preparing to do something like this though. But he had to do this! He had told himself this a thousand times since Friday afternoon and every time it became more and more convincing. While the thought of what he might do to Benjamin Grunwald wasn’t easy for him to take, he could accept it. An eye for an eye only makes the world blind. He used to live by that line, but he would throw it to the side this evening. He was going to make everything right; a gargantuan thorn was about to be plucked from his side, and he couldn’t wait. Consumed by vengeance, he didn’t think of the potential consequences of his actions for a second. All that mattered tonight was making things right. Act now, think later. ? this wasn’t like him at all. He who makes a beast out of himself gets rid of the pain of being a man. Inhaling deeply he grabbed the bag from the bed and slung it over his left shoulder, the hammer digging uncomfortably into his shoulder blades. Time to do this. ---- FRIDAY 15th 2:10pm Andy burst through the front door of the diner with authority, and hell followed with him. He hadn’t even changed out of his sweaty workout clothes since Easterbrock had called him; he just hopped in the first cab that would stop for him. He was far, far too eager to hear what the private investigator had to say to him to even consider getting changed. It didn’t take long for him to spot Wesley sitting alone at a tipping, sipping on some coffee. Right there in the very same spot the two of them had sat when they’d first met just four weeks ago. The diner was sparsely populated but by-no-means empty, and the sudden arrival of the 6’7? Scotsman caused a degree of surprise to many of those in attendance. Regardless, the soon got back to their own business as Andy strode across the cafeteria and slipped into the chair opposite Easterbrock. “What have you got then?” asked Andy in a cold, dead tone of voice, showing no emotion whatsoever. His eyes were almost popping out of his head as he watched the investigator slowly take a sip of the lukewarm liquid. “You’re ten minutes late,” Wes said as he glanced at his watch. Andy didn’t have time for his attitude. He pounded his fist against the table, the shockwaves causing a little bit of Wesley’s coffee to sway out over the top of the cup. “GOD DAMN IT! I don’t have time for this bullshit!” Andy snarled at a volume close to a shout. This was a conscious decision; he needed to speak at a level that would successfully emphasises his point, but at the same time he didn’t want to cause too much of a fuss in a public place. Easterbrock submitted: “Alright, alright?” Obviously he knew that it wouldn’t have been a good idea to anger the Scotsman further, given the size difference between the two and Andy’s chosen profession. “Can I ask you first of all, have you heard from Jane today?” “Why does that matter!”” barked the anxious Andy. “No, I haven’t, she’s seeing her sister this weekend, just spit it out?” “At her Sister’s” I wouldn’t be too sure of that?” Shaking his head, Wes reached into the pocket of his long, beige trench coat and pulled out a tiny, folded piece of paper and slid it across the table to Andy. Without hesitation the Scotsman unfolded and read it in his head. “The fuck is this?” he asked. “That is the name and home address of the guy that your wife is seeing behind your back.” ? “What!”!” Andy’s question went unanswered as Easterbrock swallowed another mouthful of coffee. An unpleasant miasma of mixed-up thoughts collided in Andy’s mind. He knew it, he fucking knew it. As soon as Wes called him in the middle of the afternoon he knew that something was up, and his worst suspicions had just been confirmed; Jane was cheating on him? SHE WAS SEEING SOMEONE ELSE. ? but they’d only just got re-married a couple of months ago! Already the bitch had gone behind his back, brought a massive blight on his family name and fucked him over royally. “FUCK!” Andy’s capacity to process thoughts reasonably left him as he once again slammed his fist onto the table; this time a heck of a lot harder. This disturbed the majority of the members of the public in attendance; especially the elderly woman seated behind Andy, who almost spat her false teeth out in shock. From behind the bar a staff member took note of Andy’s rage; “Sir, I’m afraid I’m gonna have to ask you to leave?” she drawled in a thick Southern accent. Too late, he was already gone. ---- SATURDAY 16th 11:45pm As he stepped out from his car and into the suburban cul-de-sac the darkness of his clothes colluded with the black of the night. He felt at one with the shadows. On his feet he wore a heavy pair of steel toe-capped Dr. Martens; if it came to the worst they’d certainly make dealing with Mr. Grunwald a heck of a lot easier. A pair of black paints occupied his lower half, and on his upper a tight, black long-sleeved t-shirt. He had a black scarf wrapped around the lower half of his face ? concealing everything below his eyes ? and had a forward-turned black and white trucker cap seated on top of his head. Dressed not unlike a cross between a ninja and a Los Angeles gangbanger, he took a deep breath before stepping along the lawn leading up to the home of Benjamin Grunwald. The claw hammer hung from the back pocket of his trousers ominously. From the outside, at least, it looked like your average suburban Florida home; painted white exterior, medium-sized, porch out front with an old rocking chair sitting just left of the door; lovely home to bring up a family. He couldn’t afford to think like that though, not tonight. If he found himself thinking about this guy’s everyday life then the difficulty of this task would be multiplied tenfold. No time for contempt, no time for remorse; he needed to get in, do the job, and get out as quickly as possible. This bastard deserved every second of pain he was going to experience tonight, and his assailant wouldn’t let his own conscience prevent him from gaining retribution. He stepped up onto the porch and knocked on the door. He waited a couple of seconds. No answer. Feeling himself becoming nervous, he clenched a fist and attempted to knock again, but the door swung open before he could do so. “Yeah?” the groggy figure in the threshold asked, somewhat taken aback by the sight of the clad-in-black figure. The assailant took a good look at the guy; he looked to be about forty years old, but judging by his physique he had taken good care of himself. He had an extremely muscular upper-body and stood at about 6’2?; a lesser man might find such statistics intimidating. “Benjamin Grunwald?” croaked the assailant. The man in the doorframe squinted, trying to get a good look at the face of the man at his door underneath the layers of clothing. Almost instantly he realised that something wasn’t right? he attempted to slam the door shut in his face, but the assailant managed to lodge his boot between the doorframe and the door itself. CRASH! He almost knocked the door clean off its hinges as he burst through it and into Grunwald’s home. Without thinking he threw a fist at Ben’s face; it connected, somewhat fortunately, with his nose, sending a spurt of blood flying across the room and dripping down onto Grunwald’s perfectly-presssed white shirt. “AAAAHHHH!” Benjamin yelled in agony as his body fell to the floor. Before he had a chance to even contemplate retaliation his assailant was on top of him, kicking him a couple of times in the gut. Grunwald arched over, clenching his stomach tightly, but as he did so a knee flung into his mouth, instantly dislodging two of his feet. The man in black watched as Benjamin again yelped like a helpless animal, now feeling away at the front of his mouth for his missing teeth. “What the hell do you want!”!” screamed Ben in pain, tears welling up in his eyes. The man in black reached behind himself, searching for the handle of the claw hammer, but his body just wouldn’t let him pull out it. He knew immediately that taking this man apart with the hammer would be far too severe a punishment; he was only here to teach the guy a lesson, not to murder him. The assailant took a step backwards and glanced to his left, straight away he noticed the hob. His mind wandered to an episode of The Shield he had recently watched in which a detective had planted the face of a criminal onto a scolding hot hob as a method of interrogation? ? too much. He quickly turned heel and fled the building; he had taught the guy a lesson. He wouldn’t do it again. He quickly pulled open the door of his Mercedes and pulled off without even fastening his seatbelt. Andy pulled the scarf from his face as he escaped into the night, and oddly a wave of relief swept over him. It could have been a lot worse; Jane would’ve been there, in the house, with Benjamin Grunwald. Murray wasn’t a religious man, but at that moment he thanked the lord that she wasn’t? he would’ve been in all kinds of deep shit if she had been. ---- SUNDAY 17th 12:45am After the car had passed and sped off into the night, Andy left the safety of his own vehicle, dragging the bag behind him. Gently he placed it on the hood of the car and pulled the black shirt over his head, before pulling out a fresh white t-shirt and putting it on. The claw hammer soon followed it. Murray pulled the zipper on the bag shut and walked across the road. It was a good thirty feet at least until the water, and there were many trees in the way, but he didn’t want to risk getting any closer. In the dark he could step on anything; a poisonous snake, an alligator? he wasn’t willing to take those kinds of risks. With a single swoop of his arm he tossed the bag aimlessly at the water? he didn’t know if it would make it. SPLASH! It made it. “I replaced your tears, your loneliness, with cigarettes and wine, But you needed more and we ran out of time, Now your love is gone.” - The Bronx, Dirty Leaves
