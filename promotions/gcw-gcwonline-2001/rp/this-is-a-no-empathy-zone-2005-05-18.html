This Is A No-Empathy Zone

18 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 18, 2005 Fade in. His eyes grew dim for a moment before lighting up with enough intensity to brighten the room. The room could’ve used it. Most of lights had burned out and chipped paint and cracked walls seemed to surround Toxic as he sat in silence. Of course this was not the type of place you would expect a man of considerable wealth to dwell in. It hadn’t always been like this though. The house used to be an example of luxury and over-indulgence. Every dark corner of the American Dream had been exercised in here. Times change and people, even in their most violent moments, can resort to pacifism in nature. That’s what really happened here. Long bouts of aggression and angry out lashes had torn the place apart. What actually caused it to become a decaying ruin, however, was the ensuing bout of pacifism that followed almost immediately afterwards. He’d stopped caring. They say evil comes in many shapes and forms but nobody would’ve expected something like this. He didn’t particularly enjoy watching people suffer or the destruction of items, it was a matter of necessity that he felt indifferent about. That being said, there certainly were parts of it that gave him a high that no drug could possibly equal. Sociopathic tendencies? Maybe so. But in the end that didn’t really matter. The point was to get the job done in such a brutal fashion that it was ultimately and completely finished. Adding an exclamation mark was only used to preserve the memory. Supposedly competition entered into the equation but in Toxic’s mind there was no real competition. The pangs of failure were all that were needed to keep this beast moving. Although - even if he refused to admit it ? the glory of winning is what made it all worthwhile. Others in the company needed drugs, money, the promise of fame, or the comfort of a woman to motivate them. Motivation rarely entered his mind however. What was needed to be done got done. Time and time again the point was to prove beyond a doubt that he was the best, the self-proclaimed future of the company. Or that’s how it was at least. The same couldn’t possibly hold true now. There was a fire in his belly and an unquenchable thirst in his mouth that could only appeased by total and utter destruction’annihilation. Annihilation of threats, foes, and prodigies alike. There was no turning back now. Where Peter Maxwell left off was only the beginning, but also the opposite. No he wasn’t on a mission to destroy GCW, he was going to save it by destroying its past and it’s present. Why? Because that was the only way to control them. As Orwell once wrote “He who controls the past, controls the future. He who controls the present, controls the past”. The future he was guaranteed could only be his by taking the current course of action. In essence, he was The Dark Messiah’s second act. If GCW was destroyed inadvertently he would only hold those not strong enough to survive responsible. He would not be blamed for a failure on such a colossal level. Once he had control of the spotlight’control of the future he could morph it, or dictate it the way he saw fit, ensuring its survival. This, of course, led back to who he’d always been. Something he could never change in the core of his persona no matter how vicious he became. Pure egotism. He wanted the spotlight. On top of it all, GCW’s survival was intertwined with his soul. If it died Toxic would fade into oblivion which was something, despite all of his heartless cruelty, he could not bear. All this returned with force to the current issue of his passive attitude towards pain. Once again, it was what needed to be done. Toxic had to stake his claim now or risk losing it forever. That’s why he was indifferent because more than anything in the world, the thing that mattered most to Toxic was Toxic. He was selfish and most certainly egocentric. These thoughts were interrupted by a loud bang on the door. Toxic slowly walked over to the door. His eyes were still ablaze as all of these thoughts and many more crowded his head. The door seemed to swing open voluntarily in his hand. Standing before him was his neighbor, Mrs. Hopstein, a Jewish widow with a closet fascination for voyeurism. Toxic had caught her aiming a camcorder at his bedroom window last Sunday morning. Mrs. Hopstein also had a ridiculous, well ridiculous to Toxic, of cats. In truth she had about four. They had recently chosen to use Toxic’s yard as a crude litter box and had killed most of the grass along his front walk. “Hello Mrs. Hopstein.” He greeted her in a polite manner. “Have you seen my cats?” She asked with a suspicious tone. “No. Why, is their shit on my lawn again?” He responded sharply. “My cats wouldn’t do that.” She defended. “Well then is it me pissing on my lawn and turning it yellow while I’m in some kind of drunken stupor” I mean you would know after all, you would have it on tape right?” Toxic grinned, obviously amused with his cutting remark. “How dare you-? She started, beginning to blush. “Confront you on your obvious obsession” Now are you really looking for your cats or did you just come here to catch an obstructed view of me?” He said and winked. “I know you haven’t seen them!” She exclaimed. “Because they’re dead! You killed them!” She adamantly accused. “Well actually I think the anti-freeze had more to do with it than I did.” Toxic said with a devious grin. Mrs. Hopstein gaped in horror. “You admit to it?” “Of course. Why would I lie about something so trivial?” He responded callously. “You’re an animal!” “No I did what any blue-blooded American would’ve done ? I defended my home.” “I’m calling the police!” She exclaimed, trying to fight back tears. “And tell them what” That your cats were trespassing on my lawn and I took a preventative action? I did what needed to be done.” He said with a cold stare. “Even if they did respond what do you think they would do?” She said nothing still shocked by Toxic’s confession. “Nothing!” He said with surprising glee. “The only thing they might be able to do is slap me with a $100 fine and force me to find you new cats. And let me tell you I would find you the mangiest, most ill-tempered little fuckers you could ever imagine. Now get the hell off my property!” Toxic harshly commanded her before slamming the door in her face. Toxic sat back down, his eyes still gleaming. To others, it would seem cruel, but it was a necessity. This was his reality. It was his world and he was determined to control it one way or another. Fade out.
