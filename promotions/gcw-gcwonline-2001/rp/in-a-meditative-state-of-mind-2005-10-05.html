In a Meditative State of Mind

5 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/5/2005 [The camera fades into a small lonely gym in New York City during the early 90s. The camera pans around the scene and catches sight of all sorts of equipment thrown about the vicinity. In one corner a large power bag is scene hanging from the ceiling. Not that far away from the massive power bag is a speedbag. The camera continues to pan around the vicinity and catches sight of a large rack full of dumbbells of various weights and sizes. The bench press and a few universal weight lifting machines are scattered about in the same area. The camera continues to pan and catches sight of a large empty wrestling ring set in the middle of the vicinity. All of a sudden the creaking sound of a door opening is hard from the other side of the gym. The camera quickly turns towards the source of the sound. The door opens and a slightly younger Vince Jones appears in the doorway with a black gym bag thrown over his shoulders. He is wearing a pair of black jeans and a white tanktop t-shirt with a bandana on his head. He steps inside and takes a look around the scene and appears a bit angered by the fact that there appears to be no one there.] Vince Jones: (mutters to himself.) Aiight What’s up with this, sh*t? [Vince scans the vicinity with his eyes for a trace of life once again and sees no one at all.] Vince Jones: Hey! Old man! You here? [Vince continues to look around the scene and just shakes his head upon seeing no one at all.] Vince Jones: Old man! Where the hell you at, huh?” V ain’t got all day to be waitin? on ya! Ya heard? Now come on out or The Violence is leavin?! [Vince Jones takes another look around the gym and appears enraged at the fact that he has been invited to this gym and he is the only one there. He slams his gym bag on the floor in frustration and kicks it to the side.] Vince Jones: Look here, old man! You the one that invited me here sayin? you had some things you wanted to teach me! Now if this sh*t a game the game betta end soon cuz V. Jones getting? sick of playin? around here! Now get the hell out here and let’s get down to busines [Vince folds his arms across his chest and waits momentarily but gets no answer and it doesn’t appear as though anyone is coming out at all. Vince shakes his head, grabs his gym bag and tosses it back on his shoulder in fury.] Vince Jones: Enough of the bullsh*t! Screw it! V outta this joint! A voice: Where do you think you’re going so soon, rook? [Vince hears the voice, drops the bag on the floor, and turns towards the source of it. The camera catches sight of an older man with graying hair who looks to be in his early fifties. For being an older man he appears to be in good shape physically as if he was a former athlete of some sort. He closes the door to the back room that’s behind him and just stands there, eyes locked on Vince in slight amusement as he chuckles lightly to himself. Vince appears a bit frustrated with the old man’s amusement and just glares back.] Vince Jones: What’s so funny, old man, huh? And don’t you eva call The Violence a damn rook eva again! That’s that kinda sh*t that can cost you yo damn life! [The old man chuckles to himself and shakes his head.] The old man: Like your life was almost taken from you not so long ago? You should be grateful to me. If I hadn’t found you there in that alley you might be as good as dead right now. Remember that. [Vince pauses in recollection of what the old man is mentioning and lowers his head.] The old man: Now, why are you a bit taken back by my stating of the truth? Despite what you may believe, you, in my eyes are nothing but a rookie. [The old man puts up a single finger.] The old man: Lesson one, if you ever plan to rise above the level of skill you currently reside at then you’re gonna have to learn to have patience sometimes. I had you wait here on me to test your patience and you failed, miserably. [Vince appears a bit confused by the statement and then flares up in rage.] Vince Jones: Whoa, whoa, whoa! You know how many chumps V. Jones has beaten the crap out of, huh?” V. Jones is a fighta! He ain’t no damn rookie! And you can take your damn patience test and stick it! Enough of these damn games! [The old man lowers his head and chuckles to himself in amusement before glancing back at Vince once again.] The old man: You don’t get it, do ya? Anyone can fight. Anything can fight. A child can fight. A chicken can fight. You have great talent and you have an extremely large amount of intensity and determination, but it's gonna do you no good whatsoever if you can't utilize it with control. That is how a wrestler handles himself. A wrestler fights with control. A wrestler fights with his head. I cannot begin to go in to all the aspects of what it takes to be a wrestler and a successful one at that. [Vince Jones just shakes his head as the old man makes his way across the gym towards the wrestling ring.] Vince Jones: Look here, old man. Why you talkin' 'bout control? V ain't a damn wrestler. Haven't you seen what V's done to each and every man that's stepped up to the plate in the NYUFC? V. Jones the king. V don't need your advice. V can handle things on his own. [The old man nods and folds his arms across his chest.] The man: Oh, is that so? Well, how about you and me in the ring right now and I'll show you what I’m talking about. [The older man climbs into the ring and stands in the opposite corner awaiting Vince’s challenge. Vince looks up at him in surprise and laughs outloud.] Vince Jones: What the hell you doin' now, old man? You callin' V out? That's suicide. [The old man motions for him to enter the ring.] The old man: Come on, rook. Show me what you've got. I've been around the ring for years. This is my home. You might know that 'Lion's Den' of yours, but this is the ring and I know it like the back of my hand. Now come on. Let’s see what you’ve got. [Vince Jones rolls into the ring and stands up.] Vince Jones: Old man, you're crazy. V ain't about to kick your ass. It'll be too easy. Come on. We really gotta go through this sh*t? [The old man just gazes across the ring at Vince and motions for him to just bring it.] The man: You scared you're gonna get beat, boy? [Vince looks at him in fury.] Vince Jones: What!” That's it! [Vince Jones comes charging across the ring at full speed ready to pounce, but the old man swiftly steps forward and rolls Vince over into a single leg boston crab in the middle of the ring with nowhere to go.] The old man: Control, is the key to victory, Vince. That was lesson two. You’re never gonna get anywhere if you flip out and fight out of control all the time. That mindset may work with others like yourself, but the most skilled of wrestlers would turn you against your own self and make your own hot temper and rage be your downfall. Now go ahead and tap out. [The pain can be seen wearing down on Vince as he winces in pain, but he closes his eyes and shakes his head defiantly.] Vince Jones: Never! [The old man wrenches back on the leg applying more pressure to the boston crab and making Vince wince with even more added pain.] The old man: Are you gonna give up now? Or am I gonna have to make you give up? [Vince continues fighting as hard as he can to break free of the submission hold.] Vince Jones: Hell no! You're gonna have to rip my damn leg off, old man! The Violence ain’t tappin? for sh*t!! [The old man looks impressed by this as he smiles and tightens the hold even more.] The old man: Well, I guess that brings us to another very important lesson in wrestling. If you want a submission to be broken you must go for the ropes. I will say this though. I’m impressed by your heart. [Vince looks up and spots the ring ropes and pulls himself slowly towards the ropes, stretches out his arm, and grabs the bottom rope. The old man sees this and immediately releases the hold. Vince Jones slowly gets back up to his feet, slightly favoring his leg. The old man puts a hand on Vince's shoulder.] The old man: You may destroy fighters. You may destroy some of the most aggressive fighters there are, but a wrestler that can utilize a balanced mix of control and aggression can conquer anyone. Remember that Vince. [Vince nods his head, half in agreement and half in defeat.] Vince Jones: Yeah. The Violence hearin? ya now. Damn. You know yo sh*t pretty well. [The old man laughs to himself and nods.] The old man: Well, I’d like to think that I know a thing or two when it comes to wrestling. I was a wrestler since the young age of sixteen. I’ve learned a lot over the years. You gotta learn. You have to learn as much as possible. That’s how you last in the sport. If you close your eyes and close your ears to everything that’s going on around you in the sport you’ll never survive. That brings me to another lesson. Always keep an open mind and learn from every experience. [Vince hears his words and nods in agreement.] The old man: If you learned anything at all and changed your perspective on things somewhat from the encounter you and I just had in the ring than that’s far more helpful and important than any win you’ll ever get in your careeer. Always learn from your mistakes and be able to better yourself by doing so. Remember these words, Vince, and you’ll go far. [The old man pauses in thought.] The old man: Ah, and here’s another thing? [The camera slowly fades out and fades back in as we are in the present. Vince Jones is seen standing in the middle of the ring he will be facing Seymour Almasy in at WorldWide this week in Las Vegas, Nevada with his head bowed in silent thought. The words of the old man ring through his mind as he says them outloud.] Vince Jones and the voice of the old man (simultaneously): “always go to the ring or venue in which you must fight or wrestle in before your match. Prepare yourself. Get acquainted with the feel of it. Let it get acquainted with you. Be one with the ring and it will be one with you. Do so in whatever way comes best to you, even if that means just sitting there in the middle of the ring and lettin? your thoughts drift. That’s the way I used to get myself prepared for some of my biggest matches in my heyday, Vince. The more you get acquainted with it before your match the more comfortable you’ll feel in it when the big match comes around ? The Voice of the old man in Vince’s thoughts: Remember this Vince and you will go far. Keep the advice I’m giving you in mind always. I want to see nothing but the best out of you?” [Vince Jones nods to himself and begins to talk in a soft, low tone of voice.] Vince Jones: Don’t worry, old man. The Violence will remember your words always. V been tryin' live off yo words and succeed in this game off of them. It’s rare that V eva says this, but you’ve got my word on that sh*t. You’ll only get my best and nothin? else. That’s the way The Violence been operatin? since that day years ago. Don’t worry. This week at WorldWide V gonna make you proud. V gonna represent you to the fullest, old man. This week V. Jones comes out ontop and walks away with his held high in your name. This week V. Jones silences Seymour Almasy and takes one step closa to bein' where he wanna be in this game...'Nuff said! [At the conclusion of Vince’s statement he takes a seat on a metal folding chair that’s set in the middle of the ring and continues to think and meditate on his match at WorldWide alone and in silence as the camera slowly zooms out and fades to black.]
