Origins

30 September 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/30/10 I. She glanced in the rearview mirror to get a look at herself. She was a mess, tears stained her cheeks, eyes were red and swollen from crying and then there was the dark bruise that stretched from the top of her cheek bone down to her chin. She took one hand off of the steering wheel and lightly touched the bruise. A slight pain rattled her from the touch and she could feel the tears that formed in her eyes ready to pour out again. It was not the pain that made her want to continue crying, rather the events that lead up to that moment. She created as much saliva inside of her mouth as possible before swallowing it down. She shook her head to fight back the tears. She knew that crying wasn’t going to solve anything, it was the reason it all happened in the first place. Adam would never hit her if he wasn’t provoked, he loved her too much to do that. She complained too much and was too needy. She knew how to fix it and she would do everything in her power to do so. She placed both hands back on the wheel of the car and continued to drive. She couldn’t let her mistakes influence the way she performed at work. She also knew she had to hide the mess on her face as best as she could when she got to the Hotel. Other people wouldn’t understand, they would get the wrong idea. They would think this was Adam’s fault. They would say that he should never touch a woman and that he doesn’t really love her. That maybe he has anger issues. Janelle didn’t want to hear it because she knew they would be wrong. Adam loves her and would never do anything to hurt her. It is her fault and she knew she had to accept that even if no one else would. She planned the morning out in her head. She would take a few extra moments before going in to the office to put some makeup on her face. Her hair would be fixed and her tears would be wiped clean. She’d control her breathing and she’d smile. She would pretend that things were as they should be. Janelle arrived at the ‘French Quarter’ Hotel early enough to start her process. It was a process she was all too adjusted to completing. When she was finished it was as if nothing had happened at all. Hair, makeup, tears and smile all good to go. The finishing touch was her name tag that read, “Janelle Ricci”. She took an extra moment to take in what the name tag said. Janelle … RICCI. Her last name had changed just a few months prior. It was proof to Janelle that Adam did love her. That she must be doing something wrong. She knew she had to fix it and fast. She didn’t want to displease her husband. After one last look at herself in the mirror, Janelle pushed open the car door and slid out. She walked towards the doors of the Hotel. She smiled and greeted customers and other cleaning people like herself as she walked. No one bothered saying anything, perhaps they couldn’t notice. She felt relieved. She wanted it to be just like any other day … So far, for better or worse, it was. But it wouldn’t stay that way for long. II. “So how long will you be staying with us?” The man behind the counter, or Jerry as his name tag stated, made Krystal irritated. His wide smile, chipper attitude and his bright eyes. What the hell did he have to be so happy about? Things weren’t going well in Krystal’s world and she figured that everyone should be just as miserable as her. “Just a few days.” She said it with no emotion at all. “three maybe.” Jerry smiled wider and Krystal’s irritation became stronger. Jerry went about his business without a care in the world. He took some notes, checked some paper work and eventually presented Krystal with a key. “You will be in room 235, Ma’am.” He nodded emphatically. “Nice room.” “I’m sure it is.” “I’ll have someone help you up to your room with you being in your condition.” Jerry whistled, made a few hand gestures and quickly a bellhop showed up at Krystal’s side and took her bags in his hands. He introduced himself but Krystal didn’t listen and never really got the name in her memory bank. During that journey towards her room, Krystal couldn’t help but think of what Jerry said. The condition that she was in. CONDITION. Like a disease. Like cancer or HIV. She didn’t disagree either. To Krystal, pregnancy was a disease just as terrible as any other. Especially for a 17 year old who doesn’t know who the father is. Especially for a 17 year old who’s parents would never forgive her for ‘ruining her life’. When she found out she was pregnant, Krystal told no one. She did everything in her power to stay as thin as she always had been and every part of her wished the baby wouldn’t make it because of the things she had done. Two months ago, however she could tell there was no more hiding it. She couldn’t face her parents or the shame of the entire experience. She ran away instead. Her parents could forgive her for being a rebellious teenager but not for being pregnant. No school, no work, no phone calls to family or friends. Some nights she slept in bus stations, others a hotel room and occasionally a shelter. She wanted her life back. She wanted her friends, her school and her family. She wanted to be normal again. Soon she could be. When she could get rid of the tumor that was living inside of her, she could be free. “Here we are,” the bellhop smiled too. Sickness inside of Krystal stirred up again. She unlocked the door and allowed the young man to place her bags inside. “Do not be afraid to give us a call downstairs if you need anything.” “Yeah, sure.” She slid into her room without another word and slammed the door behind her without another look back. Her bright green eyes scanned the room around her. She fights back tears. That would be the place that her freedom finally came to her. She couldn’t wait for her hell to be over. III. “The fuck happened to you?” Janelle’s disguise wasn’t as fool proof as she had believed. Not to her best friend anyways. Nichole, friends with Janelle since High School, had been concerned for awhile about these nagging injuries that continued to build up with Janelle. “Nothing Nikki,” Janelle turns her face to get away from Nichole’s hands. “Just an accident.” “Accident my ass.” Nikki stepped in front of Janelle as she tried to walk away. “That asshole did this didn’t he?” “An accident.” Nichole’s jaws clench, tiny fists ball up at her sides. “Like the sprained ankle? Like the bruised stomach? Like the stitches on your arm?” “I’m clumsy.” Janelle gave out a half hearted laugh in an attempt to steer the line of questioning. She knew it didn’t work. Nichole hated to see her best friend that way. She was once a bright, lively person who loved to be with her friends. Her husband turned her into a shell of her self. He scared her but she loved him. Janelle was confused and afraid. It broke Nichole’s heart. “Why do you put up with it? WHY?” Nichole pleaded. “You don’t have to. He doesn’t deserve you!” Janelle fought back the tears. “NO!” She cleared her throat and glared at Nichole. “I don’t deserve him. I make too many mistakes, I let him down too much.” Nichole throws her hands up into the air. “So he has the right to do that to you!?” “I deserve worse.” “GOD DAMN IT!” Nichole’s own tears found their way to her eyes, one fell down her cheek. “Leave him. Please.” Janelle looked away as she was unable to look her best friend in the eyes. It became harder and harder for her to hold back her tears. “I can’t.” The words came out as a whisper. The two women just stood there for awhile, contemplating what the next move would be. Minutes passed with nothing being said. Nichole is the one who tried to break the awkward silence and the thick tension. “I’m … I just….” “I’ve gotta get to work.” Nichole didn’t go after Janelle as she grabbed a hold of her cart with all the cleaning supplies needed for the day’s work. She wanted to but didn’t know what else to say. Janelle just wanted to work. She could fix the problems in her life. She just knew it. At that moment however her main problem was cleaning her first room of the day. Room 235. IV. Krystal’s body was on fire and the pain was like nothing she had felt in her almost 18 years of existence. The tiled bathroom floor offered no comfort for her back but at least it had been cool on her bare skin. She bit down on a rolled up towel to stop herself from getting hurt or screaming too loud. Moments into the event, unfortunately, fear swept over her like a tidal wave and it washed away any sense of reason. Like any other girl, Krystal had pictured what this day would be like. In her dreams, she had a loving and quite handsome, husband standing by her side. Even with a mask covering his face his smile could be seen because of how happy he was. No camera though, not even Krystal could stomach watching it. Outside, in the waiting room, she imagined her Dad pacing back and forth, destroying all pieces of the floor that he came in contact with. He would get in trouble numerous times from nurses and doctors alike because he just wouldn’t be able to help himself and he would light up a cigarette inside of the hospital. He’d ask for updates about his ‘little princess’ every 2 minutes or so. Krystal’s mother would be shaking and crying the whole time. She would want to bitch at the nurses for not doing their jobs but she would be far too nervous. She’d yell at Krystal’s father to ‘sit the hell down” numerous times to scream at SOMEONE though. Then when it was all over and the proud grandparents could hold their first grandkid in their arms? It would be a little slice of heaven. A happily ever after for a happy family. Krystal wanted that moment. She wanted that life. She would’ve done anything to preserve it. She balled her fists up and placed them on the floor. She gritted her teeth and pushed. Tears flowed freely down her face. She wanted her condition to be done. She wanted things to go back to normal. And with one final, exhausting push, they were. Her body went limp, her head hit the hard floor. She heard the cries from the baby. Her baby. No. Her tumor. Her disease. Her sickness. Her hell … … Was over V. “House keeping!” Janelle received no response so she knocked again. “House keeping!” No answer again. She shrugged and unlocked the door herself and pushed it open and quickly pulled her cart inside. Immediately the stench filled her nostrils which caused her to scrunch her face up and cover her mouth with one of her hands. “Oh my god!” Her stomach felt weak within seconds and she had no idea if she would be able to look long enough to find out what made the horrible stink. She forced down bile that had risen up and shook her head in an attempt to block everything out. She would have to focus like she never had before. It didn’t take her long to realize that the smell was coming from the bathroom. Suddenly fear gripped her as she realized that she had no idea what was behind the door. She remained frozen, staring at the door for a few moments before being able to bring the courage out to do what needed to be done. Her shaky hand moved upwards and with just enough force, Janelle pushed the door open. What she saw, what she felt … it would never leave her again. The world seemed to move in slow motion all around her. A sickening puddle of bodily fluids spread out across the tiled floor. In the center laid the most beautiful child that Janelle had ever put her eyes on. Her heart broke and she could hardly breath in that moment. She fought the urge to throw out, to cry and to remain motionless. She reacted. Not caring that she might be exposed to, Janelle fell to the floor and picked the baby up into her arms. Instantly she felt warmth. She felt love. The baby was hardly breathing, he was hardly alive. Janelle reacted again and sprung to her feet and moved towards the main room and to the phone. Quickly she dialed 9-1-1. “Send someone to The French Quarter Hotel,” Janelle’s voice was frantic. “Room 235. There’s a dying baby!” She hung up. She sat down on the bed. Janelle’s head spun. She had a million questions that she did not know how to get answers to. How could someone leave this angel to die? Who stayed in the room? Did anyone see her leave? Did anyone love this child? Well … Janelle could answer the last question. She did. She just knew it. That baby was meant to be hers. She even had a name already picked out. Her favorite name in the whole world. She smiled at him. “Dominic.” And she felt whole again. Her life was finally complete. VI. Things didn’t seem real to Krystal anymore. Thinking about what had happened made everything feel so far away, so cloudy, as if it were a dream or a movie. She managed to clean herself up enough to not draw attention to herself as she left the hotel. She slowly walked down the hallway, to the elevator which she took to the bottom floor. She stumbled only a few times while going through the lobby and out the doors. She waved for a cab. She got into the car. “Home,” She told the cabby in nothing more than a whisper. “Slater street.” She lets her body sink into the seat and she leans her head into the glass. Her eyes looked out of the window as the car begun to move. She watched the world flash by until it made her sick. She turned her head very slowly to look down at her stomach. She felt a rush of emotion come over her. Tears came again. These weren’t tears of sorrow or sadness but of joy instead. She wasn’t sick. She could finally go home. She would have a million lies to tell once she got there. Just the idea of all the lies made her fatigued, weak, sleepy. She figured that she would have time to get just a little nap. She thought she could regain her strength. She smiled. She closed her eyes. She never felt the blood begin to drip again. She drifted to sleep.
