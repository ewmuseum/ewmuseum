Date: Sometime Five men sat around a poker table, smoking cigars and generally enjoying the company of close friends. It was an honored and time-tested tradition that any self-respecting middle-class American knows all too well; when the wife goes out to spend your money, it’s only customary to gamble the rest. “So Joe,” Bernard said, pulling a drag from a slightly droopy Black & Mild into his cheeks, “when does the deal close on your house” You’ve gotta be pleased as a pig in shit to flip that flea motel as fast as you did.” Joe smiled, laying his cards on the table. His hand held a full house, but even it couldn’t compare to the full home that waited for him when the game was finished. “The buyers were pretty excited to hear we installed those runner lights along the back path. The patio still looks like a stiff breeze could knock it over, but at least it’s well-lit. They move in next week.” He smiled. “Why, you lookin” to outbid?” Bernard smiled right back. “No sir, just lookin” to outbid you. I raise two bucks.” Greg slipped his favorite Cub’s cap off his scalp and scratched it. “Damn Bernie, Vegas must have been good to you. You grew some balls ever since you came back. Call.” Both Harry and Daniel threw their milk money into the pot, each holding a high pair and praying to hit trips on the re-draw. Each man considered his options carefully, treating their little poor-man’s poker game with the kind of respect usually reserved for foreign royalty and Al Pacino movies, as per usual. They loved a good mobster flick almost as much as they loved trading their pocket change every Saturday, just a good ol? fashioned guy’s night in. Daniel excused himself to the kitchen for another beer and offered to grab a few more for his friends. Greg dared Joe to take a shot of Jager without the Red Bull. Harry and Bernie traded in a few cards, ribbing one another with half-hearted jokes about one another’s mother and what (or who) she happened to be doing at that very moment. A typical night with the boys; just what the doctor ordered. Daniel returned with the beer and passed one to Bernard. “That’s gonna cost you, buddy. I’d sure like to take that new Lexus of yours for a spin down south Broadway, maybe see if I can pick up your mom while I’m at it.” “Fuck you,” Bernie replied, dealing out the last of the re-draws. “Okay boys, we ready to wager?” A few more dollars trickled into the middle, forcing Greg to fold and Joe to rub his hands together in excitement. “Bring it on, boys. Full house, read “em and weep!” “I can’t read, “cause I’m illiterate,” said Bernie, exhaling a plume of smoke through his nose. “But I can sure read shapes good, and all of these baby’s are hearts. Flush!” “Well, you sure don’t play poker good either, because my boat beats your flush.” “The hell it does.” “Yeah Bernie, the hell it does.” Joe snickered. “Where’d you learn cards from, a street hustler” Dumbass.” “Vegas must have been good to you all right,” Daniel added. “Jesus man, have we taught you nothing?” Bernard dug the stub of his cigar into an ashtray, grumbling as Joe claimed his cash. More beer was passed around. The more beer they drank, the more poker they played. It was the same routine every weekend; they might go to worship on Sunday, but on Saturday by God, that green felt table might as well have been their alter. Table-talk confessions seemed to work better than a priest, and hell, they could drink while they said their Hail Mary’s. An hour passed. Bernard hadn’t managed to win a single hand, but at least he got his card ranking right. They were all getting ready for the re-draw when a steady pounding came from the front door. “You expecting anybody else, Danny?” Harry set his cards down. “I thought Jason was working tonight.” “He is,” Daniel replied, rising from the table with a twinkle in his eye. “I was wondering if this dude would show up.” “Who is it?” asked Joe. “It better not be that one guy you brought two weeks ago. What was his name, Kent” He had longer hair than my wife and smelled like a garbage truck.” “That’s because he works for the sanitation department, asshole. But no, it’s not Kent.” Daniel grinned, making a beeline for his apartment door like a kid bolting for the tree on Christmas morning. “You guys sit tight and let me pretend to be a good host.” “Knock yourself out,” Bernie said, turning back to the table to replace three of his cards. “I really hope it isn’t one of Kent’s friends. I don’t want to have to shower twice toda-? “You have got to be shitting me.” Harry’s mouth dropped as he looked to the door. All heads turned in unison. “Evenin” boys,” said Rich Rollins, strolling into the apartment like he owned the place. Way he figured, by the end of the night he would. ~<>~<>~<>~ Earlier? Chicago in November could be as cold as an ex-lover looking for alimony; long and inclement, the days began to cut to the bone before the kids had a chance to see the bottom of their Halloween candy. Even so, Rich Rollins rather liked the chill. It kept his senses sharp and his wits sharper. He was almost thirty years old. Most people didn’t hit their mid-life crisis until well into their fifties, but Rollins was finding it harder to find his place in the world, a place for his life, without wrestling having some part in it. The few battles he’d had in the last year, since the implosion of the now-infamous Fuck You faction, had almost seemed to happen outside of himself. Like he was looking through a bell jar at the body of work he used to call a career, all reduced to nothing more than a series of phoned-in performances and half-assed appearances. That was all he felt like he was doing these days; putting on appearances. Everyone asked him the same question these days: “Why come back” What do you have left to prove?” Standing in front of his bathroom mirror, Rich Rollins took one look at his shaggy reflection and searched the contours of his own unshaven face for an answer. It brought back a feeling from his childhood, a memory stuffed somewhere between the happy mirage of his youth and the dismal reality of his adolescence. He remembered taking a trip to the local humane society, trying to convince his mother that they needed a dog. Even then, the sharp pangs of loneliness had been part and parcel of the Renegade’s relatively short existence, and at the time a furry companion seemed to be the only remedy. “C’mon ma, look at that one! He won’t make a mess, I promise I’ll clean up after him!” Even at seven, Rollins knew how to manipulate people. He just used the bright-eyed plea of innocence instead of a steel chair. “Can we take him home, please” Please?” His mother was never one to negotiate with emotional terrorists, however. “For the last time Richie, no. No, no, no. We can’t have a dog in the apartment because the landlady would find out and I can’t afford to pay pet rent. You can have a goldfish, maybe a cat. Cats can live outdoors.” “But cats are stupid,” young Richie pouted. “Their puke looks like yellow jelly.” “I said no. Now come on.” And there it was; the I-mean-it Mom stare. Rollins poked his tiny finger into the puppy’s cage, giggling as the little Labrador lapped at it. He thought it was sad to see so many caged animals in one place, waiting either to be claimed or killed, to be rescued or discarded. His seven-year-old brain might not have been able to understand the importance of it at the time, but looking back, Rollins realized that that little dog run was a metaphor for his entire life; waiting in a cage, hoping to either find a place in the world or be left for dead, forgotten and alone. Snapping back to the present, Rollins grabbed a towel and dried his face. Scooping up his keys as he pulled on a clean white tee-shirt, he began going about the usual ritual of making social appearances; first, a stiff drink, usually whisky with a splash of coke, followed by a shot of tequila and a Celebrex. For the headache. Sporting his trademark red-and-white head scarf, the Renegade snatched a plain gray hooded sweat shirt and made his way out the back door of the shoddy south Chicago warehouse he called home. He had some work to do tonight, and it had nothing to do with wrestling. Well, not directly, anyway. One of the page boys who worked in the office for PRIME, Daniel Hawthorne, had called him two days ago. He organized a weekly poker game for his friends and wondered if Rollins was interested in making some quick cash. “Like I give a fuck about some chump change pick-up poker,” he had replied. “Why should I care?” “Because,” Daniel said, “it gives you a chance to take money from a few PRIME fans.” The page boy had a point. Why the hell not? ~<>~<>~<>~ “So,” began Bernard as he lit up a new Black & Mild, “how does it feel to be back in the ring, Rich” It’s too bad you left PRIME, but hey-? “Chet Worth didn’t need me, and I sure as shit didn’t need him,” Rollins cut in, taking a seat at the table between Joe and Daniel. “What’s the game?” “Five-card draw,” Daniel replied as he dealt. “One dollar minimum bet, no blinds.” “A dollar?” Rollins snorted as he rolled up his sleeves and unsheathed a cigarette. “Fuck that. Care to make this a little more interesting?” He lit up and took a drag as he adopted a provoking smirk. Bernard’s eyebrows perked. “What do you have in mind?” “Here we go,” said Harry, settling back to watch the show. “Mr. Vegas is ready to make a run at the World Series.” Rollins reached into his pocket, flipped out his wallet and opened the flap, giving the table a good long look at the pirate’s stash of a bank roll nestled within. “I say we up the minimum to fifty.” “What do I look like, a millionaire?” Joe laughed. “My wife would kill me if I lost fifty alone, never mind a month’s mortgage.” “Fine,” Rollins said, reaching back into his pocket. “Let’s try this.” Every eye at the table popped as the keys to Rollins? Mustang landed smack dab in the center of the felt. The Renegade crossed his arms and smiled. “Any takers, or are you PRIME pussies too afraid to go toe-to-toe with a living legend?” Awkward silence. Harry cleared his throat three times as Joe shifted in his seat uncomfortably. “Are you for real?” Greg couldn’t take his eyes off the shiny key chain. “That thing has to be worth twenty grand.” “Forty,” Rollins replied. “I had it buffed last week. Who’s with me?” “This is crazy. I’m done.” “Ditto.” “No fucking way.” “Bring it on, baby. I’m about to slap GCW around worse than PRIME will this weekend.” Bernard produced the keys to his Lexus and threw them on top of Rollins? chain. The Renegade couldn’t help but notice the quick way the man started breathing, but the look on Bernard’s face was set in stone. Confident, stupid stone. “Bernie, are you nuts?” Harry looked at his friend like he had just grown a nipple on the end of his nose. “You just bought that thing!” “Yeah, and it’ll look great in my garage next to Mr. Wash-up’s Mustang,” Bernard replied. “Deal the damn cards.” “You sure?” Daniel hesitated with the deck in his hands, strumming nervously across the corners. “Sure he’s sure,” Rollins said, blowing more than just smoke. “You can tell by the way his lip quivers.” Again with the smirk. Bernard glared. “All right boys,” Daniel said. He began to deal. “Jesus,” Joe whispered, mostly to himself. “Winner better buy a taxi for the loser.” “I ain’t ridin” in no Ali Baba’s back seat,” Rollins said with a touch of indignation. “I’ll let Bernie there drive my new toy back to my place, then I’ll give him a ride home.” “Listen to this jackass, talks like he never loses. How’s Jordan White and boys by the way?” Grabbing his five cards, Bernard anchored his elbows to the table. “I hear that GCW has really been booming ever since you left. Not that I watch second-rate wrestling, but I hear things.” “You hear things,” Rollins mimicked, taking his own cards. “I do things. Ask your wife.” Nervous laughter erupted around the table. The air thickened into something that Rollins could only identify as the noxious, choking molasses of yellow jellied cat puke. It amused him to see the little man squirm so obviously; looking at his cards, he only wished Bernard knew how bad it was about to get. “So, you guys gonna order the pay-per-view on Sunday?” Rollins picked at his cards and put them in a readable order, trying to keep his tone as civil as it was level. It was hard. “As satisfying as it’ll be to take ol” Bernie’s bullshit town car there, it’ll be that much more gratifying when I bust a seam across White’s nose. I’ll take two, puh-leaze.” Daniel just shook his head as he slid two cards Rollins? way, realizing now that it might not have been such a good idea to give Rollins a call after all. The boys back at the office would never let him live it down if they found out he was fraternizing with the enemy. “I’m not hauling your ass around town Bernie,” said Greg, now on his feet with the rest of the spectators. “A flush doesn’t beat a full a house.” “Yeah, yeah,” said Bernard, pulling a single card from his hand and sliding it over to Daniel. “Good thing my hand doesn’t have any hearts.” Rollins realized he was chewing on his lower lip and stopped. “You ready to blow my mind?” he said. “I’ve got other things you could blow too, you know, if you needed the cash.” “I would, but I wouldn’t want to give away any of your sister’s secrets,” Bernard shot back. With a deep breath and a big flourish, he laid his cards down. “Two pair, threes and sevens. ? All eyes turned to Rollins. Rollins, in return, turned his cards over. “Sevens and threes. How “bout them apples?” “What the hell?” Joe almost choked on his beer. “What’s the kicker?” “A king,” said Bernard. “An ace,” said Rollins. “FUCK!” Snatching back his own chain, Rollins took the keys to his new Lexus and jangled them a bit above the table. “C’mon Bernie, you should be used to failure by now. You watch PRIME.” “FUCK!” “And probably listen to Justin Timberlake when nobody’s around.” “FUCK!” “I’ll expect the title in the mail,” he continued, slipping both sets of keys into his pocket. “You can get the address from Daniel here.” “FUCKFUCKFUCKFUCKFUCK!” Already heading for the door, Rollins tossed the stunned spectators a cocky salute as he reached for the handle. Everyone watched him go, glaring with intent to kill. Especially Daniel; he knew he’d be taking shit for this until the Second Coming. At least Kent only smelled like garbage; Rollins was just a complete piece of shit. “Fuck you very much,” Rollins called as he made his exit. Out in the hallway, the Renegade paused to listen to the din of destruction that rose up on the other side of the door. A slow smile slid across his face. He looked up and down the apartment halls, the dog runs of the domesticated American, and finally nodded with satisfaction. He had found his place again. ~Fin~
