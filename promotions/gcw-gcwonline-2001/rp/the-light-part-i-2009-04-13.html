The Light - Part I

13 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 13th, 2009 The apartment is dry, simple, and silent. The walls are bare, boxes are plentiful, and furniture seems to have lost its? color with each time the sun rises. An open Chinese food container sits on the small table before a recliner in the center of the room. Beside it sits a phone book, a phone, and a message machine with the red light flickering on and off. A stack of tapes pile high on top of the television set. In the corner rests a set of large dumbbells and a jump rope, which seem like they’ve been dropped and left in a rush? blatant signs of use. The bolt unlocks with a scratch and click. It sounds off in the poorly lit apartment and seemingly brings it life. With a twist and click the door springs open, revealing sunlight to the desolate, lackluster shadows. A man steps into the room carrying a brown paper grocery bag. His face is hidden by a mask that dons a single white F over the face. F is for Friar Global Championship Wrestling’s newest mask overlooks the situation and takes a few steps toward the table. He clicks the play button on his message machine while taking the time to roll his neck around, cracking it a few solid times. BEEEP “Yo Fry! It’s Truck. Where ya at, man” I’m headin? to the gym. Call m’back!” Friar nods slightly and begins unpacking the bag and loading the groceries in their proper place. Friar opens the refrigerator. He pulls out an almost empty bottle of OJ, swirls it around to judge the amount inside, and then throws it back quicker than a shot of Whiskey. Wiping his mouth, he goes on as does the machine. BEEEP “Fry-Man! Can I borrow a dress shirt” I met this blond backstage with a giant rack. No kids, either! Biiiiig win. Call m’back, bro!” Shaking his head in disbelief and grinning, Friar aims his focus to the empty cupboards before him. He turns back to his pile of freshly picked cans and boxes; he loads them in while waiting for the next message to play. BEEEP “Hello Friar. This is President Caldera wishing you, your family, and friends a wonderful Easter holiday! Make it a good one. And we’ll see you in DC for WorldWide!” Another subtle chuckle sends Friar’s head on a side-to-side shake. Friar: Sure, Boss. See you there. Gonna bring those no-good cronies with... (sudden pause) Wait... DC? Friar watches the machine as if it would actually answer him. He takes a few curious steps to the middle of the room and looks down. He slowly realizes what his new employer means. Friar: That must mean- Slamming his fist down on the table, Friar shoots over to the phone and picks it up in a fury of speed and dialing. After seven rapid and hard strokes of the number keys he holds it to his ear. His eyes dart around the room in anxious anticipation. Friar: Truck! What’s up brother? Got a minute? A smile comes to Friar’s face as he receives the recent happenings of his partner’s life. Friar: Take it easy, T. I just stepped in the door. I got one from Caldera, too. Friar waits and nods. Friar: He wasn’t too specific but he did say something that caught my ear. “See you in DC!” What’s with that? We just put ourselves through Hell at Schockwave and he wants- Friar stops talking instantly. He stares ahead blankly. Friar: What do you mean singles action? The perplexed look smears over the eyes and mouth. Friar: Yeah... but doesn’t the Road to Rampage start this show? The only people with matches are... Another pause but this one is brought on by Friar’s own end to that sentence. He swallows. Friar: ...in the tournament. He takes a deep breath and comes to grips withthe fact that he was indeed one of those people. The person on the other line is way more enthusiastic at this point in the game. Friar: Thanks, bro. I appreciate the vote of confidence. Another brief pause followed by another nod from the Hellhound. Friar: No sweat. I’ve got all the tapes I need here. Scouting clips, highlight reels, and even some technical review stuff; those guys at GCW know how to throw a scouting package together, that’s for sure. Friar chuckles as he listens for a few solid seconds. Friar: That too! When I was working in Tibet, they made us do a simulated match as a scouting report. So yeah, I’d say this beats the Tibetan scouting system by a long shot. Friar purses his lips. He glares forward and runs a finger over the chin of the mask. Friar: Alright, bud; I’m going to get going. I’ve got a lot of work to do. Who do I get for starters anyway? Any idea? Friar stops and grins. He looks down to the ground and shuffles his feet nervously and in a humble manner. Friar: DeBough? Really? Friar grins slightly. Perfect. Friar: Thanks, Truck. I like my odds having you on my side. It should eliminate any complications from... Caldera’s Clowns... you know? But think about it. Take it easy, pal. Friar listens and nods in good humor. He clicks the phone dead and sets it back on the resting place for this particular phone. Finally sitting down, Friar throws his arms over the chair’s and looks at the sunlight creeping through the holes in the blinds and drapes. He stretches and arm out, watching the light turn his hand from a grey pale to a brilliant and lively golden tone. Of course! Shooting out of the chair, Friar lands in front of the stack of the television. He scans the pile, selecting one half-way down the stack, and slides it out like a Jenga Wizard. Jenga! He pops the tape into the VCR. Sitting back down, Friar glances to the Chinese container before him. With a curious eye he leans over it. My Lord! That’s no good. He shortly flails his hands around to remove the stench from his area ? which is pretty tough with the mask and all. Launching it toward the garbage, it hits the wall and drops to the floor with a wet thud. Much like the type you may hear if a wet sponge hit you in the earhole. The TV comes to life and fades into a GCW logo screen. Tyson DeBough Match Highlights flashes over the dusty glass window and then fades out to reveal a montage of DeBough action. Friar sits back and watches the scenes of skill play out before him. He sits upright and square; studying and watching DeBough move through the ring and through his career at the same time. Images of the Television Title are shown around Dice’s waist. Come on now... Pressing the remote down with a forceful thumb, Friar fast forwards past the celebration and crowning for DeBough. He stops it and sees the same concept, different angle. Oh please! Who is this guy? Friar sits up and jerks the tape out of the VCR. In one discuss-slash-frisbee throw motion, he launches the tape out of his ninth story window. Staring at the outside and sky for a few seconds he takes a deep breath and sits back down. Once again, he extends his hand to lighten up his mood with a stray sunbeam on his otherwise dark, grim flesh. I don’t need tapes. Still entranced by the sunlight, he watches his fingers and fist change in the golden ray and shadows. His eyes focus intently on his rotating appendage. Once more, I’ve got to take down a tree to clear the forest of its darkest days. He opens his hand and watches as the light spreads all over his weathered hand. The gashes, cuts, and scars all add up to create the image of the Moon’s surface on one man’s right hand. He pays attention to the shadows. Friar angles his hand around in the light until there are no longer any shadows. And, once the dust settles, in that final moment... The Light will shine brightest. His eyes peer down at his hand more with ambition and courage. Noting all the scars and running through their each and every happening was a task Friar’s mind was no stranger to. It was the pain that made the memories stick. And memories are all Friar has ever had. Losing focus of his hand he catches a glimpse of the television. On it is footage of the Shockwave 2009 match between AXEL Action, Face, CJ Woods, and Tyson Debough. DeBough’s arm is raised sky high as the match comes to a climactic end. “And by pinfall...Tysooooon DeeeBoooooough!” The blue eyes glare at the screen as if somebody were to flip a switch in Friar’s mood from optimistic to sadistic. Friar: I’ll see you soon... His knuckles crack like jiffy pop. Breathing deep, Friar finishes his thought: Friar: Establishment swine. The Hellhound sits back, calmly stretching his neck from left to right. He folds his hands together in his lap like a meditating monk. He becomes eerily still and only the up and down motion from his chest shows the viewers that this man is alive. That is when this forest will see the Light.
