An O'Neil Synopsis

25 March 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/25/10 Brief History Belmont has always been considered a cursed town. It’s in upper England where the weather is always gloomy and rainy. It’s almost like Seattle where you wonder whether or not the sun is going to come out today or not. In the summer it only reaches highs of 60 which some view as strange because London which is not that far south can get into the 80’s. Once again, the town must be cursed. During the Industrial Revolution many of Belmont’s citizens did fairly well for themselves considering that Belmont was originally used as farm land. Belmont was notorious for apple orchards, pumpkins, squash, and cattle. Cattle being the town’s best export. Most people during that time were farmers or worked in the factories in nearby Birmingham. Belmont’s claim to fame is far from something you’d want to claim. Belmont’s most famous resident was the Countess Elizabeth Bathory of present day Slovakia. The house on the hill was purchased as a summer home for her by her husband Nádasdy Ferenc. While he studied in Vienna during these summers Elizabeth would often stay here in Belmont. It is rumored that she killed and bathed in the blood of nearly 100 young children and teenage girls in 1575 here in Belmont. Although many have disputed such atrocities in Belmont (mostly to save the city’s face) the proof of such horrific acts were held within the confines of the house. After her death in 1604 the house was left in Bathory’s children’s names, none of whom ever step foot into the residence. After being abandoned and still in the Bathory name for over 290 years the house was secretly sold off to a man named Dion O’Neil a farmer from Ireland. Dion would raise a family on the grounds and start a legacy. In the late 19th and early 20th centuries the O’Neil name was synonymous with success. He was rich beyond all belief because during the famine of 1906, and Stalin’s forced famine of Europe in 1932 and 1933 his cattle, chicken, and hogs were not touched by the disease that was crippling cattle farmers all over the continent. Instead of inflating his prices to triple his pocketbook, he lowered his prices to help feed the starving city. Throes of people would crowd his land ready to sell off whatever they could just for some meat. His brother would also later join in the venture, but would add a disturbing twist. After hearing about the atrocities in Belmont at the hands of Bathory more than 300 years prior, Dion and his brother opened the house to tourism. They “claimed” to have kept the house as is. Most of that can be proven as true because Dion rarely lived on the land. His negro slaves used the house as quarters to help cultivate his land. He lived 45 minutes south of Belmont in a palatial house in the heart of the country. It almost seemed as if the O’Neil family was running the city by themselves. That can be disputed, but that all came to a crumbling end in 1942 when Anton O’Neil was born. He would later go on to run the farm for his uncle and eventually send it to the decline we all know it today. Anton would have three children. Bryan, Jason, Jeffery(deceased) and it’s this branch of the O’Neil tree that is succumbed in a black cloud, and this branch of the tree that has forever scared the life out of Belmont’s residents. Anton’s Obsession Anton O’Neil grew up with sick and twisted fantasies. He would find himself as a young teen locked in the bathroom looking at pictures of dead Vietnamese and German civilians from World War II. He would often finish himself to the pictures of the dead women. He never viewed this as revolting, only as a fantasy. ‘all boys and men have fantasies, but they are just fantasies nothing to act upon’ was what he always told himself. Anton was not a good looking man by any means. He was not scared of women but they were scared of him. His face was long, his facial hair was patchy, and his hair was always thin enough to see the scars on his head because of the lack of showering and the constant picking of sores. He never finished high school. He didn’t have to, he inherited millions, and intended to keep those millions. Anton flashed his money around like it was his job. The people of Belmont would often snicker at this wannabe who is living off the hard work of his family. Anton became the laughingstock of Belmont. He did nothing and flashed the money around like he earned it. Anton was under the impression that money could buy him anything, and to an extent he was right. Because his looks were not ravishing and his personality was suspect at best he was never able to bed the right woman. Anton became addicted to prostitutes. He would take a cab to Birmingham, frequent the pubs that the factory and steel workers frequented and waited for his opportunity. This area was littered with whores, and if the price was right he could get anything he wanted. He had a few women, none were remarkably attractive, but looking at Anton these women looked like Bette Davis next to him. He would always try to take it to the next level. While inside some of these whores he would attempt to choke them only to be slapped, kicked, and spat upon. He started to develop a reputation amongst the whores and most would ignore him. He was even roughed up by a few patrons of the bar and even had a knife pulled on him by some of the pimps of the area. He did however manage to meet the one that would allow him to do whatever he liked. Her name was Brenda. She was short, thick, and homely looking. He would offer an exorbitant amount of cash to choke her until she passed out and than he would finish that way. Anton became so obsessed with her he moved her in. She did whatever he asked because he paid for everything. He paid her to live there, he paid her to act like she was in love with him, he paid her to take beatings and verbal forms of abuse, and in return all she asked for was drugs. It started with pot and LSD, and than would later turn into Coke and Heroin. Brenda’s life was being abused, whipped, and choked by Anton along with filling her veins with the Blue. She was making more money than any of the other women she worked the streets with, so she figured what’s a little abuse when u can buy anything you want. A month into this “relationship” Brenda became pregnant. Anton was bewildered but also excited. Maybe a child could fix his relationship with the town he grew up in. Sure he was rich, but day in and day out Anton would be ridiculed everyday he went into town for alcohol or bread. It’s almost funny seeing this ghastly looking man walking around a grocery store with cases and cases of beer, and 2 loaves of bread. He would be stared at by adults as the children skittered to cover behind mom or dad’s legs. They decided to keep the child, well Anton decided to keep the child. Brenda was so strung out she was rarely able to make such decisions. Anton went to the library and began to investigate names. Boys’ names only, he never once took it into consideration that he could possibly make a daughter. Luckily for “her” he only made boys. He came up with the name Bryan because it was Celtic and stood for strong. He thought to himself, “Yes my child will be strong!” Anton left the library with a grin on his drunken face and decided to go show off. He stopped at the tobacco store, purchased some of the finest Cubans money can buy and ran to the strip he use to frequent in Birmingham. He would celebrate his wonderful news with all those that use to shun him for being a woman beater. He had visions of the beer flowing, shots being passed around as people sang songs and ran around the bar like madmen. They will be standing in line just to shake his hand and congratulate him. Nothing could be farther from the truth. No one gave a damn, as matter of fact he was laughed at for “fathering a whore’s child.” Most would have expected him to get into a fight, but he walked out with his head down. Rumour has it he went around the town slashing tires and keying doors. He was never caught but most assumed it was him. Anton did not become the man he wanted to, and the citizens of Belmont would later pay for it. Belmont and The O’Neil Children Let’s face it, the people of Belmont HATED the O’Neils. They feared Bryan because of his freakish size and violent tendencies, and feared for Jason. Jason was small for his age, quiet and meek. He was the exact opposite of his father and Bryan. Jason would spend his time playing outside looking for animals. Jason was an animal lover of all kinds. He adopted many little pets for himself, gave them all names and would often be seen walking into pet stores to get stuff for them. Anton never shared his money with anyone in the family so Jason would often steal. He got away with it more than he was caught, and when he was caught he was often dismissed from being handed over to the authorities because everyone felt sorry for him. To say the town knew what was going on in the haunted house on the hill was far from the truth. Most thought there was satanic rituals, animal sacrifices, and other unspeakable atrocities. At a town meeting one yelled out “If they ain’t mutilating animals than why is the little retard boy always in pet stores?” Animal sacrifices would be far better than what was really happening. Bryan was verbally and emotionally abused. Most think Anton feared Bryan because of his gigantic abnormal size. So to prove his superiority over his son Anton would often resort to verbal attacks. Bryan wanted love from his mother, but needle in her veins meant far more than the love of a child. Bryan’s sexual abuse came not at the hands of his father, but at the hands of the plethora of whores that made their way through the house. Anton in a drunken stupor would often force them to “Make a man out of my son.” At the age of 11, Bryan’s innocence, or whatever there was of it, was now extinct. Jason’s abuse was far worse and far deeper than that of his older brother. Jason spent the early part of his young adult life being sodomized and molested by his father. Jason would run to his room after the deed was done and just look at himself in the mirror. It was stated in his autobiography that he often contemplated suicide, but declined because he wanted to be there the day his father’s sin were paid for. During their visits into town the boys were often frowned upon, and most would move out of their way as if they carried the plague. If Jason was alone he’d get rocks and bottles thrown in his direction from other children. Jason was cast out as a reject because of his father and his brother. Jason was your normal child outside all the abuse, but because he harbored the O’Neil name, he was crucified. Bryan would often enter town wearing all sorts of black and white makeup on his face. To scare people he would often play in the remains of a slaughtered sow at home and keep the dried blood upon him as he made his way through Belmont. He did everything he could to bring attention to himself, and on that one cold November morning, he got the attention he asked for, but not the way he wanted it…….
