As blue as Rich

21 February 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 21, 2008 He was getting cozy on a barstool when he first heard the news. The bartender had just mixed his Jack and coke and placed it down on a "Huey's" labeled napkin when Brooke Anderson's sweet voice spilled out of the TV speakers and into the room. Even through the clamor, Jay Terror was able to make out the story and translate it into words that he knew. In simplest terms, Rich "The Renegade" Rollins was dead. Jay shook his head in suspicion and took a swig from the glass hoping that it would deter the sour taste in his mouth from spreading. A man to the left of Terror laughed a little and lowered his head, 'Well that figures.' He turned around to look Jay in the eyes but wound up setting his sights on the red rag resting on his head instead. 'I'd suspect you're distraught?' 'How do you figure?' He pointed out the heirloom resting silently on Jay's skull, 'Just seems like something the guy would wear. Hell, looks just like the sloppy towel.' Jay carefully dropped his glass back on the bar in front of him and faced the nameless man, 'Just a thing of mine. Merely coincidence.' 'I'd hope so. I figured he lost all support after joining up with that filthy Caldera fellow.' He paused a second, 'Can't say I didn't see it coming though.' Jay looked over his yellow teeth and bloodshot eyes making judgments in his head. Everything about the gentleman spelled out drunkard but something about the way he talked told Jay otherwise. He seemed like a worldly man, someone who had an idea of what he was saying. For that reason alone, Terror reserved his opinion and kept up with the conversation, 'You saw him dying like this?' 'I'm no oracle but yeah. Come on, look at the breadcrumbs.' 'All I saw was a string of losses. That why he did it?' The man laughed off the idea, 'You think losses will do that to a guy? Hell, look at what Valentine went through a while back. She went months without a victory only to come out of the funk and take over the entire federation. Nah, I doubt a loss to Terror or Ambrosi did this to him. It was something in his head.' 'He lost it?' 'He lost it the day he joined up with those established types.' The man paused again and this time took a quick dash of liquor from his glass. 'Look at his history. He made a name for himself defying people like Caldera and Knight. For years he was a true renegade fighting for his own cause. Thanks to one choice and a damn fine paycheck though, the guy gives up on that cause and leaves his fans in tow as he joins up with the same clan that slandered him. Something in him had to give.' Jay is the one to laugh the idea off this time, 'You think Caldera killed him then?' 'Hardly. It was what he stood for. Rollins was in too deep with the political bullshit of the fold and most likely couldn't take it. I bet he wanted out all the while.' 'Why not do what White did then?' 'Get thrown out?' 'Something like what White did.' The man shook his head and leaned back on his stool, 'You can't just quit on the establishment, it's like a cult. What would happen to a KKK member if he were to ditch out on the guys and start hugging black people? He'd be killed. Same thing here. If he were to have left then Caldera would have made sure that he did everything to hold Rollins back. Stipulations, screw jobs, the whole nine. Rich Rollins would never climb higher than midcard if he decided to defect.' Jay took another drink from his glass and itched his forehead. The sweaty edge of Rich's bandanna draped over his skin and seemed to expel pieces of the renegade as the hours ticked by. 'So he killed himself to get out?' 'Seems reasonable enough.' 'Who said he needed GCW though? Dude coulda' went back to PRIME or somewhere else.' The man disagreed, 'You think a guy who's done all that Rollins has done would want to move again? He's a legend, the first to be inducted into GCW's hall of fame, and you think he's going to pack it all up and move somewhere else? He was done his career and the only way to end the legacy was in his home fed.” 'What makes you so sure?' 'I'm not sure at all, it just makes sense.' 'To someone outside of the GCW circle maybe. Just seems too farfetched still.' The man took another drink from his concoction and swirled the contents around as he spoke, 'I don't have to know how GCW works or who's behind the scenes to formulate an opinion on someone. I can just do it. Like you, I figured you were a Rollins supporter by the way you were sporting that rag. I was wrong but still made the opinion.' 'True enough.' Jay let the silence overtake them again as he glanced up at the TV monitor. Footage of Rich's last match with Jay flashed on the screen and Jay watched as he leaped from the turnbuckle delivering the Anarchist's elbow. 'You sure it wasn't losing?' 'That's one thing I'm sure of. You didn't do him in that bad.' The man winked and turned back to the TV where he watched as he finished what was left of his glass. He didn't look back at Jay for the rest of the time they shared beside one another. It was almost as if he had forgotten the entire ordeal within minutes. Jay on the other hand finished his glass slow and took in all the details the man had placed. Even with all that was on his mind, his thoughts seemed to always spiral back to the white business card sitting safely in his wallet. Did Steven Caldera really want Jay Terror to join forces with him? Would the same outcome plague Jay that took Rich Rollins life? __________ “Dead?” “As a doorknob.” “Shit. That’s news.” Jay nodded and rearranged himself in the black recliner. His hands were folded across his lap with a cigarette burning in the ashtray beside him. The smoke climbed to the ceiling and Jay and Caroline took in the byproduct graciously. “I’d say. Figures, I take his bandanna and he takes his life. Kinda” makes me sound like a murderer.” “A bit.” She shook her head in a denying manner, “You’re safe though. I doubt they’ll question you about what you used to do it.” “Yeah.” She could smell something awry a mile away, “What’s irking you?” “Just, shit. A whole bunch of shit combined making one big shit. I don’t know, there’s just a bunch going on and not enough memory in my hard drive to handle it.” “Like what?” Jay shrugged, “Like everything. Rich, Conrad, the round robin, you, everything. I don’t know, it’s messing with me.” Her ears perked up and her eyes spread wide, “I’m messing with you?” Jay leaned back in the recliner and shut his eyes fast, “No, not like that,” he opened them and looked at her wholeheartedly, “You’re the first girl I spent this much time actually getting to know. I had a short history of fuck and pass and then I came to you.” “That a bad thing?” He shut his eyes again trying to formulate the right words. He had feelings for her that he never felt for anyone before and had trouble putting them into words that made sense. “Not at all. It’s the second best thing to have ever happened to me actually. Besides wrestling, you’ve given me something that not even my dead father gave me: love. I was born into that kind of mindset and feeling it now is so foreign that I feel like I’m on a different continent. No pun intended.” Her confusion switched to glee and a toothy grin crossed her lips, “Do you like it?” “You’ve been traveling with me for weeks now, it’s gotta” be for a good reason.” “Well look, just push it aside and focus on the rest of that list. Rich killed himself over the problems he found through wrestling and I don’t want you ending up the same.” “Right.” Caroline walked to the recliner and swiftly stepped into an empty space beside him. In her arms, Jay felt at peace. He felt as if there were no problems and no one trying to push him to do things that he didn’t want to do. For a second, Jay Terror became Jamison Talley again and he lived in the moment. __________ “I figured I’d see you back here.” Anita Baker’s voice was calm and welcoming. She guided him to a chair across from her desk and smiled politely. “I saw the news on Rich Rollins and figured things were getting to you.” “A bit.” “What’s on your mind?” “A lot of stuff. I Figured this stuff went away when I buried Talley.” Anita nodded to her friend and patient and leaned forward in her chair, “That’s not always so. A person can lose a personality but that doesn’t mean that the thoughts completely die away. They come up in spurts and when they do I’ve seen how people react. Are you falling out of sync with Jay again?” “Not at all. I think I’m getting too into him.” She looked intrigued, “Go on.” “Well as of late I’ve been unstoppable. I haven’t lost since we last spoke and that’s been at least a month. Things are rolling so smooth that I don’t know how I’m being so irked.” She nodded as if she understood his situation, perhaps that’s why he liked her so much. Anita Baker was a woman who could delve deeper into a person’s psyche than anyone else Jay had ever heard of and that power was just what Terror needed from time to time. She leaned back, almost as if to relax, and spoke. “You’re confused Jay. You need a healthy balance between Terror and Talley and you’re not finding it. Winning is good for you but the pressures to keep winning is my guess as to what’s got you questioning. You’re putting too much emphasis on being the best that you seem to be losing your core concerns.” “My core concerns?” “What you stand for. Tell me, who is Jay Terror?” The question used to be so easy for him but suddenly Jay was at a loss for words. “He’s rebellious and a freelance. He does what he does because that’s who he is.” “Does he enjoy winning?” “Who doesn’t?” “To the extent that he lives or dies by the outcome of his match?” Jay shook his head, “Not typically.” “That’s what I thought.” She blinked a few times and found a pen on her desk. “You’re neither Jay nor Jamison, you’re something new.” “How did that happen?” Her pen scratched down a few characters on a pad of paper, illegible to Jay’s eyes. “I’m guessing the business is working its magic. I’d suggest taking a step back and reevaluating what you’re doing. From what I’ve heard, Rich Rollins seemed to have been suffering from the same things that you are now. We both know how that story ended.” “Yeah.” “Take a week off, do something away from the ring. You need to relax your mind and find that balance again.” “Yeah.” Jay had gotten what he needed to and stood from his chair. Anita did the same and offered him a hug before he left the office. Stepping into the hall, Jay knew exactly what he had to do. The question wasn’t that though, it was if he could do it.
