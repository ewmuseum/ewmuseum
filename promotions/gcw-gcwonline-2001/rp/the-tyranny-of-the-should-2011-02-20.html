The Tyranny of the 'Should'

20 February 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02/20/11 1. The curse of pepetual youth. SSSSSSSSSSSSSSSSSSSSS- I can hear my father now. Never drive in a hurry. Five miles an hour doesn't mean anything, just get to where you're going. Be patient. And never, ever, ever pass on the right side. -sssssssssssssssssssssssss- The rain is particularly cold right now. I'd prefer it to be snowing...at least snow doesn't fall as heavily, doesn't soak into my clothes the same way. But I suppose if I was really sick of being rained on, I could always get back in the car. I could always stop staring at my mangled passenger side tires. -sssssssssssss...ssss...ssss...- Truth be told, I used to know these roads so well that I could tell you where the shoulder was going to cut out with my eyes closed. Back in the day, before I moved away, I grew up on these roads, driving whenever I could. Driving just for the fuck of it. As soon as I turned sixteen my little world expanded and any place that wasn't my father's house was where I wanted to be. Years go by, the entrapment of "home" has enlarged to a town, then a city, then a way of life. -...sssss...ssss........... The back tire lets out its death rattle. The front one took the brunt of the damage from the roads left in disarray. Even if I did know these streets like I used to, neglect and the elements have eroded their sides so to be tire-eating death traps for any teenager full of piss and vinegar stupid enough to try to pass a semi truck on the right side. "Jesus Christ. I'm pushing thirty." You'd think I'd stop acting like a kid someday. You'd think the last ten years would've had any sort of lasting effect on my cognitive abilities. On my decision making. You'd think that I'd realize by now that every single choice we make in our lives leads us exactly where we are at this given moment, whether it is in the arms of a loved one, at the peak of success, or standing on a roadside watching your tires die a final death. You'd think a lot of things. ---- ---- 2. Kate and motherhood. The lobby of Akron Central School bustled and buzzed with busy bee parents, each one excited for their precious little snowflake's upcoming performance. Overweight grandma's working on developing heart conditions, out of work fathers who made the most of these unfortunate economic times to spend every possible moment with their child, and bored siblings avoided bumping into each other and said needless "sorry's." And then there were the mothers. Looking around, Kathryn Jagozynski was pretty sure she hated the mothers most of all. "Oh, Kath, aren't you so excited to see your little Sarah in the show!" was supposed to be a question, Kate supposed, voiced by a pretentious and over-animated soccer mom who surely dyed her hair. The shoulder pads on her jacket reeked of her having been young in the 80's and the scarf around her neck did a piss poor job of hiding the wrinkles. Age hadn't managed to touch Kate, and when it did she prayed it didn't rot her mind to mold her into one of these zombies. "I'm sure it's going to be wonderful," Kate put on her Mom voice. Ten years after the birth of her daughter, she was still getting used to it. It killed her to be here, to pretend to be happy to see people she couldn't stand. But it didn't matter if it killed her, it only mattered if she was here for Sarah. "Our Jillian hasn't stopped talking about this for the last month and a half," the woman who called Kate by the wrong name droned on, "It's because of this show that we're going to start her on singing lessons. She is really so very talented, and her voice is like an angel's." Kate couldn't manage anything beyond a smile. This woman who called her Kath wasn't special, and odds were her fucking daughter wouldn't be anything special either. There was a time ten years ago when Kate was accepted to Juilliard on a scholarship, when she was going to act and sing and dance and network and never have to listen to some mother prattle on about her precious little kid at a show. There was a time where- Stop. she pressed those days from her mind. I wouldn't trade my life, my daughter, for the world. Playing the 'what if' game just makes me think about- Ring-a-ling-a-ling-a-ling-a-ling-a-ling-a-ling! "Oh dear," the lady that called her Kath perked up, "You have to be sure to turn your phone off when you go in there." "Yeah," Kate looked at the screen before answering and stepping away, "Thanks. I know." "You know what?" John's voice pierced out of the speaker and carried into the lobby, drawing wall-eyed looks from the patrons as though it were a show on Broadway, "Kate? Y'hello?" "Yes, John, what is it? I can't talk right now," she hissed into the phone, turning to face a corner. "Any chance you can come give me a lift? I've got two flat tires." Kate waited a half moment out of bewilderment, half a moment to hear if there was a punchline. "Are...are you kidding me, John?" "No. I've got two flats and it's fucking freezing. I'm stuck in the middle of nowhere, about ten miles South of the city." "Don't you have AAA? Or a girlfriend," the first suggestion was practical, the second was to let him know what her role was in his life. There was a time where Johnathan Fitzwillis being stranded on the side of the road would have meant she would gallop to his aid. That used to be her problem. His choices made it her problem no longer. "Sarah's at work," it took Kate a heartbeat to remember that he meant his girlfriend Sarah, not their daughter...she doubted she'd ever get used to that, "And my membership expired. Look, do you think I'd bother you with this if I didn't have to?" "Glad to know I'm your last choice," was her quick turn of the tables. If John was playing a game, as he is so inclined to do, she could play a better one. Make him paint himself into a corner. Make him retract statements, and watch him squirm. Both of them had always known she was much, much smarter than him. "Fine. I'm not gonna beg. If you don't fuckin want to-" "Our daughter's musical starts in a few minutes," Kate could kick herself. She shouldn't care if he walked along the roadside or had to call a tow truck. She shouldn't give a shit about him, but she did...and he knew that, "I can come get you, but you're a good forty minute drive right now." John understood. If there was one thing that he always conceded to, it was to keep disappointment away from their little girl. "It's alright. That's a perfectly good reason I suppose." "Wait," she chewed her manicured fingernail for a moment, unsure if this was a good idea, "I'll call Keith. He's on his way to meet me here, but he's not here yet-" His sigh ripped through the speaker and it spoke more than words could. He would rather be eaten by wolves than have her boyfriend pick him up. "John, please," she pressed the issue, "Sarah will understand. And Keith can come some other night." That was a feint, of course, for why this was a good idea. She knew damn well did didn't give a shit when Keith would be able to come to see the show. Silence. "Fine," he sounded exhausted. Ten miles South of Buffalo...she didn't even know he was in town. He must've just landed, flown in to one of the smaller airports, and it must've killed him to have to call her, to admit that he was home unannounced. He wanted a guilt-free weekend to see his girlfriend without having to deal with Kate and Keith and their bullshit just so he could see his daughter. For the briefest of moments, Kate's heart swelled with pity. "Text me where you are. I'll send it to Keith and he'll come pick you up," she paused, unsure if she should say the next part, "He can drop you off at Sarah's." Silence. "Okay," was his resignation. ---- ---- 3. So this should be nice and awkward... Land the plane, get a cab to my apartment, take my car to Sarah's house, have sex. That was the plan, wasn't it? Nice job, Johnny. "You got a station you like, Johnny?" Keith asks. I hate it when he calls me Johnny. I shake my head and imagine my soaking wet leather jacket slowly ruining his leather seats. If I can gain one little victory from this night, it's that the mud from my boots did a number on the floor of his SUV. "Oh and don't worry about the floor, man. I'll have the dealership take care of that. It's all good," he says, crushing my dreams. The best laid plans something something. I don't really know that full saying, but it's something along the lines of "sometimes shit gets fucked." It took every bit of humility in me to call her and ask for a ride, but in the end it was spite that won. I could hear it in her voice when she offered to have Keith pick me up...it was a challenge. She knows how much I can't stand him, she's got to see how much of an asshole he is. Sometimes I wonder if that's why she dates him, just to spite me. My God. Do I really think like that? "So I saw your little Jersey Shore trip last WorldWide," quoth the raven, "You really bang Bagwell's girl? I mean...like, really bang her?" "Nah," I lie, "Just for show." "Ha! Good thing bro," some guy who is not my bro says, "You'd prolly have to get shots. And I don't mean whiskey." Keith smiles and looks at me to see if I'm smiling. I pretend I don't see him fishing for approval and with a touch of a finger I flutter through the radio stations. It's the same bullshit as usual, this town isn't much for variety and the rain isn't exactly amping the FM signal. Forty seconds later I cut it off and we proceed in silence. "If you want, dude," he can't take a hint, "I got XM radio and an iPod-" "I'm good, Keith," meaning I don't need his fancy crap. Just get me where I'm going. He nods his head a moment, seeming to consider how to keep trying to talk to me. You'd think over the year I've hated him that he would've picked up on it, but he's either the dumbest person I've ever met or he's so incredibly wrapped around Kate's finger that he keeps listening to her to "just be nice." Glorious quiet envelopes several minutes before he finally shatters it again. "That's all good, man. Gives us a chance to talk." That one catches me off guard. Eyebrows peaked in the middle, I roll my head toward him and take a real good look. It's not the first time I've noticed that we look a lot alike. Kate started dating him a couple failed relationships after her and I didn't work out. The second time around, that is. She couldn't deal with my travel schedule, my carnal needs, my energy...my life. It was a mutual decision, best for us and best for our daughter, if we tried our damnest to be happy elsewhere. I genuinely felt bad for her as she dated loser after loser, but this is the first guy she really latched on to. At first I thought it was cute that she was trying to replace what she's lost in me by finding another brown haired, slim built guy who shared some of my facial features. Digging a little deeper, I realized he is everything I'm not. He's everything I should have been. College education. Suit wearing. Office working. Home at 6. Simple. Everything in his life is routine, regular, and fucking God-awful boring. He's me if I were a douche. And what's worse is he fucking loves me. He was a fan of mine until he became too wrapped up in his work, too wrapped up in a "normal" life to care about it. Prick. "And what did you want to talk about?" I ask out of morbid curiosity. "About you staying the fuck away from me and Kate." ---- ---- 4. Like, ten minutes ago. Many people thought Keith was lucky. Truth is, the man made his own luck. Only the brash and arrogant announce their intentions to the world. The keen, the truly successful, smile at everybody. The best way to position yourself well is to be friendly from Day One, even if they never respond in kind. Sooner or later, they'll drop their guard. Keith read the text message from his girlfriend. His and no one else's. "Hun, Im sry...can u pick up John? 2 flat tires. On the 90 N-bound just past Hamburg." What fortuitous circumstance. Just as he'd been growing tired of waiting in the proverbial deer stand, watching and waiting for his prey to give him a shot. Ignoring traffic laws, his smirk was lit with a dull blue hue as he sent a quick text back. "Sure thing babe. N E thing 4 u!" Poor Johnny stopped knowing how to please a woman somewhere in high school. Sure, the guy was probably good in bed. But he just couldn't understand that doing things for her is the only way to win in the end. And Keith had no qualms about giving up some of his pride, some of that bullshit his counterpart clung to, if it got him points in the end. It got him out of that shitty musical. Most importantly, it got him time alone with Johnathan Fitzwillis. "Thnx hun! Yur the best!" Score one for the good guys. "I know." ---- ---- 5. Meanwhile, back on Hamburger Hill: "The fuck did you just say, asshole?" "I think I choose my words very well. Johnny." He's calm. I'm not. The two of us haven't really said much to each other before. At least not in dialogue. He's always been quick to give me a hidey-ho like Ned fucking Flanders, but I've never been subtle about not wanting to talk to him. He's a prick! And I'm, like, the only person that sees it! "If you think you can come between me and my daughter-" "Oh, you can still see Sarah. But you'll do it when I say it's okay, and you'll do it because I say it's okay." "You think so, huh?" "Yes," he's talking like a manager, not like someone in an argument. He makes business deals all day, so this is definitely more his ground than mine, "Because you don't need any help from me to fuck up your relationship with her." "Fuck you!" "Trust me, Johnny. Give her three years, she's gonna fucking hate you and I won't have to do anything to make that happen." A tiny angel somewhere in the back boiler room of my mind pulls on a comically sized lever with all his might, and it's the only thing that stops me from taking a swing at him while he's driving. I guess winding up dead does neither of us any good. Instead, I take a deep, seething breath. "So what's this all the sudden?" I ask with plenty of venom. It's time to control the game, "You pretend to be all buddy buddy with me, tell me how much you love me, and now you get all high any mighty? Because I have to travel?" "I told you. Sarah's going to hate you because of your own bullshit just like Kate already does." "And you think I'm going to stay away because you say that?" "No," Keith reaches up above his sun visor and pulls out a manila envelope, then plops it into my lap and keeps on driving, "This is why." Keith's eyes are on the road and he's 100% concentrated on driving, apparently not giving a damn if I examine the contents of the package he gave me. His confidence doesn't waver as I wait him out for a few seconds, then curiosity gets the better of me and I pull out a large photograph. It's a picture of me pressed against a building...making out with someone. Making out with a girl who I later took to the ER with a drug overdose. Making out with a girl that turned out to be seventeen. Flipping through the pictures, the tale is told in full, my entire little escapade last week. The two of us kissing, fondling, entering the hotel. Then me carrying her passed out body to the car, my hands cuffed around her. The final page is a medical transcript with her file photo and detailed information. Oh, wow! So that's what her name was? It was- "What the fuck is all this?" I hardly recognize my own voice, filtered through about twenty different emotions and shaken and rolled up and lit on fire as it is, "You been following me?" "I have a guy," he says, all confidence, never taking his hands away from 10 and 2 on the wheel, "I make it a point to know everything I can find out about my woman's ex-boyfriend. Even one that I'm a fan of." "Some fan." "No," for the first time he almost loses his cool, "You don't get to say that! How many times have I had to hear you say that you're not Johnny Borealis? That you're Johnathan Fitzwillis, and that the stuff you say on that show is a character?" "I-" "Either you're full of shit and you aren't always like that, except this one time that you've been photographed banging a seventeen year old-" "We didn't have sex!" "Jeez, sorry Johnny, my man didn't get shots inside the hotel room of you two reading the Bible. Either way, the evidence shows you're either full of shit there or you're full of shit when you tell Kate and Sarah and Sarah and everybody else that all that crap you do on TV is just an act." I'm about to respond with cunning when he makes his point known loud and clear. "Either way, Johnny, you are full of shit." Those words are the drop hammer. Anything I have to say is pointless. He is right. Seconds feel like hours, ticking by with an excruciatingly slow pace before I find my footing again. "What's Kate gonna think when you tell her you've been spying on me?" Keith smirks. He's impressed in the way a hunter is impressed by a deer that stumbles along, leaving a blood trail. It refuses to die just because it wants to cling to life so very much, despite the pain of being ripped open. "Let's make a wager," he starts, "I bet...that Kate would be more likely to kick you out of your daughter's life if she sees those photos than she will be likely to break things off with me." I would call him lucky. Truth is, he prepared this meticulously. He gambled that I would fuck up, and he just had to wait. Yet again it hits me in the face. Find Kate, leave Kate, find Kate again, leave Kate again, join GCW, come home for sex, pass the semi truck on the right, call Kate, be the bigger man by letting Keith pick me up...every single choice I've ever made has led me right here, right now. This is checkmate. "...alright." I've made it my life goal to never admit defeat. Even when I tap out in the ring, it's fighting for another day. It's saving my bone or saving blood vessels to the brain, admitting short term loss for the long game. But this is the first time someone has had me by the throat and I haven't had it in me to spit in their eye. Stunned silence is my follow-up to the empty word that signified it. He's won. "Alright," I repeat. I don't want him to confuse what I'm saying, "Whatever you say." This is a choice. Right now I can choose to fight the good fight, to dare him to show those pictures to Kate, to gamble on whether or not she'd believe the truth of what happened...to bet that she'd ever let me see my daughter again. Or I can choose to give up on her, let Keith have her, and be able to hold on to Sarah a little while longer. I have my choice to try to prove him wrong. Sarah won't hate me. And if I have to give up Kate for it, if I have to let this son of a bitch beat me to do it, she is worth it. He parks the car. We're in Sarah's neighborhood, my girlfriend's neighborhood...even I get confused sometimes, but he's made it clear this is as far as I go. "First good decision I've known you to make," he says. Fuck him. I open the door and unbuckle. Contemplate taking a swing at him, but that's quickly dismissed as something for the cowboy movies. I doubt being arrested will be a good topping to those pictures. "No hard feelings, buddy," his tone lacks all the mockery his statement contains, "You would've done the same. If you weren't such a fuck-up." I can feel my teeth grit together as I step out into the rain. "Don't forget the pictures," he says and I turn as he throws the envelope at me. I make no move to catch it and it falls to the ground, the incriminating photos spilling out into a puddle, "I've got plenty more. You can hold onto them as reminders." I should say something. This is where I enact a brilliant plan. This is where I dodge the bullet, yet again, just like last time. Just like when I brought that seventeen year old girl to the hospital and the cops believed that I found her passed out, nothing to do with a hotel room. Nothing to do with thinking she was in her twenties. Yet again I should have something to say or do that lets me get away scot-free. I should have something. I should be better than this! I should be better than him! But there is nothing. Keith accelerates and the door swings shut from inertia. In the rain I bend down and pick up the envelope and the pictures of a nightmare. Alone, in the rain, it is here that I can choose to lay down and die, or I can choose otherwise. Where, oh where, will my choices lead me to tomorrow? This isn't over.
