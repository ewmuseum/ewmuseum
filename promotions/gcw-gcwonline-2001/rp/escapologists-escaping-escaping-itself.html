Escapologists Escaping Escaping Itself

undated

Date: 90021040 Escapologists Escaping Escaping Itself A Garbage Bag Production I was locked in a trunk, and though it might not look it, there was an art to being locked in a trunk. Some punks had rendered me unconscious and stuffed me back there. I can't remember how many of them it took to take me down, but I estimated it was a pretty decent number of them to have clocked me so good. I felt the tires shock along the road beneath me, and I noticed that I was unrestrained, aside from being shut in the trunk. I reached into my pocket and grabbed a lighter, flicked it open, and noticed that I was surrounded by a plethora of supplies. A man could live comfortably off these for quite some time. It was all quite confusing. These were the markings of some grand scheme, but the end result was cloudy. I couldn't tell whether they were planning on killing me and fleeing or just dropping me off somewhere, left to my own devices with only quite some time worth of supplies with which to live off comfortably. They were not comfortable supplies, though, to be to have been surrounded by in the trunk of this car, which for all I know, could have been loading itself on some tanker ship to be sailed off to some deserted island, where I would be dumped off to live out the rest of my years in maddening solitude. I got seasick in cars when they were on boats. Of course, maybe the supplies around me were boating pieces. I could hardly tell. Everything was in cases, and I didn't have the luxury of space to open them up and look inside them. Perhaps they'd drive me ashore, assemble the watercraft from its cargoed components and set me sailing, at the mercy of wayward currents to carry me hither and thither and yon. I moved the flickering lighter around as best as I could, and I saw a spark shoot off only to trail away through to the other side of where darkness was divided. It smelt like burnt upholstery. There were artists of the escape before me who had contorted their ways out of even more impossible situations, but they were all given the benefit of preparation. Without time to plan, I was left to grumbling around for a safety release mechanism that seemed to have been gnawed at to the point of malfunction. "Dammit!" I echoed in the trunk, but garbled outside of it, virtually inaudible under the noise of the road and the roar of the engine. I had seen some of these artists perform before. To escape, they had all made friends of their oppressors, be they handcuffs, padlocks or safes suspended by cranes on timers to drop into oceans. A successful artist of the escape becomes his captor and is able to merge minds with the devices that bind him in order to control the movements of those devices. He is able to turn keys and combinations to free himself. Unfortunately, car trunk interiors with gnawed at safety release mechanisms didn't have the minds that safes possessed. Even if they had, the mechanism had been twained from the mind. There was nothing safe about it; nothing safe-like neither. *** The car stopped, a door slammed and the trunk pried open. I held an arm out as my eyes readjusted to the sunlight. Through shrunken pupils, I squeezed the image into my brain of an exasperated Hispanic man, waving his hands in some circular samba of desparation. "Mister Johnny, we have to go now!" he cautiously reached in as I recoiled, like a snake, but one that kicked. "Who the fuck are you?" I shouted. "What's all this racket about anyhow?" "Mister Johnny, I am Emilio. We must leave now. The car will soon exploding!" "Not until you tell me what this fuss is!" "The car will soon exploding!" Emilio danced around. "We must start the running away!" God damn, he was excited, and perhaps it was that excitement that roused me from the trunk. He rushed one of the cases into my hands and then took out two more small trunks from the trunk and handled one per arm himself. We ran -- God, we ran -- to escape the blast radius of the will soon exploding car, but we had more time than we had estimated, so we slowed down to catch our breaths, and from a safe distance away, but not as safe as if we had been in safes, watched the car and wondered when the car even was going to explode, and if so, will soon or ever or some business between the two. It was desolate out there, and I could see nothing but sky and dry ground, tire tracks and settling dust gluing them together. "So what's all," I paused for air and there it was. The boxy sedan blasted to smoldering bits in some magnificent maelstrom of soot and fire, and it was the kind of thing you just had to stop and light a cigarette to. "Cigarette?" I offered Emilio, and he accepted without so much as a word or a glance, but with an arm and some fingers puppeting pincers snatching, snatching, snatching. "God damn," he accented, fingers snapped like a vice around a cylindrical square through puffs, like a steam powered Hispanic robot. "God damn," I concurred, and for a stretch, that was all either one of us could really say, though we didn't try too hard to say anything else. *** I patted down the last hills of sand as the circus of lights and alarms parked itself on the frontage road that ran alongside the beach as a megaphone fuzzed loudly garbled orders. I had buried sand beneath sand and other property of suspicious nature beneath that. "Dammit," I cursed, looking up at the parked patrol car and the patrolman approaching across the beach dust in his patrol shorts. With a complete disregard for diplomacy, he carelessly stomped and booted balled sand in his beeline to dust He wore sunglasses like two squares and a boxy mustache centered on his face. It looked like a fat pencil. "Sir, didn't you hear me?" he firmly dangled and yanked back, giving me little time to respond. "The beach is closed." "Closed?" I questioned, squinting through the ray of flashlight he pointed at me. "How can you close a bunch of water and sand?" "This beach is city property," he defended, but the logic didn't follow. I knew he was onto my game. "You have to desert the beach immediately." "What's that supposed to mean?" I questioned his verbal quicksand, even if quicksand was just an invention of moving pictures. "It means you have to leave, or we'll get the cops out here to arrest you for tresspassing." "If you're not the cops . . . " I fragmented while I pieced. Could any obnoxious asshole just buy a megaphone and a siren and close down various stretches of the outdoors at will? "Who are you? I demand to see some identification." "I'm beach patrol," he enforced with the sobriety of a stubborn anchor. "Alright," I put my hands in the air. Something told me I better not mess with him. "I'm leaving." "I don't want to see you back here tonight," he flagged, not waving but warning. I backed away slowly as he beamed his flashlight to spotlight me away. *** Now a man can lie, and a man can lie, but the lines on his face can't, and I could tell by the straightness of Emilio's mustache that he wasn't lying. It was trimmed level, not a hair encroaching his upper lip. "So let me get this straight," I recapped, mostly for my own sake, but surely for his too. "You want me to pay you what for driving me out to the middle of nowhere and abandoning me there?" "You say to me that you would pay one thousand dollars for the car and five hundred for the ride, sir," he said with an obediently level mustache. "Why would I say that?" I, more so than anything, wondered aloud, but Emilio shrugged his shoulders. "Well, forget what I said. I'll double whatever I was paying you if you just drive me back." "You said you would say that, and that I should not listen if you say that," his cursed honesty radiated like sunlight off his silky black mustache. "I bet I didn't tell you then that I was going to tell you now not to listen to me then if you told me now that I said I would say that, and that I should not listen if I say that because I know better what lies I have told than I knew what lies I would tell!" "Excuse me, sir?" Emilio replied confounded at my chronologically ambiguous babble. "Never mind," I quat. I reached into my pocket and procured a generous ball of sand. I offered it to Emilio. "Take it." He just stared at the currency, not quite knowing what to make of it. "I know it's more than we agreed upon, but keep the change." "This is not money?" he asked, the look on his face was one of honest confusion. "You want paper?" I laughed. "There's nothing backing paper anymore. It doesn't have any intrinsic value. If the economy collapses in on itself, paper ain't going to be worth more than the paper it's printed on, but sand . . . sand will always be sand." He nodded in mock comprehension, mustache leveling at the trickiest 180 degree angle I'd ever seen, and he took the ball of sand. Holding it in his upturned palm, Emilio walked off out toward the great vastness. He shrank and disappeared, and it took some time, but he did it, and all that was left was desert. He was the best god damned liar I'd ever known. *** I emerged soaking wet from the water, and it seemed that the threat had passed. I hastily recovered what I could find of my belongings, which had all been even more expediently buried in a poverty of time. The flashes dimmed like choking bulbs, and I could only hear echoes of the sirens. I waited to certify safe passage before cautiously spanning back across the beach towards the metropolitan guise of anonymity. The patrollers had wheeled along, and I dripped along the sand, exhumations and sand in arms as I tried to protect my wealth from breaking apart and streaking down my bare stomach. I had cleared the beach, but I barely had time to catch my breath when I saw dim lights growing to a glow as the patrol car relapsed its course for another sweep. I crossed the frontage road, clumping sand to my chest, my adrenal gland worked to soreness as my feet bounced off the ground, padded by a moist caking of pebbles and grains and ground. I kept on full blast towards the main road, oncoming traffic swerving to avoid me like peeling a big, mechanical banana with a pair of blunt scissors until it was mushed into jam. Where arms ground to a halt and met fruit, I picked one of the stalling motorcaders from the crowd and ascended his hood. I kicked on the windshield in front of him, and he responded in tow by spraying fluid, like some cowering poisonous lizard, to wipe the smears of mud and other various wet pedestrian ill doing mildewing from his glassy pavis. He rolled down his window using a rotating lever on his door and poked his head through with a disturbing natality. His balding crown rested atop a tanned, wrinkled face with eternally tired eyes and a truly well balanced mustache as much as truth is a part of every well balanced mustache. "Please, sir," he pleaded, reasonably in fear for his life. "Please get off of my car. I don't have any money!" "Get out of your car, open the back door and step a few feet away from the car!" The tension of the slowly unraveling pursuit fueled this vigorous spouting of paralyzing pith. I was like a plague to cattle. "Please, sir! I don't have any money!" he repeated. "Dammit! Just do it!" I raised my voice. It was all happening so slowly, and it was the waiting that set me on edge. The man shook himself out of the car in some kind of shock. He moved like he was being cartoonishly electrocuted. "Now step back a bit," I instructed. "I'm going to get in the back of your car and slide to the far end." "I don't have any money," he searched as he slowly backed off, looking for logic in illogical places. "When I'm all the way to the far end of the car, close my door, get in and drive like hell," I foretold, casting the narration as a shadow is cast while one is fishing or, more appropriately, telling stories about fishing. "I don't have any money," his failing defenses tapered as he watched me unload my chest of sand and other incredulous tricks I hoped to somehow assemble in some relevant way in regards to evasion. "I know you don't have any money!" I acknowledged, frustrated like a rusted saw through a swath of cork. "You want to make some?" His body relaxed as he still fought sluggishly against a bewildered fog so thick it tunneled out of him and hung above him in a syrupy cloud. "Get in." "What is all that stuff?" he asked as he milled alertly towards the car. "Don't worry about it," I guarded. "It's just stuff." I inventoried my belongings, and aside from a small fortune of sand, I had a bag of wild vegetables, a slit rubber ball that had been pitted clean and filled with match heads, a pack of cigarettes, a roll of tape and a decent length of fuse. I hadn't been able to undig everything I had buried, like a ripped shirt that had come crawling back along a current, but I came back with enough to make do. *** "Fuck!" I realized after checking my pockets. After my brush with the law, I had forgotten all about the things I had buried. "Where are my fucking cigarettes?" I spotted the fluorescence of a gas station further down the block and briskly paced towards it. There was nothing between me and that gas station except a whole lot of silence, and I ripped that apart like an uncomfortable shirt with a noose collar thrown defiantly seaward. The light in the store was on, but the door was locked. A predispositionally angry man lording restlessly over some sort of repository hollared at me, and I kicked past a display of coolers and charcoal to reach his station. "Cigarettes!" I gasped, hungry lunged. "What kind?" "Spirits," I pointed. "Yellow box." "It's nine fifty," he charged. "That's highway robbery!" I recharged. Begrudgingly, I reached down out of habit, only to rerealize that I had also left all my money at the beach. I opened up the repository and feverishly brushed the sand from my hair and my body into drawer. "Is that enough?" I had hoped. "Is what enough?" he mocked. "I know it's just flakes, but it's all I got." "We only take cash or credit," he tapped a sign I didn't bother to read. "Look, man. I've got plenty of sand already balled up just sitting there on the beach, but I can't go back and get them," I bargained. "You give me the cigarettes, and you can go take however much you need to cover the smokes and a little extra for the inconvenience and my gratitude." There was a tribe in the Micronesian islands somewhere that used massive stone disks for currency. The biggest and most valuable stones were so hard to move that eventually, they stopped moving them altogehter and just kept track of who the stones belonged to whenever they transfered ownership across various transactions. I hadn't realized how far we were from Micronesia. I would have to go back and get my shit. *** By all means, it appeared that I had successfully escaped. Maybe I had to break a few eggs to do it, but that's why you can't buy eggs one at a time. There were no eggs in the desert. There wasn't much of anything, and any way you juked, you were just getting closer to wherever it was you'd run from. It moved with you, the desert, and you were always just as far from where you didn't want to be, and you were always right there, too. It seemed like a perfectly fine place to end it -- to just stand about in the desert until vultures picked my bones clean. Perhaps it was better that way. There are certain things you can't escape, and those are the things that can't escape you. An escape artist escapes as a way of escaping. I was no artist. I escaped to escape and nothing more, and there I was, stuck with the knowledge that I knew I was still there, and somewhere, they were still there, too. I had left too many clues. I left my wallet there and my tattered shirt and some sand. If they dug deep enough in the right places, they'd be able to put it all together. "Only a matter of time," I stood, drying. They'd appear out of nowhere like inconsistent specks along the dissolving shades of sand color under the placid sky. They would replace the sameness, and I would use them as a reference point to move. For example, I could judge them to be about fifty ticks off in the distance, and then move a bit around them, and they'd see me gain a distance and could move about the desert so long as they could see where I was in relation to them. I could see how it would all end. They'd dance me into a corner in desolation where their patrol lives unchecked and unbound to the laws they enforced as intermediaries to law enforcement. There I was, with more sand than ever in my wildest dreams, drowning in my riches. Oh, the irony of it all! I couldn't help but laugh. *** The back seat wouldn't do. Even foxholed, I soldiered up every now and again for a look, fearing each passing flash of streetlight more than the last. "Hey!" I shouted towards the driver. "What's your name?" "Emilio," he peeped, both arms unbent as he nervously tilted the steering wheel port and starboard. "How much you think this car's worth." "I don't know," he chattered dentally. "Maybe one thousand dollars . . . maybe." "Hmm," I sounded thoughtfully. "You see that gas station? Pull over and get some gas." Emilio obeyed, and I ducked from sight of the repository clerk. He pulled the car up next to a pump and looked back at me. "Stop looking back here. It looks like you're looking at someone." Emilio looked away. "Go distract the clerk. Put some money on the pump and buy a few coolers and a couple cartons of cigarettes." "I have no money!" "Then forget the smokes," I compromised. "But I'm still going to need those coolers." "No money! No money!" "Just take them, then, for crying out loud! They're just sitting out there!" His lip twitched, the straight bottom edge of his mustache rippling uneasily above it. For a man with no money, Emilio had some strange issues with bankruptcies. I figured he just needed a little bit of incentive. "You do whatever I say, and you get out of this alive and five-hundred dollars richer." His eyes widened at one prospect or the other, and depending on the way you looked at it, it was all about perspective. It wasn't necessary, at the time, to tell Emilio that he'd get out alive regardless. "Five hundred dollars?" he sought approvals for his justifications. "That's a lot better than having no money, and then on top of that, being dead," I theorized. "Wouldn't you say?" He nodded in agreement, and conscious of his own trembling, opened the car door and looked out at the coolers. "Don't just go out and take them," I reminded. "I need you to buy me some time." He nodded again, or maybe it was just that he was bobbling so heavily. I couldn't tell, and it was of no matter. As soon as he had the repository operator's attention, I had slithered out of the car to drizzle caught droplets of gas from all three nozzles onto the surface of the gutted rubber ball of match heads. I threaded one end of the length of fuse into the slit in the ball and wired the other end through a nook in the upholstery that I presumed led to the trunk, but just as I was nearly finished feeding the fuse through the partition of seating did a clueless, fleeing Emilio chuck coolers into the back seat. I dodged them, one by one, and Emilio, vitalized by acts of coercion and the awkward sensation of teenage misdemeaning while I, like some midseaman, held tight the masts of reason to mentor him past the crashing tides towards calmer voyage. "Drive! Drive! Drive!" I yelled as he accelerated, and I held fast to the back window to make sure that if we even were being followed, any patrolsmen were well behind us. "I think we're clear. Just keep driving a little bit, and when you don't see any other cars, pull over to the side of the road." "Why?" he stuttered. "What are you going to do?" "I'm getting in the trunk." I ducked back down and laid across the back seat; let some silence build a bit as I started packing stray sand into the coolers. "Listen, we're going to have to lose this car." "But I need this car." "They'll be looking for us in this car," I solidified. "We're going to have to destroy it. You keep driving this around after I'm gone, and they'll catch you and break you. I can't have that." "I won't talk. I swear!" and he might have meant it, knowing what he knew, but I wasn't about to let his uninformed assurances jeapordize my destiny. "I have a length of fuse running from the trunk to this gas covered rubber ball full of match heads. Pull over to the shoulder, and I'm going to get in." I informed, three coolers full of sand richer and on my way to the promised land. "Get me off road a ways to some stretch of desert. As soon as I feel the car stop, I'm lighting the fuse." Emilio settled the car onto the shoulder and we opened our doors and met at the back of the car. "As soon as you stop the car, you come and open the trunk, and then we run like hell away." I maneuvered my body into less of a knot. "Then from there, I think it'd be best if we split up." He threw open the trunk, a beaten contraption that didn't catch closed quite properly. I surveyed the inside of the trunk and climbed in. I surveyed his face as he stood there waiting to shut the trunk. It was a portrait of concern. "Don't worry. I'll give you a grand for the car on top of the five hundred I'm already going to give you." I watched a look of relief surface behind his sullen eyes. "Maybe you can buy a better car with a bigger trunk or something." *** I looked out towards the water and convinced myself that there was nothing past it. It was just water on and on like that forever. I guessed I was alright with that, and I sat on the beach with my legs stretched into the water, packing together balls of sand and lining them up around me. It was a picture captured under perfect conditions, and time was as slow as a picture. In a long, paused instant, I had amassed an economy of sand; balled it smooth with my palms. I looked at the rows and rows of perfectly aligned balls and choked back tears. I finally had a means to freedom. This is what civilizations were built upon. I stood and roared and tore at my clothes. I tossed them aside and waded naked into the sea to cleanse myself, and though I kept walking forward, and the sky rolled over me, the shore never moved when I looked back. Only the things deep beyond it moved. Lights hustled across and the pulses of noise snored from the inconceivably absurd notion of industry there. I felt myself dragging back, as much as I had resisted. A pallor recast itself over an imaginary glow. It was only a matter of time, and then the alarms would sound. The current would push me back, kicking and screaming, all because I had found a way to beat the system; to render myself invisible to the slivery regime of physics, at least temporarily as temporarily as timelessness could possibly be. I returned to land to bury what I could of my things while a megaphone blared something all kinds of incoherent at me.
