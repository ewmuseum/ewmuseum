The Job

13 March 2012
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/13/12 The man in the suit had approached Him with the proposition first, foregoing pleasantries in a cut-to-the-core fashion that was beyond his years. It was important in establishing how desirable an asset He was, and not just some commodity that the blank cheque could snare on its own. The man in the suit had an aquiline stare and a silver tongue with a hook in it and his pace made it difficult to get a read on him. With those traits he was known for breaking deals off on his opening paragraph alone. In His stoic gaze the man in the suit saw a reticent focus permitting him his plinth. “You've got skill. Your name is money. We want you with us.” He contemplated the job out of the corner of His eye. No hushed tones or prying eyes anywhere amidst this clew of cigar chompers; this was an open invitation to another plain of existence, in a room thick with the kind of chatter that would see government shift to reach a handshake. The man in the suit needed Him for two reasons, one of which meant negotiations had to take place in this snake den looking down on the City; high above and away from the interest of lesser men. Of the triumphs and accomplishments of His profession cited for purpose He recalled each and all with aplomb, regarding the man in the suit with mutual esteem that he should know of His work in the scrapyard. The first deal was struck in no time with a handshake, and toasted with a flute of champagne. They walked in similar circles which had once or twice overlapped, but in the context of this environment came from very different places, and it was that which concerned Him as they came to the next matter. The man in the suit smoothed out the difference, and shocked Him by recounting one tale in astonishing detail regarding His partnership with his last employer. After walking Him through the memory as though he laid its very foundation, the man in the suit sat back in his chair and clasped his hands, pressing both index fingers to his lips and studying Him with that eagle stare. A shared understanding of business etiquette meant His silence was challenged immediately by the man in the suit. No one dared pause for thought in this crowd of sharks; where a stutter or a moment's silence was as a drop of blood in infested waters. Anger and nausea overwhelmed Him; that His secrets, things no one could know, could be readily summoned and sold for profit disturbed Him, considering those few that shared His experiences in the past had much to lose themselves. The man in the suit's expression never changed as the charges rolled off his tongue as though he were reciting the letters of the alphabet, and they translated into a very long sentence indeed. The man in the suit made the truth easy to digest. Nobody in this room wanted to get their hands dirty; they were too busy playing multi-national corporate Jenga to get their hands on anything else, and sometimes the loser needed a hand picking up all the toppled pieces. It was made clear to Him that the man in the suit was in the process of one such game and required a stronger hand to help shift one block currently obstructing his path. A smartphone was placed before Him and the man in the suit explained that it contained all the information necessary to complete the job. He spent a moment familiarizing Himself with the device and thumbed through the menus until He came across a profile of the target. The name was familiar to Him, nothing else. A pre-emptive sip of alcohol told the man in the suit to sit forward as He pocketed the phone and raised His glass. The man in the suit raised his and together they closed the deal with a drink, no handshake this time. A crooked smile caught His eye, and he raised an eyebrow in retort. The man in the suit chuckled and waved off suspicion, harking back to their earlier conversation and contract signing. “We'll bring you in at the Dangerous Games pay-per-view. Under the name Rasa. When your situation is resolved you can drop it and do that thing you do.” He nodded and rose from His seat, followed by the man in the suit smoothing his lapel before extending his hand. “I look forward to working with you.” While the man in the suit basked in his resplendence, He took His leave of the snake den and returned to street level. He towered over everyone else, in years gone by that was the attraction to Him, but many more flocked now at the mention of His name and the reputation that preceded it. They looked at Him in awe, muttering fanatically of His great power and never really knowing the man behind the moniker or what he was truly capable of. When He looked back at the ominous skyscraper reaching into the heavens He knew then what that felt like. The scrapyard was once a bustling local enterprise, filled with the sound of blue collar sarcasm and the roaring of machinery chewing up the corpses of old cars and spitting them out onto the junk heap. Twenty acres of space was filled with rusting carcasses, framework and fixings, and that was just the yard. Near the back of the facility stood a warehouse that had been around for three generations yet looked no more than a few months old. Before its purchase the hangar-sized structure appeared to be shedding its skin; the concrete walls had barely budged with age though appeared fresher thanks to years of graffiti being sandblasted into vapour. A collapsed roof meant new beams and supports had to be erected over two thirds of the building and several window spaces bore threatening sets of jagged glass teeth. When He bought the land, it took all of several months of hard graft to clear the junk and add all the necessary touches to get the place running once more. Inside, the old copper stacks, seized-up equipment and in-house smelter and been cleared, and the debris with it, opening up floor space equivalent to a dance hall or gymnasium. A new layer of cement coated the floor beneath the blue crash mats covering the whole area. The walls were painted maize and tiled up to head height, and the remaining space plastered with posters and flags promoting some of the greatest rivalries in His profession. Exercise equipment filled one half of the room, while the other half was occupied by a brand new and painstakingly fitted ring. Black turnbuckles and ropes wrapped in duct tape and a flowing apron under which lay the tools of his trade. Adjacent to the ring, built into the wall was a sports bar and lounge area, with an LCD screen showing whatever sports channel had been left on, while surround sound speakers around the gym pumped out hard rock to get the mood right. The local area was pretty run down, and budget apartment blocks stamped the lots that weren't abandoned or ancient piles of broken brick and mortar. A few bars and garages had survived and served the local community with the option of minimum wage manual labour or alcoholism. As with every other city the streets were a mess of drugs and violence, but beyond the high fences of the scrapyard a handful of young hopefuls had found their way in out of the devil's rain to His domain. A door behind the ring led to the head office in which He sat behind a modest desk, resting on a plush custom leather chair. He thought back to the times when He had been summoned to the office to see His boss stooped over some huge gothic number, sat on an ivory throne, all the while signing off holiday slips and making tired social puns that had been echoing around the internet for weeks beforehand. This was understated. A desktop PC, neat stack of forms, stationery and his favourite icon. A simple approach to business, as he saw it. The rest of the office however was far more brash in its design, and the walls and cabinets were strewn with posters, cut-outs, framed pictures, trophies, championship belts and old memorabilia from His glory days. He signed off one application form and placed it into the outbox, but before He could move to the next the door swung open and a head popped in just above the six foot mark. He had piercing blue eyes set deep into dark sockets, and a sun tan that made the wrinkles on his high forehead appear more leathery. His black goatee beard was neatly trimmed and flecked with silver, while greasy shoulder-length hair was pulled back tight from the receding hairline into a ponytail, as he had always worn it. He was of those guys that had seen success in his career then drifted into the background as the profession continued to expand. He was a veteran and his name was William, or Swamp Willy as the young hopefuls called him, and he was the scrapyard's senior trainer. “Yo' two-o'-clock is here, boss,” William said gruffly in a thick southern drawl. Behind the desk He gave a curt nod and walked out into the gym with His colleague, ten students waited nervously. Four were beanstalks that fit the profile of the (Ethiopian) 100m sprinter, two short-asses, one of whom had an eastern-european face and promise of definition while the other looked like a little Irish drunk. Three athletes that were on their way to graduating tool academy in a few years and one tub of lard resembling John C. Reilly stuffed into a fat kid's body joined them. They were new-starts who had been to the facility once for the tour, and behind them at the weights the older boys eyed them like prey; flexing muscles that would take the newbies the best part of a year to achieve. A few of them held gormless expressions and stood as though someone had forgot to screw their heads and limbs in firmly. The athletes' body language was much the same, but their faces said they knew it all before it had been taught to them. The tub of lard was the only one standing straight and firm, hands by his sides, chin up and never removing his gaze from the giant man in front of him. The welcoming speech was anything but, and very quickly the lads found themselves on the canvas following William's lead and throwing themselves into the mat with their back straight and arms flat out. The older boys leered at them, draped over the equipment and hooting with laughter at the way the beanstalks rattled off the mat like drumsticks, while the tub of lard threw himself backwards, landing with a massive thump and making his boobies jiggle, much to the amusement of the older boys. Their hyenine mocking caught His attention, and they were ordered to the yard to flip wrecks until their arms popped out the sockets. With peace restored He turned His attention back to the new-starts, and teaming each with a partner began teaching them how to lock up. The afternoon wore on, and an hour later the bigger boys barged back into the gym, slick with rust-coloured sweat and dusty all over. Nobody acknowledged the yard time, but with a flick of the wrist and a holler He cleared the new-starts from the ring and set them to work on the weights and machines while the older boys slid straight in and continued practicing their throws, counters and slams. William monitored the new-starts at the machines while He watched the rest on the weights. The athletes were all powering through repetitions with 20kg in each hand, while one of the short-asses attacked a dead lift like one of the world's strongest men, even though the weight was equivalent to two full bags of groceries. The others were gingerly treading the machines, including the tub of lard who yanked out three reps on the rowing machine, shaking quite violently on the third, before dropping the reigns and making himself more comfortable on the metal. “Set ya pace steady son,” William called out, “slow an' steady win da race.” “Pacemaker more like,” one of the athletes sniped as he broke thirty reps. Unfortunately William had heard it. “You watch ya fuckin' mouth boy!” yelled William, so that everyone stopped and took notice. “Time I'm done wit' him'n he be pushin' fo'-hundred pounds'a solid muscle we'll see if ya like to run ya mouth then! If we get two-t'irty outta your sorry ass it'll be a God damn miracle.” Seething with anger, William retracted the accusing finger he had thrown out at the young athlete and spun on his heels to address the whole gym. “We got a ZERO-TOLERANCE policy on bullyin' here! Intimidation! And any other bullshit you think makes you better than anyone in here! Won't fuckin' stand for it. Only ones in here better than anybody is me an' da boss man here, and if we hear ANYBODY been throwin' they weight around ya better man up fast 'fore I put yer face through the fuckin' tile. Do we got an understandin'?!” A cry of YES SWAMP WILLY went up from the ring, while the new-starts mumbled back guiltily by association. The session ended with a twenty minute warm-down before the students hit the showers and the older boys went back to the equipment. William accompanied Him back to His office, where they sat down for a couple of minutes. “Sure beats doin' ten outta twelve on da road anyway...” William sighed, slumping into his recliner by the door and twisting the cap off a Mountain Dew. Behind His desk He mimicked William's sigh and quickly wrote off more paperwork. “...Y'know dat Gulliver boy got potential to be da next big t'ing. Literally.” Under His breath He murmured in agreement, spotting a mistake on one of the forms and turning his attention to the PC monitor, shaking the mouse to waken the screen from sleep. “Be a shame if dat little Larsen prick shake his confidence, that takes more time to build up than it take the fat ta fall off!” Another disinterested hum from behind the monitor let William understand it was time to go out and hustle the older boys into some more punishing exercises. The clicking of the lock on the door at his back sounded like an alarm bell in His head, and whispering the name under His breath He produced the phone from the top drawer of His desk and thumbed through to the profile of the target set by the man in the suit. Larsen, Espen. It hit Him like a bolt of lightning. That's why He had been selected for the job. It made sense and it unnerved Him that the man in the suit was able to orchestrate it from the shadows. He leapt up from his chair, keeping the phone hidden in the grasp of His paws and storming into the gym in time to see three adults moseying through the doors. Quickly glancing at the profile picture He stopped in his tracks and scanned the adults for a match. To his dismay none of them resembled Mr. Larsen. The students were waiting at the sports bar, and when the tub of lard, one of the beanstalks and the bully athlete got up to greet them He watched like a hawk as each new-start matched up with an adult. Larsen. Larsen. He thought back to William's scolding of the boy and his comments later in the office. Gulliver boy got potential......dat little Larsen prick. Larsen got called out. The pale blonde kid. He caught sight of the Larsen boy as he disappeared behind the tub of lard who gave Him a big wave and a thank you before leaving with his dad; a much more rotund and moustachioed version of his son. Trying to juggle a farewell with a spy effort resulted in Him catching sight of the side of the man's face escorting the Larsen kid out the door. He had a strong chin, straight thin nose and at least one green eye. He was bald, but a black inky spike jutting up from his collar at the nape let Him know the escort had a distinguishing mark. Cursing under His breath He bid farewell to the rest of the group and told the stragglers to help themselves to the bar until they were picked up or left of their own accord. Disappearing back into the office once more He threw His weight into the chair and unlocked the phone screen, bringing up the contacts and hitting Call. “Finished already?” the man in the suit answered. “Getting there,” He replied. “What is it then?” “I need confirmation.” “...” “I need to know what-” “Weapons free, soldier. I'll take care of the rest.” Before He could open His mouth to reply the line went dead. He stopped for a moment, waited and listened until he felt the thu-dump against his chest once again. Then he scrolled the contacts in the phone once more and stopped at the name he needed. Raymond Armagh. Ray Armagh. Armagh Ray. Clever. The corner of his lip turned at that one. He dialled the number and held the phone to His ear, waiting with bated breath for the pick-up. “Hello,” came the dry growl down the line, devoid of tone or character. “I'm calling for Raymond...” He replied, eyes darting back and forth along the middle distance as He assured Himself this was going to happen. “May I ask who's calling?” asked the ghost down the line. “It's Rasa.” There was a moment of definitive silence on the line then, as if the ghost had forgotten to draw breath. When he found voice and started talking, Rasa found Himself immediately at ease with the job as the ghost bombarded Him with information and advice on the target objective, acknowledging Him as any private would a higher ranking officer. This wasn't just going to be a job. This was going to be fun, and he pondered it with a smile. Let the games begin.
