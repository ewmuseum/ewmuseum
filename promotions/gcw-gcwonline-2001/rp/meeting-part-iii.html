Meeting...Part III

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Tonight A black stretched limo is driving down Fifth Avenue in Manhattan. It appears to be heading in the direction of Central Park. After having spent the last few days setting up this elaborate scheme, Gaius Octavianus appears calm and collected in the back of his car. Adjacent from him is a large, Middle Eastern looking man nicknamed 'The Turk' who has on a black suit, just as Gaius does. Gaius is the first to break the silence. "Everything is in order?" "Yes, Sir." "You are sure?!" Before the Turk can respond however, Gaius answers for him. "Of course you are sure. You would not have dared summoned me to this meeting if everything was not in order." "Sir, he is there. Trust me. He thinks I am a defector who is going to help collaborate his story. He is in the park as we speak at the meeting point I told him to be at. Your security detail made sure the park was empty as soon as the sun went down, and they have confirmed Zakaria's arrival a few moments ago." "And he is alone?" "Yes, Sir. This too had been confirmed." "Excellent. You have done well 'Turk.' Remember, the first priority is the evidence. Once we make sure he has no further copies and have the evidence in our possession, you may commence with the plan." "As you command, Sir." Gaius turns away from him. He takes out a bottle of water and take a drink from it. He offers some to the 'Turk,' who respectfully refuses. The car reaches a corner of Central Park and rests by a small entrance to the park. The driver of the limo gets out of the car and opens the door for his patron and his guest. The two men step out of the car. Gaius turns to the Turk. "Remember, I don't want him to know I am here until the proper time." "I know, Sir. Stay in the shadows. And he shouldn't be able to see you." The two men walk into the park and head toward a quiet area that is surrounded by bushes and trees. A very secluded spot that would allow the men to conduct their business in private. Gaius places himself out of view in the shadows as the 'Turk' walks toward a Gazebo where Fareed is waiting. The reporter speaks first. "I thought you weren't coming." "I'm here soon enough. It's not easy to get away from him." "I bet." Fareed takes out a tape recorder and hits 'play'. "So, what can you tell me exactly about the financial and secret dealings of Gaius Octavianus and the rest of his ruthless family?" The Turk looks Fareed over once, then twice, before he locks eyes with the reporter. Suddenly, his amicable smile turns into a frown, and he speaks with strength and determination. "Eager are we? There will be time for that soon enough." "What do you mean?" "Put the recorder down. First, we talk off the record." Fareed presses the 'stop' button on the recorder and puts it back in his pocket. "Ok, so what is so important that you need to talk to me off the record? 'The Turk' lights a cigarette and walks closer to his prey. "Fareed, when was the last time you went home to Cairo?" "I really don't see what point of this..." "Just answer the question!" Fareed suddenly has a cautious look on his face. "About six months, why do you ask?" "No reason. And how are your two young sons?" From the area of the shaded bushes, Gaius stands watching the scene unfold. He puts his hand in his coat pocket, whips out a gold cigarette container and a gold zippo. He takes out a cigarette, lights it, and puts the zippo back in his pocket. "They are well. I spoke to them saturday. One is nine and the other is seven. They are studying and preparing to start school again." "Splendid! Family is truly the most important thing in this world. That's good to hear they are well." "Why are you stalling? I need to have this done by this weekend. I have a deadline for this...." 'The Turk' loses his false demeanor once and for all. "Your deadline no longer matters." The larger of the two men grabs the shorter by the shoulder, and they begin to walk toward the bushes where Gaius is smoking a cigarette. "What the hell is this? Where the hell are you taking me?" The concerned man keeps asking this question, failing to see the small orange light and smoke that is seen among the shadows. Suddenly, another voice is heard, with a thick Roman accent. "Fareed." The reporter turns around, startled but unafraid. 'The Turk' disappears for a moment. Leaving the two men alone. "Adding assault to your family's list of crimes Gaius?! I should have known he was full of shit. You are not the type of gentleman who would rely on disloyal people." "You also should have fully realized who you were playing with Fareed. If I can't buy you or have you fired, you must have known I wouldn't quit there. The ruthlessness you dug up on me is nothing but the tip of the iceberg." "I knew you were cunning Gaius, but I doubt there is anyway you can switch the tables on me. I have all the advantages." Gaius smirks and replies in a powerful voice. "For now." "What do you mean?" Gaius smiles and inhales his cigarette again. Then, he snaps his fingers and out comes 'the Turk' once more. Both his hands wrapped around the tiny necks of two young boys, who can not speak or make a sound. A look of shock and disbelief on Zakaria's face. Gaius speaks once more. "Just had these brought in from Cairo this morning. I think these mongrel children belong to you?" The reporter looks at his two young sons, who have a terrified look on their face. He looks as though he is contemplating calling Gaius' bluff but then his emotions get the best of him. "My children! How can you? I thought this was between me and you!" "It is between you and I. But you are going to take me down for the sins of my father, so it is only right that if you take down my family, I will destroy yours." "You are bluffing. Even you wouldn't hurt children." "Really?" He turns to 'the Turk' and nods his head and his intensifies his grip. All of a sudden, the two boys begin to turn red. "NO!" the reporter yells. "NO! I'll do anything...stop!" Gaius turns to his friend and raises his hand, to signal to 'the Turk' to lesson his grip. The two boys can breath again. "It's all about family, Fareed. Would you not agree?! You want your sons back? Then you will turn over all evidence of my family's past to me and all copies are to be destroyed. If you fail to do so, then it looks like your family line will end with you. Those precious sons of yours will know a kind of torture and physical pain that few live through, and the ones who do never fully recover from the trauma." The defeated looking reporter can not stand watching his children suffer. "Alright. Alright. You'll have it. I swear. There aren't multiple copies, there are just two. One that I have, and one that my editor has at Newsweek. I'll get them to you." Gaius walks from out of the shadows, his cigarette dwindling. "See that you do." He takes one final drag, blows the smoke into the reporter's face, and plunges the burning remainder of the cigarette into Fareed's arm. "AAARRRRGGGHHH." The reporter lets out a painful scream. He then steps away from Gaius and the man who is holding his sons hostage as he caresses the fresh burn on his forearm. "Let that remind you of what's at stake. And of the kind of cruelity that I am capable of. I am not a patient man. Get the evidence. Now! There will be a man waiting for you at the Newsweek offices. He is to be your shadow, to make sure you don't double cross me. Once the evidence is mine, your sons will be released." "I'll get it done immediately." "See that you do. Otherwise your children will be fished out from the bottom of the Hudson." 'The Turk' grabs the two children, still by the throat, and begins to walk over to the limo where the driver is waiting. Gaius turns and walks away from his reporter nemesis, who is standing there a broken man. Gaius laughs sadistically as he walks away. Fareed looks at the fading figure, and then runs off in the opposing direction, to the Newsweek building. ....to be Concluded...
