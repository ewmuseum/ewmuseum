Emotionally Numb

23 May 2006

Date: 23/05/06 Then? McKail entered his apartment and allowed the to breeze shut behind him, in one smooth, fluidic movement he removed his black jacket and delicately place his keys in the dish upon the table beside the door. The funeral went as well as could be expected under the circumstances, though McKail did notice the way everyone looked at him, pitting him for his loss and wondering above all else why he didn’t even shed a tear. Truth be told, he kind of wondered about that himself, but he didn’t spend too much time dwelling on something that could not be changed. From what time he did allow to examine this quandary, he rather found himself to be emotionally numb. He considered that he family and friends might’ve found him slightly cold, perhaps distant, almost definitely uncaring and decided the notion to be completely irrelevant. Perhaps their anger and, in some of the more extreme cases, hatred would help them get through the rest of the day just that little but easier, but he had to admit it wasn’t the primary reason for it. McKail had made himself emotionally numb long before the funeral came around, soon after Mary died in the hospital and he grieved - rather too forcefully for some of the nurses. Now he chose to distance himself from such feelings in order to accomplish what he had to do. He’d made a promise to Mary to follow his dreams no matter what and as far as he was concerned he’d set off on that path as soon as the life drained from her delicate, and unbearably beautiful, body and to do the things he needed to do, his judgement could be clouded, his mind could be distracted nor his determination wavered. As well as making the appropriate arrangements for the funeral and the rest of Mary’s possessions, McKail had wasted no time manoeuvring his way back into the wrestling the circuit and into the life he so loved. That was what Mary wanted him to do once she was gone and he intended to complete it no matter the cost. He didn’t even allow everyone else’s opinions on the matter to cross his mind for a nanosecond. He would have time, and the inclination, to grieve for her once this was all over. Now? Another motel, another completely different city, though the ones McKail had recently visited had now all merged into one big scrambled blur, different aspects of different towns now almost indistinguishable from the rest. His insomnia probably didn’t much help matters either, but that problem was far beyond his capability to fix. McKail sat up in bed and turned on the tacky porcelain that rested right by his head, rubbing his face with both hands, as the small cascade of light quickly spreads faster than his eyes can properly adjust. He reached over to the bedside table and extracted his second to last cigarette of the packet and lit it, swiftly, with his heavy metal lighter. Taking a drag of the cancer stick, he found his mind racing back toward last weeks broadcast of World Wide. For the past week pretty much nothing else had crossed his mind, so therefore McKail had found the forced recollection hardly surprising. That particular night had become firmly entrenched in his mind for all the wrong reasons. True enough, he did stroll into and out of the arena the GCW Television Champion, but it was what happened after the match that really concerned him. Another, self-proclaimed Global Championship Wrestling rookie crashed in on the proceedings, laying a most obvious claim to his television title. The nerve of Northsky astounded him, but it didn’t unnerve him nearly as much as she undoubtedly intended it to. He would treat the upcoming match with her as he would any other opponent and potential victory. His run within the GCW had put him five for five and he had no doubt in his mind that this week would be his sixth straight victory and his third successful title defence. She claimed that he’d fought nobodies during his relatively short span in GCW and McKail had to concede that she did have a point in regards, but he didn’t accept the notion that Northsky was any more formidable than the cadre of underachievers that McKail had previously demolitions. Northsky was just his next target, only she didn’t know it yet.
