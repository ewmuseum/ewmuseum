Shoot Style

5 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/5/05 [It had been a long time. It was summer of “99 that the young Ryan Stephens won his first title in one of the most storied umbrella organizations in the world, the NWC. Those of you who never met that Ryan Stephens missed out on a great man, far from the methodical, cynical, calculating prick that resides in GCW today. His tale is about to unfold, but first let’s delve into the man that used to be and how he became the monstrosity he has become.] VOICE OVER: I’m in ML Kurly and I’ve been invited to sit down with “Scandalous” Ryan Stephens this evening and ask him all kinds of questions, pick him apart if you will. I’m one of two on-air personalities inducted into the illustrious NWC Hall of Fame so apparently I’m qualified for this duty. [Kurly sits directly across from Stephens in a dim lit room. Both men sit in large black leather chairs with small side tables on their right sides. ML has a scotch and Stephens a bottled water. Stephens seems a bit more uptight than we’ve ever seen him as ML dishes out the first question.] KURLY: Why are you back Ryan? You’ve got all the money you could ever need. STEPHENS: There’s always more money to be made ML. One day I will have a family and when that day comes I don’t want my kids to have to worry about money, or their kids, or their kids. I’ve already stated to my adoring public that I’m back to put my beautiful face back on the map. KURLY: You know I have to call bullshit. STEPHENS: I can’t imagine why. KURLY: Because I’ve seen you Streak. [Stephens shifts uneasily at the reference to his old moniker.] KURLY: I saw you get pummeled, get the shit kicked out of you unmercifully for four and a half years. Every time, every [bleeping] time you got up, gave some horrible promo about how you could endure more physical pain than any man, had more heart than any man, and would keep getting up until you prevailed. STEPHENS: Streak is dead ML, he died a long time ago and with him a lot of pain and a lot of suffering. [He chuckled.] STEPHENS: And I’m WAY better on the mic now. KURLY: Why’d you do it? [For those of you familiar with “it” that’s all that needs to be done because the deed was so dastardly everyone knows the “it” that ML mentions. For those of you who don’t shortly before the NWC closed it’s doors Stephens turned on his tag team partner, the young upstart Chance McMillan, brutally beating him within an inch of his life, the kid is still in a wheelchair to this day.] STEPHENS: It was time to turn over a new leaf ML. What do you want me to say? I’m where I’m at now because I made that decision. I don’t regret it not for a second. [Kurly stares at Stephens not with contempt, but with disappointment.] KURLY: You know he’s rehabbing every day. He vows to get revenge. [Stephens, calmly with no ounce of remorse in his body.] STEPHENS: He’ll never walk again. KURLY: For christ sakes Ryan. You betrayed a good friend. A good kid that looked up to you. What you did to him was ten times worse than what LeStatt did to Jason Dread. People to this day can’t look you in the eye because of what you did to that kid. And you shunned your wife because she was a [bleeping] liability? STEPHENS: Don’t attach yourself to anything you can’t walk out on in a moment’s notice. KURLY: Sounds lonely if you ask me. STEPHENS: It’s the price of perfection ML. I’m flawless. [Kurly lifts his glass of scotch, takes a sip and nods towards the glass as he sits it back on the side table.] KURLY: You got it wrong Ryan. Before, when you were a tenacious little bastard. When you drank yourself into a stupor, when you bugged the shit out of the veterans for tips, when you trained so hard that you didn’t have time for others, when you lived recklessly and loved reckless ? you were flawless then. Now you personify this image of perfection, but at the core of you, you’re more flawed than any man I know. STEPHENS: Every man is entitled their own opinion ML. KURLY: Let me ask you this. What are you going to do when you lose it all? When it all comes crashing down around you? You’re so stripped of what made you, you that you’ll never get back up. [Stephens smiles, he welcomes the face that Kurly is trying to break him. Hell he invited Kurly to do this shoot style interview just so he could match wits with the guy.] STEPHENS: I know it’s your job ML, but how many times do I have to tell you, you’re barking up the wrong tree. I used to live for intangible things like honor and respect, but that stuff is a non-factor now. KURLY: Just like the rest of your life. You’re indifferent to the world Ryan. You’re indifferent to winning. Look, you may not have had superstar status in the NWC, but you were solid. People like Jonathon Borealis, Eva Van Lorne and Trey Tremendous wouldn’t be able to hold a candle to you EVER. And you know it, and I know it, and they can all say to stop living in the past, that the NWC is dead but it’s legacy lives on with the few guys still competing, with you Ryan. You’re carrying the torch whether you realize it or not. [Kurly bleeds NWC as Stephens once had. Take Primtime Championship Wrestling, then multiply it by four or five because at all times there were four or five NWC regions filled with PCW like talent. Stephens participated in both places, he’s a honest man, he’ll tell you. Stephens interrupts the tangent.] STEPHENS: [BLEEP] the NWC. It’s dead. Its? heroes are gone. And the fact of the matter is ML, I don’t care about winning and losing because it doesn’t matter as long as I get adequate face time. As long as people know Ryan Stephens and my face is synonymous with money because money talks my friend, it makes the world go round. [Kurly sharpens the verbal daggers.] KURLY: Your whole career. The one thing that plagued you and help you back. Inconsistency. For every legend you beat, you lost at least two HUGE matches with career defining stipulations. You were Mike Hardy, but better. And that’s why you’re here. That’s why you’ve dodged promotions you could get lost in like fWo, like PRIME. That’s why you wound up in GCW where you thought you could be a face on a poster. [He pauses, this is like talking shit about a guy’s batting average. It stings whether you show it or not.] KURLY: You can’t win in GCW either Stephens. Sure you’re 3-0, but what happened in both your big matches. You came up just a little short didn’t you? So hide behind your money, your ridiculous Austin estate, all your career “opportunities” because if you keep doing that nobody will see you for what you really are, the biggest FRAUD in wrestling. STEPHENS: Are you finished? KURLY: It stings doesn’t it. To know I’m right. That fa’ade of indifference, sure these chums over here see it, but that fiery kid is still in there somewhere. Check your pride, check your ego and put the sky blue tights back on. Fight guys like LeStatt Knight and Rich Rollins, and stand up for the few Mike Hardy’s left. You owe it to them, to yourself. [He could lose his cool here. This is promptly the point when most would have enough, but Stephens makes you believe this is all going in one ear and out the other contrary to the truth of course. He sits there snide, with that smug grin of his as Kurly tears at his defenses like the teeth of a leopard gnawing on a broken zebra.] [Thousands of memories dancing about in his head, but he stays on track ? his train won’t be derailed not tonight, not even by ML Kurly who would make Roy Firestone look like a pansy ass.] STEPHENS: Why don’t you come back to the announcing booth Kurly? Or perhaps you should get yourself a backstage gig? Then you could document all the wins I obtain and all the wins I GIVE away. KURLY: I’m not through with you Stephens. Maybe I can’t penetrate that wall, but I’ll send others this way. Guys like Lance Marshall, Alexander, GD West, perhaps one at a time until you check yourself and become what you always should’ve been. The platform is there, seize it you bastard. STEPHENS: I think it’s time for you to go ML. KURLY: I suppose you’re right Ryan. [Kurly slides forward in his chair. He eyes Stephens who stares a hole through ML as he stands and departs. Stephens falls back into the depths of his chair and loses himself in thought.] [He’s been through more than most men, he’s one learned hombre’perhaps the most conflicted man you’ve ever met ? and yet he personifies evil and a collective nature better than a hybrid of Tyler Nelson and Clyde himself. This is one time bomb I’d be certain to keep an eye on.] [End]
