It Means Everything (this one)

6 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/6/06 The moon cast an eerie glow over the abandoned ranch that Russell Braun once called his home, but that did not deter the two men dressed in black that wended their way through the thigh-high weeds that had been left to grow wildly throughout the former corn field. Except for the decrepit farm house and the barn that had been left to rot in the daily abuse of Mother Nature, the land appeared to be genuine and untouched. The figures seemed to blend in with the shadow cast by the two story home, as they stealthily made their way to the abandoned abode. The years of abuse from the weather had taken its toll on the exterior of the home, leaving the once white aluminum siding looking dark and dingy. “Preparing to enter,” one of the figures whispered. In an unmarked van a few miles up the road, Nicholas Pride sat with a large pair of headphones over his ears. He leaned toward one of the monitors, as a video feed was transferred to the monitors via a satellite that kept surveillance over the entire situation. “You getting all of this on tape,” Pride asked one of the technicians that was punching away on a few buttons on the console in front of him. “Yes sir.” Pride smiled confidently as he watched the figures make their way up the stairs of the front porch and to the front door. “It’s not locked,” the mechanical voice squawked into his ears. “We’re going in.” There was a total of three locks -- one of them being a deadbolt -- that had been installed on the door, but the entrance had been left unlocked. As the figure twisted the knob and pushed the door open, the hinges began to squeal like a stuck pig. “So Nick, what’s with the cloak and dagger bullshit,” the tech looked asked as he looked over his shoulder at Detective Pride. “Isn’t the sick bastard that owned this place dead?” “Yeah,” Pride replied, his eyes fixated on the monitor in front of him. “But the judge said he wanted something concrete if we were going to go after this Bradley guy. As far as everyone is concerned, this case was closed when Braun ate the bullet. Which means this place is off limits since there is nothing left to investigate.” “Yeah, Rachel told me you had a hard on for this guy.” “Night vision goggles on,” was what Pride heard through the headphones, completely ignoring what the technician had to say as the image on the screen changed from dark and shadow filled to a dull shade of green. Pride watched intently as the two men rummaged through the house, foraging through drawers and cabinets as if they were searching for buried treasure. “Some sort of clue,” Pride thought to himself as he rubbed his forefinger along his temple, massaging away the pressure to find something that may not be there in the first place. “Have you thought about what you’re going to do if you can’t find anything, Nick?” Pride shook his head, resting his elbows on the small area in front of the monitor as he begins to nibble on his thumb nail. “There’s something there, Paul, I can feel it in my gut.” “That’s probably just the Mexican food from earlier,” Paul the technician replied as he began to press a few more buttons on his keyboard, before stretching out to press a mid-sized red button a few inches away from where he sat. “It’s more than that, Paul. There is something strange about that old man and that wrestler that just happened to be passing through when it happened. I can’t put my finger on it yet, but if you give me some time to get everything in order’Well, let’s just say that prison is the last thing Bradley is going to have to worry about.” =?=?=?=?= “Ronery’I so ronery’I haf no borry’to caw my ohh?” The small Asian man butchered the song like he worked in a meat market, but most of the audience clapped and sang along as if he was the next coming of 2Pac’What, you were expecting Elvis? The place was packed, little men in business suits making fools of themselves on stage while others drank the day’s earnings away as if it were water. One man sat alone in the far corner of the karaoke bar, his features hidden by the shadows. “You like more drink?” The waitress leaned slightly to her left, her head cocked to the right as she kept her hand pressed against her hip. With a wave of a hand she was dismissed, scurrying on to the next table where four men that had arrived fifteen minutes early for happy hour started asking her to take off her top. She smiled politely at first, hoping that they would come to their senses or just drop the subject altogether, but they did neither. One of the men, his Brooks Brothers look-a-like suit stained from the red wine they had started their evening on, got a firm grip of her hind side and pressed a wad of bills into the front of her low cut shirt. She screamed something in her native tongue, slapping the man hard across the cheek. To which he responded to with a backhand that knocked her from her feet. He started yelling as the rest of his friends stood up and held him back. The woman was in tears, cowering on the ground in fear of what he would do next. What he did was shove his friends aside, ignoring their pleas to just let it go. Still yelling, he hardly noticed the man that stood between him and the woman; pressing a finger against his chest to persuade him to take a step back. The little Asian man became enraged, brushing the gesture away with the back of his hand before using every ounce of force he had to push the intervener aside. The only problem with that, was that the intervener used that momentum to knock his attacker onto his back with a perfectly timed side step. “Seriously,” he pleaded, “you don’t want to do this.” Apparently his friends did, as two of them were quick to grab the man by his arms. The third stood in front of the man who interfered in their friends affair. “Who you tink you is,” he scolded, pointing a finger in the mediators face. “You Americans all same, always looking at trouble. Now you--Umph” A kick to the chest sent the big winded little Asian back onto his rear with a thump as the air escaped from his lungs. An instantaneous gasp escaped from the crowd that had gathered around the scene, even the crooner on the stage had quieted down as his audience had found something else to pay attention to. The American dropped his weight to his left, bringing the weight of the man on his right crashing down onto himself and the one clinging to his left. The three men toppled over, the weight of the other two men causing the man at the bottom to release his grasp on the American interloper. A steel toed boot connected with the side of the other man’s face, blood splattering onto the floor from the man’s now broken nose. The man that intervened on the attack of the woman, looked up into the crystal blue eyes of Global Championship Wrestling’s Cruiserweight champion; Jayce Bradley. Jayce extended his hand out to help the gentleman to his feet, “You okay?” “Yeah, didn’t expect them to gang up on me like that,” the man gripped Bradley’s hand and rose to his feet. “I thought they had some sort of code to fight alone.” “That shit’s just in the movies.” The two shared a laugh as the first man brushed himself off. The four Asian men helped each other up, as what looked to be security corralled them into a group. The one with the busted nose could hardly stand up straight, his head tilted back as he attempted to walk with the aid of another of the men. “The name’s Oscar Calhoun.” He said with his hand extended and a smile as big as Texas on his beet red face. “Jayce Bradley,” he responded as he took the man’s hand in his own. “Shut the fuck up!” “Excuse me?” Jayce looked surprised, the man’s reaction causing him to pull his hand back in confusion. “I’m sorry,” the man pleaded with his arms raised. “I didn’t mean that in a bad way. It’s just that me and a few buddies of mine are on leave, and we came down here to catch World Wide.” Jayce nodded his head slightly, understanding why the man reacted the way he did. “Sorry if I freaked you out.” “Don’t mention it, always glad to meet fans of the show.” The four Asian men were being escorted out of the bar, as the waitress barked out what could have been obscenities -- if I actually understood what the hell she was saying. Oscar motioned for Jayce to take a seat, waving a waitress over to take their order. “The drinks are on me man,” Oscar gestured. “For you saving my ass and all.” “You don’t have to?” “I insist,” he persisted. “I’m not much of a drinker.” “Hell get a water if you want,” a genuine smile on his face. “Just sit here and amuse me a while. My friends should be back in a few, and they won’t believe me if I told them.” “Only for a few minutes,” he replied as he relaxed in the chair. “I have somewhere I need to be in a bit.” “That’s perfect. Besides, it’s not everyday you get to meet someone you see on television..let alone have them save your ass from a beating.” Jayce managed a chuckle in response. “So man, you excited about your match against Shadow?” With an eyebrow raised, Jayce sort of shook his head. “You’re not serious are you?” “Yeah, why?” His brow furrowed in confusion. “I don’t know,” Jayce replied. “It just sounds funny to have someone ask me a question like that out of nowhere. Like if it was rehearsed or something.” “Seriously man, I just wanted to know if you were excited. Hell, I’m excited and I’m just going to the show. I could just imagine how I’d be if I was going to get my hands on any of those sexy bitches.” “It isn’t all fun and games, you know.” With a smirk on his lips, Oscar shook his head. “I wouldn’t care. Five minutes of tie-ups where my hands would accidentally slip, and I’d be able to die right there on the spot.” The answer was enough to show the lighter side of Jayce Bradley, as he actually had a genuine smile on his face as he broke out in laughter. Trying to contain his laughter, “I..I never thought about it’like that.” The waitress walked up and placed two drinks on the table in front of them. “We still haven’t ordered yet.” “On house,” the waitress replied with a slight bow, before turning and heading to another table. “Okay, so I’ll get the next round.” “I really need to be going,” Jayce said politely. “I just came in here to take a piss when I saw you needed help.” “Hey man, it’s cool. Do you think you could sign a napkin for me or something, you know’so my friends won’t think I’m bullshitting them.” “Yeah, sure thing.” Jayce grabbed a napkin from the stack on the table and the pen that Oscar offered him, then proceeded to scribble something over the paper before handing it back to him. “That’s fucking sweet.” Oscar grinned like a schoolgirl as he glanced at the signature. “I wish I would’ve had a tee shirt or something’any idea when you’re going to get some made?” “No clue.” “Damn.” Oscar straightened the napkin on the table. “Hay, any chance you can get Rollins to sign something for me?” “I doubt it,” Jayce replied, “I don’t socialize much with the guys backstage. Nice meeting you though.” “Nice meeting you, too.” Jayce managed a polite wave as he departed. Oscar crumbled up the napkin and tossed it into the half full drink glass as soon as Jayce walked through the doors, pulling a small cell phone from his pocket and pressing a few buttons before pressing the phone to his ear. “Hey, this is Jefferson, I made contact with the suspect.” =?=?=?=?= “Perfect.” Detective Nicholas Pride held a cell phone to his ear, grinning as he stared at a photo of Russell Braun and a teenage boy. That teenage boy just happened to be wearing a black wrestling singlet. He also happened to share a striking resemblance to a GCW Cruiserweight champion everyone knows. “Good Samaritan my ass.” =?=?=?=?= Where we are is a studio. Caldera decided to have a few of the Rampage tournament contestants shoot a few spots about what the GCW means to them, and we just happen to be here for a behind the scenes look at Jayce Bradley’s spots. The small sound stage is mostly used for public access, but it has been jazzed up a bit for the occasion. In the background there is a huge white canvas with the GCW logo on it. What the word says underneath the logo is 'WorldWide'. This is as nice as this place can look. Bradley is seated on a stool in the center, he has been given makeup, his hair is combed back, and he wears a black tee shirt with the GCW logo over the right breast where a pocket would have been, the shirt is tight, so as to show his lean muscular frame. His face is clean shaven and his crystal blue eyes look like liquid under the hot studio lights. 'Okay Jayce, just a few quick spots, something that tells us what the GCW title means to you.” “Like what’Ten’Thirty seconds or something,” Jayce questioned, not wanting to mess this up. Jayce looks nervous under the lights. The only other time he has been in the spotlight like this was when he was in GTT5, and we all saw how that turned out. 'Well, we're doing a few takes. We can cut it up.' “Alright, I’ll try my best.” They shoot this in a black and white take, add a little bit of music to it to spice it up, and bam’they have gold. 'And...ACTION!' 'The GCW title’I had never really thought about until a few days ago. Now’Now it’s the only thing on my mind.” He rubs away a bit of perspiration that had begun to bead up on his brow. 'It’s when you realize that this title, how it makes people feel, is so much bigger than just your own personal glory. You start to hear the sound of all the people cheering you or booing you, and you know that you have their attention.” “It’s invigorating.” 'You’re up there in front of the entire World, and you are their champion.' 'Cut. Good. Now, again....ACTION!' “The title brings a sense of honor with it, wrapping you up in it’s little bubble and making it seem as if you are untouchable. Whether it lasts a month’a week’or just those measly fifteen minutes people like to talk about’that feeling of accomplishment will stay with you for the rest of your life.” 'Good! You got more?' 'I could give you these all day.' 'ACTION!' 'It’s why you do the things you do. Why you train to the point of exhaustion, why you eat foods that are good for you but taste like two day old crap. You miss things that are important to people that are important to you. It becomes an obsession, a drug’a symbol of something that you were never promised to get. It means so much, because of everything that you give up just to brush your hand against it’s face. It means so much because of all the people that have tried their entire lives to obtain it, only to fail with every opportunity.' 'It means everything.' 'It costs you everything you have.' 'I will bleed. I will fight. I would rip through my organs with blood stained fingernails to feel it just once. I know I can’t make everyone understand, but I know what price I am willing to pay for it, and it is more than I am willing to admit.” 'Do you have more?” 'I think so.” 'Then continue’ACTION!” 'It's the best feeling there is.” “Anything worth having, you end up having to pay more than what it’s worth, but what you pay makes it worth it. If that makes any sense. It should not be that way. It seems like everyone deserves to have this kind of feeling at least once, but not everyone will.' 'Making it mean that much more.” Jayce looks up into the camera, his eyes focused and full of determination. 'That’s what it means.”
