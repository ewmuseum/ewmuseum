Bad drunk... Words of rioting... When will the heat stop?

28 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/28/05 Back in the heat--America's personal hell--it's all my fault this time around. Swarmed for several hours by insects and kids graduating high school, now covered in bites that've swollen to tiny lumps and unsure of who exactly is to blame. It always comes back around to me. I shot down Trey's idea to spend some time in Detroit and he'd been moping ever since. He's beyond reproach; this climate is designed to drive a person into deep drunks and the wretched depths of stoned. It's the perfect place for a war when the sun's hanging orange in the sky and breathing burns. The city heat is worse than the country heat, no doubt. It swelters and warps off the concrete, leaves a person suffocating and sticky with sweat. So, to falling in line with the climate's will. I'm far enough back from my life that there's no real point in trying to catch up, or to justify Tulz. She never talks much, just lingers and watches to occasionally join in. The god idea had been enough at the time I met her, but a girl can only be naive for so long before she realizes what's going on. Right now, there are more important things at hand. Trey's depression lifted into mania when the sun dipped below the horizon, elevated by the drink and boisterous. He screamed from the hotel balcony, waving a bottle of rum around and splashing it out with every roar cast down on the infidels. After long enough, he calmed back down and came inside to jitter nervously in a corner, crazy and wound up tight. 'There's gotta be somethin' to do, J.' 'Fuck you. Every fucking runt in this area that graduated's headed to a party to get fucked up and swerve their way back home. The fucking swine are all over town, and I'm not ready to go through a roadblock. I don't want to justify this cast and that eye patch to the police, do you? It's going to be hell out there for the next few days. I say we hole up in here until Warpath, and leave the city as quickly as possible afterwards.' 'I don't wanna be stuck in here all weekend, man. Fuck that.' 'Have fun. They'll hang you here, wandering around with your one-eyed criminal leer trained on all those just-legal girls. You won't be able to claim your innocence before you're sentenced.' 'It ain't that bad out there.' 'You've never been to Texas. Tulz is an illegal immigrant--for all intents and purposes--and they'll be quick to tie us up in the terrorist affairs they connect her to. No, no, fuck that. You wouldn't make it five minutes out there. Go slab some Neosporin on your eye and calm down.' 'I think I'm ready to take the patch off, man.' He pulled it up then, and revealed a completely smooth set of eye lids coated in some slime, a jagged line of melted flesh running diagonally across the slit. 'No, not ready at all. Let's think about it though, that patch seems to have increased your ability ten-fold. I say leave it and fuck the depth perception.' 'Shit, it still looks that bad?' 'Horrible. I suggest you get monks to forge you a suit of armor, and that you put the mask on when it's still hot so as to completely disfigure your face.' 'Ain't Dr. Doom, man.' 'Alright. I've got some saline solution, if you wanna use it.' 'What is it?' 'Shit they give you to help keep the area clean after piercings or tattoos. Can't be bad for your eye lid--toss me my bag.' The heavy black duffel caught me harder in the chest than I'd expected, so I dug through more shit than I remembered bringing before I pulled the bottle up and hurled it to Trey. He went into the bathroom and closed the door, at which point Tulz started laughing. It was a first, and it was an infectious giggle that tried to explore a broad range of laughs before it ended. 'What?' 'That was mean, Jordan.' 'What?' She looked at me like I didn't know. I thought about puppies and played up my drunk, swerving head-on towards pointless trains of thought. Playing stupid didn't last long, the brief moment of joy interrupted by an echoing scream from a most likely sobered up Trey. Tulz and I laughed and Trey turned the water on, splashing it and trying to keep quiet after his initial burst. He eventually came out and flung the bottle at my head--missed by a foot. 'Fuck, man. Why ain't you tell me?' 'Trey, you need more to drink. As a certified doctor, I now realize that your eyes are hypersensitive, a condition which can only be dealt with by alcoholic stupors.' He fell backwards into one of the engulfing brown leather chairs that lined the room and leaned back to turn the air conditioner on to high. 'Don't think I can handle anymore of that shit, J.' 'C'mon. We can go out and experiment on the natives after we're sufficiently lit. I didn't buy all this liquor so I could build a highly intoxicating fort.' 'Shit, I dunno. You ain't like the idea earlier--what's there to do now?' 'I just wanted to convince you to use saline solution a minute ago, which would've been hard if you'd busted out the door with debauchery on your mind. Anyway, I called Sobias earlier to find some deity freaks, but he only turned up a fundamentalist compound on the outskirts of the city--real militant bastards that claim they've got Gabriel holed up with them, stockpiling ammo for an Armageddon that's just a few weeks off. Too depressing for me to fuck with them, and too intimidating for us to waltz in asking about my slacker god. We can terrorize the fucking natives, if worse comes to worse.' 'Ain't that what we always do?' 'It's always a goal, but it rarely gets focused on. Now it can be our only motivation--give the scum something to remember GCW for. I've got a few staff shirts--security drabs with the company logo screened across the front about a foot tall.' 'I dunno, shit. Your last plan wasn't that great.' 'But I broke my shoulder, not yours. Where do you have the room to complain?' 'I only got one eye, man. Less to lose, so it's more important that I don't lose it.' 'Don't worry about that, your eye's gonna be fine. It's just an average night of cruel fun.' The argument continued back-and-forth to the bottom of the first bottle of rum and half-way down the other before I caught on to the constant snickering of Tulz on the bed behind me. Trey was too far gone from sobriety to notice the sound or understand what it meant, and he slipped further and further down the slope with every shot he downed. 'You ready?' 'Fwhat?' 'Terror. Locals.' 'Shyeah.' We finished the bottle we'd been working on while I rolled up a blunt to smoke on our way to the outside world. It lasted just long enough to drop us straight into the public, bounding through tight crowds of people that massed on the sidewalks in front of the clubs dotted around the center of town. Patrol cars rolled past every few minutes, not so much to govern over us as to make sure we knew that they were there, waiting around every corner for something bad to happen. There was something in the air like it was inevitable that all of our humors were ready to ignite at any moment, starting a chain reaction that would leave the entire town soaked in gore and viscera. I stopped behind a group of teenagers in front of a club called the Doghouse, two guys wearing tight striped shirts and two girls in mini-skirts and halter tops. They wore illegal smiles, as John Prine would say, and they wore them with the pride befitting an accomplished general that had single-handedly won more conflicts than the country had ever seen. Trey bumped into my shoulder and started apologizing until I held a hand up to his face and introduced fear to the group. 'You kids see those cops circling?' They nodded in unison, 'Shit, the paper this morning talked about a riot, but I didn't think it was that serious.' 'A riot?' 'Yeah, real king-hell event. Said that some cult was headed into town, armed with hellfire and damnation. Police were figuring on about ten thousand of the bastards swarming in protest--they've got roadblocks set up on pretty much every road into town. It's looking bad.' 'What're they protesting?' 'Fucked if I know, maybe fun. They've got orders to take down troublemakers with excessive brutality, if the need arises. We're hours from martial law if things go the way the cops want them to-' 'Jesus.' 'He's no help now, I caught the pigs on a police scanner talking about inciting a goddamned riot if the lunatics don't show up. This many people enjoying themselves can give even an earnest cop hives, and this force seems to be the pure bastard type. Just a head's up.' One of them stepped forwards and took the leader spot at that, his eyes swollen half-shut and still clutching at a bottle in a brown paper bag. 'Fuck the cops. They wanna see some shit, I'll show 'em.' He grasped the bottle's neck tighter and raised it over his head--pulled back and spiraled it high in the air at the patrol car parked at a nearby McDonalds. It hung like a touchdown pass, coming down ominous and precise on the hood of the car with a shattering sound that deafened everyone around to help prepare for the long silence that would follow. I grabbed Tulz and Trey by their wrists and dragged them through the mob of people before the sirens clicked on, and was inside long before I heard them pull up against the sidewalk. Trey laughed uncontrollably, having left his trepidation somewhere in the ruins of the Roman empire, and Tulz held the same smug smirk across her face that she'd worn the entire night. Outside, glass shattered and the voices of protest raised themselves high into the night. Drunken howls overlapped the long screeching of the sirens, and the swarm outside had found their mark for the evening. Chaos would wait for me, I ordered a round of drinks for everyone that hadn't run outside to check the scene and a double-round for us three. 'Thas some funny shit, J.' 'What?' 'Gettin 'at dude pissed.' 'Oh, Christ. That's just a good backdrop to hold a conversation to. The pressing issue's your match, and preparing.' The drinks came and Tulz and I began turning them up with fervor while Trey waited for an okay. 'Drink, you son of a bitch. I said we're preparing for a match, haven't you ever trained before?' 'Usually don'train like 'is.' 'You should. It's delicious.' In the back corner at the bar sat heavy revelations, sharp twists, and enlightenment. I didn't realize that at the time, of course, so I kept drinking and bullshitting with Trey, Tulz, and the people that streamed over with thanks for the beer. The war raged on but no one inside felt the need to care as they danced or drank towards that same inevitable oblivion that all the heat beating at the walls implied; that soon the fires would grow strong enough to consume us all and ruin the carefree life. I didn't care much. Now and then came voices played over loudspeakers, warnings addressed at the small riot that had started in front of the Doghouse and progressed down the street with the same speed it had ignited at. There were hours before anyone would describe me as having convinced the first bottle to be thrown, but that means nothing. Inciting small bouts of doubt has never been illegal, though doing so for the sole purpose of starting a riot is often frowned upon by both the higher and lower courts of the land. Nothing could be proven, and I set the moment of concern I allot for each month aside until I would need it--should that ever come up. 'Trey, you know why that guy outside believed me?' 'Why?' 'I used just enough big words to sound like I knew what I was talking about. People are stupid like that.' He laughed. 'No, it's kind of sad.' I laughed. 'Bastards'll believe anyone that gets them when they're too drunk to think about what's being said--poor fucks. They're the voting bloc that both parties would kill for, but both sides know they're also the bloc that aren't motivated enough to do much, and neither team's got a clue how to motivate them. If you just give the dumbasses a reason when they've got time to act, you can start a war.' He understood it better then, I think, that my plans usually jump the line from frivolous to hard-line aggressive agendas without any warning, and are to be embarked upon only after more consideration than Trey really wanted to go through. He still had no clue to worry about the variables, though, and that's his problem--time will break him of his trusting nature, and time and I’ll have a good laugh over it.
