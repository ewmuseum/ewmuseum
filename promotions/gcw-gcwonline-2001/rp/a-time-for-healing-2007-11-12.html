A Time For Healing

12 November 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11-12-2007 To look at him, you would have thought that he was in a car wreck. There are 14 stitches, going down his forehead, crossing over an old scar from past wars. His face was still bruised and swollen. Every step that he took, he was in a little bit of pain. This was a life that he once escaped, but now he was sucked back into it. He peered into the window of the hospital room, and saw her. She was hooked up to a heart monitor. He was scared. He didn’t know what to do. He felt alone. Standing there, Dorian Ryan was washed over with feelings of things that happened in the past. He was wrestling the night that Keiko and Dan passed away. His life from that point onto when he met Andrea was filled with internal strife. The night that he was being bloodied and battered, Andrea was rushed here, not breathing. Had she passed, he would never have forgiven himself. As he was battling for her honor, she was fighting for her life. The doctors were able to stabilize her, to save her. For that, he was eternally thankful. A young nurse, who had to have been less than a year out of school, walked up behind him. She put her hand on his shoulder. “I know that it’s painful to watch this.” The nurse’s attempts to be reassuring didn’t help Dorian any. Instead, they just made him want to enter the room even more. He saw Andrea do something that she hadn’t done in a long time. She laughed. Of course, her doctor was in there. Her first laugh in over a year, and he wasn’t the one that she shared it with. At least she was alright. It’s been over a year since relocating to Minnesota from Arizona. It’s been over a year since he saw her glow. It’s strange what death does to people. Dorian’s had enough death in his life to last him the rest of his time on the planet. “You should go in and see her.” The nurse mentioned as she gave him a nudge towards the door. Dorian stead fast though, didn’t want to do anything that would make her cry. “Not like this.” Dorian gestured to how his face looked. It wasn’t a pretty site. He knew it. “If you love her, it shouldn’t matter what you look like. You know, Beauty ended up with the Beast.” Her red hair was pulled back in a pony tail. Something she hadn’t done in years. As she turned to look at the door, Dorian stepped back, so he wouldn’t be seen. The nurse entered the room, closing the door behind her. Andrea must have caught a slight glimpse of Dorian. “How bad did he look?” The nurse was surprised. “How did you know?” “I can feel him watching me. Just like the other place. I always knew he was there.” “He’s hurting. I think more from watching you being here than the cut on his head and the bruises.” “As much as I want him to come in, I don’t think I’m ready. Not like this. Not here.” “Sweetie, I don’t know about you, but if I had a person that spent all their free time here, wanting to see you, you don’t need to be ready. He loves you. That episode that brought you here, maybe it was your brain and your heart telling you that it’s time to get over what brought you there, and start healing, not on your own, but with his help.” These words did something with Andrea, as a tear started to come down her face. The nurse understood without any words and headed towards the door. As she opened it and looked out of the hospital room, Dorian was gone. He hadn’t gotten far, as the nurse was able to find him in the cafeteria at the hospital. It had been almost 24 hours since he had last eaten, and the hunger pangs had taken hold of him. He was sitting alone, eating an egg salad sandwich and a cream cheese Danish. He held his head down to try and keep from being recognized. Last thing he needed to do right now is sign autographs for fans. His solace in his meal was short lived, as the nurse instantly recognized the back of his shirt and walked up to his table. She stood there for a second before sitting down. “She wants to see you.” “I don’t want her to see me looking like this. I know she’s seen it many times in the past, but there was never a way to get used to it.” “You’re afraid of her reaction?” “I’m afraid that she’s going to think that I’m torturing myself week in and week out because she’s shut me out of her life for the last year.” “Why?” “Maybe because it could be true. I don’t want to believe it, but I don’t know what to believe anymore when it comes to her. I just don’t.” “Believe me when I tell you that she wants to see you, and that she doesn’t care what you look like. She needs you more than she realizes. You need her more than you realize. The two of you need each other. That’s all there is to it.” “I know. I also know that the woman that I saw in that room today wasn’t the same woman that I had seen in a mental ward for the last year. She looked more like the woman that I married.” Dorian picked up his sandwich and took a bite. Pain shot through his mouth. The reason he hadn’t eaten in the last day wasn’t because he didn’t want to, it was because of the stitches he had gotten in his mouth. It hurt to eat or drink. But he had to do it. “Mr. Ryan, the two of you just need time to heal. That’s all it is. I know that neither one of you want to continue life wondering when is going to be the right time to come back together. Because there is never a wrong time.” With that, Dorian threw his sandwich back on the tray and stood up. He bussed his tray and walked out of the cafeteria. He walked right back to Andrea’s hospital room. The doctor left the room and closed the door behind him. Dorian and Andrea held each other for the first time in over a year.
