Slowing Down

26 October 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10-26-07 “Sometimes in life, we ignore all of the small things that others focus on.” The darkness of the stormy evening is pierced by twin headlights guiding a vehicle down this deserted stretch of highway. The voice comes from no one visible, though the vehicle whipping through the downpour continues to be our focus. The darkness is thick; too thick to tell what make of car is defying nature's threat and barreling down the road. “We choose to ignore that which has always been there, patiently waiting for us to divert our attention away from life's challenges, our priorities, and just relax.” The vehicle plows on through the storm, a thundering behemoth down a slick highway. The left windshield wiper is stuck, a jagged line of black against the smooth, soulless gray of the tinted glass. The headlights cast about aimlessly ahead, looking for something to claim, something to take in. There is nothing. Nothing but the voice. “Life just happens so fast though, and anyone that slows down to take the time to reflect back on his decisions is quite likely to be left behind. It's not an event anyone looks forward to, and not something I thought I could ever accept.” There. Up ahead, and off to the side of the road. A blue road sign, lettered in white, is nearing, but through the rain, it’s unclear. But we’re approaching, and we’re approaching fast. “We're all in a rush to get where we're going, in such a hurry to achieve our goals, that we tend to ignore the fact that they're going to be there whether we rush or not..” There’s that sign now. “It's something I'm learning the hard way...” And as it floats by in a blur, we notice an old, primer gray car sitting at the side of the road with a man laying on it's hood. “...every time a car passes by and doesn't even bother to take a second glance in my direction.” The evening once again envelops the scene, darkness wrapping us up in it's warm embrace. “Maybe next time I'll just slow down.” ~{ o o }~ “Well look what the cat dragged in.” Ray Holland tossed his duffel bag on the ground and made his way across the empty gym floor, his eyes rolling as he shook his head. “Not really in the mood, Cole.” The old man that had offered to help train him before Dangerous Games shook his head as well, his brow furrowed due to the curious look on his face. “Really now,” he began, “because I wasn't really in the mood to wait here for three hours until you decided to drag your sad sack through the damn doors, but I still did it.” “I had car trouble,” was the lazy response he mustered up, as he dropped himself onto one of the weight benches off in the corner. “Damn alcoholics will pull over for bastard with a case of beer and an axe, but won't bother to even offer help to a man with a damn gas can in his hand.” “It's a damn commercial, Ray,” Cole Hartford had run this gym for thirty some odd years before Benito Minorez bought it off of him, but he swore he'd outlast the the poorly taped ring ropes. The business, though he never actually made anything of himself, was more a part of him than his weakened knees. It was his life, and it would be until he had none left. “I know, but you know what I mean.” “Still don't mean we don't have work to do,” the old man said as he stretched his back and motioned toward the ring. “Unless none of what I taught you helped you out in that big match you had.” “It worked,” Ray said with a hearty guffaw. “Didn't win the match, but I did okay.” “Well then,” Cole surprised Ray with a pat on his chest, “let's work on them suplexes I told you about.” “You nearly threw out your back teaching me how to run the ropes, and you were walking,” Ray laughed. “How are you going to help me with the suplexes?” “I'm gonna get Jerry to help you.” Ray sat up, eager to have someone to actually struggle with, someone who was willing to fight back, but there was no one there. “Who?” Ray questioned with a look of confusion etched onto his other wise gargoyle-esque facade. “Jerry.” Cole points over toward a door. “He's locked up in the closet.” “Why the hell did you lock someone in the closet for,” Ray hopped up off the bench and rushed over to the door, but when he pulled it open there was no one there. “What the hell?” “In the box there,” Cole barely showed any emotion as he pointed to the far rear of the closet. “Be careful, he's heavy.” Lifting the flap of the box, Ray came face to face with a wooden head. “This is Jerry?” It took a bit of effort to pull the tackling dummy out of the box. Dust settled in the back of his throat and made him gag a bit, as he dragged it out of the closet. “May not seem like much, but it'll help you learn how to use your upper body strength to lift a person up when they've gone limp in your arms.” The puzzled look on Ray's face said it all, especially the way one eyebrow was cocked up in a high arch to lift the rest of his face on the same side. “Trust me,” Cole said with a smile. “Just get him in the ring and do what I tell you, you'll do fine.” ~{ o o }~ “Learning the ins and outs of this business are not going to happen overnight, so I don't expect to become a household name as big as Lia Ambrosi, Jay Terror, or Jordan White.” The duffel bag once again slung over his shoulder, Ray Holland makes his way over to his old beat up Chevy, a night's worth of 'learning the ropes' pulling his face down into a morbid look of exhaustion. “I'm going to take it slow, make sure that I am ready for anything and everything that gets tossed my way.” He takes one last look at the building, from out here looking like nothing more than an old abandoned warehouse. “Not because I have to, but because I need to.” He knows now what happens inside that building. “You can throw a chicken on a hot plate and make it dance...” Future's are made in that building. “...still doesn't make it Fred Astaire.” For once in his life, he's actually looking forward to the future. “And I want to be Fred Astaire.”
