And on, into the heart of darkness. All that stuff.

11 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 11, 2005 I am a man, so I make the same mistakes many men have made before--and add plenty of new ones to the list. I guess actually meeting up with this chick was today's addition. It was timed up well enough that there'd be no way to the manor by night fall, so I didn't let Trey in on how nice of a place it apparently was during the day. All gloom and doom in building up the hall for him, quite exact in describing the numerous monsters that lurk the grounds and draw the ire and blood of the locals during all hours of the day, and especially not just at night. Oh, no, no. To be safe, we had our Backpacks O' Anti-Shiftless Beasts, full of flashlights and flares and candy and soda. 'What the hell you gettin' us into?' 'Think Scooby-Doo, with pot.' He laughed. 'Alright, think Scooby-Doo. Either way, we've got ourselves a mystery to unravel.' 'An' yeah, that's cool, that's cool--but what the hell's this gotta do with findin' your boy?' I paused then and looked around the tavern she'd picked to meet at. The table she'd told me to be at the night before was empty, so I took my seat at it as I thought. 'Not a whole lot, yeah. It's the closest we've got to a plan though, and what the hell else are we going to be doing until Worldwide that's more fun than this?' 'There was those partie--' 'Fuck the parties, Trey, and think about the world for once. This is bigger than your entire life--career, cars, houses--and almost as big as mine, so it's not some little fuck-around matter. We've got a lead on the bastards responsible for every little chance occurrence that's happened since the dawn of man, and you want to let it go so what, so you can fuck some twerpy Japanese bitch?' 'She was Korean, man.' 'So fucking what? You get my point. We get the chance to change history, to fight against forces that lead man astray, and maybe find a way to get the Raiders another Superbowl victory.' 'What was that?' 'Personal agenda. These guys, they tally up our karma like some sort of Santa Clausian governing body. They know when you're sleeping, they know when you're awake, they know when you've been bad or good, so...' '...be good for goodness sake.' 'No, so we fucking strike back at them and get revenge for all the bad luck.' 'You believe all this shit?' 'Wholeheartedly.' 'Damn. Glad I listened to Rachel Leigh Cook back in the day.' 'I always used to masturbate to that commercial.' 'Quit ruinin' my childhood memories.' Yeah. That sort of miserable bullshit conversation arose to pass the time, and so did pints of lager that helped slip us further into the trenches of discussion. Our war was fully waged over Trey's almost completely fucked inability to figure out what the point of 'Fight Club' was when this chick showed up. I'd forgotten her name by then, if I'd even heard it yet, and she introduced herself as Andrea in an accent so alien I almost thought it was fake. She was old enough to be Trey's mom, probably, but held her age well. The only real sign were the deep creases running under her eyes and the broken Y's hanging sideways from her lips. Her auburn hair was unkempt and pulled back in a loose bundle that argued at the sides from where she fidgeted with her glasses every few moments, and she wore shades of khaki that I'd never really noticed the existence of. Skipping more boring dialogue; we decided on a route to take and she volunteered to drive, noting the shape Trey and I were in. It was a bad idea, yeah, but I'm quite sure I've already gone over my long relationship with bad ideas. I paid the bill and she followed me to the hotel so I could ditch my car and ride with her the rest of the way. Trey was piled into the back of her army green Wagoneer, so I loaded into the front. 'He gonna be okay?' 'Probably; he'll ride it off before we get there.' 'Good t' hear. So, do you know the folklore 'bout the area?' 'No clue.' 'Well, you interested?' 'Is there gonna be a dragon?' 'No.' 'Oh.' 'Well?' 'No.' She faked a laugh, so I went on to keep her from ditching me in a middle-of-nowhere that wasn't the middle-of-nowhere I'd been looking for. 'How'd you meet Sobias?' 'He was my lawyer.' 'Case?' 'I'd been arrested for possession in the early 90s when I was visitin' relatives. Won, got to come back home.' I plied for more stories and kept failing, so I chain-smoked cigarettes until I was as light-headed as I was drunk. She joined in this exercise, and it turned out to be a long drive. I was right in stuffing an unopened pack in my pocket before I'd left to meet her. Around my third Newport, she asked about past relationships. I told her about the fun one--the crazy sex and crazy living with this girl whose name had been clouded by a couple years of substance abuse. It was back when I first thought I was on top, and helped lead into quick bankruptcy. But, how many people can say they did cocaine off of a supermodel's pussy? I'm not one of 'em, sure, but she was definitely one of the ones you could stand to be around in bright lighting. I broke even, basically. Had a good laugh with Andrea about it, and she ashed her cigarette with a light tap I remember from Audrey Hepburn movies. It was a drunken comparison that existed just to bring a smile to my face. So, I expect a happy little story with no real point to it at that point, right? Yeah. Started off in Amsterdam, right after college and dead broke. She'd waitressed a few bars, since I'm damned tired of saying 'pub' or 'tavern' to refer to my drinkin' holes, and realized that they were no way to cover her living expenses at the time. I knew where it was going just like anyone would know where it was going, so I lit another cigarette to keep the pace. She followed along the same avenues of quick prostitution I'd expected, until she ended up dating the clientele. Clientele happened to be Mafioso, she tried to leave him, ended up spending five years on the run before they caught her and beat her half to death. There was something about her maybe having cut one of the guy's balls off, but I was getting lost in the darkness that we were driving through. I looked her over and thought my plans through. There was a moment of silence that I broke as casually as I could. 'My mom had sex with Mick Jagger.' 'Who hasn't?' 'I mean, me, and I don't think Trey has.' 'Just born in the wrong time, you were. Was in bed with everyone he saw for near on two decades.' I didn't regret how rapidly I'd drunk earlier, and used its incoherence to my advantage. 'I, that, no.' 'Just a joke. I never had a go with him, just out of my time.' 'How old are you?' '37, old enough that I might've been offended. Yourself?' Note now that I felt like an asshole for guessing her much older earlier. '24.' It was then that Trey's limp arm fell over my shoulder and he joined the conversation with a triumphant 'whoo!' that soon faded into gibbering that loses its charm translated--so fuck that. 'Sow long left?' 'Twenty minutes, give or take.' 'Sit aunted?' 'Maybe. Not many've seen it.' 'Oh. Ats good, I un like ghosts.' 'Don't worry, I'll smack him sober when we get there.' I lied and dealt with it for the rest of the quiet ride. I had no clue how I was going to get Trey in any condition to deal with the terrors that I assumed we'd have to face--namely cyclopses, minotaurs, little girls with black hair that move in stop motion, and slow invincible bastards that tote around gimmick weapons like knives or chainsaws. It was easier than assuming the horror that awaited us was Lovecraftian. No fucking way was I headed into an old decrepit manor that housed unspeakable dread or multi-tentacled worms from another dimension. Having worked fast-food that one summer, I'd had more than enough shapeless all-consuming blobs for one life. She dropped us off with one last warning, and I dragged Trey from the backseat. He fell hard on the mucky earth, a squishy plompf that came because I didn't want to carry him. It woke him up enough that a few kicks to the face would get through, so I went ahead with that plan. 'Son va bitch. Fuckin qui.' 'Get your sorry ass up. We're here.' 'Whuz ere?' 'Castle Greyskull!' Another lie I'd deal with in time. And of course, that time's almost come. We cut through the bushes and overgrowth, following a faint howl that Andrea had told to use as a beacon. The gray branches twisted like cobwebs, dotted with hooked barbs that almost gleamed in what moonlight filtered down. Trey was in front and waving his flashlight around. The light terminated a few feet ahead always, always into an inky blackness that threatened to consume us. The ground below was wet and now and then came sharp screeches and roars that amplified as they rushed through the tangle. And always came that wavering howl that led us onward, no longer faint as we worked closer in to it. It filled my being with an acceptance of fear that most likely came from the alcohol. It'd affected Trey in much the opposite way, leading him deeper into the catacombs of sheer panic than he'd ever been before. Or something like that. Trey stopped hard in front of me, broke my train of thought. 'Sommins ere.' 'What?' 'Iunno.' 'Well, check.' 'I gotta?' 'Yeah.' He nodded and grumbled, then pushed his way into a night that his flashlight seemed to have no effect on. There was no sign of him, so I spun my light around a few times and dove through the last barrier of nature between me and the clearing. Trey already took in the building as I was getting the speared barbs from my jackets and clothing, and I think he may've been right in surveying it first. When I looked up, my life stopped in awe of just how... big-and-dark it was. The howl had stopped, and I thought I saw a thin figure slip along the roof with its head cast down towards me. Once, I thought I saw the Great Gazoo. Light was a sought-after commodity, as the flashlight beams were useless in the dense fog, just glowing spots like the fires that lined the ground and stood stoic against the wind. Only the upper spires of the hall had any detail to them, a style I couldn't place as anything but an amalgam of every major architectural period I'd heard of So I did the obvious and dragged Trey back into the bushes to smoke the pre-whatever happens next blunt. It dragged him from his paranoia and lifted me from laughing at fear to flexing at it and calling it a puny bitch. We were ready, soldiers at war, and we marched blindly to the hall after we finished smoking. As luck would have it, we came to a grand set of double wooden doors and worked together to pull open one of them enough to slip inside. I got Trey to help me close it in some show of manners, and then took in the lovely entryway that waited for us. It was warm, hardwood that shined in the steady light of flames and intricate tapestries that hung down from a ceiling thirty feet above our heads. I advanced to get a better view of one, a man standing noble in something that was between a hooded robe and a toga. At the bottom read 'Snut Oxenhu, Employee of the Month, April 2005.' I shrugged and pointed it out to Trey. He laughed a vacant laugh as I noticed that all the panels had similar tributes; Winners of the 2004 Annual Softball Game, Good Luck in Accounting, and all those typical middle management dedications. Following them long enough lead me to the small door opposite the entrance, a thin wooden panel with a door knob decorated with muses. And well, here we are.
