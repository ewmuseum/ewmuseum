Checkmate: The Story of How Chris Bagwell Manipulated His Way to the Top

Date: Written: January 10, 2009 Preface Chess is an abstract strategy board game played on a square board of eight rows and eight columns. There are sixty four squares of two alternating colors, usually black and white. There are 32 pieces in all; sixteen of them are black, and sixteen are white. Each set of sixteen has six types of pieces: one King, one Queen, two Rooks, two Bishops, two Knights and eight Pawns. Chess pieces used for play are usually tall figurines. Pieces of the Staunton set are the standard design. Each piece in a chess set represents a sector of medieval life. Pawns represent peasants. At that time the peasants were considered unimportant to people in power, and hence Pawns are the weakest pieces in the game of chess. They are used primarily as bait to set up an aggressive endgame. Rooks (or Castles) represent home and sanctuary, a sort of protection (if you will) for the king. Knights represent soldiers who, at that time, were held in high esteem, but not as much as the church or the royal family. Bishops represent priests and are considered more valuable than Knights. Then there is the Queen. The Queen is the most powerful piece on the board, and the King is the one that must be protected at all costs; otherwise the game is lost. I’d never cared much for the game throughout my youth as I looked at it with a certain disdain. The game had carried with it a negative stigma. It had been recognized as the sport of the unpopular, un-athletic, and overachieving. It wasn’t until recently that I gave the game a second look and realized there was much more to it. The theory of the game extended significantly further than wooden board encompassed with plastic pieces and stretched into the minds of the players. It was a competition and a method of strategy ? strategy for survival. I looked at the game again with an open mind and had an epiphany. Chess wasn’t so different then the game of life. Every time one moves a piece on the board, they’re making a decision in which can ultimately affect the outcome of their game. Each move could very well be responsible for shortening the game or pushing it to last longer. Similar to life, chess is determined by a series of sacrifice. Although you can gradually progress forward without any confrontation, ultimately you’ll never be able to reach your goal without giving something up ? losing something, or someone for that matter. My epiphany came when I realized that my life had recently taken the form of a well-mastered game of chess. There were two minds in control, on either end, empowering either side forward where only one would ultimately prosper. At first I thought it may have merely been the alcohol getting into my head, creating these delusions in my mind. However, when I finally sat down and evaluated my career over the last year I was able to find the making of one of the greatest matches ever constructed. Chapter I: The Art of Under-estimation Any particular match can be won by simply eliminating your opponent’s king ? it’s called a checkmate. Once a checkmate is reached, the game is declared over without question. But in order for one to reach the king, they must battle through two layers and fifteen other “guardians.” I began my career with Global Championship Wrestling with no intent of doing much outside earning a paycheck. I started, like all the rest, at the bottom of the ladder and hadn’t the slightest ambition to waste the energy trying to climb up with the rest of the cluster-fuck. But as one might expect, my ego got the best of me. It empowered me to do something more with myself than just putting other, younger guys over. It built up a hunger inside me ? an appetite for the spotlight. At that time, I couldn’t figure out what spawned my sudden craving and even to this day I’m equally oblivious. But I wasn’t about to starve myself. Lines had already been drawn in Global Championship Wrestling and rules already established, so it was up to me to craft a newfangled strategy to weasel my way towards the top. I was na’ve to think that in my first appearance I’d be able to give a shout towards the top of the ladder and get exactly what I wanted. Ironically enough, it worked. After only two matches with Global Championship Wrestling, I was granted a shot at the then-World Champion, Vivica J. Valentine. I made my first move by putting myself out there in the open. I’d talked a big game and jumped two spaces instead of one. And now it was her turn to respond. They say the first move, in any game, is the most crucial move. With the first move, you’re given the opportunity to set the pace of the rest of the match; playing it either conservatively (which seems to be the trend among the experts) or playing it aggressively and leaving yourself open to a devastating counter from your opponent (the commonplace for most rookies). I had chosen the latter and without regret. I’d hoped to catch the rest of the establishment offg guard with my aggression only to startle the rest of the foundation. However, the result of my match with Vivica J. Valentine turned out to be quite the contrary. She’d defeated me, leaving me to look vulnerable and weak. My momentum right out of the gates came to a screeching halt and she’d virtually shattered every chance that I’d bounce back to win the ultimate prize? “or had she” I’d drawn Vivica J. Valentine out from her comfort zone and found out what made her tick. I opened her up to the rest of the wolves and, although I may not have walked out with my hand high that night, I was destined for something great. I’d sacrificed my reputation in the interest of implementing strategy. I was able to take all the hype that seemed to be spewing from me and make it all worthless and meaningless. I laid the foundation for the perfect master plan. People would now know to ignore any promises I’d make. They’d feel comfortable in assuming that I wasn’t much of a challenge nor a threat, but rather a bunch of hot air. With my first move, I pushed forward my greatest pawn ? under-estimation. For a short time, anyway, the rest of the world would begin to under-estimate Chris Bagwell. They wouldn’t see him coming and would leave open the possibility that I could rise to the surface at any moment. Of course, at this point in time, those ambitions were still far into the future. For the time being, I had to contemplate my next move. Chapter II: Pushing Forth Your Pawns There wasn’t a single medieval kingdom in history that was able to rise to power without the help of their pawns. Their pawns were responsible for the dirty work and keeping the kingdom afloat, while making their leader looking practically God-like in the way that they’d respect him (despite how he’d treat them). I knew near immediately that if there was any chance of me pile-driving through the rest of the roster that I’d have to have some help. At first, I was set on approaching some of the more established members of the roster and pitching to them an idea to join me in an alliance. But midway through a conversation with the first established member of the roster, I had a complete change of heart. I thought to myself, "Why on earth would any of these guys even consider listening to a guy they’ve never even heard of?" It was a completely moronic idea and could’ve just as easily backfired on me. If I convinced one of these guys to join me, I always ran the possibility of them doing to me what I’d end up doing to others ? having me plow through the undercard while they milk the reward. I wasn’t about to spoil my game on some other ego-manic. I was enough of one already. I changed my entire aim around. Instead of looking for guys who already had histories with the company, I looked for guys who were starting fresh and sure as hell didn’t have futures. I recall meeting Depraved in the locker room the night I lost my match to Vivica J. Valentine. We’d been bullshitting about our past and our intended futures within the company. If there was one thing I noticed right away, it was Depraved’s deprivation of confidence. He was skeptical of his own ability and whether or not he’d even fair well within the company. For him, it was an extremely sad story. But for me, it was like music to my ears. I capitalized on Depraved’s self consciousness. I reinforced his ideals that he wasn’t cut out to make it in this company alone. I played up his fear of the competition and in doing so convinced him that he’d be better off underneath my wing. The thought of an ally in an otherwise foreign world made his pulse quicken and escalated his eagerness to comply. It was at that moment that I captured Depraved’s attention and turned him from an ordinary superstar into a pathetic slave. A pawn, if you will. The first of my army had signed up and not without company. Depraved’s alliance with me came as a shock to many. We’d carried such different personas where it seemed out of place that we’d have ever even spoken. I wouldn’t say our alliance broke necks, but it certainly turned heads. Soon a second showed up with something to say. He’d been another part of our induction class into GCW and was beginning to realize the lack of success he’d achieved on his own. He called himself "Marvelous," but his performance was better described as "mediocre." It was only a week before the official round robin brackets were to be announced for a tournament by the name of Rampage when Mark Matthews came knocking at my door. He’d grown tired of being overlooked and was trying to find an easy escape. Matthews shared a lot of Depraved’s qualities. While seeming cocky because of his choice of alliteration, Matthews? level of self esteem was actually more on par with Rikki Roxx? win record. He’d befriended Depraved during their time in a previous organization and the two reconnected when they arrived in Global Championship Wrestling. Depraved took the liberty of sharing with Matthews all the empty promises I’d made him. As it turned out, it took very little manipulation for me to get Matthews on board as my second crony. I’ll admit that I wasn’t too enthusiastic about the guy’s talent or his little gimmick. But it wasn’t about any of that. It was about the bigger picture. The Rampage Tournament was to start in a week’s time and I had to make sure my pieces were set up in the best possible positions. There were twelve men entered in the tournament and I now had two of them following my orders. I was never a fan of math, but I sure loved the probability. If I include myself, it came out to one quarter of the tournament entries that were ultimately under my control. Matthews and Depraved had both acknowledged the fact that they’d gladly throw the match to me if it had come down to it. And with neither of them squaring off against each other, I had the chance to eliminate two additional threats in the first round alone. Some would consider these fairly good odds. And in fact, they were. However, when you’re greedy you always seem to want more. And that’s exactly what I had wanted. I wanted better odds. I wanted a sure-thing ? a shoe-in for the finals. That cued a guy by the name of Docta Z. But he’s a story for another time. I had other things I needed to worry about. Things like protecting the king? Chapter III: Protecting the King Despite having acquired Mark Matthews and Depraved to handle my dirty work, I was still lacking a major piece in the puzzle. You can’t win in chess by only dancing your pawns around the board. There comes a point in which you’ve got to bring in some heavy artillery, especially when the well-being of the leadership gets compromised. The first round of the Rampage tournament had come and gone and both Depraved and Mark Matthews let me down. While Mark Matthews didn’t lose, he also didn’t win. He merely advanced by default when his opponent didn’t show. I wanted more of an impact than just that. Although I was pissed off, I really had no right to be. They were only pawns and pawns are meant for sacrifice. If anything, they at least helped to wear down some of the second rounders. I needed heavy artillery. Was I afraid of losing? Not particularly. And Clyde’s on and off relationship with Digital Mortality didn’t help the situation any. He was in need of a true friend and I was absolutely prepared to be that person for Clyde. Or at least I’d let him think that. I took Clyde Fox under my wing and began to convince him that bad is actually good. I taught him to disrespect women and to cheat out his opponents and he slowly began to believe in all of it. Shit, I talked Clyde into taking advantage of Sally Ford. Certainly something she’d never forget. Why Clyde? Because Fox was on the verge of breaking out into a big contender in that division, with the right direction. With Clyde Fox under my wing, I managed to disqualify the possibility of having to deal with guys like Jacob McKail, Tessa Windsor, and Digital Mortality. With Windsor on the outskirts of a World Title shot, it made perfect sense for me to try and shatter those hopes early. Clyde Fox represented my Bishop; an often overlooked piece that can certainly make a tremendous impact in the game as you never do see them coming. They don’t walk in a straight line with everyone else and that tends to completely throw off their prey. Clyde Fox helped to establish my position in the main event. He cleared out the path and I marched right through behind him. He reminded people that Chris Bagwell wasn’t a guy to take lightly. He reminded people that this Chris Bagwell wasn’t the same Chris Bagwell you saw in his third GCW match against Vivica J Valentine. He reminded people that Chris Bagwell was a sick, twisted, manipulative son of a bitch. Chapter IX: The Docta’s Number My journey through GCW saw friendships being made and transforming into bitter rivalries. My story wouldn’t be a story without bringing to light the relationship with perhaps the undisputable single greatest rising star in GCW history, Zacharia Taylor. I met Zacharia Taylor before I really got involved in the wrestling business. With both of us being New Jersey natives, it isn’t too far-fetched to imagine that we’d cross paths one night where Taylor was faced with an encounter I knew all too much about. Taylor had been involved in a bar fight with a bunch of guys who had all too much to drink. He probably could’ve handled himself just fine, except for when the police arrived and found out that he wasn’t even able to legally drink. I stepped in to pull Taylor out of the scuffle and get him the fuck away before he ended up behind bars. My relationship from Taylor from that point on grew rapidly. I learned of his troubled past and his barely-making-it life story. But our growing friendship has no pertinence to this story ? the story of my journey to the top. That’s a story for another day. When I brought Zacharia Taylor into Global Championship Wrestling, it was in the understanding that he’d never been in the business a day before in his life. I was aware of his inexperience in the ring and I was certain that he’d be constantly looking to me for guidance. Zacharia’s development expanded at an unbelievable rate. Not before long, he’d captured the Television Championship and damn near swept the division. Taylor was the sole reason for me being able by bypass the entire bottom rungs on the ladder of success. Taylor launched me into the mid-card. As my Movement grew and my pawns were defeated, Taylor decided that he was being held back by my ever growing ego. He decided that his alliance with The Movement was freezing his personal development and thus he split from the group. In a move that seemed devastating for The Movement, Taylor propelled himself into a singles career and continued his domination in the Television division. He ultimately would go on to set the record for the longest reigning champion in company history, at one hundred and ten days. Despite losing the Television Championship title to Daria Ngozi, Zacharia continued to prosper. Not before long, Taylor had cemented his mark in the United States title scene and was well on his way to becoming a legend. So what’s Zacharia’s success have to do with Chris Bagwell? I’m self absorbed. I would never bother discussing something that has no direct affiliation with me or my career. And so, as you’d probably imagine, there’s been an underlying plan all along. I damn near saved Zacharia Taylor’s life that night in the bar. He was being intoxicated on drugs and alcohol alike. The guys he was in an argument with were the same guys who happened start on the offensive line on the Rutgers University football team (before they were a winning team, but regardless ? they were still big dudes). I gave Zacharia a career in wrestling. I was directly responsible for every aspect of his life being the way it was. So if anyone was to think, for one second, that I’d just let Zacharia Taylor walk away from me without cashing in on his success? Boy, were they wrong. Call it the secret of the century, if you will. Zacharia Taylor’s split with the Movement was one hundred percent planned and agreed upon. He didn’t do it for any self gratifying desire, he did it to help Chris Bagwell capture the ultimate prize. Think of what happened when Zacharia Taylor left The Movement? He marched through the rest of the Television Title division and clobbered each and every superstar sitting in the United States division. Zacharia Taylor opened up a world of possibility that wasn’t available to either of us when we were aligned. After my meeting with Seymour Almasy, I learned Caldera’s tricks and was enlightened to the fact that he’d never live to see the day when another group would surpass his Establishment. He’d hold that group back until the disbanded and were no longer a threat to his power. So we did just that. We staged a breakup in order to advance our personal careers. And all along Zacharia, my second bishop, has been laying the foundation out to take total control over the company. Together, we were never going to get anywhere in this company. Steven Caldera straight up said that he’d never allow a second group to come along and threaten his Establishment. I didn’t want to hear that shit. Zacharia befriended the men in the main event. He seeked advice from LeStatt Knight and Andy Murray. He befriended Tessa Windsor and Digital Mortality. And he’s occupied the undefeated Garbage Bag Johnny. My alliance with Zacharia has grown behind the scenes and has helped me to wipe away the threat of well over three quarters of the roster. Together, we’ve crippled the GCW and left the World Champion with nowhere to run. We pushed him right into a corner. Chapter X: Backed Him White Into a Corner Realizing that good things were never going to come by simply waiting, I decided it was time to take matters into my own hands. Thus I launched my "hospitalize a staff member every day" campaign. President Steven Caldera made the decision that his group of cronies was more important than everyone else in the company. I just capitalized on his opinion. Unfortunately for him, I hit him right where it hurts the most ? the wallet. Without a staff, Caldera cannot operate Global Championship Wrestling and without the GCW he loses his biggest money-maker. Caldera is greedy. He’s probably greedier than I am egotistical. And if there’s one thing I know how to do well, its manipulate people’s weaknesses. Realistically, though, it’s a win-win situation for Caldera. He hates his champion, Jordan White, and he thrives on his company to feed his wallet. He’s essentially killing two birds with one stone. Jordan White is the king of GCW. He can make only very limited moves and each one of them is extremely predictable. So that leaves the lingering question ? who am I? In the game of Chess, what piece represents Chris Bagwell? As one might imagine, the king would probably be the best description of me. The mastermind behind the entire army, the most prestigious piece on the board, the piece that everyone else is looking to protect. But I’m no king. I’ve backed the GCW into a corner. Through manipulation and corruption, I’ve been able to take exactly what I want and in perfect timing. I’ve defeated every superstar I’ve needed to defeat and my alliances have taken care of the rest. There’s only one move remaining that needs to happen. And what happens when you’ve backed the Jordan White, the king, into a corner? Checkmate.
