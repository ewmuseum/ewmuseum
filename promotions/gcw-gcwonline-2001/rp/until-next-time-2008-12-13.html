Until Next Time

13 December 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/13 In what we believe to be ourselves, We tend to be someone else. Why is it that every time I look in the mirror I have to turn away or obstruct the mirror in some way that I can no longer see myself? Why is it so hard to look at myself anymore? I feel like I am no longer myself, but I have no idea who I was, nor who I am now. My hands feel hungry and tense, but no matter how much I stretch them out, they are still on edge. My eyes are no longer soft and calm, but firey pools of anger and pain. My lips curl without notice, my face stretches into a smile. I no longer have control over my body. I m screaming for help, but the words never develop into audible words, or anything for that matter. I can stand in front of the mirror and tears pour from my eyes, yet I still stare with that grin, those fierce eyes? I need help? “from myself” “Sir, you can’t sleep here! Do you have [fades into mumbles]?” A police officer stands over Taylor’s ever so familiar park bench. It is truly surprising that they don’t know him by name, but then again, it has been months since he stayed on the bench at night. One would think he is homeless, being the dead of winter, near the holidays, and nowhere to stay? One could also wonder why someone with resources like Taylor’s would be staying on a park bench instead of in a bed every night. Yeah, one could wonder. Taylor stands up waving off the light from the police officer. Mid wave, his hands tense up and he feels his face curl up. Feeling the unstable emotion coming from within, Taylor jumps up and runs for it, trying to hold his fist as close to his chest as possible, protecting it, or is he protecting everyone else? The cold winter air streams over his face, easing up his tension, but making his muscles tense up out of bitter cold. As long as his muscles are too busy to react, the cold is welcomed. He passes the entrance to the park and walks to the corner. He stops and looks down at his fist. His fist looks more scared than ever, long veins raising his skin to the light, adding new angles for light to bounce off the scars. His focus racks to the ground where the curb is chipped way. The inner emotions stir again at the sight of the bullet’s aftermath. He looks up and sees two men standing in front of him. They are talking quickly and moving at a matching speed. The one pulls a gun but hands from the side of Taylor reach out and grab it, turn it around, and shoot the man in the leg. The man behind him pulls a gun out and shoots, but is hit by his falling comrade, putting the trajectory of he bullet to the curb. Blood pours from the man’s leg onto the ground in front of him, now in slow motion. Taylor locks his eyes onto the pouring blood. A car slides around the corner, screeching its tires on the dry pavement. Taylor snaps out of his trans and looks around, noticing that nobody is around, nor is there any blood. His hand tenses up again, so he repeatedly opens it up and squeezes it tight. He shakes his head and runs his left hand through his hair. Taylor steps off the curb and walks to the shop in front of him. He looks around as he walks in, noticing a cashier new to the store, the other one killed the last time Taylor came by. He wouldn’t be smiling at him if he knew what was playing through Taylor’s head like a movie projector against his eyes. He walks to the back of the store and grabs a bottle of water, then heads back to the front. “Will that be all, Mr. Taylor?” He quickly looks up from his hands counting money, surprised, almost scared of the man behind the counter. “I said, would that be all, Zacharia Taylor?” “Yes, it’How do you know my name?” The cashier becomes blurry, twisting and turning wildly until he stops, standing still once more. He is now bloody and sick looking. He is the clerk from the night of the incident. “Why did you have to kill me?” “Wha..Wha” What are you talking about?” “Why did you have to kill me after the other guy ran away?” “I didn’t kill you! I was knocked out, on the ground?” “No, you fell, the other guy ran, and then you got up yelling. You killed me in a fit of rage after you decided?” “I didn’t kill you! I was knocked out. I got up when the paramedics woke me up!” Taylor’s head starts to hurt. Visions of that night fly past his eyes. He wipes his eyes with his fists, but it only makes his head hurt more. His leg starts to throb where he was shot and his arm starts to kill where he as stabbed. Christian, in the mask, runs after knocking him out. Christian killed the clerk. Taylor hit Christian in their fight, Christian had the black eye when he came back. Christian said the masked guy killed the clerk, because he was dead when he got there. If Christian ran, and it was only Taylor knocked out, did Christian come back or stop to kill the clerk? ? I don’t know, did he, Taylor? Or did you stand up after being knocked out, and in a fit of rage of losing him, did you take it out on me?” “No, I would have remembered?” “So would I, I was the victim?” Taylor punches the counter. He looks down, surprised at his actions. Still looking down? “I DIDN’T KILL YOU! GET OUT OF MY HEAD!” “Sir, I’m going to have to ask you to leave or I’m calling the cops.” Tears falling from his eyes, Taylor looks up and stares at the petrified cashier behind the counter, the one that was there when he first walked into the store. He wipes his eyes dry, only to give way to more tears. He throws a ten on the counter and grabs the bottle of water and walks away. As he pushes he door open, the sound of the cash register opening and the flipping of the bill holders sounds. What is coming of me? I keep feeling this pent up anger, this inability to move on. This brutal, impervious rock in my stomach. I need to clear my head, I need to let loose. I need to move on from the past. It was a month ago, and all I can do is go back to those nights, those terrible nights. It seems much more of a fairytale worthy of the Grimm Brothers? sense of objective than the past events of my life. I need to fight this off, or I will truly become the demon I am trying to suppress. Taylor stands chugs the bottle of water and walks off down the street? “Flight to Oklahoma City for one please.” “That is $275 sir, any baggage.” The pun is unbearable to Taylor’s ears. He feels a laughter bursting at the seams of his mind, but he suppresses it, only letting a grin come to his face. He doesn’t even know what is causing the grin, but he doesn’t have much control over much of anything lately as it is. “No ma’am. Just the ticket please. ? Taylor lays out three crisp $100 bills and waits for his change. Newark Airport is very busy this time of year. Students coming and going, families getting ready for the holidays. It only makes these trips around the world more tedious and uncomfortable. But nothing as uncomfortable as seeing HIM walking into security, with his pissed off look and better-than-all attitude. He just looks on at HIM from a distance, trying not to draw attention to himself. The rock in his stomach starts to come to life again and his hands tense up. Without warning, his feet start to move towards Chris Bagwell. “Where the hell do you think you’re going?” “What are you doing here?” sounding almost scared. “That doesn’t answer my question, and it is irrelevant to you where I am and what I’m doing?” “Have you been following me?” “I’m always in your shadow, Zacharia.” Taylor turns around to see Christian Thomas behind him, wearing his pinstriped pants and black shirt. His face is calm but authoritative. “Tell me, Zacharia, were you going to go after Bagwell, in a public place where they take violence at the same level as they take terrorism” Hmm?” “I was” I didn’t?” “I know. You didn’t know what you were doing. You know, you can’t keep blaming what you do on not knowing. Sooner or later, they will know that you are full of shit.” Zacharia Taylor hits his head with his still ready fist, and looks over his shoulder. Chris Bagwell is down to his boxers and undershirt, still being checked by the airport security. Bagwell turns around, letting the guard use the handheld scanner. Upon turning around, he sees the girl behind him walk through, without the sensor going off. He winks at her and blows her a kiss. Repulsed, she walks away. “One day, you are going to let loose, and you will feel liberated. Trust me, it is so liberating. You know what I mean. Crushing another man’s skull into the ground or breaking the cartilage of their noses with your fist. You need to start living again, Zacharia.” Taylor turns around and looks at the smiling preacher. He smiles, but not knowing why. Christian returns as guilty as a smile can be. He blinks in what seems like slow motion while nodding his head. “Until next time, Christian.” Taylor walks away and walks to the terminal. “Last Call for First Class passengers. For the direct flight to Oklahoma City.” Chris Bagwell runs past Taylor, hitting shoulders. Bagwell, clearly in a rush to get onto the plane is holding his hands in one hand, and shoes and shirt in the other. He doesn’t even turn around to see who he ran into. What a bastard. Taylor takes his seat and looks at his ticket stub. Row 22, seat A. “At least it’s a window seat?”
