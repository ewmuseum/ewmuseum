A Silent Vigil

3 July 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/3/07 You remember that mid-nineties feud between Raven and Tommy Dreamer? Probably, it’s still pretty popular and with youtube we can see it every day. But remember that there was a part where Cactus Jack was on Dreamer’s side and then swerved him? He joined up with The Flock and feuded with Dreamer for awhile putting out some of the best segments of his career in the process. You remember that? I do. Very well in fact. And you can check it out on youtube. I recommend it. In the feud between Jack and Tommy Cactus kept trying to convince Dreamer to quit pro wrestling. He kept saying that it’s a dirty business and that Tommy was too clean cut for it. He’d walked out on Bischoff and WCW for Heyman and ECW and Cactus thought that was the worst idea in the world. “It’s too late for me, but not for you!” he would cry out. He’d say that eventually his life would all be Hell and he’d never know what to do with it. That he’d be wrought by regret. That it’ll take its toll on his body and mind and family and friends. I’d say a truer statement about professional wrestling has never been uttered before or since. Twizzy sits, stoned, in Washington Square Park. The park is known for representing New York University, taking a prominent and artful place in multiple movies and for having a lot of drug dealers who mostly sell marijuana. Davis is not high on pot though, he’s high on Twistodium. Of course he is. It’s warm out, high seventies, but Twizzy is dressed in an all black suit and carrying a somewhat wilted rose that he bought for nine dollars at a deli four blocks away on 8th Avenue. He’s staring at the empty fountain and thinking about a man he only knew slightly. He’d thought of going, but ended up not even booking a flight, to Chris Benoit’s family’s funeral. But had also decided not to go to Chris?. Twizzy had worked once with Chris Benoit when he’d wrestled under the name Wild Pegasus in Japan. The year, the winner and the promotion of the match aren’t recalled by Davis though. It was probably 1993, because Twizzy had left the States for a long amount of time in late 1992 for Japan. He’d had a fight with his coach and trainer Ricky Victory that drove him away from American wrestling for awhile. He and Chris hadn’t spoken much, just before and during the match. After it Benoit wouldn’t talk to Davis, because he botched a spot that gave Chris a large bruise. That’s all they ever interacted. It’s a business of many acquaintances. It’s free lance work when you’re working the road going from town to town for a few dollars to do what you love. And the sport itself is a dying art form. Like stand-up comedy or break dancing. It had its boom and it still has an audience, but is what’s left is so minimal it doesn’t even matter. Davis, as Trash, has worked with a lot of big names when they were green and ignored. Sometimes when he was green and ignored and then when he was experienced and ignored and even when he was a veteran and ignored. Davis discovered the news story on the internet and not from any of his many acquaintances. He did call Impact quickly to check on him and got his voice mail. Then he called Jonathan Cads and the two talked about the event for awhile before catching up and promising to get together soon. Then he finally called Ricky Victory and the two talked for a long time. “I just don’t get it. D-O-N-T,” Ricky had said, using one of his old gimmicks from when he was active to sound more charismatic. It ended up bleeding into his own life and now his son Nathan does it too. Nathan and Charlie work for their father in the wrestling school that trained Twizzy. “Never saw it coming.” Twizzy choked. “How’re Charlie and Nathan?” “Oh, they’re shaken up about it. Nathan more than Charlie. He always respected the technical guys.” Ricky took a long pause and then very seriously said, “We’re dropping like flies, Trash. F-L-I-E-S.” The conversation chilled Twizzy’s bones. He wanted to go to a funeral, but instead he went to Washington Square. He wasn’t sure why he called Impact, Jonathan and Ricky. It wasn’t to gossip or let them know about it or reminisce. None of them really knew Benoit. They ran in different circles. Maybe he just wanted to make sure none of them thought it was a good idea. He started to cry. Lightly his vision blurred with tears and then they rolled down his cheeks. One by one they came out of his ducts. You wouldn’t notice it unless you were right up next to him, but he didn’t wipe them away either. He just let them come and began to catch them with the rose in his hands. Two years ago Davis? cousin was found dead in a hotel room. His name was Ilan Levin and he wrestled for a few years under the name “The Jewserweight.” It wasn’t the most interesting gimmick, but he was very likable and was beginning to get a good amount of work having done well in the CWU before it closed for the third and final time. Ilan had accidentally overdosed on pain killers that had recently been prescribed for a knee injury sustained on the road. It was without a doubt an accident and got almost no press coverage, because of a very similar death involving Eddie Guerrero. Also Ilan was not known at all. He had been on television on three times. He was nobody. But Davis thought of him often. Davis wondered for a moment if he respected Chris Benoit and then finally decided that it didn’t matter. Whatever he had thought of him before he didn’t seem to now. It was as if Chris were an old friend from preschool that he had heard became a cocaine dealer. He was really a stranger even though they had met before. And therefore whatever he did was of matter to Twizzy. He popped another pill. He gulped it down and decided he should head to the gym. He had a match to prepare for and a possible encounter with LeStatt Knight to mull over. Maybe he would be able to get together with Jonathan Cads soon.
