Words of Inspiration from 'Old School'

26 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/26/2005 [The camera slowly fades in and we see a small, virtually empty bar at night. The bar is called Spanky's and it is the bar that Vince Jones seems to frequent whenever he needs to try and relax, one of his homes away from home. Some soft music can be heard playing from a jukebox at the far corner of the room. The soft buzzing of a few tvs mounted in the wall overtop of the bar can be heard as well breaking the silence. The camera switches to a close up of the bar countertop where a black, gloved hand can be seen reaching for a large, half-empty, mug of beer. All of a sudden a shadow falls on the countertop. The camera rises and catches sight of a middle-aged, African-American bartender with a slightly graying beard on his face.] Bartender: Ah, so you're back? Well, what's on your mind tonight, son? Vince Jones takes a large gulp from out of the beer mug in his hand and sets it down on the counter. Vince Jones: Change. The bartender looks down at him in confusion. Bartender: Change? Well, what do you mean by that? Feel me in there, son. Vince Jones just shakes his head and laughs to himself. Vince Jones: Aiight. Well, its like this, man. V been talkin' to some people... Bartender: You been talkin' with some people, eh? Talkin' with people can be good, but then again it can be bad, son. What you been talkin' about with these people. Vince Jones: Well, V. Jones' agent and Jasmine be talkin' 'bout how V. Jones oughta changes his ways and all. The bartender sets the beer mug down that he was shining up, folds his arms across his chest, and leans back against the counter. Bartender: Something tells me you didn't really give a damn when your agent told you that you might need to make a change about you. Am I right? Vince Jones nods his head in agreement and takes another sip from the beer mug of his. Bartender: But it finally hit home when this Jasmine told you so as well? Vince Jones pauses and sits there in silence for a moment. Vince Jones: Yeah. Maybe you're right on with that. Maybe you're wrong. The bartender smirks at Vince's reply. Bartender: Something tells me that I'm right on. Vince Jones: Think whateva you want, old school. Bartender: So, what kind of change were they talkin' about you makin', son? Mind feeling me in? Vince Jones: Well, they say that V oughta chill a bit. They think V. Jones too hung up on gettin' by that he been slippin' up lately in the wrestlin' game ova in G-C-Dubb. The bartender nods in agreement and begins stroking his graying beard. Bartender: Well, do you think that you should make this change? Do you think that its necessary? Vince Jones: V just don't know, man. He just don't know. Bartender: Well, if you ask me, I think you should do you. You've gotten yourself to this stage in life by operating by your own standards and with your own style, son. Do you know that there was once a people that was brought to this country that had their own way of doing things? Vince looks at him in confusion. Bartender: You heard me. There was once a group people that was brought to this country before it was the country we know today. They were a strong people. They were a determined people. They had their own style and their own way of doing things like I said. They could've changed their ways when people were tellin' them that they would never last in this new world if they clung to their unique ways of doin' things. Well, some of them did change while others of them continued to be unique. Vince Jones: Well, whateva happened to them cats? Did they make it in that place? The bartender shrugs his shoulders. Bartender: Hell if I can really tell you; but, they must've made it. Vince Jones looks up at the bartender in confusion. Vince Jones: And how you figga that, old school? Bartender: Well, I figure they must've made it because there are still people like you and me around to this day that prefer to do thinks our own way. Despite what people think we go down that other track, the more lonely one. You followin' me on this, son? Vince Jones: (in confusion) Kinda... Bartender: I mean, you've made it this far in your life. Why the sudden change now? You think that's really gonna help you out? Vince lowers his head in thought and shakes his head. Vince Jones: Don't really know. All V knows is that he desperate to do whateva to get out this slump he in right now. [An awkward silence falls between the two men and the bartender just shakes his head in disappointment of Vince's response.] Bartender: You younger people these days. I don't know what to say about you all. Always lookin' for pity and can never handle hard times. C'mon! Lift ya chin up, son! Shame on you for bein' so quick to be down on yourself. Everyone gotta go through the bad times, the losses. Vince Jones shakes his head and appears a bit frustrated by the bartender's remarks. Vince Jones: Yeah, but you don't understand, old school. You don't see this sh*t like V does. Bartender: You're right. I don't see this like you do. Vince Jones: Look, V ain't accustomed to this. V doesn't go through slumps like this. V. Jones sick and damn tired of losin'. He's ready to get back on the winning track once again. V ain't tryin' to lose to this Coyle O' Connor chump he facin' this week. Bartender: Well, you can't win 'em all, son. I mean you're basically complainin' because things aren't goin' your way. You wanted the sun to shine and it rained instead. You can't control the weather just like you can't control everything that's bound come your way in life. That ain't the way life goes. You gotta be knocked down on your ass a few times. You gotta go through the bad. That's what makes you better appreciate the good when it comes your way. Vince Jones: Yeah, but V was a winna. Bartender: A winner yes, but also an ungrateful one at that. Weren't you the winner that was quick to walk out on your last job when you were in Lights Out? Well, now you're currently a loser, but a more humbled one. And when you finally win again it'll feel that much sweeter. Vince Jones rolls his eyes at the bartender's statement. Vince Jones: V ain't a loser, old school! Bartender; Right, you're not a loser. You're just an underachiever at the moment. Well, let me tell you something. I wasn't always in the position I am right now. At one time I was down and out like you feel you are right now. I was out on the streets hustlin' and scrapin' to make ends meet. I barely had enough money to put food on the table, man. I was in a humbled state; but, I didn't give up. I picked myself and kept on truckin'. Sooner or later things finally turned around for me. [The bartender turns his back on the customer at the bar and begins shining up some of the mugs and straightening things up a bit around the area.] Bartender: Now I got two jobs, a wife... [The camera turns and catches sight of Vince Jones who is sitting there guzzling down some beer as he rolls his eyes.] Vince Jones: Yeah, yeah, yeah. V knows this sh*t already. You come home each night feelin? like sh*t! Yo feet be hurtin?, yo back be achin?, barely got anythin? left in yo damn tank to tap on yo wife’s phat ass. [The bartender nods his head in agreement.] Bartender: Ya damn right, boy! And it's all good. It's all good. [The bartender slowly turns around and looks at Vince in anger.] Vince Jones: Before you get angry 'bout that sh*t V ain't mean nothin' by that. He weren't tryin' to talk 'bout you ole girl like that. [The bartender sets the glass in his hand down, reaches in his pocket and whips out his wallet. He opens it up revealing a picture of his wife, a beautiful black woman with long, brown hair.] Bartender: Well, just incase you forgot that's mine, not yours, son. Vince Jones just shakes his head Vince Jones: Yeah. V knows this. The bartender smiles at Vince. Bartender: But, I'm sure there's someone out there for you, son. You shouldn't be hangin' out here. Go find her. She prolly lookin' for you as we speak. Vince Jones: But V gotta get to trainin' for his match 'gainst O'Connor. The bartender reaches out and rests a hand on Vince's shoulder. Bartender: Son, that can wait for now. You know what you need to do right about now. Now run along home, son. Vince reaches in his pocket for his wallet but the bartender shakes his head no. Bartender: Don't even worry about it, son. Beer's on me tonight. Now run along home. Vince downs the rest of his beer, rises to his feet, and turns to exit. He takes a few steps and suddenly turns back towards the bartender. Vince Jones: How you know so damn much, old school? Seems like you always got the right sh*t to say to V. Bartender: Comes with age, son. It all comes with age. I once was young and wild just like you. And I had my share of issues from time to time as well; but, enough about that. Good luck with your match against Coyle O'Connor this week. Now run along before I make you pay this tab, son! Vince Jones nods his head in agreement, turns his back on the bartender, and exits the bar as the camera slowly fades to black.
