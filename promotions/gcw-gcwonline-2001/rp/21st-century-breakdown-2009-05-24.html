Date: 5/24/09 .::Disclaimer- May Contain Strong Language and Violence::. .::Viewer Discretion Advised::. ---- Believe what you see from heroes and cons.... ---- Mechanical Rhetoric Self-discovery is a wonderful thing. If you do what you feel is right, there's a price to pay. Sometimes it's a steep one. Life's quirky like that, you know. When you do something, some people are going to like it, others will loathe it, and there isn't a fucking thing you can do about that but grin and bear it. You think the conditioning would have kicked in by now. That whipped dog mentality that either makes you cower like a bitch, or turns you mean. Fact of the matter is this. You don't actually know me. I've been here for two goddamn years now, give or take- not that I expect that to matter to you. Maybe I'll be here longer. Maybe I won't. You're going to have to live with that. What you see, right here on your television screen, up close and personal, screaming in your face, larger than life is an amped up version of some asshole who at one time may or may not have borne a passing resemblance to the real me. I'm never here, but then you knew that. Stupid people confuse me with that image, spread around the bullshit about me. Oh, I'm sure you've heard something in the buzz. I'm sure a whisper or two have reached your ears. Jackson? Yeah, he's a real douche, total asshole. Absolute whackjob. Fucking bat-shit. Those fucks earned my contempt. Ignorance isn't something I readily tolerate. Sometimes I lose sight of the fact that people these days are conditioned to be mindless, brainless, spineless sheep. Playing follow the douchetard leader. Sure. All the cool kids are doing it. This week it's a bit more intimate. Reach out and feel the heat of the bodies in the stands, screaming for blood. I'll probably indulge that request. Whichever identity I'm using, I say what I mean and mean what I say, period. If you thought different, you probably shouldn't be reading this without your parents present, or at least an interpreter who can bash the simple concepts into your tiny brain. There's no hidden agenda. I don't have time for that level of mind game subterfuge. I mean, seriously. How stupid are you people? Sometimes I forget basic human nature, and I hold these shallow pieces of shit that I am forced to work with up to that same insane level of expectation that I hold over my own head. It's disappointing, invariably. I am a mystery, some sort of fascinating enigma. I prefer to consider myself a private person. I don't thrive on the limelight; I don't hunger for automatic hero worship. I prefer to exist in the shadows, just doing what I do best. Don't dwell on that, move on to the next glittery piece of fluff. Do not place me on a pedestal because I will let you down. I'm not another fucktard hiding behind a mask with a Batman complex- gloomy Gothic bastards who trip the light fantastic with the deadly kiss of a razorblade and a mouthful of pretty words. That's not my scene. It never was. I thought you'd know that by now. Over the years, people have speculated on who I am - if I'm who they think I am; and if not, why not? They read between the lines of every last word I write. They listen to everything I say, looking for the secret meaning. Bust out the decoder ring, and pore over each and every syllable like I'm some sort of fucked up messiah. I'm a pariah- but I see how you inbred morons can mix that up. Big words. Lofty concepts. Whatever. Hammerstein Ballroom. God, it's been so fucking long since I've been inside the place. Last time I spilled so much blood the Red Cross was sending me Christmas cards for years. Ballroom. Let's dance, Friar. I don't know shit about you, but I know this. You're nothing special. You're just another hash mark in a column. Win. Loss. Doesn't matter, because you're nothing more than a victim. Fuck me running. You just don't get it yet, do you? To those who spent the time to consider the facts before blindly pigeonholing me, thank you. You are the true gems, and the few who managed to unravel the mystery. You got your answer. To the rest, I suggest you work harder. More than half of you are still wrong. If you have an issue with what I say then get over it or drop dead- I'm an equal opportunity guy, do whatever floats your boat. There are others you could be watching. I'm not here to make a point. I'm not here to be your portrait of rebellion. I'm not here to lead some imaginary revolution, or piss all over the legacy of this place. I'm here for one reason. I love to fight. I feel more alive when the prospect of death is breathing down my fucking neck. On some sick little level, I need this. I do. ---- Sunday, May 24, 2009 New York, NY The man sat in the dark, cold hands stuffed deep in the pockets of his sweatshirt. Silence poured out around him, the cool wind caressing his exposed skin. He hadn't bothered to don a jacket, hadn't bothered to change out of his sweat soaked t-shirt. Instead he'd pushed out into the night air with nothing but the crumpled pack of smokes in his back pocket and his battered Bad Religion hoodie. Muscles ached, burning, almost screaming for respite as he sat on the bench, watching the pedestrians move down the street. Even in the gathering night, the avenue was busy. The air was cooler tonight, bracing in its coldness. He didn't care- content to sit here and brood with a cigarette clamped between his lips. His sigh was ragged, filled with equal measures of frustration and weariness. "Fuck," he looked up at the sky, watching the clouds gather around the moon. "Maybe I should just walk away before I get myself in over my head again." He pressed his fingers against his temples, trying to rub away the tension. Headache was back. No surprise, there. He drew deeply on the cigarette, making the tip flare bright orange. For a moment it illuminated his face, lending an evil glow to his features, causing deep shadows around his eyes. "I need a fucking drink," he muttered, shaking his head as he flicked the cigarette away into the quickening wind. He watched while it bounced off into the darkness, sparks flying. Really he didn't, but right now, he didn't feel like resisting that pull. Like father, like son, and lately it had become easier to cope with that pleasant buzz with a few shots under his belt. The party stores were all closed at this hour, but the pubs were just getting rowdy. He didn't want to be in the public eye, watched by others. No, if he wanted it, he'd have to go back to the motel. Silence and floral patterned bed spread with cigarette burns. Classy. "This fucking sucks. I would laugh, if it weren't so fucking sad." The words were bitter, coming from between his lips in a gravelly monotone. He turned his face towards the sky, feeling the first raindrop as it broke free from the cloud cover, hitting his forehead with a cold splat. "Fucking figures," he growled, pushing up from his perch as the misty rain began to fall harder. He struck out towards the motel, stuffing his hands deep into his front pockets, hunching his shoulders against the wind and the rain. He disappeared from sight almost immediately, swallowed by the gloom. ---- She stood in the gloom, her eyes turned towards the sign that flickered and buzzed, the letters in the vacancy sign flicking on and off at random. The place was a dive, but then, this seemed to be the sort of place she'd find him. She was soaked to the skin, her floral patterned blouse sticking to her small frame rather unflatteringly. Her hair was saturated, hanging down her neck, sticking to her face as she looked at the line of doors. His car was here, parked in front of number fourteen. Illinois plates. She wondered why, after ditching that bitch, and Chicago, that he still hadn't bothered to change the registration. The dark paint reflected the glow of the broken sign, looking ominously like blood. She wrapped her arms around her middle, shivering as she rubbed the wet gooseflesh on her arms. This was probably a mistake, but ever since she saw his handwriting on that letter she'd found in the front pocket of her battered backpack, she had to see him. It was like a burning desire, like she was a junkie in need of a fix. Fucking sick, but she wanted to wad up that paper and stuff it down his throat. She raised her hand to knock on the door, and froze when she heard a voice from the shadows to her left. "He's not there," the man remained in the shadows, hunched slightly, a hood obscuring his features. "Who isn't?" she replied. "The guy you're looking for, nice piece of ass like yourself must be looking for that wrestler that's staying here." He chuckled, the sound dry and utterly devoid of humor. Her eyes narrowed, trying to determine the identity of the man in the shadows. Somehow his voice was familiar, despite the raspy baritone. "You know him?" "Maybe." A slight shrug, rise and fall of the shoulders, revealing the crossbuster that was embroidered on the front of the guy's zip up jacket. "Maybe I do and maybe I don't." She flicked her wet hair back from her face, grimacing. "Listen asshole, do you know where he is?" Her green eyes picked up the color of the neon sign, making them flash demonically. "Possibly, I may have seen him. Really, it depends on who's asking." "I'm Kitty." The man in the hood snickered, the sound filled with derision. "Kitty. Pussy. Close enough, huh? That your real name, or your stage name, Miss Kitty? If I tell him I saw you, is he gonna know who you are?" She snorted, very unladylike. "I should hope so, I'm his wife." The man in the shadows said nothing, instead stepping forward and lowering the hood. Her eyes widened as she recognized Jackson standing there, soaked to the skin. He held out his arms at his sides, palms up as he cleared his throat, letting his voice drop back into its normal register. "Well, you found me… what do you want, Kaitlynn?" She said nothing, simply staring at him, watching the water run down his cheeks from his soaked hair. He stared right back, his expression carefully controlled, other than the muscle that was flexing in his jaw as he slowly ground his back teeth together. Time seemed to grind to a halt, the rain falling now in slow motion around them, the drops elongating as they fell. He broke the spell, reaching into his pocket for the little blue plastic keychain with the motel key attached. He shouldered past her, fumbling with the lock, trying like hell to insert the key with a hand that was badly shaking, and not just from nerves. The key fell from his fingers, clanging against the worn wooden walkway. "Goddamn it," he ground out, between his clenched teeth. He bent to grab it, only to find it in her hand, inches from his face. "Spaz." It was her turn to push him aside, placing the key in the lock with her usual graceful panache before pushing the door open. Jackson stormed past her, already unzipping his wet jacket. He completely ignored her, pulling off his wet shirt next, letting both fall to the floor. His hand went to his belt buckle, and then he hesitated, glaring back over his shoulder. "For Chrissakes, get in here and close the goddamn door. I don't need the paparazzi sneaking a peek of my life." Paranoia reared its ugly head. She stood there, wet and miserable, half in the rain, half outside, her eyes fastened on him. With a low growl he crossed the room again, grabbing her hand and pulling her inside before slamming the door. She stood there in front of him, shivering, soaked and utterly beautiful. Seeing her like this, vulnerable, did nothing more than make him angry. He grabbed her arms, letting his fingers dig in to her pale flesh. He released her almost immediately, afraid of the sudden surge of emotions. "What the hell do you want from me? You want more money?" He pulled out his battered wallet from his back pocket, extricating several mutilated twenties. He threw these in her face, making her flinch. They fluttered to the floor, littering the carpet between them. He turned away from the accusing look in her eyes, crossing to the tiny bathroom. He returned a moment later with a towel in hand, running it briskly over his hair while he paced, the tension in the room mounting with each step. It was like watching a caged tiger, or some other deadly predator. "Why are you here?" he demanded, whirling around and pinning her with a malevolent glare. "Back to destroy my career again? Is that what this is about? Don't worry about it, sister… I'm already the laughingstock of this industry. Haven't you heard, I'm the Antichrist of wrestling." "No, Brad," her voice was soft, eyes downcast as she finally replied. "I just wanted to see you. That's all." She concluded lamely. His laughter was harsh as he threw the towel in her direction. "Just like old times, huh? I remember the old times, baby, a lot better than you do. I think I'll skip the trip down memory lane, if you don't mind." "Bastard," her voice was low, her chin trembling as she ran the towel down her arms, wiping away the icy rainwater. Tears spilled down her cheeks as she stood there, looking at his bare chest, the tattoos on his arms and shoulders, the muscles as they bunched and flexed as he jabbed a finger in her direction. "Give it up, bitch. Don't play this martyr trip with me. Can I call you bitch... or do you prefer whore?" He unzipped his suitcase with angry motions, pulling out a dry shirt. Something about the way she was looking at him made him uncomfortable, but still he didn't pull it over his head. She stood there, shoulders shaking as she tried not to break down in front of him, her eyes fastened on the floor. "Look at me," he murmured, flipping the shirt over his shoulder before taking a few steps towards her. Her eyes flicked up to his face, and then back to the floor. He advanced on her, and for every step closer he came; she backed up until her calves touched the side of the bed. He touched her arm, gently this time, feeling the icy skin. "You're freezing," he chided, taking the towel from her hand, wrapping it around her shoulders like a makeshift blanket. He ran his hands up and down her arms, feeling her stiffen even as her skin warmed with his touch. "Here," he plucked the shirt from his shoulder, and held it out towards her. "At least it's dry." She shook her head, her eyes still looking anywhere but at him. "Goddamn it," he snapped, dropping the shirt on the bed. He grabbed the front of her blouse, feeling the sodden silky fabric. It was like ice between his fingers. He grabbed the fabric in both hands, tearing the sides apart. The buttons popped and flew, and she folded her arms across her chest, trying to hide herself. He turned his back, a wry smile curving his lips. "I've seen you naked before, princess. Fine, if it makes you happy, I'm not looking." He rolled his eyes, sliding the tip of his thumb between his lips and nibbling on the edge of the nail. He nearly jumped out of his skin when she wrapped her arms around his waist, hugging him from behind. She sighed. He felt the exhale of warm air against his back, and it made his chest ache. His eyes drifted closed, a strange expression flitting across his face. "Don't. Do. This." he managed to choke out as her hands stroked across his bare stomach. He turned around, pulling her against his chest as his hands tangled in her wet hair. She looked up at him, tears still wet on her cheeks. "I already told you I can't do this. Not again. You and me, that's a doomed pile of bullshit-" She put a finger to his lips, "shhhh, don't talk, Bradley. For once, don't say anything…" She stood on her tiptoes, cradling his cheeks in her hands. She could feel the warmth of his skin, the bite of the stubble on his cheeks against her hands as she pulled him down, pressing her lips against his. She tasted like berries and mint, like she had the first time he'd kissed her in that Yakima, Washington dive with Steve Perry crooning on the jukebox. Fuck, she tasted good. He made a small sound of surrender, enfolding her in his arms as he explored the sweetness of her mouth. He kissed her neck, whispering in her ear. "I missed you." All coherent thoughts fled, along with his resistance. He stopped caring about anything, other than fragmented ghosts of the past. When you have nothing, anything familiar will do. ---- Mechanical Rhetoric Remember that if I haven't said something here, haven't said something in an interview, haven't said something to your face, then I won't say it behind your back. I don't play those petty games. Don't ask and you won't be ignored. Join the fan club, email my agent, hold up a banner at the next show. Do not mention my name. Do not insert yourself into my line of sight. Friar? Holy Hell… that about sums it up. You're a real piece of work. Friar. Greasy little fuck, playing at a religious figure? Or… not. Don't talk to me. Don't beg for mercy. It's unseemly. I'm not your hero, and I'm not your savior. I'm just a guy who knows how to fight. Guess you'll learn that truth soon enough. Yeah. You will. Can't wait. Should be fun.
