Moving On: Part IV

28 September 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/28/11 Everyone faces dilemmas in their lives. Often they face numerous ones. Aaron Fujita was due for one and boy did he get a whopper. All his life Aaron had loved weather. It was his primary passion and led him to attain his Bachelors Degree and re-enter school for his Masters. With just one year left before he got another expensive piece of paper, Aaron had a decision to make. Wrestling came into his life when he was a teenager and he had utilized it as a method to pay the bills through his first few years of school. Now it was more than that; he was making a damn good living doing it now. With a new contract on the table containing a healthy raise, he was looking at living the good life as long as his body held up. Weather still had a place in his heart, but wrestling was invading. After five long years of schooling and job hunting, Aaron finally got a called for an interview. He was faced with the tough decision of weather or wrestling. Sunday – 6 P.M. The days that led up to Aaron’s first interview in the weather field were not good ones. He got caught late in the office Friday night and Saturday morning got called in to instruct a review session for the freshman weather students when a colleague had a family emergency. He was leaving later than he had wanted to, but it was time for Aaron to head for Des Moines. He thanked the clerk at the Shell Station as he dropped four twenty dollar bills on the counter to cover his gas and a few snacks for the trip. The September air was unseasonably chilly, but he removed his jacket anyway when he got to the car. He had four hours of driving ahead of him and Aaron hated long car rides. It was nothing he couldn’t deal with if the right CDs were blasting from the speakers. His car roared to life with the turn of the key and the journey began. As he pulled from the gas station, Aaron reached into the grocery bag on the front seat, opened a Monster Energy drink and turned up the volume on his stereo. He was on his way with Three Days Grace reverberating off the car’s interior. “Oh that’s good,” was all he said after he took the first sip of his drink. Sunday – 10:30 P.M. Four and a half hours, two bags of chips, three bathroom stops and one speeding ticket after leaving DeKalb, Aaron exited Interstate 80 on the outskirts of Des Moines. He turned the radio down, drowning out the Rascal Flatts that played. He was drowsy from the drive but saw the elevated sign for the Days Inn his reservation was at and told himself he was on the homestretch. He thanked God when he caught all five stop lights between the off ramp and the hotel, unsure of whether or not he’d make it to take a leak. Once in the parking lot, Aaron climbed out of his car and took only a moment to stretch before stepping into the grass and unzipping his pants. He knew he wouldn’t make it inside. “Geez, I’m peeing a river,” he said to no one but the cool night. He finished and grabbed his few things from the backseat before heading to the lobby. Within ten minutes Aaron had his room key in hand and was opening the door to a less than modest single room on the second floor. He set his things alongside the bed and kicked off his shoes, ready to climb into bed. Before he could do that, he had something to take care of. Aaron grabbed his computer bag and sat down at the table along the window. Out of it he pulled a notebook and the envelope containing his new GCW contract. He slid a pen from the spiral binding of the notebook and began writing on a blank page. James, Here is my new contract. This is a little unorthodox, but I trust you to respect my wishes. The contract is signed and ready to go, but wait to file it until you hear from me again. I’m at a major crossroads in my life here; I’m writing this from a hotel in Des Moines, Iowa. I’ve got a big job interview tomorrow with a private forecasting firm and feel as though I have a very good shot at landing the job. I’ll be at the next World Wide regardless. You’ll hear from me soon. Best Regards, Aaron Fujita Aaron tore the letter from the notebook and slid it into the envelope with his contract. Satisfied, Aaron dug a pair of shorts and a cutoff t-shirt from his bag and got ready to climb into bed. Monday – 3 A.M. He had broken into a cold sweat. He was nervous. On the other side of the door was the review committee discussing his application for employment. Aaron desperately wanted to loosen his tie and unbutton his shirt, but he knew appearance was a huge part of an interview. He had been sitting alone in the office for nearly an hour now and wasn’t sure if that was a good thing or not. He fought urges to pull out his iPhone and play a few games to pass the time, but avoided the awkward moment that would occur when they exited the conference room. “What is taking so long?” he thought to himself. Finally the conference room door opened and the beautiful young Human Resources Coordinator peeked into the office. “We’re ready for you to come in Aaron,” she said with a smile. Aaron nodded, stood up and straightened his tie before heading for the door. Heather opened the door and allowed Aaron to walk by before entering herself and closing the door. Heather took her seat at a table alongside four others. They each appeared to be in their mid thirties and were clad in flashy suits. Aaron expected to see a chair for him to sit in across from the Board, but no such seat existed. He didn’t mind and stood before them proudly. It took a moment, but the silent air was finally filled with the voice of a man sitting behind the nameplate that read “Scott Lofton; Senior Forecaster.” “Aaron, there’s no doubt that you’re qualified for the position we have open,” he complimented. “Your score on the pre-interview proficiency test was the highest of all our applicants.” A small smirk came across Aaron’s face. “Your letters of recommendation from your instructors at NIU add an infinite amount of stock into your quest for this job,” the man apparently named Phil Reynolds stated. Aaron had a good feeling. The job had to be his. “But I have to admit, we expected you to come to your interview dressed a bit more, I don’t know, formal,” Scott said. Aaron felt his pride shrink. How could they think that? He felt like a woman that morning; he spent nearly an hour making sure his attire and physical appearance were impeccable. He ironed his shirt and slacks himself. He had even decided against the tie he thought looked like a weather map because it might have been “too flashy.” Aaron was destroyed by the comment. “I have to agree,” Phil added. “I am personally taken back by the fact that it seems like you’re wearing tights and do not have a shirt on.” A look of confusion appeared on Aaron’s face. When he looked down, his tie was absent along with his shirt. His toned chest was bare. His slacks had been replaced by his black wrestling tights. That’s when it hit him. Karina Wolfenden’s forearm. Aaron went down hard from the clothesline to the back of his head. She began laying boots onto his back, but Aaron was relentless. He wasn’t going to let the United States Champion show him up in front of the Board of Directors. He fought back to a knee, then to his feet. K-Wolf fired right hands and finally he was able to block one. He fired a forearm of his own that rocked her. She fired another right hand but Aaron blocked it once more before landing a sickening European Uppercut. The champion staggered and Aaron fired another. K-Wolf stumbled towards the Board’s desk and snatched Scott Lofton’s gold nameplate. She swung around and clocked Aaron in the skull that sent a gut-wrenching thud echoing throughout the conference room. Aaron groggily stumbled backwards and fell to the floor. “What the hell is going on?” Aaron thought to himself. Karina was right behind him, mounting him and dishing out vicious strikes to the head. Aaron tried to cover up, but there was nothing he could do. She stood, dragging Aaron back up with her. She fired a kick to his side that sent pain shooting through his kidney. Aaron clutched his side, but found himself on the receiving end of another kick. The pain was almost crippling now. K-Wolf raised her boot for another kick, but through instinct and testicular fortitude, Aaron caught her leg before it connected. He dropped it and reached forward, grabbing a handful of hair before firing a headbutt. It was the opening he needed. He planted a boot into her abdomen and spun her around to a belly to back position. In one fluid motion K-Wolf was picked into the air, twisted upside down and planted on her head with the QG-Theory piledriver. He wasn’t sure why, but instinctively he fell on top of the champion and hooked her leg. Scott Lofton jumped up from his seat and rushed over to the two competitors. He fell to the ground and began to slap the floor. His hand hit once and Aaron felt a tingling inside his stomach. Scott’s hand hit the floor the second time and Aaron felt lightheaded. When his hand hit the floor for the third time, butterflies completely took over Aaron’s senses. Scott got up and walked a few feet away where the United States Championship laid. He snatched it up and proceeded to hand it to Aaron before raising his hand. The HR Coordinator’s voice rang through the conference room. “Members of the Board of Directors; I present to you the NEW GCW United States Champion,” is what she said. Aaron couldn’t help it. Tears began to stream down his cheeks. Then there was nothing but darkness. Aaron sat straight up in his bed. Sweat poured from his forehead and chest. His breathing was labored and his throat was dry. He reached for the bottle of water alongside his bed and looked at the alarm clock. It was 3:30 in the morning. After he drank nearly the entire bottle he slowly settled himself back onto his pillow and tried to fall back asleep. His attempts were futile. Monday – 10:30 A.M. Aaron sat in one of six chairs that lined a wall in the office of Human Resources Coordinator Heather Brinkman’s office. She had been absent for nearly ten minutes now after leaving to retrieve the results of his pre-interview proficiency test. Aaron’s mind kept going back to the dream he couldn’t forget and it made him a nervous wreck. Every second that passed added to his fear that something had gone wrong. He was confident about his answers on the exam, but it couldn’t help him settle down. Finally the door to his left opened and Ms. Brinkman walked from Senior Forecaster Scott Lofton’s office. She approached her desk and filed something away before looking at Aaron. “Mr. Lofton is ready for you, Aaron,” she said through a smile. Aaron nodded, cleared his throat and stood. He took a deep breath, checked to make sure his shirt, slacks and tie were still in place and entered the office. If he called it “modest” he’d have lied. The office had numerous flat screen TVs hanging on a wall that were occupied by weather maps, graphs and data. On the opposite wall was the same set up, but these TVs displayed weather broadcasts from across the country. Aaron approached Mr. Lofton’s desk and extended his hand. “It’s nice to meet you Mr. Andrews,” the senior forecaster greeted. “I’m Scott Lofton; one of the senior members of our team here at The WX Experts.” When he heard his real last name spoken by Scott, it felt awkward. He was quick to shake it off and focus on the task at hand. “Nice to meet you as well Mr. Lofton,” he replied. “Please, call me Scott. There’s no need for that much formality here,” Scott requested. Aaron smiled a tiny bit and nodded. He liked the relaxed atmosphere this company had. Scott offered him a seat and Aaron accepted. The air remained silent for a few moments as Scott organized the papers on his desk and got situated. He picked up a pen and looked up to Aaron with a smile on his face. “So, Aaron, your resume shows you have little working experience in Meteorology,” Scott stated. “Would you care to explain that?” “I attained my Bachelors Degree a year and a half ago, but have been unable to find a full-time job in the field,” Aaron started. “I have done some consulting for local businesses back home. I’d love to stay local, but the pay I was getting from those jobs wasn’t enough to support myself. With the job search not going too well, I applied for Graduate School at Northern Illinois University and am looking to get my Masters degree.” The entire time Aaron spoke, Scott had scribbled things on a blank piece of legal paper. Aaron told himself it was standard procedure for an interview, but couldn’t help it when he wondered what was being written. “Very good,” Scott said. “You also gave me a working copy of your thesis entitled Assessing Risk: Spring Versus Fall Tornadoes; I do plan to read what you have so far, but would you care to give me a summary of what your research is about?” Aaron nodded. The butterflies were about to take over his body. This was his opening to prove to Scott he knew his stuff. Aaron knew his research front to back and assured himself that he would do fine. “As everyone in the field of Meteorology should know the primary severe weather and tornado season is the early spring,” he started. “The most intense storms usually occur in the plains from mid April to late May when the conditions are nearly ideal for intense storms to develop every day. However, the reciprocal to that is when the air masses shift again in reverse as summer changes to fall. My research investigates if this secondary severe weather season regularly produces storms that are equal or greater in intensity. By assessing the risk involved with the secondary season….” Aaron was pleased with himself. It seemed like his hard work for a passion had finally paid off. Monday – 11:45 A.M. “….so I alerted the race director of the impending storm’s potential impact to the area. He took my advice seriously and evacuated the fifteen hundred or so race fans in attendance ten minutes prior to the storm’s arrival. It turned out that golf-ball sized hail fell from the thunderstorm and a tornado was produced just ten miles from the track,” Aaron said confidently. He was glad he had faced that challenging situation. Scott seemed impressed with all of Aaron’s answers so far. “That’s a fantastic example Aaron,” Scott replied. “That concludes the question sheet I have. Let’s discuss the results of your proficiency test, shall we?” Nerves cluttered Aaron’s senses. “Your score wasn’t the highest I’ve seen for this particular job opening,” Scott stated. “But it was stil impressive.” Aaron’s stomach had dropped and skyrocketed in a matter of seconds. “I don’t know about you, but I’m starving,” Scott announced. “It took until my last interview for the position, but I think I’ve found the perfect candidate in you.” Aaron had a gut-wrenching feeling that he was about to startle awake in his hotel room bed once more. This couldn’t be happening; not to Aaron. But it was real. He looked for the right words, but he couldn’t muster anything more than: “Thank you, Sir.” Scott laughed a bit as he leaned back in his chair. “When can you start, Aaron?” Scott inquired. “My Masters degree will be complete in December,” Aaron stated. “After that I can move out to Des Moines and begin full time. You stated that your company had capabilities to teleconference and connect to your servers off-location, so I could work part or three-quarters time from the university until I finish up.” Scott leaned forward in his seat again, the smile gone from his face. “I don’t know if that will work,“ he said. “The position is open to be filled by a full-time employee immediately. We would really need someone to be in office within the next two weeks.” Aaron’s stomach plummeted. He had been so close to his dream. Now he reached for it, perched on the top of a building edge, as he fell to the ground. His mind frantically searched for convincing words he could express to Scott, but nothing came to him. “Attaining my Masters degree means a lot to me,” he said. “I of course would not be collecting a full salary over the next three months, but that’s something I can handle. Please, Scott, is there any way that this can be worked out?” Aaron almost pleaded. Scoot interlaced his fingers and looked to the documentation on his desk. After a few moments of silence and a deep breath he looked back to Aaron with disappointed eyes. “I’m afraid not,” he started. “If I were to bring you in part-time for three months, the Board of Directors would have me in a sling. There are five other candidates for the job and, while not as qualified as you are, are able to start within two weeks. I’m sorry, Aaron. The only thing I can do for you is keep your resume on file. If another position opens up in the next few months and something is able to be worked out, I’ll call you.” Aaron felt like he had finally hit the ground. His fingertips hand brushed his dream and now it seemed further away than it ever had before. “I understand,” was all he could say. Scott could see the disappointment on Aaron’s face and felt sympathy for him. “I’ll tell you, I’m rather disappointed that we can’t have you on our team. You’d be a damn fine member.” He said. “As a consolation prize, why don’t you let me take you out to lunch? It’s the least I can do for somebody who drove four and a half hours to be turned down due to a technicality.” Aaron agreed to the offer and stood up, dejected as expected. Scott told him to head to the main lobby where he’d meet up with him so they could leave for lunch. Aaron did and stepped outside into the cool, brisk Iowa air. He pulled his phone from his pocket and scrolled through a list of contacts before pressing the “call” button on his phone. It was obvious that he got somebody’s voicemail. “James; its Aaron Fujita. Listen, I sent the contract off this morning with a note inside of it. Ignore the note. I’m ready for the extension,” he said. He replayed the message back to himself a few times and struggled with his emotions before pressing a button. From the phone, a digital voice projected itself. “Message erased. No message will be delivered.” Aaron sighed and closed his phone. A hand slapped him on the shoulder that startled him. “Ready?” Scott asked. Aaron nodded. It was a lie. Aaron wasn’t ready for anything anymore.
