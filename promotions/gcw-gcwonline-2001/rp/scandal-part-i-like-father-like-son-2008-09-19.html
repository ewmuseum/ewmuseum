Scandal Part I: Like Father, Like Son.

19 September 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 19/09/2008 Jacob McKail bolt upright in bed, his face drenched with sweat and his eyes heavy with exhaustion. He glanced across at the ridiculously basic digital clock resting upon the shocking motel room bedside cabinet and sighed. It was five after three. “Fuck...” he muttered to himself. Another damn nightmare. About her. His late wife. Mary. She was calling to him and no matter how he tried to get to her, she was always out of reach. There’s probably some sort of poignant metaphor embedded within somewhere, but he didn’t much care to look for it. He much preferred to forget it and try to numb the pain all at the same time. He reached down for the half bottle of Jack Daniels he knew he had resting by his bed and after a while of helplessly patting the side of his bed at the sticky carpet, he realised the damned thing wasn’t where he’d left it, so shifted his position and peered over the side of the mattress to see just where in the hell it was. There was nothing there. “Motherfucker...” he cursed, before readjusting his position and sitting upright in his bed. He was busying himself mentally retracing his steps from the night previously, when he saw movement out of the corner of his eye. “What the fuck...”” “Here,” the voice told him, “you looking for this?” With a sigh McKail, leant over and fumbled with the light switch of his bedside lamp until the damn thing came on. The voice belonged a well dressed man, in his late fifties, early sixties, who sat on the worn motel-issue chair just by the foot of the bed, positioned in between the television and the bathroom. He held up the bottle of Jack Daniels for him to see and tossed to him. McKail caught it and placed on the bedside cabinet, by the digital clock as his eyes flicked toward the motel room door, where stood the elderly gentleman’s lackey. Six foot of pure muscle, no neck and no brains. He wore an old-styled cap that covered most of his face. The idiot looked like some sort of Irish immigrant looking to work the docks for whiskey money. McKail shook his head and reached for his cigarettes and lighter resting upon the digital alarm clock. He’d already decided these two clowns weren’t worth worrying himself over, the only question that remained was whether he’d kick their ass out now or endure whatever bullshit they had to spout before leaving on their own accord, no doubt expecting him to be scared or whatever else their intended goals of this little “meeting of the minds” proved to be. He’d been through all of this before; being in his profession, having your face on broadcast worldwide every now and then and living the self-deprecating lifestyle that’d seemingly adopted him saw to that. Different idiots, different agenda but the same moronic approach. McKail would love these shithouses for their predictability, if he didn’t hate them so much. Lazily, he leant back against the headboard, placed a cigarette in his mouth and lit it. Fuck “em, he decided. Let them get on with this and get the hell out of here. Sooner they’re gone, the sooner you can get good and numb with the rest of the Jack Daniels.. “Yer just like yer father,” the elderly man began, nodding and producing a knowing smile, “yer know that, boy?” McKail exhaled smoke in the general direction of the elderly man, then shook his head. “Wouldn’t know.” The elderly man nodded. “That sounds about right. Alfie never was the responsible type.” McKail chuckled to himself and the elderly echoed his amusement. Done enough homework to know that he didn’t know who his parents were. No big thing, that kind of information could be found on any internet dirt sheet in circulation. It was angle. A way to get around McKail to get him to behave, to play by their rules. Fuck that. “You know he was a professional wrestler too?” The elderly man questioned. “Yeah, we toured with da old MWF back in da sixties and seventies. He was my tag partner. That man always had my back and let me tell ya, we used ta get into a lot of trouble in dem days.” McKail rolled his eyes. “Cut the bullshit, grandpa.” The elderly man’s expression changed from jovial to pissed off in the blink of an eye and his gaze almost burned a whole in McKail’s damned face. Either the old fool was cut up about McKail seeing right through his farcical story, or he wasn’t used to being spoken to like that. But whatever the hell his problem was, McKail didn’t really give a fuck. The elderly gentleman remained quiet for a while, before shooting his assistant with a less than subtle side glance which was probably a code or a signal for the hired muscle to get his ass menacing or some shit. But he didn’t. Didn’t even move. He just stood by the door, leaning against the frame with his hat still drawn over his face. Finally, the elderly man nodded and his demeanour changed within the blink of an eye. “Seems we’ve gotten off to da wrong foot. My name is Howson and my employers want ya to do a favour fer us.” McKail shook his head. “Fuck, no.” Howson smiled. “Sorry. That came out wrong. What I meant ta say was my employers have a business proposition fer ya. A one time deal. Very lucrative. Low risk.” “What the fuck is this, Reservoir Dogs?” McKail responded. “Fuck you and your employers. And fuck off too.” Howson’s face grew grim. “My employers aren’t the sort of people yer simply tell to “fuck off” yer ignorant little shit. Now, ya will do what we ask of ya and earn yerrself a whole heap of money you that’d buy ya a year’s supply of bourbon or ya will be dealt with, is that clear?” McKail rolled his eyes and blew another lungful of cigarette smoke at the old man. Howson shook his head, turned to the muscle at the door and nodded in McKail’s direction. The muscle at the door nodded, understanding and then promptly removed his trench coat and hat and began cracking his knuckles. Howson shook his head, disappointed. “Why yer makin” me do this, boy?” McKail shrugged. “Making you do what?” “Shouldn’t have asked that, punk,” Muscles told him, drawing nearer. “It doesn’t have ta be this way, boy,” Howson told him. Muscles lunged forward, trying to land a punch, but McKail reacted first, kicking the heavy-set man in the groin sternly, forcing the man to abandon the follow-through of the punch and cradle his injured jewels immediately. Muscles cursed under his breath, but the curses quickly turned to agonising screams as soon as McKail jabbed the lit end of his cigarette into the man’s eye. Howson watched on with interest, a little scared, a little impressed by what he saw, but never once moving an inch, whilst Muscles slumped himself down on the edge of the bed and desperately brushed the ash out of his eye. “Somebody call a goddamned ambulance for christ’s sake!” McKail chuckled to himself, whilst extracting another cigarette from the packet and lighting it without missing a beat. He took a drag of the cigarette and glared at Howson pointedly. “Now fuck off.” “McKail, what we’re asking fer ainn’t major, it ain’t gonna ruin your career and if it does, I don’t care! You ain’t got a choice in this! Next week, yer booked in a house show in Milwaukee; yer gonna drop the match and yer going to do it convincingly, ya hear me ya little shit?” Howson screamed, his face red and veins making themselves known just to the left of his forehead. It took him a couple of seconds to calm and once he did, his voice had reverted back to what it was previous, soft and calm. Too soft and calm. “Yer yer father’s boy an” that’s why you’ll do this. He was a drunk an? a loser too. Only difference between the two of ya is, yer ain’t hit rock bottom yet. Yer father did an? he didn’t get over it.” “Quit with the father angle,” McKail told him. “It’s getting real old, real fast.” Howson removed himself from the chair and pushed the rising Muscle out of the motel room door and onto the parking lot. He stopped at the door, manufactured a wry smile and shook his head. “It ain’t no angle kid. I did know him back in tha day. Why d’ya think we chose you fer this little venture” Like father, like son.” McKail shook his head. “Motherfucker...” Howson chuckled to himself and left, shutting the motel room door behind him as he went.
