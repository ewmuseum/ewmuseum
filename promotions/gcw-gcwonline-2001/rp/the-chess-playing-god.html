The Chess Playing God

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Rampage Prologue The thick air of New York City lingers in the contaminated membrane of this world. The steel bus promoting Global Championship Wrestling's Rampage tournament cuts through the thick air over the Brooklyn Bridge. Most of the passengers take no notice of the city skylines disappearing and new ones reappearing, but a few tourists stare and point like children in a motion picture advertisement of an amusement park. A man with his hood up at the center of the bus emulates someone asleep, but is fully awake. "In the short years I have spent in this world, I have seen more than most have experienced in a full lifetime. Many ponder what will come of them in the afterlife. Some are conscious of decisions they make, trying to be a good candidate for Heaven, while others feel there is nothing beyond this life. In truth, I know where I stand, and I know God already has a plan for all of us before we even have the capacity to know what morals are..." The bus slowly comes to a screeching halt after pushing over a pothole that would do damage to a car at normal height. The hooded man steps out with two gym bags over his shoulder onto the sidewalk. He looks up at the street sign: Washington Ave. "I saw God the other day buying a Marlboro Lights 100 off of a man dressed pretty suave with his beautiful date. He asked me for a light, but I had to tell Him I didn't smoke, but of course He knew this already. I watched him light His less deadly cancer stick with a wave of His hand, then take a puff. Later, I reluctantly passed Him playing a game of chess in the park against an older gentleman with the same relaxing aid in His mouth. It twitched when He spoke, 'Checkmate.' The crowd was in awe as the Master exaggerated wiping fake sweat off of His brow. 'See, I would have had you in four, but I mistakenly let go of my Knight.' " The man steps up to the Brown Memorial Baptist Church and pushes the heavy wooden doors to enter. The soft sun setting orange casts a welcoming light into the main lobby of the church and is then whisked away when the door slams behind him. "Nobody understands that we are living in HIS world as HE wishes. Whether He has control over our actions or not, where the path ends is of his choosing. I have crossed these paths and ended them or have even given them a new direction by His will. No matter what angle you look at it, God is the guy taking you for your money at the park in a game of Chess. You don't know it yet, but He has a plan, and we are the pawns." ------------------------------------------ The heavy wooden door closes behind Zacharia Taylor. The priest at the front of the church giving service stutters in the middle of his blessings and many faces break out of the trance of prayer to see the latecomer. Zacharia ignores the stares and walks to the left most isle and heads for the front. All bodies he passes take a moment to look up at him and study what caused the interruption. Several children come out of prayer to excitedly watch a GCW Superstar walk through their church. He finally makes it to the front where a stained glass image of Jesus Christ on his knees praying stands out next to the side exit. He takes in the glorious image of God's Son desperately trying to speak to his father. Jesus, the man at the age of twenty six, carried all the burdens of the world with a cross to his death. Zacharia distorts his face from a look of intrigue, to mild curiosity, than shame. After everything Father Emanuel taught him, whether a mad man's interpretation or words from God, the image of His Son still strikes him as a man of fiction or at least misjudgment as a savior. Disregarding the shushing gestures and comments, Zacharia flings open the door and walks through. The priest stops speaking as Zacharia's bags hit into the door. Silence is restored around him once the door comes to a slamming closure. The musty stairwell is disturbed by the uncommon visitor. Dust. Small dust particles freely swim through the angled beam of light from a covered window in the far corner. A piece of faded brown paper is pealed back off the corner of the window, allowing the small beam of light. Zacharia looks both up and down before deciding to step over the small linked chain crossing the stairs to go up. He tightens the bags on his shoulder as he heads up the narrow steps. He walks with uncertainty, but an assured uncertainty. After rounding the platform and climbing up ten more steps, he makes it to a landing which seems to be the top floor of the church. He takes a few steps forward but stops mid step. He turns to the dark wooden door to his right and reaches out for the door nob, which opens without him touching... Upon opening the door, the room becomes mystified and the walls stand with an unsure structure Zacharia's outreached hand becomes less scarred and softer. The bags disappear off his shoulder and his hoodie becomes much more torn and dirty looking. The atmosphere is much brighter as everything settles from memory. A man, kneeling over a mahogany table with his hands in the same position as the stained glass Jesus, doesn't even flinch as the door closes behind Zacharia. The eager expression... "I am sorry, sir, I did not know another...." Zacharia stops abruptly as the praying man hushes him. Zacharia takes cue and takes a step back, attempting to make himself invisible to the room. He tightens his eyes on the man before him. He struggles to understand the texture of his arms. Tattoo wouldn't be the right word for it, but scarred doesn't seem to define the artistic work either. Scriptures and crosses are imprinted into his skin in one way or another. Zacharia notices the cross dangling from his neck, similar to his own, but wooden and ancient looking. By the look of the man, one would think he was in a cloak and some kind of die hard worshiper of God, but stereotypes don't hold, especially when it comes to the life of Zacharia Taylor. "In nomeni patri et fili spiritus sancti. Now," the man looks over at Zacharia, his blue eyes penetrating, but spiritually healing at the same time, "who do I owe the pleasure of speaking to today?" Zacharia, taken back by the warm reception, takes an assertive step forward and reaches out his cross scarred hand in greetings. "My name is Zacharia Taylor. I'm sorry, but do I know you? You look oddly familiar, like I have met you somewhere before." "I assure you, brother Zacharia, we have never met before, but possibly another time, another place." The man stands up with grace, revealing the garb to be a religious robe and not a cloak. He stands much taller than Zacharia and has long brown hair. "Please, come and sit, and tell me, what brings you here?" "I guess you could say curiosity, among other things, but I was told that a friend of mine lives here." Zacharia digs through his pockets for a piece of torn away notebook paper with an address on it. "My friend Chris Bagwell lives here, well, I suppose not if you are here in his stead." The man looks at the address and smiles. His gentle features make Zacharia much less tense and comfortable, even though he has entered the wrong apartment room. "Brother Zacharia, you are in the wrong building. This is the Canterberry Apartment complex, not the Washington Heights complex. A simply fixed mistake that means no harm, just a bit of a walk across extensive parking." "Are you, a priest, sir?" Zacharia looks at him puzzled and then focuses on his arms once again. The images are very clear, but look more like scars than anything. There is a line of a scripture that looks like bare cut skin, glistening as his arms move against the intruding light from the windows. "I am a religious man myself, but you are unlike any priest I have ever seen." The man smirks and walks over to another mahogany table, but this one is much bigger and broader. An assortment of wines are contained in decorative crystal with wine glasses to match. He pours out each wine into a different cup, places them on a golden tray, and walks over to the low table he was praying over. He motions for Taylor to take a seat. "Religion is a lot like wine. Before you, there are several different wines from around the country. Picture these as the main religions of the world. Each one smells, tastes, and even looks different, but they are all made from grapes. In other words, all these religions may look and feel different, but they all have the same general ingredient, which is not grapes of course, but a Higher Power that we would call God." Zacharia looks at him with intense curiosity while lifting up one of the closer glasses; the darkest. He starts to twirl it around. It is a full bodied wine, very robust, and does not taste good at all on its own. Not a wine connoisseur, the wine would possibly go with a meat in his guess. "Your religion, Brother Zacharia, has a strong representation to you, more so than any other religion you would come across. It may be misunderstood or feared as radical and liberal, but it may not seem that way to you. Only you can answer that question, but the wine you have fits everything about you perfectly. Blood, sacrifice, and sin follow you around wherever you go. I understand this, my brother, because I too look into that wine and see myself." Zacharia gets lost in the wine, staring at the twirling wine. Blood. Sacrifice. That is all he knows anymore. The Modern Testament is the blood wine he holds in his hands, shared only by the selected few. "Brother, I can see you are confused. What your priest tells you, what you carry out and spread is no different than what the Hindus or Buddhists or even Catholics spread. Every man deserves a chance at peace, and you are doing divine work to keep it that way. Sometimes, you are hated and you do not know why. But I will tell you this, brother, One of God's most trusted and important angels, Brother Azrael, did the work you do, yet was still regarded as an angel." Zacharia snaps out of his trance and looks into the man's eyes. His eyes seem so much older than the man before him whom is around the age of 26. "Who are you, exactly?" "Exactly? My name is Jude Cutter. I am no priest, but I am a preacher of the word of God. I try to convey the words of our Father to the rest of the world, but times are much harder than what they were in the Bible." "How do you know so much about me?" Zacharia puts the wine down and stands up, not realizing he sat down to begin with. "You know about my religion..." "Brother Zacharia, I know a lot more than you would expect. I know more than even I have come to realize. I look at someone, deep into their eyes, and it is as if I can read their soul. I know you are a lot deeper than you put yourself out there to be. You are in a struggle of moral code due to what your priest and religion asks of you. I understand this, and I think I can help you." The walls crumble out of memory and reassemble to an open room with covered windows in Brown. A large dusty mahogany cross hangs in the center of the back wall. The room has many fine particles floating through, causing Zacharia to scrunch up his nose in resistance to sneezing. He reaches out for a light switch, but nothing can be found. He decides to pull down one of the faded brown papers over the window to cast more light into the room. Once half of the front window falls the room becomes mostly illuminated, but casts a long, dark shadow in the far left corner, where someone seems to be sitting. Zacharia walks over closer when a familiar voice mumbles out of the corner. "Brother Zacharia...." He stops dead in his tracks and stares harshly into the shadow, only seeing the whites of someone's eyes. An odd scraping sound comes from the corner. He squints his eyes and tightens his ears to focus on the image and sound. He comes to the conclusion that the image will not get any more clear, but the sound turns into more of a tearing sound than anything. "Tell me, Brother Zacharia, what brings you to the brilliant Brown Memorial Baptist Church? I hope it has something to do with your religion, and not that God awful entertainment ring you are part of, because that would be truly disappointing to hear." The voice is crisp and familiar. "Brother Cutter, why am I not surprised to see you?" "Well, surprise would not be something I tend to try to do, you know this. I look at it as more of a fortune of fate, once again." "From the man that told me there is no fate, but..." "...but a divine plan. Precisely. I am glad that after six years, you still remember our small conversations. Brother Zacharia, I come to you for I know you are facing mass confusion, once more. I want to help you, like I have before, but this is more important. You have killed many more since the last we spoke, and I am not surprised, but possibly intrigued." Zacharia drops his bags to the floor and takes three more steps forward and then rips down the crisp brown paper from the next window. This one comes off completely and the center disintegrates on the hardwood floor. Jude Cutter, sitting in the corner, in a dark jacket and black pants sits with a revealed arm and knife, cutting into himself in precise movements. "I see you still carve your scriptures in your skin..." "You know it is more about the blood than the scar that is left behind, Brother. The blood is sin released from the body, replaced with the image of our Father." "Wasn't enough of your blood spilt last time I saw you?" "Never enough. Never will there be enough blood spilt for this world to be relieved of sin. Not until the Devil gives up on his games and God stops changing the rules on each of his turns. Remember Brother Zacharia, This world is a well played out game in the dark where nobody else knows the rules except God, and that is why there is a divine plan." Zacharia sits down in front of Cutter and crosses his legs. "So, Brother Cutter, what was it like to die?" Jude Cutter's wry smile seems to freeze in time while the rest of the world around Zacharia and himself change to a street setting. Long shadows are cast from the wholesome full moon above them. The world seems to be retreating from it in soul as the shadows all recede from the blueish light cast around them. Zacharia's scars fade and his hands grow much less weathered. Jude Cutter's face once again becomes animated as the smile fades. "Death is just another part of the cycle of life and the divine plan He has for us. The souls you condemn to an untimely death are not as untimely as you may think, or your priest thinks. God has a plan for every soul, and there is no divine intervention to cut the path short, just another soul being taken at a specific, well played out time. You were in His eyes before you were even born, Brother Zacharia. This is no mistake that you are sitting here with the divine order to end evil lives." Zacharia stiffens up and kicks a rock off of the apartment's steps. He rushes his hands through his greasy hair and takes a deep breath. He raises his hand to his face and moves it around in the moonlight, showing his scars on his knuckles and fingers. "Cutter, what if we are wrong. What if what I am doing is truly evil work and it is for the Devil and not God? Is that not an easy misconception? I mean, fifty-fifty. There is room for error on our part." "If I am being told these things by the Devil instead of God, then we are truly doing evil, but we are only doing what we know, and what we are told. So if we do only what we know, and what we know is what we think to be right, who is to say that it is one thing or another? As long as we know we are just, and we are being told from a Divine Being, does it matter? Either way, we will live then die and what happens to us is in God's hands more so than our own." Zacharia looks confused and closes his eyes as if trying to picture the words being told to him, being able to make more sense of it all. "Perception, Brother Zacharia. Perception! Everything is relative to the person whom is looking at it. Hold out your hand." Zacharia reaches out, revealing his scarred hand with the cross. "Now, from your point of view, whether you look at it here or in the mirror, it is a right side up cross, which is the sign of God and righteousness. Now, if you were in my shoes, you would see that the cross is upside down, which is the sign of the Devil and sin. Perception..." Zacharia starts to move his hand around, trying to get a different angle on the scar, but from his point of view, it is always right side up. "The Modern Testament, it always has the Devil in capital letters, just like we capitalize God or Father or Him in reference to the divine power we recognize. I know that in many texts, the Devil is not capitalized..." "Some people believe that if you use a name freely, and do not recognize it as a 'proper' name, which would be to keep it lowercase, it no longer has the same effect that the capital version would have. 'God' and 'a god' have two very different meanings and it is all because the G is or is not capitalized. That is the beauty of language and the human mind." "You know, Cutter, I think you are right. My friend, Chris Bagwell uses perception as a way to observe a situation and to use it to an advantage. I guess religion does the same thing. King James wanted his Bible to read a certain way, so he used his own perception of how the stories should be told to make him look more divine and all. I think I understand." Jude Cutter pulls a knife out of his side pocket and begins to pick at a scab on his left forearm. Blood instantly begins to pour from the now open cut. Once all the scab is gone, a pink trench of skin willing up with blood is revealed. Jude digs the knife further into the skin and traces the circular scar with the knife. "There is a plan for everyone, Brother Zacharia, and once you realize this, everything will become much more clear. There is no such thing as instinct. It is your mind reading the divine plan and telling you what to do next, to keep you on the path. Even every soul, corrupt or pure, here in Newark has a destination along the path. It is important to remember this." Zacharia focuses in on the blood rushing out of Cutter's arm. Against the harsh moonlight, the blood seems to do a dance on its way to the cement steps. Cutter finishes the circle and then puts the knife away once more. It seems like centuries before something disturbs the silence. Five men are walking down the sidewalk, all in red. The oldest looking one out of the group is sporting a red bandanna over his mouth in a bandit style. Zacharia notices the them looking over and he nudges Cutter. "Yes, Brother, I believe we should go inside, it is getting late." The men cut from the sidewalk and start to walk across the grass by order of the oldest. The man with the bandanna across his mouth stops and waits in the center of the yard while the other four start to sprint towards the closing door. "Cutter, run!" "Get to my floor!" Zacharia and Cutter split up, one going to the right and the other to the left to different stair cases. Two men split up between the fleeing men. They are both black men around the age of eighteen. They are skinny in build, both in red t-shirts. Zacharia makes it to the stairwell and closes the door. He waits for a moment when the door flings open and kicks it back at the pursuers with all his strength. He hears moans and groans from the two men. Without waiting a second, he runs up the staircase towards the sixth floor. Thoughts of Jude Cutter running up the other staircase, possibly in danger plagues his mind. He considers running through the third floor, hoping to get to the staircase to cut off the other men, but he doubts it will be worth it. Taylor is trained and has the stamina to fight off the assailants, but he highly doubts Cutter would have a shot, yet he does have a knife.... Taylor is now at the third floor and the two men are close behind him. He decides against his alternate plan and heads for the sixth floor, putting distance between him and the two men with every step. He crashes through the sixth floor door and sprints down the hall and turns to the left for Cutter's room. Empty. Nobody has reached the sixth floor yet. He runs down the hall and cuts towards the other stairwell, looking behind him to make sure nobody is behind him. The door remain untouched behind him. He pulls open the opposite stairwell's door and begins to run down it without thinking. He looks over the railing to see Cutter running with two men hot on his tail. Cutter's emotions are contained as he quickly jumps two steps at a time. One of the men lunges out and grabs a hold of Cutter's jacket, which comes off quickly, sending the two men falling backwards onto the landing. Cutter continues to run when he passes the jumping Zacharia Taylor. Taylor hits the landing and falls with both arms extended, striking both men to the ground. Dealing his damage, Taylor continues to run up the stairs when he hears the sixth floor door swing open. Cutter stops and runs back down to the fifth floor door and out of sight. Taylor gets to the fifth floor door nearly at the same time the two men whom were pursuing him in the other stairwell. One of them slams the door behind him, stalling Taylor's pursuit. He gets to the floor and looks straight and down both halls beside him, but everything is quiet. He takes a moment to think and concentrate, something telling him to run straight. He takes off down the hall and narrowly dodges an opening door. The resident looks at him with disgust and places an envelope on the corner of his door and closes it again. He comes up to a divide in the hall. Left or Right. Left or Right? Left! Taylor runs to the left and sees the two men chasing after Cutter, but it's a dead end and Cutter is standing there, in a white button up shirt standing his guard. One of them men reaches Cutter but gets weakly hit in the face, causing him to stumble for a moment but then grabs Cutter. Taylor finally reaches them as the second man throws a couple knees into Cutter's stomach. Taylor is able to get a clean shot in on the man kneeing Cutter and knocks him to the ground. The man doesn't immediately get off the ground when the other man holding Cutter lets go and goes toe-to-toe with Taylor. Taylor easily overpowers him and strikes him several times before hitting the ground. Cutter runs down the hall away from the assailants. Taylor kicks the man as he is about to rise and then runs towards the fleeing Cutter. Upon reaching the intersection of the hall, Cutter gets brutally tackled to the ground by one of the pursuers from the stairwell. Cutter begins to cough, trying to regain his breath when the other man catches up, trying to regain his breath from the sprint up the stairs. Taylor looks behind him to see the other men steadily walking quickly up to the group in the center of the hall. Taylor takes a chance and attempts a superman punch on the heaving man, but is vulnerable to a strong uppercut by the man that tackled Cutter to the ground. He attempts to regain his footing, but is unsuccessful and falls to the ground. Once again, all four men and in the same spot. Taylor is picked up by two of the men and held at each arm while the other two drag Cutter towards the stairwell. Taylor tries to regain his strength by walking with the men without struggle. When they get closer to the stairwell, Taylor uses the opportunity to fight back. He breaks away from his captors and is able to get them to the ground with quick kicks. He turns towards the two men next to the beaten Cutter and jumps for the one, but is caught at the foot by one of the downed men, causing him to fall into one of the men. He is sent backwards, tripping over Cutter and they both fall next to a window about six feet away from the stairs. Taylor is able to regain himself as the other man begins to kick at his side. He catches his foot and jumps up with it, causing the guy to smash into the ground with the force of Taylor's body behind it. He gets to his feet and brings the fallen man with him. He throws a strong cross at the guy which knocks him forcibly into Cutter and the man holding him. Both men fly against the window, but Cutter falls through and out. The man holding him nearly falls too, but is caught by the man Taylor just hit. Taylor wastes no time and sprints for the stairwell and begins his hurried decent to the bottom of the steps. The sound of footsteps follow him, but his pace is much quicker than their own. The fourth floor, third, second...within moments he is out the emergency exit in the stairwell and into the courtyard where Cutter fell. The scene is devastating one in view. Cutter fell from the fifth floor window and fell through a web of laundry lines which broke his fall, but left him helpless on the ground. His right shoulder is clearly dislocated and twisted unnaturally above his head, wrapped in several laundry lines. His left arm is also hanging in lines but is held out next to him. Cutter, seemingly barely conscious, is hanging in these wires with his feet dangling and arms out. The top of his head is cut up with glass, some shards still sticking out, while blood rushes down his face. His shirt is torn open around the chest and is being held up, revealing his stomach. Zacharia can only look on in horror. In what seems like hours to Zacharia, the men rush out of the stairwell and instantly grab Taylor, throwing a couple punches to his gut for good measure before looking up at the dangling Cutter. They all freeze as they look up at the image. Behind them, footsteps drag on and resonate through the courtyard. The owner of the grueling sound is the man with the bandanna over his mouth. He begins to clap, catching the attention of everyone, even Cutter, who musters the strength to look up. "Initiation is a beautiful thing. Spike, it is time for you to join your brothers." The tallest of all the men lets go of Taylor and looks up at Cutter. He takes a step closer and kicks something that clangs against the asphalt. He looks down to see Cutter's knife at his feet. He grins and picks it up and raises it to Cutter. Taylor struggles against he men's grip but it proves futile. Spike laughs before thrusting the knife into Cutter's right side, directly into his ribs. Cutter's body shutters for a moment but then completely falls limp and lifeless. The guy with the bandanna claps once more and takes a bandanna out of his pocket and hands it over to the Spike. Taylor uses the opportunity to kick free of the guys and sprint down the courtyard and attempt to run. Gunshots echo through the yard but Taylor gets away unscathed. The courtyard fades as the men continue to fire and run after Taylor. The brown room comes back into focus with a perfectly fine Jude Cutter in front of him, not even aged in the six year span. Cutter continues to carve into his skin. "Death is just part of the plan, Brother Zacharia. We've been through this before, have we not?" "Yeah, but usually death is the end of the path, not just a little detour..." "Very good, Brother Zacharia. I like that. You are right, but not everyone has the same plan. There are a few that have different destinations and paths, much like yours or mine. We are both living proof of that, but God does have a plan." Zacharia closes his eyes. "You were hanging there..." The image of the lifeless Jude Cutter comes to mind. Zacharia walks around the corner, rain staining the asphalt and releasing the distinct smell of wet pavement. Zacharia walks closer to him and sees the blood lines running out of the wound in his ribs and dripping to the ground. The rain speeds up the blood flow from his head and ribs, now that there is no heartbeat to push it out. "I remember all the blood and your mangled body..." Zacharia accidentally kicks the knife still laying on the ground. He looks down and picks it up with fragile hands. He studies the blade with a gold handle with the texture of raised Rosary beads. He goes to clean the knife but the flashing of red and blue lights disturbs him and he begins to run. "The cops came and I had to leave, but I do remember you hanging there." "Well, Brother Zacharia, I believe you have the answer in you. I'm not the first to rise from the grave, am I not?" Zacharia pictures a grave and shakes his head trying to disprove but Christian Thomas' face comes into the image, and he quickly opens his eyes. "Are the answers the same?" "No, Brother Zacharia...those answers are not the same..."
