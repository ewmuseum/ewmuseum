A Candid Moment

4 April 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/4/07 The following is a transcript of Jimmy Thunder's recent appearance on the Larry King Show. Sit back and enjoy a once in a life time experience where Jimmy candidly speaks to Larry and talks about his life tragedies, his personal life and his future. This is Jimmy Thunder ...... King: Welcome back everyone, my next guest rose to prominance in 2003 where he took the wrestling world by storm. He not only won the DWA, AWA and GCW World Heavyweight Championships, he also won the covetered AXIS World Title, all in the same year. He continued to dominate the wrestling fraternity through out 2004 and 2005 until he shocked the world and retired with what many experts believed was way before his career had peaked. Jimmy Thunder welcome to the show. Jimmy: Thanks Larry. King: It's been a few years since you were last on the show and I must admit, I'm really glad to have you back on. Jimmy: It's great to be back, I thought I'd be a little nervous sitting here in front of you again but I'm feeling pretty good at the moment. King: That's good, now Jimmy, you're on top of the world, voted by wrestling experts as the biggest name in the sport, then you turn around and shock the world by retiring. Why? Jimmy: It was a pretty tough decision to make Larry, I had achieved everything I wanted to achieve in the sport. I guess you could say that I lost my passion to compete, I really found it hard to leave home each week and fly to another city. I was really ready to move onto the next stage of my life. King: In 2004, you lost your wife tragically in an automobile accident. Was her sudden death a factor in your decision to retire? Jimmy: Of course it was Larry. Our son lost his mother and his father was never around, I had to change the status quo. He's a teenanger who needed his dad at a very crucial part of his life. I had contractual obligations to fulfil and when I did that, I was pressured into make a decision by the Bloodbox office and when I decided to walk, the company basically collapsed and it really vindicated my decision to retire. I reached all my goals and then some, financially I had enough money for not only me to retire and never work again, my son could have retired as well. My boy had everything he needed material wise, but emotionally, he was really struggling after his mum died, as was I, and I really thought the two of us needed each other more than ever. King: Usually when successful sport stars retire, they often find other avenues to keep them occupied. Things such as media work and public speaking, but when you retired, you become a recluse and fell off the face of the Earth. Nobody heard from you, nor did they know what you were up to. Jimmy: While I was wrestling I lead two lives, one was what you and everyone else saw was my on-camera life. The other life I lead was my home life, my private life. During my wrestling days I found it critical to protect all aspects of my personal life and try to keep it as personal as possible. My son was finding it hard enough going to going to school without having the kids in his class saying "your dad's a wimp" or other kids pretending to be his friend only because they wanted to meet me. Before I retired I found a beautiful stretch of land, an hour out of Boise Idaho, which I planned to live once my career was over. Once I had made that decision it was quite easy for my son and I to slip into annonymity because when he went to school nobody knew who his father was. Well some people knew but they didn't care, he was able to really build some true friendships and his personality started to come out more. I knew it then that keeping a low profile was the only option for the both of us. King: You were set in your new life, everything around you just fell into place like one huge jigsaw puzzle, then you had a set back that nearly had you living on the streets, what happened there? Jimmy: The thing is Larry, during my career, I made a lot of money. I mean a whole lot of money. I made a fortune from my wrestling contracts, endorsments and media ventures which I had all sitting in my bank. Then one day, I walked into my bank to do whatever I had to do when I started talking to the manager. I mentioned to him that I was having trouble keeping up with what was going in and what was going out. He then suggested to me that I hire an account to handle all my fanacial matters. He suggested a guy who had a huge reputation, at the time it seemed like a good idea so I went with it. I hired this guy and the first year or so everything was sweet, then after that he wouldn't return my calls, especially when I wanted a large check drawn up for me. Then one day, I went to buy some parts for my motorcycle and I couldn't do it becuase my bank card rejected it. I tried calling him and nothing, I called my bank and found out that my account had been cleaned out. Later on I discovered that the IRS seized my money because the accountant I hired didn't pay my taxes since I hired him. Before I knew it I was as broke as broke can be. Larry: I couldn't imagine what I'd do if this happened to me. Something like this would have hurt more than any attack you faced in the ring. Jimmy: You have no idea how much this sort of pain hurts Larry. I mean, one day you're wondering if life could get any better then the next you're worried if you'll be living on the streets. The people who know me best know that I'm a very proud man and that sometimes my pride gets me into trouble. They really knew how badly I was hurting, I couldn't talk about it then and because of the embarrassment I felt at the time I kind of find it hard to talk about now, but it's something I need to do so I can get on with my life and rebuild it to where it once was. I knew when I decided I had to fight back to get my life on the right track, that it was going to be one long tough struggle, but I know I'll get there at the end. Larry: Well one of the first things you did was announce that you were returning to the ring and that triggered off all sorts of chaos in the wrestling world. Eventually you signed with Global Championship Wrestling. Jimmy: I had to start making some money, Larry, and the only way I knew how was to get back into the ring. I had no other option or any other idea how to start bringing the bucks back into the household. I was sitting in my lawyer's office one day and he suggested that I return to the ring, which my mind had already been made up before he made his suggestion. The funny thing was that the only fear I had at the time was facing the media and having to explain why I was returning. I mean, how does a man who has had a dream career, walk up to a bunch of strangers holding microphones and cameras say, "Hi, I'm Jimmy Thunder, former World Heavyweight Champion, I've decided to come back to wrestling because I was stupid to trust a total stranger with my money so now I'm broke." Anyways, after my lawyer made the announcement for me, I was inundated with offers from various promotions. I had several meetings with various boss' but the way Steven Caldera conducted our meeting really impressed me. There was no bullshit about him. All the others were telling me things they thought I wanted to hear and basically promised me the world, where as Steven sat me down, told me straight out what he wanted from me and basically made me an offer on what he thought I was worth. I went back home and spoke to my family, but it was a pretty easy decision for me to make. Larry: You're back in the spotlight, re-starting your career at GCW, how did everyone welcome you? Did they all embrace you with open arms or was it a cold reception for you? Jimmy: Yanno Larry, I found coming back quite bizzarre. I didn't know what to expect. Well I did and it wasn't what I thought. I was thinking that the guys would give me a hard time about my age but when I arrived, everyone was busy with their own thing and I found myself backstage scratching my head thinking, what the hell do I do here now? When it comes to the guys I've spoken with, most are pretty cool guys to work with, some of the others, their mental acumen leaves a lot to be desired. I've had a few run ins with some guys but most are pretty cool with me and my comeback. Larry: Let's talk about your comeback, you're currently undefeated and you've now got the Television title, do you feel that your comeback is now complete? Jimmy: Far from it Larry, this is only the beginning. When I signed with GCW I only intended to stay here till my contract was up but now, I don't know how it happened but I have this unbelievable drive to win every title GCW has to offer and really etch my name in the history of one of the most reputable wrestling promotions in the world. I've set my self several goals and now I'm well on my way on that journey to reach these goals. I know this journey is going to be one wild ride but hey, I'm going to make sure I have a lot of fun along the way. Larry: Ok folks, we'll got to a commercial break, then, when we come back, Jimmy Thunder will answer your calls on the other side of this break, you're watching Larry King Live. (Commercial Break) Larry: Welcome back folks, now, before we get into this, I've been sitting here for the last few minutes trying to work out how I was going to word my next question to you but I couldn't come up with a decent way to dress this question up so I'm just going to be straight forward with it. Jimmy: Ok. Larry: There's been wide spread reports in the media about you being detained at an airport in Cincinnati with posession of a performance enhancing drug. Are the allogations true? (Jimmy pauses) Jimmy: At first I was worried about you asking me this but now I'm glad because I want to get this off my chest and I feel that it's important that the paying GCW fans hear the story from my side of the fence. Now, I really can't get into the specifics of the situation because there's still an ongoing FBI investigation. I can say this though, I was taken into a private room and answered a few questions about possesion of performance enhancing drugs but I was released without being charged. I won't go any further into the investigation side of the allogations but what I can say is that under the advice of Steven Caldera, I've agreed to take part in weekly drug testing to prove that I'm clean. The testing will be conducted by an independant tester and the results will be given to the GCW board, who will take any course of action they deem neccessary if they get an unfavourable result from my samples. Larry: Seeing as it's an ongoing investigation, I think we'll leave it there, you've been very open and honest about it and I can't see that we need to stay on this subject any longer. Okay, let's move onto some live callers who have been waiting patiently for you Jimmy. First off we'll go to Memphis Tennesse where we have Jeremy on the line for you Jimmy. Jimmy: Hi Jeremy. Jeremy: Hi Jimmy, first of all I have to say that it's pretty awesome that I can personally ask you a question. Jimmy: No worries mate, what can I do for you? Jeremy: It's great that you're back in the ring and you're already having issues with Kyo Kushinaga. What's the deal with him? Jimmy: The whole deal with Kyo is that for a while now he's been branded as one of the strongest guys in GCW then I come along and he feels like I'm messing with his mojo, so he turns into this diva like imbecile and starts getting in my face. Let me tell you something, I'll have something special waiting for him during our cage match. I don't go and make it a habit to dislike other wrestlers, guys come and go, feuds start and stop but at the end of the day, we are all in this to pay our bills and support ourselves and our families the best we can, so I don't blame guys for what they have to do in this business to survive, but when you come across a guy like Kyo who likes to attack you from behind as well as appearing and disappearing when it suits him is just something I cannot understand or condone. If you're going to attack me for the sake of a feud then for Christ sakes do it face to face. Larry: Kyo seems like a touchy subject with you, Jimmy. Jimmy: Oh it is Larry, the thing is I can handle a guy walking up to me and smacking me in the mouth. What I can't handle is cowards who have to continually attack me from behind. Let's face it Larry, I'm not super human, if a guy is good enough to land a good stiff right hand on my face, good luck to him. Larry: Ok we have Gary from Atlanta Georgia. Jimmy: Hi Gary. Gary: Hi Jimmy, I'm glad to see you back in the ring, man. Jimmy: Thanks mate, it's great to be back. Gary: How do you compare GCW to the other promotions you've worked in? Jimmy: Good question there Gary. Larry: That is a good question. Jimmy: The other places that I worked at was usually where the guys who knew each other would just stick together and move from one promotion after it fails and closes to another till that promotion meets the same fate as the last. The guys got caught in this web where they'd continually faced each other week in and week out so much so that they knew what move was coming next before their opponent did. You could basically tell each week who was going to win and who was going to lose. It was pretty predictable. Now with GCW, there is no way you can pick a winner or a loser. There's no way in hell I'll go undefeated for over two years here. If your form is below par, you can pretty much guarentee that a loss is just around the corner. This is definately not a place where you can keep your guard down because you'll definately get your tail end kicked in. Larry: Thanks for the question Gary, we're almost out of time here but we can go to one more caller. Jimmy this last caller is from your home state of Idaho and he is non other than the Governor Of Idaho, Butch Otter. How are you today, Sir? Butch: I'm doing just great thanks Larry and thank you for this opportunity. This isn't a question as such but I would like to say one thing about Jimmy Thunder. What most of you don't realise is that Jimmy is committed to atleast five local charities that I know of. Jimmy isn't the type of guy who goes out and parade's his charity work to gain publicity. I've met Jimmy on numerous occasions and I can catagorically say that he is one fine human being. He has become Idaho's favourite adopted son and we're so proud that we can call Jimmy Thunder one of ours. Jimmy: Thank you for saying that Butch, I'm humbled by your words, I truly am. Butch: You're most welcome Jimmy and on behlaf of the entire state of Idaho, we'd like to wish you the best of luck in your up coming match with Kyo Kushinaga at High Stakes this weekend. Jimmy: Thank you, I really appreciate that. Butch: We just want you to know that we realise that you'll be in a tough match at High Stakes but keep in mind the entire state is with you and we'll all be in your corner cheering for you. Jimmy: Thanks mate, the check's in the mail HA HA HA!!! Larry: Thank you for calling Governor Otter. Jimmy: Thank you Governor. Butch: It was my pleasure. Larry: We're just about out of time here, I would like to take this opportunity to thank Jimmy for coming on our show and we wish him all the best of luck for High Stakes. Jimmy: Thank you for having me. Larry: You're most welcome, we'll be back after this station break. (screen fades to a station break)
