CWMC First Round RP: Sour

14 June 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 14, 2007 __Start Voices. Fading voices. Silence. So far, everyone that had stepped through the open pair of oak doors had the same reaction. Excitement overtook them in the hall. If not excitement, a sense of dread and hopelessness brought out their words. Upon peering into the room though, there was no speaking. Everything just...stopped. The eyes recessed to a minute orb, colored gray no matter what the original hue had been. It was almost as if death had placed his bony fingers on each of the guest’s shoulders and whispered their fate into their lobes. Was this what was supposed to happen? Jay had never been to one before. Hell, the only reason he stood in the grim home today was due to regret. It was a heavy concept, regret that is. The simple word had its way of latching its tentacles around one's throat and strangling the life out of them until they were at a point of submission. When there, the unfortunate victim was forced to do something they promised themselves they wouldn't. Jay was the victim today. He passed into the home casually, dressed rather debonair in comparison to other days. The line stretched across the back wall and winded around the room to the left side wall. Terror had gotten the pleasure of standing behind the trash man...or so he figured. His shirt was stained with a thick brown patch in the center of his back and various smells broke loose from his body. His hair jumped in many directions as if each spike outward was a different man attempting suicide. Oddly enough, he could speak flawless English. 'Don't you look pretty.' The gap in his teeth let a warm wind of sour milk enter into his nostrils. 'And you the same.' He answered while looking down at his shoes. If there was any way to combat the stench other than plugging his nose, he was open for trying it. 'Just got done my route. A little incomplete if you know what I mean.' He guessed him right. Oh what a wonder appearance is. 'I guess so.' 'He had the finest gadgets you know? Crazy things. I once found a light saber.' 'I'm sure.' 'Even one time there were magic beans for a stalk. Like that fairytale. That thing is growing in my yard as we speak.' Apparently his ability to conquer the English language didn't reach out far enough to give him a level mind. 'Stunning.' Jay looked across the room and saw the line was shrinking considerably. His new friend had yet to notice it until Terror had finally pointed it out. 'He make you an invention or something?' 'An invention?' 'Of course. The man was one of the greatest inventors the world had ever known.' 'No inventions for me.' The trash man took a dive into his pocket and swam around with his fingers. They came up and with them he showed off a little red light. Broken of course. 'See this?' He shoved it closer to Talley's face making sure he could see the dangling wires and the crack on the head of the bulb. 'This was part of a spaceship. It showed one of the controls was lit.' 'No kidding...' Three people stood between him and the front of the line. 'I don't kid. It is a genuine piece. You want it?' 'I'm not sure I could take such a gift. It's yours.' 'I have many things from this man. Here,' without warning he shoved his dirt-covered paws into Jay's pocket and patted the medium sized LED down. He felt violated. 'Thank you.' The last person stepped away and found their seat among the others in the room. It was the trashman's turn. He spoke in such an alternate universe that it was hard to tell what he was saying. Everything had such a fantastic meaning to it in his realm, no matter how much it lacked it in real life. After he was done he smiled that toothless grin again and looked for his place among the masses. Jay just shook his head. As he stepped up, something came over him. It felt a lot like a chill racing down his spine at first. At the bottom though, it warmed and felt like it was cutting through his stomach and out the other side. Talley blamed it on the regret. He looked into the eyes of the first man he had ever seen and found a hint of sadness sunken within. It was faint to anyone else surely. Jay, though, knew Dave a little better than most people ever could. Talley reached out and put his hand on Dave's in a comforting manner and tried to cry. He tried once, twice, ten times. Every time though nothing came out. It was almost as if his tear ducts had shed their supply before the visit. Instead, he shook his head left and right. People don't change, he had told him. What a rotten way to go out. He took a heavy drag on the air around him and searched for the good memories. High and low he looked for anything cheerful in the past they shared. Just as the tears though, it was a useless effort. It wasn't that there was nothing good about the man. Of course not. The fact of the matter was that anything happy that had ever been stored in him had been taped over by an episode laced with hate. There was no point. He took one last look at his unfulfilled face and with that found a seat away from the trashman. People don't change, he remembered again. 'Dave Talley did change,' he spoke faintly. No one heard him speak, no one heard anyone speak within the room. It just wasn't the place. __________ 'Aren't you the talkative one tonight. See something you didn't like?' Daniel Franco's voice had a way of driving a man crazy. Every time he spoke it came out in such a way that you just wanted to reach across the table and deck him twice in the jaw just so he wouldn't be able to open and close the damn thing. Jay, being civil and in a rather horrible mood in general, sat back in the wooden seat looking intently into the fine wood grains of the table. The smell of burning cinders and cigarette smoke filled the air of the Black Sheep pub. It was a small place but brilliant looking in its own way. The basement section of the pub seated four others at a table opposing Jay and Daniel's and about five or six stood around the main bar. 'Look, I know I've been hard on you lately about your wrestling but this past week at Warpath you really showed up.' He ducked his head down and tried to get in Terror's line of sight. Jay turned his attention to the wall instead and took a heavy inhale on the end of his cigarette. 'You're showing the people at GCW who you are and it's really getting you some talk backstage.' He couldn't be any less amused. The salt shaker caught his eyes and he looked almost as if he was counting each speck in the glass tube. 'Are you going to talk at all?' Franco was never one to let up either. He would pester a man until he turned red in the face and even as he was ready to burst he'd add in that one last bit to bust you entirely. Jay honestly didn't know why he kept him around. 'Well how about this.' Franco reached down to the bench beside him and picked up a small piece of paper. He slid it across the table gently allowing Jay to finish the salt count and shift over. He didn't bother looking. Instead he shoved it to the floor and went back to examining the salt shaker. 'Jay, it's a tournament. They call it the Chris Williams Memorial Cup tournament, something big going on with people from all over the US.' He stopped momentarily and looked across the table crookedly. 'And...?' His first word of the evening was hoping to receive an answer worth hearing. 'And you're in it! I told the event holder about you and how you've manhandled GCW taking both the vanguard and TV title. He was very happy to hear you'd be entering it.' 'More wrestling?' 'Well that is your job, yes. This can be your chance to become a big name in the business. Just think...' 'No. I'm not doing it.' He dropped the cherry off the end of his 27 and smashed the butt in his ashtray. A tall pillar of smoke ascended and found its way to the ceiling fans where it would never be seen again. 'Of course you are.' 'Says who?' Franco cocked his eyebrow upward and held it on his forehead. 'Your manager. You have to trust me here, this is for your own good.' The outlaw sunk his face into the wood boards of the table. Every reason he had given for leaving his house years back seemed to come back up after hearing anything Daniel had to say. This time though, his father was absent. A new overlord took form in Daniel Franco. He shook his head angrily and decided not to answer. 'Jay, think of it as defying what your father was all about. Show him that you are a star on your own.' It was almost motivational. In fact, if it came from anyone else it may have been slightly more convincing. Even with the reality evident about Jay's father, someone other than Daniel surely could have made him believe it. 'Impossible.' 'If you think that way, yes. He'll be watching, I know it.' 'Impossible.' 'Jay...' Franco reached across the table and took a hold of Jay's arm. He clutched him around the wrist and watched the skin redden around his grip. 'Come on.' 'Dave Talley is dead.' 'Many people are dead to you Jay, it doesn't mean they aren't watching though.' Daniel loosened his hold and withdrew his body backwards against the back of the bench. Jay took out another cigarette and with it he grabbed a newspaper from his side. He patted the page down on the table and lit the end of the Marlboro. 'Dave Talley saw his last scene when I left him at the Rouge.' Daniel looked at the page and found the name he had been hearing. He looked at Jay, back at the paper, and then across the table again. 'You're ok, aren't you?' Jay looked him over in an annoyed manner. 'You tell me.' 'You'll be ok.' As if he hadn't dropped himself to a new low already by signing Jay up for a tournament, his lack of sympathy did enough to secure a place even lower on the ladder. 'Fight in this tournament and you'll be back to your normal lovable ways.' 'Fuck yourself.' Jay had heard enough for one night and like he had on many other occasions, he walked away. With his cigarette hanging between his fingers he made his way around the corner and up the stairs. Daniel found his phone and dialed frantically. It rang twice and when the voice of a woman kicked in he spoke, 'Heather...we may have some good news.' He wore such a conniving smile that it was hard to guess who he was really looking out for. __________ Even after sitting on the bench and hearing the stories, Jay had no idea who half the people in the room were. It didn't matter. He listened to the kind words they spoke of a man Jay believed to be power hungry and greedy. They praised his cheerful attitude as Terror couldn't remember if a smile ever graced his lips. It was odd to say the least. When it was his turn to speak, he didn't. He wanted to so bad but he couldn't stand in front of them. The people in the room obviously were on a different level than Terror was with him. They pestered him over and over to get up and tell a great story of how they met. Unfortunately, there was nothing special to speak on. They were forced to meet. In resistance, Jay put on a frown and gathered himself enough to make it look like he was crying. It was enough. One man put his arm around Jay and drew him closer. Although creepy, it was enough to have the next person take the podium. For another hour he sat and listened. Afterwards he followed the mindless precession of teary-eyed men and women. They passed through the double doors and like magic, voices were heard again. People talked amongst themselves in the hall and continued to speak as they stepped outside onto the concrete. 'I miss him man.' He recognized the voice as it came closer to him. The trashman was back. 'I can't believe it.' 'Did you ever see him before?' 'You don't need to see a person to know them. What we had was spiritual.' Jay rolled his eyes and watched the people crossing the parking lot making their way into a small building just across the way. 'Where is everyone going?' 'To get a bite to eat.' 'Like an after party?' 'Kinda, yeah. You want to go over with me?' He had withstood enough of the strange mannerisms today to sit through another. 'I'd rather not. I just came to find out how he died honestly.' 'You didn't hear?' 'Nope, it didn't say it anywhere.' 'A heart attack took him down and he just couldn't get back up they're saying. It's a shame to loose such a visionary man.' He wanted to correct him once and for all about his father and how he was a nothing close to an inventor. The thought that he had broken his heart to the point of death kept him from saying anything though. Just like inside the viewing room, Jay lost his ability to speak. He was done hearing bad news. __End
