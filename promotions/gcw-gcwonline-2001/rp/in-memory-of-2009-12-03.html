In Memory Of...

3 December 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: December 3, 2009 [This piece is written in memory of my great uncle, whom passed away yesterday (December 2, 2009) at 4:00 A.M. Rest in peace, Uncle Angus.] Life is fleeting, sometimes fading away just as abruptly as it comes into being. Cold, indiscriminate and impartial, life doesn’t play favorites as relates to its brevity. It’s a limited commodity and is very precious, though some people don’t realize it until it’s too late. Others, well, they never realize it and life passes right by them, leaving nothing but heartache and loss in its wake. Sometimes, it takes losing those dearest to us to make us realize just how important life truly is. For one man in particular, it was too late when he realized it; fourteen years too late. The setting; a dark room dimly lit by a circle of scented aromatherapy candles. The light of the flames that dance atop the wick reflect off the walls, distorting the shadows that are cast over them. In the middle of that circle of candles sits AXEL Action, legs crossed, hands flat on his thighs. His eyes are closed and his breathing deep; his mind tucked within itself in a deep state of concentration. He seems to be rather deeply embroiled in a state of meditation, attempting to clear his mind and gather his thoughts. Meditation is defined as a contemplative discourse, usually on a religious or philosophical subject. His quandary of choice? Life. He ponders the ebb and flow of time and how it seems to coincide with the start and end of all life. Thoughts of how one life begins when another ends pass through his mind as well. His transcendental coma is interrupted by the sound of the door swinging open and slamming against the wall. He snaps out of his trance with a start, hopping to his feet and assuming a defensive stance. “What the fuck?! Who’s there?!” The only response he gets is a childish giggle from whom he finally recognizes as his older brother, Ace. Narrowing his eyes, he makes his irritation known with a sigh as he flips the light switch. As the room becomes illuminated by the incandescent bulb in the ceiling fixture, AXEL shakes his head in disgust. “Didn’t mom ever teach you to knock before entering someone’s personal bedroom?” “Sorry, little brother. I didn’t know you still masturbate.” AXEL can’t do anything but blink when he hears the words that come out of Ace’s mouth. Bewildered and slightly disturbed, AXEL moves towards him and pushes him out of his bedroom and follows suit. Pushing Ace all the way out to the living room, AXEL shoves him onto the couch and takes a seat in the chair across from him. “You know, for a thirty-one year old man, you’re quite immature. For your information, I wasn’t masturbating. I was meditating.” “So that’s what you crazy kids are calling it these days?” “Ugh. Never mind.” Giving a slight chuckle at the rise he’d gotten out of his younger brother, Ace shakes his head and tilts it to the left a bit. “I know, AXEL. I was just giving you shit, bro. So what were you thinking about when you were off in Loopy Land?” AXEL sighs and shifts his gaze to the floor for a moment, as if looking for the words to say. Taking a deep breath, he closes his eyes. “Well, I was thinking about Mom, and to a lesser extent, Dad. It’s my fault that they aren’t with us anymore. I’ve been thinking about them for a long time and I finally understand where I went wrong. As you probably remember, our father was an abusive man. There wasn’t a day that passed that he wasn’t beating one of us, or beating Mom. Even so, he didn’t deserve to be murdered like that. Mom definitely didn’t deserve it. The worst part is that I wasn’t even attempting to kill her.” Nodding his head, Ace listens intently to everything AXEL has to say. As he takes in his brother’s words, he strokes his chin as if seriously considering something. “You know what? I think it’s time to take a little field trip.” “What?” “I’m saying we should go visit Mom and Dad. I’m sure you’ve got some things you’d like to say to them, after all, right?” AXEL shakes his head, his features discernibly distraught. He felt as if he had no business going to visit their parents’ gravesite. At least, not after what he’d done to them. What kind of son kills his parents, even if it’s unintentional? “That’s crazy. They’re dead, Ace, and they won’t be able to hear a word that I say. Besides, you know I don’t believe in all of that ‘afterlife’ garbage anymore. Even if it IS true, what makes you think they’d want to hear anything that I have to say? They’re six feet under because of my stupidity, after all.” Ace shakes his head and stands up, snatching AXEL to his feet by the collar of his shirt. He gazes at him with cold eyes, his demeanor stern yet caring. Patting him rather roughly between the shoulders, Ace drags him out the front door, closing it behind him. “Are you going to be a big baby about everything for the rest of your life, AXEL? Come on, we’re going whether you like it or not. It’s time that you act like a man and face up to what you did. If I have to, I’ll knock you out and drag you to the cemetery. Don’t make me have to do that.” AXEL drops his shoulders and nods, letting out a sigh of defeat. Knowing he doesn’t really have much choice in the matter, he trudges toward the car, climbing in on the passenger side. “Fine. Let’s go.”, is all he says as Ace gets in on the driver’s side and cranks the car before backing out of the drive. ++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++ It’s five o’ clock in the evening when they arrive at the gates of the cemetery where their parents were interred some years prior. The sun is already sinking behind the trees, making way for the first stars of the nighttime sky. A cool, winter breeze blows dead leaves about and rustles the grass. It’s the kind of breeze that can chill a man right to his very bones. The Brothers Action walk through the front gate of the cemetery, both men bundled up from head to toe to protect them from the nippy air. “I really don’t want to be here, Ace. It’s so cold out here, and this is bringing back memories that I don’t want. Can we just leave?” Ace shakes his head, nudging AXEL onward. He has no intention of letting his younger brother out of this, because in Ace’s mind, it was for his own good. As they make their way through the graveyard, they make sure not to step on any graves, due to the fact that they were told it’s disrespectful to stand on someone’s resting spot. They finally stop when they approach the spots they were looking for. Just ahead of them were two headstones, each baring words that seemed to be chiseled into them. The one on the left read, “Allan Action (June 25, 1958 �" July 5, 1995)”, while the one on the right read, “Alana Action (March 9, 1960 �" July 5, 1995).” “Go on, get over there.”, says Ace as he gives his younger brother a little shove in the proper direction. Stumbling over his own feet, AXEL falls to his hands and knees in front of his mother and father’s burial plots. Shaking his head, he clenches his fists nervously, digging his fingernails into the palms of his hands. “I can’t do this. Do you have any idea just how unwelcome I feel here? I killed them! What don’t you understand about that concept?!” Ace shakes his head, standing his ground. AXEL’s temper wasn’t exactly something he hadn’t dealt with before. In fact, that temper is what led to several of the scuffles the two had gotten into over the years. Looking down at AXEL with the calmest of expressions on his face, he simply gives a nod of his head. “It’s times like these that build true character within a man. Now, talk to them, tell them what’s on your mind. Whether you believe in the afterlife or not, you need to clear the air and this is the best way to do it.” Narrowing his eyes at Ace, AXEL nods begrudgingly as he lowers his gaze to the ground. Closing his eyes tightly, he takes a deep breath and exhales slowly before shifting his line of vision towards the two headstones. “Hi Mom, Dad… I know you’re probably a bit surprised to see me here today, considering the fact that I haven’t been here even once in the last fourteen years. Well, the truth is, I wouldn’t even be here if it weren’t for your other son forcing me to come. I know that the two of you are probably mad at me for killing you, and I’m sorry I did it. I didn’t mean to kill you when I cut the brake line in your car. Well actually Mom, I meant to kill him at the time but not you. “ Pausing momentarily to collect his thoughts, AXEL brushes his blonde hair from his face and lifts his gaze to the sky. In what some would describe as a moment of clarity, he finally realizes why he hasn’t been able to let go, even after all of those years. He sighs softly as he loses his composure and breaks into tears. He sobs quietly as his voice fills the approaching night sky. “Dad, you might have been an abusive, alcoholic jackass but you were still my father and when you were sober, you were the best Dad a guy could ask for. No matter what you did to us, you didn’t deserve to die like that. It wasn’t my place to judge you or decide whether you deserved to live or die. I was a terrible son and I’m sorry that I took your life into my own hands. As of this very moment, I forgive you for everything you did to us, and if you really are up there and are listening to me, I hope that you and Mom can find it in your hearts to forgive me.” With that said, he reaches inside of his coat and pulls out two red roses, taking one in each hand. Looking back and forth between the two headstones, he places a rose on the ground in front of each. He gives one last look at both of them before he gets to his feet, brushing the dirt off of his knees. “Are you ready to go?”, asks Ace as he places a hand between his younger brother’s shoulders, patting him gently on the back. Turning to face his big brother, looking at him for a moment, AXEL nods. A small smile curls his lips as he turns to head back towards the car, getting in on the passenger side once more. As Ace gets in and cranks the car, AXEL places a hand on his arm to stop him from shifting the gears. “Hey, Ace?” “What is it, little brother?” AXEL removes his hand from Ace’s arm, his own arm moving to rest on the arm rest between the seats. With a sigh of relief, he allows himself to feel true comfort for the first time since he was a child. “I think I can let go, now.”
