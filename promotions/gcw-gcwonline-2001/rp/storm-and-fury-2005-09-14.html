Storm and Fury

14 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 14, 2005 For about 2 weeks now, Coyle O’Conner hasn’t made any appearances on television. With the GCW organization worried about his whereabouts, and fans wondering just why he hasn’t been around at all, with not a care in the world, Coyle has been enjoying his brief time off. Earlier today, he was notified with a threatening message that he is to return from wherever he is now, or else he won’t only be forced to forfeit his match, but he will also be forced to forfeit his career. Not taking this issue lightly, he rushed to the airport and purchased a ticket that would leave for Canada in no-time. Two hours and a first-class seat later, Coyle found himself riding over the Atlantic Ocean. Coyle had a window seat; he loved looking at the environment, whether it be nature or water, it fascinated him. He looked like a child rewarded with a lollipop, he was antsy. Considering the fact he didn’t have a window seat on the way to where he was, this meant a lot to him, and so he cherished it. Eventually though, it became too much. So, he decided to listen to some tunes. He slowly leaned back into his seat, got comfortable, popped his headphones on, and listened to the Out of Exile album by Audioslave. Not caring who was next to him or behind him, he started to hum the words to “Your Time Has Come”. This, perhaps, was a mistake for him. Opening his mouth and singing just a few lyrics drew attention to him. As fast as a bullet, he was recognized by many people in First Class. Like a flower, autograph requests flourished upon him, distracting him from his CD. But hey, Coyle wasn’t a bad guy, so he couldn’t say no; he never said no to anyone before. Honestly though, he was getting frustrated. All that went through his head was, “I can’t put up with this, I just wanna live in peace?” but he didn’t refuse one person. Eventually, when it calmed down, Coyle managed to get a little nap in. But, before long, the pilot came on the intercom announcing that they were going through turbulent weather, and that the ride may be pretty rough for a short period of time. Therefore after, the plane rumbled violently, tossing people around. “Are you kidding me?” he thought to himself, “is this really happening to me?” With the plane still bumping, and some trembling scream bursts from the other passengers, Coyle zoned out and remained calm. “God, I’m just looking to make it back to Canada safely, there is a victorious match with my name on it there.” There was no answer for that help call, but he still remained zoned out, just staring point blankly into the abyss. Shortly after, the plane started to shake even more rapidly, but this didn’t bring the O from his daydream, he was still remaining calm as the others had their arms waving and their voices crying. “These people need to shut up, it isn’t that bad, ? as he started to feel regret of being with these individuals, “it’s just a bumpy ride, I’ve been through worse, every match I’ve been in was 10x worse. They need to shut up and settle down.” Coyle started to think about what he looked like, just calm and quiet, slow motion as the surrounding people freaked out; he could practically see this picture in front of his face. It was no sweat to him, he couldn’t focus on something merely comparable to a Cruiserweight Championship Round match. As time passed, so did the storm, but the people around Coyle were still talking about it. This huge annoyance began to strike his last nerve. No longer calm yet still quiet, as his leg bounced up and down nervously, he felt like he was going to rip his hair out of his scalp and just let loose; just flip out. The whiny voices of each passenger, and the meek “oh my gods” and wussy “my life has just been tainted” made him sick to his stomach. Though he was annoyed, he bottled it up. When the plane finally landed in Vancouver, he stood up. Expecting to get off the plane quickly, each person moaped around, still talking about the storm. That was it, Coyle had enough, he couldn’t take it anymore. Every time he heard the word “storm”, he twitched his eyes and he twitched with his neck. Up until some scientist of some sort explained the science of the storm, Coyle just let it all flow? “Listen you goddamn p*****s! That was nothing”, it was a little turbulence, it was an itch on an arm, it was a feather tickling your cheek, it was fu**ing nothing! Realize that, and stop whining you damn bitches!” As he finished, every person on the plan stopped in their tracks, and just staring right through him, jaws dropped to the floor and what not. One person from the crowd said, “How could you say something like that in front of kids?” Another claimed “You’re a professional athlete, what’s the problem buddy?” Coyle did nothing but huffed and puffed, and looked to the exit. “You know what?” Looking around at each persons face as if they answered him. “Go fu** yourselves you arrogant cocky rich-spoiled no-nothing bastards.” He rushed off the plan, ran down the terminal, and found his own way around Vancouver. Now boiled up for his next round bout tomorrow night, he couldn’t get anything off of his mind except to destroy someone. He was absolutely and astonishingly furious.
