Date: November 2, 1996 Home Sweet Home ? Uncle Tom & the Business You live to fight another day. What doesn’t kill you makes you stronger. I’m living proof of both of those statements with a make shift bandage wrapped around my head and tied with a sock and a throbbing headache even a couple valumes didn’t cure. While other kids my age were studying and working, I was out making money getting punched in the face. I’m not the type to complain and seek refuge but I didn’t exactly have very many options. I was kicked out of every school I attended for being the type who wouldn’t avoid conflict, instead embrace it. I was always told my entire life? do what you love. Money doesn’t buy happiness and you have to find a career you enjoy waking up to go to, every day. Well, I sure as hell knew the idiot who said money doesn’t buy happiness might not see the look on my face when I’m countin? a stack. But I improvised ? I started doing what I loved? and getting paid for it. My uncle? He wasn’t stupid ? he knew what I was up to and he had his own ideas for me. He had his own way for me to drop people on their necks and get paid for it. He was the one who showed me this industry. Brock Alyas, meet wrestling. A legit way to fight and get paid. November 2, 1996. Things were going well for Brock Alyas. As well as they could’ve been going for a 17 year old high school drop-out who fought for a living and had to put up with a drunk uncle who was never satisfied with what he was doing with his life. Gus? bar had a couple more "group therapy meetings" but before long word got out and the DPD busted Gus and his illegitimate gambling / pit fighting operation and had enough on him to throw the old drunk in jail for a couple years. So once again, Brock Alyas was out of a job. It was one day while watching television on his uncle’s couch that Brock really thought about what he wanted to do the rest of his life. He was fine with fighting, he didn’t mind sleeping on an icepack or wearing one underneath his shirt. "Sitting around, wasting his fuckin? life away, dropping out of school, quitting football?" Tom Alyas wasn’t exactly the prize-winning uncle but he had rules and expectations for Brock that the two never really saw eye-to-eye on throughout most of Brock’s teenage years. Although he was a drunk, Brock realized that he could have very well been a drunk because all the trouble Brock had gotten himself into. It’s hard to feel bad for a guy who gets some slut pregnant and turns a blind eye to his kid and eventually that kid ends up doing 25 to life. “it’s not hard to feel bad for a guy who never got a girl pregnant, but still had to deal with a bastard child only because he was the boys only remaining family. "Morning uncle Tom" Brock was watching a television screen that couldn’t have been bigger than 12 inches and he was seated in his uncle’s low-rent apartment in downtown Detroit. It was obvious Tom wasn’t exactly a world class home decorator, there were more holes in the dry-wall than pictures hung and it looked as if the living room had been victim of numerous floods. "Look, I’m going down the street for a few. When I get back I want this room to be spotless, you hear me?" "Yes Uncle Tom," Brock was barely paying attention to his uncle and had no intention of cleaning the room, after all Brock thought going down the street meant he was going to get loaded. Tom Alyas was an independent wrestling company promoter and he worked for No Mercy Wrestling. A Detroit based farm-federation for the ECW (at that time). Tom worked long, hard hours putting his heart and soul into finding talent and helping them develop and eventually one day become superstars. He had worked with such superstars as RVD, Rhyno and Kevin Nash. He was devoted to the sport and there wasn’t many like him at a time when wrestling wasn’t exactly at it’s peak value. In fact, it was a dying sport throughout the mid 90’s plagued with shitty storylines and even the biggest promotions were hardly entertaining. So it took some hard work, but he loved the sport and at the end of the day he wouldn’t turn his back on it regardless of it’s current state. Going down the street actually meant going for breakfast, often meeting other promoters or wrestlers discussing some of the plans for NMW and generally talking business. “and then, getting loaded. This afternoon, while smoking a Marlboro ? Tom made his way to his favorite diner. Tom was going to meet another NMW promoter, Derek Grimes who was a young kid that Tom respected but questioned his undeniably bigger push and impact on NMW than Tom had, regardless of the fact that Tom had been there since day one. Either way, Tom couldn’t deny that jealousy lead to a world of evil and instead tried to swallow his pride every time Derek tried telling him "what was best for the company". Tom couldn’t afford a car just yet, not even a beater that he could abandon had it broken down. But Derek on the other hand, pulled up in a brand new, pearly white Lincoln Towncar this morning and he pulled up right in front of Tom who was standing just outside the door, finishing his smoke. Not the typical jealous type, Tom brushed it off with a brisk comment when Derek got out. "Nice car," Tom said as he stomped his cigarette out and held the door for the much younger Grimes who wasn’t but looked like he could be half of Tom’s age. "Yeah, you know. I told you about RVD, he sold. He’s not going to be around anymore," "Wait, what? Robbie made it!” Fuckin? eh! Heyman brought him in?" Tom was ecstatic that one of his prized wrestlers hade made it out of Detroit and into the "big league". The two got their seats and waitresses were so familiar with them that they need not order, instead were delivered a round of coffee. "It will be just a few minutes, boys," The waitress said as she poured their coffee. The two smiled at her and Derek opened the Detroit Free Press. "So, I’m happy for Robbie but what are we going to do now? in terms of the main event? Certainly we can hold onto him for a couple shows so he can drop the belt?" Tom asked Derek with a look of concern on his face. RVD was the main reason anyone even went to their shows. "I was thinking about that myself, Tom. Heyman said we could have him for a couple months, but by Mid 97 he’ll be gone. Now in the mean time we’re going to need to find a replacement. Now I don’t think there’s ever going to be somebody who can replace him. I don’t think it’s going to be possible to find some kid and emulate his style. He’s just to sleek and smooth, too entertaining and acrobatic, you know? I think we’re going to need something new. Something fresh?" Derek had put his paper aside. "This Terry Gerin kids supposed to be a fuckin? machine but word is he’s headed out to Germany for some Catch Wrestling program. I don’t know. All to say, we need a new image. We need a young kid who can represent Detroit. A local young kid we can build around and won’t be leaving us any time soon. A kid who LOOKS like a fighter, you know? Droppin? people on their necks and stuff? I think that’s the route we need to go?" Tom’s eyebrows lowered as he began to think about who this kid could be. "We’ve got Scotty over in Windsor he’s doing some real good things and he’s great with young kids. He turns them into ring pros within months," Tom never wanted to force anything on his nephew but he couldn’t deny that this was a prime opportunity. Send Brock to Windsor, Ontario across the border. Get him away from the hell-hole Detroit was slowly but surely turning into. Live a normal life, have a break from three in the morning knocks on the door from the Detroit Police bringing Brock home. "He’s got that little BCW campaign and he wouldn’t mind letting someone get some experience there before sending him here greener than the grass on the ground," Tom wasn’t even listening really anymore. A colleague they talked to, Scott D’Amore ran the Can-Am Wrestling School in Windsor, Ontario and had a side promotion, Border City Wrestling that wasn’t exactly huge but had a good fan base. D’Amore was a great wrestling coach and trainer and was very well known for his work on high-spots and power wrestling, making moves look very painful. "I think it’s time to look for a short, thick, mean Detroit boy who loves dropping people on their neck. You know, there’s a good, clean way to make everything look stiff these days, Scotty’s right on top of it, I think this is a great opportunity to build our future," Derek had opened his paper back up while on his rant. Tom’s mind was working like clock work with each and every thought of getting Brock out to do something positive with his life. November 2, 1996. The door to Tom Alyas? apartment was kicked open and Brock, still seated on the living room couch jumped and put the half burnt joint out in the nearest ash tray and tried quickly to spray the room down with an aerosol can not meant for air freshening. "Fuck, fuck!" Brock figured it would be anybody than who it was. "Brock! BROCK!" The affects of the highly potent joint Brock was burning had him really questioning why his uncle was running around the house like a chicken with his head cut off. "What! What!” Are you having another heart attack?" Brock asked after realizing it was just his uncle and not the feds. Normally Tom Alyas would’ve put his nephew against the wall by his throat had he smelt pot in his living room but he couldn’t care less right now because he had an idea that would benefit the both of them. Finally, some good news for the two-person "family". "Okay look. I know you don’t like wrestling but let’s sit down and talk about this," Tom still spoke with urgency, it was obvious he hadn’t exactly planned out exactly what he would say. Brock’s heart was still racing and he had a habit of pacing back and forth when something was on his mind so while his uncle was explaining the situation to him, Brock was listening intently but at the same time pacing back and forth. "Do you mind if? I? re-light this? You almost gave ME the heart attack, after all" Brock asked with a bit of hesitance in his tone. "Yes, yes. Light it! Here use my lighter, it’s a Bic! Look Brock just listen to me for a minute" Surprised by his uncles tone of voice and allowance to let him get gone in his living room, something Brock was often used to have to hide? he was all ears. "Okay. Look. At work, our heavyweight champ right now is going to be moving up and on. He signed a huge contract with ECW and we’re going to need to replace him? "Now I know we haven’t talked about this and I know you don’t exactly follow wrestling or even enjoy it, but listen to me for a second. "Brock? I see you coming home every night and every morning when I walk into your room it’s another shrimp package that’s ruined because it’s the only thing that’ll serve as an ice pack. I know you’re out and fighting and you haven’t asked me for money in months and I know you sure as hell don’t have the balls to go out and get a part-time job but listen to me. There’s an opportunity here, son." Brock’s eyes narrowed as his role-model spoke to him, not because he was upset but because he was trying to make sense of all of this. "Look, I don’t care about all of that. But if you’re going to be putting your body on the line for money, why don’t you do it where it’s LEGAL. Why don’t you do it where you get DECENT money? Why don’t you do it where somebody who cares for you can look out for you? give you a future? Look, I know I’m not the best uncle but I’d do anything for you, kid. I’d do ANYTHING to see you become a superstar." Brock’s one eyebrow lifted as the room was filled with an awkward silence for a few minutes. He put the joint out into a roach and sat forward putting his hands in his face. "Let me get this straight ? you want, me, to be the next Rob Van Dam? Look unc, I can fight but that doesn’t mean I’m good at it. How do you know this won’t be a big mistake?" Brock asked. "Because I know you’ve got it in you to be the best thing that ever happened to this business. It’s blowin? up kid and if you hit it hard and at the right time? WHICH IS NOW! Kid, the opportunities are ENDLESS! "? I’m talking MILLIONS of dollars, Brock. Millions." Brock thought to himself for a minute. He didn’t exactly have anything else going on and realistically couldn’t see himself working a nine to five? the only way he could see himself making money was by fighting. Plus he liked the sound of millions before dollars. "Alrite, well when do I start?" March 7, 1997. The two-a-day workouts wasn’t exactly the easiest routine to get in, but once he was in Brock Alyas pushed himself as far as he possibly could. Although just under the age of 17, Brock was working out and performing like he was a veteran in the art of wrestling. But what differentiated him from those he trained with was his heart. Scott D’Amore, the Can-Am Wrestling School Owner and Head Trainer was admiring Brock’s work ethic when his class for the day was wrapping up their warm-downs of lifting light weights and working on their conditioning by on the spot running. By the time the trainee’s were telling the students they were done of the week, Brock was off on his own and had no intentions of ending his daily work out. Scotty D’Amore was talking with a few friends within the business and appeared to be making fun of Brock as he was pointing over at him cracking some jokes that the others found funny. In fact ? he wasn’t cracking jokes about him at all. Instead, he was admiring the heart and determination that this kid had. "Don’t you think it’s time to wrap things up, Brock? I’ve got a family to go home to tonight, bud," Brock was kneeling over a bench press table and doing a series of up-lifts a workout based on your triceps and shoulders, a work out often done for pose purposes and often used by wrestlers right before they’re debut to give them that extra "pump". "Come on, seriously. You did great this week," Brock didn’t budge. Instead, changed arms and continued the work-out on his less dominant right arm. D’Amore and Brock were the only two in the gym now as everybody had made their way to the locker room and Scotty didn’t think he could find a better time to break the news to his extremely talented student. D’Amore found Brock’s stubbornness mildly humorous as it reminded him of a younger version of himself. In fact, D’Amore was training nearly just as hard as Brock was at the very same age, with dreams of also becoming a big superstar in a business that was moving towards it’s peak in value. Brock dropped the weight when his work-out was done and sat in the same bench, red-faced with sweat pouring down from his head. D’Amore handed him a damp towel and Brock used it to wipe his face before taking a sip from a squeeze bottle. "Look, kid. I’ve been meaning to talk to you. You’ve been training with us for four months and I don’t think there’s anybody I’ve ever trained that’s shown promise like you. Nobody works as hard. Nobody tries as hard. And there’s no way in HELL anybody works as long as you do," Brock enjoyed being praised. Despite the fact that a smile next to never appeared on his face, the youngster couldn’t hide one at this very moment instead tried by lowering his head. "I’ve got a proposal for you. I think you’re ready. I don’t think you’re done training but I think you’re ready for your debut," Brock’s eyes narrowed. He often heard Scott and the other trainers in the organization warn their students not to accept contract deals or offers before they’re ready because nine times out of ten they were subject to a poor debut, injury or a weak performance. "But, you always say you should be training for at least half a year before anything," Brock was quite confused. "And I’ve also never seen a kid respond to the training like you. It’s not just here in the gym, I’m talking in the ring, even in our ring etiquette classes ? you’re acting like a seasoned vet, man. You’ve got the character down pat," "Coach, I appreciate all of this but what are you talking about. You and I both know I don’t have a “gimmick”. I’m just myself, nothing changes," "?and THAT’S what I want. "?in Border City Wrestling," Border City Wrestling was a Windsor based company that offered and show-cased many of the Mid-west American / Canadian talents that competed in the ECW as well as have a serious future in the WWF (at that time). Scott D’Amore was not only the owner of the company but also the booker and promoter. He had full control of what went on and he was Brock’s ticket in and out of the minor leagues. Brock took a sip of his water before wiping his face again and trying his best to swallow the information at hand. One minute, he was told that he shouldn’t debut before he’s been training for at least a year and the next minute he’s being told that he should debut. "What do you thinks best for my career, Coach?" "I think? you vs. Tom Idol next week is what will be best," "Alrite. I’ll do it," March 17, 1996. The Ciociaro Club was packed this evening for Border City Wrestling’s biggest event of the year ? WrestleFest 96 and the card was jam packed with talent just waiting to be noticed including Chris Sabin, Petey Williams and Alex Shelley. While many of those backstage were just ready for another "night in the office" there was one member who was still very much an intern, in fact tonight was his "orientation". Brock Alyas had no idea what to expect. He made his way through to the backstage and past a couple fans who had no idea who he was, were just interested in some of the bigger names in the company. Brock believed that by the end of the night, these fans would know exactly who he was and he may even get the chance to sign an autograph or two. Brock found his locker and dropped his duffel bag in his compartment before sitting down and taking a deep breath. Here it was, his debut, his first test and the first taste of what was to come. He wasn’t worried, instead just anxious. He wanted things to go perfectly because he wasn’t exactly the type who had no issues with confidence, after all he was only seventeen. Although, he didn’t look it. He couldn’t have weighed more than 200 pounds soaking wet but he made any other seventeen year old look like a toddler. He sat in his locker room and revised what he’d learned in four quick months at Can-Am and promised himself he would do whatever it took to make it in this business. As soon as the show began and he heard the buzz from the crowd, he knew he’d made a good decision. “it would turn out to be a buzz that he would chase the rest of his life. “it would eventually become his heroine. “something he couldn’t avoid. “the sounds of those screaming fans and the ability to put people up and out of their seats. *** Brock’s ring entrance was nothing spectacular and he was introduced under the name Darccsyde. He decided to use an Eminem song that made the fans wonder what in god’s name he forced them to listen to. Only they weren’t ahead of the curve and being a downtown Detroit kid, Brock knew that he was about to blow up. He made his way to the ring and nobody even extended their hand for that one second embrace with a wrestler, instead they found it a perfect time to go get another hot dog or a refill on their beer. It didn’t help that it was a small crowd to begin with, but Brock figured if he fucked up, he would rather it in front of a small crowd than a big one. Rehearsing spots him and opponent Tom Idol went over the previous day, Brock paced back and forth in the ring and waited for the sound of the bell to get things started. From what the two worked out the previous day, Idol was the more praised wrestler and would eventually pick up the win. D’Amore always taught his students to "go with the flow" of things and use the fans response decide what the outcome should be. Although Brock agreed that Idol should pick up the W, something inside of D’Amore told him that he should introduce Brock the right way and have him string together a series of wins so that he would get over with the fans better. D’Amore had a strong feeling that Brock would one day be a big star in BCW. Idol just wanted his ticket out of BCW and onto bigger things. D’Amore realized this and had a plan had Idol refused to drop the match. Brock was completely unaware of anything D’Amore had planned and even that D’Amore wanted him to win. Despite the nature of the business, Brock agreed to take part only if things weren’t to be staged, he didn’t want to have anything handed to him. So with the bell sounding and the two making their way towards each other, Brock knew enough about wrestling that to pay your respects you out-stretched your hand so the other would either shake it or quickly slap it before it took place. It showed the fans that despite what was going on in the ring, the two respected each other and "let the best man win" was the general message of the interaction. Idol, being the heel that he was stopped dead in his tracks when Brock extended his hand. He looked at the fans and laughed before spitting right in Brock’s face and kicking him square in the gut. With Brock doubled over, a swinging neck breaker delivered with more force than Brock was used to in School, would put Brock on his back. Brock’s attempt to keep the match at an even-pace was denied by Idol and Brock discovered he was victim of a squash attempt. Brock learned in Can-Am that a squash attempt is when one wrestler belittles the other by not letting them hit a single move and making the crowd question their abilities in the ring and label them a "jobber" because they weren’t out there for anything but to make their opponent look good. It didn’t take long for Brock to realize what was really going on. So after selling Idol’s moves and even kicking out of his finisher, which he was well aware of what it was? Brock had enough of looking like a fool and rolled outside of the ring. Looking Idol directly in the eye he narrowed his eyes and his facial expression told a thousand words. Brock rolled back in the ring and insisted he wouldn’t be made out to look like a fool. Scotty D’Amore had the same idea because when Brock let the match switch hands and Idol take control again, D’Amore himself made his presence felt and his partner in crime, Chuck Fader distracted the ref while D’Amore brought a steel chair to the ring and smacked Idol square across the face with it. Brock wasn’t exactly impressed, D’Amore shot a wink at him before rolling out of the ring whispered to Brock that he should "finish" him off. Brock lifted his opponent into the air and used the finishing move that he and D’Amore had planned for him, a Wrist Clutch Exploder suplex that brought every fan in the arena to their feet. Brock knew his opponent was seriously hurt and he wasn’t na’ve enough to not realize that D’Amore broke some kayfabe in the match up by doing what he did. So when Brock rolled outside the ring and made his way backstage ? his reaction was less than expected. *** "WHERE THE FUCK IS D’AMORE?" D’Amore was excited to see Brock, but the look on Brock’s face made him question whether or not he made a good decision. "Brock! You looked great out there!
