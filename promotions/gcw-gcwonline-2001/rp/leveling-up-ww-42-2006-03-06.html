Leveling up (WW 42)

6 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Mar 6th ~for Leeroy~ *CRACK-CRACK* The sound echoed loudly throughout the near silent dojo. Rows of students observed in silence, resting on their knees; awaiting the opportunity to defend the honor of the dojo from the long haired gaijin that had walked so arrogantly into their midst this morning. Each held firmly their own shinai (kendo stick), hoping they would be the one to end this challenge. In the center of the dojo a student slowly circled the stranger, his nostrils flaring in rage behind his barred men (helmet). Three times he had attacked the foreigner in a flurry of strikes, and three times he had come no closer to touching the challenger then the man before him. The student gritted his teeth, plotting his next attack. Frustration was growing within the student; so much so that he could see it on his own face reflected in the highly polished helm of the foreigner. He pressed forward with a slashing attack only to see the challenger parry his blow and wheel behind him. Quickly the student turned, desperate to keep the foreigner in his field of vision; instantly he felt a heavy blow strike him at the base of neck, knocking him to the floor. Had this combat been real, he would have been dead. _________________________________________________________ For the first time on this trip the competitive fires of The Rogue Paladin were burning bright. The Second Swordsman of GCW (Let’s be honest, when you think of GCW swordsmen you think first of Almasy. That’s OK. We won’t hold it against you; yet.) was in his element. He had sought for this particular dojo in the foothills of Mt. Fuji specifically for it’s tradition of accepting any challenge. He had presented himself to the master in the proper fashion and quietly donned his armor despite the seeming sea of disdain about him. He had been disparaged for not donning the traditional bogu (kendo armor), but for wearing instead his chivalric armor and own colors. The same naysayers who would have believed this challenge a joke, found themselves nawestruck in spite of their notions when The Rogue Paladin brought forth his final piece of armor. The Dragon-helm of Calontir. Now this isn’t just a shiny helmet, it’s a piece of intricately handcrafted armor that is the stuff of legend in the SCA. For 5 years running, no man has been able to defeat The Rogue Paladin in single combat. Tournament after tournament, the emblem of the Dragon-helm near the lists marked the dread of any looking to steal victory unscathed. Its effect was near the same at the dojo in the shadow of Fuji. The Rogue Paladin strode to the center of the dojo, shinai in hand, and bade anyone brave enough to come forth. Five seconds and one resounding CRACK later, he bade the second forward. This continued for the better part of an hour, with The Rogue Paladin seeming to dance just outside the range of the students before flashing inside their guard to deliver what would have been a killing blow. After the ninth student fell, the master of the dojo rose to his feet; he would turn the challenge of the foreigner himself. The Rogue Paladin rested on one knee at the center of the dojo as the master prepared himself. When master finally approached, The Rogue Paladin saluted him and prepared for this final test. The master settled easily into a ready position, determined to best the gaijin and end his challenge once and for all. The flurry of the two swordsmen was near blinding in its speed and fury. Every thrust or strike was matched with a parry or block. For a full 20 minutes the fight raged on and on; the breath of every student watching became shallow in their anticipation and awe. As the battle reached its zenith, the rising fury in The Rogue Paladin let loose. Without thinking, he parried the master’s next stroke and swung forward with a kick that caught the elder square in the chest, sending the master reeling. As soon as the kick connected, the battle fervor that had come over The Paladin cleared, and he realized his mistake. He had violated the code of the dojo, a transgression that was made clear both by the anger in the master’s eyes and the rising note of rage coming from the 30 students who were now scrambling to avenge the dishonor. Moving quickly, The Rogue Paladin broke for the corner of the dojo. With his back to the wall at least he would be able to face the 30 students head on. In a quick bit of mental math, he came up with a 32.33% (repeating of course) chance for survival against this onslaught. Just as the horde was about to engulf him, they were stopped in their tracks by the sound of the dojo doors being kicked down. They looked back to see the imposing form of Meatshield Trainwrecker wielding a pair of clubs that looked suspiciously like the legs of the table the had been previously standing in the outer foyer. The student’s combined gaze was met evenly by the glare of the Trainwrecker. “Alright chumps, let’s do this,” bellowed the tank as he began charging across the dojo. Just as he collided with the first wave of students, Trainwrecker’s battle cry erupted throughout the room so loudly it shook the pillars. “LEEEEEEEERROOOOOOOOOY JEEEEEEEENNKIIIIIIINS!” The students were bowled over instantly by the bigger man. Seizing on the distraction caused by the Tank, The Rogue Paladin bolted from the sight of the students and made his way directly for the master. The students tried to follow, but their path was blocked by their fallen comrades and the massive limbs of Meatshield Trainwrecker; not to mention the two table legs that were now more rough and club like than they had been moments before, no doubt from several run-in’s with the masks of the fallen students. The Rogue Paladin and the elder met once again in battle. This time far less gracefully, as the two swung for each other with everything they had. In the distance, Meatshield was putting down the last of the students, their shinai shattering ineffectually across the back and shoulders of the Tank. The table leg proved itself a far more worthy and effective weapon in this instance. With a final rush, The Rogue Paladin, knocked the shinai from the master’s hand and with the tip of his own removed the elder’s helmet. With his “blade” held against the master’s neck, the Paladin finally spoke. “Yield and no further harm will be done.” The master surveyed the carnage in his dojo. His students were slowly stirring, unconscious or bloodied, but otherwise unharmed. The master looked once more into the eyes of The Rogue Paladin before lowering his gaze to the floor. “Hai, I am done.” he spoke, without looking up again. Meatshield Trainwrecker walked slowly to the wall of the dojo, and took down two of the intricately carved bokken (carved wooden swords) as The Rogue Paladin took his spoils, the men (helmet) of the master. The duo walked slowly to the door, trophies in hand. They took little time to savor their victory as greater battles for more valuable spoils loomed large in their future. But nothing could dissuade them from requisite witty adverturer-style banter. “Meat, where the hell were you?” asked Paladin. “I had to get something to eat and I saw a KFC up the road.” answered the big man. Paladin just smirked back at him, knowing what was coming next. “So you just left me to fend for myself against an entire dojo?” Meat just smiled and shrugged. “At least I got chicken...”
