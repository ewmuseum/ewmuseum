The Story Of Everything, Chapter One: Airports

2 July 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2008.07.02 I had devised quite an impressive scheme to smuggle a gaseous hallucinogen called Jenkem onto an airplane. It was made from fermented shit, which was no easy ordeal to obtain or create. I’ll spare you the details, but after proper preparation, I was able to inflate a pair of flotation devices meant to wear around the arms with the Jenkem. I wore them under the sleeves of the baggiest long sleeve shirt I could find, and if I was able to make it through the airport security check, I was in for quite a flight. Jenkem was the perfect drug for a long flight. There’s no waiting time, and the hours long trip is one of the most delightful experiences derivable from human feces. In many ways, it stood in direct defiance of airport principles. Within the whole rigmarole of air transport, flying was only half of the battle. The other half would begin as soon as security deemed me an acceptable passenger who posed no threat whatsoever to the safety and security of my fellow passengers. “Empty any coins, cell phones or other metallic devices from your pockets into the basket and step through the metal detector, sir,” drolly commanded a man in an airport security uniform as he waved a metal container at me. “No thanks,” I replied, waving him off with a shake of my hand. I traveled light. The only things I had on my person were the two flotation devices, a wad of bills tucked in my sock, some folded documents, one and a half packs of cigarettes and matchbooks from several places I had never been. I walked through the metal detector without incident, but as I passed another security guard, he caught a whiff of something awful emanating from me. I had a feeling it was the floatation devices filled with the vapors of human waste. Even the two cans of freshly scented aerosol that I had liberally applied onto myself had failed to disguise the offensive odor. I saw his nostrils flare with a nauseating suspicion, and I knew I had to play it cool as I walked past him. “You! Stop right there,” he exclaimed with a finger stretched towards me. I considered running, but I knew that my legs wouldn’t get me very far. I was outnumbered. I froze like a deer in headlights as the second guard approached me. He put his face up to my armpit and sniffed, immediately reeling back in disgust. “Pardon me,” I started. “I’ve been in and out of airports the last three days. I haven’t had much of a place to shower.” I feared he wouldn’t buy this, and in case he discovered the inflated, vaporous luggage under my clothes, I had also brought along with me a note from my psychiatrist, Doctor Wayne Gunn, that stated that my sever case of aquaphobia warranted the need for these somewhat unusual accessories. He looked me over. “Well, buy some deodorant at the newspaper stand and wash yourself up in the bathroom. I don’t want you disturbing or contaminating other passengers.” Insulted, I nodded, and I could feel his eyes on my back as I walked off. If he saw me violating his orders, it might arouse suspicion, so I purchased the deodorant and made a beeline for the bathroom. Once inside, I discarded the stick of deodorant in a trashcan and found an empty stall. I rolled up my loosely draping sleeve and slid the flotation device from my left arm. From there, I undid the plug and breathed in about as much of the vile gas as I could handle. I nearly vomited. “What in God’s name am I doing to myself” I wondered aloud. My retching lasted less than a minute as the effects of this curious inhalant answered by unhinging an instant euphoria. I knew that in twenty minutes, the hallucinations would kick in strong, and at that point, I would probably come across some people I knew to be dead. I was higher than a fucking kite. I dragged my feet out of the bathroom and towards the conveyor belt that led me to my gate. As the ground slid me forward, I took in the long, drab hallway and noticed how my proximity made the end look like the world ahead of me was shrinking. Because of the effects of the Jenkem, though, I feared that as the hallway shrank, I would stay the same size and get stuck. I began to panic and turned around to investigate the prospect of outrunning the conveyor belt. The belt was crowded, though, and I’d have no chance of making it out alive. “Fuck it,” I uttered, causing a few offended travelers to shoot me uncomfortable glances. I didn’t care. There was no way out now, and I had made my peace and conceded to my fate. My body would be forced forward until it fit through the horizon’s asshole. My bones would be cracked down to size in the process and my lungs would be popped against my sternum. Suffocation doesn’t seem so bad when you’re high on shit; higher than shit. I closed my eyes and smiled, waiting for the inevitable compaction written in my destiny, but the conveyor belt ended, and I fell flat on my face to the amusement or horror of those behind me. The sons of bitches just stepped over me, barely even trying not to step on my back, and I had to wait for a temporary adjournment in the conveyor belt line to get up and move to relative safety. “Fucking animals!” I barked under my breath, glaring at the backs of my aggressors as they strolled away, trolling with laughter. “You fucking hyenas!” I did my best to brush off the insulting incident and continued onto my gate, already feeling the effects of the Jenkem slowing me down. I looked around and wondered which ones were alive and which ones were dead, and I couldn’t tell one from the other. I sat down in a hard plastic seat and tried to ignore the maddening flourish of activity around me. A gentleman across the aisles was pecking feverishly at his laptop computer. A group of children terrorized each other as their helpless parents undoubtedly contemplated drowning the little devils. The thought was weighing heavily in my considerations as well; grabbing the miserable tots by their napes and dunking their heads underwater until their tiny limbs stopped twitching. That would be just what the doctor ordered. Still, more offensive was the man to my immediate left. He had sat down directly next to me, despite the fact that there was ample seating available. I turned to give him the eye, but he looked vaguely familiar. He looked like this guy I knew from certain social circles, except he was all burnt, and his skin had melted off. I think his name was Mark David. “You’re that guy that set himself on fire by the freeway exit, right?” “How’d you know?” he grinned, catlike. “You know, after you consider self-immolation, people only know you as the guy who set himself on fire. Sure, I did set myself on fire, but I don’t think that should define who I am.” “So what’s it like to be dead?” “It’s not bad,” he said, shaking his head back and forth. “It’s no party, but I can’t complain. I’ve met a lot of interesting people; mostly Namibians” He quickly changed the subject. “What are you doing here?” “I’m catching a flight,” I said. “It’s for my new job.” “What do you do?” “I’m not sure yet,” I half-chewed. “That’s what I’m hoping to find out.” “You don’t know?” “That’s what I just said. The guys who hired me weren’t too forward with any of the specifics,” I said, looking forward at nothing in particular. Even if I did know, I didn’t think I could trust this guy. On one hand, he’s dead, so he wouldn’t have much of an opportunity to tell anyone that might find a competitive use for this information. On the other hand, it never hurts to be too safe. “Does it have anything to do with the Jenkem?” “What the hell are you talking about?” “Come on, Johnny. Don’t bullshit me. You stink to high heaven of shit, and you’re talking to dead people.” “No,” I hastily spat. “Well, I can’t say for sure, but if so, it’s completely coincidental.” “I’m coming with you,” Mark David declared. “Why?” I asked, trying to subtly convey my disliking of his intentions. “I’ve got nothing better to do, and you’re on Jenkem. There are a lot more ghosts than there are Jenkem abusers, and I don’t really care for talking with other dead people. They’re generally more uninteresting than you’d suspect.” That got a short laugh out of me, but I couldn’t say that it wasn’t partially strategic. I needed him to trust me in order to keep him at a safe distance, and there’s no quicker way to gain someone’s trust than to laugh at his jokes. “I can’t guarantee that this job won’t involve violence,” I pried at him. “And didn’t you set yourself on fire to protest some war?” Mark David nodded with his hands folded under his chin. “I can’t have you interfering with my job. I really need this money,” I continued. “Fine. I won’t,” he affirmed. “But I want a cut.” “What?” “Twenty-five percent.” “You’re fucking kidding me, right” You’re dead.” “Twenty-five percent,” he repeated, unwavering. “Or I make things very unpleasant for you.” Something in his tone and mannerisms warned me that I should humor the burnt spirit next to me, but I never planned on paying him one red cent. I planned on devising a plan to string the dirty, deceased extortionist along for the ride and withhold any payment he assumed was coming to him. “I guess I have no choice, then. Twenty-five percent it is,” I conceded, smirking. “You’re a very wise man, Johnny.” And as he said this, I couldn’t help but notice that several of the passengers waiting in the embankment of seats were staring at me because I was talking to a dead man that those not afflicted with the unparalleled side effects of inhaling the stench of human waste could not see. *** We boarded the plane through the long metal that reminded me of the single, uncurled intestinal tract of a dissected worm. The line of human meat churned itself willfully through the grinder. We packed into our seats, and I guess the stench of sewage was enough to deter my immediate neighbors to seek other seats. “That’s good,” I said to nobody in particular. “I can stretch out now.” I took full advantage of the seating arrangements as the stewardess pantomimed through the safety instructions in the case of an emergency. Once the plane was ascending, I decided I needed some new surroundings and naturally left for the bathroom to ingest the other flotation device of Jenkem. It kicked in like a charm, doubling the effect. I realized that a small, tin bathroom was no place for a man on such a disorienting drug as Jenkem to be. The problem was that I didn’t recognize which of the walls around me was a door and which was a wall. It was a game of Russian Roulette, with no chance of survival whatsoever. I heard a knocking coming from somewhere, and my body flailed around with all joints bent to maximize the number of corners I had. The more corners you have, the more successful you will be at defending yourself from an attack. “Huh” Who’s there?” I shouted. “Sir, are you alright in there” You’ve been in the restroom for quite some time.” “Yeah,” I managed. “Just finishing up.” I circled the rectangular prism, pushing against the walls like they were Egyptian panels, and one would just magically slide open and activate some ancient pharaoh’s curse. I had haphazardly come across the handle in my turning and flailing. The door popped open beneath my disproportional weight, and I fell onto the floor, a blazing spectacle for these vultures. “It’ll be alright,” I told myself. “Things like this happen all the time. They’re perfectly normal. Standard protocol.” I stood up and a path cleared around me, like I was being digested by a snake; some kind of god damned cobra or something. I found my way back to my seats, but two of them were strangely occupied. That son of a bitch Mark David had made his way onto the plane, and there was a nihilist woman who overdosed on heroin at this party I had been to sitting in another one of the seats. The middle seat was left open. I sat in it, but I sure as hell didn’t like anything about it. I remembered the party clearly. We were both drunk and sitting at an oak table in the kitchen smoking cigarettes and drinking shots of rum after fooling. I was trying to nail the broad, and she was talking a big game, trying to impress me with heroin tales and claims that she was a nihilist. The instigator in me took over. “You’re a nihilist, huh?” I asked. “Prove it.” “Whatcha got?” she chirped. “Overdose on heroin right now.” She smiled at me, wasted beyond belief, and took out the supplies. I didn’t think the bitch was crazy enough to do it. I later rethought my courting strategies, realizing that they were ineffective. “What’s the score?” I asked. “I thought this was a “one-ghost-per-trip” deal.” “You took another hit, man,” replied Mark David. “Aren’t you going to say hi, Johnny?” “Shit!” I squealed. “What was your name again?” I fumbled around some more. “Hi.” “Eryn,” she said. “With a “y.”” “How worldly,” Mark David groaned. “Well, Eryn, it’s nice to see you again,” I chuckled nervously. “I guess you proved me wrong.” It was obvious that I wasn’t much of a card with women. “I guess I did. Who the fuck is this guy?” “It’s another ghost,” I said dismissively. “Excuse me?” shot Mark David. “I died to make a statement to stop the war. I’m a martyr. She’s just some lowlife who consciously decided to overdose on heroin.” “I resent that! I was fucking wasted, you asshole!” Eryn clawed. “Some civility, please!” I demanded. “And Jesus, Mark David. You didn’t make any difference by setting yourself on fire. Nobody has ever heard of you.” “Some people have heard of me,” defended Mark David. “I’m a cult legend.” “Maybe,” I rebutted. “But it’s the kind of notoriety where if I ask someone if they’ve heard of you, they’ll say no, and then after I explain who you are, they say “oh yeah, I have heard of him.”” “Johnny, I have something to tell you,” interrupted Eryn. I was beginning to think that all of this ruckus with the two argumentative ghosts would start to attract unwanted attention. I grew edgy. “What?” “I gave birth to your child six months ago. It’s a boy.” I didn’t know what to say. The mathematics of the whole situation cycled through my brains at fast paces with alarming inaccuracy. “That’s not possible. I saw you sitting at an oak table, we talked a bit, I dared you to overdose on heroin and then you overdosed on heroin.” “Then what happened after that?” “I don’t know. My memory of that night is a little fuzzy. I really only remember the bullet points.” “Well, think harder.” Shit. I guess I did nail the broad after all. “His name is Lennon.” “That’s a terrible name! How come you didn’t consult me?” “I’m sorry. I was dead. Remember?” “Christ, you don’t have to be mean about it.” I calmed my tone to ease the mood “How’s the little buck doing?” “He’s dead.” “Right.” That led to a bit of an awkward pause that lasted until a stewardess came by with the drink cart. “Can I get anything for you to drink, sir?” she asked with only the sunniest of dispositions. “Oh, no thanks,” I refused. “I’m a terrible drunk.” She laughed politely, as I listened to my deceased companions. “But I’d like two double shots of whisky for my friends here.” She laughed again, more slowly and uncomfortably this time. “Sir, we don’t serve shots of whisky in flight.” “Is that so?” I asked rhetorically, dejected. She just wheeled the cart on to the next row. “I want thirty percent now.” Mark David broke the silence suddenly. “Thirty percent of what?” I asked. “You know, I’m beginning to think that you don’t even have a job. I think you might be egging me on.” “I’m not egging you on,” I protested. “What do you need this money so bad for anyway?” “I’ve got my business, and you’ve got yours. You get me that thirty-three percent, and I won’t go prying around and interfering.” “You know, if you’re giving money out, it wouldn’t hurt to help me support Lennon.” “Is that why you’re here?” I asked. “And what happened to thirty-three percent?” “Interest,” replied Mark David. “No,” Eryn argued. “I want you to be a father to him. It’s hard enough being a single mother when you’re alive. It’s even worse when you’re dead.” “I can’t believe you’d still want me around after I convinced you to overdose on heroin and then, apparently, slept with you.” “I told you I was a nihilist,” she smiled coyly at me. “Can I just ask you one question?” I asked, technically redundantly. “Sure.” “Were you still alive?” I started, not intending to finish. “Was I?” She paused and understood. “Yeah, I was still alive.” “I love you,” I burst. “I love you, too.” “Thirty-three percent. Take it or leave it.” “Thirty-three percent. Fine.” *** The plane landed. I distinctly remember that the plane landed, but all my surroundings looked too familiar. Was this the same airport from which the flight originated? I wasn’t sure. I was on a plane. I was sure of that much, but where were the ghosts? Of course; they were at the baggage claim. I walked down the massive airport halls, and I grew more suspicious that I had been here before. Those sons of bitches! It’s highway robbery, I tell you. They take your money, spin you around in the sky and drop you down right where you took off. “I’ve been swindled!” The realization struck me and basically vocalized itself. “Fuck it. I’m going home. Fuck the ghosts. I know how to reach “em.” I walked around for a while, steaming, and the further I walked, the angrier I’d get, and the angrier I’d get, the more I’d end up lost. It was a vicious cycle, and I cursed this damned labyrinth as vociferously as I could muster. This only attracted the attention of airport security, those smug fucks. They happily subdued me and loaded me onto a golf course that they’d use to navigate the airport. “Where are you taking me?” I demanded. “Prison?” Neither of the guards escorting me in the tiny vehicle seemed too talkative. “It’s funny if you think about it,” I began. “Three grown men zipping through the hallway in a tiny cart?” I paused, but neither of the guards seemed to acknowledge that I was talking. “If you look at it in a more cosmic sense, you’ll surely see the humor in this situation.” The guards refused to consider an alternate viewpoint. Their simian upbringings must have rendered them closed to such possibilities. I felt sorry for them, briefly, but I quickly abandoned pity when the apes muscled me into a temporary holding cell where I would reside until the actual authorities arrived. “This cell can’t hold me,” I taunted. “I can bend these bars right apart.” I growled, and I spat, but it was of no use. I wore myself out rather quickly and sat in the corner of the cell muttering to myself and drowning in unpleasant thoughts. On top of that, my breath reeked of sewage, and I was being detained for an indeterminable amount of time. I wondered if I would make it to my appointments, and I wondered how much patience my employer would have for my state of pungent disarray. I didn’t want to think about work. And then there was the issue of the self-immolator who was blackmailing me and, of course, my dead son to take care of. I wasn’t fit to raise a child. I was in a world of shit. God, I hate airports.
