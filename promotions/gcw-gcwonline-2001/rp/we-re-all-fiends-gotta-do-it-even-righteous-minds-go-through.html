Date: pilot It wasn't a disruption. Nor was it discomfort. No light, no noise. There was little purpose for his awakening, outside of the simple fact that the time had come. A prolonged yawn and stretch accompanied the fluttering of his eyes, preparing for the dark surroundings. Only those first fleeting seconds were peaceful, as all waking moments were, until he began taking in his environment. The setting was a dank, hollow abyss - the only light coming from the sun, creeping through cracks of the mysterious structure that housed him. It burst through burnt floorboards and from openings on all sides, the source blinding and bleached white. His retinas quickly adjusted as he took a deep breath, remaining calm. Life was simple, this was simple - all he had to do was soak it in and maybe he'd arrive at some sort of conclusion. Then, and only then, would he decide where to go next. Despite everything, a few simple facts always remained: personality, looks, language, and impersonal knowledge. Two plus two would be four; the English language coated his tongue; he'd recognize himself in a mirror, save for blemishes and other minor cosmetic differences; and, oddly enough, he'd have a firm grasp of who he was. In a sense, that is. Everything else was evasive or temporary, and whether or not he'd recover the finer details of his past, it was simply a matter of time before they'd be lost once more. He'd never remember when, how or why he had laid down, and thus he woke up much like many times before: in the dark. From where he awoke, the ceiling was a mere three feet above: an abnormal height for any suitably sized living quarters. He decided that he'd been sleeping in a crawlspace, but for how long, he couldn't guess. It had been damaged; water and fire long left impressions that propelled the wood into a decayed state. The boards were uneven all around, as if it were built with leftover lumber. Some of the planks were covered in ash. Others, their grains were worn down and reduced to grooves. Sporadic holes decorated the structure with the wood jutting downward, as if someone had stomped their feet through from up above, leaving splinters and sunlight to creep through. He began to wonder how he'd slept so long and where he found warmth. How did he remain alive for however long he'd been there? Upon a quick pressing of his body, he found himself covered not only in clothing, but also draped in a thin-yet-strong, cloth-like substance. There were places that it felt firm and crisp, but much like the derelict structure providing his shelter, time and use had worn it from its once pristine condition. Deftly avoiding the protruding planks he scurried out from under the crawlspace, guided not only by the light from outside, but crisp air and cold winds alike. Upon escaping he was met with an amalgam of feelings caused by his surroundings. He was blinded; the sun's unobstructed rays reflected across a massive shimmering blanket, stretching beyond what little he could distinguish. Snow slowly began melting between his toes, telling him that his feet wouldn't remain warm for long. The elegant visage of pine-decorated, snow-covered mountaintops soon became clear; their presence looming in very short distances. Far and below, the faint makings of a city, or town, covered the base of the mountains. He found some difficulty when drawing breaths, likely accentuated by the mere sight of the elevation. Sparse clouds in the distance hung at his eyeline, and he was thankful that the weather was kind to him. Apparently, there wasn't a better time to awaken. He examined his shelter: a former cabin, long ago ravaged by fire. The ceiling had collapsed, as had some of the walls, and everything within had taken a distorted, charred state. He deduced that it had been in these conditions for quite some time - long before he ever made use of it, though why he chose such a place to reside in was still a mystery. Then he turned his attention to his own person. Despite the absence of shoes and the abundance of dirt that decorated him, he was dressed tastefully, clad in a suit colored onyx with a pearl shirt underneath the jacket. Most puzzling was the discovery that his blanket was no blanket at all, but a sheet of uncut, American one hundred dollar bills - in total, nearly $12,000 worth. Each detail raised its own perplexing questions, a cause for anyone in the situation to be overwhelmed and panicked. But he knew he'd find no answers where he stood, and so he began his long, arduous trek down the mountainside. One thing he could remember was the taste, smell, and longing for coffee, and all he could think about was how badly he wanted a cup. Time was irrelevant, though the sun had traveled a significant distance across the sky before he'd seen anything remotely resembling civilization. Halfway to the mountain's base, he stumbled upon a lone cabin, entrenched firmly into the steep slope. Normally it would be considered rude to invade the privacy of a person who'd obviously gone through painstaking lengths to maintain theirs, but he felt his situation dire - he needed coffee, and his frost-bitten feet could've gone with rest as well. He knocked firmly on the door, mostly expecting the place to be empty - in which case he had no qualms of breaking in. Not long afterwards, the door swung open, revealing a portly man. He appeared worn and tired, but his demeanor seemed friendly regardless. At first he appeared confused, as any recluse receiving a knock on their door would, but upon sight of his slovenly visitor, that look transformed into one of disbelief. "My God..." "Sorry if this comes out a bit rude, but you won't mind if I come in for a moment?" The doorway widened. "Of course, son, of course. Please. Get out of the cold; warm yourself up." He stepped inside, the old man shutting the door behind them. "Should I put a log on for ya?" "Oh, no. I'm fine. I'd go for some coffee, if you got any." "Sure, sure! Right this way, son." The old man guided him through his quaint home, around a few corners before they reached the kitchen. "Here, have a seat," withdrawing a chair from the dining table for his guest before turning to the counter behind him. "Thank you kindly." "Bah, don't mention it." He scooped a few spoonfuls of grinds into the filter before beginning the brew. "So, Mark, you still take it black?" "...what did you say?" "'Like my heart,' if I recall you sayin--" "You called me 'Mark'." "Well, that is your name. Don't tell me you forgot again..." Mark, if that was his name, wasn't sure about the best way to proceed. The old man correctly assumed his coffee preference, so he could have spoke truthfully. He decided to ask the most obvious question. "How do you know my name?" "Of course you forgot. That'd explain you lookin' like hell," he sighed. "I worked with your grandfather. Knew him nearly all my life. Well, all his life..." "My... my grandfather?" "Sure; he was the best Bridge player in San Miguel County. And if I ain't mistaken, he was buried in that there suit you're wearing." + + + Mark in the dark in... Can I Live? + + + The aroma was intoxicating, filling the kitchen with its distinct flavor. However long it had been was far too long. The simple sound of liquid pouring, filling the cup, caused Mark's mouth to salivate. "You've probably got a few questions," the old man said as the coffee reached the brim. He returned the pot to the heating plate before bringing the cup to his guest. Mark took it eagerly. The heat may cause others to hesitate before taking a sip, but he drank as if it weren't a factor. "Son?" "Oh. Right. Questions," he said, but words detracted from the enjoyment of his brew. "Well, I don't have the slightest clue of where to begin. Probably don't know the whole story myself... Do you remember anything? Your childhood, teen years - anything at all?" "Mmmm. Nothing." "Well, I guess that stuff ain't as important. Suppose I could start with-" "Mind if I get a refill?" "Goddamn, boy. You're fixin' to burn your tongue off," he chuckled, obliging the request. He stood from his chair slowly, age taking its unavoidable toll. "First thing you should know about is the family business." "The family business?" "MmHmm. It's how me and your granddad first met. Way back in ninetee--" A slight pop resonated throughout the room, followed by the sound of Mark's mug crashing to the floor. The old man spun around, clutching his neck as blood began to spurt from beneath his grip. He gasped for air as he plummeted to the ground, his eyes wider than they'd ever been. "What the fuck," Mark began to say, but was interrupted by rapid gunfire flying through the kitchen. He dove to the floor, sprawled across the tile next to his dying host. The blood pooled quickly, and just as the bullets stopped flowing did the man's struggle end. Mark crawled past his now lifeless body, in hopes of finding a safety he knew didn't exist. The ceasefire was only temporary. Whoever took those shots came to see their targets eliminated, crashing through the kitchen window. Mark looked back as he dove into the living room, seeing his attacker clad entirely in black. The gunfire followed him as he scurried towards the fireplace, grabbing the stoker from the set-up nearby. For a split-second, he found himself questioning his motives: why seek out something to use as a close-range weapon when someone with a gun was out to kill him? Why not try and escape? But that thought was put to rest with bullet-spray flying past his head. Rolling across the floor, Mark found temporary cover behind the dining set. He tipped the table on its side, pressing himself against the bulk of the wood, and waited for opportunity to present itself. The sound of the clicking firearm indicated that his attacker needed to reload - the precise moment Mark needed to take control of the situation. Guided solely by his instincts, he leaped over the table and saw the ski-masked assassin in the kitchen doorway, fumbling with a magazine at his belt, struggling to snap it into his machine gun. In that split-second he attempted to discard the weapon and prepare himself for close-quarters combat, but Mark had gotten the best of him. He struck with the curved prong of the stoker, lodging it deep within the side of his head. The assassin screamed in agony as Mark pulled his weapon free. He attempted to swing again, but the assassin, despite the gaping wound, managed to block the blow and kick him in the stomach, utilizing what Mark recognized as a steel-toed boot. Mark gasped for air, but the assassin left him no time to recuperate. He had produced a knife from his utility belt and dove at Mark to deliver a killing blow. If it wasn't obvious before, it was now - the man was a professional, and he'd come to make sure Mark didn't leave the mountain alive. He attempted to thwart his attacker with the stoker but the assassin pounced on him, knocking the makeshift weapon wayward. With the tip of the blade inches from face, the assassin forced all of his weight on top of him. Their strength was nearly equaled, and despite the leverage no man could gain an advantage over the other. The stalemate was only momentary. Mark found an opening and freed one of his legs, driving a knee into the man's abdomen. With the assassin knocked off of him, he scurried to his feet and attempted to reach the machine gun, but the masked assailant was right on his heels, tackling him into the kitchen before he could get a hold of it. They rolled around on the tile for a moment, struggling to gain the upper hand. The assassin found the advantage when Mark's hand slipped on the old man's blood, allowing him to position himself atop with the knife. The blade's sharp edge pressed right to his throat, Mark gasped what he thought would be his final breath, still attempting to hold the assassin at bay. There seemed to be no chance for escape this time. His eyes were fixed on his attacker's, staring wide at him through the slots of his ski-mask as blood dripped down his side. He was hell-bent on ending Mark's life. But the amnesiac would not give up. Frenzied and on the verge of meeting death, Mark's eyes darted about the room, searching for any means of escaping the assassin's blade. He glanced beside himself, finding the now-dead old man laying next to him and the remnants of what used to be his mug. Mark quickly grabbed a shard of the broken porcelain, so tight in his hand that it began lacerating his palm, and slashed the assassin's neck. The knife fell from his grip, narrowly missing Mark's throat on its way to the floor as he keeled backwards, struggling to breathe much the same as the old man. Blood drained from the wound, mixing with pool left from before. He fought to stay conscious, but after moments of struggling, he inevitably fell to the floor, right next to the man he'd just murdered. The once homely lodge was now reduced to a battleground, broken relics and crimson stains decorated throughout. In the only corner of the kitchen uncovered by the blood of the now-dead, Mark took heavy gasps to catch his breath. What just happened? And how did he defend himself, and survive, against what seemed to be a professional killer? He didn't know - he didn't care - but he wasn't going to remain there and ponder the possibilities. Someone else could have been on their way. For all he knew, they were waiting outside to fill him full of lead the moment he'd step out the door. Regardless, he wasn't safe in that cabin, and knew he had to get to the base before anyone else came after him. Upon examination of the now-dead assassin's boots, he found them close enough in size to his own. As he began to take them off of his feet, Mark found another firearm - a handgun - holstered to his ankle. Why the assassin didn't use it, he didn't know, as it was loaded full with hollow points. He took the boots and the gun, as well as a pair of socks from the old man's dresser, knowing his host would not object. He then searched the cupboards for a thermos, filling it to the brim with coffee. Then, just as he was about to resume his trek down the mountain, a thought occurred to him. While he didn't remember anything or anyone from his time before waking up in the crawlspace, he began thinking about who his would-be assassin was, and whether a look at his face might jog a memory. He figured there wouldn't be any harm in it, so he returned to the kitchen and wadded through the blood, leaning over the body. As he peeled back the blood-soaked ski-mask, the man that looked up at him was startling, as the still-open eyes eerily seemed to peer into his own.
