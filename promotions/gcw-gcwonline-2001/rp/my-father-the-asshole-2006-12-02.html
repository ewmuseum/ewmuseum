My Father the Asshole

2 December 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12.02.06 Throughout my twenty-six almost twenty-seven years of existence on this horrible, despicable place I call home I have felt nothing. Most people who turn out to hate the world have horrible teenage years, they have been molested, and they have been beaten. To keep a long story short they have a real reason to have turned into a hatred for this world. But there is the problem I haven’t turned it into hatred, to turn it into hatred you need to have happiness first. I don’t think since my first breath on this earth have I ever been happy. On the outside I might have placed on a show, my tenure at PRIME I may have made it seemed like I enjoyed it. I may have made it seem liked my time with Melissa, but deep down the bitch destroyed me. The test of time has come my childhood was nothing to speak about, my whole existence has been nothing but a horrible tenure, but I am hoping to turn that around with my time in GCW. The one thing in my life that reminds me of why I was never happy, any success I ever achieved was never good enough. I mean if I got all A’s on a report card the first question from my father was why did you slack of? He would look at me in all seriousness and say I expect A+. You are obviously capable. Looking back I know that he was trying to push me. He knew what I was capable of and wanted me to achieve it. The only problem with that is he never once said I did a good job. Any achievement I made I could have done better, wait rephrase that I should have done better. I know he pushed me to try and become a better person; he only wanted me to be the best I could be. But at the end of the day without any support I turned into nothing. I turned into a cocaine snorting, crack head that lived and died each day depending on the drugs or alcohol that I was on. Thanks Dad. Thanks a lot. To this day I remember the words of my father the day I hit my only homerun in baseball. I mean I played baseball pretty well wasn’t the best on the field but also wasn’t the worse. Thing was I was a contact hitter I could put the ball where I wanted to when I wanted to. So my last year of Babe Ruth after being selected to the Tri-County All Star Team I had one of my last games in a tournament in a near by town. I am going to relive this for you and want you to understand why I can fell the hurt, why I can feel the hatred for my father. This right here is how everything in my life has ever been my father’s work first, his son’s life second. June 6th Eight years ago?. BEEP? BEEP?. I roll over and hit the “SHUT THE FUCK UP” button on my alarm clock. I wipe away those crusty things you find in your eyes when you first awake in the morning. After wiping my eyes I try and focus on the time, 6:30 AM. What away to spend your summer when you are going to a senior in high school. I spend 40-60 hours a week working for my father in his business, and when I’m not forced to do that I am chilling with the wifey or playing ball. The other two loves in my life. Usually in the morning I have to drag my ass out of bed. But a couple hours after work I am going to be playing in the local baseball tournament. I get up quick to lay out my uniform, also putting on my work clothes for the day. You know I still have to go to work. Today I am installing a septic system down the street from where I live with my father. After getting ready and lacing my boots up for a day’s worth of work I make my way to the kitchen. Throw a thing of English muffins in the toaster and pour myself a cup of coffee. I look over at my dad who is reading the paper for the day. “You know I need to leave early today right?” He doesn’t even look up from the paper, he just responds. “And why is that?” “I have that game at Bicentennial Park a 4:30.” I looked at him like how can you have forgotten I have only told you about a million times. “Then take your own truck.” Still he doesn’t flinch for what he is reading; he doesn’t even change his mood. “I will do. Mom and I are going to be leaving here not later then 3:45 we have to be at the park for 4:00.” This was my way of trying to get him to agree to him going. If I could actually get him to go that would be a major milestone for me. “Alright I will try and meet you guys here so we can all go up together.” This time he put the paper down and looked at me as he took a sip of his coffee. “But go eat your breakfast before you are late, you know Uncle Richie will be over at he job promptly at 7:00 and if you are late you know he will make your day a living hell. What he didn’t understand was that most days for me were a living hell. I work all family. We have my father the owner, my uncle who acts as the foreman, then we have a cousin who is a foreman/machine operator in training, then you have me as a laborer. Of course we a have a rotation of truck drivers but they are not consistent enough to really matter. I run over butter my English throw my coffee into a To-Go mug and run out the door. On the way I turn around and scream back, “Call you at lunch, love ya Mom!” With that my day started, the usually things happen I work my balls off for my family and get nothing besides belittlement and horrible treatment. The ego on my uncle is probably about the biggest thing I have ever seen in this world. Although he had an ego he was good at what he did, too bad he couldn’t just know he was good and leave the ego checked at the door. The morning went uneventful, Richie and I working side by side him in the excavator barking orders, telling me what to do and how everything I do is wrong. I continue to work hard and keep up with him to show him that I really can do the same work as him. Lunch time rolls around I start to eat my lunch and like most days my father calls either Richie or I to see how things were going. With that I got a two way on Nextel phone and respond to it accordingly. “Go ahead.” “Adam, how you guys making out?” “Not to bad Dad, we should have this system piped together for the end of the day so I will probably call the inspection in around 2:30 and see if we can get him out here this afternoon.” As I spoke with him I continued to munch on my sandwich I had just picked up from Subway and drink my large fountain drink. “Sounds good, I will talk with you later.” He is quick to always get off the phone. For some reason he always has someone else to call, I mean whenever you see him on a job site the phone is in his ear, I don’t know how he gets any work done. Before I say goodbye I ask him about this afternoon’s game. “Are you going to be able to meet me and mom to up to the field with us?” “What time are you leaving?” I feel like throwing the phone because I have told him three times already today, but I know he has a lot on his mind and I just need to remind him? AGAIN! “We need to leave no later then 3:45” “Yeah I will be home by then see you later.” With that he hangs up the phone and me and Richie go back to work. Spend the rest of the day piping the septic system together and getting it ready for our 4:00 inspection that I was going to have to miss. The rest of the day I’m constantly staring at my watch. Three O’Clock did not come around fast enough but when it did I was on my way home to get ready. I drove home at about 80 mph hoping not to get a speeding ticket. I got home and rushed in the door taking my boots of outside like normal seeing as they were all muddy. Rush in the house and see my mother there folding some clothes. As I run by I yell,” Hey mom! Have you heard from dad yet?” As I am still running away she yells back, “Not yet, why what did he say?” I had now entered my room so the noise was muffled a little more.” All he said was he would be here.” I hurry to get ready as it is already 3:30. I get dressed and get all my gear together and make my way to the living room where my mother is. “Are you ready?” “Yeah, but dad isn’t here yet and he isn’t answering his phone, I just called him and left a message thou.” You could tell by the tone in her voice that she was concerned that he would not make it like he said he would but the only thing that would tell us that is if we just wait and see. Being a teenager at the time I am eager to get to the field and start playing ball 3:45 comes and goes and still no phone call or sign of my father anywhere near here. I look at my mom and it’s almost 3:50. ? I guess its time to leave, if I want to start like I am supposed to I need to be on time or the coach will put someone else in my place.” My mom sighed heavily and we got in the car and took off. Getting to the field in just enough time parents filled the stands some of the older kids had their girlfriends in the stands. Warm up goes as normal I keep looking to where my mom is sitting to see if my father was there, knowing very well this could be my last Babe Ruth game. Still no signs of him at the opening pitch. It’s the top of the first we are at bat. I am batting fifth because of my tendency to make good contact and usually get a double or so ensuring any man ahead of me would either score or be in scoring position after I bat. The first inning was going our way against a team that is the tournament favorite. Our first hitter got a single and was then able to steal second; we have our second hitter strike out, a loss but not that big of a deal. I mean hell we have a man in scoring position with one out. The third and fourth batters both get walked. This puts me at the plate. Bases loaded top of the first with one out not a bad situation for me. I am looking to make some contact and get a run or two in. As I make my way to the plate I take a final glance over to where my mother was still sitting. Just as I expected he was not here, yet again he broke another promise. I get to the plate and look for a double, I look at the position of the fielders and notice the 10 foot wall out in left field with the left fielder playing shallow, there was my target that wall. I step into the batters box, dig in and await the first pitch. First pitch fastball, I turn on it and I turn on it fast. A hard hit high line drive to left field, just where I wanted it to go. I started to run, not the fast kid on the team but I could hold my own. By the time I started to round second I realized what had just happened. The fans were screaming, my teams all had scored, my third base coach kept signaling me. At this point I start to slow down to a jog and realize I had just hit my first home run ever. It wasn’t just any homerun I was able to hit a grand slam! As I round third base and head for home once again I am staring face to stands once again and see the disappointment that my father did not get to see my Grand Slam. I jump on home plate signifying the run and am bombarded with my teammates cheering me one knowing his was my only homerun ever. I pretend to be happy but in the long run I really am not, I wish he could have seen it to be proud of me. The rest of the game really wasn’t significant, we ended up winning and I took home the player of the game ball along with two others. At least my father was there to see that. I mean he showed up around the third inning. We all leave the stadium in high hopes of maybe having a chance to win the whole tournament seeing as we just beat the tournament favorite. That however didn’t grace my emotion mine was filled with anger, once again my father missed something for work. I knew he meant well but that did not console my broken heart. The game was over and everyone walks to their parents who they came with. I walk over to my father in a high hopes and anticipation, ? I hit a GRAND SLAM!” “I heard.” The tone in his voice was something left to be desired. You could tell he was not happy that he missed it. “How come you missed it” We kept calling you.” “I was there right at 3:52.” “We told you we needed to leave at 3:45!” At this point I was getting angered, ? Just like you to leave and not wait for others.” When he said that to me I felt a tear coming on but knew that would only make things worse. “Dad you said you were going to be here but you weren’t. Not my fault.” Now I was fully pissed and walking away with my bat bag over my shoulder. “I was ten minutes late, sorry!” “Well those ten minutes would have made it so I couldn’t have played and you wouldn’t have seen anything, not my fault you never fulfill your promises.” “Well I guess I will just see you at home then!” I got into the car since we had walked up to it already, in my head so many thoughts were in my head at this moment. Not once did he say good job, not once did he even congratulate me. No he was to busy being pissed of that we didn’t wait for him. Well if that asshole had kept his promises then he would have been here but no he was late, we waited as long as we could. Back to the current time and date?. This moment in my life is one of the most detrimental. The biggest achievement I have gotten in my baseball career hell probably in my life to that point and he couldn’t even say good job, he was to pissed that he had missed the game. This would just be the start of the beginning of accomplishments being overlooked because he had something more important or he missed it because of work. Still to this day I have never heard a compliment from him, still to this day that day hurts me inside, I wish my father could have at least looked at me and say, “Good job son, sorry I missed it.” No that is impossible for his stubborn French blood to handle. He had to have the last word and had to have it his way? Well times have changed. I don’t need his support and no longer do I get it since his death?. But that’s nothing new.
