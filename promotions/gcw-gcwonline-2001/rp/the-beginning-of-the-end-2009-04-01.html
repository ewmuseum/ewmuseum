The beginning of the end?””””

1 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4-1-09 Mr. Danger sits in the waiting room of his doctor’s office in Kansas City. As he anxiously awaits his name to be called, thoughts and worries about his current health situation run through his mind. After all, he is 42 years old and his career in the wrestling business hasn’t been kind to his body. He continues to wait patiently as he starts to think about his match with Dan Black at Shockwave. Danger himself was just informed a few days ago that the match is now no disqualification. He worries that one more brutal match like this one may be the one that puts him out of action forever. Although he is more content with his spot right now in GCW as just a wrestler, he still doesn’t want to risk his health if it all came down to that. The door to the waiting room opens and a nurse appears in the doorway. Nurse: Mr. Fisher? Right this way please. Danger pauses for a moment and then realizes she is calling him. It isn’t often that he is referred to by his real name. He gets up and follows the nurse into one of the patient rooms and takes a seat on the examination table. Nurse: And what are you in for today Mr. Fisher? Danger: Just a routine physical. I’ve got a big match coming up this weekend and wanted the doc’s opinion on how I’ll hold up. Nurse: Okay, let me go get him. It shouldn’t be too long. The nurse exits and closes the door behind her. Danger is now starting to get a bit nervous as to what the doctor might have to say. The last time he talked with the doc the news wasn’t promising. He advised Danger that his knees were in pretty bad shape and to limit his in ring work. That was 6 months ago before his return, so Danger hopes that his condition hasn’t worsened. There is then a knock at the door and it opens slowly as Dr. Johnson walks in with a big smile on his face. Dr. Johnson: Mr. Fisher, or should I call you Mr. Danger!” Long time no see, how in the hell have you been? Danger: I’ve been all right I suppose. Holding up just fine. Dr. Johnson: Good, good to hear that. How’s the ring been treating you? Are you still trying to limit your matches to few and far between? Danger: Well, not quite. The reason I’m here is because the head office in Columbus needs medical clearance for me to wrestle this weekend. I guess those lawyers don’t want to take any chances. I’m sure I’m fine though doc. Dr. Johnson sits down at the desk and starts thumbing through Danger’s medical charts. Dr. Johnson: Uhhh huh. Uhhh huh. Hummmmm. Danger: Everything check out okay on the medical papers they sent over? Dr. Johnson: Well the GCW head medical officer faxed this all over this morning. It appears that your most recent MRI has indicated that your knees are in worse shape than 6 months ago. Have you been wearing your braces? Danger: Yeah doc, and I feel fine. I can still do everything and the pain is only minimal. Dr. Johnson: Something tells me that you’re not being totally honest. When I cleared you 6 months ago it was under the condition that you would wrestle a minimal schedule. I can still grant you a release to wrestle this weekend and maybe a bit longer, but honestly one of your knees could go out at any time. If you land on it the wrong way, it would be career ending for sure and definitely require surgery. Danger: So I’m good to go then right? All I need is for you to sign the release form and I’m out of here. Dr. Johnson: I want you to think about this long and hard. I know you’re doing something that you love, but when you are putting your permanent health at risk, that’s a decision you are going to have to live with if something happens. I’m telling you that as of right now, one of those knees could give out at any time. Danger: Well that’s just a risk I’m going to have to take. Just sign the damn paper and I’ll get out of your hair. Dr. Johnson lets out a sign and shakes his head. He signs the release paper and hands it to Danger. Dr. Johnson: Well there you go. Good luck this weekend in your match and remember what I told you. After this match you need to think about this long and hard. Danger: Thanks for the honest opinion doc. Danger gets up and shakes hands with the doctor. He leaves the office and starts thinking about what the doctor had told him. What if he’s right? Is continuing his in ring career more important than his health? Danger was torn on what to do. As he got into his car he decided to call his Dad for some advice. Dad: Well hello son, how are you? Danger: Dad I need some advice. I’m torn on what to do. I just came from the doctor’s office and he kinda gave me some bad news. Dad: Oh? Danger: He pretty much told me that every time I get in the ring I am risking permanent knee damage. I mean I feel fine, and as of right now I’m fine, but all it would take is one bad fall or bump and I’m screwed. Dad: Well son, how much does being in the ring mean to you? Are you happy doing it? You’ve got to weigh the positives and the negatives against each other and find out what makes you happy. How much do you enjoy what you do? Danger: I love it Dad. I might be towards the end of my career, but I savor every moment I have. I absolutely love doing what I’m doing right now and I don’t want it to end. Dad: Well that sounds pretty sincere to me son. I think you just need to follow your heart and do what you love to do. If you are still physically able to do it, by all means I think you should stay with it as long as you can. Not many people in this world can say they have been given the opportunities that you have son. Danger: As of right now I can still do it, but like the doc told me Dad, the knee could snap at any time. Dad: Son, I know how much you love the wrestling business. And I know you’ve been given another chance to succeed in it. By the way you’re talking I think you know the answer already. Danger: I do love wrestling, its just I don’t want to raise my kid in a wheelchair the rest of my life. Dad: Nobody ever said life was filled with easy decisions. Follow your heart son, I know you will do what’s right for you. Good luck this weekend, I’ll be watching. Danger: Thanks Dad, bye. As Danger hung up the phone he started to ponder what his father had told him. He heard pretty much just what he wanted to hear. The only thing was that he failed to tell him the match this weekend is going to be a brutal no disqualification match. Danger knew that Dan Black is hell bent on ending his career, and will do whatever is necessary to end it. He thought to himself as long as he can make it out of this match okay, everything should be fine. Or at least he hoped so. ************************************************************************ It’s the next day and Danger has just boarded a plane bound for Honolulu, the site of Shockwave. He sits down in his first class seat and closes his eyes. After all the stress thinking about his career the past few days, Danger is ready to relax on his long flight to Hawaii. Just after takeoff Danger dozes off for a few hours. When he wakes up, he looks over to his right on the other side of the plane. There is a young boy staring at him grinning huge ear to ear and next to him reading a magazine is who appears to be the mother. Danger smiles back at the boy, who immediately gets out of his seat and approaches Danger. Boy: Can I bother you for an autograph sir? Danger: You don’t have to call me sir, Danger is fine. And sure thing kid, it would be my pleasure. What’s your name son? Chris: It’s Chris. Chris Brown. Can you sign my magazine? Danger: Sure thing kid. You a big fan? Chris: Yes Mr. Danger, I am a huge fan! I watch WorldWide every week never miss an episode. I’ve never met a wrestler before, this is so cool! Danger makes out the autograph on the magazine. It is a Sports Illustrated magazine, and the kid is still smiling ear to ear as Danger signs it. Chris: Thanks so much, I’m gonna keep this magazine forever! Danger: Anything for a fan you know. So you into sports I see. What’s your favorite sport son? Chris: Basketball. I love to play even though I’m not any good at it. I tried out for my school team but I guess I wasn’t good enough. Danger: Oh come on now, practice makes perfect. You just have to stick with it and I’m sure you’ll make the team next year. How old are you? Looks like you still have plenty of time to train and improve your game. Chris: I’m 13. I just love to play the game that’s all. I practice with my friends all the time in the driveway, we always have fun together. Danger: Yeah kid, that’s what its all about. Having fun. And the more you practice the better you’ll get, and when next season comes around if you devote yourself I’m sure you can make the team next year. Chris: Yeah, next year I guess. Coach told me that too. Danger: You know what kid, let me tell you a little story. When I was your age I loved wrestling. I wanted to be a professional wrestler when I grew up, but I wasn’t a big kid at all. I was about your size actually. I wrestled in high school and was okay I guess. When I got out of school all I wanted to do was wrestle. Everybody told me I was crazy, that I would never make it. So I trained, and trained, and trained some more. I spent countless hours in the gym and working out trying to get in shape. My family thought I was nuts but I was so devoted to proving them all wrong. I wanted more than anything to make it, and look at me now. I made it pretty big, made a great living, and I’m still loving it. Just goes to show you kid, if you really want to do something in life training and dedication and the will power to improve will pull though in the end. Chris: So are you still loving your job? I read on gcwonline.net yesterday that this match with Black is going to be your last? Are you retiring? Danger: Well a lot of that is politics. More goes on behind the scenes than you can even begin to understand. There is just a lot of obstacles for me to overcome in the office, and my age and health are weighing a big part in my decision also. Chris: But you still love what you do right? You still have fun when you go out there don’t you? Danger: Absolutely. I love performing more than anything in the world. It’s a feeling that can’t be duplicated. It’s just when you get older like I am that other factors start to influence you. Chris: I don’t understand why you are thinking about giving it all up. Just like you told me, if you love what you do don’t let anything stop you! I hope you kick Dan Black’s ass this weekend! I’ll be pulling for you. Danger: Thanks kid, it really does mean a lot to me. The mother puts down her magazine and lets out a sigh as she motions for her son to come back to his seat. Mother: I’m really sorry sir, I know you are probably trying to relax. I won’t let my son bother you again. Danger: No problem at all madam. He’s not bothering me really. We were just having a little conversation about, well guy stuff. Danger shakes the kid’s hand as he returns to his seat. He is very excited about his autograph as he shows it to his mother. Danger just reclines in his seat and smiles. He starts to think about what little Chris had told him. Was the answer really that simple? Maybe it’s time for him to start acting on his own advice. He thought about how much he loves what he does, and decided that he wasn’t going to let anything get in his way of performing. As long as he can still physically compete, he was going to. After all, if you can’t enjoy what you do in life to the fullest then what’s the point? As the plane started to descend into Honolulu, Danger had made up his mind. He was now more confident than ever about the match with Black. No matter what happened, Danger was determined to give it his all in the brutal no disqualification match, but more importantly he was going to have the time of his life performing in front of the live crowd and all his fans watching at home. He had forgotten that was the reason he has loved this business for years. Even if something were to happen and this was destined to be his last match, at least he was going to Shockwave to give it all he had and was going to savor every moment of this match, and perhaps at the end, his career.
