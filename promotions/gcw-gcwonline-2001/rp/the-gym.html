The Gym

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Present Day ---- Thom LeStrange's personal gym. New York City. Early evening. ---- In the wash room of his personal gymnasium, Thomas James LeStrange bathed in utter tranquility. The bath he reclined in was a fine creation - large, octagonal, a one-off build commissioned by LeStrange himself, and specially decorated by the finest mosaique artist in New York. There were no hot or cold taps - simply one faucet and a small, waterproofed LCD touchscreen, which controlled the room's dedicated standalone water heater to the degree. The water heater not only warmed the water to the faucet, allowing the user to choose their exact temperature of their bathwater, but was also linked to panels beneath the bath, which would maintain the temperature too. Also accessible via the LCD touchscreen was the gym's entertainment system, located in the control room. A vastly expensive Bang and Olufsen speakers were hung from brackets around the room, and were capable of playing music at the most sumptuous quality. Leaving no possibilities unconsidered, a set of waterproof underwater speakers were built into the inside of the bath, for the aural pleasure of the submerged bather. An extravagance really, LeStrange had often mused - he couldn't really hold his breath long enough to appreciate an Ewald brass quintet from a new, aquatic perspective. The room was eqaully lavish. The understated and elegant tiles lining the floor looked cold and uninviting, but they were in fact heated, as was the large towel rail that stretched the length of one wall. It was lined with a gamut of towels - from large bath towels, to hand towels to face flannels. A particular habit of Thom's was to drape his bathrobe over the heated pipe so that when, after his bath, he donned it, he would feel warm flannel against his skin. The walls were painted in a simple, but elegant creamy beige colour. He had had black-and-white photographs from his personal collection blown up and framed, and they now hung on the walls, so that in his moments of relaxtion he could survey in nostalgic fashion the wonderfully noble main building of Bedford School - lush, verdant cricket pitch in the foreground, red-brick main building looming in the background, the sun glinting playfully off the long windows of the Great Hall. Next to it, in another frame, the rural idyll of his beautiful Bedfordshire home could be seen beyond the delicate hanging leaves of a willow tree in the foreground. Another frame proudly displayed the beautiful Main Court of his old college - St. Catharine's College, Cambridge - the beautiful kept lawn, forbidden to all, the wonderful ironwrought lanterns, the heavy oak door leading to the Dining Hall. All images which invariably relaxed Thom in his moments of bathtime repose, transporting him effortlessly back to his native England in Proust-like memory. Beyond the wash room, almost serving as an antechamber, was the changing room. Rather a large room for the one and only member of the gym, but this couldn't be helped. Thom had had it redesigned so it was much like the dressing room in his Bedfordshire manor. An enormous mirror adorned one wall, making the already rather oversized room seem even larger. Facing it, against the opposite wall, was an exquisite oak bench for changing with a shoe rack underneath. Built into the wall were rather fancy silverwork hooks for his clothing, and to one side stood a Corby trouser press. Next to this was a three-shelved fridge - on the top shelf lay many bottles of mineral water, essential for rehydration after exercise. On the middle shelf lay several expensive bottles of champagne, as a personal reward for a good sweat pumping iron. On the bottom shelf lay a selection of fine cheeses and pates, with some delicious bruschetta. One had to spoil oneself sometimes. In a small cupboard nearby lay a change of clothes - another of Thom's beautifully tailored Jermyn Street suits - and some toiletries - deodorant, toothpaste, a razor, and aftershave. An oak panelled door led from the changing room to the gym itself, a large, airy room with high windows. The floors were polished wood of high quality. Row upon row of exercise machines covered the floor, each with a mat underneath to protect the perfectly buffed floor. The room was practically a catalogue for gymnasium equipment, and it appeared not one machine, or even variation of a machine, was absent. More expensive speakers were attached to brackets at regular intervals along the walls, which were decorated in the same creamy shade of ivory-beige as the other rooms. All in all, this was a beautiful gym - it almost seemed wasted one sole user. But not in Thom LeStrange's mind - in fact, that was why he bought it. It had previously been a small local gym for the neighbourhood - not part of a large corporation;, with exorbitant prices, intimidating and overbearingly muscular staff; all protein drinks, supplements and superficiality designed to instill an inferiority complex in each and every customer so they would sign away their life working at a physique that they could never attain and didn't need to anyway. This was a friendly local gym for people who just enjoyed a bit of exercise, wanted to lose a few pounds, and liked a sense of community. And in one fell swoop, Thomas James LeStrange had swooped in, opened his chequebook, and bought the place out, gutted it, rebuilt it, and created his own exercise sanctuary. Cruel and selfish, many might say, but you had to look at it from his point of view - who gave a monkey's?! The reason Thom had built this ludicrously expensive, wholly selfish and singularly un-utilitarian edifice was not really because he didn't care for the greedy corporations - if anything he sympathised. Nor was it due to any great contempt for small local businesses, even if they were irritatingly and incessantly upbeat and chirpy. No, it was because he detested with a passion people who went to gyms - any of them, all of them. If gym members are a cross-section of society as a whole, Thom often thought, then Western civilisation is utterly contemptible. Example number one - the gym horse. These sweating, chauvinistic meatheads often caused Thom to lose all faith in the male sex, if not humanity itself. The furrowed brow and listless expression of the neanderthal, accompanied with their appropriate atavistic, pre-linguistic, caveman-like grunting, naturally led Thom to assume that the complex notion of repeatedly lifting something heavy was about all they could handle. Often distinguishable by bald heads, wifebeaters (not so much an item of clothing as a job description, Thom often postulated) and tattoos, and they arecharacterised by three traits that Thom detested most - ignorance, excessive machismo (perhaps the most pathetic thing of all), and an abysmal sense of humour. Example two, the businessman. The arrogant, self-important twirps that frequented gyms were an insult to money. "Bloody nouveau riche..." Thom had often murmured. Each one of them identical to the next, a clone of another, all believing they were the centre of the universe. Supercilious arses with tacky Italian sportscars, who seem to find it impossible to adhere to any of the rules of smart dressing despite their claims of sophistication; who refuse to wear a tie with a suit ("It's casual Tuesday at the office"), who truly believe that one can wear a sports coat with jeans, who insist upon using their laptop or their Blackberry in public at any available opportunity, as if to advertise their status, to validate it, oblivious to how god-damned rude and anti-social they are. "Come back when your family's got hundreds of years of aristocracy behind it, you own a whole county, and you know what it is to be a gentleman", Thom often seethed. And then of course, there were the middle aged women waging a prolonged and explosive war against the tides of age, squeezing themselves into the tightest velour possible, bleaching their hair peroxide blonde and wearing more makeup than an air stewardess. Thom found this phenomenon of the menopausal woman most tedious - he much preferred the sitting-on-the-stairs-cradling-a-half-empty-bottle-of-gin-moaning-plaintively-that-no-one-has-eaten-her-vol-au-vents approach. At least that was entertaining. All this begs the question; why not just build a home gym? And the answer is simple. To build a home gym is to be in denial about being a gym character. Thom's solution was to build his own private gym - thus he could avoid being lumped in with the pathetic stay-at-home gym users, and also avoid the risk of his own bile poisoning him every time he visited a public gym. And so we return nicely to where we began our scene; Thom LeStrange reclining into the magnificent bath of his opulent exercise palace. As the warm water relaxed every inch of his toned body, he was able to enter a state of complete tranquility and contemplate matters at hand. His short time in the GCW so far had been exhilerating to say the least. There was not a single aspect of the job he hadn't enjoyed. His first ever WorldWide had been most entertaining - sanctioning his own weekly item had been much easier than he'd expected. And the results had far exceeded his expectations - the moronic fans had enjoyed it! "How could they not?!" he had mused - after all his quick wit and natural charisma was difficult to deny, but he had openly mocked American culture and they had lapped it up. Perhaps they didn't understand irony. Or maybe they enjoyed self-mockery. In any case, Thom couldn't complain - manipulating people's emotions was a particular favourite pastime, and who didn't enjoy indulging in a spot of narcissism? His thoughts turned to NC-17. It had been a while since he had seen proper ring-action. His body still bore the aches and pains, but it had been worth it - a chance to showcase his abilities in front of a record crowd, including the suicide dive he'd performed from the top rope over the guard rail, had given him a rush he'd not felt his last Absinthe party at the East India Club with some old school chums. And it had been he who had walked out with his head held high - not like Murray or Matthews, who by the end of the match had ended up face down and oblivious to what was going on, nor like Bagwell or Docta Z, who had scarpered like children caught scrumping in an orchard. No, Thomas James LeStrange had managed to come out of the fracas with his head held high and his dignity in tact. And he hadn't even needed to lift a finger to start the whole thing off - it had been Murray and Bagwell's scuffle - he'd just been in the right place at the right time. And the thought of Bagwell turned his mind to the upcoming WorldWide. He had to admit, he was a tad outraged when the bookings had been announced - he had naturally assumed that being Thomas James LeStrange he was of such great magnitude that it would be unthinkable to slum it with the other newcomers. Just like everything else in life, he had expected to cruise in, get exactly what he wanted and deserved, and then cruise out again when he grew tired or weary of it. No matter, he had told himself, no reason to get into a tizzy over nothing - if they were going to offer him this facile opportunities, he might as well accept. A swift dispensing of the urchin Bagwell would be the perfect way to see in the New Year. With that, Thom LeStrange let out a contented sigh and closed his eyes, relishing the combined bliss of perfect comfort and sublime self-confidence.
