Across the Sea

2 August 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/02/10 <"Where the Hell am I?"> The man in the white facepaint groaned as pain shot through practically every nerve ending in his body. He lay on the ground, the buzzing of something ringing in his ears. Slowly, his vision returned to him, and he could see that no, this wasn't the ground. It was a professional wrestling ring, and the buzzing was a capacity crowd. Rolling over, the painted man could see the three letters on the commentator's table through the ring ropes: GCW. <"GCW? But I haven't even debuted there yet..."> Now on hands and knees, the man known as Bhava pushed up with his hands to both knees, trying to stand. It was an effort almost beyond him, but the capacity crowd's roar inspired him up. Whether it was for him or his opponent, Bhava didn't know. It is only then, when he is on his feet that he caught...something out of the corner of his eye, flying towards him. The barely formed shape was male. No, it was female. No, Bhava could not tell. Regardless, instincts kicked in, and he reached up, trying to somehow block whatever was coming. Impact came a few moments later. By the time he realized what has happened, the figure was laying across his shoulders, and his arms held the mystery opponent in place. <"This a dream? Haven't had a dream like this in forever."> The sounds of GCW's intrepid commentary team were faint in Bhava's ears, so faint as to be barely audible, and even then only in small snippets. Bryan: .... ght..... p...d.... of... Yale: Wait, ....... over... and...again! It was in that split second that he listened, though, that the moment was lost, and the figure escaped his grasp. Bhava reeled from the shock of it, and tried to effect a counterattack, but all was black once more. <"The Hell was that? Maybe it was a vision, of my glorious future in GCW. Nah, I'm probably not that lucky..."> * * * 07.28.10 Narita International Airport Terminal 1 North "Hey, man, you'd better wake up! Your flight's gonna leave in ten minutes!" My eyes drifted open, and I blinked twice, taking in my surroundings. The change from a Global Championship Wrestling show to the hustle and bustle of Narita International Airport was a harsh one in many respects, though at least my ears were used to the constant buzzing noise of people. Before me stood Mark Rogers, the other gaijin from Canada to work a regular tour schedule for WORLD WARRIORS, the Japanese company that I had worked for up until yesterday. That door of my life was drifting closed for the time being, to give way to the opening up of an opportunity in America with GCW. That was alright, though. I was used to doors closing in my life. A few months earlier, this too had nearly closed. My chance at being a GCW star almost faded on a missed tope con hilo when practicing the move at the WARRIORS dojo. The injury was bad. Losing my memory was worse. Having to be told your name, needing to learn over again facts that one had known for an entire prior lifetime was a humbling, humbling experience, one that I was still undergoing. Every day it seemed, I was relearning a small fact that I had undoubtedly once taken for granted. "Ten minutes? Already," I asked, groggily. "I've only been out..." "For an hour," he finished, shaking his head. "You were almost asleep the second you sat down in that chair, man." "Yeah, well, I didn't get much sleep last night," I admitted. "I ended up meditating instead of sleeping." He chuckled. "You and your mediation. I swear, if you're not in the ring or training, you're meditating. You haven't gone out for a drink with the boys since you got out of the hospital. And worse still, man, you're making the rats lonely! Fumiko keeps asking me where you are." "She probably knows I'm going to Japan. Besides, no matter how much you tell me about the raunchy details that she's relayed to you, it's...awkward to sleep with someone when you don't remember having done it before." Mark's dubious look told me that he didn't consider it awkward in the least. "Dude, she's hot, so who cares? If you're not going to take advantage, I think I will." "Go right ahead," I shrugged. "It's not like I'll be here to take advantage of her offer, anyway." "Scooooorrrre!" Mark seemed genuinely thrilled. Going by the picture of Fumiko that he'd showed me in the hospital, I couldn't exactly blame him. Even so, I decided that it was high time to change the subject. When Mark got started about women, he had a tendency to go on, and on, and on, and I only had ten minutes before boarding. "Had this weird dream when I was out," I told him. "Dreamed that I was already in GCW, in a match. Couldn't see who my opponent was, though, and everything was really fuzzy..." "Maybe it's a premonition," he grinned, punching me on the shoulder. "It's telling you of your glorious success in America." "Yeah, right," I said, shooting him a dubious look. "I'm a kid from Canada who got lucky as it was to get here, let alone to GCW." "Don't give me that shit, Ger. You're as good as anyone I've ever seen in that ring. Except maybe me, but Hell, we can't all be perfect, can we?" As arrogant as he could be, it wasn't entirely unfounded. Mark was damned good. He and I had met in the ring several times in Canada, trading victories. Our chemistry had been noted by WORLD WARRIORS, who had paired us up on numerous occassions as a tag team. "No, we can't," I agreed. "I do wish we'd have gotten a chance to go for the tag straps together, though. The Canadian Kids with gold around their waist was our dream when we left Halifax, right?" It was what he had told me, anyway. "It was," Mark said, "but situations change. I'm happy for you. You've gone from an almost ended career to the big time in a few months. Go pursue your dream. I'll follow you as soon as I can. And hey, if worse comes to worse, the office loves you here. WORLD WARRIORS would have you back in a heartbeat, and I know for a fact a lot of other Japanese companies have had their eye on Gerard Roy for a while now." We embraced, and in that moment, thinly-buried memories came back to the forefront of my mind. I could see the two of us engaged in battle, both against one another, and against others. "Delta Air Lines, flight 4652, non-stop to Guam, this is your final boarding call." I couldn't put it off anymore. Our eyes met, and we shared one more grin. "Kick some ass, Roy-san," were Mark's final words, as he clapped me on the back. "Will do." I picked up my laptop bag, and slung it over my shoulder. Without a glance backwards, I walked forward towards the gate. Two plane flights and more time than I cared to think about stood between me and San Diego, California, where I would make my GCW debut. Suddenly, I wished I hadn't taken such a good nap before boarding time. * * * 07.29.10 Courtyard by Marriott Hotel San Diego, CA Being in a Marriott was perhaps the first time I really took to heart that I was no longer working for an outfit that put a couple hundred people in the stands for most shows. WORLD WARRIORS had been beyond good to me, as had the Chiba Fight League and Halifax Wrestling Alliance, but I was now working for a company with "Global" in its name. I was camped out in the Marriott's lobby in one of the cushy chairs, laptop bag at my side. Even after doing nothing but sit on a plane for hours and hours, I was nonetheless exhausted. Tired as I was, though, I could not complain about the scenery. I was astounded at the seemingly never ending stream of leggy, attractive women that seemed to pour from the elevators of the hotel as if out of an assembly line. It wasn't necessarily the quantity of them that was odd. Japan had its share of very attractive women. Most of my fellow competitors knew that fact quite well and intimately. For some reason, I found my thoughts drifting to Fumiko. If I knew Mark, though, he'd already have his hands on her by now. Thinking about it a bit harder, my surprise was likely due to how much skin they were baring. Canada could get warm, but much of the year was spent in the cold, especially when I left the maritime region to go on a tour of one of the colder provinces. Even in Japan, where one could go to red light districts that made Las Vegas look like Salt Lake City, for the most part in public there were expectations that guided modest outward behavior. Here, though, there were plunging necklines and dresses so short that they barely qualified as such. Some of the women looked to me as if they'd had their attire painted on, their hair immaculately done. It made me feel like a bum, with my closely-cropped scalp starting to slowly sprout hair again, and a five o-clock shadow. The goatee that I had worn for so long was the final casualty of my life before leaving Japan, so that I could put on the facepaint that I had decided Bhava was going to wear. That facepaint, along with my new tights and other gear, was why I was sitting in the hotel lobby and not upstairs in a room, in spite of having already checked in. I supposed I was fortunate. My bag had been left behind in Guam, but Delta had realized and put it on the next flight. All told, I would only have to wait four hours, they'd told me. As the fifth hour wound to its conclusion, I was beginning to doubt what I had been told. Fortunately, I had a laptop and the lobby's free wireless internet, and so boredom was at least mitigated. GCW's website was an excellent time killer. As I surfed through page after page, I saw the names and images of my new coworkers. Many of the names I knew, as GCW had come though Japan many times, and had television coverage there. Competitors like Tessa Windsor and Tempest were well known to me, as were the company's gigantic bruisers such as Bryan Mayhem and Shaman. Seeing my name alongside theirs on the GCW roster was incredible. Only two years since my debut, and I was already here at the pinnacle of my profession. Well, the bottom of that mountain, at any rate. I looked up hopefully from my laptop at the faint sound of the automatic front doors opening. Finally, mercifully, a hotel employee dragged what appeared to be the duffel bag that I'd checked at Narita through the automatic doors of the hotel. "Here you are, sir. Delta sends its apologies, and welcome to America." "Thank you." I took the bag from him and slipped him a couple of bucks in exchange. The faces on the bills had been as foreign to me as had those on the yen bills I was paid with in Japan. In time, I'd get used to it, I figured. A quick glance at the clock confirmed that it was 10:30 at night. Not that ungodly an hour except for the fact that I had been awake ever since my power nap at the airport. It was probably early enough for me to go to sleep, but I wanted to take care of a few things first now that I had all of my belongings. Mission one was seeing what room 391 looked like. * * * Twenty minutes later. Room 391 One of the few good habits that I thought I had was my willingness to get down to business, even when the body wanted nothing more than to just spend a few hours doing absolutely nothing. I was a firm believer in mind over matter, which explained why I was still awake, propped up against the pillows of the bed in room 391. There was a fruit basket on the room's table, bearing the words "Welcome to GCW!" on its gift tag. Whether it was from the company itself or a crazed GCW fan I didn't know; in Japan, either the office or the hardcore fan could be the source of such a gift. I was not the sort of man to look a gift fruit basket in the mouth, so I was contentedly chomping away at an apple, greener and tarter than any in adopted homeland of Japan. While I had what would likely qualify as the evening's dinner, I watched the DVD that GCW had sent me to get me up to speed on the in ring talents of their Hardcore Champion thanks to the glory of laptops with DVD drives. In the Halifax Wrestling League, I had debuted against Rick "Hurricane" Reynolds. He had defeated me. WORLD WARRIORS was gracious enough to give me one of their young boys, who I prevailed over with my Ashen Verdict. It was an impressive show of strength, but I had decided immediately upon arriving in GCW that it wouldn't work against the Shamans, Bryan Mayhems, and even the Triumph Frosts of the company. It didn't take me long to realize from watching a handful of Frost's matches that this guy was easily the best wrestler I would ever have been in the ring with. He was well rounded, and the Hardcore Champion of a company that employed some of the best hardcore stylists in the world. Thankfully for me, this wasn't a hardcore match, but it sure didn't look as if Frost needed a steel chair to beat me. Watching Triumph Frost was intimidating. There was no way around that fact. I had never in my career faced anyone over six foot two, and here was a six foot six man carved out of granite, performing moves that I'd seen done by my contemporaries in Japan. It wasn't like he was throwing around guys my size. He was picking up big, scary 350 pound guys and manhandling them. The thoughts of what he could do to me weren't especially pleasant. That, though, was why I had worked so hard to free myself of the limitations imposed by decades and decades of professional wrestling experience. Zen, as a faith, was all about shedding what the senses can see in the favor of true perception. In the sport I had chosen, matches began slowly, while both competitors felt one another out. They tried to set up their submissions by attacking a chosen body part. They waited until an opponent was worn down before trying to pin them. All of it was gospel. All of it had never been challenged. To cross religious metaphors, Bhava was the Martin Luther nailing his "heretical" 95 Wrestling Theses to the door of the Church of Pro Wrestling. That fact excited me. My laptop was full of ideas I'd jotted down over the course of my trans-Pacific flight. Ideas for moves, and times to use them. Ideas for ways to confound and confuse opposition. Ideas for ways to create something that no one had ever before seen. That was appealing. Wrestling, too often, degenerated into the same tired cliches, the same characters, the same moves. GCW was one of the exceptions to a rule of corporate wrestling. It had a wide variety of characters, to which Bhava had been added. I chuckled at that, if only because I occassionally thought as if Bhava was anything but a work in progress that was being constantly tweaked and rethought. Shaking my head, I rose from the chair and closed my laptop. A few steps away from the desk was a full length mirror. I looked at my reflection, but it was Bhava who stared back at me. Bhava was a man who wore white facepaint. There would be the "om" symbol painted in red over the white paint once I got good enough at making it. Bhava wore long, baggy red pants with white lightning streaked down the legs, and a pair of white boots. He was also a Zen Buddhist, who let that come out a bit too much to be comprehensible. When I had first begun, I was astonished to discover that questions such as "what is the sound of one hand clapping?" were not, to a Zen Buddhist, meant to be rhetorical. They had answers, and one's answer's determined one's ability to perceive. I wanted to give Bhava that sort of persona. The ability to say things that on the surface made no sense, but could be deeply profound if you thought about them. Maybe. "One can only finish something when it has not yet begun," I tried, watching myself in the mirror as I spoke for any trace of hesitance or The hardest part of coming here had been, by far, figuring out just what else Bhava was. In Canada and Japan, I had been a Canadian Phoenix. It was a comfortable name. It was what I was known as. It also really sucked. I'd only somehow realized this after losing my memory and being told that the Canadian Phoenix was my ring name. One would think someone might have looked out for me when I was vetting the name two years ago. I shook my head. Worrying about such things was not particularly Zen. Nor was it particularly relevant, now that I was here, in GCW, and ready to make something new of myself. That was, above all else, why I was so thrilled to be in America. It was the chance to start over from practically scratch. I had no reputation here. I had no friends, no home, and precious few possessions. The opportunity that I had was unique, as far as I was concerned. Two months ago, I had lost my identity my cracking my skull on the dojo floor. Now, I had the opportunity to build it back up from scratch. Gerard Roy's glorious rebirth, like a phoenix from the ashes. That was why I took the name Bhava. It was the rough Sanskrit equivalent of "rebirth" or "resurrection." Even with a new cool Sanskrit name, I couldn't entirely give up the dumb phoenix idea. Old habits died hard.
