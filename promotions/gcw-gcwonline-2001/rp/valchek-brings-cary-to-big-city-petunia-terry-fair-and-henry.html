WWF? David Blaine? These things didn’t even exist at that time, and yet it made complete sense. Valchek, the old son of a gun, didn’t know what he was getting the both of us into. I agreed to go to a show and give it a look. At that point I had nothing to lose: I was homeless, physically and mentally unwilling to go fight again (the crowd would accept nothing less than a rematch; my body would reject anything remotely associated with that), and dead broke, essentially living at the whim of whoever would throw a few crumbs into my mouth. The fiery spirit that I carried in my heart was extinguished and replaced with an electric candle you placed in your windows during Christmas time. For Orkly I needed to cease the acts of violence in his name and instead perform in his honor, and from what Valchek told me this was the perfect opportunity. Valchek picked me up at the soup kitchen a few days after I woke from my slumber in his 1988 Chevrolet Camaro. The guy acted like a prick and sure knew what kind of car ameliorated the image. All the other bums laid a hairy eyeball on me; all I could do was silently shrug and plop into the vehicle. “Awesome vehicle you’re commanding, this is what Commissioner gets you, huh?” “I paid the cost. No one ever really gave me a chance without trying to exploit me somehow. Yeah, there was Orkly, but with all due respect he doesn’t exactly count as a human being. Every case of someone trying to help has always had a few strings attached. But with Valchek’what could he be getting out of this? Sure, his brother ran the organization, but Valchek had no money invested in it as, and he says it the best, “little fairies wearing spandex slapping each other around is ok sometimes, but people can’t get the wrong ideas about the commissioner.” I think the guy might have been gay himself, BUT that’s a completely different subject. We arrived at the gymnasium, parking in the midst of the dozens of cars that packed the lot behind the place. The hustle and bustle grew as we made our way towards the entrance. Since Valchek’s brother ran the thing we were going to get in for free, which was pretty awesome. The last “free” thing that fell into my hands before that was the pile of trash some guy tossed out of a window last year that happened to have a few chicken bones with the marrow still encased, so this was luxury for sure. Yeah, the line was long, but I’d wait until my death bed for anything free. “Now, Cary, when we get in here there are a couple people I want to introduce you to. We won’t have much time before the show starts, so we gotta be on top of things.” “Wait, who?” “My brother’s wife, Petunia. She’s the floor manager, and if she’s got something nice to say about you, then so does my brother. Oh, and Terry Fair?” “THE Terry Fair”! The guy probably stood no higher than five feet and yet probably outweighed me by at least a hundred pounds. And this wasn’t just fat-fat; oh, Lord no, this man was FAT! I could see the ripples of blubber layering over the ripples of fat underneath his skin-tight white Big-City promotional t-shirt. (I didn’t rip my eyeballs out; I’m happy about that in retrospect.) With all that girth, it must have been tough to really clean every inch of his body, something anyone with a nose could tell. The scent that crawled up into my nostrils was the result of who knows how many days worth of neglect to areas that needed attention like a foster child. On top of that, he had one of those “make me look thinner” mustaches that only made things silly, not serious. “Tickets.” He spoke like a frog but acted like a God. For Valchek, I held back. “Hey, I’m Freddy’s brother, Valchek, so excuse me.” “NO TICKETS, NO ENTRY!” The fat man’s open palm collided with Valchek’s accelerating chest, freezing them in place. Valchek slowly turned his head towards the fat man. His face was priceless: are you kidding me, Bud? Do you NOT see Freddy in me? I mean, shit, WE’RE BROTHERS! Valchek didn’t have to say a word; his expression said it all. It was only a matter of seconds before things got ugly. “I got two things to say to you, kid. Valchek’s could possibly be the fact that he’s an Ass Pirate sailing down the River Homo during the weekends, all the while flaunting the straightest voice since Bronson’s. Who knows though. “RED! WHAT’S THE HOLD UP?” If Chewbacca spoke English and had an axe-wound, it would’ve sounded like that. When the monster turned the corner, Red’s frame paled in comparison. She was a beast, the ugliest human being I’d ever seen. Her mouth always remained in a frown, and when her lips parted her three rotten teeth jutted out. Bags of purple and gray hung below her eyeballs right next to her pig-nose, visibly dripping with snot. The stringy hair that needed a gallon of shampoo in a bad way hung down to her shoulders which were tattooed with stretch marks.
