Babysitter

10 June 2011

Date: 06/10/11 “Thank God! You are a lifesaver!” It wasn’t exactly the situation Triumph Frost was expecting to be a part of on a Friday morning. He was still a tad drunk from the previous night, exacerbated by the fact that it was 10 AM and he was still sipping from a bottle of Cognac. He was sitting on the couch in the living room, shirt off, Plasma TV showing “Heat,” the echoes of gunfire and the last gasps of Tom Sizemore as he’s turned into a corpse echoing off the marble floors. And it was only during a break in the action that he heard the cacophony of bells ringing out. It wasn’t a wise thing to interrupt Triumph’s movie time, or bother him in general, really. To him, it didn’t matter who it was: the sheriff’s department, one of Caldera’s goons come to prep him for the shuttle launch, or a randy Mila Kunis offering to give him a BJ. Be they the authorities, hired NASA castoffs, or an attractive actress with an oral fixation, he would read them the riot act all the same. He made the trek through his cavernous mansion, following the noise all the way to his front door. Of course, he wasn’t expecting to see some shades-wearing frazzled-looking dressed-for-success soccer mom. And he sure as hell wasn’t expecting her to have a kid in tow with a couple bags of luggage. She let out a weary sigh. “Finally,” she frantically belted out. “I thought I’d never find you!” Triumph did a quick mental check through his facial rolodex. He didn’t recognize her, he thought, but he did drink a lot. And he was known on occasion to have “lapses” in memory. And the kid, he thought. Christ, the kid. The kid was probably too old to be his, but he still didn’t want to go through with the whole Maury Povich song-and-dance nightmare. Again. “Sorry.” She extended her arm, her hand outstretched. “Jess Macher. I have the house over on Oak Road.” Triumph sucked in a nice, deep, and relieved breath, and took her up on the handshake. “Right,” he said, acting like he knew where she was talking about. “So, err…Jess?” She shook her head quickly. “Yes, what do I want, right?” Triumph clicked his tongue and nodded. She raised her sunglasses and rested them on the top of her head. “I know this is going to sound absolutely insane.” She took a quick pause and stared right at the ground, before raising it a few moments later. “And trust me, if I could’ve gone elsewhere, I would’ve. But…” She paused once more. It took her a few seconds to gather those last strands of courage together to finish what she needed to say. “Can you watch my son for the weekend?” Triumph tilted back slightly. His mouth agape, no words would exit. He tried to process the information handed to him as best as he could. He wondered if this were some sort of practical joke. Maybe Lexi was testing him? Whatever it was, he wanted to at least make sure he heard her right. He was slightly tipsy, after all. “You want me to what?” “I know it’s forward, trust me, but I have to go to L.A. to finalize divorce proceedings, and my friends are out of town, and…” She babbled on about her sob story such-and-such for about a minute, but to Triumph it was just noise. Her mouth moved, her narrative pouring out in buckets, but all he could think about was whether or not she was serious. And why him? Does no one else live in this goddamned neighborhood, he wondered. There had to be other people around here, right? Right…? Finally, she made it to the end of her tale. “So, can you watch my little Timmy? I’ll be back Sunday night. It’s just a few nights, I swear.” Triumph rubbed the back of his neck. The unease had fully set in. “I dunno about all this…” “Look, I know this sounds crazy,” she returned. “If I were you, I’d probably turn me away right off the bat. But…” She grabbed at his wrist and held it tight. “I’m really in a bind.” She fluttered her eyes. “I can make it worth your while.” “I’m married,” Triumph said with a smile, displaying his ringed finger. She thought for a moment. “My soon-to-be ex-husband turned our basement into his personal play den. Boxes of comics, baseball cards, arcade video games. You know, kid’s stuff.” She shook her head disappointedly. “My Senator husband, the 38-year-old teenager.” “Hold up.” Triumph’s eyebrow’s shot up. “What kind of kid’s stuff?” Her eyes went to the top of her head as she rattled off what she could recall. “Oh, like Spider-Man number one. A Ken Griffey Jr. rookie card. Something called like ‘Marvel against Caption 2.’” She chuckled. “I’ll probably get rid of all of it once all this is done--“ But Triumph needed to hear no more. “Hell yeah, I’ll take it!” Her eyes lit up like a titled pinball machine. “You’ll watch my Timmy?!?” “Sure, that too,” Triumph elatedly said. She lunged at him and hugged him tight, thanking him profusely. She said goodbye to her young son Timmy, telling him to behave and do what Triumph had asked him. She kissed her son on the forehead and went on her way. And as they stood at the entryway together, watching Timmy’s mom drive off to the airport, it was about that moment that Triumph realized what the hell he had just gotten himself into. Day One: Friday Triumph rubbed the back of his neck, staring down at this precocious kid, no more than ten years of age, his luggage by his side who was standing next to a six-and-a-half foot teenager with a tattoo of a naked woman on his pectoral. “Ahh…” Triumph groaned. “Shit.” Timmy reacted slightly at the curse word, enough for Triumph to notice. “You alright?” Triumph queried. “Mommy says curse words are bad,” Timmy blurted out innocently. “Only diseased-riddled mean thieves say curse words.” Triumph struggled to not look mortified. “Christ, kid,” he muttered. “Your mom say that about everyone?” Timmy shook his head from side to side. “Just daddy,” Timmy replied. Triumph rubbed away at his forehead, his body language screaming ‘uncomfortable as all get out.’ And his lack of comfort was only exacerbated by the realization that he was standing in broad daylight out in front of his house with a strange kid he only just met. “Err, let’s get you inside,” Triumph muttered, and hastened their entry into his mansion. He took a peek for any prying eyes before slamming the oak door shut and locking the deadbolt in place. ****** A now-shirted Triumph led Timmy to a colorful room in the east wing. Action figures and various games were scattered about the light blue carpet, surrounded by painted white clouds on a similar colored wall. Timmy tossed his bag at the foot of the racecar bed and turned back to face Triumph. Triumph couldn’t help but grin. “This used to me my old room,” Triumph beamed. He extended his arms to show off the sky on the wall. “Me and my mom painted the walls.” He chuckled. “That was a fun week.” “Your mom sounds nice,” Timmy said. “Where is she?” Triumph grimaced. He walked up to Timmy and pressed his hand on his back to lead him out of the room. “Let’s get you something to eat.” “Okay,” Timmy said with the vigor one would expect an enthusiastic ten-year-old to exude. Triumph guffawed. “You like sushi? I know a great place.” ****** Inside the bathroom of a sushi joint in Tokyo Town, little Timmy puked his guts out into the toilet in the bathroom. Triumph leaned against the stall and rubbed his face. “Okay,” Triumph muttered. “So sushi was a bad idea.” Timmy heaved and let loose another string of violent vomiting. After the flush of the toilet rang out, Timmy opened the stall and staggered out. Triumph helped him to the sink and assisted in the washing of the face, the clearing of the mouth with tap water, and the wiping away of tears. Triumph grabbed Timmy by the shoulders and stood him firm. “Sorry about that, bud,” Triumph said, doing his best to comfort a kid most likely mortified. Timmy sniffed, and a clear drip of snot ran out from his nose. Triumph grabbed a towel from the dispenser and wiped his nose clean. Triumph tossed the crumpled paper into the wastebasket nearby, an arcing float, nothing but ruffled plastic bag. Triumph went to one knee, a face-to-face leveling with the young boy. “You alright?” Timmy nodded and sniffed once more. He stammered a bit, still a tad shaken. “I’m…okay.” Triumph double-checked. “You sure?” Timmy nodded once more, a firmer nod. Triumph patted him on the back. “So what do you want to do? Wait, better yet” Triumph looked away for a second, mired in thought. He came right back to Timmy. “What would your mom not want you to do?” Timmy tilted his head to the side like a puppy startled by a strange noise. Triumph grinned. “I mean, your mom ain’t here, right?” Timmy nodded once more. “So?” Triumph pressed a bit further. “Haven’t you ever wanted to eat, like, a gallon of ice cream or see an R-rated movie?” Timmy spoke like a true Mormon momma’s boy. “Mom says ice cream is for fat women with no self-esteem and bad movies are for evil adults.” Triumph blinked twice. His blank stare told the story. Triumph quickly shook it off. “Yeah, well, mom told you there’s a Santa Claus when you were younger, right? Not everything your mom says is true.” Timmy shrieked. “There’s no Santa Claus?!?” And the waterworks began. Triumph did what was to be expected of him: he freaked the fuck out. Triumph tried to calm down Timmy with continued shushing, an effort that worked not at all. Triumph’s eyes darted about to find something - anything - to fix his malady. And so he grabbed a handful of towels and let out a bellowing laugh. Timmy paused for a split second, and Triumph took his opening. “Ha! Just kidding! Santa is totally real!” Timmy’s words traversed through sobs and mucus. “R-really?” Triumph dropped to one knee and gave Timmy the towels. “Totally,” Triumph reassured the scamp. “Why, me and him shared a beer and some cookies just last year. Fat bastard’s the tits.” Timmy blew his nose with the towel, a tiny hurricane blowing from his nasal cavity. “T-tits?” Triumph soldiered on, not missing a beat. “The best,” Triumph said as he snatched one of the towels and helped Timmy get back to Defcon 5. Triumph stood up, towering over the young kid. He placed a hand on Timmy’s shoulder. “Look,” Triumph said. “Why don’t I just take to some of my favorite places? We’ll have the kinda fun I have. Cool?” Timmy sniffed once again. “Cool,” he said. “Yeah,” Triumph reassured himself as he and Timmy exited the bathroom. “I mean, can’t get worse than this, right?” ****** “Are you out of your fucking mind?!?” The sun had set and the lights and sounds of San Francisco were in full effect. Triumph, with little Timmy in tow, sidled up next to the perturbed quite sizeable black bouncer Triumph knew as Coast outside the Havana Nights strip club and took him off to the side. “I’m all outta fuckin’ ideas here, guy,” Triumph told the bouncer, who was none-too-enthused about the situation in the least. “Miyagi’s wouldn’t let me bring him in. I mean, the fuckin’ bitch at the front screamed at me. Screamed!” Yet through Triumph’s beseeching, Coast just stood and stared daggers right through Triumph’s very being. An angst-ridden Triumph shook his head from side to side. “I’m outta my depth here. I mean, what does one even do with a kid?” After much glowering, Coast finally let loose his long southern-fried drawl. “Aw, jeez, Tri. I mean…here?!?” Triumph used whatever resources he possessed as best as one of his style could. “What if he hangs in the dressing rooms? I mean, they’ve all got kids and shit already.” Triumph slapped Coast on the back. “He’ll fit right in!” The incredulity of Coast hit an all-time fever-pitch, but Triumph would not stop his bargaining. “I haven’t seen my girl in months, dude! I…” Triumph leaned in to whisper into Coast’s ear. “I’ve got needs, man.” And it was then that Coast shoved Triumph away. He grabbed Triumph and brought him back to a confused Timmy. When Coast spotted Timmy, looking like he walked right off the set of his elementary school’s production of “Oliver Twist,” he couldn’t help but grin. Timmy couldn’t help but be scared by the intimidating figure that was the approaching Coast, but when the big man leaned down and said “what up, little man,” Timmy loosened up just enough. Coast helped with the rest. “You see this dude here?” He pointed to the apprehensive Triumph. “This dude right here’s gonna take you over to Fat Slice right now, and get you the biggest…” Coast’s voice rumbled with the purr of a muscle car. “Most baddest slice of pepperoni pizza you ever done laid yo’ eyes on.” And just like that, Timmy radiated like a slot machine paying out the big bucks. Coast closed the rest. “That sound good to you, lil’ man?” Timmy’s body vibrated, his head shaking, his head bobbing about. Coast shot Triumph a quick look, and Triumph nodded. Coast stuck out his fist, and Timmy bumped it back. “My boy.” Coast walked back to man his post, and Triumph put his hand down towards Timmy’s back. “Come on, bud,” Triumph said to the newly-excited Timmy. “I think I’ve got this figured out now.” Day Two: Saturday ”I told you a long time ago you fuckin’ little monkey not to fuck me!!!” As Tony Montana asked Alex who the fuck he thinks he was talkin’ to, Triumph and Timmy lounged on the living room couch for that Saturday noon-time viewing of the unedited and uncut super-platinum collectors’ edition DVD of “Scarface.” Triumph tapped Timmy on the shoulder with the back of his hand and pointed to the television screen. “I love this part,” Triumph proudly professed as he tilted back the beer. His eyes narrowed. “Let’s you know this is the beginning of the end.” Timmy turned to Triumph. “What’s a ‘fucking monkey’?” And Triumph did as he was wont to do. He laughed loudly. “Your dad, according to your mom.” “Oh,” Timmy said, nodding his head. He spoke softly to himself, sounding out the phrase. “Fuck-ing. Mon-key.” Triumph watched out of the corner of his eye and chuckled as he sipped his beer. “Don’t let your mom catch you saying that.” Yet through the booms of the movie, through the laughter of Triumph, and through the edification of ten-year-old Timmy Macher, something even more expected was to bring itself into the fold. ****** It had been months since Alexis Frost had set foot in the mansion her and her husband lived. Triumph had let her loose to chase down her own dreams and she did nothing short of only that. She laid the foundation for her very own fashion empire, stamping stickers and placing flags all throughout Europe and Asia. It was taxing, it was exhaustive, and it was nothing short of difficult. And she loved and hated every moment of it. To find herself home, where she could be with the man she missed so, was bittersweet. But she had not seen Triumph in quite some time, and she missed the sweet enough to completely ignore the bitter. Standing in the grand foyer entrance, she marveled at the place she had left behind and now had returned to. She took in a deep breath, inhaling the essence that was home. Her words lingered about, bouncing off the stone tiles. “Hey baby, you here?” Nothing returned. When the crackling of Hollywood-induced gunfire rang out, she made her march towards the living room Triumph so loved to frequent. “Baby,” she said as she made her way there, tossing off her heels along the way. “You have no idea how much I’ve missed you.” She lowered the spaghetti-straps of her dress and let it drop to the ground in the hallway mere feet away from the living room. And in only her lace bra and panties, she found herself at the entrance of the living room, eyes closed. “God, I just want to…” When her eyes opened, Timmy was her first image. “Damn,” Timmy said. “You hot, girl.” Alexis’ left hand and right hand went to instinct and spent alternating time covering her nether-regions up. Triumph, however, remained entranced upon the climactic finale of “Scarface,” but not captivated enough to eschew some advice. “Nice,” Triumph said. “But it’s ‘da-yam.’ And a ‘bitch’ here and there don’t--” His head swiveled toward his woman. Triumph filled in the rest. “Fuck!!!” To say Alexis was anything but the most livid anyone had ever been in the history of mankind would be the understatement of forever. ****** Triumph told Alexis the score, and she was anything but pleased with the deal. But upstairs in their master bedroom, words were hushed and voices remained exclusive whispers. And if anything, the last thing Alexis wanted was to scare this kid to death. “Are you insane?!?” “Everything’s under control,” Triumph tried to reassure her. “Trust me.” His assertion fell on deaf ears, as expected. “This isn’t a damn monkey! He’s a…” Alexis blurted out while catching herself before the yelp. “He’s a kid. A real life kid.” “I know,” Triumph said. “A talented one, too. He whomped my ass on ‘The Show.’ Belted three fuckin’ home runs with Asdrubal Cabrera off my Cliff Lee. I mean…” Triumph rubbed his face with disbelief. “Who does that?” Alexis grabbed Triumph’s biceps, digging her nails into his skin. “This is serious…!” Triumph leaned down toward her face. “I know the deal.” He brushed her hands away and blood dribbled down his arms. “Look, it’s only ‘til tomorrow, okay?” “I know, but…” Alexis tried to make heads or tails out of the situation… But Triumph just couldn’t help but not let her finish. “He’ll be gone tomorrow, and then I gotta go get prepped to jet to outer space so me and Space Monkey can fuck up Lorenzo Lamas’ illegitimate ‘tard child and…” He paused, but not before groaning. “Andy fucking Murray on the surface of the moon.” Alexis blinked twice. A familiar blank stare shot right back in Triumph’s face. “How much have you had to drink today?” Triumph thought for a moment, before getting back to the moment at hand. “It’ll all be good, okay?” He leaned in and kissed her on the cheek. “Think of it as a trial run.” And when he walked out of the room after he said what he said, the look on her face, her posture, her body language, the whole lot was…different. ****** “Go directly to jail,” Timmy told Triumph, who wasn’t about to let the incident slide without a fight. “Bullshit,” Triumph bellowed out. “This game is rigged!” Timmy held up the chance card that was the bane of Triumph’s property-buying existence. “Card don’t lie.” Timmy smirked. “Bitch.” Triumph eyed Timmy wearily, but not wearily enough to mask his amusement. “Shit, you keep this up I might need to learn a thing or two.” “Let me help you with that,” Alexis gleefully remarked as she picked up Triumph’s brass-plated worn-out shoe and placed it upon the man grasping bars on the Monopoly board. Triumph rolled his eyes and groaned. “You made me a prison bitch!” Both Timmy and Alexis let out the kind of laugh that Triumph reveled in, the kind that put him at ease. There weren’t many sounds that could accomplish such a feat. But here, with the woman he loved with every fiber of his body, and the kid that would be his son in a million fantasies that might never come to fruition, he found a foreign feeling. A feeling that he would likely never understand, no matter how many different ways he broke it down. A feeling that had driven even the most grounded man insane. It was calm. And damned if Triumph knew what to do with it. Day Three: Sunday Their last day together was a good day. The type of day you don’t enjoy when you’re in the moment. The type of day you long for when you’re far removed from it. The type of day you compare every other ‘good’ day to, only to see it just come up short. The perfect day one only dreams about. Triumph, Alexis, and Timmy started off with their type of bang: one more extended Monopoly run. Triumph won this go-around, but of course they knew he would. He demanded to be the banker, and cheated with the deftness of a Wall Street executive. Even when both Alexis and Timmy watched him pocket hundreds in plain view, they let it slide. All for the good of the game. The best game of Monopoly they had played in quite some time. It’s not often one gets to see eight hotels on a single property. After that was movie time. Alexis’ pick that go-around, and she decided on a movie called “Something New,” which brought about a sigh of relief from a Triumph who was tired of another lamented viewing of “The Notebook.” Unfortunately, Triumph’s lament returned about two minutes into Alexis’ choice. Though the running commentary of Triumph had Timmy in stitches, and that was enough for all of them. And when the movie was over, they knew that the day was running short and that eventually mom would be back to pick him up. So Triumph and Alexis asked Tommy what he wanted to do. And Timmy just said how much he loved to hear the seals. ****** Timmy munched on blue-colored Cotton Candy as he raced down the wooden planks of Fisherman’s Wharf, under the watchful eyes of both Triumph and Alexis Frost. Triumph kept his arm deftly tucked around Alexis’ waist as they followed mere feet from the sugar-rushed kid in their charge. And as they strolled through the arcade, Alexis looked up at Triumph. “You’re gonna make a helluva dad, you know that?” Triumph laughed nervously. He knew no other way at this point. “Are you kidding? I’d make a terrible father.” “I know a certain ten-year-old that’d say otherwise.” Alexis squeezed his clutching wrist. Triumph pulled away from Alexis and faced her dead-on. “Oh yeah?” She flashed a smile he hadn’t seen in ages. One he missed as much as the first taste of a beer on a hot summer day. “Yeah,” she returned, before she grabbed the back of his neck and pulled him close for the first deep kiss they had shared since either of them could remember. ****** And so they followed Timmy all the way to the end of the docks, where the sun was beginning to set, but not before shining off of the shimmering water ahead. Timmy watched the gleam of the flickering sunlight as he scarfed down his coagulated sugars. He looked out of the corner of his eye, to see Alexis and Triumph gaze upon him much like he was their own. And instead of saying how much he enjoyed the moment or how he wished that they were his real family, he did the next best thing and simply enjoyed the moment. And when the seals made themselves known with their distinctive barks, not a single solitary thing could wipe the smile from Timmy’s face.
