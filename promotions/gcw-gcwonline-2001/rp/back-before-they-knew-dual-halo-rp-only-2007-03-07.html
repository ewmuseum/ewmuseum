Back Before They Knew (Dual Halo RP only)

7 March 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 7, 2007 There once was a man with a legacy so grand that his only wish in life was the continuation of it. Above his health, his wellbeing, and his happiness, he wanted his name to be plastered on billboards even after he was dead. Thankfully for him, he helped give birth to a son who he believed would be his golden ticket to eternal remembrance. As that son lay half inside of a broken crate though, that dream seemed like even less of a reality. Jamison Talley, who seemed unconscious by the way his eyes rolled, held an empty bottle of whiskey as he slowly degraded a once great name. Luckily for him, his father would never see. In fact, if it wasn’t for the hoarse cough he had contracted, no one would have seen him sprawled in the alley between Fourth and Third Street. As the blood leaped off his tongue though, a crowd of casual onlookers couldn’t help but notice the bruised drunkard. He was shirtless with a cut across his right pectoral covered in a set of meaningless tattoos. His lower half held onto a pair of loose fitting holy jeans that sagged off his backside revealing his lack of under garments. As he hacked another cough of blood onto the pavement beside him, Talley rolled out of his crate and overtop of the day’s front page story. “Get up. C’mon now.” The voice was foreign to Talley’s ears and came through just barely. He neglected to recognize the speaker and instead found himself using that front-page spread as his napkin. An image of a smashed up car turned crimson with the introduction of the liquid and he dropped the side of his face into it unbeknownst. The owner of the voice dodged a splotch of blood by stepping over it and toward Talley. He flicked at Jamison’s shoulder with his gator skin boot hoping for an answer. “C’mon. You’re pathetic. Get up already.” Jamison took the hit and forgot about it just as easily. Still, the man was persistent and, again, stomped at his shoulder. Without answer once more, he lowered his aim and struck Talley’s ribcage. “Get up.” The hacking wasn’t far off and soon enough Talley released a fresh batch of blood for the floor of the alley. A string hung on his bottom lip as he turned over and looked up. His eyes looked stoned by the way they swirled and his expression was a stupid one showing his lack of togetherness. “Who’re ya?”” “Shut up and stand.” “Can’t.” “Sure you can.” “Can’t.” The man with the alligator boots ignored the excuse and delivered another kick to Talley’s side. Talley gasped for breath and sucked in as he held in the contents of another spill. “The fuck?” “I saw you standing earlier. Get up.” “Who’re ya?”” Reaching down, the man grabbed Jamison by the head and forced him to sit upright. For a second he remained still but lost his balance afterwards and toppled to the concrete again. “I’m the one who’s going to save your ass. Now quit fucking around.” “I don’eed savin”. Just skit.” His drunken slurs meshed together in a haphazard pattern making hardly understandable words. “Jay, you got one more chance. Get up.” “Fuck yo ?” Before he could finish, the gator skin boot bit into his forehead and tossed the back of his head into the pavement. Jamison bounced once before shutting his eyes and rolling to his side. With a brisk shake of the head, the man began to lift Jamison up willing him to follow. __________ He was sprawled across the bed sheets of a foreign bed looking up at a ceiling he had never seen before. His head was still spinning and as he touched it he noticed the bandanna that packed the blood in. Jamison struggled to sit up and when he did he got a good glimpse of a man he had seen in his dreams. Unlike the vision though, he wasn’t encased in fire. He was an average size man with box-frame glasses shading his eyeballs. “It’s about time.” “Huh?” “You. You’ve been out for nearly eight hours now.” “Who are you?” “Forget formalities. Wait “till your head stops spinning.” “I don’t understand.” “You will, just let it come to you. I’ll be in the kitchen when you’re feeling like normal.” With that, the man from Jamison’s mind stands from his chair in the room and exits gracefully. He turns a corner and falls out of Talley’s line of sight. Grabbing at the wound on his head again, Jamison tries to remember something. Anything. Thinking it over though, the only memory that comes to mind is the one of him in the ring. An image of Skip Skippin lunging his boot forward and digging it into his throat is the last image he can recollect. With that in mind, he grabs at the place of impact and notices that he is sore there as well. Feeling like a hostile hospital patient, Jamison tries to stand from his bed. It takes all of his energy to get to his feet and as he’s there he can feel the numbing affects of a serious hangover. He stumbles into a dresser and knocks over two separate pictures. They drop face first and he attempts to pick them up. With trouble, he gets them to stand and makes notice of the photos within. He can see the dream man and alongside of him is a very familiar man. Feeling brand new, Jamison shakes off the after affects of his whiskey and blood encounter and begins out of the room toward the kitchen. He rounds a corner, much like the man had, and looks around until he notices a tile floor two rooms over. Upon entering, he sees one seat open at the table and takes residence within it. A steaming brew of tea tickles his nostrils. “That’s yours.” “Yeah?” “Yeah.” Jamison grabs the cup but withdraws his hand after feeling the heat. He takes the handle on his second try and sips lightly from the edge. “Where am I?” “My home.” “Who are you though?” “Someone who got fed up with you.” Talley sets his mug back onto the table and presses on the rag around his head. “So you did this to me?” “The head, yes. The throat, no. That was you being a moron.” “The fuck?” “Who goes down to a superkick. I swear, that’s the most overused move I’ve ever heard of.” “Skip?” “Yeah. He made you look like a real piece of shit. I’m starting to believe him.” Jay seems confused but the second wave of his hangover advises him not to argue. Instead, he sips the tea again and shuts his eyes. “You always drink after a loss?” “Not always. Why?” “Because I have to figure these things out. Can’t have a client piss drunk sitting in crates after his matches. Bad publicity.” “Client?” The man sighs and moves around in his chair. “I’m under the impression that you need someone in your life that’s going to lead you to stardom. I’ve seen you at Concord ? you’re god awful. Hell, you may even be worse. Thing is, I’m offering to help you.” “This is my dad’s idea.” “Who’s your dad?” He takes another sip from the mug of tea, “The guy in those pictures.” The suave gentleman smiles a half smile and nods a few times. The look in his eyes is oddly gleeful. “Good call. He told me about you, yeah. He never asked me to do you favors though. He knew I was better than that.” “Then quit wasting my time.” “What, do you have to train or something” Drink another bottle of Jack Daniels? C’mon Jay, where do you honestly have to go?” “Fuck off.” “Give me a month. If you keep losing and decide it’s best to rip my head off after that, I’ll give you back to that blood soaked alley. I’m sure the police will be glad to give you a room at the station.” “And if I walk out.” “Guests don’t walk out of my house.” Jamison knows how easy it would be to slip away but the fact that the hangover still has him in its claws keeps him from running. Talley nods to the man and takes a last sip of tea. “Name?” “Daniel. Daniel Franco.” Franco grins and lets the silence put questions in Talley’s head. He knows that he’s already doing better than his father ever could have hoped. __________ For once in Jamison Talley’s life, a bottle of whiskey doesn’t sit beside him in his locker room. Hell, even the traditional cigarettes are vacant as he unlaces his boots. Instead, the always-watching eyes of Daniel Franco take note of Talley’s typical routine. “It’s weird you know. A win with me already that is.” “Quit being cocky.” “Admit it. Something’s working.” “You’re cocky.” Franco smirks, as if to accept the sentiment, and takes his glasses off. He rubs the lenses with his shirt sleeve before placing them back on the bridge of his nose. Blinking a few times, “I think you could use a name change.” “Why’s that?” “Well Jamison Talley is ? well it’s got a legacy and all but ? how about Jay Jones?” “Jones?” “It works with Jay well.” Talley shakes it off, “Great, they can all call me double J too, huh?” “I know names. Trust me. Everyone I’ve helped has wound up booming with one of my names.” He peels the boot off his foot and tosses it toward his gym bag. “Trust me. Jay Jones won’t boom.” “You have a better idea then” If so, please enlighten me.” Jamison rips the second boot off his foot and sits his socks on the plastic crate in front of him. As his feet breathe he rubs his eyes and thinks. “What about Terror?” “Huh?” “Jay Terror.” “And you thought Jones was bad.” “Fuck off. It means something.” Franco leans inward in his chair and cocks his brow, “Go ahead. What’s so striking about Jay Terror,” putting emphasis on Terror. Terror leans in as well, in a mocking fashion, and smirks for Daniel. “Let’s just say that there are people that will recognize it and others that will fear it.” “Just because your name is Terror doesn’t mean you’re scary.” Mocking again, “Let’s give it a try. If it fails I’ll disown it and crawl into a hole.” “Quit trying to be funny. You’re not.” “Get used to it.” __________ Sitting in a leaned back recliner, a man with a very thick mustache pages through the latest edition of the Daily Times. “This should be Jay in here.” Answering him is none other than Daniel Franco. He nods to the man and sits up on the couch. “Concord doesn’t typically make the paper.” “Then why is he still there?” “Because he has no chance in making a real federation.” “Isn’t that why I hired you?” Franco laughs it off, “It’s been a week. Relax.” The mustached man places the paper on a side table and uses the arm on the side to push his body and the chair forward. His feet stop the rocking and his eyes focus in on Franco’s box frames. “Look, Daniel. I’m not paying you to let him glide into the person I want him to be. I’m paying you to ?” “To mold him into that person. I know. Thing is, you can’t mold a reformed drunk in this time slot. It’ll take a few more hits.” “You going to get him those hits?” “Something like that.” “Just make sure he doesn’t find out.” “Right.” “Right?” Sarcastically, “I get it. I mean, I would hate to hear that my dad is scheming to make me succeed. That’d be the worst.” Jamison’s father sets his head awry, “Look. You’re going to come to know Jay and you’ll see how he is. Finding out about the arrangement wouldn’t be something that would aid in this upward push we want.” “Right.” “Good.” __________ With three wins under his belt, a certain sense of larger-than-life had begun seeping into Jay Terror. His ring name, unlike Franco had predicted, was enough to drag a few fans on his bandwagon and aided in a push that he had never expected. For the first time, Jamison Talley was beginning to think that Daniel Franco was right. He was the key to his success. “Bo Kliq this week. The guy’s been on a rampage lately.” Franco steers the wheel of his worn Civic and answers, “Maybe.” “You been watching?” “Yeah. Why?” “Haven’t you seen the clips. Six seven and all he does is lift and toss. The guy can’t be stopped it seems.” “I don’t doubt it.” Terror turns to his manager, “Why the maybe?” “You said that you had him this week. Maybe.” “Cough it up.” Franco negotiates a turn and glances toward Talley. He smirks for a second but it vanishes as he slams his fist on the horn. “Fuckin” learn to drive!” Jay continues, “Maybe?” “Jay. Jeez. Look, I’ve been in talks with a few people.” “People?” “Some of the higher ups. Seems like I’m a good salesman too.” Jay looks lost still and continues to stare at Franco. “What does that mean?” “Means we’re headed to the airport now.” “Huh?” “You ever hear of GCW?” Jay shakes his head at the name-drop and denies the statement. “Never.” “They’re world wide, how the hell not?” “You think I care that much?” Franco glances over at Jay again, this time for a few seconds longer than last. “I got you a try-out of sorts. They want to see what you’re made of.” “You sure” Me?” “You.” “The fuck?” “Just roll with it. We’ll be at the airport in twenty minutes.” “What about Concord?” Daniel laughs it off, “Forget it. You’re too big for them now.” He pauses, “Now quit with the questions. I got a headache.” Jay obliges and leans back in the passenger seat. He isn’t sure whether he should smirk or frown at the news so instead he decides to do neither. Franco, on the other hand, grins the entire way to the airport praying that he’s made the right decision. __________ There once was a man with a legacy so grand that his only wish in life was the continuation of it. Above his health, his wellbeing, and his happiness, he wanted his name to be plastered on billboards even after he was dead. Thankfully for him, he helped give birth to a son who he believed would be his golden ticket to eternal remembrance. His belief would become a reality.
