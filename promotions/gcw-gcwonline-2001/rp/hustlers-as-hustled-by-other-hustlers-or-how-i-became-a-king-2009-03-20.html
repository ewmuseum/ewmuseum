Hustlers, As Hustled By Other Hustlers (Or How I Became A King)

20 March 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009.03.20 Hustlers, As Hustled By Other Hustlers (Or How I Became A King) A Garbage Bag Production It had been a boring day so far, and I wouldn't have been near the place if I hadn't called in and won tickets on the radio. It was the principle of the matter, though, and I figured I'd stick it out to the end because you're not supposed to look at a horse's mouth unless you saw it lift its tail and there was nowhere else to look. Then God help you if you weren't looking at its mouth. Down below, it was hard to tell what was going on. There was all sorts of games being run, and there was no order to any of it. There were men in uniforms shuffling cards and all sorts of lights and shiny machines. People walked around counting money, and they were all moving so fast that they were bouncing off of each other like particles in a god damn centrifuge. They were all hustlers, trying to outhustle other hustlers, though with the various games and all the moving about, it was hard to tell whose game was whose. They were all feeling each other out, losing when they thought they were supposed to lose and winning when they thought they were supposed to win. It was the art of the hustle and being the best at the hustle and the hustle alone. The games didn't matter at this point. This was pure hustling at it's best, and it didn't really look like anything, but it was some kind of contest. A champion had to be crowned if it would take all night, and though I hoped it wouldn't, it didn't appear that my hopes would be realized. Nobody appeared to be gaining any ground. They were all chiseling away at each other, slowly, and as broken people went home broke, reinforcements would filter in. Maybe they were all trying to break me. "It's a fucking carnival out there," I noted to a fellow spectator standing a few feet away from me. He looked over and looked back. "Are you watching this shit? I can't even tell what's happening. Is anything happening?" "Who the fuck are you talking to?" He glared at me and then paced backward to watch from a different spot. "Was that a rhetorical question?" I was late to respond, and he was well out of earshot, and to tell you the truth, I had no idea where he went. Maybe he had entered the commotion. Had I been hustled without even knowing that I had? I couldn't count out that possibility. That brought up even further dilemmas, like the even likelier possibility that I had been hustled without even knowing that I had only to be made to believe that I had been hustled without knowing that I had so that I could be hustled again later. Had I known if I had been hustled, even if I had, I would have been eased, but had I been eased, I would only be more susceptible to hustlings that had yet to be executed. Keeping alert proved to be difficult. The floors were offsetting, damn near paralyzing, and their hellish swerving, swirling patterns combined with the bustling of the hustlers. The whole thing was just dizzying, and I wanted to leave. Following the exact opposite of my instincts, however, I concluded that the wisest option was to wade into the tumult and find the hustler that had either hustled me or hustled me into thinking he had hustled me when he hadn't so I could settle the score. The first steps were more of a task than I had imagined. The ground felt like it was going to give out beneath me if I stepped the wrong way, and my legs felt like fins. I had only managed to scuttle a few feet before my progress was halted. A badge stood in my way. "ID, please," he eyed me cautiously from his towering height and held his palm upwards with wriggling fingers beckoning. "I've got my ticket right here," I warned. "I'm going to slowly reach into my left pocket -- I mean your left pocket. No, that's not it. My pocket on your left." He nodded, looking at me skeptically, and that was what put me on the spot. Reaching to my pocket had thrown my body off balance enough, and I ended up falling down under the pressure of surveillance. "Sir, I think you've had too much to drink." "I'm fine," I lied, followed by a truth. "The floor's just playing games with my head." "Uh huh," he agreed, lacking much fervor. "I've never seen a floor like that before, man." I put my hands on the side of my head to hold it in place. "Is it moving?" "No, it's just a very tricky pattern," he lied. "I still don't think I can let you through." "That's balderdash!" I exclaimed, the furious rush of furious blood offsetting my head with a new top-heaviness. "I won this ticket fair and square!" I reached in my pocket, and it was empty. I began to panic. "My ticket!" I was dumbfounded that I couldn't find it in my pocket. "That hustler picked my pocket!" "I don't know what you're talking about, sir, but I have to escort you out of here," he grimly informed. "You're causing a scene." "You have to catch that guy who stole my ticket!" I rebutted as he grabbed me tightly by the arm. "This is a travesty! You can't do this!" "We don't have tickets here, sir. You must be mistaken." He parted me through the doors and back outside where it was brisker than I had remembered what had to have been over a day ago. It had been so long that I didn't remember how I had gotten there, and if I had made any arrangements to return, they were lost on me. I guessed I'd just have to sit there outside for a while and figure things out. Perhaps there was no ticket, and the radio had hustled me. The radio, after all, was nature's greatest hustler. "Damn you, Guglielmo Marconi!" I aimed towards the skies. "You got a problem with Marconi?" I looked over to see the producer of the voice ambling towards me with rolled sleeves. "Yeah," I stood up. "I do. He's the son of a bitch that invented the radio." "Well, so do I," he said, and tensions eased. "But Tesla really invented the radio, you dumbshit. Guglielmo was a phony, and so are you." He spat at me feet and walked in, and it made me so irate that I couldn't sit still. I stayed on my feet and began pacing around in circles of relatively exact average size as I was only spiraling past my approximated origin. I wondered if I had been hustled again, this time not knowing what I had left that could be hustled from me. My ticket had been hustled, and then my rights. Dignity must have been the target, for I was indignant, though I never recalling having been dignant in the first place. Had I been hustled out of something that I had no idea whether or not I had? Had I had that which I had not had, had I known I had what I had had? James while Johnny had had had had had had had had had had had a better effect on the teacher. I felt like whistling, but didn't know how. I bet James knew how to whistle, that gallant, do-gooding asshole. The circling ceased, and I knew I had to go back in. The guard was there, though, and I knew he would be unwavering to my wavering advances. I'd either have to take him out or fool him. If I tried to take him out and failed, I'd have no chance at fooling him, but if I tried to fool him and fail, I'd still have a shot at taking him out. The decision was made. I'd just have to wait for a disguise to approach, and with the lavish coatings and bejeweled ornamentations so prevalently adorning the influx and outflux of participants, it'd just be a matter of time. I hopped behind the bushes as crowds squeezed in and out two curved tubes that butted into each other. I had to select just the right mark, and one shined out like a gem, wearing an extravagant red robe and a heavy crown. I waited for him to pass and sprung out from the bushes, snatching him from behind around the neck back into the leafy cover. When I felt him slip out of consciousness, I disrobed him of his regencies, suited myself with them and assumed a spot in the oozing troupe. I noticed a dagger sheathed in the robe's lining and noted this would prove to be useful later on. I was nervous as I filtered through the door and approached the guard. "ID please," he automated as I looked down to avoid eye contact. "I don't need ID. I'm the king," I bluffed. "I command you to let me pass." He didn't buy my story, and I couldn't avoid him for long as he tried to peer at my face beneath my tipped crown. "I thought I just kicked you out of--" I unsheathed the dagger and knocked him hard in the side of the head with the butt end. It wasn't his blood I was looking to spill. It was James'. Or was it Marconi's? I didn't remember, and I didn't have much time at the time to remember anyhow. Once the guard came to, it'd all have to be over, and they'd even find him before that happened and lock the whole arena down. I'd have to act fast. I drew the dagger and charged, zig-zagging across those damned carpeting patterns. It wasn't immediate, but it didn't take too long for all the buzzing hustlers to realize that there was a man charging at them wielding a dagger. They were mostly sad, tired folk who looked like they wanted to go home but were instead somehow trapped. They moved away, albeit slowly with no greater sense of urgency than the dull force that shuffled them about from table to machine and back again. They shuffled all the way to a clearing, but men remained. There were three, and they were all as robed and crowned as I, one with a similar red robe and two in black robes. The two in the black drew broadswords and the one in red swung an ax. I was experienced in the sport of swordplay to the point of excess, but I had never faced two men with swords and one with an ax left to the defenses of a dagger. I had drawn the shortest blade at the table, and at this point, I had two options. I could try to take them down, or I could try to fool them. I drew my sword and held it high in my right hand. The fellow kings tensed up on their weapons and presumed moving forward, but their progress halted when I turned my blade inward and appeared, by result of an optical illusion, to stab the dagger through the back of my skull. The conditions were just right -- the way that the flashing lights were playing off of the reflective surfaces. The flashes, beaming on pulses disjointed from one another, played the angles just right to blind all three kings at the same time. I fell to the ground, and the light flooded through. They all three covered their eyes, their synchronization mirroring the causal alignment of awkwardly moving patterns. I made sure that by the time they'd uncover their eyes, I'd never be found. I had done it, and I'm sure there was some point to it all, even if I couldn't remember it. The important thing was that there was a point at all, and I escaped no richer or poorer of a man than I was before save for a little bit of increasingly expensive passages of time.
