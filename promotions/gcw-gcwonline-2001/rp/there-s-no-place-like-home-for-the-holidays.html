There's no place like home for the holidays

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Thanksgiving Day 'New York, New York It's a hell of a town The Bronx is up and the Battery's down The people ride in a hole in a ground... and I forget the rest of the words.' In fact I can't even remember what musical that's from, something in the 50's with sailors running around singing. Ahh, who cares, gimmie Frank singing 'New York, New York' anyday. The great thing about NC-17 being in New York is I lived in New York. Of course that also meant taking a redeye after last night's show so I could have Thanksgiving dinner with my family. That also meant getting into LaGuardia at 6:00 am on Jeff Wylde's private jet and having to drive home myself. But it was nice spotting all the familar landmarks. Like that pizzeria over there, best calzones and baked ziti outside of Little Italy. That record store over there, still has actual records, and rare, hard to find imports. If they don't have it in stock, chances are they can get it. Wait a second... I have to. I just have to. I circled around and took a quick detour. There it is. The most famous arena in the world. Madison Square Garden. As I watched the marquee outside switched from an ad for information on how to get season tickets for Knicks games to 'GCW Presents NC-17'. I smiled to myself as I circled around the arena and headed home. MSG would be the place where Stevens and Knight would meet one more time. It would also be the place where I would get the US title from Johnny Borealis. Thanksgiving may be today, but it would be sunday that I would have something to be thankful for. (Later that day) 'Does he still hate me?' 'A little.' 'I'm sick and tired of being nice to him if he's gonna keep being an asshole. Cin, there's only so much I can take.' I was doing a good job of keeping my voice down. An accomplishment considering that I was talking about my brother-in-law, who for one reason or another didn't like me. And it had nothing to do with my in-ring persona. 'Try, hon, it's Thanksgiving.' Cindy said, I frowned as I finally found the carving knife and serving spoons. 'I won't make any promises,' I said, 'I don't get it, your parents love me. Your brother...' I trailed off as my mother came into the kitchen and grabbed the mashed potatoes and headed back out. A moment later Cindy's mother came in to get the cranberry sauce. I took the turkey out of the oven and Cindy grabbed the stuffing and headed out to the table after helping me get the bird out of the pan and onto the serving tray. I walked into the dining room where my parents, my sister Elizabeth and her husband were on one side of the table and Cindy's parents, her bother and his wife Elisa were on the other side of the table. Her brother glared at me, and I tactfully ignored him. I set the turkey down and the feast began. (Several helpings later) The women had gathered in the kitchen to clean up, while the men retired to the living room to watch the Dallas/Denver game and my father came in with the beers. My brother-in-law still giving me dirty looks. My other brother-in-law, the one married to my sister, I got along fine with. But Steven Matthews, my wife's brother didn't like me. I got tired of getting the stink eye from him, and I had taken about all I could stand. 'What is it with you?' I asked him as the game went to commercial. 'Did I do something somewhere along the line to piss you off?' 'Oh shit...' I heard Chris, my sister's husband mumble. My father-in-law shifted in his seat but said nothing. 'Really, Steven I need to know. I don't get it, ever since I married Cindy, it seems like you've had it in for me and I just want to know why.' I said, 'What did I do to deserve this treatment from you?' 'You really want to know?' 'Yes, I really want to know.' 'It's your job.' 'My job?' I asked, 'What you mean wrestling?' He nodded. 'What about it? Wait...you don't think that I got Cin into that do you? She was involved with the biz before I met her. In fact, it's how I met her. You can't be blaming me for that.' 'It's not that.' Steven said, 'It's the business itself. It's what it does to people. I've heard that sometimes what happens on camera sometimes translates to what happens off-camera as well. That it causes problems, and that you people...' 'You people?' I said, incredulous, 'You people? You mean wrestlers? Those people? Us people? Let me tell you a little something about us people. Wrestling may not be the most glamorous job in the world, it may not be the most high class job in the world, but it's what I love to do. I love to hear the crowd cheer or boo me when I step out onto that stage. I love the rush I feel when I'm in the middle of that ring and the bell rings. Winning is just the icing on the cake. I'm not that guy you see on TV. That...' I pointed to the TV because just at that moment a commercial for NC-17 came on, and as perfect timing would have it, a shot of me came up from the WorldWide where Johnny Borealis and I had our knuckle duster backstage. '...is not the real me. That's a side of me I play for the fans, that I play for the other wrestlers. Away from the ring and all that...' I waved a dismissive hand at the TV as the NC-17 logo came up, '...I'm really a nice guy. Ask anybody. I love Cindy, and I take care of her. Sure I may not always be here because I'm out working shows, but that doesn't mean I don't think about Cindy, that doesn't mean I forget about her. I'm here today because I want to be, not because I have to be. It's Thanksgiving and I wouldn't want to be anywhere else. I chose to be here with my family, and like it or not Steven, you're a part of my family, and will be until the day you die. So frankly I don't care if you don't like me, I don't care if you don't like what I do for a living, and I don't care if we never see each other again. What you feel about me isn't going to change the way I feel about Cindy, and isn't going to make me stop feeling this way. Not after seven years.' Satisfied that I made my point, I settled back into my recliner and grabbed my beer. Chris, who was sitting at the end of the couch next to me raised his bottle to me. 'Well said.' I toasted him and glanced over to the kitchen where Cindy, Elisa and my mother were standing looking back out into the living room. They had heard most of what had happened. 'How long have you been standing there?' I asked after toasting Chris and taking a sip of my beer. 'From 'You people' on.' Elisa said. 'I didn't realize you felt that strongly about it.' Steven said. 'You never asked. Apparently you just took what I did at face value and decided I wasn't good enough for Cindy.' I said, Cindy had walked into the living room and stood behind my chair and rested her hands on my shoulders. I glanced up and put one of my hands over hers. 'I love this woman, and I would do anything for her.' 'And I love him, Steven. I've told you that I don't know how many times. Nothing's going to change that. I can't help who I fall in love with. I can't change your mind, but I can ask that you at least be happy for me.' Cindy said. A uncomfortable silence fell over the living room, except for the TV which blared away the football game which was about to go halftime. Finally Steven looked around as Elizabeth, and my mother-in-law joined us in the living room. He raised his beer. 'To family.' I raised my beer, 'To family.' We all toasted and the women of the family sat next to their respective husbands as we watched the rest of the game. It amazed me that all it took was one tirade at Steven to get him to understand how I felt about Cindy. Hell, if I'd known that I would have done it a long time ago. And there was something else. Oh yeah. Happy Thanksgiving everyone.
