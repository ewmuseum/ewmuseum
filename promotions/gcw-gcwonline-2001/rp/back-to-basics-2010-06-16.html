Date: 06/16/10 From the desk of Shaman… I’ve been keeping a journal of sorts lately. Something a friend of mine suggested trying. So here goes... I was given a second chance to get into the Rampage tournament. I sometimes wonder how or why I got accepted back into this. Heard some rumors that people had noticed the talent for once, and was given the shot. Don’t know about all that. All I can say is I am going to have to grab this bull by the horns if I am going to stand a chance. Do I have some doubts? That is the obvious answer. I can't just go out and kick someone’s ass and be satisfied anymore. It’s simply not enough. I need to go out and win it all to get somewhere now. I need to go back out there, get my own mind right. Get myself back to where I know I should be. I think it is time to ask for some help… 14:22 hours. Inside a dark and moist concrete gymnasium, grunts of exertion echo about. The gymnasium remains docile otherwise, lined with gym equipment from the 80's and 90's, including dumbbells and various types of free weights. Amidst these free weights, Shaman lays out across a bench, pressing the weights straight up in the air. His normal, everyday workout. Placing the bar back square on its resting pieces, he pinches the back of his hand in the weight. With his hand pinned momentarily, he actually pushes it weight straight up with the trapped hand, nearly sending the weights crashing to the ground. Now free, the black steel smashes back into its resting place. “Son of a bitch!” He grasps his hand as he leans forward, sitting on the bench and examining the damage. Grimacing slightly, awash with disappointment, he spots someone walking toward him. The man, in his late fifties, hovers over him. The light gleams off of the man’s eyeglasses. “Andrew.” His voice booms out into the cold night air. “You’ve gotten soft. Your last performance, you feel you had underachieved. Why do you doubt your abilities?” He places a hand on Shaman’s shoulder. “It is time to get off your damn high horse and start from the bottom again.” Shaman glares through him, pure rage burning in his eyes. The old man laughs. “Good thing you at least still have anger to use. Seems like it’s the only thing that you can use properly these days, isn't it?” The old man walks over a weight lying on the floor, and picks it up. Shaman seethes quietly. “You need to face the truth, Andrew. It stares you in the face like your own mirror image.” He lets the weight drop from his hands, as it clangs onto the ground. “You never were any good.” Shaman snaps up to attention and stalks right up to the old man, finger wagging in his face. “Like your damn career was any better!” The old man simply flashes his stained yellows. “Maybe. But I never endured a losing streak that would put Custer to shame.” The smile fades, as he seems to grow reminiscent. He lets out a deep sigh and takes off his glasses. “I always thought of you as one of my own kids when you first came here.” He holds them up to the light, the smudges clear as ever. He wipes them clean on his shirt. “You remember when you took Suzy to the prom?” He puts them on and smiles. “You both looked so happy together. Do you remember that night?” Shaman says nothing. The old man nods. “I thought as much.” The man walks over to a stack of dusty moldering magazines that have piled up in the corner. Reaching down, he picks one up, and brushes off the mites. They float in the air, tiny little dancing specs in the moonlight. He holds it up, in clear view of Shaman. It’s of Shaman. World Champion. “I’m sure you remember this?” Shaman burns a hole through the magazine, before eventually conceding with a nod. The old man tosses it Shaman’s way, and it lands on the ground in front of him. “This isn't you anymore. You need to understand that, and you know why.” Shaman massages his chin, chuckling at the man’s notion. “Is it now?” The man swoops up the magazine and holds it in Shaman’s face. “You have lost direction! Focus! Drive! The things that made you great!” The man taps on the cover repeatedly. “When I watch you, I see a shell. An apathetic shell, too bullheaded to give a damn! I see a fool!” He whips the book to the ground, as it snaps on the unforgiving floor. “I don’t see a champion.” Shaman tilts his head down. His eyes on the book, on him, a younger man, with a shiny tin belt that signified so much. Victory. Glory. History. His voice suddenly grows somber. “I was thinking, you know? Wondering if this was my time.” He laughs. “I was good once, you know? But now…now…” His voice trails. He lifts his head. “Is it my time to give in, to--“ Suddenly, Shaman gets smacked by the old man upside the head with a yard stick. “Quit bellyaching, damnit! I’m gonna talk, and you’re gonna listen, and I’m sure as hell not listenin’ to you whine about this and that and the other.” The man taps Shaman along the shoulder with the stick. “Now, on you’re feet. I’mma teach you something.” His eyes become that of the wise teacher. He’s Pei Mei, straight out of a Shaw Brothers flick. “Even if it kills me.” 15:25 hours.   The old man marches back and forth, as Shaman stands at full attention, gauging his every move “Just look at you. Where have you been hanging out, Muscle Beach? Should I get you some oil, rub you down?” Surprise covers Shaman, as the old man just grunts. “You probably haven't had a real workout in years.” The old man taps the medicine ball in front of him with the yardstick, and Shaman picks it up. “If I have to tell you what to do with this, so help me God, I will give you the thrashing of a lifetime.” Shaman obliges, cradling the medicine ball and doing squats. The old man circles him. “I’ve seen you lately. Punching holes in walls. Burning down buildings. Wasting time with that other idiot you pal around with.” Shaman starts to sweat in the midst of squats as the old man watches on. Shaman feels his thighs and hamstrings burn from the workout. “You think you’re some tough guy? Hah!” The old man continues to circle like a vulture. “I’ve watched you out there. You’re like a board of wood! Where is your style?” Shaman starts to struggle, as in comes a whack from the yard stick to the back of his knee. Shaman grunts loudly. “You’re quitting on me already? I was wrong. You’re not soft.” The man gets in Shaman’s ear, as spittle flies. “You’re pathetic!” Shaman would shoot bullets from his eyes if he could, but the old man keeps on him, and Shaman does as the old man says. It had been about fifteen years since the last time this man had trained him. Hell, it was nine since he even saw the guy’s mug. Figured he was dead by now, but he always did have a habit of sticking around. But if anyone could push hard, it was him. Anyone could get someone into tip-top shape, it was him. Anyone to turn things around, it was---OWWW!!!! “Quit daydreaming, I said, damnit,” the old man hollers after a few whacks with the yard stick. Shaman struggles through the routine, as the old man barks encouragement. “You better start pushing back right about now. I’m an old man. You keep pressing on like a layabout, I might die.” The old man gets in, eye-to-eye. “You’d like that, huh? Put an old man in the grave? See me pushin’ the daisies?” Shaman’s head shakes back and forth. The old man just grunts and slides away. Surveying the wall, the old man mumbles to himself. “Been awhile since I’ve been here.” He fixates on a picture of a man with a teenage boy, and to the left of the two a teenage girl. He gets close in and focuses, fixing his glasses. The three are smiling, having a good time. The man smiles.  “This brings back memories.” He stalks back up to Shaman. “I think I’ll enjoy whipping your hide back into playing shape.” Shaman nearly has reached his breaking point. “Good…for you, you…old…bastard!” The man rushes up to him. “What’re you doing?” He rights Shaman, who’s nearly fully off balance. He grabs the medicine ball from Shaman’s sweaty palms. Shaman drops to the ground, exhausted. From a distance, the old man checks out Shaman. “It’s been awhile, you know?” He wraps the side of his noggin with his fist. “I’ve not the memory I’ve used to.” He wraps the side of Shaman’s head. “I need to get back inside there.” The old man rubs his chin, entrenched in thought. “So maybe you can tell me something. Why’d a lughead like you get another shot? Obviously not your beauty!” He lets out a wheezing laugh, and slaps his knees. After a few moments of laughter and uncomfortably on Shaman’s part, the old man acts like nothing happened. “You got beat, fair and square. So, what goes on there, buddy boy?” Shaman opens his mouth to say something, but the man hems and haws instead. “Maybe we forget all that. Focus on your opponents, instead.” He paces, dragging the yard stick along the ground. “You know one, that’s for sure. You don’t know the other.” Shaman won’t sit idly by anymore, and pipes up loudly. “You don’t know a damn thing about me and him. So leave that one alone, old man. For your own sake.” The man tilts his head back, one eye wide open. A sheepish look crosses his face. “Come with me,” he says, as he turns towards double doors across the room and walks towards them. Shaman rises and follows, as the old man pushes through the doors. Walking through, it’s an old boxing ring. The top corners have garnered cobwebs, the ring covered in a thin layer of dust. Shaman and the old man walk past the walls, of pictures of boxers from years passed, including a young Shaman himself. The old man enters the ring, and Shaman follows. Shaman seems taken aback by the whole place, if only for a moment. “This seems…familiar.” The old man smirks. “It should. It’s where it all started.” Shaman soaks in the room once more. “Damnit, son,” the man’s voice rings out, directed towards Andrew. “You can’t be sixteen forever! What’re you gonna do with your life, huh?” Jacob, the boxing trainer, looks around the room at the other kids. “They know what they want out of life.” He points towards the pictures on the wall, yard stick in hand. “They knew, too.” The yard stick swings back into Andrew’s face. “But you? Hmph.”   Andrew stares, wide-eyed at the pictures, but stops on one in particular. A man, in a boxing pose, gloves draped over his shoulders. Andrew points at it. “Dad…was here, too?” Jacob nods. “Your old man was one helluva boxer, he was. Won the light heavyweight title twice here in Nevada.” Andrew’s posture straightens slightly, a bit more poise about him. His eyes scan the pictures on the wall once again. Not just boxers. Men of distinction. Police. Military. Doctors. Lawyers. Andrew observes them carefully. “All these people came through here?” Jacob moves outside the ring, and places a new picture on the wall. A picture of Andrew. Pumping gas at the gas station he had a job at. Andrew’s pride drops away. “Something to be proud of, Andrew?” Jacob remains focused on Andrew’s horrible reaction. “I’ve known people that’ve done good. People who set goals, and achieved dreams. And they’re all long gone.” Andrew’s eyes float back to the image of his father. “Do you know why your father was successful?” Andrew shakes his head “no”. Back in the ring, Jacob places a hand on his shoulder. “He was successful because he married the woman he loved. ‘Cause he had three wonderful children. ‘Cause he made sure his kids had something for the future.” Andrew looks up at Jacob. “What did he leave us?” Jacob pats Andrew on the shoulder. He walks over, and grabs a medicine ball. He drops it in front of Andrew. “Now, who do you want to be? Do you want to be one of them, or do you want to blaze your own path? Make your own mark.” Andrew stares at the wall of pictures. His eyes light up, as Andrew lifts up the medicine ball.  Jacob eyes the picture of Andrew’s father. “He’s got your fire, you know.” Jacob hollers at Andrew. “Okay, kid. Let’s get to it! Squats, now!” Andrew starts in on his squats, as Jacob yells encouragement at him. He was what his dad always wanted him to be. Jacob taps Shaman with the yard stick. “What gears you got grindin’ in that melon of yours, huh?” Shaman shakes off his Dad's picture and looks at the aged Jacob. “Remembering some things you said to me. Back when you weren’t decrepit.” Jacob howls with laughter. “At least you’re thinking again. That’s as good a start as any!” Jacob walks around the room, ravaged by time, and points at a picture of Shaman holding a wrestling title belt. “You remember this title? Remember how it felt when you won it? Remembering how you felt, achieving a goal you set for yourself?” Jacob taps the yard stick in his open palm. Shaman ponders for a moment. “That first title, that was a monstrous achievement. It opened the doors for me to enter the big stage. I met a man named Mark Harrington, shortly after that win. He helped propel me up the ranks. Mark was more than just a federation owner. He was a colleague. No,” Shaman tapped his hand on his leg as he observed the picture. “A friend.” Shaman displays a rare moment of happiness. “I was good back then. First time in a long time I was good. You know, I showed off to my Mom and Dad when I became a champion? I held that title up to the sky. I screamed out into the night that I loved them.” He shuts his eyes. “That this was for them.” Shaman slowly opens his eyes, as the light creeps back in. “Now, it is much, much more than the drive to prove something to someone. I want to win. I want to beat people. I want them to remember, to never be able to say they ever stood a chance against me.” Shaman clenches his fist, smashing it against the wall. His knuckles go white. “I am so damn tired of losing. Tired of being cheated.” “I want to get my hands back on Shipley. I made a simple mental error.” Shaman holds his index finger high in the air. “One mistake.” Jacob looks Shaman dead in the eye. Shaman’s eyes flash with something different. A flame, one he hasn’t seen before. It used to be just revenge and rage all rolled into a ball. But now, Shaman was a gun that just needed to be aimed. Jacob clears his throat “Do you believe you’re a dangerous man, Andrew?” Shaman looks up at Jacob, his voice seething with rage. “Yes.” Jacob continues. “Why?” “I am unpredictable. I use every situation to my advantage. And I don't let sleeping dogs lie.” Shaman laughs deeply. He puts his hand on Jacob’s shoulder. “Shall we get back to it, old man?” A twinkle forms in Jacob’s eyes. He slaps Shaman’s chest. “Hot damn! ‘Bout time you got a bug up your ass.” Scuffling causes Jacob to turn around, where Suzy stands, frowning at him. Jacob swings back to Shaman. “And the hits keep on rollin’, eh?” Shaman can’t help but smile as he spots Suzy. “Hey Sooz.” She cuts him off quick as she smirks. “Before you go on about your business, you’re going to go handle your terror on wheels back at the house. I swear, that kid inherited your wild side.” Shaman kisses Suzy's cheek as he walks past her and out the door. Jacob sidles up alongside Suzy. “You’re quite the lion tamer, Suzy.” “He didn't need to be tamed, dad,” she replies. “He just needs someone there for him. His whole life has been nothing but betrayal and loss. Why do you think he has all that rage inside of him? Why do you think he suddenly cares now, after all these years?” Her finger moves to a picture on the wall, of Shaman and Mayhem, together, with the GCW tag titles raised. “When he is in that ring, he is as dangerous as they come. You don’t think I spend every night, lying in bed, wondering is this the night? Is this when he goes over the edge?” She wraps her own arms around herself. Jacob walks over to her and hugs her close. “You know I’d never let that happen.” He releases her from his grip. “He’s stronger than all that. Family is the only thing that’s meant something to him. I think he’d fight himself before he let anything happen to you both.” She smiles weakly at him, and kisses his cheek. “Thank you, dad.” Suzy quietly exits the gym, as Jacob leans against the wall, rubbing his temples. “Jesus, I hope I’m right.” Jacob takes his turn and walks out of the gym, shutting off the lights. Jacob slowly makes his way out, into the hot and humid air. He watches Suzy walk up the steps to the deck, where Shaman plays with his daughter. “Why do I feel like I am opening Pandora's Box?” Jacob peers at them from afar, talking to no one in particular. “What happened to you all these years? Is the beast just itching to get out?” Jacob walks down the side of the building to a door. He pulls out a key and unlocks the door. Noticing some movement, he pauses and turns his head, seeing another man standing there, looking at him. “Well. The two siblings, in the same place. Never thought I'd see the day.” Jason looks at Jacob and smiles. “Been a long time, Jake.” Jacob motions for Jason to follow him through the door, and, once both are through, he shuts the door behind them. Inside, the large form of Jorge Samuelsson towers over both. Jacob motions for both men to sit, and moves over to a refrigerator nearby. He grabs two cans of Pabst Blue Ribbon and tosses it over to them. “It’s time, boys. I need all the information you’ve got before I cut him loose.” Jason looks at Jacob. “Several years back, we formed a group called Militia with my good friend Charlie Longtooth, better known as Iron Bear. Andrew was the big dog, had all the show creds. He was going to take us to our promised land.” Jason continues on explaining the story, as Jacob and Samuelsson listen on intently. To be continued...
