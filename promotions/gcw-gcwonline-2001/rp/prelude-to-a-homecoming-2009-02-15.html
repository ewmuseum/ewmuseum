Prelude to a Homecoming

15 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02/15/09 The pictures lay scattered across a stained and dirty mattress. What remains of the building is barely supported by framework long ago losing to the invasion of time and decay. Somewhere in the west across the Atlantic ocean, the sun is dawning on a brand new day. But here it gives ground to the stretching claws of night. The clippings are a random collection of acquaintances and places, times, memories, dreams and aspirations . Some of them best left to the scattered nostalgia of a mind long moved forward, and some best kept in the warmest reaches of the soul. The man sitting at the foot of the bed called this room home for twelve years. His eyes aren’t scanning around the barracks though, they’re not picking out friends and playmates. It was a world of education and preparation, training and fighting. There were no friends; there was no playing. Those aren’t the monsters he’s come to slay this night. No, that was a beast felled long ago as boy transformed into man. He’s returned to this tortuous place to find something else. He buried it many years ago, tucked away in a little dented tin with news paper and magazine bits and clips. It’s the pictures, the only remnants of the life he was supposed to have. He holds the select few in a steady hand, and he can see the man he was meant to be. A banker, or a sales man. He can see his mother, grown old and baking cookies for grandchildren. These fleeting images of a better existence replace the truth for but the briefest moments of bliss. They hide the fire that took her, and the father that took him. They wash away all the years of coming to age in an all boys military school removed so far from civilization the rest of the world became fable. His final sigh is neither melancholy nor nostalgic, but instead relieved if not peaceful. As he starts placing the final images of his mother and himself on the bed the shape comes together all at once. The paper man is complete as a door protests entrance at the other end of the dorm. “Johnny?” He doesn’t answer until her sneakers squeak across the floor. She’s as sultry and proud as he’d ever want her to be. He can’t help but watch her thighs roll with every step, the casual sway of her hips or the curves of her figure. His crusader against the dark clad in a short skirt and a long jacket. “I found it Steph.” He offers her a half smile as he takes back a single picture left over the paper man’s heart. “Found what?” Emerald green eyes examine his creation. “My hope.” Getting to his feet they head for the door, their hands not reaching for one another. Not a single outward sign of the affection passed between their eyes. Johnny fixes his scarf and the resting of his own long wool jacket, reaching for the door. “I have a feeling I’m going to need it.” “Don’t say it.” She warns as they emerge from the building and face a long black limo. “I want to wrestle again.” The driver opens the door as Steph climbs inside and Johnny follows in anticipation of her tirade. But instead, as the portal to the world beyond mood lights and tinted glass is slammed shut: “I know.” Calm. “You know?” Quizzical. “I’m going to say it straight because I love you. There’s only three things you’re good at: Fighting, Fucking, and Shooting people.” She lists them off on her fingers in point format. “We’ve been chasing ghosts and fucking for months so you either have to start fighting or shooting people. I’m only glad you chose the lesser of two evils, but while we’re talking about shooting people-? “Enough.” Johnny snickers. “I’m good at more than that.” His playful indignation nearly evokes one of the elusive smiles from his icy counter part. “Two weeks Johnny.” She reaches over to put her hand on his knee. The first physical contact between them since leaving history behind. “Finish the book tour, take the publisher buy out, and then we’ll do it right.” She takes her hand back. “No more games, no more small stages.” “Global Championship.” Those emerald eyes move to meet his own pale blues. “No.” The corner of his lips curl into the barest hint of a smile as he nods. “No.” She shrugs and looks away. “Yeah.” “Johnny.” She levels a finger in his direction, reasons poised ready at the tip of her tongue when he grabs her hand gently. Her digits dwarfed by his calloused weapons, knuckles swollen and pink with scars. “No more games. No more small stages.” She pulls her hand free and turns a wicked scowl on him. “Every time you and Jordan are together you end up poor, broken, and bitter. I’m not putting you back together again. Have you forgotten what you let that place do to you?” She raises a hand to silence him, The Ice Queen surfacing in all her bitchy glory when Johnny grabs her. She could fight him off as every instinct in her body suddenly urges, but she doesn’t even flinch as he takes her head in his thick fingered hand. There’s a feral electricity as their eyes meet and Steph finds what she’s come all the way out here looking for. “Have a little faith that I haven’t wasted the best years of my career in half watched promotions for seven years.” His rough baritone is little more than a mumble. “With or without you Steph, I have to prove this to myself.” There’s a moment of long silence as their wills are tested by the intensity between them. “I’m only good at three things.” He admits with genuine sincerity. “But I’m really, really, good at them.” She finally cedes with a hard fought smile that fades as quickly as it appeared. She lowers her head to prepare her next words. “We finish the book tour, we take the buy out and we put some money away. Then we do this, but if you end up broken-? “You’ll put me back together again.” She looks up again into those eyes to make sure that what she saw was real, what she’d very nearly forgotten lay within those pale orbs had once again returned with a fiery passion. “I hope they’re ready.”
