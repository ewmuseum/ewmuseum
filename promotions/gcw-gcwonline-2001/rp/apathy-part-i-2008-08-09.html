Apathy [Part I]

9 August 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/9/08 I sat alone in a booth with my legs propped up on the bench opposite of me, at a local Village Inn. (For those of you who don’t know, a Village Inn is basically a really cheap version of IHOP. Yes, it really is that quality.) Orange juice on the left side of me, and a half empty cup of coffee on the right, I stared blankly at the table in front of me, waiting for my breakfast tacos. How had I gotten to this point? How was it possible, for a world-class fighter, to suddenly be reduced to $4.99 breakfast specials?” It didn’t make any sense. Three years ago, I had everything: A child on the way, a beautiful wife, a career that was going strong, strewn with championships, and fans galore. Where did it go wrong? How was it possible to suddenly be known as the member of the strongest faction, in one of the strongest federations he’d ever been in, to get beat by a man who didn’t even fight for the first half of the match? And, to make matters worse, he had the mentality of a six year old! I’d lost my wife; I’d lost my child, and was now dating a woman who was as hollow and empty as I was. Sure, Cami was a gorgeous woman, and extremely intelligent, but when it came to emotion, and having any sort of real depth, she was a vast wasteland of nothingness. She was more arm candy for events, and parties, and things like that. A few more minutes passed, and my breakfast came. Three tacos with eggs and chicken, wrapped in a slightly stale flour tortilla with a side of browned lettuce, and a small cup of salsa for dipping purposes. As I picked up one of them, and bit into it, I had a startling realization. My life was very similar to these half stale, browned tacos. I had sat out for so long, inactive, and not focusing on the things that I needed to focus on that I had gotten “stale” and “browned.” Again, the question that rang through my head: “How did I get to this point?” .~. December 12th, 2004 Had I not have gone ice skating that day, I would never have fallen flat on my ass (which NEVER happens, seeing as I had been skating for years), and had I not fallen, I would never caused her to trip over me and land practically in my lap. Funny how things work out, and how sometimes things very literally get dropped into our laps in life. Glori was one of those times where I just sat in awe of God. I got to my skates, and helped her up, and our eyes met. She was absolutely beautiful; emerald green eyes, with auburn hair that flowed down just past her shoulders. A smile toyed at the corners of her mouth as she saw that I had lost any motor skills that my mouth had previously had. “I’m Glori.” She said, with a voice that suddenly felt as smooth as honey. I continued to stare, and she broke into a giggle, breaking the stare. I finally came to my senses, and was able to form a sentence. “I’m Tyson.” I stammered. “Sorry about that, I hardly ever fall on skates. I’ve been doing it since I was a kid, so I’m usually pretty?” I stopped myself short. I had a bad habit of rambling when I was nervous, and even more annoying it was generally about myself. Nobody liked to hear someone talk about themselves incessantly. She giggled again. “Apparently you are human as well as the rest of us, good sir.” My eyes widened. She had a confidence about her that made her extremely attractive. She flipped her hair back, “Thanks for the break from the monotony of skating in circles.” Then she winked. And I felt myself melt. She turned and started to skate away. I stood frozen for a moment, knowing I wanted to follow her and ask for her phone number, but not knowing exactly how to do so. So I did the only thing I knew how to do. I skated up to her, flipped around to my back, and glided next to her, as coolly and confidently as I could. “So, um’do you think?” I tried to force out what I wanted to ask, but it just wasn’t working. I had never really been that great with women. She grinned, and pulled a card out of her coat pocket, and handed to me. Glori Allysin Rose. Professional Wrestling Manager. Needless to say, I lost my balance again. .~. Meeting Glori was the beginning of a crowning point of my career. As a young fighter, I hadn’t really reached a point of political standing amongst my peers. And as any seasoned veteran in this industry will tell you, it’s as much politics as it is skill. I had the skill, Glori added the political value. She made the contacts, built relationships with different people in key positions, and allowed me to use my skill in matches, and federations that mattered. She made me care. Before her, I just fought to fight, just because it was a semi-decent paycheck. Once she came around, I started fighting with more passion, which made me that much more valuable to any company I was with. Glori always said it was as much her reputation as it was mine if I fucked things up. Which, I completely agreed with. She got me matches that had I taken lightly, would have destroyed my career. I sipped at my coffee, and pushed the half eaten plate of tacos over to the edge of the table for the server to pick up. Maybe that was where I was lacking. I had nothing to care about. Caldera was being very generous with me, including me in the Establishment, and I now had an opportunity to win a match that would ensure me a U.S. Title match, whenever I wanted it if I won. The opportunities were there, yet I just really didn’t care at all. Garbage Bag Johnny, Zachariah Taylor and Jacob McKail. GBJ had been on a winning streak with his unorthodox style of fighting, Taylor had just come off of a run as the “Greatest GCW Television Champion” ever, and as far as McKail was concerned, I really didn’t know to much about him, other than the fact that obviously he was good enough to be in this match. I would never admit it to anyone else, but I somehow felt like the underdog in this situation. Sure, I’d come into GCW with a few wins under my belt, but since that point, I’d only seen moral victories, nothing for the stat sheet. Hell, I was pretty sure Caldera had an ulterior motive for me being in this match. I don’t think anyone had confidence that I would win at all. Fuck, even I didn’t. Where had all my confidence gone too? .~. Over time, Glori and I developed a very strong relationship. We were married a year after we met, in the winter of “05. I was at the peak of my career, holding various titles, with a very well known name around a variety of federations. I was bringing in a very large sum of money, allowing Glori and I to live extremely comfortably in a Villa on the side of a mountain in South Lake Tahoe, California. Shortly after we were married, we brought a little girl into the world. Aurora Jade. She was my pride and joy, and I would have done anything for that little girl. The wrestling world started to change, to a much darker business. It seemed that the days of honorable fighting, and working your way up the ladder were gone, replaced by a time of lying, cheating, brutal side attacks, and unholy alliances. Glori suggested I stay current with the times. I didn’t have to sell my soul for real, but I at least had to play along. The problem with playing along, is most of the time you end up getting sucked into whatever it is you’re playing along with. Do it long enough, and it becomes a part of you. I went from being an upstanding individual, to being someone people loathed. Including Glori. Ultimately she left. I came home to an empty house, and a note, saying that she had taken Aurora with her to a safe place, because I was no longer stable enough to be around her. In a fit of rage, I tore the house apart, destroying anything I could possibly get my hands on. In retrospect, I think that night was pivotal in my downfall. I snapped. My focus went from winning a match, to destroying whoever it was that faced me. Everyone was helping Glori in my eyes, and I had to get my little girl back. Glori had stolen her from me. She had taken my light, and my reason for being alive, and I was determined to get her back. I became reckless. I started fighting with no technique, I was fighting in a blind rage whenever I stepped through the ring. Ultimately, my actions consumed me. I became a man with no morals, and no emotion. Glori started fighting on her own in the industry, and gained favor very quickly. It was like she was blatantly trying to mock me, which further angered me. So, I did what any cold hearted, emotionless man would do. I put her in the hospital with a sledgehammer. .~. My phone broke my own pattern of thought, abruptly. I pulled it out of my pocket and looked at the caller ID. It was Cami. It dawned on me that it had been a few days since I’d seen her. She was at Warpath, with me when I cleaned up and watched Terror get screwed out of his World Title. Then I told her I would be back later that evening, that I had some things to take care of. And that was the last time I had seen her. I opened the phone up and placed it to my ear. “Hey babe.” “Jesus Tyson, where the hell have you been” I’ve been scared out of my mind!” “I know, I’m sorry hun, I lost track of time. Been doing some soul searching I guess you could say. Trying to figure out who the hell I am.” The response was partly true. I had only just started to think about those sorts of things this morning. The other days were filled with alcohol, cigarettes and a relapse into the world of cocaine. There was a brief pause on the other end of the line. I knew what she was thinking. We’d never discussed anything serious in the entire time we’d known each other. It just wasn’t in our “unspoken agreement.” “Are you ok?” “Yea, I’m fine. I’ll be home in about an hour or so, and we can talk more if you’d like.” Another pause. “Ok’see you soon.” I didn’t respond. I just hung up the phone, and put it back into my pocket. I knew she wouldn’t be there when I got home. She’d be gone, a note left in her stead. Cami wasn’t the type of girl that wanted to be a part of anything that involved emotion. Not yet at least. And honestly, I didn’t feel like explaining it to someone. I had too much to figure out in my own head. I stood, and tossed some money on the table for the meal, and shitty service, and walked out the front door. Pulling out a cigarette, I lit it, and took a drag. Now what? .~. Glori never recovered from the attack. She ended up dying with her family around her in a hospital room. Aurora was taken to a foster family, away from me. I hadn’t seen her in over a year. She was almost three now. After Glori’s death, I shut myself off even more. And once that happened, I became a man that no one wanted to be around. I fought, but just to do something. I lived, but for the sake of living. No other reason. I became a shell of a man, walking around, and doing things just because they were there. My passion was gone. My drive was gone. I just didn’t care. My own apathy overtook me. I found solace in a small baggy, filled with a white powder. I knew it was something that would always keep me “up.” Even if it was a fake high, only to end in a horrible crash if I didn’t keep up with it, it still felt good to have something there. Eventually I crashed again, ended up in rehab, and cleaned myself up from the drug life. But the apathy was another demon entirely. I couldn’t seem to get rid of it. The women I dated, and/or fucked, were merely toys. None of them held any value whatsoever. The friends I had were “single serving” if you will. I was still alone, drowning in a puddle, with no desire to do something as simple as picking my head up and taking a breath. Eventually, I left the wrestling world. Took a sabbatical from the entire thing, and didn’t tell anyone where I was going. The thing about this industry though, again as any veteran will tell you, is that you never leave for good. I got to a point where I felt an itch to be a part of the glamour again. I wanted to feel the accomplishment of winning. That was when I found AWC. Unfortunately for me, the talent had only gotten better in the time that I was away, so shaking off the ring rust became that much more difficult. I won a match here and there, until the federation closed. Another few months passed, and all of the momentum I had built back disappeared again. Then a talent scout for Global Championship Wrestling approached me, and I jumped at the opportunity, anxious to try and get to the point where I mattered again. I joined, and worked my way up to becoming the GCW Television Champion ? with a win over Jay Terror. I couldn’t have been more excited. It seemed like I had things going my way again. I held a championship, had a streak going, and was seemingly at a point where I could gain my respect back again. Or so I thought. The past as an interesting way of catching up with you, if you’re not paying attention. .~. I pulled my keys out of my pocket, and inserted the house key into the slot, opening it and stepping inside. “Hello?” No answer. “Anybody home?” I called again. There was a small part of me that was disappointed; a piece that wanted to believe that Cami might have been different than the rest of the girls that I had dated. Again, I was proven right in my own mind. She had left, but the one thing I was wrong about, was that there was no note this time. She just up and left. Apparently her void of human emotion was deeper than I had expected. Kudos to her for being able to detach so quickly. I tossed my keys onto the counter and walked into the bedroom, and my heart skipped a beat. The shower was on. Maybe she hadn’t left? She was just in the shower?” For the first time in a long time, my heart started pounding as I pushed open the door to the bathroom. The mirror was fogged, and the humidity from the hot shower exploded out of the door. “Jesus babe, do you think the water is hot enough?” I opened the door fully, and turned the fan on, and walked towards the shower curtain. “Babe?” My stomach suddenly got extremely uneasy as I pulled the curtain back just enough to be able to see into the shower. Suddenly I saw exactly why she wasn’t responding to my playful banter. Her nude body was slumped against the tub, eyes frozen wide open, her throat sliced wide open from ear to ear. Blood was dribbling down her chest, onto the shower floor, forming a crimson stream into the drain. I turned off the shower, and closed the curtain. The entire room started to spin, slowly at first, and then continually faster. Then, the tacos that I had just eaten decided to make their way to the surface once again. Then everything went black. “to be continued.
