A Promo!”

1 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6-1-05 “Have you ever heard that history repeats itself my dear friend Mr. Marshall?” It had been a LONG time since Stephens had cut a promo for a wrestling event. Have patience with him. “I touch upon old accolades partly because I’m proud of them. I speak of them because they’re not meaningless mid-card titles from some Podunk town, USA. They’re significant achievements in our sport. And you’re right, the past is just that, and now is the present. But I mention my past as a forewarning so you will be prepared for the future because quite frankly Mr. Marshall, history does indeed repeat itself especially if you’re too na’ve to see it coming.” He smirked into the camera. His gray eyes wandered as the shot zoomed out and showed his full body. He wasn’t in his usual suit, but white gym shorts and a cut off T-shirt. Physically he looked as good as he used to. He deferred back to the camera. “You’re a walking contradiction Bryan, and you want me to take you seriously” I don’t deny that you bring some passion to this sport, and maybe you’ll win a few matches along the way, but come on man. First you say that you have eaten your words, but then you claim you talk because you know you can back it up. If you can back it up so well, then how come you’ve had to eat your words?” A stern glare. Beads of sweat formed near the brow. “And then you proceed to blabber on about this and that and how I can’t prevail and then you claim you respect me” Make up your mind man. My favorite thing about you is how you’re all nice and chatty and then all of a sudden you just flip out and it’s so unconvincing. Are you certain it isn’t an act? I am certain you’ll come back with something to the effect of, “I’ll show you act when I throw you over the top rope come Thursday. You’re as predictable as they come chief.” Stephens walks over to a weight bench and sits down. It has become increasingly apparent that he’s been working out in his downtown workout facility in the basement of the old law office he used to clerk for before he became a wrestler. Unlike his normal lifestyle, it was anything fancy. It wasn’t anything that you couldn’t find in a junior high weight room hidden away beneath some old wooden stairs. “And don’t get me wrong either. I’m not saying I’m going to win this match. Not to discredit the television title belt by any means. I would give it a solid run if it fell in my lap because let’s be honest, that means more publicity for me. But, if I don’t win, well that’s certainly okay too because that means there is one less numbskull like you trying to beat me down for a taste of the gold. Everyone is all about the gold these days. If it is not about money and publicity then it should at least be about the respect.” Stephens laughed aloud to himself. He cared less about the respect. He paused as he searched his brain for more useless verbal banter. Promos weren’t really his style, which was moderately surprising seeing as how he was a phenomenal public speaker. He just never saw the use in them. It was what he called the others out about when it came right down to it? it was just talk. And anyone could talk. Winning a talking match didn’t ensure winning in the ring. It was something he’d learned eight years ago when he broke into wrestling. “I honestly want to know if it will be hard for me to wrestle tomorrow night with you hanging from my nuts Bryan. I mean hell kid, you talked me up more than I have ever talked myself up. I hope I don’t disappoint you my friend. It’s been a long time since I’ve actively wrestled. Just watch your back come Thursday. I’ve been known to sneak up on people at very inopportune times for them, and usually the outcome weighs in my favor.” He smirked before adverting back to the serious expression. “Now enough warning, and enough banter. I have business to attend to.” Stephens reached a hand out and turned the camera sideways as it faded out. “Do you want me to send this on to Caldera?” Lance Nobile was a monstrosity of a man that worked out in the facility with Stephens. Very few people worked out in the basement facility. One because it was a shit hole and two was because Stephens didn’t like many people around him. If someone ran it per say, then that man was Lance who frequently used the equipment himself. Lance had a barrel-chest and huge quads. He approached 280 most days. His sandy blonde hair was accompanied by a hideous furry blond mustache. He looked like an American Gladiator, but loved when people perceived him to be as dumb as one. Stephens didn’t associate with dumb people and Lance was a fairly sharp character, just not very ambitious. It worked out great for Stephens because Lance could be used as hired muscle and to do jobs such as run the workout facility and film promos from a camcorder for Stephens. He was on the payroll and had been since Stephens had taken the business route. “No!” Stephens said. “Don’t send that God-awful thing anywhere. I had solid points, but they got all mixed up somewhere between the brain and the mouth. I hate doing promos.” “Fair enough. I’ll toss it,” Lance replied. His voice was deep and raspy; he was quite an intimidating figure. Stephens watched Lance has a confused look draped itself across his face. God that mustache was awful. “Something one your mind Lance?” Stephens asked. “Not really.” “Not really. What does that mean” Come on, out with it?” “Well,” Lance spoke hesitantly. “It has just been a long time since you’ve wrestled.” Stephens watched the bigger man for a minute debating on whether or not he would dodge the question or give him a fair answer. Lance didn’t know about all the business Stephens was involved with. That list was limited to Shelly, and he had just soon keep it that way. “I know, but you know me Lance. I’m pretty indifferent. It’s the face time I need, not the dubya’s. At least that’s the case these days. It’s all about getting your image out there so you can use said image to get you the things you desire,” Stephens explained. Lance frowned. “It’s just that’the things you desire aren’t title belts anymore, it’s not even respect, it’s not even simple self satisfaction.” Stephens surveyed the old place. The various pictures of him on the wall reminded him too much of the person he used to be. The kid that kept getting knocked down and kept getting back up. He didn’t miss that kid and the ignorance that accompanied him. Those kind of beatings weren’t worth all the cheers and respect and gold in the world, at least not anymore. Now the world was at his dispense and it was rare that he wrestled hard enough to lose breath. “Can we take those pictures down?” Stephens pointed to some of the pictures on the far wall. Lance looked that way and then back at Stephens. “Well yeah sure, but those things are awesome boss.” “Nevermind, forget I said it,” Stephens said “Is everything okay Ryan?” Lance was one of the few people that still called him Ryan. He was Mr. Stephens to the majority of his colleagues these days. “Fine.” He said it, and as he did a chill seeped into his spine. He wasn’t fine. He had money, but no respect. He had intelligence, but nobody to share it with. He had the ability to succeed at a high level, and yet he could careless. At any time he could cut all ties and vanish. He had enough operations going to head to the Caymans or the Bahamas or wherever and live a quiet life, a new life and surround himself with new interesting people. And yet here he was in the States working a new project for more money and up until a day or so ago he knew exactly why. It was all part of the perfect course of action, but lately that bastard kid with the heart, that tiny flame flickered within. “I’m fine Lance. I guess I just have a lot on my mind right now. I’ll catch you when I get back from San Antonio.” “Good luck Ryan.” Ryan just nodded and departed as Lance looked on at the skeleton of a man he once knew. ******** He had called her the other day, but he got a machine. Her voice was quiet and cute. Women didn’t do him this way and he couldn’t grasp any remotely reasonable reason as to why he was so enamored by her. He didn’t think he had the gall to call her back. Betraying old friends, beating them to near death and claiming everything they had as your own, that was right up his alley, but calling a girl who you were into’well that was as hard now as it was in high school. He sighed. This would all go away tomorrow around noon. That’s just how he operated, he’d been doing it for so many years that he didn’t even dictate it, it’s just what happened. All of it, the ex-wife, the Barratas?, the girl, the stock, the contracts, the whole elaborate plan would leave his mind for about eight hours and he would go through the whole process of mentally preparing for the match, and then participating in it. He wasn’t sure why he even bothered anymore. Tomorrow night would be the first night of many to come. He’d suppress the little flame burning inside of him. He wrestle very intelligently and avoid the majority of the action. If he got into a situation where it was just he and one other competitor for all the marbles’well in that scenario he might just flip the switch. After all a title belt in his first televised appearance in two years would be a rather impressive feat. Something he could boast about for sure. He was curious how it would all unfold. There were others curious as well. Thursday night. A Rebirth.
