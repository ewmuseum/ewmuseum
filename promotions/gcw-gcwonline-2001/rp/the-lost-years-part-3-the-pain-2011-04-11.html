The Lost Years Part 3 - The Pain

11 April 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/11/11 “It Was Never Finalized” The drive from San Jose to Cupertino allowed me to get some things out of my head. Knowing that Jason would fund the purse if asked was bothersome for me. I decided that instead of asking him, I would put in a call to Kelly as soon as I was done with Andrea. She would weigh it a little better than just blindly putting the money out there. As soon as I got to Andrea’s new office, I saw that this wasn’t going to be the same lawyer that I had married. When we first got together, she practiced law out of a little building near Fisherman’s Wharf. Now, she was in a more professional looking setting. I found a visitors parking spot and looked towards the entrance to the building. Andrea had emerged, her red hair tied back into pony tail wearing a pant suit. I never remembered her ever wearing a pant suit the entire time we were dating. She hadn’t seen me yet, so I knew that I could make a break for the freeway without her being the wiser. But, that’s not the kind of person that I am. I open the car door, and she immediately locks eyes on me. It’s been months since she left, months since we last saw each other. I had no clue what she was going to do when she walked up to me. Was she going to slap me or hug me? Was I going to yell at her or be civil? I just didn’t know. As she reached the car, I finally got out. “Dorian, let’s talk in my office.” “Whatever you needed me to fly out here for, we can talk about it right here.” I knew that she didn’t like that idea. The partners at the firm could see out the windows of their “ivory” tower and see this “hoodlum” talking to one of their underlings. I knew that it wasn’t going to help her reputation with the firm, especially seeing as they took her on with spotty experience over the prior few years. “Dorian, please, don’t be like this. I like it here, and I don’t want any problems. It’s about the divorce.” I looked at the ground, shaking my head. “I thought you got everything you wanted out of it.” “It was never finalized. We’re still married.” I did not like hearing those last three words. “What do you mean we’re still married? I sent them back by courier.” I found myself starting to walk towards the doors to her firm. “Where are you going?” “Your office, I have a feeling that I’m going to need to sit down for this.” We walked through the entrance and I saw the rather ornate reception desk. “Sally, this is Dorian. We’re heading up to my office.” Sally couldn’t even be bothered to look up from her painting her nails. I almost wondered how the coffee tasted. Then, after thinking about it for a minute, I decided that I really didn’t want to know. We walked into the buildings elevator. The ring of light from behind the 2 started to illuminate as she barely pressed down on it. “There was a different problem with the paperwork, Dorian. I’ll have to show you when we get to my office. It was something that I missed also.” The doors of the elevator closed and we started to go up. I didn’t look at her; I just stared at my reflection of the polished copper-colored accent on the door. We arrived at the second floor and exited. “So, what do you do here that you already have an office?” I was trying to keep my mind off of why we weren’t divorced yet. “I would think that you would have had to been here longer to have more than just a cubicle.” In the past, she would have chuckled at the fact. I knew that she was a capable lawyer before she gave up that life to become the wife of a wrestler. “Stan owns the firm.” Her answer was as serious as her demeanor the day that she said that she was leaving me to come back to California. “I wonder if he still holds the same contempt for me that he did when I took you as my…” “He hates your guts. He never thought you were good enough for me.” That was one thing that I didn’t miss, Andrea interrupting me as I was saying something. I don’t know how many dinner parties we went to when I was a stuntman that she wouldn’t let me get a whole thought out. Even when I proposed, she would let me say all the words. “I was going to say lawyer.” “I know.” Yeah, sure. We finally got to her office. Before we could enter, I caught out of the corner of my eye a rather sizeable man taking a look at me from behind his ivory rimmed glasses. I stopped in my tracks, turned and walked up to him. Before I could say anything to him, he entered his office and slammed a door emblazoned with the words “M. Stanley Kane, esq” in my face. “Whatever.” I muttered under my breath as I headed back towards Andrea’s office. If I wasn’t such a nice guy, I would have kicked his door in and finally given him that piece of my mind that I should have given him years before. I saw Andrea standing there with a look of contempt on her face. “You know, you can cause a lot of trouble for me here still.” I said to her with a sly grin. “Dorian, the reason it never finalized is because…I don’t want it to end this way. I’ve had time to thi…” “No, you don’t get to do that.” I had to interrupt her. “You made it perfectly clear that you wanted a certain lifestyle, and that I couldn’t give that to you. I gave you everything that you wanted, including those signed papers. Where are they, I’ll take them to the court myself to finalize them.” I was upset. I was about to really get angry when “Tom Sawyer” started playing in my pocket. “Don’t answer that.” Andrea knew as well as I did who was trying to call. I decided to do like I did with the divorce, I gave in to this request, and let it continue until it went to voice mail. “Andrea, I just want to end this crap. I have a child on the way, I could use my portion of the money that we…” Shit, what did I just say? “You’re…going to have a child?” The look of shock on Andrea’s face was astounding. She was pregnant twice while we were married. Both times, she miscarried. After the second one, she decided to have an Essure procedure done. “Yeah, surprise, surprise. Something that you gave up on.” “Who is she?” “Does it really matter? It was after you left me anyway.” I could have told her who it was, but why should I. “I’ll have them filed in the morning. Are you…” Rush went off in my pocket again. The only time that Jason would call back right away was when it was really important. “Can you turn that thing off for a minute?” I reach into my pocket and pull my phone out. “Jay, can I…” That’s when Jason laid the news on me. “Is she OK? I’ll be there as soon as I finish some business here.” “What, something wrong with your baby’s mama?” “You fucking bitch.” It was hard for me to even think about everything that I wanted to say to Andrea right then and there. I just got up and walked out of her office. I think that she knew what she said was out of line, but I didn’t give her a chance to apologize. Then again, I had more pressing matters to attend to. “The Worst Is Upon Us” “So, you doctor or something?” The cab driver’s query was a break in the monotony paced by Latino music. It had been a week since by brother called me. I ended up staying in San Francisco for the better part of a week before I finally decided to hop on a plane and come here. “Nah.” “Funny, most people go to hotel from airport. Not hospital. Maybe you sick?” “Listen, I really don’t want to talk about it. If you don’t mind, just keep driving and let me know when we get here.” Monotony was good. Not having to think, being able to go off in a daze looking at the cars as they passed by. At least I wasn’t on a plane anymore. After a few minutes after the cab driver trying to start up a conversation, he decides to try once more. “So, what bring you to Phoenix?” I seriously didn’t want to talk to this guy. I figured that if I just ignored him, he would just shut up. “I know you not fly here just for hospital.” I was wrong. “Listen, pal, I just got off a flight that was way too long. Just get me to the damn hospital. Peacefully, quietly. Isn’t there a baseball game or something you can listen to?” I was thinking that when I got there, maybe the nurse on duty in the trauma center would have some aspirin for the headache that I had developed. I was now regretting leaving my iPod in my carry-on. I made a note to myself to purchase an iPhone when I got back to Detroit. “You not very nice man.” No shit, Sherlock. I wasn’t very nice at that moment. I was tired, I was cranky, and most importantly, I was annoyed by this jerkoff. “If I tell you why I’m going to the hospital, will you fucking promise to shut the fuck up?” I was hoping that he could detect the hostility in my voice. If he didn’t, hopefully the fact that I used the word fuck twice in the same sentence would have gotten the point across. “You don’t treat me like nasty person. I make you walk rest of way. I don’t need your shit.” “Shut up and drive the fucking car. You’re getting money for this. I’m sure that if you told your boss that you let a fare out without paying that it would come out of your pay.” He went to respond, but realized that I was right. I didn’t hear another word from him the rest of the ride. I was finally at the hospital. It didn’t take me too long to get my things and enter the facility. I walked over to the reception area. “Excuse me, miss.” The receptionist was reading some romance novel when she looked at me through her wire-rimmed glasses. I don’t know why I’m talking about her glasses, other than the fact that they reminded me of Con..…er…..Araceli’s. “Yeah, how can I help you?” There was an accent I hadn’t heard in a long time. It was apparent that the receptionist wasn’t some desert rose from Arizona, but an old fashioned Jersey Jew. “I was wondering if you can tell me where Serena Ryan’s room is” I had calmed considerably from that horrible, horrible cab ride. I was almost, what you could say, pleasant. She looked through the patient registry, and then just shook her head. “There’s no one that’s been admitted under that name, sir.” “I could have swor……” Just then, it hit me like a Bryan Mayhem right. “Try Serena Shaw.” She went back to her maiden name after the divorce. “Oh, there she is. She’s back in the ICU, but there are already visitors back there. But, you look a little like the gentleman that went up to see her a little bit ago.” Wow, someone that could see past the fact that I look like my dad’s half-Samoan bastard son. “Head on back.” “Where would that be?” She just rolled her eyes. “Follow the signs.” She probably thought that I was a moron. I made my way through the corridors of the hospital. I looked at my phone. Quarter after midnight. It didn’t seem that late. Eventually, I found my way to her room. Jason was sitting there. Kelly was standing behind him with her arms around him. As much as Kelly and Serena disliked each other, they had that common thread in Jason. They had been there since they heard the news. I was nearly on a plane from San Francisco a week ago when Jason told me not to come unless something changed. After a week of sitting in a San Francisco hotel room, I couldn’t wait any longer. I wasn’t going to go home. I was going to be there for my brother, for my niece, and for the kid that I still thought of as my nephew. But it was hard for me to stand there and look into the room. Her head was heavily bandaged. Her face was purple from the bruising the crash caused. I later found out that they removed a portion of her skull to relieve pressure on her brain. Kelly turned her head towards the door, that’s when she saw me standing there. She walked over and gave me a hug. “What are you doing here?” “They need all the support that they can get.” There was despair in my voice as I kept my gaze fixed on Serena. It was hard, a once vibrant woman, now struggling to stay alive. “How’s Jay holding up?” Kelly released the hug and looked back at her husband. The sadness in her eyes told the story. She was hurting because she could tell that he was hurting. “It’s been hard for him.” “Well, you should take him home. Get some rest.” “You gonna come with us?” Kelly didn’t like the thought of spending the night in that house, after all, it was the house that Jason and Serena once shared. “I wanna see how Mike is doing, then I might head back there.” “Well, let us take your bags for you.” After they had left, I sat down where Jason sat. I said a prayer, I asked God to bring Serena back to Mike and Samantha. I walked around, trying to make sense of everything. Eventually, I found myself standing at the foot of her bed. “Listen, I know you’re in there somewhere. I know you must be in pain. You must find the way to fight back. I will not stand here and believe that the worst is upon us.” I couldn’t believe that I was talking to her. “I know you too well to know that you‘re not going to give up. I remember what you told me when Dan and Keiko died. You told me that no matter what, you have to keep going. Well, damn it, you have got to keep going. If not for you, then for Mike and Samantha. “You are a strong and powerful person. You always have been.” I looked at her, and I remembered that night in Honolulu where we let our inhibitions go. She was broken up from Jason, and I was taking a break before heading to the Pacific region. It started the same as that night in Buffalo with Araceli. “You remember that night. Yeah, you do. It’s hard to forget me doing a haka after drinking as much rum as I did that night. You looked at me, you took my hand, and you told me that if I ever needed someone to talk to, that I could always talk to you. “I’m sorry that I couldn’t be there for you. I’m here now, though. That’s all that’s important.” By this time, I had her hand in mine. A few minutes later, a nurse told me that I had to leave, that we had gone way beyond visitor hours, but that the hospital was allowing us the extra time due to the severity of her injuries, and the fact that they didn’t know if she was going to come out of it. I once again found myself walking through the halls of the hospital, this time trying to find the waiting area that was specific to the ICU. I couldn’t find it so I ended up heading to the general waiting room. I sat down and picked up a magazine. After a few moments, I felt someone’s arm across my shoulders. I diverted my attention to who’s it was. “Araceli, how’d you know I’d be here?” She just looked at me through her wire-rimmed glasses and shook her head. She brushed some loose hair back behind my ear and comforted me. I was starting to feel relaxed, especially when she slid over to let me lay my head in her lap. Just as I was about to fall asleep, I woke back up. It was just a dream. I looked at my watch, I had been out for quite a few hours. “Well, well, sleeping beauty is awake.” The receptionist this time was a little more pleasant than the one from last night. I went back to the ICU, only, this time I was able to find its waiting area. As I walked in, the only thing I could hear was Joy Behar’s annoying voice. I looked around for a little bit, and then I saw them. I checked my phone, there was a text message from Jason. They were young, he was 18 and she was 17. Her formerly brown locks were dyed black with red streaks, which almost reminded me of how Araceli’s hair looked when we were a part of the NME. I met her once, I had come to Scottsdale with Jason for his graduation party. She was cradling his head as he slept. He had a clipboard in his hand. That wasn’t a good sign. The worst was upon us. “How’s he holding up?” I vaguely remembered her name, something like Tasha or something similar. “It’s been hard on him, sir.” Sir? I was only ten years older than she was. Then again, I was once her boyfriend’s uncle. I walked over to them, and pulled the clipboard from his hand. As I sat it on the table in front of them, I noticed that it was the form to take Serena off life support. The worst really was upon him. “I know what he’s going through. I lost my sister because of a drunk driver.” I looked at him, you could see the struggle on his face, even as he slept. “His sister was named after her. I hope that he realizes that he’s not alone in this.” I pulled off the baseball cap as he started to wake up. “Dorian, when did you get here?” “I arrived from San Francisco early this morning.” I didn’t tell him how early I got there, or that I had already seen the condition that his mother was in. Mike was having a hard enough time as it was. Mike got up, and gave me a hug. Even though he wasn’t legally my nephew anymore, we still considered each other family. “Is he here?” “Jason, no. He should be on his way back to the house from picking up your grandmother. How are you taking this?” There were tears starting to form in his eyes. That was all the answer that I needed. “How hard was it for you, when Keiko…” He was searching for something, I could tell. I did the best I could to comfort him. “There isn’t a day that goes by that I don’t think about that. But remember this, no matter what, you can do something with your mom that I wasn’t able to do with Keiko. You have the chance to say goodbye.” Mike sat back down. You could tell the pain that he was in. He picked up the paperwork and stared at it. He had to make a decision that would affect him the rest of his life. “As long as you remember, a part of her will live on.” Those words, I found out, gave Mike greater comfort than I thought they would have. “What about Samantha?” “It will be your job to teach her how good of a person you remember your mom to be.” Through all of the sadness, I could see that Mike had finally decided what he needed to do. He slowly took the pen from the top of the clipboard and looked at the paperwork one more time. As he was about to put the pen to paper, Jason stopped him. “Not yet.” Mike got back up, and looked at the man that was more of a father to him than anyone his entire life. He dropped the clipboard to the ground, hugged Jason and finally let all of the emotions go. I left them to make a hard decision. I caught a cab and took it to the house. An hour after I had settled in, Jason called me. They took Serena off the ventilator. She was gone. I sat there, alone in that house. I started thinking. I called up Araceli. As soon as I heard her voice, it hit me. I didn’t want to lose her.
