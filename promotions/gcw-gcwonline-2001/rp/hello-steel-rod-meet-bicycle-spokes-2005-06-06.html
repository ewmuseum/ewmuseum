Hello, steel rod. Meet bicycle spokes.

6 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/6/05 I was drunk on bad vodka, not to imply that any of it's particularly good, when Trey finally showed up in Dallas. I'd had him meet me there in a haze of paranoia--my own fault for being loaded enough at Worldwide to start blabbering about Lexicon. It'd been hours since my cell phone had been useful, too busy with the frantic calls of Anarch's upper management trying to worry me about running the company. I was two shots away from changing the name to Shitenstein & Son before I turned it off, which apparently had left Trey to wander around for hours checking motels until he found my car, caught in a bad drunk of his own. So neither of us was ready for me waving the bottle in a fit of rage. When things got figured out, faces and names remembered, he took a fall into the nearest chair and grabbed the bottle from my hand. 'Horrible shit,' he said. Gulp. 'I know. Gotta go clean soon,' he said. 'Bullshit,' I truthed. Gulp. 'No, serious. Some shit's gone on this week that got me thinkin', y'know, about--uh--my life and shit.' 'Getting called a garbage wrestler sent you into the Angst?' 'Not just that, man, not just that. Went to see my mom.' 'And...?' He looked down and kept his eyes closed, serious like only monks and the otherwise devout get, before taking a heavy shot. 'Never really knew my dad.' 'You grew up with him.' 'He kept his secrets. Used to run drugs, was a Panther, crazy shit. Didn't even make sense to me, like, I'm this guy's seed? Nah, nah, can't be right. My dad was a respected lawyer, I thought, and when this shit came down, man, I didn't even know how I could trust him anymore--lyin' to me my whole life. But fuck, after awhile, it made sense. He wanted to be a good role model for us, y'know, and didn't want to let us think that all that shit he'd done was straight. Changed his whole life up, so I figure, I might as well follow him.' 'So it wasn't Stevens?' 'Man, that's some bullshit. Ain't a garbage wrestler.' 'Haha. You've got your priorities straight.' 'Yeah, yeah. So, few more nights of partyin', then I'm gonna clean up for awhile. Just long enough to check it out and see what the deal is.' 'That has nothing to do with Stevens, Trey. The fuck'd you even acknowledge him for when I asked?' 'Because it's a way of life, man. I got Knight next week--think about how everyone's gonna be if I just stomp his ass like I was on some Benoit shit. I'm gonna be one of those good guys, all around.' 'So, no more death-machining?' 'Kinda crazy only being able to fuck someone up when I'm hypnotized.' 'It's hoodoo. You're a goddamned zombie.' 'Well, aight, but you know what I'm sayin'. I should be able to do that shit anytime, so I gotta try.' 'Christ, that was the point in the first place--trying didn't get you anywhere.' 'Long time ago an' before I'd even done half the shit I know I can do now. Little confidence'll go a long ass way.' 'You've been cocky since the water broke. What the fuck are you talking about?' 'Haha, yeah, yeah. Still, somethin' about knowin' that I can stomp the shit out of a veteran like Lexicon, all smooth as hell, gets me feelin' a lot better about the whole thing. What do you think? As the head of fuckin' Anarch, hah. That was crazy shit.' 'I think you've lost your goddamned mind, but whatever. Money's money.' 'Shit, you don't think it'll work?' 'Empirical evidence thinks it's a horrible idea. I'm sure you'll have fun, though, so run with it.' 'Cool, cool. Best I'm gettin' outta you, man. 'Nough about alla my shit, how's it feel bein' the king?' 'What? I haven't done a damned thing yet. I need a comprehensive business plan when I talk to those bastards, not some mumbling bullshit. That's all too fucking serious for me, though. Isn't there anything to talk about than work?' 'Shit, where's Tulz?' 'Taking a shower.' 'Ah, yeah. What's up with her?' 'She's reading my mind right now. So, I don't know. It's hard to figure out exactly what she's trying to get from me.' 'Think she's playin' you?' 'Who cares? She's along for the ride, no matter why.' I was feeling like a burnt-out shell, crusted and cracking, long before then. I was too rich to do any of the nothings I could do, so I sat around and stayed perpetually wasted for some time. My shoulder was swollen and still bruised, a lingering pain that became a good excuse to not be able to go to sleep. I kept cocking my jaw to the side and it had worn a chancre from being stretched against my upper teeth constantly, and I don't even know why. As I said, hideously lost. 'Yo, Jordan?' 'What?' 'Got new ink today.' 'A tattoo?' 'Shyeah.' I stopped, looking for any signs that a change had occurred. 'I thought you didn't like tattoos.' 'Had enough from Casey, figured I might as well wear one a' my own.' 'Enough bullshit, what is it?' He rolled his left pants leg up a foot, revealing a sun outlined in white ink. The fresh wound was being treated by his body as such and was swollen, red, puffy, slathered in some solution. 'Ain't finished yet, just got the outline now. Shit glows under blacklight, man. It's sick.' 'That looks a lot like...' 'Your Alice in Chains sun. I figure, we're as good as brothers right now. You're about the best family I got.' 'Thought you were around the real one last week.' 'That's my point. Calvin wanted to kick my ass and my mom just treated me alright to make Andrea happy. And Andrea just, man, I don't know.' 'Pities you?' 'Nah, she was real glad to see me, just, fuck, distant. Like I was an old friend she'd lost a long time ago, wanted to see if she could get what she remembered back.' 'What about your kid?' 'Never saw Alicia.' 'So you don't know?' 'Mom was shady about answerin'. So yeah, no clue.' And then, as if to tell me that I hadn't done enough yet that day, came forth a sound like thunder from outside. It reverberated its way through the room from front to back, freezing Trey and I long enough for our brains to say 'loud noise--just chill.' Tulz burst out of the bathroom, wrapped in a towel and doe-eyed, so the three of us got to sit in silence until someone got a bright idea. 'What the fuck was that?' Trey looked back to me with the bottle just pulled down from his mouth: 'Let's check it out.' 'No. It's bad. We ignore it until morning.' 'C'mon, man. Ask Tulz, y'know she wants to see what it was too.' 'God damnit, don't you get it? We're pawns, Trey, and there are rules that we move by. Going outside now would be the move he wants us to make, not the move we need to make.' 'He?' 'Jayce.' Dead stop. The record has come to an end. The game is over. Last call. Trey stares out at me with dead eyes, caught in some revelation that I'd been keen to beforehand. 'That fool can't be God.' 'He's not; bastard isn't bound by the same pacts you expect out of a creator. That's what makes it bad. We go out there, we walk into a horrible situation full of the bizarre, just to entertain him. We stay, bore him to death--or at least until he gives up on us and goes on to bother some nascent lives.' 'Never seen you back down before. What's up with that?' 'I'm not backing down. We can't win. This guy controls us.' 'You don't even know that he did that shit, man.' 'Who the fuck else did? You wanna go out and walk into the completely implausible, go ahead. Some of us are happy getting fucked up and wiling away the hours glued to the TV.' I looked over to Tulz and she was either invisible or gone; left me staring at wallpaper either way. The rest of the scene was obvious then, thanks to the city noise coming in through the open door. Christ, I'd moved too slowly. I shot out of the chair with a clear head regarding the con that'd just been pulled on us. Trey was whining from behind me, out of his seat when I was already out of the door and staring in awe. I had told him it was a bad idea.
