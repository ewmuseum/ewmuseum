Date: 08-11-05 Thump. RoadKill is a bitch but what can ya do when the thing runs right out in front of you? So much for evolving to live in harmony with your habitat. Seemed wild rabbits hadn’t quite learned to cross highways yet but you’d think the huge imposing saloon ploughing their way at 50mph would give them a nudge in the right direction. Of course that direction turned out to be the highway itself, which brings us back to? Thump. Christ that was the second one in as many minutes. They felt like lop sided speed bumps. She dared not look, the sound alone was traumatic enough without seeing the fuzzy lil guy made into road pizza with a innards and entrails special sauce topping. She snuggled down inside her sleeping bag in the back seat of the car. It felt like her bed but when you’re barely five years old, that’s not a hard feet for the backseat of a car to perform. The sun glinted through the window but her wild raven curls played the part as something of a screen between her and the bright rays. Her sleeping bag was made of cow hide, it looked hand made, like something an Eskimo child would sleep in she thought. She’d learned about Eskimo people on her first day at school. She’d been put into the class for gifted children after her first week. Turns out smarts ran in the family. She’d heard her Father talking about her cousin once. She was put in a higher class too. They’d never met though. Ashura her name was. “Daddy” Will there be children like me there?” Her father smiled concentrating on the road coming up to an intersection in the distance. “Sure there’ll be children there, your age. But children just like you?” He reached a hand and ruffled her hair as she kneeled between the two front seats. “There’s no one like my lil Marianne babygirl.” He smiled at her through the rear view mirror. One of those smiles that made you smile back, those smiles that warmed you down to your toes like warm cocoa on a Christmas night. She stared at her father driving for a while. His arms seemed ridiculously muscled, well to a five year old girl anyways. His skin was the warm brown of burnt sienna and his hair was cut short on the back and the sides in a neat fade. His wife beater was white, lily white. For some reason Marianne found that comforting but she didn’t know why. She turned to her mother. “Momma, what if they don’t remember me?” Her mother turned to her and smiled a loving smile with full pouty soft lips that kissed Marianne before bed every night. Her hair was jet black and wavy almost as much as Marianne’s. Her eyes were big and slightly almond shaped and a chocolate brown that you could pour out and drink with marshmallows. Her native American looks were in contrast and compliment with Marianne’s father’s African American features and together they made one of the most beautiful babies in the entire hospital that night, or so the matron of the ward had said. Marianne’s mother past Marianne a Popsicle from the cooler box by her feet. “Of course they’ll remember you, and then they’ll fall in love with you all over again sweety. I’m sorry we don’t get to see my parents much but I promise once your settled in school we’ll go more often.” Marianne looked quizzical, “Billy at school says grandpa and gramma live in tents. Is that true?” Her mother smiled. “No that’s not true hunny, Billy doesn’t know what he’s talking about. You can tell him all about it when we get back.” She turned to Marianne’s father. “Craig, wanna Popsicle babe” You’re sweating.” She started rooting around in the cooler box again as Craig stroked her thigh through her denims. “I’m good Maria, but I know what I’d rather su?” Maria coughed and nodded her head in Marianne’s direction raising her eyebrows at him. He started to laugh and rubbed her leg again as she eased back into the seat. He flicked on the radio as Marianne curled back up in her seat. “We’ll be there in...” He glanced at his watch, “about two more hou”.” WHAM!! He didn’t get to finish his sentence. A truck had run the red light at the intersection and slammed into the car. They went spinning then flipped four times before the car landed on its roof in the dirt of the roadside. Then there was silence. Marianne opened her eyes, still silence. Her head hurt, her legs hurt, she hurt all over and she was still tucked in her cow hide sleeping bag. Wetness ran down her forehead and she glanced at her parents. Silence. She called out their names and she couldn’t hear herself speak. She focused her eyes and saw her mother looking back at her in shock. She went to reach out again when she realised her mothers body was at a right angle to her head. She glanced over at her Father to call him, but there was a staring column through his chest. That’s when she screamed, and screamed, and didn’t hear a single word of it before everything went black. She woke up in a strange place with strange lights and smells. She couldn’t move, their were wires in her arms and tubes up her nose. She closed her eyes trying to shut it out like it was a bad dream but in her head she saw her parents, mutilated and crushed in the wreckage of the car. She didn’t open her eyes though, she couldn’t, something was keeping her there, forcing her to stare at the images and for all she was worth, she couldn’t scream. Someone came into the room, no two people, a man and a woman it sounded. “Something isn’t right.” “Maybe she’s in shock. Children handle things in different ways.” “I know but, to witness something like that and be completely calm. She was talking and everything, not as if nothing had happened but as if she as ok, like she could cope. She’s five years old for goodness sake.” The voices died down and Marianne fell into a sleep again. She wasn’t sure how many days or weeks had past but there was a wheel chair by her bed and the tubes were gone. Her mind was choosing to let her remember what it felt she needed to and none of it was entirely bad. She felt numb inside, but surprisingly calm too. The doctors were talking outside again. They were talking with that lady who said she was a different kind of doctor, the kind that helped people feel better inside when they were angry or sad. Marianne pressed her ear against the door and listened. “Are her Uncle and Aunt ready to come get her next month?” “Yes they said it’s fine. They seemed quite enthusiastic, turns out they lost a child not so long ago and they haven’t seen Marianne since she was a baby but they want to help.” “That’s great but they need to know that Marianne has suffered a great shock. Her mind is still developing, is still so complex. She is in a permanent state of crisis and will need counselling for the foreseeable future.” “What do you mean state of crisis.” “Marianne’s reaction to her car accident isn’t abnormal. She’s employed the only coping mechanism she knows how and that’s to be accepting of everything that’s happened so long as she doesn’t analyse it too much. It’s similar to long term shock. What’s important and you must let the Uncle and Aunt know is that Marianne probably has a trigger developed in her mind and waiting for setting off now.” “A trigger?” “Don’t they teach you people anything at medical school” Yes a trigger. She will be relatively fine as long as she doesn’t suffer another emotional and mental trauma in the near future.” “And if she does?” “Well, if she does, I cant say exactly what will happen. Again different people cope in different ways but with the trigger tripped, none of them will be pretty for very long. We need to keep an eye on her.” They started moving and Marianne got back into her bed as quick as she could. Marianne had been at her Aunt and Uncle’s house for over a month now and was settling in just fine. She was happy enough for a little girl who’d lost both her parents horrifically. She was still attending her same school so the continuity helped. It was five months until her 6th birthday and her Aunt and Uncle had promised she could have a party. She wasn’t sure she wanted one as she kicked a ball around in her room gently. It was getting late. Her Aunty was working late and her Uncle was watching TV recovering from a cold. “Bed Time hun!” He yelled up the stairs as he eased his aching frame up them to say goodnight. Marianne got into bed in her moo cow pj’s and braided her hair into two pig tails just like her Momma had shown her to do. Her Uncle peaked his head around the door knocking. “You in bed darlin?” Marianne nodded, “uh huh.” He pushed the door and came in smiling. “You still feeling ill Uncle?” He nodded this time, “Ya Uncle ain’t as young as he used to be sweetheart. Don’t ever get as old as me, it ain’t purdy place.” Marianne giggled. “Where’s Aunty Shona?” She asked wide eyed. “Oh she’s working late, she’s left us to fed for ourselves tonight, so it’s my job to tuck you in. That cool with you?” “Ok,” She scooted down under the covers. “Would you like a story?” Her uncle asked and she nodded again, “Yes please.” He plucked a book off the shelf. Gulliver’s Travels. “Scoot over, give your old Uncle a seat.” He nudged Marianne playfully and she giggled, scooting over to the wall so he could perch on the end of the bed. He put his feet up and snuggled down beside her opening the book and beginning. Marianne listened intently to the first four chapters before her eyelids got heavy and he noticed proceeding to read one more chapter. Marianne’s eyes fluttered shut and open again before shutting. He closed the book and put it aside smiling at her. “Tired out huh?” It was more a statement than a question. “I’m tired out too sweetpea. Think I’ll bunk up here till ya Aunty Shona gets back.” He shifted so he was under the covers with her, his one arm above her head and the other resting on her stomach stroking comforting circles. Marianne’s eyes flew open when his hand moved further south. “Shh?” he whispered in her ear realising she was awake. “It’s just me, don’t be afraid.” His hand searched for the waistband of her pyjama bottoms and Marianne shook her head. “Please don’t do that Uncle, I don’t like it.” His eyes seemed to change. “Well I like it, don’t you like to make ya Uncle happy?” Marianne nodded. “Well then, why don’t you give your Uncle a kiss.” Marianne quickly pecked her Uncle on the cheek hoping this would satisfy him and he would leave but he looked at her smiling with mock disappointment. “No, no, hunny, the way ya Momma kisses ya Papa. Kiss me that way.” Marianne shook her head now visibly uncomfortable. “Oh go on, make Uncle happy. We love each other, it’s how people show they love each other. You’re my special lil girl remember. No one is as special as you Marianne.” He tugged at her pyjama bottoms this time with a subtle force and Marianne scrambled to get away but his grip was fast and strong. He grabbed her flailing limbs and dragged her back. His face was now a mask of anger and confusion. “I have been very sick Marianne! Very fucking sick and you will make me feel better damnit! I love you! Don’t you get it” I’m going to show you how much I damn well love you!” A fist came down into her stomach and Marianne could barely make the sound of pain that came from her lips. Her eyes teared up instantly as the pain welled in her so much so that she couldn’t fight her Uncle off so winded. “Please don’t, please?” His hand clamped over her mouth. It was so big she couldn’t speak around it at all and her eyes widened when she heard her underwear tearing and saw her Uncle fumbling with one hand to undo his own pants. The look in his eyes was something in human, something base as he forced her short legs apart. He was almost more than twice her size, she wasn’t a tall five year old. She was trying to shake her head and scream no but it was an act of futility as he looked down into her tear streaming eyes. “Oh Marianne, don’t cry, this is what people do when they love each other.” He leaned down close to her and whispered low, “And if you tell ANYONE, I will kill you!” Those were the last words Marianne heard before the searing pain ripped through her body. “So I waited and, O my brothers, I got a lot better munching away at eggiwegs, and lomticks of toast and lovely steakiwegs and then, one day, they said I was going to have a very special visitor.” Alex-A clockwork Orange
