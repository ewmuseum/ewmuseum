Date: n/a (DG RP #1) .:: ::: ::. I’ve been thinking about what you said. No, really thinking about it, not just giving it the arbitrary mental once-over when I’m bored. I know that seems? well, it seems incomprehensible, given that I pretty much treat everything you say like the utterances of someone with an acute case of tard rage. We have a few things in common, you and me. First among them being that we know exactly where we stand’that among the wrestling world, there are those of us who constitute the aristocracy. The lynchpins. I don’t know your story, and in fact, your story doesn’t much matter in the long run. I don’t know how you got here, but all I know is that you are now a part of my reality. It was like that one fateful moment: I looked into your eyes, you into mine, and it was history. I have never known with more certainty, and so quickly, that I was looking into the eyes of a man who I absolutely detested. Maybe it was your bulbous, Neanderthal looking head. The rest of us are caught somewhere in between crippling nausea and the fight-or-flight response. It’s a triad that offers appealing options: to throw up, to tear one’s hearing appendages wholly off one’s head, or to try to pound your skull in with the butt-end of an axe. Choices, choices. If it weren’t for all the posturing, I might have some sort of respect for you. Even bigger than Rampage. Bigger than Warpath. Big. The tale of the tape is deceptive. You’re bigger. Stronger. Maybe even more experienced. But I’m faster. Deadlier. I suppose that all of you thought that by not making a decision, you were making the best decision. At times, the most negative consequences can come not from what you do, but from what you don’t do. It’s a level-playing field for all of you, as far as we’re concerned. Ascension member, unaffiliated, it doesn’t matter, all of those fall under the heading of enemy. Maybe you think that’s stupid policy’but how else would you have it? There are no degrees of “enemyness” here. To let up on anyone who is against us would be foolish. In any case, where do you get off taking the moral high ground? It’s easy enough to say, when all the smoke has cleared from the battlefield, “I am better off, because I chose not to take a side.” True enough, one side loses, one side wins, and to be on the losing side is the least desirable thing of all in a war. So instead, you hedge your bets. You say “one side must lose, so I will take no side.” That, at its core, is simple cowardice. You are afraid of what may happen to you personally, so you sneak around in the shadows, stealing steps up the ladder when no one is looking. But cowards don’t get very far’not far enough to take the boldest steps of all, not far enough to wear the crown of the kingdom. In the end, is it really better to say that you chose nothing? At the very least, the fallen side has the honor of saying that they stood for what they believed in. All the lot of you will have to say is that you stood for yourselves’your insignificant selves who, when they are nothing but vague memories, will mean positively nothing. Don’t delude yourselves into thinking that I don’t take you seriously. I take you seriously for the sole reason that I must leave no stone unturned in this quest of mine. I must take no enemy for granted. I must take each battle as seriously as if the entire war depended on it. There won’t be any hiding of your motives at Dangerous Games. We all know who you are, skulking around back there, thinking that the circumstances here will offer you the best possible chance to make your move. Well, everyone’s thinking that. That’s where the problem comes in for you individualists’Dangerous Games isn’t stacked to favor any one person over another, save the person who comes in wanting it the most. What does it mean to you? It’s all personal gain for all of us, right? A singles event, in which one person walks out as the single most dangerous competitor in GCW today. Something that configures the landscape of GCW for months to come. But that’s not all it is for me. Yes, it’s a personal dream to walk away from something like this with a victory in my hands. That’s not something I would surrender’but perhaps what is more important is that there are no less than nine pairs of eyes watching me. Rooting for me, yes, but that’s the inconsequential part. Watching me’in the hopes that I will prove my worth. In the hopes that I will not fail them as a commander. It’s more than a chance for me. And no matter what skills any of you may have, that motivation is something that you can’t take away. If I win, I walk away with not just a chance, but the respect of the people who are the most important to me. People who believe in me nonetheless, but people who want to see me succeed in order to prove my worth. Not the people who, lurking in the background of PTC, went over to spread dissent about how I’d never walk home with a Global Title win over the Uber Judge. Not the people who think I’m nothing but a fraud, playing a gimmick on TV for fun. The people who matter. My friends, no, more than friends, my family. For them, I will spill what blood needs to be spilled. I will be their general, yet I will not expect them to put their own selves on the line to protect me’I will go forward, just as they do, to the vanguard of the battle. I will help them rout their enemies, and if it comes down to two of us, I will show them why I am worthy to be called a leader. If you want to try to stop me, be my guest. But for them, I will win Dangerous Games. I will walk away with the world in the palm of my hand. .:: ::: ::. Quotes are taken from, of course, Sun Tzu’s “The Art of War.”
