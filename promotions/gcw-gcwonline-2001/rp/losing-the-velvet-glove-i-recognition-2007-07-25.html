losing the velvet glove i: recognition

25 July 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/25 People crave one thing more than anything else in this world. Instant gratification. Why else do tabloids exist, if not for the instant gratification of thousands of housewives in the grocery aisles, wanting to read the latest dirt on Lindsay Lohan, or Britney Spears, or the latest celebrity tramp-of-the-moment? Perhaps no one is more aware of this than GCW's walking Tabloid, a man who prides himself on being able to know the story when no one else does. But has he misjudged? Indeed, in the world of Global Championship Wrestling, seen by millions of people worldwide every week, KNOWING a story is not enough. One must BECOME the story in order to succeed. "This," Tabloid mused, looking over the Global Championship Wrestling web page, a dubious look on his face, "cannot be right." The main story featuring the heated lawsuit between Banned and Exiled and Steven Caldera was not Tabloid's objective, though it was a potential source of useful information. No, a rather throwaway poll on a sidebar had caught the eye of the Global Enquirer. "Terror. Jackson. Atkin. Cross. O'Shea. GCW's newest generation of talent." The five men in a poll asking GCW fans who the fastest rising star was. Tabloid's reason for being perturbed by this was simple; he wasn't on the list. Perhaps that was fair. After all, he'd only had two matches, one victory, and one...well, no one seemed quite sure if he'd won or lost. Least of all Tabloid, which was an odd sensation to say the least. Much information had been gathered by simply not opening his mouth. Tabloid was, in many ways, a model employee to date. He showed up, competed in his match, left the arena, and accepted his paycheck without a fuss. While gathering information, this was all well and good...but Tabloid had come to the unfortunate realization that he could not gather information much longer. "It is the squeakiest wheels in these parts," he told himself, deciding to click on Mr. Jackson's name, "who get the grease." It was only sporting, after all. Banned and Exiled were more or less gigantic thorns in the side of Steven Caldera. It seemed to be tantamount to career suicide, to cross the man who signed your paychecks. If nothing else, though, Markus Stone and Chris Storm were well known names, who mattered in the scheme of things. Movers and shakers in the world of GCW. It would take time to reach their level. Effort. Hard fought contests. OR, you could do what they did, which was, well, make an ass out of everyone. "Got to hand it to them, they're idiots," Tabloid said, shaking his masked visage sadly, "but they're brilliant idiots. Not afraid of what Caldera can do to them...and thus, they prosper." Tabloid was too smart, or, alternately, too stupid to cross Steven Caldera. That, though, left him with few options for instant notoriety. No crossing Steven Caldera meant no crossing LeStatt Knight, a GCW stalwart and probably the longest tenured man on the roster. No, Tabloid would have to look away from the Establishment. Or, more accurately, look FROM the Establishment. "Alright, Tabloid, think," the masked man mused in a melancholy manner, "Jordan White will be challenging for the biggest prize in GCW. Who does the Establishment have it most in for right no--oh, DUH." It was so blatantly and utterly obvious that he had completely neglected it as a possibility. If Steven Caldera was the top of Global's food chain, than it stood to reason than the World Champion was #2. One look at Lia Ambrosi told Tabloid several things. First and foremost, she looked like a woman who had skeletons the size of tyrannosaurs in her closet. Secondly, and equally importantly almost, she looked like someone who would try to decapitate him if he pried too deeply into those secrets. Interesting. Most interesting. "Not like I'm going to be across the ring from her any time soon," Tabloid reasoned. After all, he was a curtain-jerker, someone who had just showed up on the scene, with about six months of wrestling experience to his name. But Tabloid's aim was not to defeat her. Nor would it be to defeat ANY of GCW's top names, for that matter. The Twizzys, the Vivica Valentines, the Shamans of the world were all safe from any attempts by the Global Enquirer at physical violence. Rather, it was the Mullets of the world that Tabloid's physical aggression was targeted at. The Brad Jacksons. The Jay Terrors. Tabloid was nothing if not a realistic man. His most immediate in ring goal was the GCW Television Championship. Obtaining such a difficult to defend title and having any sort of a run with it would prove that he was here to stay, that he wasn't just a flash in the pan who wore funny clothes. The question then would be...why? Why was Tabloid so insistant on finding a top-ranking target? He'll answer that question. Just be patient. "Ability in the ring only goes so far to get you noticed," the masked man affirmed to himself. "What happens outside is equally important. The battle against opponents never ends, in OR out of the ring..." If Tabloid had a mantra, that would be it. Always being on one's guard. Always aware. No matter WHAT. Soon enough, he would see how ready the rest of GCW was for such an attitude.
