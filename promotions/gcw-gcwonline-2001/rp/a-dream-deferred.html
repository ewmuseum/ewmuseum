A Dream Deferred

Mobile, AL 7:02 am Early morning sunlight cast uniform glowing squares on the breakfast table, but a sleepy and slightly red-eyed Dawn padded into the kitchen of their rented house without really paying attention to the soldiers-in-line symmetry of it all. It was a purely human and not spiritual motivation that brought her here near the crack of dawn’a growling stomach. Half-seeing, she pulled open the first cabinet she could get her hands on, intending to stare at the stacked boxes of cereal until something got her attention. Her eyes flicked past the small boxes of Frosted Mini-Wheats, past the yogurt-of-some-flavor Cheerios, well past the wholesome-looking granola bars, her gaze finally sticking on the toaster pastries, the same kind she’d eaten since she was a kid. It wasn’t something she could always indulge in, but today, it sounded about like it would hit the spot. She stood on the balls of her feet, reaching for the box. But rather suddenly, an ear-piercing noise came from the garage’the howl of a bike’s motor. Virgil had already left for the week to do some business while he didn’t have to wrestle, as always leaving an ambiguous clue as to the dealings of his equally ambiguous self. That left only one person who could be preparing to leave: Lia, on her recently-finished custom sportbike. A hand touched to her lips, and the brief terror of what had happened the last time Lia cut out on somebody’s bike shot through Dawn at bullet-blast pace. She thought of checking the table for a note, like the one Lia had left before, but there was no time’if she went now, she could catch her before the garage door got all the way up. Before she could really think it through too deeply, she found her feet carrying her at a panicked breakneck through the hallway. “Lia!” She burst through the door with the name on her lips, shouting loudly enough to get the vampire’s attention over the growling pretty purr. Lia’s head snapped to the side, her hands arrested in the motion of sliding her matching purple helmet on. She sat the helmet on the seat behind her and chokcd the engine, both with deliberate, elegant motions that showed no emotion, reminding Dawn painfully of how tense and impersonal things had been between the two of them lately. Lia’s eyes focused in on Dawn’s, and she found her arms folding over her chest protectively, the knit of the baggy sweater she was wearing looping close around her, and increasing that feeling of attempting to fall into herself so she didn’t have to endure Lia’s censure. “Uh” you hadn’t said anything about leaving. Everything’s alright, isn’t it?” Lia turned, opened the small suitcase that slipped perfectly into the bike’s back basket, and pulled out a long-stemmed white rose. She held it like a scepter. “I received a message a day or so ago. Someone Virgil has come into contact with has something to tell me, and I’m driving to South Carolina to meet him.” “Alone”!” Dawn took a tentative step forward, wanting to scream all the reasons that she didn’t need to go, but she clammed up, save for that one word of protestation. Slowly, Lia shook her head. “No, Virgil has offered his plantation estate as a meeting place, though I presume that Virgil will be absent during the meeting” a formality, to ensure his guest that he has the confidence and privacy he requires.” “I” don’t much like that idea. Not after what happened during the fall with that other creep.” Lia found a way to look down her nose at dawn, nonplussed. Dawn was beginning to wonder if there was any other way that Lia knew how to look these days. “We have our precautions, Dawn. I can assure you that Virgil will ensure my safety.” Dawn nodded, glancing away from her lover with an attempt at hiding the crestfallen look that had come over her features. “I don’t feel too comfortable with this is all. I know you have to go because you want to find your brother, but I” just wanted you to know.” Lia nodded in that half-detached way she had taken as her own these past couple of months. “Understood” I’ll see you at WorldWide, okay?” Dawn nodded, and Lia carefully packed the rose away again, and snapped her visored helmet on. The roar of the bike was her only answer to Dawn’s obvious displeasure. This time, there was no respite, no reason that dawn could come up with to call Lia back, no matter how hard she scrabbled. It was over, just like that. All she could do was muster a weak-feeling wave with one hand, all those desperate thoughts getting crushed as the seconds past, lost like so many other things that had not been said between them these past long weeks. .:: ::: ::. I-59 Eastbound 2:30 pm Lia’s mind was practically deadened by the silence of the road. There was the droning of the motor, the rush of wind past her, but those had since ceased to be pleasant elements of the driving experience, and had turned to nothingness, to void. Now, there were still hundreds of miles northward to go, and a pounding sense of urgency that told her she needed to fly, when she couldn’t. Her mind seemed to tangle itself in knots, circular things that led from one place, then back around to the beginning. The beginning, of course, having been only a couple of days ago, a few hours after Virgil had left for South Carolina, and she’d found a package lying on the dressing table in their room with her name scrawled on it in his overly florid script. Lia’something for you inside. I believe you’ll find it quite interesting. And indeed, inside the little box, there had been the white rose, the rose that she was still carrying with her, and a note written in a strange boxy handwriting that was formal, but not really European seeming. Perhaps one of Virgil's Asian contacts, but why on earth would he have had cause to come into contact with a being like Laszlo without some sort of murder attempt? It didn't have to make sense, she supposed. And there was no point in sitting here playing guessing games about what she would or wouldn't see when she got to South Carolina. There was just the note. Milady Ionescu, I was made aware by a companion of yours that you are seeking certain information, an area where I believe I can be of assistance. Your companion offered to convey this information personally, but I found it best to decline this kind proposal in favor of delivering you such information via my own lips. It is not that I do not trust Senor Ambrosi, only that this is something very sensitive that I wish to tell you in privacy, and of my own accord. Graciously, he has agreed to provide his estate grounds as a neutral location for the conveyance of my information to you. Do me the honor, please, of meeting me there at your earliest convenience this week. Your faithful servant, S. The moment she'd read it, she'd put in a long-distance call to Cyrus, up in Toronto. Suspicion was the main reason. 'Cyrus, I'm not entirely certain I should trust this,' she'd said, 'The formality of tone seems archaic and respectful enough, but you know that quite often, so-called polite obeisance among our kind is the prelude to a knife in the back.' There was a derisive snort from the other end of the phone. 'Yes, I understand that concept... the Kine brought you no help at all?' Lia rolled her eyes on the other end of the phone. 'He seemed too frightened to do anything other than tell me the bare minimum required to keep his little whelp of a sister alive and untouched by the Society of Leopold. He really told me nothing, other than that a Vasek Kral was present at a Society meeting as an emissary for someone.' 'Did he say who?' 'No,' she said starkly, 'And it would be a lot easier if he had. As it is, all he did was give me some sort of kenning, I'm sure. The Rejected, I believe it was, and that could be just about anyone. Anything.' Cyrus made a disgusted noise. 'Yes... that is most unfortunate. I apologize for his wasting your time... in the meantime, I will do a bit of research to see if that kenning holds any water.' 'And the other?' Cyrus paused for a moment. 'Do you feel like it will be safe?' Lia chewed on that a moment herself. 'I feel fairly neutral about the idea. But the meeting is at Virgil's estate in South Carolina, so I don't think that I really have any reason to be wary.' 'Then I would say to go. It can't hurt anything, since you have such a limited amount of information right now.' 'Good point.' She gripped the handlebars a little tighter, and narrowed her eyes in concentration. As far as she was concerned, the South Carolina border couldn't get there fast enough. .:: ::: ::. Blackthorn Estate Rural South Carolina 6:40pm Blackthorn Estate was positively stunning, but Lia could have said that about any of Virgil's properties, scattered throughout the world. This one had something special about it, though, and she could see why he would take pains to maintain it. The property was gated, and a long avenue of trees led up to the front drive, which was a cobble of dark stones. Glass-covered gas lanterns burned on either side of the sweeping front staircase, leading up to a house that itself was tiled with the same dark shale-stone as the drive. Overhanging the front walk on one side was a weeping willow, immaculately kept and swaying slightly in the still-not-yet humid air. She stood up, the muscles in her legs slightly cramped from sitting in the same position for so long. Road dust covered her leather miniskirt, and she dusted the biggest patches of it off. She was not so eager to meet this contact to forget that appearances always counted among her kind, and among most kinds, really. She hung her helmet off the back of her bike and slowly mounted the steps, taking a moment to survey the lavish furnishings on a sun-porch off to the side. Probably a recent development, considering the vast amount of his life, Virgil wouldn't have been able to partake in sunning without becoming a flambe. Her fingers had barely rapped the knocker thrice when the heavy polished wood of the door swung backwards. Instead of an unfamiliar face, there was the master of the Estate. Almost unwittingly, her heart leapt into her throat. No matter how hard she'd tried to push him away since Rampage, there was no denying the effect that he had on her. He bowed to her, his usual custom, his handsome form well-attired in a black suit that was cut to accent the width of his shoulders and chest. She allowed him to take the hand that wasn't clutching the rose, and he pressed it softly to his lips. She closed her eyes for a moment. But when she opened them, that brief girlish gust of excitement spent itself, and she remembered business. 'Virgil... I thought you weren't going to be here this evening...' Disappointment flickered through her eyes, despite the fact that he could see her trying to suppress it. And that furthered his drive to do what he was about to do... which was piss her off. 'I am afraid that the note was only a formality, cara mia.' Delicate lines etched across her forehead. 'What do you mean?' He stepped aside, and she slipped through the available space as he shut the door behind the two of them. Her eyes were consumed with details for a moment, the sweeping marble staircase that spiraled up to the second floor, and the ornately carved banisters that rimmed the balcony. But when her eyes came back down to the main level of the house, they were back on him, drilling in and still demanding an answer. He bowed his head. 'No such contact exists. I wrote the note.' Her eyes instantly sparked with anger, as he had expected they would. 'What the hell do you think you're doing?' 'You needed time away from the things that are stressing you. This is your week, your week to stay here and have the run of things until WorldWide. I had to make sure you would come.' Her teeth clenched, eyes still burning. 'This isn't some kind of game, Virgil! I just wasted a lot of time going to Las Vegas to find out bullshit about my brother, and now you want to write cryptic little notes because you want some time alone with me?' He felt his own ire start to rise a little bit, wrote it off as his stereotypically ill temper, but still couldn't contain the look in his eyes. 'It's not just a dead lead, Lia... I gave your information to McClancy. His organization has amassed so many files that it may take him a few weeks to get back to me, but he is looking for you. As a personal favor.' She opened her mouth to say something, but nothing came out. Virgil actually contacting one of the KB on his own was shocking enough, but to actually cooperate with one of the Order, and a lupine, no less... 'Thank you,' she said, looking down at her feet and feeling a bit like she'd overreacted. A finger cupped under her chin and pulled it back up so that she was looking at him eye-to-eye. 'Mobius gave me an estimation of your arrival time, so dinner is ready on the terrace... join me?' Allowing herself to feel somewhat breathless about the trouble he'd gone to, she nodded. 'Of course.' .:: ::: ::. Blackthorn Estate Great Room Dinner had gone by fairly quickly- seared steak was about the only thing that they'd found they could consume without getting ill at the strong taste of spices or vegetables, so lingering over dinner would have been fairly pointless. The great room was a far better location for the two of them besides. Finally, a place to unwind, and forget about the troubles of GCW for a few moments. Or at least, Lia felt that way- she didn't really know if Virgil had ever worried about GCW. The warm crystal glass she held was filled with some sort of drugged vitae. She could tell by the warm way it washed down her esophagus, the soft feeling it gave her after a few moments. Enough of this, and she could imagine herself simply floating away for the next few days, only coming down from this peaceful cloud when it was time to head back to Atlanta. A slight smile was on Virgil's lips, rare enough of an expression that it might as well have been a beaming grin. She found herself smiling back at him, without really being able to control the reaction. Smiling felt foreign somehow. 'I've missed spending time with you lately,' he said, leaning close enough to brush a strand of hair off of her cheek, 'I don't like it when everything is as busy as it has been. Not enough time to slow down and enjoy your company.' She closed her eyes, leaning her head against the back of the couch. 'It's been a necessary evil lately. Had I not put so much time into GCW lately, we wouldn't have what we have right now.' His arm circled around behind her back and his lips gently brushed her neck. A plea, or a request... it had been a long time since they had shared blood, long by their standards, and her neck pulsed in response to the touch as though prepared once again for the pierce of a fang. Instead, he spoke there, half-muffled by her skin. 'I am grateful for your sacrifices, but I cannot help thinking sometimes that they are not work making if you're going to be so unhappy about them.' 'What do you mean?' She stiffened slightly, but he didn't pull away, and instead pulled himself closer, lacing his fingers through her long, thick hair. 'Lia, you don't have to play strong with me. I know something's bothering you.' The silence led him to believe that he had gravely offended her pride, which was not something one did lightly to Lia Vlaszy, not without expecting some serious reprisals. She sighed deeply, but the next thing he felt was a soft touch on his back, rather than a hand pushing him away. 'I'm having trouble reconciling a few things.' 'Like?' 'Being a leader, and not leading by example. I'm supposed to be the one with the answers to everything, and yet I truly have shown you nothing... the last one of us to hold a singles title, the last one of us to garner any attention...' His free hand pressed against her lips. 'Shh... That doesn't mean you're an unsuccessful leader, it means that you are teaching us about self-sacrifice for the benefit of the group. A lesson that we could all stand to learn, as I see.' She paused, quiet as she considered that. But whether or not it was true, it made her feel a little bit better that Virgil saw things that way. It was an undefinable confidence that he gave her, and she wasn't able to make it clear even to herself why it was that if Virgil had been the only person on her side, she still would have felt invincible. 'Let's not talk about GCW this week,' he said, 'There is much more to you and us than that, and I'd like to enjoy it.' Despite her vow to herself that she wasn't going to let anyone get inside her head right now, it was a proposition that she found herself readily agreeing with, nodding to his suggestion without even considering why. Another soft sip of vitae had her head spinning slightly again. She almost missed his next question, spoken from what seemed to be a musing haze that had clouded around his head and made him far more thoughtful than usual this evening. 'Lia... do you ever think about the past?' 'Of course I do.' 'No,' he backed up, 'I mean, do you ever think about it to the point where it seems so real to you, but just out of touch? Do you ever wonder if those memories you have are just something that you made up to lie to yourself?' She dropped her chin to rest on top of his head. 'So many gaps...' she murmured, 'I just wish that I knew why there were so many blank spaces that I can't seem to fill when I think about my life.' 'Mm.' The noise was one of assent. 'It seems so unfair to be given the gift that we have, and to not be able to remember everything you have experienced.' 'Yes,' she whispered, half-hoarsely. In her brain, jumbled thoughts of her life with her family before she'd been given to Master Ionescu were strong, picture after another, but at a certain point, they turned to nothingness. Her children, her husband, what happened after Vaclav her sire had died... these were all things that had faded, no matter how she wished she could bring them back. 'I wish that I could remember my wife,' Virgil said starkly, a bit dully. It wasn't often that he waxed poetic about his past, but it was almost like whatever was in the vitae had opened up his brain for her perusal, 'I remember that she had remarkable features, so different than the ones of my people. I remember she was eager to please me, though we had never met before, and I remember how easily we fell in love with one another... and although love was out of convenience then, as you know, so perhaps it is only my sentimental head that makes me believe that we loved one another so sincerely.' 'I can't remember my children,' she rejoined. 'Nor I mine.' Virgil sighed softly, breath coming semi-warm through the fabric of her velvet shirt. 'I can almost feel a tangible veil between me and what I want to remember. Yet I cannot discern where it came from, or how to remove it...' It hurt, thinking about things that way. Perhaps that was something that people didn't often take into account about being able to live long past the deaths of most of your loved ones. You could never do anything but romanticize the past into something that you'd never have again, and move listlessly lost through the world searching for someone to make you feel halfway like you did back in your glory days. An endless, and mostly fruitless, search. Frustration welled deep inside and came forward, cutting off any more reminiscence about a past that had been out of reach for a very long time now. 'Why dwell on that? Why do we do this to ourselves?' Virgil's thumb rubbed gently against the small of her back. 'Identity, I think. We need a way to hold on to who we were in order to define who we are. And so we lose ourselves in the past, attempting to remake an age that has long passed. What do we have without what we were?' She reached down, digging her fingernails into his forearm. 'The present, Virgil. No one can take that away, because it is in the moment. No matter how fleeting it may be, it's something better than swimming in misery.' He shifted position, and she found the hand behind her back impelling her to stand. The two of them, for a moment, only locked eyes, and said nothing. But Virgil gently took her wine glass from her hand and set it down, before beginning to lead her. 'You are quite correct, cara mia...' With the present in mind, she followed where he lead her, up the sweeping carpeted staircase, down the wide hallway, and into the oak and leather furnishings of the master suite. .:: ::: ::. Blackthorn Estate Master Suite It was a measure of a good night for Lia to note how she finished it. This one met its end with an arm draped over Virgil's chest, a head on his shoulder, and the covers pulled up to about their waists. It didn't take long for both of them to drift off to sleep near in unison, the physical exhaustion of journey and exertion and the web of the drugged vitae lulling them into dreams. .:: ::: ::. He sank to his knees in the dirt, overcome. Blood, there was blood everywhere on his hands, he could see it seeping into every crevice, but it only reminded him that she was dead now. He wasn't supposed to feel so attached to her, only a wife, they said, and a man could find another, only he didn't want another, he wanted the one he had had to come waltzing back into his life, whole and pretty and happy again, for everything to be fine. A common thing, they said, to lose a wife like that. He didn't want to believe them, because it wasn't fair, and he cursed God with every fiber of his being, finding it hard to keep himself from wandering the gardens of his estate screaming about its unfairness like a madman to whatever being would listen. His fingertips touched the dirt, her dirt, where she had sat busy at her tasks many days while their children played along with the dogs. When the weather was sunny, as it often was, he would come out from his business in the house and play with them, and at times, she would stop her work and smile at him, her eyes half-narrowed in the sunlight. ... This wasn't it, wasn't what he was supposed to expect out of his life. Everything had been perfect... He wanted someone to blame for her having gone away so suddenly, but everyone to blame was anyone who told him it was God's will that he be so miserable. The solution lay written in the letter on his dressing table. His servants would find it in the morning, and they would wail and misunderstand. And his family would be scandalized, some grief-stricken at the way that their clan had already lost an elder brother, and now, another. But he didn't care. There was no other way. And knowing this, he took one last look around his bedroom at all the lavish furnishings that had come to mean so much of nothing, the bed that seemed so empty without her soft curving form curled next to him. His will hardened. Thinking of her one last time, he threw himself off the parapet. .:: ::: ::. Her hands went to her stomach as she smiled up at her husband. '... I am with child.' She had said his name, and somehow she knew that, but she couldn't make it out coming out of her own lips. S, it started with an S. Perhaps a C? But there was no time to question that, because she felt strong arms and broad shoulders squeezing her in jubilation. A baby was something they had hoped for for so long, and now it was going to happen! Elation wrote itself across her face, and she turned to look up at her husband, that strong face that she'd grown to love so well since their arranged marriage. But instead of his face, there was only an indiscriminate mist, a sort of formless nothingness that made all those features unclear, features that she had stroked so many times at night that she knew them by contour as well as by sight. ... But everything had fallen apart. She didn't know why, save it must have been a judgment upon her from God for the wrongs she had previously committed. The baby was dead, taken by some horrible blight that even Mama's enhanced blood did not cure her from. And now, these men at the door, who had come with their insidious stamp of horse hooves, men dressed in ecclesiastical robes. Her husband had tried to bar their entrance into the house, saying 'Surely you respect the right of a man's private domain.' But they had said to him 'Senor, this is far more serious than that.' And though he had stood there, her towering monolith of protection, they had somehow managed to push him aside. 'Run!' His words to her made her act, picking up her skirts and bolting as quickly as she could out the door of the kitchen and into the estate's sweeping side yard. And she had heard behind her the noises of a fight, but she knew he could take care of himself. If she turned back to look at him, she would be lost... she had to fight, fight to get back to him. They caught her in the garden, two of them surrounding her and one reaching out and snatching a wrist. She fought wildly, her long dark hair coming unpinned from itself and tumbling loose down her back, but they were stronger than her, and not disposed to be too kindly to a woman wanted for murder. She remembered from there, turning back towards the house where he was standing, sword in hand in the doorway, three men brandishing their own weapons. He was the only thing she saw, the panic of leaving him the only thing she felt. And with a helpless scream, she called his name. '...' Only now, she could not make her lips discern the formless word. She remembered, remembered the wildness of the look on his face, the way he fought to get to her, and by the time one of the guards lay on the ground, they had already tucked her away, tied, into the carriage. She would always remember the way he fought. .:: ::: ::. Blackthorn Estate Master Suite A terrified gasp tore her out of sleep, and she couldn't stop her hands from their clawing, still trying to tear the eyes out of the officials who held her back from her Lord. But suddenly, she found her arms arrested, encircled at the wrists by a manacle grip. 'Lia... Lia, you're awake.' For the first time in what felt like eons, she whimpered, her sleep-bleared eyes forcing themselves open to see the darkness all around her. Virgil's arms pulled her close, rubbing the small of her back. 'You had nightmares too?' A slow, shuddering sob tore its way through her, and the sound to him was both alien and familiar at the same time. 'What happened?' Slowly, she curled her hands around his shoulders, clinging to him in a way that did not at all suit the fierce huntress he knew. 'It was? memories, so vivid. They took me from my Lord, and after that?' She trailed off, her voice breaking up at the end of the sentence. 'I understand? my wife was taken from me, not long after my children.' He swallowed, and more words followed in a flow that couldn't be stemmed. 'I remember she always thought I would die first,' he said, his voice about as raw as she had ever heard it, 'that I would be killed in battle and leave her a widow? we swore we'd never marry anybody else if something happened to one of us?' 'I know how she feels.' Laid against his shoulder, remembering how her own Lord's shoulder felt, she could say that--could say that she remembered complaining to her own husband of how horrible it was to be left alone while he spent weeks a year in battle for his lord. After she spoke, she realized that the air around them had gotten still, thick with tension, and heavy with memory. But there was nothing but pain in memory, they'd already agreed on that. The present was all that held meaning. .:: ::: ::.
