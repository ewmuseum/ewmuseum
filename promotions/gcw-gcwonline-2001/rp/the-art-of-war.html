The Art of War

undated

Date: 4/27/2007 - 5/3/2007 April 27th, 2007; 1840 hours Rottonbottom home; Burlington, VT “Damn!” Katie swore bitterly, angry at herself after dropping the tray that carried the Cornish hens she’d prepared for the night’s dinner. The loud noise of the breaking porcelain tray, and the sound of his mother shouting, caused John to start crying. Seeing this, and knowing she’d caused it, made Katie swear again, and louder, tears starting to well in her own eyes. Percival reached out to hold John, but Denise had been sitting closer and was there first, before him. Right away she started making the funny faces that her brother always found so amusing. Percival went over to Katie and hugged her, holding her head against his shoulder. “Shhh, it’s all right, Katie,” he tried to comfort her. But the Monarch Butterfly was less forgiving of herself. “Sure and I can’t do anything right these past several days!” she complained. “First I forget to get the car’s oil changed. We were lucky we didn’t need to have the engine replaced. Then me hand shakes while I try to mend Denise’s white church blouse and tear it so bad we have to get a whole new one. Then I can’t get me workout routine right at the gym, and Stuart is wondering if I need to see a doctor!” Percival continued to hold Katie in one arm while reaching out to stroke John’s head with the other hand. “You do seem a tad unsettled,” he had to admit. “Sure and crazed is more like it,” Katie said. “I have nay been this anxious since me first day with Green Clover back in Ireland.” Percival looked down at his wife’s feet. Katie was still wearing the dressy black pumps she wore to the public appearance she’d made that morning at a local school. “Change into some loafers or sneakers,” he suggested. “Perhaps it’s time to cheat a little on our healthful eating habits.” He turned to Denise. “Put on your sneakers and socks. Dinner at Wendy’s tonight, and we’ll catch a movie afterward.” Denise gave a small cheer and headed to her room to change, and even Katie had to grin a little. “I know who me own hero still is,” she said, kissing her husband on the cheek. April 28th, 2007; 1015 hours Thanwareth’s Studio, Burlington, VT “Come on, Katie, level with me... please,” her yoga instructor coaxed, trying to make her tone both gentle and playful. Thanwareth Chomsiri had come to know the diminutive Irishwoman well over the past several years they had spent as teacher and pupil. Chomsiri also had come to notice that Katie’s physical agility and coordination were rather off. Katie sighed and gave a weary shrug. She wasn’t sick of discussing this, but just mentally fatigued. The Monarch Butterfly wasn’t herself the past several days... and her friends and family were noticing. “I wish I could answer ye, Chomsiri,” Katie said. “Sure and mayhap I do need to see a doctor.” “We Thai often find that old-fashioned home remedies beat any modern drugs a doctor can pump into your bloodstream,” Chomsiri said with a sly smile. “The old saying, ‘take two of these and call me in the morning’ probably wasn’t invented by a doctor, but by a chemist or pharmacist. You’ve seen the movie, ‘The Constant Gardener,’ haven’t you?” Katie giggled lightly and stretched. “Aye, that I have,” she said. “So if me doctor cannot help... what can me swami do?” The word swami always made Chomsiri have to laugh, but she always took it in good stride. “My half brother is visiting me this month... he lives in China and teaches Tai-Chi. Let me call him, and I think he might have some answers for you.” Katie nodded and sipped some of the tea Chomsiri always kept available in her studio. Soon her brother, Tsi-Quan, was ringing the studio’s doorbell. Chomsiri opened the door and gave her half-brother a hug. “Hey Chomsiri,” Tsi-Quan said amiably. “I didn’t interrupt you during work, did I?” “More like I called you so you could help a little with it,” Chomsiri answered. “You’ve heard of Katie O’Clare, right? The Monarch Butterfly?” Tsi-Quan shook hands and smiled. “I sure have,” he said, “My daughter is a big fan of yours. She wants you to stomp Chris Storm flat come your warpath match!” Katie’s face and her handshake made it very clear that Chris Storm was a very touchy subject with her. Tsi-Quan looked at his half-sister and smiled in a way that Katie didn’t quite understand. “Let me take off my sweater and show you a few Tai-Chi exercises,” Tsi-Quan suggested. Katie nodded quietly. Who knows, if this exercise helped to relax her, it would be worth it. Tsi-Quan demonstrated some exercise moves very slowly, and Katie copied. Her rhythm and coordination wasn’t quite right, but she was able to grasp the gist of the movements. “Good,” Tsi-Quan smiled. “Now, take this small flashlight... pretend it’s a knife, and you’re trying to mug me. Lunge at me with the knife.” Katie accepted the flashlight but looked at Tsi-Quan like he’d lost a few marbles. “Sure and what does that have to do with the price of tea in Tijuana?” she asked, quite non-plussed. Tsi-Quan winked at her. He understood the slang statement from some American friends he corresponded with over the internet. “You’ll see,” he said. Katie shrugged and went into a fighting crouch. She circled back and forth a few times in a 45-degree arc, trying to angle for an opening. Her body darted forth like a striking snake as the pocket flashlight shot forth at Tsi-Quan’s upper left chest. The movement pattern was exactly the same... there was no doubt about that. Tsi-Quan’s body and hands had just moved in the exact same movement pattern and rhythm as when he’d just demonstrated a gentle exercise move to Katie. But the SPEED and TEMPO were markedly different. He executed the movement much faster than when he performed it as a simple exercise. And the results were, well... simply mind-boggling. He’d dodged the lunging attack with the most perfect timing, and sent Katie sprawling to land unceremoniously on her dainty derri’re. Tsi-Quan smiled and helped Katie up. “Again,” he said. “Do it from a different angle this time, like you’ve got the element of surprise.” A little warily this time, Katie went back into a fighting crouch and angled for the right opening. Tsi-Quan went into another pattern of exercise moves, doing them slowly, with careful rhythm. Katie spotted her opening and lunged with lightning quickness. But Chomsiri had always counseled Katie that the fastest thing of all in the whole universe was the speed of thought. Tsi-Quan’s body evaded the attack as if he’d anticipated the exact angle it was coming from, and grabbed the Monarch Butterfly’s dainty arm and wrist, twisting sharply to force her to drop the pocket flashlight. Both fists struck like homing pigeons at her temple and solar plexus, pulled with masterful control to stop a mere centimeter from her skin. Katie balked at repeating the demonstration again. She looked at Tsi-Quan with an expression that was a bit deeper than simple wariness. Tsi-Quan gave a ‘bring it’ motion with his hand. “Come on,” he said. “Don’t be chicken.” He’d said the wrong word again, and at the wrong time. Katie’s attacks came in a dizzying blur of speed, driven by raw anger as she weaved in and out. But Tsi-Quan matched her, move for move, his simple exercise motions sped up appropriately to dazzling effectiveness. He landed few counterblows, but Katie landed even fewer. By the time he backed off, it was clear who the winner would have been if they’d actually been fighting. Tsi-Quan bowed, and Katie did the same. But as she did, Tsi-Quan slapped her lightly on the back of the head. “Never take your eyes off your opponent,” he said. “Even when bowing.” Katie repeated the bow, this time keeping her head lifted and her eyes on him. Tsi-Quan smiled. “Sure and I never fancied an exercise like Tai-Chi to be so useful in fighting,” she said. “Not many people do, especially in the West,” Tsi-Quan said. “How... how were ye able to move so much faster than I?” That was the one thing Katie still didn’t understand. “I didn’t,” Tsi-Quan said. The diminutive Irishwoman quirked an eyebrow. “Then why didn’t I gut ye like a fish, or would have if the pocket flashlight had been a real knife?” “There’s more to battle than just speed and power,” Tsi-Quan said. “You lost the fight before it even began.” “Let’s all do the Cosmic Nebula meditation,” Chomsiri said, and smiled. Cosmic Nebula was the name she gave to the meditation exercise. She thought it was catchy, and most of her students did too. Chomsiri led the traditional ‘aum’ chant as they assumed proper position and meditated. Katie’s face crinkled every so often, showing she was still troubled, and it wasn’t just in her body. But little by little she seemed to relax and let stress melt away. “See the white light that is your breath,” Chomsiri said. It was one of the traditional mantras she repeated during the meditation. Katie embraced the empty black-and-white swirls of mist she envisioned. This state of mind was meant to give visions of emptiness; a place of mist and contrasting swirls of black and white. It was one of the incentives that led Chomsiri to call it Cosmic Nebula. She wasn’t quite in the intended state of mind, but she was fairly close. She didn’t see the motion behind her—it was executed right in her blind spot. But Katie had sensed barely perceptible shifts in air pressure, and a sixth sense in her, urged her to twist counterclockwise. Even on her knees, the response was dizzying. She evaded a feigned attack from Tsi-Quan and countered with a simple savate maneuver. West met East as Tsi-Quan’s Tai Chi movement was completely neutralized by Katie’s speed and the fluidity of her savate technique, and she sent him tumbling to the ground. She blinked at him, and looked at her own hands, her face lighting up in wonder. Tsi-Quan sat up cross-legged and grinned at Katie. “Battle is won in the stillness of the mind. Before the first sword is even drawn.” Katie looked at Tsi-Quan, and then at Chomsiri, and back again. Slowly their grins started to make sense to her and she smiled like a child being given a $20 bill while standing in a candy store. She gave a sheepish giggle and put her hands on her hips. “Sure and how long have ye known?” she asked Chomsiri. “How did ye figure it out when even I couldn’t?” “I think you just didn’t want to,” said Chomsiri. “After chasing Chris Storm down, after he had you drugged rather than wrestle you, and then just plain didn’t show up for the tag-team match; then accusing him of being chicken, to the point of making all kinds of bad poultry jokes to make him the butt of it... now that you’re finally getting your chance at him, there was no way to face yourself down and admit to some pre-match jitters.” Tsi-Quan shook his head lightly and put a finger on Katie’s lips. “You don’t need to worry about losing to him, even when you’re confident that you won’t,” he said. “What you need to worry about is living with yourself if you do... ESPECIALLY when you have full confidence in yourself. We’re our own worst critics; it’s something you’ve always preached from studying theater. Losing to Storm wouldn’t be the knife in your belly that made life unbearable. That knife would be getting up in the morning and looking in the mirror. It would be making your next public appearance and looking at all your fans thinking you’d let them down. Battle is won in the stillness of the mind. Anger, frustration, rage... they give you power but make you reckless. And power has never been your strategy in a battle.” Katie swallowed hard. “Then pray, both of ye... teach me mind to be still,” she said. It was just like when Katie began studying with Chomsiri. She’d learned so much since her time studying yoga with her... and now it was time to go back to the beginning. ---- The next five days were spent in deep meditation and trance work. They practiced both old, well-known avenues they’d done many times, and a few new ones more closely tied into the Tai-Chi technique that Tsi-Quan taught. He showed her more exercises, and showed her how they were done both slowly, to relax and limber the body, and quickly, to use in battle. May 3rd, 2007; 1142 hours Thanwareth’s Studio, Burlington, VT Chomsiri led the ‘aum’ chant in the Cosmic Nebula meditation. She, Katie and Tsi-Quan had been at it somewhat longer than was usual, although they’d all lost track of the time... emptying their minds and thoughts was designed for exactly that. It was designed to where the practitioner had no concept of time until meditation was broken. They stood up and did a few yoga stretching moves, still chanting the mantra and focusing on the white light of their breath. The stretching was designed to give the sensation of liberating themselves from the confines of mere flesh and bone. A flash of motion from the corner of Katie’s eye, and her body reacted. The fluidity and grace of Tsi-Quan’s Tai-Chi were a perfect match for the same in Katie’s Savate... but Katie was moving faster. She was moving with perfect coordination. She was moving... more like herself. She disarmed Tsi-Quan of the pocket flashlight and raked it down the center of his chest, and twisted her wrist deftly at the apex of the maneuver. Tsi-Quan and Chomsiri chuckled, and Katie had to do the same. “Okay, now you have to pay for my funeral,” Tsi-Quan joked, and Katie giggled lightly. The diminutive Irishwoman hugged both of them; her embrace filled with gratitude. “Pray let me take ye both to lunch,” she asked. “Lunch sounds great,” Tsi-Quan said. “One of my buddies here in the States says that American pizza is the best in the world, and I kind of resent that remark. You show me an American pizzeria with better pizza than what I can get back home in Chongqing, and I’ll take HIM to dinner next time I get together with him.” Katie laughed. “Sure and I think I’ll take on that challenge,” she said. “Just wait until ye visit Armondo’s twenty minutes uptown.” Stuart and his wife Maggie met Katie and her guests at Armondo’s. “Hey, Katie,” Maggie smiled cheerfully, hugging Katie. “How’d the session go?” Katie nodded. “Sure and it went well,” she said. “Sure and I am ready for some pizza. Armondo’s special, topped with barbequed chicken.” Stuart did a double-take and fidgeted away from Katie. Just talking about chicken was not a smart idea with Katie the past few weeks. He bit his tongue, afraid to say anything. Katie laid her head on Stuart’s shoulder affectionately and put an arm around his shoulders. “Thank ye for caring about me,” she said, “but I think I shan’t be needing to see a doctor.” Stuart noted she seemed much calmer. He smiled and nodded. “Good, good,” he said. “Feeling better?” Katie motioned to Chomsiri and Tsi-Quan. “I took two of these and called ye this morning,” she winked, and all five of them laughed.
