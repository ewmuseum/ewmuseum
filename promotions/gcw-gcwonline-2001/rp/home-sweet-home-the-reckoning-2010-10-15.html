Home Sweet Home, The Reckoning

15 October 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/15/10 “Why here?” Troy asked, eying the thin paper placemats with distaste. He looked up at the faded paper lanterns adorning the inside of the Chinese restaurant and shook his head. “This isn’t exactly the pick of the litter for Chinese around here. We could go to Cho’s over on 5th or…” “Troy…” Tempest peered up at him through the tousle of black bangs dangling over her eyes. “Why would I want him to know where I really ate? Let him think I have piss poor taste and like the Americanized version of my own native cuisine. Besides, if he knows better, maybe he’ll say so.” She sipped absently at a steaming cup of tea in a garishly ornate cup covered in orange blossoms. “It’s odd, in all that questioning, in Hong Kong it was very clear that Wei knew some things and really had no idea about others. It seemed like he’d tried to fill in the blanks or maybe had some misinformation, from where I don’t know. “ Troy nodded thoughtfully; his own tea untouched and said, “You’re still trying to figure out what he really knows.” Tempest nodded, her high tufted ponytail bobbing along. “Pretty much. I tried to do that some in Hong Kong as well. But then I had an eye toward getting out. And I was trying to figure out who was really behind my being arrested.” She leaned closer to him, perching her chin on his arm, and spoke quietly, as if already trying to avoid any unfriendly listeners. “It’s not like I committed any crime or there were any outstanding warrants, none they showed me anyway. But the Chinese government is a big, bureaucratic labyrinth. It’s totally common for corrupt officials to operate on their own agendas without much of anyone knowing what they’re up to. So the reality is that Wei can be totally official and that means nothing at all about who he’s really working for.” Troy sighed, his eyebrows arched and said, “Great… So as if being on Zenith’s payroll wasn’t bad enough, you’ve gotta wonder what else might be going on and if all that stuff you did with the special investigators back in O-four was for naught.” “Or made things worse,” Tempest said and slumped against his arm, prompting him to hug her close against him in the booth seat. “I was in protective custody and witness protection for four years, longer if you count the time I used my real name but laid low back here in the states. I thought I was finally out of the woods. Now it seems like it was yesterday, but…” She shook her head and frowned. “It doesn’t make sense. Back then I knew who was out to get me or would be if they found out. Now I’m not so sure.” “You’re sure this isn’t all just a big smokescreen that Zenith’s putting up?” Troy asked, peering down at the tiny warrior woman he loved with concern. “From what I’ve seen, it doesn’t seem like a stretch that he’d go to some lengths to mess with your head.” She shook her head very slightly and said, “Yeah, but it’s like… He doesn’t want to actually give me that much attention.” She sat back and shook her head in contemplation. “He’s so attached to image and looking like he’s in control that he can’t actually make it look like he thinks I’m important. And coming out to do something against me does that. Like at Rampage when I finally showed, he didn’t do anything.” Troy shrugged. “Well, if he had, that would be almost an admission that he had something to do with trying to keep you away in the first place.” “Yes, and it would also show that he gave a damn.” She smirked slightly over the edge of her teacup. “And that’s too much power to give me. No, I think he gave me just enough attention to find someone with an agenda of his own, and then set him loose on me.” Troy folded his arms and sat back, his leather jacket squeaking against the Naugahyde. “I hope you didn’t mean that to be reassuring, ‘cause it’s not.” Tempest smiled and said, “Don’t be so pessimistic. Between Wei’s questions and his actually coming here, I’m starting to believe that he’s a lot more interested in things I might know than in destroying me for Zenith’s pleasure.” She paused and with a thoughtful look added, “In fact, by now, I imagine he’s had to cede that right to Vivica Valentine.” She chuckled, bubbling over with the pure mischief of her recent pranks against the US Champion. Troy frowned. “Keep it up, Short Stuff. Locking you in a tower is sounding more like a viable option every day.” “Oh come on, that was good!” Tempest squealed. “You have to admit it was.” “Yes! It was good!” Troy raised his hands. “I especially liked the Johnny Cash song. But that doesn’t change the fact that you’ve got Team Vivisection after you now and never mind that guys like Frost and Terror would just as soon kill you as look at you. Oh and Terror gets a fully sanctioned shot this week.” His hands flopped to the table and he shook his head at the burdensome situation. Tempest smiled softly and watched him a moment before resting her much smaller hand on top of his. “You see things so differently sometimes. The way I see it, I have a fully sanctioned shot at Terror this week, and his title, the highest title in GCW. He should fear me, not vice versa.” Troy smiled very slightly, worry still shadowing his slim, straight features. “You always see it that way. And I know it makes you great at what you do. But yeah, it still scares the hell out of me, for you.” “I know,” Tempest said, and patted his hand. “But hey, at least you’re watching.” “Are you kidding? I don’t feel I can afford to miss anything anymore.” Troy exhaled slowly. “But it’s this off-camera stuff that has me more worried. Guys coming to our home, that creeps me out.” “Yeah.” Tempest nodded silently, staring for a moment into her cup as if reading the as yet unsettled leaves swirling in its depths. “I’m not big on that either. That Zenith is perfectly willing and able to interrupt my life outside the ring is a little too close for me. But he can’t do it with impunity. That’s why I need to understand what it is that China still wants with me.” “I would like very much to help in that concern.” Tempest sat up straight as the voice from behind her invaded their privacy. Troy looked over her shoulder at Wei, who smiled pleasantly and passed them, seating himself opposite the two. Tempest’s eyes narrowed slightly, but only slightly as she reseated herself, having risen just a bit. Troy eyed the unremarkable Asian man cautiously, even though he sat across from them with a placid expression and ordered tea quite calmly when the waitress appeared. “I’m surprised you don’t know of some of the better restaurants nearby. But… maybe you do.,” Wei said and a smile touched his lips briefly. “One does not invite the stray dog into the kitchen after all. One serves him scraps on the doorstep.” Tempest nodded slowly. “Not a bad analogy. You have to earn a bit more trust to be let into the kitchen, so to speak. So, why have you come all the way here, to my doorstep?” The reference wasn’t lost on Wei, but his smile didn’t falter, a sign of years sitting across tables and giving nothing away. “Well, I simply need to know what you told the special investigators five years ago. And now that you are no longer compelled to tell me, I had hoped that you might see the wisdom in giving me this information.” Tempest looked back at him with a gaze no less steady. “If you are who you say you are, then how could you possibly not know all of that?” “That information has been lost.” Tempest’s head tilted ever so slightly forward and she eyed him more intently. “You don’t lose the kind of information I gave them. You’re either not with them, or your not privy to it. So I don’t feel overly compelled to share. And your association with my enemies does nothing for your case, nor does the fact that you kept me imprisoned.” Wei took a sip of the tea that had been placed before him and nodded slowly. “All of that is true… It was, on review, a regrettable way to have begun this search for the truth. But Mr. Zenith was quite insistent that you not arrive in Japan for that particular show.” Tempest fumed like a small teapot but kept her voice even as she said, “The only thing you regret is finding out to late that I was more than you bargained for when I escaped. And if you’re still working for Zenith…” “Mr. Zenith has no further need of my services,” Wei interjected with an agitated shake of his head. “His interest was in your connections to China and your murky citizenship status that allowed you to be held. As I understand it, you’re in the process of sorting that out, yes?” “I am,” Tempest said evenly. “Moreover, I have a proper visa while it’s in process.” Wei seemed to study her for a moment and then said, “When I told you Liu was alive, you seemed surprised by that. You can’t be unconcerned that he’s out there, in charge and you have now resurfaced. He’ll be looking for you.” “Why?” Tempest asked in her best imitation of nonchalant. “He has no reason to suspect me of anything.” “Oh, but he does.” Wei stared across the table meaningfully and this time dropped a look toward Troy. “And why would that be?” Tempest asked and leaned forward. “Unless someone told him. Someone like you. I think it’s time you told me who you’re really working for Mr. Wei.” Wei calmly reached into his jacket and removed a small leather case. Flipping open it revealed a badge and ID card. He handed it to Tempest to review and while Troy peered at it with interest, it was clearly in Chinese. “Kong Kong Police Department, Special Investigations unit,” she read and handed it back to him. “It doesn’t say what you’re investigating, but then I guess it wouldn’t.” “No, it would not be wise to state that I am the lead investigator of gang related crime in Hong Kong, on a card,” Wei said calmly. “So you’re not even national security or central intelligence? That’s who I talked to back then. They should have all that information.” Tempest’s brow furrowed as she ran over the possibilities, and which ones she was willing to share. “Why wouldn’t they share it with you? I mean maybe not at first, but I would think they would by now.” “As I said, it is lost. They no longer have the information either. It has been deleted from all of their systems. And of course it’s not the sort of thing someone has a copy of on a home hard drive.” Tempest stared in shock. “It’s all gone? Every bit of it?” “Every scrap,” Wei said quietly. “Someone was very thorough and very well connected. It had to be an inside job, of course. But that means someone also knows that you provided that information. And odds are, that person or persons, works for Papa Liu. He is still the most powerful of the triad leaders, even though their influence has been diminished.” Tempest folded her hands in silence and Troy looked at her intently, occasionally shifting his gaze to Wei. At last she spoke and said, “There are several levels on which I would have to trust you to believe any of this. And as much as I might like to believe you are who you say you are, you could be the very one who deleted the information.” “I would then have no cause to try and retrieve it from you,” Wei said evenly. “Talking to me, so that I might discern who was behind the theft of the information, as well as its original content will be the best chance at keeping you safe from whatever threats still lurk in the world of organized crime for you. You should consider it.” Troy, who had been silent, watchful, and attentive, but left the exchange to Tempest piped up. “One question: You pulled her in on Zenith’s orders, not because your department told you to. Why should we believe that now you’re motivated by Justice, all of a sudden?” Wei smiled and said, “A reasonable question. It is most regrettable now, as I said. I would have been happy to apprehend Ms. Tyrell without anyone paying me to. But Mr. Zenith provided the access to a person of great interest to me. That he paid me… well yes, a policeman’s salary is nothing remarkable. But if I really wanted money, I would have gone to work for Liu or someone like him years ago, just as you seem to think I have. Search my records and you will find out that I’m clean.” He smiled wryly and added, “Or at least as clean as it gets in Hong Kong.” Tempest nodded slightly. “Oh don’t worry, I will. You’ll hear from me again if I have something for you.” She started to scoot out of the booth seat, but stopped as Wei raised his hand slightly. “One more thing; I have something for you.” He produced from another of his pockets a folded sheet of paper. Tempest unfolded it and read, her eyes slowly widening. “You private investigator has turned up some direct family connections, which I know you’re reluctant to pursue. But he has failed to connect the dots to this event. I was certain you’d want to know.” Tempest blinked as Troy peered over her shoulder and read. “Isn’t that… your dad?” “It certainly is,” Wei said. ~ “What? You can’t go to London now!” Miyuki followed Tempest as she bustled through the warehouse loft she shared with Troy, scooping up clothes, dodging ferrets and texting simultaneously. “Remember the show? The World title shot? all that kind of important stuff?” “Don’t worry, I’ll be back in plenty of time for the show and to prepare. This is something I have to do.” Miyuki was trying to keep her tone even as she watched Tempest tear around. “Tempest, why? Why now? What could possibly be so important?” In a swift passage, with a laundry basket in hand and Trouble at her heels, carrying a ball and hoping for some playtime, Tempest thrust the sheet of paper Wei had given her into Miyuki’s hands. She froze. “This is… Oh my god.” “Exactly,” said Tempest as a ball came flying back into the room for trouble to chase. “I’m going. Don’t worry, I’ll be back. But this is a once in a lifetime thing.” ~ The affair was somber even for a British ceremony. Moved inside because of a light rain, the quarters were a little cramped in the chapel which still smelled of new paint. It was light blue, an airy, hopeful color. “What is important to remember about Ken is that he had a tremendous sense of giving.” The man speaking was older, a little bit of gray showing at his temples and he looked quite distinguished in a dark blue suit, speaking in front of the throng. “And so it’s only appropriate that his memory be recalled in a facility such as this one, meant to care those most in need, regardless of means. My brother led a valiant struggle to care for the indigent and in so doing, became subject to the same dangers they faced on the streets of Hong Kong. “ At the back of the chapel, one of the doors opened and a petite Asian woman stepped inside. Or partly Asian. Even from the front of the chapel, Peter Tyrell could make out her sparkling blue eyes. She wore a short, black dress and boots and her long black hair was slightly tinted with blue in places. “The Kenneth Eldon Tyrell Free Clinic will stand as a testament to his generosity and his sacrifice.” “But will you live up to his example?” The voice sounded from the back of the chapel like a sudden crack of thunder that made people jump. Heads turned and voices murmured quietly as the woman who said it walked up to the front. Peter stared at young woman as if she were a ghost, and in the gathering of patrons and benefactors and staff, she seemed just as ethereal as she stepped up to Peter’s level and turned to address the crowd. “Dr. Tyrell died in 1983. Was it less fashionable to care for the poor until now, or did you just find out about his life’s work? Maybe… you finally decided it was worth acknowledging, honoring.” She looked around the room at the faces of those who were now uncomfortable, even if intrigued, and who clearly had no idea who she was. Not a wrestling fan in the bunch. “Were you aware that his first clinic was his own home? And that he took drug addicts there to go through detox?” She watched the proper horror settle in over the faces of the generous and benevolent donors who had made this place possible, but would clearly never hold the hand of a junkie with the shakes. “No, you never heard about that because you cut him off. You weren’t proud of him when he was alive. Has it really taken this many years to forgive him for not doing what was expected of him?” “Now see here…” an older man in a coat of some generation past stood up and peered at her through thick glasses. “Who are you to come in here and scold the very people who have made this fine new facility possible and have been friends of the Tyrell family, in some cases for generations?” A moment passed before the answer, but it was already there in the face of Peter Tyrell, and the faces a of a small number of people in the first row, most notably an old woman whose bright blue eyes stared at Tempest with a strange mixture of fascination and shock. “I’m Rachel Tempest Tyrell. I’m the daughter he had in Hong Kong.” Silence and astonishment fell over the room. The man who had called her out sat down very slowly. Only the family looked a bit less surprised and more as if they were hunkered down now that the storm they had always seen coming had finally hit. Tempest stood in the center of it all and with a quick glance toward Peter, she smiled. “It’s all good though. Because what you should also know about my father is that he always gave people another chance.” She looked pointedly at the family, who seemed bewildered by the statement. “He never gave up, especially on people. It didn’t matter if someone slipped back into their old habit of using drugs; he’d try and help them clean up again. I once asked him if my mother was a drug addict and he told me she was at one time.” The shuffling in the room at the admission didn’t seem to slow Tempest down. “But when I asked how many times she had fallen off the wagon, he said he didn’t know. He said he never knew for anyone, because it didn’t matter. He didn’t count failures. He didn’t want to be weighed down by regret, so he simply didn’t choose to accumulate any.” Tempest looked around the room, her smile broadening as a few faces started to crack a smile. “My father believed in the possible at all times, that as long as there was life, and just the tiniest bit of will, there was hope. When you needed a friend to make it happen, he was there. Our house was never empty. So if this is going to be a place like that, then that’s great. But this time don’t leave anyone behind. My dad never would. He’d do whatever it took. And so do I.” She turned and smiled rather pointedly at the family of her father, now hers, and found the smile returned among a few tears. She had brought him home to them and perhaps to herself. ~This RP is dedicated to the memory of Christine Fox who left this world on 10/10/10.
