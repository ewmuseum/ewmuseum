Date: 10/14/10 “You are not paid to do your best, you are paid to yield results,” – Los Aztecas Biker 1 The sun was setting on the outskirts of San Diego, but the drop in temperature was barely noticeable. The city was in the middle of the heat wave, unfortunately for Miguel Erazo the city was also in the middle of a crime wave. Things had been heated in his city for the past few months, the city was like a battleground, something in the criminal underworld of Saint Didacus’ city was brewing, a gang-war was looming. The rate of violent crimes was up almost fifty percent, the citizens were starting to get scared. People were reaching out to the politicians, calling for help. That meant that the politicians were banging down the doors at police headquarters to get results. The main culprit so far appeared to be a looming biker war. There was a new player in town, an allegedly Mexican biker gang calling themselves Los Aztecas had begun to infringe on the city and it was pissing off the already established ones. The sound of his partners fingers clacking away at his cell phone, texting to his wife back at home drew Miguel out of his trance. They had been staking out this supposed meeting place for almost an hour and still nothing had happened. Either the members of Los Aztecas were extremely tardy or their informant had given them bad information. “We should have known better than to trust that junkie,” Miguel said. “This is a waste of fucking time, should have booked that motherfucker and taken him in.” His partner, Tommy looked up from the dim, glowing light of his cell phone screen and stared at him. He didn’t offer a response, simply shrugged his shoulders and went back to his qwerty keypad. Beside him, Miguel began to get restless, his legs began to shake, his fingers tapped against the window of the unmarked cruiser. That’s when he heard the sound, the faint rumble of motorcycle engines. He looked back at his partner, who had already pocketed his cell phone and was looking intently around. “Looks like it’s going down,” Tommy said. The two men slumped lower into their seats. The rumbling of engines was beginning to draw closer and closer. Something was about to happen. 2 “Who the fuck are you?” Captain Phillip Toms was surprised by the harshness in his own voice. But it wasn’t often that anyone burst into his office unannounced, especially not someone that looked like a bureaucratic dickhead like this guy. He had warned the front desk to keep the politicians out of here, everyone was on edge about this new gang, but the politicians didn’t help his people. The intruder let a soft smile spread across his lips. He reached into the front pocket of his black sports jacket and pulled out a leather wallet. He opened the wallet to reveal a gleaming gold badge, a shield adorned with a golden eagle and the words ‘Federal Bureau of Investigation.’ “Unit Chief Kevin Erwin,” said the silver-haired man, extending a hand. “Sorry to burst in on you captain, but I’m afraid we have urgent matters to discuss. “FBI?” Toms said with a hint of surprise. “What are you guys doing here?” “May I sit down?” Erwin asked, although he was already pulling himself into the leather waiting chair before he got a response. Toms followed his lead and sat down behind his desk. He sat, waiting for an explanation. “I’m a leader on a new gang task force, focussing on the San Diego and southern California regions. We’ve heard about your problems from our higher-ups and we’re here to help.” “Bullshit,” Toms started. “You guys are never here to just help. Who is it you’re looking for?” The FBI Unit Chief allowed another soft smile to spread across his lips. Apparently this Captain Toms had dealt with the FBI before. “We’re looking to infiltrate the new Mexican biker gang that has emerged in your city...” “Los Aztecas? We barely know anything about them, as far as we know they haven’t done anything criminal. They might still be just a riding club.” Erwin wasn’t smiling now. His face was stern and he stared ahead at the police captain. He was holding back already, this was why Erwin hated dealing with the god-damned local PD. “We know a little about them already, probably more than you do, but I know that you don’t think they’re actually just a riding club. They’re the cause of all of the violence and disturbances on your city’s streets and you know it. There is a war looming Captain, and you’re going to need our help to stop it.” Toms slumped his shoulders. Erwin was right, they hadn’t admitted it to the press yet, but they had already begun digging up information on the new motorcycle club. They were pressuring informants, scouring the streets, hell he had two of his best staking out a meeting right now. “What do you know?” Toms asked. “That will come in due time. We’ve been sent here to set up a task-force. I have members of my team at a local hotel checking in. We are prepared to brief you and your department, but we need only your co-operation, a place to set-up shop and back up if requested.” This guy had done this before, Toms was sure of it. This was typical FBI procedure, come in, bully themselves to the front, show you how much smarter than you they are and then take all the glory. Hell if he had unlimited resources and men, he could have a full profile on the members of Los Aztecas as well. Unit Chief Erwin interrupted Toms from his thoughts after only a few moments. “So about that room?” 3 It was beautiful, the roar of the engines. There was no mistaking it, the sound of a motorcycle’s engine roaring in the quiet of the night. Miguel sat quietly, gripping the digital camera and doing his best to stay out of view, doing his best not to blend in. The tattered black trench-coat covered his entire body, and an oversized toque covered most of his head and face. To anyone not paying attention, he should look like any other vagrant in the slums of San Diego. Somewhere on the other side of the street, Tommy was doing the same. Miguel scanned the streets, trying to find any sign of his partner, but he could find none. He supposed that was a good thing, but how good would it be if things got ugly. He didn’t think they would, they weren’t getting into any of the heavy hitters. From what the informant had told them, this was simply an exchange house, somewhere to drop money off for laundering and pick up new supplies of drugs. From the initial ruckus of the engines, you would have thought that there would be hundreds of bikers, but strangely only a handful emerged in the parking lot of the warehouse. Stranger still were the makes and models of the bikes, all of them sport bikes, most of them Asian imports. “What the fuck? Hondas and Kawasakis?” Miguel muttered to no one in particular. The riders of the bikes lowered their kick-stands and stepped off of the bikes. The odd biker acknowledged one of the others, but for the most part, they were quiet and methodical. They stepped off of their bikes and were careful to keep their helmets on. Across the street he finally caught sight of Tommy, but he was getting a bit too close to the complex. “Step away, not so close Tommy. Come on, you know better than that,” again to no one that could hear him. One of the bikers took a longer than normal stare at his partner, and for a moment he thought there was going to be action. Instead the biker seemed to think better of it and almost let out a shrug of his shoulders as he turned to enter the warehouse. As soon as the last biker entered the building, the large steel door slammed shut, the sound echoing throughout the neighbourhood. Across the street Tommy quickly pulled out his digital camera and began snapping pictures. From where he was sitting Miguel had already managed to capture a few images, but he was sure to get pictures of all of the bikes and all of the plates. Most of them were probably fakes, or stolen, but it was worth a shot. Perhaps these guys were sloppier than everyone thought, but so far, it didn’t appear that they were. Tommy quickly finished his paparazzi impersonation and quickly stuffed the camera into his jacket. He slowly made his way back into the shadows of the streets before darting across the street to the unmarked police cruiser. Miguel was already waiting, slumped down out of sight in the driver’s seat. As soon as Tommy closed the door, they began to pull away. “What the fuck were you thinking getting so close? They almost made you man!” Miguel chastised his partner. “Don’t worry about it man, they didn’t!” Tommy replied. “Yeah, well use your fucking head next time Tommy. We haven’t even scratched the surface of these guys yet, and already you’re trying to tell us that we’re onto them.” “Oh would you fucking relax,” Tommy answered. “It’s fine, besides I think I may have managed a couple of shots of the inside of the building.” Suddenly there was a buzz in both of their pockets. Both of their cell phones going off at once. The two quickly pulled out their phones and almost in unison looked at one another after reading the short message. “Ah fuck,” Miguel sighed. “What the fuck does he want?” 4 “Captain you’re never going to believe what we got in these guys tonight,” Tommy said as the two hurried into their Captain’s office. “Yeah, Cap. This is some good stuff, we might have something here, something important,” Miguel said as he followed his partner. The two men froze in the doorway when they saw the stern face of their captain sitting in his desk. Behind him was a large man in a black suit with salt and pepper hair. The noise in the room immediately went to a hush as the two officers stared at the suit behind the Captain of their unit. Captain Toms wasted no time breaking down the situation for his two star detectives. “Boys, this is FBI Unit Chief Kevin Erwin. He’s heading up an investigation into the new biker gang that’s moved into San Diego,” Toms said. “WHAT?!” Tommy said. “Captain, what the fuck?” “Yeah, that’s supposed to be...” Miguel started, but the look on Phillip Toms’ face told him not to speak another word. The four men let the silence in the room linger for a second, before Erwin stepped forward to speak. He looked at the two plain-clothes detectives in front of him. They had been excited about something when they walked in, they also seemed quite offended when they heard about the case. He assumed that Toms already had them working on the gang. No problem, he thought to himself, maybe they’d already have some good information. “Gentlemen,” Erwin began. “I’m not certain what exactly your interest is in this gang quite yet, but I assure you that any help you can offer will not go un-thanked. We will be relying heavily on SDPD to help us with our investigation.” The two detectives immediately looked to their captain for advice. His quiet but firm stare told them both everything they needed to know. “We were just assigned to gather information starting this week,” Miguel lied. They had already been gathering information for nearly a month. Not that the FBI would know any different, even with a month’s worth of recon and scout work, they’d barely been able to put two and two together so far. The FBI Unit Chief nodded his head, seeing right through the blatant lie. He smiled to himself, acknowledging the young Hispanic officer in front of him. The kid had balls that was for sure. You had to if you thought you’d be okay lying to a member of the FBI. Erwin turned to stare at the man’s partner. This one was chubbier than the Hispanic. His hair was more tattered, the bags under his eyes were a bit bigger, he looked more stressed, more weathered. Or perhaps maybe that was just his age shining through in contrast to the youthful stupidity of his partner. With defeated eyes the man told Erwin everything he could, nothing. At least, not right now, Erwin made a mental note to check up with this one later. “They’re going to be going over the case tomorrow with the whole gang-unit. Right now, it’s just us. They’re not pulling in any other officers unless needed. We’re going to run backup for them, they’re leading this one guys,” Toms said. “That’s bullshit and you know it Captain, we don’t need these fucking feds in here stepping on our toes,” Miguel cursed. The smile faded from Erwin’s face. This smug prick was starting to cross the line. “Whether or not you like it kid, we’re here and we’re running the show,” Erwin replied coldly. “So unless you want to be getting me coffee for the entire duration of my stay, I’d suggest you shut the fuck up. We’re in charge now, like it or not kid, you’re working for me now.” Miguel stared at him, his gaze almost ablaze. He said nothing, he simply bit his tongue and turned to leave. With that same defeated and dejected look his partner turned to follow him. “8:00 AM tomorrow boys,” Erwin called out. “We’ve got a briefing that you’re both going to want to hear.” 5 O’Grady’s on 18th Street was a famous watering hole for the members of the San Diego PD. Most of the cops from the entire city knew about O’Grady’s and every single cop from the West Side had been there before. When Miguel and Tommy burst through the door, it didn’t surprise them to see several members of their unit in there, even though it was only a Wednesday night. “Erazo, Chase where the fuck have you faggots been?” someone called out. It was Garrett Williams, another one of the cops in their division. Like most of the other officers in their unit, Garrett was a standard officer. He was still wearing his SDPD blues and shield, even though they now were unbuttoned and hanging off of him. They also smelled of light beer and cheap whiskey, a cops two favourite things, next to his gun and pussy. “Fuck you Williams,” Tommy chided with a smile as he flipped him the bird. The partners slumped themselves into stools at the bar and waited for the bartender, Mickey, to come over. When Mickey saw the two of them at the bar, he immediately began to make his way over. On his way he stopped at the fridge to grab two Miller Lites and a bottle of Jamiesons whiskey. He sat the two beers down in front of the men and then pulled two glasses from under the bar. He sat both of those in front of Miguel and Tommy and began to pour. As Mickey walked away, Tommy and Miguel both offered a nod as their thanks and reached out for the glasses. They clinked the two glasses and knocked back the shots. The harsh liquor burned the whole way down and starting a warming fire in the pits of both of their stomachs. Miguel reached for his beer and took a swig, trying to put out the whiskey fire inside of him. “Can you fucking believe that?” Tommy asked with a shake of his head. “Three weeks we’ve been scouting these guys, three fucking weeks. Just tonight did we actually get somewhere, and now they’re taking us off of it.” “They’re not taking us off of it,” Miguel said, between another sip from his beer. “You heard them, they’re running point and we’re running backup.” “Oh please Miguel,” Tommy answered. “Like you don’t fucking know what that means. That Erwin motherfucker might as well have said hand over all of your information to me and bend the fuck over.” Miguel laughed, slightly louder than he had anticipated. His partner was right of course, but he had been trying to lie to himself. Trying to fool himself. But you couldn’t fool Tommy. Nope, he was too smart for that. “Motherfuckers,” he said. “I say we don’t tell them fuck all. Run the case on our own, we’ve got those plates. We’ve got the pictures, maybe we’ll get lucky, make an ID or something, maybe they got sloppy.” Miguel still held the smile over his face, hoping for a second that his partner was on the verge of something. But deep inside both of them knew it wasn’t true. The FBI was on the case, the god-damned FBI. They weren’t even in the picture anymore. As that realization began to set in over Miguel he called out to the bartender once again. “Hey Mick, fill it up again. In fact, you might as well just leave the fucking bottle.” 6 Nearly all of the lights in the building at 5215 Gaines Street were out. The police headquarters of the Western Division of San Diego was a ghost town. Only one soul seemed to be stirring in the silence. The light in Captain Phillip Toms office was still burning brightly. A stack of files was already beginning to build on the corner of his oak desk. Background files from the FBI, already beginning to take over the space on his desk. He flipped through the pages of the files. Highlighting important information, scanning for new facts, looking for anything of note from the files. The box that Kevin Erwin had dropped off was filled with over forty file folders and Toms was already through nearly half of them. He had to give credit to these guys, the FBI. They sure as hell knew what they were doing. His crew had been checking these guys out for almost a month and the FBI had probably thirty or forty times the amount of information that his crew was able to gather. That bothered him... a lot more than it should have. Erazo and Chase were good cops, two of his best for sure, but why had the FBI been able to attain so much more information about Los Aztecas? Something jumped out at him then, something that he hadn’t noticed in any of the files until now. With a concerned look taking over his face, he reached his hand out cautiously and took the phone handset from its receiver. Without looking away from the file he tucked the phone into the nook between his shoulder and ear and began to dial. Nine digits and nine touch-tones later, the phone was ringing, he sat in silence, still not breaking his stare from the folder in front of him. Finally after the third ring there was a connection and a voice on the other end of the line. “Hello?” “Hey, it’s me,” Toms said. “Tom’s? What the fuck do you want?” “We need to meet,” Toms answered. “What, why? No meeting.” “You’re going to want to hear about this. I’ve got the FBI here,” Toms said emphatically. There was silence on the other end of the line, but it lasted only a few seconds. Finally, the mysterious voice brought life back to the conversation. “Not on the phone. Chicano Park, two hours from now,” the voice instructed. “See you there.” 7 The bar was starting to empty out a little bit more now that it was past midnight. But for all the people who had left, it hadn’t made the bar any quieter. Officers were throwing insults across the bar at each other, a loud group was playing a game of pool that consisted of more yelling accusations of cheating than actual pool playing but most others were just drinking themselves stupid. Miguel and Tommy were in the last category. There was already nearly a case’s worth of empty beer bottles in front of them and apparently Mickey had done as instructed and left the bottle of whiskey in front of them. The whiskey was now nearing half-way gone and neither man looked to be slowing down. “You know we’re going to regret this tomorrow,” Miguel said, trying to fight the slur out of his speech. “Fuck it!” Tommy yelled. “Fuck the FBI and Fuck the Diaz brothers!” Most of the bar’s patrons looked confusedly at Tommy, but Miguel was simply smiling, the reference was not lost on him. His partner had a love for quoting Al Pacino movies, and Scarface just happened to be one of his favourite quotables. “Ah fuck Tommy. We better get out of here, love it or hate it, if we’re not there for that brief by the FBI tomorrow, Toms is going to have our asses.” His partner looked at him and managed his best straight face, before a large smile erupted onto his face. He waved his finger at Miguel and he nodded his head slightly. “You’re right my friend. You always are,” he then turned his attention to the bottle of whiskey in front of him. He grabbed hold of the bottle and tilted his head back. He slammed one last mouthful of the burning liquor down his throat and slammed the bottle back onto the table. He then slammed his hands against the bar and stood up. He stumbled for a second, but was able to catch his balance pretty quickly. He staggered his way out the door, leaving Miguel to pay the tab. Miguel took some bills from his wallet and placed them on the bar, protected by a fortress of their empty beer bottles and shot glasses. Tommy was staring around the streets. His hands were stuffed into his pockets as he scanned the midnight sky. It was quiet around here, a lot quieter than it had been lately. Tommy wondered if this was just the calm before a coming crime-storm and the non-stop noise pollution that a biker war was sure to unleash upon the city. The orchestra of violence that would play this city to sleep every night in the near future. Miguel stepped out from the bar beside him, a smile on his face. They had drunk a lot that night, almost enough to forget about the FBI. Almost... but not quite enough. Miguel stepped into line with Tommy and both of them turned their attention to the unmarked cruiser in front of them. They didn’t say anything for a few seconds, but finally Tommy broke the silence. “Well... rock, paper, scissors seems like the only fair way to figure out who has to drive home.” 8 It was slightly colder than it had been in the past couple of nights. It looked as if the heat wave was finally over. The street was almost completely empty, there were a few vagrants wandering the streets searching for food or change. However, none of them had been brave enough to approach his car. None of them would dare approach a car with San Diego Police Department written across the side of it. Even if it was a Supervisors car. He looked out into the streets, there was nothing here. Nothing but a lot of darkness and warehouses. He wondered if he was getting over his head. There was nothing stopping them from executing him out here. He was so far away, so far from home and without backup. He thought about how easy it would be, but he was hoping that it didn’t make sense to them. He was ripped from his thoughts by the roaring sound of a motorcycle’s engine. He looked into his rear-view and saw the bright halogen lights coming from behind him and approaching fast. The engine roared and cut through the silence of the night, lurching the bike towards the car. The dark black Ducati rolled to a stop beside him and killed its engine. However, the rider never got off the bike. Phillip Toms rolled down the passenger window and stared at the rider. The facial shield of his helmet was heavily tinted, too dark for him to see anything. He preferred it that way, it made him less of a liability in their eyes and more of an asset. “What have you got for us?” the biker asked, his accent was thick, obviously Mexican. Probably another illegal. Fucking aliens were taking over this city, he thought to himself. “Big news. The FBI is in town. They’re heading up an investigation into you guys.” The biker was surprisingly calm. He simply nodded, a gesture barely noticeable with his helmet on. “Is that all?” “No,” Toms continued. “They’re only gathering information right now, but they don’t have much. I’ve seen their files. They’ve got a whole lot of bullshit and not much that will actually stick.” Again the biker offered only a nod. He seemed to pause for a few seconds, perhaps thinking about something. “You will throw them off, of course,” he said, more of a statement than a question. “I can do my best, but they’re going to be getting information from everywhere. I can’t filter it all, that’s impossible.” “You are not paid to do your best, you are paid to yield results,” the biker said, sternly. “But, still this is true. We will need to be more careful. I will pass along the message. And unless you have anything else, I see no reason to delay our meeting any further.” Toms simply shook his head. The biker reached into the inside pocket of his leather jacket and fiddled for something. Toms froze for a split second, before he saw the biker’s hand emerge once again. He tossed the white envelope to Toms through the window. He quickly opened it and glanced inside, the faces of several former presidents looked back at him. His usual fee, there was no need to count it. They weren’t stupid enough to cross him. “Pleasure doing business with you,” Toms said as he rolled up the window. The biker offered only one final nod, before keying his ignition and letting his engine thunder back to life. He hit the clutch and poured on the throttle as the tires burned out and squealed before the bike took off down the street and quickly out of sight. Toms took a final look around, scanning to see that everything had gone quietly, before keying his own ignition and starting the car. The engine of his cruiser came to life and he quickly pulled onto the street and made a U-Turn, heading back towards the Interstate and back towards his city. 9 The two of them were late rolling into the office the next morning. It was nearly ten after eight when they finally hustled into the station and quietly snuck into the meeting room where the FBI team had gathered. Miguel was sure he could still smell booze on one of them, whether it was him or Tommy he wasn’t sure. One thing he was sure of was that his head was throbbing. Even after four Advils and going onto his second cup of coffee, he couldn’t fight the ringing from his head. “Officer Chase, Officer Erazo, so nice of you to join us,” the condescending tone in Erwin’s voice definitely hadn’t faded from the night before. Neither man said anything as they took seats near the back of the room. They caught the stare of Captain Toms, but he didn’t seem overly upset by their tardiness, surprisingly he looked uninterested. It looked like his mind was somewhere else. They also drew glares from some of the other officers, most of whom had seen them getting trashed the night before at O’Grady’s the night before. Perhaps now their sadness from the night before seemed to make a bit more sense. “As I was saying, our current goal is to merely gather intel and information. We are looking for any kind of connections. We’re also looking for known associates and associated businesses. These guys are going to be bringing in a lot of money. According to our sources they’re already into guns and drugs, it won’t be long before they add prostitution and protection money to their list of revenue sources,” Erwin instructed the room. “Now, Agent Magalas will go over the second phase of the operation. A younger agent dressed the same as all of the others with a black business suit stepped forward. This man was slightly smaller in stature than the others, slimmer in both the chest and shoulders, as well as having a soft face. His glasses covered soft brown eyes, although he had the suit that was the only thing in common. But when Miguel saw his position written on the whiteboard in front of him he understood why. Robert Magalas was a technical analyst for the FBI, not a field agent. “Thank you Agent Erwin,” Robert spoke to his boss before addressing the room full of SDPD officers. “I will work with all of the information you gather in step two. We’ll begin to construct a profile about the gang. We’ll identify key businesses, key income sources, perhaps bank accounts, assets, financial information. We’re going to construct as big a picture as we can. The more we can find out, the bigger we can make our target. From that end I’ll work on putting the rest of the puzzle pieces together.” He paused for a few seconds and let that sink in with all of the police officers in the room. It obviously didn’t sit very well, as many of the men realized they had just gotten assigned to scout details. Most of them hated scout work, they’d rather be on the street busting heads and chasing leads than taking photographs and staking out meeting places. There was some murmuring in the crowd before Magalas continued. “There is one important thing that we’d like to stress. From all of the information that we have gathered so far, these guys don’t operate like most biker gangs. Other Outlaw Motorcycle Clubs such as the Satan’s Riders or the Black Hands are based on membership. Once someone is a full member, they act on their own, under the protection of the club. However, Los Aztecas are highly regimented and militarized. There is a strict hierarchy. All orders come from the top and trickle down, they’re structure is more like the modern day Cosa Nostra.” There was a loud murmur now. People were beginning to jump to assumptions. Miguel hoped that the yelling would subside quickly, he wasn’t sure that the pounding in his head was going to get any better with all of the screaming. “Cosa Nostra? The fucking mafia? Are you saying that these guys are Mexican Mafia members?” Vinny Matthews blurted out. At the front of the room, Kevin Erwin was already stepping forward and putting his hands up to silence the questions. “At this time we don’t believe they are connected the Mexican Mafia,” Erwin stated. “However, it is important for you to know that they do act like them, as opposed to a normal Outlaw Motorcycle Club. Also, it indicates that the higher-ranking members of the gang have been at this for a long time, quite possibly starting in Mexico and operating for years now. However, we have received no support from our Mexican neighbours.” The Unit Chief paused before continuing. “Most of you will be involved in helping Robert. He’s one of our best, so the more pieces to the puzzle you can give him the more he’ll have to work with. On the other side of it, will be my field team. We’ll be sending an agent in undercover to try to infiltrate the gang.” Instantly Miguel’s headache seemed to evaporate. His ears perked up and all of his attention was focused forward. An undercover gig was something he’d always wanted. He’d been clamouring to Captain Toms about getting something with a bit more action for a long time. Maybe this was his shot. “This is where SSA Rios is going to come in.” A Hispanic looking man in plain-clothes stepped forward from the side of the room. Miguel hadn’t noticed him slumping in the corner before. Instantly his heart sank and he felt his headache return, this time his head throbbing worse than before. At the front of the room Rios began to speak. “I’m Steve Rios. I’m going to be the one going undercover. We’re already working on establishing a back-story for me. Hopefully with the intel your team gathers, we can make it more believable. These guys are highly secretive, there’s not a lot out there for us to base a profile of what they’re looking for in members off of. So the more you get, the more it’ll help us.” “The target for Agent Rios’ insertion into the gang is roughly three to four weeks,” Erwin stated. “That means you’ve got just less than that to gather enough information for us to get him in there successfully. We’re starting today people. Get out there and press informants for information, chase down some leads, whatever you can. Get me something to work with!” Everyone in the room stood up and made a bit of small talk before heading towards the door. Miguel and Tommy didn’t even bother going anywhere. Both of them were too hung over to be rushing out the door for bullshit work assignments. Besides they already had some information, they could take it easy today. “Fucking bullshit, I told you they were going to take this over,” Tommy said. “Yeah, but it doesn’t matter. At least we’ve got a head start on everyone else today. My head is fucking pounding, I just want to go back to bed,” Miguel answered. Suddenly Captain Toms was walking briskly towards them. His eyes were like laser beams, focused on the two of them, unwavering as he hurried over to them. He didn’t even stop, but his gaze never broke and his words were just loud enough for the two of them to hear. “My office... now!” As he walked out of the meeting room and towards his own private office, the two of them watched him walk away. Their moods instantly went south once again. “Ah fuck. What the hell does he want?” Tommy moaned. “I don’t know. But I don’t think this day can get much worse.” 10 “Close the fucking door, this is important,” Toms said as the two of them entered into his office. Erazo did as he was told and closed the door quickly before he and Chase sat down in front of him. “Listen, I know this is the FBI’s case, but this is bullshit and we all know it,” Captain Toms wasted no time getting to his point. “This may be their case, but we working it first and I think we’re better than them. I’ve seen their files. They have more information than us, but none of it is confirmed. It’s all just speculation and bullshit. Just enough for them to build a case and get permission to come down here and use all of our resources.” The two officers said nothing, this was unlike the Captain. He usually wasn’t the biggest suck up, everyone in the office knew that if he was a bit more political he could have been higher up the ladder by now. But, he was also never one to ruffle feathers, at least not feathers that were above his head. “You’re my best two guys, probably the best two officers in the entire West Side Division,” Toms continued. This was really getting weird, if Toms was rarely ever insubordinate, he was never in his entire life one to offer praise. “As far as I’m concerned, this is still our case. This is still your case.” “What are you saying here Captain?” Tommy asked. “What do you want us to do?” “I don’t want you to do anything. At least not yet. Keep acting like you normally would. Do exactly what Erwin wants you to do. Drum up leads, press informants for information, gather intel, but I want you to give it all to me first and we’ll decide what we’ll give to the FBI.” “Captain, you want us to hold out on the FBI?” Miguel asked. “Do you know how much shit we could get in for that?” “Don’t worry about the fucking FBI. I’ve got them under control.” Miguel and Tommy exchanged curious stares. They were confused, but somewhat impressed by the balls their captain was showing. The two nodded to one another and stood to leave. They walked towards the door, neither of them saying anything. Miguel turned to say something to the Captain, but he had turned his chair and was staring out the window already, Miguel thought better of it, and the two of them left the room in silence.
