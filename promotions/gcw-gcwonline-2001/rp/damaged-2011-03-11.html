Damaged

11 March 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/11/11 Then comes before now. Let us begin. * * * * * Usually he’s never more at home when surrounded by bright lights and an adoring audience, but enduring the GCW Year End Awards Ceremony has been tricky. Andy sits sweating in his suit with his head's thump-thump-thumping intensifying with every heartbeat and a noose-tight tie reducing him to short, shallow breaths. Prickly heat crawls across every inch of skin and his chest feels like it's going to explode. He clenches his fists, grits his teeth, and hopes they don’t let the last award drag. David Yale and James Bryan are on-stage and looking immaculate. A hand pats Andy's shoulder and a voice wishes him “good luck” as the Wrestler of the Year nominees dictated slowly, but his only acknowledgement is a nod. Hurry up, he urges GCW's commentary team. Hurry the hell up. “And the winner is...”. JB's beaming face looks around the packed room once or twice, stirring the anticipation with an invisible spoon. Yale takes the oversized envelope from his broadcast partner and lets his brows rise. “Aww, man. Two years in a row! Congratulations, ANDY MURRAY!” Suddenly everyone's on their feet, wearing Joker smiles. A forced plastic grin stretches across pale Scottish features as Andy rises and shakes hands. Muffled ripples of what he thinks is applause seep through his pounding head as he pushes through the crowds, shaking hands and nodding. His wobbly legs take him to the stage and soon he's gazing out across a sea of thousands for the third time that evening. Bryan hands him his prize, Yale offers his congratulations, and Andy feels his plastic grin segue into a real one. He waits for the noise to die down. One of his ears pops and he can hear again. “Wow, I.” He pauses. Fumbles. Something's growing in the back of his head. Something familiar. Something awful. Not a good time to be lost for words. Andy takes a deep breath. “This is incredible, guys. Thank you so, so much,” he says, trying desperately to force his ship of sincerity through the oncoming storm. “I just wanna say 'thank you' to a couple of peop--”. He stops. The throbbing turns quickly from the pounding of drums to a thousand tiny daggers stabbing at his brain. The pain comes hard and fast; it's strong enough to make him wince. Concerned faces look on from the front few rows, but Murray shakes his haze away. “I just want to say 'thank you',” he says. “Goodnight.” The music plays and he's disappeared without even pausing to hear the applause. James Bryan's eyes follow his exit and he mouths concern to David Yale; for the curtailed speech and the very loud, very audible groan Murray roars as he heads for the back. “Uhh, well guys, it's been one hell of a show,” James Bryan says after turning back to the audience. “Let's hear it one more time for Andy Murray, huh?!” 2,000 people sound their appreciation in unison, but none are sure of what they've just seen. * * * * * ”Andy, I'm just glad you've finally accepted that this problem exists.” Dr. Nick Stearman leaned forward on his forearms, looking at Andy through bronze-rimmed glasses. “I know it sounds cliché, but acceptance really is the first step to recovery. Now we can start thinking out how we're going to make this better.” “I'm just fed-up, Doc,” said Andy. “Fed-up of the migraines, fed-up of not knowing when I'm going to pass out next.” “Have you noticed things improving since switching to a lighter wrestling schedule?” “A little, yeah, but this schedule's killing me.” Andy huffed. “You have any idea how much shit I copped when I told Johnny I couldn’t wrestle last week?” Dr. Stearman nodded his head, eyes-closed. A blonde-locked man of slender build and clean appearances, Stearman been Andy's doctor since his GCW signing in 2007. From broken ribs to lacerations and bruises, Stearman had taken care of every ache and pain incurred by Murray inside a GCW ring. A broken rib had required some attention in 2010, but the Scot's recent troubles had been far and away the biggest challenging the two had encountered since their relationship's start. “It's never easy for me to tell a man he can't work, Andy, especially a man of your profession. We'll have to do a few more scans before we're able to truly root-out the cause of your problems, but I strongly believe your job is having a detrimental effect.” He paused, removing his glasses. “How many times do you get hit on the head during and average night's 'work'?” Andy squirmed, not wanting to answer. “Yeah, yeah. I know.” “The only logical thing I can advise at the moment is that you take a step back. Let us run some tests. Give us a chance to figure out what's causing that. Maybe then we can move you back towards a full-time schedule.” “I can't just stop, Nick,” Andy said bluntly. He put his hands on the doctor's desk and felt his body tense-up. “I'm in the NC-17 main event. Bail on that and I'm finished.” “I know it's difficult, but it might be the smartest option at the moment. I'm sure your more than aware how many premature deaths there are in your line of work.” Dr. Stearman felt the eggshells he'd been treading on smash beneath his feet. “You're 33. You've won titles, awards... you've had a great career, Andy. For your health's sake, maybe it's time to settle down.” “I can't settle down, dammit!” Andy bashes a balled fist against the desk. The shockwaves spill water from the edges of the doctor's glass. “I'm not done, Doc. I can't be done. You have any idea what'll happen to my legacy if I pussy out of this fight with Terror? I'm a wrestler, man. I ain't got nothing else.” Stearman leaned forward. “Andy, we could be talking brain damage here.” “And I don--,” Andy stammered. “I don't. I. Oh, goddammit.” He shook his head and ran a hand down his face, sighing. “I'm sorry Nick, I didn't mean to raise my voice.” “It's alright. Things like this are never easy to deal with.” “'Brain damage' though?” “It sounds extreme, but...” Stearman said to a face that looked like it didn't want to believe what it was about to be told. “It's a possibility, yeah.” “Jesus Christ,” Andy whispered, falling back in his chair. “Jesus fucking Christ.” “The blackouts, the headaches, the numbing of the senses: all could be symptoms. It's too early to confirm it at this stage, but it would be unwise to rule it out completely. Like I said, we'll need to run a few more tests.” He looked at Andy Murray and saw a man he'd never seen before. Gone was the lumbering giant with a personality that filled the room, replaced by a terrified, whimpering shell of a man. “When was the last time you passed out?” “Month or so ago.” “So that's, what, three times in total?” “Yeah.” “Not including your 'dizzy spell' during the Dan Black match.” Silence. Stearman let the ideas coarse through his brain one last time. He cocked his head back and gazed at the ceiling, then looked back and found desperation in Andy's eyes. “Okay.” Nick nodded. “Okay.” He took a pen from the desk and pulled out a cupboard. Soon he was scribbling details onto a headed notepad. “'Okay'?” Andy enquired, eyes widening. “What are you doing?” The doctor said nothing until he'd finished writing. He tore the sheet from the notepad and slid it to the Scot. “This should keep you going until we can get those tests arrangement.” Andy struggled to read the jumbled prescription. “What is it?” “It'll help keep the headaches at bay and it should stop the dizziness. From what I gather, the blackouts you've suffered only seem to occur when you've put yourself in high-stress situations. The medicine will stop that happening too.” Stearman watched Murray's face fill with colour. “There are side effects too though. They'll almost certainly sap your energy and enthusiasm for things. If you start feeling too depressed, lower the dosage or stop it all together. This'll help your smaller ailments greatly, but it's also an incredibly strong depressant.” Andy waved the paper at his doctor. “So if I take these pills, I can work NC-17?” “You should be able to, yes, as long as the side-effects aren’t too pronounced. Just try to avoid as much unnecessary head trauma as possible: we don't want the situation to worsen.” “I'm not sure how feasible that'll be, given my opponent.” Andy slid the paper in his pocket and stood-up. “Thank you, Doc.” He offered a hand and Nick shook it. “Just be careful, Andy. For your own sake.” Unable to make a promise, Andy let the handshake go and turned for the door. “Oh, uhh, one more thing,” Nick said, stopping Andy as he turned the doorknob. “I've only ever prescribed those things to one other professional athlete. And, uhhh--” “Yes?” Dr. Stearman suppressed a sigh. “Well, he had a drugs test while using the medication. He didn't pass. I can't say that there wasn't something else in his system that might've messed with the results, but it's something to keep in mind at least. Be vigilant.” “I will,” Andy said, smiling, as he pulled the door open and left the doctor's office. A spring entered his step as he bobbed along the corner, Stearman's final warning not yet registering. * * * * * James Bryan pulls his earpiece out and walks through the curtains. The applause dies down behind him, replaced by muffled mumbling and the shuffling of feet as the ceremony reaches its conclusion. He slackens his tie and strides through the halls. Bryan's trying not to ignore the good jobs and well dones, but he's not gonna stop. The journey through the building's cavernous recesses takes a good few minutes, but he arrives there eventually. A rich mahogany door, it's surface marked only by a gold star bearing Murray's name. Bryan takes a breath and knocks. He enters after a "come in" and sees the Wrestler of the Year seated before him, surrounded by trophies but not celebrating. "Andy, hi," James says, approaching the dishevelled Scot. Andy's tie is loose, his shirt's top few buttons are undone, and he's already discarded his jacket. Beside him sits an open bottle of pills. "Not what I expected to see, I gotta say. Congratulations." Andy's handshake is weak and clammy. He says "thanks" but it's barely louder than a whisper. "You okay?" "I'm fine." "You don't look it. You just equalled another of LeStatt's records, Andy: wrestler of the year twice in a row." Bryan closes the door. "Where's that million dollar smile, eh?” He grins. He attempts a smile, Andy, but it soon falters. Bryan's facial muscles slacken with Andy's. As Murray dabs sweat from his brow his shirtsleeve, Bryan steps further into the room and pulls open a folding chair. He sets it down opposite the former champion and leans forward. "Alright, what happened out there?" "Nothing," Andy mumbles. "It wasn't 'nothing'. No speech, Andy? Nobody in this company loves addressing his fans more than you, Andy, not even Vivica. Everyone was geared-up for that big, headline-making speech but all you gave us was a couple of words." Reaching into the pocket of his black pants, Andy pulls out a small, folded piece of paper and passes it to Bryan. "You want a speech? There it is." James doesn't bother unfolding the paper, just sets it aside. He tries to look into Murray's eyes, but it's difficult with his eyelids sagging and head lolling. Then he notices the pills again, and his mind begins to wonder. "Does this have anything to do with the blackouts?" A Scottish head pops up like a prairie dog's. "You remember.” "Of course I remember. You don't just forget something like that, especially from a man of your standing." "Have you told anyone?" "No, of course not." A faint shadow of the man he should be, Andy lets a long, warm puff of air escape his oesophagus. A man with titles to his name, awards on his resume, and enough accolades to put him with the industry's greatest, and there he was, in pieces. "I'm fucked, James," Andy says, downtrodden and weary. "I'm fucked." "You're still blacking out?" Andy shakes his head. "Been two months since the last time, but these pills..." He pauses, tapping the bottle. "They keep it at bay, but with them I've barely the energy to lift a finger. Doc said there'd be side effects, but fuck. Can't do anything with these drugs but shuffle 'round the house like a zombie." Andy takes a moment to catch his breath. "I can't do anything on these, man, that's why I didn't take 'em before the show. I wanted to be fresh, I wanted to tell everybody how much this means to me, but the headaches just couldn't leave me be for one bloody night." For James Bryan, a man who'd found empathy and civility even with Jay Terror, it was easy to be sympathetic towards Murray's plight. James had watched Andy's career grow from foundations of honour and morality until he'd bloomed into GCW's brightest light. He'd had a ringside view of every match Andy had every wrestled, of every trial and tribulation he'd face inside that ring, and he'd watched him overcome them all. This latest struggle, away from the cameras, is an entirely different matter. "I guess that explains why we've not seen as much of you lately," says Bryan. "A lot of people have been asking what's up with you." "And what'd you tell them?" "It's not my business to tell them anything, Andy." Andy's appreciation manifests with a nod. "They wanted me to stay away completely, but I can't. Had a pretty bad dizzy spell after Dangerous Games and they wanted me to quit. Told me to just give the whole thing up." "It's that bad?" asks Bryan, voice raising, jaw dropping. "Jesus, Andy. I'm sorry." A silence falls. James Bryan stands and gently taps Murray's shoulder for comfort, but there's no perking-up the giant Scot. What about NC-17, though? What are you going to do?". Andy Murray looks down. Sighs. "I don't know. I just don't know." * * * * * Missing his usual 6am alarm for the second time that week, Andy rose just before ten. After dragging himself from his bed's sweet sanctity he threw some sweats over his tired frame and crawled down to his basement gym. Soon he'd punched his regular HIIT routine into the treadmill's computer. After the second sprint interval he felt his muscles turning to jelly and his lungs struggling to keep up. He reduced the speed. Another interval and, already leaking sweat, he could go no further. It’d been three weeks since he started the medication and Dr. Stearman's warnings were creeping back into his system. He went to the heavy bag, slapped the Everlasts on and threw some jabs. Footwork, head movement: bob and weave, stick and move. Not even a minute into workout and his arms were burning. When he stood back from the bag his arms hung like pendulums, and when he came forward for a second round he barely had enough strength to make the bag wobble. He kept growing, trying to shake away the lethargy. Trying desperately to beat away the worry inside his mind, but it was no use. The gym had defeated him again. Andy loosened his bag gloves and dropped them to the floor. His groggy body laboured up the stairs and he shuffled towards the sofa, feet barely leaving the carpet. Andy dropped his giant frame on the couch and closed his eyes. He remembered a time when his life wasn't ruled by pills, blackouts and headaches, but the memories only made it worse. Stick with the pills and he'd barely have the energy to make it down the ramp, let alone wrestle Jay Terror. Ditch them and God knows how long he'd be able to fight until the mud crept back in. “Brain damage,” Stearman had said. Andy wondered if Joseph Heller was penning his story. "Daaaad," a young voice chimed. Andy's eyes crept open to see Marvin's blurred outline standing before him. "Will you help me with my project now? I really need to get started." "Not right now, son." Andy's eyes closed again. "I don't feel so good." * * * * * In his dreams he's flying. Up from the ring and soaring through the atmosphere, not stopping ‘til he reaches the heavens. Andy's higher than the clouds, but it's not from a drug. It's not from a bottle and it's not from a pack of smokes. It's not from the wealth, the fortune, or the fame. It's from 20,000 people singing his name as one. It's from the fire in his veins as he bursts through the curtain and the joy in his heart as he makes the pin. It's from the dominating victories and the heroic comebacks. It's from knowing he's put everything into his performance, regardless of the result. It's from the gold around his waist. It's from the people who buy his t-shirts and hold his signs up. It's from the smiles on their faces when he signs their autograph book. It's from the game. It's from the love. But every night the ending's the same. The headache forms, the blackout looms, and he throws the pills down his throat. A dark mass clouds his blue sky and spits terrible rain. The fans leave, the crew pack the ring up, and the lights go out. He's back home and the medicine takes hold. Now glory's gone and he's scared. Scared of what'll happen to him if he keeps taking these pills, scared of what'll happen if he doesn't take the pills. Now John Hood's looking up from hell, laughing at what's become of Andy Murray. Now LeStatt Knight scoffs at the man he unwittingly mentored. Now his Father hangs his head with shame. Now Rueben Dallas looks on, aghast. Andy sees his headstone. He sees his son, his brothers, and everyone he's ever called “friend”. Everyone's dressed in black, and none look any older than they are today. “If only he'd just stopped,” one of them says. Then he wakes up in his multi-million dollar home, surrounded by the spoils of one of professional wrestling's most glittering careers, and all he can do is sob into his sodden pillow. * * * * * The ceremony long-finished, Andy found himself on his hotel balcony high above the illuminated city. Knowing his shattered body wouldn't be able to handle the after-party, his celebrations were muted. Just him, the Texas night, and the mini-bar. His bleary gaze drifted through the night. Surrounded by empty bottles, Murray took a swig of Pinot Grigio straight from the battle and swallowed hard. Below him engines roared, heels clicked, and lights twinkled. It was 1am and party time in Dallas, but not for the man who had it all. While his colleagues were out painting the town read, Andy was left alone with his one remaining solace. A vice he'd abandoned ten years prior wrapped its warm arms around him and whispered to him that everything was going to be alright. Mister Jack Daniels made him forget about the pills, and after spending some quality time with Jim Beam he could barely even remember Jay Terror's name. It was a permanent solution, but at least it would get him through the night. So stuck in a haze was Andy that he barely noticed when the balcony door slid open beside him. A bulky, suit-wearing figure stood over him with a twisted mouth and furrowed brows. The saw the empty bottles and the one Andy was swigging from and what he walked into became very clear. “Andy,” he said, but the word drew not even a flinch. “Andy, come on.” He put a hand on the GCW wrestler that was soon swatted away. “Lea'me alone,” Andy growled. He didn't bother to look round. “For fuck's sake, how much have you drunk?”. He looked around. Two half-empty bottles of whiskey and countless Budweisers on the table. He almost tripped over the beer crate on the floor, and an unopened bottle of rum rolled around at Andy's feet. The man took the wine from Andy's hand and wrestled it easily from his grasp. “What the fu--.” Andy's eyes met his brother's. “Shit, Sid.” “Hell are you doing here alone? Could've gone with the party if your plan was to get blackout drunk. Less depressing than this at least.” “Gimme my wine back.” “You told me you were headed back for an early night, and this is what I come back to?” Sid shook his head vigorously. “God's sake, man. What's wrong with you?” “I'm celebratin',” Andy said through a hiccup. “This ain't celebrating.” “What is it then?” “This,” Sid said, picking a bottle from the table and waggling it at his brother, “is a goddamn mess. You're a goddamn mess.” Andy waved him away. “Lea'me alone.” “This sure ain't how a man handles his problems.” Sid watched his brother tilt his head away. He stepped around the table and parked himself inches from Andy's chair. “Listen to me, Andy. For once in your pissing life just listen to me.” “What the fuck do you want me to say, Sid?” Andy snarled. “I got a match with Terror, probably the last shot I'm gonna get at that title, and I--” Andy stopped, hesitating. “I can't do it. Can't fucking do it. My career is fucking over.” “Says the 'Wrestler of the Year'.” “What use is an award if I can't get through a night without flushing pills through my system?” His head clearing and his intoxication sharply lifting, Andy's eyes flashed with anguish. His gaze dropped to the floor. “I can't win this fight, Sid.” “You've beaten him before.” “I was healthy before.” Sid sighed and clenched his teeth, trying to decide if Andy was being difficult for the sake of it. “That's what the pills are for, Andy. To ease the pain.” “You don't know what these things are doing to me.” Andy felt his breaths becoming jagged and laboured. His heart pounded like a bass drum behind his ribcage. “I can't wrestle with this shit in my system.” “So give them up.” “And risk blacking out in the middle of the biggest match of the year?” Andy shook his head. “Don't think so.” “You're worried about a medical suspension? Pathetic.” “I'm worried about my health.” “Bull-fucking-shit.” Sid spat on the floor. “You've gone through worse shit than this. Remember the first big Bagwell match? Doctor's said you'd be a dead man walking if you didn't take a few months out after that one. The war with LeStatt? Fuck it, you kept on going. The tumble from the cage and the slipped disks that followed? As soon as they got you out of the hospital bed you were back in that ring. Fuck the risk, Andy. You're risking your goddamn life every time you step inside that ring, what makes this so different?” Andy's booze-addled mind couldn't formulate the response he wanted. The man who'd pushed him into seeing a doctor in the first place was now the man pushing him back towards the ring. “It's not that simple,” Andy said. “Yes it bloo--” “James Jones.” “Who?” “Failed a drug test in 2009. Hasn't wrestled since.” Confusion twisted across Sid's bearded face. “And?” “He was the last athlete Dr. Stearman prescribed these drugs to.” Andy felt his left arm trembling. “I, uhh. I have a test soon.” “When?” “Two days after NC-17.” Sid's heart sank, his chest deflated. “Fuck,” he grumbled. “Fuck. Fuck. Fuck.” “Never mind the fatigue: if I fight Terror with these things in my system, I'm failing that drugs test. I'm finished in this business.” “Then fucking quit them!”. “It's too much of a risk, Sid. Without 'em, Doc says Terror could do some permanent damage.”Andy paused. “I gotta think about my future. Sure Marv doesn't want a vegetable for a Dad...” “I'm sure he doesn't want a fucking pussy either.” “I'm sorry.” “So that's it?” Sid said, arms held sidewards. “You've given up.” Andy looked to the stars and inhaled slowly, filling his lungs with the cool night air and exhaling pure catharsis. “Yeah,” he said. “I'm sorry, Sid.” “Yeah,” Sid said, nodding. “So am I.” * * * * * Andy wakes from another interrupted sleep without any pretensions of trying to get his body in-gear for NC-17. Instead of heading for the gym, he heads straight for the computer. Typing the document takes a lot less time than he'd imagined, and soon the hard copy is in his hands. He checks it one last time for typos and inks the dotted line at the bottom. He sighs relief. It's almost over. Andy takes the sheet over to the fax machine and sits it in the tray. “So this is it,” he mumbles. “The end.” Quivering fingers hang over the keypad. Almost fifteen years of building one of wrestling's biggest brands and he's about to bring it to an end. He thinks of the good times. He thinks of Vivica and LeStatt, he thinks of Bagwell and Johnny. He thinks of winning Rampage and cleaning-up at the awards. He thinks of breaking Knight's records and silencing every critic. He thinks of his first night in the business and his last. He thinks of the NC-17 main event and what'll happen to it without him. He thinks of his fans, his friends, and his family. He thinks of everybody he's about disappoint and a lump forms in his throat, but he doesn't have a choice. He's reached the end. Andy opens his eyes and starts to dial GCW's number but something startles him. “Hey Dad.” It's Marvin and he's right behind him. Andy catches his breath. “Sorry, I didn't mean to interrupt.” “It's alright son, just startled me is all.” “What are you doing?” “Oh, uhh. Nothing,” Andy says. Embarrassed, not yet ready to face the inevitable and admit his failures to his son, Andy turns his back to the fax machine. “Sleep well?” he asks awkwardly. Marvin nods. “I was just wondering if you had a moment, maybe you could help me with my project.” “I dunno Marv, I--” “Please Dad. I need to have it done by Monday.” Sigh. “Alright, son. Alright.” Andy puts a hand on Marvin's shoulder and leads him away from the fax. “What's it about, anyway?” “You.” * * * * * When Sid Murray got back to Tallahassee the first thing he did was get online. “James Jones” went into Google, but the results were too many and he didn't feel like sifting through a haystack to find a needle. The terms narrowed. “James Jones, wrestler, drug test.” Click. Bang. It was there at the top of the page: Sid didn't even need to click the link. He saw “failed” and “suspended” in the title and matched the medicine's name to that which branded the bottle he'd swiped from Andy's hotel room. “Damn, bro,” he said to himself. “You gotta get off those drugs.” The pills went into Google next. Ten thousand results and none of them cheery. Eventually he clicked on a Wikipedia article and sipped to the side-effects section. Loss of energy, crippling lethargy, diminished enthusiasm, drowsiness, depression, and nausea: a laundry list of grim unpleasantness. Sid put himself in his brother's shoes. He imagined trying to make a living as a pro-wrestler with no energy to train and no will to fight. That's when the gravity of Andy's situation hit him. Rubbing the bridge of his greasy nose, Sid groaned loudly. His brother's voice dripped with despair at the hotel, but he'd written it off to drunkenness. Now he knew the truth. * * * * * “What's this one, Dad?” Marvin points up to a picture hanging from the wall between their bedrooms. A baby-faced, t-shirt-clad Andy Murray stands over a downed opponent, hand held high, belt over his shoulder. “OCW Intercontinental Title. Night I won it.” Andy takes his turn to look at the photo, but turns away when he sees his reflection in the glass. “That was your first belt, right?” Marvin asks, scribbling something on his notepad. “Sure was.” That's it. Nothing else. Marvin's note-taking ceases and he looks at his Father, blinking. “Why don't you like talking about it?” “I do, I'm just tired.” “You're always tired.” Stewing, Andy struggles for a response. Marvin's been aware of the change in his Dad's demeanour for weeks now, but that fax will need to be sent before Andy's ready to tell him the truth. On the day he intended to plunge his wrestling career into the abyss, the last thing Andy wants to do is take his son through history again, but he has no choice but to put on a brave face. “I'm sorry, I'll try to waken up a little,” he says. Marvin smiles and asks his Dad how it felt to touch gold so early in his career. Andy's response comes but there's none of the enthusiasm his son's looking for. The project was going to take more effort than Marvin had thought. Andy takes his son down the corridor and gives him the same bland answers every time they reach a new picture. They get to one showing a sweaty, ring-beaten Andy stood with a well-dressed African American, and Marvin knows to tread lightly. “You and Rueben, he says.” “Yeap.” “We can skip this one if you want.” “It's fine, Marv. Been a while since he passed. I've come to terms with it.” “What should I say about him?” “You knew him: I'm sure you can come up with something.” “Yeah, but this project isn't about me.” Andy's eyes meet Marvin's. He barely has to look down: still only fourteen, Andy's son was a lanky beanpole of a boy. Already up to his 6'7” Dad's shoulder and still growing, Marvin Murray had inherited his Mother's brain but his Father's genetics. “'A good man who demanded the best from himself and worked hard right 'til the very end, no matter the cost'. Something like that'll do.” The sound of pencil scratching on paper fills the air. Marvin looks up briefly. “Just like you, Dad.” He smiles. Andy feels that lump in his throat again. “Come on, I'll show you the rest.” They pass his 2001 “Rookies Night Out” victory, his first World Championship victory, his early days in the AWC and his famous triumph over LeStatt. Eventually they reach a photo of Andy grappling with Jay Terror. Andy doesn't even stop. “What about this one, Dad?” Marvin protests “Forget about that one. Come on, I'll take you through the gym then we'll have some lunch.” * * * * * His living room was illuminated only by the television's flicker, but that's all Sid need. He sat in his modest bungalow with a cigarette between his lips and sitcom he'd seen a thousand times before on the tube, but he wasn't laughing. This was how the elder Murray brother spent his evenings at home. An engineer by trade and an independent wrestler whenever there was a show that would have him, he made the most of the little leisure time he had. Nothing set him up for the next day's work like the comfort of his couch, sweet nicotine, and a cold, sharp whiskey. Something in the background, tap-tap-tap. He leaned forward and muted the volume, and sure enough there it was again. He stood-up, turn the lights on, and cursed whoever was knocking on his door at that time of night before plodding through the hallway. The knocks came again, harder and more impatient. “Alright, alright! Keep your hair on,” Sid said as he slid back the deadbolt and twisted the latch. He readied his best grumpy face, but wasn't ready for what stood on his porch. “Jesus, what are you doing out at this time of night?” “Can I come in?” Marvin asked. * * * * * Andy's basement gym is a room Marvin rarely has a reason to enter. When Dad's in there he knows he's preparing for a match and that he's not to be interrupted, and Marvin's lack of interest in athletic pursuits gave him no reason to use the equipment himself. He'd occasionally used it as a quiet refuge when Uncle Cayle was still living with them, but Cayle was gone now and so was his pounding sound-system. Sunlight still struggles to dribble in through the tiny windows at the top of the walls, and it takes the flick of a switch and a flood of artificial light before he can see clearly. It's the first time he's been down here in months, and on first glance it looks like nothing's changed. An old AWC ring still sits at the back and an elliptical, a bike, a rower, and a treadmill still sit before it. More punchbags than seem necessary hang from the roof and there's a large, matted section for grappling. More pertinently, four replica belts still line the wall beside the refreshment area. OCW, XWA, Just, GCW: every World Title to ever have Andy Murray's name engraved on its surface. “This is the place that makes all those belts possible,” Andy says as he walks down the stairs. He immediately heads for the centre of the room, but Marvin predictably makes a b-line for the belts. Marvin stops and takes time to admire the gold. “Wow,” he says, trying not to gawk. “You've seen 'em before,” Andy says, joining his son. “They sure are pretty though.” Marvin doesn't catch the ruefulness in his Dad's voice. “They are. Can I hold one?” “I left the keys for the cases upstairs I think. I'll get them for you later.” “That's okay,” Marvin says, smiling. “When you've got a new one to add to the wall after NC-17, I'll hold that one.” His smile grows to a grin, but it quickly fades when his father looks back with reddening eyes. Andy wonders if he's ever going to be able to tell his son the truth. Sensing his father's trepidation, Marvin turns and heads for equipment. He passes the cardio machines, and slowly paces towards the bags. His spirit is soon blunted when he reaches them and runs a finger down the side. “Is that dust?” he says, flicking tiny specks from his fingertip. He turns around and Dad's staring a hole through the floor. “Dad?” Andy looks up. “Yeah, I. Uhhh. I haven't been down here in a while.” “'In a while'?”. “Couple weeks. Maybe three.” “But what about NC-17?” Marvin prods, walking towards his son. “Shouldn't you be training for Jay Terror? The show's not even a week away.” Again unable to meet Marvin's eyes, Andy casts his vision on everything but his son. With a hung head and heavy steps he shambles towards the wrestling ring. “Dad?”. Andy hops up onto the side of the room and looks at Marvin. He's ready. “Son, come here,” he says, tapping the apron. “Take a seat.” Marvin puts his notebook down on the ring and slides beside his Dad. He pulls his hands up and finds the ring's just as dusty as the bags. “What's going on, Dad?” he asks. “Why's there so much dust everywhere?” “There's so much dust everywhere because...” Andy stops as his voice drifts away. He takes a breath and runs his hand down his face, taking a few beads of sweat with it. “I don't know if I can go through with this. I don't know if I can do it any more.” Marvin's curious glance only twists an already broken heart. “Dad?”. He prods Andy's arm. “What do you mean, Dad?”. Fishhooks tug at the corners of Andy's mouth. His lips start to tremble and he knows he's losing it. Marvin knows it too and shuffles closer. “Is this anything to do with that time you fell over?” Marvin finally asks. “Or that time you went to the hospital last month?” One look at his son is all it'll take to set Andy off. He reaches deep into his drug-slowed body and looks for the inner strength that's carried him through all his life's struggles, but comes back empty-handed. All he can do is nod. “I thought you were better now?”. Andy shakes his head. “But the doctor gave you medicine. Isn't it working?” He shakes his head again. “Oh, Dad.” Andy hears something in Marvin's voice that compels him to slide from the ring's edge and make for the stairs. “I'm sorry I couldn't help with the project, son.” He darts up the stairs and leaves his son alone in the gymnasium. The door's slam resonates loudly around the concrete walls, and Marvin prays that he's doing the right thing. * * * * * ”Here you go kid.” Marvin gratefully took the warm cocoa and held the steaming cup close to his chest. “Thanks,” he said. “Drink up quickly and I'll give you a lift home. Your Dad's probably worried sick.” “Nah, he's sleeping.” Marvin raised the cup to his lips and tried to sip, but the heat made him recoil as soon as it nipped his senses. “Besides, it's the other way around.” One of Sid's thick eyebrows raised. “Come again?” “He's not worried about me: I'm worried about him.” Sid sat down on his favourite armchair and pressed the remote to kill the TV. He wondered if Marvin knew what he knew. “You're not the only one.” “All he does is sleep and watch TV.” Marvin sighed. “He's never in the gym, he's never up when I go to school, and when I get back he just sits on the couch and says nothing. He barely even cooks for us anymore.” “You know he's been sick lately. He's taken a few nasty falls; those kinda things'll scare the life out of you.” Sid paused to wonder if his façade was working. “He's probably just taking some time to realign is all. I'm sure he'll be right as rain soon enough.” Marvin's shoulders shrugged. “I don't think so. I can't remember we talked about wrestling or school or anything. I've got to do this project for English clash. I thought it might cheer him up if I did it on his career, but he keeps saying he's too tired to help me.” His nephew's face was a sea of sadness and confusion, but Sid remained stoic. “Your pop's going through a phase, Marv. It ain't easy for him to talk about his career at the moment.” “But why? He's gonna beat Terror and be a champion again, but he's just so depressed.” He paused. “I don't understand, Sidney. What's wrong with my Dad?” Sid took a deliberately long drag from his cigarette and tapped the end against a Coors Lite ashtray. Telling the truth may not have been the smartest thing to do, but he couldn't like to the kid. “Do you know he's on medication?” Sid asked, readjusting his posture and straightening up. “Yeah, but I thought it was supposed to make him better? Even after he passed-out he was still smiling. I haven't seen him smile since the doctor gave him those tablets.” “So what do you think's making him depressed?” It took Marvin's teenage mind a few moments to deduce Sid's meaning. “The tablets.” Sid nodded slowly. “No way. Medicine is supposed to help you, not make you worse.” “Everything has a side-effect though,” Sid explained. “Remember that time your little doggy hurt is leg and the vet gave you and Andy some medicine to mix with his food?” “Yeah?” “Remember how Andy hardly let him in the house until he'd finished the dosage?” Marvin smiled, his mind turning to happier thoughts. “Yeah, poor little guy. That medicine sure made him stink though.” He paused. “Hey, wait a minute.” “Get it now?” His tone turned melancholy. “Yeah, I think I do.” “Those are some powerful drugs your Dad's on, Marvin.” Sid leaned forward, letting the cancer stick burn down between his fingers. “Strong as hell in fact. They'll stop the blackouts and kill the headaches, but there's a reason they're not widely prescribed. He's depressed because the drugs are throttling him: they're killing his buzz, sapping the life outta him. Not everyone who takes them experiences such a negative response, but your Dad. Well, I ain't ever seen him like this, even when Rueben died.” “Oh God,” said Marvin's sad mask. “I guess they're making him better, but is he gonna be able to wrestle? Is he gonna fight Terror?” “Not if he stays on those pills.” “Why not?” “You've seen him. Guy can barely stand for a minute before he needs to take a seat. The drugs are killing him, kid.” “We've got to help him.” “We've got to get him off those drugs.” “Won't that make him sick again?” Sid put the cigarette between his lips again. “That's a chance we have to take. Your Dad's finished if he doesn't wrestle this match. Steven Caldera won't tolerate another pull-out, especially from the NC-17 main event.” Marvin took a few seconds to soak everything in. “So what do we do?” “You said you were doing a project?” Marvin nodded. “I've got a plan,” said Sid. * * * * * Andy had the portrait commissioned over a year ago, but it's taken him until now to fully appreciate its beauty. Just him and his son side-by-side, smiling broadly before a smudged grey backdrop. Marvin in a smart plum jumper, and Andy in his best white shirt with the GCW World Title strapped around his waist: his whole entire world in one photograph. There's a tin of beer in his hand but it's unopened. He won't need it tonight. Andy looks through his son's eyes and deep into his soul. It takes him back to when he was 19 and Jane told him she was pregnant. He sees Marvin gargling his first “daddy” and riding his bicycle without stabilisers for the first time. He sees him performing in his school's nativity play and he sees himself beaming with pride as he takes home is first “Best in Class” certificate. He sees Marvin standing beside him when Jane left him, and he seems him in the front throw as he strides down the GCW ramp on one of the biggest nights of his career. He sees the only thing that's remained constant in his last fourteen years on Earth, then he feels that fire in his veins again. Andy dashes up the stairs. He passes the photos and everything's clear again. The OCW title, the Terror battle, him and Rueben: every picture is like a cold bucket of water, and when he sees his reflection in the last one he just smiles. He sprints downstairs and soon he's in the gym. Not even waiting for the lights to flicker on, Andy slides into the ring and hops to his feet. His eyes close and the music hits. He's in the NC-17 ring, Jay Terror's at his feet, and the fans are screaming their throats raw. Murray feels a passion he hasn't felt for months, and he's not even there yet. Andy gets carried away. He throws a few quick combos, fakes a superkick, and soon his limbs are tiring. Grim reality rears its ugly head, but he's distracted before it kills his buzz. Marvin's notepad sitting by the ring's edge. Andy leans over and picks it up, before flicking his way to the pad's first page. The title hits him immediately. My Hero: My Dad, Andy Murray. GCW Superstar. Soon he's back upstairs and at the fax machine. He takes his GCW resignation letter from the tray and tears it into a million tiny pieces that he throws over his shoulder. The rest of the mud in his head sifts away and Andy smiles, silently thanking his son for this moment of clarity. * * * * * ”You think this'll work?” “Maybe,” Sid said, “maybe not. But it's the only shot we've got, kid. You know what you're doing?” “Yeah, it's just...” Marvin pauses to sigh. “I don't know. What if it doesn't work?” “Then at least we know we gave it a shot.” Sid stubbed his cigarette out in the ashtray and rose to his feet. He sat down beside his nephew. “Your Dad needs your help now more than ever, kid. Nobody else is gonna get him out of this funk. If he doesn't wrestle this match then he'll be miserable forever, you understand? There's a risk, yeah, but you don't want him to be like this forever, do you.” “Of course not.” “Well then, let's give it a shot. Talk to him in the morning, and if he bites just go for it. He might wrestle Terror, lose, and be sad for a while, but if he doesn't take this opportunity he's gonna regret it for the rest of his life.” “You really think it'd end his career?” “There's no doubt about it. Caldera's one of the most powerful men on this side of the equator: if he blackballs you, nobody else is gonna touch you with a barge pole.” Trying to lighten the situation, Sid cracked a smile. “Can you imagine your Dad working in Wal-Mart?” A short-lived laugh escaped Marvin's mouth. “Heh, don't be silly.” “You gotta do this for him, kid,” Sid said, solemn tone return. “You're the most important thing in the world to him. He'll listen to you.” “I've asked him to help me with the project three times and he's said 'no' every time.” “Tomorrow'll be different. You keep asking him until he hasn't got a choice, you hear me?” “I don't know...” “Marvin,” Sid said, grabbing Marvin's shoulders, “listen to me. This is the lowest moment in Andy's career, and only you have the power to help him. Understand? Don't do it for me, don't do it for yourself. Do it for Andy. Do it for your Dad.” “Okay.” Marvin's head nodded several times. “Okay then, I'll do it.” Relief crept through Sid's head, cooling his angst immediately. “Good lad,” he said, “and make sure he sees this.” Sid took Marvin's notebook and pointed to his project's title. “Nothing speaks to a man quite like the realisation that he's hurting those he treasures most.” * * * * * He's standing on the edge and everything's cold. A light burns through the darkness and it's not too far away, but he'll never reach it if he doesn't take the plunge. The bottle rattles in his trembling hand. Andy clasps the lid with his other hand and twists. It takes his body's every once of strength to dislodge it, but eventually the lid comes loose and hits the tiled floor with a clatter. Andy holds the pills over the edge and tries to tip. Something stops him. The mud's creeping back in. Doubt starts to overcome him and he remembers what it feels like to wake-up from a blackout. He sees his limp body being carried out of the ring on a stretcher, and he says Jay Terror's face and the GCW World Championship hoisted in the ai-- Something clamps around his wrist. He looks down and sees four skinny fingers and the thumb, then looks sideways and sees the light's reflection in Marvin's glasses. “It's alright Dad,” his son says. “It's okay.” “How did you know?” Andy tries to ask, but the words don't come before he feels his wrist twist. Pills fall one-by-one into the toilet water until his jittery hand has shaken the bottle empty. Without thinking he pulls the flush and within seconds his poison's gone. Andy stumbles backwards, quivering, but when his son throws his arms around him he knows he's done the right thing. A single tear creeps down Andy's cheek. “Thank you,” he whispers. * * * * * They stay up through the night and watch the world come alive. The sun slowly creeps over the horizon until all is bright again. Tweets and chirps fill the air. Colour returns to the garden and a honeybee buzzes harmlessly by. Winter's dying days end with warmth and sunshine. Spring has arrived. It's gone 8am when Andy checks his watch. He cradles his sleeping son in his arms and carries him back inside, up the stairs, and into his bedroom. Andy watches Marvin's nestle among the sheets and leaves him in peace, his smile the perfect placebo for his brewing headache.
