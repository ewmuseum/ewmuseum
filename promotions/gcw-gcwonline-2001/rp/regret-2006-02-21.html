Regret

21 February 2006

Date: 02/21/2006 It was impossible to be stealthy when you were certain the entire world could hear your heart thudding like a jackhammer against your ribs. For what seemed like the fortieth time in the last few minutes, Dawn checked the floor under her matte black boots to make sure she wasn’t disturbing any loose piece of rock or gravel. Virgil and Alex had faded into mere shadows ahead of her, picking their way quietly and confidently over the terrain with an ease that belied their lack of familiarity. She swallowed hard, trying to get some moisture into the aridness that had enveloped her mouth, cracking her lips and making her tongue feel dry and swollen. The sound of her swallowing seemed to echo down the corridor, carrying far ahead of the sneaking trio and making Dawn wince at her own clumsiness. The two figures ahead gave little recognition of the sound, as though they hadn’t heard anything at all. With a start she realized she had fallen behind, forcing herself to pick up the pace as much as she could in an almost crab like crouch. The lacquered scabbard at her left side slapped quietly against her thigh as she moved, seeming to have wriggled around in it’s bindings until it had gotten loose of its own accord. She readjusted the grip on the firearm she had been given, the unfamiliar weight of the pistol having turned out to be much heavier then she had been lead to believe. Letting go with one hand she wiped the sweat off her palm, trying to keep a solid grip on the weapon that would be her lifeline if things went sour. “What are you doing”!” Alex hissed at her suddenly, having seemed to appear like magic from the dim tunnels ahead. “You’re lagging behind!” Clamping her mouth down on a startled yelp, Dawn managed a stifled grunt in its place. “I have to be slow if I’m going to be quiet right” This isn’t exactly my forte.” Alex peered at her searchingly, the dim light of the tunnel making the dark paint on her face look like gaping holes in her pretty visage. “Did you want to go back” We’re not too far ahead, you could?” “No.” The response was flat, and carried an air of finality. “I’m not going back’I’ll try to hurry up.” “Fine. Walk where I’ve walked, you won’t have to worry about stepping on any loose footing that way.” She patted Dawn lightly on the shoulder and turned back around, slipping forward with her submachine gun raised to her shoulder once again. Gritting her teeth Dawn followed behind, holding her pistol pointed at the ceiling to avoid any accidents while walking. Virgil was still in view but only barely, his lithe form crouched near the mouth of an intersection and surveying the available choices of direction. Alex slipped up next to him and they exchanged a flurry of hissed words before seeming to decide on the left most passage. Beckoning Dawn to follow, Alex set off after Virgil. Forcing herself to pick up the pace a little more, Dawn tried to close the gap between herself and her friends. Sweat beaded in the small of her back, slipping down between her shoulder blades and running down the length of her spine. Though large enough to accommodate a medium sized vehicle, the dark stone tunnels seemed immeasurably tight and confining to her. Just the thought of thousands of tons of rock between herself and the light of day made her stomach lurch, and filled her with the desire to run back towards the entrance they’d found, falling on the grass and staring up at the sky. Shaking herself to free the thought, Dawn reached up with one hand to swipe away the sweat collecting on her brow’and paused as a shadow moved across the ceiling of the intersection. Her throat constricted suddenly and for a moment the act of breathing was a force of will, the cold fear that snaked around her heart freezing her body’s basic functions with it’s chill. She opened her mouth to speak, twice unable to force words out’then? “Virgil?” She hissed, trying to pitch her voice as low as possible. The report of gunfire was her reply, the tunnel lighting up suddenly with the flash of muzzle flare as the sound roared over her. Fighting the desire to clap her hands over her ears she ran towards the sound, easily picking out Alex’s crouched by the light of the guns report. Dark shapes swarmed in the darkness, hissing and cursing in a language she didn’t understand, the sound echoing down the tunnels until it seemed like it came from all sides. She ran towards Alex, fear quickening her steps. As the other girl turned her eyes widened, and expression Dawn had learned to interpret from years in the ring. Without warning she threw herself prone, rocks digging into her knees and forearms as she hit the tunnel floor hard. A sound like angry insects whipped over her head and there was the sound of tearing meat, and a howled scream of pain. Rolling onto her back Dawn fired blindly into the dark, her own vision clouded by the afterimage of the muzzle flare from her pistol. This close it sounded impossibly loud and the muscles of her arms protested at the sudden and violent recoil from the weapon she was holding. Scrambling back she fired one handed, pulling herself along the ground until her back touched a warm body, Alex’s familiar voice piercing the roar of gunfire. “Dawn! Are you alright”!” “Fine!” The response was laughable, but she couldn’t think of anything else to say. There was a dry cough from her weapon and the top most slide locked backwards. She had just enough time to think “empty” before Alex bellowed in her ear, “RELOAD!” Her hands moved of their own accord, flicking the release switch and dropping the metal clip to the ground below. It took two tries to get the smooth sided reload fitted into the handle, but as the hammer locker forward Dawn felt a surge of relief. As long as she had this weapon, she would be alright. The light from combined gunfire threw strange shadows on the walls, twisting and distorting shapes and silhouettes into demonic versions of themselves. She knew Virgil was out in the dark somewhere, his superior strength and speed of most use when he was close enough to come to grips with his opponents. Dawn did not envy any who fell in his path. She could not see her attackers, the dim light punctuated only long enough to cast a bright afterimage onto her eyes, but she knew they were out there. Howling they attacked in small groups, the vast majority cut down by barking chatter from Alex’s weapon, Dawn’s fire doing little more then to deter or force them to seek cover. When a tall shape appeared at her elbow Dawn spun in a panic, only to have her aim spoiled by a strong hand on her arm. “It’s me.” Was Virgil’s only comment before he turned to Alex, “We’ll be flanked before long, we need to move deeper in.” She nodded, gesturing the two of them ahead as she backed into the chosen tunnel, her weapon bucking and howling in her grip. Dawn turned to follow Virgil, her eyes meeting his for a brief moment as they widened in surprise. He struck her hard in the shoulder, flinging her to one side as something hard and bright passed through the area she’d occupied mere seconds before. Blood flew in the dark and he grunted in irritation, a quick gaze assuring him that Dawn was still alive. From her place on the floor she stared at him for a long moment, the impact of what had just happened racing through her mind. “Y-you saved my life.” She stammered. “Later.” Was the clipped reply as he dove back into the dark, his limbs a blur of blood and violence. Something moved along the ceiling and she froze, her eyes locked into the misshapen orbs that stared back at her. Pale flesh ringed a set of almost insectile eyes, set above a gaping black hole filled with small, needle like teeth. It screamed and dropped from the ceiling, claws extended, and she rolled onto her back, drawing her sword left handed and cutting desperately up towards it. Something thick and warm splashed onto her face as a heavy weight hit her chest, she opened her mouth to scream but found it full of the same substance, muffling her voice? ************************************************************************** “and she jerked upright, slapping her hand to her mouth to muffle a horrified scream, the hellish sounds of her fight in the tunnel still ringing in her ears. Her tank top clung like a second skin to her naked body, drenched so heavily with sweat that it might as well have been submerged. Biting hard into the palm of her hand she tried to force her body to quiet, attempting to stop the shaking of her broad shoulders through force of will alone. Alone in the darkness of her guest suite, Dawn wished fervently for the comforting familiarity of the McFarlane estate. She wrapped her damp comforter around her shivering body, holding it tightly as though she could squeeze the thoughts that plagued her dreams right out of her mind. There were no tears, no sobs nor anything that even approached the depression Dawn had battled with for months’instead there was something more insidious, a deeper feeling that had not often plagued the elder McFarlane. Fear. Out of instinct her hand went to her right ear, locating the silver stud there and stroking her thumb across it. Since Lia had given her the earring for her birthday, it had become a point of strength and solidarity to Dawn. As she stroked the smooth surface she imagined she could draw on the other woman’s strength, pull on some of the endless reservoir of endurance that Lia possessed. Letting her eyes slip shut she took a deep breath in through her nose before it shuddered out her mouth, quickly chased by another and another’and the trembling began to ease, the shaking of her body subsiding as she brought her whirling mind under control. “Alex said it would be like this?” Dawn murmured, removing her hand from her mouth to gaze in displeasure at the teeth marks there. Since emerging from the tunnels, Alex and Virgil had kept a close eye on Dawn, watching her for the signs of strain that came from the first time’first’time she killed. The thought brought a wave of nausea flooding over Dawn and she scrambled to the adjoining bathroom, clutching the sides of the bowl as she heaved her guts into the tepid water. Releasing the sides of the bowl she sagged against the hardwood walls, sucking in breaths of air greedily. Holding her hands before her face she glowered at them, willing the shaking to subside. “I do not regret?” She whispered, reaching for the familiarity of her Master’s Doctrine. ??regret is a sin. I do not regret, regret is a sin.” She repeated the mantra over and over, letting the familiar words soothe her mind and quiet her body once again. Pushing off the floor she wobbled to the sink, rinsing the bile from her mouth and spitting it down the drain. She paused for a moment, examining her pale face in the mirror. “Bah.” She grunted, turning the cold tap on. “I could wake the dead with a face like that.” A few splashes of freezing water satisfied her, and she rubbed the droplets from her face before shucking the soaked tank top into the dirty clothes hamper. Though it wasn’t home, the guest suites in the Mifune School had all the amenities she was used to and then some. Her panties followed the tank top and she strode into the bedroom baked, stretching her arms over her head and rolling her shoulders. A fresh tank top replaced the first, followed quickly by jogging pants and a pair of comfortable sneakers. A black elastic served to tie her unruly hair back from her face in a small foxtail, and her red training wraps wound slowly around her fists and forearms until they were settled snugly into place. The door to the hallway opened with nary a whisper and slid shut again with the same lack of noise. Dawn slipped quietly down the hallway, easing out into the adjoining corridor with as little noise as possible. Lia and Virgil might be awake somewhere on the grounds, but she didn’t want to wake any of the slumbering students any sooner then she had to. The familiar turns melted away until she entered one of the schools gyms, shutting the door behind her and flicking the lights on. Rows of exercise machines stood against the far wall, interspersed by small stations to hold towels or water. A large ring dominated the majority of the room, but it wasn’t the ring she was looking to get into this time. Crossing the padded floor she came to a stop before the punching bag, nodding to herself as her hands came up into a comfortable guard. The first punch sounded like a gunshot in the quiet of the gym? ********************************************************************* “and Dawn wasn’t even sure she had pulled the trigger. The menacing figure before her staggered heavily to the side, caught totally unprepared by the blow from his side. Blood and bone had sprayed against the far wall, painting an eerie picture of the effectiveness of Dawn’s attack And still he did not fall. He turned that predatory smile on her, his fangs glinting like spears in the candlelight of his haven. The hole in the side of his head seemed a small matter to him, and if the smile on his face were any indication, he seemed to find the entire thing rather amusing. “Well hello little morsel.” His voice was deep and soft, like the pleased purring of a jungle cat, but with an undercurrent of horrid menace that made Dawn’s blood run cold. “I don’t believe we’ve been introduced.” Wasn’t this where you snapped off a witty one liner? Some kind of cheerful banter that took the edge out of the situation? As if in defiance of that thought, Dawn’s throat seized again, barely letting air slip past the fear tightened muscles. She did the only thing she could do. She shot him again. BLAM! This time the bullet caught him high in the shoulder, forcing him a step back with a grunt. The gun was a blurry outline, the metal sights along the top wavering as she tried to sight her opponent. Alexis had given her a crash course before coming into the caverns, but Dawn was far from an expert. BLAM! He staggered again, this one closer to his center of mass. “Don’t aim the gun, point it.” Alex’s voice came back to Dawn, her calm instruction seeming to float out of the air. BLAM! “Don’t pull the trigger, squeeze it.” BLAM! “Don’t shoot him’kill him.” BLAM!!!!! Blood and grey matter hit the wall behind him as he fell; the report of that final shot seeming louder then any Dawn had fired before. Her shoulders heaved as she gasped for breath, her arms ached from holding the gun steady. “Are you quite finished?” Whatever blood still flowed through Dawn’s veins turned to water at the sound of his voice, her knees going weak as he rose unsteadily to his feet. The hole in his forehead was gone, replaced by the smooth, unblemished skin that had been there previously. “Because I do believe it’s my turn now.” Instinct kicked in and she rolled to the side, narrowly avoiding a blurred figure that hit the wall she’d been standing near with such force that it shattered into a thousand pieces. Like a log she rolled to one side, the ground she had previously occupied suffering the same fate as the wall. Blindly she lashed out with one leg, striking something solid and tripping it off balance to send it careening into the wall. “Get up.” She thought, willing her thoughts to where Virgil lay prone. “GET UP!” Dawn came to one knee, pressing the trigger as fast as she could manage, the gun jerking and jumping in her grip. “Missed me.” Came the smooth voice as the hammer locked back, “Now I get to kiss you.” A heavy weight hit her side and something gave a painful snapping sensation that reverberated through her whole body. The impact against the wall didn’t help matters any, a searing pain burning through her right side. Her back had barely hit the wall before he was atop her, a smashing blow thundering home into her unprotected stomach. The wind whistled out of her lungs as he drove what felt like a truck into the side of her face, flinging her now limp body across the room. From her place on the floor she stared desperately at Virgil, each breath triggering a painful rattle. “Get up?” she whimpered. “He can’t hear you my dear, of that I assure you.” The figure was right on top of her again, seizing her by the back of her collar to drag her roughly up. For the second time in the night she drew the sword she’d been given with a left handed strike, arcing the razor steel through skin and bone. She hit the floor next to his severed hand, delighted to hear the roar of agony from his lips. Another blow hit her side and lifted her straight into the air, careening into the far wall with only slightly less force then before. She raised her face from pool forming on the floor, tasting salt and copper as she did. Vital fluids. She could feel her arms and legs, but it was becoming increasingly hard to make them respond. The dawning pain was horrifying, like a thousand metal spike piercing out of her bones through her skin. Am I dying? A firm hand wrapped around her throat, raising her high into the air until her brand new boots just brushed against the floor. “Yes my dear, I’m afraid you are.” Dawn had not realized she’d spoken out loud, but that wasn’t what concerned her. Behind her attacker a figure stirred, hands and knees slowly finding a place to push himself up. She squeezed her eyes shut, not wanting to see the final blow coming. “Please god Virgil’get up.” ******************************************************************************** She sagged to the ground at last, her energy expended. Breaths came in ragged gasps, each intake of breath feeling like the scraping of sandpaper against her delicate lungs. One hand rested on the punching bag still, feeling the multitude of dents and dings she had inflicted on it. Dawn had no idea how long she’d struck at the bag, fueled by the feelings of helplessness and despair she remember from the tunnels? ??but I’ll never’never’be that way’again.” She gasped, shoulders heaving. There was a creaking sound above her, and she looked up in shock as the chain that held the bag finally gave out and sent it crashing to the floor in front of her. The sheer force of her attack had weakened the metal until it had given away completely, no longer able to support the weight of its passenger. Dawn stared in amazement at her hands, the voice of her Master slipping back to her. “With each defeat comes an invaluable lesson. Only by learning our limits, do we learn how to surpass them. Do not regret, that only dulls the importance of the lesson.” She flexed her hands, as if feeling them for the first time, staring at the rough skin around her knuckles. “I do not regret.” She whispered. “Regret is a sin.”
