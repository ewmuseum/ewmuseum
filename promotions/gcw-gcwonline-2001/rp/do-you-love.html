Can you still feel that last goodbye?" ---- Christmas Eve. Back at the airport, feeling the ache in my chest. Place is named after George Bush, and I wonder- will they name anything after me? Doubtful. Lies, and loss, breaking me in half. Still haven't slept, and I feel ragged. Hollow man, running on the afterburn fumes of adrenaline. I can fuel myself with the self-directed anger. Plastic title belt on the plastic seat beside me, fucking insulting. Seems to me that I've been through too much- too much pain, too much cynicism. All at someone else's expense. Pain makes the world a lonely place, when that's all you're trying to survive. Nights behind the tree line, living like a mechanical animal, going through the motions. Trying not to feel the hurt. I vow to myself that I will make this count, even though I'm walking away. Waiting for the boarding call, feeling irritable and brittle. If you touch me right now, I will shatter. A million fucked up pieces, twitching on the floor. Pulling the longest all-nighter ever and all I want right now is to crash out beside someone who cares. Touch skin; see her face before I close my eyes. Keeping in touch all these years, and now I want nothing more than to call her, despite the hour. I know she'll be asleep. Christmas is tomorrow, and all I want for that holiday, is a million miles away. How have I fallen this hard, this fast, without ever noticing? Shake my head, and try to figure that out. Can't. I have a lot of things on my mind right now, trying to rip me in half. I rage against that, rail inside. I want to hear her voice telling me not to worry, that it will be fine. I know that won't happen, because she's not here. I confide in no one- spending time in my own solitude prison. Whatever. Feel the control slipping, and I want to lash out. I want to freak right the fuck out, and destroy this waiting area. Rip the bolted down chairs from the floor and pitch them through the plate glass windows. The image is so vivid I spend a moment entertaining it, seeing all there is to see. Breaking glass. Screaming people. Blood on my hands. Smell my own stink. Fucking crazy psycho tearing up the joint. Can you imagine? I'm feeling too much right now. Sun glares through that tinted glass, and stings my eyes behind the dark shades. I can feel the heat on my shoulders. It's not a nice feeling. Soon the sun will shine right through me. Hollow and transparent. Pure. Simple. Burn me up like a candle. Immolation. And then... nothing. ---- FLASHBACK-- Friday, November 18, 2005 1630 hours, local time Chicago, Illinois Serpentine hiss of tires on wet asphalt filled his ears, blotting out the racing thoughts. "I have to get there before Eastwood tells her," he thought to himself, knuckles white on the wheel. Dashed yellow lines strobed past in his headlights, rolling to the left under the car, dividing the asphalt into neat little lanes. The buildings flashed by in the darkness, blurred purgatory grey. It was already getting dark. Early afternoon, and the torrents of icewater from the sky echoed his current state of mind. Hopeless desperation. Color bled out of the day as he slammed on the brakes at the intersection, cursing the lapse in concentration. The plane tickets were on the dashboard, a reminder of the need to be at O'Hare in the morning for that flight out of this place. Regrets and second thoughts were all he had as he reached down, snapping on the radio with a shaking hand. White Zombie exploded out of the speakers, obliterating thought: " . . . eye for an eye and a tooth for the truth . . ." "Damn right," he muttered, raking a hand through his disheveled hair. Truth. That was something he needed right now, especially in the face of this new turn of events. He was flying to Denver, and he'd be wrestling his first match in PCW on Monday, against a guy who called himself Bobby Digital. Daunting prospect, no, not really. He was more than ready. He had been for more than three months, but he'd managed to fake a lingering twinge in his knees that kept him here- because of her. "Fuck." He growled, shaking his head as he reached for his cigarettes inside his battered leather jacket. Slipping one between his lips, he jerked the wheel, swinging to the curb outside Windy City Promotions. He twisted the key, silencing the growl of the Torino's high-toned engine. The world appeared to melt beyond the silvered glass, the basalt buildings blending in with the darkness. For a moment he lingered there, feeling numb as he lit the Camel between his lips, taking a few deep drags off it. A dark blur emerged from the doorway as he watched, the amorphous figure pausing there in the rain. Without the benefit of a clear view, he still knew it was her, and as he opened the door and stepped out onto the road, he knew that she'd already heard the news. He flicked the cigarette away, oblivious to the rain as he approached her. She was crying, and he could tell, despite her attempt to masquerade it with the rain pelting down on her beautiful face. "You're going to get a cold," he said softly, his lips curving into a smile he only had for her as she lowered her head, looking at him. "Doubtful," she replied. "When was the last time you saw me get sick?" The cheeky grin on her lips refused to reach her eyes. No, they were sad. He knew then that she knew, and that she was going to miss him. She bent her head, and that dark curtain of hair blocked the view of her wounded eyes. He smirked at her, putting on that other persona with ease as he flashed that that devil may care smile. "Yeah, that's because you're too stubborn to ever get sick. What are you doing out here, pudding?" He wanted her to say something that would take the pressure off him, something that would make sense of the sadness he saw in those beautiful blue eyes. Instead, she lied. The words were flat and false, but he nodded as she said them. "Too hot in there. Been sweating my ass off. You know how it is. Your stuff is in Eastwood's office." Nodding her head towards the door, she tore her eyes away from his, staring into the darkness of the street. "Tell her," he thought to himself, "tell her how you feel." His hands clenched into fists, and he said nothing, chewing on the inside of his cheek. "Georgie...." He trailed off, a million emotions in that one word. He looked at her with that blank poker face, but his eyes were filled with pain. "You're healed up, this wasn't permanent, and you were already signed." She forced a smile, looking back up at him. "I'm happy for you." She meant the words, as much as it hurt to say them ? she meant each one. He could tell, and for that he was grateful. He was thirty-four, and she was twenty-one. The thought of a relationship was almost scandalous, but that didn't stop his head from pounding, and his stomach from aching as he looked at her. "You gonna be all right with the assholes around here still?" A wry smile curved his lips, as he looked down at her, stepping closer as the rain, driven by the shifting wind, pelted at them. "The biggest asshole is leaving." Both shared a laugh at that, as Jackson moved in close, putting his strong arms around her and hugging her to him. The embrace lasted longer than a friendly one should have, and neither cared as they ignored the rain for the moment. "I'm going to keep in touch." He vowed, something in his words ringing honest truth as he whispered them. "You better." She turned her head up, looking into his eyes, wishing that time could just stand still in that moment. "Jackson, I...." Before she could even blurt out the words the gym door opened, and three men left. They parted almost immediately, both looking slightly sheepish as they waved to the trio of men. "Fucking chicken," he muttered to himself, the sound lost under the mournful howl of the wind as it pushed her against him. "Come on, you need to go get your stuff." Georgie finally said after the men left, effectively dismissing him. Just like that, the moment was gone, never to be given back. The words that had been on the tip of his tongue choked him, and for a moment he just looked at her, weighing saying it regardless. After today, he may never see her again. He stared a moment longer, and then bowed his head in assent as she reached for the door. They stepped inside, and Jackson took hold of her arm, halting her progress before she could move out of that darkened alcove. "Promise me, no matter what, you won't give up on this dream of yours." "I won't." She replied immediately, sticking her tongue out at him. "I don't know the meaning of the word." He pulled out a card from his wallet, and flipped it over, stealing the pen from the bulletin board behind him to scrawl a number on the back. "My cell number. I'll give you my email too, and that's the same as my MSN. I mean it, Georgie. I'm not? going to pull a vanishing act on you." He pressed the card into her hand, and watched while she pocketed it before turning away. Before she could leave, she turned, looking back over her shoulder at his hangdog expression. Closing the gap, she pulled the pen from his grasp, and took his hand in hers, scrawling a number across his palm. It was a quick goodbye, and the silence, although comfortable, was ominous. Neither one spoke what was really on their mind, and as Jackson left Eastwood's office, she was nowhere to be seen. Probably just as well. He would have never made it out of there intact if he'd seen her. The walk to the door was the longest ever, but he endured the catcalls and congratulations with a grim smile, and his usual stoicism. ---- I lost to Shaman. The thought rattles around, meaning nothing. I lost. Who gives a shit? Plane lands, but we can't get off. They have to de-ice it first, before we can taxi in. Time to think, as I look out that little window at O'Hare. I can still remember the first time I saw her, like a wrathful angel, beating the hell out of a guy twice her size. Her long hair flying, moving in a blur, like something out of an action movie. Charlie's Angels or something, but this was no Cameron Diaz or Lucy Liu. Nah, this chick was the real deal- not some plastic Hollywood skank. I asked Eastwood what her name was, and got nothing more than a grunt by way of reply. Wave of the hand, and the Chicago asshole version of "these aren't the droids you're looking for". Nobody knew, but watching her fly across the ring with that effortless grace, and exuberance struck a chord with me that resonates to this day. She was the most beautiful thing I'd ever seen, and as I made my way past that ring, our eyes locked. For a split second it was like time stood still, I know it's clich?, but I swear it's true. Every eye in that place was on me, the lame horse, come to get himself back in shape. It was October 2004, and I could barely walk without a cocktail of painkillers, limping along and trying to avoid the looks. Across the room, someone called my name. I turned to look, and when I looked back at the ring, she was gone. Oh I know, you think this is some romance movie filler. She rocked my world, and tore me to pieces. For months she was the air I breathed. At first, I hated her for making me feel this way, for making me want to spend every second in her presence. I waited around after training, when my body was screaming for rest, just so I could walk her home. I timed it, like some fucked up high school crush so I'd walk out the same time as her. I took her to Denny's down the block so many times they knew our names. They must have thought I was her father then. She looked so fucking young to me. That fresh-faced kid, hanging on my every word. Fuck age, I wanted to spend every second with that girl. One look at that genuine smile that lit up her whole face, and I was spellbound. I wasn't prepared for these emotions, I hadn't felt this before. Want. Need. Love. It scared the hell outta me. I wanted to run away, and leave her behind, but I couldn't. I hated her for finding her arms around me when I woke up that night I had way too much to drink, and she played the sensible one. I remember the feel of her in my arms when I carried her up the steps of that brownstone the night she puked all over the side of my car. Too much coffee, and too much greasy food. I loved her for being that real with me. I hated myself even more for liking it. She was my everything, and she never knew. I don't know what she thought of me as we progressed beyond a year as friends. I never asked, because I was happy with imagining everything in that look she gave me. Every night she stepped into that ring, I watched like a hawk, biting my nails, hoping she didn't get hurt. It's funny, how little things can matter so much in retrospect. A touch, a glance, and the way she chewed on her lip when she was thinking about something. The way she looked at me, when she thought I wasn't looking. Those were the things that hurt. Like a knife twisting in my insides. Those were the things I felt the most when that plane lifted off. Sadness and longing. I wrote her a letter to keep myself from falling apart. I've kept it in my wallet for more than two years. Sometimes it's good to feel that depth of emotion. The hollow ache of denial. It prepares you for life, somehow. It's not always pain. Sometimes you catch a break. Not always, but there's enough of a silver lining in that sometimes, that I'm willing to gamble on it. ---- FLASHBACK-- Monday, December 24, 2007 1915 hours, local time Chicago, Illinois Sun slipping below the horizon, it lit the low-hanging snow clouds on fire. He could feel the burn in his knees, that telltale ache. The storm was seconds from breaking, but he didn't much care. Silence stretched out along the deserted street as he pushed off from the black Torino, parked at the curb. One last drag of the cigarette accompanied the bite of the cold wintry air before he flicked it away into the quickening wind. "Here goes nothing," he muttered, shaking his head slowly. It had been more than a year since he'd crashed one of the Windy City Promotions shows, and surprised her. It was easy to pretend he wasn't getting so damned old on the phone, and on the piss poor quality webcam. He raked a hand through his windblown hair, wishing he'd taken the time to get a few hours of sleep before doing this. Still wearing last night's clothes, and reeking of sweat, he wasn't really something to behold. Settling the Cubs ballcap over the gray-shot, sweaty hair, he looked almost presentable. Maybe- if she'd suddenly gone blind. He groaned softly, and hung his head. Groping in his back pocket, he pulled out the mangled pack of Camels, only to find it empty. "Fuck." He growled, reaching into the pocket of his jacket, fishing out a pack of gum. He unwrapped a stick, and slipped it between his teeth, wincing at the tenderness in his jaw as he bit down on it. He always had gum when he was flying, anything to keep his ears from popping. After today's glorious aerial adventures, though, he could recommend another way. Spend the majority of the flight in the broom closet they called a bathroom, hugging that stainless steel bus. Puking and then dry heaving. Works every time. Resolutely, he moved towards the steps of the familiar brownstone, seeing the light spilling through the windows as dusk fell around him. The first of the snowflakes fell, sticking to his face, and the blue hat before he reached the shelter of the porch. Seven steps from the sidewalk to the door, but they seemed endless. Exhausted, he lifted his fist, and knocked on the door. Three loud raps that vibrated up his arm. "Shoulda called first," he thought wildly. What if she wasn't home? The fear was unfounded, however, because a moment later the door opened and he was bathed in that warm, cheery glow. Blinking up at him, she seemed almost shocked to see him standing there. "Brad?" The wind whipped snowflakes around them, and her hair tickled his face as she looked up at him. "What are you doing here? I thought you had-" "Merry Christmas, Georgie," he said softly, looking into her eyes. "I don't have much, since I just? walked out on GCW, but? I wanted to give you something." Homecoming never felt so good. His hands pressed against the smooth plane of her back, feeling the heat of her skin beneath the cotton shirt. She smiled up at him, those sapphire eyes dancing with exhilaration as her hands pressed against his chest. "I can't believe you're here!" She said softly, tilting her head back to study him. Tears filled his eyes as he looked at her, seeing the snowflakes in her hair. "I want you to know how much you've meant to me these past few years. I treasured every email, every message from you. She could read everything in that intense gaze of his, and it was very nearly her undoing.
