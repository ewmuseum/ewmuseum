Date: 04/27/11 Present Dial tone... "How's it going Tony, it's William?" "Yeah, there's a thing called caller-ID." "Good for you... So, what's up?" "Same 'ol stuff, you know the deal." "Yeah, I hear you. How's Susie?" "Eh, the usual. But honestly, she's been bugging me to the point of suicide, William, I need to get away from her." "She's your wife, how do you expect to do that?" "Murder." "Yeah, we'll see how that one goes over ...Tony, how's about me and you take a trip up and over to Oregon?" "What the hell is in Oregon?" "The mountains, the trees---you said you wanted to get away." "Yeah, but only if there's gonna be hot, single females?" "You wish, you don't have it in you anyway, you're all talk. You're just upset because she's been blue balling you all week long. "Whatever." "So, yeah, what do you think, Oregon that is?" "I think...why were you already randomly planning on going to Oregon. That's fuckin' weird, man." "My Dad was from Oregon, and I was going to visit him, his gravestone I should say. So yeah, real fuckin' weird of me, huh!?" "Shit, sorry man. I didn't know." "It's fine, just answer the question, do you want to go?" "Yeah, but I need some details, you know. I just can't up and leave." "Don't worry, I'll keep in touch... Bye." Click. Past "Honey, are you sure 'bout this," She asked in a heavy Chicago dialect as she stroked the young teenage boys golden hair from his crisp blue eyes. "Yeah, Mom. I know you guys got problems but he's still my dad." His response was felt by the mother, who actually looked nothing like him. But, she couldn't be mistaken for anything else, because her compassion and love ran deep for her child. The two sat closely on a brown leather coach which was positioned at the center of what appeared to be a small living room. That particular living quarter was assembled together with knick-knacks and pictures with a TV and coffee table off in a corner. The pictures mostly consisted of the teenage boy, and none with anyone other than the two individuals currently present. "I know he is, Willy. I just--I'm gonna miss you!" She was barely able to let it out without shedding a tear, which of course usually meant more than a single tear. He shifted his mouth in a look of discomfort, and possible guilt, but he was quick to reassure his mom. "Don't worry, Mom, please don't cry. It's one summer, I promise you that. You know I love ya, and I still need you more than anyone or anything else. But, I need to get out there with him, you know? I'm 15 now, almost 16. I need to get away from the busy streets and get out to the wilderness, and see what his work is all about. I've been cooped up too long, and my wings are ready to soar free for a summer." She wiped her eyes with the sleeve of her blouse and slowly kissed the boys cheek. She got to her feet slowly and at that, the two said their goodbyes. ***** The smell of burning fuel filled the air and it made the boy wince as he walked next to an older fellow. The loud screams of two-cycle engines echoed intensely in every direction and it was becoming difficult for the boy to hear anything but the ruckus. The man walked up to a white pickup truck and opened the door. He stepped up and into the cab, reaching with his arm for something on the passenger side. When he emerged from the truck he had in his hand a hard hat and hearing protection. He approached the boy sternly and handed him the hard hat, speaking a few words as he did so. "You're gonna need this...follow me." The boy took the hard hat and sort of inspected it loosely and away from his body. As the man was about to lead the way, he hesitated. "Yeah, you see, it goes on your head." "Yeah, I know that." the boy responded, looking back at his butch father with a grin. He placed the hard hat on his head and adjusted it while on the move, as he was already lagging behind a bit. He got within distance of his father and casually followed the man's footsteps, looking beyond his frame to see a rather large piece of equipment. "What's that." He asked, looking up at the big, yellow machine as they approached. His father turned back and hollered, "That's what we call a Yoader, now c'mon Will--an' hurry along, would ya?" William rolled his eyes but did what his father said and followed him closely. The amount of conifers was breath taking, and the smell of pine needles was almost fragrant enough to overpower the smell of burning fumes of gas and bar oil mixed with freshly cut wood, which almost had a consistency of burnt popcorn when processed through ones nostrils. William studied his father as they went along, almost as if he'd never seen the man before. It wouldn't be half off to say it, but the fact remained that it had been nearly five years since the two last came in contact physically. He examined the stains that seemed endless as they scattered themselves about in an almost artistic fashion all along the exterior of his pale blue wrangler jeans. His flannel shirt was tucked in gracefully, but a section in the front stuck out sorely, and William had noticed it earlier but had just resurfaced the image at that moment, almost admiring it. William pulled his attention back to the job site and his surroundings, taking in the beautiful scenery and trying to adjust to the loud array of sounds, mostly chainsaws and the deep bellowing roars from the diesel monsters parading about over the saturated land. The job site in itself was at the very top of a rather large foothill located a bit west of center within the beautiful state of Oregon. This was exactly what he needed, he thought to himself, and in return he felt compelled to share his feelings. "I'm happy to be here, just so you know." He said with intent as he eyeballed his father's movements and watched as he slowly halted his progress to a stop. "I know you are--ya wouldn't be here if you weren't. Don't you think for a minute that I'm not happy that you're here either, cause damn, William, it's been too long. But, we got work to do, c'mon I want to show you something." William nodded his head, "Right." And at that, the two continued to make way towards what appeared to be the end of the road. The muddy, tire track-infested road was getting thicker and harder to move through, but the elder of the two kept his pace steady, leaving the younger to lag behind with each slipping step. It wasn't crucial that they be anywhere soon, but time was money around these parts and that was learned very quickly. William was wise enough to know this, and from what he knew from stories, he knew that his father wanted nothing else but this life, and the only thing keeping the two apart was a certain female. The marriage was a disaster, and shortly after moving to Chicago for a woman, Jack Curr was no longer the happy man he was before he met that brunette at the Tavern. She was in college, and he was younger then. He had no idea that logging kept him ticking, but he found out soon enough. And so it was William's time to embrace his father's true love, and he too would understand why Chicago amounted to nowhere near the wealth that glistened in the state of Oregon. After finally making it through the muddiest section of road, William found himself standing at the bed of another truck. The tail gate was down and on it sat a rather large Chainsaw, but William's eye's took a glimpse further into the bed to discover a saw that seemed to fancy him more, and its size was most likely the primary factor, as it was at least half the size of the saw being inspected by Jack. William resumed attention on his father, who used a funny looking tool that appeared to have both a wrench and a screwdriver all in one. "What's that," the boy questioned, looking on as Jack flipped the tool around and began using its flat-head screw driver side to tighten the chain to the bar. "It's called a scrench. It's about all you need when working with a saw, that and a file. You see, if you get in a jam out here, you gotta be able to fix the equipment on the spot, in the elements, you know. See that saw there," he pointed to the saw that William had already been eyeing, "Go ahead, grab it." William grinned a bit with excitement as he walked over to the side of the truck and reached over the wall of the bed. After feeling around and peeking over, he finally grasped the overhand grip of the saw and raised it up and out of the truck, looking at it in awe. It was heavier then what he expected, and he couldn't even imagine what it'd be like to run the thing. He almost didn't want to know. "Kinda heavy," he said softy, but his father picked up on it. "Nah, try this guy out for an hour and you'll be all about that one real quick." He said, pointing at the saw in his sons hand. William looked at the saw and admired its orange glow as the sun bolted down rays of light which intensified the object as a whole. "C'mon over, place it up here," Jack said slowly, slapping his large, leathery hand onto the tail gate. His hand clanked against the metal and the scrench popped into the air like a kernel of popcorn and quickly rattled itself back down onto the tailgate. William walked around and over to his father, looking up at him before setting the saw down where his father had directed him to place it. He lifted the saw up and onto the tail gate. "M'kay, now what I want'cha to do is take this here scrench and open those two caps," he requested as he motioned his hands to the areas of interest. "Yep, that one right there," he claimed with certainty as he watched William bring the screw driver end of the scrench to the first cap, which was larger and had a gasoline can and an oil droplet pressed onto the surface of the cap. William knew enough to realize that this was where the gas went, but other than that he was unfamiliar with any other aspect of gas-powered equipment. Maybe if Oregon had been where William was raised he would have already known much about this sort of thing. But, he was raised in the big city, and with only a mother to look up to, the opportunity's never presented themselves as they now did. His view towards his father never faded, and actually being with him at this point was certainly more than he could have ever wished for. Jack wanted his son to know this stuff, and at that point it still wasn't too late, for William, at the age of 15 was still early enough to kick start an interest in the business. The last thing he cared about was keeping the boy away from his mother, as one could probably guess why. "OK, now for this one you're gonna put a mixture of gas and oil, and this other one over here will be filled with bar oil." "Bar oil?" he asked, looking up at his father. "Yeah, it keeps the chain nice and lubed which in return reduces the friction. This saw's runnin' up to 3,500 RPMs and that's fast. Gotta keep everything lubed so it doesn't break." William nodded his head and sort of understood. Either way, he knew it needed to be done. He reached over for the red canister which he knew was the fuel, and slowly tipped it towards the opening on the saw. "Whoa, whoa, whoa, watch it. That's where the bar oil goes..." William looked up before pouring and cringed with anger. He didn't want to look stupid in front of his father but he shook it off and proceeded to fill the correct opening with the 2-cycle gasoline. He took his time and was careful not to spill, and he was pleased when he filled the tank nearly full without spilling a lick. "Alright, now grab that orange jug right there and fill the other tank up. Careful now, that stuffs like syrup, real messy stuff. Just pour nice and easy like you just did with the gas...go on." William proceed to fill the tank with the very coagulated, stringy syrup like mixture and almost overfilled it but luckily stopped in the nick of time. "Alright, I think it should be good to go. Get your caps screwed back on and we'll be set." "So, what are we doing?" William asked as he tightened the caps. "You'll see," his father replied, turning around and beginning a trek down a large hill side. William grabbed the saw and watched as his father began the descent. William approached the edge and peered down to see endless amounts of trees, which many had been scattered about on the forest floor. Men worked below and with the "skyline" which was a cable that ran through the skies and to a large crane device for attaching and pulling logs up to the top where they were limbed and stacked. His father wasn't stopping for him, so he quickly began the walk down, trying not to slip and fall which was beginning to feel like a real possibility. "Wait up," he called, but his father kept going...and so he continued down, deep into the thicket of unknown, inching closer with each sketchy step made. He thought to himself as he went, keeping an eye on his father while looking for his next step. He went up and over fallen trees and passed other people working nearby, who simply tipped their heads as he passed by with the chainsaw at his side. Before long he had to switch hands because his arm wasn't use to lugging around that kind of weight, not to mention the walk down the slope was brutal in itself. In his mind he began to imagine what was in store. He tried to eliminate the use of the saw he wielded, yet he could foresee no other opportunities around it. He had prepped the saw for a reason , and that reason was to use it, and the thought speared into his gut like a bayonet exploding through the skin of an unsuspecting victim. He had to overcome the butterflies beginning to flutter restlessly within him, yet the more he tried the worse it became. He was starting to regret it all, and who could blame him? He wasn't use to all of this, and at no other point during his life had he ever been put before a task as daunting as the one he stared off with now. His father was looking up to the sky, and for a brief moment the thoughts began to loosely break away from his tormented consciousness. "Take a look, she's a ready to fall." He shouted loudly, looking back towards his son who was trudging down the last remaining fall of the slope. "This one's yours, son... hurry on over." William processed what his father had spoken and it sent chills through his spine and his throat went dry like a drought, not a word was expelled from his jittering mouth. Present Dial tone...
