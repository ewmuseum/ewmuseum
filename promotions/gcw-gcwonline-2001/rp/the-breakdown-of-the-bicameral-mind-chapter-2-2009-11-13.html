The Breakdown of the Bicameral Mind: Chapter 2

13 November 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 13th, 2009 I was always damaged goods. It was hard not to be when one goes through what people like me have gone through. In order to get into this business in the first place you must have some fatal flaw. This is a relentless business. I tried hard to survive it. I did my best. In the end though, I was just like all those people I claimed to be better than. Now it was up to me to figure myself out. To understand myself. If there is one thing you have when stuck in a mental institution is time to think. Most of the times there is nothing left to do but to look at the other lost souls in that place and to hope that you don’t end up forever in there. I feared being in that place evermore. It tugged at the core of my being that I had to find a way out of there. Those yellow stained white walls were closing in on me quickly. Thoughts of ending it all were not uncommon, in fact, they were probably the norm. There wasn’t much I could do with my arms tied around my own body behind my back. After the doctor told me that I was hallucinating and seeing people, I didn’t believe him. I wonder if he is truly right. I don’t know what’s real or what’s false anymore. Did I ever know? I’ve tried looking back at my life and to understand what happened to me, but all I can recall is the day I met her. The day I met Death. She was there. She must have been. There is no way that she wasn’t. --- A few years ago… He had been walking through the streets of that desolate place for years. Every step he took a day of his life was lost. All he needed to survive was his bottle of scotch and his thoughts. More so the scotch than anything else. He stumbled through the empty streets in search of everything and nothing. With the moon as his guardian, he walked and walked some more until he could feel his feet no more. Antonio sat down on a slightly rotted bench as he toasted to the night. His fiancée had just left him after years together. She had left him a note. She hadn’t even bothered to tell him to his face. He was wounded. The moon didn’t judge him, though. She understood what he was going through. She had seen many a man and woman broken by the opposite sex. Out of the corner of his eye, he saw a small poster stuck to a light post that didn’t work. It was a small advertisement for some place called Oblivion. The Diner on the Outskirts of Time. He felt like a child of the city. Like every nook and cranny had embraced his very being and was swallowing him whole. “I guess something other than alcohol in my system wouldn’t be so bad for me,” he said to himself as he began to walk towards where the little poster told him. He saw the place as he rounded the corner of White’s Ferry. It was as nondescript a place as you could ever find. He saw no lights inside or anything, but the sign outside claimed the place was open. He went inside. The place was dank and wretched. It was in a deserted corner of Beale Street in one of the most run down places in town. A red moon had followed Antonio’s every movement into that little establishment that locals liked to call Oblivion. As soon as Antonio went inside, he could immediately tell why. The world seemed to be living and breathing independently from that place. Time appeared to have stopped completely as soon as Antonio stepped inside. He walked towards an empty table and quietly sat down with his thoughts. There were quite a few patrons and they all seemed to be out of towners. Then again, who in that hell hole of a town wasn’t or wished he was from far away. A man in the table right across from him gave Antonio a cross look. It was pretty obvious that Antonio was out of place, but he had hoped no one would notice the fact that he was human. There was a deep growling sound as his neighbor disapproved of Antonio being there. Antonio ignored the growling and never looked away from the eyes of his opponent. He could hear the whispering all around him as they all waited for what they would do. Before anything could start, the waitress arrived at Antonio’s table. How he could tell that it was a she would probably surprise anyone who heard this tale. Antonio ordered some coffee and was responded to with a series of clicks and with antennae moving around excitedly. That is when he saw her for the first time. There was an audible gasp from the patrons as they all stared, dumbfounded at the door. The only thing Antonio heard was the sound of the door as it opened and closed, announcing that someone was there. With his back turned to the door, he waited patiently while the rest of the place seemed to have been vacated abruptly. The thing that sat in the table in front of him had stopped growling and it stared intently, almost fearfully, at the new patron. For the rest of his life, he would never forget the sound of her voice as she ordered some orange juice. Antonio felt like his own life hung by a thread as each syllable left her mouth. And it did, he just didn’t know it yet. He watched as everyone’s eyes followed the woman who had just arrived. She moved in into Antonio’s periphery but he still only saw a shadow. Antonio had decided to act as if he didn’t notice her. He wasn’t there to meet anyone. He was there to drown his sorrows with coffee and scotch. “Let’s see, which table should I choose?” she asked herself, almost aloof. She continued to eye all the tables and the patrons sitting in them until she found the one she wanted. Antonio never saw it coming, but she sat right in front of him. “You’ve never been here before, right?” Antonio looked at her for the very first time. That was all he needed. “So, what brings you here to Oblivion? I don’t know if you’ve been told already, but you seem really out of place. What’s your story?” she asked with a good natured smile as she took a drink of her orange juice. Antonio didn’t really know what to do at first. He saw that all eyes were on him and everyone was holding their collective breath, waiting for what would happen. They all obviously knew her. He was the only person in that whole place who had no idea who had just sat with him. “I don’t have a story. I’m story less,” he answered. “Well that’s just too sad to be true. We have to do something about that!” Her unusual chipper attitude was something Antonio had not really expected from someone that was apparently feared and respected. “But before we get out of here, I have work to do.” She took one last drink of her orange juice and stood up. She gave him an innocent grin and turned towards the creature that had growled at Antonio. “You knew this time would eventually come, Asterion. Not even hiding here in Oblivion can save you from your fate. No one is beyond my reach,” she told him almost apologetically. The Minotaur gave a growl of disappointment and not long after his head bounced on the plate of meat on the table in front of him. The legendary Minotaur of Crete had finally fallen. “My work is done. Let’s go,” she told a dumbfounded Antonio as he stared at the fallen creature. Everyone else had gone back to whatever it was they were hiding from in Oblivion once they knew that Death was not there to give them their final notice. Death grabbed Antonio by the hand and literally dragged him away. “Let’s go find you a story.” --- You try and try but with your arms tied behind your back, there is no way to reach to your head to scratch those memories from your brain. Fingernails digging into flesh and entering the brain matter as the search for your memories or your hallucinations takes you deep within your mind. Your head snaps back and forth into the white padding of the walls. There is no pain, even though the constant pounding has opened a laceration in your head. You feel the blood dripping through your face and you can see the red stain on the sea of white in front of you. Stumbling to your feet, you see everything as if through a crimson mask. The world around you has finally turned blood red. The way you wanted it. The memories keep reminding you of things that might or might not have happened. You start to pace around the room, counting the drip, drip, drips of blood as they fall from your chin and make a trail. As if in a trance, your pupils dilate and look like jet black orbs. Your mind keeps playing tricks on you, or is it? You keep pacing around your room, leaving a red trail all around the walls and the roof and to the other wall. The room seems like a white treadmill when you leave your footprints behind for some unlucky schmuck to clean up. The sound of the door finally awakens you from your trance. It’s that time of the day. --- “How are you feeling?” There was no answer. Antonio didn’t think that the question deserved an answer. He was being held prisoner in a place full of crazy folks for attacking his ex-fiancée and to top all of that up, he had lost track of reality. He was just peachy. The mere sight of the doctor’s hateful grin was enough to make him feel sick to his stomach. The doctor passed his hand through his greasy black hair that was starting to show some shades of gray, even though he wasn’t really that old. The doctor was tapping his pen on the notepad he was holding to the rhythm of some song he had inside his head. It was probably something Alex Foley would have in the background. Antonio felt like killing himself at that moment. Or killing Dr. Matheson. He wasn’t sure which urge was stronger. He hung his head and looked at the floor. There wasn’t much he could do but to space out and hear the music inside his head. “Don’t worry, I know that you are going through some really drastic changes in your life, but I’m here to help you. The medication we have been giving you will take the edge off for a while, but in the mean time, I need to assess your situation, see where you are,” he told Antonio, a hint of irritation showing on his features. He had gotten nowhere the last few weeks. Besides the big break in proving to his patient that he was hallucinating, he hadn’t gotten anything out of him since then. He had become less violent with the staff and more violent with himself. Dr. Matheson didn’t know if it was his consciousness punishing itself or a way to take his head off his problems and into pain. Dr. Matheson was lucky that a case like this one had fallen on his lap. Ever since he was young, he was a very motivated man. His goals were lofty, and while he was probably not the brightest or the hardest worker, he was ruthless. The doctor had decided that this was the case that would make him famous. The plans were already made, now he had to get Antonio to talk. He was having no such luck on that day. After a few minutes of awkward silence, Antonio spoke for the first time since the day he had supposedly spoken to her. “What is wrong with me?” His voice was slurred but he was still able to form a coherent sentence. The cocktail of painkillers and anti-depressants was playing tricks with him and he just couldn’t help but know that he was just screwed up. Damaged. “You are showing signs of schizophrenia. It seems to be that in order to protect your mind from all the punishment it has had to take all through your life, decided to put up a wall that is disrupting your normal brain functions. If you talk to me about your past, maybe I can fix you,” he said, scribbling furiously on his pad. Antonio never looked up. What was he supposed to do? He would spill his guts out and then suddenly the buffoon standing in front of him would diagnose him and help him? If there was something he had always learned, it was to be careful. He tried to stand up to leave his session. He wasn’t telling his life story to anyone. As soon as the doctor saw him standing up, he motioned with his hand to the big burly guy that worked as an orderly in the mental institution. Without hesitation, the big guys grabbed Antonio’s shoulder and shoved him back down into his chair. Antonio was too drowsy and with his arms tied behind his back with a straightjacket, there was little he could do to defend himself. “Fine, Mr. Vargas. If you want to do this the hard way, I have no other choice but to oblige your wish,” he said as he stood up and gave his back to his patient, throwing his notepad on top of his desk in exasperation. Antonio looked up with matted red hair as huge hands were clasped around his head and lifted him up. “I have something that will loosen your tongue. Soon, you will be singing my tune, Mr. Vargas,” the doctor said with a malevolent grin as Antonio was dragged out of the office and into another room. Antonio tried to assess his surroundings. He had trained himself to always be aware of his situation and that skill had helped him out of tough situations, but the drugs in his system really didn’t allow him to do anything. He was brought next to a ten feet cylindrical metal shaped object. It looked like some kind of space shuttle. Before long, a needle was sticking out of his arm and his clothes were off. The last thing he remembered was the feeling of drowning of sinking deep into the depths of the ocean. He would never be the same again. --- I finally woke up from my slumber that day. The man that you see before you is not the one that has been misrepresenting himself through all these years. This is the real deal. The real Antonio Vargas del Valle. The real Man in Black. I remember that day as if it was only yesterday. It was the day I shed away my skin. The day I realized who I was. I still remember what happened clearly. The experience was mind bending. As I was put inside the tub of liquid, I floated. The liquid in inside my ear and the drugs inside my system had messed up my sense of time and direction. I was one with the darkness. I was floating in death. They also put some loud music to mess with my head. As if my head wasn’t already completely messed up. They were more like harsh sounds than music, but I began to see it all, even in the darkness. I could hear the colors and see the noise as they coursed through my numb body. I tried to speak but no sounds would come out. I felt blind, but I was seeing and I felt deaf but I was hearing. I was everything and nothing at the same time. I was dead. That was the second time I saw her.
