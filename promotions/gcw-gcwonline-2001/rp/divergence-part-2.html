Date: It took a long ass time The place stank, desperation, stale cigarettes, cheap burned coffee. Even as he stood outside the aroma wafted into the hallway through the open doors and made him feel sick. The people inside, they milled about, talking to each other, some shaking hands. Others sat by themselves, head down, alone, lost in their own little worlds. Yeah, he was in the right place, no matter how much he didn’t want to admit it. He wasn’t like these people, he couldn’t be. Not Michael Stevens there was no way he could have possibly fallen so far. Even if he had, he could easily pull himself up right? He’d always been able to before, no matter how far down in the dumps he’d fallen he could always be trusted to dig himself out of the hole. This time he wasn’t so sure, things had become so bad, maybe he’d finally hit rock bottom. This was supposed to be the way out. He’d heard so many stories, people coming to places like this, bringing meaning back to their lives. All things considered it was probably just what he needed. Yet, for some reason he couldn’t bring himself to step through those doors, it was as if some invisible force was blocking his passage. Or it could just be his ego was too damn big. Walking these doors would mean that he’d finally come across a problem in his life that he couldn’t solve for himself. Worse yet he’d be admitting to the world that he needed help, that was something a Stevens never did. He supposed he got most of that stupid pride from his father. It was one the reasons the two could barely stand each other. When he was a kid his father had been constantly working, not really absent just distant. As an adult the rift had grown even wider, maybe if he wasn’t so proud he could have told his father how much he needed him back in those early days. But Stevens men just couldn’t show that kind of weakness, it was simply unacceptable. Of course neither was drinking like a fish just to be able to get out of bed. Whether he’d admit it or not he had a problem that needed to be solved. And right now he wasn’t going to be able to do it alone. He watched them pass, watched them chat, maybe just watching them tell the stories, could that be enough help for him to break out of the cycle? Probably not, but it was the best he was going to get. These people were losers they drank because they didn’t like their lives, maybe daddy hit them. They didn’t know about true pain, the kind of things Stevens had gone through over the last couple months. Even if he told them, even if he explained they wouldn’t understand, couldn’t understand. Meh, he was probably wasting his time here anyway. These people couldn’t even help themselves, how in the hell then could they possibly help him? The plain and simple fact was, they couldn’t and he was wasting his time here. Lighting a cigarette he turned, ready to leave. After taking several steps he turned once again. Maybe he should go back, after all it couldn’t hurt to attend just one meeting right? Quickly he walked back to the door, and once again turned, head down, heading out. This is wasn’t going to be worth it, he couldn’t do it. Never happen. “Hey wait.” The voice from behind caused him to stop cold. He should just keep walking, ignore the voice. There was a bar right down the road, he’d get smashed and forget he was even here. Yeah that’d work, just keep walking, have a drink, forget, do it all over again tomorrow. God he was sick of it. Slowly he turned around to face the voice. It belonged to an older gentleman, maybe in his late forties. The guy wore flannel looked beat down by the world around him. Yet he still had a smile on his face, one that just barely made it’s way to his eyes completely surrounded by worry lines. “Coming in?” “I don’t think..” “Listen man, it’s all up to you buddy. Just let me say though, I’ve been where you’re at. Hung outside these places for years before I ever actually went in. Years of my life wasted just because I couldn’t admit I had the problem.” “Look man I don’t.” “Fair enough. You can keep telling yourself whatever you need to tell yourself. Just letting you know, you’re welcome in here. If nothing else it’s a cup of coffee right?” “I suppose so.” Without saying another word the man turned and headed back into the room. Stevens shifted uncomfortably watching him go. He’d excepted a little more of a hard sell when the guy first started talking. Instead he’d just left it up to Stevens to decide what he was going to do. Sighing, he realized that really he only had one choice to begin with. In those moments it really all came together, without waiting he strode forward into the room. He knew what had to be done. Upon reaching the center of the room he turned took in everyone that surrounded him. Taking a deep breath, he spoke. “Hi, my name is Michael, I have a problem.”
