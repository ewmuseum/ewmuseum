New Life, Old Life (Not for judging, Dangerous Games RP)

7 October 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10-07-2007 The dreams just don’t die. Every night for the last few months, I would be awaken by the site of flames destroying everything that I loved and held most dear. I see her screaming in pain, on fire, but I’m not able to save her. I wake up covered in sweat, but relieved. I know that she’s still alive. I know that she’s safe where she is, that the fire never reached her. But it still doesn’t help that I wish that she was here with me. I moved here to be even closer to her. Hopefully to help with her recovery. So far, that hasn’t happened yet. She still has a hard time looking at me. There are days where I have a hard time looking at myself. Maybe I didn’t do enough to keep her in my life. Maybe I didn’t do enough to make her feel like it wasn’t her fault. Maybe I failed her. But I can’t think about that now. It’s five in the morning, good. I have to be at the shop in an hour. I get up and I get ready. The work shirt that I’m wearing doesn’t have my name on it. I preferred it that way. I didn’t need people that I didn’t know to recognize me while I was working. I look in my dresser drawer to pull out a pair of work pants. I see in the corner of my eye something from my past that I thought I got rid of. A pair of used, black spandex shorts. I put them out of my mind, it was a lifetime ago that I last needed those. I entered into a different sector in my life. Found the pants that I was looking for. Pulled them out of the drawer and quickly threw them on over my boxers. Grabbed a pair of black socks out of the drawer and threw them on as well. I headed towards my front door. I think I’ll take my bike today. Haven’t ridden in a while, and always found the freedom exhilarating. My helmet is in the closet by the door. I grabbed that and threw it on while taking my jacket out. Worn patches adorn it. Another reminder of a time I would be best to forget. There it was. The bike that has seen better days. Paint is scraped up from when some asshole hit it with his car. Just replaced the panel that was destroyed the other day. I put my tools in a bag that I can sling over my shoulder. I get onto the bike, put the key in the ignition and start up the engine. The exhaust roars in appreciation. I turn on the headlights as I peel away. I will see her later. That’s why I go in so early. Spend a few hours in the hospital at lunch. I arrive at the shop. I see the bike that I was working on is already where I need it. Today I finish it off, so that the basketball player that brought it in can take it home. I didn’t like this bike. It was faster than mine, and had better lines. The rider wasn’t meant for a machine like this. Replaced the engine, the exhaust and the fuel tank. I asked the boss if I could put on training wheels as well, but that didn’t go over too well. Years of working on my bike and those of my brother has done me well. I didn’t have to go back to my old life to make a living. The only reminder at this shop of those days is an old autographed picture in the office. At least the customers didn’t see it. I sure did. I wish the boss would get rid of it. I didn’t really need the reminder. I put my helmet and jacket in my locker. “Kid, if it wasn’t you, I would yell at you for not wearing sleeves on your shirt.” I didn’t realize that this was the shirt that I grabbed. My tattoo was showing. This was only for when we were doing a bike show. Unfortunately, I get noticed when I’m there and get asked for autographs. But even that’s dying down and I’m just autographing because of a bike that I’ve built might be on display. “Sorry, Mr. Henderson. I might have another shirt in my locker.” I moved my jacket over, and I saw a shirt in there. There was a name patch on it. I never wore shirts with my name on it. Because I would invariably head into the showroom and people with instantly put the name with the face. “Kid, how many times have I told you. Call me Dave.” “Not a problem, Mr. Henderson.” I set my bag on my work bench and pull my tools out. Unlike the rest of the dregs that work back here, I don’t worry about using the shops tools. Never have, and never will. Well, with the exception of the air tools. I’ll use what the shop supplies then. I get to work. The new engine’s come in. Perfect. I can get that mounted. I didn’t expect it until well into the afternoon. The baller is going to be happy about this. I just continue to work, getting the bike ready. The rest of the crew arrives hours after I do. They see that I’m almost done, and they learn that I’m the best at what I do here. My deadline was tomorrow. I wanted it done today. Work on my next project. But I don’t get to far before the boss chimes up. “Kid, you’ve got a phonecall.” It’s 11:38 in the morning. By the time it was 11:39, my old life returned in a flood. GCW was on the line. They found me, and they wanted me back.
