La Ciel Bleu: Une Jour dans la Vie (Part 1)

Date: Today 'I don't know...' I trailed off, trying to avoid answering the question. The traffic that morning was unusually heavy for 7:22 AM. Cars were backed up for two miles on both sides of the road. I'd never before spent so much time in his car just hanging in a single spot. While it was the case that I'm often coming and going, handling a number of jobs including newspaper columnist, community advocate, wrestling trainer, and single dad... I guess part of it must be that the area of town in which I live-- it just got routine to me. The scenery was changing all the time what with new hi-rises going up, but even that was just something that I got used to. All it's meant is more landmarks to track my position without street signs. Fort Lauderdale isn't a small town, so anything that goes up to help find one's way through the city is a good thing, in my opinion. Tania kept pressing the issue. 'Come on... it's not like I'll be missing much. And besides, you didn't take us on a vacation this summer.' 'That's because the vacation *was* having the summer off,' I said. Kids today, they keep expecting more. And I just wanted to get her on to school so that I could put this off until later. In truth, two careless drivers further ahead of the pack had both decided to run a stop sign. The intersection was blocked. I would just have Tania walk the two miles to school, but it was abnormally cool that morning, and raining. She didn't need to be put out in that. So for the time being, we just sat in the car, my daughter trying to convince me to let her come to Dangerous Games with me. Allen: But it's Las Vegas... The concern in my voice conveyed that I was worried that something might happen to her if she showed up with me. GCW is not home to all-around nice people. I can handle myself, but I'll be distracted if I have to even think of the necessity of protecting her. But my inability to properly voice my opinions meant that Tania was dominating this conversation. Amazing that my own teenage daughter is the only person in the world I have trouble speaking to. Traffic still wasn't moving. I spared gas by keeping the heater on low, though I still had to work the defrost. 'And what are you supposed to do while I'm out?' I wondered. 'I'm not about to give you a load of cash to go out and blow in the city, if that's what you're was hoping.' The enthusiasm didn't completely drain from her, but she changed her uppity body language and quieted her voice. But she was still going to get her way. Tania knew me for a long time; she knew how to push the proper buttons. Tania: I know, Sky, and I can respect your concerns. But I'll be safe. I know I will, because you're bringing everybody else too! 'What?' I said. She smiled mischievously. 'Yeah... everyone nominated me to tell/ask you that we all want to come with. Trying saying no to a house full of girls.' ... I really needed some backup in that house. _________________________________________________ It becomes apparent that I can't follow the same formula as before. That I can't be the same kind of wrestler that I was in the past and succeed in GCW like before. You see, I'm not much of a person to get hype for myself. I just... if I'm on the show, I'm wrestling. Not to say that I've never been in segments, but I'm the kind of person whose only real marketability in personality comes from being my friend outside of the ring. Tessa will attest to that. But really, I can say that I'm not all a charismatic person. I don't inspire people to emotional movements either for hating me or loving me. I don't give anything in the way of especially-heroic performances; hell, a lot of the time I'm not even doing high impact moves. It's often professional suicide to say this, especially for someone like me who's still relatively young and healthy with a lot of active years ahead of me. But I have to be completely honest with myself, both to myself and in representation for others. I only have a nickname because I gave myself one to serve as a callsign for the ring. Not to say that this is special; hell anybody who has a nickname in this day-and-age gave it to themselves. But I'm being real here. Do people like me? They might, but I don't appeal to every wrestling fan, or even most of them. I'm technically proficient without the meanstreak. I can fly but limit the crazy stuff. I can hit hard but don't do it that often. Much of my true ability is reserved, to the point where I'm not really perceived as a flashy wrestler. Even if I did go all-out, I'd still not be incredi-freaking-amazing that I'd steal the show. Then again, that's never really been my intention. From Day One, I do the job that I have to, either for bills or now for family. The most I've ever been in terms of being ambitious has just been to face and defeat the best wrestlers I can find. When I've won titles, I won them not because I was greatly concerned with the championship itself. No, I simply wanted to face the person who held it. And I knew that they would put more effort into it if their belt was on the line. Should you find any footage from PCW's past, or in RPW, or in PURE, look at what I did there. When I won or lost, I picked up and moved on. I managed to get a fan following somehow, but really I don't know how it came about. Still, again, I'll freely admit that there's no 'it' factor for me. And I suppose it comes from the fact that I know that no matter what happens, I can and will get work. I'm still a solid wrestler and even when I'm not terribly title-ambitious, I'm not an easy task for anyone. That said, you know what? I'm not interested. Not interested in Dangerous Games. Not interested in the prize for winning, not interested. I don't need it. And really, you know my opinion on battle royals... too much skill. I'd rather win by pin or submission rather than doing anything over the top rope. So instead, I have a different plan. I'm not working for myself, I'm working for the team. Well, not all of Ascension; I don't even know all of them. And really this match is more about personal advancement. No, I'm working for a smaller team. ... Pretty much just assisting two people in particular. Because I don't have to win this match, I can live with doing well. There are others who stand more to gain from this, and I've got to look out for them. Well, especially one person in particular. Yeah, her, you know who I'm talking about. Really, I am going to be helping her, perhaps more than I help myself in this event. She deserves it, and I don't need it. Might as well work to someone else's overall benefit if I'm just going to be coasting in my own career. The paycheck is right either way. Yeah. Alright, I'll see you later. Goodbye for now. So ended my phone conversation with Saadra that morning. I talked with her about what she'd done to deserve a trip to Las Vegas that she had Tania ask. Apparently her many contributions to the household had gone unnoticed or unappreciated by me, despite the fact that I'm home all the time and she's the one who stays out so much I almost feel like I'm cheating her by charging her rent. Needless to say, I lost that conversation as well. The family (plus two... dunno why Saadra and Zha can't stay home) is going to Las Vegas. Now I just have to change my one ticket into 6. I hope they like flying cargo-level. _________________________________________________ Saturday Avenue Slam Masters. One word off ('Avenue' instead of 'Night') from the Super Nintendo Entertainment Game of the same name. But it's nothing like the wacky game that had Mike Haggar as the only known and worthwhile character there. Here, in a non-descript building on Las Olas Boulevard right across from The Art Lounge, head trainers Frederick Glick and Persephone Waters teach kids and adults alike the basics of the mat. They cover all the usuals -- falls, simple holds, and the most rudimentary of ring psychology. Beyond that, they cede their time to the true professionals, the ones who are actually working the circuit. Both Fred and Seph are former indy wrestlers. They were the first really popular intergender tag team, working the Southeast region in the 1990s. But now both of them, married with children, have chosen a more sedentary lifestyle. In their mid 30s, they keep in shape by working with the new blood that comes in hoping to get a foundation in the basics so that they can make their own mark on the sport. Today, it's no time for the basics. Weekend classes cover intermediate and advanced techniques and psychology. That's always the realm of the guest lecturers. And this time, they have a special guest. 'Listen up!' Seph called out to her students. For having only a 5 foot frame, her voice carried a great deal of strength. Just like the woman herself. No wonder her old ring name was Sicily Firebrand. 'Today's guest is one of the kind that doesn't often come here.' Fred, in the office just a few feet away from the group (they didn't have a lot of horizontal space with three rings cluttering it up, but they used everything they had to maximum efficiency), said, 'Somebody with a reputation.' Seph smirked. 'Yeah, well, he's here. Works out of a place called Global Championship Wrestling, if you've heard of it.' She knew that the students were familiar with it. It was a national fed within the PTC organization; hard not to have heard of it. Standing off to the side of the room was none other than... me. A pair of black and red break-away pants and a black (man, I hate the name of this shirt) wifebeater covered my body. I unfolded my arms and walked closer to the group. 'For those of you that don't know who I am,' I began, 'my name is Skylar Green. In GCW, I'm called Ciel. I don't have a gimmick. I'm a professional and I take job very seriously. I was asked to come here and show you a thing or two about throwing and being thrown around the ring. If you have any questions, ask before we start.' A hand in the front went up. It belonged to Millicent James. A deceptively-named individual, one wouldn't think that was his name before seeing him as 5'8' 159 pound half-Vietnamese kid. 'Yeah, just one quick question.' His eyes were wide open, he looked enthusiastic to start. 'If we get one over on you, do we get a job?' The others stepped back. Stupid question, but he was known as the 'Failed Joker' of the group. Always tried to be funny, but no one ever laughed. And I definately wasn't laughing. I just moved on with it. 'So do we have any serious questions, or is this Comedy Central?' A real question came from the middle of the pack. A bright young woman from in town, hair tied behind the back of her head, sports bra and tight athletic shorts being her only clothing, she definitely appeared to be here to train. This was one of the better students, America Dimiglio, a promising young woman whose family owned an Italian restaurant in town. 'What is your area of expertise?' she asked. But immediately realizing that she might need to clarify, she said, 'We get the basics from Fred and Seph. What do you intend to be showing us, so we can at least have an understanding of what's in store for us before we start?' I calmly responded, 'Just some advanced holds.' Really, he didn't answer her question sufficiently. And Millicent, who had stepped away to get a last drink of water before they started, swallowed the cool liquid and said in a bit of an aside, 'Sure thing, Mr. Holly.' It was a clear comparison of myself, who came in with no arrogance to my name, to Hardcore Holly. In his mind, Mill immediately connected Skylar Green to Mr. 'Don't Call Me Sparky'. Figuring that I had come to the training school to beat up on some trainees to feel like a big man, guised under the false pretense of teaching them what respect is all about, Millicent was scathing and critical of his assessment of me. I continued. 'If any of you are interested in how to take high spots without killing yourself, I'll show you. Other than that, I'll show you some of the more advanced stuff, that you wouldn't normally learn until you go into the business. Now how about we get started. Somebody tell me how far along you guys are so I know where to start.' Then I smirked. 'Or better yet... Show me.' Edward Bauer (no relation to the furniture store, and yes, he had to phrase it like that the way people asked him) shook his head. 'Oh, great. Now we get to what this really about.' Though he was tall, 79 inches tall, Eddie was still rather skinny. He was working out every day, but trained for the ring at the same time. He wanted to get into the ring as soon as possible, rather than building up the muscle first, then doing the ring training. 'What are you talking about?' Greta George, the only other female in that class, asked. Eddie looked around for cameras. As far as he could see, there were none, but they could have been hidden. 'We get in the ring and go a set with him, he beats the crap out of us, and all we get at the end of the day is sore.' Millicent nodded. They were partners in crime, friends that joined the school together. Bauer continued, still scanning the open converted-warehouse room. 'What are the odds that he's going to be cutting a promo while he's kicking our asses? Wouldn't be the first time someone's used a training ring to make a point to opponents.' Pointing to Seph, Millicent added, 'You sold us out!' Greta, and another student who to this point had been quiet, shook their heads. Though Ms. George added a 'Shut up, Mill.' From inside the office, Fred spoke up. 'Don't forget, boys, you answer to me and Seph the rest of the week.' That little bit of reminding did well enough to make them be quiet. Though they're respectful enough, the head trainers can be harsh taskmasters. They went ahead and got into the ring without further words. I truly wasn't impressed with these two. Sure, they probably could cut a few good promos, but in the world of pro wrestling... talk doesn't mean anything. I watched as the two entered the ring. 'Now,' I started, 'one of the things that you should know is that you have to be able to damn near read your opponents mind while you are getting dropped on your head. If you're gonna give a good fight, you HAVE to be able to do this.' I leaned up against the outside of the ring as the two stood inside, confused. It seemed as though they couldn't believe that I wasn't in the ring tossing them around and was instead watching from the outside. The two got the 'I'm waiting' face as they dawdled. Really, they weren't any good, they just talked a lot. And I mean, a lot. But so it goes. I wasn't concerned about them; I had to book 6 tickets to Las Vegas. How plans change...
