Don't read this, Ben--it's for Super Series!

27 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/27/05 Okay. I heard a joke once; a really bad joke, I'll admit. Not that people tell good jokes most of the time, of course. Tangent, eh? Nah. So, there's this kid, call him Jimmy, walking to school one morning, already late enough to have missed the bus and getting later with every step, when one of the legitimate career bums leaps from an alleyway and just starts shaking Jimmy by his shoulders. Real vice grip, one eye staring into Jimmy's soul and the other staring at a sign hanging to their side, and just roaring out: 'purple box.' Stop me if you've heard this one before. Having shared his divine insight into the true nature of mankind, el hobo leaps back into the alleyway and Jimmy continues to school, shaken but not stirred. His head's a little off, only that natural reaction the normal have when locked onto by the depths of madness. Not enough to slow him down, so he gets there 20 minutes late instead of 19. The halls, empty, echo rapid-fire footsteps all the way to the door to his class. He lets his tardy slip to 25 minutes and catches his breath, wishing he'd have gone to the bathroom when the teacher opens up the door. 'And where have you been, James Smith?' Okay, I'm not that good with making up names. 'Well, uh,' stammering, for those of you that like to be reminded of those things outside of the dialogue, 'I missed the bus and on the way to school... never mind.' 'On the way to school, what? Did someone touch you?' 'Well, yeah. Not like that, just some bum that grabbed my arms and screamed 'purple box.'' And the color drains from her face almost quick enough to justify spelling it 'colour' to add a little impact to the situation. 'Go to the principal's office, young man.' A simple miscommunication, yeah. Don't remember if I ever told it to Trey or not. Let's assume I didn't--if I ever did, we can just ignore it and rewrite history. Nothing new. Speaking of Trey, he's half-drunk and lingering like pot smoke. Back to haunting the same cemeteries he always followed me to, out of his new-old bad habits and back into his old-old bad habits after managing to skip the phase in between where he was actually a pretty decent person. Can't ever trust kids alone, I guess. 'Fuck, man. Shit ain't right.' 'Yeah, what's new? We just need another round or two, should make things look a little better.' Okay, it's a bar. 'Looks ain't everything. Rather feel good and look like you than bum around these fuckin' dumps still pretty as shit.' I'm looking over a dark face outlined and cross-hatched with pale scar tissue--mostly my fault--topped with nappy red hair and book ended by earlobes hanging low, empty holes where stupid piercings used to lie. I just don't see where he's getting this pretty thing from. but don't have the heart to break it to him. 'Doesn't always have to be either or, you know. Could feel like shit and look like me--aye, there's the rip.' 'That from a book?' 'Sorta, I think. Why?' 'Shakespeare, man.' 'High school flashbacks? Ouch.' 'Shyeah. I guess.' 'Drown 'em in alcohol.' 'Shakespeare, though?' His mug slams into the counter, empty. 'Close. No clue why I remember it though, 'Aye, there's the rub,' maybe.' 'Yeah, s'it. I liked that one, Hamlet.' 'Most people say they do.' 'Nah, actually did. Only thing I really read for school.' 'Maybe you should quit this whole wrestling thing and start teaching English.' 'Yeah, maybe.' He drinks his beer long enough that he's definitely considering it. 'Was a joke.' 'Was a bad one.' Bad jokes, yeah. So Jimmy, almost as entertained as he is confused, walks to Principal Smyth's office in hopes of getting a little clarity on the situation--or just calling his teacher out on being a psychotic twit. Whichever. The door's open when he gets to it and he goes inside. 'Could you shut that, James?' He does, sits down. 'Your teacher told me that you said something rather inappropriate for a learning environment to her, and has recommended an immediate suspension. Now, you've always been a good student and, from what I can tell, a good kid, so I felt it necessary to get your side of the story.' 'Thanks, Principal Smyth. I was walking to school today 'coz I missed the bus and a hobo grabbed me and shouted. He left and I came to school as fast as I could and Ms. Smithie caught me in the halls and I told her the story and she sent me here.' 'That doesn't seem inappropriate to me. A bit lackluster in your tardiness, but not as unwholesome as she'd lead me to believe. Is there anything you're leaving out?' 'Well...' 'Go on.' 'I told her what the hobo yelled.' 'Which was...?' 'Purple box. Thanks for letting me clear it up with-' 'Get out. Go sit in the lobby, I'm going to call your mother. I expected much better of you, Mr. Smith.' Before the bar, there was Gino's--a cheap pizza joint like I always try to find--and before that, there was nothing. We wandered through an abyss, swirled with the clouds of creation and tripping in the primordial ooze, trying to figure out something to do. Trey'd laugh, and I'd laugh, and I'd mention a name, and he'd go quiet until laughing again. Weren't really on the verge of great conversation at any point, but there was definitely some unspoken undercurrent to it; even if it was as simple as Trey being confused. Whatever. 'So, Eva.' He laughed, immediate. 'Shit, man. bitches ain't shit...' 'Yeah, not Ice Cube. Not fooling anybody. Serious.' 'Solid? S'a stupid girl, like alla the rest. S'your bitch now, you got the contracts an' shit.' 'Yeah? Anarch, sure.' I lit a cigarette and laughed. 'Tired of this dance, Trey. I remind you of your actual job, you remind me of my pet company, then we move on without settling shit.' 'Dunno, J. Was gettin' used to it.' My name's Jordan White. Love me. 'Habits are a bitch. Kill them or they kill you, though. Might as well make the choice yourself.' 'Which habits we goin' after here?' 'Yours.' 'Aw, damn. Coulda torn your junkie ass a new one.' 'And still not have half as much money as me--you don't tell someone how to live their life until you can look down on them. Good advice, you know.' 'Shyeah, shyeah. Lead me along like a puppy 'til I'm famous an' rich as shit, an' you're gettin' richer if I win or lose. I get it, you're my friend--you don't hang around Borealis or Eva an' bitch them out 'til they listen, so I should feel all honored an' shit. Fuck that. Can't you just chill anymore, man?' 'Yeah, when I'm not around someone fucking up.' 'Fuckin' up?' 'You're turning into me, asshole, just without the charisma. From cokehead to lying cokehead to general abuse of anything illegal, and leaving just enough money to buy food. I know how it goes, and I know you're not a third as lucky as I've been.' 'Well, if I'm so much like you were--when someone sat you down to tell you how you should handle your shit, you ever listen?' 'Not usually.' 'Then why you tryin'?' 'You're smarter than I was. Might not be a better wrestler, but you're not exactly the one-chance superhero.' 'Dude, you have any clue how lucky I was to get any of these jobs inna first place?' 'People like you. Deal with it. People hated me and I did well, so it's pretty clear that being loveable--or marketable--isn't any better than being the biggest prick in the universe.' 'Shit; you happy with where you're at?' 'I dig life--being head of a company fucking sucks. You?' 'I dig bein' rich--havin' to beat some little piece I used to fling on sorta sucks.' 'So, Eva?' 'Back to that? Man, what you even wanna know?' 'You're gonna have to hurt her.' 'Shyeah.' 'You up to that?' 'Didn't love a bitch, didn't much like her. Always was physical, J, s'gonna stay that way. Just, y'know, little different physical.' 'Domestic violence style?' 'Ain't like that, man. Know that.' 'Just fucking with you--it's completely different, and there's no way in hell you're gonna use this to vent some frustration. No, you're not the type.' 'Shit, wrestled her before.' 'Wasn't like this. Couldn't jam a crowbar in her eye to pry her head up and see how little brain there was under all the layers of bitch and useless, now could you? This time around, no limits. No stopping.' 'S'life.' 'Guess it is. I mean, call me old-fashioned, but I could never get used to wrestling chicks. Just not my style. Always choked.' 'Shit--you, old-fashioned? That's some crazy shit, if you believe it. Hell, just an excuse, man. You always needed something around to fuck you outta wins, make sure you couldn't blame yourself after you fucked up.' 'I quit, Trey, and I quit a long time ago.' 'Some thing's don't change. Man's head's a man's head; no matter how hard he fights it he can't get but so far from who he is. Amazed you ain't tanked Anarch just to get it over with.' 'Too many people rely on us.' 'For what, marketin' their faces out, keepin' 'em from gettin' too big?' 'Lawyers. Health care. Managerial decisions.' 'Shyeah, s'all just fuckin' people over.' 'Didn't you read Lexicon's manifesto? This is to benefit people's careers, give them breaks they would've missed. It was Anarch that got Eva into GCW in the first place.' 'Hah, quotin' Lexicon? Becomin' what you hated, man. Y'actually buy all that bullshit, don't you? S'like you realize how hard he fucked you over, so you gotta try an' live up to the image he wanted to have. In the end, you're gonna get tired of bein' the good guy, just like Lexi, and turn into him, J. You're gonna fuck everyone.' 'This is about your life, not mine.' 'Shit. Look, everyone knows you think you're the best person ever lived, an' just won't say it too loud in case you turn out to be wrong. Just make a fuckin' stand, Mr. White.' 'Alright. Beat Eva or Anarch's over.' 'Nice bet. What if I throw it?' 'Then you're a whiny deceitful bitch instead of just a whiny one?' In the thriving gray pools of scum at our feet, something opened its eyes and turned a thousand ears to our words. Nothing changed; something roared and sent us back to where we belonged. Jimmy's mom comes about twenty minutes later; she marches past him into the principal's office and comes out with a scowl on her face and a song in her heart--admittedly, a mean song meant for bastards and bitches to croon. Her eyes carry him to the car, and the ride back to the house lasts double for the silence. Jimmy, he counts trees blurring past. Another, Christ, let's say thirty minutes pass of a heavy nothingness at the kitchen table before the mom speaks: 'What did you say?' 'To who?' 'Your teacher.' 'Purple box.' She sighs and leaves the table, casting in a 'wait until your father gets home' from the other room. Jimmy's head collapses against the glossy fake wood and doesn't move--at least on the outside--until he hears his dad's car pulling up for a lunch break. Eyes twitch, ears perk, salvation steps in and pats him on the shoulder. 'Your mom said something bad happened at school. Wanna talk about it, tiger?' 'Not really.' 'Well, she already told me most of it. Just, there's a time and a place for anything, and school's not the time for some words. You might hear me say something like that around the house now and then, but only when they're called for.' Jimmy picks his head off the table and looks around to his towering dad, head stretching into the heavens and beaming down with 31 shining teeth. 'What'd you say, anyway? Couldn't have been that bad.' 'Well...' 'You can tell me--I'm your pa.' 'Alright,' he smiles. 'Purple box.' 'Oh.' He leaves Jimmy alone and disappears through the door. The sobbing mother becomes the yelling one as the heavenly dad turns into Satan. They roar for centuries, somewhere between the moans of the damned and the chuckle of the executioner. Finally, quiet hits and his dad comes back. 'Jimmy, your mom and I talked, and... We want you to leave. Don't ever come back.' Stop. 'Where's the surprise?' The word's tear the dad's eyes up. 'There's no surprise. We'll give you the money for a taxi and a plane ticket, a little to start off wherever you end up, but we don't need to be seeing you again.' Stop. 'Where's the surprise?' Days from now, we're standing at the peak of the blackest mountain imaginable outside of an incense-stained goth club, faces ripped with the screeching wind and bodies beaten from the climb. I'm lighting a cigarette when I feel the first quake rise through my body. I'll look to Trey and he'll look to me and we'll nod. He's gonna ask 'bum a smoke, J?' I'll look around and hand him one right before the second rumble hits--closer and louder. The sky'll drain black and streak red at the ascent of a thousand disturbed bats. Below, the warring waves reflect the bloody sky and add just a little more mystery to the situation than a few odd cloud patterns could on their own. A lone bear in the distance will roar once before it's torn to shreds. We'll both try to picture what could've done it, and Trey'll come closer just on tentacle count. I'll muster: 'fuck' and he'll reply with 'shyeah.' There'll be another roar, bubbling the acid in my stomach, and a shadow will fall from the peak to the valley. If there are gods, they will cry at the fate of their creations. In a quick estimate, I'll count a thousand eyes, all glowing a sickly yellow with a thousand criss-crossed veins around abyss pupils, pasted on the silhouette. Trey will count a thousand and four, but agree with my guess at his second judgment. Wings, stretched from the north pole to the south for as much as we're concerned, will slowly close in around the mountain. The smell of rancid fish, festering in a pit of raw sewage and laced with the entrails of unlucky divers, floods my nostrils immediately. Trey vomits. 'Surprise? Fuck. Didn't have one planned.' In on the third pizza, up to our eyes in cheese and pepperoni and still a few more pounds from eating away the high, they were getting tempted to close up at Gino's. The growing tip pile, however, lessened their concern and kept the lights on for another whatever-we-needed. 'Fuck, J. You ever feel like there's some cosmic joke that you ain't in on?' 'That I'm not in on?' 'Like, victim of fate. Alla that shit.' 'No. Pretty sure the Fates weaved the punch line into my head a long time ago.' 'Shit.' Trey's head dropped long enough for his eye to catch the pizza again and pull another slice. In between gooey chomps came: 'Well, what they tell you?' 'Who?' 'Fates, man.' 'Oh. Can't share, might ruin the whole thing.' 'They actually gonna deliver the punch line someday?' 'Hell, maybe. I don't see how everyone doesn't figure it out in their last few moments alive, though. It's actually pretty simple.' 'C'mon. Not like I'm gonna tell the whole world.' 'Yeah? It's a secret.' 'Damnit. You sure?' 'Definitely.' 'Wait, though. S'only one punch line? Like, for the whole world.' 'Yeah. Whole of existence.' 'Any good?' 'Kinda black.' 'Black like sick, or black like my ass?' 'Not really sick, but definitely not your ass.' 'Well, what's the joke?' 'You getting your ass kicked for a month straight.' 'Ain't that funny.' 'Maybe not to you, but you did say you felt like there was a joke you weren't in on. Shit, might be a couple of 'em.' 'C'mon, J. Lighten up, man. Just a job.' 'Remind me: are you an athlete, a celebrity, or an employee?' 'Depends how I'm feelin'.' 'Lately.' 'Employee.' 'Bitter, bitter. No way to get your head into actually winning a match.' 'Beat the big boy, LeStatt, didn't I?' 'In the head. With a chair.' 'Sounds like I'm into hurtin' people. Should be good against Eva.' 'Because you're going to take the premise of the thing just a step further and hit her with a car? I don't see your logic here.' 'Because I can get into it when there's blood on the line. Makes it a real fight.' 'Which makes you a real psycho, right? And, if you say 'nah, s'all cool' I'm going to fucking kill you.' 'Damn. Now I gotta think of somethin' else.' 'Good that you're still scared of me.' 'You still got the reins, J.' He leaned back in his booth and lit the last Newport in his pack, crumpling the exess and dropping it on top of the stack of plates, napkins, pans. 'So, I spent a month or two with you turnin' me into scary psycho fucker for every match, kept winnin'. Spent a month or two winnin' by myself, just out-wrestlin' people. So, shit, what happened in there?' I asked the waitress for another large with extra cheese and even extra pepperoni, lit a cigarette of my own, and laughed. 'You lost. Been stupid for self-doubt ever since. Got this great plan to try and harness that beastly part of you, turn yourself into a great wrestler, but you've been failing a million times a minute. Maybe you're a niche wrestler, or maybe you already had your one great run and now it's time to slide down the other side of the mountain and chill with the losers.' 'Think that?' His tone dropped, an octave or two, and his eyes narrowed to slits. Saying 'yes' would've been like kicking a puppy with a broken leg. 'No. I think you're too goddamned caught up in what everyone else in the world sees you as to be yourself anymore, at least without trying, and that defeats the whole purpose. People think you're a stupid beast that always expects to hurt someone, so you play up to it without even realizing. Letting bastards with big mouths determine your fate's not the best way to go.' 'So I should stop listenin' to you?' 'Hey, I'm not one of the bastards that thinks you're cocky about wrestling. You're at least smart enough to learn from your fuck ups, right?' 'Shyeah.' 'Then keep fucking up, if that's the only way you learn.'
