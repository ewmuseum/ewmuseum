Something: A JUST Wrestling Piece

11 November 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 11, 2009 Words: 1,810 The Miami sun peeked through the sliding glass door of his hotel room gathering Jay Terror in its warm embrace. It attempted to repair the wounds from the night before but Jay found salvation in a white stick of tobacco instead. He let the inner toxins transform his bruises into simple sores. Each inhale closed the cut over his eye that much more making the pain continue to decrease. The television was blasting MTV Jams in the corner of the room but Jay ignored Kanye West and focused intently on a patch of the wall. He wasn't his usual snappy self, but rather in a comatose state. His mind was muck from the boos he received the night prior and so were his senses as he ignored a sliver of ash that drop onto his arm. The phone had rang three times and each time he was congratulated by a different voice. Cynthia was the first. Her words were weak and her pleas were worse. 'We should see each other again, I had fun last time,' she managed to spit. The fact that he used her for an ash tray after a less than satisfying fuck hardly mattered to her as Jay listened to the empty words she spoke. 'I heard you came by the office,' she started. Jay wasn't willing to hear the rest and tossed the phone back on the bed side table replacing it with a half empty bottle of Jack. The liquor ran freely down his throat helping his body forget about the open gashes. Steven Caldera would be the next to call mostly making sure that he caught his jet to Vancouver for WorldWide. The night before he gave him his praise and showered him with compliments; it'd be against his nature to do it again. Still, he manufactured promises off greatness on the horizon. He reminded him who he was and who he was working with. 'It wasn't the people against you,' he added. 'It's the people sided with you.' Jay answered with empty 'yeahs' and was left no room to talk as Caldera himself had to catch a plane. With a click, he found himself alone with the patch of wallpaper again. Tim Shipley was the last and most recent. His praise was flowery and encouraging stating 'I'm glad that you're on the tour with us.' He made sure he knew of Lexicon and the match ahead of him. That was all besides a reminder to catch his jet to Rhode Island. Two Presidents, two jets. Something was obviously standard protocol. As Shipley cut out of his ear, Jay set his sights on the wall once again. It was a simple pattern of two circles and a triangle that repeated effortlessly around the room. He was amazed how one arrangement of shapes transformed the cell from white drab to decorated goodness. As his eyes slinked across the wallpaper he couldn't help but realize how similar he and the patch were. After Dangerous Games, he was no longer a wildcard wrestler booked under a bogus moniker. He had a legacy to fulfill, a patchwork to maintain. He wasn't allowed to break the pattern and stray away from the NC-17 title scene since no one else had ever done so. He was locked in to fate and there was no changing that unless by divine intervention. Unlike divine intervention was even an option though. Like the arrangement of shapes, he'd continue to repeat what other winners had done. He'd be a reproduced object of affection. It wasn't a bad thing - not at all. Having a shot at a championship was never considered bad no matter which context it was taken in. But still, something about the situation wasn't as appealing as it was booked to be. Perhaps it was the idea of being like the others. Like the ones who had come so close. Did he want to come that close? Did he want to fall short of himself? Jay reached to his nightstand and grabbed the bottle of Jack again emptying another full swig down his pipes. It killed the lump in the pit of his stomach and allowed him to focus on a patch of paper in the top corner of the room. The circles were incomplete as was the tip of the triangle. Thanks to the dimensions of the room, the pattern wasn't as perfect as it seemed to be. It was that patch that did it for Terror. That single defect would be the reason that he'd deny falling short. It'd be the piece that decided his fate. Instead of tripping on his way up he'd prove something. Something. Whatever that was. --- He was in the air sitting first class for only the second time since June. It wasn't that he couldn't afford a ticket but rather the speculation a person gets when they're seated in the more cushioned seats. People expect others to be doing something right in their lives when they fly in luxury and Jay didn't exactly feel like he was doing just that. He was an unwanted puppy for some time - simply tossed out of a slowing down truck so it landed decently. He was more or less Jamison Talley in his mind instead of Jay Terror. As he turned to face the woman sitting next to him though, he automatically didn't care who he was. Her figure was striking, one a person would have to run for at least two hours a day to have. Her face was pleasantly smooth and her eyes were a beautiful shade of slate. He pulled loose what was left of his bottle of Jack, "Want some?" She turned to him in a somewhat dignified manner and cocked her right brow slightly, "What is it?" He showed off the black logo, "Jack Daniels. Fine whiskey if you asked me." She huffed, "I'm not a whiskey girl actually." "Vodka and cranberry?" "It's a better thought, yeah." He took a sip from the bottle before suit-casing it again, "I take that as a no then?" "You could say that." Obviously a model or something better, she turned to look out the window at the passing Cirrus clouds. Jay shook it off and fetched a cigarette and a bandana from his pack. The cigarette was comfort food while the bandana was habit. It seemed that every cigarette he had was shared with Rich Rollins, the original owner of the tattered rag. Now with the obvious scent of tobacco and sweat, it was his one and only possession of true worth. He sprawled the blood stained cloth across the drop down table in front of him while lighting the cigarette. It only took two seconds before the model turned his way again. "Smoking?" Jay sized her up as he took his second drag and blew it up towards the fan which subsequently blew it back down over them. "The light's off so yeah." She put on one of the more disgusted faces he'd seen in his time with full crinkles. "You think you could blow the other way?" "I guess, sure." He obliged and turned his shoulder away avoiding any further confrontation. The cigarette mixed well with the ass of Jack he had left and after all was said and done he felt a healthy head high. It was enough to negate the fact that he was going to be in the air for another three hours. Shutting his eyes, he leaned his head into the cushions of the headrest recapturing the night before. His rest wasn't long though due to the mousy call of the woman beside him. "Hey you," she pestered. Jay popped his eyes back open and looked back at her pointing finger, "Yeah?" "Could you move that?" "Have to pee?" "Huh?" "To pee? You know, urinate?" She pointed harder forcing his eyes at the bandana. "That rag, could you put it away?" "It's not bothering you." "Its' smell is getting into my air." He half smiled, "Who are you?" "What do you mean?" He tried again, "Like what do you do?" "I don't get it." He huffed, "Never mind." Shutting his eyes, he hoped the conversation was dead before his head rush was. She was insistent though and pressed that slim finger against his cheek. He pushed his weight forward and gave her a heavy glare, "Look, just let it go." "What were you trying to say about me?" He brought his eyebrows to a sharp point in the middle of his head, "Forget about it." He turned back and shut his eyes again half expecting another jab. He didn't get it. Instead, he could feel the wind whip as the bandana was snatched from the table and tossed to the floor. He immediately sat up and swept it back into his now fists. "Are you serious?" "What did you say about me?" He could feel the large vein in his head pop out as he channeled his rage into his fists. "Look lady, shut the hell up and quit bothering me." Not just first class heard him but he was pretty sure that the entire coach section caught wind of the outburst. Quickly, a stewardess rushed to his side. "Is there a problem here?" The model spoke up first, "I just received a threat. Could I change seats?" Jay shook his head, "Really?" The stewardess took sides with the model faster than you could say 'hot pocket'. She escorted her out of her seat to another spot a few rows ahead of them. She watched Jay like a hawk for a good five minutes before Terror shut his eyes again leaning back into the headrest. Unfortunately his head rush was gone making the flight that much longer. --- As Jay strolled down the not-yet-done rampway in the Pizzitola Sports Center he recalled the dream he had on the plane. The chairs reminded him of the people on the island he had encountered. They surrounded him like a shackled dog and taunted him to near submission as they wrestled him to the floor for a pot roast. In the midst of it all, a man stood tallest and strongest among them. He commanded the group of natives and advised them to not give Jay room to breathe. They didn't take his advice. Out of sheer desperation, Jay was able to fend off the hundred men for his freedom. When he came face to face with the chief he asked him but one question: why don't they listen to you? The leader answered simply: they don't understand. As Jay stood where the ring would be in a few short days, he knew that if John Lexicon had a shot at beating him, he'd have to understand that he'd need to cut all room to breathe.
