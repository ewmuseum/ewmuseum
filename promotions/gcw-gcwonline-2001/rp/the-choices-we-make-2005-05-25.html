The choices we make...

25 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/25 Stepping stone. Just a rung on the ladder. Nothing more than a tally. A number on a sheet. What are you, Johnathan Borealis? Are you a man, a wrestler, an actual being? Or are you, in fact, a bump in the road? 'Just what exactly are you trying to say?' I can remember her as clear as crystal. Kate Jagonzynski. The woman I love. The memory plays over in my mind a thousand times a day, and every time she's exactly the same. It's my perception that changes just the slightest bit to notice this or that standing out, calling to me, beckoning me to have done one thing or another just a little differently. And one thing in particular different by a longshot. 'I'm trying to say, Kate, that I love you. But I'm not really too sure on how I can say something like that.' 'You could try saying 'I love you'...like you just did.' 'Well, that wasn't a real statement of my feelings. That's somebody's prefabricated...manufactured...tripe sentence that wraps and warps all my feelings and turns them into something that anybody and everybody can understand. When the fact of the matter is that I don't understand them, and you're the only one that I want understanding them, and that there's more to it than three stupid little words. But...I love you, Kate.' Her shoulder length dark brown hair is disturbed for a second only by her hand running through it, lifting it for a split second before it falls back into the exact place that it was before. A pointless gesture, or is it? It doesn't move the hair from where it is. It can't relieve any itching or discomfort that came from where her hair was a second ago, because it's right back to the same spot. But it's an excuse. It's an excuse to look down, smile that gorgeous smile, do something, and show me some sort of reaction to my bleeding heart being put on the table for all to see. And it's as simple as that. A reaction. The visual representation of what's going on in her head. With no meaning other than the one I can't see. Story of my life. 'Johnny-' She was the last person I liked calling me 'Johnny.' I told Eva that she could call me 'Johnny,' and for my efforts I got kneed in the balls. I guess that just goes to show what it's worth to extend yourself to embrace the welcoming of others. You get kicked in the balls, both literally and metaphorically. ?You can be so full of shit sometimes.? ?I try, Kate. I really try.? Her smile lights up the room and she clings her embrace to my frame. Hot passion runs through her as lips meet, and breath goes out of one body and into the other. Sharing souls, sharing feeling. There's no two ways about it. We are in love. ...we were in love. Seven years ago. Seven years ago, I laid in bed late at night and looked over at the woman I loved. I sat there, and I burned for her. Her and I just got through one of the best nights of my life... You see, Kate and I didn't 'fuck.' When it's two people that feel the way that we felt about each other, you can't compress all of the raw emotion, surging feelings, and unstoppable passion into four letters, one syllable, and such a simple word as 'fuck.' And we didn't 'make love' either. That's the most acinine thing I've ever heard of, since you don't actually make love unless you're having sex with someone that you don't love and you fall in love with them while you're having sex and it's because you're having sex that you fall in love with them. Which is complete bullshit. The best way to put it is that Kate and I were engaging in a mutually beneficial act that gave us both pleasure. And it meant a lot more than that because it was the two of us. End of story. But of course, it wasn't the end of the story. It wasn't just another stepping stone, bump on the road, mile marker in our lives. I sat there that night, looking at her, like I had done countless times before. And that little monstrous voice started talking to me. You know the one. We've all heard it. Every last person on the face of the planet has heard the voice, even if they deny it. Even if when they hear it, they ignore it. They block it out. But it's there. It's the voice that when you see a cute little kitten, it says, 'What if you broke it's neck and left it for its owner to find it?' It's the voice that when you watch the news, it says, 'What if gunfire broke out and the reporter got shot through the head on national television?' It's the one that wonders what the most shocking, revolting, and damaging thing that could be done is. And because it is so horrible, we ignore it. 'Look at her, Johnny.' 'I see her.' 'Look at her. Laying there. She's a real fucking piece of work, isn't she?' 'That she is.' 'Look at that body. That perfect body. That slender, tender, teasing body. Don't you want to just ravage it?' 'I did just ravage it. Where were you?' 'But what if you hurt her? What would that be like?' 'Go to Hell. I'm never fucking hurting her.' 'Think about it, Johnny. Look at her. Perfect. Boom. Perfect. Nothing else can be said about it. And you can change that. Boom, she's damaged goods. And I'm not talking hurt her physically. Then she don't look perfect.' 'Forget it.' 'I'm talking about scarring her. Mentally. Permanantly. Then she's gone, she's gone! And once that happens, what happens next?' 'No idea. Guess we'll never know.' 'I guess we never will, Johnny. Maybe nothing. Maybe if you end it, nothing will change. Maybe the fucking Earth will keep spinning. Maybe the grass will keep growing and the sun will keep burning and people will still be people until the day this fucking experiment called 'existance' is over with and in ashes and all we have to show for it is our gravestones. Maybe that'll happen.' 'Or maybe my life as I know it will end. Maybe hers will end.' 'I bet it won't. I bet that if it ended now, you'll go on. And, barring any moronic suicide by her, she'll go on.' 'She'd never kill herself. She's way too reasonable.' 'So how does your life change? Do you stop getting up every day and going to your job and eating and drinking? Will you two stop having sex with people? Will either of you really, honestly change in such a way that nothing in your life will ever be the same again? Do you actually think that you won't fall into yet another routine?' Don't do it, John. That's all I can say looking back. But you can't turn back. 'I love her.' 'You love an idea. You love the idea that you and her are meant to be.' 'She means everything to me. She means the world to me.' 'Oh yeah? Prove it. Give her up, Johnny. See how much your world really changes. See if you fall into depression and turn into a bum on the street. Or see if you beg her on your hands and knees to forgive you. See if you become and entirely different person and walk out on yourself because you walk out on her. Let's find out.' There's a million good reasons to do a lot of things. There's no good reason for getting up and leaving the woman you love while she sleeps, dreaming of you. And I can argue for myself the idea that part of me finds life to be a futile venture. That's no excuse. And looking at what Kate's become in these past seven years, one would wonder if she was affected much in the long run. Was I really just another stepping stone that got her where she is today? You wish. But it's not the only time my actions, my person has been underestimated. Luke Hanson stood up from seven feet, four inches and he literally said the words, 'Speaking of stepping stones.' Now LeStatt Knight is doing the same. I'm not saying I'm not a simple tally, and I'm not saying I am. It's not the impact that one makes that is necessarily the changing event of one's life. It's the choices that lead up to that. Seven years ago, I made a choice. A simple choice that I never once thought that I would ever make. I chose to get up out of bed at three o'clock on the morning and leave the woman I love. Just to see what would happen. And what did happen? The sun still rises, the Earth still rotates, the grass still grows, and the two of us are still alive. What's going to keep happening? The same things. Even when the sun goes supernova and all this pathetic experiment of human life is over with, there will be some sort of continued existance. And nothing will change that. Not me being with the woman I love, and not me leaving her. I can still see Johnny Borealis, back when he was Johnny Borealis, laying there, saying goodbye. 'I love you, Kate. I love everything about you. I love your laugh. I love your smile. I love your eyes. I love your hair, your hands, your lips, your spirit, your voice, your breath, your smell, your neck, breasts, sex, attitude, tone, vibe, persona, lifeblood, ego, thinking, and beliefs. There is nothing about you that I want to give up... ...but I am.'
