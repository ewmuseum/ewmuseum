Had to be a Big Shot

20 December 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: December 20, 2009 December 20th, 2009 Dear Diary, Body trembling, mouth is dry. Your toes are numb and your heart beat steadily increases. You curl yourself in tight to channel the sensation of body warmth. It's either a night of sensuality, the magic created between a man and a woman... ...or a night in the end of December. The swift, summer winds have passed, making those warm, humid days seem like nothing more than a distant memory. The days you used to take for granted during July and August, you now crave and find yourself pathetically counting down to. It never truly occurred to me how quickly time passes you by. All our young lives, we've spent hoping and planning for the future - planning for adulthood. The truth is, when it finally comes, we're wishing we could go back in time. Friendships are made and bonds are broken - we make decisions that no longer just shape our future but that shape our present. Repercussions become more severe and less easy to fix. I've always said from the get-go that wrestling was never a business I envisioned myself to be a part of. Growing up, I'd always envisioned myself as the corporate type. I saw myself leading a major company and drawing a huge paycheck from merely sitting at a desk and calling the shots. I can relate to all the traits of being a big time company executive - I'm cocky, self-serving. I'm arrogant and willing to stab even my best friend in the back for the satisfaction of making a quick buck. Despite constantly surrounding myself with a group or entity, I'm really a loner - I depend on only myself. Wrestling was merely a fail-safe; a quick fix. Regardless of what kind of person you are, it's hard to turn down your five minutes of fame. Everyone wants to see themselves on television or have a magazine write an article about them. The thought of being a celebrity consumes the dreams of the entire human race - and justifiably so. It's that 'dare to be great' situation. In a fucked up sense, it gives your life meaning; makes you think you were put here to make at least one complete stranger happy. If there are 4.6 billion people in the world, I'm sure you can imagine what the odds must be like to get more than five minutes in the spotlight. It's an opportunity that doesn't come knocking often and, when it does, is a door you have to answer. It's not an annoying salesman or a Hasidic Jew that comes banging on your door every Sunday to see if you've changed your mind. It's one and done - you say no and you've shut it out for life. When the opportunity arose to become famous, I knew I had to take it. I aspired to eventually use my fame to spew into the corporate world - become rich and powerful. But the wrestling business doesn't work in that fashion. There are far too many guys who leave and end up cashing welfare checks a few years down the line. And that's where my arrogance took over. I figured that if I made a strong enough impact in any given company, if I forced them to build their brand around me, I'd become unexpendable. I wanted people to think that if I left, the walls would come crumbling down. But they didn't. I was expendable. Very expendable. Warren Buffet said to "never invest in a business you can't understand." I invested my entire adult life into wrestling, yet I never understood it - not one bit. I was like a blind man walking into a topless bar. There are certain aspects of the industry that carry over from the business world. For example, the strategic alliances created and the monetary agreements arranged are a mirror image to the corruption in corporate business. But then there are so many other things that play a critical role in your success which are tuned to the wrestling industry. Despite the fact you may be the biggest ratings draw, in turn producing more revenue for the company, you won't necessarily be wearing the title of champion. And in a business where status is only gained by wearing a glorified leather belt around your waist, that title tends to hold great value. In two years since returning to this industry, I've slept with who I had to, I've betrayed my closest friends when necessary, and I've even aligned myself with a man whom I swore I'd take down...and it's gotten me nowhere. Nowhere. If there's one thing I've learned thus far in life, it’s that when money or fame is involved, you often lose sight of those things that are dearest to you. You begin to find yourself eating meals alone and having nobody at your bedside when you're sick. And when you need a shoulder to cry on, the only one you have is your own. Life is like a coin; you can spend it any way you want, but you can only spend it once. It's rather ironic, I'd say, that with all my fame and glory - the only person I can talk to right not is you, diary. I've spent my life's coin already and now I'm desperately trying to get a refund. If I could take it all back and do it all over, I would. You fucking bet I would. I remember when I was younger and took a young, misguided child underneath my wing. His family had fallen apart long before I'd met him and he'd had no friends - only his hope. I remember what it was like to help him get on his feet and get his thoughts together. He listened to everything I had to say - and he appreciated it. He really appreciated it. But even in the mist of all we went through, there's still one thing that's stuck out in my mind. I remember it being a night like tonight, a cold one in December while we were both on the road, when he asked me what I wanted for Christmas. I chuckled and assumed he was kidding - but he wasn't. So I told him, "all I want for Christmas is to be with my family." He smiled at me and replied, "You've done so much for me in the last year - I hope St. Nicholas can honor your wish." The peculiar thing about his statement and the reason it's stuck with me through the years is that he genuinely believed in St. Nicholas...in Santa Claus. He believed that if you portrayed one act of genuine kindness in a year's time, you'd be rewarded by a jolly spirit. Coincidentally, the remainder of our tour was cancelled that year and I was able to spend that holiday with my family. And as fate would have it, it'd be the last Christmas I would ever have with them. Perhaps it was the pure coincidence I saw my family that Christmas that cemented that boy's words in my head for my entire life; or perhaps it was the fact that I hadn't had a merry Christmas since. When God took both of my parents from me in a matter of months, I completely broke down. Sure, he rewarded me for being a saint the year before - but then he went and fucked me. I pulled a boy off the streets and changed his life...and God took my family for it. Naturally, I grew pissed at the world. I wanted every fucking person on Earth to feel my pain. I wanted everyone else to know what it felt like to suffer - and to feel absolutely helpless. I wanted to avenge their death by committing enough acts of evil to make up for what he took from me. I saved a boy's life...and I got a fucking dinner for it. When God took my parents, he not only killed them but he also killed me. I wanted retribution for the lives he let perish. I wanted to be cruel. But cruelty is tiring. And I'm fucking exhausted. It's no accident that things are more likely to go your way when you stop worrying about whether you're going to win or lose and focus your full attention on what's happening right this moment. And right now, here in this moment, I've got the single greatest woman at earth trying to throw her heart at me. A woman whom, for the last six years, I've spent nights crying over. She's been right by my side for the past few months and I've neglected that. I've neglected her. But I've been just so hung up on this 'business' and the rush of being notorious that she's nearly left me again. What hurts the most was being so close - so close to the top of the mountain, the glory. I knew there was a shadow of a chance that if I kept pushing, I'd push her away. But I was so sure I'd capture that ultimate glory that I ignored the fact I was about to lose her. Needless to say, I came up short. I've made a promise to myself - to Rebecca and to myself - that I'd stop living for the winning and the losing; that I'd focus on the here and now...just like I used to. Just like I did when Zacharia asked me what I wanted for Christmas. There's an old Russian proverb that says, "one who sits between two chairs may easily fall down." For me, the double life has got to go. I had my chance. I let it slide. I just had to be a big shot. Dear Diary. My name is Chris Bagwell and I was the Rajah of Ratings; the Human Hype Machine; the Torn Soul. But today...I stand before you...a reverend of a revolution.
