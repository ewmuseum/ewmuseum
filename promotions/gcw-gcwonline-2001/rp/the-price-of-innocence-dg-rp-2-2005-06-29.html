The Price of Innocence (DG RP #2)

29 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6-29-05 Cruising at 15,000 feet somewhere above New York, en route to Boston? “Man, the Red Sox are kicking ass this year,” said the head on the left. The head on the right shook his capped cranium. “Were you even watching the Indians game” They got shut out, seven to nothin?. The Sox are slipping, and you know it.” Unable to do anything but eavesdrop, Rich Rollins sighed as he propped one leg against the back of the Red Sox fan’s seat, looking out the round porthole window at the passing clouds, trying to drone out their senseless banality. “Fuck you, they’re slipping,” the head on the left shot back. “A seven-game streak is something to be proud of, you know.” The head on the right laughed. “Whatever. Hell, the Angels have a better record this season.” “Yeah, but that’s the West. Fuck them.” In his head, The Renegade began slowly counting to ten. Cramped up in the not-so-luxurious confines of American Airlines 747 coach seating, Rollins was more than thankful that the seat to his immediate left was unoccupied. The thought of having to suffer through the same repetitive small talk with a complete stranger was the furthest thing from what he wanted. Which, as it happens, was to do exactly what he was doing. Absolutely nothing. “Boo.” Startled, Rollins banged his shins against the Red Sox fan’s seat in front of him, interrupting the flow of their conversation for a brief moment. Cursing under his breath, The Renegade turned around to find the source of the unwelcome intrusion. With his head pressed firmly between his seat and the next, Andrew grinned a mischievous twelve-year-old grin as he blinked, his young face smeared with wonder for his bandanna-sporting hero. “Whatcha doin?”” he bubbled in that infuriating childlike sing-song way. “Losing my will to live,” Rollins retorted, trying unsuccessfully to keep his agitation in check. “Where are your parents?” “I’m sorry, is he bothering you?” a soft female voice drifted over the seat. Arching his head over the headrest, Rollins frowned as he realized that the boy and his parents were indeed sitting behind him. This is the last time I fly commercial, he thought dryly. “Andrew, leave the poor man alone,” the mother admonished, pulling the bright-eyed youngster back into his seat. “Just sit still, we’ll be back on the ground in a little while.” To Rollins she said, “I’m sorry, mister. He’s just a little excited.” “You know, they have medication for that,” he responded, settling back into his seat. Turning back to his window, Rollins absently ran a hand through his thick mop of brown hair, trying once again to find himself some peace and quiet. Not one minute later, the little boy pulled a small travel pack out from under his seat. Diving into a side pocket, he produced two plastic action figures. With an opposable Rich Rollins in one hand and a replica Mike Hardy in the other, Andrew started going to town. “Bam! Omph! Bam!” he cried, repeatedly slamming the two pieces of plastic together. With every imitated grunt and groan, the hairs on the back of the real Rich Rollins started to stand on end. In his mind, Rollins began slowly counting to one hundred. ~<>~<>~<>~ Four hours later? To Andrew, my favorite fan. -The Renegade The words struck a deep chord in his heart, now far more meaningful than when Rollins had absently scrawled them on page 26 of little Andrew’s magazine earlier in the day. He re-read his own autograph again and again, the levity what had just transpired still weighing heavily on his conscience. The police had questioned him, the FBI had grilled him, and the airline authorities had thanked him for going above and beyond the call of duty. Yet despite the sudden media shitstorm that now threatened to delay his arrival at the Boston Fleet Center, it was the conversation with Andrew’s parents that had stung the most. “my favorite fan. No matter where he went, tragedy seemed to be right as his heels. ~<>~<>~<>~ Four hours earlier, back in the sky? “I gotta take a piss,” said the head on the left. Excusing himself from his seat, the Red Sox fan quickly stood up and worked his way back to the restroom cubicles, leaving his sports enthusiastic friend alone to think up a good explanation on how the Indians were able to unseat the best road trip winning streak the Sox had had since 1977. Rollins simply wanted to shoot himself. He honestly cared that much. “Does it hurt?” Andrew again, his tiny head propped between the seats. Rollins exhaled an exasperated sigh. “Does what hurt?” “Wrestling,” he said. “My dad says that it’s all fake.” This was too much, and Rollins was redlining. Yet try as he might, he couldn’t bring himself to raise his voice at the kid. “I’m not sure,” he responded instead, “maybe you should ask Mike Hardy.” Andrew laughed, the sarcasm obviously lost on his innocence. “Yeah, you must have hurt him pretty bad with that stapler. My mom made me turn the channel when you did that, she said it was too violent,” he grinned, “but my dad turned it back right after. We watch GCW together, most of the time.” “Andrew, that’s enough,” came the stern reprimand of his mother, her patience wearing about as thin as Rollins felt. The father simply smiled, evidently entertained. “That’s her,” Andrew whispered, milking the moment for everything he could. “She gets kinda grumpy when-?” “Andrew!” He sat back, huffing impatiently. His frustration quickly evaporated however, as he was back at the grunts and groans of the Rollins versus Hardy bout in his lap a minute later. Both his attention span and nerves hopelessly shattered, The Renegade just shook his head as he rearranged himself in the uncomfortably cramped padding of the airline seat, trying to zone out to the soft and steady thrumming of the plane’s engines. Another handful of minutes passed by in relative silence before something in the back of his head began gnawing at him, like a sense that something was amiss. Where did that Red Sox fan go? he thought, turning around in his seat to look back at the restroom cubicles. Down at the end of the aisle a blue-suited stewardess caught his gaze and smiled, but the annoying fan was nowhere to be seen. Casually glancing at the “head on the right” seated in front of him, he caught a glimpse of the man’s face for the first time. Sporting a generic black baseball cap, the young man looked to be no more than twenty-seven, his face clean shaven and his hair kept short underneath the hat. All in all, Rollins figured that the guy was about as uninteresting as you can get, not the kind of person that would stick out of a crowd. The only problem was that he was sweating. A lot. “Bam! Auuragh!” exclaimed Andrew, enthralled in his own epic clash of the plastic titans. The man on the right began fidgeting nervously, occasionally glancing backward at the restrooms. Rollins continued to watch his odd behavior for several minutes, an eerie feeling creeping up the back of his spine. He knew the look on the man’s face, and it didn’t do anything to ease the knot slowly building in his stomach; he was waiting for something or someone to make a move, and the consequences had him scared sick. But for what? The question wouldn’t leave Rollins alone, and his sudden paranoia told him that something was seriously wrong. A man who minutes ago had been idly chatting about sports didn’t suddenly develop a case of cold sweats for no reason. A stewardess passed two more times offering Rollins a beverage, but he declined. He merely observed, his muscles tense for reasons he couldn’t explain. It wasn’t until the Red Sox fan finally emerged from the restroom, his right arm soaked to the shoulder in blue sanitation liquid, that the knot in The Renegade’s stomach finally snapped. For curled tightly in a fist dripping blue droplets of airline toilet liquid, was a gun. “Sir, please take your?” the stewardess? cheery voice dropped instantly, her eyes quickly finding the not-so-concealed weapon. Her hands involuntarily jerked to her mouth, stifling a sharp gasp. The man silently pressed the barrel of the gun against the blue fabric between her shoulder blades, giving her a no-nonsense stare that spoke volumes in intimidation. Using his free hand to jerk her forward by the elbow, the Red Sox fan began slowly marching her down the aisle, trying to be a discrete as possible. The subterfuge was irrelevant, because as soon as he had walked three steps the man sitting in front of Rollins yanked himself out of his seat, extending his hand to catch a second pistol that the Red Sox fan produced from his jacket. After that, it was all over. The entire plane descended into chaos. Women screamed and men began shouting curses. The words “terrorist” and “Al-Qaeda” began spreading like wildfire, attracting the attention of the people up front. Horrified cries for forgiveness filled the cabin as people began to pray, began to cry. But nobody moved. “Everybody just calm the fuck down!” the Red Sox fan bellowed over the instantaneous calamity of the passengers, shoving the stewardess forward to fall gracelessly in the middle of the aisle. With a calculated precision that screamed of military training, both men managed to cover all the angles with their weapons, causing a frantic rippling effect to pass over every row as the guns grazed over them in turn. “We’re not here to crash any planes, folks,” the Red Sox fan addressed his unwilling audience, his voice showing only the barest hint of fear. “We’re just making a detour, that’s all. So before any gung-ho cowboys start to thinking that they’re gonna stop some terrorists, please remember that we’re at fifteen-thousand feet and nobody wants to hurt anybody. I promise.” His own shock now subsided, Rollins couldn’t help but snort. The promise of a man holding a gun in your face wasn’t worth much, in his estimation. The man who had been sitting in front of Rollins took a visual cue from his partner, then dashed toward the pilot’s cockpit. The door was closed, but the man wasted no time in grabbing a fire extinguisher from a small emergency cubby, taking swings at the handle. While his partner chopped away, the Red Sox fan slowly began walking down the narrow aisle until he was less than three rows from where Rollins sat. Suddenly, the pilot blared over the speaker system, his voice as neutral as was humanly possible. “Attention passengers, please do not panic. The pilots on this plane have been licensed and trained to use firearms, so to the men attempting to hijack this cockpit, be warned that lethal force will be used if you refuse to surrender your weapons immediately.” The warning did little good, as the man bashing at the door handle finally dropped the fire extinguisher, aiming his weapon at the lock itself. With a curse muted by the earsplitting blast, he fired a single round into the frame, popping the lock backwards and into the cockpit proper. The gunshot frightened the entire cabin, wrenching another round of alarmed shrieks from the passengers. “You heard the man, don’t panic,” the Red Sox fan repeated frenetically, quickly regaining control. With a final crack, his partner finally kicked the cockpit door open. It wasn’t until the inevitable firing began that Rollins made his move. With the Red Sox fan’s attention turned toward the sudden firefight, Rollins hauled himself into the aisle and slammed into the hijacker’s blindside at full speed, wrenching the man’s gun arm to the floor as quickly as possible. The man was stronger than he looked, but the surprise attack took him off his balance as they tumbled onto the thin carpet. Cracking his jaw with a solid blow, The Renegade felt little remorse as he felt the bone snap, but it did little to slow the man down. Quickly regaining his senses, the hijacker slammed his forehead into Rollins? face, blurring his vision for a moment as stars swam across his field of view. The Renegade released a restrained grunt as the attacker sent a swift knee into his groin, but seeing as how it wasn’t the first time someone had tried to knock his manhood through his pelvis, he recovered with startling ferocity. Landing another blow Rollins grappled for the man’s firearm, trying to keep the muzzle pointed away from the wide-eyed innocents who watched on in complete shock. Jerking his weapon from Rollins? forceful attempt, the gun went off once, aimed low through the seat in which Rollins had just been sitting. Finally snapping out of their traumatized coma, a few male passengers took the initiative and jumped the aisle alongside The Renegade, overpowering the frantic Red Sox fan-turned hijacker. With the combined might of too many people to ward off, the attacker finally submitted as his gun was wrenched from his grip. A stunned silence filled the cabin as one of the pilots emerged from the cockpit, a seeping red blemish spreading across the shoulder of his starched white uniform. “Is everyone all right?” he cried, his voice filled with strain. Her voice painfully shrill, the mother who had sat behind Rich Rollins with her son and husband suddenly broke the awkward hush. “Andrew” Andrew? Oh god!” Jerking to his feet, a terrible weight crashed into Rollins? stomach as he rushed over to where he had been sitting only moments ago. The image of Andrew, his toys sprawled by his feet and blood on his shirt, would haunt Rich Rollins for the rest of his life. ~<>~<>~<>~ A Boston hospital, two hours later? The stark white walls of the waiting room offered little comfort as Rich Rollins sat with Angie and Ron Townsend, Andrew’s parents. The doctors had made routine visits to update them on Andrew’s condition, and thankfully the bullet had only punctured tissue and hit no major arteries. They said the surgery was going to be slow going to remove the slug for fear of collapsing his left lung, but his prognosis looked better than they had originally thought. The kid was going to make it. The news had sent Andrew’s parents into a relived sobbing fit that sliced straight to Rollins? heart, watching the emotional tension unload. He retained his own composure enough to keep the peace, offering his support where he could to a family that had suddenly had its GCW weekend extravaganza taken away. When the tears finally subsided, they spoke. “You did what you had to do,” Angie murmured through her grief, “You truly did. Had you not done something, we may all have been much worse off.” Rollins fought back his emotions, scarcely keeping them bay. “I got your son shot in the process, though. That’s not a victory, not in my book.” “It’s not a defeat either,” Ron replied, shaking his head. “It’s not your fault. We have nothing but admiration and respect for what you-?” “For getting a little boy put in the hospital?” Rollins spat, the words much harsher than he had intended. “If I had only taken the gun and hadn’t worried about my own petty aggravations, your son would be getting ready to see Dangerous Games right now, not sleeping in a hospital bed while some doctor yanks a bullet out of his chest!” “You’re his hero, Rich. He was scared to death up there, and he watched you take that man down without regard to your own safety. You’re a saint,” said Angie, her face crinkling with pity when she saw that her words had no effect. His anger with himself growing at every step, Rollins shakes his head as he reaches into the carry-on duffel that had had on the plane with him. Producing a laminated security pass, he hands the card to Andrew’s parents by its black necklace. “You give that to him,” he said, his voice warbling with a distress deeper than he had experienced in as long as he could remember, “and you tell him that any time he wants to come to a GCW event, he’ll have a backstage pass and a ringside seat waiting for him.” Intentionally avoiding their penetrating eyes, Rich “The Renegade” Rollins excused himself from the waiting room, stopping at the door only because his conscience forced him to. “I promise him that.” And with that he walked out on Ron and Angie Townsend, heading out to where a waiting taxi was ready to whisk him away to the hotel that had been reserved in his name. The name Richard Andrew Rollins. ~Fin~
