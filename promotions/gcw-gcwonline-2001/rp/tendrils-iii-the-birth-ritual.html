First: Dawn The sun flushed over the desert, drawing surreal colors across the windswept sands with otherworldly fingers. The delicate blush of the earth's cheek at the touch of her lover's hand was increasing by the moment, from the East. Fate rose inexorably with it, her Fate. There would have been a time when she would have shied away from the sunlight, still. As recent as two years ago, she would have hidden underneath the covers, thankful that the curtains were drawn against the day's beginning. But here she sat now, her own skin taking on the chameleon colors of the dawn’she was the earliest riser in the house now. She was a part of it all, organic movement up against the backdrop of human ingenuity. This giant house, her Desert Castle, was cold, but she herself was warm and receptive, her senses open and fluid to the experiences that flowed in and out of the ether breathing like an animal around her. She was inside it all. She was it. Death had a way of separating its victims from the web of consciousness that wrapped close round the Earth's surface like a halo, and it still tended to do so to the Nightwalkers. But somehow, she had slid back through the net, once again enmeshed. A tiny smile touched the corners of her rose-rouged lips. It was an expression of great serenity, dancing over her elegant features and staying there’a rare occurrence lately, for her to maintain that sort of stability. Today, she could not afford to be reminded of anything else save the day that was at hand. Today, she would move on Sinclair, and that left no room for considerations of anything else. The payoff had been months in coming, and it seemed almost unlikely that today would finally be the day that the plan would come together. Her bag had been packed and ready all week’a bag just like the one she had been carrying with her for weeks now when she went to the Compound to work, only it had a false bottom that would hold her KB battle suit and the weapons that she would use to make Joseph Sinclair a distant memory. It was not far from the forefront of her thought process that today could very well be the last day she ever saw. A tiny sip of the warm and mild white tea that sat steaming in the cup at her side soothed away the nervous flutter that rose up with that thought. It wasn't so much that she feared losing her own life. It was somewhat redundant of a fear in a vampire, she supposed, even though all vampires feared Final Death, which only the most powerful of the Tzimisce clan had a way to circumvent. She knew from the moment she had accepted a position as a Captain in the Knightmare Brigade alongside her companion, even friend, Blaise McClancy, that she might be one day asked to lay down her life in the service of the Order of the Hammer. She wasn't unfamiliar to these sorts of sentiments. They were the commitments asked of all soldiers. But she hadn't been able to tell Virgil. Nor Dawn, Alexis, Rune, Cyrus, Vivica... not Gray, nor even Blaise, who was for all practical purposes her partner when it came to all things KB. None of them knew what Lia had been doing all this time, save that she had been doing some undercover work at an office. Aine Leanan and Ian Childress were the only ones who were at all aware of what was going on. Neither of them were aware of the intimate details’couldn't be. She had no secure place to communicate those details to either of her superior officers. The call could come at any time. She sat up this morning, waiting for it, knowing somewhere in her bones that it would most likely be early. Her only regret would be that she never got to tell any of them goodbye. Her cheeks flushed blood-red, tears threatening to tremble on the edge of her eyes. But a warrior had no time for tears. She closed her eyes and imagined Virgil's still-sleeping form, stretched out like a smooth-skinned lion in the middle of their king-sized bed. He'd be laying there, on his stomach, one leg curled up to his side, like he'd been trying to roll over and had stopped halfway. His head would be laying on his forearm, the other arm curled up underneath the pillow, his long, straight black hair spilling down his back and over his face. It was a position that would have made it impossible for her to kiss him without waking him up and getting him to move, and she sheltered a brief smile of annoyance. But it was brief. She imagined her fingers, curling through that silky hair, fangs distending to nibble gently on his bottom lip as she slid into bed next to her husband. He'd sleepily curl his arms around her, kiss her back more out of instinct than conscious reaction, and then he'd make that low, growling purr in his throat that meant he was happy, that there was no better way for him to wake up than this. It was a halcyon vision that seemed surrounded in her mind by a delicate gold filigree, just like a picture frame. But life was no picture frame, and she felt the French door out to the balcony opening almost before it moved. Squinting into the sunlight, Virgil, still quite naked, propped his chin on the sandy-colored wooden doorfacing. “Lia... someone just called for you.” His voice was sleepy, more tinged with Spain than ever, and it made her smile to think of him stumbling out of bed and answering the phone half coherently. But along with her amusement, her senses clawed themselves painfully awake from the calm. She gave him a serene smile, no movement in the soft depths of her green eyes. “Any message?” “No, she just said to tell you someone had called for you.” That was the signal. Lia stood up immediately, as it looked like she'd be forced to leave her cup of tea. Her rush back through the doors cued Virgil, who, despite being sleepy, managed to be fairly observant of her mannerisms most times. “Is there something I should know about?” he asked, stretching his arms behind him as he raised his eyebrows at her. Lia shook her head firmly. “No, just my contact from work. Needs me to come in early today to work on something. She's a bit skittish of other people picking up the phone.” He bought it, but she didn't know how long she'd hold out if he kept hanging around. She was in front of her dressing table almost immediately. Her battle gear wasn't anything particularly impressive’a drab dirt-brown tweed suit that was too frumpy to show her curves, with conservative square-toed dark brown pumps accented by a square gold buckle on the toe. She pulled her near waist-length ebony hair back into a mid-level ponytail, and curled it up into a coil, pinning it in place. Practiced fingers twirled open contact case lids, and slid in deep brown irises that made her eyes look like calming chocolate pools. Her eyes being one of the most distinguishing facial features she had, it wouldn't do to have them peeping out from under the fringe of auburn hair she was about to have. That went on next, a shoulder-length conservative cut wig, with thick bangs sliding down to cover her forehead. A thing coating of brown mascara and a dab of dark mauve lipstick was the last touch, and once again, she was Aurelie Mason for the day’conservative, but slightly clumsy, slightly shy businesswoman. The businesswoman who had gotten her an in at Joseph Sinclair's compound. And the businesswoman that, today, would put an end to Sinclair himself. She looked up at Virgil’even as someone else, he thought it was remarkable how striking she was. He kissed her softly on the cheek, a surprisingly loving gesture for a predator, but for those that traveled in mated pairs, as these two did, it was a protective gesture. He seemed to sense the weight of the day without even knowing what was going on. “I'll see you this afternoon...” “Te amo.” She smiled at him, and practically whispered the same. She shouldered her big brown leather shoulderbag, the one with the KB jumpsuit, battle recorder, and twin soul blades buried down in the bottom of them. The last look she got of him that morning was him leaning in the doorway of their bedroom, soft smile on his features and a warmth in those oceanic blue eyes that very few people ever got to behold. At least she would have that memory to hold in her palm as she went if today was her day to die. .:: ::: ::. Morning The morning crush had already started by the time she arrived at the Compound. The line at the metal detector was almost always long, but today, it seemed interminable. She didn't let anything other than placid resignation show on her face’otherwise, they'd know something was up. Aurelie Mason loved her job’the Lia behind Aurelie felt a little ridiculous having to play a cog, because she was anything but. But cogs who didn't question were not threatening, and in order for Lia to be able to go where she needed to go in the Compound, she had to be the epitome of unthreatening. And so, she was a mouse. She flipped her auburn hair behind her shoulders as she approached once again the metal detector. The guards looked bored, but they always looked bored. “Put your belongings in the tray, and step through, please.” She stepped through. Not a beep, especially considering she was using the Mark to cover up whatever metal shards she might have had stabbed through parts of her skin’the knuckle spikes in particular. The real thing was making sure her bag got through. If she managed to get that through, it was the biggest step down in a whole series of big steps’without her bag passing muster, she'd have none of the equipment that she needed to pull off the job. They seemed to scan it for an awfully long time, peering at it through the x-ray machine. But in the end, the guard on the stool passed it through, and she picked it up at the end of the conveyor belt. She shouldered her bag, and started towards the sea of cubicles in the open room downstairs. Hers was only one in the middle of all of that, and had she not had a purpose for being here, she would have dreaded seeing it again. A few minutes of preparatory work for the morning took up her quota of time. The whole Sinclair machine seemed to be run on dividing up teensy blocks of time so that employees were always doing something every second of every hour, no matter how useless it ended up being in the long run. She had grown to hate that about the place, but it was no different in any other office building, all across the world. She waited until about five minutes after the scheduled break, and stood up, stretching slowly. They tended to wonder about people who were too eager to take their breaks; it drew undue attention, especially in a place where everyone professed fanatic love of their job. She had made it a habit to stop off by the bathroom every morning first’and then over to the coffee machine for a cup. Break time was each employee's own, so she had often made it her habit to walk down to the bathrooms at the end of the hall, nearest Sinclair's favorite haunt. The view there was better, or so she said when anybody asked her, and it seemed like enough of an explanation to go over. At least, nobody asked any more than that, but more than likely, nobody was even remotely interested. Today, she took one of those long walks, and she took her usual two to three minutes in the bathroom’something that seemed reasonable. Nobody was over on this side of the building in the morning; Sinclair tended to conduct all of his business at lunch or later, so she saw not a soul’and the better for it’while she was waiting. Then, it was time to move. She shouldered her big, bulky bag once again, and felt for the crystal relay in her pocket. This one tiny piece of quartz was going to do her a big favor today. Out in a van about half a mile off was one of the Order's stranger agents’an illithid, perhaps one of the strongest psionic creatures in the world. Sinclair's haven was alive with scans for bugs and camera-plants... but nobody could detect a master psionic reading the goings-on This little crystal would allow the illithid to scry. And what the illithid could see and hear with that crystal relay, she would be able to see in her own quartz’in a pendant, hanging around her neck. She hadn't had much experience with scrying, but Annie Leanan had given her the crash course when she'd set her up with the illithid agent this past week. She could feel it probing her mind even at the moment, its senses alerted to the fact that she had her fingers wrapped around the crystal that was going to feed it its information. Instead of turning to the right, in the direction back towards her cubicle, she turned to the left, meandering casually back down the hall, her eyes in a totally different world than the drab and sterile environment set before her. Towards Sinclair's office. She could feel him lounging in there, so secure and smug in his happy little enclave. She had to try her hardest to keep from smiling. “Hey!” Muscle control had her jerk her neck up with a gasp of feigned surprise. She jerked her hand out of her coat pocket, holding the tiny crystal within her fingertips as she looked up. The two bodyguards stood near his door, both staring her down. They were slabs of walking meat, and Lia had taken to calling them Dirk and Biff in her head’the two best bodybuilder names she could think of. She looked up at them, her brown eyes widening in surprise. “Oh my gosh, I...” “Yeah, you walked in the wrong direction, little lady,” Biff said, standing up from his post. “Mr. Sinclair don't appreciate nosy little girls, so you might want to move along back to your box and go type some more or something.” It sounded like he was having to make supreme effort to insult her, but she supposed she should have given him a little credit’he hadn't dragged her off and raped her, so at least he was thinking with the upper head, even if those thoughts were a bit rudimentary, and barely past 'Oog hungry.' “I'm so sorry... I just got lost in my thoughts.” And with a flick of the wrist, she tossed the crystal down onto the deep piled carpet. She couldn't see quite where it landed, but her sharp eyes followed it past the heel of Dirk's boot, and very close to right under the crack beneath Sinclair's door. She didn't know if it would work’but she didn't see any reason why it wouldn't. Turning abruptly on her heel, she started meekly back towards the general office area, her arms pulled in towards her body and her eyes dragging on the carpet. She heard Biff and Dirk make some sort of comment about her, but she couldn't quite hear what it was. Nor did it really matter. The thought that she might have some useful information as soon as she sat back at her desk caused her steps to tap a little bit faster down the marbled hallway. They probably thought she was scared of them, but so much the better. .:: ::: ::. The water poured through its source, sparkling in the light that filtered through overhead. The wellspring was hot today, and she was glad of it. It needed to be. A soft feminine sigh carried her great relief on it as the water hit her skin, which was already slightly sweat-coated due to standing in the humid tile room while she prepared herself. For several long moments, she let it hit her, the water pounding on her skin like drumming fingertips. It felt like lightning strikes at first, it was so hot. Then as her skin got used to it, the pain gradually subsided into an ache, and then her muscles slackened and let go of the tension that had built up inside for the weeks coming up to this point. Nobody had intruded on her sanctum today, nor all of this evening. They would not, because they did not think of her as anything particularly special on this night. All eyes were on the Valentines and Knights and Whites and... gods only knew who else. But not her. Not the former World Champion. Warpath was about crowning a successor, someone who had come through the fire and beaten a large field of other opponents and had thus been dubbed worthy of taking on the mantle in the next few months. It was an ominous sentence to be the World Champion around Warpath time. It was an ominous sentence that she wanted. Lia Ambrosi was everybody's red-headed stepchild, the champion that was overrated, or never-should-have-been. But she'd been tired of that for a long time now’all the accolades in the world wouldn't have changed their mind. If she had to beat it into them, then she would. And she evidently had to. But the first course, the first action, was to be prepared. She could talk all the shit she wanted to, but if she wasn't prepared to make it so, then none of it mattered. That was something that had seemed to escape the roster lately, full of big-talking shitheads who couldn't compete in anything successfully, except perhaps a contest over who had the biggest ego. She was beginning to understand why none of her comrades were interested in the sport anymore. But walking away, would have been giving up. She had made herself a promise’this was to be a decisive battle. If she lost it, she would accept the fact that perhaps she wasn't cut out for this sport. If she won it, she would make a renewed effort to stamp her name into everyone's memories, and to prove herself. That, in the end, was all she really cared about. She enjoyed people taking notice of her. In truth, she even enjoyed the people who had begun to root for her against Mayhem. But she had to be able to answer to herself, and had she not set an ultimatum and kept to it, she knew she'd keep throwing herself at the ivory tower until she was broken. All those thoughts were, HAD to be, water under the bridge at this moment. Crystalline droplets beaded on her skin up around her shoulders, and in the cups of her shoulder blades. Prismatic rainbows gleamed a thousand times over in their tiny depths, but even the most trained eye would have missed their beauty, as they were small things, and people often tended to overlook small things. Steam billowed up towards the ceiling. If she looked up, she could see it puffing a humid footprint onto the gloss-coated ceiling, making yet another mirror-surface in this room in which she saw reflected a hundred thousand diamonds each catching the light and bouncing it off again in another direction. It was all light and heat at the moment, the water pounding down like a falls, down and down and through her epidermis, which wasn't really dirty’at least not on looking at it. She could feel it, though, that it was the inside of her that needed cleaning, not the out. One could care for the body all one wanted, but if the spirit were not groomed as well, then all these preparations were for naught. Slowly, she felt the tension release again, further than last time, and the dirt slid from her, whirling down into the drain with gravity and suction. She took a deep breath, then another. The moist air filled her lungs, hot and pleasant. It seeped in underneath the top layer of skin. She was all of that now’she was sparkling diamonds underneath rippling waves of piping hot water. Each breath in made the sheen glow brighter, like polishing a stone. She, too, was polished. She too, was purified. She, too, was cleansed. She was ready to begin again. .:: ::: ::. Mid-Day She had been waiting with baited breath all morning, ever since she'd dropped the relay. But no word had come, except faint, soft murmurings in the depths of her psyche, which she felt rather than heard sometimes. Nothing significant. At her desk, she was running out of things to do. She needed an excuse to take a lunch-break at her desk, but considering she usually feigned eating in the cafeteria, she hadn't brought a lunch with her, being generally not interested in it at all. The Compound had been busy today, and she'd had plenty of busy work to keep her from thinking overmuch about what was going on out in that van, and what the creatures might have been listening to, or hearing. It was getting to be too much. She checked the crystal for the umpteenth time, but there was nothing inside it. And that meant that she had to go back to work instead of fiddling with her necklace, because if she did it long enough, someone else was going to take notice of that necklace. And at the Compound, you never knew who and who didn't have the psionic power to figure out what she was doing with it. For the most part, they were all harmless, not to mention clueless, but there was no point in taking stupid chances. It was when she had settled back in to tapping on her computer that she felt a huge jolt go through her person. It was almost painful in the way that it worked its way through every nerve and synapse and tendon, snapping and electric. Her eyes took a moment to clear for a moment, but her fingers were already grasping the quartz pendant. She held it in between her thumb and index finger and looked deep into it. To an onlooker, it simply looked like a larger than average piece of quartz, reflecting its crystalline prisms onto the ceiling from the sunlight that trickled in through the window behind her. In it, Lia saw... Her vision blurred for a moment, then came back, in colors more vivid than any she had ever seen. There was an echo in her mind, a sort of humming chant that sounded distinctly like more than one person talking. She could have concentrated on that all day and perhaps even fallen asleep in her cubicle, it was so soothing. The rational part, however, was very not soothed at all, considering the noise came from a beast that particularly enjoyed human brains. Slowly, it cleared, and she heard, clear as day, the conversation. “...it has been a trying day, Reginald. I'd much rather leave early if I could.” Her countenance fell. If he left early, there was no way she'd be able to pull this off today, and Sinclair was going out of the country within the next week some time, for an indefinite amount of time. “I understand that very well, sir. However, there's still the matter that needs to be dealt with, and you're the only one who can do it.” She didn't know what they were talking about, but didn't really need to. It wasn't important, save the fact that they get to some numbers. She heard a gusty, arrogant sigh from Sinclair. “Very well. What time do I have to be prepared for this meeting?” “Two o'clock, sir. I will tell them to expect to be finished by three.” “Very good.” Very good indeed, Lia thought to herself. Three o'clock, or a little after’well within the time between two and four that Sinclair insisted on being unguarded. He put it to both himself and his employees that he was guarded all day, and needed a little privacy. She had to move fast. The humming slowly died down in her consciousness as she let the pendant fall forward and thump flat-side down against the skin in between her collarbones. It crossed her mind briefly that, after all this time had elapsed, Aine Leanan might want to know when the plan was about to come to fruition. Her mind scanning quickly through her options, she thought of the Comm in her bag, and thought of trying to go into the bathroom and get a signal out. Too risky. No matter how much she wanted to talk to Aine, who had become practically her only link to someone she could really talk to since she'd started this job, it simply wasn't possible. It would have endangered both herself and Aine, her mission, and the Order. None of that was acceptable, none. Aine would know roughly that the time to move was soon, and that would have to be the extent of it until the job was done, and she could say that to her old friend face to face. It fell on her all at once’this could quite possibly be the last sentient moments that she spent as Lia Ambrosi, in this body, in this consciousness. She'd leave behind Rune and Alexis and Cyrus’three lost souls who, for some reason or other, had ended up clinging onto her proverbial cape. She loved them all for different reasons, Rune for his inability to tell people that he cared, Alexis for her dogged insistence on knowing things that she probably didn't need or want to know, and Cyrus for his matter-of-factness, his all-business demeanor that really did conceal very little of the personal, because that's who he was, save having a rather macabre sense of humor. Dawn McFarlane, the epitome of stubborn. But for all she was stubborn, Lia loved her, or perhaps loved her because of all that. She wasn't so stubborn, anyway, once you had worked underneath that gruffness and gotten her to lay in your arms, quiet. She liked to be held, and to hold, and she liked the warmth of someone else against her’she, at the very basest level was concerned only with human contact, real contact that touched her deep down within, and Lia couldn't blame her for seeking wherever she could find it. She clung to it with a determination that would have rivaled any bulldog. And then there was Virgil Ambrosi’her husband, her lion. He'd protected her fiercely once they'd come to know one another again, and they had a love that not even Lia herself could fathom the depths of. It was he who came to mind more often than any of the others when she thought about the dangers of her current work. She had thought, lately, and perhaps a bit morbidly, that if she lay dying, she'd imagine that she was in his arms for a few moments longer, if only to have peace as she left the world. She wondered if she'd have time to press the button on her Comm to send the message she'd readied for him, should she go. But these were thoughts for people who chose to dwell in the present, and attempt to hold it in the palm of their hand. Lia couldn't do that, nor could they, but she had the great fortune of realizing this, instead of continuing the feeble attempt. It was time for her to take action’and she was not fearful of the potential consequence of it. She was reminded of something that she had heard someone say, once: no goodbyes, only good memories. .:: ::: ::. She was alone in the room, and it echoed like a cavernous chamber with the emptiness. She remembered a time, not too very long ago, when this room would have been packed to the gills with people milling around waiting to get to their business. And now it was only her. That did not, of course, take away from the fact that she had business here. Some battles would always need to be fought, and some warriors would not always be able to take up the cross, but that was neither here nor there when one was prepared to do what one had to do. She stood near-naked, her hair tied up in a twist on her head until she was ready to take it down. She slid into her short pants with ease, buckling the clasp of the belt that she had already twisted through its straps. The garment hugged her hips with ease, and she noted with satisfaction that though she had worn it many times, it had not yet started to lose its elasticity. The top matched it, and hooked across the front with similar buckles to the one on her belt. It only came to just below her breasts, but it hugged the curves of her upper body with tenacity, and left her free to breathe and move as she fought. Its sleeves were long, and fit slimly around the muscular curvature of her arms. She twisted her arms to and fro; good fit, still. It was familiar fabric that she'd worn every time she fought since she'd won the World Title against Lestatt and Dawn. It had memories. Now it was time for the bigger guns. She stepped one foot into the calf-tall leather and steel boot that enshrined those dangerous feet of hers. The buckles went on one by one, the somber clicks of the latches coming along with a certain feeling of impending doom’and not hers. Her right foot slid into the other boot just as easily. And this was The Foot. It was The One when it came to delivering the powerful busaiku kick that had become her trademark since she unveiled it. With reverence, she ran her fingers down the clasps of the boot, slowly buckling herself in. Her feet felt heavy now, like they were girded with plate mail. There were only the two left’slowly she slid her right hand into the leather fingerless glove. With precision, she snapped the small button on its back. The same for the left. As he flexed her hands, the tinny creak of the leather was reassuring. She could smell it on her hands, and it reminded her of the many times that her opponents must have smelled the same as they lay defeated at her feet. And now, she reached up and unwound her coiled up, straight black hair. It had dried off nicely, and was thick and scented with jasmine. It hung down her back, encircled by a hair tie, but there was one more accent piece’Lia's fingers deftly snapped around the coil of hair a metal band. Silver accents stood out in dazzling contrast to her dark, dark hair. The picture that responded to her every move in the mirror was a pleasing one. It was her refracted, reminding her of all the previous times that she had done this’no matter what was at stake, this was no different than a regular night. The same stakes on the line, and the same experience behind her hands and her feet. The same spirit guiding her movements. The story practically wrote itself. .:: ::: ::. Afternoon It was afternoon break, the last tangible barrier between work and home. After its fifteen minute span, everyone would throw themselves back into their work with renewed vigor, in hopes of somehow making the last two hours of it disappear. Lia's (or rather Aurelie's) heart was in her throat as she shouldered that ugly bag one more time, and made for the distant bathroom that would take her close enough to Sinclair's office to move. Once inside and alone, it was time to move. They didn't keep cameras in the bathrooms to her knowledge, but even if they did, it wouldn't matter by the time she got done with all this within the next twenty minutes. Off came the wig, first. Her black hair was flat but still full underneath it as she pulled down the pins that held it. It cascaded down her back, kinked from having been pinned up so tightly, but she loosened it with her fingers, and it lay a little flatter. The brown contacts were pulled from her eyes, thrown into the trash beside the mirror’and there was Lia's own face again, looking back at her from the mirror with hurried, but not frightened, eyes. And then, the last touches’she shimmied out of her suit and its trappings, and slid into the black, slim-fitting battle jumpsuit of the Order. The patch with the flaming unicorn on it identified her as part of the Knightmare Brigade. She slid the tiny electronic battle recorder into place; hovering somewhere near the edge of her left eyesocket, it would record everything that she saw today, and later provide a record for the Order's files of Sinclair's death. A few buttons pressed, and it was recording’its setting right now would transmit only directly to Aine Leanan. There were the final touches of course. From the bottom corner of the bag she pulled the weapons that she would use to make the hit’soul blades. They were made specifically for users with the Mark, because they were virtually undetectable, and mimicked the Mark's somewhat inter-dimensional qualities. With them on, Lia's Mark became even more obvious, and she could herself see it warping the space around her, subtly chewing at the visual edges of it. Not like Korwyn Malik would when showing his, nor Ian Childress himself, but enough that it would identify her as one of is holders if anyone saw her. The soul blades fastened along the bottom edge of her forearm, and had edges that she slid her fingers through’to keep her arm straight and out of their way, but to give her some added armor, and also more control. They were psionic, so even a being like Lia whose psionic control was limited to the disciplines she had learned as her master's Childe, could simply think the draw command, and the blades would respond. It was one among many things the blades would respond to’whether to be short of long length, whether to be serrated or clean edged, whether to use its Mark, or to hover at the fringes of invisibility. They were powerful, versatile weapons, and the moment she had seen them, she had known that this was what she would need to use to kill Joseph Sinclair. That was that, then, and now’it was time to move. Strapped for battle, she took several deep breaths, as Ian had taught her, warping her Mark slowly into an invisible barrier that wrapped around her. When she could no longer see herself in the mirror, it was time to move’those with the Mark could possibly see through the barrier, depending on the skill, but most of the people in the Compound did not possess it at any level to pose a danger to her. Sinclair's meeting had gotten out half an hour ago, and he had said something to his guard about enjoying the afternoon until he had to tend to his evening business. He'd be in his office along until at least five-thirty. Carefully stepping, so as to hide the noise of her footsteps, and the impression they might make on a deep rug, she emerged from the bathroom, fingers clutched tight in the soul blades' controls. It was not more than fifty steps down the hall, and the bodyguards were on their allotted two-hour rest period. It was a stupid vulnerability for Sinclair to allow himself to have, but hubris often came with as much power as a being like him had. But he did not have enough power to avoid the Fate that was coming to him now. She inched towards the office, concentrating every step of the way on keeping her Mark intact. There was not even a reflection of her in the highly polished marble floors. Ian would have been proud of her stealth, but Ian wasn't here to see it right now’and would not see it even on the battle recorder, because she might have been invisible to that too; she wasn't sure. Slipping the door was the hardest part, but she had known that it would be. Her hand touched the knob, and turned it slowly, attempting to make as little noise as possible. If she could catch Joseph Sinclair unaware, then so much the better, but it was not likely. As soon as she was inside the office, the thought the draw commands: 'Draw. Serrated. Long.' The soul blades unsheathed, making her arms twitch with the speed. They sounded like drawing a sword in the middle of a museum to her. And that was all it took to let Joseph Sinclair know what he was dealing with. The big leather chair turned, and she found herself looking into the disgusted face of a man who she had grown to hate for what he'd done to the man who trained her’no matter if the grudge was personal or not, if she did not believe in her general, she wouldn't have been able to perform the hit in good faith. He nodded, noting her uniform. “I was wondering when Ian would decide to exercise his personal vendetta. And a member of the Knightmare Brigade, too! Good, then that means he'll be personally offended when I kill you.” He was cocky, but then, he also had a gun. That was where Lia realized that she had the advantage’whether it was filled with normal, iron or silver bullets, Sinclair didn't realize that none of them would really harm her. He didn't know what she was. He fired the gun almost before she could think, and it took all her resources to roll to the ground and dodge. When she was back up on her feet, he preternatural speed propelled her over to his bookcase, dodging another two bullets before she came close enough to start considering the use of her blades. It was a revolver’it would only have six in the chamber, and he had used three. Another fired at her as she ducked, and clawed herself another step closer on the carpet. She rolled to the side, into the shelter of the desk's front, then sprang to her feet. Sinclair went to fire point blank, but Lia jabbed the soul blades underneath the bottom of the gun, and the shot ricocheted up and harmlessly lodged itself in the ceiling. There wasn't much Sinclair could do now’she leaped onto his desk, standing over him with a sneer. “Your brother sends his regards.” “Give him mine.” He fired the last bullet and it ripped through her shoulder with searing pain’but not enough pain to keep her from remembering why she was here. “Nazdar, Mr. Sinclair.” SLASH. .:: ::: ::. Last: Dusk Dusk was always supposed to be the end of the story, because when the sun set, everyone went to sleep. Everybody forgot about the story that had been told the previous day, and woke up ready for a new one, since they'd already forgotten the old one. Lia might have awoken with them, but too, she ruled the evening they hid from. She stood enshrined in a glittering mantle in the darkness that they tried to ignore. The simple truth of the matter was, there was hardly a single demesne where Lia Ambrosi was not Queen. There would be no story ending here tonight, but the beginning of a new chapter, a new leaf scribbled with the beginnings of what would turn out to be poetry. And people didn't always appreciate poetry for its good points, not immediately. She didn't plan on worrying about whether or not they accepting what she had to do tonight, she just planned on doing it. She was girded, ready to go. Everything was strapped into place, and her mind was sharp, ready for the physical challenge that she was going to face tonight. Her heart swelled in her chest with the anticipation of the battle before her. It was time that turncoats would pay for their sins, and it was time that the bully would become the victim. Bryan Mayhem had hurt enough people that she cared about, and it was time for him to do the hurting. She was only waiting now, waiting for someone to give her the signal that the battle was to begin. And as she stood waiting in the wings, she knew it would only be seconds now. She was ready for this. She had practically been born ready, the Fates sweeping up behind her and bearing her along on an inevitable tide. The tech nodded to her, and she stepped into place behind the curtain. The first strains of the Cruxshadows' “Winterborn” began to dance through the arena, and she took a deep breath. A fanged and dangerous grin overtook her features for a moment: it was go time. The curtain parted, and into the sunlight she went. Most would cheer her, no doubt, but she had the requisite battlecry buried inside her being if she needed to provide her own. Whether or not Bryan Mayhem realized it (and there was little doubt that he wouldn't), she was a one woman army, waiting only for the right moment to lead the charge across no man's land. Others might have called it stupid, or even fatalistic, but Lia Ambrosi had decided long, long ago that she would rather go out in a blaze of glory, and on her own terms, than to be forced groveling out on her knees. She'd never been a good groveller. There was no turning back now’onward she marched. To the victor go the spoils.
