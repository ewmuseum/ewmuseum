Flashback

28 September 2005

Date: 9-28-05 MORNING “What the hell are you looking at?” I nodded in the vicinity of the new intern as she bounced across the room. Landon turned abruptly. “Don’t make it so damn obvious,” I said. I peered over Landon’s shoulder at her as she noticed us and acknowledged our gaze with a smile. “Are you serious man?” Landon asked. “What?” “Ryan man, there’s nothing special about her,” he said. “Besides in comparison to what you go home to every night, that girl is nothing.” I looked down at the documents that adorned my large mahogany desk and counted the seconds it would take for Landon to start rearranging my name plate, cup full of pens, and picture frames that attributed to the mess. “Yeah, you’re probably right,” I said. “Look, if you must cheat, and I firmly advise that you don’t, check out the broad at the water cooler,” he said. “She’s Tony’s new secretary and I’ve been watching her this past week, everyday is casual Friday for that vixen.” I looked out of my office and over towards the water cooler where a tall, dark-haired woman was bending over to refill her little snow cone cup full of water. Her jeans were exceptionally snug, so much so that they seemed as though they were airbrushed on, and her blouse just covered the essentials. I looked at Landon and shook my head in disbelief. “She’s just like Robin, except obviously skankier and less intelligent if she’s only a secretary,” I said. “Now replay what you just said in your mind,” Landon said without hesitation. “That’s the perfect woman! She’s just dumb enough to believe everything you say and the combination of her easiness and lack of an elite job will ensure plenty of time to sex it up with you.” I laughed aloud and watched as the new intern glided from cubicle to cubicle delivering various files to all agents that hadn’t yet made partner. I was enthralled by the way her blonde pony tail bounced as she pranced. “Maybe you should go after her,” I said returning my attention to Landon. “Yeah I’ll do that,” he replied sarcastically. “We can go on a double date with you and Robin and the two of you can talk over our heads and we can nod and pretend to be interested, all the while shamelessly groping each other under the table.” “Ah shit!” “What?” “You just reminded me I’m supposed to meet Madison for dinner tonight. Apparently we’re going to discuss the logical progression of our relationship to the next level or something like that,” I said as I rolled my eyes. “Those are always a buzz kill.” He hesitated and I could tell he was thinking, which didn’t happen often, so I waited for what would inevitably be a dumb ass remark alluding to me having more sex or something to that affect. “You better play nice tonight,” he said catching me off guard. I momentarily shook the petite blonde intern from my thoughts and listened rather intently to Landon for quite possibly the first time since he explained to me how to get to second base in eighth grade. “Seriously though bro,” he said nodding in the general vicinity of the water cooler despite the fact that the secretary had returned to her office. “If she didn’t elicit a response from your secondary brain then you might as well stay with Robin, for good.” “What are you trying to say man?” “I’m trying to say that if she gives you the whole marriage spill, maybe you should think about it this time,” Landon said. “You two are perfect for each other. You two are every parent’s wet dream of relationships. You’re a successful sports agent, she’s making bank as a pharmaceutical sales rep. You’re both genetic marvels, who eat right, exercise, and generally make the rest of the general public quite sick. You two just make sense man; it’s high time you admitted it to yourself.” “I know, I know,” I said as I tried to dismiss his advice. “Golf tomorrow?” I asked tugging at my collar. “Yeah, I’ll bring the beers,” Landon said excusing himself. “There was little doubt,” I said as I plopped down in the large black swivel chair. I interlocked my fingers behind my head as I leaned back and propped my loafers up on desk. AFTERNOON “I’m out,” I said to nobody in particular as I closed my heavy office door and weaved my way through all the cubicles, toting my briefcase in hand. I looked all around the office, but didn’t see her. I was, however, fairly certain that Tony’s secretary had winked at me as I passed his office, but I politely nodded, forced a half smile, and focused on the exit sign above the double glass doors at the front of our building. I walked briskly, rehearsing various responses to anticipated questions for tonight’s climatic dinner. As I got to the part where I say “I’m not ready,” I collided with something, or rather someone. I heard myself yell as I dropped my briefcase to the ground and the steaming contents of a Styrofoam cup spilled out all over my blue blazer and the white silk shirt beneath it. “SHIT!” “Oh my gosh! I’m so sorry!” wailed a voice I didn’t recognize. “What the hell?” I said. “Oh sir, sir, I’m so sorry,” the voice pleaded. The intense burning did not subside, but at the first notion of hands wiping away at my shirt I looked up and noticed the blonde intern. “How about watching where you’re going” Figure it out dot com!” I said. “Sir, I really didn’t mean to?” I gently took her wrists and removed her hands from my mid-section. “I’m going to lose my job, I’m going to lose my job,” I heard her whisper while simultaneously trying not to hyperventilate. I looked into her emerald eyes as the tears welled up and the breathing increased. Her pale skin had quickly transformed into a lobster red that was significantly darker than her thin pink lips. I surveyed the situation and became acutely aware that everyone in the office was still watching. She was much shorter than I and put both hands on her shoulders and drew her in just a bit. “It’s going to be fine,” I assured her. “Don’t worry about it. I promise I won’t sue.” She sniffled and forced a smile. “Seriously, it’s a cheap suit anyhow,” I lied. “I’m done for the day anyhow. I’ll rinse it out and it’ll be fine. No worries.” She looked up at me still trembling. “Let me give you my number,” she insisted. Immediately I thought to myself, now why would you do that, besides I have a girlfriend. I was a vain sonvabitch, but it was something I’d come to terms with a long time ago. “Now why would you do that?” I said somewhat baffled. “Well after I get fired you can call and send me the bill for the dry cleaners. It’s only right that I pay for it,” she said. “What’s your name doll?” She crinkled her nose still looking up at me. “Paige,” she said softly. “Well listen here Paige,” I said. “I assure you that it’s really not that big of a deal. Accidents happen. Now if you’ll excuse me?” “I insist Mr. Stephens,” she said. I watched as her tiny hand reach inside my jacket, gently brushing against my chest as she retrieved a pen from my pocket. “You big shots always keep pens there,” she said smiling and wiping her eyes with the back of her left forearm. She grabbed my right hand with her left and scrawled her telephone number and name in nearly illegible print across the top of my palm. Robin had immaculate hand-writing. I hated it. I felt the hairs stand on the back of my neck as I surveyed first my palm, and then the rest of the office. Everyone had gone on about their business and that made me slightly less uncomfortable. She extended her hand and I took the pen and put it back in my jacket pocket. As her hands brushed against one another I felt my heart pound against the inside of my chest as though it was trying to jump out and I said quickly, “I have to run.” “You don’t have to do anything,” she said quietly as I side-stepped her in route to the door. As she stood their regaining her composure I mustered a wave and continued out the door as I heard, “Seriously, you better call.” I had heard that plenty of times I thought to myself, but this was a different scenario and something inside me told me that I might just take her up on her offer. LATER THAT EVENING I heard the doorbell ring and quickly lifted the blue-collared shirt off of its? hanger and slipped into it as I made my way across the living room to my front door. I took a quick gander through the peep hole and laughed before swinging the door open. “Since when do you ring the door bell as opposed to your usual method of intrusion?” I asked Landon as he extended a bottle of cheap champagne in my direction. “I didn’t want Robin to get all bent out of shape and claim I was ruining your special night together,” he explained as I took the champagne mildly perplexed. “Nah, she’s in the shower, but to be honest I think I’m going to move to a neighborhood where you don’t live two doors down. I see enough of you at the office. I might as well be married to you,” I said with a laugh. I held up the bottle of champagne. “How much did this run you?” “It was about four bucks at the filling station right up the road,” he said. “I figured you’d need something special to commemorate the night. “Plus I figured you might be in a bad mood after having scalding coffee spilt all over one of your suits.” “Yeah, you saw that?” “Oh no, I wasn’t there or anything. I had deuced out way before then. You know me and work,” he said. “Besides I had to go get you this bottle of champagne in case you proposed tonight or something.” “Yeah right, what am I going to do” Wrap a rubber band around her finger?” I looked over my shoulder and listened carefully. The water was still running. “Actually,” I said leaning in closer to Landon. “I was thinking of calling Paige, the one that spilled the coffee on me and seeing if maybe she wanted to grab a bite after we played golf tomorrow.” Landon looked at me appalled. “Have you already been indulging on the celebratory juices my friend” You have a smoking hot broad that practically lives with you in your shower right now and you’re thinking about that little blonde intern girl, who’s not that hot, and clumsy to boot?” “Maybe,” was all that came out. “Dude, even I’m not that stupid!” he said. “Well maybe it’s not stupidity,” I said mustering a little courage. “Sometimes I think I’m with Robin for all the wrong reasons. I mean I realize that we make sense or whatever, but we’ve never exactly set the world on fire.” “Gorgeous, rich, smart, athletic, stable, she’s got all the ingredients man.” “Yeah, she’s got a lot to offer, but with Robin, I already know how my life would turn out,” I said. “Five years from now, ten years from now, I can see it all.” “Well then wise up and marry her man. Besides, this is the only rational advice I’ve ever given you,” he said pausing. “Or anyone for that matter.” “Maybe I don’t want to know what’s going to happen from day to day man. Maybe I want a little spontaneity, some unpredictability. I’m completely enamored by Paige, and I really think I should take her out,” I explained. “If nothing else, it will help me make sure Robin is the one for me.” “Is that what you’re going to tell her when she finds out” She’s not the dumb Barbie’s you’ve dated in the past.” I heard the water stop running. “Look man, I got a short window here once the hair dryer starts going.” “Don’t do it.” “Thanks man,” I said holding up the bottle of Andre and shut the door in his face. I anticipated she might call out for me, but she didn’t and when I heard the blow dryer I slithered back into the bed room and removed the tiny slip of paper from my wallet where I had transferred Paige’s number, after promptly washing it from my palm upon leaving the office. DINNER I sipped on my draft beer, but she had declined a drink when the waiter propositioned her so I knew she had something serious to discuss tonight. I wondered how long it would take before she began what would ultimately be a small tirade and I continued to rehearse responses to various scenarios as we made small talk. I had pretty much settled on the “I’m just not ready for that quite of commitment,” response, quite sure that this was going to be another “we should move in together” spill. I wasn’t prepared for what was coming. “What’s wrong with you tonight?” she asked. I almost started in with “I’m not ready,” but snapped out of it just in time. “Nothing, why?” “I don’t know, you just seem out of it tonight and I wanted tonight to be special.” “Every night with you is special,” I heard myself say. That was pretty good I told myself internally and she smiled and reached across the table for my hand. My initial panic was offset when I didn’t see any ink smudges on my hand and I looked back at her with a nervous smile. She really was beautiful. “I’ve been waiting for the perfect moment to tell you this,” she started. “I have some really good news.” Good news. Good news? My mind raced. “I’m pregnant.” I’m glad I hadn’t taken a drink or I would’ve sprayed it all over the restaurant. So that’s why she hadn’t ordered a beer this evening. I held on to the table in front of me with the hand she wasn’t holding, so as not to succumb to the dizzy sensation that was overcoming me. “When” How do you know?” She looked at me kind of sad, kind of confused and smiled slightly out of the side of her mouth. “Well I was a couple of weeks late and I took the test a few days ago, three times, all positive.” “Well I’d say that’s pretty clear indication,” I said. “I’m pregnant!” I yelled and I promptly heard a few muffled laughs and a lot of clapping as I looked across at Robin, careful to maintain a tight grip on the table. “So’You’re happy?” she said. “Elated,” I responded with a smile, but I couldn’t stop thinking about Paige. Certainly I couldn’t take her out now, not after all of this. Or maybe I still could. Maybe this was one big elaborate hoax and Robin was testing me. Yeah, women test men all the time’try and see what kind of reaction they can get and stuff. This could be Robin’s way of seeing if I was ready to take our relationship to the next level, something I’d obviously never done before, nor intended on doing before thirty. But what if she really was pregnant? If I had a kid on the way, I needed to propose and we needed to move in together and sell her apartment. There was so much shit to do. THE FOLLOWING EVENING I had convinced Robin to enjoy a lavish bubble bath complete with candles surrounding the tub while I went out to rent the two of us a movie, and probably buy Bill Cosby’s book Fatherhood too, if I saw it in the store. On the way to the video store I drove by the sandwich shop I’d asked Paige to meet me at and I couldn’t resist the urge to pull into the parking lot and take a peak. There she was, thirty minutes after I was supposed to arrive, sitting their anxiously awaiting my arrival. It didn’t seem like her to wait that long, but maybe she’d felt the same thing I had. I knew she was sitting there telling herself I wasn’t worth it, to just leave, that I was like every other douche in an expensive suit. I wanted so badly to go inside, to kiss her porcelain cheek, to laugh with her, to take her to a baseball game and snuggle under a blanket while we sipped eight dollar beers. She’d be up for it too; she’d be up for anything spontaneous and exciting. I took a deep breath, put my sports car in reverse and said goodbye to my first love. I needed to go home to Robin and either Rhiannon or Cade for we’d discussed it thoroughly after last night’s dinner and after I’d wrapped a blue rubber band around her ring finger. It’d have to do for now.
