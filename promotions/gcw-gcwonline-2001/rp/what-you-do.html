Date: Nebulous Head pounding, eyes nearly gummed shut. You wonder where the hell you are, before you realize it’s a hotel room. Different accommodations, different town, same carbon copy room. Maybe the paints change just a little bit, but the furniture is all the same, the channels move around on the same crappy TV set. In days gone by you would have found something better, now you just don’t care. It’s still not a money issue you could afford a better hotel, you just don’t feel the need anymore. It’s just going to be you, four walls and dead silence. This is your new life. Rolling over your hand reaches for something. You know it should be there, if you’ve stayed true to form it’ll be on the night stand just waiting. Your fingers touch room temperature glass, and you know you’ve found what you’re searching for. Fingers probing you open the bottle, the stench of raw whiskey fills the room, it’s nearly enough to open your eyes. One gulp kills the pain building in your temples, another settles your stomach. You try and sit up, but someone must have snuck in an put an anvil on your chest. Same thing happens every night. Taking a pull big enough to kill half the bottle, and the sun streaming in through the curtains doesn’t seem quite so sharp. The warmth from the liquor threatens to burn you alive, roast you in your own juices. You either need to slow down, or speed up. Either would work, neither seems like a good idea. You keep drinking, it’s the only thing you really know how to do well anymore. By the time you finish the anvil’s melted away. You can finally move, it’s time to get up and face the day. The fire has faded away into an aura of warm that surrounds you like a halo. This is your shield, it’ll keep them out and keep you safe. Stumbling to the bathroom, you nearly fall down, stumbling over the beer cans and empty bottles littering the floor. That was last night, before the bar, after the bar, you don’t know. You take a piss but decide not to shave, really what’s the point. It’s not like you’re trying to impress someone, at least not someone you have any chance of ever seeing again. You didn’t mean to think of that, think of her. Just like that the warmth fades away, and you’re cold. Taking a step from the bathroom to the main sleeping area is like stepping out of a hot kitchen into a sub zero weather. You shiver, and go about looking for something more to drink. You couldn’t possibly have drank everything, but the sick feeling in your gut says you did. Looked like it was time for another trip to the liquor store. Maybe a bar it wasn’t like you needed to be anywhere today. You could spend the entire day there. Just like every other day you didn’t have to wrestle. You don’t want to blame anyone else but yourself for the sad state your life has become, but you can’t help it. You blame everyone, god, country, family. You blame her for not loving you enough, you blame her brother for doing the wrong thing at the wrong time. It’s just too damn hard to lay all the blame at your own doorstep. Of course when it all comes down to it, this is all your own fault. The great karmic pay off for all the rotten things you’ve done in life. But then again, there’s always HIM. Yeah, it’s alright, you can blame HIM. There’s a good chance that if it hadn’t been for HIM, none of this would have happened. He’s the one who took her, he’s the one who made her crazy, he’s the one that made her need to forget. So yeah, maybe all this is HIS fault. That makes you feel a little bit better. Well, not better, just angry, but angry is better than nothing you think. At least it’s not empty, at least it’s not the void that try as you might you just can’t fill up. So you try and direct your anger towards HIM, towards Lestatt. Kidnapper, stealer of your life, defiler, liar. You can’t think of enough vile words to describe him. It’s time to make him pay. You’ve spent the better part of the last month completely besotted, acting the fool, in hopes that you’d be able to come back. You had your true self from everyone, they said you were to sick to compete. But you knew the truth, you were ok, you just needed a drink to make the pain go away. So you showed them, competed in the most handicapped fashion you could muster, and you proved once again that you’re on the top of the game, even at your worst. You think of Lestatt, but more than that you think of his blood covering your hands. You think of what it must feel like to smash his face into a wall. You think of how much fun you’d have killing him. The last time you felt human you were pounding his skull. It was revenge, a sick need but it was what you needed. You can’t wait to do it again. HE put you here, and he deserves to pay. You’ll make him pay. At least you think you will. Instead you turn into the bar. Sit down. Order a drink. Forget.
