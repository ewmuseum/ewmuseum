Judicial Jeopardy: Take II

12 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 12, 2005 Recorded September 21 appeared at the bottom of the screen. He stood on the courthouse steps and watched his best friend walk away. He hadn't seen him in months, but the first thing that came to his mind of how less troubled he was during those days gone by. The two of them traveled across the country, filling many of the bingo halls frequented by the industries top independent organizations. For three years they ran rampant through many of the top stars from 1997 to 2000. During that time Jake learned a lot from Darren. In fact, Jake began to look at Darren as more than just a tag-team partner. Instead Jake felt as if Darren had become part of his family, a brother that he never had. The two of them shared many special moments in the sport and for that Jake would always feel indebted to the many formerly known as 'Hard Rock.' Darren and his young son continued to walk away. Jake waved and turned back to his attorney, the same beady-eyed individual that Ryan Stephens had suggested to him. 'Take my advice, this guy will do you right,' was the lone statement that kept playing through Jake's head. Jake studied the man who was straight out of 'Evening Shade.' The balding lawyer was dressed in tattered brown khakis and a green jacket that could only be described as a tailor's mishap. With one sleeve longer than the other, the two-tone lime colored jacket and the worn slacks clashed to say the least. However Jake always tried to remind himself to not judge a book by its cover. For all he knew this man could be the next 'OJ lawyer' lying in wait. The legal discussion had yet to begin, but at this point Jake was beginning to become a doubting Tom on whether things would turn out in his favor. The two of them, Jake and his attorney, the incomparable Franklin Felix Foxworthy IV began to make their way up the steps. Suddenly the fledgling lawyer dropped his briefcase, to which scattered papers amuck on the white marble steps. The lawyer stared intently at Jake, as if to say please help. Trying to conceal his humor and what dignity had he left over, after associating with such an entity, Jake reluctantly obliged. Finally the two men had all the documents back in hand, briefcase per say. 'Thanks Mr. Summers. Now what were you saying about heaven?' asked the lawyer who pulled at his jacket, trying to conceal the white nametag sticker. Perplexed by the presence of the nametag on the lawyer's tacky jacket, Jake did the first thing that came to mind, 'Oh I was just praying that things will go smoothly. What's the name tag for?' asked the former GCW tag-team champion. His lawyer pondered his thoughts before answering Jake. 'You have nothing to worry about Mr. Summers. We'll take that slut for everything she's worth!' stated the fidgety Franklin. 'Slut?' and 'everything she is worth?' thought Jake. He knew Ryan Stephens suggested him, but how stupid was this lawyer. This was a child custody/visitation hearing, not a lawsuit for money and Jake still loved his wife very much so. Jake certainly wasn't out for blood by no means. Instead he just simply wanted to come to an agreement for visitation, before their divorce preceding took place. He still held onto a slim chance that perhaps they could reconcile. Nevertheless Jake smiled as the two men entered into the court house. 'So I was saying, what's that nametag for?' asked Golden Boy once again, pointing to the crinkled up paper in the lawyer's grasp. 'Oh just a price tag from the department store my girlfriend bought if from. You know, the little misses some times forgets to removes the tags and stuff, but she's a keeper.' replied the lawyer. Who was this guy trying to fool, 'Little misses and a keeper?' Heck the guy couldn't get laid in a hen house, let alone have a woman care enough for him that she'd shop for him as well. Simply stated, the guy looked more like a poster child for Dial-A-Date rather than Casanova. He was a regular Don Juan Degeeko to say the least. Jake and his lawyer entered the small room where their visitation hearing for Jacob Summers III and Jeb Summers would take place. Jeb was not Faith's biological son, but two years ago after coming to their home Mrs. Summers adopted him as her own. As Jake took his seat, the two lawyers exchanged handshakes. Meanwhile Faith stood at the opposite end of room, admiring the vibrant sun that shined through the window which she stared out. 'Lovely day isn't it?' asked Jake of his wife. 'We should be out on the beach with the boys instead of in here.' he continued. As Faith took her seat, she simply nodded ignoring her soon to be ex-husband's suggestion. “Mr. Summers, my name is Rory Callahan, I’ll be representing Mrs. Summers in this hearing,” Faith’s lawyer stated, extending his hand to Jake. “I’ve took the time to prepare a schedule that I think both you and your wife will find appealing to each of you,” he continued. The obese Irish lawyer handed Jake a document, which suddenly his own lawyer tore from his hands. Jake entered the room fully peaceful, willing to compromise with whatever his wife suggested. However now he sit across the table from her at the peak of exploding. In the back of his mind, he knew that they didn’t need any lawyers, but two weeks ago when he received a letter in the mail stating the date and time for the meeting, it was also requested he show up with his lawyer. Sure Franklin Felix Foxworthy IV may have meant well, but at this moment he was doing more harm than good. Faith look straight through him and the only thing Jake could do is just set there. He wanted to say that this wasn’t what he wanted, but yet he was speechless. “This is most certainly not appropriate. Why your client even try such is beyond me” Obviously she is not willing to compromise. I suggest you the two of you work on this agreement and meet us at a later date,” Felix yelled as he stood up. Had his loud voice not drawn their attention that of his chair slamming against the wall would have. The force of his suddenly uprising had caused the chair to slide across the room. “No!” Jake yelled as he rose and headed towards the door. His attorney was quickly run after him. He caught Jake midway down the hall. “I told you Mr. Summers everything would work out. We have them off their balance. Her lawyer should be calling any minute now giving into our demands.” Franklin stated. Jake suddenly stopped in his tracks and turned back to the skinny lawyer, “What the hell were you thinking” Ryan Stephens is an asshole and so are you!” he said walking away. “I’ll call you,” yelled the lawyer, as Jake exited the door. The scene suddenly flashed a few times. When the flickering ceased, Jake was setting on side of some hotel bed in downtown Phoenix, AZ. At the bottom of screen the date read October 12, 2005. Jake He was bent over with his head resting in his hands. “Stephens I’m going to kill you. Not only did you try to kill my GCW career, but you tried to kill what chances I had getting back together with my wife as well,” he noted while running his fingers through his hair. He stood up and made his way towards the liquor cabinet. Jake studied the various “famous” labels, but chose a different white lighting. He opened the refrigerator and reached for the jug of skim milk. Making his way to the window he stared at the Phoenix nightlife. Years ago he would have been out there living it up with the rest of the crowd. But these days age had settled him. Fatherhood had matured him. “Mayhem! It’s pure madness out there these days. I couldn’t imagine still being out there right now! Many of us grow as we age and most of our youthful ways part us. However there will always be a few who never learn,” Jake said, turning to discard the empty jug into the waste. “Brian O’Neil, perhaps you should see an urologist. Maybe Ryan Stephens can offer one. You both seem to have a problem with a leaky faucet. He almost pissed his pants at Fallout. And then there’s you, painted face and all, wetting your bed,” he said laughing. “Speaking of your sheets was the blood stains suppose to scare me?” he asks. “If so they’re not working!” he stated, just shaking his head in disbelief as the scenes faded out.
