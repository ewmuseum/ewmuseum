Date: dunno “So what are you saying?” Darren's slate gray eyes stared at me over the top of his half raised glass, which had paused halfway to his mouth. Those eyes wore a guarded look, a look of distrust, which wasn't unlike the look I'd expected to see. After all, it was me he was talking to, and at that moment in time, I knew, he had no reason to trust me. “Now you need me, so you want to what, Daria?” He didn't get it, which was fine. To be perfectly honest, neither did I. “I've been... struggling, I guess you could say. I mean, I can't seem to find my groove, to find my rhythm. I need a trainer, I need a friend. I just need somebody to keep me going in the right direction, Darren.” His eyes had hardened instantly at the word “friend.” I knew in that instant, in that singular and diminutive bit of time, that he wouldn't help me. Wouldn't even consider it. This was the first time we'd seen each other in close to seven months, the first time we'd talked had been even longer, And that last conversation had been a barn burner, a verbal jousting match for the ages, that had resulted in the end of a relationship that had begun in another world, another place, when we were both far different people. The only problem for us was that while Darren had grown, had moved on, I hadn't. I was then, and I still am now, the same fucked up African chick I was before. That was, in a nutshell, what killed Darren and I. I couldn't move on, couldn't get past the horrors that lurked in my past, that still haunted my psyche. “Darren, I...” “No, Daria.” “But...” “Don't you get it? Can you not understand just how messed up this is, walking out of my life and now, out of the blue, suddenly waltzing back into my life and wanting my help?” Darren had a miraculous talent for keeping his voice low pitched, not raising it at all, but making it abundantly clear when you'd pissed him off. “Really? You expect me to just welcome you back, huh?” His eyes drifted off, scanning the busy restaurant that had been, in a happier time, our favorite place. It had been here that I'd sampled some of my first American food, so long ago that it felt like an eternity. It was a quaint, out of the way little place, classy despite the fact that it was completely lacking in the fancy, overdone pomp of other eateries. I dropped my eyes to the table while the conversation took an awkward silent turn, running my eyes over the cursive words “River Valley Inn” printed in gold on my napkin. The silence stretched on. And on. Twice, the waiter came by, offering first to refill Darren's wine, and second to ask if we'd like desert. Both times, he was sent away with a shake of the head, and perhaps sensing the awkward tension of the situation, he failed to return for a third time. I contemplated leaving, just getting up and walking out. But the thing was that I needed him, whether or not I cared to admit it. My career was in the toilet, teetering on it's last legs, and even though my efforts the week before had earned me a hard fought victory, I hadn't been myself. Ring rust, perhaps, could be blamed, for my lack of the timing, the quickness that was my trademark, that much could be explained. But something in my mind wasn't right, the spark that ignited my talents completely absent. I needed to find it, to reignite that spark, if I were to topple Cheap Labor and M3 and regain the Television Title. So I sat, and I let Darren go through his process, his quiet way of thinking things through. It took some time, far longer than my short attention span would have liked, but in the end, he made up his mind. And when he spoke, it was in a calm, quiet voice, flat as a pancake. “My answer is no, Daria.” Somehow I'd expected it, but it didn't stop it from hurting any worse. “Not because I don't want you to succeed, I do. I want nothing more than for you to go out there and take that belt, to look like you did a year or more ago. The problem is,” he said with a slight shake of his head and a rub of the slightly graying hair around his temples. “That I'm not willing to go through the ringer with you again. The years we were together were the best and worst of my life, you know that?” He pulled his wallet free of his jeans and dropped a few bills on the table. I watched them fall as if in slow motion, their landing on the table had an awful air of finality to it. “I couldn't help you then, and I don't think I can help you now, Daria,” he stood, pulling on his jacket. “Good luck at 122.” And with that, he was gone into the softly raining night, leaving me alone, rehashing his words over and over again in my head. The way his voice had been so calm, so even, his ever present South African accent not so much as rising an octave, had been worse than a slap in the face. I would have preferred it if he had screamed at me, had yelled and roared and carried on and slapped my face in front of everybody at the Inn. It would have been better by far than those soft, measured words. I don't think I can help you. Maybe, just maybe, I couldn't help but think as I walked on shaking legs to the bar and flagged the bartender down, nobody could. ---- His name was Rick... no, Ron... Robert? That should have been a sign right there, that I was too messed up to be doing this, that I couldn't remember the man's name. But, of course, the massively drunk and the perpetually emotionally fucked don't often think clearly before doing things, and on the night in question I was both. We'd met at that same bar, only a short way from where my hopes had been crushed, three hours or so after my first shot, and somehow at the time it had seemed like a good idea. I'd needed, I told myself, something to get over the hurt Darren's words had caused me. This would put him out of my mind, because, after all, Darren wasn't here, and this kid was. I put away my last shot, felt the spinning in my head, the slightest tinge of nausea spiraling up from my gut, and aimed my best smile at old what's his name, batting my eyelashes like a retarded deer in some stupid Disney movie. He was, as near as I can remember, as drunk, or more, than I, and thus far too out of it to think of how stupid I must have looked, otherwise the encounter never would have happened. Which would have been good, because it never should have happened in the first place. "Wanna get out of here?" He mumbled in my ear, running the most unsubtle caress ever across my thigh. "This shit's getting boring, huh?" Looking back, I wish I'd been there to slap some sense into myself, because I nodded, ran a hungry tongue over my lips as I stood. Somehow, we managed to wobble our way outside, and by some divine miracle were able to give understandable directions to my apartment. You probably get where the story goes from here. It wasn't pretty, I'm sure no voyeur would ever get his or her kicks from watching through my window on that night. Whatever his name was spent a few minutes of sloppy foreplay, groping blindly away with no thought whatsoever to me, and promptly passed out attempting to remove his own pants. I remember being conscious for a few minutes after that, and feeling kind of sorry for him, the way he was sprawled out can't have been very comfortable. I don't remember what my last thought was before sleep, I don't know where my head was at, but my subconscious picked a beautiful time to try and mess with me. ---- The peaceful darkness of the apartment had been broken only by the occasional snore of my guest, who sounded not dissimilar to a chainsaw, yet when the world suddenly came to life with a giant surge of adrenaline, that silence was shattered utterly. "What the fuck!" Was the first thing I was aware of, the first thing that invaded the private space of my swirling mind. I struck out at the sound, felt my nails dig deep into flesh and tear a jagged line, felt blood on the tips of my fingers. "Get the..." I snarled, tearing loose another chunk of flesh. No thoughts existed except for this panicked fight, the adrenaline coursing through me. My blood burned, I could feel my heart pounding away in my chest, felt it in my eardrums and just behind my eyes. Poor what's his name rolled away from me, but came up against the wall, unable to defend himself from the onslaught. I would later find out the screaming I heard was only partially from him. The rest of it was me, my voice so high pitched that it didn't sound human. I don't know how long it went on for, but it can't have been much more than a few minutes. It felt like an eternity, my fight or flight reflex stuch on "fight," with nowhere to go. I didn't even hear the cavalry come in to rescue him. Strong arms encircled me, dragged me off of my unfortunate guest, and heaved me across the room. "Stay." The voice was calm, collected, but still I sprang at it, claws bared, knees and hands and feet flailing wildly, not even remotely close to my normal fighting style. Once, twice, three times, I sprang, trying to wound, each time being thrust back, springing forward again. "Oh, fuck this," that voice, still so calm, sounded weary, I noticed as I was thrust back again. "Sorry..." Something hard, powerful, world shattering, slammed into my cheek, thrust me back into a heap on the floor... What had happened? "Going home?" The voice was not asking, it was telling. I could hear muttered assent, distinctly heard the phrase "crasy bitch" several times, mixtures of other words that made no sense at all. "... for the best." Whatever had hit me, it had turned the world upside down, everything spinning and twirling, multicolored. Nothing made sense, nothing was... Blackness. When the world returned and the lights came back on, my first thought was that I wanted to return to that confused void. It was a head throbbing, stomach heaving nightmare, my return to consciousness, and, in that moment, death would have been far preferable. It was hell, that feeling, pure hell. It smelled really, really bad, too. Open my eyes and try not to puke. Black leather in my face. Roll over, ever so slowly... "How do you feel?" Darren. He sat in the recliner he'd never taken when he moved out, feet up, a small smirk twisting his mouth upwards. I could tell he was tired, his eyes sporting dark circles, the eyes themselves cloudy, yet I didn't need anybody to let me know that he looked a damn sight better than I. "Like shit, that's how I feel." "You threw up all over yourself." The smirk grew, ever so slightly wider. "Little too much to drink, I take it? You never could handle liquor." "Piss off and get me a glass of water, would you?" I closed my eyes again, waiting for the water, desperate for it but not trusting myself enough to move. "Darren?" No response. "God damn it, I need..." "Get it yourself, Daria." I guess I deserved that. Staggering, feeling like I was going to throw up, nearly losing my balance, but managing to make it to the sink. A glass was the last thing on my mind, so I simply gulped water from the tap for a few minutes, waiting for Darren to say something. To do something. Anything. "You made a fucking idiot of yourself, you know that?" I hated it when he did that with his voice, so calm yet so devastating. "A real fool. What the hell were you thinking?" Still sick, I couldn't answer, just flopped back on the couch and tried my best not to pass out. "I felt bad, leaving the way I did, but when I found you in the state you were in, drunk and trying to claw the eyes out of some poor bastard, I didn't pity you a bit. In fact, I probably should have just left again." "I'm sorry." "I don't give a shit. What the fuck is wrong with you?" His voice rose for the first time, the curse word that I'd only heard from him a couple of times an octave louder than the rest. "Do you honestly think this is going to help you at all?" Fuck... the match. It was less than a week away, and I was probably going to lose a full day of training for it to this hangover. GCW had me on a very short string, thanks to my previous failures, and I couldn't afford a bad performance. "Was it the dreams again?" I nodded. "You need help, Daria." I need you. Didn't say it, couldn't say it, just thought it. He sighed deeply, looking me over. "Get in the shower while I make some breakfast," there was no questioning that tone. "First things first, we need to get you cleaned up." "Then what?" He smiled at me over his shoulder as he pulled a frying pan from the kitchen cupboard. "Then we try to put your sorry excuse for a life back together." As the hot water poured down through my hair, rushing over my face, I smiled in spite of how sick I felt. Maybe it would all work out after all.
