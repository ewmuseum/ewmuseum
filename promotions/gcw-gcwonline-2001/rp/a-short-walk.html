A Short Walk

Date: Unspecified An unspecified time and place Snow began to fall in a particularly dark set of woods, but somehow never seemed to settle on the ground. It crunched underneath Virgil's feet, but didn't make the right sound. Instead of snow, it sounded more like rocks, or maybe the hard carapaces of beetles crackling under his boots. He looked down to make certain that he was, in fact, still where he thought he was. Somehow, he already knew without any other intuition that it was a dream, without any occurrence making it obvious. He'd never been here before, but the trees looked vaguely familiar, as though they'd been transplanted from another memory. Was he lost? He turned his head to the left, and saw what looked for a moment like initials carved into one of the trees. V.A. They shimmered slightly as he stared at them. Impossible, he thought. Just a creation of the dream. After all, he would have had no use for carving initials after taking the name Ambrosi for himself... whenever it was that he'd done that. He would have been a little old for that sort of behavior. He continued walking, deciding it would be useless to ponder the point further. Hadn't someone already explained it in another dream? The hazy memory of a dimly lit castle occurred to him. He looked up at the sky, and noticed the moon full, surrounded by millions of blurs of white. There wasn't a cloud in the sky, and it appeared as though the moon was spewing the snow forth. He scowled at it for a moment, before the 'face' that every child sees in the moon became apparent. He continued to stare, his expression softening. Shifting blame wouldn't do any good... after all, he was alone here. He was the one in control, and thus the creator of the entirety of the snow. The moisture evaporating from the far-off oceans he was sure existed or would exist when he thought of them, the extra moisture that provided the impetus to fall, the clouds which held the moisture. He controlled all of it, and it suddenly occurred to him that he had made himself extremely cold. The snow caked on his shoulders like useless epaulets, impeding the movement of his arms. He reached up and swept it off with a sigh, before looking up at the sky. ?Well, I'm not going to do it anymore. Are you?? Apparently, the sky didn't want to waste the effort. The last flakes of snow drifted lazily to the ground, and Virgil realized that he was currently standing in a drift nearly up to his knees. How long had he been wasting time standing like this? It shifted apart as he moved his feet, and continued through the woods. A warm wind whips through the woods, and Virgil's first foot step made an extremely loud splashing sound, which covered the legs of his pants with water. That was when he heard the howls. They weren't very loud right now, but Virgil knew they would be coming for him shortly. He'd always been taught as long as he could remember that wolves were creatures to be feared. Even when he had technically died, wolves of another breed became another problem entirely. He knew he would never get away from them, no matter what he did. But, like always, he ran. The trees shot past at an alarming rate of speed, but every footstep of Virgil's made an even louder sound. No matter how fast he ran, he would have to stop sometime, and they would have heard every step with their strong ears. They probably knew where he'd go in a time like this, and they'd show up there one day when he didn't expect it, pressing the matter farther. He had lost the race, and so he turned to greet the victor graciously. They seemed to come from every crevice, every space not occupied by a tree or the ground, which had dried up fairly quickly in the muggy heat. They didn't surround him, like he would have expected, instead forming a well-organized brigade in front of him, a million eyes of amber, deep green and bright blue staring straight into him. He stared the leader-dog down, and it turned its ears forward. Somehow he knew it was listening to his mind, looking for the fear that lingered there, no longer betrayed by the pounding of his heart. ?I'm not afraid of you, wolf.? A psychic hammer assailed him, nearly knocking him back with the force of every wolf looking upon him grumbling softly with anger. What do you mean you're not afraid of us? Everyone should be afraid of us! We know everything and everybody! Resources unavailable to god himself are at our command! Why shouldn't you be afraid of us? Everyone is afraid of wolves! There is no creature on this planet that is to be feared more than us! Certainly he must be a fool if he is not! Why, we could set upon him now and cut him to ribbons! Look at him, standing there quaking! He's obviously afraid of us! Let us attack him! ?I stopped the snow. What makes you think I couldn't do the same to you?? Virgil queried, hardly expecting his question to be answered. Was that what happened? That's impossible, he can't have stopped the snow from falling, this wood's always had snow, just like we've always had the full moon and enough rabbits to eat forever, and that guy that kept carving his initials into the trees that disappeared a long time ago. Or was it really that long ago? I can't remember. I can't remember either. Nobody knows when that happened but the elder wolf, but even he can't stop the snow. Virgil smiled. They knew now what kind of situation they were in. A great power was standing at his side, and no longer was he alone, like he had been all those years ago, when he'd tried but failed to get out of these woods. Even the wolves hadn't caught up with him that day, because he was younger then, and didn't make so much noise in the snow. ?You're not in a position to fight. Even if you were to kill me, you'd all be dead at the end of it. I've got this, you know.? He held a long femur bone high over his head. It had been twisted into an odd shape by a vaguely natural yet horrible process, but it felt smooth and comfortable in his hand, like it had been crafted specifically for him. The bone! The bone! The bone! The bone! The bone! The bone! The bone! The bone! The bone! The bone! The bone! He's got the bone! We've wanted that forever! The bone! Who gave him that? We want that! ?It's not yours. I'm the only one that can use it.? A truce! A truce! A truce! A truce! The leader approached him cautiously, his ears laid back flat against his head. You've got that... we want that. We know who made it for you, and if they want, we can all be friends. I know it's not supposed to happen, but there are other things here in the woods besides us, you know. Things you didn't create, and things we didn't create, and things that neither of us can eat, not like the rabbits and the deer that we didn't make. We don't like them, and you wouldn't like them much if you saw them, no. They're not good. ?How am I supposed to trust you to not harm us?? The leader tilted his head at Virgil quizzically. Don't you remember? You left that crow that flew away from the Nest here a while ago... we took good care of it, didn't we? Yes yes yes yes yes yes yes yes yes we did yes yes yes yes yes yes yes yes yes! ?I suppose you did, since I see it over there.? In one of the trees, a crow cawed loudly, flapping its wings at one of the older pack members, that almost seemed to Virgil like it was wagging its tail. Bizarre, he thought. ?I'm not sure... but if the truce is broken, we'll all be killed... we can't kill the others by ourselves.? Now you understand. He heard the sound of ligaments and muscles tensing across joints as millions of them smiled simultaneously, and he beheld one of the strangest sights he'd ever seen, and finally understood why it was necessary for them to join forces. The wolves had long, razor sharp claws, but in their smiles, he saw no teeth.
