Descent

6 March 2006

Date: 3/6 Well, THAT had gone spectacularly well. Even by recent Seymour Almasy standards, WorldWide 41 had been a fine night in the field of idiotic achievement for the Final Fantasy. He had managed to act like a COMPLETE and utter moron on national television by kissing Eva VanLorne in a depressed and inexplicable haze, then had to fight her later that night, using his patented Cactuar Crossface to get the submission and move on to the Rampage Tournament proper. “What a fucking career this is,” the Final Fantasy said. Slumped over on a bench as he was, with his head hung low, it was hard to miss the gravity of his statement. It was a “fucking career” because it never stopped. Grandfather dies? Show goes on. Ex-wife kills herself? Show goes on. Get caught in a confrontation with the guy who wanted her to kill you instead, after watching a woman burned alive and two men shot? Show goes on, AND the owner of the company ponies up $2,000,000 so he can keep wrestling. Sometimes, Seymour wonders if the sport plays by different rules than everything else. It was a “fucking career” because you always had to fight people you didn’t want to. People like Eva, the only quasi-friend Seymour had in GCW. People like Dawn, back in GW, who he had a schoolboy’s crush times twenty on. And last but not least, it was a “fucking career” because he was alone now, and had no choice but to bravely soldier on. Seymour was where he was finding himself more and more after shows, in the locker room looking down at either tiling or carpet. After all, Seymour had no friends backstage. It wasn’t as if he was going to go out with the boys. No, the Final Fantasy was content with the routine he’d been doing for the past few nights: go out and get some noodles, then turn in for the night. Simple and effective, and it tended to keep the number of people who recognized him down. “Can’t even sign autographs for the kids anymore,” he said, disgusted with himself. He’d always been approachable by the little ones. Always. Now though, he would wave and continue walking. He couldn’t sit down and talk about his career or life with pride. Because right now, to Seymour, both were dismal failures. “Rollins,” he grumbled, looking down at boots that had stepped into wrestling rings on four continents. GTT5 was Seymour’s chance for revenge. Seymour’s chance to make the GTT5 finals and beat the shit out of Rich Rollins in the process. The latter had indeed happened. Early reports from fans were of a five star classic, but not in the sense of a classic match. No, Almasy/Rollins V had been a fight, a brawl, a war. Seymour had flung his 180 pounds at Rollins with the tenacity of a pitbull, and Rollins had fought back with equal fervor. Seymour couldn’t deny that he HAD gotten some vengeance on the Renegade. With every punch and kick, he felt a tiny bit better. Every move gave him a smile. But when it came to wins and losses, Seymour had taken the L, and Rollins had achieved the big W that sent him to the finals of the biggest tournament in PTC history. Sure, Seymour had a great tournament. But in the face of losing to Rollins, it was hard to say it wasn’t all for naught. “That fucking cop killer,” he spat, “is in the finals. That WASTE OF HUMANITY is going to wrestle for the GTT5 crown and not me!” Seymour could only hope that Rich got executed before that match. Then PTC would have to call him up to take on Shanty Jones, due to a lack of living Rich Rollins. Maybe Seymour could watch the execution. Oh, how glorious that would be? Never mind the fact that Seymour could get him off. Rich Rollins had sentenced Seymour Almasy to Hell on Earth. Sentenced him to never see his best friend again. NEARLY had him killed. As far as the Final Fantasy was concerned, humane execution was too good for the shitstain who’d fucked Laura. “There’s still Rampage,” Seymour whispered, repeating the word like a mantra. “Another tournament. My only chance left to make something of myself. My only chance to not fail again.” The more that went wrong, the harder Seymour treated himself. It was a self-perpetuating cycle, since it seemed that things were going to go wrong from now on. LeStatt Knight was his opponent, a man renowned for being merciless and compassionless. A man who discarded women like toilet paper when he so chose. Fuck, he’d done it to Eva, which was probably part of the reason she’d become so guarded. But in spite of all of that, he was a former GCW World Champion, and a top flight competitor in the ring. With the devastating Knightfall, he’d taken on and turned back all most of his opponents, including an incredibly game Blaise “Hazard” McClancy last week. In short, Knight was vicious, hungry, and knew what it was like to be The Man. And he would do fucking anything in the world to become The Man again. Taking all of that together, he was an utterly nightmarish opponent for a battered, saddened, and weakened Final Fantasy. “Go time again,” Seymour said, simply. Because it was. LeStatt was tough enough to beat him, but so were Kimbusa, Eva, and Scott Taylor. And he’d triumphed there. Sighing and shaking his head, the Final Fantasy scooped up his gym bag, and headed for the door. Where he was going was as of yet undetermined, but he reasoned that anywhere was better than “right here” at this particular moment in time. -- --------------------------------------------- A few minutes later? ----------------------------------------------- Almasy had walked down many hallways before. Usually, he walked happily; confidently. Other times he was nervous, or afraid. Rarely, if ever, though, had he stomped down a hallway as he was now doing. Seymour was angry. Pissed off at the world, at GCW, at Rich Rollins, at Steven Caldera, at Shadow, at Dawn, at life, at everything. NOW he understood why Rollins was an anarchical Renegade. People were shit, so it was sort of hard to blame him The former PTC Global Champion marched to his own drummer down the hall, eyes cast downwards, muttering curses under his breath. Unfortunately for Almasy, there was someone that was about to affect his day. Good? Bad? Didn’t matter. Seymour wasn’t much of a people person right now. “Seymour!” Almasy was jarred from his walking and stewing by a suited GCW employee, holding a telephone in his hand. As Almasy’s eyes rose to look at the man, Seymour could see that he was shaking, trembling, and pale-faced. “Call for you, Mr. Almasy.” Seymour, though, kept walking. “I’m not in the mood for any more bad news.” He kept walking. Kept walking, kept walking, kept walking. “Trust me,” he heard, over his shoulder, in a shaky voice. “You want to take this call.” Under most circumstances, Seymour Almasy would have kept right on walking. But something in the man’s voice told Seymour that this was a call he needed to take. “Give me the phone.” The Final Fantasy walked slowly back towards the employee, arm outstretched. It probably WAS more bad news, but Seymour was used to that by now. After all, it couldn’t help but be better than the news he’d been receiving. Taking the cell phone, he jammed it quickly to his ear, and spoke. “This is Seymour Almasy. May I ask who’s calling?” The person on the other end of the phone said one word. One, solitary word. And yet, it changed Seymour’s life for the umpteenth time. “Tessa.” Almasy’s eyes widened, a million thoughts going through his head at once. Nodding sagely, the employee turned to leave, offering only a final bit of sage wisdom. “Figured you’d want to take the call.” “What can I do for you, beautiful,” he asked. Happen what may in the world of GCW, Tessa Windsor, UWF’s loveliest lady, was the one person who made it all okay at home. His only stable presence. Her response chilled him to the core. “We can’t do this anymore, Seymour.” The Final Fantasy took a slow, DEEP breath in. Blew it out. Took another, and blew it out. The fuck did she mean? And NOW, of all times? “What do you mean?” Almasy was hoping that this was a bad dream. A nightmare. Not real. Unfortunately, it was every bit as real as everything else that had been happening to the poor man. “What I said. We need to talk?” “No..” he whispered, low enough that she couldn’t hear. “No, no, no, no, no?” What had he DONE, for God’s sake? What had he done to deserve this? To deserve everything crumbling down around him? Laura was gone. Grandpa Cid was gone. His father had left ages ago. His mother was still a bitch. And now, Tessa was going to..what? Break up with him? “I’m sorry,” she said, seeming to genuinely mean it. Which helped a little, but admittedly not much. Seymour was wounded, but he had to respond. “So am I. Is it okay if we talk after this week’s match” I’ve got LeStatt Knight in a few days, and with everything on my plate?” “It’s okay,” she responded, sounding as if her voice was about to crack a bit. “I understand. I’m so sorry?” Something had happened, Seymour was sure of that. What it was, he didn’t quite know. But he DID know that it was going to be added to the list of shit that he’d endured. The list of crap that had been forced down his throat by Life. “I’ll see you soon, Tessa, okay” Take care.” Almasy clicked the phone off. Looking at it carefully, he knew he was about to owe a GCW employee a c-note. “God. Fucking. DAMNIT!” And with that, the telephone was launched into the nearby concrete wall, shattering into a few dozen pieces. At this rate, Seymour Almasy’s going to take on the nickname of the “Cell-Phone Killa”. Really, it’s a better moniker now than the “Final Fantasy”. Because for Seymour, it’s become all too literal. His fantasies of a married life with Tessa were gone. His fantasies of winning GTT5? Ditto. The only fantasy he had left was winning Rampage, and even that wasn’t an especially strong one. Ergo, Seymour’s Final Fantasy. And if that fantasy died out, what would be left? Seymour Almasy hoped that he wouldn’t have to find out. Because if he did, that fantasy was likely to be Seymour’s final ANYTHING.
