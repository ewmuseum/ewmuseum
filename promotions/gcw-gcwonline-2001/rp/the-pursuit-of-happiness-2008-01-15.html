The Pursuit of Happiness

15 January 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: January 15, 2008 __Start There was something spacey in his eyes as he peered through the motel room window and over the parking lot. It held no noteworthy qualities and certainly had no activity whatsoever going on in it but still, Jamison Talley stared hard enough to make a person believe they were missing something grand. His eyes were squinted and his lips were pursed at the time and in the hollows of his mind was a blank slate. It was euphoria at its finest and Talley wouldn't sell it for anything in the world. Selling it wasn't the problem though - 'Getting any better?' Caroline surfed his waves of Zen and knocked him clean off his board and into the tide allowing the water to rush in and cloud everything. His aura was damaged and with a quick shake, Jay opened his eyes wide. 'Wha?' 'Something good. It'll happen.' 'Yeah. Real good.' She smiled and he didn't as he turned back to the lot in pursuit of his evaporating high. He clawed for it with his mind but the sweet aroma was already drifting too high above his head to grasp. He'd lost it all and now he was back in reality. The lights were dimmer than he had seen in his head and the entire mood was more dramatic than he hoped it was. His eyes darted back to Caroline, who perched on the edge of the bed warily, and at the box of cigarettes smacking her palm. She packed them good and with a sudden tear in the package she broke free two sticks. 'You need one?' 'I need three.' She smiled in a warm sort of way which was just enough to trigger a grin on the outlaw's lips. She tossed him the tobacco and he lit the end with his Zippo. 'What's the spacey face for anyways?' Talley shook his head, 'Dunno.' he paused and took in a thick wad of smoke. It passed along from tooth to tooth with his tongue before exhaling a puff of gray, 'I guess I'm just trying to figure shit out, ya' know? It's all kinda' jumbled these days.' 'Jumbled,' Caroline took a drag of her own and ashed the end over the carpet, 'what's jumbled?' 'Dunno, remember? I mean, things are just complicated.' 'We're complicated?' She asked. Jay smirked and raised his brows in shock, 'Look at us, you see any complications?' 'We are complicated though-' 'A bit, yeah. Who isn't?' She nodded and laid backwards across the flowery jungle of sheets. They swallowed her as her weight pressed inward, 'It's wrestling?' 'It's always wrestling, isn't it?' He leaned back against the windowsill and took in another dose of poison from the 27. It filtered down his esophagus and to his lungs where it cultivated and waited to do its damage. 'It's just - everything. Why am I bothering?' 'Cuz you're damn good.' 'I'm a crook. I walk in and swing hoping I connect. That's good?' She shrugged, 'Looks good to me. Just because you fell to Knight doesn't make you a failure you know. You hear what they say about you anyways?' 'Do I care?' 'Doubtful.' Jay paused and dropped off the cherries from his cigarette. They dispersed onto the floor and glowed beside the heater as they do their final dance before disappearing completely. 'I just wish that I was better. I keep on blowing it.' 'So you hit a wall. Big deal. I've seen dudes smaller than you hop taller walls than this.' 'This is different though-' 'Different?' She paused and sat back up. Her eyes lit up as bright as the end of her cigarette and focused in on Jay's, 'It's different because you say it's different. Other than that, it's all the same. It's not like the wall changes; you still have to get over the sucker.' 'I guess.' 'You need a drink is what you need.' 'Rather not.' 'Too bad.' For the month and a half they had been seeing each other, he had learned that her word was law. As much as she didn't press her power, he knew that objecting her was just as bad as denying Steven Caldera his morning blow job. There were certain things in life a man shouldn't do and those two things ranked at the top of the list no matter how many other options you toss in. __________ The bar was quaint, a small rag-tag kinda' place, with the lights flickering and the channels set to sports. About twelve or so men filled the tables and stools with one man behind the bar himself. The guy was short, pudgy, and had a messy patch of hair that danced under his lips as he spoke. Jay and Caroline stepped up to the stools, with eyes coming from all sides, and sat on the cushions in a commanding sort of way. They didn't feel like they owned them - far from it. They just looked confident about how they did things. 'Ain't you prettier than the day is long,' said a man beside them. His teeth were yellow and a putrid stench drifted from the backside of his mouth as he annunciated. Caroline nodded to him and looked forward to the pudgy bartender, 'Two Jack Daniels. Straight.' Jay looked at his companion and gave her a quick smile, 'How's this going to make things better?' 'Just wait, I bet you'll be back to yourself within the hour.' 'Getting me drunk isn't myself you know.' It was her turn to grin now and she did so with such prestige, 'I know what counts and what's out of bounds. Just sit tight and sip your Jack please.' He did as he was asked and even took the liberty of leaning back. He soaked in the heavy scent of alcohol and tobacco and ran through his memory banks trying to figure out the last time he had actually been in a bar. The thinking became hard after a few seconds and Jay decided to leave it up for interpretation as he took another swig of whiskey. 'Well look who we have here,' said a voice beyond him. It was raspy and limped into his eardrums. Jay set down his drink on the mahogany counter and spun around to look over a middle-sized man with a white beater and jeans on. His chin held onto a long beard that draped down the front of his body and to his pectoral muscles. 'Where is he?' Jay inquired. 'The Outlaw himself, in the flesh.' 'I am rather fleshy, huh?' 'I didn't think people like you came out to places like this.' Jay shrugged and let a smile settle on his lips for the man, 'I'm normal enough. Just making a little more cash these days.' He nodded agreeingly and crossed his arms over his abdomen, 'I saw you at that Pay Per view you know, you're some big star now.' Caroline shouldered him and grinned wide for him to see. Either this man was a true Jay Terror fan or she had paid someone some serious money to get Jay antsy. Of the two, Jay preferred the first. 'To him his own. I'm just doing the same thing I've always been doing.' 'Modesty, what a great quality.' He paused and looked to his friends on both sides of him, 'Seems like the only real quality you've kept.' Stunned, Jay puckered his lips, 'How you figure?' 'Look at you, what happened to the kid who didn't follow the rules? What happened to the guy against the mainstream?' 'You saying I abide now?' 'You heard me right.' His minions showed signs of agreement and looked back to their leader waiting for him to go on, 'I've seen you on the television, you've lost that spunk. You seem more like Knight than anyone else I know.' 'You've had a bit too much to drink man.' Jay swiveled back around to the bar where he found his three-quarter full glass of Tennessee whiskey. He took a large gulp of the alcohol and let it burn his throat as it inched down to his stomach. Caroline looked at him, still, and wrinkled her face. 'You see what I mean; now you're running. You used to stay and fight the big fight Terror. Where'd the outlaw go?' Jay ignored his speech and focused harder on the glass in front of him. He didn't know what was happening or why he was feigning away the way he was. Everything seemed surreal to him and he could feel the room get hotter and hotter as the seconds passed. A bead of sweat dripped from the hair in his armpit and rolled down his side creating a slimy trail down to his jeans. 'Figures. The last hero we ever have goes to the dark side. Ain't that just life at its finest.' The man and his crew turn and talk under their breath as they walk back to their table in the corner of the room. Caroline leans in on Jay. 'I'm sorry, I-' 'I got forty-five minutes left in this hour. Might as well try and forget it.' He tips his glass back and downs the remainder of his glass. Terror slams it onto the fine wood again and looks up to the pudgy man behind the bar, 'Another?' He obliges and fetches the bottle. Filled to the top, Jay examines his fresh batch and takes it down just as fast as his half glass. 'Jay,' she tried. He looked at her with the most serious and insecure face he's ever worn. His eyes twinkled in the light and she could almost hear his heart beat as it pressed against his tee-shirt, 'Might as well join me.' She wanted to apologize but words weren't what he wanted to hear. She settled with failure and tipped her own glass back. The liquid coursed through her, like a steady stream of guilt, and lingered with her for the rest of the evening. __________ “In all honesty, I can admit that you were the last person I was expecting to see again.” “Shit happens, ya” know?” “Not for Jay Terror. Shit never happened.” “So it goes.” She dropped her pen in the center of her desk and leaned back in her plush swivel chair. It had a soft red cushion on the back that supported her lean frame and pressed her shoulders forward in way that brought her breasts to their fullest. Her face was littered with confusion and by the look of things it was going to remain that way. “You traveled to Washington for some reason, mind sharing?” Jay cleared his throat and looked around the all-too-familiar room. The same green wallpaper coated the walls and it wore a splendid flower pattern which repeated to the floor of the office. “I was hoping you could tell me.” “I don’t read minds, you should know better than anyone.” “You mind trying?” She could sense a genuine quality in him that he had lacked from the last meeting the two underwent. His voice was less pretentious and his demeanor was scaled down greatly compared to the last time. Everything about Jay was different and for that reason alone she agreed to give him a second chance, “It is my job, right?” “Thanks Anita, I appreciate it.” Anita Baker, his former prescribed therapist, stood from behind her desk and guided Jay to the leather cloud in the corner of the room. He mounted the hide and sunk into it feeling like he would crash through the heavens and to the ground if he moved any more. Baker took her seat beside him, without a notepad, and smiled dimly, “Not smoking?” “Not today. Too much bouncing around for a cigarette to cure, ya” know?” “Not yet. I’d be glad to listen though.” Jay sighed and accepted the situation. He had come to her for advice and hope and if there was anyway of receiving either of the latter he would need to spill his guts unlike ever before. Forget the attitude, the track record, forget it all. If he was going to get what he needed out of this he would have to let it all go. “I can’t explain things very well. It’s hard somehow. I’m not really Jay Terror anymore I guess. I mean, I can play him on TV, but I’m not him. Not anymore.” She nodded as if she understood his predicament, “You’re Jamison Talley now, aren’t you?” “I guess. I don’t know.” “Well tell me, would you be more prone to speak out at non-smoking conventions or would you rather sit back and contemplate the presidential candidates?” “What kinda” question is that?” “Just answer.” He swished the answer around in his mouth and spit it out, “I’m not one for politics.” She smiled again in the same almighty sort of way, “You’re not one for smoking rights then are you?” “I am, of course I am.” “Look Jay, I’m not accusing you of anything, just making my point. You’ve slipped out of character and fallen back into someone you have been trying to subdue. Didn’t you think that you’d shed your shell and go back to the body you were given at some point?” Again, Jay thought it over and this time noticed that she was right. That’s right, for the first time ever he was admitting that she had a point that he agreed with. It was at that moment that he knew he needed her now more than ever. “How do I get him back?” “You aren’t comfortable in your own skin?” “I’m more comfortable in his.” She leaned back in her chair and thought of what she’d jot down if he were an official patient and she had her notepad with her. Instead, he voiced it for him, “I can’t make any promises but I’d suggest you step back a minute and look at everything. Wrestling, girls, your diet, your habits ? look at it all and find out which part of your life is failing.” “I already know.” “And?” “I have the girl, I eat right, and I smoke two packs a day; the only thing left is wrestling.” “Are you not doing well?” Jay shook his head, “I’m doing well, real well.” “So you can’t handle it?” “I don’t know.” She processed the information in the best way she could and pieced it together until it made a clear picture which she could produce for him, “It sounds like you haven’t lost Jay Terror at all but instead he’s just been overwhelmed. If you’ve done as well as you say you have, I bet it’s too much for you to handle this quick. Maybe you should step down and take a load off?” “I can’t. I’m in the main event, I’m a star now. I can’t just step down the ladder and say I’ll do it when I’m ready.” “Funny, I never saw Jay Terror calling himself a star or classifying himself as one of them.” Jay sat up from the leather glove and turned his face so his eyes met hers, “You too?” “Me?” “You think I’ve lost my foundation too?” She nodded solemnly, “I can’t be sure but it sounds like all this action has transformed you too quickly.” “What do you suppose I do?” “You know,” she paused and brought her pen to her lips. The end bobbed between her teeth and she sucked on it thinking of what to say, “Your best bet is to either let it hit you or adapt. You can have it smack you around until you’re sick of it or you can do something about it.” “How though?” “What would Jay Terror do if someone was suffocating him?” Jay answered without a second of thought, “Break free and beat them until they couldn’t see straight.” “There’s your answer,” she said with a broad smile across her lips. Her dimples showed off her quirky beauty and Jay couldn’t help grinning with her. “Thanks Anita.” “I’ll always be here.” He knew she would. __________ With his hands around the stems, he raised his sunglasses into the air and looked through them to the sun. Its radiance faded leaving room for his own glitter and, as he placed them over his eyes, he could feel the shine overtake him. He sparkled as bright as he knew he was supposed to. The thought alone was enough to propel him to new heights. The bandana followed and once it was on he knew the transformation was complete. No more Jamison Talley, he wouldn’t let him endure the upper level. That was a job better left to Jay Terror. Talley was only the placeholder.
