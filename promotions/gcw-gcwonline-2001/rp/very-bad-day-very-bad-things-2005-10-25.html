Very Bad Day, Very Bad Things

25 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/25/2005 Sam Wolack is sitting in an interrogation room. Two cops are sitting at the opposite side of the table. Wolack: I told you, I don't know where she is. Cop 1: You were the last one to see her. There are witnesses that will state that you went up to her room. Wolack: Listen, she called me up, and I went to see her. After that, I went to a costume shop to pick up a bald cap. If you don't believe me, get a copy of my credit card records. Cop 2: Listen, I know that you two had a strained relationship, but you might be able to help her parents out in locating her. Wolack: F***, you guys suck at this good cop, bad cop thing. I will say this one more time in a way that you can hear them up in the cheap seats. Wolack cups his hands around his mouth. Wolack: I have no f***ing clue where Andrea is. There's a knock at the door. Cop 1 answers it and takes a folder from the clerk that was standing there. Cop 1: So you don't know who did it? Wolack: Are you stupid? If I knew where she was, then I would also know who did it. Stands to reason, right? Cop 2: Listen, Samuel...... Wolack: Only my family calls me that, Cop 2: Then what should we call you. Wolack: You should call my f***ing lawyer. I have the right to have an attorney present through questioning. Because I do not have an attorney present and you continue to question me, you are violating my Miranda Rights. Cop 1: You want to play hardball with us, fine, we'll play hardball. Wolack: Get me my f***ing lawyer. Cop 2: I think we should let him go. Wolack: Very smart idea. Wolack stands up as the Cop 2 opens the door and lets him out. Wolack: F***ing pigs. Wolack walks towards the elevator of the police station. We then cut to a couple minutes later, when Wolack is leaving the station. A reporter is standing there waiting for him. Reporter: Mr. Wolack, what can you tell us about the disappearance of Andrea Scabbia? Wolack: Get lost. Reporter: Was that why you were here? Wolack: Beat it. Reporter: Can you give..... Wolack puts his hand over the lens of the camera. Wolack: Why don't you f***ing vultures go to hell? I've had a very long and very bad day. At this rate, I may not wait until I'm in the ring to do very bad things to someone. If you don't back the f*** off, I'm going to do very bad things to you. Got it. Scene fades to black.
