The Devil Went Down to De-troit

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Halloween Steamy streaks of shower residue had turned the mirror into a foggy window, and in that window Rich Rollins could have sworn he could see his future. Either that or the NyQuil had just kicked in. Less than an hour after WorldWide concluded and the Denver natives packed up and drove home, the only thing left to comfort the Renegade was his own blurry image, his reflection twisted like a carnival mirror in the warm dew that had coalesced across its glassy surface. His nose stung when he caressed it; his confrontation with Seymour Almasy earlier in the evening had left a mark on more than just his ego, and even as he tried not to wince a single droplet of blood jumped off the tip of his nose and landed in the sink, spreading like a red virus through the water’s crisp and clear domain. A little drop of blood goes a long way, he mused. But it was true. He had waited long enough, and there was nothing left between himself and the final confrontation with the one man who eluded him. The next time Rich “The Renegade” Rollins stepped into a wrestling ring, Seymour Almasy would be waiting for him. The next time he heard the shrill shriek of the timekeeper’s bell, nothing would be in his way to finally getting his hands around the Final Fantasy’s scrawny little neck. This wasn’t a dream match. Rollins hated it when he looked at all the wrestling dirt sheets or browsed the internet flame forums, reading about how the Rich Rollins and Seymour Almasy confrontation was a wrestling fan’s wet dream. Dream matches don’t happen. That’s why they’re just dreams. This was an inevitability. Mr. PTC was about to meet Mr. GCW. Another droplet of blood took a swan dive off the tip of his tender nose. Rollins merely sighed and stuck a wad of tissue paper into his nostril to stop the bleeding, neither his mood nor his morale very positive. After all, Seymour had gotten his memory card back. What the hell did it matter now? But it mattered quite a bit. Rollins couldn’t kid himself, not now. Not three days away from one of the biggest matches of his career. Drawing a splash of lukewarm water to his face with hands cupped like grizzled paws, Rich Rollins looked at his hazy visage in the mirror one last time. His lips turned downward in contemplation; it was the hollow face of a broken man that frowned back. He could see the match a million times over in the misty depths of that mirror, every outcome different than the last. Every move, every blow, everything felt like it was happening all at the same time. In some cases he won, outmaneuvering the seemingly infallible Seymour Almasy to claim his spot amongst the wrestling elite. In some scenarios he evened turned the crowd, basking in their adoration as the Final Fantasy is booed out of the arena. But most of the time he saw defeat. A bitter pill to swallow, watching your inevitable demise replayed over and over like a bad re-run. It was like a horrible sitcom with a goofy soundtrack, but the images were so vivid and violent it put stones the size of fists in his stomach. Every time Almasy scored a blow, the laugh track just got louder. Rollins gave a start, wrenching his thoughts back into reality. His face was moist, but most of it came from his pores. His eyes were so cold, so indifferent. And so he smiled. “The Devil went down to De-troit,” he sang softly to himself. His smile widened. His nose had stopped bleeding. Devil’s Night, indeed. ~Fin~ ((Sorry it's so short, but I just wanted to put something up tonight. This doesn't count towards Devil's Night in any way, but I wasn't about to no-show a PPV.
