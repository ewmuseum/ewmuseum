Letting Go

14 June 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06/14/10 “The fuck you mean, you’re not gonna call her?” Clayton Sutherby wasn’t even sure why he and Maynard had remained such good friends throughout all these years. Two people could hardly differ more from one another, after all. The subject of women and relationships was a common subject on which they disagreed most heatedly. As a happily married man of ten years, Clayton found it difficult to understand Maynard’s apprehension towards the whole idea of long-term, monogamous relationships. Yes, there was that whole thing with she-who-must-not-be-named, but that was over a decade ago. How long could the wound possibly last? “No,” said Maynard, holding the phone an inch away from his ear as to keep his ear drums from bursting with the force of his best friends shouting. “I’m not gonna call her. What do you care anyways?” “What do I care?” Clayton cried. Maynard pulled the phone away another inch. “Maynard, I’ve watched you be the Lone fucking Ranger for the better part of both our adult lives. You walk into a bar somewhere, find some slut -- and make no mistake; they are sluts, the ones you pick up -- have some sleazy one night stand with her, and then throw her out the door like yesterday’s trash. And after all this, you’re still not happy, Maynard. You pretend real well that you’re happy, but you’re not.” “I am happy,” mumbled Maynard. Perhaps even he remained unconvinced. “The fuck you’re happy, man,” shouted Clayton. “Maybe you can convince yourself you’re all shits and giggles, but fuck ... I’m your best friend; have been for a long time. Believe me when I tell you this: you’re not fucking happy. Not really.” Maynard shrugged. “Happy enough.” Clayton burst out in sarcastic, angry laughter. “Happy enough? Happy enough!? Now I know you’re out of your fucking mind!” “Just ‘cause I don’t wanna call some broad?” breathed Maynard, angrily. “No,” said Clayton so quietly that Maynard had to put the phone against his ear again, “because you’re happy not calling this girl. Because you’re happy not knowing what could be with her.” “Yeah,” scoffed Maynard, “because the whole relationship thing worked out so fucking great for me last time, right?” “Oh, grow up, will you?” said Clayton. “That was fifteen years ago! Fifteen fucking years! And you’re still going on about it? Jesus Christ, man. That’s pathetic!” The two men sat in seething silence for several moments before Maynard spoke. “You know what?” he breathed, heart pounding an angry rhythm in his chest. “I don’t have the time for this shit right now.” “What a fucking surprise,” Clayton shouted, “Maynard McGarrity doesn’t have time for something fucking real in his life. Go ahead, buddy! Go ahead and run away, again!” Without another word, Maynard flipped the phone shut. ---- SUMMER 1995 Hours after Maynard had tried, unsuccessfully to ask Sara’s father for her hand in marriage ---- “So, how’d it go?” Maynard smiled weakly at his best friend. “How d’you think it went?” “Sorry to hear that, man” said Clayton, putting his hand on Maynard’s shoulder. “You’re still gonna ask her, huh?” “Of course,” said Maynard with a determined look on his face, “I just had to do the right thing and ask her dad, you know? She’d mentioned a couple times he was very ‘old fashioned’.” “I say we give him a good ‘old fashioned’ beat down, what do you say?” said Clayton with a smile; his attempt to lighten the mood. Maynard smiled. “Nah, what good would that do? Just land ourselves in jail.” “Yeah, I guess” Clayton said, taking his hand off Maynard’s shoulder. “So what’s the next step?” Maynard shrugged. “Just going to, you know ... ask her. Tonight.” “Tonight? So, your heart is really set on this whole marriage thing?” Clayton said; a one last stitch effort to talk his friend out of it. Maynard smiled warmly, placing a hand on Clayton’s shoulder. “I’m sure, and there’s not a thing you can do about it.” * Sara looked beautiful. Her sandy blond hair was tied back into an intricate bun that left Maynard wondering how she’d managed to do it herself, and she had on her best diamond earrings; a birthday gift he’d given her. She wore make-up, but as usual was able to find a balance in such a way that besides the fact that her lips were ruby-red, one might not even know she had anything on at all. She wore an elegant purple dress, with light blue lace around the seams that hung just below her knees. And her eyes, of course: the most striking blue eyes you’ll ever see. Even after all that preparation, thought Maynard, my favourite part of her are those eyes, and she had nothing to do with them at all. “You look,” started Maynard, sounding as though he was thoroughly out of breath, “incredible.” “Thank you”, Sara said, going a bright shade of pink that even her blush couldn’t hide. Maynard held out his hand. Sara placed hers into his and he raised it to his face, kissing it softly. “You ready to go?” She nodded and he led her down her front path to where his car was parked. He opened and closed her door for her, then wheeled around the car to get in himself. He took one last glance at the house and saw her father standing in the bay window, the television lighting him ominously from behind. Maynard smiled to himself and got into the car. * “Are you sure you can afford this place?” Sara said for the eighth time after swallowing a mouthful of filet mignon. She was always worried about money, which was odd for a girl from a fairly rich family. Maynard smiled at her from across the table. “Stop worrying, hun. I’ve got it covered.” After dinner and once their desert arrived on the table, his heart began to pound in his chest. Soon she would have finished her cherry cobbler and it would finally be time for him to step up and just do it. Why did this feel so difficult? Sara must have noticed he’d gone a bit pale, because she asked, “are you alright, Maynard?” She was looking concernedly at him. He shot her a nervous, but convincing smile. “Of course, hun.” She eyed him curiously for a moment. “Okay,” she said and returned to her cobbler. Maynard, on the other hand had hardly touched his large slab of carmel cheese cake. At the moment, the thought of eating anything, even his favourite desert, was causing his insides to writhe uncomfortably within him. For the first time, all the things that could go wrong with one’s proposal was rushing thorough his head. He hadn’t thought of it before, but what if she said no? What if she laughed at him? What if she simply stared at him like he was crazy? There was so much that could go wrong ... “Are you sure you’re alright?” Sara asked again. “You haven’t touched your cake.” “Yeah,” he said in a voice that didn’t sound much like his. It was slightly high-pitched and croaky. “Just not really hungry.” She appeared to accept this as a reasonable excuse because she returned to the last few bites of her desert. Once she’d finished, she pushed away the plate and groaned with satisfaction. “Oh my goodness! I couldn’t eat another bite. It was delicious, Maynard, thank you! Are you sure you can afford this place?” Maynard nodded. “Of course.” She eyed him suspiciously. “Something’s going on with you.” “Sara, I ..” he started, she leaned in closer, interested. “We came here for a reason,” he mumbled stupidly. Why was he going to pieces now? Why did it have to be now? “Yes?” she said with her one eyebrow cocked slightly. “I’m going to be leaving next month,” he blurted, wishing he could find a less blunt method of getting to his point. She groaned. “Maynard, you know I don’t like talking about this ...” “I know,” he said quickly. “But it’s important to me. We’ve been together eight months, and there really hasn’t been a better time in my life.” He could hear his heart beating in his ears. Sara was looking at him, wanting to speak but not doing so. “When I look at you, all I can see is the future,” Maynard said, sliding his hand across the table to take hers. “I see white picket fences, kids running around the yard, taking family trips in our mini van. You know, family stuff. “But we have this elephant in the room that we’re not allowed to talk about: me moving to Iowa for school. Sara, I know it’s hard for you, but it’s going to happen and I’m really not sure if either of us can handle this long-distance thing.” Her eyes widened. “No, no!” backpedaled Maynard. “I’m not breaking up with you!” He took a deep breath. Clearly this whole thing wasn’t his forte. He kept mixing up his words and meanings. “Sara, I love you with every inch of me. Without you I am honestly just an empty shell of a man. You are the love of my life, and I’m not going to let my leaving ruin that.” Maynard stood up, reaching into his suit jacket and pulled out a small black box. Sara gasped, her hand on her heart. He knelt down on one knee beside her, opened the box to reveal a simple gold band with a single small diamond at the head of it. “Sara Isabelle Quenton, I know we’re young; I know people will say we’re rushing things, but I don’t care. I want you in my life now and forever. I want you to be my wife, Sara. “Will you marry me?” Sara’s breath seemed to have caught in her throat and she found herself unable to speak for a moment. Maynard remained on his knee, smiling nervously up at her as a hundred eyes looked on, anxiously waiting the young woman’s response. When she spoke, she too seemed out of breath. “Maynard, I ... I don’t know what to say!” she said quietly. “Just, you know ... say what’s in your heart”, said Maynard, who, at the moment, seemed able to only speak in cliches. “I love you, but” she started, her eyes still on the ring in Maynard’s hand. “But?” Maynard breathed, heart stopping. “But, are you sure?” He couldn’t help but chuckle. “Are you serious? I’m here, down on one knee in front of a, now that I’m noticing, really crowded restaurant, asking you to marry me ... and you ask if I’m serious? Of course I’m serious! I love you so much.” She smiled a very toothy smile down at him. Her blue eyes glistening, one the verge of tears. “Yes,” she said happily. “Of course I will!” Maynard sprang up and wrapped his arms around her as the whole restaurant exploded into a lively round of applause for the young couple. Everything was right in the world. ---- Present ---- With Clayton’s words still ringing in his ears at what seemed like unbearable volume, Maynard sat alone in his darkened hotel room. The only light stemmed from the white-blue glow of the television. He had a glass of Jack on the rocks in his right hand, his cell phone in his left; on its screen was the name ‘Gertrude’ and ten numbers. Time and again, he would scroll to the ‘call’ option, take a long sip of his drink, shake his head and flip the phone shut, only to open it and again scroll to the woman’s name to begin the internal struggle all over again. Why did this woman affect him so? What was different about her from any of the other women he’d picked up at some sleazy club? He tried to think back to a specific moment of their evening together for an explanation, but it basically went down the same way it did with anyone else. They drank, they laughed and she went back to his hotel with him. Game over. Why, then, would she give him this feeling in his gut? Almost as if he was falling for her. Maynard hadn’t fallen for a woman since she-who-shall-not-be-named. Maynard could still see her when he closed his eyes: her long long blond hair, slender frame, long legs, brilliantly blue eyes ... That was it, wasn’t it? Her eyes. She had her eyes. Those big blue eyes the colour of the ocean. That had to be it, though it disgusted Maynard thoroughly to even think about that horrible woman. But then he thought of what Clayton had said: that was fifteen years ago. Fifteen long years. Maynard had been an emotional recluse: afraid to feel even the slightest of intimacy for a woman; the only motive behind his strictly sexual, preferably one-time relationships being to drown the pain of his failed relationship. For fifteen years he’s allowed this woman from his past to dictate his future. What better time than now, he thought to himself, to let it go?” He flipped open his phone one final time, scrolling quickly to her name and pressed the call button before his brain had the chance to talk him out of it. He drained his glass as the phone rang once -- twice, a third time before it was answered by a woman’s voice. “Hello?” it said “Hi, Gertrude?”
