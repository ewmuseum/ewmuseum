The Battle Approaches

6 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/6/06 Somewhere in Navarre, 800 a.d. The constant clash of metal shot through the open field sprawling out to the mountains in the distance, as the small expeditionary force met their attackers with a tired bravery, something that surprised their commander, Virgiliu Sterescu, as he sat atop his horse, surveying the layout of the battle. His lieutenant, Miguel, sat beside him, peering carefully at the attackers. The battle hadn't been going on quite long enough for the scent of death to wash over the ordinarily placid plains. 'Lord Sterescu, there should be none hostile to us here... we're in the plains of our homeland. There are no Franks here.' Virgiliu shook his head, as he rode down the hill a little farther, in order to get a better look at the actual men attacking. Miguel paced him quickly, and Virgiliu paused at a ridge a few feet before the land flattened, dismounting, and pulling his bow off of his shoulders, nocking an arrow, and glaring out into the melee. The last thing he'd expected to come between him and his continued life at home was an army of villagers. 'No... they are not Franks. The way they attacked is far too cowardly for Franks or Gauls. They are Vascones. Look how they fight, with staffs and stolen swords. I don't understand their motive... we didn't attack one of their towns.' Miguel's face twisted in confusion, as Virgiliu loosed an arrow into the back of the attackers' lines, felling a tall man carrying an axe, his face a mask of pain and surprise as he collapsed limply to the ground. Virgil smiled grimly, hiding the slight tinge of fear he felt at the unexpected battle. He expected his soldiers could put this incursion down, but there was always a worry hidden deep in his heart, that he wouldn't make it home to his wife and daughter. A warm breeze blew across the field, carrying with it the clangs and scratching of steel against steel, and the screams of the wounded. 'But Lord Sterescu... why would the Vascones attack us? Didn't we make peace with them, and agree to stay out of their homeland?' Virgiliu nodded, and fired another arrow, which shot into the fray, but he couldn't tell if it found a suitable mark. He slung his bow back onto his shoulders, and climbed back into the saddle, drawing his longsword, and glaring out to the battlefield. However much he wanted to get home, he was going to ensure that his men did the same. 'Where are you going, sir?' Virgiliu stared down at Miguel, the hatred in his expression not directed at his most trusted of friends, perhaps his only one. Miguel swallowed a gulp of air at the sight. 'To fight. Our soldiers have endured enough from the Celts, I will not sit idly by and let their weary arms finish this battle.' Miguel reached out and placed a hand on Virgiliu's shoulder, his eyes pleading, but his other hand already on his scimitar, knowing well his pleas would fall on deaf ears. Virgil looked to him, certain he knew what Miguel had in store. However, he still wanted to at least let him say he'd tried. 'But think of what would happen to them if their leader were to fall... there would be no controller of your father's land, no husband to your wife, no father to your child, Lord.' Virgiliu's eyes went vacant for a moment, as he remembered the lush mountain-scape of his home, where his father's castle sat majestically atop a plateau, in view of the surrounding valley and hills for miles around, with everything he lived for inside. His perfect wife would forgive him, as well as his beautiful daughter, if he were to die pursuing their safety from Breton raids... but he had no plans to die. They had only been married for two years. He swallowed, ridding himself of a faint metallic taste in his throat. 'I'm going. We faced the berserk Celts to the North, do you think we cannot repel a tiny force of Vascones? You may stay back if you wish, but I will not let this affront to my family's honor go unpunished. The treacherous mountain- dwellers will not keep me from my home. And with that, he pressed his heels into his horse's side, and rode off into the battle, Miguel not hesitating to follow him to an early grave, if it was necessary. Suddenly, the fields of Spain started to feel a little cooler, and the world around him flickered, and abruptly shot into blackness, followed by a bright, rather painful light. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Mifune's School, Sapporo, Japan 'Ugh...' Virgil's hands instinctively covered his eyes, though he had begun to get used to the lack of panic at waking up during the day, covered in sunlight. The abruptness of his rousing from such a deep sleep would have already put him in a foul mood, but the imagery of his dream flooded back to him. He shook his head, and glanced at Lia, asleep next to him, the sight of her offering him at least some relief from his irritation. He rubbed his eyes with the bottom of his hands, before lying back, gently, so as not to wake Lia with his sudden moving about. Something about that dream was severely bothering him. It seemed so real, not in the sense of every dream had as a child, but as one would feel deja-vu when performing some common, everyday task, or even something completely new. However, something in Virgil's mind told him that it certainly wasn't 'jamais-vu', something he'd never come across. Too much of it was familiar. He laid quietly back down. The face of the man next to him, the name he'd used, it seemed familiar, really familiar. But if it had been a memory, why couldn't he remember it now? All he remembered was what was in the dream, although he knew the plain that battle had occurred on, some of the soldiers on the field, his lieutenant, even his horse and his bow... but beyond that, he remembered nothing more of them, a feeling that caused him to tense up in bed, gritting his teeth in frustration. The one memory he'd been able to have, and he couldn't even have another just to make him certain he wasn't really just losing his mind. Today was not going to be a pleasant day. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ He resisted the urge to slam the heavy door of Mifune's home and school, remembering that few things could have possibly been ruder. He swore that these people, these interlopers, people like Dawn McFarlane, Seymour Almasy, and especially Vivica Valentine at the moment, were doing nothing but causing him the greatest of misery, and if they were responsible for this erosion of stability between he and Lia, then he would see to it that they all suffered somehow. A cool wind brushed against his face, cooling his anger, but doing little to prevent the hurt from festering inside him, seething and waiting for the next opportunity to manifest itself. That was why he'd gone out to search for Lilith in the first place. At the least, his anger would be better directed at a being he loathed far and beyond all others. Mother of his race or not, she was no kin of his after laying a hand on his odd friend Cyrus, and far worse, his beloved Lia. He knew Lilith wasn't searching for him, but her intentions be damned, she would have to completely destroy him before she ever got a chance at success in her quest. He knew she was here. It was just a matter of finding her... what Virgil was going to do with Lilith when he found her was a mystery to him, and it likely wouldn't end too well for him. Lilith was, after all, the most powerful being in the world, likely, which brought to his mind the question of what use she could possibly get after hunting down one of her own children, presumably with the intention of killing her. Of course, he knew the answer. The fact that he was walking in the sunlight, albeit with dark shades over his eyes, was all owed to her, which in turn was owed to Leinad Soma. By taking his blood into her, she created a race that grew past the limitations of both her and Soma's. She could walk in the daylight, be sustained by water, however poorly, yet she avoided the doom of madness that Soma himself would one day fall prey to. If she desired, she could be the mother of an entirely new race, of which Virgil would have been able to call himself the first descendant. He paused on the rocky path leading down the mountain, struck by an unfamiliar scent, one that smelled lightly of camphor and something he couldn't quite identify. It lay somewhere between formaldehyde and decomposition, and he was instantly reminded of what had happened in Bratislava, where he'd walked through the gaping hole in Cyrus' library to see Lia, facing the terrifying Lilith fearlessly, fully prepared to fight to the death, both supernaturally beautiful, almost similar to each other in a way, like two lions fighting for control of a prideland, neither planning on backing down. However, for reasons he hadn't ever been able to divine, Lilith did exactly that. No conflict, no injuries aside from Cyrus, who had recovered well. He started backpedaling, against his own will. At this point, it was pure instinct, telling him it was probably for the best to flee. She was close. Close enough for him to know, however, that it wasn't quite Lilith. There was a different feeling... perhaps a more dangerous one. He knew immediately that Wang had been wrong, or so he could only think. If the Cathayans were wrong, with their massive intelligence networks, then something was definitely amiss. But who else could it possibly be? Suddenly, the acrid scent of rotting flesh, real rotting flesh, assailed his strong nose, and he turned to the side, what he assumed was the source of the horrible stench. What he saw was nearly enough to horrify him, a being that had been witness to slaughter of countless humans and animals; a large pile of exsanguinated (by his judgement) animals lay in a clearing, carelessly piled on top of each other, like a pile of bones after a great feast. However, he could immediately tell that they hadn't been killed by one of their own, as though the way they were stacked wasn't enough proof of this. The claw marks were larger and more powerful than anything he'd ever heard of inhabiting Japan. That was all the proof he needed. He turned, and dashed back toward Mifune's school, kicking up sprays of gravel behind him. He had to tell someone. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ He nearly slammed into the door of Mifune's academy as he reached the top of the mountain, and hurriedly pulled the laces out of his boots, tossing them to the side, still somehow possessed to follow the Japanese custom when entering the house. He slipped through the door, shutting it securely behind him, and locking the bolt, before wandering through the hallway at the front, looking around the large common room, rather irritated at finding absolutely nobody there. He stormed through the remarkably spacious hallways, something he'd never encountered in a Japanese residence. There had to be someone here somewhere. Even Dawn would be someone to tell at this juncture. However, he couldn't hear a thing. That power was getting closer, or stronger, he couldn't tell, but it expedited his step just a bit, as he crashed into the dojo, where Master Mifune was sitting on the sparring floor, apparently meditating before Virgil's disturbance. 'Do be careful in my house, Ambrosi-san.' Virgil bowed his head slightly. 'Shitsuree shimasen.' Mifune opened his eyes, something Virgil hadn't noticed, but which puzzled him slightly, as he didn't think his particular style of crashing into a room was that distinguishable from anyone else's. Mifune waved his hand. 'Thank you... but why the rush, Ambrosi-san?' 'Have you seen Lia?' His words tumbled out in a panicked rush, his eyes wide with what could only be termed fear. He would never admit it to anyone, but facing Lilith alone did terrify him. With Lia, there was no fear whatsoever, but she was nowhere to be found. Mifune, however, nodded. 'Yes. She went to confront the being outside.' Virgil paused, frozen in surprise, or perhaps just simple confusion. 'But it felt so much like Lilith did...' Mifune cast his eyes downward momentarily, and shrugged. 'She mentioned that name, I believe... I'm afraid I don't know much of your kind's mythos. Forgive my rudeness.' Virgil shook his head. 'Where did she go? I have to fight alongside her!' Mifune's eyes snapped up toward Virgil, the steely glance setting Virgil off- guard. Aside from the occasional glare from Dawn, or incorrigible stubbornness from Vivica, no human ever dared to look at him as inferior. 'No. This is something she must do alone.' Virgil threw his hands up in frustration. 'Alone! What foolishness is that?!' Mifune ignored Virgil's rather rude (in his opinion) outburst. 'If this one is as powerful as you seem to think, what use would you be to Lia? From what I have gleaned about Lilith, she seeks power that Lia possesses. That makes her a weaker being' Virgil blinked, one of the few mannerisms he still possessed from being a human, though he no longer needed to. 'I... I don't know. Lilith is the mother of our race.' Mifune nodded. 'Ah... but, is then Lia not the mother of her own race? I assume one of you is a progenitor, as you don't conform to the usual folklore, or at least I assume so, since neither of you have burst into flame as of yet.' Virgil looked at the polished wooden floor. 'She gave me the power I have today.' Mifune nodded, sighing lightly, remarkably peacefully given the weight of the situation. 'Then she is, in a sense, your mother, though I don't accuse you of incest, Ambrosi-san.' Virgil was slightly puzzled by the non-sequitir. 'Anyway... as this is the case, be confident in her. She will protect you, and everyone here. I have no fear of this being.' Virgil shook his head. 'The last time I stood back, and let her take matters into her own hands, she was abducted and spent almost a month underneath some bar in the middle of the Nevada desert!' Mifune nodded, once again, a very simple, calm gesture. 'Ambrosi-san... all things happen for some kind of reason. At least, I believe so. Her trial had nothing to do with a lack of independence. She came out in a different mental state, and it also weakened your dislike of Dawn-chan, hm?' Virgil nodded, resisting the urge to pace back and forth nervously. 'Yes...' 'What weakness she had when she faced Lilith before has been purged from her, I'd imagine. If I'm wrong, then you wouldn't have the power to destroy her either, and we're all doomed. Either way, stay here. Support her with your belief in her as the mother of your new race.' Frustration threatened to boil over in Virgil's mind, but he knew beneath his immovable beliefs that Mifune was right... if Lia couldn't defeat Lilith, or Roshael, whoever that was, then they really all would be doomed. Suddenly, Virgil felt very weak, powerless like he assumed his prey must feel. He didn't like the feeling, nor did he like the idea of staying behind during the inevitable battle. However, his stubbornness couldn't be completely subverted. If there was any sign of trouble, he'd burst through the wall if he had to, even if it was just to die alongside her. Thus began the most difficult wait of Virgil's existence.
