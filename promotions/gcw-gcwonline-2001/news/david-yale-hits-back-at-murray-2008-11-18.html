David Yale Hits Back At Murray

18 November 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Several days ago, GCW phenomenon Andy Murray defied his critics, demanding respect for his impressive accomplishments of late. Noted Murray critic David Yale, however, isn't buying it. "This is just another example of Andy Murray failing to understand how this business works," Yale said on his radio show in Columbus. "Dangerous Games is damn near impossible to win. And with the exception of LeStatt Knight, nobody has ever been there at the end twice. You get one crack at winning that match, one chance to get that World Title shot, and Murray came up short. If he thinks he's just going to walk into another World Title opportunity, he's going to be very disappointed." Of all Murray's critics, Yale has been the most vocal. Of course, it helps that Yale is present at ringside for every GCW event with a live microphone. For the most part Murray is beloved by the company and most believe he is still destined for stardom. But Yale isn't letting up. Just today he continued to blast Murray. "I won't speculate about what effect his recent encounters with LeStatt Knight has had on Murray's head. But I don't need to speculate about what Knight revealed about Murray. Murray's a great wrestler with a fatal flaw, and Knight found it and exploited it. Murray lives in this fantasy land where everyone is nice to one another and treats each other with respect. That's not how pro wrestling works. Andy Murray's got a message more suitable for teaching kindergarten than being GCW World Champion. We don't all share and get along. This is a sport where the best take what they want, when they want, by any means necessary. And if Murray thinks his way is deserving of respect, I'd like him to explain why LeStatt Knight is going to the NC-17 main event and he's not." When asked by his co-host why he's being so hard on Murray, Yale replied, "You play to win the game. It's all or nothing. It's like Murray wants a silver medal for finishing second, but in reality all he gets is a front-row seat to LeStatt Knight's World Title shot." David Yale's comments can't derail Murray's impressive ride. But it's certainly shining a light on the first cracks to appear in Murray's armor. Murray has been so dominant since his arrival, however, that this level of scrutiny was, perhaps, inevitable. When someone is as great as Murray has been, it's bound to attract attention. The truth will be revealed in Murray's reaction. Can he handle the first real adversity he's faced in GCW, or will the pressure be too much for him? Murray's showing signs that his critics might be getting to him, or that LeStatt Knight may have exposed a fatal flaw. But Murray just defeated Vivica J. Valentine at Devil's Night in usual Murray fashion, so it doesn't appear Murray will be slowing down just yet.
