Dangerous Games Flashback: 2001

18 August 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
With Dangerous Games now growing near, we here at GCWOnline.net will be taking a look back at the previous editions of the year's most anticipated event. This week: the first ever Dangerous Games, which took place at Kemper Arena in Kansas City in 2001. The Card Dangerous Games 2001 was filled out with a number of big matches. The event saw Zippy capture the Television Title from Capo, becoming the second-ever holder of the belt. Chris Van Burren and Dan Rivero, the Real Deal, defeated Assassin One and Mikado for the GCW Tag Team Titles, and the Cruiserweight Champion Chris Jordan defeated Toad for the Hardcore Title to become GCW's first double champion. Later, Luke Hanson took on Shane Lane for the United States Title in a match that would set a trend for future events: someone from the United States Title match would go on to win the next three Dangerous Games events. The Match The first Dangerous Games match was by far the biggest event in GCW's short history. The original Final Four was John Reed, Trash (later known as 2002 Games winner Twizzy), and the United States Title rivals Luke Hanson and Shane Lane. Despite losing to Hanson earlier in the night, Shane Lane entered at number four and survived to the end, eliminating Hanson and Trash simultaneously to win the 2001 Games. Lane's win marks one of the most controversial moments in a company that has seen many of them. Lane was highly unpopular despite his natural talent, but his alliance with GCW Commissioner Mr. Danger gave him unprecedented influence. The Dangerous Games match marked only his sixth match with the company, making him the fastest rising star in GCW history by far. The Main Event Dangerous Games 2001 was headlined by a huge battle for the World Title between The Jhub and champion Rich 'The Renegade' Rollins. Rollins, who had beaten Establishment leader Octavian in an Iron Man Match to capture the belt the month before, was outnumbered with Caldera at ringside. Mr. Danger was the special referee for the match, but he was unable to outfox the Establishment. Caldera aided The Jhub to his first GCW Title win. The Aftermath The Jhub's championship reign would be short lived. Mr. Danger, outraged at the actions of The Establishment, ordered The Jhub to defend his title four days later at Meltdown against the Dangerous Games winner Shane Lane. Lane defeated The Jhub in a colossal shocker, beginning the most criticized championship reign in the eight year history of the belt. Lane's reign wouldn't last long, either, as Caldera forced him to defend his title in a Six-Pac Challenge match at Annihilation. There Rollins returned and recaptured the belt for his second reign. Lane was gone shortly thereafter, and his Dangerous Games victory and subsequent Championship reign remain a dark spot on GCW's history.
