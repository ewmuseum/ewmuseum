Remembering Rich Rollins

20 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
One year ago today, professional wrestling lost a true giant. GCW Hall of Fame member Rich "The Renegade" Rollins was found dead in his hotel room, a tragedy that is still sharply felt by many in Global Championship Wrestling. Rollins was a wrestler of worldwide notoriety who toured with a number of promotions, but he was a true GCW original. As a bright young high flier out of Chicago, Rollins caught the eye of the CWU, GCW's predecessor. Rollins became the franchise star of the young CWU, and upon the company's closing he followed Steven Caldera and a number of former CWU wrestlers to Global Championship Wrestling in February of 2001. Rollins was part of the strong foundation that enabled the fledgling promotion to survive and flourish. He got off to a rocky start, however, after suffering a back injury in the main event of the first episode of Meltdown. The injury put Rollins, an early favorite to become the first GCW World Champion, out of contention for the belt, opening the door for blooming star Octavian to claim the belt. Rollins returned to action, however, and met Octavian and Khan in a triple threat Iron Man match at Shockwave. Rollins continued his existing Iron Man winning streak, capturing his first GCW World Title to become GCW's second World Champion. After losing the belt the following month, Rollins re-united with the resurgent CWU and recaptured the title at Annihilation in a six pac challenge match. The reign would be Rollins' last experience with a World Title, but his career had really just begun to grow. Things looked bleak for Rollins, however, after suffering a devastating back injury in an ACWF Match with The Jhub at NC-17. The injury was so severe that doctors informed Rollins that his career was over. He spent the next four years out of the sport, rehabbing diligently in hopes that his body might some day permit him to return to the ring. That return happened in 2005, when Rollins reappeared in GCW as color commentator. Rollins was initially unable to wrestle, but shocked the world by re-entering the ring to attack Caldera's protege Mike Hardy. The two met in an Iron Man match at Dangerous Games, where Rollins was once again victorious. This match is regarded by many as the greatest match in GCW history. Rollins would go on to engage in a brutal rivalry with Seymour Almasy, after which he left GCW for a time. Rollins returned to the ring in 2006, but he snubbed GCW and signed with rival promotion PRIME. Despite their rocky relationship, Rollins and Caldera maintained a heavy respect for one another, but the move to PRIME opened a bitter rift between the two. Rollins teamed with Jason Snow and Angelo Deville in PRIME, but his biggest impact occurred at GTT5 that year. Rollins tore through the competition, dominating the field en route to the most one-sided tournament of all time. Due to the growing rivalry between GCW and PRIME and the personal rivalry between Caldera and Rollins, the two agreed to a match at NC-17 2006. Caldera teamed with Almasy to take on Rollins and Deville in a tag team ACWF match, which Caldera ultimately won when he caused Rollins to lose consciousness. The match was expected to end the matter, but Rollins shocked the world by agreeing to a new GCW contract in 2007. Rollins picked up where he left off, returning to NC-17 the following year in yet another ACWF match, this time against Jordan White. White, who many compared to Rollins in terms of career evolution if not talent, fell to the legend Rollins in a brutal and bloody battle. The victory re-established Rollins as a premier talent in GCW, putting him in contention for the GCW World Title in early 2008. However, in the midst of a round robin competition for the number one contendership, Rollins was discovered dead in his hotel room. His death was blamed on an overdose of painkillers, which Rollins was apparently taking heavily due to his many lingering injuries. GCW has openly condemned the circumstances surrounding Rollins' death and acknowledges that Rollins should not have been permitted to compete in his condition. Rollins, however, was an intense competitor and hid his medical condition, unwilling to let up with his career riding high. It was one final controversy for the always controversial superstar. Fellow Hall of Fame member Jason Dread remembers Rollins warmly. "Ever since my arrival in Global Championship Wrestling, Rich Rollins was there and he was a real pioneer. From his in ring presence to his overall attitude and demeanor he was a major part of the heart and soul of GCW. It was Rollins who was also a major inspiration in me and the direction I undertook during my years in GCW. He was a real inspiration to many more than just I and his death was indeed a tragedy, not only to GCW but to the sport of Pro Wrestling as a whole. He is, and will continue to be missed for generations to come." GCW President Steven Caldera, perhaps Rollins' oldest friend in the business, could not be reached for comment on this story. To honor Rollins' contributions to GCW, the company has announced it is resuming work on the much anticipated Best of The Renegade DVD compilation. The DVD, which will include 15 of the best matches and moments from Rollins' GCW career, is expected to be released by the end of the month.
