Bhava Vetting Process Among Most Difficult in GCW History?

4 August 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Sources within GCW claim that Bhava was nearly not signed due to difficulty in obtaining information about the Zen Buddhist. Global Championship Wrestling prides itself on having a very thorough researching process on any athlete who seeks to enter the GCW ring. Now, sources within GCW are claiming that new talent Bhava was perhaps one of the most difficult prospective talents to vet in the company's long and illustrious history. Usually, the prospective talents themselves are able to provide much of the information needed, with further research done by GCW staff. However, the circumstances surrounding Bhava and his rumored retrograde amnesia made even this basic task difficult. "The guy lost his memory," one staffer told us, on condition of anonymity. "His friends and co-workers were helping piece it back together. We had to send a small team of people over to Japan to verify a lot of the information we got. His home promotion over there, WORLD WARRIORS, was very helpful, as were a bunch of the Canadian indies that he competed for." Ultimately, of course, GCW was comfortable enough to let Bhava in the company. Some do wonder, however, whether or not Steven Caldera will be so comfortable allowing the newcomer into the Dangerous Games battle royale. It is a known fact that Caldera personally vets every entrant into the huge match, and there remains some unanswered questions about Bhava. "Sure, he's a newcomer with no likely shot at winning the thing," our staff source told us, "but Steven Caldera didn't become the businessman he is without dotting every single i and crossing every single t. If Caldera isn't comfortable with the kid, no way he gets in the match even with zero chance of winning." Neither Bhava nor Steven Caldera could be reached for comment.
