Establishment v 12.0: Best Ever?

30 October 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
At WorldWide this week, President Caldera's fabled group The Establishment underwent a rearrangement. The show kicked off with the stunning announcement from Jordan White that he'd decided not to serve anyone's interests but his own, and be subsequently became the first man to willingly leave The Establishment. Not to be outdone, President Caldera had a resounding response to offer in the night's main event. Caldera forced White to team with his Dangerous Games opponent, GCW World Champion Lia Ambrosi, against the new tandem of LeStatt Knight and Octavian. LeStatt Knight and Octavian are the undisputed greatest champions in GCW history. United now after being separated by two different eras in GCW history, the two have made this version of The Establishment the most heralded of all time. The group featuring Jason Dread and a young Michael Stevens is no doubt a tremendous one, but in terms of accomplishments, nobody tops Knight and Octavian. But one man can rival them. In the main event, Jordan White got the shock of his life. Rich "The Renegade" Rollins made his surprise return to GCW, delivering a crushing superkick to White and exacting a measure of revenge for White's Iron Man victory at Tsunami. The closing moments of the show saw the three greatest champions of all time now united together under the banner of The Establishment. Though Rollins has not held the GCW World Title in over six years, he has since gone on to become perhaps the most successful GCW product of all time. His exploits abroad and in rival promotions are well documented, and his addition to The Establishment makes it the most potent group GCW has ever seen. One could certainly make the case that former Establishment leader (and rival) Jason Dread deserves to be in the conversation of great champions. Dread won the title twice and was a great champion in both reigns. The case is also getting stronger for Lia Ambrosi to join the group of all-time greats, as she has either held or been in contention for the World Title for the entirety of 2007. Her win over Jordan White might have solidified her Hall of Fame status, and she has yet another opportunity ahead of her at NC-17 to build on that resume. But LeStatt, Octavian and Rollins have been around so long and accomplished so much that all three are already in the Hall of Fame. They have seven World Title reigns between them in GCW alone (that's not counting Rollins' two CWU reigns). Perhaps most troubling of all was Caldera's allusion at the end of WorldWide that The Establishment will "only get better from here." Rumors, with number one contender Vivica J. Valentine at the center, have been flying all day regarding the next member of The Establishment. It is clear that the group is looking to gain a monopoly on big-name talent, and Valentine would certainly be a massive acquisition. Of course, most fans and friends of Valentine are quite certain that Valentine would never seek to take a shortcut to the World Title. However, with the odd events at WorldWide this week involving Valentine and Caldera, speculation is bound to continue until Valentine clears the air herself. (Does Peter Maxwell know the truth?) The Establishment isn't yet in a position to dominate GCW, and perhaps now more than ever the company has the talent to fight back. Jordan White has already proven he can beat The Renegade in the biggest environment. Jay Terror is a fast riser with a huge following, and he seems to have his sights set on Knight. And, as currently constituted, there is no Establishment member in the main event at NC-17, a match reserved for best friends Lia Ambrosi and Vivica J. Valentine. The subtraction of Jordan White certainly means less youth and upside, but The Establishment has never been about the future. It's about the present. And the present certainly looks good for what could become the wrestling world's most dominant group. The Establishment has always dominated GCW, but it has never had a group of talent like this before. The onus will now fall on rising stars like White and Terror, who will team to take on Rollins and Knight at Devil's Night, to prove they can ascend to a higher plateau and compete with the first-ever all-Hall of Fame group in GCW history.
