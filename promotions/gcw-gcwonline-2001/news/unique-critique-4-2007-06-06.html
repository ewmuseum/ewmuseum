GCW — Column

Unique Critique #4

6 June 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
GCW's trademark column is a comprehensive review of the entire active roster. Written by a different anonymous contributor each week, Unique Critique is the most honest assessment of each member's performance you can find. The commentary ranges from complimentary to mercilessly cruel, so if you can't handle it, don't read on! But if you want to know how each roster member stacks up in the eyes of one writer, Unique Critique offers you the chance. -Mediocre -Average -Good -Great -World Class Lia Ambrosi-- Her recent card presence has been very strong and she's been pretty active since losing the belt. While the angle of her trying to find a way to overcome Mayhem has in many ways added depth to her character, it does feel like it is ready to move on. Her best segment comes from WW68 in Do What You Gotta Do. Overall: Did the right thing with the strong card presence after losing the title, but don't keep giving more of the same old, same old. When it comes to roleplays, I don't think you'll find a single person who won't agree that Lia is an amazing writer. And while I do feel that it was nice to see bits about the match thrown in, it actually took away from the story rather than add to it. Mainly because at the bones of it, those parts didn't matter or even connect to the story. Reading the entire thing, I actually kept looking for some kind of relation between Mayhem and Sinclair, however unless I missed something, none came. I was disappointed. While I don't feel that you need to roleplay about wrestling, I feel that this series would have just been brilliant had it been a symbolic representation of her battle with Mayhem. Jayce Bradley-- Where has Bradley even been? His only segment since High Stakes was clearly all Shaman. His last roleplay was well over a month ago. As far as I am concerned, he's getting near NPC status. He comes in, jobs, and leaves. Tyson DeBough-- He's a new character, so only one segment from him thus far. While I was not a fan of the segment, I do like the fact that he's already putting in the effort. With the roleplay, I can see some potential but with that said, it needs improvement. The story jumps to the weird gangster style setting out of nowhere. Then jumps back to the agency just as oddly. Reactions were as fake as a bad movie script. Emotions did not feel sincere. It felt more like a detailed outline than a fleshed out roleplay. However, that can easily come with time. High Roller-- Only reason he has 1 star is because he at least has a bio filled out. However, unless he magically reappears, he needs to be fired. Hydra-- The bio is weird to say the least. His card presence doesn't exist. But at least he has a roleplay up. Well, that's if we want to call it a roleplay. I was thinking of outline for a roleplay. Obviously it can use some work, but at least he seems to have a good ability to tell a story. He just needs to flesh it out and build more for the reader to skin their teeth into. Kyle James-- Here is a person I hope we continue to see more of. He is someone GCW can build into a good home grown talent, and really represent the company. It is one thing for a place to hire already established stars like Lia, Storm, Ramey, Vetra and others, but it is a complete other for them to build the stars themselves. I think he needs to build himself more as a stand out though. LeStatt Knight-- He needs a stronger card presence than what he has. The majority of his appearances are just interferences. From what I hear, his time is limited. That is more than understandable. I'm just sad because the Establishment was given such hype and haven't lived up to any of it. They have did very little to build themselves as top heels. His best segment would be on WW68 with Twizzy. His strong suit, however, and what makes him such a favorite is his roleplays. He has a way with a story that can keep a person interested without drawing it out for too long. In this hobby, that is a great thing. His main area for improvement is those long stretches of dry dialogue he gets into. It makes things confusing, as it is easy to lose track of who is saying what if not done right. Description is great to solve this problem. Bryan Mayhem-- The World Champion, the representative of the company to all others. He is the first person outsiders look at when looking at our talent depths. He has great card presence, but far too many of the segments are WAY too short and almost unimportant. Too many of his segments read exactly like the ones before it, creating a bad monotone for the character. He needs to mix it up if he wants to stay fresh. The roleplay itself was dry. It lacked the emotion it needed to get me to connect. It also dragged in a number of spots. The only true bright spot of the roleplay comes at the end, where I get the feeling that after Warpath, we could really see something interesting from the character. Where does he need improvement? The segments need depth, badly. The roleplay needs feeling and emotion, or at least something to connect with the reader. Jack O'Toole-- A new character, not much to comment on. Looking forward to them. They seem to be having some interesting storylines building up with the Establishment and the tag team champions. Paddy O'Shea-- Here is another new character that can make a huge impact. His segment on WW68 was solid, though the dialect was a bit hard to grasp at times. The roleplay seemed rushed, sadly. But work is a bitch. At least the roleplay showed just how talented he can be. It is going to be a great sight to see him hit all cylinders when he does. His ranking is low by nothing of his own fault, and will only increase as time goes on. Shawn Porter-- His segment wasn't as strong as some of the other newcomers, but his roleplay made up for part of that. Granted, like other newcomers, he could use some work. But with time he could be a strong mid card talent. The main areas of concern is keeping constant, polishing his roleplays, and staying strong on the cards. Jesse Ramey-- Out of all the new talent, no one has thrown themselves into GCW like Jesse Ramey. His roleplays are improving, his card presence is strong (if not the strongest of the entire roster) and his is putting out talented work. His main area of concern is not to burn out, and to not over do it. It is very easy to grow old which so much work. His best segment is Smoke Break-In from WW68, and he looks to be developing quite the feud with Brock Shepherd. Rikki Roxx-- Roxx might as well be a NPC, which is sad. He has potential and had a nice segment on WW68. Tiara Belle Russell-- From my understanding, Please & Pain has a new handler. Hopefully they can do something with this tag team to once again rise them through the ranking. Brock Shepherd-- The second hottest of the new talents, he hit a road block with a no show on Warpath. Hopefully this doesn't mean a disappearance act for what could be a very interesting and bright character. The feud with Ramey looks to be something to watch, and the gimmick is something I think people can root for. The talent is there, the commitment just needs to follow. Chris Storm-- Banned & Exiled~! haven't lost any steam since entering GCW and continue to be a fan favorite. Their only lacking area is a constant feud, though that is no fault of their own. But even with a few road blocks, they always seem to work around it and make it work for them. Their current storyline with the Establishment looks to be something interesting, and could help the Establishment solidify themselves as a major heel faction. Shaman-- A for effort applies here. What Shaman doesn't have in pure talent, he makes up for with drive. He is improving, slowly, but hopefully working with some others will help to polish his talent. 'The Outlaw' Jay Terror-- Jay has everything he needs to be a big success. He just needs to pick up the card presence to really make an impact. There is a huge crop of new talent gunning for his belt, so he should have plenty to work with. His segment with Ramey was a highlight from WW68. His roleplay is solid, not much to complain about. In time it will continue to develop and improve and Jay will be shedding the TV title for much bigger prizes. He's right where he should be in terms of development. Jimmy Thunder-- Jimmy has never been the most solid of workers, but he does enough to keep himself above the new crop of talent. He has all the tools to be a major player, and hopefully the rumors of his retirement are just rumors. His roleplays are his strong suit, something that keeps him as a front runner for the mid card. A bit more of an effort on the cards and he could be a contender. Twizzy-- Twizzy has been a great addition to the roster, with his old school feel. He brings a bit of old GCW to the new GCW that creates an interesting mix. His segments are funny, his feud with the Establishment is interesting, and his bid for the Hall of Fame is something to look forward to. As long as a retirement doesn't soon follow, he should be a great character for a long time. The only thing keeping him from a higher ranking is the roleplays. They are quick, short, and fast hitting. While great for a good laugh, they do little to leave an impression or give the sense of greatness. You read it for amusement, rather than entertainment. Vivica J. Valentine-- One person had to get a 5 out of 5 to set the curve for all the other characters. Vivica was an easy choice for that role. In every field, she has excelled. She has great segments, a great character, interesting storyline, purpose, drive, and talent to back it all up. The roleplays help to fully compliment everything on screen. Possibly GCW's best roleplayer at the moment, there is little room for improvement. My only concern is that it will be hard to keep up at that level and burn out could easily follow a World title win. Jordan White-- Much like others, card presence is not there. There is much room for improvement in that area, more so given the fact that he is a member of the Establishment. Roleplay wise, there is also the room for improvement. Dialogue stretches on for too long at times, dominating too much of the roleplay. This is sad because when he is writing, his use of words are excellent, and the way he sets up his roleplays are top notch. Then it goes into the roleplay where the dialogue begins and it just stops. It gets boring. There are different, and better, ways to write the same thing rather than continuing in the same format.
