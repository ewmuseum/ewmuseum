The Ivy League #3

27 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
By Cheering Knight, GCW Fans Are Missing the Point An amazing thing has happened these last few weeks. As I've sat at ringside and watched GCW's greatest star perform his final act, I've witnessed as the usually intelligent and informed GCW crowd has completely and utterly ruined the honesty of this moment. LeStatt Knight is going to retire at Shockwave, and it's going to be a lie. It's not Knight's fault. He's doing what he's always done--his job. He's never given a damn what people think of him, he's just gone out to be the best. Sometimes they cheer him; more often they boo. And that's exactly what they should be doing. They should boo LeStatt Knight. They should boo him during his match with Valentine, and they should boo him as he takes his final steps out of the stadium. It's not that he doesn't deserve their respect. This has nothing to do with Knight, who, as we all know, I think will never be touched when it comes to his importance to this company. The people should be in awe of LeStatt Knight and they should appreciate all he did to build this company. But LeStatt Knight, in a strange way, represents vindication for a strange truth always touted by President Steven Caldera. It's why Caldera was always Knight's biggest fan, even as their personalities clashed. It's why GCW has been so successful, and why the Caldera-Knight marriage earns the two of them equal credit in having built this company. Caldera's theory was this: You need heroes and villains to have an effective wrestling promotion. But a hero can only be a great as the villains he has conquered; a villain is great on his own merit. Take James Bond, for example. Everyone loves Bond. He's dashing, charismatic and deadly, not unlike yours truly. But the success of a Bond flick is judged on the greatness of its villain. Truth be told, Bond isn't that interesting, and he's only worth rooting for if he's taking down a particularly dastardly baddie. That's why Goldfinger was so great, and why Octopussy was so terrible. LeStatt Knight is Auric Goldfinger, Ernst Stavro Blofeld, Emilio Largo and Dr. No all rolled into one. Some of the greatest heroes in GCW history are considered great because they waged war with the villain Knight. Knight has had his softer moments, and though few, they were among his most forgettable in GCW. When Knight has been at his best, he's been the bad guy, ruthlessly chasing his goals, hurting anyone who got in the way, and making interesting characters out of boring good guys along the way. What was the greatest moment Michael Stevens ever had? How about Andy Murray? How about Lia Ambrosi? Fighting LeStatt Knight. Knight has meant quite a bit to this company. He's as much responsible for its current success as anyone else. But he did it by joyfully playing the role of the most hated man in GCW. And that's why the fans are lying to themselves when they cheer him today. LeStatt Knight deserves that respect. He deserves those thanks. But they should come after. Thank him by buying his DVD, not by cheering him in a match. That's not what LeStatt Knight's career has been about. LeStatt Knight will go out to the sounds of the cheers of the Hawaii crowd, but that's not how he should be remembered. He should be remembered as the most cruel, sadistic, relentlessly driven man in GCW history. He should be remembered as the man that routinely dashed the hopes and dreams of GCW fans, and built and empire in the process. The fans won't listen. They'll cheer him, they'll thank him, they'll lavish him with much deserved praise. Just know that when I'm sitting at ringside, singing his praises, it's only because I'm a villain, too. And villains are the ones that make the story worth watching.
