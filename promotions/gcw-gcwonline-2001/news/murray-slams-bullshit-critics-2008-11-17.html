Murray slams 'bullshit' critics

17 November 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
GCW wrestler Andy Murray has today dismissed media speculation that his career may be about to take a downturn as ?complete and utter bollocks.? In an uncharacteristically vitriolic diatribe, Murray pulled no punches as he slammed the critics who have written him off since his failure to win Dangerous Games. Murray told reporters at a press conference this morning; “I’ve never heard such nonsense in all my life. So the fact that I didn’t win DG means I’ve run out of steam” What a load of bullshit.” “Look; in the past couple of weeks I’ve taken on the strongest force that GCW has ever had, one-on-one, and beaten him. I’ve finished second in the Dangerous Games match and consolidated my place at the top of the card. How anyone can call a victory over LeStatt Knight and a second-place DG performance a “failure” is completely beyond me.” Murray, who has never been cleanly defeated in one-on-one GCW competition, has established himself as one of the most popular competitors in the promotion following recent big wins over Chris Bagwell, Bryan Mayhem and LeStatt Knight. Since arriving in GCW in December “07 the resolute Scot ? known worldwide for his devotion to sportsmanship and play fair ? has look nigh-on unstoppable in his ascent to the top but speculation is rife that Murray has been shaken and left “damaged” by recent encounters with LeStatt Knight. The Scot, of course, has rubbished these suggestions. “Again, complete nonsense. Last time I checked, technically, I beat LK. Dangerous Games is a total wildcard, anything can happen. So I got tossed out of the ring by a guy. Doesn’t mean he’s a better wrestler.” “The Andy Murray train will keep rolling on. Forget about LeStatt Knight, he and I are done for now. Maybe sometime in the future we’ll draw swords again, but for now, it’s finished. In the meanwhile I’ll keep on doing what I do until I get where I want to be and no amount of speculation and media spin is going to stop me.” A strong, bullish response from a man formerly known for his light-hearted antics that will doubtless come across as curious to anyone who has followed the Scotsman’s GCW career since it’s inception. Murray may claim that the psychological warfare of LeStatt Knight has not affected him, but for some this uncharacteristically vehement response may suggest otherwise.
