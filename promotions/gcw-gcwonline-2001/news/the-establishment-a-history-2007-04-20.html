The Establishment: A History

20 April 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
At High Stakes this week, one of the most infamous entities in Global Championship Wrestling history made its return, forever affecting the landscape of our great promotion. However, it has been some time since President Caldera last turned his back on the fans and infected the company with his group. Most of the current fanbase and roster has yet to experience The Establishment, and as such many in GCW fail to realize just how much their lives are about to change. I, Kevin Jacobs, have been in GCW since the beginning. I was there the very first night The Establishment came to life, and I've been there every step of the way since. So I present to you a brief history of The Establishment, in hopes that you all might realize precisely what this means for GCW. To understand The Establishment, you must first understand its leader. President Steven Caldera started GCW out of his own pocket, investing the entire fortune he'd amassed over his brief years in professional wrestling. He'd wrestled in the ACWF and the AWA, and he'd had his first experience running a promotion with the CWU. With GCW now his own to control, and with every penny of his personal wealth on the line, President Caldera's already well pronounced infatuation with control became full-blown obsession. From day one in GCW, President Caldera sought to maintain total control over every facet of the organization. In the CWU, Caldera was an overnight success, taking the modest regional territory and turning it into a national powerhouse in a matter of months. Despite the rapid inflation of his ego, the wrestlers were loyal to Caldera, who had generously shared the increased revenues in the forms of bigger contracts. It became rapidly apparent that Caldera was a promotional prodigy, excelling at everything he undertook. Though Caldera was very, very good at what he did, the ownership returned and demanded Caldera capitulate to their style of management. Caldera refused, left, and GCW was born. However, Caldera had quickly figured out how successful he could be if unrestrained. So, with his own company to run as he saw fit, Caldera went overboard. Rather than simply installing his own philosophy as the basis of the new company, Caldera sought to keep his fingers on every string, manipulating even the most insignificant details according to his prodigious whim. The most tangible result of this was The Establishment. The original group consisted of John Reed, Toxic, his bodyguard Khan, and his hand-picked choice to become the first GCW World Champion, Octavian. Octavian's star had risen quickly in the CWU as a talented wrestler and powerful stage presence, and in him Caldera saw dollar signs. Octavian's most serious competition in the very first Warpath tournament, which would decide the first champion, was Hall of Fame superstar The Jhub. Caldera and The Jhub were lifelong friends turned bitter rivals, and Caldera saw The Jhub as being insubordinate and sloppy. The refined, edgy Octavian, as Caldera's favorite, came away with the World Title, just as Caldera planned. The Establishment, however, has never been a static entity. Caldera is notoriously fickle, which he attributes to the rapid changes in attitude by the fanbase. Caldera always sought to make the biggest profits, insisting that his methods and his chosen stars were the ones to bring the cash in. In this there can be little argument; The Establishment has always drawn huge ratings, though perhaps not for the reasons Caldera suspects. When Caldera saw bigger opportunity elsewhere, he turned on the Champion and favored his long time henchman Khan for the belt. Khan got his shot, and Octavian lost the title as Caldera planned, but things did not go as expected. In a Triple Threat Iron Man match at Shockwave 2001, Octavian and Khan lost out to a new rising star, and a man who would go on to be the bane of The Establishment for many years; Rich "The Renegade" Rollins. The defeat was devastating to the group, which lay dormant for some time. Eventually it rose again, this time with a different leader. A new star, Tristain Mayhem, had risen in GCW and defeated Caldera's golden boy Octavian at Christmas Chaos in 2001. Octavian again fell out of favor with Caldera, who sought a new leader for his group. He found that man in Jason Dread, whom Caldera helped capture the World Title in January of 2002. Dread went on to a dominant World Title reign as the leader of The Establishment, which at that time consisted of a brash new United States Champion, Michael Stevens, Khan, and the eternally young Toxic. Dread held the title for some time, until in an odd contest on Meltdown, Steven Caldera himself was pinned by The Jhub, costing Dread the title. Infuriated, Dread retaliated against Caldera, and the balance of power shifted again. Michael Stevens became Caldera’s right-hand man, but GCW itself would close shortly thereafter. Throughout its history, however, The Establishment has had one constant, one never-changing parallel; the GCW World Title. Caldera has always sought to control it, an instinct dating back to his beginnings as a promoter. CWU’s success after the very first Caldera-era World Title change was so exceptional that Caldera has been bent on duplicating that moment ever since. And to some point, Caldera’s insistence that his way works is valid. Though most backstage despise Caldera and everyone in his group, there can be no denying that The Establishment has garnered record pay-per-view buyrates, television ratings and live event gates. However, this is no doubt due to the overwhelming desire the fans have to see someone destroy The Establishment. Though many have managed to derail Caldera’s plans in the past, including Rollins, The Jhub, and Badger Benandanti, none have ever succeeded in destroying The Establishment outright. Still, there has never been a more polarizing, unifying force in GCW as The Establishment is. This new version, frighteningly enough, might be the most talented yet. Though Jason Dread and Octavian are each multiple-time World Title winners and members of the prestigious Hall of Fame, they have both been eclipsed by the unrivaled dominance of LeStatt Knight. Michael Stevens, a longtime Knight rival, went on to greatness after his Establishment days and holding the United States Title, much like the immensely talented Jordan White. Many believe White has the same type of career ahead of him, and though the fans and his fellow wrestlers will undoubtedly disagree with his methods, White seems poised to seize that success. To be sure, The Establishment will change the landscape of GCW. Though this current GCW roster may not be aware of it, President Steven Caldera and his group, when bent on domination, have only rarely been denied their desires. Which could ultimately mean that this Warpath tournament is nothing more than a charade, much like the first one. In that first tournament, President Caldera hand selected Octavian to come away the winner, and win he did. In this tournament, it seems clear that LeStatt Knight is the Chosen One. And if history repeats itself, Knight will emerge victorious from this tournament and go on to capture the GCW title a third time. But, as usual, success in The Establishment is fleeting; it is only a matter of time before Knight himself loses favor, and The Establishment and all of GCW changes once again.
