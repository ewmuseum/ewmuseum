The Scottish King of GCW

16 July 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
New GCW World Champion Andy Murray ushered in a new era this week on WorldWide. After three of the most dominant title runs in GCW history, shattering nearly every record in the book, there can be little doubt that LeStatt Knight is, if he wasn't already, the greatest wrestler this company has ever seen. But at Rampage last week, he was left bloody, battered and beaten at the hands of a man whose rise to this point may well have been inevitable. New GCW World Heavyweight Champion Andy Murray looked just right with the gold on his shoulder this week on WorldWide. Despite the stitches in his head and the bruises on his body, Murray looked like a weight had been lifted. The dark cloud that had been hovering above his head, and all of GCW, for that matter, was gone. The charismatic, irresistibly likable star was in top form, and there can be no denying that he's settling right into his new role as top dog in the wrestling world. There was never any doubt that Murray would look the part, should he ever win the belt. The real doubt was in whether Murray could survive an encounter with the greatest and most evil wrestler in the world. The affable, good-natured Murray was up against his worst nightmare, a wrestler known for absorbing inhuman punishment from wrestlers far more violent than he. In the end what looked like a hellish end for Murray turned out to be a glorious beginning, as Murray emerged from the war with his body broken but his heart still very much in tact. With the larger-than-life status achieved by LeStatt Knight in GCW, the company's programming certainly takes on a special air when he is the reigning champion. But Murray, perhaps like no other before him, seems poised to match that aura of greatness. Murray, like Michael Stevens before him, managed to beat LeStatt Knight at his best. But unlike Stevens, Murray seems like he's emerged from the fight better than ever. Michael Stevens was never the same. And while there's no doubt this experience has changed the Scottish King of Cool, he sent a message this week that he's ready for the challenges ahead. And he's already had his first challenge, in the form of a wrestler more similar to Murray than many realize. Jordan White, former GCW World Champion, was offered by GCW President Steven Caldera one last crack at the World Title, in what appeared to be an effort to convince the enigmatic and temperamental star to stay. White agreed to the match, but Murray proved up to the challenge. On any other day this might have been a match-of-the-year candidate. But Murray outclassed his opponent, and Jordan White's GCW career came to an unceremonious end. And this is an example of why Andy Murray has the makings of a great champion. Jordan White was a great champion. His tenacity in chasing the belt for a year and a half made him a fan favorite, despite his apparent apathy toward the business. And as one of the most supremely talented wrestlers in GCW history, White's reign might have reached even greater heights had he not run into a rejuvenated LeStatt Knight. It was bad timing for White, an ill stroke of fate that may have crushed his spirits for good. It's what LeStatt Knight does. It's what happened to Michael Stevens. Even in victory, Stevens couldn't withstand the pressure of life post-LeStatt. Jordan White never quite recovered. And when it came down to it, he took a dive to the insufferable Chris Bagwell just so he could walk away. White's career should be celebrated. Though he was impenetrable, at times aloof and always mysterious, he was undoubtedly one of the best wrestlers this company has ever seen. But Andy Murray took notice of Jordan White's cautionary tale. That's why Murray has truly mastered his craft. He beat LeStatt Knight. It's not something that happens often, and doing so often costs the victor more than he has to give. Even in defeat LeStatt Knight has claimed countless careers. It's what he demands of his opponents, and it's what makes him the best. Andy Murray's defiant smile this week on WorldWide is a signal to the world that he's not going to fall into the same trap. Andy Murray is the GCW World Heavyweight Champion, and he's here to stay. He's the Scottish King of GCW, and he might just be embarking on something pretty special.
