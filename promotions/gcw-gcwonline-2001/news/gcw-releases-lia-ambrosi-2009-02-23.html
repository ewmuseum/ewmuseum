GCW Releases Lia Ambrosi

23 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
As expected, GCW today announced the release of Lia Ambrosi from her GCW contract. That the move was anticipated makes it no less earthshaking. Ambrosi had become a GCW mainstay after arriving in the company in early 2006. Part of the much-hyped group of talent that left GCW rival Global in late 2005, Ambrosi was undoubtedly the crown jewel. The group would become highly controversial and opinions about the hires are mixed, but there is no doubt that Ambrosi and close friend Vivica J. Valentine are now ingrained into GCW's history. Ambrosi made a fast ascent, winning Dangerous Games in 2006 and defeating LeStatt Knight and Dawn MacFarlane for the GCW World Championship at NC-17 that year. Ambrosi would dominate all of 2007, a feat unmatched in GCW lore. The only interruption in her title reign came at the hands of a suddenly dominant Bryan Mayhem, who held the title for two months before Ambrosi regained it. The year of domination climaxed when Ambrosi took on her friend Valentine at NC-17, where Valentine claimed the belt. 2007 would go down as the year of both GCW's resurrection and the year of Lia Ambrosi. But wrestling at such a torrid pace reportedly took quite a toll on Ambrosi. She entered into a five-wrestler Round Robin challenge at the start of 2008 to vie for a World Title shot, but her in-ring performances noticeably declined in quality. Though Ambrosi was notorious for being highly guarded about her personal life, hints from Valentine suggest that the effort Ambrosi gave in 2007 expended her body's capabilities. 2008 saw Ambrosi in sharp decline, ending in a devastating loss to up-and-comer Face at NC-17. The uninspiring year Ambrosi experienced in 2008 led the former two-time champion and GCW officials to meet to negotiate a buyout of her extremely rich contract. Ambrosi had two years remaining on her original four-year deal, which was said to be in the high eight-figures. GCW is renowned for being highly generous during injury settlement negotiations. While GCW maintains that the door will be open for Ambrosi to return, the superstar is said to be content with the prospect of retirement. Some speculate that she may have suffered a career-ending injury near the end of 2008, but Ambrosi has refused to speak publicly and GCW does not release medical information. Be it one massive injury or a series of nagging problems, the fact remains that Ambrosi simply could no longer physically handle the demanding schedule of a worldwide-touring promotion. The release adds to the tumultuous week experienced by Valentine, who finds herself down yet another ally in her suddenly intense fight against The Establishment. Debate will immediately spark surrounding Ambrosi's potential induction into the GCW Hall of Fame. Ambrosi's fans will point to her unprecedented time at the top, while opponents may cite the relative lack of competition during that era. Either way, the debate will have time to rage, as GCW typically likes to wait some time before inducting former stars. Early indications, however, are that Ambrosi has plenty of support within the company to land her among GCW's all time greats.
