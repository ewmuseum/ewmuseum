Knight's Fourth Reign Earns Applause

18 March 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
There may not be a more polarizing figure in GCW, but LeStatt Knight is universally heralded as the best ever after his fourth title win. It's getting pretty hard to find new superlatives for LeStatt Knight. It's not even accurate to refer to him as the "new" GCW Champion this time. There's nothing new about seeing that belt around Knight's waist. In 2002, Hall of Famer Octavian walked into an ACWF Match against Badger Benandanti and The Jhub at Shockwave and won the GCW World Title for a third time. It was an amazing feat, especially during a time when the competition was so even that acquiring the belt was next to impossible. Things became even more top heavy when GCW returned in 2005. Jason Dread became the first champion of the new era, but another name rose and joined the ranks of the best. LeStatt Knight simply outworked everyone and took the belt from Dread, a reign that was heralded as the best in the history of the company. Turns out he was just getting started. That third reign won by Octavian was never expected to be matched, much less beaten. His three world titles represented the most sought after record in GCW. Of course, Andy Murray might be quick to point out that having three title reigns means you had to lose the belt twice. Still, Knight's record must be applauded. Even those who recognize Knight's cutthroat brutality are forced to acknowledge this week that he has removed any doubt about his status as the best GCW wrestler of all time. And it seems like the crowd is beginning to recognize this, as well. At NC-17, when Knight gave Clyde Fox an appreciative pat on the back for giving him a difficult challenge, the fans seemed to soften on him. Up against the biggest star in the world in Andy Murray Knight found himself booed once again, but by the end the fans knew they couldn't deny what they had seen. Everyone in Denver knew they were there to see LeStatt Knight break GCW's unbreakable record. They knew they were witnesses to history. Even Knight, the unflappable, unalterable rock that he is, seems to be changing under the pressure of his own legacy. His conduct against Clyde Fox was strange, yet oddly made sense; Knight, who has done it all in the GCW ring, just wants to be challenged at this point. Clyde Fox challenged him, made him work hard for an NC-17 win. For that, Fox earned Knight's respect. Andy Murray already had Knight's respect. There may not be another wrestler Knight respects more. Murray took the GCW World Title from Knight nine months ago, and while Knight had been plotting his revenge ever since, he always appreciated Murray for his ability to match him. That culminated in an unexpected moment at WorldWide, when Andy Murray and LeStatt Knight shook hands. Make no mistake about it, those two men don't like each other and never will. Knight doesn't have friends, and Murray won't align himself with such a cruel individual. But they acknowledged each other as their greatest adversary, a sort of moment Knight may not have been capable of a year ago. Knight's relentless desire to thoroughly defeat everyone he faces is what makes him great. But from his perch atop GCW history, LeStatt Knight has learned to respect those who can step into the ring and challenge him. Knight acknowledged Murray as his equal; but where Murray's just getting started writing his legacy, Knight has just sealed his. What comes next for LeStatt Knight as he embarks on this unprecedented fourth title reign is anyone's guess. But even Knight seems to know that the eye of history is upon him, and his next moves will be remembered forever. Every brutal act, every heartless beating must now be seen as the actions of a man driven to greatness. Having accomplished what he has, LeStatt Knight's guilt won't be absolved, but his methods may be understood. And though they may never like him, the fans and his opponents will not be able to help but respect him from this point on.
