The WorldWide Web #1

17 March 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Commissioner Bag Brings Major Changes to GCW What a difference a week can make. Nobody was sure what to expect from WorldWide 127. President Caldera had left the tour before, but never in his ten years with the company had he openly handed off controlling interest. GCW was his baby, his lifeblood, the thing of which he was proudest in this world. So when he followed through on his guarantee and handed control of GCW to the Commissioner at NC-17, it made for an uncomfortable feeling. While Caldera isn't loved by everyone, his business sense is universally trusted, having built GCW into a global powerhouse from an investment of a few hundred thousand dollars in 2001. Adding to the uncertainty was the identity of the new Commissioner. Christian Zenith was intelligent and had experience. Tim Shipley is the hottest name on the independent circuit and was already under GCW contract. Either one would have been a solid choice. But instead Caldera went with supposed Establishment lackey Garbage Bag Johnny to ensure GCW's future. Despite being named GCW's most missed wrestler of 2009, Johnny's leadership credentials are questionable at best.WorldWide Stud LeStatt Knight What can you say? He ended Andy Murray's amazing title reign and started a fourth of his own. Octavian's unbeatable record just fell, and I don't think there's any chance anyone ever tops this. WorldWide Dud AXEL Action AXEL lost a heartbreaker to Dan Black at NC-17. It was another setback for the promising young star, but he could have done a lot to prove his fortitude by showing up at WorldWide. Instead he stayed home to lick his wounds. Stock Watch LeStatt Knight: He just won his fourth World Championship and further cemented his legendary status. Vivica J. Valentine: Ok, so she didn't win the World Title. But she'll add prestige to the US belt. Andy Murray: His reign ends so his stock falls, but he's still among the best in wrestling. PAZUZU: He shocked many by defeating the veteran Tempest in her first title defense. Dan Black: He retained at NC-17 only to lose to Valentine. Rumor has it he's out of GCW. And yet at WorldWide, Garbage Bag Johnny blew the lid off the Pepsi Center in Denver. I watched as the wrestlers funneled into the arena at the start of the day. They were nervous, uncertain, possibly afraid for their jobs. GCW is soaring, but for how long could it stay afloat with the barely lucid Johnny at the helm? After the show, though, nobody was worried. People were shaking hands. The staff was openly celebrating. WorldWide 127 was a massive success, and everyone knew it right away. Garbage Bag Johnny started things off with a blast. Floating down to the ring on a Jetpack (though there may have been some wires involved) and shooting t-shirts to the crowd, he made it clear that WorldWide is his show and he's going to do things his way. And as it turns out, his way is pretty exciting. Every Championship in GCW was thrown into flux on that night. We knew after GCW Radio that the Tag Team Titles had been suspended, which drew the ire of Bryan Mayhem. But Mayhem was satisfied; he gets credit for a Tag Title reign, and now he's the sole holder of the reinstated GCW Hardcore Title. The anarchic Hardcore Championship seems to fit what Johnny is attempting to do with WorldWide, and with Mayhem at the helm things are sure to be violent. We've got new World, United States and Television Champions, too. I don't think anyone saw any of this coming, least of all Dan Black. While there will be plenty of time to break down the title matches, what becomes immediately clear is how determined Garbage Bag Johnny was to start his tenure off with a bang. The meddling that was common during the Caldera Era was nowhere to be seen on this night. Johnny didn't pull any strings to get the outcomes he wanted. It wasn't about the result, it was about the thrill of the fight. And his first show had no shortage of thrills. It's being talked about across the wrestling world this week. Somehow, Johnny managed to assuage the fears of those worried about wholesale changes, while simultaneously surprising long time fans with some unexpected twists. The result is a program that kept all the things that are so great about Global Championship Wrestling, with a unique flavor adding some unanticipated freshness. Of course, there was one man who was less than thrilled with the event. Jay Terror, who would appear to be the heir apparent to Steven Caldera's place in the wrestling spotlight, got shut out entirely. Not only did his expected World Title shot not come to fruition, but he watched as LeStatt Knight became the man to dethrone Andy Murray. Knight's place as GCW's Icon only got stronger, while Terror found himself forced to watch from the sidelines. Don't underestimate Jay Terror. He'll have a plan. But without Caldera here to guide him, he'll have to figure it out for himself. Because it seems that Garbage Bag Johnny's most brilliant move didn't even come at WorldWide. It came last week at NC-17, when he told Caldera everything he wanted to hear, and then set to work on creating his own identity as GCW's head of operations. From the first night of the GBJ Era, it seems clear that he's not out to undo everything President Caldera accomplished. Rather, Johnny revealed himself to be quietly ambitious, hungry to apply his own unusual personality to a company that, under Caldera, was regimented and militaristic. The new GCW is the same as the old GCW. It's just got Garbage Bag Johnny pulling the strings, and I'm not sure he even knows what he's going to do next. But if he lacks a plan, he certainly seems to be making up for it in vision. WorldWide 127 was a spectacle, propped up by the whimsy of its new Commissioner. There's no telling where GCW is headed next, but for now, there's no reason not to be excited.
