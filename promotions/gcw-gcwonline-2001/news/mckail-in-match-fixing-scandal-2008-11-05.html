McKail in match-fixing scandal?

5 November 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Earlier this week, reports emanating from a local news network in New York claim that GCW superstar, Jacob McKail, has to be at the center of an alleged match-fixing scandal during his brief stint working in a local independent wrestling organisation. The wrestling organisation in question, the AFWA, are a small upstart company who booked McKail for a number of special appearances during what they call the ?Wrestlevania Extravaganza? last month in order to raise their profile and improve their standing amongst rival organisations. For the three nights originally booked, it is alleged his first two matches were ?fixed? and a number of unknown individuals illegally profited from them. Although booked for the third night, he was pulled from the card by the bookers and replaced by someone else. When approached, AFWA representatives had the following to say. "The rumours of match fixing within the AFWA are entirely groundless," the owner, Jim Crocket stated. "The AFWA never have and never will be involved in illegal activity of any kind, match fixing or otherwise." When questioned in regards to the third night McKail had been booked, Mr. Crocket added, "Jacob McKail felt a twitch in his elbow and withdrew from his match. Although from a business point of you this is a little disappointing for the AFWA, with GCW’s Dangerous Games looming around the corner it was entirely understandable." Both Jacob McKail and Steven Caldera have declined to comment.
