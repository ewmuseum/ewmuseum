GCW — Column

Unique Critique #2

9 March 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
GCW's trademark column is a comprehensive review of the entire active roster. Written by a different anonymous contributor each week, Unique Critique is the most honest assessment of each member's performance you can find. The commentary ranges from complimentary to mercilessly cruel, so if you can't handle it, don't read on! But if you want to know how each roster member stacks up in the eyes of one writer, Unique Critique offers you the chance. -Mediocre -Average -Good -Great -World Class I want to say, first of all, that I have looked at what was put out for this week (WorldWide 62), and used that as a measuring stick for the Unique Critique. That means that I am offering commentary on everybody's RP's, but that is not the only thing I am considering- I am also considering segments, and whether or not the handler wrote matches for the card. I would also like to note, in light of the recent conversations in GCW, when I judge RP's, I am looking for neither relevance or character development stuff in particular. What I try to do is look what's there, and rate it in terms of whether or not I feel the writer really captured who the character is. For wrestling-based or 'relevance' based RP's, I look for whether or not the writer has found an original way to deal with the subject matter instead of delving into cliches. For 'creative' or 'character development' pieces, I simply look for creativity, and the skill of the writer in taking what is pretty much a blank page and turning it into an interesting story. Lia Ambrosi- RP: It's good to see an RP from the champ again, since it's been a good while since there's been one. I feel like this RP is good- it's a good read, and leaves a lot of doors open at the end of it, though I think from the title of the piece, it's evident that it's supposed to, since it is the beginning of a story arc. It's a fun read, and the one thing that I could say about it is that there are a few errors here which I don't feel like are typical (or should be typical) in the writing of a World Champion. I'm hoping that the next stuff in this series will pick up a little bit- this was a bit dull in places. Card: Card presence is good- three segments written, and a match too. Right in the middle of things, and pushing this Bryan Mayhem stuff in a way that I think is not only unique, but interesting too. I think this was a good week for the champ, though eventually, obviously, we need to start seeing some more interaction between champ and contender. And what about the rest of KB: DI? Except for Virgil, it's almost like they've fallen off the face of the earth, at least as far as interactions with one of their leaders goes. Virgil Ambrosi- I have to say that it's disappointing to see a writer that I think has a lot of potential from reading some of his past work just sort of fall off like this. It's been a long time since I've seen an RP from Virgil, and token appearances in segments really aren't doing it for me. Jayce Bradley- RP: This RP is honestly disappointing for me. It's so short, and seems really like this should be an introduction scene for something that's a lot more in-depth than it is. John is no slouch when it comes to writing- what he has here has very few errors, and is an easy and clear read that allow you to understand the character. It's just a shame that there's so little of it. Card: I feel like the card presence is also really abrupt. I love what is going on with Shaman here, but I want to see more of it- I want to see Bradley start to show some pluck and keep coming at Shaman no matter how many times he's knocked down, instead of giving it one go and then disappearing for the rest of the card. I feel like more meat to hang on to would make me rank Bradley a lot higher than I did this week. Hope Clarkson- Again, I've already said that I'd have liked to see more Fiery Passion this week, and I'm going to reiterate here. I see no segments, and no RP from Hope either really de-emphasizes the importance of the other half of the Tag champs. 'The Deathscythe Outlaw' Kyo Kushinaga- RP: First of all, and this is just my personal feeling, but this is very long. It is exceedingly hard for me to wrap my mind around this much stuff in one sitting. However, that being said, I have to say that the piece as a whole is wonderfully vivid and creative, which I have always thought is one of Kayin's strengths as a writer. It was a very enjoyable piece because it literally took me into that other world with Kyo and allowed me to get to know something more about him- what is normally a very enigmatic character I got to know on what I feel like is a more intimate level. The inclusion of wrestling at the beginning seemed almost out of place, though. Card: I think that Kyo's presence on the card was good. I'm a bit disappointed to not have the results of a match which both competitors obviously RPed very hard for, but the seg presence for Kushinaga was great stuff- I especially like the stuff he's getting going with Jimmy Thunder, and I see the emergence of a stronger on-air presence for the Deathscythe Outlaw. Kayin, as always, is no disappointment- and a hard worker to boot. Draven Lox- I don't know where Eric has gone, but wherever it is, I wish he'd come back. Lox is always an interesting character- and needs some presence to have any impact whatsoever. Blaise 'Hazard' McClancy- With RJ and Kimi still out for the moment, I have to say... I really miss seeing these characters around. I hope to see some more Hazard, and soon. Bryan Mayhem- RP: The output here puts a very interesting spin on the Bathory legend, and I definitely have to give Bryan credit for spinning that into something useful for his character. The story is a bit sparse on details for me, in the sense that it covers a lot of ground, and covers it so quickly that I don't really get a chance to sink my teeth into it as a reader. I'm not sure what the purpose of it was- especially by the ending, where I think that something that had admirable value as a character sketch was turned into some weird public service message type thing. Card: There's a lot of heat on Mayhem right now given the way he was portrayed, but in some of the segments that he appears in, he really sort of appears in a sideshow, side character sort of way that's a little disappointing- especially in his segments with his tag partner Shaman. Everything seems to be about other people this week, and I'd really like to see Mayhem sort of get the spotlight on himself if he's supposed to be the number one contender for GCW's biggest title. The amount of involvement is great, but I'd really like to see a different type. Dawn McFarlane- RP: I'm of two minds about this piece. In a sense, it seems to be a little bit lacking on the action side, and some of the actions I find to be a little bit stretching on my belief (like having her get the crap kicked out of her as a test at a new gym- maybe it happens, maybe it doesn't. I can't say. But it didn't work too well for me). Although, I have to say that in terms of RP's, this one is good for you- James is a fantastic writer, and everyone knows that, but it seems like sometimes, he has a tendency to get himself bogged down, and you don't so much feel it in his writing. This is not a return to form of the Dawn McFarlane of the past, but it's getting there. I see a lot more freedom in the writing, the descriptions are clear and easy to imagine, and in general, it just feels like a piece that the writer should be satisfied with. Card: Card presence is alright. The one segment that I see Dawn in is very humorous, and overall, a good read. Yet, I feel like for a former World Champion, the on-card presence really doesn't have a whole lot of punch to it. Danielle Northsky- There hasn't been a whole lot of Dani lately, which, while completely understandable due to Kimi's personal situation, sucks in terms of seeing the US Title get some heat on-air. Scott Nielson- I have... essentially nothing. I know from reading some other people's RP's that he was involved last week on the card, but this week... where'd he go? An on-air presence like this isn't going to help turn any heads- this guy needs something going for him. Katie O'Clare- RP: I'm aware that this is not really intended to be a full-on RP, since it was noted that HSB didn't have time to write one this week. I do think, however, that though it's short, it does display Alan's tremendous talent as a writer. He's extremely evocative, and really knows how to capture a mood. Card: On-card involvement is passable, I think. I would have liked to see more, but I loved what I did see. The interaction between her and Storm/Stone provides this really great, gritty nastiness on their end, and makes Katie look all the more like a face. I enjoyed this sequence a lot. Rufio- My problems here: Rufio's great. I love his on-camera personality, but there just wasn't much of it this week. Couple that with no RP, and I guess I'll just have to chalk this up to an off week for what is otherwise a very rewarding read week in and week out. Chris Storm- RP: The RP is middling for me. I enjoy the content, and find it humorous, but it also uses some things that I think are pretty convenient, but not necessarily realistic, such as having the Dawn fans around for Chris to argue with in the beginning. Whatever else the RP is, the character voice shines through very clearly, and you get a definite sense of who he is and what he's about, however unsavory that might be. Card: The phrase "A for effort" could be applied here, especially since it's particularly impressive effort. Two matches, and a handful of segments that I feel like really do a lot to further the character's impact. A very impressive outing overall, and I feel like between the RP and the quality of the segments, that is enough to earn him a high ranking for the week. Markus Stone- RP: I'm a bit torn on what I actually think of the RP. The style is well-done, the read fairly enjoyable, and there really aren't any mistakes- there are even a number of sections where I think the character voice shines through clearly, and that's something that I'm always looking for when I read RP's. One thing that I'm not certain of as a reader is how the first part matches up with the second part, because the RP seems to sort of be doing two different things at once: trying to sort of show Markus as a 'pathetic' type person who takes out his own frustrations on people who don't deserve it, but by the ending, trying to show Markus as someone strong who will make an impact... for me, the two pictures don't quite add up. Card: Involvement here is impressive. I see Stone pretty much all over the place, and if you're wanting to come in and make an impact, this is how to do it. For this card, the amount of effort expended gets him a solid score. Shaman- RP: Overall, there's one thing I am going to harp on above all else with this RP, and that is structure. These are big, thick, meaty paragraphs, and unfortunately, that makes it very easy to lose the point. I don't feel like I need to explain the reasons for structuring a paragraph, but this seems entirely arbitrary, without any thought as to what might make for easier reading. Too, I'm not entirely turned on by the content, which I don't think is all that interesting- it's one thing to do match relevance, but I like to see it done in an interesting way, and I don't really feel this is any sort of trash talk that all of us haven't already read a hundred times in other promotions. Card: I feel like card presence is where Andy really excels with Shaman. He has a lot of things going on at once, and that makes for a very deep on-air character. I like what I see starting with Bradley, and I like that I see a lot of effort. I've already mentioned in my review of Mayhem for this week that I think Shaman was over-emphasized in their team segments; in terms of a team, that's maybe not so good, but in terms of self-promotion, it works. A lot of effort put forth here, which again, is where I feel that Andy really shines. There aren't a lot of people who put this much into the card each week. Though I feel that his RP weakens his overall score, that is not at all to de-emphasize what he's done on the card. Jimmy Thunder- RP: The RP is sort of all over the place for me. I can't decide what I think about it, mostly because it's such a rollercoaster- but for some reason, it rings a little bit hollow. I think it may possibly be because the writer tried to go through too many emotions a little too fast without putting in the necessary details to get the reader to experience them with the character. It's a good RP, but a little flat to me- in places, the details are great, but in others, I sort of lose a sense of what's going on and why I should be interested. Card: Involvement here is good, though not as entertaining as I had hoped it would have been. The two segments with Kyo build a lot of good heat, but I feel like they don't build enough, as each one pretty much says the same thing. They are an enjoyable read, however, and I hope to see an interesting payoff. Peter Vetra- I would otherwise rank Vetra a lot higher, because I know that Adam puts a lot of effort into all the cards, and otherwise generally works his ass off on stuff. However, this week, the effort is just not there. No RP, and only one segment from a champion with a brand new title? Let's get some heat on that thing, instead of expecting it to promote itself. Vivica J. Valentine- Again, I'm not impressed with the lack of RP here. Mostly because I feel like as a reader, Vivica has just gone through a very major character change, and this would have been an awesome opportunity to showcase that in an RP. But... alas, I can't rank on what's not there. With only one segment and the Rufio stuff this week, this is about where I feel Vivica needs to be- after a major alliance change, she needs more momentum than she had this week. Jordan White- RP: I have to say a couple of things about this. The first, being the negative, is that this RP is so long that I find it a little bit tedious to read. As enjoyable as I might find it otherwise, it took me a long time to get through, because I found myself getting distracted by how long it was, and how I basically felt like I was just trying to get through it. The bonus is, you're a very good writer. Very enjoyable, very talented, very spot-on with character portrayal, and very daring with writing style. I like seeing that kind of confidence in someone's writing. It's just a little unwieldy. Card: I feel like the card presence (or distinct lack thereof) by White is a big-time disappointment. With writing this good, I feel like the character could go straight to the top if he wanted to- but not without securing some more on-card heat. It doesn't even look like there's been any work or thought put into the on-card side of things, and that makes what could be a great overall character into a character with a great writer who is a little one-dimensional in e-fed terms. Keiko Yoshimoto- RP: Normally, I'm very into Fiery Passion, but this week, I sort of feel like a little of the oomph was missing. Jeremy has the knack for telling a wonderfully creative story, but I feel like maybe this week, he let the last critique get to him a little bit. It's sort of like I can't read the confidence that is normally in the way that he writes in this piece, and it's a little bit passionless for me as a result. Overall, though, I do enjoy the subject matter, and really see no problem with the sexuality either. Card: In terms of card presence, I don't actually see anything that was attributed to Jeremy this week. I know he may have been busy, but I do find that somewhat disappointing, considering both the fact that there was such an interesting storyline going on between Keiko and Kyo, and the fact that Fiery Passion has really started to gain some heat lately as tag champions. I would have hoped/expected to see more.
