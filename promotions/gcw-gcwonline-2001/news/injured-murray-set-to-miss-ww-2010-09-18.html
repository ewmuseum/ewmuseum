Injured Murray set to miss WW

18 September 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Newton's Paradox grinds Scot to a halt. The Scottish King of Cool is set to miss this week’s WorldWide broadcast thanks to injuries sustained at High Stakes. Murray fought Tim Shipley for over twenty minutes and eventually walked out victorious, but The King’s biggest victory in months has not come without a cost. Towards the middle of the match, Murray fell victim to Newton’s Paradox (Shipley’s signature reverse figure-four), but this was no ordinary hold. “The guy’s legs were like a vice,” Murray explained, “but I wasn’t going to tap. Not to him. I was hobbling around afterwards but I thought I’d be fine by the next morning.” When he went backstage after the match, however, Murray was having problems. “I could barely move my legs when I stood-up, and by the time I got out of the shower I couldn’t walk.” This is not the first time Murray has suffered an injury at Shipley’s hands – Shipley and Cheap Labor broke one of Murray’s ribs shortly after Shipley’s GCW arrival – but Murray insists he is not bitter about this latest setback. “I can’t stand Tim, but it’s not his fault. Injuries happen when you’ve got two guys going all-out in a pay-per-view contest. He was desperate to submit me, and I was desperate to survive. C’est la vie.” Murray’s nonchalant reaction may come as a surprise given his recent fiery demeanour, but things could’ve been much worse. A quick trip to the hospital revealed nothing more serious than strained knee tendons, and The King should be back in action next week. “Realistically I could coming along this week, but I’d rather not. I’ve turned-up to shows injured before and it’s not a lot of fun, especially if someone tries to get in your face. I’m going to stay at home and heal-up this week. There’s no point in risking serious injury with Dangerous Games looming.” Victory over Shipley at High Stakes has again made Murray an odds-on favourite to win Dangerous Games. With his credibility now re-established, it is understandable that Murray would not want to risk a competitor taking advantage of his injured state. Critics may sneer at his absence, but on a week when he has nothing on his wrestling schedule perhaps staying at home isn’t such a bad idea after all.
