Vacant GCW Title Not Unprecedented

19 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The GCW World Title has suddenly been thrown into limbo with the unexpected retirement of LeStatt Knight, but this isn't unprecedented. LeStatt Knight has done inhuman things to keep hold of the GCW World Heavyweight Championship. He's wrenched his own shoulder out of its socket. He's stapled his own wounds shut. He's engaged in so many legendary moments that they're getting their own DVD. So when LeStatt Knight vacated the GCW Title on WorldWide this week, you know something pretty significant was happening. Something extremely personal is happening between Knight and his Shockwave opponent, Vivica J. Valentine. Something big enough, significant enough to cause Knight to relinquish the title voluntarily. There will be plenty of time to analyze his motives and their implications for his historic final match. But of more immediate concern is the state of limbo into which the GCW World Championship has been thrust. Knight's decision has caused some significant backlash among GCW fans and observers, fearing that, with the title pushed to the background, it may damage the image of the belt. But this is not the first time the GCW Title has been vacated, and it has always retained its tremendous value. In fact, the history of this event dates back to the CWU, when the title was managed by Commissioner Caldera and held by Vince Reed. In a match between Reed and not-yet-legendary Rich 'The Renegade' Rollins, Caldera vacated the belt and held it up for over a month. The belt was finally given a new owner at the conclusion of a six-man triple-tiered cage match, which was ultimately won by Caldera himself. The belt was briefly vacated in 2002, when a rebooted GCW debuted with its popular Dangerous Games event. Octavian was still technically the Champion, but President Caldera stripped him of the belt and put it up in the battle royal. Twizzy came out of the field of twenty with the belt later that night. Most recently, the GCW Title was vacated after Michael Stevens was forced to retire due to injury. As a result, the 2006 Rampage Tournament was held with the belt on the line, with Dawn MacFarlane emerging from a talented field as the victor. Stevens' retirement is often attributed to Knight, who inflicted severe punishment over the course of their four month rivalry in 2005. Stevens finally bested Knight at NC-17, but took such a beating that his body didn't survive to defend the belt. It is somehow fitting, then, that the belt should once again become vacant thanks to LeStatt Knight, the man who seems to loom over the title even when he doesn't hold it. As GCW awaits an announcement from Commissioner Garbage Bag Johnny regarding the fate of the title, one thing is certain: with Rampage coming up and the biggest field of title hopefuls in GCW history, a lot of hands will be out for a chance to be the next to wear the biggest prize in professional wrestling.
