Murray Set For Historic Defense

29 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Dangerous Games 2009 marks the first time in two years that Jason O'Neil has stepped inside a GCW ring as a PPV competitor. Dangerous Games 2009 marks the first time in two years that Jason O'Neil has stepped inside a GCW ring as a PPV competitor, and he will do so against a GCW Champion who's going through a patchy run of form, both on-air and off. It was at 2007's Dangerous Games that the 2008 hall-of-fame inductee last competed as a singles wrestler. The Scarecrow swerved the nation that night, as he took a duck to long-term rival and incumbent GCW President, Steven Caldera, before clocking the boss post-match and disappearing through the crowd. This time round, however, O'Neil's cards are very much on the table. At WorldWide 119, in a similar move, O'Neil laid Murray out after their victory over GCW Tag Team champions FishBough, having previous led the charismatic Scot into believing the two of them could work together. The move was something Murray should have seen coming, given his track record, but now the Sociopath is in a fantastic position to benefit from Murray's lack of foresight. At thirty-six, O'Neil is reaching the twilight of his career, and having never held a singles title during a colourful GCW career, Dangerous Games is his opportunity to final win the belt that's eluded him for the duration of his career. His body may not move like it used to, and he might have dropped a few pounds since GCW has last seen him, but O'Neil's mind is as sharp as ever, and he'll need to be at his most devious if he wants to build on the psychological advantage WW119's events have given him over Murray. While O'Neil may be a man with everything to gain and nothing to lose, Murray is quite the opposite. His formative days as GCW Champion were as dominant as we've come to expect from the Scottish King of Cool, having fended off stern challenges from Clyde Fox and Jordan White, but his victory over Chris Bagwell at Warpath was anything but convincing. Andy Murray did not beat his long-term foe at Warpath; he merely outlasted him, after it took four finisher moves to knock Bagwell to the floor. Since then, Murray has endured a torrid time on-air, and O'Neil's betrayal will no doubt still be fresh in the King's mind. As if worrying about O'Neil wasn't enough, Murray also has good reason to worry about his relationship with long-term ally Vivica J. Valentine to worry about going into DG. Sources close to the GCW Champion speculate that Murray has been left feeling “incredibly uncomfortable and distracted” by comments made to him by Valentine following a GCW press conference yesterday afternoon. While the nature of said comments remains undisclosed, any potential dissolution in the Murrentine legislation can be treated as a major concern for the Scottish King, who is said to value his friendship with the future HOF'er almost as highly as he does the GCW Title. Uncertainties aside, however, Murray remains one of the most dominant forces in professional wrestling today, underlined by an impressive victory over PRIME hall-of-famer Tchu in the first round of PTC's GTT7 tournament last week. While he holds a significant psychological disadvantage over Jason O'Neil, he is in prime physical condition, and is a better wrestler when he's at his worst than most are at their best. Dangerous Games 2009's World Title clash is straight down the line and almost impossible to call.
