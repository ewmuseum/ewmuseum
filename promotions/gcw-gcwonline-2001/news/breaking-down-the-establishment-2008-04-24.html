Breaking Down The Establishment

24 April 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
There can be no denying the legendary status of President Steven Caldera's group The Establishment. There have been many incarnations of the team, featuring extremely different sorts of personalities. But every group has had two things in common: they always make headlines, and they're always relentless in their pursuit of the GCW World Heavyweight Championship. So join me, Kevin Jacobs, as I walk you through the dynamics at work behind the scenes in GCW that have led to The Establishment's existence. It's pretty clear what the motives are on both sides of the battle lines, but there may be more to the situation than you know. The Establishment first came to be in Global Championship Wrestling almost immediately after its inception. Octavian was tapped to lead the group, and he went on to capture the first GCW World Title reign. Along with Octavian were John Reed and Toxic, forming a formidable group for the wrestlers of the fledgling company to contend with. Whenever it has been present in GCW, The Establishment has been one of the most powerful forces in the company. But for some reason, a large percentage of the fanbase seems to take this for granted. On occasion, however, a curious fan will write me wondering why the roster doesn't simply strike, or stage some sort of coup de tat, or simply not sign with GCW at all. After all, President Caldera has always sought to anoint champions that fit with his own model for the company, and that doesn't seem like the sort of place most wrestlers would like to work. And yet all the big stars want to work for Global Championship Wrestling. With President Caldera's power seemingly unmatched in GCW, why is the company still so attractive to the best stars in the world? GCW is unique among wrestling promotions because it is owned completely by Caldera. Caldera runs GCW and its parent company, Caldera Enterprises, Incorporated, without any of the usual delegation of power. The company is not publicly traded, meaning Caldera is the most powerful man in the corporation. There are many highly intelligent and capable men and women that assist in the operation of both GCW and CEi, but at the end of the day, there isn't anyone that can overrule Caldera. Except Vivica J. Valentine. No, Valentine's got no actual interest in the operation of GCW, but as GCW World Champion, Valentine possesses power equal to that of Caldera. Though Caldera is technically the man in charge of scheduling all the matches in the company, the World Champion is afforded a large number of perks. The rules governing the GCW Champion are somewhat loose: the Champion must defend the title once every thirty days, and must honor all title shots earned by competitors within ninety days. But beyond that, the World Champion has a tremendous amount of power to control his or her fate. But this begs the question: if Caldera made the rules in the first place, why give the World Champion that kind of power? This is where one must give credit to the foresight of the boss. Caldera anticipated the points raised above; if he were to pull all of GCW's strings without obstruction, he would have a hard time finding employees to fill his roster. But, unwilling to share power when it came to the actual operation of the company at large, Caldera built in an incentive for the wrestlers on the roster: Win the World Title and you're the most powerful wrestler in the business. And so what Vivica J. Valentine said this week on WorldWide is true. Jay Terror may be the Number One Contender, but all that means to Valentine is that she must grant him his shot within three months. That's three months she can string him along, and there is nothing he nor Caldera himself can do about it. In this scenario, Valentine is calling the shots. This is why The Establishment came into existence to begin with. Caldera knew he needed to find allies on the roster that fit what he wanted from a champion. In return for their loyalty and willingness to do his will, Establishment members have been routinely given the inside track to obtaining the World Title belt. That's why Jay Terror did what he did; he was seduced by the knowledge that, with Caldera on his side, the World Title would be just a step away. If Terror manages to win the World Title, Caldera will have a monopoly on power in GCW once again. With the World Title in his grasp, Caldera and The Establishment can run the company their way, and the wrestlers of GCW will have no grounds on which to protest. If Terror wins the title, he will have to do it fairly, meaning he will have earned the power he possesses. But he's still got to win it. Yes, having Caldera on your side can be helpful, but Jordan White got surprised. Now Terror's cards are on the table, and the World Champion won't be caught off guard. When Valentine finally grants him his shot, though, the stakes will be higher than GCW has seen in quite a while. Valentine is staking her claim as one of the top champions in GCW history, but the company will be pulling for her for more reasons than that. Not only will she have the chance to cement her legacy, she will have the chance to deny Caldera and Terror what they want the most: control. Vivica J. Valentine's holding all the cards, for now. But her ninety days are down to eighty-one and counting.
