The Ivy League #2

3 February 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Caldera's Coming Departure Feels Final As you're all no doubt aware, the hot topic in GCW this week is the latest impending departure of GCW President Steven Caldera from the tour. They say he wants to step aside for Jay Terror, he wants to hand control to someone else, he wants to focus on other projects within CEi. I can't blame a few of you for rolling your eyes. Let's face it, we've heard this same song and dance before. Hell, we've been seeing it since 2001. Though most of you are probably too late to the dance to remember your history, President Caldera once named John Reed his heir apparent in November 2001. John Reed, kid brother of former CWU legend Vincent Reed, anointed as the next head honcho of Global Championship Wrestling. I think that plan lasted six hours. It didn't take long for Caldera to decide to yank that title away from Reed and move on without him. Since then Caldera has frankly made no secret of the fact that he's been looking for someone to hand the ball to. Now, don't mistake this for a lack of love or passion for GCW. Caldera's got more of both than the rest of GCW combined. This company is his baby, his lifeblood, and he's not trying to pawn it off. Rather, I think this has always been about some weird paternal instincts the boss seems to have. If you think about where Caldera came from, things become a bit more clear. When he started, he was just another raw, no-name wrestler on the independent circuit. But his potential was identified by ACWF Owner and sometime GCW competitor Mr. Danger, and Caldera was given a mentor. Caldera soaked up knowledge so quickly that within three months he had won a World Title and secured his status as the hottest indy wrestler of 2000. Then came the knee injury. Though Caldera would go on to win three World Titles in the AWA from 2001 to 2002, these were nothing more than side projects. He could never wrestle for more than a few weeks at a time before needing another invasive corrective procedure. His career ended just when it was getting started. And yet he managed to become one of the biggest forces this industry has ever seen. And he did that thanks to Mr. Danger. For all his megalomaniacal tendencies, Caldera has always remembered where he came from. He remembers that he was once a nobody, and needed a mentor to guide him to the top. But moreso, he remembers how he mercilessly ripped the ball away from his mentor and took it further than Danger could have ever dreamed. People resent them for it, but the most successful men in this business are the ones who know that greatness is honed by unwavering brutality. Caldera learned how the business worked from Mr. Danger. Then he grew and learned how to make the business work for him. I think that Caldera's knee injuries traumatized him. It's the one thing that didn't work out in an otherwise charmed career. Caldera has never had trouble wooing talent, building a roster or making money. But he was once a world-class wrestler on his way to the top, and he never got to find out what his true potential was. Ever since, Caldera has been searching for someone just like him, someone with both the physical skill and the mental aptitude to transcend the game. Caldera will die before he loses ownership of GCW, but he longs to give someone the opportunity he was once given. But if he's going to hand it off, he's going to make someone rip it from his hands. That's what he anticipates Jay Terror doing at NC-17. John Reed never had the cutthroat bloodlust necessary to operate next to Caldera. Octavian had it, but lacked the tenacity. Jason Dread was never quite evil enough. LeStatt Knight is too big in his own right to be in the conversation. Jordan White lacked the will. Chris Bagwell lacked the focus. None of Caldera's Establishment leaders fit the bill. Until now. Jay Terror embodies everything Caldera has been looking for; everything Caldera was and wanted to be. The people may hate him for it, and perhaps rightfully so. Terror, once a freewheeling rebel-without-a-cause whose biggest delight was blowing smoke in the face of the owner, cheerfully sold out and began living the high life. But look at the door it has opened for him. He's received on the job training from Steven Caldera. He's been tapped to become the next icon of professional wrestling and will have the GCW hype machine driving him. And if he beats Caldera's nemesis Jason O'Neil at NC-17, he'll have proven beyond any doubt that he has the tenacity, the lust, and the balls to be the new Steven Caldera. That's why this latest departure feels final to me. This doesn't feel like any of the other times Caldera has left the tour. Before, Caldera seemed to leave and cross his fingers. This time he seems sure. Of course, this could all blow up in his face. If Terror loses, the luster will definitely be lost from this plan and things could get chaotic. Or if Terror fails to meet these astronomical expectations, Caldera could return again. But even if Terror fails to win the World Title again, or fails to overtake LeStatt Knight, Andy Murray and Vivica J. Valentine as the icons of GCW, he's still quite clearly growing into a bonafide superduperstar. So don't miss WorldWide this week. And definitely don't miss NC-17. These might actually be the last glimpses we get of Steven Caldera on GCW television. And I hope you all realize just how significant a statement that is.
