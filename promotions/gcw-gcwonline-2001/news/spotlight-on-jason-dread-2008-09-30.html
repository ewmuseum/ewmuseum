Spotlight On: Jason Dread

30 September 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
With yesterday's stunning announcement that Jason Dread is returning to GCW for WorldWide 100, the company has been abuzz at the prospect of the Hall of Fame wrestler receiving another shot at the GCW World Championship. Most of GCW's current fanbase, however, might not be familiar with the career of this enigmatic superstar. Like many GCW wrestlers over the years, Dread's connection to GCW starts with Steven Caldera. Dread and Caldera first encountered one another during Caldera's time in the recently closed American Wrestling Alliance. Dread signed with GCW midway through 2001, and he found immediate success. Dread defeated Dan Rivero for the GCW Television Title at FallOut in 2001, just a few short weeks after his debut. Dread would lose the title back to Rivero, but by that point he had already reached stardom. Dread made his first big mark at Devil's Night that year, joining Mr. Danger in aiding Octavian to retain the GCW World Title. This would be the first thread in a longstanding rivalry with challenger Tristain Mayhem, as well as Dread's first step towards obtaining the title himself. Dread fought Badger Benandanti to a draw in a number one contenders match the following month at NC-17, leading to a massive Fatal Fourway at Christmas Chaos for the belt, Dread's first title shot. Mayhem would win the title on that night, but it would clear the way for Mayhem and Dread to finally lock up, now with the belt on the line. The big showdown occurred at Rampage in 2002, a moment that will remain as one of GCW's most important events. Dread defeated Mayhem with the help of President Caldera and Khan, becoming both GCW World Champion and the new leader of The Establishment all at once. Dread would go on to lead one of The Establishment's most successful and talented incarnations, including Hardcore Champion Khan, United States Champion Michael Stevens and Toxic. Stevens and Toxic would both go on to hold the World Title. GCW's early years were defined by incredible competition at the top, leading to frequent title changes. Only Octavian, the Establishment's previous leader, had managed an extended run with the belt. Dread would follow in Octavian's footsteps, holding the title for over three months while dominating the company. Dread's reign came to a shocking end, however, in a triple threat match on Meltdown with The Jhub and President Caldera. Thanks to a special stipulation, The Jhub pinned Caldera to take the title and end Dread's reign. This led to a rift between Caldera and Dread, and Dread's career would never fully recover before GCW closed later that year. Dread vanished from the wrestling scene for three years while GCW was dormant, and he has been guarded about how he spent his time. Dread made several appearances in independent companies and on the Japanese tour, but never wrestled for another worldwide promotion. It was assumed that Dread was finished with wrestling, but he surprised his fans by signing a deal with GCW upon its revival in 2005. His return to the company was even more successful than his initial debut, as he immediately staked his claim as one of the top talents in the world. He quickly shook off whatever rust he might have experienced and walked into GCW's first return Pay-Per-View, ACWF, intent on reclaiming the GCW Title. In the company's first and only six-man ACWF match, Dread topped The Jhub, Toad, Mike Hardy, Octavian and Twizzy to win his second GCW title. The following month at Warpath, Dread successfully retained his title against Octavian in a Colosseum Cage Match. It was at the end of that match, however, that Dread's career would turn forever; he was assaulted by former friend LeStatt Knight, who signaled his own designs to finish his ascent with a GCW Title victory. The two met at Dangerous Games in one of the most infamous matches of all time, a brutal "I Quit" match with the belt on the line. The two destroyed nearly everything in their path before one of the most heinous scenes in GCW history, which marked the beginning of the legendary phase of LeStatt Knight's career. Dread said the words and Knight's title reign began, effectively ending Dread's GCW career. Dread reappeared later that year at NC-17 to battle The Dark Messiah, but was never seen in the company again. Three more years have passed, however, and now Dread has resurfaced once again. Is the timing coincidence, or is Dread ready to replicate his past success? Dread is officially wrestling on a one-day contract, but as a Hall of Fame member it is understood that he may come and go as he pleases. The few with the company that know him believe that Dread is simply hoping to find out something about himself but has no long-term plans with GCW. It is said that senior GCW staff, including Caldera himself, are holding out hope that the match will respark Dread's drive and lead him to sign a long term deal with the company. Caldera and Dread still do not see eye to eye, but there is no denying that bringing back the Hall of Famer would represent a significant addition to the roster. So WorldWide 100 will feature the collision of two of the best of two different eras in GCW. Jay Terror, the best of today, locks up with Jason Dread, one of the best ever. The fan poll on gcwonline.net indicates that most believe the star of the current era will succeed, but those with longer memories recall just how talented Dread was. If he can shake off the rust, Dread is capable of pulling out a stunning win. And if he captures his third title and ties Octavian's hallowed record, will he stick around to defend it or vacate the belt and ride into the sunset? Only time will tell.
