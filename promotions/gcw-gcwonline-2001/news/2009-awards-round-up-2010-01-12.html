2009 Awards Round-up

12 January 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The 2009 GCW Award Winners were announced at Christmas Chaos. We're here with the results. 2009 marked another 365 days of highs and lows in the GCW history books. It proved to be another year of intense competition, particularly towards the upper-end of the card, with several of GCW's big names having to fight hard to stave off their younger, hungrier rivals who would seek to take their place. A few “big names” were lost throughout the year; Garbage Bag Johnny and Jordan White moved on to pastures new, but the likes of Zacharia Taylor and Clyde Fox were there to ably take their spots. Meanwhile, the burgeoning stars of Andy Murray and Chris Bagwell continued to grow, while long-serving GCW'ers like Tessa Windsor, Vivica J. Valentine, and LeStatt Knight maintained their relevance within the company with a number of thrilling encounters and engagements. But if “stability” was the upper-card's buzzword for 2009, “chaos” could certainly be used as that of the mid-card. New faces seemed to arrive on the Television Title scene every week, only to disappear just a few matches into their career. The likes of CJ Woods and Aaron Nothings failed to live-up to their early promise, while others, the Joe Milone's of the world, never really got out of second gear. 2009 saw six new Television Champions crowned as the likes of AXEL Action and Dan Black saw their profiles soar out of the division and to new heights, which is what TV division newcomers like Tempest, Cheap Labor, and SurReal will surely be targeting in 2010. In the tag division, FishBough looked like the most threatening GCW tandem since Banned & Exiled~! hit their prime and held the straps for a solid period of time after winning them from solo champion Vivica J. Valentine. Tag team wrestling in the twenty-first century is often maligned for not being able to compare with the nineties' golden era, but the likes of FishBough, Church of the Unholy, and new champions Mortifera all took big steps to restoring the art of tag wrestling to its former glory. On the event front, GCW continued to deliver blockbuster broadcasts the defied the wrestling industry's apparent decline. Steven Caldera's flagship enterprise globe-trotted their way across a multitude of different countries and arenas, including several sold-out appearances at the fabled Teater Dome, Northern Ohio. All of these trends and more were well-represented by Global Championship Wrestling's 2009 Year-End Awards, which were announced at last week's Christmas Chaos broadcast. First off the bat was the Most Missed award, which was taken home by the almost universally-praised Garbage Bag Johnny. GBJ featured heavily in GCW's 2009 but his activity dropped off towards the end of the year after a spell in GCW's signature power stable, The Establishment. Fans and critics alike will be hoping for a GBJ return in 2010. Andy Murray collected his first award of the night after successfully retaining his Face of the Year accolade from 2008. While 2009 was undoubtedly an incredible year for The Scottish King of Cool, Murray may count himself as lucky to stave off competition from Clyde Fox, who many had tipped to pick-up the award for GCW's most-loved good guy. Speaking of Murray, his greatest foe, LeStatt Knight, unsurprisingly took home the 2009 Heel of the Year accolade. LK did more than enough to cement his status as GCW's most devious force with a 2009 that was peppered with his trademark blend of psychological warfare and physical intimidation. Shaman swept home the award for Radio Personality of the Year, and in turn managed to win himself a lifetime supply of $25 sandwiches, courtesy of J. Kalvelage's Illinois Delhi. Rumours that Jeff from Northern Ohio drove his car off a cliff after hearing he'd missed out on the award have yet to be confirmed. And finally, to complete the first set of awards, the Segment(s) of the Year accolade went to the ever-popular Clyde Fox, who continues to charm GCW audiences with his hysterical weekly escapades. GCW fans voted Dangerous Games as their 2009 Show of the Year, and it's an accolade that a night that saw the returns of Jay Terror, LeStatt Knight, Bryan Mayhem, and Tessa Windsor (among others) and Jason O'Neil's official return to GCW singles competition deserves. Next up was a double swoop for AXEL Action, who took home 2009's Most Improved Wrestler award, as well as the 2010 Rising Star gong in his second full year with the company. These awards surely cement AXEL's status as the most determined superstar in GCW today, and if he maintains his current rate of improvement, there's absolutely no reason why he shouldn't be competing for the GCW World Title on a regular basis by the end of the year. Andy Murray and LeStatt Knight both won their second awards of the evening after scooping the Rivalry of the Year in a result that came as a surprise to no one. For four months Murray and Knight waged war across the country, stemming from an initial confrontation at WW100 and culminating in Murray's World Title victory at Rampage. Next up, Mortifera overcame strong opposition to win the Tag Team of the Year prize. Since aligning himself with Jorge Samuelsson, Shaman has been nothing short of a dominant force in the GCW tag division, and critics are already predicting an incredible run for the new Tag Team Champions. The biggest star of the night, however, proved to be Andy Murray, who rounded-off an incredible 2009 by winning the Worker of the Year, Wrestler of the Year, and Match of the Year for his clash with LeStatt Knight. The former AWC and PCW journeyman has transformed his career in GCW and gone from a hopeless never-will-be to one of the most exciting stars in pro-wrestling today, and these awards only further justify that.
