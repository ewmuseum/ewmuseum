Murray Breaks Four-Month Media Silence

18 November 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The Scottish King of Cool talks retirement, his son's health and says no to a comeback. Former GCW World Heavyweight Champion Andy Murray has given his first interview since bringing the curtain down on his 16-year wrestling career in July. The Scottish King of Cool claims the urge to compete hasn’t left him, but don’t expect to see him back in full-time action anytime soon. “I got back in the ring tonight and had a blast,” Murray said, speaking just moments after competing for Tallassee-based Leon County Wrestling. “It was good to come out and shake some of the rust off. There can’t have been more than 200 people here, but the fans were as loud and passionate as any I’ve heard. The promoter called me last week, and I couldn’t resist the chance to have some fun and make a surprise appearance in my home city.” Murray emerged victorious in a 20-minute main event. “I’m surprised I lasted ‘til the end of the match: I forgot how tiring this was,” he joked. “I haven’t so much as thought about hitting the gym since leaving GCW, so this was a big step. This local guys know how to work.” But has the return to competitive action rekindled Murray’s desire for pro-wrestling? “I never lost my desire,” he said. “I just can’t be a pro-wrestler any more. My situation hasn’t really changed. I’m not coming back.” A two-time Wrestler of the Year, Dangerous Games winner, Rampage winner and two-time World Champion, Murray left GCW four months ago to care for his son. Marvin Murray was hospitalised after being struck by a car on his way home from school in late-June. The King revealed at July 4th’s Shockwave event that his son may never walk again. “I’m a single Dad, and being on the road constantly was enough of a strain when he was healthy. Maybe I can think about wrestling again if he makes a full recovery. I’d love to do a couple more shows like this: the venue’s just a ten-minute drive from my house and it’s a good group of guys. I just don’t want to leave my boy’s side for any longer than a couple of hours.” Have GCW fans seen the last of The King? “I hope not. If WorldWide came rolling through the south, I’d love to come out and say ‘hello’. The fans have been brilliant. I got home the day after Shockwave and I could barely get in my driveway for all the gifts that’d been left for my son at the front gate! It’d be great to repay that kindness someday.” “We’re in a much better place now. The doc’s talking about getting Marv out of his wheelchair by the end of December, so fingers crossed. I’m not on television any more, but the fans should know that Marvin’s gonna win this battle. It might take a while but we’ll get through this, like we always do.”
