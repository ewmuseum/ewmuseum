Rampage Finalists Near History

17 June 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
This week at Rampage, four men will have the opportunity to join a short list of wrestling's biggest stars. It's a point that has been hammered home time and time again. Last year, Andy Murray won the Rampage Tournament, and is now standing on the verge of his first GCW World Title win. A year may seem like a long time to wait, but in the competitive world of GCW wrestling, there can be no denying that Murray's tournament victory was the catalyst to his success. Even runner up Chris Bagwell has gone on to achieve soaring heights, though his personality may have demanded it. A star is made in every Rampage tournament. In 2006 it was Dawn MacFarlane, shocking the world with a finals victory over Lia Ambrosi. In 2002 it was Michael Stevens, claiming ultimate victory after the real winner, Jonny Montano, suffered a career-ending injury in the match. This year's Final Four is already filled with stars. Clyde Fox is already one of the most popular wrestlers in GCW. Brian Fisher has rapidly become one of the most despised. And Zacharia Taylor has steadily been building momentum ever since his arrival. Perhaps it is Lantlas Anduril who has the most to gain. Anduril was undefeated as EPW World Champion before signing his GCW contract, but got off to a slow start. He lost his first three matches and was regarded by some critics as a bust. However, Anduril simply needed time to adapt to the new environment; he has proven his immense talent in reaching this Final Four. Anyone who counted Anduril out before is no longer looking past him. But he has a long road ahead of him. Taylor, one of the early favorites in the tournament and a two-time United States Champion, seems to be on the brink of a breakthrough. He has tremendous natural in-ring talent, but his skills are still raw and in need of refining. He seems to improve with every match, however, and his moment may arrive at Rampage. The x-factor in the tournament is no doubt Brian Fisher. Fisher, who was brought into GCW and The Establishment by his heterosexual lifemate Tyson DeBough, has risen quickly up the ranks, thanks in no small part to his affiliation with Caldera's group. Fisher has successfully defended his TV Title twice in this tournament, and is looking to retain it for the duration. With DeBough falling to Anduril, Fisher becomes The Establishment's last hope for a major victory. Fisher is more than capable of pulling it off--especially with the United States Champion DeBough in his corner. The crowd's undoubted favorite, however, is Fox. The longest tenured wrestler left in the field, a former holder of the TV and US Titles, Fox is adored by nearly every fan in every city. But this starry-eyed simpleton has undergone an evolution of his own. Fox may never have the savvy of LeStatt Knight or the charisma of Andy Murray, but his in-ring ability has improved to the point that he may be a threat to anyone in the business. There may never be any hope of Clyde growing up (which is just fine with the fans), but his skills have certainly matured. Fox will no doubt be the full-on fan favorite, and this could be his time to explode into the upper echelon and compete with the best of the best. Whatever happens, a new star will be made at Rampage. The stakes will be higher than ever--there has never been a field this talented. The winner will be poised to follow in Murray's footsteps and begin the road toward the GCW World Heavyweight Championship.
