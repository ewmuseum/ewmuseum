The Elephant in the Room

16 October 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Andy Murray on Tim Shipley's disappearance. It’s been a while since Global Championship Wrestling has seen or heard from Tim Shipley. The English was released from his contract shortly after a tough loss to rival Andy Murray at High Stakes, and his departure came as a shock to many. Shipley entered the company with a bluster comparable to none. He immediately shot to infamy by competing with Garbage Bag Johnny and Christian Zenith for the vacant Commissioner’s position. Tim would go-on to miss out on the job, but things soon picked up for him. A tough loss to Chris Bagwell prompted a giant surge in his fortunes. Soon the former Just promoter was tearing through the competition on his way to winning the 2010 Rampage Tournament. He’d become one of the brightest stars on the GCW roster, but also one of the most reviled. Cementing his devious reputation by orchestrating the return of Cheap Labor and a brutal assault on Murray, Shipley rose through the ranks not only by proving himself as a wrestler, but by being truly Machiavellian in his backstage approach. As one of the most vocal members of the roster it is perhaps a surprise, then, that Shipley’s sudden departure has been met with such a muted response. There were no parting shots, no press statements, and no fanfare. One of the federation’s most valuable players seemingly disappeared without explanation. Until now. “Tim Shipley is not a fighter. I’ve known it since day one, and I think he realised it for himself after High Stakes,” Andy Murray explains. “He doesn’t have the heart the compete at the top level of this sport. The guy talks the talk, sure, and he’s a far better athlete than most: he just lacks the one attribute that separates the men from the boys in this game.” Andy’s history with Tim certainly is a coloured one. The two first encountered each other in Dave Harber’s Atlantic Wrestling Club back in 2005 and it was assumed that the backstage bond forged there would strength with Tim’s GCW arrival. Things weren’t to be. When Murray approached Shipley he found his attitude to be much removed from that of the bright-eyed rookie he’d met in AWC. A bitter rivalry ensued, culminating in their High Stakes showdown, but Murray insists he has no qualms about what has happened. “I’m finished with Tim Shipley. He thought he’d try and use me as an example. He thought he’d try and put me on the shelf just to make a name for himself, and look what happened. I finally got him in a one-on-one situation from which he couldn’t run away, he failed, and then ran away from GCW with his tail between his legs. I won, he lost. End of story.” Despite his bullishness, Murray acknowledges that fortune might’ve played a part in his victory. “He definitely didn’t look comfortable on the top rope, I agree, but he made the choice to go up there. He’s apparently scared of heights, yet he decided to climb up and try to finish the match. I guess he’s not as sharp as he thinks he is.” When asked if he thinks we’ll ever see Shipley in a GCW ring again, Murray said: “no, I don’t think so. On a technical level his skills are almost untouchable, but I think he’s shown he lacks the passion to succeed here. Tim’s a very troubled guy with a lot going on in his life. The independents are probably better suited to him.” Whilst nobody would claim Murray’s opinions to be fact, his explanations certainly seem plausible given Shipley’s walkout. It remains to be scene whether or not Shipley will respond.
