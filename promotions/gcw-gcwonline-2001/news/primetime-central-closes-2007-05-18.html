Primetime Central Closes

18 May 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
GCW's affiliation with the wrestling media conglomerate Primetime Central came to an unceremonious end this week, as PTC officials announced the organization is closing its doors. While the company itself will remain open in some format, the organization as it was previously constituted no longer exists. While this does not have any immediate ramifications for GCW programming, it will have a tremendous impact on GCW as a company and how it does its business. While GCW's parent company, Caldera Enterprises, Inc., is a worldwide media conglomerate in its own right, Primetime Central was a major hub in the world of professional wrestling. Most aspiring wrestlers went through PTC to find employment, and PTC acted as a link between wrestling promotions to work together for the betterment of the industry. President Caldera credits PTC with making his recent match with PRIME's Lindsay Troy possible. "Personal feelings aside, the match with Troy was great for our ratings, was great for PRIME's ratings, and was great for the sport. As wrestling searches for new ways to stay relevant with modern fans, it's important that we are able to work together while maintaining the stiff air of competition that makes this industry great," Caldera said in an interview today. Rumors did begin to surface a few weeks ago regarding friction between President Caldera and PTC officials. It was said that Caldera was attempting to position himself for a takeover of PTC, which could have potentially led to a massive merger between PTC and Caldera Enterprises. While those rumors remain unfounded, the conflict between PTC and Caldera did appear to lead Caldera himself to withdraw his support from the community. Still, Caldera has maintained his usual guarded front. When asked about the situation, Caldera refused to divulge any information. "I'm not going to comment on that," Caldera said. "Business is business, and what happens in the board room stays in the board room," Caldera stated. "Everyone is certainly free to speculate, but I've really got nothing to add. I can confirm that Caldera Enterprises made a proposal, and I can confirm that we were unable to come to a deal. Whatever else you may have heard is irrelevant." Caldera said he was just sorry to see PTC go. "I've always had a soft spot in my heart for PTC," said Caldera, who actually headed up the company for a time before founding his own. "Our business has concluded, and that's unfortunate. The wrestling industry needed Primetime Central. But the decision came down that its time had ended, and we'll just carry on as best we can." Speculation has been rampant that Caldera, ever on the lookout for ways to increase his own stature in the industry, might be in talks to create a PTC-like entity within the bounds of Caldera Enterprises. But when asked about it, Caldera was coy as usual. "That's not in the works right now," he said. "But I won't lie and say we haven't explored it. There are people in the company working on a potential plan of action right now. But that's all it is: a potential plan. Right now, my personal goal is the betterment of GCW, so that we might make take this company to an even higher level." GCW's involvement with PTC had been love-hate from the beginning. President Caldera's relationship with then-owner Ron Meischker had its ups and downs, leading to several separate periods of affiliation with the organization. GCW was briefly a member of PTC rival operation Fedwars, and for a time Caldera was an active consultant for both companies. But PTC has always been the premier cross-promotional organization in professional wrestling, and GCW never stayed away for long. GCW quickly rejoined PTC after reopening its doors in February of 2005. It had been affiliated with the company ever since. Now, with PTC officially changing gears, Global Championship Wrestling is without official affiliation for the first time since its inception in 2001. But for the time being, officials appear to be content with that fact. Most of the roster, too, feels a sense of relief with GCW's newfound free agency, as the interpromotional workload will drop to zero, leaving them free to focus on their work within the GCW ring. Still, GCW extends it appreciation to all those who spent their careers working to make Primetime Central one of the most influential entities in professional wrestling. Even the boastful Steven Caldera admits to PTC's role in GCW's evolution. "Without PTC, GCW would not be where it is today," Caldera laments. "I can't say for sure what role PTC has played in GCW's history, but there's no doubt that PTC was an integral part of this company for many years."
