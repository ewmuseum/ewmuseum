Brad Jackson Released Before WorldWide

13 July 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
After a week of speculation, Brad Jackson's status with GCW has been resolved: the big man has been released. Neither side has commented officially, but Jackson's representatives have released a statement: "Brad enjoyed his time in GCW but felt it was time to move on. He thanks all his fans for their years of support." Jackson hasn't exactly tried to hide his dissatisfaction with the company, however. Since Rampage, Jackson has been vocal with his discontent and obviously had one foot out the door. What isn't clear at this point, however, is whether Jackson walked out or was pushed. GCW President Steven Caldera is notoriously impatient with his roster, especially Establishment members, and never likes the appearance that one of his stars isn't happy. There is some speculation that Jackson was in fact notified of his release before a request came, but if this is the case it's purely symbolic. It is clear that Jackson was on his way out. Caldera was said to be furious with Jackson during the week, following his high-profile Pay-Per-View loss to Garbage Bag Johnny. Caldera, who showed no remorse in injuring his supposed best friend Jay Terror, certainly would have given Jackson his walking papers this week on WorldWide. While Jackson made his name through brawn and not brains, he certainly knew the score. It is assumed that Jackson, not content to let Caldera embarrass him on national television, notified the company of his intent to quit, and Caldera fired him before he could make it official. Jackson was a superb talent who never quite lived up to his full potential in GCW. Jackson was always a feared opponent, and more than one fledgling superstar has quit the company rather than accept a match with him. But Jackson never quite got comfortable in the company. Jackson was the classic locker-room loner, and even his recent Establishment teammates were intimidated and wary of him. His unpredictable temperament made him difficult to work with, even as it made him a feared opponent in the ring. Despite the fact that Jackson was never liked, there is still a sense within the company today that it has lost a potential superstar. Jackson always had superb in-ring skills, and his power always made him a big draw. Jackson is another high-profile loss for GCW this week, and the company is already scrambling to find replacements.
