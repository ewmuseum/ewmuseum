Slash Nearing Mental Breakdown?

27 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Joey Harris, who wrestles under the name "Slash" in GCW, was removed from his Continental Airlines flight for causing a disturbance. Already with a string of instances that question Slash's mental health and stability, he was removed from his Continental Airlines flight for an incident in which the airlines describe as, "threatening to its staff and other travellers". Reports are that Slash, who was flying to his home in Seattle from Hawaii, became increasingly agitated by the volume level of the earphones the person next to him was wearing. Slash, whose real name is Joey Harris, ripped the earphones from the person beside him and began to stomp on them prior to the flight taking off. In effort to stop the disturbance, a stewardess rushed to the scene and was threatened by Joey Harris. Witnesses close to the situation say that Slash uttered threats of "killing and raping" the stewardess. Hawaii State Police were called to the scene and Slash was removed from the flight in handcuffs. He spent the evening detained by Police before being released with a fine of undisclosed amounts. This is not the first instance Slash has been involved in, that has raised eyebrows. He has been described as "reclusive" and "introverted" backstage lately. He was seen around Hawaii with prostitutes, at bars very drunk and was kicked out of his hotel for indecent exposure. Could Joey Harris' past be creeping up on him again? A string of bad luck? Or a young superstar pushing the limits while the Commissioner was on tour in Japan? A GCW official confirmed that Slash will still be involved in the Rampage Battle Royal , although on wonders if he will have mental clarity and stability to compete and qualify for the Tournament. The Continental Airlines flight was delayed for four hours, and airlines have assured the passengers who were stranded that they will be compensated for their incoveniance. Joey Harris was not available for comment.
