Caution: A Plot Afoot Ahead

4 March 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Who got buried in Rich Rollins' tomb? In case you blinked during the milestone episode of WorldWide last week (and if you did, shame on you), you might have missed one of the most shocking moments in GCW history. I'm talking of course about the controversial footage depicting Rich Rollins playing five-card draw with Elvis Presley and Tupac Shakur. For anyone who might have taken a bathroom break after Borealis retained his Television title, my faithful accomplice and part-time broadcast technician Nathan Perchman has uploaded the candid footage, mirrored below. Viewer beware: these images are of a disturbing nature. http://img651.imageshack.us/img651/1977/rollinspoker.jpg How is this possible? In a word: Conspiracy! Three years ago, GCW legend Rich "The Renegade" Rollins was found dead of an apparent drug overdose. The media blamed the usual round of pain-and-fame killers; it was reported that a concoction of Percocet, Vicadin, and an unspecified oxycodone had done in the rapacious Renegade. It was either that or the oft-rumored sexual deviancy of the Olsen twins, but let's not split hairs here. What I found interesting was that none of the articles indicated what was found in Rollins' toxicology report, only what was found on hand in the apartment. Upon further investigation, I was told that the toxicology results had been sealed by a judge of the Southern District of New York, who for legal reasons asked not to be identified. Curious, indeed. I initially brought this information to the attention of Kevin Jacobs, but he "wouldn't touch that story with a ten-foot cattle prod." His words. His loss. Determined but too afraid to push it far up the GCW totem pole, I decided to take a risk. Receiving an advance on next month's salary (sorry Steven, I didn't really install that hot tub or the time machine), I managed to grease the wheels of our great judiciary system and opened the toxicology report for myself. What I found was even more puzzling; not a single pain killer was present in Rich Rollins' system at the time of his death, commercial or otherwise. Instead these three words jumped off the page: Risperdal. Haldol. Zyprexa. These are high-caliber anti-psychotics, folks. While Rich Rollins was many things, a schizophrenic sociopath he was not. He had no licensed prescription for any of these medications. No pre-existing conditions. No history of (obvious) mental illness. It begs the question, what would such a high profile superstar be doing with enough psycho suppressants in his system to cure then subsequently kill Charles Manson? Then it hit me. Rich Rollins had many skeletons in his closet, including the much-publicized 2003 incarceration of his baby brother, Jason Rollins (or, as the greater Chicago area knew him, the Slipknot Strangler). For one horrifying summer, this depraved black sheep of the Rollins flock murdered six young women, binding them to various things with straps of wet leather that would constrict as they died. Jason has since served eight years of his six consecutive life sentences, first in a high security ward of the Chicago-Read Mental Health Center until being transferred to Thompson State Prison in August of 2004. I immediately tried to contact the corrections facility, but they refused to release any information to the media regarding Jason Rollins. The plot thickens. While I was unable to procure a copy of Jason's medical records directly from the state, a helpful nurse at Chicago Mercy was more than eager to make a few hundred bucks. Armed with these files, a quick comparison against Rich Rollins' sealed autopsy report proved it: their dental records matched. Who got buried in Rich Rollins' tomb? Not Rich Rollins, that's for sure.
