Shooting Star: Valentine's Fall is Going to Shock You I know you'll find this hard to believe, but I was unpopular once. No, really. In high school I had acne, a patchy beard and no social skills. You know the type, the type that gets zeroed out by those primates who develop early and start losing their hair by age twenty-four. It didn't feel good to be leered at, exiled, hated. Eventually things filled out and I developed the angelic voice you hear on WorldWide every week, but up until that point your beloved David Yale was, gasp, a loser. Of course, now I'm hated for all the opposite reasons. My features are perfect, I'm handsome and rich, and I'm not even forty yet. And of course I'm outspoken, and fond of poking holes in your so-called heroes. So naturally, I find myself hated once again. I know what it's like to be hated, and I enjoy the sort that's born of jealousy rather than mockery. Vivica J. Valentine knows what it's like to be hated. When she first arrived in GCW, winning the United States Title her first trip into the ring, Valentine was one of the most hated wrestlers in the company. She had a bad attitude, the fans and wrestlers disliked her, and even her so-called friends were cruel to her. But then something happened. She grew up, she filled out. Her brash attitude developed into something more dynamic, and she used it to intensify her performance in the ring. Soon enough she was on fire, and it seemed like everyone loved Vivica J. Valentine. So here's the difference between us: I have embraced the hate. Valentine fears it like Pete fears a treadmill. Valentine didn't just embrace the love of the crowd. She fed upon it. Those deafening crowd reactions, many of which so powerful that they've drowned out even my perfectly projecting voice, were the very blood that pumped through her veins. She came to develop a sort of symbiotic relationship with the crowd, and the people fueled her to a World Title and numerous accolades. In some ways she owes the crowd everything for her career. She gave back, no doubt about it, but lately it seemed like it was growing desperate. Like she could hear another voice on the lips of the crowds, as if her lover were whispering another name in his sleep. Murray... Murray... She started donating even more time to charities. She spent countless hours at bedside with sick children, and never missed a public appearance. And yet, for as smart as Valentine is, she seemed to miss a fundamental truth about the human wasteland that is our viewership: they're fickle. She didn't want to believe it. It was clear from the beginning that she was in denial. I think she honestly believed that she would never be overtaken as the most beloved star in GCW. Sure, a better wrestler might come along, but never did it occur to her that the fans would abandon her. Of course, the fans didn't abandon her, but that's what seems to be going on inside her head. This is typical of Valentine; she makes excuses, points the finger and refuses to hold herself accountable when things go bad. So when Andy Murray, and even Clyde Fox, started getting the big ovations, Valentine couldn't handle it. She didn't know what to do. So she took it out on the Champion. Murray might be the only wrestler more naïve than Valentine, but he's the one with the crowd on his side. Murray's been so great this year that he's untouchable with the fans. But the true test of Murray's greatness will come when he's forced to cope when the fans abandon him. Valentine can't cope with this simple fact of the profession she's chosen. She's been around a long time and done it all in the ring, but that she didn't see this coming is a sobering indicator that Vivica J. Valentine isn't the hero they all thought she was. She was just an insecure little girl, so desperate for attention and love that she would put her body through anything just to get it. You may think that my intent here is to bash Valentine, and sure, maybe I'm taking some jabs at her past. But believe it or not, I actually see this as a good thing for Valentine. The veil is off, the truth it out. Like Jay Terror to a big-breasted blonde, it cannot be denied. The fans are just as culpable as Valentine in this fiasco, and it's time for Valentine to cut the cord and find a new source of motivation. Unfortunately for Andy Murray, it seems like she's found it this week. When Valentine came back to the ring and assaulted the Champion, she may have sent a message that there's something else driving her now. Hate. Pure, unadulterated hatred for Andy Murray. He's got the thing that means most to her, the GCW World Title, and all the adulation that comes with it. He swept the 2009 Awards, he's on the verge of breaking her title reign record, he's replaced her in every way. Don't get jealous. Get angry. If Vivica embraces this new, edgier side of her personality, it could mean serious trouble at NC-17. In fact, the good-hearted Murray may not be able to handle it. Sure, he handled LeStatt Knight, but this is a different kind of situation. Knight respected Murray even as he was trying to break him psychologically. Valentine doesn't want to break Murray's mind. She wants to break his body. She wants his belt. If she gets physical, unleashes all the pent up angst and anxiety that has been building up to this point, I don't think there's any way Andy Murray walks out of NC-17 the champion. Murray is a great wrestler, but he doesn't need the title like Valentine needs it. Murray wanted the title because he wanted to be great. Valentine wants it because she simply cannot breathe without it. Murray will be fighting for his legacy. Valentine will be fighting for survival. And when human beings are pushed to the limits of their ability to survive, they're capable of truly superhuman feats. Like pinning Andy Murray and taking away his title.
