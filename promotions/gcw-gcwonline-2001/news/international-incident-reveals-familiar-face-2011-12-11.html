International Incident Reveals Familiar Face

11 December 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Early Saturday morning, in the Somali pirate lair of Harardhere, UN & Interpol agents uncovered an international mystery when they engaged pirate bandits in an impromptu exchange of gunfire. Once the firing stopped, upon further investigation, agents found two oil tankers, warehouses full of high-end firearms and cinematic-quality fireworks, tens of millions of dollars (printed in twelve different counterfeit currencies), and forty-seven hostages, coming from Sweden, Spain, Italy, and the US, as reported by the official Interpol Twitter account (@Interpol). The hostages were found to be held by the newly deceased squad of criminal sailors in a lavish hotel, casino, and entertainment palace standing twenty stories tall, often kept in rooms nicer than found in Las Vegas. Among the forty-some hostages were thirteen former America’s Top Model contestants, 22 ex- or one-time professional Mexican baseball players – including one American – and twelve cruise ship workers, according to one UN official. One American hostage who investigators and officials at Interpol are most curious about is a 26-year-old from Buffalo, New York; Daniel Francisco. Of all the persons involved, Francisco is most likely to be the target of a pirate kidnapping for ransom. It is debatable whether Francisco is more notorious for knocking out his teammate while playing for the St. Louis Cardinals or as a wrestler with Global Championship Wrestling. In his 10 month run in GCW, Danny Francisco, better known to the fans as ‘Dynasty,’ was what some believed to be a rising star with good potential. His career included a fierce rivalry with El Moreno and Aaron Fujita, an HDTV match against Johnny Borealis, and a match for the ages at NC-17 VIII for the American Dream. Francisco began missing work and regularly scheduled appearances after August 15th, after his match against Borealis in Chicago. His whereabouts, until today, were unknown. The hostages are scheduled to be released and returned to home Monday morning after a full debriefing over the weekend. More on this story as it develops.
