Remembering Rich Rollins

20 February 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Three years ago today, Global Championship Wrestling lost one of its defining talents. Three years ago today a wrestling legend was lost. Rich Rollins was not simply a great wrestler; he was a founding father, one of the pillars of the fledgling Global Championship Wrestling. In the year 1999 Rollins was simply a young, up-and-coming wrestling trying to break through in the CWU. His rise to stardom coincided with the rise of a shrewd young wrestling mind in Steven Caldera. With Rollins as one of his top stars, Caldera was able to break out, form his own wrestling promotion, and take on the world. Rollins did his fair share of world beating. After two GCW World Heavyweight Championship reigns in 2001, Rollins went on to wrestle for Primetime Championship Wrestling, the biggest wrestling promotion in the world at the start of the decade. A strong sweet sixteen showing in GTT3 put his name on an even larger map, but with GCW's closing in 2002 he left for Japan. Rollins returned in 2005 to capture both the Television and United States Titles. (In 2008, LeStatt Knight would posthumously award Rollins a Tag Team title, making him a Grand Slam Champion.) Shortly thereafter came a contentious divorce between Rollins and Caldera, leading to Rollins competing for GCW's number one competitor, PRIME. Rollins would later return for spot appearances, before making his full time return in late 2007. At only twenty-nine years of age, Rollins appeared to be on track to regain the GCW World Title for the first time in seven years. But tragedy struck when Rollins was found dead in an apartment of an accidental drug overdose. His stunning death revealed the painful dark side Rollins always carried with him, but never quite revealed. A cheerful rebel with a decided mean streak, Rollins was one of the top all-around wrestlers of his generation. He loved to brawl, but could match styles with the best in every weight class. He fought technical wars with The Jhub, put on an aerial show with Mike Hardy, and stood toe to toe with LeStatt Knight. The unpredictability that made him one of wrestling's most popular and prodigious stars also hinted at the volatile personal life that led to his demise. But his biggest fans like to say that he died as he lived; without regret and with a whole lot of controversy. Few wrestlers in GCW history have had the sheer talent that Rollins had, and he only got better as the years progressed. As the talent in the wrestling world improved, Rollins had the rare ability to stay one step ahead. His years of tireless work helped him win PTC's GTT5 tournament in absolutely dominant fashion, and helped seal his spot in the GCW Hall of Fame. GCW is hoping to have its Hall of Fame members appear at NC-17. It is one of GCW's great tragedies that Rollins will not be among them. There are those that refuse to honor Rollins because of the habits that led to his death, but there can be no denying the impact he had on Global Championship Wrestling and the entire wrestling world. Rollins was GCW's original great, its first transcendent legend, and a man who will live forever as one of those special few who gave birth to a ten year dynasty.
