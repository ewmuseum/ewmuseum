Dangerous Games: Murray first first shots

21 July 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Scottish King of Cool uncharacteristically bullish ahead of major event. Dangerous Games may still be two months away but one GCW superstar has wasted little time in outlining his intentions for the event. Ever since LeStatt Knight ended his record-breaking run as GCW World Heavyweight Championship, Andy Murray has made it known to everyone who’ll listen that winning Dangerous Games is his #1 goal for 2010, and it happens that things haven’t changed. “The past couple of months have been good to me,” said The Scottish King of Cool this morning, “but it’s time to get back to the grind. I’ve had a couple of months away from the spotlight to recharge my batteries, but now’s the time to get my name back in the mix.” Murray has been a part of the GCW roster for two Dangerous Games events, but it remains the one major accomplishment to allude him thus far in his glittering GCW career. “In 2008 I came second to LK, and in 2009 I couldn’t enter because I was the champion,” Murray said. “”Well LeStatt isn’t here anymore, and I don’t have a belt. Make no mistake: I’m in it to win it. I’d be an idiot if I was to guarantee victory in such an unpredictable match-up, but I’ve got as good a shot as anyone. There’s not a single name on the GCW roster that scares me at the moment. I’m ready.” Though Murray has been away from the title scene for a few months, his name remains in the headlines. His allegiance with Chris Bagwell and their rivalry with Tim Shipley and Cheap Labor has become one of the year’s most compelling stories, and The King will face-off against SCCW Champion Jared Sykes in the first round of the PTC Elite Title tournament at WorldWide 134. Though he hasn’t actively chased the title, Andy remains GCW’s most popular wrestler and one of the most high-profile wrestlers in the country. “Everyone on the roster is a threat,” Andy said of other potential victors. “That’s the beauty of this event: truly anybody can win it. Star power isn’t even a consideration: Aaron Fujita is just as likely to walk away victorious as Vivica J. Valentine. All it takes is one lapse of concentration and poof, you’re gone. As far as I’m concerned, though: if you’re in Dangerous Games and you’re not worried about me, you’re a fool. The whole roster’s on notice – no exceptions.”
