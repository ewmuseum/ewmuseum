Dangerous Games Preview, Take 2

3 October 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
With GCW's most highly anticipated event on the horizon, GCW has asked some of its long-time employees to preview the event. This week, 2005 winner Michael 'Spitfire' Stevens weighs in from his home in Greenwich, Connecticut! Well, well, it certainly has been a long time since I've been able to address the GCW faithful. Kevin Jacobs of gcwonline.net dropped me a line recently to ask me to write this preview of the Dangerous Games match, and I fully intend to do so. But before I can get to that, I have to address what's really on everyone's minds: the current status of your hero! In case anyone has forgotten, I won Dangerous Games in 2005 and went on to NC-17 to defeat LeStatt Knight for the World Title. It was the toughest thing I've ever done. Ironically enough, after all the hell I went through against Knight, I hurt myself in an untelevised charity match that I didn't even get paid for. I lost my World Title and I've spent the last year and a half here in Greenwich doing my best to get my body back in line. Rehab has been hard, but it's given me time to do some things I didn't have time to do before. Now, Dangerous Games is on the horizon again and rumor has it old Stevie C has a whole lot of surprises lined up, but it saddens me (though I'm sure not as much as it saddens you) to report that I won't be among the entrants. I've still got a long road ahead before I can even think about stepping back into the ring, and even longer before I'm ready to win a match like Dangerous Games. So I'm going to stay home and keep working, and with any luck I'll be writing this column and picking myself to win Dangerous Games 2008. That said, let's go back to the topic of surprises. The one thing I always liked most about Dangerous Games was the unpredictability. Your average one on one contest gives you the chance to do a little homework and scout your opponent long before you ever lock up with them. But Dangerous Games is a whole new kind of challenge. Not only are you facing everyone in GCW, you're inevitably going to come face to face with someone you never expected. I think this year there are only 20 confirmed entrants on the list (which does not get released to anyone but the active roster, so I've got no inside information for you). That means anyone who wants to win Dangerous Games has ten names floating around out there with a big upper hand. You simply have no idea who you might encounter in the ring. That makes winning this match one of the toughest challenges any wrestler can face. Not only does it take tremendous strength, endurance, awareness and ring knowledge, it requires a great deal of luck. In 2005 I had the bad luck of entering at number seven, but seven turned out to be a lucky number anyway. But there's definitely a myth attached to those early draws. I won from number seven. Lia Ambrosi won from number thirteen. Twizzy entered in the first half of the 2002 match. Even that fuckwit Shane Lane won from number three. The point is, early numbers are certainly a bigger challenge, but typically only to those who lack the talent to win the thing anyway. Where the luck comes in is equivalent to which direction the wind is blowing in deciding whether a baseball flies over the fence or falls short for an out. When a 300 pound musclebound wrestler has you perched on the top rope, inches from elimination, balance and technique and tenacity are valuable, but it takes at least a little dose of luck to survive. That said, I see some lucky faces on the current GCW roster. I get asked a lot about Vivica J. Valentine, who some fans seem to find similar to me. Frankly, I don't see any similarities at all; she's got bigger boobs than I do but I have a much tighter ass. Still, despite the fact that she quite literally couldn't wear my jockstrap, she's been impressive. I suppose I could stretch far enough to see some similarities in our United States Title reigns leading to big rises, but Vivica's got one problem: she's not lucky. She went a long time without a win at the start of this year, and she has to work too hard to get what she gets. That's good enough to get her to the promised land eventually, but not in this match. There's Chris Storm, the poor man's Michael Stevens. Storm and Stone would have made excellent Spitfire lackeys last year, but they seem more crafty than lucky. I like Brad Jackson. He's got an edge that the rest of GCW just seems to lack. But his luck is untested. But GCW is due for a true surprise winner, and if there's a dark horse in this match, Jackson is where it's at. Then there's my old nemesis LeStatt. James Bryan picked him to win in last week's preview. I won't bother to go through the long list of names I'd like to call him, but there's one thing I can't deny: LeStatt Knight is lucky. How else could you explain him defeating me two months in a row on GCW Pay-Per-View? But I'm not going to pick LeStatt Knight, either. Something in my gut tells me the wrestling gods (and no, I don't mean Steven Caldera) won't allow it to happen. So I'm going to tell you who's winning this thing, based on my experience as a winner and a champion: The winner of the 2007 Dangerous Games- Shawn Porter. Why? Because if it's not me, it really doesn't matter. -Michael "Spitfire" Stevens
