Ranking the GCW Champions

27 November 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
As a special feature to gcwonline.net, head GCW Interviewer Kevin Jacobs has ranked every World Champion in GCW history. With the ascent of Lia Ambrosi into the ranks of the all-time greats, once again the topic of what it takes to reach that next level has risen. Is Ambrosi among the best of all time? See what Jacobs thinks. ---- The GCW World Heavyweight Championship is ostensibly the biggest prize in GCW. But there is yet another level to reach beyond even that tremendous accomplishment. In GCW's history there have been fifteen World Champions, a very short list of the most amazing talent ever to enter a wrestling ring. Everyone on this list is deserving of high accolades; even the last names on this list are among the best ever to lace up a pair of boots. But some took the World Title and rose even higher, reaching a plateau that only a select few will ever climb to. Here's my all-time World Champion power rankings: 15: Shane Lane There's no question that this is the lowest moment in the history of the GCW title. Lane arrived early in 2001 and quickly took GCW by storm, proving himself to be quite capable. However, he also rubbed most of GCW the wrong way and became hated by nearly everyone in the company. After winning Dangerous Games Lane challenged The Jhub, who had just won the title, and defeated him at Meltdown. After dethroning one of GCW's most loved stars, Lane promptly began mailing in his performances, refusing to put forth any effort on GCW programming. He finally lost the belt at Annihilation to Rich Rollins, but the damage was done and Lane went down as the worst champion in GCW's seven years. ---- 14: Tristain Mayhem That this name is so low on the list represents a huge blow to GCW. Tristain Mayhem engaged in one of GCW's most historic rivalries with Octavian in late 2001, and finally vanquished him at Christmas Chaos in December. But after winning the big prize, Mayhem seemed to push the belt to the side, choosing instead to focus on a tag team with his friend Jennifer Krug. The name "Double Cool" causes GCW fans to cringe, and the entire debacle was mercifully ended by Jason Dread the following month at Rampage. ---- 13: Dawn McFarlane Dawn McFarlane accomplished an amazing feat when she won the 2006 Rampage Tournament, defeating Lia Ambrosi in the finals to capture the World Title. But her subsequent reign was a tremendous disappointment, and those close to the backstage situation reveal her insecurity and utter lack of confidence. McFarlane was a supremely talented wrestler that didn't seem capable of handling the World Champion's spotlight. McFarlane deserves to be remembered as a worthy champion, but one that fell short of accomplishing everything she could have. ---- 12: John Reed John Reed never was quite able to live up to the stardom of his brother Vincent. Vincent Reed was one of the CWU's mainstays and John always sought to live up to his considerable legacy. Reed finally got there when he beat Rich Rollins for the title, but lost the title a month later to Octavian. ---- 11: Toxic Toxic was a physical phenomenon and a freakishly talented athlete, but most amazing was his age. At just nineteen years old Toxic won the X:Wrestling World Title, which ultimately became absorbed into the GCW Title history. Toxic never did reach his full potential, which was limitless; for a long time President Caldera himself believed Toxic could one day become GCW's greatest wrestler. Toxic never quite reached the height of all-time great, but he remains one of GCW's better wrestlers. ---- 10: Michael 'Spitfire' Stevens Stevens deserves to be higher. A no-brainer future Hall of Famer, Stevens participated in one of the most intense, bloody and brutal rivalries of all time with LeStatt Knight in 2005. Stevens tried and failed to wrench the title from Knight's hands twice, then came back one last time at NC-17. The conclusion of GCW's most historic feud came with Stevens walking out on top, finally defeating his greatest adversary. The reign should have ended up among one of GCW's greatest, but Stevens suffered an injury at the hands of Johnny Borealis and was forced to vacate the title after a short reign. ---- 9: Twizzy Twizzy's World Title reign was short and interrupted by GCW's closure, but the way he won it gets him points. He defeated twenty other highly motivated wrestlers in the 2002 Dangerous Games to capture the title, finally reaching the pinnacle of the sport. Twizzy is deserving of high accolades as one of GCW's most unique superstars that always competed at an extremely high level. ---- 8: Bryan Mayhem Though he only held the World Title for two months, Bryan Mayhem's run in 2007 was the stuff of legends. Mayhem was almost unstoppable, and the only wrestler to even faze him was the incredible Lia Ambrosi. Mayhem ran roughshod over GCW and dominated the entire field, culminating in his first and only title win at High Stakes. Ambrosi recaptured the belt but Mayhem's title reign punctuated one of the most dominant runs by any GCW wrestler. ---- 7: Badger Benandanti One of the most quick-witted and unique wrestlers of all time and the inventor of Bad-Fu, Badger Benandanti (and his cohort General Tso) brings nothing but fond memories. Though Badger held the GCW World Title only once, he was in fact a two-time Caldera-era World Champion, having been the final CWU World Title holder as well. Badger was, in fact, expected to be awarded the first GCW World Title, but was unable to join the company right away due to injuries. Still, Badger's unorthodox style and incredible big-match performances make him a perfect choice at this spot. ---- 6: The Jhub What The Jhub (pronounced: joob) lacked in pure talent he made up for in heart and charisma. Perhaps GCW's most beloved star, The Jhub's two World Title reigns remain as high points in GCW's history. No World Champion generated more pure excitement and energy. As the leader of the famed anti-establishment group System X, The Jhub remained on top of GCW throughout its initial run. His first title reign remains one of the most infamous of all time (see the final entry), but his second vindicated him and cemented his legacy as one of GCW's best. ---- 5: Rich 'The Renegade' Rollins Three current Establishment members in the top five? I'm afraid so. The first is Rollins, who has not held the GCW title in over six years, yet his two early reigns remain among the best of all time. Helping his case is the fact that Rollins has continued to make history throughout GCW's existence, once returning in 2005 to capture the US and TV titles... simultaneously. Rollins stands as perhaps GCW's most heralded graduate, having accomplished incredible feats outside of the company as well as within it. ---- 4: Lia Ambrosi The current World Champion suffers slightly from a lack of perspective, but it's already apparent how great she has been. After finally capturing the World Title by beating LeStatt Knight at NC-17 last year, Lia held the title almost non-stop through the calendar year. With the exception of her defeat to Bryan Mayhem, Ambrosi held the title for almost all of 2007, something once believed to be impossible. Ambrosi loses points due to a lack of top-quality challengers; she defeated Mayhem in the rematch and defeated a future World Champion in Jordan White, but otherwise she was not seriously challenged. Ambrosi will have a chance to answer her critics as she appears once again in the main event of NC-17. Still, her two reigns undoubtedly have secured her a top spot in GCW's history. ---- 3: Jason Dread Dread is the only man to hold the World Title in both of GCW's two major incarnations. He first won the title in January of 2002, then came back and won it three years later in April of 2005. Both reigns were highly impressive and Dread cemented his legacy as one of GCW's most understated legends. Despite his membership in the Hall of Fame and his two great runs, Dread remains an underappreciated GCW alumnus. Still, it cannot be denied that Jason Dread was one of the most superb wrestlers of all time. ---- 2: Octavian Until the rise of our final entry it seemed unlikely that Octavian would ever be surpassed. The original Establishment member is the only man ever to hold the World Title three times, a feat that seems unlikely to be surpassed. Octavian was the first GCW Champion, then came back and won the belt twice more before the company's closure in 2002. Octavian's second title reign stood for a long time as the most impressive in GCW history. Though he was eventually surpassed, Octavian stands as the original transcendentalist. ---- And the greatest champion of all time... 1: LeStatt Knight There is hardly anyone who would dispute this. What LeStatt Knight accomplished in his two title reigns is absolutely unprecedented and unlikely to be repeated. While his second reign is often scrutinized due to the lack of serious competition, the first reign is the single most recognized accomplishment in GCW history. Knight terrorized GCW in 2005 and ran wild to the World Title, and his grip on the belt looked unbreakable. It took an inspired effort from Michael Stevens to take the belt from Knight. What's even scarier is the fact that Knight might be better now than he was during either of his two title reigns, suggesting that his name might appear once more in the title records.
