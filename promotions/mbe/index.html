A long-running message-board wrestling promotion that moved to the FW Central hub in 2007 for what its own operator called a restart, running a Lord of the Boards tournament toward a world-title unification match before its own record runs out. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Message Board Entertainment traces its own origin, by its own FAQ's account, to a 1999 message-board thread on 1wrestling so contentious it became known simply as The Streak — a wrestler called The Spoiler “Spoilerbombing everyone in the thread” until the argument turned into the promotion's first match. By 2007 it had passed through several hosts and a merger with Underground Xtreme Wrestling before restarting on A1Wrestling.com under its own long-serving head booker, moving from a weekly schedule to a biweekly, event-driven one “not unlike what ROH uses for a real world type example.”

Its restart carried two titles forward: a World Championship held by Doc Silver, who had won it from Justin Evitable at Total Elimination the previous December, and a World Tag Team title held by the Thrillbillies, Hoss Garrison and Jake McCody. A rival claimant, a wrestler billed only as Promo, spent the 2007 season insisting through his own legal threats and interview segments that a disputed finish had made him “the still undefeated World Champ of MBE” regardless of the belt's own physical holder.

Its own summer tournament, Lord of the Boards, ran a bracket toward a title shot and the tournament's own name; Jeffrey Roberts punched his ticket to the final by defeating General Mayhem, and the other semifinal, Justin Evitable against Johnny Onan, closed out a card that opened with Jeff Roberts sneaking a chair shot and a piledriver on Evitable hours before the two ever reached the ring. The same pay-per-view was billed to finally settle the promotion's own World title dispute, Doc Silver against Promo, “to settle the question of who the real world champion is once and for all.”

By November, Doc Silver was staging his own retirement in a filmed vignette, only to reveal the whole grim scene as a movie shoot; Justin Evitable used the same broadcast slot to demand one final match for the World title before, in his own words, the promotion “becomes nothing more than a shadow of its former self.”

How the Lord of the Boards final, the Doc Silver–Promo unification match, and Evitable's own last challenge played out isn't in the surviving record; Doc Silver's retirement vignette, and Evitable's answer to it, are the last documents to survive.
