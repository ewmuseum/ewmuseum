A personal-homepage IWF run from iceonline.net under the joke pseudonym "President John Dough," crowning four champions in an October 1996 tournament and continuing its "Wednesday Night Fights" broadcast through a December 1996 show script, a February 1997 title-slate update and a December 1997 anniversary post marking fourteen months of continuous operation. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

A recruitment post signed “John Dough” opened this IWF on 11 October 1996, pointed at a personal iceonline.net homepage and inviting “anyone interested in helping out writing or such.” Nine days later it announced a full slate of champions in one post: American Ninja as Heavyweight Champion and Butterball as Television Champion, each ranked against nine further contenders, with the Soul Takers and the Russian Nightmares holding the Tag Team and Television Tag Team Titles over their own ten-team fields.

Its first recorded title change followed on 3 November: Death & Destruction, “better known as The Franchise and The Regal,” took the Television Tag Team Titles from the Russian Nightmares when Regal dropped a flying leg drop across a semi-conscious Boris while the Franchise occupied Nikolia.

A Wednesday Night Fights script that December, broadcast from the Rose Garden in Portland, Oregon, ran the Brain Busters defending the Television Tag Team Titles inside a steel cage against former champions Flying Thunder. By February 1997 its Universal Heavyweight Champion was Ricky “Top Gun” Santana, and its own studio update previewed a “War of the Alliances” card putting three championships on the line at once between its rival in-house factions, Power Team USA, the Golden Alliance and the Empire of Power.

Its own Vice-President, Tony Bucks, marked fourteen months of operation on 12 December 1997, calling it “home of some of the greats like Alan 'Sideswipper' Kalord, Ricky Santana, Brute Force, 'All American' Atlas” and still “getting stronger every day.” No closing statement survives; that anniversary post is the last surviving document.
