Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

A High Impact Wrestling Federation opened 18 February 1997, its own president posting anonymously to recruit sixteen wrestlers, one per handler, with rules stated bluntly: “NO REAL WRESTLERS. NO COPIES OF REAL WRESTLERS... I don't want 25,000 Ric Flair wannabes,” and no joke gimmicks either — the ad names a prior league's own 400-pound “Caveman” character as exactly what it wouldn't accept.

It ran booked, scoring roleplay on a stated nine-point scale from “never send anything” to “active in sending flashes, strategies, and responses to others,” with weekly Sunday shows and a bigger card roughly once a month.

A last-call recruitment post, 23 February, reports seven handlers already entered into an eight-man World Title tournament, with one slot still open (though the president would rather have had sixteen entrants than eight). A community column the following day named the league's own ad, unprompted, as one of two new federations worth watching that week.

Its inaugural card, a fifteen-man battle royal, aired 3 March 1997, called by a two-man comedy announce desk that spent as much time bantering and spilling coffee as calling the action. Fifteen characters entered — among them the Dark Knight, Killer Instinct, Jonney Gold, Ricky Johnson, the brothers Franky and Richie Delgado, the Stormtrooper of Death, Hellraiser, Blaze, Obscuridad, the Siberian Strangler, Khan, the Megalomaniac, the Highlander, and Damon Oscar Avonoiaa, billed as “DOA” — and Hellraiser won it, eliminating Franky Delgado last to take the tournament bye.

No further document survives. Who won the eight-man World Title tournament that battle royal opened, or whether the federation ran another card at all, isn't in the surviving record.
