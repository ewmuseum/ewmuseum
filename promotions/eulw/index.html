Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The federation's own later retrospective dates its founding to May 1997, under the name Ultra League Wrestling, and calls its first War Rumble supercard “ULW's greatest card ever.”

A rebrand announcement in the surviving record recasts the promotion as Extreme Ultra League Wrestling — “the same old ULW with one eXtra letter” — promising a return to “blood and gore,” no-disqualification matches by default, and a tournament for a World Heavyweight Championship left vacant at the old promotion's close, alongside United States, Hardcore, Television, Cruiserweight and Tag Team titles each carrying its own defense schedule. It planned a weekly “One eXtreme FridayNight” card and named its next two pay-per-views, “SH@T HITS THE FAN” and a “2nd anual War Rumble 98.” The federation's own later handbook, written after a further relaunch of its own, instead dates this same EXtreme rebrand to December 1998, a year later than this announcement's own placement of it; the record doesn't resolve which telling is right.

The one match the surviving record holds from either era pits the champion, Dan McCain — a former Intercontinental Champion billed as the promotion's 1997 War Rumble winner, wrestling amid rumors he was about to jump to a rival league — against Lover Boy for the World Heavyweight Championship, best two-out-of- three falls, no disqualification. McCain took the first fall and Lover Boy answered in the second with his own finisher before winning the title outright in the third by submission to a Texas cloverleaf.

A community tag team ranking for 1998 tagged two more of the promotion's teams to its name, both otherwise unattested anywhere else in the surviving record.

The promotion's own handbook, reissued 29 December 1999, narrates the years in between as a rise and a fall: a strong 1998 it calls one of the year's most original AOL-based e-feds, followed by a 1999 “plagued by managment problems, which eventualy lead to its bitter down fall.” The same document announces yet another reopening for the new millennium: “The Year 2000, much like 98, belongs to the EULW.”

No further document survives. Whether that promised Y2K return ever happened isn't in the surviving record.
