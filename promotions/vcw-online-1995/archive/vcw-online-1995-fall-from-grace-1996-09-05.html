Virtual Championship Wrestling — the Cup of the Nine Worthies

"VCW Online #70, Fall From Grace"

5 September 1996
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
From: VCW Online #69, 8/26/96
=====================================================================
[The usual cameras and flares and stuff, including the pan to the box - but
Misty is not there, suddenly we cut to Misty Mountainhop just outside the entry to the lockers on a slightly raised platform.
[MM] Fans, we have a real treat for you tonight! As you know, VCW is hosting the tournament which will determine who will have the honor of winning one of the most prestigious prizes in wrestling today...the Cup of the
Nine Worthies!"
[crowd pop]
And, as you know, VCW has invited top talent from other federations to compete in this grueling match. One of these competitors has just recently won the AWI North American title, and that man is...JACK
FURY!!!!
["Here Comes Trouble" by Bad Company blared on over the speakers. The crowd reacted somewhat enthusiastically given that he is a face with an occasional pop here and there from AWI fans. Fury was wearing his trademark black shades and black leather duster with chains on it. He entered the arena smiling and holding up his North American title belt.}
Welcome to VCW, Mr. Fury.
[Jack Fury] I'll have to say one thing right off the top, you people sure know how to make a guest feel welcome!
[crowd pop]
[MM] Let's get some background info on you first...where has your wrestling
career been up until now?"
[JF] Well, Misty, I started out in the UWA as the North American Champion,
but when we merged with the AWE to become the AWI, I lost the unification match against "War Machine" Greg Gardner. I struggled for a while trying to regain the title, but it took two more matches against the War Machine to finally beat him. Then there came Gary
White, the self-professed Olympian. He's been trashing me over there for over a year now, but look what I have now to show for it!" (holds up belt as crowd pops)
[MM] Let's look at some footage here...here is you knocking out the Olympian
in a submission match where the loser had to hang a gold medal around the winner's neck.
{Footage of the North American title match at AWI The Long Hot Summer}
[JF] Let me just interrupt by saying that I am NOT wearing that medal, as
you can see, because I am keeping it safely where it belongs and not shoving it in peoples' faces! The only title I care about and EVER cared about is my North American title!
[MM] Let's talk about your opponent in the Nine Worthies Tournament, The
Electric Youth. There is some controversy surrounding him now because of his actions at the Night of the Legends in the KWF where he brutally attacked the Next Breed. Have you scouted him out yet?
[JF] No, I have same tapes to review before I have anything to say about his
style in the ring. I know him only by reputation...I've heard he's quick, and he moves like a Mack truck, but I'll just say this. I am a technical wonder, and a Mack truck can't go anywhere with a flat tire.
You don't want to see me angry, and you DEFINITELY don't want to feel the pain of the Jackhammer, one of the few submission maneuvers that ties up both your legs and your arms. I won't pretend to understand you, Electric Youth, but let me leave you with something to think about...there's only been one man to escape the Jackhammer, and I'm wearing his belt. Polish that cup up, because I'm taking it to the
AWI!!!
["Here Comes Trouble" came up again and the crowd popped a little louder than before. Fury shook hands with the fans as he exited, the long way going up the aisle and then down the other one.]
MM: Some strong words from Mr. Jack Fury, who you will see in action
at our next card, Fall From Grace! Tonight, though, we have action with the Texas Rangers and Greater Glory, Silent Thunder and Desire, and a World Title Match between the reformed
General Failure, now calling himself Red "Union" Steele and the Champion, the Purple Haze. Let's go ringside with Vile Vince
Vendetta and Rocky Baroque.
**********
From VCW Preview 70, Part 2 9/5/96
===============================================================
Standing in a sparse stone hallway in what looks to be a temple of sorts, stands a bald Asian man, who despite his advanced age contains the fires of aggression. His bald head bares the scars of ring wars, his scowl does little to hide his contempt. He clutches a kendo stick with strong hands which peek out from beneath his jade-colored robe. Those familiar with the AWI recognize the man as the manager of the Crystal Crow -- the Jade Tiger.
[Jade Tiger]
BAH! Pitiful American swine. Nine Worthies??! Once again you deny the greatest, and most worthy their place by basing a tournament on some Eurocentric ideal! How can anything dealing with combat not mention Sun Tzu. In due time, all of the so-called worthy competitors will fall to a superior power.
What is this? Warfare?!? the mere name bespeaks a sheltered soul who must hide behind a fearsome name. If you want war, Mr. Warfare then welcome to my temple. Here you will meet your master, trained by my hand, hardened by years of denying life's pleasures which have made those like you soft. People say you once dominated VCW as their champion, HA!
a second rate federation, second rate champion."
As the camera pulls back, another Asian man dressed in white sits quietly, deep in meditative thought, a shock of bleached white hair on his head.
[Jade Tiger]
"People of the world, Summit, and VCW.....gaze upon the worthiest of them all, my protege......the Crystal Crow."
(fade out)
>From VCW Online #70, "Fall From Grace"
=======================================
RB: Thanks Misty.
Folks, some of the best that SWA wrestling has to offer are entered in this next match. As you may have heard, some of our scheduled competitors were forced to drop out due to contractual obligations or injury, which means that Bodybreaker Hal Johnston will not be facing Powerhouse Pruitt, and the 600lb Gorilla will not be facing
Lord Ferral. Instead, yep, you guessed it! The Gorilla and
Bodybreaker will face off instead! We have six matches instead of eight, but the excitement hasn't dropped off at all!
Ring 1 Here we go, in ring number 1 [SFX of bones snapping], at 6'7" and 320 lbs, from Kitchener, Ontario, Canada, representing the USeW,
Bodybreaker Hal Johnston, accompanied by Jo-anne McLoud. [Hal is wearing a nice suit jacket and dark shades, his head is shaven clean. McLoud bears a megaphone and berates the crowd as she comes.]
His opponent, the personification of Gorillamania, here on a Legends ticket, at 7'4" and 597 lbs, the Wrestler Formerly Known As The
600 Pound Gorilla! [Cue "Welcome to the Jungle" and massive cheers.
The gorilla wears a full black leotard with black and yellow starbursts on the legs and chest spelling out "Gorillamania". Clyde the chimp rides to the ring on his back.
Ring 2 In ring number 2, ["Debbie Gibson Is Pregnant With My Two-Headed Love
Child", and a variety of responses, boos from the mutants, cheers from a lot of the marks] representing the EWF, at 6'2" and 272 pounds, from the Next Generation, The Electric Youth!
His opponent [cue "Here Comes Trouble"], The AWI North American
Champion, Jack Fury! [Cheers and yells.]
Ring 4 In ring number 4, at 6'5" and 265 lbs ["Purple Haze"], from right here in VCW, our World Champion, The Purple Haze! [In his purple tights and mask, as usual. Also as usual, he gets quite a nice crowd reaction.]
His opponent, here on a Legends ticket, and fresh off an impressive victory at the Night of the Legends [cue "It's Showtime"], from
Reno, Nevada, at 6'3" and 232 pounds, Roker Showtime! [Roker strides confidently to the ring, making sure to high five the fans and just pump them up. He wears red amateur wrestling tights with gold trim, black wrestling boots, red elbow & knee pads and a red jacket with "It's Showtime!" in gold. Once in the ring, Roker removes his jacket and tests the ropes.
Ring 5 ["Somebody to Love" by the Airplane] From Philadelphia Pee Ay, at 6'8" and 290 pounds, representing the USeW, "Devastator" Derek
Machismo! [The fans are a bit unsure of how to react to the big man who keeps making turns, but on the whole they approve.]
His opponent, one half of the Legendary KO corral [SFX war drums],
Running Eagle Feather! [Again the fans don't know what to make of a man who's been a face and heel so many times. The mutants like both these guys, though.]
Ring 6 From Las Vegas Nevada ["Emotional Rescue"], representing the KWF, at 6'2" and 230 pounds, 'The Strutter' T.R. Parker!
[The Strutter demonstrates how he received the moniker;
A peacock strut unlike any seen before as he makes his way down the aisle, looking at ALL the ladies thrilling some with a wink, and berating the men. He wears tights with leggings, matching boots and a wide array of rainbow colors.]
His opponent ["The Anvil of Crom"] at 7'1" and 400 pounds, here on a Legends ticket, Grim Weibaq! [Weibaq waves to his cheering fans as he comes ringside. For those unfamiliar with him, he has
Onyx-black skin, Red (pupilless) eyes and Extended Canine Teeth.
His long white Mohawk-style haircut hangs halfway down his back. He wears black, full-length tights and black, pull-on boots.]
Ring 8 And in ring number 8, our final pairing! At 6'1" and 228 pounds, from the Celestial Temple of the Orient and representing the AWI, accompanied by the Jade Tiger, The Crystal Crow! ["Flying High Again"]
The Crystal Crow is an Asian male who dresses in solid white martial arts pants, and tabi. He has bleached white hair cut to give the appearance of bird-like features (long and feathered in the back culminating in a beak-like widow's peak in the front.) As he enters the ring, he makes his way to ringside and performs his pre-match ritual of touching each ringpost, "defining the boundaries of his nest."]
His opponent, three time VCW World Champion, from Cincinnati, Ohio, at 6'5" and 260 pounds ["Star Spangled Banner", Hendrix' version], get ready for Warfare! [Warfare wears blue full legged tights with the word "Warfare" in white down the right leg. Also wears Bret Hart-esque
"admiral" jacket in blue with an American flag on the back and red tassles on the shoulders. ]
There they are, 12 of the best facing off for the right to hold the Nine Worthies Cup! The various referees and timekeepers are ready, here we go!
[Just in case you were wondering, this a cut and comment version of the matches, since there's no way all the matches could be shown simultaneously on your TV screen]
DING DING DING
Ring 8 Warfare took the initiative, charging the Crystal Crow and pounding away with openhanded chops and a whip into a leaping clothesline. The
Crystal Crow took the hint about who was the bigger and stronger man and as soon as the opportunity presented itself, he slipped away and began to play tag - staying at range and trying to hit the occasional kick or punch.
Ring 1 A slow start here as nearly 1,000 pounds of wrestler faced off.
If these two weren't the biggest the other had ever faced it had to be close, so they were moving slowly - a few test moves and a slow face off seemed to be the order of the day.
Ring 4 Showtime started out cautiously, his ribs seemed to have healed mostly, but he'd been working them hard and wasn't taking any chances.
After a quick lock up, Showtime took the Haze down with a waist-lock takedown and worked an armbar for maybe thirty seconds. A release and a bounce to the ropes to come off with a spin kick that drove the Haze into a corner. Once there he started laying in some chops and forearms smashes, one too many though, as the Haze blocked it and fired back with a couple of his own! In a moment the Haze had worked free, a whip sent Roker across the ring into the buckle where he landed hard, and then caught a flying corner crush from the Haze.
RB: Wow, did you see that!
VVV: Cheap shot, right into the ribs. I like it. When you see a weakness,
work it over!
Intentional or not, that move really did seem to put a hurting on
Roker, so much so that he slipped outside for a moment.
Ring 2 Jack Fury and the Electric Youth were also starting out slow, testing each other's defenses trading a few holds and counters.
Back and forth in a display of mat wrestling that had the mutants on that side fairly impressed.
Ring 5 Running Eagle Feather decided to end it early and went for a gorilla press right out of the lock-up. He got it, and the follow-up backbreaker, but Machismo reversed the Indian Pain Dance attempt into an over the shoulder stungun and bought himself some breathing room.
Ring 6 The Strutter was going to take no chances with the grinning giant
Grim Weibaq, and the pair danced about. Grim tried to close several times only to have Parker slip away at the last moment, sometimes landing a blow or a punch that seemed to have no effect.
The frustration mounted on both sides. Finally Parker could take no more, he dipped into his tights and came out with something clenched in his fist (get your minds out of the gutters). The next shuck and jive put Grim just in range and Parker laid in a vicious chop with the object and a followup low blow. The timing was perfect, the ref never saw a thing. Parker slipped over quickly and dumped the object (a steel bar, apparently). That meant that he didn't notice
Grim no-selling the blows. Grim walked up behind him and grabbed.
With a frightening smile, he scaled the ropes - time for Grim's Reaper. Parker, inverted and unhappy, did what he could.
This involved grabbing the ropes flipping his feet up and around Grim's neck and yanking as hard as he could. Have you ever seen a 400lb man take a frankensteiner when he's on the top rope? Owch. Grim landed hard, but slowly began to get to his feet. Parker scaled the ropes himself and leapt off for a vicious bulldog just as Grim got on one knee. He flipped on top, hooked a leg and threw both legs on the ropes.
RB: Oh my gosh, I don't believe it! T.R. Parker has pinned Grim
Weibaq!
VVV: Yep, in 3:53. That means that he's the first man into the center ring.
Ring 6 Sure enough, as soon as the pin was counted, Parker jumped out of the ring and raced towards the center ring (ring number 9). Grim got to his feet bare seconds later and looked a little confused and more than a little chagrined.
RB: That's got to be one of the biggest upsets we're likely to see
tonight.
VVV: I don't know if it's that much of an upset, the big guy got cocky,
and Parker put him down. Parker's slick ...
RB: I think he's just lucky.
Ring 5 REF and Machismo were back on their feet and trading punches, although
REF seemed a little shaken by the fact that his initial assault had
failed, and Derek seemed to have a slight edge. He solidified that
edge with a series of clubbing forearms, a whip into the corner with a followup clothesline and The Ultimate Sin, his clawhold.
Ring 2 Fury had managed to get behind the Youth and score a quickly belly-to- back suplex. Sleeperhold, locked in tight. Settle in for a rest hold in ring 2.
Ring 4 Showtime was definitely nursing those ribs now. The Haze wasn't going out of his way to punish the ribs, but he wasn't avoiding them either. Things were kind of slow here, mostly the Haze trying to close with Showtime who would avoid it, or hit a quick takedown to buy some more time.
Ring 1 Hal Johnston was taking the fight to the gorilla, boots and punches set up a truly impressive snap-suplex. Impressive in that he actually got the gorilla off the ground; admittedly it looked more like a forced somersault than a real suplex, but still that's damn near
600 lbs going over.
RB: It looks like Johnston might have strained something there.
VVV: Wouldn't you, my God! That was amazing.
Johnston was slowed up enough after that move that the gorilla managed to get to his feet first. He pummeled Johnston back down and then hit a GorillaSplash. THUD. Johnston fought free before the Gorilla could press his advantage, but the gorilla refused to let him go too far and caught him in a Gorilla hug.
Ring 8 Both men must have been thinking long term, because they were both playing conservatively and avoiding the other's strengths.
Warfare kept trying to close so that he could bring his power into play, and the Crystal Crow kept slipping away and trying to land a punch or a kick as he did so. The pace was beginning to tell, though, and both men were starting to slow down a little.
Ring 4 Showtime decided that he couldn't afford to play this game much longer - time was against him. The next time, the Haze tried to close,
Roker let him, then stepped inside. He went for a bridged spinebuster slam. He scored it, but couldn't keep the bridge, collapsing under the weight as his ribs gave out. The Haze kicked free and just went nuts.
Kicks, stomps and a high suplex took whatever wind remained out of
Roker's sails, and Showtime slipped outside for a rest. The Haze followed and chased Showtime around the ring for a bit. Showtime slipped back inside, the Haze followed and Showtime met him at the ropes with a series of European uppercuts. The Haze fought back with forearms smashes of his own. Eventually Roker got the upperhand and hooked the Haze's head under his arm.
RB: I can't believe it! He's going to suplex the Purple Haze back into
the ring!
Indeed he was. And did. Almost. He got the Haze up, and then crumpled. The Haze landed on top in sort of an involuntary splash.
The Haze had enough presence of mind to roll onto Showtime's shoulders and the ref counted the pin. As the Haze shook his head to clear it and started to walk towards the center ring, where the Strutter awaited him with gyrating hips, a team of paramedics went ringside to check on Showtime's ribs.
VVV: Another shocking upset here at 10:34 as the Purple Haze defeats
Roker Showtime!
RB: Well, the Haze is VCW world champion so it's not all that shocking...
but I have to admit that Showtime's recurring injury played a part in the match.
Ring 2 Meanwhile, the Electric Youth after suffering for a good long while in the sleeperhold of Jack Fury, eventually rallied and elbowed his way out. He was obviously pretty groggy though and when we returned,
Jack Fury was in the process of dropping him to the mat.
RB: There's Fury going for the Jackhammer - step-over toehold, double
hammer-lock, one arm and two...he's got it!
The Youth fought it for as long as he could, but in combination with the sleeper it proved too much. He submitted at 11:33
Ring 9 The Strutter, TR Parker had had an eight minute or so rest while the Purple Haze had been battling Roker Showtime, so it was not really surprising that he was able to keep away from the Haze, hitting a quick dropkick before bounding away. Even when
Jack Fury joined the Haze a minute later, they were hard-pressed to keep up with the well-rested Strutter. Parker, on the other hand, knew that if they did grab him there would be problems, and so was taking extra care to stay out of a clinch with either of the bigger men.
Ring 5 This one had spilled to the outside and REF was busy cleaning Machismo's clock with a conveniently located steel chair. He dropped it at the referee's warning and delivered a piledriver instead before rolling
Machismo back inside and following. Once there, he lifted Machismo up into a torture rack (although it took him two tries, these guys were both a little worse for the wear.)
Ring 8 Warfare had managed to corner the Crystal Crow and was going to town.
Chops and punches were driving the Crow against the turnbuckle until
Warfare gorilla pressed him out. Cover. No count, though, as the
Crow kicked out with authority. Warfare kept the pressure on, and went for an abdominal stretch.
Ring 1 Bodybreaker was just in the process of ringing the Gorilla's chimes by linking his hands behind the Gorilla's head and swinging them back and forth. He looked like he might have been doing this a while now.
Abruptly the Gorilla dropped the Gorilla hug, and pistoned his legs driving Johnston back into the corner. With a mighty Gorilla yell, he backed out and charged in for a GorillaSplash. Johnston leapfrogged him.
RB: Good lord, the ring shifted about an inch and a half there!
The Gorilla took one step out of the ring and dropped to one knee.
Johnston was there. He turned the Gorilla around, snapmared him and covered. 1 count. Johnston got up, helped the Gorilla up, and hit a neckbreaker. Cover. 1 count. Johnston left the gorilla where he lay, and climbed the ropes.
VVV: Wait, I've got the card here....yes, this ought to be "Cement Boots."
It was. Bang. This time the Gorilla did not kick out.
RB: And at 14:03, Bodybreaker Hal Johnston goes to the center ring.
Ring 9 Speaking of which, Parker was still playing tag with the Haze and
Jack Fury. Those latter two almost seemed to be working as a tag team, one of them harassing Parker while the other rested up.
Out of nowhere, Parker suddenly hit the Haze with a hard flying cross-body block. The referee got to two before Jack Fury broke the pin by kicking Parker in the back of the head. Before
Fury could do anything else, though, Bodybreaker Hal Johnston had entered the ring and spun him around. As a reward for this interference, Fury drove the open palm of his hand up into the big man's chin.
RB: Ouch. I think I heard those teeth clap together from here.
One leg trip and Johnston was down. This distraction had, however, allowed the Strutter to do two things: kick the Purple Haze in the head a couple of times and mount the buckle. Off he came in the
Struttersault - a top rope somersault splash. Just like that, the
Purple Haze was out of there at 15:44.
RB: So VCW's hopes to keep the cup ride on Warfare who must defeat
the Crystal Crow and then go the distance in the center ring.
Ring 8 The chances of the first part of that looked pretty good. The
Crow had managed to work his way to the ropes to break the abdominal stretch, but in exchange took a powerslam and a cover for 1. Warfare rolled to his feet and hit a legdrop - missed as the Crow rolled all the way out of the ring to consult with the Jade Tiger.
Ring 5 REF and Machismo were beating each silly but both men have an enormous capacity for pain and punishment. We returned just as
REF hit a nasty DDT on Machismo and covered. 1 count. REF slowly climbed the ropes, too slowly in fact. Machismo met him at the buckle and threw him off and over just like REF was Ric
Flair. Cover. Kickout at 1. Machismo just pushed REF back down and covered again. Another 1.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Commercials... commercials... commercials...
Ring 9 T.R. Parker and Jack Fury had formed an impromptu alliance against
Bodybreaker Hal Johnston, perhaps on the theory that he weighed almost as the two of them combined (I know, I'm exaggerating). Be that as it may, their combined assault was pretty impressive even if more less by accident. Fury had Johnston in a spinning toe hold and Parker dropped a knee on him from the top. That was good for a one. The Strutter looked around, the positioning seemed right.
Another Struttersault and Hal Johnston was history. As soon as
Johnston was eliminated, Fury grabbed Parker and pulled him over in a schoolboy, Parker kept rolling and ended on top. No count as Fury simply tossed the Strutter off. The Strutter tried to escape but Fury pinned him against the ropes and started to fire off forearms and such.
Ring 5 Machismo was on a roll, Powerdriver and a 3 at 19:51
Ring 8 The Crystal Crow hopped back to the apron to reenter the ring, and as Warfare charged him, he slingshotted himself over the ropes for a sunset flip. The momentum was such that Warfare landed HARD on the back of his head. No count, though, as the Crow was bounced loose by the force of the impact.
Warfare was staggered though, and easy prey for the Crow's
Tiger's Pounce scissors piledriver. At 20:24, the Crystal Crow advanced to the center ring.
RB: So we're down to the last few! Jack Fury, T.R. Parker, now Derek
Machismo and The Crystal Crow.
VVV: Machismo and the Crow have been put through the wringer, though,
my money is on Parker or even, though I hate to say it, Jack Fury.
Ring 9 Machismo arrived in the ring first, naturally, and promptly charged both Fury and Parker where they were pinned against the ropes - big clothesline and all three men went out and down to the concrete floor since Parker grabbed Machismo as he went over. Parker landed sort of on top of Machismo and avoided most of the impact.
The Crystal Crow arrived at this point and charged right at Jack Fury, who looked like he was stunned. Overconfidence took it's toll as it often does. The Crow got up to Fury just in time to catch a shoulder in the gut. From there a modified back drop sent the Crow up and down, right onto the Parker and Machismo dogpile - only Parker wasn't there.
He saw it coming and rolled out of the way. The Crow and Machismo looked like they were in bad shape, after all both of them had just come off of grueling 20 minute matches. Fury slipped back into the ring and Parker joined him, pausing only to deliver a suspiciously low stomp to the "thigh" to each of the two downed men.
Of course, that meant that he was later in the ring than Fury who just yanked him over the ropes and made the cover. No count, but it did catch Parker by surprise. As Fury started to scale the buckle, the referee finished counting out Machismo and the Crystal Crow.
As Parker rose, Fury came off with a double ax handle that drove
Parker back down. He covered again, no count as Parker kicked a leg onto the rope. Fury went behind for a sleeper and was surprised when Parker ducked out and went behind for a sleeper of his own - or at least he claimed it was a sleeper when the ref broke the hold as a choke. Fury used the interruption to turn around and start punching, Parker backed away blocking most of the incoming blows and firing back with knife edges of his own. A Strutter kick was caught by Fury, and then turned into an enziguri by Parker. He got behind Fury as he rose and locked on an abdominal stretch. After a moment or two, Fury began to force Parker back into the ropes, Parker shifted grips quickly to a full nelson. Fury backed him closer to the ropes.
Parker began to climb them while maintaining his grip. When he could hold the full-nelson no longer, he let go, bounced up off the ropes and hit a beautiful bulldog!
VVV: Wow! Brewster Cobgurn would be proud. That was nicely executed.
RB: Even I have to admit that that was pulled off well.
Parker covered, but only got a 1. He turned to complain and was yanked over for a quick roll up. No count. He kicked loose and the two men rolled to their feet. They immediately locked up, collar and elbow. Fury shifted to a side-headlock, turned into a takedown, hooked a leg and got a two count before the Strutter kicked out. As Fury rose, Parker kicked his leg out from under him, and made a cover of his own, no count.
RB: Fans, this Nine Worthies Match has just gone past the half hour mark!
Another collar and elbow (Parker seemed to be tiring and didn't have the energy to keep dodging), shift to a side-headlock by
Fury, push off into the ropes by Parker, who dropped to the mat as Fury leapt over him, as Fury came back and dropped an elbow
Parker rolled out of the ring for a breather and a quick strut for the fans.
He came back in with a burst of energy by hopping to the apron, vaulting to the buckle and delivering an Air Pillman clothesline to a flat-footed Jack Fury. Back to the abdominal stretch, but as
Fury started to move him back, Parker dropped it in favor of a quick small package style roll up. He got a 1. They both got to their feet, kind of slowly, and locked up. Fury broke the collar and elbow and sent The Strutter into the ropes, then bent over for a backdrop.
Parker came off ... and went over for a Sunset Flip. Off balance, Fury went down, 1,2, and 3!
VVV: Yes! T.R. Parker wins the Nine Worthies Cup at 34:44!
RB: Comissioner Big Bill Shakespeare is entering the ring with the Cup
and presenting it to the Strutter who, it must be admitted, wrestled a heck of an event. In addition to the Cup itself and the cash prize,
Parker is also ensured an invitation to next years Nine Worthies.
VVV: He's got the cup and is strutting his way back to the lockers. Whoops,
there go the fireworks.
RB: Right, those fireworks mean that we're out of time! Thanks for tuning
in folks, and we'll see you next week Online!
