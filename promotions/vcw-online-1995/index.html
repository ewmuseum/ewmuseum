Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Virtual Championship Wrestling ran as a long-running numbered newsletter, VCW Online, its earliest surviving issue (#8) dated 24 April 1995. That issue introduces Blind Melon Chitlin, a blind heel wrestler managed by Marcelle Keller, mocking a Greek-gimmicked rival with a homemade “chariot” built from a dumpster and a pair of crutches — “they symbolize what you're gonna' need to walk with after I beat the crap out of you.”

By 1996 Blind Melon Chitlin had become the promotion's own defining monster heel, holding the World Heavyweight, Intercontinental, and Six-Man Tag titles all at once; the Light Heavyweight Championship passed through Johnny Dominant, ranked #14 on the newsgroup's own Top 75 wrestlers of 1995.

On 5 September 1996 the promotion staged the Cup of the Nine Worthies, an interfederation gauntlet across multiple rings inviting outside champions to compete alongside its own roster — among them the reigning AWI North American Champion, Jack Fury. World Champion Purple Haze and three-time former champion Warfare both represented the promotion in the field.

Purple Haze fell to T.R. Parker's top-rope Struttersault at 15:44; Warfare, after battling the Crystal Crow to a standstill, was finished by a Tiger's Pounce scissors piledriver at 20:24. The tournament came down to Parker and Fury, and Parker won the Cup with a sunset flip at 34:44, presented in the ring by the promotion's own on-screen Commissioner, Big Bill Shakespeare.

A third party tracked the promotion running continuously into at least December 1997, and its own operator, in a 1997 interview, put its own age at “two and half years give or take.” No closure document survives; its ultimate fate is unknown.
