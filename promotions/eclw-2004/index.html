A federation opened by two self-described roleplay veterans, running a weekly televised show and a bi-monthly pay-per-view calendar behind an all-original, no-real-wrestlers roster policy. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Elite Championship League Wrestling announced itself 27 September 2004 as the work of two handlers with a combined twelve years in e-wrestling, promising to open its doors 1 November on the strength of on-time cards and, in its own words, “a fed that will work it's ass off to put the cards out not only ontime, but keep them fun and unbiased.”

It planned two weekly shows — a Friday-night flagship, Ring Rage, carrying six live matches and open promo segments, and a Monday-night preview show, Slander, airing on the FX Network with matches told in summary rather than full detail — built around three championships: Heavyweight (230 pounds and up), Lightweight (under 230), and Tag Team, open to any weight class.

Its bi-monthly pay-per-view calendar named a different show for each date: January's Mind Games, which changed its own gimmick every year; an ungimmicked March card, Stormbound; a 25-man battle royal each May, Master Of The Mat, styled after the WWE's Royal Rumble; another standard card, Animosity, in July; a September Lethal-Lottery-style pairing show, Deadly Pairings; and a cage-match main event closing the year, Capital Chaos, staged, as its name promised, in the nation's capital.

A follow-up post (21 October 2004) reminded readers the promotion would open its doors as planned on 1 November, pointing prospective wrestlers to its own Yahoo Group.

Whether Elite Championship League Wrestling ever ran that first Ring Rage isn't in its surviving record, which ends with this reminder.
