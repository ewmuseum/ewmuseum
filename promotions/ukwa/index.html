A Southampton-based British promotion that ran a Champions League tournament toward its first pay-per-view before an inter-promotional defeat closed it and a fan campaign pushed for its return. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The United Kingdom Wrestling Alliance opened with a teaser post on 5 March 2004 and its first full broadcast, UpRising, a month later from an 8,000-seat arena in Southampton. Staged owner “The Major” Richard Kendall opened the show flanked by his son and daughter, addressing the eight wrestlers entering the first round of a Champions League tournament built to crown the promotion's first UKWA Heavyweight Champion: “Tonight we begin again. Tonight we write a new chapter in British Wrestling's history books.”

That first card turned on a backstage attack: Marcie du Toit, competing as Veronique, was found beaten before her scheduled tournament match, and General Manager Morgan Payne threw Joe Kame'eleihiwa in against her opponent, Ed Sharples, in her place. Kame'eleihiwa won, and Payne ruled that under Champions League rules the win carried Kame'eleihiwa into the tournament outright, eliminating Veronique from her own bracket. Elsewhere on the card, a chaotic, candyfloss-eating Cicatriz was disqualified against former champion Tim Worthington for a chair shot, only for a clause in her own contract to force the match to continue anyway.

The federation ran its first pay-per-view, CyberSlam VI, on 30 May 2004, headlined by the first defense of the UKWA World Title and an open “One Night Only” match that any wrestler, contracted to the UKWA or not, could enter for a shot at the belt.

UKWA closed after losing an inter-promotional event called Independence to the Millennium Wrestling Alliance on 4 July 2004: the federation's own later account states plainly that the promotion passed into the control of a company called BrandCorp, whose own head, Barrett Brand, released the roster UKWA had built. It stayed closed for more than six months.

By January 2005 an Unofficial UKWA Supporters Club was petitioning for the promotion's return, citing interest from e-Wrestling's own top-ranked star, Tommy Danger, and rumors that former president Richard Kendall had gained enough standing inside the MWA to force a revival himself. On 6 February 2005 UKWA announced it was again accepting applications toward a relaunch “in a few weeks.” No further document survives. Whether that relaunch took place isn't in the surviving record.
