Sabotage 41 31 Mar 2002 The Compaq Center, San Jose, California Doctor Healey expands his office and lays out the master plan Primetime Championship Wrestling From The Compaq Center in San Jose, CaliforniaMarch 31st, 2002 The ‘Earlier’ logo appears at the bottom of the screen as PCW President Duke Williams stalks down the darkened hallways of PCW Corporate Offices, unusually busy this night. He casually looks into various doorways, and is struck by just how few employees are here. Each office he passes is ghostlike in appearance, empty cubicles and unused copy machines. Duke rushes down the hall, crunching a paper in his hand as he heads towards the corner of the building on the top floor. The unusual smell of sawdust and plaster greets him as he looks at the plaque on the door: Doctor Rupert Healey. Grabbing the knob, he practically kicks in the door… Hundreds of workers are busy knocking out walls and completely renovating the place! Constructions men with sledgehammers take huge swings into the walls. Redecorators fret and posture, trying to figure out the best scheme for the newly enlarged space. A bevy of ‘secretaries’ tend to menial tasks. And standing in front of a huge window overlooking the city, Doc Healey faces the glass, a drink in hand. Duke growls angrily and pushes his way through the crowd towards the venerable Doctor. He gets but a few steps away before stopped by the voice of Healey… Doc: “Look at it, Dom. Look how beautiful it can be at night.” Duke: “Listen hoss, it’s DUKE!! Get it right!” The Doctor whirls, grinning at Williams. He steps forward and drapes his non-alcohol bearing arm across Duke’s shoulders. Doc: “But of course it is! Drake, things are going just swimmingly! Phase two of my plan is going even better than I could have possibly hoped! So good, in fact, I felt it necessary to make my office more accommodating to my stature here in PCW!” Duke: (shaking the arm off) “I don’t remember giving you the go ahead on that.” Doc: “Details, details. It’s not like the offices next door were being USED anymore…” Duke: “You fired Legal too?” Doc: “Indeed I have! Brought in my own people, of course. They work cheaper and more… morally ambiguously. In fact, I have been a very, very busy boy!” Duke: “I heard… not too many offices have been spared your creative methods. But what I need to ask is… when are you going to actually do anything other than fire the PCW workers?” Doc: “Dirk! I’m hurt! This is all part of the BIG DEAL PLAN. I needed to bring in people who were on the same page and more efficient to the new direction PCW shall be heading. I hoped you would have the vision to see that, but cest la vie. I’ll take you by the hand and be your guide if need be.” Duke: (snarling) “Well then, ya slimy bastard, explain how there are nearly 200 people picketing the front doors and the Labor Department is considering taking action against YOUR firing policies!!” Doc: “Pfft! The fine levied by those Labor jackals will be significantly lowered than what we’ll save by having MY people in here. And that is assuming my boys in Legal don’t scare them off. As far as the people out front go, I have a few boys I trust who work in my… (ahem!) ‘insurance’ business that’ll prove quite effective strike breakers. In fact, if my Bulova is correct, they should be arriving right about… now…” Doc joyfully motions Duke to the window and they both look down the building. Twenty stories down, a pick-up truck full of toughs drives up on the curb, nearly taking out a row of picketers. Eight huge men pile out the back wielding Louisville Sluggers, beating all who aren’t fast enough to get away. Healey cackles with malicious glee, as a stunned Duke looks on. Doc: “Ah, those goons are gods among men…” Duke: (collecting himself) “Well hoss, the shareholders don’t seem to be buying your plans! Stock is down almost 20%!” Doc: “Relax, Domino! We’ll just raise ticket prices while adding promotions night.” Duke: “Promotions?” Doc: (grand gesture) “Of course! You know like bat night in baseball, puck night in hockey, tunnel of chili night… whatever to bring in the crowds! We’ll raise prices and the goofs won’t even care when we offer, I don’t know, beanie babies of PCW wrestlers or something, and they’ll shell out an extra five or ten spot without thinking!” Duke: “Tunnel of chili night?” Doc: “And the beauty is, we get SPONSORS to get involved, thus having them pay for the promotion to get their name out into public view! We pay NOTHING and reap untold PROFIT!!” The paper is thrust into Doc Healey’s face, who takes a pull at his bourbon. Doc: “What about it? Seems pretty self-explanatory, Derrick.” Duke: “How can we fire THIS many wrestlers? Look at these names! Hell, this is roughly 40 percent of our roster, with the big names people love!!” Doc rolls his eyes as if dealing with a child. He turns his back and goes over to a newly placed wet bar, and has his bartender freshen his drink. He takes a long drink before turning back to Duke. Doc: “It’s really quite simply a matter of supply and demand. People DEMAND wrestling entertainment, but are willing to only pay so much for it. We SUPPLY that entertainment, but quite frankly, the DEMAND for what we produce isn’t quite there. We need to cut payroll, and while ‘streamlining’ the offices works to a point, it’s important to cut the fat at the REAL cash cows.” Duke: “But…” Doc: (interrupting) “What you have failed to recognize, Darrel, is not just WHO these wrestlers are, but WHAT they are. Do you see what I mean?” Williams gets a look of confusion across his features before brining the paper back to his person and reading carefully. Soon, a knowing look crosses his face. Doc: (wry) “So, I see you have noticed a pattern. Good for you. As you can see, these wrestlers shall make the perfect pasties for our cost-cutting – we can appeal to their ‘loyalty’ and their sense of ‘duty.’ Yech. Just saying those words makes me feel… dirty. Then we contact their agents and re-negotiate the contracts at a FAR more reasonable rate… for us.” Duke: “But the stock…” Doc: “I’ll tell you what. With the GENEROUS stock options included with my contract, check the fine print, I will have my personal companies invest in PCW stock in order to artificially inflate the stock price. That way everybody will win – your stock won’t go down as much, and I’ll have a true stake in the PCW, thus making me MORE involved. We can’t fail if I stand to lose money!” Duke: (grumbling) “I guess I don’t have much of a choice.” Doc: (rubbing hands in anticipation) “You worry too much, Dan! Now, let’s hurry.
