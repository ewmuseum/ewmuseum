Ethan Richards Biography Real Name William Nathaniel Richardson Nicknames The Dream Weaver, the Devil's Pawn, Monster X Hometown Rancho Santa Fe, California Birthday May 25th, 1978 Height 6' 4' Weight 224 lbs Biography William Nathaniel Richardson was quite literally born into a lap of luxury on May 25th, 1978. The son of a corporate tycoon and the illegitimate clone of Paris Hilton, Ethan grew up without ever having to experience a hard day’s work. He was tutored at home by some of California’s most influential educators, and went to school in Silicon Valley’s most private, elite facilities. Despite his respectable grades in High School, his most cherished accomplishments during his years in school would fall under his athletic resume. A varsity letterman in three different sports, Richards would soon find his penchant on the football field, leading Hillsborough Senior Academy to it’s first California State AA Championship in 1994. As the starting quarterback, he would lead the team to another AA Championship in 1995, and would finish his career there as the All-Time Career Passing Leader in Hillsborough County with a little over 2,100 yards. Richards also excelled at baseball, where he made the McDonald’s All American High School team in 1995 and 1996, respectively. He lead all other players in Division AA High School baseball with 21 Home Runs, a .352 Average, and 63 RBIs. He would earn a full scholarship to Cal State Fullerton for his accolades on the baseball field. But, for all his accomplishments on the gridiron and the diamond, Richards would find his greatest success on the wrestling mats. At a tall 6’ 4”, Richards managed a muscular physique for his stature, and every bit of his strength went into leading his school to four consecutive California State AA Championships. Richards himself went on to win four Individual State Titles during that stretch, and was once again named to the McDonald’s All American High School team, this time as a wrestler in 1994, 1995, and 1996. When he graduated from Hillsborough Senior Academy in May of 1996, Ethan found himself mentioned inside Sports Illustrated as one of America’s most promising young athletes. But his vices outside of school would ultimately jeopardize his promising career before it began. Richards, who was blessed with movie star good looks, flowing blond hair and a crisp physique, was one of the most popular men in school, and on the beaches of California. Ethan’s female conquests are no secret; it’s a well known fact that he slept with no less than twenty-five girls during a two week stretch at one point, and his pension for alcohol and marijuana got him in trouble with the law on more than one occasion. Though he managed to break his addiction to drugs, his dependency on alcohol have continued unaided to this day. Despite having a falling out with his parents shortly before leaving for Cal State Fullerton, Richards managed to clean himself up enough to impress upon his arrival. Ethan quickly gave up baseball when he arrived, concentrating on his growing passion for wrestling. Despite the anemic condition of Cal State’s wrestling program, Ethan Richards quickly became one of California’s greatest exports, sending him on an incredible 121 match win streak, the likes of which were unmatched and unheard of since the days of Ken Chertow. He would win the NCAA I-AA Heavyweight Championship in 1997, but would fail to repeat his success in the following years. Despite being recognized as Cal State Fullerton’s greatest wrestler, Richards graduated from the school in 2000 feeling somewhat under whelmed by his experience there. It wasn’t long before Richards found himself in trouble again with drugs, as his Spring Break-worthy escalades with alcohol, drugs and women grew in frequency. It was only on an off-hand encounter with Chris Smith in July of 2000 that Richards made his first contact in the wrestling business. Smith, who had been following Richards since his final days wrestling in Japan, had saw great potential in the young athlete, despite his personal demons. Smith offered to take him to England to train alongside his other prospect, Dom DeMerius, but Richards politely refused. Determined to see the conflict of interests through, Smith remained in California and took the burden of training the undependable Richards himself. For two and a half years, Smith personally trained Ethan along with other legends of the sport, including Mando Guerrero and Rey Mysterio Sr. Even Satoru Sayama, the original Tiger Mask, was brought over from Japan to assist in Richard’s training. Before long, Ethan Richards would shed his drug dependency once and for all, and would quickly become the hottest thing to hit California since refried rice and chalupas. Endearing himself to young women who viewed him as a heartthrob and men who viewed him as a bonafide badass, Richards enjoyed much success working through California, Arizona, Nevada, the Pacific Northwest and Vancouver. In late 2003; however, Smith decided that it was time for Richards to take the final step into superstardom.
