Redemption Song Biography Biography Nickname: The Career Enders (Redemption Song has injured opponents so badly that they ended their careers. Presently Song has ended the careers of 3 tag-teams [Gold Rush, Children of God, Mystery Assailaints] and 1 singles wrestler [Keith Gray]) Watkins Group Breakdown Doc Watkins - singles wrestler, whereabouts presently unknown Jim Surge - ½ of Redemption Song, Watkins Group President Ben Nighthorse Campbell - ½ of Redemption Song Tom Core - lawyer and manager, ex-wrestler, whereabouts presently unknown Misty - beautiful raven-tressed valet Ring Entrance A loud BOOM! is heard as a quarter stick of dynamite explodes and the house lights go down. Misty always appears first from behind the curtain and poses for a second as the Grateful Dead's 'New Speedway Boogie' slinks its way over the house PA. Jim Surge and Ben Nighthorse Campbell then come out onto the platform behind Misty with their arms folded to drink in the boos from the crowd. There is no other fanfare with their entrance (no pyros or laser lights or whatnot) and Misty leads Redemption Song down to the ring and depending on his mood, Surge will sometimes hold open the rope for Misty. BIOGRAPHY Jim Surge Jr. was born the middle son to the respected cattle ranching Surge’s of Arizona & New Mexico. He spent the first 12 years of his life between school and working on his father Jim Sr.’s Steel Bell Ranch just outside of Tombstone, Arizona. Jim Sr. would bring his three sons (Virgil, the oldest & Andrew, the youngest) to Tombstone often to see the recreations & daily performances of the Shootout at the O-Kay Corral which inspired Jim & his brothers in a fantasy of being gun-slinging cowboys! Shortly after his 12th birthday, his mother Brotta told him that his father had been killed during a mugging after having sold some prize cattle for a large sum at an auction in Tuscon. Understandably, Jim was never the same. The Surge family immigrated from present-day Hungary (his last name is properly pronounced "sur-ga" but a ring announcer mispronounced it "surge" like a powersurge and that has stuck as his "ring name") to the Tombstone area right around 1879, during the times of Wyatt Earp & Doc Holliday. In fact, a Tombstone newspaper called the Epitaph invented the term "cowboy," which the men of the Surge family certainly were, and still are, to this day. Jim remained at the Steel Bell until his 20th birthday, at which time Virgil took over solo operation of the entire ranch. Under the brothers, the ranch had rebuilt it’s reputation as one of the "New West’s" best cattle producers. Jim learned the values of hard work & long hours and his being thrust into adulthood at such a young age made him a hard man, both physically & mentally. He especially developed his dislike of "prettyboy" types during these delicate, painful, yet ultimately triumphant years. At the age of 20, Jim made his way to Dallas, Texas to become a professional boxer, a sport he thought was extremely noble & a great way to prove individual prowess. He demonstrated a great ability to box on his ranch where informal "fight nights" would be held in one of the reserve pens or out in a barn late at night when his mother was sleeping. Jim quickly became disillusioned at the high degree of corruption in boxing and called a contact he had met along his travels in the boxing world. His name was Mort Swarmly, a professional wrestling road agent & manager who used an ultra-pro-American gimmick sort of like the second-rate version of boxing’s classless Don King. Mort said he would be Jim’s manager & booked him to matches in a lower Texas league which held its matches in empty warehouse parking lots & high school gyms. After five long years of independents, Jim had built a reputation for himself as a devastating risk-taker who could absorb seemingly inhuman amounts of punishment & still be fresh enough for big crowd-wowing moves at & after the one-hour mark. His seemingly aloof nature towards the fans, which is more an intensity for his business inside of the ring than anything else, has made him the villain that fans enjoy to root against. He was drawing larger & larger crowds until Swarmly signed a 7-year exclusivity contract for him to go to one of the major regional federations, a subsidiary of the one large major national promotion. The regional fed wanted him to adopt a "Gay Pirate" gimmick & when Jim refused in horror, Swarmly dumped him to pursue his American Dream with another young talent. Jim’s contract, however, prohibited him from participating in any other regional federations, so Jim went back to the Steel Bell Ranch for some time, and then resumed concurrent Ultimate Fighting & Independent Hardcore Wrestling careers. He also had sufficient time to brood about Swarmly’s definition of the American Dream & decided that he hated it, hated all people associated with it & would do what he could to wreak his own brand of vigilante justice to those who were "blind." Just as Jim had gone out of contract, his anti-American attitudes would take another swing when his younger brother Andrew, a private in the US Army, was killed by "friendly fire" in the ongoing "world police action" in Afghanistan. Devastated and ready to unleash his newfound well of hatred, Jim made his way to PWA where he dominated his debut match, winning with a front face lock and forcing the foe to retire from wrestling in shame! In his distraught state, Jim fell into the arms of Mandy Watkins, a wrestling manager who signed Jim to a contract to work for her father’s company, Watkins International. Mandy & her father Marty Watkins died recently in a limousine crash on their way to Kennedy Airport in NYC. As it turns out, Mandy had introduced Jim not just to his tag-team partner, but to HIS HALF BROTHER, Ben Nighthorse Campbell, as was revealed at the reading of the Watkins’ will!! Marty Watkins was actually Jim’s Uncle, and at the reading of will reading, Marty left all of Watkins International to charity except for a part called the Watkins Group, which was to be run by Jim & Ben as they see fit to further their wrestling careers (yes, this means Jim has slept with his cousin!). Ben Nighthorse Campbell was floored by the revelation that Jim Surge Sr. was actually his father. His whole life had essentially been built around an underlying hatred of the unfair stereotypes put forth by the white man’s media. And now to find out that his father was only one-half Apache and one-half HUNGARIAN (Ben’s mother is full-blooded Apache) has taken some of the pride out of his strut for the time being, but he keeps close hold of the strength he draws from his Apache heritage as he is still three-quarters Apache. Ben’s family has been in North America for as long as recorded history. Trouble first began for the Apache in 1600 with Spanish colonization & exploration into Mexico. After the Spanish, it was the Mexicans, and after the Mexicans, the white Americans, whose government offered a bounty for Indian scalps right after the Mexican-American War in 1848. The Apache, along with their ally tribe the Navaho, are known for being fearless warriors who were able to put up the fiercest resistance to American ethnic cleansing and relocation to government-funded reservations. As you can see, Ben’s resentment of the white man is in his nature. Ben is a Mescalero Apache and can speak the ancient Athapascan language of the Ghostriders. The Mescalero Apache held the first Ghost Dance and are still the keepers of this great awareness medicine, which many say can help one see the immediate future through meditation techniques. Ghostriders are also well known as being close to the Great Spirit, commonly depicted as a soaring eagle, which is more than appropriate for Ben’s high-flying counter-attack technique where he waits for his foe to commit and either his foresight or great reaction time usually wins him the reversal. Ben became "Nighthorse" on his 16th birthday, when a boy becomes a man within his tribe. Among the Mescalero Apache is a peculiar tradition to get wacked out of your skull on mescaline and then report back to the tribe what animals you see and then they name you. It could be a really funny joke if you see something lame like a penguin or something, but Ben got a cool one. Ben’s immediate history has not been plagued by death and wars, far from it. He has been raised on a State Reservation called Watchung, in central New Jersey, USA. Ben never properly knew his mother or father; he was just considered a child of the reservation. However, he received a normal education and a normal childhood, with such things as TV, video games, etc.. When he was old enough, he wondered why this particular group of Apache had moved (or had been moved) to New Jersey. Desperate to learn more about his heritage, Ben dove deep into his tribe’s history, leaving him always with a faraway look on his face as if he has become privy to great and ancient secrets. More recently, he has been a key speaker for the National Movement for the Enhancement of American Indians. He has proven himself a fine amateur wrestling talent, first coming to notice competing in a long-jump competition at an inter-tribal pow-wow and leaving little doubt in the mind of Mandy Watkins as to who should team with Jim Surge. Now they are together. They still don’t completely get along. They don’t know if blood means enough this time around. But gold does. Redemption Song continue to raise eyebrows by gaining promotion to CHZ after twice taking the PWA's #1 ranking and showing up well in OSW's Most Extreme Team competition. After a month-long streak in Canuck Heat Zone that saw Redemption Song wreak havock and end three more promising careers, Song was able to capture and defend the CHZ tag belts. They were recently rated the #9 tag-team in all of Wrestlingtattler and have been signed to contracts in the granddaddy of all feds, PCW!!
