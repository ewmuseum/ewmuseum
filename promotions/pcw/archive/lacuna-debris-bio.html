Lacuna Debris Biography Real Name Lacuna Firenze Devries Nicknames English Lionheart Hometown Nottingham England, Saint Cecile France or Washington USA Birthday 16/05/82 Height 6ft1 Weight 172 lbs Biography I can see what you’re thinking, she’s just another rich kid who inherited money when her parents died. Her parents dieing is also very cliché, just like every other kid in black in the wrestling world. You could think that, and in a few ways you may be correct, but I wouldn’t recommend saying it to her face unless you want yours removing in a hideously bloody way, possibly involving a raven and a shotgun. See, Lacuna isn’t a happy little girl. Money brings less happiness than you may imagine. Born in Nottingham, England on May 16th 1982, she was dropped into a slightly odd little family. Her mother, Jane Devries, was a volunteer worker in the local church and so, a devout Christian. She tried to drill her morals into Lacuna, but even as a child she knew what she believed and what she didn’t and she disregarded these teachings. Her father, Lorenzo Devries, was an English-Italian lorry driver and so, he was away from the rapidly developing Lacuna for most of her childhood. This upset her and, quite possibly, was a reason towards rejecting the Christian morals thrust upon her by her mother. In the logic of Lacuna, if God listened to the pure and innocent then why were her constant requests for the stable love of her father ignored by this character with an unproven existence? You see; her father was the only person she actually wanted to see more of. Lacuna wasn’t anti-social, she just didn’t like people around her constantly; all the pressure of keeping up appearances would weigh down anyone. But her dad, he was a different story. When he was around, it was like all her Christmases rolled into one huge bundle of happiness. It was fair to say that he spoilt her and it probably would have been different if she had a sibling, but this tattered bond between them was healed in one moment when they held hands. Lacuna was a bright girl and still is. She was flying through primary school with nothing but expectations of huge, great things. Teachers loved her, girls idolised her and boys drooled over her, even if they were only seven years old. And then it happened. One night, one awful night, drenched with rain and the dark black clouds of hell, her parents took a ride in her father’s truck. They hit the roads hard, laughing and joking all the way, or so she imagined, and they had reached Sheffield by the time the roads had become unbearably wet and slippery. The story she told herself went like this: they were happy when they went, the careering Transit van that hit hard into the side of the cab and, eventually, sent them rolling down the embankment was a sign that something about them was wrong. They burned to death. They burnt! Burnt for Gods sake! Where is the humanity in that? If she had any of the Christian morals left inside her, then this was the death of them. In her mind, her parents were legends. Except they were not respected, they were unknown to most of the population. How could they be legends if nobody knew about them? Sure, they got an article in the newspaper, but that wouldn’t get them much but pity, and Lacuna detested pity like some sort of pox. Maybe this was a major change in Lacuna. She had a new fixation, she would get her parents the recognition that they deserved, and she would do it on the biggest stage of them all; the world stage. She was seven years old, and these were big thoughts for such a little girl, yet she became obsessed, her isolation grew as she studied and studied, reaching with an open hand for that light at the end of her winding tunnel. She was living with her grandmother and, to break the awful cliché forming here, was sympathetic and, to be honest, perfect with the growing Lacuna. She didn’t care that the young girl’s social life was dwindling; she put it down to the stress of the situation and continued to do so for a long time to come. Lacuna valued her fitness and it repaid her by developing her into a strong and incredibly agile individual. She was not against a good fight every now and again either, her nature of semi-hatred of all things breathing saw that she got into a few as well. At sixteen, she began to seek the help of those developed fully in the art of wrestling. You see, she was interested in the pure science of ramming some ones arms up behind the head and tying their legs in some sort of knot. It was far more cultured than hitting someone in the face with a big stick or chopping them over the back of the head. It was also bigger, which helped. The money came in an odd way. She had a large amount saved from the sale of he her parents house and belongings (gratefully accepted from her Grandmother) and, after swiftly getting through College, she got herself a nice little job, earning enough money to get through and still save. Here comes the odd bit, she decided she would get money by beating up as many people as she could for bets. Yes, people would pick out some poor unsuspecting sap and Lacuna would go up and kick them as hard as she could muster. It soon mounted up, as Lacuna battered her way through half of the drunks and thugs of Nottingham, lowering the crime rate significantly as she did so. It wasn’t long before she got offered a small a job wrestling in some underground, probably illegal, federation. The betting system continued as she worked her way slowly towards the top, making stupidly large amounts of cash. She fed hopped a little, making even more, until she had the happiest bank manager in England. She bought the biggest house she could find and subsequently made even more money by renting it out to many rich families. Her mind for business was sharp and concise and while receiving more and more money, she bought houses in France and America, renting them out until she had enough money to live the life of a major star without working ever again. Then her Gran died. It turned out that she was a wealthy woman, and Lacuna was the sole beneficent, hence making even more money. She had little else to live for, so she moved to America and applied to many wrestling federations. UWF was the most successful, wielding records and a promotion to stardom, then on to PCW… Some say she’s delusional, others are just scared of her.
