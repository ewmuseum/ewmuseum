Duzza Biography Real Name Malek Williams Nicknames Duzz Hometown Harlem, NY Birthday 13.02.1978 Height 6'0 Weight 238lbs lbs Biography Malek ‘Duzza’ Williams has not lived long, but has fit more into his years than many people will do in a life time. From the projects of Harlem to the mansions of the Hampton’s, this is Behind The Wrestler: Malek ‘Duzza’ Williams [Pictures of a large African American family all huddled together. There are three girls, three boys and two parents. All together smiling, looking happy on a sunny day. A soft female narrative voice starts to speak while the still stays on the screen] The Williams family, early 1980, Duzza and his family together as one. These were the good times, but sadly bad times were not far behind [The picture cuts to Duzza himself sitting down, with a black background behind him. Only the top of Duzza is visible to the camera. Duzza is dressed in a white T-Shirt with ‘TP-2.com’ written across the front in a black font. Duzza is looking straight into the camera. He has a large platinum chain across his neck, the bottom of which cannot be seen. Duzza is a good looking man, with pecan brown skin, reminiscing of R. Kelly’s. He has some small stubble, as if he hasn’t shaved in a few days. He has a silver hoop coming out the left side of his nose. In his ears he is wearing a pair of big diamonds in each ear lobe. His hair is set back in long cornrows] Duzza: “ Y’all wanna know what I been through? How my life has been? Well y’all ‘bout to find out” [Stills of Duzza as a child, with his brothers come across the screen while the narrator speaks again] Duzza came from a loving family. Born from parents Tyrese and Sharmell Williams. Born 13th of February 1977. Tyrese worked hard, every day as a constructor to make money for his large family, while Sharmell took care of the ever expanding family at home [The picture cuts back to Duzza in the studio] Duzza: “ Yeah my earliest memories, where of my home life, surrounded by my familiy. They were probably the happiest times. They were good times. I remember being seven or eight, all being around the piano which moms played, with my two brothers Jamal and Rob, and my three sisters Sharmell, La Toya and Beyonce, all around the piano Moms made home life entertaining and fun, that we wouldn’t have any want to go out onto the streets. Harlem is a dangerous place and there are many things that could ruin a kid, and my mother didn’t want us getting involved in that [bleep]” [Stills of newspaper cut outs reading ‘Man murdered by white supremacists’ flash across the screen] But sadly for the Durham family, the good times were not to last. On the 21st of May, 1988, while coming home from a baseball game with 11 year old Malek, Tyrese Williams ran into a white supremacist gang in the white New York suburb of Port Ewen. After beating Tyrese Williams up, they killed him with baseball bats. He died on the way to the hospital. His killers were never found [The pictures goes back to Duzza] Duzza: “ Seeing your father, some one you look up to, some one you think of as being invincible beating up, murdered right before your eyes was obviously something which caused me great pain. I looked up to my dad, like every kid and on one night, for no real reason he was taken from me. I remember the night like it was yesterday. We were driving home, the sun was setting. We’d been to watch a game, it had been a great father-son outing. We were coming home and for some reason we went through this suburb instead of taking the busy road That was a big mistake I guess, out of nowhere to cars appeared and started ramming us. I stayed in the car, my dad got out, there was about six or seven of these skinheads, tattoos over their arms, they were hurling insults at my father and just struck him” [More stills flash across the screen as Duzza continues to talk.] Duzza: “ I think it was that one incident which caused my friction between me and white people. That started it at least. I felt that these white fools had ruined my life, with no reason for doing it other than the color which my skin is. I had never heard of that happening to a white dude and it angered me. Also the killers were never found. I find I hard to believe that in a big suburb full of people, before it was dark a gang of white guys ramming then killing a man with baseball bats could have gone unseen. I felt someone must have seen it, someone must have known who did it, but still no one was caught. That made me distrust white people, with the affects I still feel to day. If I’m in a room full of white people and I’m the only brother, then I’ll get nervous to the point that my nose will bleed because in my mind I think that someone is gonna start something.” [Pictures of Duzza and his family in Harlem flash over the screen while the narrator again speaks] Duzza fell into his own world, after seeing his fathers murder, he would stop going to school and instead of staying at his house, Duzza would spend most of his time at the basketball courts. At the age of 13, disaster struck again for the Williams family when Rob, the oldest son, passed away of natural causes Duzza: “ Yeah that messed me up real bad, it seemed like life was going good again and then I wake up one morning to find out my bro has passed away in his sleep. That took a long time to get over. After Robs’ death I spent most my time in front of the TV, watching TV. Which made me worse. Every time I switched on the TV, what would I see? Rich, happy, smiling white families, living out in the suburbs, same one I saw my pops murdered in. Man, that made me so angry. Here I was in the ghetto, surrounded by poverty, on welfare, living in the projects, and the only thing on TV I ever saw was white people acting like they’d never had a bad day in there lives. That annoyed me so much. Either nobody cared about what was happening in the hood or no one knew. Both ideas annoyed me. It made me sick to my soul. And I was looking for someone to blame, inevitably that blame landed on white people. I grew a hatred built through jealousy, and it took a long time to get over it” The Williams family managed to go the next few years without any trouble. Duzza graduated out of high school and landed a position in a college. But that was not to last Duzza: “ Man the day I got accepted into college, that was one happy day, I as the first person in my family to get into college. I remember the look in my mom’s eyes, when she saw her son get accepted into college. That day I truly felt I was going somewhere. But I guess it wasn’t meant to last” “Only two weeks into his first semester at college, Duzza was kicked out for assaulting a teacher Duzza: “ You know, it’s not something I’m proud of, but I felt I had to do it. The fool was dissing me from the moment I stepped in, I acted polite to him, did everything he asked, but still he continued to dis’ me. One day I’d just had enough, after he mentioned something about crime rates in black neighborhoods, I just snapped. I just beat him up. Like I said I ain’t proud of it, but what’s done is done I guess.” [Stills from a party are shown.] Duzza returned back home to Harlem, three weeks after he had left [Pictures of Duzza, with his hair in a small afro are on the screen] Duzza: “ Man I felt like such a failure, there I was trying to get a good job out of college, to get my whole family out of Harlem and what do I do? I go and blow it 2 weeks after it started. I wanted the world to just eat me up” After coming back from college, Duzza started hanging with all the wrong crowd and at the age of 20, Duzza had a brush with the law which ended up with doing a sentence 1 year in prison and then a year extra due to continuous fighting Duzza: “ I got a year for GBH and another year because I wouldn’t stop getting into fights with white guys. I was so pissed that I was in there you see, because I hadn’t done shit wrong. I was just in the wrong place at the wrong time. Some guy got beaten up, I stumbled upon him when all the police found him. I had his blood over my hands and stuff, they just guessed I’d beaten him up. So I was so angry I was in the pen, I done nothing wrong and here I was loosing a year of my life. In hindsight I should of just taken the time and gotten out, but that ain’t my style” Duzza got out of prison and almost immediately met his wife Britney [Pictures of Duzza and Britney flash across the screen] Duzza: “ It was a great day when I met her. It was love at first sight, I knew the moment that I had to get out of the hood, I had to do something to make her proud of me. Later that month my younger sister was stabbed while minding her own business one day. When that happened I knew I had to get my family, even some of my close homies out of Harlem, out of the projects, before it was too late” [Pictures of Duzza, cornrows in his hair, chilling with stars like R. Kelly, Jodeci, a young Fresh Prince and a short haired Snoop Doggy Dog move across the screen.] Duzza turned all his energy into music, eventually being recruited by his idol and personal friend R. Kelly and put on his ‘Rockland’ but although he made money, he did not achieve the superstardom he had dreamed of, and soon left Rockland for a different career Duzza: “ Man, R. Kelly was and still is my idol, and to get to chill with him every day, work with him in the studio, for him to sign my checks was a great feeling. I made enough money to get my mom and sisters a small house out of the hood, but music wasn’t really for me” [More pictures of Duzza on stage with a mic in his hand go across the screen.] On a trip to Tokyo, Japan, Duzza met a man called Red Rock Duzza: “ For whatever reason I was in Japan and I was with this guy, who was a wrestler called Matt D. and I was chilling with him, he was the only black dude in Tokyo, in my part anyway, so like we was drawn together and one day he met a guy named Red Rock, who was really the first white guy I could trust y’know It made me change my entire outlook on life, like maybe they ain’t all bad, that kinda thing. And I became a wrestler, me some hood rat became a professional wrestler. Me and Red Rock did well, we was in a fed called NZW, I was the first cruiserweight champion, life was good.” [Stills of Duzza in the ring, with Red Rock, Ricky Flame and The Crows move across the screen] Duzza: “ We tore the house down, every night, we was the A.K.R, unstoppable, not long after that I got the call up to PCW, didn’t last there long, but it was a cool experience, made couple of friends, it was all good” [A still of Duzza in the ring opposite Riptah of Da Westsidaz is shown. In the picture Duzza is flipping Riptah the bird.] Duzza: “ From there, I went to PCW2, which was fun, it was me and Red Rock again, back as A.K.R, but as a tag team now, we ruled the place, had a great fued with Drummer and Ganker from Team Nirvana. Then Rock left and I teamed with a up n coming talent known as D-Kay Calyx.” [A picture of D-Kay and Duzza winning their first match is shown] Duzza: “ D-Kay is a great guy, he deserves all the success he’s had and he’ll go even further. From there I went to RSE, which was a great fed, I had a killer feud with a guy named Jason Kit Lee, one match involved R. Kelly another involved me putting him through a limo window. I became the world champion there, had the time of my life. But all things come to a end. After there I went to EW, had fun there, but due to reasons out of my control I ended up leaving and coming to PWA. Which is where I met DJ Johnson. The second white guy I could really trust. Who introduced me to The Pube… which ended up with me meeting my partner Grey Simmons. But D2 did not work out and I found myself in Rush and then soon after back in the greatest fed on the planet PCW… this time vowing to make a better name for myself than before… Other Feds PCW2, PWA, NZW, RSE, EW, GWF Other Fed Awards 1st NZW Cruiserweight champion, RSE World Champ Quotes Damn Cracker Fool
