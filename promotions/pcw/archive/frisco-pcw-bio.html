Frisco Biography Real Name Francisco Cassius Valerius Nicknames El Presidente, Jefe, El Mero Mero Hometown Buenos Aires, Argentina Birthday May 7th, 1973 Height 5'11 Weight 235 lbs Biography Francisco Cassius Valerius was born on May 7th, 1973 to Monica and Antonio Valerius in Buenos Aires, Argentina. For Francisco, life was an easy ride as he was brought up in wealth from his father's ownership of an architecture empire and his involvement in city government. Young Francisco was raised with English as his first language and was sent off to many private schools throughout the Americas upon his own request. However, his love for knowledge was only superseded by his love of the violent arts. As a teenager, Francisco was gifted with a strong build and natural athleticism. With these qualities, Francisco developed skills in many forms of martial arts, amateur wrestling, gymnastics, and combat survival in which he was privately tutored by men who had a great command in them. After finishing high school, Francisco attended Harvard to earn a degree in Government, and immediately went back to Argentina, where he would serve the next 4 years in the Argentine Marine Corps as an Infantry Officer. While stationed in Japan, Francisco gained an interest in professional wrestling, which quickly gave way to a burning curiosity when he met the son of a great, but little known retired Japanese wrestler named Kazushi Yakamori. Upon completion of his military service, Francisco left to Guatemala to train with the retired great. It did not take long for the skilled youngster to pick up an excellent command of the sport, and within months, a charismatic young cruiserweight named 'Frisco' appeared in the small federations of the Central American Circuit. Frisco dazzled the dozens of villagers he performed in front of and caught the eye of the owner of WFF--a locally televised indy promotion in the United States. Upon arrival, Frisco found success, and success would happen again and again as he stormed through other small federations, gaining many fans and titles to his name. In August of 2000, the sensational South American performer signed with the MWF. Though Frisco's time in the MWF was short, it was highly successful, as he captured the MWF Rising Star Title twice and became the MWF American Champion in a matter of weeks. This proved to be an important level in the career of the Argentinean Cruiserweight, as Millennium Wrestling Federation announced its merger with Primetime Championship Wrestling. With the MWF American Championship Belt slung over his shoulder, Frisco found himself in his new home, PCW. In the first order of business, Frisco and his stablemate Pgod came to the assistance of their friend and fellow stablemate Doug Troy, at PCW Dawning. It was that day, October 22nd, when the whole world finally got a glimpse of greatness. This greatness was seen, but not yet registered, until the mysterious MWF superstar attained his first match. With the merger, an agreement was made that stated every MWF Champion would get a shot at his PCW equivalent's title. Frisco found himself aligned with the popular Evil Supergenius, PCW Continental Champion Alan Kriegman. When the exchange of words between the champions begun, a startling revelation was revealed; Frisco and Kriegman were brothers. The title match was now a family affair of epic proportions, Good Twin versus Evil Twin. And on October 24th, good prevailed, as Frisco defeated his Evil Twin and captured the PCW Continental Championship in his very first match. A shocked silence overtook the sports entertainment world, as a moderately successful minor-leaguer walked into a PCW ring and took the 2nd most coveted prize in professional wrestling from an established superstar. Frisco was now someone to be kept an eye on. And 2 of those eyes belonged to a title-seeking pot smoker named Stewart Hix. Only weeks into Frisco's reign, Stewart Hix managed to get a shot at the PCW Continental Championship. This was of little concern to Frisco, as he proceeded to enjoy the good life and collected endorsement deals with companies such as ABSOLUT VODKA~! When the match came, Frisco faced his challenger with the natural preparation he always had and defeated Stewart Hix, with some help from one of Hix's foes. Unfortunately for Frisco, his association with Stewart Hix was not over yet. Hix was persistent, and would not accept defeat. Hix would end up getting yet another shot at Frisco's Continental Title at the PCW Alliances PPV. Leading up to the PPV, harsh words were exchanged between the champion and challenger, both awaiting what would ultimately lead both men to different paths. When the time finally came, the fists flew just as freely as words, and both men fought with all they had in this grueling match. But in the end Stewart Hix had Frisco locked in his 4:20 and the world watched in shock as Frisco's hand fell once....twice....three times. Stewart Hix had won the Continental Title. However, the story wasn't over yet. Frisco would get a rematch on the upcoming Fusion. Alas, Frisco regaining his title would not happen this time, due to Stewart Hix resorting to the use of the title belt to knock Frisco out. Immediately after the embarrassing second loss, Frisco was far too disgusted with Stewart Hix and his company to continue with his career. That December 1st, he decided to take a break from wrestling and went back to his many other income-accumulating endeavors. As the months went by, however, Frisco started to miss his passion for wrestling. Frisco yearned for the feeling he would get when driving someone's head into the ground, and also the feeling he would get from watching the anger and shock in the eyes of his opponent after a witty insult. So, on the Tuesday Turmoil of January 23rd 2001, Frisco would make his unexpected return, and immediately make his presence known by F-Bombing the World Champion Reverend and reuniting with his old friend, Doug Troy. This return came at the most convenient time, as a few weeks later, a 64-man tournament would soon get underway for the recently vacated PCW World Heavyweight Championship. This tournament would prove to be a beautiful story that nobody but one man could've possibly conceived of. Frisco would meet his first round opponent in Mike Douglas and it took all of 2 and a half minutes for Frisco to put 'The Big Daddy' away. The next round would prove slightly more difficult as he would meet the hungry newcomer, Slade. A brutal war of words would be exchanged between the veteran and rookie, and during Frisco's free time leading up to the second round match, Frisco managed to capture the PCW Extreme Title in a Frat House Brawl. Coming into the second round match with his 2nd singles title draped over his shoulder, Frisco fought off the motivated young man and planted him with his Endorphin Rush to move on to the next round. The popular Putter Mayhem would be up next, but he would also fall Frisco, as would Chet King on the same night. Frisco had awakened the world by advancing to the Final 4 along with Kremmen, Meanstreak, and the Super Duper Masked Jobber King. March Madness finally arrived and it was time to crown a new PCW World Heavyweight Champion. In the first of his round robin matches, Frisco came across a minor setback as he lost to Meanstreak in the first meeting, however he rebounded by defeating both the Super Duper Masked Jobber King and Kremmen to avoid elimination. It was now down to Frisco and The Monster Known as Meanstreak, and with a combination of heart, skill, and the assistance of Macaber Payne, the unstoppable Meanstreak, had fallen and Frisco was the new PCW Champion. A Cinderella Story had unfolded before everyone's eyes, and the magical kingdom of PCW would never be the same again. Frisco went on to successfully defend the title against Miyagawa and Howard Porter and held the title for an astonishing 40 days. A heated Meanstreak at Spring Fling, but a rush of interfering wrestlers prevented a decision. Meanwhile, Frisco's arch-nemesis Stewart Hix had arrived back in PCW to go after the champion's strap. Hix earned the #1 contendership and was set to face Frisco in the coming weeks, however newcomer Mike Extreme attempted an assassination of the champion by trying to drive a sledgehammer into his head, through the roof of his vehicle. Frisco was taken to the hospital and the title was vacated at the mark of Frisco's 40 day reign, which still stands as the longest in the history of the PCW World Title. Frisco was said to have been in a coma, but it was later revealed that this wasn't true and Frisco vacated the title so he could use his foes Meanstreak and Stewart Hix as pawns. The plan didn't work as perfectly as he had hoped, so Frisco returned back to action to get a shot at regaining his championship in a four way cage match between himself, Meanstreak, Stewart Hix, and Tyler Nelson. Despite Frisco F-Bombing his arch-nemesis off the cage as Streak ADD'd Tyler Nelson off the cage also, Tyler Nelson had the luck of hitting the ground first and won the title. After this, Frisco decided to let the CEO enjoy his reign, and set his sights on the titles he overlooked. Frisco rampaged through the 64-man Continental Title Tournament and defeated Jacob Jackson, Kent Anthason, Darkstar, Miyagawa (winning the Cruiserweight Title in the process), and finally Jeremy Howard and Ivan Stanislav at PCW Supremacy. The dual title holder would lose his Cruiserweight Title back to Miyagawa shortly, but would add the Television Championship by defeating Sean Matthews in a best of 3 title vs. title match. As PCW Gold and Glory arrived, Frisco was the target of a title takeover by Tyler Nelson, and saw his titles taken by Badge with assistance of Nelson's other goons. With 6 title reigns, including the top prize in professional wrestling for a record 40 days, the winning of 2 64-man tournaments, 21 victories and 9 losses, with only 4 of those being in singles matches, numerous awards, and the love and respect of millions of fans, Frisco decided to retire from Primetime Championship Wrestling. On June 29th, just before his glorious performance in the Match of Champions, where Frisco finally got even with Stewart Hix with one powerful shot of his finger, Frisco stepped out of the spotlight he so adored, stating that there was nothing more to accomplish. Shortly after his retirement, Frisco joined the Spanish announce team with Boca del Inferno and was inducted into the PCW Hall of Fame. On Friday, September 21st 2001, Frisco returned to attack Dan Hollywood due to the fact that Hollywood was inching closer to becoming the first true PCW Title Sweeper. Frisco had kidded himself when he stated there was nothing left to accomplish, and now his goal was to win the titles that had eluded him one way or another. After defeating the obstacle of Dan Hollywood, Frisco heated up and won the Black Friday Mall Brawl to gain entrance into the main event scene once again. However, Frisco would find out that he had tough company in Ivan Stanislav and Jeremy Howard. The two men that Frisco had defeated months earlier now grew exceedingly difficult to displace and Frisco soon found himself denied of the World Title and severely injured by Stanislav at Decimation. During his short stretch of rehabilitation time, Francisco Valerius announced his bid for the presidency of his troubled Argentina in February, and hinted at taking his campaign through PCW--as his desire to hold every piece of PCW gold still raged on fiercely. His lofty goal of a full title sweep was finally realized on March 15th, 2002 when the team of Frisco and Legend Inc. defeated The Golden Game. Months earlier, Frisco had won the Rising Star Title in a deceptive scheme involving a masked Guatemalan wrestler named El Magnifico Busmuchacho de Mysterio. After turning on Legend Inc. and defeating him at Spring Fling, Frisco went on to join Tyler Nelson's Ultimate Authority which employed the experienced veteran to defeat World Champion, Big Alan Steele. Though days passed and Ultimate Authority dissolved, the militia-commanding presidential candidate was involved in a long, bitter war with the demi-god in a classic battle of good and evil. The story would finally end at the Wrestle Vessel pay-per-view with Frisco defeating Big Alan Steele to become 2-time PCW World Champion and subsequently driving the great champion BAS into retirement. However, Frisco's 2nd reign left much to be desired as he would lose the title to Xavier Kannon on the same night. After epic battles with The Pharaoh that involved the memorable mobilization of Frisco's elite militia to being confronted with the wrath of a revenge-seeking Rent-A-Hero, Francisco Valerius finally decided to step out of the ring for good to focus on gaining the presidency of his beloved Argentina. He announced his retirement on October 11th, 2002 and offered his sympathy to those who would miss his departure, but he had no idea what kind of departure would follow. Frisco's newly appointed running mate, legendary soccer player Diego Maradona, secretly won over Frisco's militia and party officials and successfully overthrew the dictatorial candidate the very night of his retirement. Frisco was beaten by his former soldiers and taken away as prisoner. The ruthless, controversial, but perennially adventerous South American is now in an Argentine prison awaiting execution. But regardless of how much time this master of the mat has left in his life, his legacy will forever remain in the hearts and minds of every one of his critics and fans.
