Terlayne Sterling Biography Real Name [name withheld] Hometown Connemara, Ireland Birthday 20/01/80 Height 6'3" Weight 230lbs lbs Biography Terlayne Sterling was born in Connemara in the West of Ireland in the turn of the 1980’s. Terlayne’s family was a typical Roman Catholic family and was no different to any other family in rural Ireland, or at least Terlayne saw it that way. His father, Jimmy O’Shea worked as a coal miner in the mines of Rosmuc, Co. Galway which was passed down through generations of O’Shea’s until eventually Jimmy held the keys to the kingdom he had been waiting forty years to hold in his possession. Terlayne enjoyed helping his father carry buckets to the mines and his family prospered from the business. Under his fathers dimmed complexion and dirty shirt lay secrets which haunted him to his grave however. One day while carrying buckets to the mines he saw his father’s lifeless body laying in the mines. Upon his outstretched hand was a note written on a small piece of paper. Terlayne, don’t let the girls see me like this. It happened to my father and it’s going to happen to you too. Get out before it’s too late. Terlayne, being unprepared for the collision of emotional trains hid his father’s body under large stacks of coal and solemnly swore to his father that he would never follow in his footsteps. Tears were soon gone and it was time to act. He told his mother that his father had gone to Dublin on business and that he himself was going away for a while to clear his mind. On the third of April 1997 during a drunken confrontation with his brother John, he was accused of being unfaithful to the family and to his forefather’s coal mining industry. Terlayne knew there and then that it was time to leave, that there was no longer any place for him in Ireland or in the coal-mining industry. In May of 1997 he had received word that John, along with the other miners were planning a conspiracy against him and were willing to take the business from his grasp….. At all costs. At midnight on the 16th of May Terry O’Shea died and was replaced by Terlayne Sterling. Things would never be the same again. On the 24th of May he arrived at Kennedy airport. He knew that from America he could gather his Irish allies and plan an attack on the miners who were planning on conspiring against him. Under the leadership of Thomas O’Connell and Roari O’Riordan the gang planned a night time attack on Terlayne’s brother John, his uncle Damien and two miners who were working with the conspirators. The plan was successful though Terlayne’s Uncle Tom escaped and fled Connemara. Terlayne, however, was left feeling unjustified and was far from satisfied with the gang’s killings. He felt no remorse however for their actions and knew that he was safe in America, only his finances were running short. On the 2nd of January Terlayne became involved in a bar room brawl, the brawl however would open new, bright prospects for Terlayne however as Victor Manfield happened to be in the bar watching the fight with great interest. Later that month he received a phone call from Victor Manfield offering him a job in the wrestling business. The curious Irishman went and was amazed at what he saw before him. He shortly made a name for himself in evolution and Manfield began to get worried about the Irishman’s growing popularity. It was for this reason that Victor planned an attack on him during his world title match. It was all perfectly orchestrated and with Victor pulling the strings, the puppeteers followed his orders and Terlayne lost his shot at the world title. After accusing Manfield of treating them like pawns he was once again out of work. He took the break from wrestling as a chance to discover more about himself and his past. His father’s death soon came up once again during a conversation with Thomas O’Connell who had also fled Ireland after the attack on the O’Shea’s. The story slowly began to unravel and it became apparent that his Uncle Tom had killed his father in order to take over the mining business. After Terlayne took over the business however, he planned his death along with the death of John. Terlayne took matters into his own hands and spent the money he had left on a return ticked to Dublin airport. That night he confronted Tom about his father’s death and Tom confessed to everything, the conspiracy, the murder of his father and the murder of his brother John. Terlayne felt his brother’s pain, his father’s pain and instead of killing Tom, he put a bounty on his head. The next morning, Tom’s body floated into the Aran Islands peer. Two days later Sterling was once again unemployed in America until he found Corrello’s. After returning from his trip with the money his Uncle Tom had left with the mining business and stopped off at a truck stop on the way back to his temporary home. It was here he saw a group of middle aged truckers take on the latest bright fighting prospects the town had to offer. He saw this as his chance to earn ends meat and was soon picked up by Mike Sullivan of the HHF where he went up the ranks earning the European, Intercontinental and Tag Team Titles. Mike was like a father to him and took him under his wing until a bleak day in March of 2002 when Mike was declared bankrupt. The HHF went out of business and Terlayne was once again back on the streets. In the Summer of 2002 he had received word that Thomas O’Connell had been killed. Thomas had been like family to Terlayne and it took Terlayne weeks to recover from his death. Sterling worked in a string of small-time federations until eventually he found himself on the street unemployed. He was once again a lonely wanderer. On the 16th of November 2003 Terlayne happened to be at the right place at the right time. For you see, the astonished owner of the PCW happened to be a member of the civilians standing in awe as a fight broke out between Terlayne and a petty vandal. Starr, seeing the fire in Terlayne’s eyes, his passion for the fight, was the first to step out of the crowd of onlookers. For Terlayne his job offer had been a dream come true.
