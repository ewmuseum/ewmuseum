Turmoil 33 15 May 2001 The Air Canada Center, Toronto, Ontario, Canada PCW Supremacy preview with champions from eight nations The show fades in from black as the Olympic theme plays in the background.  Suddenly, highlights of JJ delivering What Comes After "L" Bow upon a pathetic opponent with the US flag waving in all its glory serving as a backdrop. JJ fades from the screen leaving only the US flag, which suddenly bursts into flames as the opening theme for the Bruce Willis' version of "The Jackal" begins to play.   The flames on the left side of the screen give way to the crimson of the flag of the now defunct United Soviet Socialist Republic, while highlights of the Russian Bear, Ivan Stanislav, throwing Wes Outlett three rows deep into the crowd.   The camera focuses in on the roaring Stanislav as the scene suddenly cuts to snow filled static.  Then, abruptly, The Immigrant Song from Led Zeppelin begins to roll, as highlights of Lord Crom Griffin Dagoth throttling various opponents with the dreaded Black Crash upon a backdrop of the unfurled flag of Finland begin to roll. A sizzling sound begins to break the driving sounds of The Immigrant Song, as the image of LCGD begins to fade to blackness.then to what can only be described as crackling cooking oil.  The Immigrant Song is then cut abruptly for the ear grating sounds of the Frugal Gormet theme, as highlights of The Chef delivering The Tenderizer on an unfortunate foe faded in to the sizzling oil with a semi-transparent French flag flying in the background. Abruptly, the red, white and blue of the flag representing the United Kingdom swoops across the screen, completely obscuring the image of The Chef.  The flag moves from left to right across the screen leaving behind highlights of Jak Nemesis delivering the Destiny DDT on Dusty during the semi-finals of End of Days tourney.  Nemesis stands with his arms raised as the triumphantly over the 500 lb Dusty as the scene abruptly cuts.  "Don't Cry For Me Argentina" from the play Evita begins to play.  To the right are real footage of controversial Argentine leader Evita Peron, while on the left, highlights of former PCW World Heavyweight Champion, Frisco begin to roll, upon an unfurled Argentine flag. Frisco stands with his arms held above his head triumphantly.  The scene begins to fade to black as the volume of "Don't Cry For Me Argentina" softens, eventually until both the image of Frisco disappears and the music is no longer audible. New World Man from Rush begins to play as the following words appear in succession.  The words are stark white in contrast to the black back drop. Eight Men Eight Countries All for National Pride PCW Supremacy Sunday, March 27th Live and only on Pay Per View The scene then fades to black. Cherub Rock and baseball bats outside the Air Canada Center The usual opening for Tuesday Turmoil featuring PCW wrestlers in action upon a backdrop of a ruined and burning city.  Wrestlers highlighted are Sean Matthews, JJ, Frisco and Lord Crom Griffin Dagoth.
