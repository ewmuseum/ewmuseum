Kris Burden Biography Real Name Kris Burden Birthday 9 May 1976 Height 6'4" Weight 271lbs Biography <b>May 9, 1976</b> in Detroit, Michigan...Just a typical day to most people but it was the day that Kris Burden was born, to parents Michael and Laura Burden. He was an only child. Growing up, he didn’t have many problems with living up to honor student expectations in school all the way up to 5th grade...where the worst days of his life began. Kris’s parents seemed to be any normal couple when they were together. All around the town they were considered one of, if not the, best couples around. Kris didn’t think they made a good couple at all. They were so peaceful and loving when they were together, but what about when they weren’t? Kris had to sit around listening to his mother weep over the phone, take his father’s pictures and put them in a closet, and wipe away her tears every single time that she noticed something representing his father. It was hard for Kris to even get to know his father...especially since his father was a 3 star general for the United States Military. He would be gone for months at a time, even years. Granted, when his father was indeed around, Kris took advantage of it and did everything a kid would do with his father. Kris loved his mother dearly, but loved his father in different ways since he never had many chances to see him. <b>March 3, 1991</b> The Corporals, the Lieutenants, and all were there. Kris was up front beside his crying mother as he stared at the coffin with the American flag draped over the sides. It was the day that Kris’s father was buried at Detroit Cemetery. As he stared at his father’s coffin and anything to represent his father for the last time, he never once cried a single tear. Nobody knows what was going on in Kris’s mind then...and probably never will. A couple days before, Kris’s mother had received a letter in the mail that read the following: <b>Day 42/Day 4 of the Ground War/Wednesday, February 27, 1991</b> Military-- Wednesday was the day of destruction for the Iraqis. The VII Corps moved due east from their positions in Iraq, flanked by the 18th Airborne Corps to the North and the Marine forces to the South in Kuwait. Beginning the night before and throughout Wednesday, allied tanks conducted a series of running battles with the Iraqi forces, calling in close air support to destroy hundreds of Iraqi tanks, many of them still dug into the ground. Allied commanders reported that a number of Iraqi units had their guns pointed the wrong direction, given the mistaken belief that the allied attack would come from the South. Around sundown Kuwait time, late morning in Washington, the Republican Guard was destroyed. West of the VII Corps, the allies continued mopping up operations against Iraqi forces and airfields. The 82nd Airborne conducted one raid against an airfield which brought in over 1,000 prisoners of war. The estimated number of prisoners throughout the ground war had grown to over 5,000 with accurate counts being nearly impossible. In Kuwait City, the lead elements of the Pan-Arab forces entered to the cheering and thanks of thousands of Kuwaiti citizens. People were out on the streets waving Kuwaiti and American flags and firing weapons into the air in celebration. Many American's likened the reception to that allied force received when liberating Paris in 1944. Having been briefed on the allied success, President Bush telephoned General Schwartzkopf and General Burden with what many say was his only military request: Stop the killing. Bush wanted to know if a cease-fire was possible and he was told yes. Iraq's Republican Guards had been defeated, its Army had surrendered and allied forces were engaging in mopping up operations throughout the region. It was later determined that of the 42 divisions of Iraqi troops, all 42 had been effectively destroyed with stragglers making up about one division widely spread throughout the entire region. It had been a 100 hour war, with unbelievably low casualties among allied forces. Diplomatic— Bush addressed the world at 9:00pm EST with a simple message. Effective at 12:00 all offensive operations would cease. Iraq would have to agree to specific military conditions for a temporary cease-fire, to be followed upon later by a U.N. resolution setting forth the terms of a permanent cessation of hostilities. One condition that was put forth forcefully was the immediate release of all P.O.W.'s from Iraqi custody as the President wanted to avoid prolonging the crisis. Throughout the capitals of the coalition countries, there was support for the President's actions. In several Arab nations, many believed that Saddam Hussein had beaten the United States, but the television footage eventually dismissed these beliefs. Domestic— The cease-fire announcement took many by surprise. For months, the commentators and professional pundits in the media had predicted a ground offensive of weeks even months costing thousands of casualties. When it was all done, 100 hours had passed with miraculously low number of Americans killed. Despite any initial surprise, across the country euphoria was spreading. Although President Bush said in his statement it 'is not a time to gloat,' American flags were displayed everywhere and the countdown to the return of American forces began. The families of the servicemen and women were finally able to sleep better as now knew their loved ones were coming home. <b>Initial End of the War Report</b> As Kris’s mother read on to the last paragraph the phone rang and she finally heard the news. Michael Burden had been killed the day after the report was printed up. While entering his plane back to the United States, a local Iranian had shot General Burden three times in the chest, before being restrained by security guards. He was pronounced dead at the scene. <b>March 15, 1991</b> Another miserable day for Kris. The local news picked up on the suicide of Laura Burden. Her alleged death...overdose. She was full of grief from the love of her life’s death and just couldn’t take it anymore, after that. She ended it with a couple bottles of Zannex, Aspirin, and Morphine mixed together. How she got the Morphine was never answered… After that, Kris didn’t really talk to many people. He was sent to live with his new family, The Reastons. <b>September 5, 1992</b> They were a loving family when Kris first met them at age 16, although he didn’t take much pride in meeting new people. That was until his drunken foster-father, Kevin, came home one night, relentless and hell-bent on losing his job at work. He took his actions to Kris and his wife, blaming them for everything that had happened. Words became violence and violence became death. Kevin Reaston put a bullet into his wife, believing his whole world was over, just because of losing one job. Kris retaliated to help his new mother by jumping on his father and having a battle. Eventually a piece of beer bottle glass became embedded into his foster-father’s neck…sending him to the hospital, where he would be announced dead later that night. Kris Burden was not charged with anything and the case was called a mistrial, due to self-protection. Kris went out to live on his own, with no family and no reason to live for. Finally, in November of 1992, he would find love. Finally his life was picking up for a good cause. <b>February, 1993</b> Kris Burden and Erin Saunter were to be married on April 28, 1993. This was put to an end due to a terrorist attack on America. Kris stayed home that day, not feeling well. His fiancé had a trip to New York to take the day before. February 26, 1993, a truck bomb full of Urea-Nitrate exploded in the parking garage underneath of the World Trade Centers of New York City, the location of the business trip. Erin Saunter was killed instantly from the attack…bringing more grief than ever to Kris Burden. He tried to commit suicide in almost every way possible, to no avail. Kris stills walks today as what he believes is a punishment for being born. He lives in misery with no reason to live for, besides getting his anger out in the ring. So many people have taken away his reasons to live for…and revenge on others is what he endures inside. A lot goes on in his mind, now…He barely speaks to anybody, afraid that they will turn to shit…or they will just be another one of ‘them.’ Finisher Move The Misery Driver
