The Supreme Machine Biography Biography All that is known about The Supreme Machine's childhood is that somewhere around the ages of 3 - 5, even he can't remember exactly, his parents made the ultimate sacrifice and sold their youngest son to a mysterious man, known only as The Master, in order to support their remaining ten children. The Master immediately set about training his young charge in as many forms of combat as he could possible be exposed to. For years the two of them traveled the globe, seeking out only the best of the best in their various disciplines to tutor the growing, young fighter. Under The Master's watchful eye, The Supreme Machine rapidly grew into a highly disciplined and highly dangerous fighting machine. Following years of preparation, The Master decided in 1997 that it was time to unleash his creation on the unsuspecting world of Professional Wrestling, which he did to dramatic effect. Within a year, The Supreme Machine had taken the wrestling world by storm, winning numerous titles in the process. But that was not enough. The Supreme Machine needed tougher competition. So The Master set about finding an arena worthy of his charge. In late 1998 The Master found exactly what he was looking for. The Supreme Machine debuted in CCW in September of 1998 and soon began to dominate the Heavyweight Title scene, winning the prestigious title on 5 occasions and teaming with Australian wrestling legend Shane Thunder to form The Supreme Team. All was going along well until a fateful night in 1999 when The Master suffered a fatal heart attack during on of The Supreme Machine's title defenses. Shattered by the loss of the only father that he had ever really known, The Supreme Machine continued on in CCW but all was not as it had once been. In early 2000, The Supreme Machine mysteriously turned his back on the fame and fortune he had gained in CCW and vanished from the public eye. He spent the next two years traveling the globe, trying desperately to find some sort of meaning in his life. His journey continued until early 2002 when he got the terrible news that his former tag-team partner was losing his battle with lung cancer. The Supreme Machine immediately flew to be by his only friend's side during his last days. Before Thunder died, he made The Supreme Machine promise on his deathbed to take care of his younger brother, Ricky Thunder, who was following in his footsteps and had entered CCW. It was this promise that finally brought The Supreme Machine back to CCW. After several months of competition, it became apparent that the new powers that be in CCW didn't welcome the return of their most lauded champion. Even though they knew of his promise to the dying Thunder, he and Ricky Thunder were constantly put in situations where they had to fight each other and they were all but ignored when it came to the tag-team division. It all finally came to a head in May of 2002 when acting CCW president, Sylkk, fired both The Supreme Machine and Ricky Thunder.  They both immediately signed contracts with PCW, where they are today.
