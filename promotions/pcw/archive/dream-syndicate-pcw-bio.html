Dream Syndicate Biography Biography Dominic Valerio DeMerius, the son of a shop keeper and an Italian seamstress, was born on August 1st, 1974 in the heat of summer. Raised in the Canarsie District of Brooklyn, Dominic was forced to live an abbreviated childhood, working in his father’s shop at the age of five. Unlike most other children in the slums of Canarsie, Dominic was well schooled, and was literate unlike his immigrant mother. In his early years, he would split his time between his school studies and helping maintain his father’s parsimony shop, but later on, he would become more and more involved with the local shoot fight clubs in Red Hook, Sunset Park, Bay Ridge, and the now infamous Gravesend Underground, site of the first murders committed by David "Son of Sam" Berkowitz during his 1977 massacres. "Dom" as he was called by his closest friends, would take advantage of his gargantuan size to manhandle some of the toughest street thugs in all of Brooklyn, often engaging in borderline-street wars with gangs from Red Hook during the turbulent 1980s. Even at thirteen years old, DeMerius was actively involved with gangs throughout his teen years and into his early twenties. In 1995, two weeks shy of his twenty-first birthday, fate struck DeMerius at a crucial point in his life. Both his mother and father were murdered, gangland style while he was off fighting at Gravesend for some extra money. The loss was tragic, but it also provided DeMerius with an extremely lucky break. In an extremely unexpected turn of events, DeMerius was spotted fighting the next night at Gravesend by legendary wrestling promoter Ryan Walker, then-owner of the National Championship Wrestling Alliance, or NCWA. Though Walker passed on the opportunity to sign the green rookie, he did reference him to Chris Smith, who was on a two week tour of the East Coast during the New Japan/WCW "Invasion" that year. Smith caught up with DeMerius in September of 1995 and, after witnessing several of his fights in Canarsie, offered him the chance to train professionally with him in Japan. DeMerius accepted, and on October 1st, 1995, Dominic would make his first appearance in the famed New Japan Fighting Dojo. Though under the guidance of legendary IWGP World Champion Tatsumi "the Dragon" Fujinami and Masa Saito, DeMerius only trained for six months at the Dojo before leaving the academy, seeking to continue his career in PRIDE. Though his shoot fighting career was short-lived, his experience in mixed martial arts lent him an undeniable edge. Returning to New Japan, DeMerius quickly came under the guidance of Smith, where he exploded on the Japanese scene as the masked giant Diavolo, or "Devil" in Italian. For several years, DeMerius would be the foreign sensation in the Land of the Rising Sun, drawing the largest crowds as a non-Japanese athlete since the days of "Wild Pegasus" Chris Benoit, Vader, and Owen Hart. From the dog days of summer in 1996 up until the end of 1999, Dom would enjoy great success in Japan, feuding with Toshiaki Kawada of All Japan Pro Wrestling and Shinjiro Ohtani of Zero-One, marking the first time since the 1995 Super J Cup that promotions had worked together on a large scale. His career in Japan made DeMerius and Smith, who was on his last legs as a draw, very rich. In January of 2000, Chris Smith retired from active competition. He took over the managerial duties for DeMerius. From there, Smith took DeMerius back to his own native England, where Dom would spend almost a year training at the brutal Snake Pit, where legends like the Dynamite Kid got their start in the sport. During his time at the ‘Pit, Smith had his eye on another up and coming rookie in the sport out in California. DeMerius took the opportunity to complete a tour of duty in Europe, spending the better part of two years maiming almost every single heavyweight challenger that the CWA had to offer at the time. Despite losing in his two chances to become the CWA Heavyweight Champion due to his constant disqualifications, DeMerius made enough of an impact to be made an honorable inductee into the Hall des Fremden Athleten des Ruhmes (Foreign Athlete’s Hall of Fame) in Germany. But his career took one final, unexpected turn in December of 2003, when Smith sent for him in California. Arriving in Los Angeles on December 17th, 2003, DeMerius would meet his new tag team partner, Ethan Richards. Despite his already-turbulent life, Dom DeMerius couldn’t have known the drastic change that was on the horizon for his career… *** William Nathaniel Richardson was quite literally born into a lap of luxury on May 25th, 1978. The son of a corporate tycoon and the illegitimate clone of Paris Hilton, Ethan grew up without ever having to experience a hard day’s work. He was tutored at home by some of California’s most influential educators, and went to school in Silicon Valley’s most private, elite facilities. Despite his respectable grades in High School, his most cherished accomplishments during his years in school would fall under his athletic resume. A varsity letterman in three different sports, Richards would soon find his penchant on the football field, leading Hillsborough Senior Academy to it’s first California State AA Championship in 1994. As the starting quarterback, he would lead the team to another AA Championship in 1995, and would finish his career there as the All-Time Career Passing Leader in Hillsborough County with a little over 2,100 yards. Richards also excelled at baseball, where he made the McDonald’s All American High School team in 1995 and 1996, respectively. He lead all other players in Division AA High School baseball with 21 Home Runs, a .352 Average, and 63 RBIs. He would earn a full scholarship to Cal State Fullerton for his accolades on the baseball field. But, for all his accomplishments on the gridiron and the diamond, Richards would find his greatest success on the wrestling mats. At a tall 6’ 4", Richards managed a muscular physique for his stature, and every bit of his strength went into leading his school to four consecutive California State AA Championships. Richards himself went on to win four Individual State Titles during that stretch, and was once again named to the McDonald’s All American High School team, this time as a wrestler in 1994, 1995, and 1996. When he graduated from Hillsborough Senior Academy in May of 1996, Ethan found himself mentioned inside Sports Illustrated as one of America’s most promising young athletes. But his vices outside of school would ultimately jeopardize his promising career before it began. Richards, who was blessed with movie star good looks, flowing blond hair and a crisp physique, was one of the most popular men in school, and on the beaches of California. Ethan’s female conquests are no secret; it’s a well known fact that he slept with no less than twenty-five girls during a two week stretch at one point, and his pension for alcohol and marijuana got him in trouble with the law on more than one occasion. Though he managed to break his addiction to drugs, his dependency on alcohol have continued unaided to this day. Despite having a falling out with his parents shortly before leaving for Cal State Fullerton, Richards managed to clean himself up enough to impress upon his arrival. Ethan quickly gave up baseball when he arrived, concentrating on his growing passion for wrestling. Despite the anemic condition of Cal State’s wrestling program, Ethan Richards quickly became one of California’s greatest exports, sending him on an incredible 121 match win streak, the likes of which were unmatched and unheard of since the days of Ken Chertow. He would win the NCAA I-AA Heavyweight Championship in 1997, but would fail to repeat his success in the following years. Despite being recognized as Cal State Fullerton’s greatest wrestler, Richards graduated from the school in 2000 feeling somewhat under whelmed by his experience there. It wasn’t long before Richards found himself in trouble again with drugs, as his Spring Break-worthy escalades with alcohol, drugs and women grew in frequency. It was only on an off-hand encounter with Chris Smith in July of 2000 that Richards made his first contact in the wrestling business. Smith, who had been following Richards since his final days wrestling in Japan, had saw great potential in the young athlete, despite his personal demons. Smith offered to take him to England to train alongside his other prospect, Dom DeMerius, but Richards politely refused. Determined to see the conflict of interests through, Smith remained in California and took the burden of training the undependable Richards himself. For two and a half years, Smith personally trained Ethan along with other legends of the sport, including Mando Guerrero and Rey Mysterio Sr. Even Satoru Sayama, the original Tiger Mask, was brought over from Japan to assist in Richard’s training. Before long, Ethan Richards would shed his drug dependency once and for all, and would quickly become the hottest thing to hit California since refried rice and chalupas. Endearing himself to young women who viewed him as a heartthrob and men who viewed him as a bonafide badass, Richards enjoyed much success working through California, Arizona, Nevada, the Pacific Northwest and Vancouver. In late 2003; however, Smith decided that it was time for Richards to take the final step into superstardom. With the summoning of Dom DeMerius from Europe at the end of the year, a new chapter in Richard’s life was about to begin… *** In December of 2003, legendary British grappler Chris Smith put together the Californian party animal Ethan Richards and the brutal New Yorker Dom DeMerius together for the first time, forming the ultra-successful Dream Syndicate. Despite Dom’s natural distrust in new people and Ethan’s cocky nature, the two quickly became a cohesive unit on the West Coast Circuit, dominating the few tag teams they managed to face in their short time there. With Richard’s speed and DeMerius’ power, the two managed to decimate all that opposed them in the west, while paying homage to each other’s heritages from both coasts. The lifestyle of Richards quickly consumed DeMerius, and the two would eventually find their new found wealth going towards excessive journeys through the Los Angeles nightlife. But all that changed in February of 2003, when Chris Smith managed to get the Dream Syndicate into Primetime Championship Wrestling, the most elite organization in North America. Despite their first match resulting in a loss to MSA and E2 at Shanghaied, the Dream Syndicate have found their niche as a tag team, and plan on wreaking havoc over all that stand in their way in PCW. Only time will tell if they manage to rise to the top and lay claim to being one of the greatest tag teams in wrestling history.
