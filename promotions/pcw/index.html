Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The alliance's own host federation. What survives of PCW is its match results; its forum administration and third-party credentials are not reproduced here. What survives cleanly is the shape of the promotion itself — a Front Office storyline running across seven active titles, and a deep news engine that outlived its own title-history page.

Storylines

The H.I.T.'s two tours Written by AI, 2026

The H.I.T. ran twice, years apart, and the same enemy stands at both ends of it. His own PCW biography (served in full) covers the first tour: joining Kent Anathason and Nuke's Neurosis faction in the summer of 2001, feuding with the Dark Ages, and being thrown against The Red Army after his allies departed. He eliminated Alexander Popov and Nikita Morozov from PCW outright, won the Cruiserweight Championship from White Mexican and the Television Championship from Kid Wonder, and lost the run itself in a retirement match against Rent-A-Hero, where Red Army leader Ivan Stanislav planted him with The Red Scare to engineer the eviction. No independent PCW results page survives to date any of it — the fed's own results archive begins at /results/189, after this stretch already happened, and the bio's own prose is the only surviving account.

The second tour is recovered entirely from the fed's own live-site roleplay archive, targeted-mined for his character in August 2026 (57 of the 279 roleplays the site's own character-filter search returned for him, two served in full below). He returns unmasked that October, the Front Office — Xavier Kannon, Tim Walden, Duke Williams, Brandi, Bob Charlie, Jeff Marx — his target, over an incident his own new nickname preserves: “The 10/18 Assailant.” A Department of Education autograph tour billed as promoting math takes him from Washington D.C. to Italy, where he needles owner Xavier Kannon's Scientology under a “F.U.C.K. Math” banner (Fun, Unviolent, Caring, Kind) — served in full, “In D.C.,” 24 October 2002. Days later his own classroom is broken into and its math books stolen; he answers by drafting his students into “Operation Delta Pi,” a protest against the school board timed around the same week's PCW matches. The arc builds toward a five-way Cooking Utensils Match for the Continental Title against Tim Walden, Rush Mason, Skylar Green and Tommy — served in full, “Ten Ways To Hurt Tim Walden With A Melon Baller (Part 2),” a full weaponized-cookware promo staged for the match — whose own outcome does not survive in the material recovered. Two of his own 2001 titles outlived the record of how he lost them; this 2002 arc ends the same way, mid-build, the corpus running out before the payoff.

Politically Incorrect and the Tag Team Championship Written by AI, 2026

Politically Incorrect (William S. Powell & Reginald V. Lovecraft) is the one PCW arc with a complete, dated record — the team's own biography (served in full) carries its own sixteen-match PCW ledger, Fri. Fusion 8 March 2002 through Fri. Fusion 7 June 2002. Two politicians — Powell a corrupt Iowa city councilman, Lovecraft a former Atlanta city council representative — stormed PCW attacking Chris Chase and Corey Williams, then lost a first Tag Team Title shot to Golden Game before recruiting owner Xavier Kannon as an ally (“Facing one's Fears..,” 27 March 2002, the team sizing up a Gauntlet Match opponent ahead of the show). At Fri. Fusion, 30 March 2002, they won the Gauntlet Match outright, outlasting Fear Factory, Misplaced, Metallyx, Cowabunga Connection and Golden Game in succession, and retained through a fourway dance at Spring Fling with Kannon's own interference turning the finish. The team's own wealth is played for comedy in the same stretch — “Aquiring transportation.,” 7 April 2002, the pair outbidding a bankrupt billionaire for his private jet ahead of Spring Fling. Their reign, and the record's own clean run of dates, ends 11 May 2002 at PCW Supremacy: the bio's own words, “in a match with no interference, cheating, or other assorted BS, The Heartbreakers succeeded.”

Affiliations

Primetime Central (PTC)

Operated by PCW (Primetime, 2000–2004) (as of the Feb 2002 relaunch) — User attestation, 2026: true, but only initially — PTC later went independent of PCW's operation. Consistent with (but not fixed to a date by) the corpus's own PTC Global Title lineage, which names no PCW-affiliated champion after the Feb 2002 relaunch (DWO by mid-2003, RPW/Chris Storm Jan 2004, PRIME Apr 2004) — no exact split date survives in ptc-site.md or elsewhere in the corpus. (user-attested)
PCW is PTC’s founding host — PTC’s own site calls PCW “host federation” outright, PCW’s forums were hosted directly on PTC’s Ikonboard, and PTC’s recurring cast (Batee, Duke Williams, The Phantom Booker, Tyler Nelson) all have their day jobs at PCW. In tension with this: PTC’s own FAQ explicitly excludes PCW from its official “Channels”/rankings/network tier even while hosting PCW’s boards — two different senses of “member” coexisted in the alliance’s own writing.

Old School Wrestling (OSW, Easton, 2002)

Shared continuity with PCW (Primetime, 2000–2004)
Jane Reagan is PCW’s Personnel Director and OSW’s owner — a mid-storyline reveal that the same in-fiction person runs both front offices. Roster raid traffic ran both directions (Kid Wonder moved from PCW to OSW), and PCW’s own champions challenged for OSW gold: at OSW’s 9 Feb 2003 Slam! broadcast, Politically Incorrect challenged for the OSW World Tag Team Championship and lost, with Heaven & Hell retaining. Off camera, the two were also sister companies under one real-world operator, alongside a wider family of promotions that included PTC, PURE, and others — the operator isn’t named here.

Old School Wrestling (OSW, Easton, 2002)

Rivalry with PCW (Primetime, 2000–2004) (as of Apr 2002)
OSW's own text calls itself "PCW's greatest rival" verbatim (K-Dub/Kid Wonder signing angle, Apr 2002). Ivan Stanislav's Red Army storyline is explicitly framed as a campaign against PCW owner Tyler Nelson ("I am here to educate the younger talent of OSW and lead my army into battle against Tyler Nelson!"); a Howard Porter promo frames OSW stepping "out of the shadows of PCW and their overbearing dictator." Recorded as a second, distinct edge alongside shared-continuity above because both are independently load-bearing in the source material, not two readings of the same fact.

New True Internet Championship Wrestling (NTICW)

Owned by PCW (Primetime, 2000–2004) (as of Oct 2001)
PCW CEO Kremmen von Smackdown, quoted outright: “I don’t think its any secret that Primetime Central owns NTICW... Yoder will go there to ensure that NTICW’s operations are running well and up to PCW standards.” NTICW functioned as PCW’s direct developmental/overflow fed and closed under its own last owner.

RUSH Wrestling

Acquired by PCW (Primetime, 2000–2004)
Matt Elia’s PCW bio quotes PCW President Xavier Kannon outright: he “bought RUSH outright... his goal isn’t to show that he has it to win anything, but he has it to help regain RUSH’S freedom as it was stolen right from underneath everyone’s nose.” RUSH is the single most-mentioned outside promotion in PCW’s own corpus, with its CEO named as “Day Johnson.” Whether this reflects a storyline buyout or a real cross-site business arrangement isn’t clear from the text alone.

Primetime Wrestling Academy (PWA)

Academy — feeds into PCW (Primetime, 2000–2004)
PCW’s own FAQ, verbatim: “It is a wrestling academy, which has been open since late 2000... We take the best workers from the PWA into PCW.” It is by far the most-cited outside promotion across PCW’s own wrestler bios — nearly every long-tenured PCW wrestler passed through it first. OSW’s own record independently confirms it as a shared academy feeding both OSW and PCW.

PCW2

Sister promotion of PCW (Primetime, 2000–2004)
Repeatedly described in PCW’s own corpus as PCW’s “second company”/“Sibling Company” (Boda and D-Kay’s bios). Ran its own titles (World, Tag, Cruiserweight, Rising Star) in parallel to PCW for a period, then closed, with its championship-holders and roster folding back into “the NEW PCW” reform.

Puroresu Ultimate Ring Entertainment (PURE)

Crossover booking with PCW (Primetime, 2000–2004)
Roster flow both ways in the fed’s own words: “Jordan X Shabazz (from PCW)” signed 6 May 2002; The End’s “excellent record in EW … sending him to PCW and eventually PURE”; “PCW and OCW super-heavyweights Akiyama and Kimbusa are expected to sign”; a rumour that “PCW former champion Chris Sloboda, AKA Boda” would appear to punk out PURE’s owner; and the Reverend Jesse Kintei praising Shabazz’s work “here in PURE and the PCW.” The H.I.T.’s own move from PURE to PCW is the same crossover, seen from the character side.

Cyberspace Championship Wrestling (CCW, 1998–2005)

Rivalry with PCW (Primetime, 2000–2004)
CCW’s own Revolution pay-per-view is staged “From the PCW Pavillion in Atlantic City”; a fan sign in its own Lockdown script reads “The Phantom Booker fears Sylkk”; and its own 2005 history page carries a paragraph on “PCW under The Phantom Booker” — the rivalry told three separate ways in CCW’s own voice.

Puroresu Ultimate Ring Entertainment (PURE)

Crossover booking with PCW (Primetime, 2000–2004) (Sep – Dec 2002)
PCW's own newswire stages PURE as its "Japanese affiliate, and rival": PCW's CEO challenged PURE in September 2002, PURE accepted a 10-man tag team match at Red October (6 October 2002, the Tokyo Dome), where "Primetime Championship Wrestling sets foot on the turf of PURE", and after PURE's re-opening that December PCW loaned it three talents.

Ultra Fantasy Wrestling (UFW)

Predecessor to PCW (Primetime, 2000–2004)
Ultra Fantasy Wrestling closed in August 2000 and its membership carried on at Primetime Championship Wrestling, which had opened the month after. Ultra Fantasy Wrestling says so twice in its own voice — its front page's closing notice places its former members at PCW, and its links page says in the first person that many of them joined that federation. PCW's own shows then narrate the move from the other side for two years: an announcer bills a wrestler as a former three-time Ultra Fantasy Wrestling Television Champion, two stables reform under their old names, a departing World Champion's farewell traces his own path through the closed federation, and one biography of PCW's own records a ten-month run there under a different ring name. A diaspora rather than a handover of the same organisation: the two federations had different operators, and a month of overlap. Recorded on the same reading the GIWF-to-UWF line carries — one membership's continuous history under a second banner.

Five Star Wrestling (FSW, Five Star Wrestling, 2000–02)

Crossover booking with PCW (Primetime, 2000–2004) (2002)
PCW's Front Office challenged OSW, FSW and RUSH on 29 October 2002 for a spot in its Alliances show: "Four OSW members, four RUSH members and four FSW members to fight against or alongside the PCW roster." The Alliances card of 24 November 2002 stages Team FSW, four wrestlers from this federation's roster, and a PCW commentator calls Mob Rule "FSW's power stable".

Shared ownership
