You're visa hasn't cleared Sivasian?

24 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 24 April Brian slipped through the door of the Krikorian household. The newly emigrated family, who he hadn’t seen for almost 3 months were lying in the front room, watching a Mongolian television stadium. He had seen the family many times before, but the state of them in their jet lagged condition left him annoyed. ‘Hello, Sivasian…..’ muttered the inquiring Brian, running his hand threw his golden brown hair. Sivasian didn’t reply, he didn’t need to, he merely flicked his head towards his long time friend, and nodded. Brian didn’t say anything back, but walked towards the nearest cream sofa, and sat down. It was tatty, but the family hadn’t had time to go out of their house yet, and they found the old broken sofa in the house when they arrived. ‘You’re wrestling now, my friend?’ asked Brian, helping himself to one of the sweets sitting on the a small china bowl on the table, a tradition in Russian houses. His friend was exactly as he had described, a smallish figure, but with large, scarred hands. He had large dark blue eyes, and thinning white hair, something which showed the age of the 43 year old. He’d been through a lot, but living in a society where you speak little or no Engish was hard for him. He had money, he had brought everything he had to the states to make his new living, wrestling for money, but for the moment they could do nothing without the clearance of the American government Sivasian’s bank account couldn’t be switched without a visa, a visa he needed. A visa was something which had cost the jobs of several other PCW wrestlers, with many of them leaving, and unable to support themselves and their families without the working. His wife was a beautiful lady , only into her late 20’s, she was a stunning blonde , leggy, but not tall enough to tower over the 6’3 Sivasian. ‘I need money….’ muttered Sivasian, drinking a small glass of water, ‘I need to get my clearance, to earn money, we have nothing at the moment.’ Sivasian said, still keeping his eyes firmly ahead of him, staring at the small battered 60’s TV set in front of him. Cheesy music blurts out, as he still stays lying, trying to recover from the huge jet lag of an 18 hour flight from the Ukraine. The first time that he had come to another country from his homeland, except when he had made missions abroad for his homeland, when he’d presumably taking another mans life. Pondering whether he was allowed to be working in the USA when he had previous killings on his record, Brian asked. ‘Hey Sivasian, you allowed into this country with a record of killings like you…’ questioned the American, staring at the television screen, even though he had absolutely no idea what was going on. Sivasian shifted slightly, and swung his legs round to sit on the edge of the sofa. ‘That’s all in my past; I’ll never do it again. Maybe on a computer game, or maybe to show others how to shoot, but never another man, woman or child’s life shall be taken by a gun handled by myself….’ He replied, sharply, before staring at his friend. ‘What’s it like when you kill someone with a sniper gun Siv, I mean do you feel sorrow, regret, or is it all part of the job.’ His friend asked again. Sivasian took another sip of his water, before blinking and staring again. He looked the whole of the cool, calm and cold as steel character to which people had associated him. He never appeared phased, and, just like a sniper, used his time to pick and choose his answer. ‘Well….’ He started, crunching his hand into his fist, gnashing his teeth and grinding them. ‘You never feel anything; you just slowly pull the trigger when you have the head of the man in your crosshairs, feel it recoil back into your arm, and reload. You never think anything, you just get back on with the job. You may get paid to be a reporter for some big international magazine….’ ‘The Eagle….’ Brian replies, smirking and flashing his photographer and reporter badge. Sivasian stares at him, annoyed with his butting in. However, remaining as calm as usual, simply takes a deep breath and carries on. ‘You get paid to do what? Take reports, report on my life right now, tell them people what they wanted to know. I was paid to shoot people, it was my job, I took home the pay if I got the job done.’ He replied, quite satisfied that he had done the job of answering the question correctly. ‘So anyways, wants you’ve got cleared to wrestle and work, earning money, you’ve got a match on ….Friday Fusion?’ asked Brian, stopping part way through his sentence, ‘ and you’re gunna go and wrestle two people, one an Egyptian and one an American called…SOCIETY? I mean what sort of name is that, when the states have the worst kind of society in the world. Overweight women, hugely paid men who play sports for maybe 15 minutes a week and the plain ignorance of the country as a whole make it THE worst society in the world..’ Sivasian, as usual, doesn’t move. He blinks once more and stares at Brian, ‘and the Egyptian culture?’ asked. ‘Well…’ said Brian, thinking for a second…..’errr….I’m just gunna assume they’re really, really poor….I mean living off the pyramids, that’s so err…..2000 years ago!’. Brian smiled lightly towards Sivasian. ‘Moron…’ Sivasian muttered back at him, standing up. ‘Oh honey….’ His wife blurted out, ‘can we see this American culture down at this mall, I need some clothes if we’re going to a restaurant tonight.’ Sighing at the price of keeping a younger woman with him, he slowly nodded, ‘Fine, fine, grab your coat and let’s go.’ ‘YAY!’ she squealed and stood up quickly, before exiting the room. ‘Women,’ Sivasian said, ‘You can’t live with the, and you certainly can’t live without them.’
