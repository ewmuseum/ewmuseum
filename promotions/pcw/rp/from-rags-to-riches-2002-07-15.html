From Rags to Riches

15 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7-15-02 New York City, New York. The year was 1976, the year I was born. I was born to a working class family. My father owned a meat market down to the street from us while my mother ran a hair salon next door to my father’s meat market. I was there oldest child and the first of three. I began working at the age of nine, I’d package the meat after my father sliced it and I’d give it to the customers after school. On weekends I’d work at my Mother’s hair salon sweeping up the hair that was cut. From age nine to eighteen I worked with my family in there business as times changed I began to slice the meat and sometimes watch over the meat market when my father was out of town. For my mother I would run around order hair products or picking up shampoo. I was always gracious for the parents I had. It taught me the value of the dollar, but times changed after high school. Once I was out of high school I began going to NYC College, which was just a couple of blocks away from my house. So when I was free I would work for my parents helping them out. They always appreciated my help and I always enjoyed working for them while earning a couple of dollars. It was my second year of college where times changed for me. It was my second year of college where things began to change for me. For the first time in my life I was living on my own. I moved out of my house and joined some friends of mine in a dorm room on the campus. They would always talk about going to a casino but never did. Well one Saturday afternoon, we were very bored as could be and we decided to go for a little road trip to a casino in upstate Connecticut, The Mohegan Sun. It was a very long drive and we decided we would stay over night, so we brought most of the money we earned from our respected jobs. By the time we got to the casino it was around seven thirty in the fall and it was complete dark outside. The lights on this place boggled my mind it was amazing. I had never seen anything like this before it was truly amazing. From the lights, the atmosphere and the money. I came from a family where we earned our money and invested it in something that would make us happy or a necessity. So I figured, this wouldn’t hurt; I would just spend a couple of bucks here and there, maybe get a little lucky and possibly win some money. I knew I was going to lose money but little did I know what the experience would be for me that night. It was something I will never forget. I was nineteen years old, a very young kid living on his own, experiencing more stuff then I ever did in the city. They never asked how old we were or ask for ID like they are suppose to and let in three nineteen year olds and one twenty years old. Once we entered the casino it took my breath away, I had never seen anything this colorful, beautiful or energetic ever. I came from a working family who never had the time to go to a theme park or go somewhere that is fun for the whole family. This was like seeing a new life right in front of my eyes. Everything in the casino changed me from the lights to the waterfalls to the lounge signers to beautiful women to the money, it was everything I wanted to have, and I wanted to be a “Vegas” hustler. It was something my parents would never allow me to do, so like ever teenager I just had to do it, I had to try something I would never try before, little did I know that from age nineteen to age twenty six it would become a hobby for me. Little did I know that I would be known around the world in casinos as Scorpion, a nickname given to me by one of the greatest card players in the world, John “Da Man” Riggero. I remember when he gave that name to me like it was yesterday, we doing a huge event that was promoted in Mohegan Sun. I was twenty-one years old, two years and I was hooked. Like someone on drugs I couldn’t get away so easily. We played the best of seven in poker. It began at 10:00 a.m. and ending at 11:00 p.m. thirteen straight hours. It was all tied up three to three; John showed a full house of two nines and three six’s. Then I put down my hand and shocked everyone, in my hand I had the very lucky, royal flush, game, set, match. I beat the best and he gave me the nickname Scorpion because right when you think you had me, WHAM! I injected venom into you out of nowhere and took all your money. From there on I was known as the best and it was something I have been proud of ever since. In Casino’s across the globe I’m consider to be known as “Mr. Lucky” because when I first stepped foot in a casino and since my last step in a casino a couple of days ago, I have never ever lost money. From the slots, to the crapes, to blackjack, even if it’s a little, I still get more then I put into the machine, on the board or in the money pot. I consider my so-called “luck” common sense. I have been born with a talent, a knack for the games in the casinos. Besides wrestling I believe it was one of my true callings, something that I will always be successful in. If there is one thing I have learned from casino life is, if you have won it, flaunt it. If you got the money, show the people how filthy rich you are and people tell me that I’m a cocky son of a bitch and I look them square in the eyes, I see there fear, the sweat dripping off there forehead and I tell them simply, “you dam right I’m cocky, and if you don’t like it to bad.” That usually shuts them up and then they tell me that I have a problem, a addiction to the game, I laugh in their face and they tell me that’s it’s insecurity. So then again I look right in there face and I say very calmly, “If I’m insecure, then insecurity never looked so pretty.” They just walked off pissed as I laugh with some people I met at the casino that night. One thing I don’t like people say is that my hobby is an addiction. My wife and my kid don’t know my casino greatness, nor do they know about my hobby. One of the hardest things is to look at my parents square in the eye and lie to them. When my father passed away, I felt I had the obligation to end my casino ways but the demons continued to haunt me and I just forgot about the promise I made to myself. I know I’m letting him down as he watches me from up above and witness my love the casino, something he consider sin full. But then again, I have been called the mayor of sin city. Eventually my demons will catch up with me. All my dirty laundry will be shown and I will be exposed of my love the casino right in front of my family. I hope that day never comes, they cannot know. I cannot bring my family name down to the ground in front of them. I know my wife is suspicious cause when I come home with a brand new necklace for here, she always asks me where I got it, I just tell her it’s a present from working so hard and putting up with me. She then gives me that look like, who the hell are you foolin’ I’m not a idiot. I just hope she never finds out. I love the casino cause it took me out of the hard working, working class and put me in the wealthy class and it’s keeping my son from ever going through what I went through, working my ass off seven days a week for nine straight years, from age nine to age eighteen. I thank my parents for teaching me the value of the dollar and earn it. But I have to admit winning that money is easier then working your ass off for it. I can truly say that it’s good to be me.
