A step in the right direction?

26 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3-26-03 “My fucking head kills” A.J. Trinidad says as he steps into the back seat of a taxi cab on a brisk night in Chicago. “Well, what did you expect” retorts Amira slightly sarcastic, “You got drank enough booze last night to get three sailors drunk. The wind whips violently through the sharp, cold air as car horns ring out loudly in the dark Chicago night. A siren disappears off in the distance as both doors of the taxi come closed and A.J. rests his head back against the vinyl seats and groans. “You go to airport?” the cabby asks in a middle-eastern accent. Looking up to the driver and noting the towel wrapped around his head A.J. groans again, “Yes, to Airport”, he says mockingly. The cabby sneers back to A.J. before shifting the car into drive and peeling out into traffic. “Hey, try to get us there alive” A.J. shouts out angrily as the taxi curbs around a bend getting onto the interstate. A.J. stares out the window at the moon that seems to be sliding along the star blanketed sky in unison with the cab. “Fucking Tim Walden.” A.J. whispers out more so to himself than anyone else, yet it manages to catch Amira’s ear none-the-less. “Pardon dear?” She inquires. “Oh, nothing, just thinking about my match tomorrow night against Tim Walden.” A.J. says. “I just can’t believe that I have to fight Walden when all I want is to get my hands on that pothead Smoke. It seems like such a waste of a victory.” “The match isn’t over yet A.J.” responds Amira. “By no means do you have Walden beat, at least I don’t see that Television Title around your waist yet.” “I’d rather have Crespo on my lap than any god damn title around my waist.” A.J. answers growing more irritated as the conversation moves forward. “Hey, don’t get me wrong,” Amira begins, “I want nothing more than for Crespo to return home as well, but this give you the opportunity to get something that Smoke wants. Something at least for you to barter with. I’m just saying that you need to keep you eye on the big picture here A.J., getting Crespo back is number one priority, but there are small steps to achieving that.” “I know it is baby,” A.J. whispers over to Amira. “I know it is” Silence lays on the cab like a blanket after the brief conversation between Trinidad and Amira as the taxi cruises on at speeds of over 80 miles per hour towards O’Hare airport. A.J. continues to stare blankly out the window seemingly at a total loss to focus on any one thought as Amira silently empathizes across from him. The silence lasts about five minutes until the Middle Eastern cabby reaches for the radio. “Please don’t” asks A.J. of the cab driver while raising a hand in his direction. “My head is ringing and I really don’t need any extra noise right now.” Agitated, the taxi driver focuses back on the road as Amira sighs off bringing A.J.’s attention back over to her. “You remember that George Carlin comedy special where he did that bit about white guys wearing their baseball hats backwards?” Asks A.J. “Sure A.J.” Amira replies. “Why” “What?” A.J. questions quickly before answering, “Oh, no reason, I was just thinking of how funny it was.” “Listen A.J.” Amira stars while placing a hand on his shoulder. “Right now you need to focus. I fear that your mind is going in about a thousand different directions right now, I mean you don’t even sleep unless you’re drunk, and even then you only pass out for a couple hours.” A.J. rests his head onto Amira’s shoulder as the taxi exits on the O’Hare route and his breathing begins to match hers. “I’ve watched plenty of video on Walden this last week, drunk or not, I feel I’ve managed to get a pretty good idea of what he’s going to do in the ring.” A.J. says softly nuzzling his head further into her soft bosom. “The only worry I have, is that I’m so out of control, infected with rage that is so repressed with in me that I rip his fucking throat out. When the rage comes out, it’s going to be a flood Amira, and I don’t want to lost control.” “This may be just what you need A.J.” Amira says softly stroking his hair. “Get in the ring, release your hatred, then you can gather your thoughts and work on Crespo. If ya want, during your match I can scour the backstage area and see what I can turn up?” “Against Walden I don’t expect to need your assistance ringside, looking around backstage couldn’t hurt, just make sure to watch your back. I don’t know what I’d do if Seymore managed to take you from me too.” A.J. speaks quietly as his eyes slowly close. “Don’t worry about me A.J., if I even see Smoke in the hallway backstage it’ll be him you need to worry about. After I get through with Seymore, he’ll have three new holes in his head to rip bong loads from.” A.J. chuckles slightly on Amira’s lap as the Taxi cab pulls to a stop at a red light. Water droplets begin collecting on the windshield of the cab before the wipers brush them aside. “Drops of water so easily pushed to the side, disregarded and left to fester and vanish along journey,” A.J. says to himself as his neck relaxes, “only if Walden will be as easy. Just brush him aside and continue my path to what I really want, what I need, what I know I’ll have all in due time.” “What was that baby?” Asked Amira. Getting no response from A.J. Amira repeats her inquiry but the only reply is sound of ruffled breathing from a sleeping man. Finally it looks like A.J. will get a short amount of decent shut eye, and Amira is not about to interfere with that. “I know we’ll get through this alright,” Amira whispers into his ear as he continues to drift farther off into his slumber. “You will not allow yourself to lose, not now, not this battle, not today.” The taxi cab pulls off from the stop and go light as the rain begins to fall more heavily in sheets, leaving A.J. and Amira on their way to what they hope will be a step in the right direction in Long Beach, California. “It has to be” thinks Amira to herself as the scene fades to black.
