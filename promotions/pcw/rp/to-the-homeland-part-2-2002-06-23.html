To The Homeland (Part 2.)

23 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06/23/02 When we last left Jordan X Shabazz he was on a plane, heading towards his homeland, Africa. His first thought was to deliver small televisions to all the children of Africa, so they could see the man that would become Television Champion, or at least do everything in his power to become Television Champion. And to Jordan, being Television Champion constitutes being on Television a lot. So wouldn’t it be great to have starving children watch their hero on television? Yeah, right. But sadly, the televisions had to be cracked open to check for weapons, or illegal drugs. Jordan was enraged, but he went on, trying to forget what has happened to him. Jordan was now on the plane, thoughts of his upcoming tag team match on Turmoil were in his mind. Thoughts of bringing televisions to the children of Africa were now out of his head. Instead, when he got to Africa he would go to all their stores, and buy, as many things from the stores that his money allowed, and then he would give them to the children, though he was doing it for the publicity, it would make him efel really good. First he had to be a handheld camera though, there was no way he was going to fly to Africa, claiming N.I.G.G.A, and not get it some publicity. N.I.G.G.A was going to hit the New York Stock Exchange in a mere three days, and this would definitely help some people into buying stock. They would think, like the ignorant white folks that they are, “Oh look honey, that nice black man is giving things to those children. How nice, let’s support him by digging into our huge pockets and tossing a few dollars at them, maybe they won’t steal our cars now.” And they would give the money to N.I.G.G.A, which really was money for Jordan. He had used the money from N.I.G.G.A to place a down payment on his house, buy a new car, get his two butlers (Read: Slaves.), and generally live the good life. Live the life that he was sworn to fight against. Jordan fought against the man, or so he said, and in reality, he was the man… in a way. The scene opens up with Jordan X Shabazz sitting on the plane, watching an airplane movie; this movie is Rush Hour 2. Jordan wasn’t particularly fond of this movie, in his mind, the way they were portraying Chris Tucker was wrong. They were portraying him as a loud, abrasive, unintelligent, black man. That’s not what Jordan wanted to see. Jordan was becoming increasingly angry. A half full glass of Pepsi sat before him on his airplane trey. He took a sip of his Pepsi as one of the flight attendants neared him. He threw his right hand in the air, almost as if hailing a cab, and much like a cab driver would, the attendant ignored him and went to another man first, even though Jordan raised his hand for help before the man. Or at least that’s what Jordan thought. To add more fuel to this fire, the man was white. He was an old white man in his mid fifties. He appeared to be physically disabled, and probably rich, this was first class, paid for by the great sponsors of N.I.G.G.A. When the lady was finished dealing with him, she made her way down to a seething made Jordan X Shabazz. Attendant: “Can I get you…” Jordan X Shabazz cuts her off. Jordan X Shabazz: “Yes you can, bitch. What you can do is bring that trolley thing back around here, and give me some more friggin’ Pepsi. And could you please turn off this movie?” The flight attendant is taken aback at Jordan’s blatant disrespect. A few passengers see her having trouble with him and look back, thinking that possibly a terrorist is in their midst. She doesn’t argue with him, she just tells him something that angers Shabazz even more. Attendant: “Sir, we can’t turn off the movie because you don’t like it. Just don’t put on your headphones, we have some other people on this plane enjoying the film.” The attendant says while refilling Jordan’s drink. Jordan X Shabazz: “Of course they’d like it. I paid GOOD MONEY to fly first class, and when I am DISRESPECTED by the white man I get VERY ANGRY!” The woman is now becoming afraid of the screaming black man in the tenth row. He wasn’t too far anyway from the front, and it could be easily assumed that a terrorist caused the ruckus. Attendant: “Sir, I apologize, you can go to sleep if…” Jordan X Shabazz was angry now, and he screamed. Jordan X Shabazz: “NO THE FUCK YOU DIDN’T!!” That line, made famous by Jordan X Shabazz when punking out crackas with the Phantom Booker on PTC Malice, is heard loudly. Suddenly, two agents spring up to their feet from their seats. They walk over to Jordan and force handcuffs on him, it makes a big ruckus. In the end, Jordan is cuffed, and the plane is forced to make a stop in Amsterdam. Jordan was temporarily arrested and detained. Jordan had screamed “IT’S NOT LIKE I HAVE A FUCKING BOMB OR SOMETHING!!” And you know, saying the word bomb on a plane after 9/11 is something that you just don’t do, if you’re sane. But Jordan was on one of his ‘Fuck the white man, turn this movie off’ rants. Our scene now opens up in the detention office in Amsterdam, where Jordan X Shabazz sits in a chair, speaking to some old border police officer, he was being interrogated. Inside the room Jordan held his head up. His hands were on the table, showcasing a brand new Rolex watch, which was bought with the money that N.I.G.G.A brought in. The man that was questioning Jordan was a black man. He looked tired and old. Kind of like David Palmer from the Fox hit series, “24.” He eyed Jordan with steely intensity, his name was Robert Law, he was an American, and working at the Amsterdam National Airport. Robert Law: “So, Mr. Copeland, you threatened to blow up the plane to *Reads from report* “kill the white devils.” Jordan smiles but he doesn’t say anything. Jordan X Shabazz: “Sir, what I said was that if I was white, they wouldn’t be questioning me if I had a bomb or something. Alls I asked for was the movie to be changed because it was demeaning to African Americans around the world.” Robert Law just looks through the report that he has in his hands. He appears to be stressed out from a long hard day of work. The only Civil Rights Activist in professional wrestling is sitting up straight in the chair, like a proud man. Robert Law: “Well, we don’t have any evidence of you doing anything, but if there is another disturbance with you, you will be prosecuted. Jordan thinks for a moment as he stands up to walk out the door. Jordan X Shabazz walks out the door, and into the airport. ************************************ 7 hours later, Jordan has finally arrived in Africa. His anger had been getting the best of him. The pressure of new Malice Crew members, the pressure of N.I.G.G.A hitting the NYSE, the pressure of possibly becoming Television Champion. All of it. Now he was at home, he was in Africa. It was South Africa, but it was Africa. Jordan smiled as he walked off the plane. He would spend at least two days here, getting some publicity for N.I.G.G.A, by the time he stepped into the ring next Sunday against John Crichton for the Television title, all eyes would be on him, and he would not disappoint.
