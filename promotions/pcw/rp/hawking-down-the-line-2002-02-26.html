Hawking down the Line

26 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 26th Febuary 2002 Miyagawa is sitting at a computer terminal. He wears a black Linkin Park t-shirt, and well, actually, you can’t see from his waist down because of the computer desk, so I won’t bother to describe it to you. He sits and stares at the computer as if something incredibly dumb has just happened. Although it’s that kinda look he makes when someone asks him a rather stupid question, something like “Why?” forcing him to respond “Because... I’m... Excellent” because it is after all, the most obvious answer to hand, and the fool should know better than to try any of that jesting rubbish. The table is white washed and is clear of junk. Ok that’s a total lie, the table is filled with crap... CCG cards, stuffed monkey toys, a mobile phone, pocket watch, several dozen one cent coins in a tall tower towards the left hand side (which incidentally gets knocked down and rebuilt at least twice a day). A game manual for X Wing: Alliance sits on top of another one for Allied Assault. A can of shoe waterproofing coating stands next to a box filled with ink jet replacement ink. Several cover cds from several different computer magazines are strewn around randomly, like most of the stuff is to be honest. A copy of Lord of the Rings sits there, it’s top few pages lifting off the rest of the book, proving it to be rather well read. Several Games Workshop models are strewn around, including a large section to the left hand side which could be best described as a graveyard... full of half models, arms, legs, heads... a mockery of the modelling system. He reaches out and grabs a small Pokemon toy... a small yellow plastic Pikachu... he lifts it up and the camera picks up a small signature on the side of it. It reads something close to “Jeremy Johnson... at least it wasn’t a rabid Koala!”. He reads it for probably is the six millionth time and grins at the memory before placing it back down onto the desk. The desk itself is butted into the corner of a room, presumably somewhere in the Asylum Gym, or as it is now more regulary known around the stable as “that damned maze” considering that Miyagawa has about an infinite number of rooms in the place... which does also mean an infinite number of rooms to decorate... which probably explains the entire room just having been painted white. White walls... white ceiling... wooden waxed floorboards. Admittedly the wax probably does help the casters of his office chair skirt around a little bit better than it did if there was carpet on the floor. He looks to the wall in front of him which is smothered with photographs in frames. He stands up from his chair, pushing the chair back... it rolls smoothly across the waxed floorboards (what did I say? Is this narrator a genius or what?) He lifts down a framed photo from the wall. The camera zooms in on it and shows that the photograph is of three faces... Isawa Miyagawa... The Trashcan Man and Bang Davenport. Miya grins at the photograph and holds it as if it was a precious object. Miyagawa: Those were the days. No stress... people who looked out for each other because all we had was each other. Not any more. Sure Asylum is still alive, but is the spirit of Asylum alive any longer, or has it just become a tag that several people use when wrestling on the same side in tag matches? He waits for a few seconds as if expecting an answer from the rest of the empty room. He then seems to hear an answer, before continuing with his conversation... with himself. Miyagawa: We fought them all... Jake Keeton... Ed... Mike Storm... Clyde. We made them fear us because we were great. And then it happened... or rather, the end happened. PCW restarted in August with New Dawning and Bang wasn’t rehired. Asylum was never the same... it’s amazing what a void a inbred person from Kentucky can create when he is gone. Plus his sister was kinda hot too... just don’t tell Natalia that, you hear? We knew we weren’t that great, but we fought on anyway. And now... Bang is... well I last heard of him several months ago... he’s big in the farming industry, he found some organic stuff that works just as well as chemicals and he’s flogging it at fifty bucks for a barrow full. He called it Essence of Bang... personally I said it sounded like he was selling his own shit... it kinda made me nervous that he just grinned back at me with his tooth missing smile of his. I think he was selling his own shit, but then again, it’s a quick way to make a buck... rather like those blokes from fight club with the bags of fat. He reaches down and quickly types something into the keyboard, and hammers enter a few times with his index finger on his right hand, as if to make some sort of point even clearer, both to himself and to the unknown audience. Miyagawa: Oh this? I’m just writing an email to Natalia’s father... or rather, I’m writing a letter to the Russian Consulate to translate into Russian to send on to Natalia’s father as a telegram. I’m inviting him over to see our daughter when she is born... **looks around rather shifty**... make that our son, but don’t tell Natalia that either. It’s going to be great... a few years and I can teach him how to play basketball and football and all that stuff. He stands there for a few more seconds before he reaches up and scratches his chin. Miyagawa: I guess I’d better get some practice in... my slam dunking sucks. I’ll ask Boda to give me some lessons. No wait, he jobs to people who don’t even wrestle... I’ll track done Michael Jordan... or maybe I could do some trading of coaching with Dennis Rodman... I’ll show the guy how to wrestle properly... no wait, even I’m not that good. Something on the computer makes a loud “bing” noise. It’s called the speakers for all of you people reading this on a Dreamcast (ok, only the one then). Miyagawa reaches down towards the console and picks up a microphone with a rather long lead. With the other hand he hits the enter button hard again. Miyagawa: Mr. Hawking, is this on? A voice comes over the speakers... a robotic voice... it’s that voice... it’s Professor Stephen Hawking, author of Life the Universe and Everything. No wait that was Douglas Adams, Hawking wrote A Brief History of Time. Or was that Adams... damn, I’m so confused. Terry Pratchett wrote the Hobbit, right? Shite! Damn, ok but Tolkein defiantly wrote about Discworld, correct? And Hawking did the Johnny and the Bomb series... oh fuck it. Let’s just get on with the promo and leave my piss poor knowledge of books alone, ok? Hawking (in his extremely robotic voice): Hell-o the-re Mi-ya-ga-wa. It is nice to see you. In a fig-ure of spee-ch of cou-rse. Miyagawa: Hey wassup Professor, how’s it hanging? Hawking: Do not try to stree-t jive me you-ng man, I am down with th-em ho’s in da hoo-d big sty-le. So how is you hang-ing th-en my man? Miyagawa: Can’t complain, can’t complain. Have you finished your new book, a Brief History of Time: The Next Generation yet? I keep saying you should bring out a movie of that... make it nine million years long, get Peter Jackson to shoot it in New Zealand or something. Hawking: Ve-ry fun-ny ass-hole. Miyagawa: Hey, I aim to please, ya know? Ok, enough with the street jive crud. Scientifically speaking is there such a thing as an angel. Hawking: The on-ly def-i-ni-tion of an an-gel that I can sci-en-tif-ic-all-y prove is that of a low fly-ing pi-ece of cr-ap on an air-cra-ft-‘s ra-dar. So that is the on-ly type of an-gel in cur-rent exis-tance. Miyagawa: A simple no would have sufficed. Hawking: No. Are you hap-py now? Miyagawa: Very. That means that Drake Maxwell is scientifically a piece of low flying debris on an aircraft’s radar. That’s fantastic, and I’ve got the best mind in the industry to prove it for me. Did I ever tell you that you have got this great voice for radio? Hawking: Go to hell, Mi-ya-ga-wa. And I rea-lly mean that. Miyagawa: Bah, Hell would kick me out for being too much of a revolutionist. I'd end up making the demons revolt to install a democratic system of goverment. So what about the other thing, Tim Walden? Hawking: You’-re go-ing to lose big time. Wal-den is six times the wrest-ler you are. Have you seen the spee-d at which he lays down to let oth-ers pin him? It’s amaze-ing... he rea-lly is at the top of his indus-try in this. He makes Hank look like an amat-eur. Miyagawa: Do did you say? Hawking: Hank. I said Hank. Miyagawa: Sorry, can you say that louder please? Hawking: HANK~! HANK I SAY~! HANK~! Miyagawa: Sorry Stevie, I just wanted to check. I still reckon you could be a massive rap star... why you could just hit that little program of yours, and then sod off backstage to drink beer, smoke some fags and get down and dirty with some groupies. Westsidaz featuring the Professor H. I can see it now... number ones... massive crowds... Hawking: Max-well put it ri-ght. You are not fun-ny. Miyagawa: I’m very funny. I’m funny in the head. Everyone knows that. Hawking: That is the most accu-rate thing I have heard you say all day, Mi-ya-ga-wa. Miyagawa: It’s all coming together, my plans are working and now I only have two title defenses of my TV title this week, and a World Title shot on March Madness on Sunday. Christ, who the hell am I kidding? I’m screwed! Nelson has overbooked me on purpose, so that Snow can retain! Damn him! Damn him and his infernal plans! Some jackass like Boda is going to win and ruin that belt for an eon. Boda would damage that belt more than Stryfe or Kriegman ever did! We need someone truly old school to win... and that is me. We’ve had the relatively new people win it recently, with a Ivan hold leading to a Jeremy Howard hold leading to now a Jason Snow hold. Well it’s about time I held it, heck I’ve screwed up less chances than Boda has, right, so surely my time is here and it has come. Well damn it I ‘m going to friggin take it, and become a double champion... and then after Tuesday... I’ll give the TV Title away to whoever wins that six pack match at the PPV... even Walden if he somehow scrapes a win. Miyagawa glances down at the PC and notices that the modem disconnected some twenty seconds ago. He simply sighs and removes the microphone. Miyagawa: Screw this for a game of pinball, I’ve got some training to do. And with that thought, the camera slowly fades out as it always does... in fact, if I was a camera, I couldn’t keep the job for that long... far too repetitive for me. I like variety. But that’s just me, and as a narrator I get that, but they don’t like me talking a lot... they tend to cut me off. But I run the risk of doing tha...
