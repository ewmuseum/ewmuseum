The Dance of Death

29 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10.29.02 First Presbyterian Church, Downtown Chicago… -A quaint stream of technicolored sunlight filters in through the stained glass windows, bathing the gothic cathedral in an aura of supreme serenity that causes a kaleidoscope of spectral rainbows to dance across the marble floor, the carved visage of Jesus Christ at its center. The pews lie empty; the sound of some far-off choir rehearsing in the massive sprawl of crossing hallways drifts in from the ornate double sanctuary doors, the language something resembling Latin. The graceful lull of the archaic tongue wafts through the massive domed sanctuary as if carried by wings, resounding off the gilt and livery that sprawls up the wooden walls. Twenty-foot tapestries accompany the matching stained windows, the early morning sun exposing multicolored dust motes in the air before them.- -Sitting with his head in his palms, Rich “The Renegade” Rollins leans against the dark marble base of a wall, his grainy brown hair falling in tangles around his shoulders. Dressed down in casual blue jeans and a white tank top, he rests with his knees pulled up near his chest which expands in constant rhythm, smooth and calculated. In a half-whisper he begins to speak, more to himself than anything else.- Renegade: If I’ve learned anything in my twenty-five years on this planet, it’s to never second guess yourself. You are always the most important asset to yourself… that’s what dad told me before he went to Vietnam. But that was a lifetime ago, another world away. What I need to figure out is… where does that world end, and mine begin? -Pulling his head up, Rich Rollins surveys the calm sanctuary with a cold gaze, his deep blue eyes burning like twin coals in the soft sunlight. Slipping his red bandanna over his wild hair, The Renegade poises himself to stand before…- Voice: My granddad always told me that talking to yourself wasn’t a crime until you answered back. You’ve been sitting there quite a while, Mr. Rollins. Have you found the answers you are looking for? -The Renegade scans the massive domed room, searching for the man behind the echoing voice. Not a soul save himself is near… but the voice sounds close, almost right in front of him.- Voice: Yes, I know who you are. Quite the popular wrestler, and talented to boot. But most people don’t appreciate such things in my profession. Renegade: (cautiously, still searching) And what profession is that? Sneaking up on people and askin’ them rhetorical questions? -The voice laughs, not an unkind sound. It vibrates off the high walls with a clear ring, almost like thousands of bells tolling at once.- Voice: A question is only rhetorical if the answer is, too. I don’t think you have any rhetorical answers to give me, Rich Rollins. Renegade: Where in the bloody hell are you? Voice: Walk down the center aisle. Hiding in plain sight is always the best place to lose oneself. Or, so my granddad told me. -Slowly, Rollins stands and walks about ten paces, his heavy black boots thundering in the hushed cathedral. Just as the man promised, a single figure sits cross-legged near the steps to the front of the sanctuary, his body shrouded in a monk’s cloak. Hunched over the floor, the figure holds a paintbrush loosely in his right hand, half a dozen jars of paint scattered around him. Curiosity takes the better part of The Renegade’s mind as he walks through the abandoned pews, looking over the man’s shoulder to see what he is doing.- -Startled, Rollins almost wishes he hadn’t.- -Sprawled out along an empty section of white marble flooring is the picture of a group of men, all reaching to the sky in torment and agony. A small bonfire blazes in the center, the black persona of Death hovering just above its fiery fingers. Confused, The Renegade inhales deeply.- Renegade: What is that supposed to be? Painter: The Dance of Death. Renegade: (pointing) And that one is Death? Painter: Yes, he dances off with all of them. -The strange figure’s words do nothing to ease Rollins’ mind, as the graphic depiction of Death contrasts harshly with the beautiful tapestries and paintings elsewhere. From where he stands The Renegade can see the impression of Jesus Christ less than ten feet away, his arm reaching “up,” seemingly through the two-dimensional picture and into the living world.- Renegade: Why would you paint something like that in the middle of a church? Painter: (simply) I thought it would remind people that they must die. Renegade: (sarcastically) Well, it ain’t gonna make them feel any better. -Still keeping his face shrouded behind the monk’s hood, the painter continues his work. His voice changes slightly, more of an admonition than the cordial tone of before.- Painter: Why should one always make people happy? It might not be a bad idea to scare them a little once in a while. Renegade: Then they’ll just close their eyes. They don’t have to look at it. -The figure responds coolly, his voice tinted with the trace of a smile.- Painter: Oh, they’ll look. A skull is almost more interesting than a naked woman. Renegade: But, if you scare them… Painter: They’ll think. Renegade: (becoming frustrated) But if you make them think… Painter: Then they’ll become still more scared. -Running one hand through his thick brown hair, Rollins shakes his head as he watches the figure etch out the next contorted face on the marble floor, his hand moving with a flawless grace over the smooth impromptu canvas. Speaking frankly, The Renegade responds.- Renegade: You’re insane. Painter: Perhaps. But that’s none of your business, now is it? Renegade: (pushing on) Then why are you only painting this Dance of Death? Painter: I’m only painting things as they are. Everyone else can do as they like. Renegade: (shaking his head) This is really gonna piss people off, and you know it. -The painter only shrugs his covered shoulders, continuing his artful masterpiece.- Painter: Maybe. But then I’ll just paint something amusing for them to look at. I have to make a living somehow… at least until the plague takes me. -Suddenly becoming very wary of this odd individual, The Renegade stiffens. He looks down at the dark figure, a mental alarm clamoring inside his head.- Renegade: The plague? What the hell are you talking about? -Without warning, the figure turns around and tosses back his black hood. There, staring Rich Rollins straight in the eye, is a man whose age is impossible to determine for all the cracked skin and shriveled patches under his eyes. A few strands of dark hair cling to his rough head, raw with blisters and scars. The Renegade instantly takes a few steps back, instinctively putting a hand against his nose. Speaking more calm than Rollins can imagine possible, the figure continues.- Painter: You should see the boils on a diseased man’s throat. You should see how his body shrivels up so that his legs look like knotted strings – like the man I’ve painted over there. -The painter points with his paintbrush. Laying near the edge of the circle is a small human form writhing in the grass, its eyes turned upward in a frenzied look of horror and pain. The man is an exact replica of the painter himself.- -Now, more than ever, Rich Rollins wishes he were somewhere else.- Renegade: (avoiding eye contact) That… looks terrible. Painter: (amused) It certainly does. He tries to rip out the boil, he bites his hands, tears his veins open with his fingernails and his screams can be heard everywhere. Does that scare you? Renegade: Scare? Me? You don’t have a clue as to who I am, pal. Painter: The remarkable thing is that the poor creatures think the pestilence is the Lord’s punishment. Mobs of people who call themselves Slaves of Sin are swarming over this godforsaken city, flagellating themselves and others, all for the glory of God. -Rollins laughs, although no humor enters his voice.- Renegade: Yeah, they call it Catholicism. Painter: Do not joke about that which you do not understand, Rich Rollins. -Turning his eyes away from the grotesque painting, The Renegade licks his dry lips, trying to work moisture back into his throat. It’s not the lesions and tears on the man’s body that unsettles Rollins; an ungodly aura of finality emanates from the painter’s very presence, placing an uncomfortable lump deep into Rollins’ parched throat. Trying to lighten the mood, The Renegade speaks.- Renegade: I don’t suppose you have anything to drink with a little kick to it, do you? I haven’t had a thing to drink all day, and my throat is killing me. -Looking into Rollins’ face with black eyes that challenge the intensity of the sun itself, the cracked lips of the painter part in a wry smile.- Painter: I think I frightened you after all. ~Fin~
