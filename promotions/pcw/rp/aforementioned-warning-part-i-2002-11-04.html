Aforementioned Warning, Part I

4 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11.4.02 Downtown Chicago… -After a brief introduction from both attorneys, the trial of Jason Rollins, known the world over as the Slipknot Assassin, begins.- -A thick air of anticipation hangs over the packed courtroom like a blanket, seemingly almost visible under the pale white florescent light. Someone in the back of the observation area coughs; the tension builds as Judge Thomson adjusts a stack of papers at his desk, eyeing the mixed array of jurors. He pounds his gavel once, addressing the prosecuting attorney Harold Guffman.- Judge: The Prosecutor’s witness may approach the bench. -Taking the cue, a young woman of about twenty-three adjusts her pressed blue blouse, nervously avoiding the eyes of the man sitting in an orange jumpsuit and chains at the defendant’s table. Walking up to the bailiff, local radio personality Cindy Freedman swears to tell the truth, the whole truth, and nothing but the truth.- -Taking her seat on the witness’ stand, Miss Freedman looks solemnly ahead, still avoiding the eyes of Jason Rollins. The jurors watch her tired face closely, noticing the dark bags under her eyes as a tale-tell indication of many sleepless nights. Clearing her throat, Cindy begins her testimony.- Cindy: On the evening of September 6th, I went to a party at America’s Pub with my radio station’s producer, Jack Franklin. We were there for PR and all that… mingling at the annual Swinging September Bash the Pub has. I told Jack to walk on ahead, because I needed to fix my make-up. When I was ready, I began to walk out of the parking garage when I heard a voice behind me, or in front of me… I really couldn’t tell you. It came from everywhere, and it was so dark. (her voice cracks as she tries to remain calm) The voice asked me if I was Cindy Freedman, and I said yes. Before I knew it, a powerful arm was around my throat, and someone stuffed a chloroformed rag in my face. I blacked out… and the next thing I remember is being tied by my hands and feet, with duct tape over my mouth. -She pauses, unnecessarily smoothing her blouse to escape the peering eyes of the jury. Dabbing at her red, moist eyes with a Kleenex, she chokes back a sob as the memories overwhelm her carefully constructed composure.- Judge: Continue whenever you’re ready, Miss Freedman. -Cindy looks at the judge and nods her head, sniffling a little. Sitting up straighter in the uncomfortable wooden chair, she takes a deep breath before continuing.- Cindy: I was held captive for four days, or so I’m told. I honestly don’t know how long I was in this dark room, but it seemed like four years. This… figure kept watching me from the corner, sometimes talking to himself, and sometimes talking to me. He rambled about stuff I don’t remember, some poetry or music, something like that. I knew I was going to die, because he just let me starve. He never raped me, or even touched me. It was when he poured… (she chokes up again, but continues)… he poured gasoline over me, and stuffed my head in a burlap sack, that I knew. He loaded me in his van and started driving, but I don’t know to where. That’s when the accident happened, and his van crashed into another van. I fell out of the vehicle and just started rolling, too afraid to move on my own because I thought I was already dead. That’s when Rich Rollins pulled the sack off my head, thinking I was somebody else. I honestly don’t know what happened in the crash, but somehow I escaped and another woman was taken in my place. When I looked around, the van I had been loaded in was nowhere to be seen. -Rising from his seat next to the shackled defendant, defense attorney Ozwald Grant clears his throat, approaching the bench at the judge’s consent. He speaks precisely, well prepared for a case he knows he can’t win.- Grant: Miss Freedman, you say the van was nowhere to be seen. But you stated that you were confined in a sack…? Cindy: A burlap bag, yes. Grant: (nodding) Then would you please explain to the court how you would be able to identify the vehicle in question? -Cindy boldly meets his challenging glare, unflinching despite the dark circles under her eyes.- Cindy: He didn’t put the bag on me until I was already in the van. I was tied up, and never got a good look at his face… but I recognize his voice. And I identified the van as the property of Jason Rollins. Grant: Right, so you did. No further questions, Your Honor. -Returning to his seat next to Jason Rollins, Ozwald Grant tries to hide the helpless frown on his face, but fails miserably. Aimlessly leafing through a manila portfolio, he absently wonders whom he’d pissed off to draw this case.- -With her testimony finished, Cindy Freedman is escorted from the courtroom by a security officer, her high-heeled shoes echoing in the painfully quiet silence. She steps briskly, looking down to avoid the set of eyes she feels at the base of her neck. The feeling unsettles her stomach, but she refuses to acknowledge that damnable stare with one of her own.- -Following her departure with his gaze, Jason Rollins smiles.- ~~ ~~ ~~ “Christ, I thought I’d never get out of there.” -Shakily lighting a cigarette, Cindy leans against the cold stone wall outside the courthouse, her curly tufts of long red hair blowing in the biting wind. Standing with his arms crossed firmly before him, Rich “The Renegade” Rollins studies her face carefully, pushing down a deep sense of guilt that bubbles in his stomach.- -After all, it was his brother that kidnapped the poor woman.- -Inhaling the death stick deeply, Cindy lets out a suppressed sigh as she looks up at The Renegade, returning his frown.- Cindy: Don’t pull that self-blame shit on yourself, Rollins. You’re brother is a lunatic, and you have no control over that. You can’t feel sorry for someone else’s mistakes. You saved my life… he tried to end it. Renegade: You make it sound so much lighter than it is. -Rollins kicks at the concrete with the toe of his boot, turning away from her penetrating stare. Her eyes were still red from the emotional testimony, which made the image that much harder to take. Sensing his discomfort, Cindy tries to change the subject.- Cindy: So, we’re still on for the interview tomorrow night, right? Renegade: Yeah, we’re still on. PCW only, right? Nothing about my brother and this (he motions toward the courthouse)… stuff? Cindy: (nodding) I don’t feel like discussing my brush with death with the entire city of Chicago, least not anytime soon. Shedding a little happy light on our mutual existence would do us both some good, so we’ll keep it wrestling-related. I promise. -She smiles, the first in a long while. Even with her tear-streaked face, her gentile nature shines through her dual green eyes, drawing a grin from even The Renegade’s stony visage.- Renegade: All right, then it’s on. Tomorrow night… I’ll see you then. -Pushing off from the stone wall, Rich Rollins casually mixes into the bustling crowd of downtown tourists and businessmen, strolling off into the heart of the city he calls home.- ~Fin~
