Dorn Tracer vs. The Greek Gods: Part 1

4 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2 - 4 - 03 Mt. Olympus. Seat of extraordinary power and haven to drama, adventure, and stories that will never be forgotten. Forever enshrined within the mystical tower of marble and rock is a unimaginable world of beautiful Goddesses, powerful Gods, and horrifying monsters that only the those with a varied imagination would be able to conjure up such a vision. Beings of untold power and masterful cunning call this place “home”, as it has been their seat of power for quite some time. The Greeks once considered the mammoth of stone a forbidden place of heroes, Gods, and villains. Only those worthy of being the presence of divine beings were allowed to step foot in the glowing aura that surrounded the beautiful palace of Olympus. Those days are long gone, of course. Beautiful marble and lime statues of the power denizens of Mt. Olympus once blessed the halls of temples and palaces alike in Ancient Greece, but no more. The force known as Christianity stormed its way into the humble halls of Greek temples and forever changed their way of life. Soon, the legends of Mt. Olympus were forgotten, and the Gods were cast aside as mere fictitious characters that were only used to put the young ones to sleep. A grave injustice, in the eyes of the Gods themselves. For years on end, they held the imagination of an entire race of people, inspiring them to great things and becoming the very apex of the Greek civilization. When the mighty Greeks were at their peak, the Gods were heralded as the bringers of gold and glory. The Greeks claimed they could not have done it without their divine protectors. Even the once beautiful city of Athens boasted being named after the goddess Athena, who had battled Poseidon, the God of the Sea, for rights to own the city. Oh yes, all that is in the past. And frankly, the Gods don’t like it. High atop Mt. Olympus, those same beings still sit. Zeus still sits upon his mighty marble throne, lightning bolt in one hand and a staff of royalty in the other. But he sits not with his chin high and his mind full of wonderful thoughts, but sits grimly, with a feel of depression and apathy washing over his body like the high tide of the sea. He can feel nothing but woe as he sits upon his dimmed throne, merely staring at the marble floor of his beautiful palace. “Alas, it’s been so long since a sacrifice was made…” The deity mumbles to himself, his voice losing its once mighty boom. “I’ve almost forgotten what the sweet voices of my disciples sounded like.” “Oh quit your bitching, you big baby!” A shrill voice cries. The giant, wooden double doors of the elaborate throne room burst open with an untold force, causing a great light to spill into the room. The same voice emits from within the light. “I’m sick of all your moping around and crying!” The God of Thunder sighs and cradles his chin within his huge, rough hand. His elbow is propped upon the arm of the throne and he leans to the side somewhat, dismissing any godly posture he once allowed himself to have. “Oh Hera, you do not understand,” Zeus groans sadly. “I’ve felt so alone for so long. I cannot remember what it’s like to have someone yell your name in pure orgas-“ “WHAT?!” The Goddess exclaims in fury. She bursts forth from the light with a fiery red in her eyes. Her silky brown hair flails about as if possessed by demons and her tiara saunters around on her head, trying to stay steady. Zeus realizes what he was about to see and clears his throat quickly, trying to ramify his misjudgment and overall foolishness. “To have someone yell your name in pure faith, my dear.” “Uh huh, you fucking player!” Hera shrieks, a tone of dragon-like fury hissing from deep within her throat. She stomps over to Zeus and folds her arms across her chest, causing her flaring white sleeves to flutter in the wind of divinity that still hangs within the air. “Don’t give me that shit! You think I’ve forgotten all the little hootches you’d bring home, screw, then dump out the door like nothing happened?!” The Thunder God’s eyes shift around nervously and he clears his throat once more. He pounds the side of his fist into his broad chest, trying to bring forth any distraction that will deter from the current conversation. But Hera isn’t one to be ignored, and Zeus knows that. He looked into the fiery eyes of his wife for some sign of forgetfulness, but the Queen merely stares back with a venomous intention. “I suppose I may have done such a thing,” Zeus exclaims, rather nervously. “But what does it matter? It’s all in the past. Fate has dealt us such a cruel, cruel hand…” Zeus droops back into his self-pity demeanor and sighs again. Hera just rolls her eyes and shakes her pretty head. Suddenly, another divine presence bursts forth into the room. The figure comes crashing in at lightning speed and flies about in the air, leaving a trail of buzzing electricity in its wake. Zeus takes absolutely no notice while Hera covers her head, trying to prevent premature decapitation. “STOP IT!” She cries, trying to get the intruder to cease and desist. “COME DOWN THIS INSTANT, OR I’LL CAUSE YOUR GENITELS TO TURN INTO MUSH!” The awe-inspiring speed of the force begins to slow down as the figure completes its circling of the room and bounds towards the floor. It comes crashing down onto the marble and rebounds right back up, flying towards Hera. Bewildered and suddenly frightened, Hera shields herself with her thin arms, trying to ward away the sudden attack. Her mighty husband just looks away and sighs, his eyes sad and pitiful. “AHHHHH!! NO!” The Queen lets out a blood-curdling shriek as the lightning zooms forth towards her, only mere seconds from crashing into her. She closes her eyes and awaits impact, but no such thing happens. Hera waits for a moment or so before coming out from behind her arm shield and gazing at the intruder. And it’s none other than Hermes, their messenger. The Queen Goddess starts to fume from all holes in her face, clasping her teeth together tightly and slowly burying her head into the collar of her dress. She tries to hold back any violent outburst or cruel intention to turn Hermes into Richard Simmons, but she seems to be failing. The grin on Hermes’ glittery face doesn’t help, either. “Hello…Hermes…” She hisses, trying to control herself. Hermes doesn’t seem to notice. “Hullooo! My, me lady, you sure are looking positively radiant!” Hermes beams, with a big goofy smile on his clean, boyish face. “As do you, sire…” His tone when referring to Zeus is lower and somewhat suggestive, but gets pretty much no reaction from the Thunder God other than a simple greeting. “Hello, Hermes…” Zeus groans. “What do you want, boy?” Hera growls at him, angered by the intrusion. “Well, me lady, I’ve got a message ‘ere from some bloke that wants to see you ‘an Zeusy.” Hermes beams again, his voice high and chirpy with excitement. “’e claims to ‘ave some sort of business deal with you two.” Zeus merely waves a hand dismissingly and turns away from Hera and Hermes, looking to the beautifully decorated marble wall and joined columns. “I care not, Hermes.” “Yes, send him away. He have no patience for visitors at the moment,” Hera exclaims in a rather snobby manner. Hermes gives them both a confused smirk and pushes his white traveler’s cap aside to scratch his beautiful blonde hair. He flutters off the ground and cocks his head at an inquiring angle. “You wish me to send ‘im away?” Hermes asks, despite knowing the answer. “Yes, yes! Immediately! Are you deaf??” The Queen Goddess snarls, losing all semblance of patience she has left for the annoying, homosexual messenger. “And do not come back upon completing your task, Hermes!” The young man sighs and props his hands on his hips, looking away and rolling his eyes. “As you wish, me lady. Anything else?” Hera smirks angrily, a look of venom-filled irritation in her eyes, and points beyond the door. Hermes sighs and slumps away, fluttering out of the door. The mighty wooden portals slam shut behind him, sending an echoing wave thundering through the throne room. Hera straightens out her garb and leans against the throne, using her under arm as support. “I really hate him, Zeus,” Hera exclaims, with apathy spilling from her voice. “Why must he continue to be here?” Zeus remains steadfast in his look away position and just shrugs his burly shoulders. “I know not, dear…” “Why don’t you get rid of him, then?” “Because the young man still has a fruitful life ahead of him,” Zeus says grimly. “Unlike I, the misbegotten God of Thunder…” Hera rolls her eyes in irritation and shakes her head. Zeus’ self-pity parties have been much more frequent lately, as even the various learning centers across the globe have begun to distance themselves from explaining Greek mythology. The Gods and Goddesses of Olympus are becoming specters… but that’s no reason from Lord of the Sky to slump around like an alcoholic without his fix. “Zeus, you’ve GOT to come out of this stupid ass slump you’ve been in,” Hera demands, a mighty force behind her tone. “You never listen to me anymore. In fact, you don’t even LOOK at me!!” The Thunder God doesn’t respond. He just exhales with a sorrow-filled tone and continues to gaze at the ever-present wall. Hera throws her hands up in defeat and shakes her ahead again. “The things I do to keep this family together…” She mumbles below her breath. Suddenly, Hermes comes fluttering back into the room, followed by a couple of armor-clad soldiers. The huge, red fans on their helmets are unmoving as their steel boots clank against the marble. The insignia of an eagle is painted upon their torso armor as they come marching into the room, one of them carrying a basket. Zeus slowly comes out of his self-pity demeanor and gives Hermes and the soldiers a wondering eye. Hera just rolls her eyes once more, displeased at the intrusion. “What is it now Her-“ “Me lady, horrible news! Oh, so horrible!” The messenger exclaims, a look of terror upon his face. Hera raises an eyebrow and begins to believe that something really but be rather urgent for Hermes to have come bursting back into the room in this condition. The presence of the stone-faced soldiers declares the same. “What is it, Hermes?” Hera inquires, a slight quiver of worry in her voice. “Me lady, urgent news from Thebes! Look!” Hermes exclaims. He motions towards the two soldiers, who slowly march up to the throne of Zeus. They hand the messenger the basket, then take a step back away from the action. Hera waves them a way and looks at the basket, rather puzzled. “What is this?” The queen asks. Hermes lets his head slump and begins to snivel, obviously very frightened and sorrowed by whatever has taken place. He hands the basket to Hera, unable to bring himself to open it. The Queen Goddess takes the basket and gives a concerned eye to Zeus, who doesn’t seem to care. He nods towards the basket and leans against the arm of the throne, watching quietly. Hera gives a long, slow exhale and removes the cloth cover from the woven basket. A letter sits upon a large, rounded object wrapped in white cloth. Cradling the basket in her arm, she removes the letter and flips it open. One religion at a time. Signed,‘The Lunis Superia’ Dorn Tracer Hera hands the letter to Hermes and gives her husband, the Sky God, and strange look. “Who’s Dorn Tracer?” She asks, her voice bloated with confusion. “I know not,” replies Zeus, not really all that concerned. “See what he sent us.” Hera nods and stares down at the white object. A wave of concern and suspicion begins to arise in her body. Something… somewhere, deep in her divine soul, is telling her NOT to open it. But what could it be? Surely it has to be important if it came all the way to Mt. Olympus from the underground city of Thebes. Delicately, she moves her fingers towards the tab of clot that will unravel this mysterious. The tension in the air is thick as butter, as Zeus and Hermes look on. Hermes is twisting his traveler’s cap in his hand tightly, growing more and more nervous as the Queen inches towards the object. “Well, here it goes,” Hera says quietly, letting out a breath before gripping the tab and pulling the cloth away. And as soon as Hera catches sight of the object, she lets go of the basket and turns away, letting out a disgusted gasp. She covers her eyes and leans against the throne, unable to bring herself to look at it again. Hermes lets out a high-pitched yelp and turns away as well. Zeus slowly leans forward and looks into the basket, and as soon as he does, his eyes widen in horror. “It’s… our only remaining priest…” Hera exclaims, tears flowing freely from her beautiful eyes. Zeus sighs and leans back into the throne, rubbing his forehead with his rough fingers. “Who could do such a thing…” Zeus mumbles. If only he knew, for the same person… is coming for him next. [End.]
