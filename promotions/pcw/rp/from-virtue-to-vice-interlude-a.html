From Virtue to Vice: Interlude (A)

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 3rd of 2003 A.D. The treacheries of the forbidden cosmos, obscenely tantalizing and feral in nature, often seduce mortals of depraved mind into perpetrating the most vile acts against humanity. Promising unheard of power and wisdom, men are beguiled by the prospect of otherworldly power and vow service to the dark gods and their abyssal servitors in return for these gifts. Some later atone for their grievances while others descend into the darkness forever, lost eternally in the great void between life and death. To revere those daemons whom lurk about the outer nebulas can bring only pain in the end, and unfortunately many men never realize this truth until death overcomes and they find their souls drawn into unyielding conjugation with the dark ones. Woe by unto those swayed into everlasting service to the demon-sultan Azathoth.. .. .. Bruce Shanahan, my long-time friend, has been averted from the light and consciously chosen to become a minion of the universe’s more sinister denizens. I have received occasional correspondence from my one-time ally in recent months, and his rhetoric continues to be tainted by a seething animosity for mankind and sympathy for the cosmic immortals he supports. I am loathe to believe Bruce lost to the forces of darkness; one does not support the cause of liberating humanity from their clutches for so long and have no hope for re-conversion.... A light rapping at the door interrupted Father Randall Craven from his journal writings. He lifted his head and aligned his eyes on the fair-haired, muscle-tinged gentleman poised in mid-knock at the threshold. The youth’s green eyes flashed as he expelled a chuckle. “Knock, knock, Randall.” Craven drew himself up in his leather chair and grinned broadly. “Nice to see you, Marcus. Please pull up a seat, I wasn’t expecting you so soon.” Marcus crossed over the faded carmine rug and took a seat in a polished beechwood chair opposite Craven’s desk, leaning back and setting his eyes on a portrait of a halo-adorned Christ that had served as a backdrop for the clergyman for years. As the reverend fumbled with a folder in the rust-gray file cabinet to his right, Marcus smiled. “Nice area around here,” he commented. “Less traffic, quiet…..” “Possibly, but it’s not home yet,” remarked Craven solemnly, swiveling back to face his caller. “But the Vatican felt it safer to relocate me after what has happened in recent months. But the neighborhood isn’t my taste, in all honesty.” “Pfft,” scoffed the other. “It’s a heck of a lot friendlier. Never once got a ‘hello’ from passerby back when I visited in Boston….” “Precisely,” the Catholic priest interjected. “Preaching to the converted isn’t suited to my style. I can’t do the sort of good here I could on the outskirts of Boston’s slums. They stuck me in the pampered end of Philly where an alley mugging headlines the evening news. There’s no challenge advocating the faith in a neighborhood where prayer is already part of local dogma.” Marcus, feinting a sort of understanding, merely shrugged as a manilla file was passed into his lap. He thumbed it open to reveal a set of paper-clipped documents and a pair of shadowy photographs. “As much as I’d prefer this to be a mere social visit, Captain Stryker, I fear it’s much more serious.” The captain’s expression was of earnest as he perused the records. A familiar name popped up several times before revealing the nature as to its purpose, and upon reaching that location in the account he let out a sharp whistle. “Desmond Muldoon huh?” Craven was somber as he nodded in reply, his hands clasped on his desk. “Surely the name rings a bell?” Stryker acknowledged in the positive. His mouth had gone dry upon reading the report and his stomach had subsequently sunk studying the aftermath of the situation described within. “Yeah, guy was from Buffalo. Masqueraded as a banker or loan officer or somethin’. Vital informant for the Blackhelm Coalition, operative for the Delta Green organization to my understanding. Good man. Did charity work and all that junk.” Craven casually pulled himself up and paced across the room. “A very good man. He caught his wife cheating on him and was devastated, tried to kill the man with her when he walked in the room. There was nothing truly artificial about that part of the scenario. But from there…..” He gestured to the report in Stryker’s hand, prompting the military officer to read further into the exposition. He took several moments, but when his eyes widened Craven knew he had hit the jackpot. “It was all a setup??” He took the priest’s silence as affirmation. “He knew the sect was based in northern Vermont and he went in without support? Dammit, Randall, what about the attempted murder? The partners of Muldoon’s that she slept with?” “Muldoon was incapable of killing the man, the lamp he reportedly wielded has been switched with a fake earlier in the day,” resumed Craven. “She was far from oblivious to the situation. She wasn’t too different from Desmond, Marcus.” “How’s that? She was a housewife caught between a rock and a hard place….” “No, captain, she was with the Wilmarth Foundation.” Stryker’s response was one of bewilderment. He nearly tumbled from his seat at the revelation that remained undisclosed even in the report, forcing an amused snicker from Craven. The Wilmarth Foundation, an affiliate of the Blackhelm Coalition, was operated under different specifications and regulations but existed for the same purpose as the unavowed Vatican-headed organization. “Muldoon worked for us and Delta Green, and knew of the cult’s basis in northern Vermont,” explained Craven, drumming his fingers nonchalantly on the windowsill. Krissy however worked for Wilmarth, unbeknownst to Muldoon. She had the same information. The ‘second honeymoon’ was a double-operation; Muldoon used the trip to his and our advantage, and went in thinking Kristen knew nothing of the cult, driven by the dual intention of making amends with his wife and investigating the black faction on the side. Kristen went in with the sole intention of investigating the sect. She knew of his intents, but he knew nothing of hers. She recommended the lodge as their honeymoon destination under the pretense they needed a remote place to “bond again”. He took it, both used the opportunity.” “Reminds me of that Schwarzenegger movie,” quipped Stryker. “True Lies?” “Yeah, that one.” “Kristen was expecting it all, she was never a victim of anything. She manipulated Desmond’s partners into the situation. She had a distinct charm and wit to go with her beauty, an uncanny knack of influencing men. The Foundation used that to their advantage several times, and she used it for this objective. She was never wholly innocent or victimized, at least not until things went awry. Obviously the Sect of Black Wisdom is far more powerful than we suspected. And considering the group’s hierarchy and leadership, that is a vastly frightening prospect.” Stryker was agitated, feeling left out of the loop by the tone of Craven’s disclosure. He brusquely shut the file and gazed at Craven, whose inner sentiments had brought a bead of sweat to his brow. “What am I not being told, Randall? Who the hell leads this group?” Father Craven tilted his head back and sighed, setting his eyes on the ashen ceiling. Nary a word crept from his lips, and from his demeanor and silence Stryker drew the conclusion which prompted a muffled curse from his lips. “Bruce……” Craven, unwilling to lock eyes with Stryker, could only nod his verification. “Read the dossier on the matter, Captain Stryker….I’ll pull some strings and have it made available to you. I’m sorry, but Bruce is the catalyst behind the recently intensified activity in the sector. That’s why we can’t allow you or Delta Fox to be involved in this situation. There’s simply too much history, especially with his rescuing you several months ago during his tenure as one of our agents.” “I’m a soldier, Randall. What the fuck makes you think I can’t deal with it?” Craven, though sympathetic, was offended by his operative’s language. He spun to face Stryker, whose eyes flared with rage and a diminutive sense of betrayal. “You sit in a house of God, Marcus, need I remind you? Please re-think your choice of words.” “Don’t patronize me, Randall, I know where I am! What are Bruce’s objectives?! What’s he up to in those God-forsaken hills?!” Craven couldn’t help but revel in the irony of the statement. “I think ‘God-forsaken’ sums it up pretty well. He has a vast wilderness to sustain those abominable rituals he supervises in Vermont, Marcus. We don’t know what all he’s doing up there, and beyond his professional wrestling capacities he doesn’t appear often beyond that region. We do know he’s unifying various sects worshipping a fairly diverse set of beings, and thus he has informants saturating the northeast region of the US. Followers of virtually every blasphemous god in the Necronomicon have been integrated into a ‘new order’ of sorts.” “Bruce has lost his mind, it’s tragic….” “Perhaps, but his planning has been brilliant. He’s garnered affiliation with every cult and every obscure order within three-hundred miles. Worshippers of Cthulhu, Hastur, Tsathoggua, Nyarlathotep, Shub-Niggurath…..even the Brotherhood of Xada-Hgla, degenerate worshippers of Azathoth’s lone material avatar located in northern Maine. Kristen and Muldoon were slaughtered in the hills about 30 miles from Rutland, the location of the Sect of Black Wisdom. It wasn’t pretty.” Stryker, a stoic veteran of warfare, coughed as he glanced at one of the attached photos. “Um…..are you even sure it was them? I mean, there wasn’t much left….” “Dental records, skin DNA……yes, Marcus, positive identifications.” Marcus, fighting back bile rising in his throat, shoved the graphic prints back into the confines of the folder. Craven, flipping open the shutters of the east window, shifted his eyes away from his subordinate and through the pane where clouds had begun to shirk and wisp through the skies. “There have been sightings in that area by natives, Marcus. Of living things resembling trees, reeking with the stench of an ancient grave. Only spotted in the bowels of the wilderness by overly curious hikers. I’m sure you catch my drift.” Stryker’s jaw slowly dropped and he pivoted his head to face Craven. The priest’s expression was stern and indifferent. “Bruce…..he’s brought forth the Dark Young…..” “Yes, Marcus. Bruce has come to revere that debauched fertility god, Shub-Niggurath. This is where you and your boys come in. And with you being based in the immediate area, it shouldn’t be a problem for you.” “Investigate the woods and the manor?” “Indeed. Sift through the nearby villages for information, check out the woods and the lodge where Desmond and Kristen were staying. We still don’t know how Bruce got them out of that bungalow and onto that ridge. We want you to stay in that lodge for a few nights actually.” Stryker seemed somehow taken aback at the request. Dangerous missions drew no qualms from the hardened soldier, but for some agonistic reason the demand brought goosebumps to his flesh. “There’s more, but something I can’t detail much right now.” Stryker lofted an eyebrow. “Bruce’s rival, this ‘Skylar Green”…..I don’t have the full story yet, Marcus, but he’s an unusual one. A real enigma to our organization it appears. The Coalition has a file devoted him, and much of it centers around abnormal psychic capacities instilled within him. I’m looking for the full story, but time will tell what I can get out of the ordeal.” Stryker, clutching the dossier in sweaty palms, rose to his feet and bid his leave in silence. “Marcus?” The commander of the Delta Fox unit halted, the sound of Craven’s hollow footsteps resonating through the room. A car rumbled by outside, penetrating the drab silence. “If you find Bruce, and he gets in your way……bear in mind he is our foe. Show no discrimination. If he’s left to plunder the mountains of Vermont with his perverse ceremonies and spread the faith of the damned, he may very well succeed in his cause. “That won’t be an issue, Randall.” “The man saved your life. You know as well as I do it is an issue. You’re not even supposed to know Bruce is the mastermind of the Vermont cult, but I’m breaking regulations to let you know. I want you to be prepared for the possibility of facing down your one-time savior. He’s not the same man anymore, Marcus, his mind has been poisoned by the barbed tongue of Nyarlathotep.” Stryker, his curiosity fulfilled, walked from Craven’s office and out the door. A beam of sunlight briefly illuminated the dreary interior of the reverend’s office. Craven, satisfied yet empty within, was emotionally torn. Bruce’s evil was monstrous and his thirst for vengeance was insatiable. Yet he mourned his lost comrade and what he had become. He’s not lost. I pray he shall someday return to us, and in turn betray the darkness that has deceived him. Hope still lingers…..
