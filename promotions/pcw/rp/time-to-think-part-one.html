Time To Think (Part One)

Date: Oct. 24/02 Since I was a kid, all I wanted to do was go to the University of Michigan when I was old enough. Eventually, that dream came true. However, during my first year there, I began to envision yet another dream . . . and that was to become an elementary school teacher. Graduating from the U of M seventh in my class and with a degree in teaching, I was on my way to realizing this dream as well. Just the thought of someday walking into a classroom filled with dozens of smiling children calling my name and showing me that they learned to tie their shoes kept me going. But somewhere along the way, I took a wrong turn! Now, instead of the smiling faces of children, I have to stare down the beaten, bloody faces of my opponents while I entertain thousands upon thousands of paying audience members all over the world. The whole wrestling thing was definitely not what I expected at all. Now, I find myself driving at furious speeds to reach the airport in time for my flight over to Europe. Figures my first PCW show would have to be in Ramat-Gan, Israel. I had missed the PCW’s company charter flight overseas, so that meant I’d have to drive all the way from my home in Grand Rapids, Michigan to the John F. Kennedy International Airport in New York. This kind fo thing was not in the PCW’s brochure. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The one good thing about traveling so much, was that it really gave me time to think . . . time to plan out a strategy, or simply put everything into perspective. I had crossed the American/Canadian border between Detroit and Windsor, Ontario hours ago . . . and the border between Buffalo and Fort Erie was hours ahead of me. The lights from motorists going in the other direction flew by like lightning fast fireflies, and the highway signs whizzed by so fast I could barely make them out. Seconds turned into minutes, minutes began to seem like hours, and hours felt like long, hard years of agony. The whole time I had been driving, all I could think about was if this was a good idea or not. Traveling hours on end, just to fly halfway around the world, and for what? To get my ass kicked by a complete stranger, in front of hundreds and thousands of people who want to kill me cuz I’m an American? Somehow, it just didn’t seem to be worth it, ya know. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ As I drove, I began comparing the pros and cons of being a professional wrestler. Since I began my wrestling career, I’ve been in the best shape of my life. My stamina and endurance were at an all-time high . . . seemed like nothing could slow me down. But on the other hand, always have an ache or pain somewhere on my body. I’ve received numerous bumps and bruises, almost too many to count. But like I said, it seemed like nothing could slow me down. I’ve learned to fight through the pain, and perform my best. My lower back is continuously throbbing with fiery intensity, like I was being branded by some wrestling cowboy . . . and I swear you could tie the Titanic into port with the knots in my muscles. However, the pain in my back doesn’t really bother me all that much . . . I just have to simply work through it all . . . ya know, mind over matter and all that. One of the other aspects of the wrestling world is the adrenaline . . . the rush you feel when the lights go down your music hits the speakers. I have the worst before match jitters in the world. No matter my opponent, or opponents, the last few minutes before my match seem like an eternity. I just can’t wait to get out to that ring and strut my stuff. And when I step into that ring, I’m like a kid in a candy store. I’m just sooo excited, and my energy almost bursts out the top of my head. But, all that energy and excited can also be a let down. If my match doesn’t go the way I would it to, it can just ruin a whole week’s work and preparation. Like everything else in my life, if I can’t accomplish exactly what I set out to do, I get so down trodden and exasperated. But like they say . . . you gotta take the good with bad. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ With all those thoughts running through my head, and the fact that I was making pretty good time, I decided I’d make a little pit stop in Toronto and decide if this wrestling thing was what I really wanted. I rolled into downtown Toronto around six in the evening, and after ten hours on the road, getting out of the car and stretching my legs felt some damn good. Let me tell you, being cramped up in a 91' Honda Accord for hours on end is not healthy, but it’s something I had to do. Anywho, I found an open parking space on the fifth level of a parkade and began walking. I walked down the narrow stairwell to street level, and just kept walking. I walked around the downtown area like it was a zoo. I’d stop to admire the sights, or to listen to one of the many street performers. I wasn’t really paying much attention to the time, and suddenly I felt free . . . like I didn’t have a care in the world. But somewhere in the back of my mind, I knew there was life altering decision that had to be made . . . and going back to that decision made my few moments of freedom seems as though I was just out for a stroll during my free time, and now I was being put back in my cell. The sun was almost out of sight, and just the top edge of it peeked out from above the SkyDome. With the CN Tower looming ominously in the background, and the orange red glow of the sun filling the sky, it was definitely a sight to see. An old man sitting on a sidewalk bench admiring the sunset as I was, his grandson played with a toy fire engine. Suddenly the young lad looked at me in amazement, and began tugging on my jacket. Young Boy - “Wow mister . . . you’re big enough to be a wrestler!” Old Man - “Now now Tommy, leave the nice man alone.” I told him it was no problem, and stated that I was indeed a wrestler. The young boy’s face lit up like a Jack-O-Lantern on Halloween. He said he never saw me on TV before, but regardless he also asked for my autograph. Tommy - “Can I have your autograph Mr. . . . um?” He dropped his gaze, almost embarrassed that he didn’t know my name. I assured him it was no problem, then began to tell him who I was. Tommy - “Oh, can I have your autograph Mr. Michigan? Then I can tell all my friends I got your autograph before you were popular!” I asked him what he meant by that and he said something that I really didn’t expect him to say. Tommy - “Well, you just look really tough. I think someday you’ll be a really big star, and you’ll have lots and lots and lots and lots of money!!!!” I couldn’t believe this . . . I haven’t even had a match yet, and already people believe I’ve got what it takes to be big! I was amazed . . . speechless almost. When I gathered my wits again, I thanked the kid for his kind words, then made my way back to the parkade where my car was parked. I raced up the narrow stairwell like the world would end if I didn’t reach my car that very instant, but as I stepped out of the stairwell, I looked toward my car, then out toward the edge of the parkade. I walked to the edge of the parkade and gazed out across the seemingly endless Toronto skyline. Then at the top of my lungs, I screamed possibly the most important words that have ever rolled off my tongue . . . “MARK MICHIGAN . . . PCW WORLD HEAVYWEIGHT CHAMPION!!!” . . . yeah, I liked the sound of that.
