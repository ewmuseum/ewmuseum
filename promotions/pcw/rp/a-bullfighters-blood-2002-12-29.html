A Bullfighters Blood

29 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/29/02 A Bullfighters Blood A stadium of screaming fans waiting and anticipating something out of the normal to happen. They want to be shocked and surprised. They want to say, “We were there” or “We witnessed that”. They don’t blink an eye as they look upon the large dirt ring. At one point everyone will be silent, then just seconds later the place will be shaking in mayhem. Every second is important. It’s the type of sport where all it takes is one charge of the horns and everything changes. The people look on hoping. Not hoping that everything goes all right, but rather hoping that everything goes wrong. The shock factor comes into play as these men and woman fill up the stadium. All they really want to see is the bullfighter’s blood. And when it happens it’s drastic. The man once flaunting around and being as charismatic as possible is now being hauled away in a stretcher with a two-foot splash going through his chest. They’re taken to this little room with an inexperienced doctor and they die there. That’s what it takes to be a warrior, and that’s what it takes to get the people love. You can be the best and give everything you have, but until you shed blood for them, they will not love you. Nothing to them is more exciting then the bullfighters blood. The day was December 29, 2002. I stood comfortably numb in front of a cage of carnage. I saw the beasts, the animals that everyone looked at and saw as these beasts of mass destruction. They stay in their cage and they run around angrily not knowing what’s going on. Imagine if someone had just taken you and stuck you in a cage with people you didn’t know. I think I’d be a little upset myself. So I stand there and go eye to eye with this viscous beast. I see these deep eyes that are lowered in sadness. I see a bright light within, showing that there is still hoe alive within this animal. He still believes even though he’s confused. He wishes to be free and back with those who love him. You can tell a novel by just looking into someone eyes. They’re the same tools you use every second of everyday, so we know them pretty well. If you can get a good look into someone’s eyes, you can actually feel the way they’re feeling. I could feel the way this bull was feeling. And it’s not the type of feeling I’d like to live with for the rest of my natural born life. Signor, you must get to your seat, the event is about to start. Thank you good man. I then handed the bull keeper fifty euros and whispered in his ears. Treat them like you’d treat yourself. “Thank you, Thank you ever so much signor.” He said those words as he kissed my hand in happiness. My facial expression had never changed. I made my way back to my seat, accompanied by the man who had brought me here. Where were you going, Sir Borro? Just taking a look at the beasts just before action. Ferocious beings, eh? You’d never know how ferocious they really are. Let me give you the short summary. You see I was still on the run from the police of Barcelona for scientific research. I had come back from my stay in the United States and adopted the new personality of Ricardo Borro. Of course I didn’t know if that was my real name, but it worked. I had a fake birth certificate made for me by a woman I had an “encounter” with. I was now somebody totally different. What I didn’t know was that I was a new bullfighting sponsor. It required the use of a lot of money, but I had enough to burn. I guess that was my punishment for never calling her back. So here I am sitting on my seat during an event I helped sponsor. I didn’t condone of what was going on, but I just had to let it take it’s course. The man to the right of me was a fellow sponsor. The way he spoke to me made me think he was the God of bullfighting. I didn’t know what I was doing here, but I came here through faith. And the message was one that would take time for me to understand. So the match began and everyone was so pumped and ready for action. Smiles all around. Everyone looked happy except the two in the ring. The matador made like he was perfectly calm and happy, but I could tell that he wasn’t. He did some twirls and quick little moves. He held his hands in the air for praise. The people cheered, but not as hard as they would cheer in just a few moments. The matador kept putting on a show and it drew out for a few minutes. The shock was almost gone as the match was just about done. I could notice the actual disappointment in people’s faces. Then it happened. Everything was calm when suddenly the bull made a charge. The matador moved his robe, but not quick enough. The bull had hit the matador right in the middle of his chest. Blood was shed and the bullfighter fell to his feet. The bull stomped on him like a killer putting away his victim. Help entered the ring trying to distract the bull. The same bull I just saw sadness and confusion along with a bit of hope in, was now something different. Their was no longer a bright light in his eye, but more like a red splash of blood in his eye. He was filled with anger and the only thing he wanted to do was hurt. And is that so unreasonable. That one would want to hurt after being hurt. It wasn’t so much the bull that got to me, but the people. The instant the bull hit the matador the crowd rose with their hopes high, knowing that what they wanted to see had happened. Then when they saw the man laying there in blood, those hopes turned to sadness. They all sat back down and looked in caution. They realized “Wow, that could have been me.” The flaunty charismatic man was now lying in his pool of blood, but he got those five seconds of love, so I guess it was all worth it. I guess that’s what it takes to win the crowd. I look at myself as a wrestler and wonder if that’s all I’m worth. I’m a piece of shock value in everyone’s daily appetite. Can I live with that, or must I strive for more? I look back in the ring and I see the bull. He’s surrounded by men and has nowhere to go. The bull charges a man to the side of him, but before he gets there he falls to the ground thunderously. Behind him is a man holding a pistol. The crowd goes wild as the beast falls to the ground. I guess they though he deserved it, but I didn’t. I watched as his eyes rolled to the back of his head and everything he had ever dreamt of was gone. Then I looked in the matador eyes and saw the same exact thing. For them it was an exciting sports event. For me it was a public double suicide. Is that what it takes?
