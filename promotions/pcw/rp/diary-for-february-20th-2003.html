Diary for February 20th, 2003

Date: Today February 20th, 2003 Dear Diary: Today was the most amazing day. It started out great, from the pancake breakfast, to the lunch at McDonalds, it couldn’t get any better. The excitement of my day though, was at the Krishna community center. Yesterday, I was approached by two people in brown robes, inviting me to a religious center. I wasn’t catholic and never had went to church, but I decided that since I was now wrestling and had a bright future ahead, I might as well pray. I still don’t understand why the two people we in robes, I would never do that if they asked me to, but something about them told me that they were interested in me. And they were. Today after lunch, I ventured down the streets here in New York to the religious center where I would go, to ‘talk’ with a missionary as the brochure said. The center wasn’t a new building, but kind of an old one, with rust along the sides of it. The building was only one or maybe two stories high, nothing big, but it stretched almost a whole city block. As I entered the center, there was an entrance way, and then you walked into an opening room. To the left was the office where I guessed you could get some information. Beside the office was a door leading to a big hall. Taped onto the door was a piece of paper that said, ‘Krishna Monthly Supper, open for any Krishna members. February 28th, 2003'. I ignored the hall. Straight ahead of me was a hallway. In the hallway, there were bathrooms and various doors, leading into unknown rooms that I had yet to learn about. I guessed the were for clubs and groups within the Krishna. My focus turned to the right of me, where there was a door labeled, ‘church’. I guessed that was where I was supposed to be headed, so I headed through the double doors. In the sacristy, as it was called. There was tons of pew’s. All around the pews, there were little doors, leading into booths. In the pews, there were some people there, praying. Some of the people looked up at me with glassy eyes. I ignored them and walked down the row of confessionals, deciding on which one I would go to. In some of the doors leading into the booths, there were green lights shining, signifying that someone was in the booth. I took the closest free booth near me, #17. If the same missionary was always in the booth, I figured I would remember the number for later on. I stepped into the booth that had a single light to light my way. I quietly closed the door and sat down on the bench. There was a curtain blocking me from seeing the person on the other side. SyN: Hello? I heard a grunting noise on the other side and the man responded back. Man: Hello, I’m here to listen to your problems. Anything you would like to talk about? I had never realized it but I had a lot of problems that needed to be addressed. I had a lot of things to talk about so I just started from the beginning. SyN: Well, okay. You see, I just got this new job as a professional wrestler in a federation called PCW. It’s been going great and I even one my first match against Mark Makonie. Now, that I’m in the federation, a bunch of new things are open to me. An example would be the Krishna. I never really was a religious person before. On the other side of the curtain, I heard the man breathing, thinking of what to respond back with or something like that. Man: The Hare Krishna isn’t a religious place. It’s a place to listen to you problems and things like that. This PCW, it sounds important to you and you should stick up with it. Don’t do anything you don’t want to do. Whatever you want in PCW, you will get if you let me listen to your problems. You ARE the best in that federation! The man’s voice seemed so confident an focused, it was a little bit eery now that I think back about it. Either way, I really had some confidence in me to do well in PCW. SyN: Ok, I understand what you mean. There is some built up anger inside of me though, from my rotten childhood. How should I let out that anger? There was silence in the booth. All was silent except for mine, and the missionary’s breathing. After a short pause, the missionary replied. Missionary: Well, you work in a wrestling ring, I suppose you could say. In the ring, you try your best to win your matches. In other words, you can take out your anger inside the ring. It can be all against one person, or just anybody who crosses your path. Either way, you will feel a lot better when your done with it. What the guy was saying made sense. I could take out my anger on my opponents. I decided that I would take out my anger on one person at a time until I got what I wanted. A title. SyN: I see what you mean and your right. I am going to take out my anger on a person. Missionary: Who would that person be? SyN: THE OVERWEIGHTER! Missionary: Could luck with that and you tell me how that goes. I stayed and talked with the man behind the curtain for a long while. Everything he said seemed to make sense to me and I left the Krishna community center happy. Now, as I write this in my journal, I have some confidence in PCW. Only time will tell if my confidence has worked. This Friday, I will be in a tag match. I usually am not the guy to tag but we’ll see how it goes. Maybe, just maybe, I’ll end up with another win!
