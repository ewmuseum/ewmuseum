Behind the Ego of Jason Snow: Family and Relationships, Chapter 2 - Paternal Heir

Too important in the development of Snow not to post.) “Don’t touch me!” screams Krista from the floor, as her brother-in-law approaches gently, reaching out a hand to help her up. Her face is still contorted and soaked in tears, unable to hold back any of the emotion that’s pouring out of her. In the background, we hear little Haley screeching from her room upstairs. Snow wants to go up and quiet his infant daughter, but is afraid that Krista might run out if he leaves her alone - he put her through so much a moment ago. “Just come into the kitchen, Krista,” he says calmly, in gentle tones, “Just come into the kitchen.” ... Haley screeches wildly, even in her father’s arms. He doesn’t blame her. In her position, he would probably do the same thing - he feels like he wants to do the same thing now. The little curly blond-haired little girl cries hard, tightly gripping her father’s ripped t-shirt. It took almost ten minutes to coax Krista into the kitchen, and Haley had screamed the whole time. It was another five before he was able to get Krista to calm herself and accept a cup of tea, and then he headed upstairs to deal with the other young lady who he had scared so badly with his booming voice and wild antics. Now, as he gently bounces his baby daughter in an attempt to calm her as well, he cringes at the thought of what has just occurred. “It’s still early,” he whispers to the little girl, “Go back to sleep. Please - just go back to sleep.” Haley refuses, but he’s pleased that she’s finally beginning to quiet. Her ear-shattering wail has now mellowed into loud, constant, moaning. ... The bathroom is Jason Snow’s home is large and private - exactly how his wife had wanted it to be. Immediately, he plunges his face into the water-filled sink, light blue sink. He had finally managed to quiet Haley, and left her happily in her crib, chewing on her toes. Now, he knows he will have to go downstairs and face Krista. Would she want some explanation? Or would she realize that Snow’s actions have no explanation - no one has an explanation. He simply reacted to the situation he saw. What will he say to her? What can he say to her? Jason Snow - loudmouth wrestling personality, silent family man. What a life... wrestling is his life. It’s an addiction. It brings out the best and the worst in him - but he needs it. His wife always asks how he can be so vocal while working, yet so silent at home. Will he be silent with Krista? He comes up out of the water quickly, snapping his head back - his long hair flings droplets of water all over the room. As he stands there, ready for a second plunge, he realizes that he’s standing directly in front of the bathroom mirror. Krista really did a number on him. The result of her vicious claw-based attacks are evident all over the left side of his face - bright red scratch marks. He looks like he’s been attacked by an ornery kitten. His left arm and chest aren’t so harmless-looking. Beneath the tear in the center of his plain white t-shirt, he sees that Krista’s claws drew a bit of blood. His left arm is covered in small cuts and scratches. A few bruises are even appearing - evidence of some of the small young lady’s harder punches. He wishes she’d done more damage. He wishes she had clawed the heart from his chest. He wishes that she had been able to do something - anything - to stop him from feeling the sick feeling that now lurches in the pit of his stomach. But she couldn’t do enough. He was a monster - a lion. He still resembles a lion as he stares deeply into the mirror. His golden beard, soaked black, the same as his long, usually blond, hair. He can’t believe what he’s done... Or did he do it at all? He remembers grabbing Krista by the hair and pulling her down - but part of him sees David Snow grabbing Mary Snow by the hair. David Snow, with his biceps rippling under his shirt, his face contorted in a display of rage, holding Jason Snow’s mother’s head down to his waist, screaming in her ear. Did he, Jason Snow, look like that? The mirror shows him familiar, paternal, traits. The tone of his skin... the thickness of his jaw... the hard, cold, blue eyes... For a moment, he can clearly see David Snow - especially with the mental picture of Krista, sitting helpless on the floor, crying wildly. That’s a paternal trait too, he knows. Snow sighs and turns away from the mirror, rubbing the dripping water off his cheeks and pulling his long hair back - the water holds it away from his face. He hopes that Krista is still downstairs, waiting in the kitchen. He also hopes that by the time he reaches the bottom of the stairs, he’ll have something to say to her - something to make the reality of his actions a little less horrendous. ... His heart lightens when he spots her in the kitchen, sitting at the table where he left her, leaning on the table with her elbows - her back to him. His heart shatters when she turns around and he catches a glimpse of her tear-streaked face. One glance at Snow is all it takes to set Krista’s lip trembling. She can’t believe that the man she had always looked up to as an authority figure, as a protector, was capable of such actions - He can’t believe that she’s looking at him that way. He sees the fear slapped all over her face and wants nothing more than to be able to take it away... but he can’t. She fears him because of the way her eyes sting for wanting to cry from a well of tears that has gone dry.... She fears him because of the way she can still, almost, feel feel how cold the wall felt against her back... She fears him because of the way the roots of her hair still sting from his violent grip. “Don’t look at me like that,” Snow says - pleads - to her, with sad eyes and his head tilted slightly. She sustains her look, complete with trembling lip - Snow has to fight his own from trembling. “Please, Krista...” he says in a near whisper, approaching her slowly. “Krista...” he repeats, knowing nothing else to say. Her breath sputters out of her mouth with every step he takes creating an eerie rhythm. He kneels down behind her chair and puts his hand on the back of it. It hurts her to look at him over her shoulder, but she doesn’t turn away. He’s thankful - though he wishes that she didn’t have such fear in her young eyes. “Krista...” he says once more, and again, his voice trails off before he can add anything. Snow’s pleads come in whispers, Krista’s came in screams. She must have screamed his name three times, but got no reaction from him. His whispers do nothing to her, either. She hasn’t had a sip of tea yet, though the steam flowing from the top suggests that maybe it was too hot - maybe. “Jason,” she finally says in a voice almost too cold for him to take. He knows that he deserves this treatment - he deserves far worse, actually. Had anyone else put their hands on Krista in a violent manner, he would die to protect her. In fact, as proven earlier, it’s not only violent hands that Snow objects to. “Yeah?” he whispers, his eyes pleading with her. “What’s wrong with you?” she asks, a tear finally squeezing its way away from her eye. “I don’t know,” answers Snow softly, “You know I’d never hurt you though...” He wishes he could have found a better way to phrase that - he’s already hurt her. He’s hurt her more than she ever thought him capable. The scariest part to Snow is the memory of his own father’s philosophy - it’s not hurting until a closed fist is involved. Snow just can’t run far enough away to leave that dreaded mirror out of sight. “I just...” he hesitates, “I just do really stupid things sometimes - I don’t know why.” She nods - she’s heard the stories of Snow’s infamous temper, she just never thought he would turn on a loved one. A lion is one of the few animals in the world that can never truly be tamed. From the time she knew him, she had seen him as a protector - never an attacker. She never would have believed that. “I never thought...” she begins, and then her voice trails off. “I know.” “You can’t be like that, Jason,” she sobs quietly, “You’re a husband - you’re a father!” Her words make Snow thankful, for the first time, that he spends ten months out of the year on the road with PCW. Still, an interesting question has been raised - would he have turned on his wife, had the worst of their arguments not seen them linked only by a telephone wire? Would he someday turn on his own child. These things all seem unimaginable to him... but then again, he never thought he would ever see Krista like this... at least, not by his own doing. “I know, Krista,” he says, wiping a tear from her cheek while biting his own lip. “You were so...” her voice trails off again and she’s forced to turn away from him. With a sigh, he rises to his feet and takes a seat on the opposite side of the table from her - she still hasn’t touched her tea, he notices, and then tries to recall if she really does like tea afterall. “Look, Krista,” Snow begins in a voice that’s a bit firmer, “I know what I did was wrong. It’s like it’s not even real to me, and you know how I feel about you, but...” his voice trails off and into a sigh. As she stares across the table at him, she wonders how he really does feel about her. She assumes that he cares, but he’s always so vague on the subject. Is he like that with her sister too? “Krista,” Snow continues, deciding that the direct approach is best, “What were you doing with that guy.” His voice has a layer of pain in it that Krista detects - it only angers her. The young brunette bites her lip and shakes her head. Snow is almost relieved to see that her expression of fear has turned into one that he’s pretty sure is made up completely of anger. “You can’t keep doing this, Jason,” she says coldly, still shaking her head and staring at him through narrow little brown eyes, “I’m not fifteen anymore.” “What does that have to do with anything?” asks Snow. “Everything!” Krista says, her voice rising, “It was cute back then, Jason. And to tell you the truth, I used to like how all the little high school boys were afraid to come near me. But it’s getting old, Jason - and I’m getting old too.” Snow smirks - the first version of a smile he’s given in quite some time, “Oh,” he begins, “I forgot how old and wise you are.” She looks at him with disgust, “What are you talking about?” she demands, “When you were my age, you’d been married for two years!” “That was different,” says Snow in a firm tone, “and you know it.” She does know it. But she felt trapped and needed something to say. The fact is, when Snow was twenty years old, he had already been an adult for years. He had to be. “I...” Krista begins, and then concedes, “I know...” She’s glad that Snow married her sister when he did - at the tender age of eighteen. He passed up post-secondary school to pay his young wife’s way through. She’s also thankful that Snow has been using the millions that he’s made in the wrestling business to pay for her own education. But most of all, she’s thankful that Snow hasn’t, nor ever has, mentioned the reason why he was the one who had to pay for all of it. “I just don’t want to see you do anything stupid,” Snow sighs, closing his eyes, “so much so, that sometimes it makes me go a little crazy.” “I can make my own decisions though, Jason,” Krista insists, and then softens her voice as she reaches across the table and puts her hand on top of his, “Billy and I have been together for almost a year now.” “A year?” Snow is shocked, “Why didn’t anyone tell me?” Krista shakes her head, but doesn’t remove her hand - Snow is thankful, “You’re gone all the time and all you think about is wrestling...” She’s wrong. It’s not so much the wrestling. It’s not the sport of it that drives Snow - it’s the other things. It’s being legally able to break a man in half while the audience goes wild. It’s the fame. It’s knowing that he’s the one of the very best at what he does. And that’s what drives him on in Wrestling God 3, despite the personal trauma - the chance to be known throughout the world to be known as the best at his profession... at his addiction. “I told you, my sister told you... you just don’t pay attention when you’re gone. All you think about is the ring.” “A year?” Snow repeats. “Well,” Krista sighs, about to admit some of her own wrong-doing, “Maybe I shouldn’t have brought him here. That’s not the way I had planned to introduce you two.” Finally, she smiles - Snow had been waiting so long to see that smile... too bad he doesn’t think her joke is funny. “Just...” he knows that he can’t lock her away, “Just be careful.” “I’m not as stupid as you think, Jason,” she says, squeezing his hand a little tighter. He hopes she’s right. ... The empty room... It’s hard for Snow to be surrounded by blue walls right now. This was the room - this was supposed to be such a happy room. Happiness withdrawn leaves a greater void than was ever there to begin with. There’s a crib on the far side of the room - empty. It will always be empty. The toys that were beginning to fill the shelves will never be played with by tiny hands. The books on the tiny blue bookcase may one day be read, but their stories will never be heard. He would fall over if Krista weren’t still holding his hand - finally, he realizes what the comfort of another human is worth. “You’ve been through so much,” Krista says, her eyes welling up with tears once more - she’s cried so much over the last few days. He’s not the only one who’s going through this, however. His wife must be in agony. Her agony, however, is both emotional and physical. He suddenly wishes she were there to hold his free hand. He also would like to be able to hold hers - to finally give her that comfort that she’s craved from him for so long. Why does he always deny her of it? He knows that he’ll deny her of it again. His match with Michael Trey has been scheduled for tomorrow night, and he’ll soon be blinded by that old hunger. Snow assumes that Trey considers that hunger a sickness - a weakness. In most cases it is... In every case it is... except when Snow is standing in the middle of the ring. Then he feeds on it. His opponent is only an obstacle between himself and another fix - another high. High on success. “It’s going to be ok though,” Krista says, looking up at her brother-in-law and snapping him out of his daze, “I know it.” Snow closes his eyes and prays that she’s right. After giving him one final squeeze on the hand, she releases her grip, “I think I should feed Haley,” she says quietly, before rubbing Snow’s muscular right arm. It’s unscratched, as opposed to the other. The moment she steps out of the door, Snow knows that he can no longer stand to be in a room so full of emptiness. He walks - nearly staggers - out of the room and makes his way back to the bathroom down the hall where his sink of warm water has now gone to room temperature. Before dipping his face back into it, in an attempt to wash something emotional away, he catches a glimpse of the mirror. There is David Snow again - not Jason Snow. David Snow... workaholic... lunatic... a man who hurt everyone he loved a time or two. But it is Jason Snow too, which brings the disturbing thought to his overworked mind - would his son, had he been born, turned out the same way? Snow doesn’t think about the question - he reacts to it. He slams his right forearm into the mirror, sending shards of broken glass flying in all directions, crashing loudly into whatever they strike. “Jason!?!?” Krista yells worryingly, but he doesn’t hear her. By the time she gets to the doorway, holding baby Haley gently in her arms, Snow has not moved. Once again, she’s frightened by him - frightened by that monestrous look that has taken over his body. Standing there in front of the broken mirror, with shards of glass scattered about his feet, blood begins to trickle from the many new small wounds on his forearm. The reflection in the shards on the floor show him David Snow. The reflection from the shards in the sink, beneath the water, show him David Snow. What’s left of the mirror itself shows him David Snow. With every action, he is surrounded... there is no escape.
