Truly Misplaced--Part 1 of 2

9 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/9/02 The sky is mixed with various different colors. Pale orange, purple, blue, and red colors are all blending to make a splendid sunset. A bright orange sun is shining down on the city of New Orleans. The streets of this party city are crammed with tourists of every kind. Of course, the regular person would prefer to be hanging in the crowds and enjoying the streets, right? Wrong. The tag team of Misplaced has been driven to go visit there sidekicks home, which is in the suburbs of New Orleans. It’s a farm house though. Lets visit this scene, as it could lead to adventure. We see Carey J. Nowski and Stephen Cairns with a look of shock on there face as they gaze over the rugged farm house that is right in the middle of a suburban ghetto neighborhood. Jackson “Hoe” Pallish, there sidekick, jumps happily as he notices his mama’ come out of the house. The house is nothing special. In fact, it’s ugly. It’s a small wood house with holes in the roof and the sides. It appears to have enough room to have three rooms. Anyway, Jackson’s mama’ comes out to greet the men and six little kids run behind her. “Jackson: Kwami, Jamar, Bob, Teka, Misty, Macy, is that you guys? Ya’ll look so damn big. What you been feedin’ these motha’ f*ckers mama’?” Mama: “First off, watch yo’ language around the children! I don’t wanna’ have them ended up talkin’ like me and you. I want dem’ to be smarft’ and go thru’ dat’ coll..collag…college thing. Second off, I been feedin’ them da’ classiestest’ koons’ you could find.” Jackson looks down at the ground and shakes his head in shame. Jackson: “Sorry mama’, but you mean yo’ feedin’ my kids dem’ negros dat live down the street? That’s not right mama’.” Mama: “No moron. I mean koon’ koons’. Who dese’ white boys with you?” Jackson: “Oh, mama’. These are da’ guys that I’m workin’ for. They’re those wrestler people I was talkin’ to you about. I’m…I’m tryin’ to make a livin’ outside of dis farmin’ stuff.” Mama: “Oh my baby! I’m so proud of you. You keep on kickin’ dem wrestlers ass! Dese’ guys any good, cause’ from the looks of it I dun’ like them. They look like dey’ got an attitude.” Mama’ glances over at the team and gives them a look. It appears as if she’s going to speak, but Jackson cuts her off. Jackson: “Yeah dey’ good, but they need to do dat’ focusin’.” Mama: “Yeah, you mean dat’ drug focusin? I heard of dat’.” Jackson: “No, focusin! Like concentratin…” Carey and Stephen approach mama’ to greet her. She takes a good look at them as if she’s deciding whether she likes them or not. Carey makes the first move by puttin’ out his hand, but mama’ just gives him a twisted look. Mama: “I dun’ shake white boys hands. I dun care how good they is. Till’ you prove me somethin’ I ain’t talkin’ to yo’.” Stephen and Carey glance at each other, and then at Jackson. They pull Jackson off to the side to talk to him. Stephen: “Why the hell is she such a bitch? I mean…” Jackson: (sighing) “I know wut’ you be meanin’. Mama’ musta’ ate a bad meal or sumthin’. She always has picked the bad snakes for dinner.” Stephen: “What are we doing here anyways? This isn’t helping us for Fusion at all. This is our first match in a while, and I want to be ready for it.” Jackson: “Well where the hell you wanna’ go? I dunno’ much bout’ this city.” Carey/Stephen: (Together) “Anywhere but here!” Jackson: “Lemme’ tell mama’ and we’ll bounce out of this crib.” Carey and Stephen take a seat on a bench in the yard, and Jackson runs off to find his mama’. Carey: “We’re falling to nothing. Look at us. We went from being one of the most hyped up tag teams to sitting at a black farmers house while his mother insults us. We need to focus.” Stephen: “I need to do something. I can’t just sit around Jackson anymore. We need to get away.” Carey: “Yeah, but where exactly can we go? We don’t have much of a choice. We have to stay here for Fusion, and we know nothing about the city. I don’t have Mulder with me, and I’m pissed. I just want to sit!” Before there conversation could continue, Jackson approaches them with a bum look on his face. Jackson: “Aight’, you guys can get out of here. Mama’ is makin me stay wit’ her today cause she dun’ wanna’ handle all the kids. I’ll hit ya’ll up tomorrow. Peace.” Without any more conversing, Carey and Stephen rush to there vehicle. They start the engine and make there way through the suburbs of New Orleans. Not much is going on in the world outside them. It’s a gloomy evening now, with signs of thunderstorms coming. People are walking there dogs, gangs are roaming the streets, and tourists come and go through the streets. Then there is Carey and Stephen. They’re a team with no abilities to communicate. They’re a struggling team. Stephen: “Where are we going?” Carey: “I’m fine with going back to the room. It looks like it’s going to rain, and I’ve got some catching up to do with my lawsuit against the X-Files producer.” Stephen: “What the hell are you suing the X-Files for? We have a wrestling career to worry about.” Carey: “They took the show and Mulder off the air! It’s an outrage. I will not let it stand. Wrestling comes last next to the X-Files.” Stephen: “I’ve had enough.” Stephen grabs his hair and starts to shout. He’s yelling at Carey to pull over and let him out. Carey just slouches in his seat with an afraid look on his face. Stephen threatens Carey if he won’t let him out, so Carey pulls off to the side of the road. Stephen doesn’t even look at Carey, he just hops out of the Volkswagen. He shouts at Carey to leave, and Carey does so. As Carey is pulling away it starts to rain. There is a separated team. One man has nothing better to do than file a lawsuit against a Sci-Fi show, and the other is doing nothing with his life. Meanwhile, there is Stephen standing on the side of the road, waiting for a cab to come by. The time passes, and so does the rain. There’s nothing more than a light mist outside, but it had been coming down from the looks of Stephens soaked hair and clothes. Finally, a yellow cab comes driving by and Stephen jumps in the air. He’s desperately trying to get the attention of this cab driver, and he succeeds. The cab pulls off to the side to pick up the drenched Stephen. Stephen enters the cab with a smile on his face, but frowns whenever he’s asked where he wants to go. Stephen: “I’m not exactly sure where I’m heading. Can you just take my downtown and drop me off there? I need to explore this city.” Cab Driver: “Sure thing, mate.” Stephen sighs when he realizes that it’s an Aussie driver, and he has something against Aussie’s. Since he wants to avoid conversation, he just lays his head back against the seat. The other half of Misplaced is just now arriving at the hotel they’re staying at, which is a Hilton in downtown New Orleans. He parks the car in the lot, and makes his way towards the lobby. The lobby is a fancy looking place, with beautiful carvings in the wall, and delicate furniture all throughout the downstairs. Carey makes his way towards the elevator to get to his floor. His room is stationed on the sixth floor. He enters the elevator, presses six, and begins his exciting journey to his room. He enters the room to find an ordinary hotel suite. Two rooms and a bath are all that it consists of. There’s also a balcony overlooking the street. Carey grabs his laptop and a tablet immediately and takes a seat at the desk. Now he begins his even more exciting journey of lawsuits, but it’s not exciting enough to keep him awake. Stephen has finally arrived in downtown New Orleans, where the streets are empty on this muggy night. Every once in a while a person will come by, but that’s rare. It’s going on midnight now, and Stephen is in the middle of a big city with no clue what he’s doing. So he does the logical thing, and just starts walking. Well it may not be too logical, but it’ll work. He looks around him and sees nothing but Cajun Food and Jazz advertisements. He despises both of those things. Finally, to his surprise he finds a European club. He enters this club to find a raggedy old bar and a television airing a soccer game. What am I doing here has to be running through his mind at this time. So here we have a split up tag team who has to face three talented tag teams on Friday. One is wasting time in a junky European bar, and the other is asleep and drooling all over his laptop. Quite a team, huh? Yeah, they’re definitely fitting there name of Misplaced. Hopefully they’ll regain some sense before Friday.
