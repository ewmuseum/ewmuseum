You Haven't Gotten Rid Of Me Yet

18 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07/18/02 Tuesday Turmoil RP#1 As a child, I hated hospitals. From the day I was born in Queens up until I was 7 years old, I never went to a hospital. You may not know it, but many black families are ghetto ass fucks. Meaning that no matter how badly you were injured, you wouldn’t go to the hospital. I used to horse around with my brother Drew; we used to always play wrestling. Pretending I was Ron Simmons (My favorite at that time) and he was Butch Reed (His favorite at that time) and we would hurt each other. Not on purpose, but as a part of the game. I remember a time I hit Drew with Ron Simmons patented power slam and he landed directly on his nappy ass hair. He jumped up and down crying as if he found out he was a white boy. My mom came to him, and told him to lie down… He could have had a concussion, and she told him to sit down. It wasn’t too long again before the same thing happened to me. I was hurt as a result of a body slam or some shit like that, I don’t really remember. But I remember being in pain. And my mom saying ‘Lie down.’ I was thinking… WHAT THE FUCK?! Shiiiiiit, my fucking back and head were throbbing and all she could say is ‘lie down.’ I knew why she said it too. Because she couldn’t afford it. Because she was spending all of her money on some God damn drugs. That’s when I developed a hate for white people. Isn’t it obvious? Little white boys step on a rock, they start crying and run to their mommy, and they get taken to the hospital. That’s why when I was seven, and I was moved to that white family, I didn’t want to ever go to the hospital. I don’t want to seem like one of them stupid, rich, bitch ass white people. Pro wrestling kinda made me change that attitude. But I’ll only go to a hospital if I really need it… *** The scene opens up as an ambulance rushes through the busy streets of Oxford, Mississippi. A cold breeze fills the night, as this ambulance gets closer to the hospital. Inside of the ambulance lies PCW superstar, Jordan X Shabazz. He had just been in a match for the PCW Television Title. A title that he had worked his ass of to get a shot at. He had done his best getting in shape, but when he got to the ring, his performance was dismal. Long considered one of the PCW’s most talented superstars, many thought that his chance against John Crichton would be the end of the reign. A reign that many thought that Crichton didn’t do a damn thing to earn. Jordan may have disappointed his fans, who were now to a very few amount, but he was happy with himself. The match was a disappointment, to him personally, because he knew that he could defeat John Crichton. He was supposed to be Television Champion from the last Pay Per View, but Jaden got involved. Now, his beef with Crichton was practically over. The white man had screwed him again… *** Shabazz reached the hospital very much still in pain. His face was contorted in a grimace as he was rushed through the busy hospital halls towards the emergency room. He told Rush Mason and Thug roC not to worry about him; he would survive. Now Jordan lies, propped up in a hospital bed. Like his friend, Thug roC, his arm is in a sling. The doctor would come in the room in a few minutes, to tell him if he could continue wrestling, or how long he would be away from the ring. His arm throbbed unnaturally. After sitting around for a few minutes, the doctor finally entered the room. In his hand he carried two copies of an X-Ray. He handed one to Jordan. “If you’ll look at the chart right around there Mr. Shabazz, you’ll see that you have a small tear in your bicep. What I suggest is that you take some time off from wrestling. Perhaps for a month or two, just stay away from the ring, or any physical activity involving that arm for that matter. If you continue, you can cause serious damage to it.” says the old looking doctor solemnly. Jordan X Shabazz raises his uninjured arm to his face and he pulls off the glasses that adorned his face and he looks the doctor in the eye. “You don’t get it whitey,” says Jordan X Shabazz defiantly. “If I take a month off, by the time I come back, ten crackas like you will have taken my spot. I will be forgotten.” The doctor is quick to reply. “Mr. Shabazz, I understand your position, but if you continue to wrestle at this rate, within a few months you’re entire left shoulder will have given up on you,” replies the doctor apologetically. “Fuck you, I got a match on Tuesday, and I sure as hell am not going to miss it.” Jordan X Shabazz gets out of the hospital bed that he was lying in, and he begins walking towards the door, his left arm being held really closely to his shoulder. The doctor tries to call him back to the office, but Jordan isn’t turning around. As he nears the elevator, he whispers. “These white devils haven’t got rid of me yet.”
