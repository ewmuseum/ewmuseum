THE SNOW SHOW: Goomba Christmas

26 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: December 26, 2002 Snow stares at Shena as they sit atop the great oak, high above the valley road – he’s never seen a horse in a tree before. Then again, he has never pictured a leprechaun hanging from a branch either. But there’s Shena, sitting properly near the top while O’Reilly dangles with pleasure by the back of his knees. “Any idea how we’re getting down?” Snow asks, turning to Shena. He knows that O’Reilly will be just as pleased to stay. The ugly little leprechaun shouts for glee while he tries to murder a passing sparrow. “Not really,” the horse answers. “You got us into this mess.” She gives Snow a cold look. “Yeah,” he sighs, “but you’re always the one to get me out of it.” “Not this time,” says Shena. “Why did you agree to do the truck jacking?” “What fucking choice did I have?” Snow snaps. “I’m sitting at a fucking table with three guys who would put a bullet in my brain as soon as they would send me a fucking Christmas card, and you want to know why I agreed to it?” “Easy, soldier!” Shena warns him. “We both know that the truck jacking’s not what you have a problem with.” “Why the hell do they want me to deal with fucking narcotics?” Snow rages. “Doug Hatt, the fucking Don of the family, didn’t even make me work at that end of the business.” “It’s different on the outside – you’re not in prison anymore.” Shena looks frustrated. “In there, every one you harmed deserved it, in one way or another. When you inflicted your will on them, you could tell yourself that they deserved it for one reason or another. But out here… things are different.” “I know,” Snow sighs. “But I’m about to lose everything. Eighty fucking million – gone.” “You know it’s your fault,” she says. “No fucking way!” he bellows. “It’s the damn system! We’re in a society where people actually WANT to be victims! Someone steps on your toe, and you can steal their whole life’s work!” “You knew how things worked,” Shena retorts, “and you didn’t merely step on a toe.” Snow sits back on his branch and looks helplessly to the sky. Birds line the clouds, but he can’t make out if they’re doves or vultures. The sun beams down from its hollow in the middle, and the beams are burning his skin. Suddenly, he sighs: “Fuck it.” And with that, he lets himself fall off the branch and to the ground, nearly a hundred feet below. Death comes slowly to release him from his pain… Location: Snow Residence, New York City SuburbsDate: December 24, 2002Start of Scene Time: 8:50 pm Christmas Eve was like a house warming at the Snow residence, where Jason Snow’s wife had invited her entire family. Snow footed the bill, of course, for their plane tickets. Then again, his fortune was about to be drained due to three separate lawsuits, so what was the difference? Of course, Snow’s family wasn’t coming, nor were they invited. The only people left in his family that he was on speaking terms with had died while he was in prison – two grandfather’s and a grandmother. The rest, he either didn’t know or wished he didn’t. He leaned against the counter in the kitchen, drinking a glass of eggnog and staring out into the winter just outside his house. Snow, the substance and not the man, trickled lightly down the sky until it gently landed safely on the ground in front of his house. Or maybe the landing wasn’t so gentle. Maybe the pile of white powder lying on the ground was a pile of snowflake corpses that had been cast aside by the heavens, or wherever they came from. From the living room came laughing and joking and all sorts of things that Snow found revolting on this night. He took a sip of eggnog and secretly wished it was something stronger – but those days were gone, sadly enough. Now, he had to treat alcohol like poison. His mind crossed itself from one horror to the other – his wife’s family and his new profession. It wouldn’t be long now before he had to lead a crew to jack a truck that was transporting bananas, of all things, from Florida. Of course, no one was interested in the bananas. One batch, however, was supposed to be lined with heroin, and their job was to get their hands on it. Seemed simple enough, and the chances of getting caught would be slim to none, but dealing with drugs was a thing that Snow hated above most else. “What are you doing out here?” asked Raven as she rounded a corner. She loved this time of year, and accordingly, she was dressed in a bright red turtleneck with a long black skirt to add a touch of sophistication. “Just looking out at the snow,” he answered, returning her smile, though the warmth in his was a fraud. “Just like those perfect Christmases that we’ve always heard about,” she said as she approached him and wrapped her arms around his broad waist. “It’s going to be so nice to have everyone here,” she said. “You know, I have to tell you, I was a little worried that you weren’t going to… you know… get out in time.” “I know you were,” he said with a nod and then shrugged her off to take another drink. “Everything’s so great!” she said joyfully and her hands rubbed his strong back. He wore the grey sweater, which he hated, just to please her. She smiled. “You know, this is the first time that my parents have been in the same room since I was a kid. And I think I even caught them speaking a while back!” She chuckled. It was true. Her parents had divorced nearly fifteen years earlier, and hadn’t spoken since. After that, her father married again, to a woman that Snow considered pure evil, and recently divorced. Now, both her mother and father had been invited to spend Christmas in her new room, and both surprisingly accepted, in full knowledge that the other would be there. “How are things going between them?” Snow asked as he refilled his eggnog. “Seems good,” she shrugged. “I mean, I haven’t heard either take shots at the other, even behind their back, so it’s definitely an improvement.” “If we get through the night without bloodshed it would be an improvement,” smirked Snow. He took another sip of eggnog. “Why don’t you come and sit for a while?” she asked. “People are starting to think you don’t like them.” In his brain, Snow chuckled, but his face remained strait as an arrow. “I’ll be in. Just give me a second.” Location: Snow Residence, New York City SuburbsDate: December 25, 2002Start of Scene Time: 7:45 am Snow woke up to the sound of screaming children, downstairs, in the living room. None were his, of course, since Haley was just over a year old now, and she still hadn’t grasped the idea of Christmas. He was sure she was down there, however, but she was probably just in awe of the madness. The rest were no doubt his wife’s nieces and nephews whom had made the trip to New York. Snow was rich, so they were probably looking forward to spending the week in the lap of luxury. For the most part, they did, though many had pictured their new house like a mansion. Instead, they found a modest little two story home in a nice little suburban area. Snow felt someone pull on his bare shoulder. “Come on!” he heard a voice say. It was so similar to his wife’s but he instinctively knew that it wasn’t. It was her sister, Krista. “Haley’s about to open her gifts. Raven told me to come drag you downstairs.” Snow looked to the open door of his bedroom and sat up in bed, looking down at the sweat stained wife-beater tank top that he was wearing – his sleep must have been more troubled than he had remembered. “I’ll be down,” he said in the middle of a yawn. “Just let me get dressed.” Krista left while Snow stood up and tried to shake the weariness from his bones. He had grown too used to Johnny Silver’s clock. With great determination, he forced himself up, dressed, and went downstairs where he was bombarded by children trying to show off whatever their most expensive toy was. Raven caught him off guard by snapping a picture, and upon seeing it, he realized that bed-hair was something that he would have to deal with now that he had cut all of his off. “You could at least smile, you know,” teased Krista with a playful push. “Christmas only comes once a year.” “And too damn early in the morning every time,” Snow retorted before yawning. “Hey!” Raven snapped at him with heated eyes. “If you must use that language, I would appreciate it if you held back in front of the children.” “He’s just a grinch!” Krista teased. He looked at her sourly – that stupid little Santa hat on her head made her look like a brown haired elf. Without offering anything back, Snow sat down on the sofa next to his mother-in-law and watched the children go crazy with greed. They swarmed beneath the Christmas tree like bees gathering honey. The most patient of them took the time to shake their gifts and have a guess at its contents before thrashing through the paper. The least patient didn’t even check the name tags on whatever they were opening. “It’s about the children,” smiled Sara, his mother-in-law. She was always full of bits of wisdom. Truth was, Snow thought she was crazy with some yet to be determined disease. “We always get caught up in ourselves at this time of year, but it’s really about the children. And Jesus, of course.” Snow nodded and quickly decided that he would no longer pay attention to her speech. Of course, she went on just the same, never noticing how routine his nods were. On the floor, Raven lead little Haley to a pile of presents on the side that belonged to the toddler. Her eyes lit with joy when she realized that there were goodies, for her as well as her cousins, beneath the brightly decorative wrapping paper. Before long, she was sitting on the floor in her little elf suit (Raven’s idea, of course), accepting the gifts handed to her and neatly pulling the paper off. She was so much like her mother. Picture flashes lit the room as the children continued making their way through the mountains of gifts. Before long, it would be the adult’s turn. The thought of it scraped against Snow like sandpaper. Not only would he have to open gifts that he had no use or want for, but then he would have to act happy and even thank the person who gave it to him. Maybe Krista was right – he was a grinch. Before long, the dreaded hour came, and Raven set a gift on his lap – it was from Sara, his mother-in-law. Oh the joy, he thought. Just then, he was saved by the bell – the door bell. “I’ll get it,” he said suddenly. He sat the gift on the floor and walked toward the kitchen before any protest could be made. When he opened the front door, he was shocked to see his guest. “Merry fucking Christmas!” boomed Johnny Silver, no doubt loud enough for the family in the living room to hear. “Whoa!” Snow said with wide eyes. “Keep it down or my wife will grill my ass for dinner! What are you doing here?” “Jesus,” laughed Johnny, “look at the Christmas spirit in you! Can’t a friend say hello to a friend on Christmas fucking day? What’s the matter with you?” Snow’s eyes narrowed with curiosity – he had never known Johnny Silver wake before eleven in the morning. “You’re up early,” he commented. “I’ve been up for hours,” Johnny said. “Ma takes Christmas very fucking seriously. Oh, that reminds me,” he reached into his pocket, “for you!” He produced a jar of Rosalini’s spaghetti sauce. “Thanks,” said Snow. “Your gift is in the living room.” In truth, he had no gift for Johnny, but when they got to the living room, he snatched Sara’s gift to him up and handed it to Johnny. Sara didn’t notice, and Johnny opened two jars of imported coffee. “Fucking A!” Johnny said. “This shit comes all the way from Italy, you know. You sure you ain’t got no Italian in ya, kid?” Snow looked around the room and then realized that Johnny had caught enough stares that he would have to introduce him. Luckily, his wife was much better at these things, and took relieved Snow of his duties. “Everyone,” she said in the meek voice that was her loudest, “this is Johnny Rosalini. He’s a friend of Jason’s.” They all nodded and stared at the odd looking man with the solid unit of silver hair combed from back to front to cover his obvious baldness. They all nodded and smiled to him. “A business associate,” Snow added. “How are you all doing?” Johnny asked rhetorically. At his first opportunity, Snow manipulated Johnny into the kitchen, where the older man’s foul mouth wouldn’t get either of them in trouble. Once there, Johnny finally got onto business topics. “The truck will be in tomorrow,” he told Snow. “Petey and I have the crew all set. Once you get on the scene, you’re in command.” “Wait a minute,” Snow said. “Tomorrow? You said it would be a week the day before yesterday! How do you expect me to do something like this with all this fucking family roaming around the place?” “Hey!” Johnny snapped. “You stepped into this business on your own two feet, and this is how it is. Tomorrow morning, a couple of guys are going to pick you up and take you to the stake out. When the truck gets there, do the job. It’s simple.” ”Where do I tell them I’m going?” he asked through clenched teeth. “This will likely take all day.” “Tell them it’s business,” shrugged Johnny. “I mean, in reality, that’s all it is anyway. Am I right? I’m right. Be ready, kid, but don’t worry. It’s an easy job to pull. Get the bananas and get them to my boys on the south side, and there will be a big fat brief case waiting for you to split with the boys, and instructions on how to split it will be inside. Got it?” Location: Snow Residence, New York City SuburbsDate: December 26, 2002Start of Scene Time: 12:15 pm The morning came and went, and at noon, a pair of Johnny Silver’s crew came to fetch Snow. When Raven’s aunt answered the door, she couldn’t understand why two young thugs, each smoking cirgarettes, were looking for Jason, who seemed like such a sweet young man… then again, he did just spend five months in prison. Reluctantly, she let the pair in. Snow took them out of the house as soon as he could and, as Johnny suggested, told his wife that he was going out on business. Raven’s eyes narrowed as she watched them go. She was holding Haley in her arms and Krista was standing next to her. “What was that all about?” asked Krista. “Jason’s been hanging around with some strange people lately.” “I know,” answered Raven, leading the younger girl into the living room where they could get out of ear shot from the rest of the women. Most of the men were upstairs in a room that was the beginnings of a game room. “It’s starting to get suspicious.” “How do you mean?” the younger woman narrowed her pretty brown eyes. “Well,” Raven sighed, “I’m not sure if it’s politically correct to say this, but have you noticed a certain… common thread in all of the people who’ve been visiting lately.” “Well, they’re all horrible dressers,” shrugged Krista, “but so is Jason.” “Not that,” said Raven. “They’re all… Italian.” Krista chuckled uneasily. “So? What’s that got to do with anything?” “Don’t you watch movies?” Raven said while rocking Haley gently on her shoulder. “Yeah,” said Krista, “but what’s that got to do with Jason’s friends all being Italian.” Raven rolled her eyes. “That’s just it, Krista,” she whispered through clenched teeth. “He never calls them friends, they’re always ‘business associates.’ Don’t you think that’s a little suspicious?” It took Krista a moment, for the girl was quite naïve, but soon enough, her face turned white with shock. “No way!” she finally said. “He wouldn’t go into something like that!” “I wouldn’t think so either,” Raven responded. “But he’s been acting so strange lately. I thought it was just because of these lawsuits, but now… I’m not so sure what to think.”
