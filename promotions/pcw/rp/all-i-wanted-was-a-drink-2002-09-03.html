All I wanted was a drink.

3 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9-3-02 RP 2 Turmoil 3rd “American Pride. Southern Heritage? Dumb Americans.” A female’s voice enters Teresa’s ears. She stands near a table at a nightclub in Saskatchewan, Canada. In one hand, she holds a margarita she holds the thumb of her other hand through her belt loop. Teresa is wearing her usual loose fitting jeans and a black Jack Daniel’s tee shirt. Over her shoulders is her newly made brown leather jacket. The jacket rides along her waistline and on the back are the American flag and the Rebel flag crossing. Over the top of the jacket embroidered in silver threading reads ‘American Pride’. Across the bottom written with the same silver threading reads ‘Southern Heritage’. The front left side has the two flags crossing but smaller. No writing is seen on the front of the jacket. The female standing behind Teresa obviously reading her jacket is a short girl. She stands just over five-foot and looks like she could use more meat on her bones. She wears a hockey jersey top with jeans. Her hair a whitish blonde and her eyes a pale blue. Her skin is pale and her complexion is obviously fair. As Teresa turns around, she glances down at the girl thinking she must be sick or into some sort of drugs. Teresa standing around five foot seven appears to be towering the girl. “Excuse me? I don’t think I said anything to you.” With Teresa’s strong Southern US accent, the girl laughs. Teresa thinks she must be a local and haven’t been out much. “An American with a funny accent.” Teresa still looking down at the girl doesn’t utter a word. She does however, tilt her head back finishing her margarita and sets the empty glass on the table beside her. She wasn’t really talking to anyone, just standing around watching everyone and enjoying her drink in peace until now. Teresa combs her fingers of the hand which was holding her drink through her own long blonde hair. The exception is Teresa’s hair is more of a darker sandy blonde. The girl’s hair looks almost white. Teresa raises an eyebrow watching the short thing laugh again now placing the thumb of her other hand through another belt loop feeling a bit annoyed. “Got anything else to say to this dumb American with a funny ass accent, Shorty?” The girl steps defensively. She brings her empty hands up with the simple bend of the elbows sarcastically. “Oooo! The dumb American called me Shorty. I’m so scared. I hear the southern US are a lot dumber than the Northern. Looking at you, I see it’s true.” It appears that the girl doesn’t know who or what she is talking to. She doesn’t have anything against Teresa personally; she just enjoys toying with the American visitors, then laughing at them as they storm away mad. Teresa unfortunately has been chosen for the girl’s next victim, but will she end up being Teresa’s next victim? “Would you rather me call you Short Shit? I don’t have the patience or time to bother with you, so why don’t you take your smart-ass, hockey sucking, annoying self somewhere far away from me before I get angry. You have no clue who you are talking to.” “I know who you are Teresa Tomas. You’re the Hillbilly that recently signed to PCW. My little brother adores you. What kind of an example are you to the children that watches you?” Teresa listens quietly to the girl. Just moments ago, she was ready to slam the pest’s face into the table near her. Now, for some reason, Teresa doesn’t want to. She tilts her head slightly speaking with a calmer tone. “Listen, I didn’t become a wrestler to be an example to anyone. I became a wrestler to kick some ass and make a name for myself. I am me; not a lump of clay people can shape and mold. Is that understood?” What at first appeared to be the beginning of a typical American/Canadian fight has turned into a discussion on Teresa’s career. The girl listened to Teresa as well and gives a small sigh. “That’s what’s wrong with you Americans. Too much pride. You talk about pride in your country and only have pride in yourselves. American pride is just for show when you get into trouble. Ok, Teresa thought about slamming this girl’s face in the table, then after the tension settled, she changed her mind and decided to give the poor Canadian a break. Now, this girl has done it. She thinks she can diss Teresa’s country, which she can’t wait to get back to and get away with it? Let’s choose not. After a moment of silence between the two, Teresa grabs a huge chunk of the girl’s hair from behind her head and jerks back so hard, the girl screams. Teresa narrows her eyes watching the pain and catching a slight hint of fear in her eyes. No pride you say? Would you like to see pride? Huh? Would ya? Pride is lighting up the sky on July 4th. Pride is remembering our soldiers on Memorial Day as well as those that have died. My father devoted 20 years of his life to the US Navy. He died at sea when his ship sank in battle. Every Memorial Day, I dust off his tombstone and place fresh flowers by his name. And you know what else I do? I stick a three-foot tall American flag in the ground beside where he lays. He died for his country and by damn I would too. You still want to see pride? I’ll show you some pride right now.” As soon as Teresa stops speaking her mind, she forces the girl’s forehead into the wall busting her nose. The girl screams and starts crying in pain. Teresa pulls the girl back by her hair and looks at her bloodied face. “That was for my country, and this is for being such a pain in the ass.” Teresa slams the girl’s face into the wall once more and drops her where she stands. Looking around, everyone had stopped to watch and now are frozen in shock at what they just witnessed. Teresa releases a deep sigh stuffing both her hands into her jacket pocket and casually steps out of the club. No one attempts to stop her.
