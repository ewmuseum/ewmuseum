A Place For My Head

16 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2 - 16 - 03 I’ve still got quite a way to go before my journey reaches its apex, deep within the swirling chaos of purpose and competition. With the silver moon hanging high above my head like a great colossus and a heavy feeling weighing down my heart, I don’t know if I’m feeling completely up to the task. Now that I doubt my own ability, mind you… I’m quite capable of derailing any wanna-be that would be foolish enough to throw themselves at me. Oh no, it’s nothing of the sort. Fueled by a will for glory and a hunger for gold, I’m not to be stopped by an mere man that wishes to do so. No, it’s an entirely different. Something beyond physical composition or mental prowess. It’s all about the feeling we have, deep inside that swells up when a situation depicting a choice between life or death pokes its head up. That strange, tingling feeling that makes us want to duck for cover instead of recklessly throw ourselves in. Don’t fucking lie, you know exactly what I’m talking about. We’ve all felt that way. I don’t know anyone for the life of me that has NO fear. Fear is what fuels the human spirit to achieve amazing things beyond their normal prowess. Fear does a body good. When someone fears something, they’re more inclined to do something to it, so they’ll no longer have a problem. The Greeks feared the Trojans, so they waged war upon the people of Troy and ultimately sacked the jewel of the Mediterranean. The Romans feared the Carthaginians getting a little too close, so they collided with steel swords flying with the much larger empire. Could have they achieved victory without the adrenaline-pumping effects of fear? I think not. When a man is afraid, they think to themselves “Fuck, what am I going to do?! I’ve got to do something!” and end up doing WHATEVER it takes, no matter what the cost. Take Chris Storm for example. The putz obviously fears me, Dorn Tracer, as he went to quite an extent to defeat me. He hid behind a chair-swinging wench and took his opportunity to pin me upon my person getting knocked the fuck out by cold steel. Quite an ironic twist of fate, and my Goddess just idly watched as the event transpired. But is it her fault? Absolutely not. Is it mine? Perhaps, for being so foolish as to not expect a weakling such as he to cheat. But ultimately, is it Chris Storm’s? Of course. It cost me my match and perhaps a precious piece of my dignity, but it made me realize something: Fear drives a man to go beyond the bounds of his conscience. Would the fool have cheated if he did not fear me? No, probably not. He would have taken defeat like a decent human being and would have lied down to the supreme power of the Lunis Superia. He seems intelligent enough for such. But no, things don’t always go as you wish. Unfortunately, that night was not to be mine. But I digress, as I am the number one contender to the Rising Star Title. I could honestly care less about that asshole. I’ve got much bigger problems to conquer. He can take his whore and cheat his way to the top; he’ll fail sooner or later. His fear will only fuel him so far before his own ineptness will take over and crush him beneath its cruel, leathery heel. It’d be bitter sweet, at best. Ah, but what does it matter? My point is, I wish to feel this ‘fear’. I do not want to be driven into cheating insanity, but I wish to be fueled by this strange human emotion. Men fear for different reasons: For their life, money, loved ones, and sometimes just because their minds cannot handle the circumstances. Chris Storm was afraid, and Jackson Rust is surely afraid at having to face a beast such as I. It’s only logical. With the power of the head Tiamat firmly transfixed within the depths of my being, I cannot be defeated fairly. It’ll take the punishing power of cold steel to knock out my mortal form. Or perhaps some other means of cheating. Rust will definitely resort to such means, but I will be ready. I can smell the stink of his fear from this place within my head. I can feel it… He’s so afraid. But why aren’t I? I fear no man or beast. I fear not death, for it means nothing to me. Because of this, are my will and soul becoming sloth? Must I achieve this physical and surreal state of panic and despair to achieve ultimate victory? I doubt it. But it wouldn’t hurt, as there is nothing to fear. Jackson Rust can do me no harm, and the Rising Star Title is only blockaded by a small wall of sandbags. Easily scaled with little or no effort. So why do I wish to fear? To realize what it’s like to have to resort to an enigma to achieve something that can be gotten so much easier. It compels me. I really do not understand it. I have not felt fear since I was a young boy. It’s a stranger to me now. But do I really need it? No, I do not. I must be kidding myself? Fear works for the regular mortal man. I am above and beyond. I’m smarter, stronger, fast, and have a much bigger cock than the rest of my brethren. Why would I need such a foolish thing to achieve victory? I don’t. There’s nothing that can stop me now. I must clear my mind and prepare for the coming battle that looms over the horizon like a great flame. It threatens to engulf my very being, but alas, it shall be disappointed. Dorn Tracer will not fail. He cannot. He must not. He WILL not. I guarantee it. [End.]
