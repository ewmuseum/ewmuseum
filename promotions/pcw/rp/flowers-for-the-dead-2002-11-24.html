Flowers for the Dead

24 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/24/02 Flowers For the Dead {Quiet hush} I stood there above Juan’s burial place. I was there earlier in the week, but I thought I’d go again to stir up the courage to actually meet with the man who I can call my father. The ground was so soft. The feeling was so serene. I was in peace. I just hope Juan was too. I got on my knees and I prayed. I prayed for Juan, and I prayed for myself. I knew I had to move on, but it was hard. Everything in my life was hard. My public persona makes me look like a strong fighting, woman magnet, and smart assed genius. I’m fine with that, people can think what they want, and hell I’ll help them into thinking that of me. I don’t want people to know the real me. The little boy trapped in a man’s body dying for love. The man who no longer sleeps because he fears what he might see in his dreams. I am not strongI am weak. I’m not afraid of what people might say. Fighting, I can do that; I can do it well enough. It’s something that makes me a living and lets me throw all that anger inside of me at someone else. Not too bad huh? So I stand here above my brother’s grave, with a cigar in hand and long black trench coat touching the floor. The cold air gives me shivers, but the shivers don’t bother me. It’s my mind, which bothers me. It makes me question my life and wonder why I’m here. But this isn’t about me. It’s about him, the young man who had so much to live for. Sure I could go and shift all of that blame at Charlie Loc, the murderer of my brother, but that won’t solve anything. Everyone chooses his or her own destiny; Juan knew he was going to die. You see there were these words my brother muttered one day in PCW footage, the words that haunt me till this day. “I’ll fight for my life, and in the end I’ll either be living with my belt or just plain dead. Nothing in between what so ever.” He knew it. He knew he was going to die. He didn’t know how, but he knew it was coming. I know it wasn’t suicide, but he played mind game with Loc to draw him into killing him. He did it because he knew he’d be in hell for suicide. As much as I hate to believe that, it’s true and I can never change the truth no matter how hard I try to. So here I am, standing in front of a man, who’s now happy. He got what he wanted. He left a world that treated him so well and then left him there to rot. Was it the right choice? I don’t know. Was it a hard choice? It must have been. Juan and I had one thing in common. We always hid our emotions, and just did our jobs. We weren’t ones to make a big deal over everything. We dealt with it by ourselves and didn’t make it completely public. Thank you Juan, thank you. I left there that day, with flowers for the dead. May he remember me up in heaven and put in a good word. One day we’ll be together brother, but for now I have business to take car of. I have a whole life in front of me, and I’m going to live it. I took a taxi later that day and an hour later I arrived. I stared at it for some time. It was nothing special. It was a small white house, with broken hinges on the door, dirt on the side panel, and a ripped up coach outside on his all dirt lawn. I drew closer and closer to the building and then I heard it. Daddy where are we going today? I don’t know it depends on what your mother’s doing. A family, he had a family. How could he just move on like that? His son had died and he just moved on like nothing ever happened. How could this be, what kind of man would do such a thing. I was disgusted and I no longer wanted to even be in the presence. I spit on his property and left in disgust. I drove away and never looked back again. For all I cared, I had no father and I never would. My thoughts of finding out what I really was were over. The man I thought I could of understood I couldn’t. Was this rock bottom? Where do I go from here? I have nothing else to look forward too, all these things as I drove away from that block. All I knew was that tonight I had to fight. Fight for what I don’t know, but I was so filled with anger that somebody was about to be seriously hurt. I just hope it wasn’t myself. I was gone. {Back at Romantico’s fathers house} Let’s go kids get in the car. All right dad! Where is you mother? She said she has to put on her makeup. The young boy wanders across the street and finds himself right in front of a heart torn man who is just staring up at the sky. GET AWAY FROM HIM RIGHT NOW! The father screams and the son come back to him. The father grabs him by the arm and whispers in his ear. Do not go near that man. He has lost many people in his life, and he is very angry and dangerous. Promise me you’ll stay away from him. I promise daddy, I’m sorry. It’s all right son. You see Romantico was mistaken, for the man with the family was not his father. For it was the lonely man next door, who saw no reason for living? He had nobody to talk to, all he had was himself. His life was simple. He’d eat, go outside stare up at the sky where his wife and son were living, and then he’d go visit them at the cemetery. When he got home he’d sleep. He’d do the same thing, every day, for the remainder of his life. And everyday at the cemetery he’d leave: Flowers for the Dead
