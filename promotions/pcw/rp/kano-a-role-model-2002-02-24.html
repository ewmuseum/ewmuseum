Kano, a role model?

24 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/24/02 ~Role model. Children look up to different people in their lives as people they would like to grow up to be. These are role models. People like parents, teachers, and public servants are the TRUE ROLE MODELS. Still, constantly people in the public eye are thrust into the role model role unwillingly and unknown to these people. Jon Kano is one such man. He is probably the greatest single example of what a role model SHOULDN'T be. He doesn't want to be a role model, he never asked to be a role model. Yet these little bags of flesh and bone known as children still worship him. This does not sit well with 'The Man Who Rocketh the Mosteth.' And in all of this involuntary christening of different celebrities as role models, where are the parents of these little worthless slugs? Shouldn't they be their children's role models? No, that would require too much responsibility on the parents' part. That is one thing that parents in this day do not like to do. Sure, they will say what great parents they are when their children grow up and are successful as adults, but when they are on the street corner giving blowjobs to homeless men for quarters, then it is some public figure's fault because that public figure wasn't a good role model. Jon Kano is not a role model, he never once asked to be a celebrity. It was God's will that gave him the ability to wrestle with such flare that he has been able to enter the realm of the public eye from the realm of the wrestling world. Jon Kano smokes, drinks, and womanizes. He drives too fast, doesn't wear his seatbelt, and he swears. Yet parents still don't have the balls to tell their children not to watch Jon Kano on television. Maybe it is time for Jon Kano to give the parents in the world a reality check.~ *The scene opens up during an edition of the American Broadcasting Company (ABC)'s late night news program, Nightline. Ted Koppel, the hairpieced, aging host of Nightline, is the first man seen as he begins the show after the typical Nightline lead-in talking about role-models in America. He then introduces his guests for the evening, first a woman representing the Parent's Council, second, a man representing the Television's Council, and third, Jon Kano, representing the correct opinion. Kano is joining Nightline via satelitte from his home in Las Vegas, Nevada. In the background of Kano's picture, you can see various PCW paraphenalia promoting the upcoming March Madness Pay Per View.* Koppel: Let me start with you, Mr. Kano, since you are probably the most publically recognized man on this program right now. You have been very vocal as of late on you not being a role model. Why? Kano: Mr. Koppel, anyone who knows me would never want their children to admire me. What you see on television is me. I am not a different man off-camera. I smoke like a chimney on and off camera. *Suddenly the woman representing The Parents' Council interrupts Kano.* Woman: And that is one of the reasons that we at The Parents' Council put you on our top ten worst role model list. Smoking is evil, Mr. Kano and we do not appreciate your smoking on national television. *In response, Kano is seen lighting a cigarette.* Kano: Ohhhhh, how sweet the smoke is. I really don't care what you at the Parents' Council think of me. I don't give two sh*ts, a f*ck, and a God damn what any one thinks of me. Yeah, I drink, I smoke, and I swear. Personal choices that I made on my own will with a clear mind many years ago. Woman: And what about your recent gun incident in Mexico? *Kano chuckles to himself.* Kano: Hey, the little bastard was trying to rob me, all I did was show him that I was packing heat. Perfectly legal. I have all the paperwork on me at all times showing that I have all of the necessary permits to carry a concealed weapon. I am a public figure who many people know. Some of those people would like to do bodily harm to myself. I have a God-given right to protect myself and I have a legal right to protect myself. So damn you, Miss Jenkins, for judging me for defending myself. Miss Jenkins: I take offense to that. Kano: I don't care. I never asked to be a role model, I never wanted to be a role model, but you sick f*cks in the public thrust me into this compromising position where I am supposed to be a good little choir boy who never does any wrong. I refuse to accept the responsibility. Miss Jenkins: And who was your role models growing up, Mr. Kano, Satan? Kano: Ok, now you have pissed me off. I had two role models growing up and they both told me the same thing, to be myself at all times and to never front someone that is not me. Those two role models were my brother and my father, two of the greatest men I have ever had the pleasure of knowing. Koppel: I guess I should take this opportunity to take a commercial break. *A commercial for PCW MARCH MADNESS plays.* Koppel: It has been awhile since a guest has ended their feed from us on this program, but during the commercial, Miss Jenkins from the Parents' Council has ended her feed out of frustration with her recent conversation with Mr. Kano. Mr. Gentry, you represent the Television Council, what do you believe about role models today? Mr. Gentry: Well, I personally think that children should look to their parents as role models and not look to public figures as role models. Kano: TESTIFY!!!!!! Mr. Gentry: I guess that means we agree. Kano: Yeah, I mean why should I, as a wrestler, be a role model? Doesn't that half-way condone violence? Mr. Gentry: Yes. Koppel: I hate to interrupt, but the program is almost over, and is there anything else you two men would like to say? Mr. Gentry: No. Kano: Yes, I would like to say something. Koppel: Mr. Kano, go ahead. Kano: I never asked to be a role model. This Tuesday, I take on three other men who are not role models in the PCW ring. I am a bastard as are the other three men. One of the men, Brock Newbludd, is as old-school PCW as I am, but he is great tag-team wrestler. I am a great singles wrestler. Brock is probably the greatest tag team wrestler in the world, but I am one of the greatest wrestlers to ever grace a PCW ring, period. Then we have the two others. I have beaten both of them in the last two weeks. I introduced a green rookie to PCW ring mat. He realized after our match that he was in the big leagues now. He realized that I am the essence of PCW. He realized the pain of a match with me. I am not some no-talent jobber, I am a REAL LEGEND. And then we have a man who believes that he is 'Amazing.' My ass. He wasn't even mediocre in our previous match. He gave the likes of Bad Battio, B. A. Bruiser, and Shadowman a bad name. He sucks. He is pathetic. I hope he rots in a bad part of a town for all eternity. Because after Tuesday, none of these three will ever be able to say that they defeated 'The Man Who Rocketh the Mosteth, The REAL LEGEND OF PCW,' Jon Kano. On Tuesday Turmoil, I shall show each and every one of these three wastes of flesh what REAL LEGENDS ARE MADE OF. *The scene fades to black as the Nightline program goes to its final commercial before Ted Koppel's final word.* ~With people like Jon Kano in the world, can there ever be a real role model in public figures? Hell no. Jon Kano is just a man who does what he wants and doesn't care about being a good role model for the little slimeballs known as children.~
