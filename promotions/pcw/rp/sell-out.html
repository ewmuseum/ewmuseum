Sell Out.

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: not entered Ding-a-ling-a-ling! We rejoin our Big and Bold hero, Big Alan Steele, as well as his faithful companion, The Masochist, as they wearily drag themselves through the front door of the notorious superhero hang-out, The Steak-Out Diner, 24 hours-Breakfast Anytime. Looking beat up and disheveled from a long night of work, they prop their tired bodies up to the diner counter, just as the first rays of light peek over the horizon, stained red by the morning. A gruff waitress who may or may not have been a member of the 1988 Female German Swim team saunters over, ashing directly in front of them on the counter top. She grunts, and the two men take their queue. “I’ll have a large coffee, black,” slurs the Masochist. Steele grins at the waitress, “And I would like to procure some sweet orange nectar from yon fine establishment!!” “Well, well, well… what do we have here?” Big Al and the Masochist swivel in unison on their stools to come face-to-face with the Red Pope, his flowing crimson vestibules and pontiff hat matching the rising color in his cheeks. The holy hero’s knuckles are turning white as they grip his Staff of Pope-litude. His lips curl back in a vicious snarl as he continues his sermon, while behind him, a small group of spandex bristles angrily. “I had hoped to never see a wretch such as yourself come in here…” Big Al smiles that disarming smile of his, “Greetings, bastion of a false idol! Tis wonderful to meet you again on a neural ground! You of course remember the Lord of Pain…” The Red Pope screams in rage and leaps for Big Al’s throat. Fortunately, a great hulking man-beast in tattered clothing, hideous plumes of black hair sprouting from exposed skin, grabs the pontiff’s cape and reels him back. Another man dressed in a typical red, white and blue latex uniform adorned with golden stars steps forward, acid on his tongue. “I have a mind to make Super Maul allow the Red Pope at your unworthy hide.” “Greetings, Captain Latex! Tis I, Big Alan Steele…” “We know who you are,” spits out Captain Latex, “Only we have a different name for you.” “Oh? And what, pray tell, may that name be?” A Mexican Lucha in a sterling white mask rises, pointing an accusing finger into Steele’s chest. “The name it is being of a ‘Sell Out,’ a most offensive term, yes?” Steele’s face assumes one of concern, “A sell out? By the gods!!” “LET ME AT HIM!! I’ll show him how many gods there are!!” screams an enraged Red Pope. “SHHHH!!” shushes a drab female in a gray suit, the Librarian, “You aren’t helping things.” Big Al’s eyes narrow. “A sell out? I find this moniker most offensive indeed, and I demand an explanation post haste!!” “You disgust me!” growls Captain Latex, “Mostly because you don’t even REALIZE you are a sell out! Well, I’ll explain it to you.” The Captain takes a deep breath. “Our powers are unique to this world. We come equipped with unprecedented attributes never before seen in evolution. Thusly, everyone imbued with these powers must make a choice…” “Then I see not the problem,” speaks Al plainly, “As I have made the conscientious decision to follow the path of good and righteousness…” “WRONG!!” shouts the Latex hero, “Good and evil is IRRELEVANT, as we essentially cancel each other out. No, the decision is far, far more equitable…” “Equitable?” “Yes!!” Captain Latex rears back, eyes shooting daggers at Steele as he basks in the full support of his fellow heroes. “You use your powers DIRECTLY for monetary gain!!” Latex nods, as if delivering the final blow, his compatriots patting him on the back in encouragement. Steele furrows his brow in confusion. “How so?” Truck Punch, a trucker caught in a horrible accident which left him without a right arm, but luckily was found by a mad scientist who replaced the arm with a cybernetically enhanced model, rushes up, brandishing his gleaming fist menacingly. “You wrestle against normal people!! You have a clear advantage over them!!” “I beg your pardon,” snickers Big Al, “But tis not I that has superhuman attributes! Tis my faith in the just and true gods of Olympia that give me strength, not some abominable accident, radioactive spill, or even a graphic mutation. Just faith in the old gods.” “LET ME AT HIM!!!” squeals Red Pope as he strains against the silent Maul. “Liar!!” accuses the Justice Cheerleader, Mercy Candor. “It’s true,” reiterates The Masochist, as he sips at his coffee, “I am actually the superhero here, on account of my unnatural abilities to absorb pain.” “No!” shouts Captain Latex, “No! No! No! No! You don’t GET it!! You fight super-villains!” “Indeed I do!” “Then you are a superhero! End of story! You have no place wrestling against common people!” “What then, good heroes, is a demi-god to do? I have been given a holy mission, ordained as right and just to lead PCW! Shall I go against the wishes of the gods?” “You violate the unwritten rules of superheroes! We aren’t supposed to make money directly off our lifestyle choice!” The Masochist raises a questioning finger, “Wait a minute… what about those other guys. You know… Superman, batman, Spiderman, Wonder Woman… what about them? They make fistfuls of money off being superheroes.” The Art House Chameleon, able to blend in any post-impressionist environment, materializes before the two. “You don’t get it… they make their money indirectly through comic book deals. It just snowballs from there. That’s why you don’t see them fight crime anymore. They made their money. They don’t even have to act!” “Ah, the goal of every superhero…” fantasizes the Truck Punch. “I hear Superman is running for the State House of Indiana,” the Librarian quotes. “The goal of EVERY superhero?!” mocks Big Al, “You pander to the comic book set in order to fixate a deal to bring in money?! I am ashamed of my brethren this day! We should be working to stamp out crime in all forms, most notably the Hades-spawned demons that cabal to destroy the very world we live in! Not to get paid, as it were!” “Do you think it’s easy being us?” a visibly shaken Captain Latex begins, “Oh, don’t worry… we definitely fight crime! But do we get credit? No! Do we get enough money to feed our families? NO!! Being a superhero doesn’t pay much... in fact it pays NOTHING! Sure, once in a while we get a pat on the back from the mayor and a key to the city. Whoop-dee-shit. Those things aren’t worth the metal they are made of! Carrot Top has one, for crissakes!!” “Ooof!” cringes the Masochist, “That’s gotta bring down the value…” “But we do our jobs day in and day out, hoping that one day, a talent scout will notice us and give us that fat deal we need to make all our efforts worthwhile. A man can only survive on morals so long…” “BREAKING INTO ‘HULK’s’ MARKET NOT EASY!!” grumbles Super Maul. “Yeah!” agrees Truck Punch, “They said I wasn’t ‘marketable’ enough with my ‘trailer park’ image!!” “They claim I am not a real pope, yeah?!” seethes the Red Pope. Art House Chameleon weeps bitter, black tears onto the linoleum countertop, “I’m so tortured!” “Yeah! Get your Hi-Keeba-ed ass outta here!!” Steele frowns as he drains his OJ in one gulp, the Masochist following suit. Producing some bills from the folds of his toga, he turns to pay the waitress, who shuns his advance. “We don’t accept money from YOUR kind here.” Steele quickly raises an eyebrow, before gesturing to the Masochist to exit. The masochist leaves, and Steele follows, the angry stares watching him coolly. Outside, the Masochist smoothes out his t-shirt. “Whoa… that was some nasty business, huh?” The Masochist breaks into a wide grin, mirrored by his partner, Big Alan Steele. “They were more riled up than usual!!” “Indeed! Quite a humorous anecdote to a rough and long night! Truly a high point in hilarity!” “Man,” thinks the Masochist aloud, “Those guys were ready to throw down in there.” “Hmmm… indeed they were…” Cut to a grinning Big Al, pushing his way through the superheroes at the counter, seemingly oblivious to the angry faces. “More OJ!! Hi-Keeba!!”
