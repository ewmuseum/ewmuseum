I Fought with My Heart

22 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/22/02 I Fought with My Heart Sunday, December 21st. On a day where I had everything in the world running through my mind, I managed to defeat this large obstacle in my way, that’s been ruining my spirit. I came in knowing, what I had to do, but I wasn’t sure that I was fit to do it. “I had my doubts.” Sometimes I felt like I’d let the world down. Little training and little faith followed me as I made my way to the ring that night. My face may have been smiles, but my soul was nothing but tears. “I didn’t think I could do it.” I had believed in myself throughout Vilas and my whole conflict. But right as crunch time hit everything changed. I went from a confident man, to a scared man on the run for my own life. I kept thinking about myself and how I would fair, in this struggle for freedom, that I almost forgot about the so many supporters I had cheering for me to win. “I remembered.” I remembered the people who believed in me, the children who hung my poster in their room, the woman that craved to be in my life and have my children. So many people wanted to see me give it my all, but I had let them down. I came into this with my head in a completely other planet. And as I was their walking down the ramp, I said to myself in a soft humble way, “I’m sorry”. “Then I realized.” It wasn’t over it hadn’t even begun. Training helps and planning is strong, but until it’s time to let it all on the loose, nothing is decided. I looked over at my opponent and then I thought to myself. “Is he feeling these same feelings?” I smiled at the fact, that he might have been just as scared. He didn’t know what was coming. I could flip this thing around and still make everyone’s support not be meaningless. Mark my words; I was going to give it my all. Suddenly the little training and little faith following me to the ring left my arm. I walked a new free man, and I felt different. “Everything changed.” I had a chance, now if I could just give it my all and be determined. I could succeed. I so very wanted to succeed, because lately success had not entered my mind. I went my whole life never feeling happiness, and maybe now I could finally feel an inch of it. Would the hard shape of my face dare conform into a smile? How much I would love to relive that moment over and over. “I still can.” And all it takes is me willing to lay out every ounce of my body and soul in the ring. I could hold nothing back, and I could not show remorse. This was not only for myself, but everyone. I was willing to let others rejoice in my accomplishments. I do not want to segregate myself forever. I want to be embraced and loved by many. “I was ready.” I entered that ring with a confident smirk. Not a fake one this time, but one I really felt. I looked Vilas in the eyes and saw doubt within him. Like a lion chasing his prey, I jumped on it. I knew he was worried. He had never been in a match of such magnitude, and neither had I. My first instinct was “Hit him”. And then I saw everyone by his side. Suddenly I forgot everything he ever said to enrage me. I looked at the men and woman cheering for him, than I looked at those cheering for me. I noticed his flag rose in the air, and then I noticed mine. I saw him bouncing up and down on his toes, and as did I. It was at that point when I realized we’re all human. “I extended my hand.” It wasn’t just to show I respected him, but I did it because I wanted him to know that he’s not the only one who was scared. Not a single man walks into the ring without an ounce of fear, anyone saying any different is just straight out lying. “I threw it all out there.” When the bell rang, I left nothing to hide. The world saw everything I was made of and so did Vilas. I had my ups and I had my downs. But in the end the ups overruled. I was in a trance, and nothing could stop it. “They cheered.” I looked around and saw everyone cheering. I felt embraced and suddenly cleansed. Nothing could stop me now I was ready to win. It was on the last move where I really felt the self-satisfaction. I dropped him and heard the count. It was a hard echo of: 1!2!3! I had done it I had won. A feeling that will never be duplicated, but hopefully I can feel something similar to it in the future.
