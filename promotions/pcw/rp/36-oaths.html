36-Oaths

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Some Time Ago Have you ever wondered what the infamous 36-Oaths are? Did you ever wonder just what they did to become a full-fledged Black Dragon member? Well you don’t have to wait another second! The following is brought to you by Black Dragon Inc. let the show begin… There they are three kids about to become men. Some boys would have sex to become a man while others would have their first beer, but these three among many others have decided to make a choice… To be or not to be a Black Dragon! Chiang Ho Nam, Chen Lei Chia, and Chi’ing are all in their respective Chinese cities waiting to undergo the trials of the 36 Oaths. A young Chiang Ho Nam stands outside a large abandoned warehouse down in Shanghai wearing torn blue jeans and a stain covered t-shirt filled with holes. The young 12 year old is standing there next to a large man in a red velvet monastery like robe hiding his identity with a black dragon spitting fire stitched on the back of it. The other two, Chen and Chi are both standing outside similar abandoned warehouses wearing similar clothing with similar ominous looking robe clad men standing next to them. Chen’s waiting down in Fuzhou while Chi to the west at Xi’an. ‘Are you ready to undertake the 36 Oaths?’ asks the ominous man in the robes asks. With a nod of their heads, the three boys get ready to take their step into manhood. Chiang Ho Nam will now be the focus as we follow him through the trials he underwent to become a Black Dragon. ‘If you are seriously ready then proceed…’ the ominous man replies. The double doors to abandoned warehouse slide open and reveal a dark and smoky interior. Little Chiang cautiously walks in and as he enters the doors slam shut behind him causing him to jump around in fright. When he turns back around a small torch is mysteriously ignited revealing that Chiang is standing in a small room and that the warehouse is divided into many similar small rooms. This room has a large stone water filled basin in the middle of it and a pair of red velvet robes with the similar black dragon stitching on the back of it. There is a sign written in Chinese hanging from the ceiling above the basin. “Trial One: Prepared” Chiang walks up to the basin stripping himself of his ragged excuse of clothes. He stands behind the basin buck-naked reaching in and taking a sponge soaked with the mysterious water out. He looks at the sponge for a second and then begins to cleanse himself by draining the sponge over his head and body. ‘With this Black Dragon holy water I cleanse myself from my previous life washing away all my previous problems and all my previous family…’ Chiang recites aloud. After the bath he walks over and puts the red velvet robe on tying it around his waist but leaving the hood hanging on his shoulders. ‘With this robe I proclaim my desire and dedication to the Black Dragons…’ Chiang announces aloud. ‘You may proceed…’ the ominous voice proclaims aloud from the shadows above. Chiang continues through a wooden door into the next room, which holds an empty stone basin and a dagger sitting on some bandages next to it. A sign hangs above reading “Trial Two: Blood Brothers” as Chiang walks up to the empty basin he notices the basin is stained by some sort of red liquid. Chiang folds the sleeve of his robe back exposing his right wrist and forearm. He then takes the dagger in his left hand placing the blade above his exposed wrist away from any vital or deadly veins. He cuts across with the dagger as he recites. ‘I offer my blood to the Black Dragons and swear my loyalty to the Dragon family…’ he grimaces with every word. ‘With my blood I offer to be a Black Dragon till the day I die!’ Chiang fills the basin with his own blood until it reaches a certain point. He then reaches over dropping the dagger to the ground and picking up the bandages. The weary Chiang tightly wraps his arm with the bandage quelling the flow of blood and unfolding the robe re-covering his arm. ‘You may proceed…’ the ominous speaker proclaims. Chiang makes his weary way through the door and into the third room. This third room has a bamboo hoop hanging vertically from a string. A nearby sign reads, “Trial Three: Re-Birth.” Chiang walks up to the hoop and stands ready clearing his throat. ‘By stepping through this hoop I, Chiang Ho Nam, am re-born into the Black Dragon family!’ Chiang says as loud as he can muster. Chiang tries to get himself through the small hoola hoop sized bamboo hoop. After minutes of trying he finally gets to the other side. As he gets to the other side he raises the hood of his robe completely hiding his face. ‘Continue my Black Dragon brother!’ the ominous voice announces with a bit of acceptance in his stern voice. Chiang makes his way through a pair of wooden doors with golden knobs to a well light large room that is adorned in beautiful tapestries and in the center of the room is a small set of stairs leading to a stone chair where a man is sitting comfortably in a black velvet robe with a black dragon outlined in gold stitched on the back of it. There are numerous men in red velvet robes standing in two columns leading to the man on the chair. The man on the chair says, ‘My name in Pai Po, I am known as the Dragon Head or the Mountain Lord…’ Chiang and the rest of the men in attendance get down and bow to the leader. Chiang looks up from under his cowl as he hears a rooster cawing nearby. He sees two men walking in from the sides of the room, one man is holding a rooster by it’s neck in one hand and an axe in the other while the other is holding a large bowl and a bottle of red wine. ‘Rise…’ The Dragon Head demands. ‘Chiang Ho Nam, you completed three of the four trials so let’s not waste anymore time and proceed with the final trial.’ The two men with the wine, bowl, axe and rooster make their way to Chiang who pulls back his hood and stands ready. Chiang takes the axe in one hand as the man with the rooster holds the rooster down on a chopping block that a nearby Dragon member brought over. The man with the bowl and bottle of red wine places the bowl on the ground at his feet and proceeds to empty the bottle of wine into the bowl. He then leaves the bottle on the floor and takes the bowl in both hands holding it out in front of him. With one quick swipe the rooster is beheaded and it begins to squirm in the man’s hands as its life force fades away. Chiang then holds the bleeding rooster in his hands over the bowl of red wine letting the blood of the rooster mix with the red wine. As the bowl is filled with the mixture Chiang hands the dead rooster to the nearby man who brought it. ‘By drinking this I become a full fledged Black Dragon!’ Chiang announces. Chiang takes the bowl and begins to drink drowning the horrible liquid swallow for swallow. He finishes with a long loud belch with half the liquid remaining. He then hands the bowl to his new sworn brother and he takes a drink of his own. Soon everyone in the room has taken a drink with the Dragon Head Pai Po being the last one to finish it off. Chiang walks up the steps and kneels down on the first step beneath the Dragon Head. He is a little light headed from the loss of blood earlier and now the mixture of red wine and rooster blood. But he shakes his head and tries his hardest to show the respect that the Dragon Head should have. ‘There is one last thing that I must do…’ the Dragon Head announces. ‘You must take the 36 Oaths.’ Chiang nods his head signaling the Dragon Head to begin with the reciting of the Thirty Six Oaths. “After entering the Dragon gates I must treat the parents and relatives of my sworn brothers as my own kin. I shall suffer death by five thunderbolts if I do not keep this oath.” “I shall assist my sworn brothers to bury their parents and brothers by offering financial or physical assistance. I shall be killed by five thunderbolts if I pretend to have no knowledge of their troubles.” “When Dragon brothers visit my house, I shall provide them with board and lodging. I shall be killed by myriads of knives if I treat them as strangers.” “I will always acknowledge my Dragon brothers when they identify themselves. If I ignore them I will be killed by myriads of swords.” “I shall not disclose the secrets of the Dragon family, not even to my parents, brothers, or wife. I shall never disclose the secrets for money. I will be killed by myriads of swords if I do so.” “I shall never betray my sworn brothers. If, through a misunderstanding, I have caused the arrest of one of my brothers I must release him immediately. If I break this oath I will be killed by five thunderbolts.” “I will offer financial assistance to sworn brothers who are in trouble in order that they may pay their passage fee, etc. If I break this oath I will be killed by five thunderbolts.” “I must never cause harm or bring trouble to my sworn brothers or Mountain Lord. If I do so I will be killed by myriads of swords.” “I must never commit any indecent assaults on the wives, sisters, or daughters, of my sworn brothers. I shall be killed by five thunderbolts if I break this oath.” “I shall never embezzle cash or property from my sworn brothers. If I break this oath I will be killed by myriads of swords.” “I will take good care of the wives or children of sworn brothers entrusted to my keeping. If I do not I will be killed by five thunderbolts.” “If I have supplied false particulars about myself for the purpose of joining the Dragon family I shall be killed by five thunderbolts.” “If I should change my mind and deny my membership of the Dragon family I will be killed by myriads of swords.” “If I rob a sworn brother or assist an outsider to do so I will be killed by five thunderbolts.” “If I should take advantage of a sworn brother or force unfair business deals upon him I will be killed by myriads of swords.” “If I knowingly convert my sworn brother's cash or property to my own use I shall be killed by five thunderbolts.” “If I have wrongly taken a sworn brother's cash or property during a robbery I must return them to him. If I do not I will be killed by five thunderbolts.” “If I am arrested after committing an offence I must accept my punishment and not try to place blame on my sworn brothers. If I do so I will be killed by five thunderbolts.” “If any of my sworn brothers are killed, or arrested, or have departed to some other place, I will assist their wives and children who may be in need. If I pretend to have no knowledge of their difficulties I will be killed by five thunderbolts.” “When any of my sworn brothers have been assaulted or blamed by others, I must come forward and help him if he is in the right or advise him to desist if he is wrong. If others have repeatedly insulted him I shall inform our other brothers and arrange to help him physically or financially. If I do not keep this oath I will be killed by five thunderbolts.” “If it comes to my knowledge that the Government is seeking any of my sworn brothers who has come from other provinces or from overseas, I shall immediately inform him in order that he may make his escape. If I break this oath I will be killed by five thunderbolts.” “I must not conspire with outsiders to cheat my sworn brothers at gambling. If I do so I will be killed by myriads of swords.” “I shall not cause discord amongst my sworn brothers by spreading false reports about any of them. If I do so I will be killed by myriads of swords.” “I shall not appoint myself as Dragon Enforcer without authority. After entering the Dragon gates for three years the loyal and faithful ones may be promoted by the Dragon Enforcer with the support of his sworn brothers. I shall be killed by five thunderbolts if I make any unauthorized promotions myself.” “If my natural brothers are involved in a dispute or law suit with my sworn brothers I must not help either party against the other but must attempt to have the matter settled amicably. If I break this oath I will be killed by five thunderbolts.” “After entering the Dragon gates I must forget any previous grudges I may have borne against my sworn brothers. If I do not do so I will be killed by five thunderbolts.” “I must not trespass upon the territory occupied by my sworn brothers. I shall be killed by five thunderbolts if I pretend to have no knowledge of my brothers' rights in such matters.” “I must not covet or seek to share any property or cash obtained by my sworn brothers. If I have such ideas I will be killed.” “I must not disclose any address where my sworn brothers keep their wealth nor must I conspire to make wrong use of such knowledge. If I do so I will be killed by myriads of swords.” “I must not give support to outsiders if so doing is against the interests of any of my sworn brothers. If I do not keep this oath I will be killed by myriads of swords.” “I must not take advantage of the Dragon brotherhood in order to oppress or take violent or unreasonable advantage of others. I must be content and honest. If I break this oath I will be killed by five thunderbolts.” “I shall be killed by five thunderbolts if I behave indecently towards small children of my sworn brothers' families.” “If any of my sworn brothers has committed a big offence I must not inform upon them to the Government for the purposes of obtaining a reward. I shall be killed by five thunderbolts if I break this oath.” “I must not take to myself the wives and concubines of my sworn brothers nor commit adultery with them. If I do so I will be killed by myriads of swords.” “I must never reveal Dragon secrets or signs when speaking to outsiders. If I do so I will be killed by myriads of swords.” “After entering the Dragons I must remain loyal and dedicated to my sworn brothers and the Dragon Head himself. I must follow every command given to me by my superiors. If I become disloyal or turn my back on the Dragon family may I die by a hundred days of torture!”
