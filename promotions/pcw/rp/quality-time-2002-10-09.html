Quality Time

9 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/9/02 Just when it seems like someone is making a comeback, the tide turns just slamming them back into the unforgiving ocean. One prime example of this is with Kramer Wild Wing. Just when it seems as though he is back on track, he slips off going back to the bottom. It's been a very LONG time since he had made an impact and stuck around. Anymore you see him in a match every week or so and that's it. It's like he doesn't even care about his career anymore as the losses keep piling up and it doesn't bother him. In a few short hours, Kramer gets the chance to step back into the spotlight as he goes head to head with Loc and Mandrake for the number one contendership to the Cruiserweight Title. If Kramer manages to secure a victory, it opens up a glorious path to much oppertunity. But if he continues down the wrong path which he has constantly taken, could this mean that the end is near for the career of the Wild One? The scene opens up, and we see a small room with a dresser off to the side, a bed, and a few other small pieces of furniture which are settled atop a nice thick beige carpeting which goes quite well with the dark grain colored walls. There is no single person to be found in the room, but we do here talking coming from outside of the door. The talking gets closer and closer until the door is pushed open. In walks Walter with a black and green jogging outfit on. As he steps deeper into the room he wipes away some collected sweat from above his brow as he takes a seat at the end of the bed making loud panting noises, trying desperately to catch his breath. Walter: You...know...it's been a very long time since I ran. The person he is talking to steps into the room with his arms to his side bearing the exact same outfit as Walter. The figure goes to sit down next to him and reveals himself as Kramer. Kramer too wipes away sweat but he doesn't seem as out of breath as his father. Kramer: I think it would do you good to get out in the fresh air a little more often. Walter: One thing is for sure. It beats staying up in this crammed room all of the time. Kramer: You know I already said that if you wanted to stay at my house you are more than welcome. Walter: I don't want to be an inconvenience. Kramer: Don't worry about it! We can add on rooms if you really want to. There is a long and collective pause as Walter stands up and peers out the window of his room. He then turns halfway towards Kramer as he sits on the ledge of the window. Walter: No offense son, but the reason I left in the first place was to get away from you. I mean you're a middle aged adult and don't need your funky old father around to bug you. Kramer: Why don't I see that as the real reason? Walter: What do you mean? Kramer repositions himself on the bed as he looks down on his shoes trying hard not to make eye contact. Kramer: It just doesn't add up. I mean never once have you been a bother to me and you know that. If you really wanted to stay you know I would bring you over in a split second without hesitating to think about it. Walter: Well.... Before Walter can throw out the reply, an older man passes by the door and Walter jumps up and rushes over towards him, as fast as his wrinkly body can take him. Walter: Joe are you still up for that game of poker you promised me? Joe: Of course Walter! Bring all of your money because I plan on making you bankrupt. Walker: You sure can talk the talk, but don't consider yourself too lucky. Both men walk down the hall leaving Kramer in awe all by himself. There he was having a conversation with his father, and his dad just ditches him like it's nothing. Kramer is a tad infuriated at first, but lets it slip away as he lies back on the bed looking up to the ceiling with arms crossed over his stomach. Kramer: Gee thanks dad. That was yet another awesome father son talk. Oh well I was gonna leave soon anyway. Kramer sits up and steps out of the room and into the long narrow corridor. He takes a few steps out and crosses over to a candy machine. He reaches into his wallet, pulls out a crisp one dollar billy, and pushes it into the slot selecting some form of chocolate bar. He pulls it out and unwraps it shoving it into his mouth, soothing his starving stomach. Instead of continue the walk down the corridor, he leans against the side of the vending machine. The silence and solitude causes his mind to start wondering on about his match on Turmoil. I finally get a chance at another title. It's been so long since I had last had that feeling of such great victory. The feeling of gold around your waist, and millions of people admiring all of your hard work. It was so awesome to be champion. But then I screwed it up and now am considered a laughing stock to the whole of PCW. Maybe this time will be different, although before I start thinking about the gold, I have to worry about beating Mandrake and Loc which won't be an easy task. His thoughts on the subject come to an abrubt stop as an older woman stops in front of the candy machine disturbing the quiet atmosphere. Kramer walks down the corridor but suddenly stops shoving his right hand into his pocket. He obviously doesn't find what he is looking for as he comes out empty handed. Kramer: I left my keys on the bed. He rushes back to the room and steps back inside seeing his keys on the bed. He walks over and grabs them, but as he does his foot kicks something from underneath the bed. Being the curious person he is, Kramer reaches down and pulls a small wooden box from beneath the bed. It has a lock on it, but as Kramer jerks on it it comes off easily. Kramer pulls the wooden lid back and the thing inside brings a tear to his eye. The camera pans up and over Kramer's shoulder, but before we can see what caused the tears, the scene fades to black!
