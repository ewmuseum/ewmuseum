The Better Parent

17 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/17/03 Seventy degrees with the sun shinning overhead and not a cloud in sight. It seemed like a perfect day as I lounged on the back porch and bathed in the unusual weather for this early in the year. In the backyard the three children I had custody of played a friendly game of baseball but they were not satisfied with the numbers they had. Quite obviously with only three people to play a game the teams would not be fair or equal. With only Chris and me to resort to they looked up at the porch and immediately eyed me and began to charge like a wild herd. “Tessa!” All three of them shouted at once, each one trying to shout louder than the other. “Oh no, don’t even think of asking. Last time I played baseball with you guys I almost got hit square in the face with that damn ball.” My answer was clearly a no which caused them to stomp through the back door and inside of the house as the marched in an unorganized line towards the living room where Chris was studying the television screen which displayed a previous match that I had won again Tim Walden not too long ago. Instead of studying me though Chris was much more interested in Tim’s logic and his in ring philosophy, so into it that he did not notice the group of children getting ready to stampede him with the same question that they had asked me not too long ago. “Chris will you play baseball with us?” The oldest of the three, Alex, questioned as he tapped Chris gently on the shoulder. “Maybe later.” Chris answered robotically as he kept his eyes fixed on the screen, not bothering to look over at the child asking him the question. Instead of putting up a plea the group of children simply accepted it as an answer and walked off in the direction of the backyard once again. “You two are horrible at baseball anyways so I’ll just play against you two and still win.” Alex explained as he walked out of the house and onto the back porch with bat in hand as the younger two simply shrugged and accepted his proposal as a fair one even if his words were bashing them both. “We will see.” Was the only response that Christa could come up with to take up for the weaker side of both her and Evan’s disadvantage despite being a team of two going up against a team of one. “I could always even the odds.” A mysterious voice called out from the side of the house. Following the voice footsteps could be heard splashing through the mud and grass. Looking over in the direction of the voice I noticed the face of a person I had not seen in years, the face of Christa’s and Alex’s biological mother, the ex wife of my belated brother. Oh my…what is she doing here? The focus of my mind suddenly shifted from the unusual weather to this woman who I thought was nothing more than a mere image of the past. Why was she here and what did she want? Surely just staring at her and watching her interact with the children after all these years wouldn’t answer any questions but I could tell that Alex was just as discontent with this surprise visit as I was. “Mom?” Christa questioned as her blue eyes widened with the confusion that we had all been feeling. Was it really the woman who had walked out on Christa and Alex three long years before and why did she decide to come back now of all times? “Of course, who else would it be?” The slender woman answered as she walked over to Christa and embraced her with a hug before turning to Alex who simply shunned her by walking away before she got the opportunity to share the same show of affection with him. “I don’t want to play anymore.” Alex huffed as he tossed the wooden bat aside and walked up the steps that led back inside of the house. His disapproval of his mother’s sudden return was obvious and even she could tell that he understandably wanted nothing to do with this woman who had deserted his father, his sister, and him back while he was the young age of seven. “Well we can still play.” The woman from the past said to Christa and Evan as she walked over to the fallen bat and brought it into her own grasp as she smiled in Christa’s direction. Instead of smiling back Christa’s face remained the same as it had since the woman had made her reappearance, puzzled and confused as two what was going on. “Mom, why are you here?” Christa asked as she walked over to the woman who had given birth to her and looked her into the eye as if she were trying to make her mother feel guilty for walking out on her three long years ago. “To see you and Alex of course, silly.” Allison chuckled as she ran her hand through Christa’s head of brown hair. “Why do you want to see us now?” Christa questioned as she pulled her head away from her mother and persisted questions that she was famous for. “Well I was wondering if maybe you and Alex wanted to come live with me again. It will be just like before, only without daddy.” Allison addressed Christa’s questioned with all the sincerity she could pull out, which was enough to convince Christa that it might be a cool idea to try, but what idea couldn’t win a mischievous eight year old girl over? “Excuse me?” I cut in as I hoped up from my seat on porch step and walked over to the young girl and her mother. “Last time I checked I was the one who had the say in where these children go and just who they go with.” “Perhaps, but last time I checked they came from my womb, not yours.” Allison smirked at her own cocky response knowing very well that I could not argue that point and that even though I had taken care of these children for six months now should the two of us go to court for a battle for custody, she’d more than likely win without a struggle being the blood mother and all. “You know Christa; perhaps you and Evan should head in and get ready for dinner while I talk to your mother alone for a moment.” I said as I placed my hand on Christa’s back and directed her in the direction of the porch. “Fine.” She sighed as she pulled Evan by the arm towards the house, not bothering to look back at Allison or me. As the back door, sealing the children off from the tension between us two the tension only continued to grow larger as each of our eyes locked contact with each other. “So what’s the real reason you are back?” I muttered under my breath feeling nothing but hate for the woman who had virtually destroyed my brother’s heart three years back. “Is there something wrong with wanting to see your own flesh and blood?” She snapped trying to make me out to be the bad guy. “There is when it’s for all the wrong reasons.” I retorted in a tone that was unusual coming from me, a person who supposedly wouldn’t hurt a fly unless provoked. Well to me Allison was this fly and should she make the wrong move I wouldn’t hesitate to swat at whether it be physically or psychologically. “And what are your reasons for wanting them around so bad?” She questioned as she continued her own type of mind games with me, trying to prove that she deserved custody of them just as much as I did. “They are just as much my own flesh and blood as they are yours. Let’s not forget that the other half of them is Windsor blood. Besides unlike you I actually have been there for them and I actually care for their well being.” Was the only kind of response I could come up with to prove my case. “Oh my dear Tessa, I knew you’d come up with some pathetic response like that to try to prove the kids should be yours which is why I planned this day long in advance since I knew you would not let go easily.” Smirking as she responds Allison fishes through her pocket and pulls out a small business card. She hands it to me before walking off towards the direction of which she came in. “Huh?” I responded in confusion as I looked down at the card puzzled as to why she would hand me such a card considering as far as I knew she did not run a business of her own. “Tell the story to your lawyer, because as far as I’m concerned P.W.A. and Lacuna are the least of your worries now if you love the children as much as you say you do. Because quite honestly if you don’t come up with a better reason for wanting the children, well let’s just say you’ll be left with only one kid.” Chuckling she walks away knowing very well that her mind games worked, very well at that. Staring down at the card I couldn’t help but feel my heart tighten up. What am I going to do? There is no way they are going to give me custody of them over their blood mother, especially if she comes up with a good story even if it is all bullshit. First P.W.A. and now this. What am I going to do…
